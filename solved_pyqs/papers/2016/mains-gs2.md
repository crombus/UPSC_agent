## Q1. Discuss the essentials of the 69th Constitutional Amendment Act and anomalies, if any, that have led to recent reported conflicts between the elected representatives and the institution of the Lieutenant Governor in the administration of Delhi. Do you think that this will give rise to a new trend in the functioning of the Indian federal politics?
**Primary route:** Polity → [Union Territories](../../topics/polity/union-territories.md) → National Capital Territory of Delhi · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 2016 · Mains · GS-II · Official Q1 · 12½ marks · 200 words · Difficulty: High · Directive: Discuss; Assess  
**Primary classification:** Polity → Union Territories → Delhi’s asymmetric constitutional design  
**Secondary classification:** Polity → Federalism → Elected government–Lieutenant Governor relations  
**Canonical owners:** [Union Territories](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Polity/advanced/25_Union-Territories.md) · [Federal System](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Polity/advanced/12_Federal-System.md)  
**Proposed topic slug:** `delhi-69th-amendment-lg-elected-government`

**Demand decoding:** State the Article 239AA architecture, identify the textual and institutional sources of conflict, and judge whether Delhi creates a wider federal precedent.

**Compact framework:** Constitutional design → anomalies → conflict mechanism → qualified federal prognosis.

### Model answer

The **69th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1991** inserted Articles **239AA–239AB**, making Delhi the National Capital Territory. It created a 70-member elected Assembly and a Council of Ministers, headed by the Chief Minister, to aid and advise the Lieutenant Governor (LG).

The Assembly may legislate on State and Concurrent List matters except **public order, police and land**. Parliament retains overriding legislative power; the President appoints the LG; and Article 239AA(4) permits the LG, in case of a “difference of opinion,” to refer a matter to the President and take urgent action pending decision.

Conflict arose because Delhi combines representative government with Union control:

- the boundary between matters requiring advice, concurrence or referral was unclear;
- control over services and senior officials was disputed;
- the LG’s referral power could be read either as exceptional or routine;
- the GNCTD Act, 1991 and business rules strengthened Union supervision;
- Delhi is neither a full State nor an ordinary Union Territory.

This need not become a general federal trend because Delhi’s national-capital status is exceptional. Yet routine obstruction by discretionary offices could sharpen tensions in other asymmetric arrangements.

**Conclusion:** Cooperative conventions, limited use of referral and clearer statutory allocation—not institutional supremacy—are essential.

**Why this earns marks:** It explains the precise constitutional compromise, traces conflict to its ambiguities and gives a qualified federal assessment bounded to the law as it stood in 2016.

## Q2. To what extent is Article 370 of the Indian Constitution, bearing marginal note “Temporary provision with respect to the State of Jammu and Kashmir”, temporary? Discuss the future prospects of this provision in the context of Indian polity.
**Primary route:** Polity → [Salient Features and Constitutional Parts](../../topics/polity/salient-features-and-constitutional-parts.md) → Article 370 and asymmetric federalism · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 2016 · Mains · GS-II · Official Q2 · 12½ marks · 200 words · Difficulty: High · Directive: Examine extent; Discuss prospects  
**Primary classification:** Polity → Special constitutional provisions → Jammu and Kashmir under Article 370  
**Secondary classification:** Polity → Federalism → Asymmetry and integration  
**Canonical owners:** [Federal System](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Polity/advanced/12_Federal-System.md) · [Salient Features](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Polity/basic/Salient-Features.md)  
**Proposed topic slug:** `article-370-temporariness-future-prospects-2016`

**Demand decoding:** Explain why the provision was labelled temporary, why it had acquired continuing operation by 2016, and assess its constitutional future without hindsight.

**Compact framework:** Original temporariness → legal endurance → integration through orders → prospective choices.

### Model answer

Article **370** was “temporary” because it addressed transitional conditions after Jammu and Kashmir’s accession, pending the State Constituent Assembly’s decision. Under Article 370(3), the President could declare it inoperative or modify it, but only on that Assembly’s recommendation.

Its temporariness was qualified:

- the State Constituent Assembly dissolved in **1957** without recommending abrogation;
- successive Presidential Orders under Article 370(1), especially the **Constitution (Application to Jammu and Kashmir) Order, 1954**, progressively extended most Union constitutional provisions;
- in *Sampat Prakash v. State of J&K* (1968), the Supreme Court rejected the claim that Article 370 ceased with the Constituent Assembly;
- it became both a conduit for integration and a symbol of autonomy.

Thus, by 2016 it was temporary in historical origin but continuing in legal operation. Unilateral deletion faced the textual obstacle that the recommending body no longer existed; constitutional change therefore demanded exceptional legal care and political legitimacy.

Future policy could preserve the arrangement, deepen consensual integration, or pursue constitutional alteration. Any course had to balance sovereignty and equality with federal trust, consent, security and accession history.

**Conclusion:** Article 370’s durable future depended less on its marginal note than on constitutional procedure, judicial interpretation and political reconciliation.

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes origin from operation, uses the controlling text and judgment, and avoids projecting post-2016 events backward.

## Q3. “The Indian party system is passing through a phase of transition which looks to be full of contradictions and paradoxes.” Discuss.
**Primary route:** Polity → [Federal System](../../topics/polity/federal-system.md) → Party competition and federalisation · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 2016 · Mains · GS-II · Official Q3 · 12½ marks · 200 words · Difficulty: Moderate · Directive: Discuss  
**Primary classification:** Polity → Political parties → Transition in the party system  
**Secondary classification:** Polity → Federalism → National and regional party competition  
**Canonical owner:** [Political Parties](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Polity/advanced/43_Political-Parties.md)  
**Proposed topic slug:** `indian-party-system-transition-paradoxes`

**Demand decoding:** Identify the direction of transition and explain why apparently opposing tendencies coexist rather than merely listing party-system defects.

**Compact framework:** Old system → new tendencies → paired paradoxes → democratic assessment.

### Model answer

India’s party system has moved from Congress dominance, through coalition-era fragmentation, towards renewed single-party predominance at the Union level after 2014. Yet this transition is neither linear nor uniform.

Its central contradictions are:

- **National concentration, regional resilience:** one party commands a Lok Sabha majority, while regional parties govern major States and shape Rajya Sabha arithmetic.
- **Leadership centralisation, social democratisation:** campaigns are leader-driven, but competition mobilises lower castes, regional identities, women and welfare constituencies.
- **Ideological polarisation, pragmatic alliances:** parties sharpen identity and development narratives while forming opportunistic coalitions with former rivals.
- **Organisational expansion, internal democratic deficit:** parties expand membership, yet candidate selection and succession remain centralised.
- **Stable mandate, fragmented voting:** first-past-the-post can convert a plurality of votes into a large seat majority.
- **Programmatic convergence, rhetorical conflict:** parties broadly accept welfare, markets and federal bargaining despite adversarial discourse.

Anti-defection rules promote stability but strengthen party leadership over representatives. Money power coexists with growing voter participation.

**Conclusion:** The transition is best described as competitive multi-level predominance: greater national concentration operates within a socially diverse and regionally federal party system. Its democratic quality depends on internal democracy, transparent finance and space for legitimate opposition.

**Why this earns marks:** It makes the “contradictions and paradoxes” analytical by pairing opposing trends and situating them in the 2016 party context.

## Q4. Exercise of CAG’s powers in relation to the accounts of the Union and the States is derived from Article 149 of the Indian Constitution. Discuss whether audit of the Government’s policy implementation could amount to overstepping its own (CAG) jurisdiction.
**Primary route:** Polity → [Comptroller and Auditor General](../../topics/polity/comptroller-and-auditor-general.md) → Performance audit and policy boundary · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 2016 · Mains · GS-II · Official Q4 · 12½ marks · 200 words · Difficulty: High · Directive: Discuss; Assess  
**Primary classification:** Polity → Constitutional bodies → CAG jurisdiction  
**Secondary classification:** Governance → Accountability → Performance audit  
**Canonical owner:** [Comptroller and Auditor General](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Polity/advanced/32_CAG.md)  
**Proposed topic slug:** `cag-performance-audit-policy-jurisdiction`

**Demand decoding:** Draw the line between auditing implementation and judging policy wisdom, using Article 149 and the CAG’s statutory mandate.

**Compact framework:** Legal mandate → legitimate performance audit → prohibited merits review → safeguards.

### Model answer

Article **149** authorises Parliament to prescribe the CAG’s powers. The **CAG’s (Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service) Act, 1971** permits audit of Union and State finances, government companies and substantially financed bodies.

Auditing policy implementation is not inherently an overreach. Once the legislature authorises expenditure for a stated policy, the CAG may examine:

- **legality and regularity**—whether money was spent under lawful authority;
- **propriety**—whether expenditure avoided waste and conflict of interest;
- **performance**—whether implementation achieved economy, efficiency and effectiveness;
- whether procurement, allocation and delivery followed declared criteria; and
- whether outcomes reported to Parliament are supported by evidence.

Such scrutiny enables legislative committees to enforce control over the purse. A sound policy may still be executed wastefully.

Overstepping occurs if the CAG substitutes its preferred policy, questions political objectives, or presents speculative loss as adjudicated liability. Policy choice belongs to the elected executive and legislature, subject to constitutional and judicial review.

The proper boundary is therefore between auditing **implementation against the government’s own law, objectives and standards** and deciding the intrinsic wisdom of those objectives. Transparent methodology, executive response and legislative examination guard that boundary.

**Conclusion:** Performance audit strengthens accountability; policy prescription would exceed the CAG’s constitutional role.

**Why this earns marks:** It reconciles accountability with democratic policy discretion through a precise implementation-versus-merits distinction.

## Q5. Discuss each adjective attached to the word ‘Republic’ in the ‘Preamble’. Are they defendable in the present circumstances?
**Primary route:** Polity → [Salient Features and Constitutional Parts](../../topics/polity/salient-features-and-constitutional-parts.md) → Preamble values · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 2016 · Mains · GS-II · Official Q5 · 12½ marks · 200 words · Difficulty: Moderate · Directive: Discuss; Evaluate  
**Primary classification:** Polity → Preamble → Sovereign, socialist, secular and democratic republic  
**Canonical owner:** [Preamble](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Polity/basic/Preamble.md)  
**Proposed topic slug:** `preamble-adjectives-republic-contemporary-validity`

**Demand decoding:** Explain every adjective preceding “Republic,” test each against constitutional practice, and give a balanced verdict.

**Compact framework:** Meaning of each term → contemporary evidence → deficits → defensibility.

### Model answer

The Preamble constitutes India a **Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic**. “Socialist” and “Secular” were added by the **42nd Amendment, 1976**.

- **Sovereign:** India possesses internal supremacy and external independence. Treaty commitments constrain choices voluntarily, though dependence can reduce practical autonomy.
- **Socialist:** Indian socialism is democratic and mixed-economy oriented, seeking distributive justice rather than State ownership of all production. Articles **38, 39 and 39A**, welfare programmes and affirmative action defend the ideal, though inequality, informal work and unequal public services expose a large implementation gap.
- **Secular:** The State has no official religion and must secure equal citizenship and principled distance. Articles **25–28** protect religious freedom. Communal mobilisation and selective State intervention challenge, but do not invalidate, the constitutional commitment.
- **Democratic:** Adult franchise, elections, judicial review and accountable government sustain democracy. Money power, criminalisation and exclusion diminish participation.
- **Republic:** Public offices are non-hereditary and authority derives from citizens; the elected President symbolises this principle.

In *S.R. Bommai* (1994), secularism was affirmed as part of the basic structure.

**Conclusion:** The adjectives remain legally and normatively defensible, but they operate as constitutional promises requiring continuous institutional fulfilment, not descriptions of perfection.

**Why this earns marks:** It defines every term, tests ideal against practice and reaches a qualified rather than rhetorical verdict.

## Q6. What was held in the Coelho case? In this context, can you say that judicial review is of key importance amongst the basic features of the Constitution?
**Primary route:** Polity → [Amendment and Basic Structure](../../topics/polity/amendment-and-basic-structure.md) → Ninth Schedule and judicial review · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 2016 · Mains · GS-II · Official Q6 · 12½ marks · 200 words · Difficulty: High · Directive: Explain; Assess  
**Primary classification:** Polity → Basic structure → Judicial review of Ninth Schedule laws  
**Secondary classification:** Polity → Fundamental Rights → Rights-impact test  
**Canonical owners:** [Amendment and Basic Structure](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Polity/basic/Amendment-and-Basic-Structure.md) · [Supreme Court](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Polity/advanced/18_Supreme-Court.md)  
**Proposed topic slug:** `ir-coelho-ninth-schedule-judicial-review`

**Demand decoding:** State the precise *I.R. Coelho* holding and explain why it makes judicial review central, while acknowledging its constitutional limits.

**Compact framework:** Background → holding → importance of review → democratic qualification.

### Model answer

In *I.R. Coelho v. State of Tamil Nadu* (2007), a nine-judge Bench considered whether laws placed in the **Ninth Schedule** under Article **31B** were immune from judicial review.

The Court held that:

- laws inserted into the Ninth Schedule **after 24 April 1973**, the date of *Kesavananda Bharati*, are reviewable;
- Article 31B cannot become a device for destroying the Constitution’s **basic structure**;
- the Court must examine the law’s actual effect on Fundamental Rights, especially the principles underlying Articles **14, 19 and 21**;
- if the rights infringement damages or destroys a basic-structure principle, the law is invalid despite Ninth Schedule protection.

Judicial review is of key importance because it preserves constitutional supremacy, limited government and enforceable rights. Articles **32, 226 and 13**, read with *Kesavananda Bharati*, *Minerva Mills* and *L. Chandra Kumar*, prevent transient majorities from becoming final judges of their own power. Without review, Parliament could place rights-violating laws beyond challenge.

Yet review is not judicial governance. Courts test constitutionality, not the comparative wisdom of policy.

**Conclusion:** *Coelho* confirms judicial review as a basic-feature safeguard that protects the identity of the Constitution while leaving legitimate policy choice to democratic institutions.

**Why this earns marks:** It gives the cut-off date, rights-impact test and constitutional rationale, then qualifies judicial power.

## Q7. Did the Government of India Act, 1935 lay down a federal constitution? Discuss.
**Primary route:** Modern History → [Colonial Government Structure and Constitutional Development](../../topics/modern-history/colonial-government-structure-and-constitutional-development.md) → Government of India Act, 1935 · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 2016 · Mains · GS-II · Official Q7 · 12½ marks · 200 words · Difficulty: Moderate · Directive: Discuss  
**Primary classification:** Polity → Constitutional development → Federal scheme of 1935  
**Secondary classification:** Modern History → Late colonial constitutional reforms  
**Canonical owner:** [Historical Background](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Polity/basic/Historical-Background.md)  
**Proposed topic slug:** `government-india-act-1935-federal-constitution`

**Demand decoding:** Distinguish the Act’s federal design from its non-implementation and unitary-colonial safeguards.

**Compact framework:** Federal features → missing federal conditions → operation → verdict.

### Model answer

The Government of India Act, **1935** designed an **All-India Federation** consisting of British Indian provinces and acceding princely States. In form, it contained important federal features:

- legislative powers were divided through Federal, Provincial and Concurrent Lists;
- provinces received autonomy and responsible ministries;
- a bicameral federal legislature was proposed;
- a Federal Court was established in 1937;
- written rules distributed authority between levels.

However, it did not establish a fully federal constitution in operation. The federation depended on accession by the requisite number of princely States, which never occurred. Consequently, only provincial autonomy came into force in 1937.

The design was also heavily colonial:

- the Governor-General retained overriding, ordinance, financial and emergency powers;
- dyarchy was proposed at the Centre;
- governors enjoyed special responsibilities and discretionary powers;
- the federal legislature was not fully sovereign or democratically constituted;
- princely representatives were to be nominated, not popularly elected;
- residuary powers rested with the Governor-General rather than either governmental tier.

The Act influenced India’s Constitution through lists, provincial administration and the Federal Court, but lacked responsible federal government.

**Conclusion:** The 1935 Act laid down a federal **scheme**, not an operative or genuinely democratic federation; it was federation subject to colonial safeguards.

**Why this earns marks:** It separates textual architecture, actual commencement and democratic substance, producing a precise qualified verdict.

## Q8. What is a quasi-judicial body? Explain with the help of concrete examples.
**Primary route:** Governance → [Regulatory Governance and Independent Regulators](../../topics/governance/regulatory-governance-and-independent-regulators.md) → Quasi-judicial bodies · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 2016 · Mains · GS-II · Official Q8 · 12½ marks · 200 words · Difficulty: Moderate · Directive: Define; Explain with examples  
**Primary classification:** Governance → Institutions → Quasi-judicial adjudication  
**Secondary classification:** Polity → Tribunals and regulators  
**Canonical owners:** [Regulatory Governance and Independent Regulators](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Governance/basic/11_Regulatory-Governance-and-Independent-Regulators.md) · [Administrative Tribunals](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Polity/advanced/46_Administrative-Tribunals.md)  
**Proposed topic slug:** `quasi-judicial-bodies-features-examples`

**Demand decoding:** Define by function rather than label, identify procedural characteristics and illustrate through bodies exercising determinative powers.

**Compact framework:** Definition → distinguishing features → concrete examples → safeguards.

### Model answer

A quasi-judicial body is an administrative, regulatory or statutory authority empowered to determine facts, apply legal standards and decide rights or liabilities, although it is not a regular court.

Its typical features are:

- jurisdiction conferred by Constitution or statute;
- power to issue notice, receive evidence and hear affected parties;
- duty to follow **natural justice**—absence of bias and fair hearing;
- reasoned determinations;
- procedural flexibility and subject expertise; and
- decisions ordinarily open to statutory appeal or judicial review under Articles **32, 136, 226 or 227**, as applicable.

Concrete examples include:

1. **National Green Tribunal:** decides environmental disputes, awards relief and applies principles such as precaution and polluter pays.
2. **Competition Commission of India:** investigates and determines anti-competitive agreements and abuse of dominance, imposing statutory remedies and penalties.
3. **Income-tax authorities:** assess liability after considering returns, evidence and objections.
4. **Information Commissions:** adjudicate RTI appeals and may penalise information officers.
5. **Election Commission:** acts quasi-judicially in symbol or disqualification disputes.

Such a body may also perform administrative or regulatory tasks; the nature of power is decisive.

**Conclusion:** Quasi-judicial bodies combine expertise and speed with adjudication, but independence, speaking orders and judicial supervision are essential for fairness.

**Why this earns marks:** It defines the category functionally, supplies varied statutory examples and states the due-process safeguards.

### Model answer

Amartya Sen treats education and health as **constitutive capabilities**, not residual welfare. They expand freedom directly and also raise productivity, gender equality and democratic participation.

**Primary education reforms**

- ensure neighbourhood schools and retention under Article **21A** and the RTE Act;
- shift from enrolment to foundational learning and remedial teaching;

## Q12. In the integrity index of Transparency International, India stands very low. Discuss briefly the legal, political, economic, social and cultural factors that have caused the decline of public morality in India.
**Primary route:** Governance → [Transparency, Accountability, Grievance Redress and Social Audit](../../topics/governance/transparency-accountability-grievance-redress-and-social-audit.md) → Corruption and public morality · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 2016 · Mains · GS-II · Official Q12 · 12½ marks · 200 words · Difficulty: High · Directive: Discuss briefly  
**Primary classification:** Governance → Corruption → Causes of declining public morality  
**Secondary classification:** Ethics → Public-service values  
**Canonical owners:** [Transparency, Accountability, Grievance Redress and Social Audit](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Governance/advanced/08_Transparency-Accountability-Grievance-Redress-and-Social-Audit.md) · [Corruption and Administrative Ethics](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/advanced/18_Utilization-of-Public-Funds-and-Challenges-of-Corruption.md)  
**Proposed topic slug:** `public-morality-corruption-multidimensional-causes`

**Demand decoding:** Organise causes under all five dimensions, explain their interaction and avoid reducing morality to individual character.

**Compact framework:** Clarify index → five causal dimensions → reinforcing cycle → institutional remedy.

### Model answer

Transparency International’s better-known measure is the **Corruption Perceptions Index**. Whatever the label, low integrity reflects an institutional ecosystem, not simply private moral failure.

- **Legal:** complex rules create discretion and rent-seeking; investigation and trials are slow; whistle-blower protection and confiscation are weak; overlapping agencies diffuse responsibility. The Prevention of Corruption Act and Lokpal framework existed, but enforcement remained uneven.
- **Political:** opaque party finance, expensive elections, patronage appointments and criminalisation create incentives to recover campaign expenditure and protect loyal networks. Weak internal party democracy reduces accountability.
- **Economic:** service scarcity, regulatory bottlenecks, opaque procurement, resource rents and inequality encourage corruption.
- **Social:** citizens may tolerate bribery for speed or access; caste, kinship and clientelism can privilege loyalty over impartial rules; vulnerable people often lack bargaining power.
- **Cultural-organisational:** influence may be normalised; status rewards illicit wealth; hierarchy discourages questioning; honest officers face punitive transfers.

These causes reinforce each other: illicit money finances politics, political protection weakens enforcement, and impunity normalises misconduct.

Public morality requires transparent finance and procurement, simpler rules, independent investigation, timely adjudication, RTI, social audits and ethical leadership.

**Conclusion:** Integrity improves when institutions make honesty feasible, wrongdoing detectable and sanctions credible, while citizens reject patronage-based access.

**Why this earns marks:** It covers every demanded dimension, corrects the index terminology carefully and provides a causal synthesis.

**Demand decoding:** Explain the development costs of inherited hierarchy and procedure while recognising bureaucratic contributions and proposing balanced reform.

**Compact framework:** Continuing value → structural/cultural impediments → development effects → reform.

### Model answer

India’s bureaucracy inherited a colonial design centred on order, revenue and upward accountability. Its permanence and nationwide reach have supported elections, welfare administration and crisis response. The criticism is therefore substantial, not absolute.

Traditional features hamper development when:

- hierarchy and centralisation delay local decisions;
- rule-bound “file culture” rewards procedural safety rather than outcomes;
- generalist dominance and frequent transfers weaken sector expertise;
- silos obstruct integrated responses;

### Model answer

UNESCO established the **International Commission for the Study of Communication Problems** in 1977 under **Seán MacBride**. Its 1980 report, *Many Voices, One World*, examined global inequalities in communication and contributed to the debate on a **New World Information and Communication Order (NWICO)**.

Its aims were to:

- reduce the one-way flow and concentration of international news in a few Western agencies;
- strengthen communication infrastructure and professional capacity in developing countries;
- promote plurality of sources, cultural identity and access to information;
- enable communities to participate, not merely receive;
- protect journalists and improve professional standards;

Yet Article **19(1)(a)** means correcting concentration cannot justify suppression beyond Article 19(2). Independent journalism and plural ownership are preferable to official monopoly.

**Conclusion:** India supports a democratic and equitable information order, qualified by constitutional freedom of expression.

**Why this earns marks:** It identifies the Commission, report and NWICO accurately and gives a balanced India-specific position rather than equating equity with censorship.
