### Q1(a). What does Plato want to prove by his ‘Allegory of Cave’?
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Plato–Aristotle](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/plato-aristotle.md) → knowledge, appearance and education · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Syllabus unit: Plato—Ideas; knowledge and opinion · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Plato · Allegory of the Cave · ascent to the Good · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Plato–Aristotle](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Plato-Aristotle.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `plato-cave-knowledge-good-education`

**Demand decoding:** State what each stage symbolises and identify Plato’s epistemic, metaphysical and political conclusions.

**Argument map:** prisoners and shadows → opinion about sensible appearances → release and painful conversion → intelligible Forms → sun as Form of the Good → philosopher returns to govern.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Plato’s cave proves that ordinary experience yields opinion, while knowledge requires an educational conversion of the soul toward intelligible reality and ultimately the Good.

The chained prisoners mistake shadows for independent realities, just as uncritical citizens confuse sensible appearances and conventional beliefs with truth. Release is initially painful because education is not the insertion of information but a turning of the whole soul. The ascent from shadows to objects, and then outside the cave, parallels the *Divided Line*: imagination, belief, mathematical thought and understanding. The sun symbolises the Form of the Good, which makes both knowledge and being intelligible.

The liberated philosopher must return. Plato thereby joins epistemology to politics: one who knows the Good is best fitted to rule, though the cave-dwellers may resist or kill the educator.

**Objection:** The allegory depreciates experience and licenses elitist rule. **Reply:** Plato targets unexamined appearance, not every empirical aid, and makes philosophical office a duty rather than a privilege.

**Evaluation:** It powerfully depicts intellectual emancipation, but its sharp hierarchy needs democratic and empirical correction.

**Why this earns marks:** It decodes the symbols and connects knowledge, Forms, the Good, education and philosopher-rule.

### Q1(b). Can hallucination be regarded as an intentional act by Husserl? Explain.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Phenomenology](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/phenomenology.md) → intentionality and hallucination · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Syllabus unit: Phenomenology (Husserl)—method; intentionality · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Husserl · empty or unfulfilled intention · object-givenness · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Phenomenology (Husserl)](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Phenomenology-Husserl.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `husserl-hallucination-intentional-act`

**Demand decoding:** Distinguish intentional directedness from the real existence of an intended object.

**Argument map:** consciousness is consciousness-of → act has intentional object as meant → hallucination presents an object → no corresponding transcendent object fulfils the intention → intentionality survives non-existence.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Yes. For Husserl, hallucination is intentional because it is directed toward an object-as-meant, although no corresponding object exists in the world.

Intentionality concerns the structure of consciousness, not a causal relation that requires two existing entities. A hallucinating subject may see “a tree over there”; the act has a determinate sense and presents an object through profiles. What fails is perceptual fulfilment: further harmonious appearances do not confirm the posited tree. Phenomenological reduction brackets the existential claim and examines the correlation between the act (*noesis*) and the object as intended (*noema*). Thus veridical perception and hallucination can share an intentional sense while differing in evidential confirmation and coherent synthesis.

**Objection:** If no object exists, the act appears relationless. **Reply:** Husserl distinguishes intentional from ordinary real relations; directedness is intrinsic to the act.

**Evaluation:** The account explains how absent, fictional and hallucinatory objects can be meant, though specifying the exact status of the noema remains contested.

**Why this earns marks:** It separates object-directedness, existence and fulfilment without calling hallucination non-intentional.

### Q1(c). What is the role of dialectics in realizing the truth in Hegel’s philosophy?
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Hegel](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/hegel.md) → dialectic and truth · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Syllabus unit: Hegel—dialectical method; Absolute idealism · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Hegel · determinate negation · truth as whole · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Hegel](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Hegel.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `hegel-dialectic-realizing-truth`

**Demand decoding:** Explain dialectic as the immanent correction of partial categories, not a mechanical thesis–antithesis formula.

**Argument map:** finite determination claims completeness → internal contradiction appears → determinate negation preserves and transforms it → richer category emerges → sequence culminates in self-comprehending whole.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** For Hegel, dialectic is the self-movement through which one-sided claims expose their own limits and are preserved within progressively adequate forms of truth.

Every finite category defines itself by excluding an opposite, yet depends on that opposite for its meaning. This instability produces determinate negation: the category is not merely discarded but sublated (*Aufhebung*)—cancelled, preserved and elevated. In the *Phenomenology*, forms of consciousness fail by their own standards and advance toward absolute knowing; in the *Logic*, being passes through essence to the Concept. Truth is therefore not an isolated proposition but “the whole,” the articulated result containing the path of its development.

**Objection:** Dialectic may force history and thought into a predetermined scheme. **Reply:** Hegel presents transitions as immanent to the concepts, not externally imposed.

**Evaluation:** Dialectic illuminates relational and developmental truth, but some transitions remain disputable and contingency risks absorption into system.

**Why this earns marks:** It identifies contradiction, determinate negation, sublation and truth as an achieved whole.

### Q1(d). How does Descartes prove the existence of things other than himself and God? Discuss.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Rationalism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/rationalism.md) → external-world proof · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Syllabus unit: Rationalism—Descartes; method and substance · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Descartes · clear and distinct ideas · material world · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Rationalism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Rationalism.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `descartes-proof-external-things`

**Demand decoding:** Reconstruct the Sixth Meditation argument and assess the dependence on divine non-deception.

**Argument map:** passive sensory ideas occur → involuntary ideas require active cause → strong natural inclination attributes them to bodies → non-deceiving God would not systematically mislead → bodies exist, though not exactly as sensed.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Descartes infers material things from passive sensory experience, but the inference becomes secure only after proving a non-deceiving God.

The *cogito* establishes the thinking self; clear and distinct reasoning establishes God. Sensory ideas then arrive involuntarily, so their cause cannot ordinarily be the finite will. Descartes has a powerful natural inclination to attribute them to corporeal things. If God caused that inclination while no bodies existed, God would systematically deceive; divine veracity therefore warrants the existence of an external material world. What is guaranteed is primarily mathematically describable extension, figure and motion, not every colour or taste exactly as experienced.

**Objection:** This produces the Cartesian Circle: clear ideas prove God, while God guarantees clear ideas and perception. Dreams or divine production could also explain sensory ideas. **Reply:** Descartes distinguishes the immediate self-evidence of present clear perception from its remembered reliability and invokes coherent, involuntary sensation.

**Evaluation:** The argument restores realism within his system, but makes external-world knowledge unnecessarily dependent on theology.

**Why this earns marks:** It states the causal-natural-inclination argument, its limited conclusion and the circularity objection.

### Q1(e). Explain Quine’s arguments against synthetic-analytic distinction.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Quine–Strawson](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/quine-strawson.md) → critique of analyticity · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Syllabus unit: Quine and Strawson—critique of empiricism · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: W. V. O. Quine · analyticity · confirmation holism · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Quine–Strawson](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Quine-Strawson.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `quine-against-analytic-synthetic-distinction`

**Demand decoding:** Present both the circular-definition argument and the holistic alternative.

**Argument map:** analytic truths need synonymy or meaning → synonymy is explained by analyticity, definition or interchangeability → each account presupposes the disputed notion → reduction to individual experiences fails → whole web faces experience.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Quine rejects the analytic–synthetic boundary because no non-circular account of truth “by meaning alone” is available and because empirical confirmation applies to systems of belief, not isolated statements.

Logical truths can be recognised by form, but “No bachelor is married” requires synonymy between “bachelor” and “unmarried man.” Definition records prior synonymy; interchangeability salva veritate depends on intensional contexts; semantic rules merely label selected statements analytic without explaining analyticity. The proposed definitions therefore move in a circle.

Quine also rejects radical reductionism. Experience confronts the “web of belief” as a whole; any statement can be retained by adjusting others, while even logic might be revised under sufficient pressure. Difference is thus one of degree—centrality and revisability—not a principled analytic/synthetic kind.

**Objection:** Ordinary linguistic competence seems to distinguish conceptual truth from factual discovery. **Reply:** Quine can explain this through entrenched usage without an absolute boundary.

**Evaluation:** He undermines reductive definitions, though complete abandonment may overlook norm-governed semantic practice.

**Why this earns marks:** It joins the synonymy circle to confirmation holism and qualified revisability.

### Q2(a). How do the logical positivists account for the meaning of general statements? Can the same account be applied to metaphysical statements? Discuss.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Logical Positivism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/logical-positivism.md) → general statements and metaphysics · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 20 marks · Syllabus unit: Logical Positivism—verification theory of meaning; rejection of metaphysics · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Vienna Circle and Ayer · universal laws · weak verification · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Logical Positivism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Logical-Positivism.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `positivism-general-statements-metaphysics`

**Demand decoding:** Explain why universal laws cannot be conclusively verified, the positivist reconstruction of their meaning, and why metaphysics allegedly lacks the same observational control.

**Argument map:** meaningful claim must constrain possible experience → finite observations cannot entail universal law → laws function as rules, hypotheses or probabilistically confirmable systems → metaphysical claims supply no observation-conditions → asymmetry challenged by theoretical science and self-application.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Logical positivists preserve the cognitive meaning of general scientific statements through testability and confirmation rather than final verification; they deny the same status to metaphysics because it allegedly excludes no possible experience.

Early verificationism held that a proposition’s meaning is its method of verification. Yet “All metals expand when heated” ranges over indefinitely many cases and cannot be conclusively established by finite observations. Positivists therefore treated universal laws as hypotheses, inference-rules or compact systems whose consequences—particular observation statements—can be repeatedly tested. Ayer’s weak verification asks whether experience can render a proposition probable, not decisively prove it. Carnap’s confirmation theory similarly relates laws to degrees of evidential support. The law has surplus theoretical content, but its terms acquire meaning through their place in a network connected by correspondence rules to observation.

Metaphysical claims such as “the Absolute transcends all experience” appear different: no conceivable observation confirms or disconfirms them. They are neither analytic nor empirically testable and are classified as pseudostatements—perhaps expressive of an attitude, but not factual knowledge.

**Objections:** The demarcation is unstable. Universal laws are not verified, theoretical entities are indirectly testable, and sophisticated metaphysical theses may constrain explanation. Moreover, the verification principle is itself neither analytic nor empirically verified.

**Reply:** Positivists may present the criterion as a methodological proposal and use system-level testability rather than direct observation. This saves science, but reduces the force of declaring all metaphysics literally meaningless.

**Evaluation:** The account rightly demands consequences and evidential discipline. It explains scientific generality better under weak confirmation than strict verification; however, it establishes degrees of cognitive accountability more securely than a sharp science–metaphysics boundary.

**Why this earns marks:** It handles universal quantification, weak verification, theoretical networks, metaphysical non-testability and the criterion’s principal objections.

### Q2(b). What are the reasons for developmental changes in substance according to Aristotle? Discuss.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Plato–Aristotle](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/plato-aristotle.md) → change, causation and substance · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Syllabus unit: Plato and Aristotle—form and matter; actuality and potentiality; causation · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Aristotle · hylomorphism · natural development · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Plato–Aristotle](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Plato-Aristotle.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `aristotle-developmental-change-substance`

**Demand decoding:** Explain change through potentiality–actuality and the four causes while preserving the identity of the developing substance.

**Argument map:** substance is form–matter composite → matter bears contrary possibilities → efficient cause initiates change → form specifies actualisation → final cause directs natural development → privation explains starting deficiency.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Aristotle explains developmental change as the actualisation of a substance’s immanent potentialities through the coordinated operation of material, formal, efficient and final causes.

Change does not require being to arise from sheer non-being. A subject persists: matter is potentially F and becomes actually F while losing a privation. An acorn, for example, has an organised capacity to become an oak, not a horse. The material cause supplies the underlying stuff; the formal cause is the structure defining mature oakhood; efficient causes include the parent, nutrition, heat and growth-processes; the final cause is the developed life-form toward which the process tends. Form therefore operates both as intelligible specification and natural end.

Aristotle distinguishes substantial generation from accidental alteration. In development the organism remains the same living substance while acquiring mature capacities, although generation and corruption involve a new substantial form.

**Objection:** Teleology projects purpose onto nature, and malformed development challenges fixed ends. **Reply:** Aristotle’s end is usually an immanent pattern of functioning, not conscious design; impediments explain failures without erasing species-typical tendencies.

**Evaluation:** The model unifies identity and change better than static substance theory, though modern biology replaces some intrinsic teleology with genetic, environmental and evolutionary explanation.

**Why this earns marks:** It integrates matter, form, privation, potentiality, actuality and all four causes in a developmental example.

### Q2(c). What do you understand by Spinoza’s statement that what is, cannot be other than what it is? Explain.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Rationalism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/rationalism.md) → necessity and determinism · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Syllabus unit: Rationalism—Spinoza; substance, attributes and modes · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Spinoza · necessitarianism · freedom · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Rationalism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Rationalism.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `spinoza-necessity-cannot-be-otherwise`

**Demand decoding:** Derive modal necessity from the one substance and distinguish necessity from external coercion.

**Argument map:** God/Nature is self-caused infinite substance → modes follow from divine nature → sufficient reason excludes brute contingency → ignorance produces belief in alternatives → freedom is self-determination through adequate knowledge.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Spinoza means that every reality follows with necessity from the nature of God-or-Nature; contingency is a name for our ignorance of determining causes.

Only one substance exists, absolutely infinite God (*Deus sive Natura*). Because substance is self-caused, its essence involves existence. Finite things are modes of its attributes and follow from divine nature as the properties of a triangle follow from its essence—though finite modes also arise within an infinite causal order. Nothing is produced by an indifferent divine will choosing among equally possible worlds. To suppose that things could simply have been otherwise would make God’s action arbitrary or leave facts without sufficient reason.

Human decisions are therefore caused by desires, ideas and prior conditions. A falling stone conscious only of its striving would imagine itself free. Nevertheless Spinoza distinguishes compulsion from freedom: God is free because acting follows from God’s own nature; humans become freer as adequate ideas enable activity from understood necessity rather than passive emotion.

**Objection:** If every act is necessary, responsibility and genuine novelty seem impossible. **Reply:** Spinoza reconstructs responsibility therapeutically: understanding causal bondage increases rational self-direction.

**Evaluation:** His necessitarianism yields a powerful anti-anthropomorphic naturalism, but freedom as understood necessity does not satisfy libertarian alternative possibility.

**Why this earns marks:** It connects substance, modes, sufficient reason, apparent contingency and Spinoza’s revised freedom.

### Q3(a). Is Dasein authentic existence for Heidegger? How does he relate temporality with Dasein? Discuss.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Existentialism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/existentialism.md) → Dasein, authenticity and temporality · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 20 marks · Syllabus unit: Existentialism—Heidegger; human existence; temporality · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Heidegger · being-in-the-world · care · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Existentialism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Existentialism.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `heidegger-dasein-authenticity-temporality`

**Demand decoding:** Correct the assumption that Dasein is automatically authentic, then show how ecstatic temporality grounds care, everydayness and being-toward-death.

**Argument map:** Dasein is the being for whom Being matters → ordinarily absorbed in “the they” → anxiety and death individualise → resolute retrieval of thrown possibilities yields authenticity → future, having-been and present form unified ecstatic temporality.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Dasein is not identical with authentic existence. It is the human mode of being that can exist authentically or inauthentically, and this possibility is grounded in its essentially temporal structure.

Dasein is being-in-the-world, not an isolated subject confronting objects. Its basic constitution is care: it is already thrown into a factual world, projects possibilities ahead of itself, and deals with beings in the present. Everyday Dasein usually falls into “the they” (*das Man*), interpreting itself through anonymous norms, curiosity and idle talk. Inauthenticity is not moral wickedness or non-existence; it is a deficient ownership of possibilities.

Anxiety loosens familiar meanings and discloses Dasein as responsible for choosing without a fixed essence. Being-toward-death confronts it with its ownmost, non-relational and unsurpassable possibility. Anticipatory resoluteness does not seek death; it lets finitude individualise choice and retrieve inherited possibilities knowingly. Authenticity is therefore owned existence amid, not outside, social life.

Temporality explains this structure. The future is Dasein’s projection toward possibilities; the “having-been” is its thrown inheritance; the present is its making-present of practical situations. These are not three successive “nows” but ecstases that stand out toward one another. Primordial temporality unifies care: “ahead-of-itself” is futural, “already-in” refers to having-been, and “being-alongside” concerns the present. Authentic temporality gives priority to the finite future disclosed by death, while inauthentic temporality levels time into an endless public sequence.

**Objections:** Heidegger may privilege solitary death over embodiment and relations, and the authentic/inauthentic distinction can appear normatively obscure. **Reply:** resoluteness retrieves shared historical possibilities; it does not abolish being-with others.

**Evaluation:** Heidegger transforms authenticity from possession of a true inner substance into lucid ownership of situated possibility. Temporality persuasively unifies agency and finitude, though its existential analysis underdescribes ethical criteria and concrete social domination.

**Why this earns marks:** It rejects automatic authenticity and systematically maps care, death and the three temporal ecstases.

### Q3(b). Show how Wittgenstein’s critique of solipsism culminates in the critique of private language.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Later Wittgenstein](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/later-wittgenstein.md) → solipsism and private language · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Syllabus unit: Later Wittgenstein—meaning and use; private language · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Ludwig Wittgenstein · rule-following · sensation language · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Later Wittgenstein](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Later-Wittgenstein.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `wittgenstein-solipsism-private-language`

**Demand decoding:** Trace the movement from the privileged private subject to the impossibility of a language whose signs are in principle intelligible to only one speaker.

**Argument map:** solipsist treats inner experience as uniquely certain → assumes words gain meaning by private ostension → private diary sign lacks independent correctness criterion → seeming right becomes right → rule-following collapses → sensation concepts require public grammar, not identical sensations.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Wittgenstein’s later critique dissolves solipsism by showing that the very language in which a subject claims exclusive access depends on public practices; its decisive result is the impossibility of an essentially private language.

Solipsism pictures the self as a privileged inner spectator and words as names attached to private objects. The private-language thought experiment imagines marking “S” whenever a recurring sensation occurs, without any public criterion. But a later application cannot be checked against the original sensation: consulting memory only produces another present impression. If whatever seems correct counts as correct, the distinction between correct and incorrect use disappears, and with it rule-following and meaning.

This does not deny first-person authority or private sensations. “I am in pain” is not normally an inference from observed behaviour. Rather, pain-language is learned within shared forms of life where natural expressions, teaching and criteria give words their grammar. Others need not feel numerically the same sensation; public criteria govern use.

**Objection:** A lone survivor could maintain a diary and correct mistakes. **Reply:** such a person retains a language acquired or governed by publicly intelligible rules; that is contingently solitary, not essentially private.

**Evaluation:** The argument defeats semantic solipsism more clearly than metaphysical claims about what exists. It shows that an intelligible “only my experience” presupposes contrasts and rules the solipsist cannot privately generate.

**Why this earns marks:** It links private ostension, memory, correctness and public rule-following while preserving first-person authority.

### Q3(c). Why is Moore’s philosophy called common-sense realism?
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Moore–Russell–Early Wittgenstein](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/moore-russell-early-wittgenstein.md) → common-sense realism · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Syllabus unit: Moore, Russell and early Wittgenstein—defence of commonsense; refutation of idealism · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: G. E. Moore · external world · analysis · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Moore–Russell–Early Wittgenstein](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Moore-Russell-EarlyWittgenstein.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `moore-common-sense-realism`

**Demand decoding:** Explain both elements: defence of ordinary certainties and mind-independent objects, while noting the limits of Moore’s proofs.

**Argument map:** ordinary propositions are known with certainty → philosophical premises less certain than them → act of awareness differs from object → hands prove external things → analysis clarifies without overturning common sense.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Moore is a common-sense realist because he treats many ordinary propositions as more certain than sceptical or idealist theories and affirms objects whose existence is not constituted by being perceived.

In “A Defence of Common Sense,” Moore lists truisms: a human body has existed, the earth existed before his birth, and other persons have bodies and experiences. Any philosophical argument denying all such claims relies on premises less certain than what it rejects. In “Proof of an External World,” raising one hand and then another is offered as proof that at least two external objects exist, provided he knows the premises.

His realism is reinforced by the refutation of idealism. Awareness and its object—consciousness of blue and blue—are distinguishable; *esse est percipi* wrongly identifies the act with what is apprehended. Moorean analysis seeks the precise meaning and entailments of propositions without replacing the familiar world with an idealist construction.

**Objections:** The hand proof may beg the question against scepticism, which disputes knowledge rather than hands. Sense-data discussions also complicate direct realism. **Reply:** Moore’s dialectical point is comparative certainty: a sceptical premise must be better warranted than “Here is a hand.”

**Evaluation:** Common-sense realism is a powerful constraint on philosophical revision and later inspired the Moorean shift. It does not fully explain perceptual knowledge, but it relocates the burden of proof onto radical scepticism.

**Why this earns marks:** It joins common-sense certainty, external realism, act–object distinction, analysis and the begging-the-question objection.

### Q4(a). How does Kant argue for the transcendence of Space and Time? Discuss.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Kant](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/kant.md) → transcendental ideality of space and time · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 20 marks · Syllabus unit: Kant—possibility of synthetic a priori judgements; space and time · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Immanuel Kant · transcendental aesthetic · empirical reality · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Kant](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Kant.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `kant-transcendental-ideality-space-time`

**Demand decoding:** Treat “transcendence” as the a priori/transcendental status and ideality of space and time, reconstructing metaphysical and transcendental expositions.

**Argument map:** space/time cannot derive from experience because experience presupposes them → they are singular pure intuitions, not discursive concepts → geometry/arithmetic yield synthetic a priori cognition → valid for appearances, not things in themselves.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Kant argues that space and time transcend empirical derivation as a priori forms of sensibility, yet do not transcend possible experience as properties of things in themselves. They are empirically real and transcendentally ideal.

The metaphysical exposition gives several arguments. Outer representation already presupposes space: sensations can be located outside and beside one another only spatially. Similarly, succession and simultaneity presuppose time. We can think space or time empty of objects, but cannot represent appearances without them. Each is represented as one all-encompassing magnitude whose parts are limitations, indicating pure intuition rather than a general concept assembled from instances. Their infinity is given as an unlimited whole, not by discursive enumeration.

The transcendental exposition asks how synthetic a priori knowledge is possible. Geometry determines spatial relations necessarily and universally; arithmetic and the representation of motion depend on temporal succession. Such cognition is possible if space and time are the subject’s pure forms for receiving appearances. If they were empirical abstractions, necessity would be unavailable; if properties of things in themselves, their a priori knowability would be mysterious.

Thus space is the form of outer sense and time the form of inner sense; because every representation occurs in inner sense, time indirectly conditions all appearances. Objects as experienced are genuinely spatio-temporal, but noumena cannot be asserted to be spatial or temporal.

**Objections:** Non-Euclidean geometry weakens Kant’s identification of a priori spatial form with Euclidean geometry. The argument from representational necessity may establish cognitive structure, not ideality. Scientific relativity also treats spacetime as physically structured.

**Reply:** A revised Kantian can distinguish the formal conditions of human intuition from revisable empirical geometry. Kant’s critical point remains that experienced spatial-temporal order is inseparable from our mode of cognition.

**Evaluation:** Kant explains the possibility and limits of mathematical natural knowledge while avoiding Newtonian absolute space and Leibnizian reduction to relations among things. His strict Euclidean and subject-centred formulation requires revision, but the empirical-reality/transcendental-ideality distinction remains philosophically fertile.

**Why this earns marks:** It presents both expositions, pure intuition, synthetic a priori knowledge, empirical reality and transcendental ideality with modern objections.

### Q4(b). Is there any element of necessity in causal relations according to Hume? Discuss.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Empiricism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/empiricism.md) → causation and necessity · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Syllabus unit: Empiricism—Hume; causation · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: David Hume · constant conjunction · necessary connection · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Empiricism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Empiricism.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `hume-causal-necessity-custom`

**Demand decoding:** Distinguish objective necessary power from the felt necessity produced by custom.

**Argument map:** no impression of power in single event → repeated conjunction does not reveal hidden tie → repetition generates expectation → impression of determination in mind supplies idea of necessary connection → necessity is natural but not demonstrative.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Hume denies any perceived or demonstrable necessity connecting distinct events, but recognises a psychological necessity: custom determines the mind to expect the effect after the cause.

Inspection of one event—one billiard ball striking another—reveals contiguity, temporal priority and motion, not a power compelling the second event. Cause and effect are distinct existences; denial of any proposed effect is conceivable without contradiction. Repeated constant conjunction also displays only similar sequences, never an additional observable tie.

Nevertheless repetition transforms the observer. After frequent A–B conjunctions, the appearance of A produces a lively expectation of B. This internal impression of determination is projected onto objects and becomes the idea of necessary connection. Hume accordingly offers an “external” definition—objects constantly conjoined—and an “internal” definition—the first object carries thought to the second.

**Objection:** Mere regularity cannot distinguish genuine causes from accidental correlations or explain interventions and counterfactuals. **Reply:** Hume’s aim is epistemic genealogy, not denial that causal discourse organises experience; later Humeans enrich regularity with laws and counterfactual dependence.

**Evaluation:** There is no rationally intuited metaphysical necessity in causal relations for Hume. There is instead unavoidable natural belief grounded in habit—subjectively compelling, practically indispensable, but never logically demonstrative.

**Why this earns marks:** It states precisely what necessity Hume rejects and what natural necessity he retains.

### Q4(c). How does Sartre look at the problem of freedom of choice and determinism? Explain.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Existentialism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/existentialism.md) → freedom, facticity and determinism · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Syllabus unit: Existentialism—Sartre; freedom and bad faith · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Jean-Paul Sartre · radical freedom · situation · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Existentialism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Existentialism.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `sartre-freedom-choice-determinism`

**Demand decoding:** Explain why consciousness cannot be causally fixed like a thing while qualifying freedom through facticity and situation.

**Argument map:** existence precedes essence → consciousness negates given facts and projects ends → motives gain weight through choice → refusal is itself choice → facticity constrains options but does not dictate meaning → bad faith disguises freedom as determinism.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Sartre rejects psychological and metaphysical determinism because consciousness is a self-transcending project; yet freedom is always situated within unchosen facticity.

Being-for-itself is not a fixed thing with an essence. Consciousness introduces nothingness into the given: it can distance itself from a role, desire or past act and project a future. Motives do not mechanically cause choice; they count as reasons within an adopted project. Even obedience or refusal to decide is a choice, hence Sartre’s claim that humans are “condemned to be free.”

Freedom is not omnipotence. Birth, body, social position, past and other people constitute facticity. A prisoner may not immediately escape, but must still take a stance toward imprisonment and pursue possibilities within the situation. Bad faith occurs when one treats oneself solely as determined fact—“I cannot do otherwise”—or as pure freedom without responsibility.

**Objections:** Trauma, unconscious motivation and structural oppression can severely narrow agency; radical responsibility may blame victims. **Reply:** Sartre later gives greater weight to scarcity and social conditioning, while maintaining a margin of praxis rather than total causal closure.

**Evaluation:** Sartre rightly exposes excuses that convert reasons into forces, but early radical freedom underestimates embodied and institutional determinants. Situated freedom offers the more defensible synthesis.

**Why this earns marks:** It balances choice, nothingness, motives, facticity, bad faith and social qualification.

### Q5(c). Are the Cārvākas consistent when they hold that inference is not a source of knowledge? Discuss.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Cārvāka](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/carvaka.md) → rejection of inference · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Syllabus unit: Cārvāka—epistemology; rejection of transcendent entities · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Cārvāka · inference (*anumāna*) · invariable concomitance · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Cārvāka](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Carvaka.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `carvaka-consistency-rejecting-inference`

**Demand decoding:** Test whether the rejection is self-refuting and distinguish strict denial from fallible practical inference.

**Argument map:** inference needs universal concomitance → universality cannot be perceived → inference or testimony would be circular → transcendent conclusions fail → total denial itself uses inference → pragmatic restriction offers consistency.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** A total Cārvāka denial of inference is self-undermining, but a restricted denial of inference as certain knowledge of imperceptible realities is defensible.

Inference (*anumāna*) requires invariable concomitance (*vyāpti*), such as “wherever smoke, there fire.” Perception covers only limited cases and cannot exclude hidden conditions; establishing universality by another inference is circular, while testimony itself requires inferential trust. Hence inference cannot prove God, soul, karma or an afterlife.

Yet Cārvākas argue, identify opponents and act on signs—all apparently inferential. If “inference is invalid” is generalised from cases of error, it refutes itself. Later presentations therefore allow ordinary, probability-based inference useful in life while denying its status as an infallible means of valid knowledge (*pramāṇa*) concerning the transcendent.

**Objection:** Even practical success presupposes reliable generalisation. **Reply:** reliability and revisability need not amount to certainty.

**Evaluation:** Strict perceptualism is inconsistent and impracticable; methodological scepticism about unrestricted inference is a valuable demand for evidence.

**Why this earns marks:** It identifies the *vyāpti* problem, self-reference and the pragmatic restricted reading.

### Q6(a). How is reality defined by the Jainas? How is this theory of reality reflected in their view on judgements? Discuss.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Jainism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/jainism.md) → many-sided reality and conditioned judgement · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 20 marks · Syllabus unit: Jainism—theory of reality; relativism; judgement · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Jaina philosophy · many-sidedness (*anekāntavāda*) · sevenfold predication · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Jainism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Jainism.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `jaina-reality-judgement-anekantavada`

**Demand decoding:** Define reality through permanence and change, then derive standpoint theory and sevenfold conditioned predication without reducing them to scepticism.

**Argument map:** real has origination, destruction and persistence → substance has infinite aspects → finite knower grasps standpoint → unqualified assertion absolutises one aspect → “in some respect” yields seven predications → non-one-sided judgement mirrors complex reality.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Jainism defines reality as many-sided and simultaneously permanent-through-substance and changing-through-modes; its logic therefore requires every finite judgement to be standpoint-bound and conditionally asserted.

A real entity possesses origination, cessation and persistence (*utpāda-vyaya-dhrauvya*). A clay pot changes in modes while clay-substance continues; similarly, a person has enduring identity through changing states. Reality is thus neither the pure flux attributed to Buddhism nor the changeless absolute attributed to Advaita. Every entity has innumerable qualities and modes, so finite cognition captures only an aspect. This ontological many-sidedness (*anekāntavāda*) grounds standpoint theory (*nayavāda*): a judgement is valid from a particular purpose or perspective but false when inflated into an exhaustive account.

Sevenfold conditioned predication (*syādvāda* or *saptabhaṅgī*) expresses this discipline:

1. in some respect it is;
2. in some respect it is not;
3. in some respect it is and is not;
4. in some respect it is inexpressible;
5. it is and is inexpressible;
6. it is not and is inexpressible;
7. it is, is not and is inexpressible.

Contradiction is avoided because predicates apply under different conditions—substance, mode, place, time or relation. “The pot exists” now and here; “it does not exist” before production or as cloth. Inexpressibility marks the impossibility of simultaneously stating complex, successively qualified aspects, not total unknowability.

**Objections:** If every judgement is only relative, *anekāntavāda* itself seems relatively true; the seven forms may look repetitive or violate non-contradiction. **Reply:** Jainas distinguish conditioned truth from arbitrary subjectivism. The doctrine is reflexively conditional, and contradictions disappear once respects are specified.

**Evaluation:** Jaina logic admirably joins realism, intellectual non-violence and plural perspectives. Yet without explicit conditions, “somehow” can become evasive; rigorous standpoint specification is essential.

**Why this earns marks:** It derives *nayavāda* and sevenfold judgement from the permanence–change and infinite-aspect account of reality.

**Thesis:** Cārvāka need not deny that the syllogism is formally valid; it denies that its universal premise—“All men are mortal”—can be known with certainty and therefore denies the conclusion the status of inferential knowledge.

**Evaluation:** The critique anticipates the problem of induction and correctly separates validity from soundness. Yet demanding certainty from every means of knowledge is excessive; defeasible inference can still be rationally warranted.

**Why this earns marks:** It targets universal-premise warrant, explains circularity and distinguishes formal validity, probability and knowledge.

### Q7(a). How does Udayana prove the existence of God through Kāryāt, Āyojanāt, Dhṛtyādeḥ and Śruteḥ? Discuss.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/nyaya-vaisesika.md) → proofs of God · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 20 marks · Syllabus unit: Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika—God; causation · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Udayana · four theistic inferences · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Nyaya-Vaisesika.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `udayana-god-karyat-ayojanat-dhrtyadeh-sruteh`

**Demand decoding:** Reconstruct each named proof separately, explain their cumulative structure, and assess standard objections.

**Argument map:** world as effect requires intelligent maker (*kāryāt*) → inert atoms need intelligent conjunction (*āyojanāt*) → cosmic support and order require governor (*dhṛtyādeḥ*) → authorless-seeming Vedic sentences require omniscient trustworthy source (*śruteḥ*) → God is efficient, not material, cause.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Udayana’s proofs are cumulative Nyāya inferences from effect, atomic combination, cosmic maintenance and authoritative revelation to an omniscient, bodiless intelligent efficient cause—God (*Īśvara*).

**From effect (*kāryāt*):** The composite world has parts, arises and is therefore an effect, like a pot. Every such effect requires an intelligent maker who knows material, means and purpose. Atoms and selves cannot jointly organise the cosmos without an omniscient maker; God is its efficient cause, while eternal atoms are material causes.

**From combination (*āyojanāt*):** Inert atoms cannot initiate the dyadic conjunction necessary for creation. Unseen moral potency (*adṛṣṭa*) is itself non-conscious and cannot allocate results intelligently. An agent must set atoms in motion and coordinate karmic deserts; that agent is God.

**From support and maintenance (*dhṛtyādeḥ*):** The ordered continuation, regulation and dissolution of the world require a sustaining governor, just as organised systems depend upon directing intelligence. God maintains cosmic law and the morally appropriate distribution of pleasure and pain.

**From scripture (*śruteḥ*):** Vedic sentences convey supersensible truths. Meaningful authoritative sentences normally issue from a competent speaker. Since ordinary humans lack omniscience and are liable to error, the Veda’s ultimate promulgator or trustworthy source is omniscient God. Nyāya thus rejects Mīmāṃsā’s authorless Veda.

**Objections:** Buddhists argue that world-order may arise from causal series without a creator; Sāṃkhya invokes primordial nature (*prakṛti*); Mīmāṃsā lets karma operate without divine supervision. The world is unlike an artefact, so the pot analogy may be weak. Scripture cannot prove God if God is first invoked to authenticate scripture.

**Reply:** Udayana argues that unconscious atoms, Prakṛti and karma explain neither purposive coordination nor just allocation. The scripture argument supplements independent inferences rather than standing alone.

**Evaluation:** The proofs form a sophisticated design-and-governance case within Nyāya pluralism. They support at most an intelligent organiser more readily than an infinite perfect deity; natural self-organisation and the problem of evil limit their conclusiveness.

**Why this earns marks:** It accurately separates all four Sanskrit-named inferences, their Nyāya background, replies and limits.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Yoga would classify these persons by the functional state of mind (*citta*), not by social label: the scientist exemplifies disciplined one-pointedness, the realised devotee devotional one-pointedness and absorption, and the self-realised yogi complete restraint grounded in discriminative insight.

The five mental fields (*citta-bhūmi*) are restless (*kṣipta*), dull (*mūḍha*), distracted (*vikṣipta*), one-pointed (*ekāgra*) and restrained (*niruddha*). A serious scientist suspends distraction and sustains inquiry on an object; during discovery the mind is *ekāgra*, though outside research it may return to *vikṣipta*. Concentration alone does not yield liberation because object-knowledge and egoic dispositions can remain.

A God-realised devotee steadies the mind through devotion to the Lord (*Īśvara-praṇidhāna*), mantra and surrender. Such devotion weakens afflictions and can produce *ekāgra* and cognitive absorption (*samprajñāta samādhi*). Whether “God-realised” equals final isolation depends on Yoga’s distinction between devotion as a powerful means and discriminative knowledge as liberating insight.

The self-realised yogi advances toward *niruddha*, where modifications cease; even latent impressions are mastered in supra-cognitive absorption (*asamprajñāta samādhi*), culminating in seedless absorption and isolation (*kaivalya*).

**Objection:** Fixed labels oversimplify fluctuating minds. **Reply:** these are dominant levels and capacities, not permanent psychological castes.

**Evaluation:** The comparison shows a graded continuum from focused cognition to soteriological cessation, while preserving the distinct aims of science, devotion and Yoga.

**Why this earns marks:** It applies all relevant *citta-bhūmis*, distinguishes concentration from liberation and qualifies each attribution.

### Q7(c). What is Pratītyasamutpāda? How do the Buddhists apply this concept to prove that everything is momentary?
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Buddhism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/buddhism.md) → dependent origination and momentariness · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Syllabus unit: Buddhism—dependent origination; momentariness · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Buddhism · dependent origination (*pratītyasamutpāda*) · momentariness (*kṣaṇikavāda*) · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Buddhism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Buddhism.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `buddhism-dependent-origination-momentariness`

**Demand decoding:** Define conditional arising, then reconstruct rather than merely assert the inference to momentariness.

**Argument map:** when condition exists effect arises → nothing has independent essence → causally efficient entity produces its effect → after production continued existence is causally idle → destruction needs no separate cause → continuity is a series of similar moments.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Dependent origination (*pratītyasamutpāda*) states that phenomena arise and cease through conditions; Buddhist momentarists argue that genuine causal efficiency belongs only to a fleeting event that perishes as it produces its successor.

The principle is: “When this exists, that comes to be; with the arising of this, that arises; when this ceases, that ceases.” In the twelve-linked analysis it explains suffering without an eternal self. More generally, it denies independent self-nature: a conditioned thing exists only through a causal nexus. This is the doctrinal Middle Path between eternalism and annihilationism and supports the practical Middle Path through the Four Noble Truths and Noble Eightfold Path (*āryāṣṭāṅgamārga*).

The momentarist argument appeals to causal efficacy (*arthakriyākāritva*). A thing is real insofar as it produces an effect. Once a cause produces its effect in a moment, continued existence would add no efficacy; if it could produce later, why not immediately? Destruction is not caused by a second entity, for that cause would either destroy an already altered thing or leave an allegedly enduring nature unchanged. What appears as one flame or person is therefore a causally connected series of similar moments.

**Objections:** Dependence alone entails impermanence, not necessarily instantaneous destruction. Memory and delayed causation appear to require persistence. **Reply:** Buddhists invoke causal series, impressions and immediate transmission of efficacy, not numerical identity.

**Evaluation:** Dependent origination strongly defeats self-subsistent permanence, but strict momentariness requires the additional causal-efficiency argument and is not a simple logical consequence of conditionality alone.

**Why this earns marks:** It defines the doctrine, gives the causal proof, links both Middle Paths and qualifies the entailment.

### Q8(a). Explain the logical consequences of the metaphysical absolutism of Advaitism.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Vedānta](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/vedanta.md) → consequences of non-dual absolutism · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 20 marks · Syllabus unit: Schools of Vedānta—Śaṅkara; Brahman, world, self and liberation · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Advaita Vedānta · non-dual Brahman · levels of reality · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Vedānta](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Vedanta.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `advaita-absolutism-logical-consequences`

**Demand decoding:** Derive epistemological, cosmological, theological and soteriological consequences from “Brahman alone is ultimately real,” then test consistency.

**Argument map:** reality is non-dual consciousness → plurality cannot be ultimately independent → world is empirically valid but sublatable → ignorance and superimposition explain appearance → individual self is Brahman → God belongs to empirical standpoint → knowledge, not production, liberates.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Advaita’s metaphysical absolutism—that non-dual Brahman is the sole ultimate reality—logically entails graded reality, the dependent status of world and God, identity of self and Brahman, and liberation through knowledge; it also generates the central problem of explaining ignorance without compromising non-duality.

Brahman is existence-consciousness-infinity (*satyaṃ jñānam anantaṃ brahma*), partless and non-dual. If a second independent reality existed, Brahman would be limited. Plurality must therefore be neither absolutely real nor sheer nothing. Advaita distinguishes ultimate reality (*pāramārthika-sattā*), empirical reality (*vyāvahārika-sattā*) and dream/illusory appearance (*prātibhāsika-sattā*). The world functions in experience and moral life but is sublated by knowledge of Brahman; it is indeterminable as either real or unreal (*anirvacanīya*).

Cosmologically, change cannot be a real transformation of Brahman, so creation is apparent manifestation (*vivarta*) through ignorance/power of appearance (*māyā*). The Lord (*Īśvara*) is Brahman associated with Māyā, omniscient creator and karmic governor at the empirical level. The individual self (*jīva*) is the same consciousness apparently limited by mind and body. Scriptural great sayings such as “That thou art” (*tat tvam asi*) remove incompatible limiting adjuncts and disclose identity.

Epistemologically, ordinary knower-known duality belongs to ignorance (*avidyā*) and superimposition (*adhyāsa*). Brahman cannot be objectified; scripture functions as a means of knowledge that removes ignorance. Soteriologically, liberation (*mokṣa*) is not a new product but recognition of ever-free selfhood; knowledge, supported by ethical and contemplative discipline, is decisive.

**Objections:** If Brahman alone exists, where does ignorance reside? It cannot belong to Brahman, which is pure knowledge, or to a jīva produced by ignorance without circularity. Calling the world indeterminable may appear evasive. Rāmānuja argues that denying real plurality undermines scripture, devotion and liberation.

**Reply:** Advaita says ignorance is beginningless but removable, empirically attributable to the jīva, and invalid from the ultimate standpoint. Its role is explanatory within experience, not a second ultimate entity.

**Evaluation:** Absolutism offers a rigorous unity of reality and a transformative account of liberation. Its graded ontology preserves practical life better than illusionism, but the status of Māyā and avidyā remains the system’s logical pressure point.

**Why this earns marks:** It derives the full chain from non-duality to levels of reality, *vivarta*, Īśvara, self-identity, knowledge and the locus-of-ignorance objection.

### Q8(b). Explain the unique position of the Supermind in the metaphysical scheme of Aurobindo’s philosophy.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Sri Aurobindo](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/sri-aurobindo.md) → Supermind · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Syllabus unit: Sri Aurobindo—evolution; integral yoga · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Sri Aurobindo · Supermind · involution and evolution · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Sri Aurobindo](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Aurobindo.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `aurobindo-supermind-metaphysical-position`

**Demand decoding:** Locate Supermind between existence-consciousness-bliss and divided mind, and explain its mediating and transformative functions.

**Argument map:** Absolute is unity → manifestation requires ordered multiplicity → Supermind is truth-consciousness knowing identity-in-difference → involution descends through it → evolution ascends toward it → supramental descent transforms embodied life.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Supermind occupies Aurobindo’s unique mediating position between absolute existence-consciousness-bliss (*sat-cit-ānanda*) and the divided ignorance of mind; it is both the creative principle of manifestation and the next evolutionary power capable of transforming life.

The Absolute knows reality through identity. Ordinary mind, however, divides subject from object and grasps partial truths successively. Supermind is truth-consciousness: it simultaneously knows unity and the real articulation of multiplicity. Through its “real-idea,” knowledge and effective will are one; what it knows, it manifests. It therefore prevents creation from being either an inexplicable fall or an illusory world.

In involution, consciousness descends through Supermind toward Overmind, mind, life and matter, concealing unity progressively. Evolution reverses this movement as involved consciousness emerges from matter to life and mind. Because mind cannot permanently reconcile spirit and matter, ascent must culminate in supramental consciousness. Its descent completes psychic and spiritual transformation by changing mental, vital and physical nature, making divine life possible on earth.

**Objection:** Supermind may be an unverifiable metaphysical bridge invented to close a systematic gap. **Reply:** Aurobindo treats it as supported by yogic experience and by the evolutionary emergence of higher powers from apparently lower matter.

**Evaluation:** Supermind distinctively unites metaphysics, cosmology and transformative practice; its explanatory coherence is considerable within the system, though its evolutionary inevitability exceeds public empirical proof.

**Why this earns marks:** It identifies Supermind’s place, identity-knowledge, creative role, involutionary descent and evolutionary transformation.

### Q8(c). Which Sāṃkhya proof for the existence of Prakṛti actually shows that there can be only one Prakṛti? Justify your answer.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Sāṃkhya](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/samkhya.md) → unity of Prakṛti · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Syllabus unit: Sāṃkhya—Prakṛti; causation; evolution · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: classical Sāṃkhya · common cause of diverse effects · unity · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Sāṃkhya](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Samkhya.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `samkhya-proof-one-prakrti`

**Demand decoding:** Select the relevant classical inference and show why it establishes a single common ground rather than merely some unmanifest cause.

**Argument map:** effects share pleasure-pain-delusion structure → shared guṇa constitution requires common cause → coordinated transformation forms one ordered cosmos → equilibrium of three guṇas is unmanifest Prakṛti → multiple Prakṛtis are redundant and disrupt common evolution.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** The proof from the common nature or homogeneity of effects—“because diverse effects possess a common character” (*samanvayāt*)—most directly establishes that there is one primordial nature (*prakṛti*), not merely an unmanifest cause.

The *Sāṃkhyakārikā* argues for the unmanifest from the finitude of effects, their common character, the efficacy of causal power, differentiation of cause and effect, and the undividedness of the world. The decisive unity argument is *samanvayāt*: all psycho-physical products, from intellect to elements, display the three strands (*guṇas*)—illumination/pleasure (*sattva*), activity/pain (*rajas*) and inertia/delusion (*tamas*)—in varying proportions. Under pre-existent-effect theory (*satkāryavāda*), an effect manifests what already exists in its cause. A universally shared threefold character therefore points to one common undifferentiated equilibrium as their source.

The argument from the world’s undivided coordinated transformation (*avibhāgād vaiśvarūpyasya*) reinforces unity: dissolution returns diverse effects to a common ground and evolution forms one causally connected order. Positing many qualitatively identical Prakṛtis explains no difference and violates economy.

**Objection:** Common properties need only similar causes, not numerically one cause; many seeds can produce similar trees. Moreover, plurality of Puruṣas might suggest separate material matrices. **Reply:** seeds are already differentiated effects, whereas Prakṛti is inferred as the ultimate common cause prior to differentiation; multiple identical ultimates would lack a differentiating principle.

**Evaluation:** *Samanvayāt*, strengthened by coordinated dissolution, gives Sāṃkhya’s best case for one Prakṛti. It is not deductively conclusive, because qualitative unity does not automatically prove numerical unity, but it fits the system’s economy and unified evolutionary sequence.

**Why this earns marks:** It identifies *samanvayāt*, connects it to the three *guṇas* and *satkāryavāda*, and tests the move from commonality to numerical unity.
