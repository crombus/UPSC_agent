## Q1. Jobless growth in India
**Primary route:** Economy → [Employment, Labour Codes, Skills and Demographic Dividend](../../topics/economy/employment-labour-codes-skills-and-demographic-dividend.md) → Employment elasticity · **Secondary route:** [Growth, Development, HDI, IHDI and MPI](../../topics/economy/growth-development-hdi-ihdi-and-mpi.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2015 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 1  
**Official question:** The nature of economic growth in India in recent times is often described as jobless growth. Do you agree with this view ? Give arguments in favour of your answer.  
**Marks / limit:** 12½ / 200 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Employment → Employment Elasticity  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/employment-labour-codes-skills-and-demographic-dividend`  
**Difficulty / directive:** Moderate · Take a position + Substantiate  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/22_Employment-Labour-Codes-Skills-and-Demographic-Dividend.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/22_Employment-Labour-Codes-Skills-and-Demographic-Dividend.md)

### Demand decoding
Define jobless growth, judge rather than merely describe the proposition, and connect sectoral growth, technology, firm structure and labour-market outcomes.

### Compact framework
`rapid GDP growth − proportionate productive employment = low employment elasticity; sector mix + technology + informality explain the gap`

### Model answer
“Jobless growth” does not mean zero employment; it means output rising much faster than adequate, productive and secure work. The description substantially fits India’s recent growth, though it requires qualification.

- Growth was led by capital- and skill-intensive services such as finance, telecommunications and IT, which generated high value added but absorbed fewer low-skilled workers.
- Organised manufacturing remained relatively small; firms faced infrastructure, credit and regulatory constraints and often preferred automation or contractual labour.
- Agriculture continued to hold excess labour, masking unemployment as low-productivity, disguised employment.
- The **68th NSS Employment–Unemployment Survey (2011–12)** showed employment creation recovering from the exceptionally weak 2004–05 to 2009–10 period, but much work remained informal or casual.
- Slow growth of labour-intensive textiles, leather, food processing and construction limited structural transformation.
- Skill mismatch and low female labour-force participation prevented demographic potential from becoming effective employment.

Yet rising construction, self-employment and services did create work; therefore “employment-poor” is more precise than literally jobless.

India needs labour-intensive manufacturing, stronger MSMEs, apprenticeships, urban infrastructure, agro-processing and portable social protection. Growth becomes inclusive only when productivity gains expand both decent work and real wages.

### Examiner rationale
The answer defines the contested term, takes a qualified position, uses a period-appropriate NSS anchor and explains mechanisms instead of equating GDP growth mechanically with unemployment.

## Q2. Livestock and rural non-farm livelihoods
**Primary route:** Economy → [Economics of Animal Rearing, Livestock, Dairy, Poultry and Fisheries](../../topics/economy/economics-of-animal-rearing-livestock-dairy-poultry-and-fisheries.md) → Rural employment and income · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2015 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 2  
**Official question:** Livestock rearing has a big potential for providing non-farm employment and income in rural areas. Discuss suggesting suitable measures to promote this sector in India.  
**Marks / limit:** 12½ / 200 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Agriculture → Animal Rearing  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/economics-of-animal-rearing-livestock-dairy-poultry-and-fisheries`  
**Difficulty / directive:** Moderate · Discuss potential + Suggest measures  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/30_Economics-of-Animal-Rearing-Livestock-Dairy-Poultry-and-Fisheries.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/30_Economics-of-Animal-Rearing-Livestock-Dairy-Poultry-and-Fisheries.md)

### Demand decoding
Explain why livestock diversifies rural livelihoods, identify constraints across productivity-to-market chains, and prescribe inclusive measures.

### Compact framework
`small recurring assets + year-round produce → diversified income; breed/feed/health/market deficits → productivity gap`

### Model answer
Livestock converts crop residues and family labour into milk, eggs, meat, manure and draught services. Unlike seasonal crops, dairying and poultry generate frequent cash flows, cushion drought and price shocks, and create work in collection, chilling, feed, veterinary care, processing and transport. Women and land-poor households can participate with relatively small assets. **Operation Flood** and the Amul cooperative model demonstrate how producer-owned procurement can combine employment with value addition.

Potential remains constrained by low animal productivity, indiscriminate breeding, fodder deficits, preventable disease, weak extension, inadequate cold chains, informal slaughter and poor access to credit and insurance.

Measures should include:

- breed improvement suited to local climate, while conserving indigenous genetic resources;
- artificial insemination, mobile veterinary services, vaccination and disease surveillance;
- fodder banks, pasture development and balanced feed;
- affordable institutional credit, livestock insurance and women’s self-help-group ownership;
- dairy and poultry cooperatives, transparent testing and assured procurement;
- village chilling, refrigerated transport, hygienic abattoirs and processing clusters;
- training in animal health, waste-to-biogas and product quality; and
- implementation of the **National Livestock Mission (2014–15)** with State-specific plans.

Promotion must favour small producers rather than vertically integrated firms alone. Strong producer institutions can convert livestock growth into resilient, nutrition-sensitive rural employment.

### Examiner rationale
The response links livestock’s household economics to value-chain employment, uses Operation Flood and the National Livestock Mission, and qualifies commercialisation with smallholder inclusion.

## Q3. Contract farming and land leasing
**Primary route:** Economy → [Land Reforms, Green Revolution and Cropping Systems](../../topics/economy/land-reforms-green-revolution-and-cropping-systems.md) → Tenancy and operational consolidation · **Secondary route:** [APMC, e-NAM, FPOs and Agricultural Supply Chains](../../topics/economy/apmc-e-nam-fpos-and-agricultural-supply-chains.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2015 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 3  
**Official question:** In view of the declining average size of land holdings in India which has made agriculture non-viable for a majority of farmers, should contract farming and land leasing be promoted in agriculture ? Critically evaluate the pros and cons.  
**Marks / limit:** 12½ / 200 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Agriculture → Tenancy and Contract Farming  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/land-reforms-green-revolution-and-cropping-systems`  
**Difficulty / directive:** High · Critically evaluate two instruments  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/11_Land-Reforms-Green-Revolution-and-Cropping-Systems.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/11_Land-Reforms-Green-Revolution-and-Cropping-Systems.md)

### Demand decoding
Evaluate contract farming and legal leasing separately, connect them to fragmented holdings, and provide safeguards rather than an unqualified yes/no.

### Compact framework
`leasing → operational scale; contracts → inputs/market/standards; unequal bargaining + tenure insecurity require regulation`

### Model answer
Contract farming and secure land leasing can improve viability, but neither should become a route to dispossession.

**Benefits:** Legal leasing permits efficient cultivators to consolidate operational holdings while owners retain title; it can bring fallow land into production and give tenants documented access to credit, insurance and relief. Contract farming can assure markets, inputs, extension, quality standards and technology. Punjab’s PepsiCo potato experience illustrates processing linkages and reduced marketing uncertainty.

**Risks:** Informal tenants may be evicted if owners fear tenancy rights. Weak farmers can face one-sided contracts, delayed purchase, arbitrary quality rejection and monopsony pricing. Firms may encourage water-intensive monoculture, transfer production risk to cultivators, or exclude remote and marginal farmers. Leasing without records may also enable absentee landlordism and concentration.

Promotion should therefore require:

- written, time-bound leases that do not alter ownership;
- recognition of actual cultivators for credit, insurance and disaster compensation;
- model contracts in local languages, transparent grades and price formulas;
- quick, inexpensive dispute resolution;
- collective bargaining through cooperatives or producer organisations; and
- soil, water and crop-diversity safeguards.

Thus regulated leasing and farmer-centred contracts can create scale and market access; unregulated arrangements may replace land fragmentation with corporate dependence.

### Examiner rationale
The answer distinguishes leasing from contracting, evaluates benefits and power asymmetries, supplies a period-appropriate example and reaches a safeguarded verdict.

## Q4. Digital India for farm productivity and income
**Primary route:** Economy → [Digital Agriculture, Agritech and e-Technology for Farmers](../../topics/economy/digital-agriculture-agritech-and-e-technology-for-farmers.md) → Digital farm services · **Secondary route:** [Digital India, India Stack and Digital Infrastructure](../../topics/science-and-technology/digital-india-india-stack-and-digital-infrastructure.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2015 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 4  
**Official question:** How can the ‘Digital India’ programme help farmers to improve farm productivity and income ? What steps has the Government taken in this regard ?  
**Marks / limit:** 12½ / 200 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Agriculture → Digital Agriculture  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/digital-agriculture-agritech-and-e-technology-for-farmers`  
**Difficulty / directive:** Moderate · Explain mechanisms + Identify steps  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/27_Digital-Agriculture-Agritech-and-e-Technology-for-Farmers.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/27_Digital-Agriculture-Agritech-and-e-Technology-for-Farmers.md)

### Demand decoding
Connect digital infrastructure and information services to both productivity and realised income, then cite measures available by 2015.

### Compact framework
`connectivity + timely information + digital delivery + market discovery → lower risk/cost and higher realised returns`

### Model answer
Digital India can reduce the information, transaction and delivery failures that separate farm output from farm income.

- Mobile weather and pest advisories permit timely sowing, irrigation and crop protection.
- Soil, satellite and geographic information can improve input targeting and drought assessment.
- Online mandi prices and buyer discovery reduce information asymmetry and widen markets.
- Digital records, banking and Direct Benefit Transfer can improve access to credit, insurance and subsidies.
- Online extension, tele-veterinary services and local-language content reduce distance from expertise.
- Supply-chain traceability and electronic logistics can lower wastage and reward quality.

By 2015, government measures included the **National e-Governance Plan in Agriculture**, the **mKisan portal** and Kisan SMS services, **Kisan Call Centres**, the **Farmers’ Portal**, **Agmarknet** price information, **Soil Health Cards**, and remote-sensing support through **FASAL** for crop forecasting. BharatNet and Common Service Centres formed the connectivity and assisted-access base. The proposed National Agriculture Market sought electronic integration of mandis.

However, devices alone cannot overcome poor connectivity, low literacy, fragmented data, language barriers or weak last-mile institutions. Services must be interoperable, locally relevant and available through extension workers and panchayat-level centres. Digital India should complement irrigation, roads, storage and fair markets—not substitute for them.

### Examiner rationale
The answer explains distinct productivity and income channels, names only initiatives extant by 2015 and qualifies technological optimism with last-mile and physical-infrastructure constraints.

## Q5. Direct Benefit Transfer and subsidy reform
**Primary route:** Economy → [Direct and Indirect Farm Subsidies and WTO Rules](../../topics/economy/direct-and-indirect-farm-subsidies-and-wto-rules.md) → Direct transfers versus price subsidies · **Secondary route:** [Union Budget, Fiscal Policy and Deficit Indicators](../../topics/economy/union-budget-fiscal-policy-and-deficit-indicators.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2015 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 5  
**Official question:** In what way could replacement of price subsidy with Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) change the scenario of subsidies in India ? Discuss.  
**Marks / limit:** 12½ / 200 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Subsidies → Direct Benefit Transfer  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/direct-and-indirect-farm-subsidies-and-wto-rules`  
**Difficulty / directive:** Moderate · Discuss transformative potential and limits  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/28_Direct-and-Indirect-Farm-Subsidies-and-WTO-Rules.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/28_Direct-and-Indirect-Farm-Subsidies-and-WTO-Rules.md)

### Demand decoding
Compare price suppression with beneficiary transfers across fiscal, market, administrative and equity dimensions; do not assume DBT automatically works.

### Compact framework
`subsidised commodity price → leakage/distortion; verified cash transfer → choice/traceability, conditional on inclusion and market access`

### Model answer
Price subsidies lower the administered price of commodities such as LPG, food, fertiliser or electricity. Replacing them with DBT changes support from an under-priced product to an identifiable beneficiary.

Potential gains are:

- transfer trails can reduce diversion and ghost beneficiaries;
- the budget records the subsidy explicitly, improving fiscal transparency;
- consumers receive choice rather than incentives to overuse a cheap input;
- market prices reveal scarcity, encouraging efficient use and competition;
- portability can help migrants; and
- savings can finance better-targeted welfare.

The **PAHAL** LPG scheme, relaunched nationally in 2014–15, demonstrated identified bank transfers and reduced opportunities for multiple connections and commercial diversion.

However, DBT can exclude beneficiaries through absent accounts, authentication errors or outdated lists. Cash loses value without indexation, while remote households may lack competitive markets. Paying full price before reimbursement creates liquidity stress. In food security, cash cannot replace grain where markets are thin; in fertiliser, identifying cultivators is difficult because tenancy is often informal.

Therefore, transition should be commodity-specific, phased and grievance-backed, with universal financial access, updated beneficiary databases, inflation protection and reliable supply. DBT can improve subsidy governance, but targeting errors must not convert leakage control into welfare exclusion.

### Examiner rationale
The answer compares instruments, anchors the discussion in PAHAL, differentiates sectors and gives a conditional rather than technologically deterministic verdict.

## Q6. Food-processing supply chains and e-commerce
**Primary route:** Economy → [Food Processing, Cold Chains and Value Addition](../../topics/economy/food-processing-cold-chains-and-value-addition.md) → Marketing and supply-chain bottlenecks · **Secondary route:** [APMC, e-NAM, FPOs and Agricultural Supply Chains](../../topics/economy/apmc-e-nam-fpos-and-agricultural-supply-chains.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2015 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 6  
**Official question:** What are the impediments in marketing and supply chain management in developing the food processing industry in India ? Can e-commerce help in overcoming these bottlenecks ?  
**Marks / limit:** 12½ / 200 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Food Processing → Supply Chains  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/food-processing-cold-chains-and-value-addition`  
**Difficulty / directive:** Moderate-High · Diagnose + Assess e-commerce  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/15_Food-Processing-Cold-Chains-and-Value-Addition.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/15_Food-Processing-Cold-Chains-and-Value-Addition.md)

### Demand decoding
Trace bottlenecks from farm aggregation through processing and retail, then judge which can and cannot be solved digitally.

### Compact framework
`fragmented farm gate + cold-chain/logistics gaps + regulated markets → high loss/cost; e-commerce improves discovery/coordination, not physical capacity`

### Model answer
Indian food processing is constrained by fragmented, unreliable value chains.

Major impediments include small and dispersed surpluses; multiple intermediaries and State APMC restrictions; poor grading; inadequate pack-houses, reefer transport, warehouses and power; seasonal price volatility; weak farmer–processor contracts; costly credit; inconsistent food-safety compliance; and poor processor–retailer links. Perishability makes each delay expensive.

E-commerce can help by:

- aggregating orders and connecting farmers or producer groups directly with processors and consumers;
- publishing prices and quality requirements, reducing search costs and information asymmetry;
- enabling electronic auctions, advance contracts and digital payments;
- using demand data to plan procurement, inventories and routes; and
- supporting traceability and consumer feedback.

**Agmarknet** and the proposed electronic National Agriculture Market indicated how price and market integration could begin.

Yet an online platform cannot refrigerate milk, grade fruit, guarantee broadband, enforce a contract or overcome inter-State barriers. It may also concentrate bargaining power in large platforms and exclude digitally weak smallholders.

Therefore e-commerce should be integrated with farmer producer organisations, harmonised mandi rules, assaying laboratories, cold-chain investment, warehouse receipts, food-safety capacity and competition safeguards. Digital coordination can make the chain visible and contestable; physical logistics and institutions must make it reliable.

### Examiner rationale
The response follows the supply chain, assesses e-commerce by function, uses a period-appropriate platform anchor and clearly separates digital coordination from physical infrastructure.

## Q7. Merits of the Gold Monetization Scheme
**Primary route:** Economy → [Balance of Payments, Exchange Rates and Forex Reserves](../../topics/economy/balance-of-payments-exchange-rates-and-forex-reserves.md) → Gold imports and current account · **Secondary route:** [Banking Structure, NBFCs and Financial Regulation](../../topics/economy/banking-structure-nbfcs-and-financial-regulation.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2015 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 7  
**Official question:** Craze for gold in Indians has led to a surge in import of gold in recent years and put pressure on balance of payments and external value of rupee. In view of this, examine the merits of the Gold Monetization Scheme.  
**Marks / limit:** 12½ / 200 words  
**Classification:** Economy → External Sector → Gold Monetisation  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/balance-of-payments-exchange-rates-and-forex-reserves`  
**Difficulty / directive:** Moderate · Examine merits with qualifications  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/19_Balance-of-Payments-Exchange-Rates-and-Forex-Reserves.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/19_Balance-of-Payments-Exchange-Rates-and-Forex-Reserves.md)

### Demand decoding
Explain the scheme’s macroeconomic and financial-intermediation channels, then test practical limitations rather than merely list features.

### Compact framework
`idle domestic gold → deposits/refining/lending → lower fresh imports + productive financial asset; trust and transaction costs constrain mobilisation`

### Model answer
The **Gold Monetisation Scheme, 2015** sought to mobilise household and institutional gold through deposits after purity testing, with interest payable in gold or rupee terms.

Its merits are:

- mobilised domestic gold can be lent to jewellers, reducing reliance on imported bullion;
- lower imports can ease the current-account deficit and pressure on the rupee;
- idle jewellery or bullion becomes an interest-bearing financial asset;
- banks gain a new deposit resource and the domestic refining ecosystem can deepen;
- transparent recycling can reduce unofficial trade; and
- temples and trusts can place large idle stocks into productive circulation.

It improved upon the 1999 scheme through broader deposit tenures and proposed use across the gold value chain.

Nevertheless, macroeconomic gains depend on actual participation. Households may resist melting jewellery because of sentimental value, distrust purity testing, limited collection centres, tax anxieties and low or uncertain returns. Mobilisation also does not permanently reduce gold demand unless attractive financial savings alternatives exist.

Thus the scheme is a useful import-substitution and financialisation instrument, not a complete cure. Accredited testing centres, transparent tax treatment, convenient bank access, consumer confidence and complementary instruments such as Sovereign Gold Bonds are necessary to shift saving from imported physical gold.

### Examiner rationale
The answer traces the import and banking channels, dates the scheme correctly, compares its ambition with participation constraints and avoids overstating its likely balance-of-payments effect.

## Q8. Make in India, Skill India and labour reforms
**Primary route:** Economy → [Employment, Labour Codes, Skills and Demographic Dividend](../../topics/economy/employment-labour-codes-skills-and-demographic-dividend.md) → Skills and labour-market reform · **Secondary route:** [Industrial Policy, 1991 Reforms, PSUs and Disinvestment](../../topics/economy/industrial-policy-1991-reforms-psus-and-disinvestment.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2015 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 8  
**Official question:** “Success of ‘Make in India’ programme depends on the success of ‘Skill India’ programme and radical labour reforms.” Discuss with logical arguments.  
**Marks / limit:** 12½ / 200 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Manufacturing → Skills and Labour Institutions  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/employment-labour-codes-skills-and-demographic-dividend`  
**Difficulty / directive:** Moderate · Establish relationships + Qualify  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/22_Employment-Labour-Codes-Skills-and-Demographic-Dividend.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/22_Employment-Labour-Codes-Skills-and-Demographic-Dividend.md)

### Demand decoding
Show why skills and labour institutions affect manufacturing competitiveness, define reform substantively, and identify other necessary conditions.

### Compact framework
`investment + employable labour + predictable protective regulation → competitive manufacturing; skills/reform necessary, not sufficient`

### Model answer
Make in India cannot become a mass-manufacturing strategy unless investment is matched by productive workers and workable labour institutions.

**Skill India is essential** because modern factories require technicians, machine operators, supervisors, quality controllers and maintenance workers. The **National Policy for Skill Development and Entrepreneurship, 2015**, the National Skill Development Mission and Pradhan Mantri Kaushal Vikas Yojana sought industry-linked training and certification. Apprenticeships can close the gap between classroom credentials and shop-floor competence.

**Labour reform is also necessary.** Numerous central and State laws, multiple registrations and uncertain enforcement increase compliance costs and encourage firms to remain small or use informal contracts. Reform should simplify procedures, permit orderly adjustment and improve dispute resolution. However, “radical” must not mean removing minimum wages, safety, collective bargaining or social security: insecure and unhealthy labour lowers productivity and demand.

Both are insufficient without reliable power, logistics, land, finance, technology, stable taxation and access to markets. Training without manufacturing demand creates certified unemployment; deregulation without capabilities may produce low-wage enclaves.

Hence the three programmes should form a compact: competitive investment, demand-led skills, apprenticeships, simplified transparent regulation and portable worker protection. Make in India succeeds when labour flexibility for firms is combined with capability and security for workers.

### Examiner rationale
The response logically connects investment, skills and regulation, cites the 2015 skill architecture, rejects a false flexibility-versus-protection binary and identifies complementary industrial conditions.

## Q9. Falling solar costs and thermal power
**Primary route:** Economy → [Energy Infrastructure Economics, Power, Fuels and Energy Security](../../topics/economy/energy-infrastructure-economics-power-fuels-and-energy-security.md) → Electricity-sector transition · **Secondary route:** Environment → [Renewable Energy and Green Hydrogen](../../topics/environment/renewable-energy-and-green-hydrogen.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2015 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 9  
**Official question:** To what factors can the recent dramatic fall in equipment costs and tariff of solar energy be attributed ? What implications does the trend have for the thermal power producers and the related industry ?  
**Marks / limit:** 12½ / 200 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Energy → Solar Cost Transition  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/energy-infrastructure-economics-power-fuels-and-energy-security`  
**Difficulty / directive:** Moderate-High · Explain causes + Analyse sectoral implications  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/31_Energy-Infrastructure-Economics-Power-Fuels-and-Energy-Security.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/31_Energy-Infrastructure-Economics-Power-Fuels-and-Energy-Security.md)

### Demand decoding
Separate equipment-cost drivers from bid-tariff drivers, then analyse consequences for plants, coal-linked industries, grids and finance.

### Compact framework
`global scale + technology + cheap finance + auctions → lower solar tariff; thermal shifts from baseload dominance toward flexibility and stranded-asset risk`

### Model answer
Solar costs fell through interacting technological, industrial and policy forces:

- global manufacturing scale, especially in photovoltaic modules, reduced unit costs;
- improved cell efficiency, thinner wafers and better inverters raised output per installed rupee;
- competitive bidding replaced cost-plus tariffs;
- cheaper international finance and declining perceived project risk reduced capital cost;
- the Jawaharlal Nehru National Solar Mission, solar parks, accelerated depreciation and renewable-purchase obligations created demand; and
- India’s high irradiation and shorter construction periods improved project economics.

For thermal producers, cheap daytime solar can reduce plant-load factors and revenues, particularly for old, inefficient stations. Long-lived coal assets, mines, rail freight, equipment manufacturers and bank loans face stranded-asset risk. Distribution companies may prefer solar bids to expensive new thermal power.

However, solar was intermittent and storage remained costly in 2015. Thermal plants still supplied balancing power and evening demand. The transition therefore requires flexible operation, better forecasting, transmission through the **Green Energy Corridor**, time-sensitive tariffs and retirement of inefficient units rather than indiscriminate abandonment.

Domestic solar manufacturing also faced import competition, while thermal-equipment firms needed diversification into grid, efficiency and renewable systems. Falling tariffs improve energy access and import security, but contractual viability and grid-integration costs must be counted alongside headline bids.

### Examiner rationale
The answer distinguishes technological, financial and auction effects and gives a balanced transition analysis rather than predicting the immediate disappearance of thermal power.

## Q10. Taxation, laws and administration in SEZs
**Primary route:** Economy → [Industrial Policy, 1991 Reforms, PSUs and Disinvestment](../../topics/economy/industrial-policy-1991-reforms-psus-and-disinvestment.md) → Special Economic Zones · **Secondary route:** [Foreign Trade, WTO, FTAs and Protectionism](../../topics/economy/foreign-trade-wto-ftas-and-protectionism.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2015 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 10  
**Official question:** There is a clear acknowledgement that Special Economic Zones (SEZs) are a tool of industrial development, manufacturing and exports. Recognizing this potential, the whole instrumentality of SEZs requires augmentation. Discuss the issues plaguing the success of SEZs with respect to taxation, governing laws and administration.  
**Marks / limit:** 12½ / 200 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Industrial Policy → Special Economic Zones  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/industrial-policy-1991-reforms-psus-and-disinvestment`  
**Difficulty / directive:** High · Discuss problems under three specified heads  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/16_Industrial-Policy-1991-Reforms-PSUs-and-Disinvestment.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/16_Industrial-Policy-1991-Reforms-PSUs-and-Disinvestment.md)

### Demand decoding
Organise the answer explicitly under taxation, governing laws and administration, while linking each defect to investment and export performance.

### Compact framework
`policy instability + legal overlap + administrative fragmentation → uncertain enclave economics and weak integration`

### Model answer
The **SEZ Act, 2005** envisaged duty-free enclaves with single-window approvals, infrastructure and export-oriented production. Their potential has been weakened by three sets of problems.

**Taxation:** Introduction of Minimum Alternate Tax on SEZ developers and units and Dividend Distribution Tax reduced the credibility of promised fiscal stability. Uncertainty over exemptions, service-tax refunds and treatment of sales into the Domestic Tariff Area complicated business plans. Incentive-led proposals also encouraged land and tax arbitrage rather than manufacturing.

**Governing laws:** SEZ rules intersect with customs, labour, environmental and State laws. Ambiguity over the Development Commissioner’s authority prevents a genuine single window. Land acquisition and rehabilitation controversies undermine legitimacy; rigid area and contiguity norms can make projects unviable. Policy must also remain compatible with WTO disciplines.

**Administration:** Multiple clearances, Centre–State coordination failures, weak external connectivity and delays in utilities raise costs. Some notified zones remain vacant, while sector-specific IT zones dominate over diversified manufacturing. Monitoring often emphasises exports rather than net foreign-exchange earnings, jobs and domestic linkages.

Reform requires stable prospective taxation, time-bound unified clearances, transparent land purchase and rehabilitation, plug-and-play infrastructure, independent performance review and stronger supplier links outside enclaves. SEZs should compete through logistics and institutions, not uncertain tax concessions.

### Examiner rationale
The answer follows all three mandated heads, anchors the legal regime and tax reversals, and proposes augmentation centred on credibility, coordination and real economic linkages.

## Q11. SPS, PPS and the seven-satellite IRNSS
