## Q1(a). Environmental ethics

**Official question:** What is meant by ‘environmental ethics’? Why is it important to study? Discuss any one environmental issue from the viewpoint of environmental ethics. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2015 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q1(a) · Directive: Define, explain importance and discuss · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Environmental and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/environmental-and-administrative-ethics.md) → environmental ethics · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Emerging and environmental ethics](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/13_Emerging-Ethics-Technology-AI-and-Environment.md) · [Moral theories](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/08_Moral-Theories-Deontology-Consequentialism-Virtue-Ethics.md)

### Demand decoding

Define the field, show why it matters, and apply its competing ethical lenses to one concrete issue rather than merely describing pollution.

### Model answer

**Environmental ethics** examines the moral relationship of human beings with animals, ecosystems and future generations, including whether nature has value beyond human utility.

Its study matters because ecological harms are cumulative and often irreversible; burdens are unequally distributed; non-human beings and future persons cannot represent themselves; and market price may not capture ecological value.

Consider diversion of a forest for mining. An anthropocentric view asks whether jobs and minerals outweigh health, livelihood and ecosystem-service losses. A biocentric view recognises living beings as morally considerable, while ecocentrism values the integrity of the forest community itself. Utilitarianism requires full long-term costs; rights and justice demand participation of forest-dependent people and protection against disproportionate burdens.

The ethical course is to test alternatives, obtain informed participation, minimise diversion, preserve critical habitats, restore livelihoods and impose polluter liability. Development may be legitimate, but communities, species and future generations cannot be treated as expendable means.

**Examiner takeaway:** Environmental ethics enlarges moral consideration across species, communities and time and converts “development versus environment” into a test of necessity, justice and stewardship.

## Q1(b). Four distinctions in applied ethics

**Official question:** Differentiate between the following (200 words):  
(i) Law and Ethics  
(ii) Ethical management and Management of ethics  
(iii) Discrimination and Preferential treatment  
(iv) Personal ethics and Professional ethics (200 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2015 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q1(b) · Directive: Differentiate · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → applied ethical distinctions · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Ethics and human interface](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/01_Ethics-and-Human-Interface.md) · [Laws, rules and conscience](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/10_Sources-of-Ethical-Guidance-Laws-Rules-Conscience.md) · [Codes](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/16_Codes-of-Ethics-and-Codes-of-Conduct.md)

### Model answer

| Pair | First concept | Second concept |
|---|---|---|
| **Law / Ethics** | Publicly enacted, externally enforceable minimum backed by sanctions | Reasoned standards of right conduct, motive and character, often exceeding law |
| **Ethical management / Management of ethics** | Managing people and resources through fair means and worthy ends | Institutionalising ethics through codes, training, advice, disclosure, audit and sanctions |
| **Discrimination / Preferential treatment** | Unjust disadvantage based on irrelevant identity or prejudice | Differential support based on a relevant disadvantage to secure substantive equality |
| **Personal / Professional ethics** | Values governing private choices and relationships | Role-based duties arising from entrusted competence, power and public expectations |

Thus, rejecting a candidate because of caste is discrimination; reservation for a historically excluded group is preferential treatment directed at equality. A doctor's personal view may oppose a patient's lawful choice, but professional ethics requires dignity, informed consent and non-abandonment.

The distinctions are not absolute. Law can embody ethics; preferential treatment can become arbitrary unless evidence-based; and professional roles cannot justify manifest wrongdoing. Integrity requires coherence between personal character, role duty and just institutions.

**Examiner takeaway:** The answer distinguishes source, purpose and accountability while qualifying each boundary.

## Q2(a). Forgiveness as the strength of the strong

**Official question:** “The weak can never forgive; forgiveness is the attribute of the strong.” (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2015 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q2(a) · Directive: Interpret in the present context · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Indian Moral Thinkers](../../topics/ethics/indian-moral-thinkers.md) → Gandhi, forgiveness and moral courage · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Indian moral thinkers](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/06_Indian-Moral-Thinkers-and-Philosophers.md) · [Human values](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/02_Human-Values-and-Lessons-from-Leaders.md)

### Model answer

The statement, associated with Gandhi, treats forgiveness not as surrender but as mastery over resentment. A powerless person may merely submit; genuine forgiveness is a voluntary choice by one capable of retaliation.

Forgiveness requires **fortitude**, empathy and moral confidence. It interrupts cycles of vengeance, permits dialogue and separates condemnation of a wrong from permanent hatred of the wrongdoer. In administration, an officer may correct a subordinate's first honest error through counselling rather than humiliation, preserving learning and institutional trust. Restorative approaches similarly combine accountability, acknowledgement and repair.

However, forgiveness is not impunity. Repeated corruption, violence or abuse demands lawful protection, restitution and proportionate sanction. Victims cannot be pressured to “forgive” for institutional convenience. Aristotle's practical wisdom helps distinguish magnanimity from enabling vice.

In a polarised present, the strong response is therefore neither revenge nor passive tolerance: establish truth, protect rights, secure accountability and remain open to reform and reconciliation.

**Examiner takeaway:** Moral strength controls retaliation while retaining justice, accountability and protection for victims.

## Q2(b). Fear of light and intellectual courage

**Official question:** “We can easily forgive a child who is afraid of the dark; the real tragedy of life is when men are afraid of the light.” (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2015 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q2(b) · Directive: Interpret in the present context · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → truth, reason and moral courage · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Western thinkers](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/07_Western-Moral-Philosophers-and-Thinkers.md) · [Human values](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/02_Human-Values-and-Lessons-from-Leaders.md)

### Model answer

The quotation contrasts innocent fear born of ignorance with the culpable refusal of mature persons to face truth. “Light” signifies reason, evidence, self-knowledge and public scrutiny; adults may fear it because truth threatens privilege, prejudice or a convenient self-image.

Plato's allegory of the cave similarly shows how familiar shadows can be preferred to difficult knowledge. Present manifestations include denial of scientific evidence, suppression of audit findings, resistance to social reform and algorithmic echo chambers that reward confirmation bias.

For a public servant, courage of conviction means seeking facts, disclosing reasons and inviting review. A district officer who publishes beneficiary criteria and corrects exclusion accepts the light of accountability rather than defending institutional ego.

Yet claims of enlightenment can themselves become arrogant. Evidence must be tested through open debate, humility and respect for persons. The ethical tragedy is not honest uncertainty but deliberate blindness.

**Examiner takeaway:** Enlightenment requires intellectual honesty—the courage to expose belief and power to evidence, criticism and correction.

## Q3(a). Legal compliance and ethical sensitivity

**Official question:** “A mere compliance with law is not enough, the public servant also has to have a well-developed sensibility to ethical issues for effective discharge of duties.” Do you agree? Explain with the help of two examples, where (i) an act is ethically right, but not legally and (ii) an act is legally right, but not ethically. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2015 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q3(a) · Directive: Take a position and explain with prescribed examples · Difficulty: High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → legality, conscience and discretion · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Laws, rules and conscience](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/10_Sources-of-Ethical-Guidance-Laws-Rules-Conscience.md) · [Public-service dilemmas](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/09_Public-Service-Values-Status-and-Ethical-Dilemmas.md)

### Model answer

I agree. Law supplies an enforceable minimum; ethical sensibility identifies dignity, fairness, motive and consequences where rules are silent, outdated or permit harmful discretion.

**Ethically right but legally prohibited:** before a protective exception existed, a humanitarian might shelter persons persecuted under an unjust law. Civil disobedience can be morally defensible when it is conscientious, non-violent, proportionate and accepts legal scrutiny. A serving officer, however, should not casually substitute private morality for law; she should seek review, record dissent and use lawful emergency powers.

**Legally right but ethically wrong:** an authority may technically satisfy notice rules while scheduling a hearing in a manner that an illiterate affected community cannot meaningfully access. Formal legality then defeats natural justice.

Kantian respect for persons and the constitutional values of equality and dignity require more than box-ticking. Yet ethics must discipline discretion, not legitimise arbitrariness.

**Examiner takeaway:** Effective administration combines legality with reasoned conscience, while institutional review prevents personal morality from becoming unaccountable power.

## Q3(b). Trustworthiness and fortitude in public service

**Official question:** How do the virtues of trustworthiness and fortitude get manifested in public service? Explain with examples. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2015 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q3(b) · Directive: Explain manifestations with examples · Difficulty: Easy-Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Work Culture and Public Funds](../../topics/ethics/work-culture-and-public-funds.md) → foundational public-service virtues · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owner:** [Aptitude and foundational values](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/04_Aptitude-and-Foundational-Values-for-Civil-Service.md)

### Model answer

**Trustworthiness** is justified confidence that an official will act honestly, competently and consistently with entrusted duty. It appears in truthful records, promise-keeping, confidentiality, impartial criteria, disclosure of conflicts and responsible use of public resources.

**Fortitude** is moral courage and endurance in pursuing lawful public interest despite fear, pressure or hardship. It appears in giving frank advice, resisting political or commercial coercion, admitting error and sustaining relief work under danger.

For example, a procurement officer establishes trust by disclosing that a relative owns a bidding firm and recusing. An election officer displays fortitude by rejecting partisan directions, documenting threats and applying identical rules. During a disaster, regular public briefings combine both virtues: accurate disclosure builds trust, while continued duty amid risk shows courage.

Trust without fortitude may collapse under pressure; courage without reliability can become impulsive heroism. Aristotle's virtue ethics therefore requires habituated character guided by practical wisdom and supported by transparent institutions.

**Examiner takeaway:** Trustworthiness makes power dependable; fortitude keeps it principled when integrity becomes costly.

## Q4(a). Social values, economic values and inclusive growth

**Official question:** “Social values are more important than economic values.” Discuss the above statement with examples in the context of inclusive growth of a nation. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2015 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q4(a) · Directive: Discuss with examples · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → justice and inclusive growth · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Moral theories](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/08_Moral-Theories-Deontology-Consequentialism-Virtue-Ethics.md) · [Ethical governance](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/11_Accountability-and-Ethical-Governance.md)

### Model answer

Economic values emphasise productivity, efficiency and wealth creation; social values emphasise dignity, equality, solidarity, participation and justice. Inclusive growth requires both, but social values determine its purpose and distribution.

Growth that displaces Adivasis without livelihood restoration, excludes persons with disabilities or relies on unsafe labour may increase output while reducing justice. Conversely, universal schooling, public health, fair wages, accessible infrastructure and women's economic participation expand capabilities and also strengthen long-term productivity.

Rawls's difference principle asks whether inequalities benefit the least advantaged; Amartya Sen shifts attention from income to real freedoms. Thus a road project should be judged not only by cost-benefit ratio but by access created, burdens imposed and voice given to affected groups.

Yet social justice without fiscal and administrative capacity may remain aspirational. Economic efficiency is therefore an instrument and constraint, not the final moral end. Policy should seek productive growth bounded by rights and directed toward substantive opportunity.

**Examiner takeaway:** Inclusive growth is economically viable expansion morally ordered by dignity, capability and fair distribution.

## Q4(b). Transparency, accountability and decisional paralysis

**Official question:** Some recent developments such as introduction of RTI Act, media and judicial activism, etc., are proving helpful in bringing about greater transparency and accountability in the functioning of the government. However, it is also being observed that at times the mechanisms are misused. Another negative effect is that the officers are now afraid to take prompt decisions. Analyze this situation in detail and suggest how this dichotomy can be resolved. Suggest how these negative impacts can be minimized. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2015 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q4(b) · Directive: Analyse and suggest safeguards · Difficulty: High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Work Culture and Public Funds](../../topics/ethics/work-culture-and-public-funds.md) → accountability and decisional courage · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Transparency and RTI](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/15_Transparency-RTI-and-Information-Sharing.md) · [Accountability](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/11_Accountability-and-Ethical-Governance.md) · [Protecting honest officials](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/21_Protecting-Honest-Officials-and-Vigilance-Administration.md)

### Model answer

RTI, investigative media and judicial review expose arbitrariness, give citizens voice and improve reason-giving. Yet repetitive or malicious processes, sensational trial by media and hindsight-based scrutiny can consume capacity, harm reputation and encourage defensive delay.

The dichotomy is not accountability versus efficiency, but **fair accountability versus uncertain, indiscriminate risk**. Measures should include:

- proactive disclosure and searchable records, reducing repetitive requests;
- clear delegation, consultation and speaking orders;
- distinction between bona fide error, negligent conduct and corrupt intent;
- time-bound preliminary scrutiny before coercive action against officials;
- protection for honest, reasoned decisions taken within authority;
- independent, specialised investigation and judicial restraint on administration except illegality or irrationality;
- media ethics, prompt factual briefings and correction rights;
- sanctions for proven abuse without chilling genuine whistle-blowing or public-interest scrutiny.

Officers must not use fear as an excuse for inaction; ethical courage requires timely decision on available evidence. Institutions should reward documented good faith and correct mistakes through learning.

**Examiner takeaway:** Predictable, proportionate scrutiny strengthens rather than paralyses responsible administration.

## Q5. Bureaucratic and democratic attitudes

**Official question:** Two different kinds of attitudes exhibited by public servants towards their work have been identified as the bureaucratic attitude and the democratic attitude.  
(a) Distinguish between these two terms and write their merits and demerits,  
(b) Is it possible to balance the two to create a better administration for the faster development of our country? (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2015 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q5 · Directive: Distinguish, evaluate and reconcile · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Emotional Intelligence and Ethical Dilemmas](../../topics/ethics/emotional-intelligence-and-ethical-dilemmas.md) → administrative attitudes · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

### Model answer

| Attitude | Merits | Demerits |
|---|---|---|
| **Bureaucratic:** rule-bound, hierarchical, impersonal and expertise-led | legality, consistency, continuity, auditability | rigidity, delay, red tape, distance from citizens |
| **Democratic:** participatory, responsive, deliberative and equality-oriented | legitimacy, local knowledge, inclusion, trust | delay, populism, capture by vocal groups, dilution of expertise |

Non-negotiable rights and safety standards cannot be put to majority vote; equally, expertise cannot become paternalism.

The operating formula is: **consult widely → decide lawfully with reasons → implement professionally → disclose and review**. Digital single windows and time limits can retain due process while reducing delay.

**Examiner takeaway:** Development accelerates when professional legality supplies the frame and democratic responsiveness supplies legitimacy and contextual intelligence.

## Q6. Why anti-corruption measures underperform

**Official question:** Today we find that in spite of various measures like prescribing codes of conduct, setting up vigilence cells/commissions, RTI, active media and strengthening of legal mechanisms, corrupt practices are not coming under control.  
(a) Evaluate the effectiveness of these measures with justifications.  
(b) Suggest more effective strategies to tackle this menace. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2015 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q6 · Directive: Evaluate and suggest · Difficulty: High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Corruption and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/corruption-and-administrative-ethics.md) → anti-corruption architecture · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Corruption challenges](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/18_Utilization-of-Public-Funds-and-Challenges-of-Corruption.md) · [Legal framework](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/19_Corruption-Legal-Framework.md) · [Institutions](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/20_Anti-Corruption-Institutions.md)

### Model answer

Existing measures are necessary but uneven. Codes clarify standards but become ceremonial without leadership. Vigilance bodies detect wrongdoing but may lack independence or timely investigation. RTI and media expose networks, yet depend on usable records and whistle-blower safety. Laws deter only when detection and adjudication are credible.

Corruption persists because monopoly, discretion and opacity combine with complex procedures, political-business collusion, weak enforcement and social tolerance.

More effective strategy:

- simplify rules and reduce face-to-face discretion through traceable digital processes;
- publish criteria, queues, contracts and beneficial ownership;
- rotate sensitive posts and separate approval, payment and audit;
- protect complainants and honest officials;
- ensure independent, time-bound investigation, trial and proportionate asset recovery;
- regulate conflicts, gifts, post-employment and political finance;
- strengthen social audits, citizen charters and grievance appeals;
- recruit, train and appraise for integrity while leadership models it.

Kautilya supports verification and incentives, but surveillance must respect due process. Prevention, detection and ethical culture must reinforce one another.

**Examiner takeaway:** Institutions work when opportunity is reduced, detection is credible and integrity is organisationally rewarded.

## Q7. Ethical considerations in international relations

**Official question:** At the international level, the bilateral relations between most nations are governed on the policy of promoting one’s own national interest without any regard for the interest of other nations. This leads to conflicts and tensions between the nations. How can ethical consideration help resolve such tensions? Discuss with specific examples. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2015 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q7 · Directive: Discuss with specific examples · Difficulty: Moderate-High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/international-ethics.md) → ethical statecraft and cooperation · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owner:** [Corporate governance and international ethics](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/12_Corporate-Governance-and-International-Ethics.md)

### Model answer

National interest is legitimate, but a purely zero-sum realism produces mistrust, arms races and retaliation. Ethical statecraft recognises sovereign equality, human dignity, treaty fidelity, proportionality and duties concerning global commons.

Ethical considerations help by:

- replacing coercion with dialogue, mediation and reason-giving;
- applying reciprocity and good faith to treaties;
- distinguishing vital security interests from prestige;
- sharing transboundary benefits and burdens fairly;
- protecting civilians and using force only as a lawful last resort;
- accepting differentiated responsibility where capacities and historical contributions differ.

The Indus Waters Treaty illustrates rule-based cooperation surviving political tension. India's disaster assistance to neighbours can build solidarity without immediate quid pro quo. Climate negotiations require wealthy and high-emitting states to combine ambition with finance and technology support.

Ethics is not naïveté: agreements need verification, reciprocity and safeguards against exploitation. Kant's ideal of peaceful federation and Gandhi's insistence on ethical means show that durable interest is often enlightened common interest.

**Examiner takeaway:** Ethics converts power politics into restrained, reciprocal and institutionally verified cooperation.

## Q8. Conflict of interest in public service

**Official question:** Public servants are likely to confront with the issues of ‘Conflict of Interest’. What do you understand by the term ‘Conflict of Interest’ and how does it manifest in the decision making by public servants? If faced with the conflict of interest situation, how would you resolve it? Explain with the help of examples. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2015 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q8 · Directive: Define, explain manifestation and resolve with examples · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Emotional Intelligence and Ethical Dilemmas](../../topics/ethics/emotional-intelligence-and-ethical-dilemmas.md) → conflict of interest · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Public-service dilemmas](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/09_Public-Service-Values-Status-and-Ethical-Dilemmas.md) · [Probity](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/14_Probity-Concept-and-Philosophical-Basis-of-Governance.md)

### Model answer

A **conflict of interest** exists when a public servant's private interest can improperly influence—or reasonably appear to influence—official duty. It may be actual, potential or apparent.

It manifests through biased procurement, recruitment of relatives, regulatory leniency toward a prospective employer, misuse of confidential information, gifts or allocation benefiting one's property. Even an honest decision loses legitimacy where the conflict is concealed.

Resolution follows: **identify → disclose → recuse → refer to an unconflicted authority → record reasons → permit audit**. Assets, gifts and outside employment should be declared; divestment or cooling-off rules may address continuing conflicts.

For example, an officer whose sibling owns a bidding firm should not evaluate the tender. In an emergency where recusal is impossible, she should minimise discretion, use pre-declared criteria, obtain collective approval and invite ex-post review.

Public office is a fiduciary trust. Inner confidence in one's impartiality cannot substitute for visible institutional safeguards.

**Examiner takeaway:** Conflict management protects both actual fairness and public confidence in fairness.

## Q9. Political mob gate-crashes a private factory

**Official case and demand (verified transcription):** A private company is known for its efficiency, transparency and employee welfare. The company though owned by a private individual has a cooperative character where employees feel a sense of ownership. The company employs nearly 700 personnel and they have voluntarily decided not to form a union.

One day suddenly in the morning, about 40 men belonging to a political party gate-crashed into the factory demanding jobs in the factory. They threatened the management and employees, and also used foul language. The employees feel demoralized. It was clear that those people who gate-crashed wanted to be on the payroll of the company as well as continue as the volunteers/members of the party.

The company maintains high standards in integrity and does not extend favours to civil administration that also includes law enforcement agency. Such incidents occur in public sector also.

(a) Assume you are the CEO of the company. What would you do to diffuse the volatile situation on the date of gate-crashing with the violent mob sitting inside the company premises?  
(b) What could be the long-term solution to the issue discussed in the case?  
(c) Every solution/action that you suggest will have a positive and a negative impact on you (as CEO), the employees and the performance of the employees. Analyze the consequences of each of your suggested actions. (250 words, 20 marks)

**Metadata:** 2015 · Mains · GS-IV · Section B · Official Q9 · Directive: Respond, prescribe and analyse consequences · Difficulty: High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Conflict Administration](../../topics/ethics/conflict-administration.md) → political coercion and organisational integrity · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Corporate governance](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/12_Corporate-Governance-and-International-Ethics.md) · [Case-study method](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/22_Case-Study-Method-and-Answer-Architecture.md)

### Stakeholders and ethical issues

Employees and families; CEO and owners; job-seekers; political party; police and district administration; customers and local community. Values are safety, merit, lawful association, organisational integrity, livelihood aspiration, non-partisanship and public order.

### Options and consequences

1. **Yield and put intruders on payroll:** may end the immediate siege, but rewards violence, destroys merit and invites repeated capture.
2. **Use force or provoke confrontation:** may restore control quickly, but risks injury, escalation and loss of evidence.
3. **Negotiate temporary peaceful exit while activating lawful protection:** protects life and integrity, though operations may pause and the CEO may face retaliation.

### Immediate course

I would activate emergency and medical protocols; secure hazardous areas; ask employees not to confront the group; preserve CCTV and attendance evidence; and inform police and the district magistrate in writing. With neutral witnesses, I would communicate that recruitment cannot occur under coercion, hear any legitimate employment grievance, offer a time-bound lawful meeting outside the premises and seek peaceful evacuation. Necessary force must remain proportionate and police-led.

### Long-term measures and safeguards

File and pursue the complaint without seeking favours; conduct an independent security review; establish access control, drills and employee counselling; create transparent recruitment criteria and a local skill/outreach programme without political quotas; build an industry-administration protocol for extortion; and support lawful employee representation.

These measures may raise cost and temporarily affect output, but reassure employees and deter recurrence. Litigation or political hostility is possible; collective industry action, documented neutrality and transparent hiring reduce personalised risk.

**Examiner takeaway:** De-escalation protects life, while documented legal action and transparent recruitment prevent coercion from becoming an employment channel.

## Q10. Dalit cook, caste prejudice and school attendance

### Stakeholders and ethical issues

Dalit cook and family; all children; parents; headmaster and teachers; Panchayat; education and social-justice authorities. The conflict concerns equality and dignity, children's education and nutrition, inherited prejudice, community harmony and lawful public employment.

### Options and consequences

1. **Remove or transfer the cook:** may restore attendance briefly but validates untouchability, humiliates the cook and entrenches discrimination.
2. **Rely only on punishment:** affirms law but may harden resistance and make children bear the conflict.
3. **Protect rights while combining persuasion, demonstration and monitoring:** slower, but changes norms without bargaining away equality.

### Feasible strategy

I would publicly affirm the lawful appointment and ensure the cook's safety and non-discrimination. Convene separate listening meetings and a Gram Sabha with teachers, health workers, respected leaders, women and youth. Demonstrate common hygiene standards and invite office-bearers to eat the same meal with children. Conduct home visits, keep the school and meal operating, monitor attendance and provide catch-up support. Threats or caste atrocities should be documented and referred to competent authorities.

### Shared responsibilities

- **Panchayat/Sarpanch:** constitutional leadership, dialogue and monitoring.
- **School/teachers:** equality education, safe meals and no segregation.
- **Parents/elders:** protect children's interests and reject inherited prejudice.
- **Youth, women, SHGs and faith/community leaders:** model shared dining and counter rumours.
- **Administration:** enforce anti-discrimination law, support the cook, inspect nutrition and prevent closure.
- **Media/civil society:** responsible awareness, not sensationalisation.

**Examiner takeaway:** Constitutional equality is non-negotiable; respectful persuasion and credible enforcement make it socially sustainable.

## Q11. Developing an unprofitable drug for a tribal disease

**Official case and demand (verified transcription):** One of the scientists working in the R & D laboratory of a major pharmaceutical company discovers that one of the company’s best selling veterinary drugs, B has the potential to cure a currently incurable liver disease that is prevalent in tribal areas. However, developing a variant of the drug suitable for human beings entailed a lot of research and development having a huge expenditure to the extent of ₹ 50 crores. It was unlikely that the company would recover the costs as the disease was rampant only in poverty-stricken area having very little market otherwise.

If you were the CEO, then—  
(a) identify the various actions that you could take;  
(b) evaluate the pros and cons of each of your actions. (250 words, 20 marks)

**Metadata:** 2015 · Mains · GS-IV · Section B · Official Q11 · Directive: Identify options and evaluate each · Difficulty: High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories and Bioethics](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories-and-bioethics.md) → access to medicines and corporate responsibility · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Corporate governance](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/12_Corporate-Governance-and-International-Ethics.md) · [Moral theories](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/08_Moral-Theories-Deontology-Consequentialism-Virtue-Ethics.md) · [Case-study method](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/22_Case-Study-Method-and-Answer-Architecture.md)

### Stakeholders and ethical issues

Tribal patients and communities; research participants; scientist and company staff; shareholders; government, public-health institutions and future patients. Issues are right to health, neglected disease, fiduciary duty, scientific uncertainty, affordability, informed consent and benefit-sharing.

### Options and consequences

1. **Abandon development:** preserves capital and core business, but forsakes a potentially life-saving discovery and damages social legitimacy.
2. **Fund the full ₹50 crore alone:** maximises control and speed, but concentrates scientific and financial risk and may create shareholder resistance.
3. **Seek public-philanthropic-academic partnership:** shares cost and expertise and supports affordable access, but negotiation and divided control may delay work.
4. **License or create a not-for-profit/open arrangement:** may accelerate wider research, but requires quality control and protection against appropriation.

### Justified choice

I would first commission independent pre-clinical validation and an ethics/public-health assessment. If promise remains, I would create a ring-fenced partnership with government research bodies, universities and credible foundations, contributing compound knowledge, facilities and part-finance. Agreements should guarantee transparent trials, tribal representation, culturally appropriate informed consent, data safety monitoring, affordable pricing, local health-system capacity and post-trial access.

Milestone funding and independent review would stop futile expenditure; intellectual-property terms could allow cost recovery in richer markets while assuring access for affected communities.

### Safeguards

No trial should exploit vulnerability or bypass human-drug standards. Publish conflicts and results, compensate research injury and share benefits. Corporate responsibility complements—not replaces—the state's health duty.

**Examiner takeaway:** Partnership reconciles innovation, fiduciary prudence and justice while keeping safety and affordable access non-negotiable.

## Q12. Ethical rescue prioritisation during a disaster

**Official case and demand (verified transcription):** There is a disaster-prone State having frequent landslides, forest fires, cloudbursts, flash floods and earthquakes, etc. Some of these are seasonal and often unpredictable. The magnitude of the disaster is always unanticipated. During one of the seasons, a cloudburst caused devastating floods and landslides leading to high casualties. There was major damage to infrastructure like roads, bridges and power generating units. This led to more than 100000 pilgrims, tourists and other locals trapped across different routes and locations. The people trapped in your area of responsibility included senior citizens, patients in hospitals, women and children, hikers, tourists, ruling party’s regional president along with his family, additional chief secretary of the neighbouring State and prisoners in jail.

As a civil services officer of the State, what would be the order in which you would rescue these people and why? Give justifications. (200 words, 20 marks)

**Metadata:** 2015 · Mains · GS-IV · Section B · Official Q12 · Directive: Prioritise and justify · Difficulty: High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Conflict Administration](../../topics/ethics/conflict-administration.md) → disaster triage and impartiality · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Foundational values](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/04_Aptitude-and-Foundational-Values-for-Civil-Service.md) · [Case-study method](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/22_Case-Study-Method-and-Answer-Architecture.md)

### Stakeholders and ethical issues

Stakeholders are all trapped persons, rescuers, families and responsible institutions. Values are equal worth, urgency, vulnerability, custodial duty and rescuer safety.

### Options and consequences

1. **VIP priority:** may aid coordination but violates equality and trust.
2. **First-come priority:** appears neutral but ignores urgency and vulnerability.
3. **Dynamic risk-based triage:** ethically strongest, but needs reliable, updated information.

### Priority framework

I would not rank people by status or category alone. Incident command should triage continuously using:

`imminent danger → medical vulnerability → inability to self-rescue → lives reachable → operational feasibility`.

### Operational order

1. **Persons in immediate lethal danger**, irrespective of identity.
2. **Critical patients, children, pregnant women, frail senior citizens and persons with disabilities** unable to self-rescue.
3. **Other groups by risk and accessibility**, using simultaneous teams; safe shelters receive supplies pending evacuation.
4. **Officials, political leader and family, tourists, pilgrims and locals** under identical criteria—never VIP precedence.
5. **Prisoners** are equal-value lives under heightened state custody; evacuation must also manage public risk.

### Safeguards

Publish criteria, log departures, reserve emergency capacity and protect rescue teams. The neighbouring chief secretary may aid coordination after rescue, but office confers no priority.

**Examiner takeaway:** Ethical triage is transparent, status-neutral and vulnerability-sensitive, maximising lives saved without abandoning persons in state custody.

## Q13. School site, heritage fort and suspected vested interests

**Official case and demand (verified transcription):** You are heading a district administration in a particular department. Your senior officer calls you from the State Headquarters and tells you that a plot in Rampur village is to have a building constructed on it for a school. A visit is scheduled during which he will visit the site along with the chief engineer and the senior architect. He wants you to check out all the papers relating to it and to ensure that the visit is properly arranged. You examine the file which relates to the period before you joined the department. The land was acquired from the local Panchayat at a nominal cost and the papers show that clearance certificates are available from the two of the three authorities who have to certify the site’s suitability. There is no certification by the architect available on file. You decide to visit Rampur to ensure that all is in order as stated on file. When you visit Rampur, you find that the plot under reference is part of Thakurgarh Fort and that the walls, ramparts, etc., are running across it. The fort is well away from the main village, therefore a school here will be a serious inconvenience for the children. However, the area near the village has potential to expand into a larger residential area. The development charges on the existing plot, at the fort, will be very high and the question of heritage site has not been addressed. Moreover, the Sarpanch, at the time of acquisition of the land, was a relative of your predecessor. The whole transaction appears to have been done with some vested interest.

(a) List the likely vested interests of the concerned parties.  
(b) Some of the options for action available to you are listed below. Discuss the merits and demerits of each of the options:  
(i) You can await the visit of the superior officer and let him take a decision.  
(ii) You can seek his advice in writing or on phone.  
(iii) You can consult your predecessor/colleagues, etc., and then decide what to do.  
(iv) You can find out if any alternate plot can be got in exchange and then send a comprehensive written report.  
Can you suggest any other option with proper justification? (250 words, 25 marks)

**Metadata:** 2015 · Mains · GS-IV · Section B · Official Q13 · Directive: Identify interests, compare options and propose a justified alternative · Difficulty: High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Corruption and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/corruption-and-administrative-ethics.md) → conflict of interest and reasoned administrative action · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Public-service dilemmas](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/09_Public-Service-Values-Status-and-Ethical-Dilemmas.md) · [Corruption challenges](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/18_Utilization-of-Public-Funds-and-Challenges-of-Corruption.md) · [Case-study method](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/22_Case-Study-Method-and-Answer-Architecture.md)

### Stakeholders, issues and likely vested interests

Children and parents; Panchayat; senior officer, predecessor, engineers and architect; heritage authority; taxpayers; nearby landowners. Possible interests include: predecessor/Sarpanch favour or concealment; owners near either site seeking appreciation or compensation; contractors benefiting from high development cost; officials avoiding scrutiny; and political credit for quick construction. These are hypotheses requiring verification, not accusations.

### Option analysis

1. **Await the visit:** respects hierarchy and avoids premature judgment, but conceals known risks, wastes senior time and permits fait accompli.
2. **Seek advice:** early escalation protects the record; phone advice is fast but ambiguous, so it must be followed by writing.
3. **Consult predecessor/colleagues:** may reveal context, but risks collusion, evidence contamination and undue influence; consultation should be recorded and non-exclusive.
4. **Find exchange land and report:** constructive and child-centred, but I lack authority to negotiate secretly and an exchange could create a fresh favour.

### Preferred course

I would immediately place a factual written note before the senior: missing clearance, heritage concern, distance, cost and apparent conflict. Recommend postponing irreversible action while a multidisciplinary inspection verifies title, heritage status, accessibility and cost. Preserve files, obtain the architect's independent assessment, map alternative public plots transparently and consult the community.

If prima facie manipulation emerges, refer it for competent inquiry without prejudging guilt. Compare retain, exchange and alternate-site options through published cost, safety, heritage and access criteria.

### Safeguards

No construction or demolition pending mandatory clearances; conflict disclosures; recorded decisions; independent valuation; open consultation; audit trail and time-bound decision so inquiry does not indefinitely deny children a school.

**Examiner takeaway:** The officer should neither remain passive nor become investigator-judge; disclose facts, prevent irreversible harm and enable an independent, transparent decision.

## Q14. Girls' education, safety and patriarchal backlash

**Official case and demand (verified transcription):** You are recently posted as district development officer of a district. Shortly thereafter you found that there is considerable tension in the rural areas of your district on the issue of sending girls to schools.

The elders of the village feel that many problems have come up because girls are being educated and they are stepping out of the safe environment of the household. They are of the view that the girls should be quickly married off with minimum education. The girls are also competing for jobs after education, which have traditionally remained in boys’ exclusive domain, adding to unemployment amongst male population.

The younger generation feels that in the present era, girls should have equal opportunities for education and employment, and other means of livelihood. The entire locality is divided between the elders and the younger lot and further sub-divided between sexes in both generations. You come to know that in Panchayat or in other local bodies or even in busy crossroads, the issue is being acrimoniously debated.

One day you are informed that an unpleasant incident has taken place. Some girls were molested, when they were en route to schools. The incident led to clashes between several groups and a law and order problem has arisen. The elders after heated discussion have taken a joint decision not to allow girls to go to school and to socially boycott all such families, which do not follow their dictate.

(a) What steps would you take to ensure girls’ safety without disrupting their education?  
(b) How would you manage and mould patriarchic attitude of the village elders to ensure harmony in the inter-generational relations? (250 words, 25 marks)

**Metadata:** 2015 · Mains · GS-IV · Section B · Official Q14 · Directive: Protect, manage conflict and transform attitudes · Difficulty: High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Conflict Administration](../../topics/ethics/conflict-administration.md) → gender justice, safety and social persuasion · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Attitude and persuasion](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/03_Attitude-Content-Structure-and-Persuasion.md) · [Human values](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/02_Human-Values-and-Lessons-from-Leaders.md) · [Case-study method](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/22_Case-Study-Method-and-Answer-Architecture.md)

### Stakeholders and ethical issues

Girls and their families; boys and youth; elders; teachers; police, Panchayat and district administration. Issues are bodily safety, equal education, dignity, patriarchal control, collective punishment, social boycott, unemployment anxiety, inter-generational trust and lawful public order.

### Immediate options and consequences

1. **Accept school closure for “safety”:** may reduce travel risk temporarily but rewards perpetrators, violates equality and entrenches child marriage and exclusion.
2. **Use policing alone:** restores order and deters violence, but cannot by itself transform patriarchal beliefs.
3. **Combine protection, uninterrupted schooling, justice and sustained dialogue:** addresses both immediate harm and its social causes.

### Safety without educational disruption

Register and investigate molestation promptly through trained personnel; protect survivors' privacy and prevent retaliation. Deploy route patrols, safe transport/escorts where needed, functioning helplines, female staff, lighting and safe-school committees. Stagger or temporarily relocate classes only if required for safety—not terminate education. Act lawfully against violence, intimidation and coercive boycott; provide counselling and academic support.

### Moulding attitudes and restoring harmony

Hold facilitated small-group dialogues before a wider Gram Sabha; involve respected women, teachers, health workers, elected representatives and supportive elders. Use local women role models and evidence that girls' education improves family health, income and dignity. Engage boys on consent, shared opportunity and non-violent masculinity; address male unemployment through skills and livelihood programmes rather than blaming girls.

### Safeguards

Create a district-Panchayat action plan with attendance and safety indicators, anonymous feedback, regular review and equal voice for girls. Persuasion should respect elders but never bargain away constitutional rights.

**Examiner takeaway:** The ethical response shifts restrictions from girls to offenders and combines justice, enabling infrastructure and respectful norm change.
