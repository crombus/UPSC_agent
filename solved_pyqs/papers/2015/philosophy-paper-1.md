### Q1(a). Aristotle’s distinction between ‘actually’ and ‘potentiality’.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Plato–Aristotle](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/plato-aristotle.md) → actuality and potentiality · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Directive: Explain the distinction · Syllabus unit: Aristotle—matter and form; actuality and potentiality · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Plato–Aristotle](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Plato-Aristotle.md) · **Slug:** `aristotle-actuality-potentiality`

**Demand decoding:** The source prints “actually”; explain the standard Aristotelian distinction between actuality and potentiality, its relation to change, matter/form and priority.

**Argument map:** potentiality = real capacity → actuality = realised capacity/activity → change = actualisation of the potential as potential → matter bears capacities, form actualises them → actuality is prior in definition and end, though a particular actuality may arise from prior potentiality.

#### Model answer

Potentiality (*dynamis*) is a real capacity to be or act otherwise: an acorn can become an oak and a trained builder can build. Actuality (*energeia* or *entelecheia*) is the realised state or exercise of that capacity—the mature oak or the activity of building. Potentiality is not mere logical possibility; it belongs to a subject with an appropriate nature and conditions.

Aristotle defines change as the actuality of what exists potentially, precisely insofar as it is potential. His distinction explains becoming without deriving being from sheer non-being. In hylomorphism, matter supplies determinate capacities and form actualises them; the eye is potentially seeing while sight is its activity.

Actuality is prior in definition, intelligibility and final causation: we identify a capacity by the activity toward which it tends. Temporally, however, this individual oak follows the potential acorn, though the acorn itself came from an actual oak.

**Evaluation:** The distinction unifies Aristotle’s accounts of change, substance and teleology, but presupposes that natural capacities have objective ends.

**Why this earns marks:** It defines both terms, relates them to change and hylomorphism, and states Aristotle’s qualified priority thesis.

### Q1(b). *esse est percipi*.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Empiricism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/empiricism.md) → Berkeley’s idealism · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Directive: Explain and assess · Syllabus unit: Berkeley—rejection of material substance; idealism · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Empiricism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Empiricism.md) · **Slug:** `berkeley-esse-est-percipi`

**Demand decoding:** Explain “to be is to be perceived” for sensible objects, distinguish ideas from spirits, and address continuity and objectivity.

**Argument map:** sensible object = collection of ideas → ideas exist only as perceived → abstract material substratum is unintelligible → therefore sensible existence is perceived existence → minds exist as perceivers, not perceived ideas → God secures continuity and order.

#### Model answer

Berkeley’s *esse est percipi* means that the being of a sensible object consists in its being perceived. An apple is not an unknowable material substance behind experience; it is an ordered collection of colour, taste, shape and resistance. Since these are ideas, and ideas exist only in a mind, an unperceived sensible quality is contradictory.

The maxim does not apply identically to everything. Ideas are passive and exist by being perceived; spirits or minds are active perceivers whose being is to perceive and will. Berkeley therefore rejects material substance, not external reality: objects are external to an individual finite mind because they are not voluntarily produced by it.

Continuity when no human observes an object is secured by God’s constant perception, while stable laws among ideas ground objectivity.

**Objection and reply:** Critics charge that divine perception is an ad hoc support and that representation is confused with existence. Berkeley replies that “matter” explains nothing beyond the experienced order, whereas a divine cause explains its involuntary regularity.

**Why this earns marks:** It states the thesis without falsely making perceiving minds into ideas or reducing Berkeley to subjective solipsism.

### Q1(c). Husserl’s notion of ‘bracketing’.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Phenomenology](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/phenomenology.md) → epoché and reduction · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Directive: Explain · Syllabus unit: Husserl—phenomenological method · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Phenomenology](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Phenomenology-Husserl.md) · **Slug:** `husserl-bracketing-epoche`

**Demand decoding:** Define bracketing as suspension rather than denial, identify what is bracketed, and explain its methodological purpose.

**Argument map:** natural attitude posits world’s independent existence → epoché suspends commitment to that positing → attention shifts to how objects are given → phenomenological reduction discloses intentional structures and transcendental subjectivity → essences are investigated without metaphysical prejudice.

#### Model answer

Husserl’s “bracketing” or epoché is the methodological suspension of the natural attitude—the unexamined commitment that the world simply exists independently in the manner ordinarily assumed. It neither denies the world nor doubts it in the Cartesian sense. The existence-claim is placed “out of play” so that inquiry can examine how objects appear and acquire meaning in consciousness.

Through phenomenological reduction, attention turns from the perceived tree as a natural object to the intentional correlation between act and object-as-intended: perceiving, remembering and imagining present the same object through different modes and horizons. Further transcendental reduction investigates the constituting structures of subjectivity; eidetic variation seeks invariant essences.

Bracketing thus frees phenomenology from naturalistic and metaphysical presuppositions and makes experience describable as given.

**Evaluation:** The method powerfully reveals intentional structure, but critics ask whether all existential commitments can actually be suspended and whether transcendental subjectivity reintroduces idealism.

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes suspension from denial and connects epoché to intentional analysis and reduction.

### Q1(d). Strawson’s distinction between ‘M’ and ‘P’ predicates.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Quine–Strawson](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/quine-strawson.md) → concept of person · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Directive: Explain the distinction · Syllabus unit: Strawson—person and descriptive metaphysics · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Quine–Strawson](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Quine-Strawson.md) · **Slug:** `strawson-m-p-predicates`

**Demand decoding:** Define material and person predicates and show how their joint attribution supports Strawson’s primitive concept of a person.

**Argument map:** M-predicates apply to bodies → P-predicates apply to persons and include consciousness/action → same individual receives both → first-person psychological use is non-observational, third-person use criterial → person cannot be reduced to body plus private ego.

#### Model answer

In *Individuals*, Strawson distinguishes **M-predicates**, attributable to material bodies, from **P-predicates**, attributable to persons. M-predicates include “weighs sixty kilograms,” “is six feet tall” and “is in the room.” P-predicates include bodily actions such as “is walking” and states of consciousness such as “is in pain,” “believes” or “intends.”

The distinction supports the claim that “person” is a primitive concept: one and the same individual is the subject of both kinds of predicate. A person is therefore neither a Cartesian mind temporarily linked to a body nor merely a body from which mental predicates are inferred.

Psychological P-predicates have an asymmetry. I commonly ascribe pain to myself without observing behaviour, but ascribe it to others on behavioural criteria. Yet the concept remains the same because learning its first-person use presupposes its publicly applicable third-person use.

**Evaluation:** Strawson avoids substance dualism, though the account may describe our conceptual scheme without fully explaining consciousness.

**Why this earns marks:** It gives examples, attributional asymmetry and the anti-dualist conclusion.

### Q1(e). G.E. Moore’s refutation of idealism.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Moore–Russell–Early Wittgenstein](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/moore-russell-early-wittgenstein.md) → Moore’s realism · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Directive: Explain the refutation · Syllabus unit: Moore—defence of common sense; refutation of idealism · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Moore–Russell–Early Wittgenstein](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Moore-Russell-EarlyWittgenstein.md) · **Slug:** `moore-refutation-idealism`

**Demand decoding:** Reconstruct Moore’s attack on *esse est percipi*, especially the act–object distinction, and assess its reach.

**Argument map:** idealist identifies experienced object with experience → sensation of blue contains awareness plus blue → awareness and object are distinguishable → blue is not a mental state merely because apprehended → idealist inference from inseparability in cognition to identity fails.

#### Model answer

Moore attacks the idealist maxim *esse est percipi* by analysing sensation. In a sensation of blue, consciousness or awareness is directed toward blue. Idealists allegedly infer that because blue is experienced only in awareness, blue is identical with, or an inseparable part of, the mental experience.

Moore argues that awareness and its object are distinct. Different sensations share the transparent element of awareness while differing in objects—blue, green, pain. Introspection therefore reveals not one mental item called “blue-sensation,” but an act of awareness and the object blue. From “blue is apprehended” it does not follow that “blue is apprehension.”

This opens logical space for direct realism: objects may exist independently although known through consciousness.

**Qualification:** Moore refutes a key identity argument, not every form of idealism. A sophisticated idealist can accept an act–object distinction while claiming that both belong within experience or absolute mind.

**Why this earns marks:** It reconstructs the transparency argument and gives a precise limitation rather than merely invoking common sense.

### Q2(a). Explain Kant’s division of categories.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Kant](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/kant.md) → table of categories · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 20 marks · Directive: Explain · Syllabus unit: Kant—possibility of synthetic a priori judgements; categories · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Kant](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Kant.md) · **Slug:** `kant-division-categories`

**Demand decoding:** State the four headings and twelve categories, derive them from logical forms of judgement, explain their function and schematised application, and note the restriction to experience.

**Argument map:** sensibility gives manifold in space/time → understanding unifies through judgement → logical functions of judgement yield twelve pure concepts → categories organise appearances into objects → schemata mediate category and intuition → transcendental deduction establishes objective validity → use beyond possible experience is illegitimate.

#### Model answer

Kant calls categories the pure concepts through which the understanding synthesises the manifold supplied by sensibility. They are not abstracted from experience; they are *a priori* conditions under which representations can count as an objective experience. He derives their division from the logical forms of judgement:

| Heading | Forms of judgement | Categories |
|---|---|---|
| **Quantity** | universal, particular, singular | unity, plurality, totality |
| **Quality** | affirmative, negative, infinite | reality, negation, limitation |
| **Relation** | categorical, hypothetical, disjunctive | substance–accident, cause–effect, community/reciprocity |
| **Modality** | problematic, assertoric, apodictic | possibility–impossibility, existence–non-existence, necessity–contingency |

The triadic arrangement is systematic: under each heading the third category arises from combining the first two—for example, totality is plurality considered as unity, and limitation is reality joined with negation.

Categories operate only on the sensible manifold. The transcendental deduction argues that because the “I think” must be able to accompany all representations, the manifold must be combined according to rules; the categories are these fundamental rules of objective unity. The schematism explains their temporal application: permanence is the schema of substance, regular succession of causality, and coexistence under reciprocal rule of community.

The principles generated by them include axioms of intuition, anticipations of perception, analogies of experience and postulates of empirical thought. Thus categories make synthetic *a priori* knowledge of nature possible.

**Objection and evaluation:** Kant’s derivation is criticised as dependent on an inherited logical table and the claim that all twelve are exhaustive is disputable. Nevertheless, his central insight remains powerful: objectivity requires constitutive conceptual rules. Categories legitimately determine appearances, not things-in-themselves; applying them to soul, world or God beyond possible intuition produces transcendental illusion.

**Why this earns marks:** It supplies the complete table, derivation, function, schematism, deduction and critical limit.

### Q2(b). Discuss why Heidegger introduces the concept of *Dasein* in his metaphysics.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Existentialism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/existentialism.md) → Heidegger’s Dasein · **Secondary route:** [Phenomenology](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/phenomenology.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Directive: Discuss why · Syllabus unit: Existentialism—Heidegger · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Existentialism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Existentialism.md) · **Secondary classification:** [Phenomenology](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Phenomenology-Husserl.md) · **Slug:** `heidegger-why-dasein`

**Demand decoding:** Explain Dasein’s methodological necessity for reopening the question of Being, not merely list existential features.

**Argument map:** Western metaphysics studies beings and forgets Being → inquiry needs an entity already understanding Being → human existence is this “there” of disclosure → Dasein’s essence lies in existence and being-in-the-world → analytic of care, temporality and finitude supplies horizon for fundamental ontology.

#### Model answer

Heidegger introduces *Dasein*—literally “being-there”—because the traditional question of the meaning of Being has been obscured by treating Being as another highest entity. Fundamental ontology must begin with the being for whom Being is already an issue. Human existence has a pre-theoretical understanding of what it means for things to be; hence Dasein has both **ontic priority** as the inquirer and **ontological priority** as the site where Being is disclosed.

Dasein is not a Cartesian subject enclosed within consciousness. Its basic constitution is being-in-the-world: it encounters equipment as ready-to-hand within practical contexts before representing neutral objects. It is being-with others, thrown into inherited possibilities, yet projects itself toward possibilities. These dimensions form the unity of **care**.

The analysis also reveals falling into the anonymous “they,” anxiety, being-toward-death and the possibility of authentic resoluteness. Most importantly, Dasein’s ecstatic temporality—having-been, future projection and present engagement—provides the horizon within which Being is understood.

**Evaluation:** Dasein lets Heidegger overcome subject–object dualism and ground ontology in lived disclosure. Yet critics ask whether privileging human disclosure remains anthropocentric, while later Heidegger himself shifts emphasis from Dasein’s structures toward the historical event of Being.

**Why this earns marks:** It connects every existential feature to the methodological reason for introducing Dasein.

### Q2(c). Elucidate Quine’s arguments to show that analyticity is not synonymity.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Quine–Strawson](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/quine-strawson.md) → analyticity and synonymy · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Directive: Elucidate · Syllabus unit: Quine—critique of analytic–synthetic distinction · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Quine–Strawson](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Quine-Strawson.md) · **Slug:** `quine-analyticity-not-synonymity`

**Demand decoding:** Preserve the official wording while explaining why synonymy cannot provide a non-circular reduction of analyticity.

**Argument map:** second-class analytic truths require substitution of synonyms → definition records rather than creates synonymy → interchangeability *salva veritate* is insufficient or intensional → necessity presupposes analyticity → semantic rules label without explaining → proposed reduction is circular → holism replaces sharp boundary.

#### Model answer

Quine distinguishes logical truths, such as “No unmarried man is married,” from statements said to become logical truths by replacing synonyms, such as “No bachelor is married.” If analyticity is explained through synonymity, synonymity itself requires a non-circular account.

**Definition** cannot supply it. Lexicographic definition records an antecedent usage; stipulative definition creates synonymy only locally and cannot explain ordinary analyticity. **Interchangeability *salva veritate*** also fails. “Bachelor” and “unmarried man” may be substituted in extensional contexts, but so may accidentally coextensive expressions such as “creature with a heart” and “creature with kidneys.” In intensional contexts substitution works only if the language already contains “necessarily”; yet explaining synonymy as necessary coextension presupposes an account of necessity normally defined through analyticity.

Appeal to **semantic rules** fares no better: saying that a framework designates certain statements “analytic” identifies a class but does not explain the common property of analyticity, except relative to stipulated formal languages.

Thus synonymity does not independently ground analyticity; the concepts form an explanatory circle. Quine replaces the sharp analytic–synthetic boundary with confirmation holism: statements confront experience as a web and differ in revisability by degree.

**Evaluation:** Quine exposes failures of reductive definitions, though Carnap and Grice–Strawson reply that interdependence among semantic concepts does not prove their unintelligibility. A contextual analytic distinction may survive even if no behaviouristic reduction succeeds.

**Why this earns marks:** It reconstructs each anti-reduction argument and gives Quine’s positive holistic alternative.

### Q3(a). Bring out the relationship between language games and forms of life.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Later Wittgenstein](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/later-wittgenstein.md) → language-games and forms of life · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 20 marks · Directive: Bring out the relationship · Syllabus unit: Later Wittgenstein—meaning and use · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Later Wittgenstein](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Later-Wittgenstein.md) · **Slug:** `language-games-forms-of-life`

**Demand decoding:** Define both notions, show their reciprocal relation through rule-following and practice, explain philosophical consequences, and avoid cultural relativism.

**Argument map:** words gain meaning in rule-governed activities → activities are woven into broader human practices → forms of life supply shared reactions, purposes and criteria → language-games articulate and reproduce those forms → neither private meanings nor one essence of language → disagreement ends against practical background, not arbitrary convention.

#### Model answer

For later Wittgenstein, a **language-game** is language viewed together with the activity into which it is woven: commanding, reporting, calculating, joking, praying or asking for help. The term stresses that speaking is rule-governed conduct with diverse purposes, not the uniform act of naming objects. Meaning is therefore use within a practice.

A **form of life** is the wider pattern of natural reactions, social practices, institutions and concerns that makes such use intelligible. A builder’s call “Slab!” functions as an order because workers share a practice of construction; the same sound outside that setting need not have that role. Likewise, pain-language rests on human patterns of injury, expression, care and response.

Their relation is reciprocal:

1. Forms of life provide the practical background, criteria and point of language-games.
2. Language-games articulate, transmit and partly sustain forms of life.
3. Rules do not interpret themselves; training and concord in practice establish what counts as following them.
4. Justification eventually reaches “bedrock”—what members do—not a private mental representation.

This explains why an essentially private language is impossible: without shareable practices there is no distinction between correct and merely seeming-correct use. It also dissolves problems produced by removing expressions from their home games.

**Objections:** “Form of life” can appear conservative or relativistic. Wittgenstein does not claim that every convention is immune to criticism. Forms of life include broadly human natural facts as well as changeable cultures, and games can be compared, revised or contested from overlapping practices. Nor are language-games sealed units; family resemblances and translation connect them.

**Evaluation:** Forms of life ground the normativity and intelligibility of language-games, while language-games make those lived patterns expressible. The account rejects both atomistic semantics and meanings fixed by private consciousness without reducing truth to arbitrary consensus.

**Why this earns marks:** It explains the two concepts through rule-following, examples, philosophical implications and a qualified response to relativism.

### Q3(b). Explain Leibnitz’s principle of ‘identity of indiscernibles’.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Rationalism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/rationalism.md) → Leibniz on individuation · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Directive: Explain · Syllabus unit: Leibniz—substance and individuation · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Rationalism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Rationalism.md) · **Slug:** `leibniz-identity-indiscernibles`

**Demand decoding:** Preserve the source spelling “Leibnitz”; state the formal principle, distinguish it from indiscernibility of identicals, ground it in sufficient reason, and examine counterexamples.

**Argument map:** no two numerically distinct substances share every property → otherwise God lacks sufficient reason to place/name them differently → each monad has a complete concept and unique perspective → qualitative indiscernibility implies identity → symmetric-world and quantum objections test property range.

#### Model answer

Leibniz’s identity of indiscernibles states that there cannot be two numerically distinct entities having all the same properties. Formally, if every property of *x* is a property of *y* and vice versa, then *x = y*. This is the converse of the uncontroversial indiscernibility of identicals, according to which identical things share every property.

The principle follows from the **principle of sufficient reason**. If qualitatively identical objects were distinct, there would be no sufficient reason why God placed one here and the other there, or why they received different names. In Leibniz’s metaphysics every monad expresses the universe from a unique perspective and has a complete individual concept; perfect qualitative duplication is therefore impossible. His famous challenge to Clarke is that two entirely identical leaves cannot be found.

**Objections:** Max Black’s imagined symmetric universe containing only two exactly similar spheres seems to permit numerical difference without qualitative difference. Quantum particles also appear indiscernible under many classical properties. Defenders include relational or haecceitistic properties—“being this sphere” or occupying distinct relational positions—but this risks making the principle trivially true.

**Evaluation:** As a demand that real individuality must have an intelligible basis, the principle is powerful. As an unrestricted logical truth, it is contestable and depends on which properties count as legitimate.

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes the converse principles, gives Leibniz’s rationale and tests it against serious counterexamples.

### Q3(c). What do we understand by incomplete symbols ? What role do they play in Russell’s theory of meaning ? Discuss.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Moore–Russell–Early Wittgenstein](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/moore-russell-early-wittgenstein.md) → Russell’s logical constructions · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Directive: Define, explain role, and discuss · Syllabus unit: Russell—logical atomism; theory of descriptions · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Moore–Russell–Early Wittgenstein](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Moore-Russell-EarlyWittgenstein.md) · **Slug:** `russell-incomplete-symbols-meaning`

**Demand decoding:** Explain contextual definition, use definite descriptions as the central case, and connect logical analysis to ontological economy and meaning.

**Argument map:** surface grammar treats phrase as name → no independent referent exists → analyse phrase only within whole proposition → quantified structure gives truth conditions → apparent entities are logical constructions → meaning derives contextually, avoiding Meinongian objects and reference failure.

#### Model answer

For Russell, an **incomplete symbol** has no meaning in isolation but is defined through the propositions in which it occurs. Its grammatical appearance as a name does not require a corresponding entity. The paradigm is a definite description.

“The present King of France is bald” is not analysed as subject–predicate discourse about a mysterious king. It means: there exists at least one present King of France; there is at most one; and whoever satisfies that condition is bald. Since the existence clause is false, the whole proposition is false, while its negation can be disambiguated by scope.

Incomplete symbols perform three roles:

1. **Contextual meaning:** significance belongs to the complete proposition after logical analysis.
2. **Ontological economy:** descriptions, classes and other logical constructions need not be admitted as independently subsisting objects.
3. **Epistemic discipline:** ordinary objects can be constructed from entities with which we are acquainted, supporting Russell’s distinction between knowledge by acquaintance and description.

The theory also separates grammatical from logical form and avoids Meinong’s realm of non-existent objects.

**Evaluation:** The analysis explains reference failure and scope with great precision. Strawson objects that ordinary definite descriptions presuppose rather than assert existence, and later contextualists emphasise speaker use. Even so, incomplete symbols established a durable method: paraphrase apparent names into quantified structures before drawing ontological conclusions.

**Why this earns marks:** It supplies the formal function, philosophical role and major limitation.

### Q4(a). Explain Plato’s theory of forms. Does it entail a kind of essentialism ? Discuss.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Plato–Aristotle](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/plato-aristotle.md) → Forms and essentialism · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 20 marks · Directive: Explain and discuss · Syllabus unit: Plato—Forms; knowledge and opinion · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Plato–Aristotle](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Plato-Aristotle.md) · **Slug:** `plato-forms-essentialism`

**Demand decoding:** Reconstruct the Forms, their epistemic and metaphysical roles, define the relevant essentialism, then test whether the theory necessarily entails it.

**Argument map:** sensible particulars change and imperfectly share predicates → stable knowledge requires unchanging intelligible objects → Forms are universal, separate paradigms and causes → particulars are what they are through participation → fixed essences ground classification and definition → self-predication, Third Man and separation problems qualify the entailment.

#### Model answer

Plato’s Forms are eternal, immutable, non-spatial and intelligible realities such as Beauty itself, Equality itself and Justice itself. Sensible particulars are many, changing and imperfect; they are beautiful or equal by participating in, imitating or being patterned after the corresponding Form. Forms therefore perform several functions:

1. **Ontological and semantic:** they explain what particulars are and why many receive one predicate.
2. **Epistemic:** stable knowledge (*epistēmē*) concerns Forms, whereas changing sensibles yield opinion (*doxa*).
3. **Normative and teleological:** Forms supply standards; the Good grounds intelligibility and value.

The theory entails a strong kind of **essentialism** if essentialism means that members of a kind possess an objective, discoverable “what-it-is” independent of convention. A triangle is intelligible through a fixed nature, and just acts are judged through Justice itself. Knowledge seeks definitions that capture such invariant essences rather than cataloguing appearances.

Yet Plato does not clearly posit a separate Form for every ordinary class; the *Parmenides* questions Forms of mud or hair. Real essences do not entail that every linguistic class has timeless necessary and sufficient properties.

Aristotle’s Third Man objection shows a difficulty: if a large thing and Largeness are both large, another Form seems required to explain their common character, generating regress. The separation of Forms also threatens their explanatory role—how can a transcendent Form cause or be present in a sensible thing? The *Parmenides* acknowledges these problems; the *Timaeus* supplements Forms with receptacle and craftsman.

**Evaluation:** Platonic Forms clearly express metaphysical and epistemological essentialism: reality and definition are anchored in objective natures. But the theory need not entail a rigid essence for every linguistic classification, and its transcendent version struggles to explain participation. Its enduring achievement is to make knowledge, predication and normativity answerable to more than shifting convention.

**Why this earns marks:** It explains all major functions, defines the exact essentialist commitment, and gives internal objections and a qualified verdict.

### Q4(b). Does Descartes Cogito principle entail that he is an essentialist ? Discuss.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Rationalism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/rationalism.md) → cogito and essence of self · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Directive: Discuss · Syllabus unit: Descartes—method and substance; mind–body dualism · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Rationalism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Rationalism.md) · **Slug:** `descartes-cogito-essentialism`

**Demand decoding:** Distinguish what the cogito immediately establishes from Descartes’ later essentialist conclusions about mind.

**Argument map:** doubting is thinking → thinking cannot occur without existent thinker → cogito establishes existence whenever thought occurs → clear-and-distinct reflection identifies self as thinking thing → real distinction argues thought essential to mind, extension to body → entailment from cogito alone is contested.

#### Model answer

The *cogito*—“I think, therefore I am”—is indubitable whenever performed. Even an evil deceiver cannot make me think without my existing. Its immediate conclusion is existential: while thinking, I necessarily exist.

Descartes proceeds to ask, “What am I?” and answers: a thinking thing (*res cogitans*) that doubts, understands, affirms, denies, wills and imagines. Because he can clearly and distinctly conceive himself without body but not without thought, he treats thought as the mind’s principal attribute or essence, just as extension is the essence of body. This supports substance dualism and an essentialist account of mental nature.

However, the **cogito alone** does not entail that conclusion. “Thinking occurs, therefore I exist now” does not by itself establish a substantial, simple or continuously existing ego, nor that thought is an essential property at every time. Kant later calls the move from the logical subject of thought to a soul-substance a paralogism. Even “I” may identify only the subject-position of the current act.

Descartes replies that clear-and-distinct perception, secured by the non-deceiving God, warrants the real distinction and essential attributes; thus his essentialism depends on the wider metaphysics, not solely on the cogito.

**Evaluation:** The cogito initiates an essentialist trajectory by revealing thought as inseparable from self-certainty, but it strictly entails only occurrent existence. Cartesian essentialism requires additional arguments.

**Why this earns marks:** It separates immediate entailment from the broader Cartesian doctrine and answers the exact yes/no issue with qualification.

### Q4(c). Bring out the relationship between existence and essence in the case of human being. Explain the issues it gives rise to for human beings according to Sartre.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Existentialism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/existentialism.md) → Sartre on existence and essence · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Directive: Explain relationship and consequences · Syllabus unit: Existentialism—Sartre · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Existentialism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Existentialism.md) · **Slug:** `sartre-existence-essence-human-condition`

**Demand decoding:** Explain “existence precedes essence” and derive freedom, responsibility, anguish, abandonment, despair and bad faith.

**Argument map:** artefact has concept before production → godless human has no prior design → person first exists, then defines self through projects → consciousness is nothingness and transcendence of facticity → radical freedom entails responsibility → anguish/abandonment/despair tempt bad faith → authenticity owns situated freedom.

#### Model answer

For a manufactured object, essence precedes existence: the artisan has a concept of a paper-knife before making it. Sartre denies an analogous divine blueprint for humanity. A human being first exists, encounters the world, and only later becomes something through choices. Hence “existence precedes essence.”

The human being is a **being-for-itself**, not the fixed **being-in-itself** of things. Consciousness introduces nothingness: it can negate what is given and project possibilities beyond its facticity—body, past, social location and circumstances. Freedom is therefore situated but inescapable; even refusal is a choice.

This relation generates characteristic issues:

- **Responsibility:** by choosing myself I affirm an image of humanity and cannot transfer authorship to nature or God.
- **Anguish:** I recognise that no external rule guarantees my choice.
- **Abandonment:** without God, values are not pre-inscribed.
- **Despair:** action must be limited to possibilities dependent on my agency.
- **Bad faith:** I flee freedom by pretending to be a fixed role, or deny facticity by imagining pure unconstrained possibility.
- **Conflict:** other people’s look can objectify me, although recognition also reveals social existence.

**Evaluation:** Sartre compellingly resists biological or social determinism, but radical self-creation can understate embodied dependence and inherited moral relations. His best position is not arbitrary choice: authenticity requires lucid ownership of freedom within facticity.

**Why this earns marks:** It derives the named existential consequences from the existence–essence reversal and supplies a balanced evaluation.

### Q5(a). *Pancavidhabheda*
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Vedānta](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/vedanta.md) → Madhva’s fivefold difference · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Directive: Explain · Syllabus unit: Vedānta—Madhva · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Vedānta](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Vedanta.md) · **Slug:** `madhva-pancavidha-bheda`

**Demand decoding:** Preserve the source transliteration while explaining fivefold difference (*pañcavidha-bheda/pañcabheda*) and its role in Dvaita.

**Argument map:** reality contains independent God and dependent souls/matter → difference is real, not ignorance → five relations structure plurality → liberation removes bondage, not difference → devotion and grace presuppose enduring distinction.

#### Model answer

Fivefold difference (*pañcavidha-bheda*, commonly *pañcabheda*) is central to Madhva’s dualism (Dvaita). It affirms five eternal and real distinctions:

1. God and individual self (*Īśvara–jīva*);
2. God and matter (*Īśvara–jaḍa*);
3. one self and another self (*jīva–jīva*);
4. self and matter (*jīva–jaḍa*);
5. one material entity and another (*jaḍa–jaḍa*).

Only Viṣṇu is independent reality (*svatantra*); souls and matter are real but dependent (*paratantra*). Difference is neither a product of ignorance nor merely practical appearance. It explains finite agency, moral responsibility, devotion and the world’s plurality. Even liberation does not dissolve the self into Brahman; the liberated soul enjoys God through knowledge, devotion and grace while remaining distinct.

Advaita objects that absolute difference compromises Upaniṣadic unity. Madhva interprets unity as divine supremacy and dependence, not numerical identity.

**Evaluation:** Fivefold difference protects realism and personal theism, though eternal hierarchy and plurality make ultimate metaphysical unity difficult.

**Why this earns marks:** It accurately enumerates all five differences and connects them to dependence, devotion and liberation.

### Q5(b). Jain concept of *Dravya*
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Jainism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/jainism.md) → substance · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Directive: Explain · Syllabus unit: Jainism—ontology · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Jainism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Jainism.md) · **Slug:** `jaina-concept-dravya`

**Demand decoding:** Define substance through origination, destruction and persistence; classify six substances and connect the theory to many-sided realism.

**Argument map:** substance possesses qualities and modes → modes arise/perish while underlying continuity persists → six substances exhaust reality → change and permanence are jointly real → standpoint-conditioned predication follows.

#### Model answer

In Jainism, substance (*dravya*) is that which possesses qualities (*guṇa*) and modes (*paryāya*). Reality combines persistence with change: a substance undergoes origination of a new mode, destruction of an old mode and permanence of its underlying identity. Thus neither absolute permanence nor total momentariness is adequate.

Six substances are recognised:

1. conscious souls (*jīva*);
2. matter (*pudgala*);
3. medium of motion (*dharma-dravya*);
4. medium of rest (*adharma-dravya*);
5. space (*ākāśa*);
6. time (*kāla*).

The first five are extended substances (*astikāya*); time is ordinarily excluded from this class. Souls are conscious but bound through karmic matter; the remaining substances are non-conscious (*ajīva*).

This ontology grounds many-sidedness (*anekāntavāda*): a pot is permanent as substance yet impermanent in modes. Different predications can therefore be valid from different standpoints without becoming subjectively arbitrary.

**Evaluation:** The account elegantly reconciles identity and change, though entities such as media of motion and rest are metaphysically contentious.

**Why this earns marks:** It gives the defining triad, complete taxonomy and philosophical significance.

### Q5(c). *Ālayavijñāna*
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Buddhism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/buddhism.md) → Yogācāra store consciousness · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Directive: Explain · Syllabus unit: Buddhism—Yogācāra · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Buddhism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Buddhism.md) · **Slug:** `yogacara-alayavijnana`

**Demand decoding:** Explain store consciousness as a causal continuum, its seed theory and transformation, while distinguishing it from a permanent self.

**Argument map:** momentary cognitions require continuity for karma and memory → store consciousness carries karmic seeds → seeds generate manifest cognitions and are replenished by them → subject–object construction persists → purification transforms the basis → it is conditioned flow, not ātman.

#### Model answer

Store consciousness (*ālayavijñāna*) is the foundational stream posited by Yogācāra Buddhism to explain continuity across momentary experiences without admitting a permanent self (*ātman*). It contains dispositional “seeds” (*bīja*) deposited by past actions and cognitions. Under suitable conditions these seeds mature into perceptions, habits, character and experienced worlds; manifest activities in turn “perfume” the store with further seeds.

It therefore explains memory, karmic fruition, personal continuity and the apparently shared stability of experience. It also underlies the afflicted mind (*kliṣṭa-manas*), which appropriates the stream as “I” and “mine.”

Liberation requires transformation of the basis (*āśraya-parāvṛtti*): dualistic constructions and defiled seeds cease, allowing non-dual wisdom.

The store must not be reified into a soul. It is conditioned, momentary and causally continuous, not an unchanging owner of experience. Madhyamaka critics warn that it can function like a concealed substance; Yogācāra replies that it is only dependent flow.

**Why this earns marks:** It explains seeds, continuity, defilement, liberation and the decisive non-self qualification.

### Q5(d). *Satkaryavada*
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Sāṃkhya](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/samkhya.md) → pre-existence of effect · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Directive: Explain · Syllabus unit: Sāṃkhya—causation · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Sāṃkhya](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Samkhya.md) · **Slug:** `samkhya-satkaryavada`

**Demand decoding:** Define effect-pre-existence, state classical proofs, identify Sāṃkhya’s transformation theory and contrast Nyāya.

**Argument map:** non-being cannot be produced → specific material produces specific effect → production needs competent cause → effect is of causal nature → clay becomes manifest pot → causation is transformation, not new creation.

#### Model answer

The doctrine of effect-pre-existence (*satkāryavāda*) holds that the effect exists latently in its material cause before production. Production is manifestation or transformation, not creation of a previously non-existent entity. Thus the pot is potentially present in clay.

Sāṃkhya offers five arguments:

1. what is wholly non-existent cannot be produced (*asadakaraṇāt*);
2. production requires an appropriate material cause (*upādāna-grahaṇāt*);
3. not everything can arise from everything (*sarva-sambhavābhāvāt*);
4. only a competent cause produces a specific effect (*śaktasya śakya-karaṇāt*);
5. the effect shares the nature of its cause (*kāraṇa-bhāvāt*).

Sāṃkhya adopts real transformation (*pariṇāmavāda*): primordial nature (*prakṛti*) evolves into intellect, ego, subtle elements and the manifest world while causal continuity is retained.

Nyāya defends non-pre-existence of the effect (*asatkāryavāda*), arguing that the pot is a genuinely new whole. Sāṃkhya replies that novelty concerns manifest form, not underlying being.

**Why this earns marks:** It defines the doctrine, supplies all five reasons and locates the causal dispute.

**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Sri Aurobindo](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/sri-aurobindo.md) → Integral Yoga · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

#### Model answer

Sri Aurobindo’s Integral Yoga (*pūrṇa-yoga*) seeks not escape from the world but transformation of the whole human being—physical, vital, mental and spiritual—into an instrument of divine consciousness. ### Q6(a). Elaborate Nyaya-Mimamsa debate on *Pramanyavada*.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Mīmāṃsā](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/mimamsa.md) → intrinsic and extrinsic validity · **Secondary route:** [Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/nyaya-vaisesika.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 20 marks · Directive: Elaborate the debate · Syllabus unit: Mīmāṃsā and Nyāya—validity of cognition · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Mīmāṃsā](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Mimamsa.md) · **Secondary classification:** [Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Nyaya-Vaisesika.md) · **Slug:** `nyaya-mimamsa-pramanyavada`

**Demand decoding:** Compare both the origin and ascertainment of truth and falsity, reconstruct their arguments, distinguish Bhāṭṭa and Prābhākara nuances, and evaluate regress and error objections.

**Argument map:** cognition presents object and motivates action → Mīmāṃsā: validity is intrinsic, falsity later extrinsic → Nyāya: cognition is causally neutral; validity and falsity depend on external conditions and successful activity → dispute concerns production and knowledge of validity → regress challenge versus error challenge → qualified synthesis.

#### Model answer

The Nyāya–Mīmāṃsā debate on the theory of validity (*prāmāṇyavāda*) asks two questions: what makes a cognition true or false, and how is its truth or falsity known?

**Mīmāṃsā: intrinsic validity (*svataḥ-prāmāṇya*)**

Every cognition presents its object as real and is accepted unless defeated. Its validity arises from the very causal conditions producing cognition; no additional truth-making excellence is required. Falsity is extrinsic (*parataḥ-aprāmāṇya*), discovered when a later cognition sublates the first or when action fails. Seeing silver in nacre initially moves the subject toward it; only the later cognition “this is nacre” reveals error.

Prābhākara holds that cognition reveals knower, known and itself; error is failure to discriminate perception from memory. Kumārila allows erroneous cognition but regards its initial validity as intrinsic until sublation.

Mīmāṃsā argues that if cognition needed another cognition to certify it, that certifier would itself need certification, producing infinite regress. Ordinary action would become impossible if agents first verified every cognition.

**Nyāya: extrinsic validity and falsity (*parataḥ-prāmāṇya*, *parataḥ-aprāmāṇya*)**

For Nyāya, cognition as cognition is neither self-validating nor self-falsifying. Truth is produced by non-defective causal conditions and correspondence with the object; falsity arises from defects such as poor light or sense disorder. Validity is known inferentially from successful practical engagement (*pravṛtti-sāmarthya*): water-cognition is confirmed when approaching it satisfies thirst. Failure or contradiction establishes invalidity.

Nyāya rejects regress: not every cognition is actually doubted; certification is sought when doubt arises, and inference can terminate in successful activity. It also objects that if validity belonged intrinsically to all cognitions, error would initially be as valid as truth.

**Assessment**

Mīmāṃsā captures prima facie entitlement: cognition must be trusted unless defeated. Nyāya rightly stresses objective conditions and correction. A defensible synthesis distinguishes intrinsic **default justification** from **truth**, which depends on objective fit and reliable causes.

**Why this earns marks:** It addresses production and ascertainment, both schools’ regress/error arguments, internal Mīmāṃsā variants and a reasoned synthesis.

### Q6(b). ‘Our knowledge is confined to *gunas* alone’. Examine this statement in the light of Vaisesika and Buddhist controversy.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/nyaya-vaisesika.md) → substance and qualities · **Secondary route:** [Buddhism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/buddhism.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Directive: Examine · Syllabus unit: Vaiśeṣika ontology; Buddhist critique of substance · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Nyaya-Vaisesika.md) · **Secondary classification:** [Buddhism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Buddhism.md) · **Slug:** `knowledge-confined-gunas-vaisesika-buddhist`

**Demand decoding:** Clarify whether qualities exhaust what is known, reconstruct Buddhist bundle/phenomenal critique and Vaiśeṣika’s inference or perception of substance, then adjudicate.

**Argument map:** experience presents colour/taste/touch → Buddhist: no separate bearer appears; “substance” is conceptual construction → Vaiśeṣika: qualities require locus, co-inhere and change while object persists → substance is known as qualified whole, not bare substratum → debate turns on explanatory need versus ontological parsimony.

#### Model answer

The statement claims that cognition reaches only qualities (*guṇas*)—colour, taste, smell, touch and so forth—not an underlying substance (*dravya*). Buddhist reductionism finds support here: analysis reveals momentary particulars or streams of qualities, while an enduring bearer distinct from them is never separately perceived. “Pot” is a conceptual construction imposed upon a causally ordered complex. To posit an invisible substratum beyond presented features appears redundant and conflicts with momentariness.

Vaiśeṣika distinguishes substance and quality as categories (*padārthas*). A quality cannot exist independently or possess another quality; it inheres in a substance through inherence (*samavāya*). Several qualities are cognised as belonging to one object—“this fragrant, red, cool lotus”—and qualities can change while the numerically same substance persists. Substance also serves as material cause and locus of motion.

The Vaiśeṣika need not claim that a bare substance is perceived apart from qualities. In ordinary determinate perception, the qualified whole is known: qualities reveal their bearer. Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika may also infer a common substrate from coordinated properties and persistence.

Buddhists reply that co-occurrence and causal continuity explain unity without an extra entity; the supposed bearer adds no observable content. Vaiśeṣika answers that a mere heap cannot explain ownership, simultaneous qualification or identity through qualitative change.

**Evaluation:** Epistemically, access begins with qualities; ontologically, it does not follow that only qualities exist. Substance is justified if it has indispensable explanatory work, but not as an unknowable entity wholly detached from its qualities.

**Why this earns marks:** It separates what is directly presented from what is ontologically required and fairly reconstructs both sides.

### Q6(c). Is *anyathākhyātivāda* an adequate explanation of error ? Elaborate.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/nyaya-vaisesika.md) → Nyāya theory of error · **Secondary route:** [Mīmāṃsā](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/mimamsa.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Directive: Elaborate and assess · Syllabus unit: Nyāya—error; comparison with rival theories · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Nyaya-Vaisesika.md) · **Secondary classification:** [Mīmāṃsā](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Mimamsa.md) · **Slug:** `nyaya-anyathakhyativada-error`

**Demand decoding:** Explain misapprehension as presentation of one real thing elsewhere, detail the shell–silver mechanism, compare rival accounts, and give a qualified adequacy verdict.

**Argument map:** defective perception presents “this” shell → similarity revives memory of real silver → extraordinary cognition connects remembered silver with present locus → synthesis “this is silver” misplaces a real predicate → correction reveals relational error → rivals challenge memory/perception fusion and remote-object contact.

#### Model answer

Nyāya’s theory of error, misapprehension elsewhere (*anyathā-khyāti*), treats both elements in illusion as real but wrongly related. In “this is silver” when nacre is present, defective conditions—distance, glare or visual disorder—yield an indeterminate awareness of “this.” Similarity revives the memory of silver previously perceived. Through an extraordinary cognition associated with knowledge (*jñāna-lakṣaṇa-pratyāsatti*), the silver-character is presented in the current locus. Error consists in qualifying the present “this” by silver that exists elsewhere.

The account explains:

- the object-directed character of illusion;
- why action follows toward the nacre;
- why correction is relational—“this is not silver”—rather than denial that silver exists anywhere;
- why prior experience is needed for familiar misidentification.

However, Prābhākara’s non-apprehension theory (*akhyāti*) argues that no single false cognition occurs: perception of “this” and memory of silver are both true, but their difference is not noticed. Advaita’s indefinability theory (*anirvacanīya-khyāti*) objects that experienced silver is neither simply real elsewhere nor wholly unreal, because it appears here and is later sublated. Buddhists can explain the episode through mental construction without a remote real silver.

Nyāya’s appeal to extraordinary contact is its weakest point: it seems introduced to preserve perceptual presentation of a non-present object, and cannot easily explain novel hallucinations without a suitable original.

**Evaluation:** *Anyathā-khyāti* is adequate for ordinary recognition-based misidentification because it integrates perception, memory, defect and correction. It is less satisfactory as a universal theory of dreams, hallucinations and wholly novel illusions.

**Why this earns marks:** It gives the causal mechanism, explanatory strengths, rival objections and a discriminating verdict.

### Q7(a). Compare and contrast the notion of *Brahman* in Sankara and Ramanuja.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Vedānta](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/vedanta.md) → Advaita and Viśiṣṭādvaita · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 20 marks · Directive: Compare and contrast · Syllabus unit: Vedānta—Śaṅkara and Rāmānuja · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Vedānta](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Vedanta.md) · **Slug:** `brahman-shankara-ramanuja-comparison`

**Demand decoding:** Compare Brahman’s nature, attributes, relation to world and self, causality, scriptural interpretation and liberation; reconstruct objections and evaluate.

**Argument map:** both affirm Brahman as ultimate and world-cause → Śaṅkara: non-dual, ultimately attributeless, identity of self and Brahman, world sublatable → Rāmānuja: personal qualified whole, souls/matter as real body, unity through dependence → compare illusion/transformation, knowledge/devotion, absorption/communion → assess unity and plurality costs.

#### Model answer

Śaṅkara and Rāmānuja agree that Brahman is the ultimate reality, the ground and cause of the universe, known through the Upaniṣads, and the final object of liberation. They differ over whether unity excludes or includes real plurality.

| Dimension | Śaṅkara’s Advaita | Rāmānuja’s Viśiṣṭādvaita |
|---|---|---|
| Nature of Brahman | Non-dual existence-consciousness-infinity; ultimately without limiting attributes (*nirguṇa*) | Personal Nārāyaṇa possessing infinite auspicious qualities |
| World | Empirically valid but ultimately sublatable appearance through *māyā* | Real manifestation; matter is a mode/body of Brahman |
| Souls | The self (*ātman*) is identical with Brahman; difference rests on ignorance (*avidyā*) | Souls are real, eternal and distinct yet inseparable, dependent modes of Brahman |
| Causality | Brahman is material and efficient cause without undergoing real change; apparent transformation (*vivarta*) | Brahman is material and efficient cause; real transformation of dependent modes without impairment of divine essence |
| Liberation | Immediate knowledge of identity removes ignorance | Devotion, surrender, knowledge and grace yield communion and service while individuality remains |

For Śaṅkara, Īśvara is Brahman associated with *māyā* at the transactional level; the world is not sheer non-being but is sublated by non-dual knowledge. For Rāmānuja, conscious selves (*cit*) and matter (*acit*) constitute Brahman’s body, controlled by Brahman as inner ruler. “That thou art” expresses organic dependence, not numerical identity.

Rāmānuja objects that ignorance cannot belong to pure Brahman or to a self already identical with it. Śaṅkara replies that *avidyā* is beginningless and empirical, not ultimately real. Advaita counters that real changing modes and suffering souls threaten Brahman’s perfection; Rāmānuja assigns change to dependent modes, not the controlling essence.

**Evaluation:** Śaṅkara secures absolute unity but faces the locus of ignorance. Rāmānuja preserves personality, devotion and world realism but must explain how plurality leaves Brahman unaffected. The contrast is identity through sublation versus unity through real dependence.

**Why this earns marks:** It compares every central axis, presents reciprocal objections and ends with a reasoned rather than sectarian judgement.

### Q7(b). State and examine the Samkhya proofs for the existence of *Purusa*.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Sāṃkhya](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/samkhya.md) → proofs of consciousness · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Directive: State and examine · Syllabus unit: Sāṃkhya—puruṣa and prakṛti · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Sāṃkhya](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Samkhya.md) · **Slug:** `samkhya-proofs-purusha`

**Demand decoding:** State all five classical arguments, explain their inferential force, and assess the move to inactive plural consciousness.

**Argument map:** composite exists for another → three-guṇa objects require non-guṇa witness → experience needs conscious enjoyer → striving presupposes release-capable subject → insentient nature needs witnessing principle → plurality inferred from separate births/deaths → objections concern agency, interaction and explanatory duplication.

#### Model answer

Sāṃkhya infers consciousness (*puruṣa*) as distinct from insentient primordial nature (*prakṛti*) through five arguments:

1. **Composite-for-another (*saṅghāta-parārthatvāt*):** every composite serves something distinct from it, as a bed serves a sleeper; body–mind therefore serves consciousness.
2. **Opposite of the three qualities (*triguṇādi-viparyayāt*):** all objects, including intellect, contain luminosity, activity and inertia (*sattva, rajas, tamas*); their witness must be beyond these qualities.
3. **Need of a controller or ground (*adhiṣṭhānāt*):** insentient nature requires a conscious presence for ordered experience.
4. **Existence of an enjoyer (*bhoktṛ-bhāvāt*):** pleasure and pain must be experienced by a conscious subject rather than by unconscious matter.
5. **Striving for liberation (*kaivalyārthaṃ pravṛtteḥ*):** the quest to end suffering presupposes a principle capable of isolation from nature.

Plurality of *puruṣas* is additionally inferred from distinct births, deaths, capacities and karmic destinies; otherwise one being’s liberation would liberate all.

The arguments establish that consciousness cannot easily be reduced to an object among objects. Yet the bed analogy may beg the question by assuming purposiveness, and a materialist can attribute experience to organised body–brain systems. An inactive, changeless *puruṣa* also seems unable to enjoy or direct anything. Sāṃkhya replies that intellect undergoes modifications while reflecting consciousness, just as a crystal appears coloured near a flower; proximity, not causal action by *puruṣa*, makes experience possible.

**Evaluation:** The proofs strongly articulate the irreducibility and witnessing character of consciousness, but do not conclusively establish many wholly inactive selves. The interaction between conscious witness and unconscious nature remains the system’s central difficulty.

**Why this earns marks:** It states all five proofs, adds the plurality argument, and critically tests the precise Sāṃkhya conclusion.

### Q7(c). Explain the arguments of Carvaka to reject transcendental entities.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Cārvāka](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/carvaka.md) → critique of inference and transcendence · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Directive: Explain · Syllabus unit: Cārvāka—epistemology and metaphysics · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Cārvāka](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Carvaka.md) · **Slug:** `carvaka-rejection-transcendental-entities`

**Demand decoding:** Ground the rejection of soul, God, karma and afterlife in Cārvāka epistemology; distinguish practical inference from metaphysically certain inference.

**Argument map:** perception alone gives secure knowledge → inference requires universal concomitance → such concomitance cannot be established without circularity or hidden conditions → testimony depends on unverified speakers → supersensible entities lack perceptual warrant → consciousness emerges from elements → ritual and afterlife claims are rejected.

#### Model answer

Cārvāka rejects transcendental entities because its epistemology accepts perception (*pratyakṣa*) as the only indubitable means of knowledge. God, an immortal soul, karmic merit, heaven, hell and rebirth are not perceived; their alleged proof relies on inference or scripture.

Inference requires universal concomitance (*vyāpti*) between reason and conclusion. Cārvāka argues that finite observations cannot establish an unrestricted universal: apparently constant relations may be disrupted by unnoticed conditions (*upādhi*). To infer *vyāpti* from another inference is circular or regressive. Inference may guide ordinary life through probability—smoke usually indicates fire—but cannot establish supersensible realities with certainty.

Testimony is equally insecure. Its reliability depends on the speaker’s trustworthiness, known through perception and fallible inference. The Vedas contain unverifiable ritual promises and are viewed as serving priestly interests rather than revealing another world.

Consciousness need not imply an immaterial self. It emerges from the organisation of material elements, as intoxicating power arises from fermented ingredients. When the body disintegrates, consciousness ends; hence post-mortem reward and punishment lack warrant.

Nyāya replies that perception itself presupposes inference, that repeated positive and negative instances plus elimination of conditions establish *vyāpti*, and that testimony can be independently reliable. Cārvāka nevertheless exposes the evidential gap between practical regularity and ambitious metaphysical claims.

**Evaluation:** Its critique is strongest as methodological scepticism and naturalism, but exclusive reliance on perception is self-limiting and cannot itself be perceptually proved.

**Why this earns marks:** It derives the metaphysical rejection from the critique of inference and testimony, then supplies a balanced appraisal.

### Q8(a). What is bondage according to yoga philosophy ? Explain the method of attaining *Kaivalya* in Patanjali’s *yogasutra*.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Yoga](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/yoga.md) → bondage and liberation · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 20 marks · Directive: Define and explain the method · Syllabus unit: Yoga—citta, kleśas, eight limbs and liberation · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Yoga](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Yoga.md) · **Slug:** `yoga-bondage-kaivalya-method`

**Demand decoding:** Explain the mechanism of bondage and provide a connected path from discipline through concentration to discriminative knowledge and isolation.

**Argument map:** ignorance identifies seer with mind’s modifications → afflictions generate karma and rebirth → practice and dispassion restrain modifications → action-Yoga attenuates afflictions → eight limbs purify and concentrate → meditative integration culminates in discriminative knowledge → karmic seeds cease → guṇas withdraw and puruṣa abides alone.

#### Model answer

Pātañjala Yoga accepts Sāṃkhya’s distinction between consciousness (*puruṣa*) and nature (*prakṛti*). Bondage is not a real modification of pure consciousness but its beginningless misidentification with the mind (*citta*) and the mind’s modifications (*vṛttis*). The seer appears to enjoy pleasure and pain because intellect reflects consciousness.

The root is ignorance (*avidyā*): taking the impermanent, impure, painful and non-self as permanent, pure, pleasant and self. It produces egoity (*asmitā*), attachment (*rāga*), aversion (*dveṣa*) and clinging to life (*abhiniveśa*). These five afflictions (*kleśas*) motivate action, deposit karmic traces and generate further birth and experience.

Yoga is the restraint of mental modifications (*yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ*). The method is cumulative:

1. **Practice and dispassion (*abhyāsa–vairāgya*):** steady effort stabilises the mind; freedom from craving weakens its objects.
2. **Action-Yoga (*kriyā-yoga*):** discipline (*tapas*), self-study (*svādhyāya*) and dedication to the Lord (*Īśvara-praṇidhāna*) attenuate afflictions.
3. **Eight limbs (*aṣṭāṅga-yoga*):** restraints (*yama*), observances (*niyama*), posture (*āsana*), breath regulation (*prāṇāyāma*), withdrawal (*pratyāhāra*), concentration (*dhāraṇā*), meditation (*dhyāna*) and meditative integration (*samādhi*).
4. **Integrated attention (*saṃyama*):** the last three applied together yield penetrating insight, though extraordinary powers are distractions if desired for themselves.
5. **Discriminative discernment (*vivekakhyāti*):** uninterrupted knowledge distinguishes the witnessing *puruṣa* from every operation of *prakṛti*.

Concentration progresses toward seedless integration (*nirbīja-samādhi*), where impressions no longer generate modifications. When ignorance and karma end, the qualities (*guṇas*) cease functioning for that *puruṣa*.

Isolation (*kaivalya*) is the complete independence of consciousness: the seer abides in its own nature. It is not bodily annihilation, absorption into a universal Brahman or acquisition of worldly powers.

**Evaluation:** Yoga offers a psychologically systematic path uniting ethics, embodiment and cognition. Its difficulty is explaining how an inactive *puruṣa* can appear bound and how mere proximity to mind produces experience; the tradition answers that bondage belongs to misidentifying intellect, while liberation removes that error rather than changing consciousness.

**Why this earns marks:** It links the complete bondage mechanism to every major practical and cognitive stage culminating in *kaivalya*.

### Q8(b). What do *Samvara* and *Nirjara* mean ? Explain their significance in Jaina theory of liberation.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Jainism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/jainism.md) → stoppage and shedding of karma · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Directive: Define and explain significance · Syllabus unit: Jainism—bondage and liberation · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Jainism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Jainism.md) · **Slug:** `jaina-samvara-nirjara-liberation`

**Demand decoding:** Define stoppage (*saṃvara*) and shedding (*nirjarā*), place them in the karmic process, identify their means and explain why both are necessary.

**Argument map:** passions and activity cause karmic influx → karmic matter binds soul → saṃvara blocks new influx through discipline → nirjarā exhausts old karma naturally or through austerity → right faith, knowledge and conduct coordinate both → all karma removed → omniscience and liberation.

#### Model answer

Jainism treats bondage as the actual association of karmic matter with the conscious soul (*jīva*). Activities of body, speech and mind, intensified by passions, cause influx (*āsrava*); the particles bind to the soul (*bandha*) and obstruct its intrinsic infinite knowledge, perception, energy and bliss.

**Stoppage (*saṃvara*)** prevents fresh karmic influx. It is achieved through the three jewels—right faith, right knowledge and right conduct—together with vows, carefulness, restraint of mind–speech–body, cultivation of virtues, endurance of hardships and meditation. Ethical discipline is therefore not merely social morality but causal protection of the soul.

**Shedding (*nirjarā*)** removes karma already bound. Passive shedding (*akāma* or *savipāka nirjarā*) occurs when karma naturally matures; active shedding (*sakāma* or *avipāka nirjarā*) accelerates exhaustion through internal and external austerities, repentance, humility, service, study, meditation and detachment.

Both are indispensable. Stoppage without shedding leaves inherited karma operative; shedding without stoppage allows renewed accumulation. Their joint completion produces destruction of obscuring karmas, omniscience (*kevala-jñāna*) and finally liberation (*mokṣa*), when the soul rises to the perfected realm.

**Evaluation:** The scheme tightly links ethics, psychology and liberation, though its material conception of karma is metaphysically demanding and severe austerity requires the governing principle of non-violence.

**Why this earns marks:** It defines both processes, gives their methods and explains their complementary causal necessity.

### Q8(c). What is the role of *Pratityasamutpada* in explaining *Dukha* ? Elucidate the means to overcome it.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Buddhism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/buddhism.md) → dependent origination and cessation of suffering · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Directive: Explain role and elucidate means · Syllabus unit: Buddhism—dependent origination, suffering and liberation · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Buddhism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Buddhism.md) · **Slug:** `dependent-origination-dukkha-cessation`

**Demand decoding:** Use dependent origination (*pratītyasamutpāda*) to explain the conditioned production of suffering, then connect reversal to the Four Noble Truths, both senses of the Middle Path and the Noble Eightfold Path.

**Argument map:** ignorance conditions formations → consciousness through feeling conditions craving → craving, clinging and becoming generate birth → birth entails ageing/death and suffering → no first cause or permanent self is needed → remove ignorance/craving and chain ceases → ethical conduct, concentration and wisdom realise the Middle Path and nirvāṇa.

#### Model answer

Dependent origination (*pratītyasamutpāda*) explains suffering (*duḥkha*; Pali: *dukkha*) as conditioned rather than accidental, divinely imposed or produced by a permanent self. Its general formula is: when this exists, that arises; with the cessation of this, that ceases.

The twelve-linked formulation runs: ignorance (*avidyā*) → formations (*saṃskāra*) → consciousness (*vijñāna*) → name-and-form (*nāma-rūpa*) → six sense bases → contact → feeling → craving (*tṛṣṇā*) → clinging → becoming → birth → ageing-and-death, grief and despair. The links need not be read only as a linear three-life chronology; they also disclose the recurring structure through which each present experience becomes appropriation and renewed suffering.

This account supports the Middle Way doctrinally. Because phenomena arise dependently, they are neither eternal substances nor sheer disconnected annihilations. There is causal continuity without an unchanging self. It also grounds the Four Noble Truths: suffering has an origin in craving conditioned by ignorance; because its conditions are removable, cessation (*nirvāṇa*) is possible.

The practical Middle Path (*madhyamā pratipad*; Pali: *majjhimā paṭipadā*) avoids sensual indulgence and self-mortification through the Noble Eightfold Path (*āryāṣṭāṅgamārga*): right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness and concentration. Ethical conduct restrains harmful formations; concentration exposes feeling before it hardens into craving; wisdom understands impermanence, non-self and dependent arising, uprooting ignorance.

**Evaluation:** Dependent origination gives a non-substantialist causal diagnosis and therefore a practical therapy. Its force lies in showing that suffering is neither fated nor removable by wish alone: intervention must transform its conditions.

**Why this earns marks:** It gives the full causal series, both dimensions of the Middle Path, the Four Noble Truths and the mechanism of cessation.
