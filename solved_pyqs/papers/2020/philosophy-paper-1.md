### Q1(a). How does Aristotle argue for the priority of Form over Matter and Actuality over Potentiality? Critically discuss.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Plato–Aristotle](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/plato-aristotle.md) → form, matter, actuality and potentiality · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Syllabus unit: Plato and Aristotle—form and matter; actuality and potentiality · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Aristotle · hylomorphism · priority relations · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Plato–Aristotle](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Plato-Aristotle.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `aristotle-priority-form-actuality`

**Demand decoding:** Explain Aristotle’s several senses of priority, then assess whether matter and potentiality remain indispensable.

**Argument map:** substance is form–matter composite → form makes matter a determinate thing → actuality defines and completes capacity → form/actuality prior in account, substance and end → matter/potentiality remain real conditions.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Aristotle gives explanatory priority to form and actuality without denying the constitutive role of matter and potentiality.

Matter considered alone is indeterminate: bronze becomes a statue only by receiving a determinate structure. Form states what a substance is and therefore has priority in definition and intelligibility. Likewise, a capacity is identified through its corresponding fulfilment—the ability to see through actual seeing. Actuality is prior in account, normally prior in substance because the mature being explains its development, and prior as the end for which change occurs.

**Objection:** No embodied substance exists without matter, and generation temporally begins from potentiality. **Reply:** Aristotle concedes temporal and material dependence; his claim concerns explanatory and teleological priority, not separability in every case.

**Evaluation:** The doctrine explains directed change better than reducing objects to material ingredients, but its appeal to intrinsic ends is less persuasive for accidental products and mechanistic nature.

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes kinds of priority, preserves hylomorphism and critically limits the claim.

### Q1(b). How does Leibniz's conception of monads bear upon his views on determinism and freedom? Discuss with your own comments.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Rationalism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/rationalism.md) → Leibniz’s monads, determinism and freedom · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Syllabus unit: Rationalism—Leibniz; monads, pre-established harmony and freedom · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Leibniz · complete concepts · contingency · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Rationalism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Rationalism.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `leibniz-monads-determinism-freedom`

**Demand decoding:** Connect windowless monads and pre-established harmony to complete determination, then explain Leibniz’s compatibilist freedom.

**Argument map:** each monad unfolds its internal law → no causal interaction → God harmonises all sequences → complete concept contains every predicate → certainty without absolute necessity → freedom as intelligent spontaneity.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Leibniz’s monadology entails universal determination, yet he distinguishes certain determination from logical necessity to defend a compatibilist freedom.

Monads are “windowless”: each changes from its own inner principle of perception and appetite. Apparent interaction results from God’s pre-established harmony. Moreover, an individual’s complete concept contains every true predicate, including future acts. God chooses the best compossible world, so every event has a sufficient reason.

Nevertheless, created acts are hypothetically, not absolutely, necessary: their opposites involve no contradiction, although they will not occur in this chosen world. An action is free when it arises spontaneously from the agent’s own reasons and character, with intelligence and without external compulsion.

**Objection:** If God infallibly foreknows and selects the whole sequence, alternatives seem merely verbal. **Reply:** Leibniz locates freedom in rational self-determination rather than indifference.

**Evaluation:** The account preserves responsibility better than coercive determinism, but its “could have been otherwise” applies chiefly across possible worlds, not at the actual moment of choice.

**Why this earns marks:** It derives both determinism and qualified freedom from monadology rather than discussing them separately.

### Q1(c). What, according to Husserl, is wrong with psychologism? How does Husserl address the problems with psychologism in his phenomenological method?
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Phenomenology](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/phenomenology.md) → critique of psychologism and phenomenological method · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Syllabus unit: Phenomenology (Husserl)—method; avoidance of psychologism · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Husserl · ideal validity · intentional analysis · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Phenomenology (Husserl)](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Phenomenology-Husserl.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `husserl-psychologism-phenomenological-method`

**Demand decoding:** Identify the relativism and category mistake in psychologism and show how intentionality, reduction and essence restore objective validity.

**Argument map:** psychology describes contingent thinking → logic concerns ideal validity → reducing norm to fact yields relativism → epoché suspends natural assumptions → intentional and eidetic analysis disclose invariant structures.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Husserl rejects psychologism because it confuses empirical facts about how people think with ideal norms governing whether thoughts are valid.

Psychology studies contingent causal processes, whereas logical laws and meanings claim necessity and repeatable identity. If non-contradiction were merely a feature of the human mind, another psychology could make contradictions true; truth would become species-relative and psychology would itself lose objective warrant.

Phenomenology answers this by analysing intentional acts and their intended meanings without reducing either to brain events. The phenomenological suspension (*epoché*) brackets existential assumptions; reduction turns inquiry toward the structures of consciousness. Eidetic variation distinguishes invariant essences from accidental psychological features, while the act–meaning distinction explains how different acts intend the same ideal content.

**Objection:** Transcendental subjectivity may reintroduce idealism. **Reply:** Husserl’s claim is not that objects are private inventions, but that their modes of givenness and validity require constitutive analysis.

**Evaluation:** He decisively protects normativity, though intersubjective objectivity remains a demanding problem.

**Why this earns marks:** It states the anti-psychologistic argument and links each phenomenological device to the defect it repairs.

### Q1(d). Examine the reality of the phenomenal world in the light of Hegel's Absolute Idealism.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Hegel](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/hegel.md) → Absolute idealism and the phenomenal world · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Syllabus unit: Hegel—Absolute idealism · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Hegel · appearance, actuality and whole · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Hegel](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Hegel.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `hegel-phenomenal-world-absolute-idealism`

**Demand decoding:** Avoid treating phenomena as either independent things or mere illusions; explain their dependent reality within the Absolute.

**Argument map:** finite phenomena claim independence → relations expose incompleteness → contradiction drives mediation → appearances are real moments of the whole → actuality is rationally integrated appearance.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** In Hegel’s Absolute idealism, the phenomenal world is neither an unknowable veil nor an illusion; it is a real but partial manifestation of the self-developing Absolute.

Finite things possess determinate reality, yet none is self-sufficient. Each is constituted through relations and passes beyond itself through internal opposition. Dialectic discloses this dependence and preserves finite content through sublation (*Aufhebung*). Nature is the Idea in externality; human history and Spirit progressively make its rational structure explicit. Hence “appearance” is not sheer falsity: essence must appear, while appearance becomes adequately actual only within the intelligible whole.

**Objection:** Absorbing every finite evil and contingency into the Absolute can make concrete suffering merely instrumental. **Reply:** Hegel’s sublation preserves, rather than simply erases, finite difference and negativity.

**Evaluation:** The view overcomes Kant’s rigid phenomenon–noumenon divide and explains relational reality, but risks totalising history and understating irreducible contingency.

**Why this earns marks:** It gives phenomena dependent but genuine reality and evaluates the cost of systematic totality.

### Q1(e). “The Soul of Superman is Good.” Critically examine the above statement in the light of logical positivism.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Logical Positivism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/logical-positivism.md) → verification and pseudostatements · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Syllabus unit: Logical Positivism—verification theory of meaning; rejection of metaphysics · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Vienna Circle and Ayer · metaphysical and ethical language · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Logical Positivism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Logical-Positivism.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `logical-positivism-soul-superman-good`

**Demand decoding:** Test each component for analytic or empirical meaning and distinguish metaphysical pseudostatement from emotive evaluation.

**Argument map:** meaningful proposition is analytic or verifiable → “soul” and “Superman” lack observation-conditions → “good” expresses approval on emotivism → sentence is not a factual assertion → verification criterion faces self-application problem.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** A logical positivist would deny that the sentence states a verifiable fact, though its ethical component may express an attitude.

It is not analytic, for its denial is not self-contradictory. Nor are public observation-conditions specified for identifying a disembodied “soul” or the relevant “Superman.” These metaphysical terms therefore produce a pseudostatement rather than a false empirical claim. On Ayer’s emotivism, “is good” does not report a moral property; it voices approval and may prescribe a similar attitude. The whole utterance thus combines unverifiable metaphysics with non-cognitive evaluation.

**Objection:** The verification principle is itself neither analytic nor empirically verified, and theoretical entities can be meaningful through indirect evidence. **Reply:** Positivists can present it as a proposal for cognitively responsible language and use weak verification, though this weakens its exclusionary force.

**Evaluation:** The test rightly demands clarity, but too quickly dismisses metaphysical and moral discourse whose meaning may be inferential, practical or interpretive.

**Why this earns marks:** It separately analyses “soul,” “Superman” and “good,” then criticises the criterion applied.

### Q2(a). “I never can catch myself at any time without perception, and never can observe anything but the perception.” How does this statement by Hume problematize the philosophical notion of personal identity? How does Kant deal with this problem in his Critique of Pure Reason?
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Empiricism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/empiricism.md) → Hume on personal identity · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 20 marks · Syllabus unit: Empiricism—Hume; Kant—transcendental unity of apperception · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Hume and Kant · bundle theory · unity of consciousness · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Empiricism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Empiricism.md) · **Secondary route:** [Kant](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Kant.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `hume-kant-personal-identity-apperception`

**Demand decoding:** Reconstruct Hume’s bundle critique, then show how Kant supplies a formal condition of unified experience without proving a substantial soul.

**Argument map:** introspection finds only perceptions → no impression of enduring self → identity arises from memory, resemblance and causal association → Kant: representations must belong to one “I think” → transcendental unity is condition of experience, not knowledge of soul-substance.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Hume dissolves the self as an experienced substance into a bundle of perceptions; Kant replies that a unified “I think” is a necessary condition for having an ordered experience at all, while refusing to infer an immortal soul.

For Hume, every legitimate idea must derive from an impression. Introspection discloses heat, pleasure, pain, colour or thought, never a simple, unchanging owner. The mind is therefore a theatre-like succession—though Hume cautions that no separate theatre is perceived. Memory reveals relations among perceptions, and imagination, aided by resemblance and causation, mistakes smooth succession for strict numerical identity. Personal identity is thus a fiction useful for life, not an impression-grounded substance.

This creates a difficulty: association itself seems to require one field in which representations are compared. Kant’s transcendental deduction begins from this condition. The manifold of intuition must be synthesised under categories, and “the ‘I think’ must be able to accompany all my representations.” Transcendental unity of apperception is original and objective: it makes one experience and a public object-world possible. It is not another perceived item within experience.

Kant nevertheless rejects rational psychology’s inference from unity of consciousness to a simple, immortal substance. The transcendental subject is known only as the formal subject of thought, not as a noumenal object.

**Objection:** Kant may presuppose the unity he claims to deduce, and formal unity does not establish identity through major psychological discontinuity. **Reply:** His aim is transcendental, not empirical biography: any judgement or recognition already requires combinable representations.

**Evaluation:** Hume successfully attacks the substantial ego given in introspection; Kant more adequately explains synchronic unity of experience, but neither alone gives a complete criterion of diachronic personal identity.

**Why this earns marks:** It compares the precise problems and solutions while preserving Kant’s prohibition on turning apperception into soul-substance.

### Q2(b). Critically discuss the following statement by Moore: “If anyone tells us that to say 'Blue exists' is the same thing as to say that 'Both blue and consciousness exist', he makes a mistake and a self-contradictory mistake.”
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Moore–Russell–Early Wittgenstein](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/moore-russell-early-wittgenstein.md) → Moore’s refutation of idealism · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Syllabus unit: Moore, Russell and early Wittgenstein—defence of commonsense; refutation of idealism · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: G. E. Moore · act–object distinction · transparency of experience · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Moore–Russell–Early Wittgenstein](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Moore-Russell-EarlyWittgenstein.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `moore-blue-consciousness-refutation-idealism`

**Demand decoding:** Explain why Moore separates consciousness from its object and evaluate whether this refutes idealism.

**Argument map:** experience contains awareness and blue → they are distinguishable though related → “blue exists” does not analytically include consciousness → idealist esse est percipi conflates act and object → distinction alone may not prove mind-independent existence.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Moore’s claim attacks idealism by arguing that consciousness of blue and blue itself are distinct constituents of experience; identifying them covertly repeats what it denies.

In “The Refutation of Idealism,” Moore examines the formula *esse est percipi*. Every sensation has a common element—consciousness—and a variable object, such as blue or green. Attention ordinarily appears transparent: in attending to the sensation of blue, one attends chiefly to blue, not to an intrinsic mental modification. Since blue differs from awareness of blue, the proposition “blue exists” is not identical with “blue and consciousness exist.” The idealist who says blue just is its being experienced must distinguish the experience from other experiences by reference to blue, thereby presupposing the object-side he collapses.

This supports direct realism: consciousness is relational and its object is not reducible to the act. It also blocks Berkeleyan and phenomenalist analyses that build awareness into the meaning of the object.

**Objections:** Distinguishability in thought does not establish ontological independence. A representationalist may accept an act–content distinction while holding the immediate blue-content mental. Further, hallucination suggests that phenomenal character need not guarantee an external blue object. **Reply:** Moore need only defeat the alleged identity of object and awareness; a full proof of external independence requires additional argument.

**Evaluation:** Moore exposes a genuine act–object conflation and anticipates the transparency argument. Yet the conclusion establishes conceptual non-identity more securely than mind-independent realism.

**Why this earns marks:** It reconstructs the self-contradiction, links it to *esse est percipi*, and carefully limits what the argument proves.

### Q2(c). “The reason that I call my doctrine logical atomism is because the atoms that I wish to arrive at as the sort of last residue in analysis are logical atoms and not physical atoms.” Write a note on the nature of atomic facts according to Russell in the light of the above statement.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Moore–Russell–Early Wittgenstein](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/moore-russell-early-wittgenstein.md) → Russell’s logical atomism · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Syllabus unit: Moore, Russell and early Wittgenstein—logical atomism; logical constructions · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Bertrand Russell · atomic facts and propositions · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Moore–Russell–Early Wittgenstein](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Moore-Russell-EarlyWittgenstein.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `russell-atomic-facts-logical-atoms`

**Demand decoding:** Define atomic facts, distinguish them from physical particles and propositions, and explain analysis and truth-functions.

**Argument map:** complex statements analysed into simples → atomic proposition names particulars and predicates relations → its worldly truth-maker is atomic fact → molecular propositions are truth-functions → logical independence and negative facts create problems.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Russell’s atomic facts are the simplest obtaining configurations disclosed by logical analysis, not minute material particles.

An atomic fact consists of one or more particulars exemplifying a quality or standing in a relation: “this patch is red” or “A is to the left of B.” Its linguistic counterpart is an atomic proposition containing logically proper names and a predicate or relation. The proposition is true when the corresponding fact obtains; the fact itself is neither true nor false. Molecular propositions are built from atomic propositions by truth-functional operations, so their truth ultimately depends on atomic facts.

They are “logical” atoms because simplicity is relative to complete analysis, not to physics. Ordinary objects such as tables may be logical constructions from sense-data rather than ultimate particulars. Russell also hoped that atomic facts would be mutually independent, allowing a world to be described by listing them.

**Objections:** General propositions are not obviously reducible to finite truth-functions; false atomic propositions appear to require negative facts; and the choice of simples depends on language and epistemology. Later developments also weakened strict independence.

**Reply:** Logical atomism is best read as a method: analyse misleading grammar until the commitments and truth-conditions of a proposition are explicit.

**Evaluation:** Atomic facts powerfully connect analysis, ontology and truth, but no final inventory of simples was achieved.

**Why this earns marks:** It explains fact, proposition, logical rather than physical atomicity, construction and the theory’s principal difficulties.

### Q3(a). What does Kierkegaard mean by saying “Subjectivity is the truth” in the context of the problem of 'the single individual'?
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Existentialism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/existentialism.md) → Kierkegaard, subjectivity and the single individual · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 20 marks · Syllabus unit: Existentialism—Kierkegaard; existence, choice, responsibility and authenticity · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Kierkegaard · inward appropriation · faith · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Existentialism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Existentialism.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `kierkegaard-subjectivity-single-individual`

**Demand decoding:** Explain the maxim as existential appropriation, not relativism; relate it to Hegel, objective uncertainty, choice and faith.

**Argument map:** objective system abstracts from existing chooser → ethical-religious truth concerns how one exists → inward passion appropriates uncertainty → single individual assumes responsibility before God → subjectivity does not make factual falsehood true.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** “Subjectivity is the truth” means that the highest ethical and religious truth becomes truth for an existing person only through passionate inward appropriation; it does not mean that every private belief is factually true.

Kierkegaard opposes the Hegelian ambition to comprehend existence within an impersonal completed system. A system can classify propositions, but the existing thinker remains temporal, unfinished and required to choose. Questions such as whether to commit oneself, repent or live before God cannot be resolved by detached spectatorship alone. Their truth lies in the “how” of one’s relation to them.

The “single individual” stands irreducibly responsible rather than dissolving into crowd, institution or universal history. In the aesthetic stage one disperses oneself among possibilities; in the ethical stage one chooses oneself through enduring obligation; in the religious stage the self relates absolutely to the Absolute. Faith exemplifies “objective uncertainty held fast in an appropriation-process of the most passionate inwardness.” Abraham in *Fear and Trembling* dramatizes the tension between singular faith and universal ethics.

Objectivity still matters: passionate commitment to an absurd factual proposition is not thereby knowledge. Kierkegaard’s target is the illusion that objective information alone can produce existential transformation. The paradox of Christianity requires decision because speculative mediation cannot remove risk.

**Objection:** Privileging inwardness can sanction fanaticism and evade public moral criticism. **Reply:** Authentic subjectivity involves anxiety, self-examination, responsibility and love, not arbitrary preference; nevertheless, public criteria remain necessary.

**Evaluation:** Kierkegaard compellingly restores agency and lived commitment to philosophy, but his account requires ethical limits lest singularity become insulation from criticism.

**Why this earns marks:** It joins subjectivity, single individuality, stages, faith and the anti-Hegelian context while rejecting crude relativism.

### Q3(b). Evaluate the role of spatio-temporal thinking in objective thinking with reference to Strawson's theory of basic particulars.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Quine–Strawson](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/quine-strawson.md) → basic particulars and identification · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Syllabus unit: Quine and Strawson—basic particulars and persons · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: P. F. Strawson · re-identification · unified space-time · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Quine–Strawson](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Quine-Strawson.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `strawson-spatiotemporal-thinking-basic-particulars`

**Demand decoding:** Show why objective reference requires a shared space-time framework and why material bodies and persons are basic.

**Argument map:** objective thought requires identifying particulars → identification needs public location and re-identification → one spatio-temporal system connects perspectives → material bodies provide enduring landmarks → persons are embodied subjects → framework may not exhaust objectivity.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** For Strawson, objective thinking depends upon a single public spatio-temporal framework in which particulars can be identified and re-identified; material bodies and persons are basic because other particulars depend on this framework.

In *Individuals*, merely having a description is insufficient for singular reference. Speaker and hearer must be able, at least in principle, to locate the referent within a connected world. Space-time links “here-now” perceptions to objects elsewhere and at other times through routes, causal continuity and enduring landmarks. Material bodies are therefore basic particulars: events, processes and many properties are identified as occurring to or near such bodies.

Persons are also basic. We predicate both physical characteristics and states of consciousness of the same embodied individual; we do not first identify an immaterial mind and then attach a body. Re-identification across time gives objectivity to memory, action and interpersonal ascription.

**Objection:** Numbers, institutions and theoretical entities are not straightforwardly spatio-temporal. **Reply:** Strawson offers descriptive metaphysics of our fundamental identification scheme, not a claim that every entity is a body. Such entities are identified through practices anchored in a public world.

**Evaluation:** Space-time convincingly explains public reference and correction, but linguistic, social and causal criteria also contribute to objectivity; the framework is necessary for ordinary particulars, not a complete theory of all objects.

**Why this earns marks:** It connects space-time to identification, re-identification, bodies and persons, then qualifies the scope.

### Q3(c). When does Pure Reason enter into the realm of Antinomies according to Kant? Is Kant's notion of Antinomies of Pure Reason a natural culmination of his distinction between Phenomena and Noumena? Give reasons in favour of your answer.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Kant](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/kant.md) → antinomies of pure reason · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Syllabus unit: Kant—Ideas of pure reason; antinomies; phenomena and noumena · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Kant · transcendental illusion · cosmological ideas · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Kant](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Kant.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `kant-antinomies-phenomena-noumena`

**Demand decoding:** State when reason generates antinomies, identify their structure and argue how transcendental idealism resolves them.

**Argument map:** reason seeks unconditioned totality → categories extended beyond possible experience → world treated as thing in itself → thesis and antithesis gain proofs → phenomenon–noumenon distinction exposes shared false assumption.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Pure reason enters antinomy when it converts the legitimate search for conditions into a claim about the unconditioned world-whole as an object; the antinomies naturally expose why phenomena must not be identified with things in themselves.

Understanding applies categories within possible experience. Reason, however, seeks completeness: if an event is conditioned, it demands the total series of conditions. Transcendental illusion arises when this regulative demand becomes constitutive and “the world” is treated as a completed object. Reason then proves opposed cosmological claims: beginning/no beginning and limit/no limit; simple parts/no simples; freedom/no freedom; necessary being/no necessary being.

The mathematical antinomies are dissolved because the phenomenal series is neither a finite nor an actually completed infinite thing in itself; it is indefinitely extendable in experience. In the dynamical antinomies, both sides may be compatible at different standpoints: an event can be empirically caused while a noumenal agency is thinkable as free, although not knowable.

**Objection:** The noumenal standpoint appears introduced merely to save freedom and may itself invite transcendent speculation. **Reply:** Kant assigns noumena a limiting, negative use: they mark what cannot be experienced, not objects known by theoretical reason.

**Evaluation:** Yes, the antinomies are a natural culmination of the distinction because they diagnose the contradiction produced by treating appearances as self-subsistent things. Yet they motivate rather than independently prove transcendental idealism.

**Why this earns marks:** It answers both “when” and “whether,” distinguishes mathematical and dynamical resolutions, and gives a reasoned verdict.

### Q4(a). “You can always make something out of what you have been made into.” Critically discuss this statement by Sartre with reference to his views on existentialism.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Existentialism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/existentialism.md) → Sartrean freedom, facticity and transcendence · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 20 marks · Syllabus unit: Existentialism—Sartre; existence and essence, choice, responsibility · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Sartre · situated freedom · bad faith · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Existentialism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Existentialism.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `sartre-facticity-transcendence-situated-freedom`

**Demand decoding:** Interpret the aphorism through facticity and transcendence; avoid both absolute voluntarism and social determinism.

**Argument map:** one is thrown into facts not chosen → consciousness negates the given and projects ends → choices confer meaning → freedom is situated and responsible → bad faith denies either facticity or transcendence.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Sartre means that human beings cannot choose the conditions that have formed them, but they remain responsible for the projects through which those conditions acquire significance.

“Existence precedes essence”: unlike a manufactured object, a person has no fixed blueprint. Consciousness is a being-for-itself that distances itself from what is given and projects possibilities. Yet freedom operates within facticity—body, past, class, language, historical situation and others’ actions. I cannot erase these facts, but I choose how to take them up: as obstacle, resource, excuse or occasion for resistance.

The statement therefore unites facticity and transcendence. A worker shaped by deprivation cannot simply will away material constraints, but can organise, submit, reinterpret or oppose them. Even refusal to choose is a choice. Through action one makes oneself from what history and others have made.

Bad faith separates the two poles. The café waiter treats himself as a fixed role and denies transcendence; the irresponsible dreamer denies facticity and treats any imagined possibility as immediately available. Authenticity acknowledges both situated limits and ownership of one’s response. Since choosing projects an image of humanity, freedom entails anguish and responsibility.

**Objections:** Marxist and feminist critics argue that Sartre’s language can underestimate structural domination and unequal capabilities. ### Q6(a). How is Kṣaṇikavāda a logical derivative of Pratītyasamutpāda in Buddhism? Explain.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Buddhism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/buddhism.md) → dependent origination and momentariness · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 20 marks · Syllabus unit: Schools of Buddhism—dependent origination; momentariness · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Buddhist philosophy · causal efficacy · impermanence · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Buddhism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Buddhism.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `buddhism-momentariness-dependent-origination`

**Demand decoding:** Derive momentariness from conditionality and causal efficacy, while noting that the derivation is developed differently across Buddhist schools.

**Argument map:** whatever arises depends on causes → conditioned thing lacks self-subsistent essence → cause must cease as effect arises → persistence would make renewed production inexplicable → causal efficacy defines reality → only momentary events are ultimately real.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** The doctrine of momentariness (*kṣaṇikavāda*) develops the logic of dependent origination (*pratītyasamutpāda*): whatever exists conditionally has no enduring self-nature and functions only in a transient causal moment.

Dependent origination states: when this exists, that arises; with this cessation, that ceases. A conditioned entity is therefore neither self-produced nor permanently self-identical. Buddhist causal analysis argues that a cause produces its effect in the moment of its causal efficacy. If it remained unchanged after producing, it should either produce the same effect endlessly or require an unexplained additional factor. Destruction cannot be imposed later by something wholly external, because a genuinely permanent nature would resist alteration. Thus arising carries ceasing within it.

The Sautrāntika criterion “the real is causally efficacious” strengthens the inference: a moment performs its function and is replaced by a causally connected successor. Apparent continuants—a flame, person or seedling—are streams (*santāna*) of similar but numerically distinct events. This supports no-self (*anātman*): the five aggregates persist only as conditioned series.

The practical Middle Path avoids both eternalism and annihilationism. There is no permanent substance, yet causal continuity secures karma and the Four Noble Truths. The Noble Eightfold Path (*āryāṣṭāṅgamārga*) transforms conditions and terminates the dependent chain, making liberation intelligible without an eternal soul.

**Objection:** Dependent origination establishes dependence and impermanence, not necessarily instantaneous destruction; causal continuity also seems impossible if the cause is wholly absent. **Reply:** later Buddhists invoke immediate causal succession and inherited potency, but the inference from dependence to strict momentariness is a philosophical elaboration rather than a simple entailment.

**Evaluation:** Momentariness radicalises conditionality and blocks substance metaphysics, but its accounts of memory, endurance and causal transmission require sophisticated stream theories.

**Why this earns marks:** It supplies the logical derivation, causal argument, school qualification, continuity objection and soteriological connection.

### Q6(c). Explain the concepts of 'Anyathāsiddha' and 'Ananyathāsiddha' in the context of Asatkāryavāda.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/nyaya-vaisesika.md) → causation and causal relevance · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Classification route:** [Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Nyaya-Vaisesika.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `nyaya-anyathasiddha-ananyathasiddha-asatkaryavada`

**Demand decoding:** Define both terms as a filter for genuine causes, connect them to Nyāya production theory and illustrate close cases.

**Argument map:** effect is newly produced → cause is unconditional and immediately relevant antecedent → merely coincident/derivatively established antecedent is otherwise established → indispensable non-otherwise-established antecedent is genuine cause.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Within Nyāya’s non-pre-existent effect theory (*asatkāryavāda*), the distinction separates a genuinely indispensable causal antecedent from conditions whose presence is explainable otherwise.

A cause (*kāraṇa*) is an invariable, unconditional antecedent of an effect. A factor is non-otherwise-established (*ananyathāsiddha*) when its antecedence cannot be accounted for independently of the production of that effect; it is causally relevant. Threads, their conjunction and the weaver’s operation are respectively material, non-inherent and efficient causes of cloth.

A factor is otherwise-established (*anyathāsiddha*) when, although present before the effect, its presence or relation is fixed for another reason. The colour of the threads accompanies cloth-production but does not produce cloth as cloth. The potter’s father precedes the pot, but only through the potter; a remote or accidental antecedent is not thereby a cause.

This filter supports production theory (*ārambhavāda*): the cloth did not exist in the threads; a new whole arises when the relevant causal complex operates.

**Objection:** “Non-otherwise-established” can seem circular because causal relevance identifies the cause and the cause identifies relevance. Sāṃkhya also argues that production from absolute non-being is impossible. **Reply:** Nyāya invokes observed invariance, absence in the cause’s absence and a specified causal complex, not mere temporal priority.

**Evaluation:** The distinction anticipates relevance conditions in causal analysis, though remote conditions and causal overdetermination make its boundaries contestable.

**Why this earns marks:** It defines both terms, uses discriminating examples and relates them directly to *asatkāryavāda*.

### Q7(a). “A mango tree is grown out of a mango seed.” How will Sāṃkhya system explain this process through their theory of causation by rejecting their rival perspectives?
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Sāṃkhya](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/samkhya.md) → pre-existent effect theory and transformation · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 20 marks · Syllabus unit: Sāṃkhya—causation; evolution of nature · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Sāṃkhya · pre-existent effect theory · transformation · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Sāṃkhya](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Samkhya.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `samkhya-mango-seed-tree-satkaryavada`

**Demand decoding:** Apply all major arguments for pre-existence to the example and reject production-from-nothing, illusion and accidental emergence.

**Argument map:** specific effect arises from specific material → seed possesses tree in unmanifest form → enabling conditions manifest latent powers → cause transforms into effect → effect is new configuration, not new being → rivals fail specificity and continuity.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Sāṃkhya explains the mango tree as the real transformation and manifestation of capacities already present in the mango seed, not as an absolutely new entity.

Its pre-existent effect theory (*satkāryavāda*) holds that the effect exists unmanifest in its material cause. Five standard reasons apply:

1. **Non-production of non-being (*asadakaraṇāt*):** what is wholly absent cannot arise.  
2. **Need of a determinate material (*upādāna-grahaṇāt*):** a tree requires an appropriate seed.  
3. **Non-production of everything from everything (*sarva-sambhavābhāvāt*):** a mango seed does not produce a neem tree.  
4. **A capable cause produces what it is capable of (*śaktasya śakya-karaṇāt*):** the specific power is already present.  
5. **The effect has the nature of the cause (*kāraṇa-bhāvāt*):** material and causal continuity link seed and tree.

Soil, water, heat and time do not create mango-nature from nothing; they remove obstacles and enable the seed’s latent structure to unfold. Production is transformation (*pariṇāma*): the cause loses one configuration and acquires another while underlying nature (*prakṛti*) continues.

Nyāya’s non-pre-existent effect theory (*asatkāryavāda/ārambhavāda*) says the tree-whole is newly produced. Sāṃkhya replies that this cannot explain why only a mango seed yields a mango tree without admitting a determinate pre-existing power. Against Buddhist momentariness, it argues that a wholly perishing cause cannot sustain identity of the developmental process. Against Advaita apparent transformation (*vivarta*), it insists that growth is a real modification, not ignorance-dependent appearance.

**Objection:** If the actual tree already exists, growth becomes redundant; seed and tree have contrary properties. **Reply:** the effect pre-exists potentially or unmanifestly, not as a fully formed miniature. Manifestation is a real structural transformation.

**Evaluation:** The theory strongly explains causal specificity and conservation, but “pre-existence” is most plausible as determinate capacity, not literal identity of seed and mature tree.

**Why this earns marks:** It applies all five arguments and rejects each relevant rival through the mango example.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Buddhism treats the person as a dependently arisen stream of five aggregates, not an enduring soul; liberation is the cessation of ignorance, craving and suffering in that stream.

The five aggregates (*pañcaskandha; Pali: pañcakkhandha*) are material form, feeling, perception, volitional formations and consciousness. Each is impermanent, conditioned and incapable of sovereign control; therefore none is “mine, I, or my self.” Their causal coordination supports conventional agency and identity without a substance-owner.

Ignorance appropriates the aggregates, producing craving, action and renewed becoming through dependent origination (*pratītyasamutpāda*). Nirvāṇa is not the destruction of an eternal soul, since none existed. It is “unbinding”: extinction of greed, hatred and delusion and cessation of the conditions sustaining suffering and rebirth.

The practical Middle Path avoids sensual indulgence and self-mortification through the Noble Eightfold Path (*āryāṣṭāṅgamārga*); doctrinally it avoids eternalism and annihilationism. Causal continuity preserves karmic responsibility without numerical soul-identity.

**Objection:** If no same self persists, who acts and is liberated? **Reply:** neither strict identity nor total difference holds; later moments inherit earlier causal conditions, as one flame lights another.

**Evaluation:** The account dissolves reification and supports transformative practice, though grounding responsibility across a merely causal series remains debated.

**Why this earns marks:** It names all aggregates and reconciles no-self, karma, the two Middle Paths and liberation.

### Q7(c). Explain the differences of conception of Reality between Cārvāka and Jainism.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Cārvāka](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/carvaka.md) → materialism compared with Jaina pluralism · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Syllabus unit: Cārvāka and Jainism—reality, knowledge, self, bondage and liberation · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Cārvāka and Jainism · materialism versus many-sided realism · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Cārvāka](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Carvaka.md) · **Secondary route:** [Jainism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Jainism.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `carvaka-jainism-conceptions-reality`

**Demand decoding:** Compare ontology through epistemology, change, self, moral order and liberation rather than offering two disconnected summaries.

**Argument map:** Cārvāka perception-centred materialism → four elements and emergent consciousness → denial of transcendent order; Jain plural realism → souls and non-souls → permanence-with-change → karma and liberation → qualified standpoints.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Cārvāka offers parsimonious perceptual materialism, whereas Jainism offers pluralistic, many-sided realism encompassing material and spiritual substances.

Cārvāka accepts primarily what perception establishes: earth, water, fire and air and their compounds. Consciousness is an emergent bodily property; no independent soul, karmic mechanism, rebirth or liberation is warranted. Reality is this-worldly, and inference about imperceptibles remains uncertain.

Jainism recognises conscious souls (*jīvas*) and non-conscious realities (*ajīvas*): matter, principles of motion and rest, space and time. A real has origination, cessation and persistence; substance endures while modes change. Thus reality is many-sided (*anekāntavāda*), and judgements require conditional predication (*syādvāda*). Souls become materially bound by karma and can attain liberation.

Epistemologically, Cārvāka’s suspicion of inference contracts ontology; Jainism accepts perception, inference and testimony while qualifying every finite standpoint. Ethically, the former rejects cosmic retribution, while the latter grounds non-violence in the reality of innumerable souls.

**Objection:** Jainism may overpopulate reality, while Cārvāka cannot justify even unperceived material processes without inference. **Reply:** each accepts the cost of its method—comprehensiveness versus economy.

**Evaluation:** Cārvāka excels in evidential restraint; Jainism better explains plurality, continuity and ethics, but carries heavier metaphysical commitments.

**Why this earns marks:** It compares the systems across ontology, epistemology, change, self and soteriology.

### Q8(a). What does Aurobindo mean by 'life divine' as a possibility and inevitability?
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Sri Aurobindo](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/sri-aurobindo.md) → divine life and spiritual evolution · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 20 marks · Syllabus unit: Sri Aurobindo—evolution; integral yoga · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Sri Aurobindo · involution · Supermind · divine life · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Sri Aurobindo](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Aurobindo.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `aurobindo-life-divine-possibility-inevitability`

**Demand decoding:** Explain both modal claims: why divine life is metaphysically possible and why evolution tends toward it, while qualifying “inevitability.”

**Argument map:** reality is existence-consciousness-bliss → consciousness involutes in matter → evolution releases life and mind → mind’s limits point beyond itself → Supermind mediates unity and multiplicity → integral transformation makes divine life embodied.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** For Aurobindo, “life divine” is embodied existence transformed by supramental truth-consciousness; it is possible because spirit is already involved in matter and inevitable as the inherent tendency of cosmic evolution, though not a prediction that every individual will transform automatically.

Ultimate reality is existence-consciousness-bliss (*sat-cit-ānanda*). Through involution, consciousness conceals itself in matter; evolution is its progressive emergence as life and mind. Since an effect manifests what was involved in its cause, the rise of mind from life suggests that matter is not intrinsically unconscious. Human mind, however, divides reality, oscillates between ignorance and partial knowledge, and cannot harmonise spirit and world.

Supermind is truth-consciousness: it knows unity without abolishing multiplicity. Its descent, joined with the ascent of aspiration, enables triple transformation. Psychic transformation brings the inner soul forward; spiritual transformation universalises and elevates consciousness; supramental transformation alters mental, vital and physical nature. Divine life therefore means knowledge, freedom, harmony and delight expressed collectively and bodily, not escape into a world-negating liberation.

Its **possibility** rests on involution, intermediate grades of consciousness and yogic experience. Its **inevitability** rests on the evolutionary pressure of involved spirit and mind’s evident incompleteness. Nature has already crossed seemingly impossible thresholds from matter to life and life to mind.

**Objections:** The inference from past emergence to a supramental future is speculative; suffering and evolutionary contingency undermine teleology. Mystical experiences do not publicly prove a new species-consciousness. **Reply:** Aurobindo presents a metaphysical and experimental spiritual hypothesis, not a timetable; resistance can delay and individuals may refuse.

**Evaluation:** Divine life uniquely reconciles liberation with world-transformation and gives evolution ethical direction. Its possibility is conceptually coherent within Aurobindo’s system, but inevitability is a metaphysical faith stronger than empirical evidence warrants.

**Why this earns marks:** It separately establishes possibility and qualified inevitability through involution, Supermind and triple transformation.

### Q8(b). Critically evaluate the logical and metaphysical status of Viśeṣa in the context of Vaiśeṣika Philosophy.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/nyaya-vaisesika.md) → particularity as a category · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Syllabus unit: Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika—categories; particularity · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Vaiśeṣika · ultimate differentiator · individuation · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Nyaya-Vaisesika.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `vaisesika-visesa-logical-metaphysical-status`

**Demand decoding:** Explain why particularity is posited, where it inheres, how it avoids regress and whether it is ontologically necessary.

**Argument map:** eternal substances can share universals and qualities → numerical difference needs ground → ultimate particularities individuate each eternal → they inhere inseparably and are self-differentiating → explanatory economy and regress objections.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Ultimate particularity (*viśeṣa*) is Vaiśeṣika’s irreducible principle of numerical individuation for eternal substances; logically it explains difference where shared properties cannot, but metaphysically it risks becoming a merely stipulative differentiator.

Atoms of the same element possess the same universals and basic qualities. Likewise selves, minds, space, time and ether are eternal. If indiscernible entities were individuated only by common features, each class would collapse into one. Vaiśeṣika therefore posits innumerable ultimate particularities, each inhering in exactly one eternal substance through inherence (*samavāya*).

Particularity is self-differentiating (*svato-vyāvṛtta*): it does not require another particularity to distinguish it, otherwise an infinite regress follows. It is not a universal, because it contracts application to one entity rather than recurring in many. Yogic perception is sometimes invoked for its cognition; ordinarily it is inferentially postulated.

**Objections:** If each substance is already numerically distinct, *viśeṣa* duplicates the fact; if not, attaching qualitatively indistinguishable differentiators merely redescribes it. Buddhists reject enduring substances altogether, while identity of indiscernibles challenges difference without discernible features.

**Reply:** Vaiśeṣika distinguishes an ontological ground of numerical diversity from our epistemic ability to describe it; primitive individuation may be unavoidable in any pluralist realism.

**Evaluation:** *Viśeṣa* gives the system a consistent account of many eternal reals, but its explanatory value depends on accepting primitive thisness rather than demanding qualitative analysis.

**Why this earns marks:** It covers function, locus, self-differentiation, regress, epistemic access and the strongest economy objection.

### Q8(c). Discuss the nature and relationship of Jīva and Jīva-sākṣī according to non-dualism.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Vedānta](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/vedanta.md) → Advaita on empirical self and witnessing consciousness · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Syllabus unit: Schools of Vedānta—Śaṅkara; self, Brahman, bondage and liberation · Thinker/doctrine/subproblem: Advaita Vedānta · individual self · witness consciousness · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Vedānta](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Vedanta.md) · **Proposed topic slug:** `advaita-jiva-jiva-saksi-relationship`

**Demand decoding:** Distinguish the conditioned empirical individual from the unconditioned witness and explain their apparent relation without multiplying selves.

**Argument map:** pure consciousness is self-luminous → mind reflects consciousness → identification with body–mind produces empirical individual → witness illumines all states without acting → difference is conditioning-dependent → knowledge reveals non-duality.

#### Model answer

**Thesis:** Advaita distinguishes the empirical individual self (*jīva*) from the witness of the individual (*jīva-sākṣī*) as conditioned appearance from self-luminous consciousness, not as two ultimately separate selves.

The individual is pure consciousness apparently limited by the internal organ, body and ignorance. Consciousness reflected in mind (*cidābhāsa*) supports agency, enjoyment, memory and karmic continuity. Because the mind appropriates bodily and mental states, the person says “I act,” “I suffer” and “I know.”

The witness consciousness is the unchanging illuminator of waking, dream and deep sleep and of the presence or absence of particular thoughts. It is not an agent, object or mental operation; every cognition is manifest to it. One witness appears many because of different limiting adjuncts (*upādhis*), as one space seems divided by pots.

Their relation is often explained through reflection or apparent delimitation. At the empirical level the distinction explains experience and bondage; ultimately the witness is non-different from Brahman, and the individual has no independent reality. Liberating knowledge removes identification, not consciousness itself.

**Objection:** If the witness is wholly inactive and relationless, how can it illuminate a mind or be implicated in error? **Reply:** illumination is not a real action; proximity and ignorance generate an apparent relation, like a crystal seeming red beside a flower.

**Evaluation:** The distinction elegantly separates awareness from mental content and explains continuity across states, but the status of reflection and beginningless ignorance remains Advaita’s central difficulty.

**Why this earns marks:** It clearly distinguishes nature, empirical function, apparent relation, non-dual identity and the key objection.
