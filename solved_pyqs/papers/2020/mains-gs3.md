## Q1. Intra-generational and inter-generational equity
**Primary route:** Economy → [Poverty, Inequality, Social Sector and Inclusive Growth](../../topics/economy/poverty-inequality-social-sector-and-inclusive-growth.md) → Equity and sustainable development · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2020 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 1  
**Official question:** Explain intra-generational and inter-generational issues of equity from the perspective of inclusive growth and sustainable development.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Inclusive Growth → Intra- and Inter-generational Equity  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/poverty-inequality-social-sector-and-inclusive-growth`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Explain two linked concepts  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/23_Poverty-Inequality-Social-Sector-and-Inclusive-Growth.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/23_Poverty-Inequality-Social-Sector-and-Inclusive-Growth.md)

### Demand decoding
Define both forms of equity, connect each to inclusive growth and sustainability, and show that present redistribution and ecological stewardship can reinforce—but sometimes compete with—each other.

### Compact framework
`fairness among people today + fairness between present and future people → inclusive access within ecological limits`

### Model answer
**Intra-generational equity** concerns fairness among people today. Inclusive growth requires that income, capabilities and environmental benefits reach women, poorer households, vulnerable communities and lagging regions. Unequal access to land, education, finance, clean air or climate adaptation makes growth exclusionary. Progressive taxation, universal basic services and a **just transition** for affected workers advance such equity.

**Inter-generational equity** requires the present generation to meet its needs without reducing future generations’ ability to meet theirs—the Brundtland conception of sustainable development. Excessive groundwater extraction, biodiversity loss, carbon emissions and unsustainable public debt transfer costs forward. Renewable energy, resource efficiency, pollution control and the precautionary principle conserve natural and fiscal capital.

The dimensions are linked: poor people cannot be asked to bear disproportionate conservation costs, while short-term poverty reduction based on ecological depletion is not durable. Policy must therefore combine redistribution and capability-building with sustainable production, polluter-pays pricing and protection of common resources.

### Examiner rationale
The answer distinguishes the two time horizons and resolves their apparent trade-off through a just, capability-based transition.

## Q2. Potential GDP and India's output constraints
**Primary route:** Economy → [National Income, GDP, GVA and Measurement](../../topics/economy/national-income-gdp-gva-and-measurement.md) → Potential output · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2020 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 2  
**Official question:** Define potential GDP and explain its determinants. What are the factors that have been inhibiting India from realizing its potential GDP?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → National Income → Potential GDP and Output Gap  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/national-income-gdp-gva-and-measurement`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Define + Explain + Diagnose  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/01_National-Income-GDP-GVA-and-Measurement.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/01_National-Income-GDP-GVA-and-Measurement.md)

### Demand decoding
Define non-inflationary productive capacity, identify supply-side determinants, and separate India's structural constraints from temporary demand weakness.

### Compact framework
`labour + capital + productivity + institutions → sustainable capacity; actual GDP below it = negative output gap`

### Model answer
**Potential GDP** is the level of output an economy can sustain when labour and capital are normally utilised, without generating accelerating inflation. It is unobservable and differs from the maximum physically possible output.

Its determinants are:

- labour-force size, participation, health, education and skills;
- capital stock and the rate and quality of investment;
- technology and total factor productivity;
- infrastructure, natural resources and efficient allocation; and
- institutional quality, competition and macroeconomic stability.

India has fallen short because low female labour-force participation and skill-health deficits limit labour utilisation; stressed bank and corporate balance sheets weakened investment; infrastructure gaps raise costs; informality, small firms and regulatory uncertainty depress productivity; and agricultural underemployment reflects poor structural transformation. Weak demand, exports and the COVID-19 shock further widened the output gap.

Realising potential therefore needs human-capital investment, competitive markets, reliable infrastructure, financial-sector repair and predictable regulation—not demand stimulus alone.

### Examiner rationale
The response defines potential output precisely and classifies the constraints into factor accumulation, productivity, allocation and cyclical demand.

## Q3. Transport and marketing of agricultural produce
**Primary route:** Economy → [APMC, e-NAM, FPOs and Agricultural Supply Chains](../../topics/economy/apmc-e-nam-fpos-and-agricultural-supply-chains.md) → Post-harvest logistics and markets · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2020 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 3  
**Official question:** What are the main constraints in transport and marketing of agricultural produce in India?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Agriculture → Transport and Marketing Constraints  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/apmc-e-nam-fpos-and-agricultural-supply-chains`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Easy-Moderate · Identify and explain  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/13_APMC-e-NAM-FPOs-and-Agricultural-Supply-Chains.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/13_APMC-e-NAM-FPOs-and-Agricultural-Supply-Chains.md)

### Demand decoding
Cover the chain from farm gate to final market and explain how physical, institutional and informational bottlenecks reduce farmers' price realisation.

### Compact framework
`small surplus → weak aggregation → poor storage/transport → fragmented market → distress sale and high intermediation`

### Model answer
Agricultural marketing is constrained by a fragmented farm-to-market chain:

- **First mile:** small, scattered holdings produce low marketable surplus; weak rural roads and costly loading make aggregation difficult.
- **Perishability:** inadequate pack-houses, assaying, grading, warehouses, reefer transport and cold chains cause quality loss and compel distress sale.
- **Market structure:** restricted competition in some APMC mandis, multiple fees, trader concentration and limited direct procurement weaken farmers’ bargaining power.
- **Information and finance:** asymmetric price information, non-standard weights, delayed payments and dependence on commission agents tie credit to sale.
- **Inter-State trade:** differing regulations impede a unified market; e-NAM cannot deliver integration without physical logistics and assaying.
- **Risk:** price volatility, export-policy changes and weak processing capacity discourage production planning.

Investment in rural logistics and scientific storage, negotiable warehouse receipts, transparent assaying, interoperable e-NAM, competitive market channels and **Farmer Producer Organisations** can aggregate produce and bargaining power. Stable trade policy and enforceable, timely payment are equally necessary.

### Examiner rationale
The answer follows the actual supply chain and connects each constraint to loss, distress sale or weak price discovery.

## Q4. Food processing and farmers' incomes
**Primary route:** Economy → [Food Processing, Cold Chains and Value Addition](../../topics/economy/food-processing-cold-chains-and-value-addition.md) → Farmer income · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2020 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 4  
**Official question:** What are the challenges and opportunities of food processing sector in the country? How can income of the farmers be substantially increased by encouraging food processing?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Agriculture → Food Processing and Value Addition  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/food-processing-cold-chains-and-value-addition`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Diagnose + Explain mechanism  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/15_Food-Processing-Cold-Chains-and-Value-Addition.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/15_Food-Processing-Cold-Chains-and-Value-Addition.md)

### Demand decoding
Balance opportunity and constraint, then trace specific channels—not merely assert—that processing can raise farm income.

### Compact framework
`surplus/perishability + value addition → longer shelf life, new demand and better realisation; subject to fair procurement`

### Model answer
India offers a large raw-material base, diverse agro-climates, export opportunities and rural employment, especially for women. Processing can convert seasonal perishables into storable, higher-value products.

Yet the sector faces fragmented supply, variable quality, inadequate cold chains and testing, expensive credit and power, skill gaps, small-scale technology, multiple compliance burdens and uncertain export policy.

Farmer income can rise when processing:

- reduces post-harvest loss and distress sale by extending shelf life;
- creates demand for horticulture, dairy, fisheries and other high-value produce;
- rewards grading, quality and traceability through assured procurement;
- lets FPOs undertake sorting, primary processing and branding, retaining a larger value share;
- uses contract farming with transparent prices and enforceable safeguards; and
- develops clusters through **PM Kisan SAMPADA Yojana**, cold-chain and food-park infrastructure.

However, value addition does not automatically reach cultivators. Competitive procurement, prompt payment, producer equity in enterprises, quality-extension support and protection against one-sided contracts are essential.

### Examiner rationale
The answer links processing to income through loss reduction, diversification, quality premiums and producer participation, while qualifying distributional gains.

## Q5. Nanotechnology in health
**Primary route:** Science and Technology → [Nanotechnology and Applications](../../topics/science-and-technology/nanotechnology-and-applications.md) → Health applications · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2020 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 5  
**Official question:** What do you understand by nanotechnology and how is it helping in health sector?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Science and Technology → Nanotechnology → Healthcare  
**Proposed topic slug:** `science-and-technology/nanotechnology-and-applications`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Define + Apply  
**Canonical owner:** [`Science-and-Technology/basic/16_Nanotechnology-and-Applications.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Science-and-Technology/basic/16_Nanotechnology-and-Applications.md)

### Demand decoding
Explain why nanoscale properties matter and connect those properties to diagnosis, delivery, treatment and prevention, with safety limits.

### Compact framework
`1–100 nm scale → high surface area and tunable optical/chemical behaviour → targeted sensing and therapy`

### Model answer
Nanotechnology is the controlled design and use of materials and devices roughly at the **1–100 nanometre** scale, where quantum effects and a high surface-area-to-volume ratio produce properties different from bulk matter.

In health care it enables:

- **targeted drug delivery:** liposomes and polymeric nanoparticles can improve solubility, protect drugs and concentrate them at diseased tissue, reducing systemic toxicity;
- **diagnostics:** nanosensors, quantum dots and gold nanoparticles can detect biomarkers rapidly and at low concentrations;
- **imaging:** magnetic nanoparticles and nano-contrast agents improve localisation;
- **therapy:** photothermal particles and nano-formulations support more precise cancer treatment;
- **prevention and materials:** lipid nanoparticles can deliver nucleic acids; nanocoatings and silver nanoparticles have antimicrobial uses; and nanofibres aid tissue engineering.

Benefits depend on biocompatibility, dose, biodistribution and safe disposal. Nanoparticles may cross biological barriers or accumulate unexpectedly; hence toxicology, clinical validation, regulation and equitable affordability must accompany innovation.

### Examiner rationale
The response first supplies the operative scale and property mechanism, then gives distinct health applications and a proportionate risk qualification.

## Q6. Science in life and transformation of agriculture

**Identity:** 2020 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 6  
**Official question:** How is science interwoven deeply with our lives? What are the striking changes in agriculture triggered off by the science-based technologies?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Agriculture → Science-led Technological Change  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/land-reforms-green-revolution-and-cropping-systems`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Explain + Illustrate  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/11_Land-Reforms-Green-Revolution-and-Cropping-Systems.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/11_Land-Reforms-Green-Revolution-and-Cropping-Systems.md)

Briefly establish science's everyday presence, then devote most space to major agricultural transformations and their qualified effects.

### Compact framework
`scientific knowledge → health, communication and production → seed-water-input-mechanisation-data revolution in farming`

### Model answer
Science shapes everyday life through electricity, sanitation, vaccines and medicines, transport, telecommunications, weather forecasts, digital payments and materials. It converts tested knowledge into safer choices and productive tools.

## Q7. Draft EIA Notification, 2020
**Primary route:** Environment and Ecology → [Environmental Impact Assessment and NGT](../../topics/environment/environmental-impact-assessment-and-ngt.md) → Draft EIA Notification 2020 · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2020 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 7  
**Official question:** How does the draft Environment Impact Assessment (EIA) Notification, 2020 differ from the existing EIA Notification, 2006?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Environment and Ecology → Environmental Governance → EIA Reform  
**Proposed topic slug:** `environment/environmental-impact-assessment-and-ngt`  
**Difficulty / demand:** High · Compare legal-procedural changes  
**Canonical owner:** [`Environment-and-Ecology/basic/16_Environmental-Impact-Assessment-and-NGT.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Environment-and-Ecology/basic/16_Environmental-Impact-Assessment-and-NGT.md)

### Demand decoding
Compare the draft—not a final law—with the 2006 notification across approval, public participation, exemptions, monitoring and validity.

### Compact framework
`2006 prior appraisal and participation → 2020 draft rationalisation plus wider exemptions, shorter consultation and violation regularisation`

### Model answer
Both instruments operate under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, but the **draft EIA Notification, 2020** proposed important departures from the 2006 regime:

- it created a formal mechanism for appraisal and regularisation of projects begun without prior environmental clearance—widely criticised as enabling **post-facto clearance**;
- the public-hearing notice period was proposed to fall from 30 to **20 days**;
- more projects, including specified B2 projects, were placed outside detailed EIA or public consultation;
- “strategic” projects could receive confidentiality, and certain border-area projects obtained exemptions;
- compliance reporting was proposed annually rather than every six months; and
- clearance validity was extended for sectors such as mining and river-valley projects.

The draft also sought online processing, standardised terms and clearer categorisation, which could reduce delay. However, speed must not dilute prior appraisal, public participation and monitoring—the preventive core of EIA. Any final framework should preserve reasoned disclosure, cumulative-impact assessment and independent compliance.

### Examiner rationale
The answer identifies the principal procedural differences while accurately treating the 2020 text as a draft and balancing efficiency against environmental safeguards.

**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words

**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/irrigation-inputs-credit-insurance-and-sustainable-agriculture`

**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/14_Irrigation-Inputs-Credit-Insurance-and-Sustainable-Agriculture.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/14_Irrigation-Inputs-Credit-Insurance-and-Sustainable-Agriculture.md)

State its campaign design, geography, interventions and convergence mechanism without confusing it with the later Jal Shakti Abhiyan: Catch the Rain expansion.

### Compact framework
`time-bound mission + water-stressed blocks + five interventions + convergence and local planning`

### Model answer
The **Jal Shakti Abhiyan (JSA), 2019** was a time-bound, mission-mode water-conservation campaign coordinated by the Union government with States and districts. It initially focused on **1,592 water-stressed blocks in 256 districts**, with activities aligned to monsoon cycles.

1. water conservation and rainwater harvesting;
2. renovation of traditional and other water bodies;
3. reuse of water and recharge structures;
4. watershed development; and
5. intensive afforestation.

Central and technical teams worked with district administrations to review progress and prepare water-conservation plans. The campaign sought convergence of schemes and funds, geotagging, scientific planning through institutions such as IITs, and public participation.

JSA's significance lay in making water security a whole-of-government and community campaign rather than a stand-alone construction programme. Durable outcomes, however, require aquifer-based budgeting, maintenance, crop alignment and empowered local water institutions after the campaign period.

### Examiner rationale
The response gives the verified original coverage and five interventions and distinguishes campaign mobilisation from long-term groundwater governance.

**Primary route:** Internal Security → [Cyber Security, Critical Information Infrastructure and Cybercrime](../../topics/internal-security/cyber-security-cii-and-cybercrime.md) → Cybercrime response · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2020 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 9  
**Official question:** Discuss different types of cyber crimes and measures required to be taken to fight the menace.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Internal Security → Cyber Security → Cybercrime Types and Response  
**Proposed topic slug:** `internal-security/cyber-security-cii-and-cybercrime`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Classify + Prescribe  
**Canonical owner:** [`Internal-Security/basic/08_Cyber-Security-CII-and-Cybercrime.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Internal-Security/basic/08_Cyber-Security-CII-and-Cybercrime.md)

### Demand decoding
Classify crimes by victim and method, then match preventive, investigative, legal and cooperative measures to them.

### Compact framework
`people + property/finance + organisations/CII + state → prevent, detect, report, investigate, recover and cooperate`

### Model answer
Cybercrime uses digital systems either as the target or the instrument.

- **Against individuals:** phishing, identity theft, cyberstalking, bullying, morphing and sexual exploitation.

Surveillance must remain lawful, necessary and proportionate so that security does not erode privacy.

**Identity:** 2020 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 10  
**Official question:** For effective border area management, discuss the steps required to be taken to deny local support to militants and also suggest ways to manage favourable perception among locals.

**Classification:** Internal Security → Border Areas → Population-centric Security  
**Proposed topic slug:** `internal-security/internal-security-foundations-and-governance`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Discuss + Suggest

Explain how militants obtain local support and combine intelligence-led security with legitimacy, services and accountable conduct.

### Compact framework
`isolate militants from coercion/finance/shelter/intelligence → protect population → earn trust through justice and development`

### Model answer
Militants sustain themselves through fear, grievance, kinship networks, extortion, safe houses and information. Border management must therefore be population-centric.

## Q11. Capital formation and PPP concession agreements
**Primary route:** Economy → [Infrastructure, PPPs, Logistics and Public Investment](../../topics/economy/infrastructure-ppps-logistics-and-public-investment.md) → Concession design · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2020 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 11  
**Official question:** Explain the meaning of investment in an economy in terms of capital formation. Discuss the factors to be considered while designing a concession agreement between a public entity and a private entity.  
**Marks / limit:** 15 / 250 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Investment and Infrastructure → PPP Concession Agreements  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/infrastructure-ppps-logistics-and-public-investment`  
**Difficulty / demand:** High · Explain + Design framework  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/18_Infrastructure-PPPs-Logistics-and-Public-Investment.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/18_Infrastructure-PPPs-Logistics-and-Public-Investment.md)

### Demand decoding
First distinguish real investment from financial asset exchange, then specify how a long-term PPP contract should allocate rights, risks, returns and accountability.

### Compact framework
`saving → real asset addition → productive capacity; PPP contract → output, risk, revenue, regulation, change and exit`

### Model answer
In macroeconomics, **investment** means addition to the stock of productive capital—plant, machinery, buildings, infrastructure, inventories and intellectual property—not merely purchase of an existing share. **Gross capital formation** comprises gross fixed capital formation, change in stocks and valuables; subtracting depreciation gives net capital formation. By expanding productive capacity and embodying technology, it raises potential output.

A PPP concession agreement authorises a private entity to finance, build, operate or maintain a public asset for a defined period. Its design should specify:

- **scope and output standards:** service quality, capacity, milestones, maintenance and hand-back condition;
- **risk allocation:** construction, land, demand, financing, operating, environmental and political risks assigned to the party best able to manage them;
- **public obligations:** timely land and clearances, utility shifting and any viability-gap support;
- **revenue:** user charges, indexation, revenue sharing, payment security and affordability safeguards;
- **performance regime:** measurable indicators, independent monitoring, penalties and incentives;
- **change mechanisms:** treatment of change in law, force majeure, renegotiation and unforeseen demand shifts;
- **finance and control:** lender step-in rights, escrow, insurance and limits on transfer;
- **disputes and exit:** graded resolution, termination payments and continuity of essential services; and
- **transparency:** competitive bidding, disclosure and audit.

Over-optimistic traffic forecasts or transfer of uncontrollable risk leads to stalled projects and repeated renegotiation. A sound concession therefore balances bankability with value for money, fiscal-risk disclosure and universal service.

### Examiner rationale
The answer defines capital formation accurately and converts PPP principles into contract clauses, including risk, monitoring, renegotiation and public-interest safeguards.

## Q12. GST compensation and federal tensions
**Primary route:** Economy → [Taxation, GST, Finance Commission and Fiscal Federalism](../../topics/economy/taxation-gst-finance-commission-and-fiscal-federalism.md) → GST compensation · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2020 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 12  
**Official question:** Explain the rationale behind the Goods and Services Tax (Compensation to States) Act of 2017. How has COVID-19 impacted the GST compensation fund and created new federal tensions?  
**Marks / limit:** 15 / 250 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Fiscal Federalism → GST Compensation  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/taxation-gst-finance-commission-and-fiscal-federalism`  
**Difficulty / demand:** High · Explain rationale + Analyse contemporary dispute  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/10_Taxation-GST-Finance-Commission-and-Fiscal-Federalism.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/10_Taxation-GST-Finance-Commission-and-Fiscal-Federalism.md)

### Demand decoding
Explain the statutory bargain that induced States to surrender tax powers, then trace how the pandemic broke the cess-revenue arithmetic and produced a borrowing and trust dispute.

### Compact framework
`States surrender indirect-tax autonomy → five-year protected revenue → cess-funded compensation; pandemic → collections collapse + claims rise → borrowing/federal trust conflict`

### Model answer
GST subsumed several State taxes and limited States’ independent indirect-tax space. To secure consent for this destination-based reform, the **GST (Compensation to States) Act, 2017** guaranteed compensation for revenue shortfall during the five-year transition from July 2017. Protected revenue was calculated from the **2015–16 base** with **14% annual compounded growth**. A compensation cess on specified goods financed a non-lapsable fund.

COVID-19 disrupted this design:

- lockdown contracted consumption and GST/cess collections;
- simultaneously, the 14% protected-revenue benchmark continued to grow, sharply widening the gap;
- the cess fund became insufficient when States faced extraordinary health spending and their own borrowing constraints.

Tensions arose over whether the Union had an unconditional obligation to pay the entire shortfall, whether pandemic-related loss fell outside the original GST implementation guarantee, and who should borrow. In 2020 the Union offered borrowing options through a special window, while several States initially argued that the Centre should borrow because it commanded greater fiscal capacity and had designed the guarantee. Delayed payments also impaired State cash management.

The episode exposed vertical fiscal imbalance and the weakness of financing a firm guarantee through a volatile cess. Cooperative federalism requires transparent GST Council estimates, timely settlement, a credible borrowing backstop and a rules-based stabilisation reserve. ### Model answer

- high-yielding dwarf varieties responsive to fertiliser;
- assured canal and tubewell irrigation;
- subsidised electricity, fertiliser and institutional credit;

**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/irrigation-inputs-credit-insurance-and-sustainable-agriculture`

**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/14_Irrigation-Inputs-Credit-Insurance-and-Sustainable-Agriculture.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/14_Irrigation-Inputs-Credit-Insurance-and-Sustainable-Agriculture.md)

### Compact framework
`capture and recharge → store safely → convey efficiently → apply precisely → govern aquifers and crop demand`

### Model answer
India’s water stress reflects variable monsoons, limited storage, inefficient conveyance, water-intensive crops and unregulated groundwater extraction. An integrated response should include:

**Storage and recharge**
- restore tanks, ponds, wetlands and traditional systems; desilt them only after catchment and silt-disposal assessment;
- build check dams, contour trenches and recharge structures suited to local hydrogeology;

The answer spans supply, distribution, application and governance and explicitly addresses the efficiency-rebound problem.
