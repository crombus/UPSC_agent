## Q1(a). Ethics, values and Comprehensive National Power
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Governance and International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/governance-and-international-ethics.md) → Ethical foundations of national power · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** Discuss the role of ethics and values in enhancing the following three major components of Comprehensive National Power (CNP), viz. human capital, soft power (culture and policies) and social harmony. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Ethics and Human Interface · Subtopic: Values and national power · Proposed slug: `ethics-values-comprehensive-national-power` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Discuss three specified components

**Demand decoding:** Explain the causal contribution of ethics and values to each named component, without treating power as military or economic capacity alone.

### Model answer

**Concept:** Comprehensive National Power is sustainable capacity created by resources, legitimate institutions and cooperative citizens.

**Thinker/framework:** Amartya Sen’s capability approach links human development with substantive freedom; Joseph Nye shows that attraction, rather than coercion alone, generates soft power.

**Application:** Ethics strengthens **human capital** through equal education, health, dignity at work, scientific temper and merit. It strengthens **soft power** when constitutional democracy, plural culture, peaceful conduct and credible policies make India trustworthy; hypocrisy destroys attraction. It promotes **social harmony** through justice, fraternity, tolerance, empathy and impartial institutions, reducing hatred and enabling collective action.

Competence, security and prosperity remain necessary. Yet power detached from justice causes exclusion, conflict and reputational loss.

**Conclusion:** Values convert population into human capital, culture into credible influence and diversity into solidarity; they are productive foundations of durable national power.

**Why this earns marks:** It addresses all three specified components through named concepts, applications and a qualification.

## Q1(b). Education, human development and NEP 2020
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Work Culture and Public Funds](../../topics/ethics/work-culture-and-public-funds.md) → Education and value formation · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** “Education is not an injunction, it is an effective and pervasive tool for all round development of an individual and social transformation.” Examine the New Education Policy, 2020 (NEP, 2020) in light of the above statement. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Human Values · Subtopic: Education and social transformation · Proposed slug: `education-social-transformation-nep-2020` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Examine policy through quotation

**Demand decoding:** Explain education as enabling rather than coercive, test relevant NEP 2020 features against individual and social transformation, and note implementation limits.

### Model answer

**Concept:** Education transforms when it develops autonomous judgment, capability and character rather than merely commanding conformity.

**Thinker/framework:** John Dewey viewed education as growth through experience; Sen’s capability approach treats learning as enlarging real freedom.

**Application:** NEP 2020 supports development through foundational literacy, early-childhood care, multidisciplinary learning, vocational exposure, critical thinking, arts and sports. Mother-tongue instruction in early years can improve comprehension. Gender inclusion, open learning and attention to disadvantaged groups can widen opportunity. Constitutional values, scientific temper and environmental awareness cultivate citizenship, while research and teacher development support innovation.

However, digital divides, unequal school capacity, teacher vacancies, financing constraints and commercialisation can reproduce hierarchy. Flexibility without counselling may track poorer students into inferior choices.

**Conclusion:** NEP 2020 reflects education as an enabling social instrument, but its ethical promise depends on equitable resources, capable teachers and accountable implementation.

**Why this earns marks:** It directly examines policy features, transformative mechanisms and implementation qualifications.

## Q2(a). Hatred, conscience and national spirit
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → Moral emotions and conscience · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** “Hatred is destructive of a person's wisdom and conscience that can poison a nation's spirit.” Do you agree with this view? Justify your answer. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Ethics and Human Interface · Subtopic: Hatred and moral judgment · Proposed slug: `hatred-wisdom-conscience-national-spirit` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Take a position and justify

**Demand decoding:** Establish how hatred impairs individual judgment and collective morality, illustrate it, and distinguish moral opposition from hatred.

### Model answer

**Concept:** I agree. Hatred reduces persons to despised identities, narrows attention, suppresses empathy and permits conscience to rationalise cruelty.

**Thinker/framework:** Aristotle’s doctrine of the mean requires emotions to be directed by reason; Gandhi warned that hatred of the wrongdoer reproduces violence even while opposing wrong.

**Application:** Hatred creates confirmation bias, dehumanisation and revenge, making proportional judgment difficult. In administration it may produce discriminatory policing or welfare denial. Propaganda can convert prejudice into communal violence, segregation and retaliatory politics. Trust and fraternity then erode; resources move from development to conflict.

Yet anger at injustice is not necessarily hatred. Righteous indignation can expose oppression when disciplined by truth, non-violence, due process and recognition of the opponent’s dignity.

Constitutional education, inter-group contact, impartial enforcement and restorative dialogue can interrupt hatred.

**Conclusion:** A nation preserves its moral spirit by opposing injustice firmly while refusing dehumanisation.

**Why this earns marks:** It justifies agreement causally, applies it personally and nationally, and adds a crucial qualification.

## Q2(b). Components and learnability of emotional intelligence
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Emotional Intelligence and Ethical Dilemmas](../../topics/ethics/emotional-intelligence-and-ethical-dilemmas.md) → Emotional-intelligence competencies · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** What are the main components of emotional intelligence (EI)? Can they be learned? Discuss. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Emotional Intelligence · Subtopic: Components and development · Proposed slug: `ei-components-learnability` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Identify and discuss

**Demand decoding:** State the principal components, explain whether and how they can be developed, and avoid claiming that training guarantees ethical conduct.

### Model answer

**Concept:** Emotional intelligence is the capacity to understand and regulate emotions in oneself and relationships.

**Thinker/framework:** Daniel Goleman identifies five components: **self-awareness** of feelings, triggers and limits; **self-regulation** of impulses; **intrinsic motivation** toward purposeful goals; **empathy** for others’ perspectives; and **social skills** for communication, cooperation and conflict management.

These competencies are learnable through practice. Journals and feedback improve self-awareness; mindfulness aids regulation; attainable goals build motivation; field immersion develops empathy; role-play and mentoring strengthen social skills. Police de-escalation simulations, for example, can replace reactive aggression with measured communication.

Temperament, trauma and organisational culture affect learning. EI is also morally neutral: a manipulator may read emotions skilfully. Integrity and rights must guide it.

**Conclusion:** EI can be cultivated throughout life, but durable development requires practice, feedback, supportive institutions and an ethical compass.

**Why this earns marks:** It names the accepted components and gives realistic learning mechanisms with a moral qualification.

## Q3(a). Buddha's teachings relevant today
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Indian Moral Thinkers](../../topics/ethics/indian-moral-thinkers.md) → Buddhist ethics · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** What teachings of Buddha are most relevant today and why? Discuss. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Indian Moral Thinkers · Subtopic: Contemporary relevance of Buddhist ethics · Proposed slug: `buddha-teachings-contemporary-relevance` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Select, explain and justify

**Demand decoding:** Identify major teachings, connect each to present problems, and avoid a merely devotional list.

### Model answer

**Concept:** Buddha’s ethics traces suffering to craving and ignorance and proposes disciplined transformation.

**Thinker/framework:** The **Middle Path** rejects indulgence and self-mortification; the Noble Eightfold Path integrates right understanding, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness and concentration.

**Application:** Compassion (*karuṇā*) counters polarisation and cruelty. Non-violence and right speech challenge terrorism, hate speech and online abuse. Mindfulness aids self-awareness amid anxiety. Right livelihood questions exploitation, corruption and destructive profit. Impermanence weakens possessiveness; dependent origination highlights interdependence. The Middle Path favours balance over ideological extremes.

These teachings should not become passive acceptance of injustice. Compassion requires institutional action, and detachment does not excuse neglect of rights or material deprivation.

**Conclusion:** Buddha remains relevant because he joins inner discipline with non-harm, responsible conduct and insight into interdependence—capacities needed for peaceful, sustainable public life.

**Why this earns marks:** It selects core doctrines, explains contemporary applications and guards against quietism.

## Q3(b). Will to power, rationality and moral duty in international relations
**Primary route:** Ethics → [International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/international-ethics.md) → Power and moral restraint · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** “The will to power exists, but it can be tamed and be guided by rationality and principles of moral duty.” Examine this statement in the context of international relations. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: International Ethics · Subtopic: Ethical restraint on state power · Proposed slug: `power-rationality-moral-duty-ir` · Difficulty: High · Demand: Examine

**Demand decoding:** Recognise power politics, explain rational and moral restraints, illustrate their operation, and qualify idealism.

### Model answer

**Concept:** States seek security, influence and resources; realism therefore treats power as persistent. Yet unrestrained power produces war, arms races and insecurity even for the powerful.

**Thinker/framework:** Hobbes explains competitive insecurity; Kantian duty forbids using persons and states merely as means. Rationality recognises mutual vulnerability.

**Application:** International law, diplomacy, the UN Charter and multilateral institutions channel power through rules. Nuclear deterrence is restrained by fear of mutual catastrophe; moral duty prohibits targeting civilians and requires proportionality. Climate cooperation reflects interdependence and differentiated responsibility. India can combine national interest with peaceful settlement and respect for sovereignty.

Institutions reflect unequal power and selective enforcement. Ethical restraint therefore needs verification, reciprocity, credible defence and institutional reform.

**Conclusion:** Power cannot be abolished, but rational prudence and moral duty can civilise its purposes and methods, converting domination into rule-bound cooperation.

**Why this earns marks:** It balances realism and ethics with named institutions, examples and qualifications.

## Q4(a). Laws, rules and the role of ethics
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → Laws, rules and conscience · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** Distinguish between laws and rules. Discuss the role of ethics in formulating them. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Sources of Ethical Guidance · Subtopic: Law, rules and ethical legislation · Proposed slug: `laws-rules-ethics-formulation` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Distinguish and discuss

**Demand decoding:** Make a clear distinction, then show how ethics shapes both ends and procedures of formulation.

### Model answer

**Concept and distinction:** A **law** is a binding norm enacted or recognised by sovereign authority and enforceable through courts. A **rule** is a specific direction governing conduct or procedure, often framed under a law; it cannot exceed its parent law. Laws establish rights, duties and powers; rules operationalise them.

**Thinker/framework:** Fuller’s morality of law—generality, publicity, clarity, consistency and prospectivity—shows that legality itself has ethical requirements. Rawls adds fairness and equal liberty.

**Application:** Ethics helps select legitimate ends; assess burdens; consult affected groups; protect minorities; limit discretion; ensure proportionate sanctions; and create transparency, review and remedy. A public-health rule should be evidence-based, least restrictive, non-discriminatory and appealable.

Yet morality is plural and cannot depend on officials’ private beliefs. Constitutional values, reasoned deliberation, rights and empirical evidence must mediate ethical claims.

**Conclusion:** Law supplies authority and rules supply detail; ethics supplies legitimate purpose, fairness and restraint.

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes the terms precisely and connects ethical principles to drafting and implementation.

## Q4(b). Positive attitude under extreme stress
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Emotional Intelligence and Ethical Dilemmas](../../topics/ethics/emotional-intelligence-and-ethical-dilemmas.md) → Resilience and positive attitude · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** A positive attitude is considered to be an essential characteristic of a civil servant who is often required to function under extreme stress. What contributes to a positive attitude in a person? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Attitude · Subtopic: Sources of positive administrative attitude · Proposed slug: `positive-attitude-extreme-stress` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Explain contributing factors

**Demand decoding:** Define positivity realistically and identify personal, social and organisational contributors relevant to stressful public service.

### Model answer

**Concept:** Positive attitude is realistic confidence that effort can improve difficulty; it is neither denial nor compulsory cheerfulness.

**Thinker/framework:** Albert Bandura’s self-efficacy explains confidence built through mastery, models and encouragement; Viktor Frankl emphasises meaning as a source of resilience.

**Contributors:** public purpose and constitutional commitment; competence; mastery experiences; self-regulation; growth mindset; health and sleep; supportive family and peers; mentors and ethical leadership; role clarity; fair workload, recognition and psychological safety; reflection and solution-focused planning.

For example, a disaster officer remains constructive when trained teams, reliable information and delegation convert anxiety into prioritised action.

Toxic positivity can silence distress. Counselling, rest and institutional correction may be necessary.

**Conclusion:** Positive attitude emerges from character and meaning, but also from competence and humane institutions that make responsible action possible under stress.

**Why this earns marks:** It identifies multiple causal levels and distinguishes resilience from denial.

## Q5(a). Gender inequality and Savitribai Phule
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Indian Moral Thinkers](../../topics/ethics/indian-moral-thinkers.md) → Equality and social reform · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** What are the main factors responsible for gender inequality in India? Discuss the contribution of Savitribai Phule in this regard. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Human Values · Subtopic: Gender justice and Savitribai Phule · Proposed slug: `gender-inequality-savitribai-phule` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Identify causes and discuss contribution

**Demand decoding:** Cover structural causes of inequality and connect Savitribai Phule’s work specifically to their transformation.

### Model answer

**Concept:** Gender inequality is unequal capability, status and power produced by institutions.

**Factors:** patriarchal control of property and mobility; son preference and unpaid care; unequal nutrition and schooling; early marriage; wage gaps; violence and weak justice; caste-class intersectionality; stereotypes; and inadequate representation.

**Thinker/application:** Savitribai Phule treated education as emancipation. With Jyotirao Phule, she helped establish a pioneering girls’ school at Bhide Wada, Pune, in 1848 and taught despite abuse. She educated oppressed castes, opposed child marriage, supported widows, challenged untouchability and helped shelter vulnerable women. Her action asserted women’s dignity and agency.

Her example shows that legal equality needs schools, social support and courageous norm-changing leadership. Education alone, however, must be joined by safety, property rights, care infrastructure and economic opportunity.

**Conclusion:** Savitribai attacked the linked structures of gender and caste through education, compassion and organised reform.

**Why this earns marks:** It covers principal causes, named contributions and their contemporary policy implication.

## Q5(b). Internet values and conflict with tradition
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Emerging Ethics—AI](../../topics/ethics/emerging-ethics-ai.md) → Digital culture and value conflict · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** “The current internet expansion has instilled a different set of cultural values which are often in conflict with traditional values.” Discuss. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Technology Ethics · Subtopic: Internet-mediated cultural change · Proposed slug: `internet-cultural-traditional-values` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Discuss critically

**Demand decoding:** Explain the new values fostered online, identify genuine conflicts and complementarities with tradition, and avoid treating either category as uniformly good.

### Model answer

**Concept:** The internet changes who produces culture, how norms travel and how identities are performed. It promotes openness and networked belonging.

**Thinker/framework:** Manuel Castells’ network society explains decentralised communication; value pluralism cautions against assuming one uncontested tradition.

**Application:** Online spaces enable youth voice, equality, inter-caste interaction, scientific exchange and accountability, challenging hierarchy and community control. Conversely, attention-driven platforms foster consumerism, misinformation, trolling and performative relationships, conflicting with restraint, responsibility, civility and truthful speech.

Conflict is not simply modern versus moral. Traditions may preserve solidarity, care and ecological wisdom, but may also legitimise patriarchy or exclusion. Digital values can liberate, yet algorithms may create conformity and polarisation.

The response is critical synthesis: digital literacy, privacy, platform accountability, dialogue and constitutional morality while preserving valuable traditions online.

**Conclusion:** Internet expansion should widen freedom without abandoning truth, dignity, responsibility and social trust.

**Why this earns marks:** It presents benefits, harms, internal diversity and a balanced ethical response.

## Q6(a). Vivekananda on help without condemnation
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Indian Moral Thinkers](../../topics/ethics/indian-moral-thinkers.md) → Tolerance and selfless service · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official instruction:** What does each of the following quotations mean to you?  
**Official quotation:** “Condemn none: if you can stretch out a helping hand, do so. If not, fold your hands, bless your brothers, and let them go their own way.” — Swami Vivekananda. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Indian Moral Thinkers · Subtopic: Non-judgmental service · Proposed slug: `vivekananda-help-without-condemnation` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Interpret and apply

### Model answer

**Concept:** Vivekananda replaces moral superiority with constructive compassion. Help should empower; where it is impossible, restraint is better than humiliation.

**Thinker/framework:** Practical Vedanta sees the same divinity in every person, making service an expression of fellowship rather than charity from above. It also resembles Mill’s respect for liberty where conduct does not harm others.

**Application:** A civil servant dealing with addiction should provide treatment and livelihood support instead of stigma. Welfare officials should not shame beneficiaries. One may offer advice without imposing a vocation, faith or lifestyle. International assistance should respect local ownership.

The quotation does not require silence before harm. Wrongful conduct may need criticism and lawful restraint, but condemning the person forecloses reform.

**Conclusion:** Ethical help combines compassion with humility: assist without domination, disagree without hatred, and respect another’s responsible freedom.

**Why this earns marks:** It interprets service, tolerance and autonomy while qualifying non-condemnation where harm exists.

**Primary route:** Ethics → [Indian Moral Thinkers](../../topics/ethics/indian-moral-thinkers.md) → Selfless service and self-realisation · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Indian Moral Thinkers · Subtopic: Service and moral identity · Proposed slug: `gandhi-self-discovery-service` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Interpret and apply

### Model answer

**Concept:** Gandhi locates identity in responsible relationships. “Losing” oneself means loosening ego, not erasing autonomy.

**Thinker/framework:** Gandhi’s *sarvodaya* links individual moral growth with the welfare of all, especially the last person; his trusteeship treats capacity and wealth as responsibilities.

**Application:** Service challenges inherited assumptions, develops empathy and reveals purpose. Service can become paternalistic or performative. It must listen, build capability, respect consent and preserve legitimate personal duties.

**Conclusion:** Self-realisation is relational: by serving others with humility and justice, one discovers values worth living by and becomes a more integrated self.

**Why this earns marks:** It explains the apparent paradox, applies Gandhian thought and qualifies service against paternalism.

## Q6(c). Socrates on morality based on relative emotional values
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → Reason, emotion and moral objectivity · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official instruction:** What does each of the following quotations mean to you?  
**Official quotation:** “A system of morality which is based on relative emotional values is a mere illusion, a thoroughly vulgar conception which has nothing sound in it and nothing true.” — Socrates. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Western Moral Thinkers · Subtopic: Reason and moral standards · Proposed slug: `socrates-relative-emotional-morality` · Difficulty: High · Demand: Interpret and evaluate

### Model answer

**Concept:** The quotation rejects making anger or group sentiment the test of right. Without reasoned standards, prejudice can masquerade as morality.

**Thinker/framework:** Socratic inquiry seeks coherent definitions through questioning. Kant likewise requires principles capable of universalisation, while Rawls asks whether rules are fair from an impartial standpoint.

**Application:** A crowd’s outrage cannot determine guilt; evidence must. Sympathy cannot justify unequal allocation, nor communal fear collective punishment. Judgment needs facts, consistency, dignity and scrutinable reasons.

Yet empathy reveals suffering, compassion motivates care and indignation detects injustice. Emotions provide information and motivation; reflection, rights and impartiality test their claims.

**Conclusion:** Sound morality is neither emotional relativism nor cold rationalism; it is reason-governed judgment informed, but not ruled, by humane emotion.

**Why this earns marks:** It interprets the critique, applies it to public life and offers a balanced reason–emotion synthesis.

Delay harms development and international credibility; diversion may stall welfare and invite parliamentary scrutiny amid approaching state elections. **(250 words, 20 marks)**

<a id="2020-mains-gs4-q7c"></a>

I would record a quantified appraisal, ring-fence NHS obligations, seek supplementary grants or phased borrowing, release project funds against milestones, and place unavoidable re-appropriation with reasons before competent parliamentary authority. Resignation is premature: it removes an honest officer without correcting policy. It becomes worthy only if ordered to execute a clearly unlawful diversion after written objection and lawful escalation.

**Safeguards:** independent expenditure review, public disclosure, beneficiary-impact assessment, quarterly utilisation reporting, audit trail and restoration schedule for any temporary diversion.

**Conclusion:** Proper utilisation means lawful, transparent maximisation of public value—not spending every allocation mechanically or sacrificing the vulnerable for political urgency.

**Why this earns marks:** It covers stakeholders, competing values, alternatives, consequences, resignation and an implementable safeguarded course.

## Q8. Ethical responsibility in arms exports
**Primary route:** Ethics → [International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/international-ethics.md) → Responsible arms trade · **Secondary route:** Ethics → case-study method · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Case:** The Chairman of Bharat Missiles Ltd is asked to expand indigenous anti-tank guided-missile production for possible export to a friendly foreign country, supporting self-reliance, strategic influence and defence innovation. **(250 words, 20 marks)**

**Official demands:** (a) As an arms exporter of a responsible nation like India, what ethical issues are involved in arms trade? (b) List five ethical factors influencing the decision to sell arms to foreign governments.

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: International Ethics · Subtopic: Arms exports and responsibility · Proposed slug: `responsible-arms-export` · Difficulty: High · Demands: Ethical issues; five decision factors

### Case framework

**Stakeholders:** Indian citizens, forces and workers; BML; importing state and civilians; neighbours; future governments; international community.  
**Values/conflicts:** security and self-reliance versus non-maleficence; employment versus complicity; sovereignty versus human rights; secrecy versus accountability; strategic gain versus regional peace.

<a id="2020-mains-gs4-q8a"></a>
### Q8(a). Ethical issues
**Primary route:** Ethics → [International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/international-ethics.md) → Ethical issues · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

Arms may support self-defence, deterrence and indigenous capability. Yet they can enable aggression, repression, diversion, escalation and civilian harm. Exporters retain foreseeable responsibility beyond legal transfer. Corruption, secrecy, end-use uncertainty and commercial incentives also matter.

<a id="2020-mains-gs4-q8b"></a>
### Q8(b). Five ethical decision factors and action
**Primary route:** Ethics → [International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/international-ethics.md) → Decision criteria · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

1. **Purpose and necessity:** credible defensive need, not aggressive expansion.  
2. **Recipient conduct:** human-rights record, civilian control and conflict behaviour.  
3. **End-use risk:** diversion, re-export, terrorism and monitoring feasibility.  
4. **Consequences:** proportionality, civilian risk and regional arms-race effects.  
5. **Legality and accountability:** treaty obligations, sanctions, transparent approval and anti-bribery safeguards.

I would support export only after an inter-ministerial assessment, authenticated end-user certificate, human-rights and conflict-risk review, calibrated quantity, training in lawful use, inspection rights, no-retransfer clause and suspension mechanism.

**Options/consequences:** unconditional sale maximises revenue but externalises harm; total refusal avoids complicity but may weaken a legitimate partner and domestic capacity; conditional, monitored export best reconciles security and responsibility.

**Safeguards:** parliamentary/competent oversight, serial tracking, periodic verification, corruption audit and post-export review.

**Conclusion:** A responsible nation exports defensive capability only where foreseeable use remains lawful, proportionate and accountable.

**Why this earns marks:** It identifies the ethical field and supplies exactly five operational factors with a justified course.

## Q9. Tribal minor girls in exploitative migrant labour
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Service Delivery and Regulatory Ethics](../../topics/ethics/service-delivery-and-regulatory-ethics.md) → Child protection and inclusive development · **Secondary route:** Ethics → case-study method · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Case:** Rampura is a poor tribal district with subsistence agriculture and ineffective welfare delivery. Contractors take minor girls to neighbouring-state Bt-cotton farms, where hazardous conditions damage their health; compromised NGOs fail to act. As District Collector, you must protect the girls and improve the district economy. **(250 words, 20 marks)**

**Official demands:** Identify the ethical issues. Which specific steps will you initiate (i) to ameliorate the conditions of minor girls and (ii) to improve the overall economic scenario in the district?

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Public-service values · Subtopic: Child labour, trafficking and tribal development · Proposed slug: `tribal-girls-child-labour-development` · Difficulty: High · Demands: Ethical issues; protection; district development

### Case framework

**Stakeholders:** girls and families; tribal community; contractors and farms; NGOs; both states; schools, health, police and labour departments.  
**Values/conflicts:** child safety versus household survival; parental agency versus consent; rescue versus durable livelihood; autonomy versus protection.

<a id="2020-mains-gs4-q9a"></a>
### Q9(a). Ethical issues
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Service Delivery and Regulatory Ethics](../../topics/ethics/service-delivery-and-regulatory-ethics.md) → Ethical issues · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

The case involves child labour and trafficking, hazardous work, gender-tribal exploitation, health and educational deprivation, poverty-coerced consent, profiteering, interstate gaps, welfare failure and NGO capture.

<a id="2020-mains-gs4-q9b"></a>
### Q9(b). Protection and rehabilitation of minor girls
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Service Delivery and Regulatory Ethics](../../topics/ethics/service-delivery-and-regulatory-ethics.md) → Immediate action · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

I would create a verified child register; coordinate rescue with the destination district; provide medical examination, counselling, safe shelter, compensation and school re-entry; prosecute contractors and culpable employers through due process; protect witnesses; audit compromised NGOs; and establish village child-protection committees, helplines, residential schooling and conditional family support.

<a id="2020-mains-gs4-q9c"></a>
### Q9(c). District economic renewal
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Service Delivery and Regulatory Ethics](../../topics/ethics/service-delivery-and-regulatory-ethics.md) → Inclusive development · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

Converge land and irrigation improvement, forest-produce rights and value chains, livestock, food processing, MGNREGA assets, self-help groups, skills linked to real markets, roads and digital access. Use tribal consultation, social audits and outcome dashboards.

**Options/consequences:** raids alone displace trafficking routes; ignoring migration perpetuates harm; rescue plus livelihood convergence addresses both symptoms and causes.

**Safeguards:** no criminalisation of children or destitute parents, culturally competent staff, independent NGO empanelment, interstate tracking and periodic school/health verification.

**Conclusion:** Protection must be immediate, but sustainable freedom requires local capabilities that make exploitative migration unnecessary.

**Why this earns marks:** It separates all three demands while integrating rescue, rehabilitation, enforcement and structural development.

## Q10. Mall collapse, corruption nexus and retaliatory threat
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Corruption and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/corruption-and-administrative-ethics.md) → Integrity under intimidation · **Secondary route:** Ethics → case-study method · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Case:** As an upright Municipal Commissioner, you investigate a mall-roof collapse that killed four labourers, including two minors. Evidence indicates poor material, an illegal basement, encroachment, negligent inspections and an official-builder nexus. The approving predecessor is your friend; colleagues urge delay; the builder, related to a minister, offers a bribe and threatens a false POSH complaint. **(250 words, 20 marks)**

**Official demands:** Discuss the ethical issues. What options are available? Explain your selected course of action.

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Corruption and Administrative Ethics · Subtopic: Public safety, conflict and retaliation · Proposed slug: `mall-collapse-corruption-threat` · Difficulty: High · Demands: Ethical issues; options; selected action

### Case framework

**Stakeholders:** victims and families; public; builder; municipal staff and predecessor; minister; any POSH complainant; Commissioner.  
**Values/conflicts:** justice versus loyalty; impartiality versus influence; urgency versus due process; anti-retaliation versus genuine complaint rights.

<a id="2020-mains-gs4-q10a"></a>
### Q10(a). Ethical issues
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Corruption and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/corruption-and-administrative-ethics.md) → Ethical issues · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

Deaths reveal profit over life, child labour, regulatory capture, bribery, negligence and betrayal of trust. Friendship creates conflict; political pressure threatens law. Weaponising POSH is unethical, but any complaint requires independent examination.

<a id="2020-mains-gs4-q10b"></a>
### Q10(b). Options and consequences
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Corruption and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/corruption-and-administrative-ethics.md) → Options and consequences · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

Hush up or slow the inquiry—personally safer but corrupt and dangerous; recuse entirely—avoids friendship conflict but may enable capture; act alone and publicly accuse—decisive but risks evidence and fairness; preserve evidence and conduct an independent, time-bound inquiry with external oversight—the strongest option though retaliation remains possible.

<a id="2020-mains-gs4-q10c"></a>
### Q10(c). Selected course
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Corruption and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/corruption-and-administrative-ethics.md) → Justified action · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

Secure the site, rescue and compensate victims; stop construction; obtain structural audit; seize records and samples lawfully; disclose friendship; constitute an external multidisciplinary inquiry; refer bribery and threats to vigilance/police; suspend implicated functions where rules permit; hear every accused; and report political interference in writing.

**Safeguards:** protected evidence copies, witness security, transparent milestones, independent POSH committee, media communication limited to verified facts, citywide building audit and inspector rotation.

**Conclusion:** Integrity requires fearless action institutionalised through due process, not personal heroics or prejudgment.

**Why this earns marks:** It protects victims, evidence, impartiality and the integrity of both anti-corruption and POSH processes.

## Q11. APW and development of a backward area
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Governance and International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/governance-and-international-ethics.md) → Corporate responsibility and development · **Secondary route:** Ethics → case-study method · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Case:** Amria Plastic Works creates local employment in backward Noora, trains workers, maintains quality, pays staff during lockdown and supports transport, health, schooling, self-help groups, an ambulance and greening through CSR. It also used electricity beyond authorised capacity before rectification, and some CSR choices were discretionary. **(250 words, 20 marks)**

**Official demands:** Critically analyse APW’s story and state the ethical issues involved. Do you consider APW a role model for development of backward areas? Give reasons.

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Corporate Governance · Subtopic: CSR, compliance and local development · Proposed slug: `apw-backward-area-role-model` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demands: Critical ethical analysis; role-model assessment

### Case framework

**Stakeholders:** workers; Noora residents; women and children; APW; government; electricity users; environment; future enterprises.  
**Values/conflicts:** profit and development; philanthropy and rights; dependence and empowerment; CSR achievement and compliance.

<a id="2020-mains-gs4-q11a"></a>
### Q11(a). Critical analysis and ethical issues
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Governance and International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/governance-and-international-ethics.md) → Corporate ethics · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

APW shows stakeholder orientation through local skills, wage continuity, productive CSR, partnerships and greening. Yet unauthorised electricity use violated fairness. CSR may create paternalism, selective priorities or dependence. A temple is less urgent than sanitation unless community-chosen. Plastics create lifecycle duties.

<a id="2020-mains-gs4-q11b"></a>
### Q11(b). Role-model assessment and action
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Governance and International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/governance-and-international-ethics.md) → Qualified role model · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

APW is a **qualified role model**, not a flawless one. Its embedded local employment, training, wage protection and shared infrastructure are replicable. The model should add full regulatory compliance, worker representation, transparent CSR needs assessment, pollution and plastic-waste responsibility, occupational safety, diversified livelihoods and exit plans transferring institutions to the community.

**Options/consequences:** copying philanthropy alone creates dependence; rejecting the model ignores real welfare gains; adopting its stakeholder strengths with rights-based safeguards makes development durable.

**Safeguards:** third-party environmental and labour audit, public CSR accounts, grievance channel, community committee, measurable outcomes and no substitution of CSR for taxes or state duties.

**Conclusion:** Ethical enterprise earns profit through lawful production while enlarging—not controlling—the host community’s capabilities.

**Why this earns marks:** It credits APW’s achievements, identifies shortcomings and gives a reasoned, qualified verdict.

## Q12. Migrant workers and the care-giving state
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Service Delivery and Regulatory Ethics](../../topics/ethics/service-delivery-and-regulatory-ethics.md) → Humane disaster governance · **Secondary route:** Ethics → case-study method · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Case:** During the COVID-19 lockdown, migrant workers lost livelihoods, food and transport, feared missing the rabi harvest, and often lacked adequate lodging and support while trying to return home. As overseer of the District Disaster Relief Force, you must draw ethical lessons. **(250 words, 20 marks)**

**Official demands:** What ethical issues arose in the migrant crisis? What is an ethical care-giving state? What assistance can civil society render in similar situations?

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Public-service values · Subtopic: Migrant vulnerability and ethics of care · Proposed slug: `migrant-crisis-care-giving-state` · Difficulty: High · Demands: Ethical issues; care-giving state; civil-society assistance

### Case framework

**Stakeholders:** migrants and families; employers; home and destination states; districts; police; farmers; transporters; civil society.  
**Values/conflicts:** health versus livelihood and mobility; urgency versus consultation; citizenship versus portable entitlements; uniform policy versus unequal burdens.

<a id="2020-mains-gs4-q12a"></a>
### Q12(a). Ethical issues
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Service Delivery and Regulatory Ethics](../../topics/ethics/service-delivery-and-regulatory-ethics.md) → Ethical issues · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

The crisis exposed invisible labour, abrupt burdens, hunger, unsafe travel, wage denial, police excess, weak coordination, non-portable welfare and unequal voice. Treating migrants as disease vectors violated dignity and justice.

<a id="2020-mains-gs4-q12b"></a>
### Q12(b). Ethical care-giving state
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Service Delivery and Regulatory Ethics](../../topics/ethics/service-delivery-and-regulatory-ethics.md) → Ethics of care · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

It anticipates dependency and unequal vulnerability, listens before acting, guarantees minimum food, shelter, health, wages, information and safe mobility, and coordinates portable entitlements across jurisdictions. Care complements rights; it is not discretionary charity. Restrictions must be lawful, proportionate, time-bound and least harmful.

<a id="2020-mains-gs4-q12c"></a>
### Q12(c). Civil-society assistance and safeguards
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Service Delivery and Regulatory Ethics](../../topics/ethics/service-delivery-and-regulatory-ethics.md) → Civil-society role · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

Civil society can map stranded workers; run verified food, shelter, helplines and legal aid; arrange transport with authorities; translate information; monitor abuse; connect jobs and benefits; support women, children and disabled migrants; and document gaps for accountability.

**Options/consequences:** coercive containment protects health briefly but drives desperation; uncontrolled movement spreads risk; planned transport, testing, quarantine and support reconcile both.

**Safeguards:** government coordination, volunteer safety, data privacy, audited donations, non-discrimination, grievance redress, employer accountability and migrant representation in disaster planning.

**Conclusion:** A caring state makes the vulnerable visible and secures their agency; civil society extends reach but cannot replace public responsibility.

**Why this earns marks:** It answers all three demands through rights, care, operational assistance and risk safeguards.
