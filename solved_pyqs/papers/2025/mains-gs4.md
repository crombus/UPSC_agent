## Q1(a). Ethical dilemmas of social media
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Emerging Ethics—AI](../../topics/ethics/emerging-ethics-ai.md) → Social-media communication, autonomy and accountability · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** In the present digital age, social media has revolutionised our way of communication and interaction. However, it has raised several ethical issues and challenges. Describe the key ethical dilemmas in this regard. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Emerging Ethics—Technology · Subtopic: Social-media ethics · Proposed slug: `social-media-ethical-dilemmas` · Demand: Describe

### Model answer

Social media democratises speech and association, yet its business model can convert attention, identity and emotion into commodities.

Key dilemmas are: **expression versus harm**, where open debate coexists with hate, harassment and incitement; **privacy versus personalisation**, because nominal consent may conceal pervasive profiling; **connection versus manipulation**, through addictive design and outrage amplification; **speed versus truth**, as misinformation outruns correction; and **anonymity versus accountability**, since anonymity protects dissent but can shield abuse. Algorithmic opacity also creates discrimination and echo chambers, while deepfakes weaken epistemic trust. Children and digitally vulnerable users bear disproportionate risks.

*K.S. Puttaswamy* anchors informational privacy, while *Shreya Singhal* warns against vague restrictions on lawful speech. Ethical governance therefore needs lawful and proportionate moderation, transparent recommender systems, data minimisation, child-safety design, due process and media literacy.

**Conclusion:** The goal is neither unregulated virality nor censorship, but rights-respecting and contestable digital public space.

**Why this earns marks:** It frames paired dilemmas, supplies constitutional anchors and avoids treating regulation as the only solution.

## Q1(b). Constitutional morality in public administration
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Governance and International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/governance-and-international-ethics.md) → Constitutional morality, good governance and accountability · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** “Constitutional morality is not a natural sentiment but a product of civil education and adherence of the rule of law.” Examine the significance of constitutional morality for public servant highlighting the role in promoting good governance and ensuring accountability in public administration. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Probity and Governance · Subtopic: Constitutional morality · Proposed slug: `constitutional-morality-public-service` · Demand: Examine

### Model answer

Constitutional morality is fidelity not merely to governmental power but to constitutional values, procedures and restraints. Ambedkar’s warning that it must be cultivated explains why office-holders need civic education and institutional discipline.

For a public servant it requires legality, equality, dignity, fraternity, reasoned discretion and loyalty to the Constitution over partisan or majoritarian preference. It promotes good governance by making decisions inclusive, predictable and rights-sensitive: a welfare benefit, for example, must follow objective eligibility rather than social prejudice. It ensures accountability through recorded reasons, disclosure, hearing, audit and judicial or legislative review. The Supreme Court’s constitutional-morality reasoning in *Navtej Singh Johar* illustrates protection of individual dignity against popular morality.

However, the idea must not become an official’s subjective licence to override text, law or democratic institutions.

**Conclusion:** Constitutional morality converts administrative power into a public trust—principled, reasoned and reviewable.

**Why this earns marks:** It defines the concept, operationalises it and adds a rule-of-law qualification.

## Q2(a). Clausewitz, war and contemporary geopolitics
**Primary route:** Ethics → [International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/international-ethics.md) → War, politics and ethical restraint · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** Carl von Clausewitz once said, “War is a diplomacy by other means.” Critically analyse the above statement in the present context of contemporary geo-political conflict. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: International Ethics · Subtopic: War, political purpose and restraint · Proposed slug: `clausewitz-war-politics-ethics` · Demand: Critically analyse

### Model answer

Clausewitz’s actual proposition is commonly translated as war being the continuation of **policy/politics** by other means. It explains that force normally serves political objectives rather than existing independently.

Contemporary conflicts confirm this through coercive bargaining, proxy warfare, sanctions, cyber operations and limited territorial aims. Yet the maxim can become dangerous if read as moral permission. Nuclear escalation, autonomous weapons, civilian interdependence and disinformation make war’s effects exceed calculable political control. Just-war ethics therefore subjects even a political purpose to just cause, legitimate authority, last resort, proportionality and discrimination. The UN Charter’s prohibition on force, with narrow exceptions, similarly rejects war as ordinary diplomacy.

Conversely, diplomacy without credible deterrence may fail against aggression. Force may sometimes protect sovereignty or civilians, but must remain bounded by humanitarian law and a feasible peace objective.

**Conclusion:** Politics explains war’s purpose; ethics and law determine whether its initiation and conduct are defensible.

**Why this earns marks:** It corrects the attribution, tests the claim both ways and supplies a qualified verdict.

## Q2(b). Environmental clearance in sensitive border areas
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Environmental and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/environmental-and-administrative-ethics.md) → Security, ecology and procedural justice · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** Keeping the national security in mind, examine the ethical dilemmas related to controversies over environmental clearance of development projects in ecologically sensitive border areas in the country. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Environmental Ethics · Subtopic: Border infrastructure and clearance · Proposed slug: `border-project-environmental-clearance` · Demand: Examine

### Model answer

Border roads, tunnels and energy projects can enable defence, disaster response and local livelihoods. Yet fragile mountains, forests and rivers may suffer irreversible and cumulative damage.

The dilemmas are **security urgency versus precaution**, national benefit versus locally concentrated ecological and tribal costs, necessary confidentiality versus public participation, and present mobility versus intergenerational justice. Fragmented project approvals may hide cumulative carrying-capacity effects; delay, however, can expose citizens and forces to real danger.

The precautionary principle and sustainable development recognised in *Vellore Citizens’ Welfare Forum* require evidence-based mitigation, not automatic prohibition. A defensible process uses strategic/cumulative assessment, disaster and climate-risk appraisal, least-damage alignment, compensatory restoration, local consultation, independent expert review and post-clearance monitoring. Sensitive security details may be protected while reasons, impacts and safeguards remain publicly accountable.

**Conclusion:** National security is a weighty public good, but durable security includes ecological stability and border-community trust.

**Why this earns marks:** It avoids both clearance-by-assertion and ecological absolutism.

## Q3(a). Thiruvalluvar on equanimity
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Indian Moral Thinkers](../../topics/ethics/indian-moral-thinkers.md) → Equanimity, resilience and practical wisdom · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** “Those who in trouble untroubled are, Will trouble trouble itself.” — Thiruvalluvar. What does this quotation convey to you in the present context? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Indian Moral Thinkers · Subtopic: Equanimity under adversity · Proposed slug: `thiruvalluvar-equanimity-adversity` · Demand: Interpret and apply

### Model answer

The couplet conveys **equanimity**: adversity loses part of its power when it cannot capture judgment. Calmness is not passivity; it preserves agency, emotional regulation and practical wisdom.

For a civil servant during a riot, epidemic or disaster, panic can spread rumours, distort priorities and produce arbitrary action. A composed officer verifies facts, communicates honestly, protects the vulnerable and adapts plans. This resembles the *Bhagavad Gita’s* steadiness of intellect and modern emotional intelligence: recognise emotion without allowing it to dictate conduct.

Equanimity also has a social effect. Courage and transparent leadership stabilise teams and citizens, thereby “troubling trouble itself.” Yet stoicism must not become emotional suppression, indifference to victims or refusal to seek help.

**Conclusion:** Ethical resilience combines a steady mind with compassionate and timely action.

**Why this earns marks:** It translates the quotation into administrative conduct and preserves the distinction between composure and apathy.

## Q3(b). William James on attitude and agency
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → Attitude, agency and structural constraint · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** “The greatest discovery of my generation is that a human being can alter his life by altering his attitudes.” — William James. What does this quotation convey to you in the present context? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Attitude · Subtopic: Attitude-mediated agency · Proposed slug: `william-james-attitude-agency` · Demand: Interpret and apply

### Model answer

The statement stresses that attitude shapes perception, motivation and response. Although it cannot erase external facts, it can convert a setback from proof of helplessness into information for action.

William James’s pragmatism values beliefs partly through their consequences in lived conduct. A growth-oriented civil servant treats failed implementation as feedback, seeks citizen input and redesigns delivery; a prejudiced attitude, by contrast, can turn lawful discretion into exclusion. Gandhi’s transformation of humiliation into disciplined satyagraha similarly shows attitude generating moral agency.

However, “change your attitude” can become victim-blaming when poverty, discrimination, disability or institutional injustice constrain choice. Ethical administration must therefore combine individual resilience with structural reform, reasonable accommodation and fair opportunity.

**Conclusion:** Attitude is neither magic nor trivial: it is the internal lever through which persons use available freedom, while justice expands that freedom.

**Why this earns marks:** It explains mechanism, gives application and rejects motivational reductionism.

## Q3(c). Vivekananda on morality and social strength
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Indian Moral Thinkers](../../topics/ethics/indian-moral-thinkers.md) → Character, law and social trust · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** “The strength of a society is not in its laws, but in the morality of its people.” — Swami Vivekananda. What does this quotation convey to you in the present context? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Indian Moral Thinkers · Subtopic: Character and social trust · Proposed slug: `vivekananda-morality-social-strength` · Demand: Interpret and apply

### Model answer

Law supplies minimum enforceable conduct; morality supplies voluntary restraint, compassion and trust beyond surveillance. A society in which citizens evade taxes, spread hatred or normalise bribery cannot be made strong by multiplying rules.

Vivekananda’s emphasis on character-building therefore points to civic virtue. During a disaster, spontaneous assistance, truthful communication and respect for queues reduce the state’s enforcement burden. In administration, an officer who discloses a conflict before detection protects institutional legitimacy. Social capital makes cooperation cheaper and democracy more resilient.

Yet morality without law may reproduce dominant prejudice, and “public morality” has often excluded minorities. Constitutional rights and institutions must set the floor, while ethical education, exemplars and fair systems cultivate internal commitment.

**Conclusion:** Strong societies join just laws with citizens capable of doing right even when coercion is absent.

**Why this earns marks:** It relates morality to trust while qualifying it through constitutionalism.

## Q4(a). Reason and critical thinking in welfare delivery
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Service Delivery and Regulatory Ethics](../../topics/ethics/service-delivery-and-regulatory-ethics.md) → Evidence, empathy and ethical reasoning · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** “For any kind of social re-engineering by successfully implementing welfare schemes, a civil servant must use reason and critical thinking in an ethical framework.” Justify this statement with suitable examples. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Civil-Service Aptitude · Subtopic: Critical reasoning in welfare administration · Proposed slug: `critical-thinking-welfare-implementation` · Demand: Justify

### Model answer

Welfare administration changes incentives, access and social relations; good intention alone cannot predict these effects. **Reason** tests means against ends, while **critical thinking** questions assumptions, evidence and unintended exclusion. Ethics supplies non-negotiable values—dignity, equality, legality and participation.

An officer should diagnose the real deprivation, disaggregate data, consult users, test causal assumptions, pilot alternatives and create feedback and appeal. Aadhaar-enabled delivery, for example, may reduce duplicates but authentication failure requires an offline exception so efficiency does not defeat entitlement. In nutrition programmes, attendance or expenditure is an output; reduced malnutrition is the outcome, requiring health and sanitation convergence.

Pure technocracy can ignore lived experience, while compassion without analysis can waste resources. Sen’s capability approach supports judging whether people gain substantive freedom, not merely formal coverage.

**Conclusion:** Ethical reasoning makes welfare intelligent; critical thinking makes benevolence effective and correctable.

**Why this earns marks:** It links concept, application, evidence and qualification.

## Q4(b). Mahavir’s teachings and contemporary relevance
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Indian Moral Thinkers](../../topics/ethics/indian-moral-thinkers.md) → Jain ethics and public life · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** What are the major teachings of Mahavir? Explain their relevance in the contemporary world. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Indian Moral Thinkers · Subtopic: Mahavir and Jain ethics · Proposed slug: `mahavir-teachings-contemporary-relevance` · Demand: Explain

### Model answer

Mahavir’s ethical path joins the **three jewels**—right faith, right knowledge and right conduct—with the great vows: **ahimsa** (non-violence), **satya**, **asteya**, **brahmacharya** and **aparigraha** (non-possession). *Anekantavada* recognises many-sided reality, while *syadvada* encourages qualified rather than dogmatic judgment.

Their relevance is direct. Ahimsa supports peace, animal welfare and ecological restraint; aparigraha challenges consumerism and unsustainable extraction; asteya condemns corruption and appropriation of public resources; and anekantavada enables democratic dialogue across polarised identities. In administration, these translate into minimum coercion, truthful reasons, conflict-of-interest discipline and respectful consultation.

However, pluralism does not imply that truth or injustice is merely relative, and non-violence does not bar proportionate lawful protection of the vulnerable.

**Conclusion:** Mahavir offers disciplined self-restraint and intellectual humility—virtues essential to a violent, acquisitive and polarised age.

**Why this earns marks:** It names the doctrines, applies each and adds defensible limits.

## Q5(a). Duty, responsibility and fulfilment
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Emotional Intelligence and Ethical Dilemmas](../../topics/ethics/emotional-intelligence-and-ethical-dilemmas.md) → Duty, vocation and role morality · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** “One who is devoted to one’s duty attains highest perfection in life.” Analyse this statement with reference to sense of responsibility and personal fulfilment as a civil servant. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Public-Service Values · Subtopic: Duty and personal fulfilment · Proposed slug: `duty-responsibility-civil-service-fulfilment` · Demand: Analyse

### Model answer

Devotion to duty means conscientious service to the legitimate purpose of office, not blind obedience. The *Bhagavad Gita’s* idea of *nishkama karma* links excellence to action without selfish attachment to reward.

For a civil servant, responsibility entails competence, timeliness, impartiality, ownership of consequences and answerability. A district officer who remains accessible, records reasons and corrects an exclusion error experiences fulfilment because skill and moral purpose converge. Such intrinsic motivation sustains effort when recognition is absent and builds public trust.

Yet duty can be distorted into workaholism, rule fetishism or loyalty to an unlawful superior. Weberian role duty must remain bounded by constitutional morality, compassion and conscience; family and personal health also impose legitimate obligations.

**Conclusion:** Perfection lies not in ceaseless compliance but in responsible, competent and constitutionally faithful service that integrates vocation with character.

**Why this earns marks:** It analyses the claim without romanticising sacrifice or obedience.

## Q5(b). Civil servant as enabler and facilitator
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Service Delivery and Regulatory Ethics](../../topics/ethics/service-delivery-and-regulatory-ethics.md) → Facilitative administration and holistic development · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** To achieve holistic development goal, a civil servant acts as an enabler and active facilitator of growth rather than a regulator. What specific measures will you suggest to achieve this goal? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Civil-Service Aptitude · Subtopic: Enabling administration · Proposed slug: `civil-servant-enabler-facilitator` · Demand: Suggest measures

### Model answer

An enabling state removes unjust barriers, builds capabilities and coordinates collective action; it does not abandon regulation.

Measures include: single-window, time-bound and digitally trackable approvals; process simplification and risk-based inspection; open standards and predictable rules; infrastructure, credit and skill convergence for MSMEs and farmers; decentralised planning with Panchayats and urban bodies; co-production with SHGs, cooperatives and civil society; public consultation and regulatory-impact assessment; interoperable data with privacy safeguards; grievance redress and outcome dashboards; and targeted handholding for women, SC/ST communities, persons with disabilities and lagging regions.

The Aspirational Districts approach illustrates convergence and measurable outcomes, though rankings must not invite data gaming. Ease of doing business must coexist with labour, consumer and environmental protection.

**Conclusion:** The civil servant should shift from permission-giver to capability-builder while remaining an impartial guardian of public interest.

**Why this earns marks:** It gives executable measures and qualifies facilitation with essential regulation.

## Q6(a). Value-based and compliance-based work culture
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Work Culture and Public Funds](../../topics/ethics/work-culture-and-public-funds.md) → Codes, culture and institutional reinforcement · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** It is said that for an ethical work culture, there must be code of ethics in place in every organisation. To ensure value-based and compliance-based work culture, what suitable measures would you adopt in your work place? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Codes and Work Culture · Subtopic: Values plus compliance · Proposed slug: `ethical-work-culture-values-compliance` · Demand: Recommend measures

### Model answer

A code of ethics states aspirational values; compliance systems translate minimum duties into enforceable conduct. Either alone is insufficient: values without controls may become rhetoric, while rules without internal commitment invite box-ticking.

I would co-create a concise code linked to realistic dilemmas; require leaders to model it; discuss cases during induction and periodic training; institute conflict-of-interest disclosure, gift and recusal rules; protect whistle-blowers; provide confidential ethics advice; standardise procurement and file reasons; rotate sensitive posts; and use fair, time-bound disciplinary procedures. Performance appraisal should reward teamwork, citizen service and speaking up—not only numerical targets. Climate surveys, grievance data, audit findings and exit interviews should trigger review.

The 2nd ARC’s public-service values framework supports combining ethical guidance with enforceable standards.

**Conclusion:** Culture changes when ethical conduct is understood, exemplified, enabled, detected and fairly enforced.

**Why this earns marks:** It integrates soft norms, hard controls and measurement.

## Q6(b). Accountability in utilisation of public funds
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Work Culture and Public Funds](../../topics/ethics/work-culture-and-public-funds.md) → Leakage prevention and outcome accountability · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** India is an emerging economic power of the world as it has recently secured the status of fourth largest economy of the world as per IMF projection. However, it has been observed that in some sectors allocated funds are either under-utilised or misutilised. What specific measures would you recommend for ensuring accountability in this regard to stop leakages and gaining the status of third largest economy of the world in near future? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Utilisation of Public Funds · Subtopic: Financial and outcome accountability · Proposed slug: `public-funds-leakage-accountability` · Demand: Recommend measures

### Model answer

Economic size is ethically meaningful only when public money creates equitable public value. Under-utilisation often reflects weak capacity and delayed approvals; misutilisation reflects poor controls, opacity or corruption.

Measures should cover the full cycle: realistic, outcome-linked budgeting; timely release through PFMS; Treasury Single Account principles where appropriate; e-procurement and GeM; segregation of sanction, execution and payment; geo-tagged works and public dashboards; direct benefit transfer with offline grievance alternatives; concurrent risk-based internal audit; CAG and legislative scrutiny; social audit and community verification; empowered vigilance and whistle-blower protection; recovery and proportionate sanction; and mandatory corrective-action tracking. Unspent funds should trigger diagnosis and capacity support, not indiscriminate year-end spending.

Goodhart’s law warns that expenditure percentage alone can reward waste; outputs, quality, inclusion and outcomes must also be measured.

**Conclusion:** Accountability means traceable money, identifiable responsibility, verifiable results and timely correction.

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes leakage, low capacity and outcome failure.

## Q7. Vijay: disaster command and his mother’s last rites
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Conflict Administration](../../topics/ethics/conflict-administration.md) → Public duty, family duty and continuity of command · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

His seriously ill mother in Kerala dies. His only close relative is an elder sister living in the United States. As heavy rain resumes and the district situation deteriorates, he receives repeated requests to return home for the last rites.

**Official demands:** **(a)** What options are available? **(b)** What ethical dilemmas does he face? **(c)** Critically evaluate each option. **(d)** Which option is most appropriate and why? **(Answer in 250 words) 20**

### Q7(b). Stakeholders, values and conflicts

**Stakeholders:** victims and families; rescue personnel; district/state administration; Vijay and his family; citizens awaiting reassurance.

<a id="2025-mains-gs4-q7c"></a>
### Q7(c). Evaluation of options
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Conflict Administration](../../topics/ethics/conflict-administration.md) → Q7(c). Evaluation of options · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** Proposed slug: `vijay-disaster-duty-family-evaluation` · Demand: Critically evaluate

Immediate departure respects family but may create a leadership vacuum during escalating danger. Staying indefinitely maximises continuity but treats legitimate grief as ethically irrelevant and risks impaired judgment. A brief visit honours both duties, but travel uncertainty and renewed rain may prevent return. Remote rites plus later mourning preserves command and is safest, though personally painful.

<a id="2025-mains-gs4-q7d"></a>
### Q7(d). Recommended action and safeguards
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Conflict Administration](../../topics/ethics/conflict-administration.md) → Q7(d). Recommended action and safeguards · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** Proposed slug: `vijay-disaster-duty-family-decision` · Demand: Decide and justify

I would remain through the renewed acute phase, speak with family, arrange lawful performance/remote participation in the rites and take brief compassionate leave once command is stable. Before any absence: designate an incident commander, publish delegation, hold twice-daily reviews, secure redundant communications, and brief the state relief authority.

**Qualification:** If a competent command structure is demonstrably functioning and safe travel permits a short visit, limited leave becomes defensible. Public duty is not a demand for permanent self-erasure.

**Why this earns marks:** It treats both obligations seriously and makes the decision depend on continuity rather than heroic symbolism.

## Q8. Housing for the homeless through forest clearance
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Environmental and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/environmental-and-administrative-ethics.md) → Human dignity, ecological integrity and just alternatives · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Verified case stem:** A district administration proposes clearing ecologically sensitive forest land for housing the homeless and economically weaker sections. The forest contains old trees, medicinal plants and biodiversity; regulates microclimate and rainfall; prevents erosion; and supports tribal and nomadic livelihoods. The administration invokes welfare, human rights, wildlife conflict and law-and-order concerns.

**Official demands:** **(a)** Can deforestation be ethically justified for social-welfare objectives such as housing? **(b)** What socio-economic, administrative and ethical challenges arise in balancing conservation and development? **(c)** What substantial alternatives or policy interventions can protect both environmental integrity and human dignity? **(Answer in 250 words) 20**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Environmental Ethics · Subtopic: Housing versus sensitive forest · Proposed slug: `housing-forest-environmental-justice` · Demand: Justification + challenges + alternatives

<a id="2025-mains-gs4-q8a"></a>
### Q8(a). Ethical justification
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Environmental and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/environmental-and-administrative-ethics.md) → Q8(a). Ethical justification · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** Proposed slug: `housing-forest-ethical-justification` · Demand: Decide conditionally

Housing advances dignity and Directive Principles, but it does not automatically justify irreversible ecological and livelihood loss. Under intergenerational, recognition and distributive justice, clearance is defensible only as a lawful last resort after alternatives, cumulative impact, consent/rights and least-damage design are tested. The public-trust and precautionary principles weigh heavily against this sensitive site.

<a id="2025-mains-gs4-q8b"></a>
### Q8(b). Stakeholders and challenges
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Environmental and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/environmental-and-administrative-ethics.md) → Q8(b). Stakeholders and challenges · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** Proposed slug: `housing-forest-balancing-challenges` · Demand: Analyse challenges

**Stakeholders:** homeless households; tribal/nomadic communities; future generations; wildlife; local residents; administration; taxpayers.

Challenges include land scarcity and affordability; concentrated displacement versus dispersed benefits; insecure forest/community rights; undervaluation of ecosystem services; department silos; weak impact data; political urgency; manufactured security claims; human-wildlife conflict; and the false framing that one vulnerable group must lose for another to gain.

<a id="2025-mains-gs4-q8c"></a>
### Q8(c). Alternatives, action and safeguards
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Environmental and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/environmental-and-administrative-ethics.md) → Q8(c). Alternatives, action and safeguards · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** Proposed slug: `housing-forest-policy-alternatives` · Demand: Propose interventions

First map vacant public land, brownfields, serviced plots, in-situ slum upgrading, rental housing and vertical redevelopment near livelihoods. Use land pooling and inclusionary zoning; repair vacant housing; and choose degraded, low-biodiversity sites through transparent multi-criteria assessment. If residual diversion is unavoidable, minimise footprint, recognise Forest Rights Act claims, consult Gram Sabhas where applicable, create wildlife corridors, restore equivalent habitat and institute independent monitoring.

**Decision:** Pause this proposal for a time-bound alternatives and carrying-capacity assessment. Law-and-order allegations require evidence and targeted policing, not ecological clearance.

**Official demands:** **(a)** What options are available? **(b)** What ethical and legal dilemmas arise? **(c)** Which option is more appropriate and why? **(d)** What extra precautions should Border Guarding Police take with the uniformed soldiers? **(Answer in 250 words) 20**

### Q12(b). Stakeholders, values and dilemmas
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Security Ethics](../../topics/ethics/security-ethics.md) → Q12(b). Stakeholders, values and dilemmas · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Stakeholders:** injured civilians and children; local communities; border police and armed forces; Ashok; state/national authorities; genuine soldiers, infiltrators and victims across the border.

The dilemmas are life and non-refoulement concerns versus sovereignty and security; compassion versus infiltration risk; emergency discretion versus competence; child protection versus family separation; and temporary shelter burdens on local communities. Create a secured humanitarian reception zone. Admit the gravely injured for triage without discrimination; register and screen entrants; prioritise women, children and family unity; notify state/central control through every available channel; and document the emergency basis. This prevents immediate irreversible harm while preserving later lawful status decisions.

### Q12(d). Precautions for uniformed soldiers
