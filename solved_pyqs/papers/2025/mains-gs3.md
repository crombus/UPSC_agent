## Q1. HDI, IHDI and inclusive growth
**Primary route:** Economy → [Growth, Development, HDI, IHDI and MPI](../../topics/economy/growth-development-hdi-ihdi-and-mpi.md) → HDI and IHDI · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2025 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 1  
**Official question:** Distinguish between the Human Development Index (HDI) and the Inequality-adjusted Human Development Index (IHDI) with special reference to India. Why is the IHDI considered a better indicator of inclusive growth?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Growth and Development → HDI and IHDI  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/hdi-ihdi-inclusive-growth`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Distinguish + Justify  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/02_Growth-Development-HDI-IHDI-and-MPI.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/02_Growth-Development-HDI-IHDI-and-MPI.md)

### Demand decoding

Compare construction and interpretation, apply the distinction to India, and justify IHDI without claiming that it measures every aspect of inclusion.

### Compact framework

`same three dimensions → average achievement versus inequality discount → Indian disparities → superiority and limits`

### Model answer

Both UNDP indices cover **health** (life expectancy), **education** and **standard of living** (GNI per capita). However, HDI combines a country’s average achievements, whereas IHDI adjusts each dimension for its distribution across the population.

| HDI | IHDI |
|---|---|
| Measures potential human development at the average | Measures actual achievement after inequality loss |
| Can rise despite unequal gains | Falls below HDI as inequality increases |
| Equals IHDI only under perfect equality | Gap from HDI indicates loss due to inequality |

For India, national averages can conceal inter-state, rural-urban, gender and social-group gaps. IHDI tests whether gains are broadly shared, making it closer to **inclusive growth**, which requires expanding capabilities and equitable access.

Nevertheless, IHDI does not fully reveal service quality, intra-state deprivation, employment security or ecological sustainability. It should be read with disaggregated data and MPI.

### Examiner rationale

The answer makes the exact conceptual distinction, applies it to Indian inequality and gives a qualified justification rather than treating IHDI as a complete inclusion index.

## Q2. Protectionism, bilateralism and the Indian economy
**Primary route:** Economy → [Foreign Trade, WTO, FTAs and Protectionism](../../topics/economy/foreign-trade-wto-ftas-and-protectionism.md) → WTO, FTAs and Protectionism · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2025 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 2  
**Official question:** What are the challenges before the Indian economy when the world is moving away from free trade and multilateralism to protectionism and bilateralism? How can these challenges be met?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → External Sector → WTO, FTAs and Protectionism  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/protectionism-bilateralism-india`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Identify + Suggest  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/20_Foreign-Trade-WTO-FTAs-and-Protectionism.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/20_Foreign-Trade-WTO-FTAs-and-Protectionism.md)

### Demand decoding

Link the global shift to concrete Indian vulnerabilities, then offer external and domestic responses; avoid equating bilateral agreements with automatic market access.

### Compact framework

`market barriers + fragmented rules + supply-chain coercion → diversified diplomacy + domestic competitiveness + adjustment support`

### Model answer

Protectionism replaces predictable, non-discriminatory multilateral rules with tariffs, subsidies, export controls, carbon standards and power-based bilateral bargains.

**Challenges for India**
- weaker export demand and discriminatory market access for labour-intensive goods;
- fragmented rules of origin and standards that raise compliance costs, especially for MSMEs;
- technology and critical-input restrictions;
- subsidised foreign production and trade diversion;
- reduced WTO dispute-settlement effectiveness; and
- pressure to accept unequal bilateral concessions in agriculture, data, investment or intellectual property.

**Response**
India should diversify products and destinations through balanced FTAs, Global South coalitions and WTO reform; build resilient critical supply chains; lower logistics and power costs; improve testing and trade finance; and integrate MSMEs into exports. Skilling and adjustment assistance should protect those facing import competition.

Thus, strategic autonomy requires **competitive openness**, not indiscriminate import substitution: domestic capability must complement, rather than replace, rules-based engagement.

### Examiner rationale

The response connects protectionism to specific transmission channels and balances trade diplomacy with productivity, resilience and social adjustment.

## Q3. Farmers’ choice of high-value crops
**Primary route:** Economy → [APMC, e-NAM, FPOs and Agricultural Supply Chains](../../topics/economy/apmc-e-nam-fpos-and-agricultural-supply-chains.md) → High-Value Crops and Markets · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2025 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 3  
**Official question:** Explain the factors influencing the decision of the farmers on the selection of high value crops in India.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Agriculture → High-Value Crops and Markets  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/high-value-crop-choice`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Explain  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/13_APMC-e-NAM-FPOs-and-Agricultural-Supply-Chains.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/13_APMC-e-NAM-FPOs-and-Agricultural-Supply-Chains.md)

### Demand decoding

Explain the farm household’s risk-return decision across agro-climatic, market, infrastructure and institutional variables.

### Compact framework

`expected net return × production suitability × market assurance × risk-bearing capacity`

### Model answer

High-value crops—fruits, vegetables, flowers and spices—offer greater value per unit of land but higher cost, perishability and price risk. Farmers choose them according to:

- **Agro-climatic suitability:** soil, temperature, water availability, pests and crop duration.
- **Expected net returns:** output price, yield, input, labour, irrigation and transport costs—not gross price alone.
- **Market conditions:** proximity to towns, processors, exporters and assured buyers; price information and contract credibility.
- **Infrastructure:** quality planting material, extension, electricity, roads, grading, pack-houses, cold chain and storage.
- **Farm characteristics:** holding size, family labour, skills, risk appetite and capacity to wait for orchard gestation.
- **Finance and institutions:** credit, insurance, FPO aggregation and predictable regulation.
- **Consumption and social factors:** household food needs and local cultivation experience.

Diversification is durable only when demand is matched by logistics, risk protection and bargaining power; high prices alone can induce boom-bust cycles.

### Examiner rationale

The answer treats crop choice as a risk-adjusted decision and covers both production-side and value-chain determinants.

### Demand decoding

### Compact framework

`inputs → aggregation → grading/storage → transport/processing → wholesale/retail/export → value and resilience`

### Model answer

Agricultural supply-chain management coordinates the physical, financial and information flows from input supply and production to aggregation, grading, storage, transport, processing, wholesale, retail and export.

## Q5. India, ITER and the future of fusion energy
**Primary route:** Science and Technology → [Nuclear Fusion and ITER](../../topics/science-and-technology/nuclear-fusion-and-iter.md) → Fusion and ITER · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2025 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 5  
**Official question:** The fusion energy programme in India has steadily evolved over the past few decades. Mention India's contributions to the international fusion energy project International Thermonuclear Experimental Reactor (ITER). What will be the implications of the success of this project for the future of global energy?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Science and Technology → Nuclear Technology → Fusion and ITER  
**Proposed topic slug:** `science-technology/india-iter-fusion`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Mention + Assess implications  
**Canonical owner:** [`Science-and-Technology/basic/05_Nuclear-Fusion-and-ITER.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Science-and-Technology/basic/05_Nuclear-Fusion-and-ITER.md)

### Demand decoding

Name concrete Indian packages and distinguish ITER’s experimental success from commercial electricity.

### Compact framework

`Indian research base → nine in-kind packages → experimental gain → conditional global-energy implications`

### Model answer

India’s programme evolved through the **ADITYA/ADITYA-U** and superconducting **SST-1** tokamaks of the Institute for Plasma Research (IPR). ITER-India, housed in IPR, is India’s Domestic Agency for ITER.

India supplies nine in-kind packages: the **cryostat**, in-wall shielding, cooling-water and cryogenic systems, ion- and electron-cyclotron radio-frequency heating, diagnostic neutral beam, power supplies and diagnostics.

If ITER demonstrates sustained deuterium-tritium plasma gain—designed at 500 MW fusion power from 50 MW external heating—it would validate fusion physics, advance materials and plasma control, and support a future low-carbon, firm energy option.

ITER does not generate electricity and remains under construction. Commercial fusion still requires tritium breeding, neutron-resistant materials and economic DEMO-class plants. Success is a long-term gateway, not a near-term energy substitute.

### Examiner rationale

The answer names verified Indian contributions and grades the implication from experimental proof to eventual commercial possibility.

## Q7. CCUS and climate change
**Primary route:** Environment and Ecology → [Carbon Markets CCUS and Direct Air Capture](../../topics/environment/carbon-markets-ccus-and-direct-air-capture.md) → CCUS · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2025 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 7  
**Official question:** What is Carbon Capture, Utilization and Storage (CCUS)? What is the potential role of CCUS in tackling climate change?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Environment and Ecology → Climate Mitigation → CCUS  
**Proposed topic slug:** `environment/ccus-climate-mitigation`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Define + Explain potential  
**Canonical owner:** [`Environment-and-Ecology/basic/21_Carbon-Markets-CCUS-and-Direct-Air-Capture.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Environment-and-Ecology/basic/21_Carbon-Markets-CCUS-and-Direct-Air-Capture.md)

### Demand decoding

Explain the capture-to-use/storage chain, identify defensible sectoral roles, and state integrity and lock-in limits.

### Compact framework

`capture → compression/transport → utilisation or geological storage → hard-to-abate role → limits`

### Model answer

CCUS is a chain of technologies that **separates CO₂ at a concentrated emission source**, compresses and transports it, and then either uses it in products/processes or injects it into suitable geological formations for long-term storage. It differs from direct air capture, which removes dilute CO₂ already in the atmosphere.

Its principal climate role is in **hard-to-abate sectors** such as cement, steel and fertiliser, where process emissions may remain after efficiency, electrification and renewable substitution. It can also reduce emissions from selected existing assets and, when combined with sustainably sourced bioenergy and durable storage, potentially generate net removals.

Climate value depends on high capture rates, low lifecycle emissions, secure storage and monitoring. Some utilisation merely delays re-release. Cost, energy penalty, leakage and fossil lock-in constrain deployment.

Hence CCUS should complement direct emission reduction and target residual process emissions—not postpone the clean-energy transition.

### Examiner rationale

The answer accurately distinguishes avoidance from removal and balances sectoral utility with lifecycle and governance limits.

## Q8. Seawater intrusion in coastal aquifers
**Primary route:** Environment and Ecology → [Coastal and Marine Ecology CRZ Blue Economy](../../topics/environment/coastal-and-marine-ecology-crz-blue-economy.md) → Coastal Aquifers · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2025 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 8  
**Official question:** Seawater intrusion in the coastal aquifers is a major concern in India. What are the causes of seawater intrusion and the remedial measures to combat this hazard?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Geography → Water Resources → Coastal Aquifers  
**Proposed topic slug:** `geography/seawater-intrusion-coastal-aquifers`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Causes + Remedies  
**Canonical owner:** [`Environment-and-Ecology/basic/24_Coastal-and-Marine-Ecology-CRZ-Blue-Economy.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Environment-and-Ecology/basic/24_Coastal-and-Marine-Ecology-CRZ-Blue-Economy.md)

### Demand decoding

Lead with the hydraulic mechanism and rank pumping above sea-level rise; organise remedies by demand, recharge and barriers.

### Compact framework

`freshwater-head decline → landward/upward saline movement → regulate + recharge + barrier/monitor`

### Model answer

Seawater intrusion is the landward or upward movement of saline water into a coastal freshwater aquifer when the freshwater hydraulic head can no longer repel the sea.

**Causes**
- excessive pumping—the primary driver;
- reduced recharge due to concretisation, loss of wetlands, drought and altered drainage;
- sea-level rise and storm surges increasing saline boundary pressure;
- poorly located/deep wells causing up-coning; and
- tidal canals, creek modification and leakage from saline aquaculture.

**Remedies**
- prepare aquifer budgets, meter and regulate extraction, relocate well fields and shift from water-intensive crops;
- protect recharge zones; use rainwater harvesting and managed aquifer recharge;
- create freshwater injection/recharge barriers or suitable subsurface barriers at critical sites;
- monitor salinity and groundwater levels; and
- control saline aquaculture leakage and integrate land-use planning.

Desalination may provide alternative supply but does not restore the aquifer. Sustainable remedy requires basin/aquifer-scale management that maintains freshwater head.

### Examiner rationale

The answer explains the physical mechanism, correctly prioritises over-extraction and pairs each cause with a workable response.

## Q11. Fiscal Health Index and State fiscal performance
**Primary route:** Economy → [Union Budget, Fiscal Policy and Deficit Indicators](../../topics/economy/union-budget-fiscal-policy-and-deficit-indicators.md) → Fiscal Health Index · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2025 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 11  
**Official question:** Explain how the Fiscal Health Index (FHI) can be used as a tool for assessing the fiscal performance of states in India. In what way would it encourage the states to adopt prudent and sustainable fiscal policies?  
**Marks / limit:** 15 / 250 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Fiscal Policy → Fiscal Health Index  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/fiscal-health-index-states`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Explain + Assess incentive effect  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/09_Union-Budget-Fiscal-Policy-and-Deficit-Indicators.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/09_Union-Budget-Fiscal-Policy-and-Deficit-Indicators.md)

### Demand decoding

Explain what the NITI Aayog index measures, how comparison becomes a policy tool, and why ranking alone cannot ensure prudence.

### Compact framework

`five sub-indices → diagnosis/benchmarking → incentive and policy learning → methodological limits`

### Model answer

NITI Aayog’s **Fiscal Health Index (FHI) 2025**, based on state data for FY2022-23, treats fiscal health as multidimensional rather than reducing it to the fiscal-deficit ratio. It compares major states through five sub-indices:

1. **Quality of Expenditure**—composition and developmental/capital orientation;
2. **Revenue Mobilisation**—own-tax and non-tax capacity;
3. **Fiscal Prudence**—deficits and compliance with sound fiscal management;
4. **Debt Index**—level and burden of liabilities; and
5. **Debt Sustainability**—whether growth and primary balances can stabilise debt.

**Assessment value:** A common framework enables inter-state and time-series comparison, identifies whether weakness lies in low revenue, poor expenditure composition or debt stress, and complements FRBM disclosures. Disaggregated scores can guide State budgets, Finance Commission analysis, borrowing strategy and corrective action better than a single headline rank.

**How it encourages prudence:** Public benchmarking creates reputational incentives and competitive federalism; transparent indicators permit legislatures, CAG, investors and citizens to demand explanations; peer comparison reveals replicable practices; and periodic measurement rewards improvements in own revenue, capex quality and debt management. It can also support early warning before interest burdens crowd out development expenditure.

However, the FHI is diagnostic, not a binding fiscal rule. Different development needs, disaster exposure, inherited debt and accounting quality affect comparability. Rankings may also encourage cosmetic compression or off-budget liabilities. Therefore, the index should be used with audited accounts, contingent-liability disclosure and State-specific fiscal paths.

### Examiner rationale

The answer accurately states all five official sub-indices, explains transmission from measurement to prudence, and qualifies ranking and comparability.

## Q12. Production Linked Incentive scheme
**Primary route:** Economy → [MSMEs, Startups, PLI, Semiconductors and Manufacturing Strategy](../../topics/economy/msmes-pli-semiconductors-and-manufacturing-strategy.md) → Production Linked Incentive · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2025 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 12  
**Official question:** Discuss the rationale of the Production Linked Incentive (PLI) scheme. What are its achievements? In what way can the functioning and outcomes of the scheme be improved?  
**Marks / limit:** 15 / 250 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Industrial Policy → Production Linked Incentive  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/pli-rationale-achievements-reform`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Discuss + Evaluate + Suggest  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/17_MSMEs-PLI-Semiconductors-and-Manufacturing-Strategy.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/17_MSMEs-PLI-Semiconductors-and-Manufacturing-Strategy.md)

Explain output-linked additionality, distinguish verified outcomes from outlay/approvals, and propose reforms around value addition, competition and spillovers.

### Compact framework

`scale and additionality rationale → dated outcomes → design/implementation gaps → outcome-based reform`

### Model answer

PLI schemes provide time-bound incentives on **verified incremental production or sales** over a base year in 14 strategic sectors. Their rationale is to overcome scale and learning-cost disadvantages, attract investment, diversify supply chains, reduce critical import dependence, promote exports and create manufacturing employment. Unlike an unconditional capital subsidy, payment is linked to specified performance.

Explain hazards through mechanisms across the mine lifecycle, then pair them with avoidance, control, restoration and governance measures.

### Model answer
