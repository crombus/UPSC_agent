## Q1. Atmosphere heated more by solar than terrestrial radiation; greenhouse gases
**Primary route:** Geography → [Weather: Insolation, Pressure, Winds, Humidity & Rainfall](../../topics/geography/weather-elements.md) → Atmosphere heated more by solar than terrestrial radiation; greenhouse gases · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

### Official Set-A question (visually verified transcription)

Consider the following statements:
Statement-I  : The atmosphere is heated more by incoming solar radiation than by terrestrial radiation.
Statement-II : Carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases in the atmosphere are good absorbers of long wave radiation.
Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

(a) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct and Statement-II explains Statement-I
(b) Both Statement-I and Statement-II are correct, but Statement-II does not explain Statement-I
(c) Statement-I is correct, but Statement-II is incorrect
(d) Statement-I is incorrect, but Statement-II is correct

**Answer (UPSC official-final Series-A key): (D) — Statement-I is incorrect, but Statement-II is correct**

### Statement/option analysis, elimination logic and trap

Statement-I is incorrect. The atmosphere is heated more by terrestrial radiation (infrared radiation emitted by the Earth's surface) than by incoming solar radiation (shortwave radiation).  While the sun provides the initial energy, the Earth's surface absorbs this energy and re-radiates it as heat in the form of infrared radiation. This infrared radiation is then absorbed by greenhouse gases in the atmosphere, leading to atmospheric warming.  Statement-II is correct.  Carbon dioxide (CO2) and other greenhouse gases (like methane, nitrous oxide, etc.) are effective at absorbing longwave (infrared) radiation emitted by the Earth's surface. This absorption traps heat within the atmosphere, contributing to the greenhouse effect.  Therefore Statement-I is false and Statement-II is true.

Greenhouse gases:  These are gases in the Earth's atmosphere that trap heat.  The main greenhouse gases are carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide, and water vapor.  They absorb infrared radiation, preventing it from escaping into space and causing a warming effect.

Incoming solar radiation: This refers to the electromagnetic radiation emitted by the sun that reaches the Earth's atmosphere.  A portion of this radiation is reflected back into space, while the rest is absorbed by the Earth's surface and atmosphere.

Terrestrial radiation:  This is the infrared radiation emitted by the Earth's surface after absorbing solar radiation.  It is a major driver of atmospheric warming due to its absorption by greenhouse gases.

Nitrogen compounds: Volcanic eruptions release various nitrogen-containing gases, such as ammonia and nitrogen oxides. These gases contribute to the atmospheric nitrogen cycle.

Select the answer using the code given below:

(b) 2 only
(c) Both 1 and 2
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

**Answer (UPSC official-final Series-A key): (A) — 1 only**

### Statement/option analysis, elimination logic and trap

In January, isotherms (lines connecting points of equal temperature) generally deviate northward over oceans and southward over continents. This is because water has a higher specific heat capacity than land, meaning it takes longer to heat up and cool down.  Therefore, ocean temperatures remain relatively warmer in January than land temperatures, causing the isotherms to bend northward over the oceans. Conversely, land cools down more quickly, resulting in southward bending of isotherms over continents.

Statement 2 is incorrect. While cold ocean currents like the Labrador Current do influence the temperature of the North Atlantic, the Gulf Stream and North Atlantic Drift are warm currents that actually *increase* temperatures in the North Atlantic, causing isotherms to bend towards the south, not the north.
