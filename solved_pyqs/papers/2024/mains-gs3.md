## Q1. Public expenditure on social services and inclusive growth
**Primary route:** Economy → [Poverty, Inequality, Social Sector and Inclusive Growth](../../topics/economy/poverty-inequality-social-sector-and-inclusive-growth.md) → Social-sector expenditure · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2024 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 1  
**Official question:** Examine the pattern and trend of public expenditure on social services in the post-reforms period in India. To what extent this has been in consonance with achieving the objective of inclusive growth?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Poverty, Inequality and Social Sector → Public Social Expenditure  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/public-social-expenditure-inclusive-growth`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Examine  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/23_Poverty-Inequality-Social-Sector-and-Inclusive-Growth.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/23_Poverty-Inequality-Social-Sector-and-Inclusive-Growth.md)

### Demand decoding
Trace composition and trend after 1991, then judge inclusion through access, quality and distribution—not expenditure alone.

### Compact framework
`rising but uneven outlay → Centre-State/programme pattern → capability gains → adequacy, quality and equity gaps`

### Model answer
Post-1991 fiscal consolidation initially restrained social spending, but expenditure on education, health, nutrition, housing, water, sanitation and social protection subsequently rose in nominal terms and broadly as a share of GDP, especially after rights-based programmes and the pandemic. States undertake most delivery, while Union spending increasingly combines centrally sponsored schemes, transfers and digital benefit delivery.

This supports inclusive growth by building human capabilities, reducing catastrophic expenditure, improving school enrolment and basic amenities, and protecting vulnerable households through MGNREGA, NFSA, Ayushman Bharat and pensions.

However, consonance is only **partial**. Spending remains below sectoral aspirations; health and education quality vary sharply across States; vacancies, weak local capacity and input-focused budgeting reduce outcomes. Benefits may exclude migrants, informal workers and digitally disadvantaged persons, while regressive subsidies crowd out targeted services.

Thus, the trend is inclusion-oriented, but expenditure must be adequate, progressive and outcome-accountable, with stronger primary services and disaggregated social audits.

### Examiner rationale
The answer examines both the expenditure pattern and its conversion into inclusive capabilities, while qualifying the expenditure-equals-outcome assumption.

## Q2. Persistent food inflation and RBI monetary policy
**Primary route:** Economy → [RBI, Monetary Policy and Liquidity Management](../../topics/economy/rbi-monetary-policy-and-liquidity-management.md) → Food inflation · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2024 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 2  
**Official question:** What are the causes of persistent high food inflation in India? Comment on the effectiveness of the monetary policy of the RBI to control this type of inflation.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Inflation and Price Stability → Food Inflation and Monetary Transmission  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/food-inflation-rbi-monetary-policy`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Explain + Comment  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/03_Inflation-Price-Indices-and-Business-Cycles.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/03_Inflation-Price-Indices-and-Business-Cycles.md)

### Demand decoding
Separate supply, structural and demand causes; then assess what interest rates can and cannot do.

### Compact framework
`weather/supply shocks + structural bottlenecks + demand shift → expectations/second-round effects → limited but necessary monetary role`

### Model answer
Persistent food inflation arises from recurrent weather shocks, heat and erratic monsoons; low productivity and disease in vegetables, pulses and livestock; fragmented markets, storage and cold-chain gaps; high transport/input costs; and policy uncertainty from stock limits or trade restrictions. Rising incomes also shift demand towards protein, fruits and vegetables whose supply is less elastic.

RBI tightening cannot produce onions or repair supply chains. Higher policy rates may even raise working-capital costs. Its direct effect on primary food shocks is therefore weak and lagged.

Yet monetary policy remains relevant: because food has a large CPI weight, repeated shocks can raise wages, inflation expectations and prices of non-food services. By restraining excess demand and anchoring expectations under flexible inflation targeting, RBI prevents such second-round generalisation.

Hence food inflation needs coordinated fiscal-supply action—buffer releases, predictable trade policy, logistics, diversification and climate-resilient agriculture—while RBI contains spillovers rather than the original supply shock.

### Examiner rationale
The response neither dismisses monetary policy nor overstates it; it distinguishes first-round food supply shocks from economy-wide persistence.

## Q3. Why land reforms succeeded in some regions
**Primary route:** Economy → [Land Reforms, Green Revolution and Cropping Systems](../../topics/economy/land-reforms-green-revolution-and-cropping-systems.md) → Land reforms · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2024 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 3  
**Official question:** What were the factors responsible for the successful implementation of land reforms in some parts of the country? Elaborate.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Land Reforms → Regional Implementation  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/land-reforms-regional-success`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Elaborate  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/11_Land-Reforms-Green-Revolution-and-Cropping-Systems.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/11_Land-Reforms-Green-Revolution-and-Cropping-Systems.md)

### Demand decoding
Explain variation in implementation, using reform instruments and regional examples rather than merely listing laws.

### Compact framework
`political coalition + clear law/records + mobilisation + capable administration + complementary support`

### Model answer
Land reform covered abolition of intermediaries, tenancy security, ceilings and redistribution, and consolidation. Its success depended less on statutory enactment than on political and administrative conditions.

- **Political commitment:** Kerala and West Bengal had durable pro-tenant coalitions; early Jammu and Kashmir reform also imposed meaningful ceilings.
- **Peasant mobilisation:** organised beneficiaries reported concealed tenancy and resisted landlord evasion. West Bengal’s Operation Barga recorded sharecroppers and secured crop shares.
- **Clear laws and fewer loopholes:** effective ceilings, restricted exemptions and enforceable tenancy rights reduced benami transfers and resumptions.
- **Land records and field administration:** surveys, updated records, local camps and time-bound adjudication made rights executable.
- **Complementary institutions:** panchayats, legal aid, credit, irrigation and extension enabled redistributed land to remain productive.

Elsewhere, landlord influence, poor records, litigation, exemptions and weak beneficiary support frustrated reform. Therefore, successful land reform was a state-capacity and power-redistribution project, not simply a legislative exercise.

### Examiner rationale
The answer explains causal conditions through named regional experience and distinguishes law on paper from implementation.

## Q4. Millets and health and nutritional security
**Primary route:** Economy → [MSP, Procurement, Buffer Stocks, PDS and Food Security](../../topics/economy/msp-procurement-buffer-stocks-pds-and-food-security.md) → Nutri-cereals · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2024 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 4  
**Official question:** Explain the role of millets for ensuring health and nutritional security in India.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Food and Nutritional Security → Millets  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/millets-health-nutrition-security`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Easy-Moderate · Explain  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/12_MSP-Procurement-Buffer-Stocks-PDS-and-Food-Security.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/12_MSP-Procurement-Buffer-Stocks-PDS-and-Food-Security.md)

### Demand decoding
Connect nutritional qualities and climate-resilient production to access, diets and implementation constraints.

### Compact framework
`nutrient density + dietary diversity + resilient local supply → public programmes/markets → processing and access constraints`

### Model answer
Millets such as jowar, bajra and ragi are “nutri-cereals”: they provide fibre, complex carbohydrates and varying amounts of iron, calcium and other micronutrients. Their inclusion diversifies cereal-heavy diets, can improve satiety and glycaemic quality, and helps address micronutrient deficiencies; however, no single millet is nutritionally complete.

They also strengthen **availability and stability**. Many millets tolerate dry conditions, short growing seasons and relatively low external inputs, supporting smallholders in rain-fed regions and reducing climate-related supply risk.

Nutrition gains require access and consumption. Decentralised procurement and inclusion in the PDS, ICDS and PM-POSHAN can connect local production to vulnerable groups. Processing, ready-to-cook products and awareness can reduce drudgery and revive demand.

Constraints include low yields in some areas, weak procurement, dehulling and storage needs, higher consumer prices and displacement by rice-wheat incentives. A millet strategy must therefore combine research, local processing, diversified procurement and culturally acceptable menus.

### Examiner rationale
The answer links nutrients, production resilience and delivery systems while avoiding the claim that millets alone eliminate malnutrition.

## Q5. IPR in life materials and low patent commercialisation
**Primary route:** Science and Technology → [Intellectual Property Rights and Patents](../../topics/science-and-technology/intellectual-property-rights-and-patents.md) → Biotechnology patents · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2024 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 5  
**Official question:** What is the present world scenario of intellectual property rights with respect to life materials? Although, India is second in the world to file patents, still only a few have been commercialized. Explain the reasons behind this less commercialization.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Science and Technology → IPR and Patents → Life Materials and Technology Transfer  
**Proposed topic slug:** `science-technology/life-materials-patents-commercialisation`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Describe + Explain  
**Canonical owner:** [`Science-and-Technology/basic/17_Intellectual-Property-Rights-and-Patents.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Science-and-Technology/basic/17_Intellectual-Property-Rights-and-Patents.md)

### Demand decoding
State the global legal tension, India’s patentability boundary, and the innovation-to-market bottlenecks; do not repeat the question’s filing-rank claim as a verified grant/commercialisation statistic.

### Compact framework
`TRIPS minimums + national exclusions/access safeguards → patent-to-product valley of death`

### Model answer
Globally, TRIPS requires patent protection for inventions but permits exclusions for plants and animals other than microorganisms and allows sui generis plant-variety protection. Biotechnology patents therefore cover engineered microorganisms, processes, diagnostics and products, while jurisdictions differ on genes, stem cells and naturally occurring material. Access to medicines, farmers’ rights, benefit-sharing and biopiracy remain contested.

India’s Patents Act excludes discoveries of living things in nature, plants/animals and essentially biological processes; section 3(d) checks evergreening. PPVFR and the Biological Diversity framework protect public interests.

Low commercialisation of many Indian applications arises from:
- variable patent quality and weak market validation;
- university–industry and technology-transfer gaps;
- insufficient proof-of-concept, clinical/regulatory and scale-up finance;
- fragmented ownership, valuation and licensing capacity;
- long development cycles and uncertain procurement;
- limited manufacturing, standards and risk capital.

Thus, more filings do not equal innovation outcomes; better examination, translational grants, technology-transfer offices, industry co-development and predictable regulation are essential.

### Examiner rationale
The answer captures both the life-material patent boundary and the mechanisms separating a filing from a marketable product.

## Q6. Electronic toll collection and barrier-free transition
**Primary route:** Science and Technology → [Digital India, India Stack and Digital Infrastructure](../../topics/science-and-technology/digital-india-india-stack-and-digital-infrastructure.md) → Electronic tolling · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)
**Secondary route:** Governance → [Digital Public Infrastructure and Data Governance](../../topics/governance/digital-public-infrastructure-and-data-governance.md) → Digital infrastructure, privacy and accountable transition

**Identity:** 2024 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 6  
**Official question:** What is the technology being employed for electronic toll collection on highways? What are its advantages and limitations? What are the proposed changes that will make this process seamless? Would this transition carry any potential hazards?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Science and Technology → Digital Public Infrastructure → FASTag and GNSS Tolling  
**Proposed topic slug:** `science-technology/fastag-gnss-electronic-tolling`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Explain + Evaluate  
**Canonical owner:** [`Science-and-Technology/basic/08_Digital-India-and-India-Stack-UPI-Aadhaar.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Science-and-Technology/basic/08_Digital-India-and-India-Stack-UPI-Aadhaar.md)  
**Support owner:** [`Economy/basic/18_Infrastructure-PPPs-Logistics-and-Public-Investment.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/18_Infrastructure-PPPs-Logistics-and-Public-Investment.md)

### Demand decoding
Explain present FASTag mechanics, proposed barrier-free architecture, and transition risks including privacy and enforcement.

### Compact framework
`RFID tag-reader-clearing → benefits/limits → GNSS/ANPR barrier-free charging → safeguards`

### Model answer
FASTag uses passive **RFID**: a plaza reader identifies the windshield tag, the National Electronic Toll Collection system validates it and debits the linked account.

It reduces cash handling, queues, fuel loss and leakage, while producing auditable traffic data. Limitations are lane bottlenecks, failed/duplicate reads, insufficient balance, network outages, misclassification and grievance delays.

Proposed barrier-free tolling combines **GNSS-based distance charging** through an on-board unit with **ANPR** cameras and digital enforcement, so users pay for the highway distance travelled without stopping.

The transition also creates hazards: continuous location data can enable surveillance; spoofing, cloning and cyberattacks may cause fraud; camera errors can penalise innocent users; digitally excluded or infrequent users need alternatives; and cross-border data and dispute rules must be clear.

Therefore, seamless tolling requires data minimisation, purpose limitation, encryption, independent audits, offline redundancy, transparent tariffs, rapid appeals and a phased coexistence with FASTag.

### Examiner rationale
The answer explains both technologies and evaluates operational, privacy, cyber and equity risks rather than treating automation as frictionless.

## Q7. Industrial pollution of rivers
**Primary route:** Environment and Ecology → [Water Pollution and River Cleaning Missions](../../topics/environment/water-pollution-and-river-cleaning-missions.md) → River restoration · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2024 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 7  
**Official question:** Industrial pollution of river water is a significant environmental issue in India. Discuss the various mitigation measures to deal with this problem and also the government’s initiatives in this regard.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Environment and Ecology → Water Pollution → Industrial Effluent Control  
**Proposed topic slug:** `environment/industrial-river-pollution-control`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Discuss  
**Canonical owner:** [`Environment-and-Ecology/basic/14_Water-Pollution-and-River-Cleaning-Missions.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Environment-and-Ecology/basic/14_Water-Pollution-and-River-Cleaning-Missions.md)

### Demand decoding
Cover prevention, treatment, monitoring and enforcement, then name relevant legal/institutional initiatives.

### Compact framework
`cleaner production → segregate/treat/reuse → monitor/enforce → basin governance`

### Model answer
Industrial effluents add organic load, toxic chemicals, heavy metals, heat and colour, lowering dissolved oxygen and contaminating sediments and food chains.

Mitigation should begin at source: cleaner processes, water audits, substitution of toxic inputs and segregated streams. Units require correctly designed ETPs; clusters of small firms need functional CETPs. Treated water should be reused, while sludge is tracked and safely disposed. Real-time monitoring must be backed by independent sampling, public disclosure, polluter-pays liability and closure/prosecution for persistent violation. Environmental flows and basin-wide cumulative-load limits are also necessary.

Government architecture includes the Water Act, 1974 and Environment (Protection) Act, 1986; CPCB/SPCB consent and standards; continuous effluent monitoring for specified sectors; and Namami Gange/National Mission for Clean Ganga, including industrial inventories and inspections.

The principal gap is not absence of rules but weak SPCB capacity, bypassed plants and poorly operated CETPs. Enforcement and common infrastructure must therefore accompany river-cleaning expenditure.

### Examiner rationale
The answer follows the pollution-control hierarchy and distinguishes river missions from statutory industrial regulation.

## Q9. Narco-terrorism

**Identity:** 2024 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 9  
**Official question:** Explain how narco-terrorism has emerged as a serious threat across the country. Suggest suitable measures to counter narco-terrorism.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Internal Security → Organised Crime and Terrorism → Narco-terrorism  
**Proposed topic slug:** `internal-security/narco-terrorism-countermeasures`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Explain + Suggest  
**Canonical owner:** [`Internal-Security/basic/11_Organised-Crime-Narco-Terrorism-and-Trafficking.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Internal-Security/basic/11_Organised-Crime-Narco-Terrorism-and-Trafficking.md)

### Demand decoding

### Compact framework
`drug production/transit → organised networks → terror finance/recruitment/corruption → whole-of-government response`

### Model answer
Narco-terrorism uses drug trafficking to finance or facilitate terrorism. India is exposed to the Golden Crescent and Golden Triangle, maritime routes and darknet markets. Proceeds fund weapons, recruitment and propaganda; trafficking networks also enable arms movement and hawala. Synthetic drugs broaden the threat nationwide, while addiction harms youth and corrupts institutions.

- joint investigations by NCB, State police, DRI, NIA and financial agencies;

- precursor and pharmaceutical supply-chain tracking;
- faster forensics and lawful darknet infiltration;
- treatment, rehabilitation and community prevention to reduce demand; and
- operational cooperation through bilateral mechanisms, INTERPOL and UNODC.

Success must be measured by disrupted networks and reduced harm, not seizure volume alone, while preserving due process under the NDPS Act.

## Q10. Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023
**Primary route:** Science and Technology → [Data Protection, DPDP Act and Cybersecurity](../../topics/science-and-technology/data-protection-dpdp-act-and-cybersecurity.md) → DPDP Act · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2024 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 10  
**Official question:** Describe the context and salient features of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Science and Technology → Data Protection → Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023  
**Proposed topic slug:** `science-technology/dpdp-act-2023`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Describe  
**Canonical owner:** [`Science-and-Technology/basic/12_Data-Protection-DPDP-Act-and-Cybersecurity.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Science-and-Technology/basic/12_Data-Protection-DPDP-Act-and-Cybersecurity.md)

### Demand decoding
Give constitutional-legislative context and the principal actors, obligations, rights, transfers, regulator and safeguards.

### Compact framework
`Puttaswamy → digital processing scope → consent/uses → rights/duties → fiduciary/Board/penalties → concerns`

### Model answer
The Act follows *K.S. Puttaswamy* (2017), which recognised privacy under Article 21, the Srikrishna Committee (2018), and successive bills. It creates India’s general framework for processing **digital personal data**, including non-digital data subsequently digitised, and can apply extraterritorially to offering goods or services in India.

Key features are:
- processing on consent or specified legitimate uses, with notice and withdrawal;
- Data Principal rights to access information, correct/erase data, seek grievance redress and nominate another person, alongside statutory duties;
- Data Fiduciary obligations of purpose limitation, security safeguards, breach intimation and erasure;
- enhanced duties for Significant Data Fiduciaries, including an India-based DPO, audit and impact assessment;
- special protection for children;
- cross-border transfer except to restricted jurisdictions;
- a digital-office Data Protection Board, appeal to TDSAT, and civil penalties up to ₹250 crore for specified breaches.

Concerns include broad State exemptions and institutional independence. Its value depends on proportionate rules and effective enforcement.

### Examiner rationale
The answer covers context and statutory architecture while distinguishing privacy regulation from cybersecurity.

## Q11. Four Labour Codes: merits, demerits and progress
**Primary route:** Economy → [Employment, Labour Codes, Skills and Demographic Dividend](../../topics/economy/employment-labour-codes-skills-and-demographic-dividend.md) → Labour Codes · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2024 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 11  
**Official question:** Discuss the merits and demerits of the four ‘Labour Codes’ in the context of labour market reforms in India. What has been the progress so far in this regard?  
**Marks / limit:** 15 / 250 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Labour Market Reforms → Four Labour Codes  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/four-labour-codes-reform-progress`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Discuss + Status  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/22_Employment-Labour-Codes-Skills-and-Demographic-Dividend.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/22_Employment-Labour-Codes-Skills-and-Demographic-Dividend.md)

### Demand decoding
Name all four Codes, weigh efficiency and worker-protection effects, and give progress as of the 2024 examination.

### Compact framework
`consolidation/formalisation benefits ↔ thresholds, discretion and bargaining risks → enacted but not commenced in 2024`

### Model answer
The four Codes consolidate 29 central laws: **Code on Wages, 2019; Industrial Relations Code, 2020; Code on Social Security, 2020; and Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020.**

**Merits**
- common definitions and web-based registration can reduce fragmented compliance;
- a statutory floor-wage framework and wider wage coverage can reduce arbitrariness;
- recognition of gig and platform workers enables social-security schemes;
- appointment letters, inter-State migrant provisions and common safety standards promote formalisation;
- fixed-term employment can provide flexibility with parity of benefits.

**Demerits/concerns**
- higher thresholds for standing orders and prior permission for retrenchment may weaken security and bargaining power;
- strike-notice requirements and recognition rules may constrain collective action;
- social-security entitlement for gig/unorganised workers remains scheme- and financing-dependent;
- broad delegated rule-making and inspector-cum-facilitator discretion risk uneven enforcement;
- a uniform framework may impose adjustment costs on small enterprises, while occupational-safety thresholds can leave smaller establishments insufficiently covered.

**Progress as of the 2024 examination:** Parliament had enacted the Codes, but they had not commenced. The Union and most States/UTs had pre-published draft rules; simultaneous commencement awaited rule alignment and administrative readiness.

The reform should therefore pair simplified compliance with universal, portable social protection, tripartite consultation and strong inspection.

### Examiner rationale
The answer names the Codes, balances firm flexibility against labour protection, and dates the implementation status instead of projecting later commencement backwards.

## Q12. Regional air connectivity and UDAN
**Primary route:** Economy → [Infrastructure, PPPs, Logistics and Public Investment](../../topics/economy/infrastructure-ppps-logistics-and-public-investment.md) → Civil aviation and regional connectivity · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2024 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 12  
**Official question:** What is the need for expanding regional air connectivity in India? In this context, discuss the government’s UDAN Scheme and its achievements.  
**Marks / limit:** 15 / 250 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Infrastructure → UDAN and Regional Air Connectivity  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/udan-regional-air-connectivity`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Explain + Discuss  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/18_Infrastructure-PPPs-Logistics-and-Public-Investment.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/18_Infrastructure-PPPs-Logistics-and-Public-Investment.md)

### Demand decoding
Establish connectivity need, explain UDAN’s market-design mechanism, assess achievements and sustainability constraints.

### Compact framework
`regional inequality/market access → capped fares + VGF + concessions + airport revival → network gains ↔ route viability`

### Model answer
Regional aviation reduces the time-distance penalty faced by the North-East, hill, island and remote districts. It enables tourism, investment, emergency travel, high-value cargo and access to larger education and health centres; it also decongests metropolitan hubs and makes growth spatially broader.

**UDAN (Ude Desh ka Aam Nagrik)**, launched in 2016 under the Regional Connectivity Scheme, uses competitive bidding to connect unserved and underserved airports. Airlines receive limited-period **viability-gap funding (VGF)** for a prescribed share of seats with capped fares. The Union, States and airport operators support routes through reduced taxes/charges, security/fire services and revival of airports. Later rounds included helicopters, seaplanes, tourism and small-aircraft routes.

By the 2024 examination, the scheme had operationalised hundreds of routes and connected more than 80 airports/heliports/water aerodromes, reopening several dormant facilities and widening access beyond major cities. These are genuine network achievements, though an awarded route is not necessarily continuously operational.

Challenges include low and seasonal demand, airline exits, aircraft shortages, weak last-mile transport, airport readiness, weather constraints and dependence on VGF. Some routes lapse after subsidy support.

UDAN should therefore be judged by sustained frequency and passenger use, not route awards alone. Better multimodal links, route clustering, maintenance capacity, transparent performance data and periodic redesign of support can convert connectivity into durable regional development.

### Examiner rationale
The answer explains why connectivity matters, how UDAN works and why operational sustainability—not sanction count—is the proper achievement test.

## Q15. Alternative freshwater technologies

**Official question:** The world is facing an acute shortage of clean and safe freshwater. What are the alternative technologies which can solve this crisis? Briefly discuss any three such technologies, citing their key merits and demerits.  
**Marks / limit:** 15 / 250 words  
**Classification:** Environment and Ecology → Water Security → Desalination, Wastewater Reuse and Atmospheric Harvesting  
**Proposed topic slug:** `environment/alternative-freshwater-technologies`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Discuss three with merits/demerits  
**Canonical owner:** [`Environment-and-Ecology/basic/14_Water-Pollution-and-River-Cleaning-Missions.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Environment-and-Ecology/basic/14_Water-Pollution-and-River-Cleaning-Missions.md)

### Demand decoding
Name the wider technology set but evaluate exactly three by mechanism, suitability, benefits and externalities.

### Compact framework
`saline water → desalination; used water → advanced reuse; atmospheric moisture → capture`

### Model answer
Technology can augment supply and improve water quality, but cannot substitute for demand management, aquifer protection and pollution prevention. Options include desalination, advanced wastewater reuse, membrane/solar purification, rainwater harvesting and atmospheric water generation.

**1. Reverse-osmosis desalination**  
Pressure forces seawater through semi-permeable membranes that reject salts.  
*Merits:* drought-proof coastal supply; modular; mature technology.

*Merits:* decentralised emergency or remote-area supply; limited conveyance infrastructure.  
*Demerits:* output depends on humidity and temperature; energy cost can be high; presently unsuitable for bulk urban demand.

## Q16. Asteroids and planetary defence
**Primary route:** Science and Technology → [Human Spaceflight, Gaganyaan and Planetary Missions](../../topics/science-and-technology/human-spaceflight-gaganyaan-and-planetary-missions.md) → Planetary defence · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2024 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 16  
**Official question:** What are asteroids? How real is the threat of them causing extinction of life? What strategies have been developed to prevent such a catastrophe?  
**Marks / limit:** 15 / 250 words  
**Classification:** Science and Technology → Space Science → Asteroids and Planetary Defence  
**Proposed topic slug:** `science-technology/asteroids-planetary-defence`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Define + Assess + Explain  
**Canonical owner:** [`Science-and-Technology/basic/03_Human-Spaceflight-Gaganyaan-and-Planetary-Missions.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Science-and-Technology/basic/03_Human-Spaceflight-Gaganyaan-and-Planetary-Missions.md)

### Demand decoding
Define asteroids, grade impact risk by size/frequency and detection uncertainty, then explain detection, deflection and civil-defence strategies.

### Compact framework
`detect → calculate orbit/characterise → decide internationally → deflect with warning → verify/evacuate`

### Model answer
Asteroids are rocky or metallic small Solar-System bodies orbiting the Sun, concentrated mainly in the belt between Mars and Jupiter. Near-Earth objects have orbits approaching Earth.

The threat is **real but low-frequency**. Geological evidence links a roughly 10-km impactor to the end-Cretaceous mass extinction. Smaller objects are more frequent and can cause regional devastation through blast, heat or tsunami. Risk equals impact probability multiplied by consequence: known large objects can be tracked, but incomplete cataloguing—especially of smaller or sunward-approaching objects—creates residual uncertainty. It is therefore incorrect either to predict imminent extinction or to dismiss the hazard.

**Planetary-defence strategy**
1. **Detection and tracking:** ground/space telescopes discover NEOs; repeated observations calculate orbit and impact probability.
2. **Characterisation:** size, composition, spin and whether a body is solid or a rubble pile determine the response.
3. **Kinetic impact:** NASA’s DART struck Dimorphos in 2022 and measurably changed its orbital period, demonstrating deflection—not a universal shield.
4. **Long-warning alternatives:** a gravity tractor could slowly alter trajectory; other concepts include ion-beam or nuclear standoff methods, each technically and politically constrained.
5. **Civil defence:** where warning is short, evacuation, sheltering and tsunami planning reduce casualties.
6. **International coordination:** observations, risk communication and any intervention require shared decision-making to avoid shifting risk.

Early detection is the decisive technology: a small velocity change years ahead can produce a large miss distance.

### Examiner rationale
The answer calibrates extinction risk and explains why target properties and warning time determine the feasible defence.
