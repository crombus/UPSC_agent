## Passage for Q1–Q2

**Directions:** Read the following passage and answer the items that follow. Your answers to these items should be based on the passage only.

According to the Food and Agriculture Organization, one-third of food produced for human consumption is lost or wasted globally. Food is lost or wasted throughout the supply chain, from initial agricultural production to final household consumption. The increasing wastage also results in land degradation by about 45%, mainly due to deforestation, unsustainable agricultural practices, and excessive groundwater extraction. The energy spent over wasted food results in about 3.5 billion tonnes of carbon dioxide production every year. Decay also leads to harmful emissions of other gases in the atmosphere. Addressing the loss and wastage of food in all forms is critical to complete the cycle of food sufficiency and food sustainability.

## Q1. Passage inference
**Primary route:** CSAT → [Reading Comprehension](../../topics/csat/reading-comprehension.md) → Passage inference · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

Which of the following statements best reflect the most logical and rational inferences that can be made from the passage?

1. The current methods of food distribution are solely responsible for the loss and wastage of food.
2. Land productivity is adversely affected by the prevailing trend of food loss and wastage.
3. Reduction in the loss and wastage of food results in lesser carbon footprint.
4. Post-harvest technologies to prevent or reduce the loss and wastage of food are not available.

**Options:** (a) 1, 2 and 3 · (b) 2 and 3 only · (c) 1, 3 and 4 · (d) 1, 2 and 4

**Metadata:** Family 01 · Reading Comprehension → Passage inference · Slug: `passage-inference-2024-q01`  
**Canonical owner:** `CSAT/basic/01_Reading-Comprehension.md`

**FINAL answer: (b).**

**Full reasoning:** Statement 1 fails because the passage locates loss throughout the supply chain and names several causes; “solely” is unsupported. Statement 2 follows because wastage is expressly linked to land degradation. Statement 3 follows because wasted food embodies energy and produces carbon dioxide and other emissions; reducing waste therefore reduces that footprint. Statement 4 is an argument from silence: the passage never says technologies are unavailable. Hence only 2 and 3 survive.

**Shortest safe method:** Reject the two absolutes/additions (“solely”, “not available”); retain the two direct cause-effect inferences.

## Q5. Cube partition cuts
**Primary route:** CSAT → [Rates, Motion, Time and Geometry](../../topics/csat/rates-motion-time-and-geometry.md) → Cube partition cuts · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

What is the least possible number of cuts required to cut a cube into 64 identical pieces?

**Options:** (a) 8 · (b) 9 · (c) 12 · (d) 16

**Metadata:** Family 04 · Rates, Motion, Time and Geometry → Cube partition cuts · Slug: `cube-partition-cuts-2024-q05`  
**Canonical owner:** `CSAT/basic/04_Rates-Motion-Time-and-Geometry.md`

**FINAL answer: (b).**

**Full reasoning:** Since 64 = 4³, each edge must be divided into four equal parts. That requires three planar cuts perpendicular to each of the three dimensions: 3 + 3 + 3 = 9. Fewer cuts cannot create four layers along every dimension.

**Shortest safe method:** For an n³ partition without stacking, cuts = 3(n−1); here 3(4−1)=9.

## Q6. Operator-placement minimum
**Primary route:** CSAT → [Number Systems and Number Sense](../../topics/csat/number-systems-and-number-sense.md) → Operator-placement minimum · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

In the expression `5 * 4 * 3 * 2 * 1`, `*` is chosen from `+`, `−`, `×` each at most two times. What is the smallest non-negative value of the expression?

**Options:** (a) 3 · (b) 2 · (c) 1 · (d) 0

**Metadata:** Family 02 · Number Systems and Number Sense → Operator-placement minimum · Slug: `operator-placement-minimum-2024-q06`  
**Canonical owner:** `CSAT/basic/02_Number-Systems-and-Number-Sense.md`

**FINAL answer: (d).**

**Full reasoning:** A legal placement is `5 − 4 − 3 + 2 × 1`. Applying ordinary precedence gives `5−4−3+2 = 0`. The minus sign is used twice, plus once and multiplication once, so the frequency restriction is met. As zero is non-negative, no smaller admissible value exists.

**Shortest safe method:** Construct zero directly: `5−4−3+2×1=0`.

## Q7. Work-rate scaling
**Primary route:** CSAT → [Rates, Motion, Time and Geometry](../../topics/csat/rates-motion-time-and-geometry.md) → Work-rate scaling · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

A certain number of men can complete a piece of work in 6k days, where k is a natural number. By what percent should the number of men be increased so that the work can be completed in 5k days?

**Options:** (a) 10% · (b) (50/3)% · (c) 20% · (d) 25%

**Metadata:** Family 04 · Rates, Motion, Time and Geometry → Work-rate scaling · Slug: `work-rate-scaling-2024-q07`  
**Canonical owner:** `CSAT/basic/04_Rates-Motion-Time-and-Geometry.md`

**FINAL answer: (c).**

**Full reasoning:** For fixed work and equal efficiency, men × days is constant. Thus new men/old men = 6k/5k = 6/5. The increase is 1/5 = 20%.

**Shortest safe method:** Time falls in ratio 6:5, so workforce rises inversely by 6/5, i.e. 20%.

## Q8. Alternating-worker schedule
**Primary route:** CSAT → [Rates, Motion, Time and Geometry](../../topics/csat/rates-motion-time-and-geometry.md) → Alternating-worker schedule · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

X, Y and Z can complete a piece of work individually in 6 hours, 8 hours and 8 hours respectively. However, only one person at a time can work in each hour and nobody can work for two consecutive hours. All are engaged to finish the work. What is the minimum amount of time that they will take to finish the work?

**Options:** (a) 6 hours 15 minutes · (b) 6 hours 30 minutes · (c) 6 hours 45 minutes · (d) 7 hours

**Metadata:** Family 04 · Rates, Motion, Time and Geometry → Alternating-worker schedule · Slug: `alternating-worker-schedule-2024-q08`  
**Canonical owner:** `CSAT/basic/04_Rates-Motion-Time-and-Geometry.md`

**FINAL answer: (c).**

**Full reasoning:** To minimize time, use fastest worker X in every alternate slot and fill intervening slots with Y or Z. In six hours, X works 3 hours and Y/Z collectively 3 hours, completing `3/6 + 3/8 = 7/8`. The remaining 1/8 is done by X in `(1/8)/(1/6)=3/4` hour = 45 minutes. Total = 6 hours 45 minutes; the no-consecutive-work condition is respected.

**Shortest safe method:** Best six-hour alternation gives 7/8 work; X needs 45 more minutes.

## Q9. Trailing-zero count
**Primary route:** CSAT → [Number Systems and Number Sense](../../topics/csat/number-systems-and-number-sense.md) → Trailing-zero count · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

How many consecutive zeros are there at the end of the integer obtained in the product `1² × 2⁴ × 3⁶ × 4⁸ × … × 25⁵⁰`?

**Options:** (a) 50 · (b) 55 · (c) 100 · (d) 200

**Metadata:** Family 02 · Number Systems and Number Sense → Trailing-zero count · Slug: `trailing-zero-count-2024-q09`  
**Canonical owner:** `CSAT/basic/02_Number-Systems-and-Number-Sense.md`

**FINAL answer: (d).**

**Full reasoning:** Zeros equal `min(v₂,v₅)`; twos are abundant, so count fives. Multiples of 5 contribute: `5¹⁰→10`, `10²⁰→20`, `15³⁰→30`, `20⁴⁰→40`, and `25⁵⁰=(5²)⁵⁰→100`. Total `10+20+30+40+100=200`.

**Shortest safe method:** Sum only powers of 5: 10+20+30+40+2×50=200.

## Q10. Cumulative savings sequence
**Primary route:** CSAT → [Number Systems and Number Sense](../../topics/csat/number-systems-and-number-sense.md) → Cumulative savings sequence · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

On January 1st, 2023, a person saved ₹1. On January 2nd, 2023, he saved ₹2 more than that on the previous day. On January 3rd, 2023, he saved ₹2 more than that on the previous day and so on. At the end of which date was his total savings a perfect square as well as a perfect cube?

**Options:** (a) 7th January, 2023 · (b) 8th January, 2023 · (c) 9th January, 2023 · (d) Not possible

**Metadata:** Family 02 · Number Systems and Number Sense → Cumulative savings sequence · Slug: `cumulative-savings-sequence-2024-q10`  
**Canonical owner:** `CSAT/basic/02_Number-Systems-and-Number-Sense.md`

**FINAL answer: (b).**

**Full reasoning:** Daily savings are odd numbers `1,3,5,…,(2n−1)`. Their cumulative sum is `n²`. It is also a cube when n itself is a cube. The first eligible n among the options is 8, giving total `8²=64=4³`.

**Shortest safe method:** Sum of first n odd numbers is n²; choose first cubic n, namely 8.
