## Q1(a). AI as an input for administrative decisions
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Emerging Ethics—AI](../../topics/ethics/emerging-ethics-ai.md) → Algorithmic accountability and human oversight · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** The application of Artificial Intelligence as a dependable source of input for administrative rational decision-making is a debatable issue. Critically examine the statement from the ethical point of view. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Emerging Ethics—Technology · Subtopic: AI-assisted administration · Proposed slug: `ai-administrative-decision-ethics` · Demand: Critically examine

### Model answer

AI can improve administrative rationality by processing large datasets, detecting patterns and making similarly situated cases more consistent. For example, anomaly detection can flag welfare leakages or tax evasion for human scrutiny.

Ethically, however, reliability is not merely predictive accuracy. Historical data may reproduce caste, gender or regional bias; opaque models weaken reason-giving; automation bias can turn nominal human oversight into rubber-stamping; and erroneous exclusion can burden citizens unable to contest the system. Under *K.S. Puttaswamy*, data use must respect privacy and proportionality, while constitutional equality requires outcome-based bias testing.

AI should therefore remain a **decision-support**, not accountability-substitution, tool: lawful purpose, data minimisation, representative datasets, independent audits, explainability proportionate to risk, recorded human reasons and accessible appeal are essential.

**Conclusion:** AI is dependable only conditionally—where a publicly answerable official retains judgment and the affected citizen retains contestability.

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes efficiency from ethical reliability, uses a constitutional anchor and supplies a qualified governance model.

## Q1(b). Dimensions of ethics in professional decisions
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → Dimensions of ethical action · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** “Ethics encompasses several key dimensions that are crucial in guiding individuals and organizations towards morally responsible behaviour.” Explain the key dimensions of ethics that influence human actions. Discuss how these dimensions shape ethical decision-making in the professional context. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Ethics and Human Interface · Subtopic: Dimensions of ethical judgment · Proposed slug: `dimensions-ethics-professional-decisions` · Demand: Explain and discuss

### Model answer

Ethics evaluates not only **what** is done but **why, how and with what effects**. Its key dimensions are: duty and rights (Kantian respect for persons); consequences and welfare (utilitarian concern); virtue and character (Aristotle’s practical wisdom); justice and fairness (Rawlsian impartiality); care and empathy; and integrity of procedure, including transparency and accountability.

In professional life these dimensions operate as complementary tests. A doctor must respect informed consent (duty), pursue health outcomes (consequence), allocate scarce care fairly (justice) and communicate compassionately (care). A civil servant deciding a tender must apply rules impartially, disclose conflicts, assess public value and record reasons. An option that is profitable but deceptive fails the duty and integrity tests; one that is rule-compliant but predictably exclusionary still fails justice.

**Conclusion:** Ethical professionalism requires convergence of lawful means, fair procedure, good consequences and trustworthy character; no single dimension should become a licence to ignore the others.

**Why this earns marks:** It identifies distinct dimensions and demonstrates how they alter concrete professional choices.

## Q2(a). Ethical duties of powerful states in ongoing conflicts
**Primary route:** Ethics → [International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/international-ethics.md) → Peace, arms trade and responsible power · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** “It is not enough to talk about peace, one must believe in it; and it is not enough to believe in it, one must act upon it.” In the present context, the major weapon industries of the developed nations are adversely influencing continuation of a number of wars for their own self-interest, all around the world. What are the ethical considerations of the powerful nations in today’s international arena to stop continuation of ongoing conflicts? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: International Ethics · Subtopic: Peace and arms-export responsibility · Proposed slug: `powerful-states-peace-arms-ethics` · Demand: Identify and evaluate

### Model answer

Power creates a special responsibility: states able to prolong or restrain war cannot claim moral neutrality while benefiting from arms sales.

Their ethical duties include pursuing ceasefires and good-faith mediation; applying just-war principles of proportionality and civilian distinction; complying with the UN Charter and international humanitarian law; conducting end-use and human-rights due diligence before arms transfers; preventing diversion; supporting humanitarian access and reconstruction; and accepting accountability for proxy conduct. The Arms Trade Treaty’s risk-assessment logic illustrates that commercial gain cannot override foreseeable atrocity risk.

Yet an indiscriminate arms embargo may leave an attacked state defenceless and reward aggression. Restraint must therefore distinguish legitimate self-defence from profit-driven escalation, combine diplomacy with credible deterrence and apply standards consistently rather than selectively to allies.

**Conclusion:** Peace becomes ethical action when powerful states subordinate arms profit and geopolitical convenience to civilian protection, lawful security and a feasible political settlement.

**Why this earns marks:** It converts the quotation into state duties while qualifying pacifism through legitimate self-defence.

## Q2(b). Human greed, climate change and ecological equilibrium
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Environmental and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/environmental-and-administrative-ethics.md) → Intergenerational justice and sustainable development · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)
**Secondary route:** Environment and Ecology → [Climate Change Science Greenhouse Effect](../../topics/environment/climate-change-science-greenhouse-effect.md) → Climate justice and ecological balance

**Official question:** Global warming and climate change are the outcomes of human greed in the name of development, indicating the direction in which extinction of organisms including human beings is heading towards loss of life on Earth. How do you put an end to this to protect life and bring equilibrium between society and the environment? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Environmental Ethics · Subtopic: Consumption, climate justice and ecological balance · Proposed slug: `climate-greed-ecological-equilibrium` · Demand: Propose an ethical response

### Model answer

Climate change reflects not development per se but a model that externalises ecological costs, treats nature instrumentally and discounts future generations. The Brundtland idea of sustainable development corrects this by joining present welfare with future capacity.

Equilibrium requires: carbon pricing and removal of environmentally harmful incentives; renewable energy, efficiency and public transport; circular production and extended producer responsibility; protection and restoration of forests and wetlands; climate-resilient agriculture; and lifestyle moderation through *LiFE*. Rio’s principles of precaution and common but differentiated responsibilities require richer and historically larger emitters to provide finance and technology, while every state acts according to capability.

Transitions must be just: workers and poor households cannot bear disproportionate costs, and affected communities need participation and livelihood alternatives. Individual restraint helps, but structural regulation is indispensable.

**Conclusion:** Ecological balance demands replacing greed-driven extraction with sufficiency, fairness and trusteeship—development within planetary limits, not the abandonment of human development.

**Why this earns marks:** It links ethical concepts to multilevel action and avoids blaming only individuals or rejecting development.

## Q3(a). Vivekananda on learning without imitation
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Indian Moral Thinkers](../../topics/ethics/indian-moral-thinkers.md) → Assimilation, authenticity and judgment · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** “Learn everything that is good from others, but bring it in, and in your own way absorb it, do not become others.” — Swami Vivekananda. What does this quotation convey to you in the present context? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Indian Moral Thinkers · Subtopic: Critical assimilation and authenticity · Proposed slug: `vivekananda-learning-authenticity` · Demand: Interpret and apply

### Model answer

Vivekananda advocates **critical assimilation**: humility to learn universally, combined with the judgment and self-confidence to adapt knowledge to one’s context. Learning should enlarge agency, not produce cultural or intellectual imitation.

For public policy, India may study another country’s health insurance or urban transport, but must redesign it for federal capacity, informality and local needs. For a civil servant, best practices are inputs, not substitutes for field consultation and constitutional duty. At the personal level, diverse traditions can enrich character without erasing identity.

The idea also resists two extremes—closed traditionalism that rejects useful knowledge and uncritical transplantation that mistakes foreign prestige for suitability. Gandhi’s creative use of ideas from Ruskin and Tolstoy while developing satyagraha illustrates such synthesis.

**Conclusion:** Ethical learning is open yet discerning: absorb principles, test them through reason and experience, and express them through an authentic responsibility to one’s society.

**Why this earns marks:** It explains the quotation’s mechanism, gives administrative application and identifies both limiting extremes.

## Q3(b). Sardar Patel on faith and strength
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Indian Moral Thinkers](../../topics/ethics/indian-moral-thinkers.md) → Conviction, capacity and public action · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** “Faith is of no avail in the absence of strength. Faith and strength, both are essential to accomplish any great work.” — Sardar Patel. What does this quotation convey to you in the present context? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Indian Moral Thinkers · Subtopic: Moral conviction and institutional capacity · Proposed slug: `patel-faith-strength-public-action` · Demand: Interpret and apply

### Model answer

“Faith” denotes conviction in a just purpose; “strength” denotes courage, competence, organisation and the capacity to act. Conviction without capacity becomes sentiment, while power without ethical purpose becomes coercion.

Patel’s integration of princely states illustrates their union: belief in national unity was matched by negotiation, administrative preparation and credible resolve. Today, faith in constitutional equality requires institutions capable of enforcing anti-discrimination law; commitment to disaster relief needs trained personnel, logistics and calm leadership. For a civil servant, integrity must be supported by professional knowledge, emotional resilience and institutional safeguards against retaliation.

Strength, however, must remain proportionate and accountable. Majoritarian force or authoritarian decisiveness cannot be justified merely by confidence in one’s cause.

**Conclusion:** Great public work occurs when moral legitimacy directs effective capacity—faith supplies the end, strength the means, and constitutional restraint keeps both ethical.

**Why this earns marks:** It defines both terms, links them causally and qualifies strength through constitutional restraint.

## Q3(c). Kant on legal and ethical guilt
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → Intention, duty and legality · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** “In law, a man is guilty when he violates the rights of others. In ethics, he is guilty if he only thinks of doing so.” — Immanuel Kant. What does this quotation convey to you in the present context? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Deontological Ethics · Subtopic: Intention and external conduct · Proposed slug: `kant-legal-ethical-guilt` · Demand: Interpret and apply

### Model answer

The quotation distinguishes law’s external minimum from ethics’ internal demand. Law generally sanctions an act, attempt or legally relevant omission; ethics also examines motive, maxim and the willingness to use another merely as a means.

For Kant, moral worth arises from acting from duty under a universalizable maxim. An officer who rejects a bribe only because surveillance is present remains legally compliant but ethically deficient; one who harbours prejudice may later exercise discretion discriminatorily even before an overt violation occurs. Ethical self-scrutiny therefore prevents wrongdoing at its source.

Yet thoughts are not identical to completed harms. Intrusive impulses can be involuntary, and a liberal state must not criminalise private thought. Ethical responsibility increases when a person endorses, cultivates or plans the wrongful intention.

**Conclusion:** Law protects rights through enforceable conduct rules; ethics develops the character and motives that make right conduct reliable even without detection.

**Why this earns marks:** It states Kant’s concept, applies it professionally and avoids equating every passing thought with legal guilt.

## Q4(a). Context and changing judgments of justice
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → Justice, context and principled revision · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** “The concept of Just and Unjust is contextual. What was just a year back, may turn out to be unjust in today’s context. Changing context should be constantly under scrutiny to prevent miscarriage of justice.” Examine the above statement with suitable examples. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Justice · Subtopic: Context sensitivity and stable principles · Proposed slug: `justice-context-principled-revision` · Demand: Examine

### Model answer

Justice applies enduring values—dignity, equality and fairness—to changing facts. A rule defensible under one social or technological condition may become unjust when its effects or available alternatives change.

For example, facially neutral digital-only welfare delivery becomes exclusionary where authentication or connectivity fails; emergency restrictions justified during a severe epidemic become disproportionate once risk declines; and workplace standards must evolve when evidence reveals indirect gender or disability discrimination. The Supreme Court’s recognition of privacy in *K.S. Puttaswamy* reflects reassessment of liberty amid pervasive data collection.

However, contextualism cannot mean that justice is whatever authority presently prefers. Revision must rest on evidence, public reason, equal treatment, legitimate expectation and prospective safeguards. Core rights cannot be discarded for convenience.

**Conclusion:** Constant scrutiny prevents obsolete rules from producing injustice, while constitutional principles ensure that adaptation remains principled rather than arbitrary.

**Why this earns marks:** It balances contextual responsiveness with a stable normative floor and uses precise contemporary examples.

## Q4(b). Substance over form in administration
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Service Delivery and Regulatory Ethics](../../topics/ethics/service-delivery-and-regulatory-ethics.md) → Purposive discretion and procedural fairness · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** “Mindless addiction to Form, ignoring the Substance of the matter, results in rendering of injustice. A perceptive civil servant is one who ignores such literalness and carries out true intent.” Examine the above statement with suitable illustrations. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Sources of Ethical Guidance · Subtopic: Rules, purpose and discretion · Proposed slug: `substance-over-form-administration` · Demand: Examine

### Model answer

Rules create consistency and control discretion, but literal compliance can defeat the very purpose for which a rule exists. Ethical administration therefore uses **purposive**, not lawless, judgment.

A pension claim should not fail merely because an illiterate widow places a thumb impression in the wrong box when identity and entitlement are verifiable. Disaster relief may require documented emergency procurement rather than waiting for an ordinary timetable. Similarly, a hospital should provide stabilising emergency care before resolving non-essential paperwork. In each case, dignity and statutory purpose guide procedure.

However, “substance over form” cannot excuse favouritism, concealment or bypass of mandatory safeguards. The officer should use authorised relaxation, record reasons, apply the same principle to comparable cases and obtain ex-post review.

**Conclusion:** A perceptive civil servant treats procedure as a servant of justice—flexible where law permits, but transparent, reasoned and reviewable.

**Why this earns marks:** It protects both humane discretion and the rule-of-law safeguards against arbitrary exceptions.

## Q5(a). A model code of ethics for public administration
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Work Culture and Public Funds](../../topics/ethics/work-culture-and-public-funds.md) → Codes, integrity and transparency · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** The “Code of Conduct” and “Code of Ethics” are the sources of guidance in public administration. There is a code of conduct already in operation, whereas a code of ethics is not yet put in place. Suggest a suitable model for a code of ethics to maintain integrity, probity and transparency in governance. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Codes of Ethics and Conduct · Subtopic: Values-based public-service code · Proposed slug: `model-code-ethics-governance` · Demand: Suggest a model

### Model answer

A conduct code prescribes enforceable dos and don’ts; a code of ethics should articulate the public-service values that guide judgment where rules are incomplete.

A suitable model should contain: constitutional loyalty and non-partisanship; public interest and service orientation; integrity, objectivity and impartiality; dignity and inclusion; openness with lawful confidentiality; stewardship of public resources; conflict-of-interest disclosure, recusal and gift restrictions; reasoned decisions and audit trails; protection for good-faith dissent and whistleblowing; and post-employment safeguards.

Implementation matters more than symbolism. The code should be issued through an authoritative framework, translated into scenario-based training, leadership appraisal and citizen-facing service standards. Independent ethics advisers, annual interest declarations, safe reporting channels, time-bound inquiry and proportionate sanctions should support it. The Nolan principles and the 2nd ARC’s ethics emphasis provide useful reference points.

**Conclusion:** A credible code joins values, decision procedures and enforceable accountability; aspiration without institutions will not secure probity.

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes ethics from conduct and proposes both substantive values and operational machinery.

## Q5(b). Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita and the shift towards justice
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Governance and International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/governance-and-international-ethics.md) → Justice, equality and impartial criminal administration · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** The soul of the new law, Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), is Justice, Equality and Impartiality based on Indian culture and ethos. Discuss this in the light of major shift from a doctrine of punishment to justice in the present judicial system. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Ethical Governance · Subtopic: Criminal justice and restorative orientation · Proposed slug: `bns-punishment-to-justice-ethics` · Demand: Discuss

### Model answer

Punishment asks what penalty an offender deserves; justice also asks whether victims are heard, procedure is fair, social order is repaired and sanctions are proportionate.

The BNS’s introduction of community service for specified offences can support accountability without unnecessary incarceration. Its consolidation of offences against women and children and recognition of organised crime and terrorism seek stronger protection of victims and society. Justice, equality and impartiality nevertheless depend on implementation: police discretion, forensic capacity, legal aid, timely trial and humane prison conditions determine whether formal reform reaches citizens.

Indian ethos may contribute ideas of duty, reform and social harmony, but cannot override constitutional morality. Due process, presumption of innocence, non-discrimination and proportionality remain the controlling standards; harsher labels or penalties alone do not establish justice.

**Conclusion:** The genuine shift is from retribution-centred law to victim-sensitive, reformative and procedurally fair justice—measured by equal outcomes and rights protection, not nomenclature.

**Why this earns marks:** It identifies a concrete reform while qualifying the claim through constitutional and implementation tests.

## Q6(a). Gender-specific challenges faced by women public servants
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → Substantive equality and institutional fairness · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** “In Indian culture and value system, an equal opportunity has been provided irrespective of gender identity. The number of women in public service has been steadily increasing over the years.” Examine the gender-specific challenges faced by female public servants and suggest suitable measures to increase their efficiency in discharging their duties and maintaining high standards of probity. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Human Values and Equality · Subtopic: Women in public service · Proposed slug: `women-public-servants-challenges-measures` · Demand: Examine and suggest

### Model answer

Formal equality has expanded women’s entry into public service, but substantive equality remains constrained by unequal care work, unsafe or remote postings, harassment, biased appraisal, exclusion from informal networks, inadequate facilities and the “double bind” in which firmness is judged harshly while empathy is treated as weakness.

Measures include transparent posting and promotion criteria; implementation of the POSH Act with independent Internal Committees; housing, transport, sanitation and childcare; flexible but career-neutral care arrangements; mentoring and leadership pipelines; gender-sensitisation; secure complaint channels; and action against retaliation. Digitised workflows and recorded decisions can reduce personalised pressure, while team staffing in difficult field assignments improves effectiveness.

Yet protective measures must not become paternalistic denial of challenging roles. Probity is not a gender trait; women need equal authority, resources and institutional protection to exercise professional judgment.

**Conclusion:** Efficiency and integrity rise when the workplace removes gendered barriers while applying common, transparent standards of accountability.

**Why this earns marks:** It challenges the stem’s formal-equality assumption, supplies institutional remedies and avoids stereotyping women as inherently more ethical.

## Q6(b). Mission Karmayogi and grassroots service delivery
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Service Delivery and Regulatory Ethics](../../topics/ethics/service-delivery-and-regulatory-ethics.md) → Competency-based civil-service reform · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** Mission Karmayogi is aiming for maintaining a very high standard of conduct and behaviour to ensure efficiency for serving citizens and, in turn, developing oneself. How will this scheme empower civil servants in enhancing productive efficiency and delivering services at the grassroots level? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Civil-Service Aptitude · Subtopic: Mission Karmayogi and capacity building · Proposed slug: `mission-karmayogi-grassroots-delivery` · Demand: Explain

### Model answer

Mission Karmayogi seeks to shift civil-service capacity building from rule-bound seniority to role-based, continuous competency development through the iGOT Karmayogi platform and the FRAC framework of roles, activities and competencies.

It can improve grassroots delivery by giving officials modular, need-based training; disseminating successful field practices; strengthening behavioural competencies such as empathy and teamwork; and aligning learning with functional skills, including digital delivery, procurement and grievance handling. Competency mapping can place the right person in the right role, while supervisors can identify skill gaps rather than rely only on attendance-based training.

However, online course completion is not equivalent to changed conduct. Unequal connectivity, generic content and weak linkage to field outcomes may reduce it to certification.

**Conclusion:** Mission Karmayogi empowers when learning is contextual, multilingual and mentored, and when appraisal measures citizen outcomes, ethical behaviour and application on the ground—not merely modules completed.

**Why this earns marks:** It identifies the scheme’s operating mechanism, connects it to service delivery and adds an implementation qualification.

## Q9. Rohit: armed insurgents and charging women protesters

### Stakeholders and conflicts

Stakeholders include trial participants, patients, researchers, the company, regulators, the patent holder and the wider public. The central conflict is speed and potential lives saved versus rights, evidence reliability and avoidable harm.

### Q12(a). Action

I would reject manipulation, non-consensual enrolment and patent misuse; record the proposals; notify the ethics committee and competent management; and seek a lawful accelerated pathway—adaptive trial design, parallel administrative preparation, additional sites and regulator consultation—without relaxing safety or consent.

### Q12(c). How data and drug ethics protect humanity
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories and Bioethics](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories-and-bioethics.md) → How data and drug ethics protect humanity · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

Data integrity, preregistration, complete adverse-event reporting and reproducibility make efficacy claims trustworthy. Drug ethics requires informed consent, independent review, favourable risk–benefit assessment, fair participant selection and post-trial monitoring. *Samira Kohli* reinforces meaningful consent in medical treatment; the same respect for autonomy is foundational in research.

**Safeguards:** immutable audit trails, independent data-monitoring board, whistleblower protection and transparent correction.

**Why this earns marks:** It refuses false urgency, compares realistic options and supplies a lawful route to rapid but trustworthy innovation.
