## Q1. Manufacturing growth and MSME policy
**Primary route:** Economy → [MSMEs, Startups, PLI, Semiconductors and Manufacturing Strategy](../../topics/economy/msmes-pli-semiconductors-and-manufacturing-strategy.md) → MSMEs · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2023 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 1  
**Official question:** Faster economic growth requires increased share of the manufacturing sector in GDP, particularly of MSMEs. Comment on the present policies of the Government in this regard.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → MSMEs and Manufacturing → Industrial Policy and Competitiveness  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/msmes-manufacturing-policy-growth`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Comment  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/17_MSMEs-PLI-Semiconductors-and-Manufacturing-Strategy.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/17_MSMEs-PLI-Semiconductors-and-Manufacturing-Strategy.md)

### Demand decoding
Explain why MSME-led manufacturing matters, assess the policy mix operating in 2023, and give a qualified verdict based on productivity, jobs and domestic value addition.

### Compact framework
`manufacturing multiplier → formalisation/finance/technology/markets → policy gains → scale, payment and productivity gaps`

### Model answer
Manufacturing creates productivity spillovers, exports and non-farm jobs; MSMEs deepen these through labour intensity and supplier linkages. Policy addresses several constraints:

- **Formalisation:** Udyam Registration and the 2020 composite investment-turnover definition reduce the fear of losing MSME status while scaling.
- **Finance and liquidity:** CGTMSE guarantees, the pandemic-era Emergency Credit Line Guarantee Scheme, TReDS invoice discounting and the MSMED Act’s delayed-payment mechanism target collateral and working-capital gaps.
- **Technology and quality:** Credit Linked Capital Subsidy, cluster development and the Zero Defect Zero Effect framework support upgrading.
- **Markets:** public-procurement preference, GeM and export support widen demand.
- **Industrial ecosystem:** Make in India, Atmanirbhar Bharat and sectoral PLI schemes can create anchor firms and supplier opportunities.

Yet credit remains risk-averse, delayed payments persist, costs are high, and many PLI thresholds favour large firms. Success must be judged by investment, productivity, jobs, exports and domestic value addition—not registrations or sanctioned incentives alone.

### Examiner rationale
The answer comments rather than merely lists schemes and distinguishes policy inputs from measurable manufacturing outcomes.

## Q2. Digitalisation of the Indian economy
**Primary route:** Economy → [Services, Digital Economy, Fintech and Platform Markets](../../topics/economy/services-digital-economy-fintech-and-platform-markets.md) → Inclusion and Governance · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2023 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 2  
**Official question:** What is the status of digitalization in the Indian economy? Examine the problems faced in this regard and suggest improvements.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Digital Economy → Digital Public Infrastructure and Inclusion  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/digitalisation-status-problems-reforms`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Examine + Suggest  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/24_Services-Digital-Economy-Fintech-and-Platform-Markets.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/24_Services-Digital-Economy-Fintech-and-Platform-Markets.md)

### Demand decoding
Give a balanced status assessment, identify infrastructure, capability and governance constraints, and connect each reform to a problem.

### Compact framework
`DPI-led scale → productivity/inclusion gains → access, trust and competition deficits → inclusive secure interoperability`

### Model answer
India has moved from isolated e-governance to population-scale **digital public infrastructure**. Aadhaar-enabled identity, Jan-Dhan accounts and DBT support welfare delivery; UPI enables interoperable payments; GSTN, GeM and e-way bills digitise taxation and procurement; Account Aggregators, ONDC and digital lending extend data and market rails.

## Q4. Land reforms and ceiling policy
**Primary route:** Economy → [Land Reforms, Green Revolution and Cropping Systems](../../topics/economy/land-reforms-green-revolution-and-cropping-systems.md) → Land Ceilings · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2023 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 4  
**Official question:** State the objectives and measures of land reforms in India. Discuss how land ceiling policy on landholding can be considered as an effective reform under economic criteria.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Agrarian Structure → Land Reform and Ceilings  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/land-reforms-ceiling-economic-criteria`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · State + Discuss  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/11_Land-Reforms-Green-Revolution-and-Cropping-Systems.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/11_Land-Reforms-Green-Revolution-and-Cropping-Systems.md)

### Demand decoding
First state objectives and instruments; then evaluate ceilings specifically through efficiency, incentives, distribution and implementation.

### Compact framework
`end intermediaries + secure cultivator + redistribute/consolidate + records → equity and productivity, conditional on implementation`

### Model answer
Land reforms sought to remove exploitative intermediaries, provide security and fair rent to tenants, redistribute surplus land, consolidate fragmented holdings, update records and create an egalitarian, investment-oriented agrarian structure. Measures included zamindari abolition, tenancy regulation, ceilings and redistribution, consolidation, cooperative farming and land-record modernisation.

Economically, transferring surplus land to landless or marginal cultivators raises ownership, bargaining power and rural demand. Owner-cultivators may invest more than insecure tenants; equal distribution can use family labour intensively and reduce disguised unemployment. Kerala and early Jammu and Kashmir show that political backing matters.

But exemptions, benami transfers, litigation and poor records reduced redistribution; tiny unsupported plots may be unviable. Ceilings work best with secure titles, consolidation, credit, irrigation and markets—redistribution plus productivity support, not acreage limits alone.

### Examiner rationale
The response answers both limbs and tests ceiling policy against explicit economic criteria rather than equity alone.

## Q5. AI in clinical diagnosis and privacy
**Primary route:** Science and Technology → [Artificial Intelligence, Governance and IndiaAI](../../topics/science-and-technology/artificial-intelligence-governance-and-indiaai.md) → Healthcare Governance · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2023 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 5  
**Official question:** Introduce the concept of Artificial Intelligence (AI). How does AI help clinical diagnosis? Do you perceive any threat to privacy of the individual in the use of AI in healthcare?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Science and Technology → AI → Clinical Diagnosis and Health Data Privacy  
**Proposed topic slug:** `science-technology/ai-clinical-diagnosis-privacy`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Explain + Evaluate  
**Canonical owner:** [`Science-and-Technology/basic/09_Artificial-Intelligence-Governance-and-IndiaAI.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Science-and-Technology/basic/09_Artificial-Intelligence-Governance-and-IndiaAI.md)

### Demand decoding
Define AI, explain diagnostic assistance through a data-to-output mechanism, and evaluate concrete privacy risks with safeguards.

### Compact framework
`clinical data → trained model → detection/prediction/decision support → privacy, bias and accountability safeguards`

### Model answer
Artificial Intelligence comprises computational systems performing tasks associated with learning, pattern recognition and decision support. Machine-learning models infer patterns from labelled clinical data rather than applying only pre-programmed rules.

In diagnosis, AI can flag abnormalities in scans; predict risk from health records; assist pathology and genomics; triage patients; and combine symptoms and tests for differential diagnosis. It improves speed where specialists are scarce, but supports—not replaces—clinical judgment because accuracy may vary across populations.

Health data reveal intimate conditions; combining records, wearables and genomic data enables re-identification, profiling or discrimination. Breaches and opaque secondary use weaken consent.

Safeguards include purpose limitation, minimisation, encryption, access controls, audit logs, validation, human oversight and appeal. The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 provides a general framework, but healthcare needs sectoral standards.

### Examiner rationale
The answer explains the clinical mechanism while separating accuracy, bias and privacy risks and grounding safeguards in the 2023 legal context.

## Q6. Microorganisms and fuel shortage
**Primary route:** Science and Technology → [Biotechnology Fundamentals and DBT Missions](../../topics/science-and-technology/biotechnology-fundamentals-and-dbt-missions.md) → Microbial Biofuels · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2023 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 6  
**Official question:** Discuss several ways in which microorganisms can help in meeting the current fuel shortage.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Science and Technology → Biotechnology → Biofuels and Bioprocessing  
**Proposed topic slug:** `science-technology/microorganisms-biofuels-energy`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Discuss  
**Canonical owner:** [`Science-and-Technology/basic/13_Biotechnology-Fundamentals-and-DBT-Missions.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Science-and-Technology/basic/13_Biotechnology-Fundamentals-and-DBT-Missions.md)

### Demand decoding
Name several microbial pathways, explain what each converts into fuel, and qualify feedstock, scale and lifecycle limits.

### Compact framework
`waste/biomass/CO₂ + microbial metabolism/enzymes → liquid/gaseous fuels → scale and sustainability tests`

### Model answer
Microorganisms convert renewable feedstocks and wastes into usable energy carriers:

- **Bioethanol:** yeasts such as *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* ferment sugars; microbial cellulases and hemicellulases help release fermentable sugars from crop residue for second-generation ethanol.
- **Biogas/biomethane:** anaerobic microbial consortia hydrolyse and ferment dung, sewage and organic waste; methanogenic archaea produce methane, which can be purified as compressed biogas.
- **Biodiesel precursors:** microalgae accumulate lipids that can be converted into biodiesel while using non-arable space and, potentially, waste CO₂/nutrients.
- **Biohydrogen:** photosynthetic microbes or fermentative bacteria can generate hydrogen, though commercial maturity remains limited.
- **Microbial fuel cells:** electroactive bacteria transfer electrons from organic matter to electrodes, producing electricity while treating wastewater.

These pathways diversify supply and valorise waste. Yet feedstock collection, land competition, conversion efficiency, purification cost and lifecycle emissions determine viability. Policy should favour wastes and residues over food-crop diversion.

### Examiner rationale
The answer provides distinct biological mechanisms and avoids presenting laboratory pathways as universally commercial fuels.

## Q7. Causes and examples of dam failures
**Primary route:** Disaster Management → [Dam Safety and Failure Risk](../../topics/disaster-management/dam-safety-and-failure-risk.md) → Dam Safety and Failure · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2023 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 7  
**Official question:** Dam failures are always catastrophic, especially on the downstream side, resulting in a colossal loss of life and property. Analyze the various causes of dam failures. Give two examples of large dam failures.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Disaster Management → Dam Safety → Failure Mechanisms  
**Proposed topic slug:** `disaster-management/dam-failure-causes-examples`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Analyze  
**Canonical owner:** [`Disaster-Management/basic/08_Riverine-Floods-and-Urban-Flood-Resilience.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Disaster-Management/basic/08_Riverine-Floods-and-Urban-Flood-Resilience.md)

### Demand decoding
Classify failure mechanisms causally and provide exactly two defensible large-failure examples.

### Compact framework
`extreme inflow/design defect/material deterioration/operation failure → breach → flood wave and cascading downstream loss`

### Model answer
A dam fails when hydraulic load exceeds safe discharge or when its structural, foundation or operational integrity collapses.

- **Hydrological causes:** flood underestimation, inadequate spillways, blocked gates and overtopping, followed by rapid erosion.
- **Geotechnical causes:** foundation settlement, piping or internal erosion, slope instability, seepage and abutment failure.
- **Structural/material causes:** poor design or construction, corrosion, ageing and earthquake loading.
- **Operational causes:** faulty gates/instrumentation, sudden releases, poor reservoir rule curves, weak inspection and delayed emergency warnings.
- **External/cascading causes:** landslides into reservoirs, sabotage, upstream-dam failure or extreme rainfall amplified by catchment change.

**Examples:** China’s **Banqiao failure (1975)** followed Typhoon Nina rainfall and cascading reservoir failures; Gujarat’s **Machhu-II failure at Morbi (1979)** involved extreme inflow and breach.

Downstream settlement and late evacuation magnify loss. Safety requires design review, instrumentation, inspection, emergency action plans and warning drills.

### Examiner rationale
The answer analyses distinct failure chains and supplies two named, dated examples without treating every failure as a single “heavy rain” event.

**Primary route:** Environment and Ecology → [Coastal and Marine Ecology CRZ Blue Economy](../../topics/environment/coastal-and-marine-ecology-crz-blue-economy.md) → Oil Pollution · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2023 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 8  
**Official question:** What is oil pollution? What are its impacts on the marine ecosystem? In what way is oil pollution particularly harmful for a country like India?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Environment and Ecology → Marine Pollution → Oil Spills  
**Proposed topic slug:** `environment/oil-pollution-marine-ecosystem-india`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Define + Explain  
**Canonical owner:** [`Environment-and-Ecology/basic/24_Coastal-and-Marine-Ecology-CRZ-Blue-Economy.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Environment-and-Ecology/basic/24_Coastal-and-Marine-Ecology-CRZ-Blue-Economy.md)

### Demand decoding
Define the pollutant and sources, trace ecosystem mechanisms, then explain India-specific exposure and consequences.

### Compact framework
`spill/discharge → slick, toxicity and smothering → food-web/habitat damage → India’s coast, ports, fisheries and livelihoods`

### Model answer
Oil pollution is the entry of crude oil, refined petroleum or oily waste into marine waters through tanker accidents, offshore operations, pipelines, ports, bilge discharge or land-based runoff.

Oil slicks reduce light and air-sea exchange. Hydrocarbons poison plankton, larvae and benthic organisms; oil coats birds and mammals, destroying insulation and buoyancy. It smothers corals, mangroves, seagrass and beaches; residues bioaccumulate and impair reproduction.

India has a long, densely inhabited coastline; tanker routes, ports and offshore production adjoin sensitive waters; and millions depend on fisheries and tourism. Mangroves, reefs and estuaries provide cyclone protection and nursery habitat, multiplying ecological and livelihood losses. Monsoon currents spread pollution across States.

Vessel standards, port facilities, surveillance and contingency planning are therefore essential.

### Examiner rationale
The answer links physical and toxic mechanisms to India’s trade, ecology and livelihood exposure.

## Q10. Cross-border UAV threats
**Primary route:** Internal Security → [Border Management and Border Area Development](../../topics/internal-security/border-management-and-border-area-development.md) → Counter-UAV Architecture · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2023 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 10  
**Official question:** The use of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) by our adversaries across the borders to ferry arms/ammunitions, drugs, etc., is a serious threat to the internal security. Comment on the measures being taken to tackle this threat.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Internal Security → Border Management → Drone-based Smuggling  
**Proposed topic slug:** `internal-security/cross-border-uav-countermeasures`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Comment  
**Canonical owner:** [`Internal-Security/basic/06_Border-Management-and-Border-Area-Development.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Internal-Security/basic/06_Border-Management-and-Border-Area-Development.md)

### Demand decoding
Explain the threat’s low-altitude mechanism, assess current technological and institutional measures, and identify residual gaps.

### Compact framework
`detect → identify/track → jam/spoof/intercept → recover payload → investigate network → adapt`

### Model answer
Small cross-border drones exploit low altitude, low radar signature and short flight time to drop narcotics, weapons, ammunition and counterfeit currency without risking an infiltrator.

India’s response is layered:

- BSF and police deploy observation posts, electro-optical sensors and low-level radars.
- Indigenous and procured anti-drone systems detect and track radio-frequency links, then jam or spoof navigation/control signals; kinetic interception is reserved for suitable locations.
- CIBMS integrates cameras, ground sensors, command centres and patrol response.
- Recovered drones undergo forensic examination; NCB, NIA, DRI, police and intelligence agencies pursue the network.
- Border fencing, floodlighting, community intelligence and monitoring of landing/drop zones provide the physical layer.

## Q11. Structural unemployment and its measurement
**Primary route:** Economy → [Employment, Labour Codes, Skills and Demographic Dividend](../../topics/economy/employment-labour-codes-skills-and-demographic-dividend.md) → Labour-force Measurement · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2023 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 11  
**Official question:** Most of the unemployment in India is structural in nature. Examine the methodology adopted to compute unemployment in the country and suggest improvements.  
**Marks / limit:** 15 / 250 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Employment → Unemployment Types and PLFS Methodology  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/structural-unemployment-measurement-reform`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate-High · Examine + Suggest  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/22_Employment-Labour-Codes-Skills-and-Demographic-Dividend.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/22_Employment-Labour-Codes-Skills-and-Demographic-Dividend.md)

### Demand decoding
Test the structural-unemployment proposition, explain India’s person-and-time status measures, identify what they miss, and propose statistically feasible improvements.

### Compact framework
`sector/skill mismatch → PLFS usual and weekly status → low open unemployment but hidden underemployment → multidimensional labour dashboard`

### Model answer
Structural unemployment arises when workers’ location or skills do not match jobs created by technological and sectoral change. India exhibits it through surplus labour in low-productivity agriculture, educated youth unemployment, weak manufacturing absorption and skill mismatch. Yet seasonal, frictional and cyclical elements also exist; “most” is therefore a defensible tendency, not an exclusive diagnosis.

The **NSO’s Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS)** uses:

- **Usual Status (principal and subsidiary), 365-day reference:** suited to chronic employment and structural/disguised unemployment.
- **Current Weekly Status, seven-day reference:** captures shorter fluctuations; a person working at least one hour on any day is treated as employed for that week.

It reports Labour Force Participation Rate, Worker Population Ratio and Unemployment Rate. Earlier NSS practice also used Current Daily Status to reveal person-days of underemployment.

Limitations are important. Binary employed/unemployed categories understate low hours, low earnings and involuntary self-employment. Unpaid family work and disguised farm labour may appear employed. Discouraged workers leave the labour force, lowering measured unemployment. Informal, migrant, gig and multiple-job work is difficult to capture; annual averages conceal district, gender and seasonal variation.

Improvements should:
1. restore/publish regular hours- and person-day underemployment measures;
2. add earnings, job security, skill mismatch and willingness-for-more-work indicators;
3. increase panel and high-frequency district samples;
4. triangulate PLFS with EPFO/ESIC, e-Shram, tax and vacancy data without replacing surveys;
5. measure migrant, platform, unpaid-care and informal work better; and
6. publish disaggregated confidence intervals and transitions between jobs.

Thus India needs a labour-utilisation dashboard, not one unemployment rate.

### Examiner rationale
The answer examines both the structural claim and measurement architecture, then suggests reforms matched to identifiable blind spots.

## Q12. Care economy and women’s empowerment
**Primary route:** Economy → [Employment, Labour Codes, Skills and Demographic Dividend](../../topics/economy/employment-labour-codes-skills-and-demographic-dividend.md) → Care Economy and Female Labour Participation · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2023 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 12  
**Official question:** Distinguish between ‘care economy’ and ‘monetized economy’. How can care economy be brought into monetized economy through women empowerment?  
**Marks / limit:** 15 / 250 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Labour and Gender → Care Economy  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/care-economy-women-empowerment`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Distinguish + Explain  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/22_Employment-Labour-Codes-Skills-and-Demographic-Dividend.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/22_Employment-Labour-Codes-Skills-and-Demographic-Dividend.md)

### Demand decoding
Define and compare the two economies, then show how empowerment can recognise, reduce, redistribute and reward care without commodifying all family relations.

### Compact framework
`unpaid/reproductive care enables paid production → recognise, reduce, redistribute, reward → decent care services and higher female agency`

### Model answer
The **care economy** comprises paid and unpaid activities that sustain people—childcare, eldercare, disability support, cooking, cleaning, health and education. The **monetized economy** covers goods and services exchanged for money and generally counted within the System of National Accounts and GDP.

| Dimension | Care economy | Monetized economy |
|---|---|---|
| Exchange | often unpaid within households | market/public payment |
| Measurement | time-use surveys; much lies outside GDP | prices, wages and national accounts |
| Workforce | disproportionately women | formally recognised workers/firms |
| Value | reproduces labour and social wellbeing | recorded output and income |

NSO’s Time Use Survey 2019 found women spent about 299 minutes daily on unpaid domestic services against about 97 minutes for men, showing both an uncounted contribution and a constraint on paid work.

Women’s empowerment can bridge the two through:

1. **Recognise:** regular time-use accounts and care satellites in budgeting.
2. **Reduce:** water, clean energy, transport and labour-saving infrastructure.
3. **Redistribute:** parental leave, flexible work and social norms encouraging men and the State to share care.
4. **Reward/formalise:** expand quality crèches, Anganwadis, eldercare, community health and disability services; certify care skills; ensure minimum wages, contracts and social security for domestic, ASHA, Anganwadi and platform care workers.
5. **Agency:** property, credit, SHGs, digital access, safety and collective bargaining enable women to establish care enterprises.

## Q14. Farm subsidies and WTO issues

**Identity:** 2023 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 14  
**Official question:** What are the direct and indirect subsidies provided to farm sector in India? Discuss the issues raised by the World Trade Organization (WTO) in relation to agricultural subsidies.  
**Marks / limit:** 15 / 250 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Agriculture → Farm Subsidies and WTO Rules  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/farm-subsidies-wto-rules`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate-High · Describe + Discuss  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/28_Direct-and-Indirect-Farm-Subsidies-and-WTO-Rules.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/28_Direct-and-Indirect-Farm-Subsidies-and-WTO-Rules.md)

### Demand decoding
Classify Indian support by delivery mechanism, then accurately explain WTO boxes, de minimis limits, market-price-support controversy and India’s food-security defence.

### Compact framework
`domestic support instrument → incidence/distortion → WTO box/AMS calculation → food-security and developing-country reform case`

### Model answer
**Direct subsidies** transfer an identified benefit to farmers: PM-KISAN income support; crop-insurance premium support; interest subvention; capital grants for micro-irrigation, machinery or solar pumps; and price-deficiency payments.

**Indirect subsidies** lower input or service costs or support returns through intermediaries: fertiliser price subsidy, free/concessional electricity, subsidised irrigation and seed, public research and extension, and MSP-backed procurement. - **Green Box** support is minimally trade-distorting, e.g., research, infrastructure and qualifying decoupled payments, and is not reduction-bound.
- **Amber Box/Aggregate Measurement of Support (AMS)** covers product- or production-linked support. Developing countries have a **10% of production value de minimis** threshold.
- **Blue Box** permits specified production-limiting payments.
- **Article 6.2** gives developing countries flexibility for certain investment and input subsidies to low-income/resource-poor producers.

Disputes arise because WTO market-price support is calculated against a fixed **1986–88 external reference price**, which can inflate measured support after decades of inflation. India’s MSP procurement and public food stocks are scrutinised for exceeding limits and potentially depressing world prices through exports. Developed-country Green Box and large entitlement-based support are also criticised as formally compliant yet commercially distortive.

India invokes food security and the **2013 Bali peace clause** for eligible public-stockholding programmes, subject to safeguards. A permanent solution should update reference prices and protect food-security stocks, while India shifts ecologically harmful subsidies towards targeted income support and public goods.

### Examiner rationale
The answer distinguishes domestic categories from WTO legal boxes and explains the core reference-price and public-stockholding controversy.

**Classification:** Science and Technology → Clean Mobility → Electric Vehicles  
**Proposed topic slug:** `science-technology/electric-vehicles-carbon-benefits`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Explain + Compare

### Compact framework
`efficient electric drivetrain + cleaner grid → lower lifecycle emissions → air, energy and operating gains → battery/grid/recycling conditions`

### Model answer
Battery electric vehicles (BEVs) convert stored electricity into motion through an electric motor. They have **zero tailpipe emissions** and are more energy-efficient than internal-combustion-engine (ICE) vehicles, which lose much energy as heat.

1. Centralised electricity generation can progressively decarbonise through solar, wind, hydro and nuclear power; every grid improvement lowers the emissions of the existing EV fleet.
