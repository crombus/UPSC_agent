## Q1(a). Moral integrity and professional efficiency in corporate governance
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Governance and International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/governance-and-international-ethics.md) → Corporate governance · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** What do you understand by ‘moral integrity’ and ‘professional efficiency’ in the context of corporate governance in India? Illustrate with suitable examples. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Corporate Governance · Subtopic: Integrity and professional efficiency · Proposed slug: `moral-integrity-professional-efficiency` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Define, relate and illustrate

**Demand decoding:** Define both terms, show why neither is sufficient alone, and use corporate examples rather than generic public-service illustrations.

### Model answer

**Moral integrity** is consistency between professed values and conduct: honesty, fiduciary loyalty, fairness and refusal to misuse power. **Professional efficiency** is competent and economical achievement of organisational goals through sound systems.

Corporate governance needs both. Integrity without competence may protect intention but destroy stakeholder value; efficiency without integrity can optimise fraud. An independent director who discloses a conflict and resists related-party manipulation displays integrity; a board that builds risk controls, accurate disclosures and succession planning displays efficiency. The Tata group’s withdrawal of the Tata Finance rights issue after irregularities and the later investigation illustrated reputation-protecting accountability. Conversely, the Satyam fraud showed how apparent performance without truthful accounts harms shareholders, employees and markets.

Indian governance mechanisms—independent directors, audit committees, SEBI disclosure norms and Companies Act duties—seek to institutionalise this union.

**Conclusion:** Good governance is ethically efficient: legitimate ends, honest means, competent execution and answerability to all stakeholders.

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes and integrates both concepts, uses named Indian examples and qualifies one-dimensional performance.

## Q1(b). Ethics in contemporary international aid
**Primary route:** Ethics → [International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/international-ethics.md) → Aid, power and global justice · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** ‘International aid’ is an accepted form of helping ‘resource-challenged’ nations. Comment on ‘ethics in contemporary international aid’. Support your answer with suitable examples. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: International Ethics · Subtopic: Ethical development assistance · Proposed slug: `ethics-contemporary-international-aid` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Comment with examples

**Demand decoding:** Assess aid’s moral basis and contemporary defects, then state conditions for ethical aid.

### Model answer

International aid expresses solidarity where poverty, disaster or unequal capacity prevents basic welfare. India’s vaccine and disaster assistance and UN-coordinated relief show aid serving human dignity.

Yet aid becomes unethical when tied to donor procurement, geopolitical alignment or unrelated policy conditionalities; when it creates debt dependence, bypasses recipient institutions or imposes donor priorities. Food aid can save lives, but poorly timed imports may depress local farmers’ prices. Infrastructure lending likewise requires scrutiny of both development benefits and debt vulnerability.

Ethical aid therefore requires recipient ownership, informed local participation, transparency, grants or sustainable finance for global public goods, needs-based rather than strategic allocation, protection from corruption, measurable outcomes and an exit path that builds domestic capacity. Emergency assistance should observe humanity, neutrality, impartiality and independence.

**Conclusion:** Aid is ethical not because resources cross borders, but when power is exercised with consent, fairness and durable capability-building.

**Why this earns marks:** It balances solidarity against domination and links examples to operational ethical tests.

## Q2(a). Corruption as failure of core social values
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Corruption and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/corruption-and-administrative-ethics.md) → Value renewal and integrity systems · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** “Corruption is the manifestation of the failure of core values in the society.” In your opinion, what measures can be adopted to uplift the core values in the society? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Corruption · Subtopic: Social values and prevention · Proposed slug: `uplifting-core-values-against-corruption` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Explain premise and propose measures

**Demand decoding:** Connect corruption to normalised social behaviour, but avoid reducing a systemic problem to moral preaching.

### Model answer

Corruption flourishes when honesty, public spirit and fairness lose social esteem, while wealth and influence are admired regardless of means. It is a value failure, though also an institutional incentive problem.

Values can be strengthened through:

- family and school practice of empathy, civic duty and honest work, not merely moral lessons;
- ethical role-modelling by political, administrative, business and community leaders;
- social disapproval of bribery, ostentatious illicit wealth and favouritism;
- experiential civic education, community service and public recognition of integrity;
- transparent recruitment, e-governance, social audit and citizen charters that make honesty workable;
- protection of whistle-blowers and swift, proportionate punishment that restores faith in fairness;
- codes of ethics, conflict disclosures and ethics training within organisations.

Gandhi’s idea that means shape ends and the Second ARC’s emphasis on an integrity-based administration join character with institutions.

**Conclusion:** Values grow when society teaches integrity, leaders embody it and institutions reward rather than penalise ethical conduct.

**Why this earns marks:** It accepts the value thesis but qualifies it through structural reform and named ethical-administrative anchors.

## Q2(b). Coercion and undue influence in the workplace
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Conflict Administration](../../topics/ethics/conflict-administration.md) → Consent and power asymmetry · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** In the context of work environment, differentiate between ‘coercion’ and ‘undue influence’ with suitable examples. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Ethical Dilemmas · Subtopic: Coercion and undue influence · Proposed slug: `coercion-undue-influence-workplace` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Differentiate with examples

**Demand decoding:** Contrast source, method and effect on consent, while acknowledging overlap in unequal workplaces.

### Model answer

| Test | Coercion | Undue influence |
|---|---|---|
| Method | Threat or force | Improper persuasion exploiting trust, authority or dependence |
| Choice | Compliance induced by fear of harm | Judgment overborne without an explicit threat |
| Workplace example | “Falsify this inspection report or you will be dismissed” | A revered mentor pressures a junior to award a contract to the mentor’s associate |

Coercion attacks voluntariness directly through threatened injury, transfer, dismissal or violence. Undue influence is subtler: a superior, counsellor or trusted colleague uses relational dominance, emotional obligation or informational advantage to obtain an unfair decision. A manager linking promotion to sexual compliance may contain both coercion and abuse of influence.

Ethically, formal consent does not cure either defect. Organisations need protected reporting, recorded reasons, conflict disclosure, review of high-discretion decisions and anti-retaliation safeguards.

**Conclusion:** The distinction lies in fear versus abused ascendancy, but both corrupt autonomous judgment and fair work relations.

**Why this earns marks:** It gives a clear comparative test, workplace examples and a qualification about overlap.

## Q3(a). Gandhi on the power of simple kindness
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Indian Moral Thinkers](../../topics/ethics/indian-moral-thinkers.md) → Compassion in action · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** “The simplest acts of kindness are by far more powerful than a thousand heads bowing in prayer.” — Mahatma Gandhi. What does this quotation convey to you in the present context? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Indian Moral Thinkers · Subtopic: Compassion and practical service · Proposed slug: `gandhi-kindness-over-ritual` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Interpret and apply

**Demand decoding:** Explain the contrast between enacted compassion and ritual profession; do not denigrate sincere faith.

### Model answer

The quotation places lived compassion above performative piety. Prayer may cultivate humility, but its moral worth is tested when concern becomes service to another person.

A civil servant who listens respectfully to an elderly pensioner, a commuter who assists an accident victim, or a community that shares food during a disaster produces immediate dignity and trust. Such small acts also have a multiplier effect: they model prosocial conduct and humanise impersonal institutions. Gandhi’s constructive work and insistence on service to the “last person” embody this union of conviction and action.

The message is especially relevant amid online moral signalling, social polarisation and bureaucratic indifference. However, kindness must not substitute for justice: charity to an excluded citizen cannot excuse denial of her lawful entitlement, and indiscriminate benevolence cannot override fair procedure.

**Conclusion:** Inner faith becomes socially meaningful through compassionate action, while ethical kindness reaches maturity when joined with rights and justice.

**Why this earns marks:** It interprets the quotation, applies it presently and qualifies kindness through justice.

## Q3(b). Nehru on women’s awakening and national progress
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Indian Moral Thinkers](../../topics/ethics/indian-moral-thinkers.md) → Equality, agency and social transformation · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** “To awaken the people, it is the women who must be awakened. Once she is on the move, the family moves, the village moves, the nation moves.” — Jawaharlal Nehru. What does this quotation convey to you in the present context? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Indian Moral Thinkers · Subtopic: Women’s agency and development · Proposed slug: `nehru-women-agency-national-progress` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Interpret and apply

**Demand decoding:** Treat women as rights-bearing agents, not merely instruments for family welfare.

### Model answer

Nehru identifies women’s agency as both intrinsic justice and a catalyst of social transformation. Education, health, property, mobility and voice expand a woman’s capabilities; her participation then improves household decisions, local leadership and national productivity.

India’s women’s self-help groups demonstrate how credit and collective agency can strengthen livelihoods and local accountability. Constitutional equality, girls’ education and women’s representation in Panchayats similarly broaden public priorities; many elected women representatives have foregrounded water, health and violence prevention.

In the present context, “awakening” must mean freedom from discrimination, unpaid-care overload, unsafe workplaces, digital exclusion and token representation. It cannot place the entire burden of family or national progress on women or value them only as mothers and caregivers. Men, institutions and the state must redistribute care and power.

**Conclusion:** A nation moves when women possess equal capabilities and autonomous voice—not when empowerment is celebrated rhetorically while patriarchal structures remain intact.

**Why this earns marks:** It links agency to wider development while rejecting instrumental and paternalistic readings.

## Q3(c). Vivekananda on the return of love and hatred
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Indian Moral Thinkers](../../topics/ethics/indian-moral-thinkers.md) → Character and reciprocal social conduct · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** “Do not hate anybody, because that hatred that comes out from you must, in the long run, come back to you. If you love, that love will come back to you, completing the circle.” — Swami Vivekananda. What does this quotation convey to you in the present context? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Indian Moral Thinkers · Subtopic: Love, hatred and reciprocity · Proposed slug: `vivekananda-love-hatred-reciprocity` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Interpret and apply

**Demand decoding:** Explain psychological and social reciprocity without claiming an automatic cosmic reward.

### Model answer

Vivekananda suggests that emotions shape both the agent and the moral climate around her. Hatred narrows judgment, invites retaliation and normalises dehumanisation; love expressed as empathy and respect encourages trust and cooperation.

The “circle” is visible in polarised social media, communal rhetoric and workplace hostility: contempt is copied and escalated until institutions lose the capacity for dialogue. Conversely, restorative encounters, respectful public communication and compassionate administration can interrupt cycles of humiliation. Nelson Mandela’s reconciliation-oriented leadership illustrates love as disciplined public ethics rather than passivity.

Love does not require tolerating injustice. One may firmly resist discrimination, punish violence and hold wrongdoers accountable without hating their humanity. Indeed, Vivekananda’s message asks that means remain consistent with the humane society sought.

**Conclusion:** In the present context, ethical strength lies in opposing wrong without becoming governed by hatred, thereby converting reciprocity from retaliation into reconciliation.

**Why this earns marks:** It gives a causal reading, a public application and the necessary justice-based qualification.

## Q4(a). Emotional intelligence and lifelong achievement
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Emotional Intelligence and Ethical Dilemmas](../../topics/ethics/emotional-intelligence-and-ethical-dilemmas.md) → EQ and performance · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** “What really matters for success, character, happiness and lifelong achievements is a definite set of emotional skills—your EQ—not just purely cognitive abilities that are measured by conventional IQ tests.” Do you agree with this view? Give reasons in support of your answer. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Emotional Intelligence · Subtopic: EQ, IQ and achievement · Proposed slug: `eq-iq-success-character` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Take a position and justify

**Demand decoding:** Explain EQ’s contribution, compare it with IQ and avoid an absolute either-or claim.

### Model answer

I substantially agree. Emotional intelligence—self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy and social skill—converts cognitive ability into sustainable conduct.

An administrator may understand flood logistics intellectually, yet needs emotional regulation under pressure, empathy for victims and communication that coordinates agencies. A technically brilliant manager who cannot receive criticism may damage teamwork; an emotionally aware leader can resolve conflict and maintain public trust. Daniel Goleman popularised this competency-based account, while Aristotle’s practical wisdom reminds us that right action requires fitting judgment, not knowledge alone.

EQ also supports character by enabling reflection on impulses and the effects of one’s conduct. However, empathy can be selective or manipulative, and emotional skill cannot replace domain knowledge, constitutional values or analytical competence. Complex public decisions need both accurate cognition and humane execution.

**Conclusion:** IQ helps determine what can be done; ethically formed EQ helps decide and deliver what should be done, with others and over time.

**Why this earns marks:** It supports the proposition with administrative application but rejects the false displacement of IQ and ethics.

## Q4(b). Moral intuition and moral reasoning
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → Intuition and deliberation · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** Differentiate ‘moral intuition’ from ‘moral reasoning’ with suitable examples. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Moral Psychology · Subtopic: Intuition and reasoning · Proposed slug: `moral-intuition-moral-reasoning` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Differentiate with examples

**Demand decoding:** Contrast speed, basis, strengths and risks; show how both should interact.

### Model answer

| Moral intuition | Moral reasoning |
|---|---|
| Immediate, affect-laden judgment | Deliberate evaluation through principles and evidence |
| Often formed by experience and social learning | Makes premises, consequences and consistency explicit |
| Fast in emergencies but vulnerable to prejudice | More reviewable but can rationalise self-interest |

A doctor’s instant impulse to help an unconscious accident victim is intuitive; deciding how to allocate one ICU bed among patients requires reasoning about urgency, prognosis, equality and procedure. An officer may intuitively distrust a migrant applicant because of stereotype; reasoned scrutiny against constitutional equality can correct that reaction.

Hume highlights sentiment’s motivational role, while Kantian ethics stresses principled deliberation. Good ethical judgment uses intuition as an alert, not a verdict: pause where possible, test the response for facts, rights, consequences, reversibility and impartiality, then record reasons.

**Conclusion:** Intuition supplies moral perception and urgency; reasoning supplies consistency and accountability. Mature judgment disciplines each through the other.

**Why this earns marks:** It differentiates the concepts, links thinkers and shows a practical synthesis.

## Q5(a). Conscience compared with laws, rules and regulations
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → Conscience, law and public duty · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** Is conscience a more reliable guide when compared to laws, rules and regulations in the context of ethical decision-making? Discuss. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Sources of Ethical Guidance · Subtopic: Conscience and formal rules · Proposed slug: `conscience-versus-law-rules` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Compare and discuss

**Demand decoding:** Evaluate reliability in both ordinary administration and morally defective legal settings.

### Model answer

Conscience is the inward judgment that an act is right or wrong. It can expose morally inadequate rules: Gandhi’s civil disobedience shows conscience resisting injustice.

It is not invariably more reliable. Conscience may be misinformed, prejudiced or self-serving. Laws and rules provide authorised, predictable and reviewable standards protecting citizens from an official’s private morality. Yet rules can be unjust or mechanically applied.

For a civil servant, the sound hierarchy is: obey constitutional and lawful authority; interpret discretion through rights, empathy and public interest; record reasons; seek clarification or review; and use protected dissent where an order is illegal or gravely unethical. Rawls’s account of civil disobedience similarly treats conscientious breach as public, principled and exceptional.

**Conclusion:** Conscience is an indispensable moral alarm, not a solitary licence. Reliability comes from testing it against facts, constitutional morality, reason and accountable procedure.

**Why this earns marks:** It balances inner conviction and rule-of-law values and supplies a usable administrative sequence.

## Q5(b). Probity, governance and socio-economic development
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Corruption and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/corruption-and-administrative-ethics.md) → Probity and public value · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** ‘Probity is essential for an effective system of governance and socio-economic development.’ Discuss. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Probity in Governance · Subtopic: Integrity and development outcomes · Proposed slug: `probity-governance-development` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Explain and establish linkage

**Demand decoding:** Define probity and trace mechanisms from clean conduct to governance and development.

### Model answer

Probity is adherence to integrity, impartiality, transparency and public-purpose standards in exercising entrusted power.

It improves governance by reducing leakage, conflicts of interest and arbitrary discretion; enabling fair procurement and merit-based decisions; strengthening audit and legislative oversight; and making citizens willing to cooperate with institutions. Socially, clean beneficiary selection protects the poor. Economically, predictable regulation and honest contracting lower transaction costs and improve investment and infrastructure quality. Tools such as e-procurement, social audits under MGNREGA, asset declarations and the CAG convert probity from personal virtue into institutional practice.

However, probity is not procedural rigidity. Excessive fear of investigation can create decision paralysis, while transparency without capacity cannot deliver services. Officials need clear rules, timely approvals, protection for bona fide decisions and proportionate accountability.

**Conclusion:** Probity is development infrastructure: it ensures that public resources, authority and trust are converted into equitable outcomes rather than private advantage.

**Why this earns marks:** It defines the concept, establishes causal links and qualifies integrity with administrative effectiveness.

## Q6(a). Guru Nanak’s teachings and contemporary relevance
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Indian Moral Thinkers](../../topics/ethics/indian-moral-thinkers.md) → Guru Nanak · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** What were the major teachings of Guru Nanak? Explain their relevance in the contemporary world. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Indian Moral Thinkers · Subtopic: Guru Nanak’s ethical teachings · Proposed slug: `guru-nanak-teachings-relevance` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Explain teachings and relevance

**Demand decoding:** Present accurate teachings and connect each to a modern ethical problem.

### Model answer

Guru Nanak taught the oneness of the divine and humanity, remembered through *Naam Japna*; honest labour through *Kirat Karni*; sharing through *Vand Chhakna*; equality beyond caste, creed and gender; compassion, humility and rejection of empty ritual. *Langar* institutionalised dignity and fellowship through eating together.

His travels and dialogue across traditions model respectful pluralism rather than either intolerance or indifferent relativism.

Application must go beyond symbolic celebration: institutions should ensure non-discrimination, dignified labour, fair distribution and service to vulnerable persons. Spiritual equality without material and institutional reform remains incomplete.

**Conclusion:** Guru Nanak’s ethic joins inner remembrance with truthful work, equality and shared service—an integrated response to alienation, inequality and polarisation.

**Why this earns marks:** It accurately names core teachings and links them analytically to present problems.

## Q6(b). Social capital and good governance
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Governance and International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/governance-and-international-ethics.md) → Trust and civic networks · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** Explain the term social capital. How does it enhance good governance? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Ethical Governance · Subtopic: Social capital · Proposed slug: `social-capital-good-governance` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Define and explain mechanisms

**Demand decoding:** Distinguish bonding, bridging and linking capital and show governance effects plus risks.

### Model answer

Social capital is the stock of trust, shared norms and networks that enables people to cooperate. Putnam distinguishes civic networks that facilitate collective action; it may be **bonding** within a group, **bridging** across groups and **linking** citizens with institutions.

It enhances governance by lowering transaction costs; improving information and compliance; enabling participation, social audit and co-production; and building crisis resilience. Women’s self-help groups improve credit discipline and local voice; community monitoring can expose service gaps; trusted networks accelerate disaster relief.

However, strong bonding capital may become exclusionary caste, clan or patronage power, spread misinformation or capture benefits. Good governance must therefore cultivate bridging and linking networks through inclusive Gram Sabhas, transparent data, fair representation and accessible grievance redress.

**Conclusion:** Social capital converts isolated citizens into cooperative public actors, but it serves good governance only when trust is inclusive and institutions remain rights-based and accountable.

**Why this earns marks:** It defines types, explains causal mechanisms and recognises capture and exclusion risks.

## Q8. Emergency blood transfusion during Uttarkashi landslide relief
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Bioethics and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/bioethics-and-administrative-ethics.md) → Emergency care and legal compliance · **Secondary route:** Ethics → case-study method · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Case:** During landslide rescue, a pregnant woman is haemorrhaging. Team members can donate and basic collection/testing materials are available, but the DM-physician knows transfusion blood should be procured through a recognised blood bank. Doctors will assist if protected from penalty. **(250 words, 20 marks)**

**Official demands:** (a) What are the ethical issues involved in this case? (b) Evaluate the options available to you, being District Magistrate of the area.

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Bioethics and Disaster Administration · Subtopic: Necessity, non-maleficence and emergency discretion · Proposed slug: `landslide-emergency-blood-transfusion` · Difficulty: High · Demands: Ethical issues; evaluate options as DM

### Case framework

**Stakeholders:** woman and foetus; family; donors; medical team; other victims; DM and health authorities.  
**Values/conflicts:** life and beneficence versus safety and legality; urgency versus process; duty versus liability.

<a id="2023-mains-gs4-q8a"></a>
### Q8(a). Ethical issues involved
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Bioethics and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/bioethics-and-administrative-ethics.md) → Ethical issues involved · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

Delay may cause death, yet unsafe blood can cause fatal reaction or infection. Consent, donor safety, competence, resource allocation and liability matter. This is not simply law versus humanity: regulation embodies non-maleficence.

<a id="2023-mains-gs4-q8b"></a>
### Q8(b). Options and decision as District Magistrate
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Bioethics and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/bioethics-and-administrative-ethics.md) → Options and decision as District Magistrate · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

1. **Wait for recognised-bank blood:** safest legally, but potentially fatal delay.  
2. **Immediate field transfusion:** may save life, but creates serious compatibility, infection and liability risks.  
3. **Parallel response:** activate evacuation and the nearest blood bank; obtain specialist advice and consent; stabilise haemorrhage; and, only if death is imminent and no alternative can arrive, let qualified doctors take the minimum necessary step with available testing and documentation.

I would choose option 3, record the emergency, seek health-authority approval, protect good-faith clinicians where law permits and never dictate the clinical procedure.

**Consequences and safeguards:** preserve samples and records, monitor donor/patient, arrange post-exposure testing, report the exception, conduct independent review, deploy emergency blood-storage units and revise district disaster SOPs.

**Conclusion:** Necessity may justify a tightly bounded exception, not casual suspension of safety rules; the least unsafe life-saving route must remain clinically led and reviewable.

**Why this earns marks:** It refuses both mechanical delay and reckless heroism, while operationalising necessity, clinical authority and safeguards.

## Q9. Joint Secretary facing chronic work–family conflict
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Work Culture and Public Funds](../../topics/ethics/work-culture-and-public-funds.md) → Humane and equitable workplace · **Secondary route:** Ethics → case-study method · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Case:** Rashika, a Joint Secretary and mother of two young children, repeatedly works long hours and is assigned an urgent Sunday task. She questions whether dedication and selfless service should have reasonable limits. **(250 words, 20 marks)**

**Official demands:** (a) Discuss the ethical issues involved in this case. (b) Briefly describe at least four laws that have been enacted by the Government with respect to providing a healthy, safe and equitable working environment for women. (c) Imagine you are in a similar situation. What suggestions would you make to mitigate such working conditions?

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Work Culture · Subtopic: Duty, care and gender-equitable conditions · Proposed slug: `joint-secretary-work-family-conflict` · Difficulty: High · Demands: Ethical issues; four laws; mitigation suggestions

### Case framework

**Stakeholders:** Rashika, family, caregiver, superior, colleagues, ministry and citizens.  
**Values/conflicts:** public duty versus care; dedication versus health; urgency versus planning; equality versus gendered burdens.

<a id="2023-mains-gs4-q9a"></a>
### Q9(a). Ethical issues
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Work Culture and Public Funds](../../topics/ethics/work-culture-and-public-funds.md) → Ethical issues · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

Chronic overwork impairs judgment, family care and health. Unpredictable assignments raise fairness, dignity and whether “selfless service” normalises poor management. Care burdens and workplace expectations are gendered; citizens also suffer from burnout.

<a id="2023-mains-gs4-q9b"></a>
### Q9(b). Laws supporting a healthy, safe and equitable workplace for women
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Work Culture and Public Funds](../../topics/ethics/work-culture-and-public-funds.md) → Laws supporting a healthy, safe and equitable workplace for women · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

1. **POSH Act, 2013:** prevention, Internal Committee and redress.  
2. **Maternity Benefit Act, 1961/2017:** leave, crèche-related and enabling protections in covered establishments.  
3. **Factories Act, 1948:** health, welfare and working-hour safeguards in covered factories.  
4. **Equal Remuneration Act, 1976:** historically protected equal pay and non-discriminatory recruitment; these protections enter the enacted **Code on Wages, 2019**, subject to commencement.  
Articles 14–16 and 42 provide the constitutional foundation; applicability must be stated.

<a id="2023-mains-gs4-q9c"></a>
### Q9(c). Suggestions
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Work Culture and Public Funds](../../topics/ethics/work-culture-and-public-funds.md) → Suggestions · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

I would clarify urgency, negotiate deliverables and redistribute work without abandoning the task. Institutionally: advance planning, rosters, compensatory leave, secure flexible work, care support, workload audits, delegation, adequate staffing and protected reporting of overload.

**Safeguards:** gender-neutral care leave, outcome-based metrics, wellbeing review and senior accountability for avoidable emergencies.

**Conclusion:** Public service may require exceptional sacrifice, but routine sacrifice caused by defective work design is neither efficient nor ethical.

**Why this earns marks:** It answers all three demands, accurately qualifies the legal anchors and joins individual response with structural reform.

## Q10. Honest SRTC Managing Director facing politicised corruption allegations
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Corruption and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/corruption-and-administrative-ethics.md) → Political neutrality and protected integrity · **Secondary route:** Ethics → case-study method · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Case:** Vinod, an upright IAS officer and SRTC Managing Director, receives apparently incriminating material against the politically powerful Chairman from an Opposition Board member who offers future career benefit. Vinod risks retaliation and recognises partisan manipulation. **(250 words, 20 marks)**

**Official demands:** (a) As a civil servant, evaluate the options available to Vinod. (b) In the light of the above case, comment upon the ethical issues that may arise due to the politicization of bureaucracy.

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Civil-Service Ethics · Subtopic: Politicisation, evidence and integrity · Proposed slug: `srtc-chairman-politicisation-corruption` · Difficulty: High · Demands: Evaluate Vinod’s options; comment on politicisation

### Case framework

**Stakeholders:** Vinod; Chairman; Opposition member; corporation, vendors, government, passengers and investigators.  
**Values/conflicts:** integrity versus self-preservation; neutrality versus pressure; public interest versus partisan gain.

<a id="2023-mains-gs4-q10a"></a>
### Q10(a). Options available to Vinod
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Corruption and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/corruption-and-administrative-ethics.md) → Options available to Vinod · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

1. **Ignore or destroy the material:** reduces immediate risk but abets corruption and violates duty.  
2. **Publicly leak it or join the Opposition:** may trigger scrutiny but compromises neutrality, due process and evidentiary integrity.  
3. **Confront the Chairman alone:** signals courage but risks intimidation and destruction of evidence.  
4. **Secure and verify evidence, recuse where necessary, and refer it through lawful channels:** best balances integrity and fairness.

Vinod should reject promises, document receipt, preserve chain of custody, seek independent audit, refer the matter to competent vigilance/anti-corruption and Board processes, protect pending procurement and record unlawful directions. He should use service remedies against retaliation and communicate institutionally.

<a id="2023-mains-gs4-q10b"></a>
### Q10(b). Ethical issues from politicisation of bureaucracy
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Corruption and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/corruption-and-administrative-ethics.md) → Ethical issues from politicisation of bureaucracy · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

Politicisation replaces constitutional with party loyalty; distorts postings; enables selective investigation; chills honest advice; weakens merit and trust; and makes officials instruments against opponents.

**Safeguards:** minimum tenure, Civil Services Boards, transparent postings, protected dissent, independent vigilance, procurement audit and sanctions for political interference.

**Conclusion:** Vinod must be politically neutral, not morally neutral—acting on verified evidence through institutions rather than becoming either the Chairman’s shield or the Opposition’s weapon.

**Why this earns marks:** It compares options, identifies the best institutional course and directly analyses bureaucratic politicisation.

## Q11. Harassment of architect Seema by an insecure Chief Architect
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Work Culture and Public Funds](../../topics/ethics/work-culture-and-public-funds.md) → Workplace dignity and leadership · **Secondary route:** Ethics → case-study method · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official demands:** (a) What are the ethical issues involved in the above case? (b) What are the options available to you in order to complete the project as well as to retain Seema in the organization? (c) What would be your response to Seema’s predicament? What measures would you institute to prevent such occurrences from happening in your organization?

### Case framework

**Stakeholders:** Seema, Chief Architect, project team, ADG, department and project users.  
**Values/conflicts:** dignity; merit and credit; experience versus innovation; continuity versus accountability.

<a id="2023-mains-gs4-q11a"></a>
### Q11(a). Ethical issues
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Work Culture and Public Funds](../../topics/ethics/work-culture-and-public-funds.md) → Ethical issues · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

Public humiliation and passive aggression abuse hierarchy, violate dignity, cause psychological harm, suppress dissent and endanger quality. Gender bias should be investigated, not assumed; bullying may exist without meeting the statutory definition of sexual harassment.

<a id="2023-mains-gs4-q11b"></a>
### Q11(b). Options to complete the project and retain Seema
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Work Culture and Public Funds](../../topics/ethics/work-culture-and-public-funds.md) → Options to complete the project and retain Seema · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

Ignoring the conduct risks resignation; immediate removal may disrupt handover; mediation alone may reproduce the power imbalance. **Conclusion:** Experience deserves respect, not immunity; innovation deserves credit, not personality-based favour. Fair process can protect both dignity and project delivery.

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes bullying from automatically proven sexual harassment and combines immediate protection, due process and systemic prevention.

## Q12. Cyberbullying, viral misinformation and a parent’s counter-video
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Service Delivery and Technology Ethics](../../topics/ethics/service-delivery-and-technology-ethics.md) → Social-media responsibility and child protection · **Secondary route:** Ethics → case-study method · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Case:** A senior government official’s 11-year-old son is bullied offline and on WhatsApp. After a false viral video accuses the parent of physically bullying children, the parent posts a counter-video identifying likely minor perpetrators and explaining events. **(250 words, 20 marks)**

**Official demands:** (a) Based on the above case study, discuss the ethical issues involved in the use of social media. (b) Discuss the pros and cons of using social media by you to put across the facts to counter the fake propaganda against your family.

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Technology Ethics · Subtopic: Cyberbullying, privacy and counter-speech · Proposed slug: `cyberbullying-minors-counter-video` · Difficulty: High · Demands: Ethical issues in social-media use; pros and cons of counter-video

### Case framework

**Stakeholders:** victim, alleged perpetrators, families, school, platform, official and public.  
**Values/conflicts:** child safety versus privacy; reputation versus restraint; protection versus due process.

<a id="2023-mains-gs4-q12a"></a>
### Q12(a). Ethical issues in social-media use
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Service Delivery and Technology Ethics](../../topics/ethics/service-delivery-and-technology-ethics.md) → Ethical issues in social-media use · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

The case involves cyberbullying, false accusation, unverified virality, minors’ privacy, permanent digital footprints and unequal power because the parent is a senior official. Naming “likely” perpetrators risks misidentification, vigilantism and reciprocal harm. The son’s wishes require sensitive engagement, though parents retain protective duty.

<a id="2023-mains-gs4-q12b"></a>
### Q12(b). Pros and cons of using social media to counter fake propaganda
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Service Delivery and Technology Ethics](../../topics/ethics/service-delivery-and-technology-ethics.md) → Pros and cons of using social media to counter fake propaganda · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Pros:** rapid correction, reputation protection, evidence-based counter-speech and public education.  
**Cons:** amplification, exposure of minors, online trial, retaliation, prejudiced inquiry and apparent misuse of official status.

I would not post identifying footage. I would preserve screenshots and metadata, request platform takedown, inform the school’s child-protection/grievance mechanism, seek counselling for my son, contact parents through the school and, if necessary, report cyberbullying through appropriate legal channels. A brief non-identifying statement may deny the false physical-bullying allegation while an inquiry proceeds.

**Safeguards:** school digital-conduct policy, confidential reporting, restorative intervention where safe, media-literacy education, restricted group administration, counselling and proportional discipline.

**Conclusion:** Counter-speech is legitimate, but a public official’s higher reach creates a higher duty of verification, privacy and restraint—especially where children are involved.

**Why this earns marks:** It evaluates both sides, corrects the protagonist’s overreaction and provides child-centred, proportionate safeguards.
