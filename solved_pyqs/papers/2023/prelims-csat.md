## Q1. Water-allocation policy message
**Primary route:** CSAT → [Reading Comprehension](../../topics/csat/reading-comprehension.md) → Water-allocation policy message · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

We often hear about conflicts among different States in India over river waters. Of the 20 major river systems, 14 are already water- stressed; 75% of the population lives in water- stressed regions, a third of whom live in water- scarce areas. Climate change, the demands of rising population and the need for agriculture to keep pace, and increased rate of urbanization and industrialization will exacerbate water stress. According to the Constitution of India, water is a State subject and not that of the Union, except for regulation of inter- State rivers. Key to ensuring balance between competing demands of various stakeholders is a basin- based approach to allocate water amongst constituent regions and States. Allocating fair share of water to them requires assessments based on objective criteria, such as specificities of the river basin, size of dependent population, existing water use and demand, efficiency of use, projected future use, etc. while ensuring the environmental needs of the river and aquifers.

Which one of the following statements best reflects the most rational, practical and immediate action required to ensure fair and equitable allocation of water to different stakeholders?

**Options:** (a) A national, pragmatic, legal and policy framework for water allocation should be made. · (b) All river systems of the country should be linked and huge aquifers created. · (c) Water channels between regions of water surplus and regions of water deficit should be created. · (d) To mitigate water crisis, water demand of sectors such as agriculture and industry should be reduced.

**Metadata:** Family 01 · Reading Comprehension → Water-allocation policy message · Slug: `water-allocation-policy-message-2023-q01`  
**Canonical owner:** `CSAT/basic/01_Reading-Comprehension.md`

**FINAL answer: (a).**

**Full reasoning:** The passage asks for an immediate allocation mechanism, not a supply project. A national pragmatic legal-policy framework can set basin-level objective criteria and reconcile State interests in inter-State rivers. River-linking and new channels are large infrastructure choices, while demand reduction does not itself decide fair shares. Hence (a).

**Shortest safe method:** Match “immediate allocation” to a national legal-policy framework; reject infrastructure and demand-side diversions.

## Q2. Health, nutrition and growth implication
**Primary route:** CSAT → [Reading Comprehension](../../topics/csat/reading-comprehension.md) → Health, nutrition and growth implication · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

More than half of Indian women and almost a quarter of Indian men of working age suffer from anaemia. According to studies, they are anywhere from 5 - 15% less productive than they could be, as a result thereof. India also has the largest tuberculosis burden in the world, costing 170 million workdays to the country annually. But what is just as important as lost productivity now is lost potential in the future. It is becoming increasingly clear that on many measures of cognitive ability, malnourished Indian children perform two or three times worse than their adequately nourished peers. For an economy that will be more dependent on highly skilled workers, this poses a significant challenge. And it is one that really should be addressed given India's demographic outlook.

Which one of the following statements best reflects what is implied by the passage?

**Options:** (a) Education system must be strengthened in rural areas. · (b) Large scale and effective implementation of skill development programme is the need of the hour. · (c) For economic development, health and nutrition of only skilled workers needs special attention. · (d) For rapid economic growth as envisaged by us, attention should be paid to health and nutrition of the people.

**Metadata:** Family 01 · Reading Comprehension → Health, nutrition and growth implication · Slug: `health-nutrition-and-growth-implication-2023-q02`  
**Canonical owner:** `CSAT/basic/01_Reading-Comprehension.md`

**FINAL answer: (d).**

**Full reasoning:** The passage links anaemia and tuberculosis to present productivity loss and childhood malnutrition to future cognitive and skill loss. Its concern covers the population, not only rural education, skill programmes or already-skilled workers. Therefore attention to people’s health and nutrition is the necessary implication, giving (d).

**Shortest safe method:** attention to people’s health and nutrition is the necessary implication, giving (d).

## Q4. Worst-case shoe selection
**Primary route:** CSAT → [Logical Reasoning, Coding, Counting and Data Interpretation](../../topics/csat/logical-reasoning-coding-counting-and-di.md) → Worst-case shoe selection · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

Raj has ten pairs of red, nine pairs of white and eight pairs of black shoes in a box. If he randomly picks shoes one by one (without replacement) from the box to get a red pair of shoes to wear, what is the maximum number of attempts he has to make?

**Options:** (a) 27 · (b) 36 · (c) 44 · (d) 45

**Metadata:** Family 06 · Logical Reasoning, Coding, Counting and DI → Worst-case shoe selection · Slug: `worst-case-shoe-selection-2023-q04`  
**Canonical owner:** `CSAT/basic/06_Logical-Reasoning-Coding-Counting-and-DI.md`

**FINAL answer: (d).**

**Full reasoning:** There are 34 non-red shoes. In the worst case Raj then draws one shoe from each of the 10 red pairs, reaching 44 draws without a wearable red pair. The 45th draw must match one of those ten red shoes. Thus the guaranteed maximum is 45, option (d).

**Shortest safe method:** Worst case = all 34 non-red shoes + one shoe from each of 10 red pairs + 1 = 45.

## Q5. Run-scoring combinations
**Primary route:** CSAT → [Logical Reasoning, Coding, Counting and Data Interpretation](../../topics/csat/logical-reasoning-coding-counting-and-di.md) → Run-scoring combinations · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

In how many ways can a batsman score exactly 25 runs by scoring single runs, fours and sixes only, irrespective of the sequence of scoring shots?

**Options:** (a) 18 · (b) 19 · (c) 20 · (d) 21

**Metadata:** Family 06 · Logical Reasoning, Coding, Counting and DI → Run-scoring combinations · Slug: `run-scoring-combinations-2023-q05`  
**Canonical owner:** `CSAT/basic/06_Logical-Reasoning-Coding-Counting-and-DI.md`

**FINAL answer: (b).**

**Full reasoning:** Let f and s be the numbers of fours and sixes; singles equal 25-4f-6s and must be non-negative. Count integer pairs with 2f+3s≤12: for s=0,1,2,3,4 the counts are 7,5,4,2,1. Total 19, so (b).

**Shortest safe method:** Count non-negative (f,s) with 4f+6s≤25: 7+5+4+2+1=19.

## Q6. Letters and envelopes
**Primary route:** CSAT → [Logical Reasoning, Coding, Counting and Data Interpretation](../../topics/csat/logical-reasoning-coding-counting-and-di.md) → Letters and envelopes · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

There are four letters and four envelopes and exactly one letter is to be put in exactly one envelope with the correct address. If the letters are randomly inserted into the envelopes, then consider the following statements:
1. It is possible that exactly one letter goes into an incorrect envelope.
2. There are only six ways in which only two letters can go into the correct envelopes.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

**Options:** (a) 1 only · (b) 2 only · (c) Both 1 and 2 · (d) Neither 1 nor 2

**Metadata:** Family 06 · Logical Reasoning, Coding, Counting and DI → Letters and envelopes · Slug: `letters-and-envelopes-2023-q06`  
**Canonical owner:** `CSAT/basic/06_Logical-Reasoning-Coding-Counting-and-DI.md`

**FINAL answer: (b).**

**Full reasoning:** Exactly one incorrect envelope is impossible: if one letter is misplaced, the letter belonging to that envelope must also be misplaced. Exactly two correct letters means choose those two in C(4,2)=6 ways; the remaining two must swap. Thus statement 1 is false and statement 2 true: (b).

**Shortest safe method:** One wrong placement is impossible; choose two correct envelopes in C(4,2)=6 ways, so only statement 2.

## Q9. Digit-sum cryptarithm
**Primary route:** CSAT → [Number Systems and Number Sense](../../topics/csat/number-systems-and-number-sense.md) → Digit-sum cryptarithm · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

If ABC and DEF are both 3-digit numbers such that A, B, C, D, E and F are distinct non-zero digits such that ABC + DEF = 1111 , then what is the value of A + B + C + D + E + F ?

**Options:** (a) 28 · (b) 29 · (c) 30 · (d) 31

**Metadata:** Family 02 · Number Systems and Number Sense → Digit-sum cryptarithm · Slug: `digit-sum-cryptarithm-2023-q09`  
**Canonical owner:** `CSAT/basic/02_Number-Systems-and-Number-Sense.md`

**FINAL answer: (d).**

**Full reasoning:** Column addition to 1111 forces C+F=11, B+E+1=11 and A+D+1=11. Hence C+F=11, B+E=10 and A+D=10; the six-digit sum is 31. Distinctness can be satisfied, and the value is fixed. Option (d).

**Shortest safe method:** C+F=11, B+E=10 and A+D=10; the six-digit sum is 31. Distinctness can be satisfied, and the value is fixed. Option (d).

## Q14. Parity of five integers
**Primary route:** CSAT → [Number Systems and Number Sense](../../topics/csat/number-systems-and-number-sense.md) → Parity of five integers · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

Three of the five positive integers p, q, r, s, t are even and two of them are odd (not necessarily in order). Consider the following: 1. p + q + r - s - t is definitely even. 2. 2p + q + 2r - 2s + t is definitely odd. Which of the above statements is/are correct?

**Options:** (a) 1 only · (b) 2 only · (c) Both 1 and 2 · (d) Neither 1 nor 2

**Metadata:** Family 02 · Number Systems and Number Sense → Parity of five integers · Slug: `parity-of-five-integers-2023-q14`  
**Canonical owner:** `CSAT/basic/02_Number-Systems-and-Number-Sense.md`

**FINAL answer: (a).**

**Full reasoning:** Modulo 2, subtraction equals addition. Statement 1 has the parity of all five numbers: two odds sum to even, so it is even. Statement 2 reduces to q+t; whether q and t contain zero, one or two of the odd integers is not fixed, so it is not definitely odd. Only 1 is correct: (a).

**Shortest safe method:** Reduce modulo 2: statement 1 is fixed even; q+t in statement 2 is not fixed.

**Shortest safe method:** One witness each proves “can”: (3,9) works for all three.
