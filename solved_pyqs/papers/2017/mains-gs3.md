## Q1. Savings and India’s potential growth
**Primary route:** Economy → [Growth, Development, HDI, IHDI and MPI](../../topics/economy/growth-development-hdi-ihdi-and-mpi.md) → Sources of potential growth · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2017 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 1  
**Official question:** Among several factors for India’s potential growth, savings rate is the most effective one. Do you agree? What are the other factors available for growth potential?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Growth and Development → Potential Output  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/growth-development-hdi-ihdi-and-mpi`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Evaluate a proposition + Identify complementary drivers  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/02_Growth-Development-HDI-IHDI-and-MPI.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/02_Growth-Development-HDI-IHDI-and-MPI.md)

### Demand decoding
Test whether savings alone determines potential growth, explain its investment channel, and identify capital productivity, labour, technology and institutional complements.

### Compact framework
`saving → investible resources; growth = capital accumulation × efficient allocation + labour + productivity + institutions`

### Model answer
Domestic savings provide resources for investment, reduce dependence on volatile foreign capital and can enlarge the productive capital stock. India’s high-saving phase in the 2000s coincided with faster investment and growth. Thus savings are important, but not automatically the “most effective” factor.

Growth depends on whether savings are intermediated into productive investment rather than locked in gold, real estate or stressed projects. Other drivers are:

- human capital, health, skills and female labour-force participation;
- infrastructure—power, transport, irrigation and digital networks;
- technological progress, innovation and managerial efficiency;
- efficient financial intermediation and resolution of bank NPAs;
- competitive markets, ease of entry and predictable regulation;
- agricultural productivity, urbanisation and movement of labour to higher-productivity sectors; and
- macroeconomic stability, trade integration and capable institutions.

Therefore, savings expand the **quantity** of capital, while productivity and institutions determine its return. India’s potential growth requires both adequate savings and efficient, inclusive deployment.

### Examiner rationale
The answer accepts the savings–investment channel but rejects monocausality, distinguishes mobilisation from productive use and identifies the supply-side determinants of potential output.

## Q2. Labour-intensive manufacturing exports
**Primary route:** Economy → [Employment, Labour Codes, Skills and Demographic Dividend](../../topics/economy/employment-labour-codes-skills-and-demographic-dividend.md) → Employment-intensive manufacturing · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2017 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 2  
**Official question:** Account for the failure of manufacturing sector in achieving the goal of labour-intensive exports. Suggest measures for more labour-intensive rather than capital-intensive exports.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Industry and Employment → Labour-intensive Exports  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/employment-labour-codes-skills-and-demographic-dividend`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Diagnose + Prescribe  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/22_Employment-Labour-Codes-Skills-and-Demographic-Dividend.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/22_Employment-Labour-Codes-Skills-and-Demographic-Dividend.md)

### Demand decoding
Explain why India did not replicate East Asian export-led job creation and propose measures targeted at garments, footwear, food processing, toys and similar sectors.

### Compact framework
`small firms + high logistics/finance/compliance costs + skill gaps + inverted incentives → capital bias; clusters + scale + trade logistics + labour skills → job-rich exports`

### Model answer
India’s manufacturing exports have been skill- and capital-intensive because labour-abundant sectors face structural disadvantages:

- firms remain sub-scale due to fragmented land, finance and compliance systems;
- unreliable power, costly logistics and port delays erode competitiveness;
- regulatory uncertainty encouraged automation or contractualisation;
- credit and tax incentives often favoured machinery over employment;
- weak vocational skills, design, quality certification and integration into global value chains; and
- competitors such as Bangladesh and Vietnam gained scale in garments and footwear.

India should develop plug-and-play clusters; simplify labour compliance while protecting wages and safety; correct duty inversions; provide working capital to MSMEs; improve ports, power and trade facilitation; align skilling with employers; and support quality, design and markets. The 2016 apparel package’s employment-linked support offered a direction.

Policy should reward formal jobs and exports, not merely capital expenditure, while avoiding protection that weakens competitiveness.

### Examiner rationale
The response connects firm scale, costs, regulation and trade integration to capital bias and offers sector-specific, employment-linked remedies rather than generic industrial policy.

## Q3. Airport development through PPP joint ventures
**Primary route:** Economy → [Infrastructure, PPPs, Logistics and Public Investment](../../topics/economy/infrastructure-ppps-logistics-and-public-investment.md) → Airport PPPs · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2017 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 3  
**Official question:** Examine the development of Airports in India through joint ventures under Public-Private Partnership (PPP) model. What are the challenges faced by the authorities in this regard?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Infrastructure → Airport PPPs  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/infrastructure-ppps-logistics-and-public-investment`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Examine performance + Identify regulatory challenges  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/18_Infrastructure-PPPs-Logistics-and-Public-Investment.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/18_Infrastructure-PPPs-Logistics-and-Public-Investment.md)

### Demand decoding
Use Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru and Hyderabad to assess what joint-venture PPPs delivered and why tariff, land, risk and regulatory disputes arose.

### Compact framework
`private finance/operation + public land/oversight → capacity and service gains; weak contracts/regulation → tariff, land and risk disputes`

### Model answer
Airport PPPs combined public assets and oversight with private finance, construction and operations. Greenfield Bengaluru and Hyderabad airports and modernisation of Delhi and Mumbai through joint ventures expanded terminal capacity, improved passenger amenities, introduced commercial management and reduced the immediate fiscal burden on the Airports Authority of India (AAI). Delhi also became a major hub.

Authorities nevertheless face:

- uncertain traffic forecasts and disputes over revenue sharing and risk allocation;
- high aeronautical charges and the treatment of non-aeronautical revenue by AERA;
- land acquisition, rehabilitation and monetisation of valuable airport land;
- ensuring competition where an airport is a local monopoly;
- coordination among AAI, private operators, airlines, security agencies and State governments;
- delays in clearances and connectivity; and
- balancing bankability with affordable regional access.

Hence PPPs can deliver capacity and efficiency, but require transparent bidding, independent tariff regulation, clear land-use rules, realistic demand assumptions and enforceable service standards.

### Examiner rationale
The answer assesses demonstrated gains and then explains the distinctive governance problems of a regulated, land-intensive natural monopoly.

## Q4. Agricultural revolutions, poverty and food security
**Primary route:** Economy → [Land Reforms, Green Revolution and Cropping Systems](../../topics/economy/land-reforms-green-revolution-and-cropping-systems.md) → Post-independence agricultural transformation · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2017 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 4  
**Official question:** Explain various types of revolutions, took place in Agriculture after independence in India. How these revolutions have helped in poverty alleviation and food security in India?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Agriculture → Agricultural Revolutions  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/land-reforms-green-revolution-and-cropping-systems`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Easy-Moderate · Explain + Link to outcomes  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/11_Land-Reforms-Green-Revolution-and-Cropping-Systems.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/11_Land-Reforms-Green-Revolution-and-Cropping-Systems.md)

### Demand decoding
Name the principal production revolutions, state their mechanisms and connect them—not merely their output—to livelihoods and nutrition, with qualifications.

### Compact framework
`technology + institutions + markets → cereals/milk/oilseeds/fisheries/horticulture → availability + income + employment`

### Model answer
Post-independence agricultural transformation comprised several “revolutions”:

- **Green Revolution:** HYV seeds, irrigation, fertiliser, credit and MSP-led procurement raised wheat and rice output.
- **White Revolution:** Operation Flood and dairy cooperatives created a national milk grid.
- **Yellow Revolution:** oilseed technology and the Technology Mission on Oilseeds raised domestic production.
- **Blue Revolution:** expansion of inland and marine fisheries and aquaculture.
- Horticultural growth—the **Golden Revolution**—diversified output into fruits, vegetables and high-value crops.

The Green Revolution ended chronic import dependence and supported buffer stocks and the PDS. Dairy, fisheries and horticulture generated more regular, labour-intensive income for smallholders and landless households, improving purchasing power and nutrition. Agro-processing and allied activities also created rural employment.

However, benefits were regionally unequal; cereal monoculture depleted water and soil, while pulses and nutrition received less attention. Food security therefore requires diversification, sustainable inputs, procurement beyond cereals and inclusive access to technology and markets.

### Examiner rationale
The answer identifies genuine programmes, links production to entitlement and income, and qualifies output gains with regional, ecological and nutritional limitations.

## Q5. Small food-processing units and poor farmers
**Primary route:** Economy → [Food Processing, Cold Chains and Value Addition](../../topics/economy/food-processing-cold-chains-and-value-addition.md) → Decentralised processing · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2017 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 5  
**Official question:** What are the reasons for poor acceptance of cost-effective small processing unit? How the food processing unit will be helpful to uplift the socio-economic status of poor farmers?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Agriculture → Food Processing  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/food-processing-cold-chains-and-value-addition`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Explain constraints + Trace welfare mechanism  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/15_Food-Processing-Cold-Chains-and-Value-Addition.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/15_Food-Processing-Cold-Chains-and-Value-Addition.md)

### Demand decoding
Explain why apparently low-cost technology is not adopted and how local processing changes losses, prices, jobs and bargaining power.

### Compact framework
`technology alone − finance/skills/standards/markets = low adoption; local value addition → shelf life + farmer share + rural jobs`

### Model answer
Small processing units face poor acceptance despite modest equipment cost because entrepreneurs lack working capital, reliable power and cold storage; raw material is seasonal and scattered; machinery repair and technical skills are scarce; food-safety, packaging and labelling compliance appears complex; and weak branding, aggregation and market linkages make sales uncertain. Credit institutions also perceive first-generation rural processors as risky.

Properly supported units can:

- reduce post-harvest loss and extend shelf life;
- convert gluts into storable products, improving price realisation;
- retain grading, milling, pulping or packaging value within villages;
- create non-farm employment, especially for women;
- enable smallholders to aggregate through cooperatives or FPOs; and
- diversify diets and supply local institutions.

The welfare gain is not automatic: processors can also acquire monopsony power. Common facilities, Mudra/NABARD credit, food-safety training, cold-chain links, FPO ownership and assured procurement can make decentralised processing both viable and farmer-centred.

### Examiner rationale
The answer distinguishes equipment affordability from enterprise viability and explains the income channel while recognising buyer-power risk.

## Q6. Stem-cell therapy
**Primary route:** Science and Technology → [Biotechnology Fundamentals and DBT Missions](../../topics/science-and-technology/biotechnology-fundamentals-and-dbt-missions.md) → Regenerative medicine · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2017 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 6  
**Official question:** Stem cell therapy is gaining popularity in India to treat a wide variety of medical conditions including Leukaemia, Thalassemia, damaged cornea and severe burns. Describe briefly what stem cell therapy is and what advantages it has over other treatments?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Science and Technology → Biotechnology → Stem-cell Therapy  
**Proposed topic slug:** `science-and-technology/biotechnology-fundamentals-and-dbt-missions`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Define + Explain comparative advantages with caution  
**Canonical owner:** [`Science-and-Technology/basic/13_Biotechnology-Fundamentals-and-DBT-Missions.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Science-and-Technology/basic/13_Biotechnology-Fundamentals-and-DBT-Missions.md)

### Demand decoding
Define stem cells through self-renewal and differentiation, identify established therapeutic uses and avoid treating experimental claims as proven cures.

### Compact framework
`self-renewal + differentiation → replace/repair tissue; proven haematopoietic uses ≠ universal cure`

### Model answer
Stem-cell therapy uses undifferentiated cells capable of **self-renewal** and differentiation to replace, repair or regenerate damaged cells. Sources include bone marrow, peripheral or cord blood, and tissue-specific stem cells.

Its best-established use is haematopoietic stem-cell transplantation after conditioning for disorders such as leukaemia and thalassaemia. Cultured limbal stem cells can reconstruct damaged corneal surfaces, while skin stem-cell sheets may aid severe burns.

Unlike drugs that mainly control symptoms, stem cells can restore blood formation or regenerate tissue. Autologous cells may reduce rejection; targeted transplantation can reduce lifelong supportive treatment.

However, advantage depends on indication. Transplantation may cause infection, graft-versus-host disease or treatment-related mortality; uncontrolled cells can form tumours. Many advertised treatments remained experimental in 2017. Ethical sourcing, informed consent, long-term follow-up and regulation under ICMR–DBT guidelines are therefore essential.

### Examiner rationale
The response explains the biological basis and genuine advantages while clearly separating established therapies from unproven commercial claims.

## Q7. Obstacles to a manned space mission
**Primary route:** Science and Technology → [Human Spaceflight, Gaganyaan and Planetary Missions](../../topics/science-and-technology/human-spaceflight-gaganyaan-and-planetary-missions.md) → Human-spaceflight capability · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2017 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 7  
**Official question:** India has achieved remarkable successes in unmanned space missions including the Chandrayaan and Mars Orbiter Mission, but has not ventured into manned space missions. What are the main obstacles to launching a manned space mission, both in terms of technology and logistics? Examine critically.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Science and Technology → Space → Human Spaceflight  
**Proposed topic slug:** `science-and-technology/human-spaceflight-gaganyaan-and-planetary-missions`  
**Difficulty / demand:** High · Examine technical and logistical barriers critically  
**Canonical owner:** [`Science-and-Technology/basic/03_Human-Spaceflight-Gaganyaan-and-Planetary-Missions.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Science-and-Technology/basic/03_Human-Spaceflight-Gaganyaan-and-Planetary-Missions.md)

### Demand decoding
Show why human-rating is qualitatively different from robotic missions and cover launch, life support, abort, recovery, training and programme economics as of 2017.

### Compact framework
`human-rated launcher + crew module + life support + abort/recovery + training/mission control → very low tolerated risk`

### Model answer
Human spaceflight tolerates much lower failure probability than an unmanned mission.

**Technology:** a human-rated launch vehicle with redundancy; crew module, thermal protection and safe re-entry; environmental control and life-support; reliable navigation and communication; crew-escape system throughout dangerous ascent phases; radiation and microgravity protection; and precise splashdown, tracking and recovery. Every subsystem requires repeated integrated testing.

**Logistics:** astronaut selection and training; simulators, biomedical monitoring and mission control; recovery ships and medical teams; specialised suppliers and quality assurance; sustained funding; and international assistance without strategic dependence.

India possessed PSLV/GSLV experience and had tested a crew-module atmospheric re-entry experiment in 2014, but a complete human-rated ecosystem was still absent in 2017. The issue was therefore not scientific incapacity but cost, reliability and opportunity cost. A phased programme—technology demonstrators, abort tests, unmanned flights and then crewed flight—was the prudent route.

### Examiner rationale
The answer distinguishes mission success from human-rating, covers both dimensions and gives a historically accurate 2017 readiness assessment.

## Q8. River linking and environmental impacts
**Primary route:** Economy → [Irrigation, Inputs, Credit, Insurance and Sustainable Agriculture](../../topics/economy/irrigation-inputs-credit-insurance-and-sustainable-agriculture.md) → Inter-basin water transfer · **Secondary route:** Environment → [Water Pollution and River Cleaning Missions](../../topics/environment/water-pollution-and-river-cleaning-missions.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2017 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 8  
**Official question:** Not many years ago, river linking was a concept but it is becoming a reality in the country. Discuss the advantages of river linking and its possible impact on the environment.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Water Resources → River Interlinking  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/irrigation-inputs-credit-insurance-and-sustainable-agriculture`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Discuss benefits and environmental costs  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/14_Irrigation-Inputs-Credit-Insurance-and-Sustainable-Agriculture.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/14_Irrigation-Inputs-Credit-Insurance-and-Sustainable-Agriculture.md)

### Demand decoding
Balance claimed redistribution benefits against basin ecology, displacement and uncertainty; avoid assuming that all “surplus” monsoon water is ecologically disposable.

### Compact framework
`inter-basin storage/transfer → irrigation, supply, moderation; altered flow/sediment/ecology + displacement → basin-specific appraisal`

### Model answer
River linking seeks to store and transfer water from assessed surplus basins to deficit regions. Potential gains include expanded irrigation and drinking-water supply, drought buffering, some flood moderation, hydropower and inland navigation. The Ken–Betwa project represented movement from a national concept towards implementation.

Environmental costs can be substantial:

- submergence and fragmentation of forests and wildlife habitat;
- displacement and livelihood loss;
- altered downstream flow, sediment and nutrient regimes;
- impacts on fisheries, wetlands, deltas and saline-water balance;
- invasive species and disease transfer between basins;
- evaporation and energy costs of long canals; and
- cumulative effects overlooked by project-wise appraisal.

“Surplus” is seasonal and climate-sensitive, while environmental flow is not waste. Linking may shift rather than solve disputes.

Therefore, each link requires transparent basin-level hydrology, environmental-flow guarantees, cumulative impact assessment and consent-based rehabilitation. Demand management, watershed restoration, efficient irrigation and aquifer recharge should precede costly transfers.

### Examiner rationale
The answer gives the claimed benefits but tests the surplus-deficit premise and applies an ecosystem and alternatives framework.

## Q9. Cyber-attack threats and prevention framework
**Primary route:** Internal Security → [Cyber Security, CII and Cybercrime](../../topics/internal-security/cyber-security-cii-and-cybercrime.md) → Threats and national cyber architecture · **Secondary route:** Science and Technology → [Data Protection and Cybersecurity](../../topics/science-and-technology/data-protection-dpdp-act-and-cybersecurity.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2017 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 9  
**Official question:** Discuss the potential threats of Cyber attack and the security framework to prevent it.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Internal Security → Cyber Security → Threats and Prevention  
**Proposed topic slug:** `internal-security/cyber-security-cii-and-cybercrime`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Classify threats + Explain preventive framework  
**Canonical owner:** [`Internal-Security/basic/08_Cyber-Security-CII-and-Cybercrime.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Internal-Security/basic/08_Cyber-Security-CII-and-Cybercrime.md)

### Demand decoding
Cover targets, methods and consequences, then map prevention across institutions, technology, law, organisations and citizens using the architecture existing in 2017.

### Compact framework
`attack surface × vulnerability × intent → espionage/disruption/theft; CERT-In + NCIIPC + layered resilience`

### Model answer
Cyber attacks can steal personal, financial, commercial or strategic data; defraud citizens; conduct espionage; spread ransomware and disinformation; or disrupt critical infrastructure such as power, banking, telecom, transport and defence. Threat actors include criminals, insiders, hacktivists, terrorists and State-backed groups, using phishing, malware, denial-of-service attacks, supply-chain compromise and exploitation of unpatched systems.

India’s framework included the **Information Technology Act, 2000**, **CERT-In** for incident response, and **NCIIPC** under section 70A for critical infrastructure, guided by the **National Cyber Security Policy, 2013**.

Prevention requires asset and vulnerability mapping, timely patching, encryption, multi-factor authentication, network segmentation, secure coding, audits and tested backups. Sectoral computer emergency teams, real-time information sharing, cyber drills, skilled personnel, police forensics and international cooperation must complement user awareness.

Because perfect prevention is impossible, the objective must be resilience: detect early, contain rapidly, maintain essential services, investigate lawfully and recover from clean backups.

### Examiner rationale
The answer integrates threat taxonomy with the correct 2017 institutions and treats cyber security as layered risk management rather than a single technological barrier.

## Q10. Survival of armed insurgency in the North-East
**Primary route:** Internal Security → [North-East Insurgency and Peace Processes](../../topics/internal-security/north-east-insurgency-and-peace-processes.md) → Persistence of insurgency · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2017 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 10  
**Official question:** The North-East region of India has been infested with insurgency for a very long time. Analyze the major reasons for the survival of armed insurgency in this region.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Internal Security → Insurgency → North-East India  
**Proposed topic slug:** `internal-security/north-east-insurgency-and-peace-processes`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Causal analysis  
**Canonical owner:** [`Internal-Security/basic/04_North-East-Insurgency-and-Peace-Processes.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Internal-Security/basic/04_North-East-Insurgency-and-Peace-Processes.md)

### Demand decoding
Explain persistence through interacting historical, ethnic, geographic, governance and conflict-economy factors while recognising variation across States and organisations.

### Compact framework
`contested identity + weak settlement/governance + terrain/borders + illicit finance → organisational survival`

### Model answer
North-Eastern insurgencies are diverse, but several interacting conditions explain their persistence:

- colonial-era administrative separation, contested integration and rival ideas of ethnic homeland;
- overlapping territorial claims among tribes and States, making one group’s settlement another’s grievance;
- perceived political under-representation, cultural insecurity and uneven development;
- difficult terrain, dispersed settlements and limited State presence;
- porous borders enabling sanctuary, arms movement and extortion networks;
- unemployment and weak delivery that facilitate recruitment;
- factional splits, criminalisation and a conflict economy based on “taxation”, smuggling and contracts; and
- governance abuses and civilian harm that reproduce mistrust.

External support mattered historically, but Bangladesh’s action against sanctuaries and peace accords reduced several groups. The remaining violence is geographically uneven; treating the region as uniformly insurgent is misleading.

Durable peace requires calibrated security, implementation of accords, inter-ethnic dialogue, accountable policing, border cooperation and development designed with local institutions rather than imposed merely as expenditure.

### Examiner rationale
The response gives a multi-causal explanation, avoids homogenising the region and distinguishes containment from a political settlement.

**Identity:** 2017 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 11  
**Official question:** One of the intended objectives of the Union Budget 2017-18 is to ‘transform, energise and clean India’. Analyse the measures proposed in the Budget 2017-18 to achieve the objective.  
**Marks / limit:** 15 / 250 words

**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/union-budget-fiscal-policy-and-deficit-indicators`  
**Difficulty / demand:** High · Organise exact Budget measures under its three-part objective and assess  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/09_Union-Budget-Fiscal-Policy-and-Deficit-Indicators.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/09_Union-Budget-Fiscal-Policy-and-Deficit-Indicators.md)

### Demand decoding
Decode the Budget’s official TEC India formulation—transform governance and life quality, energise vulnerable groups and clean the country of corruption, black money and non-transparent political funding—and assess the fit of announced measures.

### Compact framework
`Transform institutions/infrastructure + Energise youth/poor/rural economy + Clean finance/governance`

### Model answer
Budget 2017–18 presented **TEC India** as: *Transform* the quality of governance and life; *Energise* society, especially youth and the vulnerable; and *Clean* the country from corruption, black money and non-transparent political funding.

- advancement of the Railway Budget merger, abolition of Plan/non-Plan classification and a single Budget date sought more integrated public finance;
- higher allocations for roads, railways and transport infrastructure aimed to reduce logistics constraints;

- political donations in cash were capped at ₹2,000 per person and electoral bonds were proposed;
- limits on cash expenditure and digital-payment promotion sought formalisation;
- lower presumptive income for digital receipts rewarded non-cash business.

The package linked fiscal reform, inclusion and formalisation. **Difficulty / demand:** High · Diagnose agronomic decline + Explain stabilisation mechanism

Focus on yield stagnation in intensive rice–wheat rotations and explain how diversification improves system productivity and resilience—not merely farmer prices.

### Compact framework
`continuous cereal rotation → soil/water/pest fatigue → factor-productivity decline; legumes/oilseeds/fodder + rotation → restored ecology + risk spreading`

### Model answer
The intensive rice–wheat system initially raised output, but repeated monocropping has produced declining **marginal response** and yield stagnation in major Green Revolution regions.

- mining of micronutrients and imbalanced nitrogen-heavy fertilisation;
- falling soil organic carbon, compaction and deterioration of soil structure;

**Official question:** Give an account of the growth and development of nuclear science and technology in India. What is the advantage of fast breeder reactor programme in India?  
**Marks / limit:** 15 / 250 words  
**Classification:** Science and Technology → Nuclear Technology → Fast Breeder Reactors  
**Proposed topic slug:** `science-and-technology/nuclear-power-and-three-stage-programme`  
**Difficulty / demand:** High · Chronological account + Explain strategic/technical advantage  
**Canonical owner:** [`Science-and-Technology/basic/04_Nuclear-Power-and-Three-Stage-Programme.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Science-and-Technology/basic/04_Nuclear-Power-and-Three-Stage-Programme.md)

### Demand decoding
Give a selective institutional and technological chronology, explain Homi Bhabha’s three-stage logic and show why fast breeders multiply fissile material for India’s resource profile.

### Compact framework
`institutions + PHWR capability → closed fuel cycle; U-238/Pu fast breeder → more fissile fuel + bridge to U-233/thorium`

### Model answer
India built an indigenous nuclear programme around strategic autonomy and peaceful applications. The **Atomic Energy Commission** was established in 1948, the Department of Atomic Energy in 1954, and Trombay’s research complex—later BARC—developed reactor, fuel-cycle and isotope capability. **Apsara** became Asia’s first research reactor in 1956.

1. natural-uranium PHWRs produce electricity and plutonium;

The programme’s strategic value lies in resource multiplication and a closed fuel cycle, provided stringent independent safety and economic scrutiny are maintained.
