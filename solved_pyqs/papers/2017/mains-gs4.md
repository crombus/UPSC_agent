## Q1. Conflict of interest in the public sector

**Official question:** Conflict of interest in the public sector arises when (a) official duties, (b) public interest, and (c) personal interest are taking priority one above the other. How can this conflict in administration be resolved? Describe with an example. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2017 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q1 · Directive: Explain resolution with example · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Emotional Intelligence and Ethical Dilemmas](../../topics/ethics/emotional-intelligence-and-ethical-dilemmas.md) → conflict of interest · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Public-service dilemmas](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/09_Public-Service-Values-Status-and-Ethical-Dilemmas.md) · [Probity](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/14_Probity-Concept-and-Philosophical-Basis-of-Governance.md)

### Demand decoding

Define the conflict, establish the proper hierarchy among duty, public interest and private interest, and give an operational resolution rather than merely advising honesty.

### Model answer

A conflict of interest exists when an official's private interest can improperly influence—or reasonably appear to influence—public duty. Official duty is the entrusted role; public interest is its constitutional purpose; personal interest must not capture either.

Resolution requires early written disclosure, recusal, referral to an unconflicted authority, objective pre-published criteria, recorded reasons and audit. If recusal is impossible in an emergency, the officer should minimise discretion, seek collective approval and permit ex-post scrutiny.

For example, an officer on a tender committee discovers that a sibling owns one bidder. Even if personally confident of impartiality, she should disclose the relationship, abstain and let an independent committee apply identical criteria. Deciding herself creates either favouritism or compensatory bias.

Public office is a fiduciary trust. Integrity is protected not by claiming inner neutrality but by arrangements that make impartiality visible and verifiable.

**Examiner takeaway:** The answer converts probity into the disclose–recuse–refer–review sequence.

## Q2. Relevance of five values in civil service

**Official question:** Examine the relevance of the following in the context of civil service: (a) Transparency (b) Accountability (c) Fairness and justice (d) Courage of conviction (e) Spirit of service. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2017 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q2 · Directive: Examine relevance · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Work Culture and Public Funds](../../topics/ethics/work-culture-and-public-funds.md) → foundational values · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Foundational values](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/04_Aptitude-and-Foundational-Values-for-Civil-Service.md) · [Accountability](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/11_Accountability-and-Ethical-Governance.md)

### Demand decoding

Define all five values and connect each to an administrative outcome.

### Model answer

Civil service converts constitutional promises into daily state action:

- **Transparency:** accessible rules, records and reasons reduce arbitrariness and enable participation, subject to narrow privacy and security limits.
- **Accountability:** answerability plus corrective consequence makes delegated power reviewable.
- **Fairness and justice:** hearing, relevant criteria and protection for vulnerability secure procedural and substantive justice.
- **Courage of conviction:** truthful advice and resistance to unlawful pressure protect constitutional government; courage is not obstinacy.
- **Spirit of service:** empathy, responsiveness and priority to the weakest prevent office from becoming privilege.

Fair disaster relief, for example, needs published criteria, traceable decisions, assisted access for excluded persons and an officer willing to reject partisan lists.

These values reinforce one another: transparency enables accountability; fairness gives it direction; courage sustains it under pressure; service keeps it citizen-centred.

**Examiner takeaway:** Each value is defined, applied and qualified.

## Q3. Motivating ethical young people to join active politics

**Official question:** Young people with ethical conduct are not willing to come forward to join active politics. Suggest steps to motivate them to come forward. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2017 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q3 · Directive: Suggest steps · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Corruption and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/corruption-and-administrative-ethics.md) → ethical political participation · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Human values](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/02_Human-Values-and-Lessons-from-Leaders.md) · [Probity](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/14_Probity-Concept-and-Philosophical-Basis-of-Governance.md)

### Demand decoding

Diagnose deterrents and propose institutional, party-level and social measures; exhortation alone is insufficient.

### Model answer

Ethical youth may avoid politics because of opaque entry, money and muscle power, dynastic gatekeeping, reputational risk and the apparent electoral disadvantage of clean conduct. Democracy then suffers adverse selection.

Reforms should include transparent party recruitment and internal elections; merit-based youth roles; timely disclosure of candidate finances, interests and criminal cases; cleaner campaign finance and expenditure monitoring; swift adjudication of serious offences with due process; mentoring, fellowships and local-government pathways; protection against intimidation; civic education and recognition of ethical service; and party ethics bodies protecting principled dissent.

Gandhi viewed politics as service, while Aristotle treated citizenship as participation in the common good. Yet ethical entrants also need winnable constituencies and organisational support. The aim is to reduce the structural penalty on integrity, not romanticise sacrifice.

**Examiner takeaway:** Sustainable motivation combines civic ideals with reform of political incentives and entry barriers.

## Q4(a). Integrity and refusal to compromise

**Official question:** One of the tests of integrity is complete refusal to be compromised. Explain with reference to a real life example. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2017 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q4(a) · Directive: Explain with real-life example · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Corruption and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/corruption-and-administrative-ethics.md) → integrity under pressure · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owner:** [Foundational values](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/04_Aptitude-and-Foundational-Values-for-Civil-Service.md)

### Demand decoding

Explain what may never be compromised and distinguish principled firmness from inflexibility over lawful means.

### Model answer

Integrity is consistency among values, words and conduct, especially when deviation offers reward or avoids personal cost. “Refusal to be compromised” means rejecting bribery, falsification, discrimination or betrayal of public trust—not rejecting every negotiation.

E. Sreedharan's public reputation in major transport projects illustrates institution-centred integrity: decisions were tied to time, quality and transparent responsibility rather than patronage. The lesson is that credibility enables resistance to shortcuts and makes teams answerable to objective standards.

An engineer pressured to certify substandard material should refuse, record tests, seek written directions and report through the competent hierarchy. Quiet withdrawal may preserve personal purity but leave citizens exposed.

Kantian duty explains why wrong means cannot be traded for convenience; virtue ethics adds courage and practical wisdom. Integrity permits consultation and compromise among legitimate interests. What remains non-negotiable is legality, truthfulness, dignity and public purpose.

**Examiner takeaway:** Integrity combines ethical red lines with flexible lawful problem-solving.

## Q4(b). Corporate social responsibility, profitability and sustainability

**Official question:** Corporate social responsibility makes companies more profitable and sustainable. Analyse. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2017 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q4(b) · Directive: Analyse · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/international-ethics.md) → stakeholder and corporate responsibility · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owner:** [Corporate governance and international ethics](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/12_Corporate-Governance-and-International-Ethics.md)

### Demand decoding

Trace the mechanisms connecting CSR to profit and sustainability, then qualify the claim.

### Model answer

Corporate social responsibility is an enterprise's responsibility towards stakeholders and the social and ecological effects of its operations, beyond narrow shareholder return.

CSR may build trust and brand value, retain employees, reduce litigation and regulatory risk, improve resource efficiency, secure a social licence and encourage innovation. Worker-safety investment reduces disruption; credible local watershed work may improve both community resilience and continuity of production. Stakeholder theory thus connects durable value to workers, consumers, communities and nature.

However, the relationship is neither automatic nor immediate. Cleaner technology and fair wages can raise present costs. Cosmetic philanthropy or “greenwashing” cannot offset unsafe products, pollution, tax abuse or exploitative supply chains. Projects chosen without community participation may also misallocate resources.

CSR makes firms more sustainable when embedded in governance, core strategy, measurable impact and transparent accountability—not used as charity to purchase legitimacy.

**Examiner takeaway:** Responsible stakeholder management can create long-term value only where the core business is ethical.

## Q5(a). Ambition, principles and rulers

**Official question:** “Great ambition is the passion of a great character. Those endowed with it may perform very good or very bad acts. All depends on the principles which direct them.” — Napoleon Bonaparte. Stating examples mention the rulers (i) who have harmed society and country, (ii) who worked for the development of society and country. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2017 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q5(a) · Directive: Interpret and illustrate · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Indian Moral Thinkers](../../topics/ethics/indian-moral-thinkers.md) → leadership and moral ends · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Human values and leaders](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/02_Human-Values-and-Lessons-from-Leaders.md) · [Moral theories](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/08_Moral-Theories-Deontology-Consequentialism-Virtue-Ethics.md)

### Model answer

Ambition supplies scale, persistence and organisational energy; it does not determine moral direction. Character becomes socially beneficial only when ambition is disciplined by justice, dignity and accountability.

Hitler's expansionism, racial ideology and totalitarian rule devastated Germany, Europe and targeted communities. His example shows how charisma and state capacity, directed by hatred and unchecked power, magnify evil.

By contrast, Emperor Ashoka—after Kalinga—redirected imperial energy towards *dhamma*, welfare, tolerance and restraint. Akbar's *sulh-i-kul*, dialogue and administrative integration likewise used authority to stabilise a diverse society. These examples do not imply flawless reigns; they show comparatively public-regarding principles directing power.

Aristotle held that virtues order capacities towards the good; Kant notes that talents are not unconditionally good without a good will. Rule of law, counsel and checks remain necessary because no ruler's claimed benevolence is sufficient.

**Examiner takeaway:** Ambition is an amplifier; principles and institutions determine what it amplifies.

## Q5(b). Parents, teachers and a corruption-free nation

**Official question:** “If a country is to be corruption free and become a nation of beautiful minds, I strongly feel there are three key societal members who can make a difference. They are the father, the mother and the teacher.” — A.P.J. Abdul Kalam. Analyse. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2017 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q5(b) · Directive: Analyse · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Indian Moral Thinkers](../../topics/ethics/indian-moral-thinkers.md) → moral socialisation · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Human values](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/02_Human-Values-and-Lessons-from-Leaders.md) · [Indian thinkers](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/06_Indian-Moral-Thinkers-and-Philosophers.md)

### Model answer

Kalam locates anti-corruption at moral formation. Parents teach honesty, empathy and restraint mainly through example: children notice whether adults reject unfair advantage or rationalise it. Teachers cultivate reason, constitutional values, scientific temper and courage to question wrongdoing. Together they shape conscience before sanctions become necessary.

This reflects virtue ethics: repeated good practices form stable character. Integrity activities, transparent assessment, community service and moral-dilemma discussion can connect achievement with service. Parenting responsibility must be gender-equal; the quotation should not burden mothers alone.

Yet socialisation is necessary, not sufficient. Even ethical persons may succumb where systems reward rent-seeking and punish dissent. Transparent procedures, reduced discretion, audits, protected reporting, fair appraisal and credible sanctions must support character.

Family and teacher provide inner restraint; just institutions make ethical conduct viable and corruption costly.

**Examiner takeaway:** Moral education and institutional design are complementary, not substitutes.

## Q6(a). Applying emotional intelligence in administration

**Official question:** How will you apply emotional intelligence in administrative practices? (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2017 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q6(a) · Directive: Explain application · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Emotional Intelligence and Ethical Dilemmas](../../topics/ethics/emotional-intelligence-and-ethical-dilemmas.md) → administrative application · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owner:** [Emotional intelligence](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/05_Emotional-Intelligence-in-Administration.md)

### Model answer

Emotional intelligence (EI) is the capacity to recognise and regulate one's emotions and respond constructively to those of others.

I would apply it by recognising stress and bias before deciding; pausing and verifying facts rather than reacting; listening empathetically to grievances; communicating adverse decisions respectfully; motivating teams through feedback and psychological safety; mediating by separating persons from issues; reading the emotional climate during disasters or protests; and practising resilience under pressure.

During an agitated compensation hearing, empathy should secure a patient hearing and clear explanation, while self-regulation prevents retaliation. Objective eligibility criteria must still determine the award.

Aristotle's practical wisdom integrates reason and appropriately ordered emotion. EI is therefore not appeasement or manipulation; it is emotion-informed rationality bounded by law, fairness and public interest.

**Examiner takeaway:** EI improves judgment, communication and trust without replacing rules or evidence.

## Q6(b). Strength, peace and security in international relations

**Official question:** Strength, peace and security are considered to be the pillars of international relations. Elucidate. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2017 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q6(b) · Directive: Elucidate · Difficulty: Moderate-High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/international-ethics.md) → power, peace and common security · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owner:** [International ethics](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/12_Corporate-Governance-and-International-Ethics.md)

### Model answer

**Strength** is a state's economic, diplomatic, technological, institutional and military capacity to protect legitimate interests. **Security** is freedom from serious threats to people and political communities. **Peace** is not merely absence of war but a stable order for cooperation and justice.

Strength can deter aggression and make diplomacy credible; security permits development; peace reduces the insecurity that drives arms races. India's disaster assistance and peacekeeping show that strength may provide public goods.

Yet unrestrained power creates a security dilemma: one state's accumulation may make others less secure. Negative peace sustained by fear is fragile. Kant's lawful federation and Gandhi's ethical means suggest that durable peace needs rules, dialogue and respect for persons. Human security adds health, food, climate and cyber risks to territorial defence.

Legitimate strength must therefore be accountable; security cooperative; and peace grounded in justice, sovereign equality, treaty fidelity and peaceful dispute settlement.

**Examiner takeaway:** The pillars reinforce one another only when power is constrained by law and common security.

## Q7(a). Narrow perceptions of the good life and ethical crisis

**Official question:** The crisis of ethical values in modern times is traced to a narrow perception of the good life. Discuss. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2017 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q7(a) · Directive: Discuss · Difficulty: Moderate-High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → conceptions of the good life · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Ethics and human interface](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/01_Ethics-and-Human-Interface.md) · [Moral theories](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/08_Moral-Theories-Deontology-Consequentialism-Virtue-Ethics.md)

### Model answer

A narrow good life equates flourishing with income, consumption, status and competitive success. When means become invisible, bribery appears as smartness, ecological damage as an external cost and persons as instruments. Relative comparison produces anxiety and endless acquisition.

Aristotle's *eudaimonia* joins material sufficiency with virtue, friendship and civic participation. Gandhi's distinction between need and greed warns that possession without restraint impoverishes society and self. Exam cheating for credentials and corporate greenwashing illustrate the crisis.

Material prosperity is not intrinsically unethical: poverty restricts capability and aspiration can drive innovation. Nor is ethical decline solely individual; unequal opportunity and weak institutions may reward misconduct.

The remedy is a broader flourishing—health, meaningful work, relationships, service, freedom, justice and ecological responsibility—supported by ethical education and fair institutions. Markets should remain means to welfare, not measures of human worth.

**Examiner takeaway:** Ethical renewal needs both a wider idea of success and incentives that reward it.

## Q7(b). Unequal gains from increased national wealth

**Official question:** Increased national wealth did not result in equitable distribution of its benefits. It has created only some “enclaves of modernity and prosperity for a small minority at the cost of the majority.” Justify. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2017 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q7(b) · Directive: Justify · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → distributive justice · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Ethics and human interface](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/01_Ethics-and-Human-Interface.md) · [Moral theories](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/08_Moral-Theories-Deontology-Consequentialism-Virtue-Ethics.md)

### Model answer

Aggregate wealth can rise while benefits remain concentrated because ownership of capital, quality education, digital access, formal credit and political influence are unequal. Urban enclaves may enjoy infrastructure and high-value work while displaced communities bear pollution, land loss or insecure labour. Privatisation of gains and socialisation of costs violate distributive and procedural justice.

Rawls tests institutions by their effect on the least advantaged; Amartya Sen asks whether growth expands capabilities—health, education, voice and livelihood. Gandhi's *antyodaya* places the last person at the centre.

The statement is too absolute if it denies poverty reduction, connectivity and new opportunity created by growth. The failure is exclusion and unfair burdens, not wealth creation itself.

Inclusive development requires universal basic services, fair taxation, labour protection, competition, regional investment, environmental accountability, participation, and just compensation and rehabilitation.

**Examiner takeaway:** Wealth is ethically meaningful when opportunity, voice, benefits and burdens are fairly shared.

## Q8(a). When discipline becomes counter-productive

**Official question:** Discipline generally implies following the order and subordination. However, it may be counter-productive for the organisation. Discuss. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2017 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q8(a) · Directive: Discuss · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Work Culture and Public Funds](../../topics/ethics/work-culture-and-public-funds.md) → hierarchy and ethical dissent · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Work culture](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/17_Citizens-Charters-Work-Culture-and-Service-Delivery.md) · [Law, rules and conscience](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/10_Sources-of-Ethical-Guidance-Laws-Rules-Conscience.md)

### Model answer

Discipline creates reliability, role clarity, timely execution and coordinated response, especially in police, disaster and military organisations. Subordination to lawful authority is necessary.

It becomes counter-productive when treated as unquestioning obedience. Fear-based hierarchy suppresses bad news, innovation and ethical dissent; encourages groupthink; shifts responsibility upward; and permits unlawful orders to travel unchallenged. The Challenger disaster remains an organisational warning about technical concerns being overridden by schedule pressure.

Kant rejects surrender of moral agency, while Weberian hierarchy depends on rule-bound—not personal—authority.

Healthy discipline combines fidelity to law and mission with speaking-up channels, written orders, reasoned disagreement, anti-retaliation protection and after-action review. Emergencies may require tighter command, but stronger documentation and later scrutiny.

Thus organisations need loyal dissent: members execute legitimate decisions while retaining professional judgment and constitutional responsibility.

**Examiner takeaway:** Discipline coordinates action; blind conformity destroys learning and accountability.

## Q8(b). Shared moral values and functioning institutions

**Official question:** Without commonly shared and widely entrenched moral values and obligations, neither the law, nor democratic government, nor even the market economy will function properly. What do you understand by this statement? Explain with illustration in the contemporary times. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2017 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q8(b) · Directive: Explain with illustration · Difficulty: High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → trust and institutional morality · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Ethics and human interface](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/01_Ethics-and-Human-Interface.md) · [Sources of ethical guidance](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/10_Sources-of-Ethical-Guidance-Laws-Rules-Conscience.md)

### Model answer

Formal rules cannot monitor every act. Institutions depend on internalised truthfulness, promise-keeping, fairness, non-violence and respect, which create social trust.

Law fails if witnesses lie and officials enforce selectively. Democracy fails without tolerance, factual honesty, peaceful transfer and legitimate opposition. Markets fail where contracts, product claims and accounts are untrustworthy; fraud and transaction costs then overwhelm exchange.

Contemporary misinformation shows the connection: lawful expression cannot sustain democracy when coordinated deception destroys informed consent. Digital commerce likewise needs not only cyber law but firms respecting data and regulators acting impartially.

Durkheim emphasised the moral foundations of social order. Yet “shared values” must not mean cultural uniformity. In a plural constitutional democracy, the common minimum is dignity, equality, liberty, fraternity, honesty and non-harm, while beliefs may differ.

Law, democracy and markets need both enforceable rules and ethical citizens; each corrects the limits of the other.

**Examiner takeaway:** Institutions rest on constitutional morality and trust, not coercion or incentives alone.

## Q9. Ethical conduct, career pressure, development and small gifts

**Official case and demand (verified transcription):** You are an honest and responsible civil servant. You often observe the following:

(a) There is a general perception that adhering to ethical conduct one may face difficulties to oneself and cause problems for the family, whereas unfair practices may help to reach the career goals.  
(b) When the number of people adopting unfair means is large, a small minority having a penchant towards ethical means makes no difference.  
(c) Sticking to ethical means is detrimental to the larger developmental goals.  
(d) While one may not involve oneself in large unethical practices, but giving and accepting small gifts makes the system more efficient.

Examine the above statements with their merits and demerits. (250 words, 20 marks)

**Metadata:** 2017 · Mains · GS-IV · Section B · Official Q9 · Directive: Examine four rationalisations · Difficulty: High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Corruption and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/corruption-and-administrative-ethics.md) → rationalisation and petty corruption · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Corruption and public funds](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/18_Utilization-of-Public-Funds-and-Challenges-of-Corruption.md) · [Case-study method](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/22_Case-Study-Method-and-Answer-Architecture.md)

### Stakeholders and ethical issues

The civil servant and family, honest colleagues, citizens—especially those unable to pay—the government and future organisational culture. Issues are integrity under pressure, collective-action pessimism, ends versus means, normalisation of gifts, equality and trust.

### Options and consequences

1. **Conform to prevailing unfair practices:** may reduce immediate career/family pressure, but harms citizens, creates legal exposure and deepens institutional decay.
2. **Remain privately honest but isolated:** preserves personal integrity, yet does little to change enabling systems and may leave colleagues exposed.
3. **Practise integrity and build institutional support:** is harder initially but combines clean conduct with collective reform.

### Evaluation of the claims

| Claim | Apparent merit | Decisive demerit |
|---|---|---|
| Ethics harms career/family | Recognises real retaliation and opportunity costs | Hardship cannot justify abuse of entrusted power; corrupt success is insecure |
| A small minority makes no difference | Identifies a collective-action problem | Ethical minorities preserve standards, evidence and reform coalitions |
| Ethical means obstruct development | Procedure can delay urgent work | Corrupt shortcuts inflate cost, reduce quality and exclude the weak |
| Small gifts improve efficiency | May seem like gratitude or facilitation | Creates reciprocity, unequal access, escalation and an informal service price |

### Justified response and safeguards

I would reject all four as justifications while acknowledging their pressures. I would comply with conduct rules, decline reportable gifts, publish service timelines, digitise and rotate sensitive processes, record oral directions, use collective decisions and report serious wrongdoing through protected channels.

The strongest objection is that lone integrity may be punished. Hence clean conduct needs whistle-blower protection, fair appraisal, vigilance against retaliation and leadership rewarding truthful performance. Family concerns require lawful financial planning and institutional protection, not illicit gain.

**Examiner takeaway:** Integrity is ethical means reinforced through collective institutional action, not naïve isolation.

## Q10. IAS interview versus immediate aid to injured relatives

**Official case and demand (verified transcription):** You are aspiring to become an IAS officer and you have cleared various stages and now you have been selected for the personal interview. On the day of the interview, on the way to the venue you saw an accident where a mother and child who happen to be your relatives were badly injured. They needed immediate help. **What would you have done in such a situation? Justify your action.** (250 words, 20 marks)

**Metadata:** 2017 · Mains · GS-IV · Section B · Official Q10 · Directive: Decide and justify · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Service Delivery and Regulatory Ethics](../../topics/ethics/service-delivery-and-regulatory-ethics.md) → duty of care and prioritisation · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Case-study method](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/22_Case-Study-Method-and-Answer-Architecture.md) · [Public-service values](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/09_Public-Service-Values-Status-and-Ethical-Dilemmas.md)

### Stakeholders, constraints and values

The injured mother and child, other road users, emergency services, candidate and family, and interview institution. Values are life, compassion, civic duty, prudence and career aspiration. Trauma care is time-sensitive; an interview, though crucial, can at least be explained.

### Options and consequences

1. **Proceed to the interview:** protects opportunity but risks preventable death and violates basic humanity.
2. **Personally handle everything indefinitely:** prioritises life but may be inefficient after professionals and responsible attendants arrive.
3. **Secure effective emergency care, then proceed only if safely possible:** best combines rescue with prudent delegation.

### Chosen action and implementation

I would stop safely, call emergency services and police, assess hazards, seek bystander help, provide only first aid within competence, arrange rapid transport and alert the hospital and family. I would avoid unnecessary movement where spinal injury is suspected unless immediate danger requires it. I would notify the interview authority promptly and retain basic proof. I would remain until professional care and a responsible attendant take over; only then, if possible, proceed.

The priority of life, the Good Samaritan ethic and the qualities expected of an administrator justify this choice. The victims' relationship strengthens personal responsibility, but the same minimum duty applies to strangers.

### Safeguards

Avoid unsafe heroics, crowding and medico-legal delay; communicate facts and seek fair consideration rather than entitlement.

**Examiner takeaway:** Save life first through competent action and delegation; career goals cannot override urgent preventable harm.

## Q11. Compensation after an intoxicated worker's on-duty death

**Official case and demand (verified transcription):** You are the head of the Human Resources department of an organisation. One day one of the workers died on duty. His family was demanding compensation. However, the company denied compensation because it was revealed in investigation that he was drunk at the time of the accident. The workers of the company went on to strike demanding compensation for the family of the deceased. The Chairman of the management board has asked for your recommendation. **What recommendation would you provide to the management? Discuss the merits and demerits of each of the recommendations.** (250 words, 20 marks)

**Metadata:** 2017 · Mains · GS-IV · Section B · Official Q11 · Directive: Recommend after comparing options · Difficulty: High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Work Culture and Public Funds](../../topics/ethics/work-culture-and-public-funds.md) → worker safety, responsibility and compassion · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Work culture](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/17_Citizens-Charters-Work-Culture-and-Service-Delivery.md) · [Case-study method](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/22_Case-Study-Method-and-Answer-Architecture.md)

### Stakeholders and ethical issues

The deceased worker and dependants, striking workers, management, supervisors, insurer and other employees. Issues include contributory fault, employer duty of care, legality, compassion, industrial peace, moral hazard and procedural fairness.

### Options and consequences

1. **Deny all payment:** upholds discipline and avoids rewarding intoxication; but may punish innocent dependants, ignore safety failures and intensify mistrust.
2. **Pay full compensation unconditionally:** protects the family and may settle the strike; but can disregard binding rules, create inequity and weaken safety norms.
3. **Independent determination plus lawful compensation and separate humanitarian support:** fact-sensitive and balanced, though slower and administratively demanding.

### Preferred course

I would choose the third option. Verify toxicology, causation, supervision, safety equipment, working conditions and whether the organisation allowed an impaired worker into a hazardous role. Determine statutory and contractual liability through due process; intoxication should not become an automatic moral conclusion without examining causation and applicable law. Pay all undisputed dues, insurance and provident benefits promptly. If compensation is lawfully reduced or barred, offer transparent ex gratia or hardship support to dependants through an authorised welfare mechanism without falsifying liability.

Engage worker representatives, publish the inquiry timetable, and institute confidential addiction assistance, fitness-for-duty checks and supervisor accountability.

### Safeguards

Use an independent inquiry, hearing, speaking order, consistent precedent and appeal. Separate family welfare from disciplinary responsibility to control moral hazard.

**Examiner takeaway:** Compassion and discipline coexist through lawful fact-finding and separately authorised relief.

## Q12. Sole eyewitness, road accident and a critical business deal

**Official case and demands (verified transcription):** You are the manager of a spare parts company A and you have to negotiate a deal with the manager of a large manufacturing company B. The deal is highly competitive and sealing the deal is critical for your company. The deal is being worked out over a dinner. After dinner the manager of manufacturing company B offered to drop you to the hotel in his car. On the way to the hotel he happens to hit a motorcycle injuring the motorcyclist badly. You know the manager was driving fast and thus lost control. The law enforcement officer comes to investigate the issue and you are the sole eyewitness to it. Knowing the strict laws pertaining to road accidents you are aware that your honest account of the incident would lead to the prosecution of the manager and as a consequence the deal is likely to be jeopardized, which is of immense importance to your company. **What are the dilemmas you face? What will be your response to the situation?** (250 words, 20 marks)

**Metadata:** 2017 · Mains · GS-IV · Section B · Official Q12 · Directive: Identify dilemmas and decide · Difficulty: High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Corruption and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/corruption-and-administrative-ethics.md) → truthfulness, conflict and corporate integrity · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Case-study method](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/22_Case-Study-Method-and-Answer-Architecture.md) · [Moral theories](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/08_Moral-Theories-Deontology-Consequentialism-Virtue-Ethics.md)

### Stakeholders and dilemmas

The injured motorcyclist and family, manager B, company A and its employees/shareholders, company B, police, courts and road users. Dilemmas are truth versus commercial loyalty; victim's rights versus consequences for the driver; career pressure versus civic duty; and short-term contract versus lasting corporate integrity.

### Options and consequences

1. **Lie or suppress speed:** may protect the deal but obstructs justice, abandons the victim and creates legal and reputational risk.
2. **Give an embellished punitive account:** appears victim-oriented but violates fairness and evidence integrity.
3. **Give a complete, strictly factual account and separate the deal from the accident:** protects justice, though the contract may be lost.

### Response

I would first ensure emergency medical help, call police and preserve relevant details. I would state only what I directly observed—speed, loss of control, collision and subsequent conduct—without speculation about intoxication or intent. I would neither bargain over testimony nor coordinate accounts. I would inform my senior or ethics/legal function of the conflict and ask another manager to continue any negotiation, with no quid pro quo.

Kantian respect forbids using the victim merely as a business cost; rule-consequentialism shows that markets cannot work if testimony is tradable. Fairness still requires due process for manager B.

### Safeguards

Written records, evidence preservation, no private-settlement pressure, victim support, recusal where needed and anti-retaliation protection. A deal dependent on perjury is not a legitimate corporate interest.

**Examiner takeaway:** Truthful, limited testimony protects both justice and sustainable business ethics.

## Q13. Collapse of an illegally extended building

**Official case and demand (verified transcription):** A building permitted for three floors, while being extended illegally to 6 floors by a builder, collapses. As a consequence, a number of innocent labourers including women and children died. These labourers are migrants of different places. The government immediately announced cash relief to the aggrieved families and arrested the builder. **Give reasons for such incidents taking place across the country. Suggest measures to prevent their occurrence.** (250 words, 20 marks)

**Metadata:** 2017 · Mains · GS-IV · Section B · Official Q13 · Directive: Diagnose causes and prescribe prevention · Difficulty: High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Corruption and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/corruption-and-administrative-ethics.md) → regulatory failure and construction safety · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Corruption challenges](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/18_Utilization-of-Public-Funds-and-Challenges-of-Corruption.md) · [Case-study method](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/22_Case-Study-Method-and-Answer-Architecture.md)

### Stakeholders and ethical issues

Dead and injured labourers, migrant families, residents, builder, engineers, municipal officials, contractors and public. Issues include greed, corruption, regulatory capture, duty of care, invisibility of migrants, child safety, professional integrity and chain accountability.

### Causes

- profit from illegal floor-area expansion and high land values;
- bribery, collusion or wilful inaction in inspection and utilities;
- fragmented approvals and weak record interoperability;
- unsafe design, inferior material and absent professional supervision;
- weak penalties, delayed trials and diffused responsibility;
- informal migrant labour, hazardous onsite habitation and poor bargaining power;
- normalisation of deviations and political protection;
- post-tragedy arrests replacing risk-based prevention.

### Preventive course

Create GIS-linked public permits and stage-wise digital inspection records; require independent structural certification at foundation and every major stage; deny utility and occupancy approval to deviations; conduct risk-based surprise inspections with geotagged evidence; fix liability on builders, licensed professionals and colluding officials; prevent worker-family habitation in hazardous zones; register workers and provide portable protection; enable anonymous reporting; and use time-bound prosecution, blacklisting and recovery of compensation and remediation costs.

Relief and arrest are necessary but insufficient. An independent inquiry must trace approval and supervisory failures, not scapegoat only the builder.

### Safeguards

Audit inspection discretion; rotate third-party certifiers and require conflict disclosure and professional liability. Publish compliance data while protecting whistle-blowers.

**Examiner takeaway:** Prevention requires transparent approvals, professional liability, worker protection and accountability across the chain.

## Q14. Genuine and non-genuine RTI applications

**Official case and demand (verified transcription):** You are a Public Information Officer (PIO) in a government department. You are aware that the RTI Act, 2005 envisages transparency and accountability in administration. The act has functioned as a check on the supposedly arbitrarily administrative behaviour and actions. However, as a PIO you have observed that there are citizens who filed RTI applications not for themselves but on behalf of such stakeholders who purportedly want to have access to information to further their own interests. At the same time there are those RTI activists who routinely file RTI applications and attempt to extort money from the decision makers. This type of RTI activism has affected the functioning of the administration adversely and also possibly jeopardizes the genuineness of the applications which are essentially aimed at getting justice. **What measures would you suggest to separate genuine and non-genuine applications? Give merits and demerits of your suggestions.** (250 words, 20 marks)

**Metadata:** 2017 · Mains · GS-IV · Section B · Official Q14 · Directive: Suggest and evaluate measures · Difficulty: High  
**Primary route:** Governance → [Transparency, Accountability, RTI, Grievance Redress and Social Audit](../../topics/governance/transparency-accountability-grievance-redress-and-social-audit.md) → motive-neutral access and abuse control · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Transparency and RTI](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/15_Transparency-RTI-and-Information-Sharing.md) · [Case-study method](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/22_Case-Study-Method-and-Answer-Architecture.md)

### Ethical and legal frame

Stakeholders are applicants, justice seekers, whistle-blowers, affected third parties, PIOs, authorities and citizens. The central trap is to let suspected motive determine access. A PIO should ordinarily decide whether held information is lawfully disclosable, not whether an applicant is morally “genuine.” Filing for another person may still expose wrongdoing; extortion should be addressed as extortion, not by weakening RTI.

### Measures, merits and demerits

| Measure | Merit | Demerit/risk |
|---|---|---|
| Proactive disclosure, searchable records and open data | Reduces repetition and discretion | Digitisation cost; privacy redaction needed |
| Help desk to clarify broad requests; permit inspection | Lowers burden and improves access | Poor assistance may intimidate applicants |
| Administratively consolidate identical requests | Saves repeated effort | Must not become an unlawful cap |
| Apply exemptions, severability and third-party procedure carefully | Protects lawful interests | Overbroad use can recreate secrecy |
| Refer evidenced threats/extortion to independent police or vigilance channels | Targets misconduct directly | False allegations may chill activism |
| Publish anonymised request-response logs | Promotes consistency and detects patterns | Requires careful anonymisation |

### Decision and safeguards

I would not create a subjective genuineness test, demand reasons, or deny information because it serves private interests. Each request should be processed under the Act, with recorded reasons and appeal rights; extortion should be reported separately on evidence. Training, workload dashboards, record management and proactive disclosure address burden.

The residual risk is harassment through volume; independent review and better information systems are safer than motive-based censorship.

**Examiner takeaway:** Separate disclosability from misconduct: preserve motive-neutral RTI while addressing extortion through ordinary law.
