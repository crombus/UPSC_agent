## Q1(a). Wisdom, priorities and effective service delivery
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Service Delivery and Regulatory Ethics](../../topics/ethics/service-delivery-and-regulatory-ethics.md) → Administrative wisdom and prioritisation · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** Wisdom lies in knowing what to reckon with and what to overlook. An officer being engrossed with the periphery, ignoring the core issues before him, is not rare in the bureaucracy. Do you agree that such preoccupation of an administrator leads to travesty of justice to the cause of effective service delivery and good governance? Critically evaluate. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Public-service values · Subtopic: Practical wisdom and administrative priorities · Proposed slug: `wisdom-priorities-service-delivery` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Take a position and critically evaluate

**Demand decoding:** Explain administrative wisdom, show how peripheralism harms justice and delivery, and qualify the case for overlooking matters.

### Model answer

**Concept:** Administrative wisdom is the capacity to distinguish material facts from procedural noise and direct scarce attention towards public purpose. Aristotle’s *phronesis* joins rules with context-sensitive judgment.

**Application:** When a pension is delayed over a curable clerical defect, or a disaster officer prioritises perfect paperwork over immediate relief, procedure displaces substantive justice. Peripheralism causes delay, red tape, exclusion, diffused accountability and goal displacement. Gandhi’s “last person” test instead asks whether administration solves the citizen’s real deprivation.

Yet “overlooking” cannot mean ignoring legality, safety, minority rights or audit trails. Apparently minor anomalies may reveal corruption or systemic risk. The correct approach is risk-based prioritisation: separate mandatory safeguards from remediable defects, allow speaking orders and correction windows, and measure outcomes alongside compliance.

**Conclusion:** I agree substantially. Good governance requires fidelity to lawful essentials, but also practical wisdom to prevent procedure from becoming the enemy of justice.

**Why this earns marks:** It combines concept, thinker, administrative examples and a qualification against arbitrary discretion.

## Q1(b). Empathy and compassion in critical civil-service decisions
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Emotional Intelligence and Ethical Dilemmas](../../topics/ethics/emotional-intelligence-and-ethical-dilemmas.md) → Empathy and compassionate competence · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** Apart from intellectual competency and moral qualities, empathy and compassion are some of the other vital attributes that facilitate the civil servants to be more competent in tackling the crucial issues or taking critical decisions. Explain with suitable illustrations. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Emotional Intelligence · Subtopic: Empathy and compassion in administration · Proposed slug: `empathy-compassion-critical-decisions` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Explain and illustrate

**Demand decoding:** Distinguish empathy from compassion and demonstrate how each improves—not replaces—competent public decisions.

### Model answer

**Concept:** Empathy understands another’s perspective and emotion; compassion adds a reasoned commitment to relieve suffering. Daniel Goleman treats empathy as an emotional-intelligence competency, while Buddhist *karuṇā* turns understanding into humane action.

**Application:** During eviction, empathy reveals the insecurity of street vendors; compassion prompts notice, hearing, phased relocation and livelihood support rather than indiscriminate force. In a sexual-violence complaint, sensitive listening reduces secondary trauma while professional investigation protects due process. During a pandemic, migrant distress should shape transport, food and communication policy.

These attributes improve information, trust, legitimacy and implementation. They help an officer recognise unequal burdens hidden by formally uniform rules. However, compassion without impartiality can become favouritism, and empathy without evidence may distort priorities. It must operate within law, competence, proportionality and equal concern.

**Conclusion:** Intellectual ability identifies feasible choices; moral values set limits; empathy and compassion ensure that the chosen policy sees people, especially the vulnerable.

**Why this earns marks:** It defines both attributes, uses administrative illustrations and guards against sentimental partiality.

## Q2(a). Positive attitude in interpreting rules and regulations
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Service Delivery and Regulatory Ethics](../../topics/ethics/service-delivery-and-regulatory-ethics.md) → Facilitative interpretation · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** The Rules and Regulations provided to all the civil servants are same, yet there is difference in the performance. Positive minded officers are able to interpret the Rules and Regulations in favour of the case and achieve success, whereas negative minded officers are unable to achieve goals by interpreting the same Rules and Regulations against the case. Discuss with illustrations. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Attitude · Subtopic: Constructive rule interpretation · Proposed slug: `positive-attitude-rule-interpretation` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Discuss with illustrations

**Demand decoding:** Show how attitude affects implementation while rejecting unlawful “positive” discretion.

### Model answer

**Concept:** Attitude shapes whether an officer treats rules as instruments of public purpose or as excuses for inaction. A positive attitude seeks a lawful route; a negative one magnifies obstacles.

**Thinker:** Weberian bureaucracy values predictable rules, but Aristotle’s practical wisdom is needed where general rules meet particular facts.

**Application:** A scholarship officer may reject an eligible student for a minor certificate defect, or permit time-bound correction while verifying entitlement. A district administration can read procurement rules mechanically during disaster relief, or use the emergency provisions that the rules themselves provide with recorded reasons and audit.

Positive interpretation improves initiative, innovation and citizen trust. Nevertheless, “in favour of the case” must not mean bending eligibility, bypassing competition or granting patronage. Relevant considerations, equality, reasoned orders and review remain essential.

**Conclusion:** Performance differs because ethical attitude converts identical rules into either gatekeeping or lawful facilitation; optimism must remain constitutionally bounded.

**Why this earns marks:** It links attitude to performance, illustrates both readings and qualifies positivity through legality and impartiality.

## Q2(b). What ethics promotes and how values resolve daily conflicts
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → Ethical ends and conflict resolution · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** It is believed that adherence to ethics in human actions would ensure in smooth functioning of an organization/system. If so, what does ethics seek to promote in human life? How do ethical values assist in the resolution of conflicts faced by him in his day-to-day functioning? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Ethics and Human Interface · Subtopic: Ethical purpose and conflict resolution · Proposed slug: `ethics-purpose-conflict-resolution` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Explain two linked dimensions

**Demand decoding:** State what ethics promotes, then provide a usable value-based method for everyday dilemmas.

### Model answer

**Concept:** Ethics promotes human dignity, welfare, justice, autonomy, trustworthy relationships and the cultivation of good character. It asks not merely what is permitted, but what one owes to others.

**Thinkers:** Kant anchors respect and duty; Mill tests consequences for welfare; Aristotle asks what a person of practical virtue would do.

**Application:** In a conflict between loyalty to a superior and truthful reporting, integrity rejects concealment, while prudence favours verified facts and the proper channel. In allocating a scarce benefit, empathy reveals need, justice requires consistent criteria, and transparency makes the decision reviewable.

A daily ethical sequence is: identify stakeholders; separate facts from assumptions; test legality and rights; compare consequences; disclose conflicts; choose the least harmful fair option; record reasons; provide appeal. Values therefore convert personal preference into principled judgment.

**Conclusion:** Ethics promotes a life worthy of trust and helps systems function by making conflict resolution fair, reasoned and accountable.

**Why this earns marks:** It answers both explicit demands through complementary theories and an operational decision framework.

## Q3(a). Potter Stewart on legality and ethical rightness
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → Law, conscience and moral restraint · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official instruction:** What does each of the following quotations mean to you?  
**Official quotation:** “Ethics is knowing the difference between what you have the right to do and what is right to do.” — Potter Stewart. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Human Values · Subtopic: Legal entitlement and moral responsibility · Proposed slug: `potter-stewart-right-versus-right` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Interpret and apply

### Model answer

**Concept:** A legal right marks what others or the state may not prevent; ethics additionally asks whether exercising it respects dignity, fairness and foreseeable consequences.

**Thinker:** Potter Stewart’s distinction resembles Kant’s separation of external legality from action motivated by duty, and Gandhi’s insistence that rightful ends require responsible means.

**Application:** A company may lawfully exploit a disclosure gap yet should reveal a material risk to investors. A public official may possess broad discretion but should recuse when a relative is affected. A citizen may have freedom of expression but should not weaponise misinformation or humiliation.

Rights remain vital shields against paternalism; “morality” must not become an excuse to deny lawful liberty. The quotation instead calls for voluntary restraint and responsibility where law sets only a minimum floor.

**Conclusion:** Ethical maturity begins when power asks not “Can I?” alone, but “Should I—and can I justify it to those affected?”

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes law from ethics, adds thinkers, applies the distinction and protects rights from moralistic erosion.

## Q3(b). Abdul Kalam on family, teachers and a corruption-free nation
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Indian Moral Thinkers](../../topics/ethics/indian-moral-thinkers.md) → Value socialisation and character · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official instruction:** What does each of the following quotations mean to you?  
**Official quotation:** “If a country is to be corruption free and become a nation of beautiful minds, I strongly feel that there are three key societal members who can make a difference. They are father, mother and teacher.” — A. P. J. Abdul Kalam. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Human Values · Subtopic: Family, education and integrity · Proposed slug: `kalam-family-teacher-integrity` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Interpret and apply

### Model answer

**Concept:** Kalam locates integrity’s first school in daily socialisation. Children learn honesty, empathy, self-restraint and respect less from sermons than from conduct repeatedly modelled by caregivers and teachers.

**Thinker:** This reflects Aristotle’s insight that virtue is formed by habituation and Gandhi’s proposition that example is a powerful form of education.

**Application:** Parents who refuse bribery and reject admiration of illicit wealth make honest means socially desirable. Teachers who grade impartially, encourage questioning and practise civic responsibility cultivate “beautiful minds” capable of resisting cheating, prejudice and corruption.

However, family and teachers cannot alone overcome opaque discretion, impunity or political financing. Value formation must be reinforced by transparent processes, whistle-blower protection, fair incentives and swift accountability. The quotation should also be read inclusively: all caregivers and educational institutions share this role.

**Conclusion:** Corruption-free institutions become durable when external controls are supported by consciences formed through credible ethical example.

**Why this earns marks:** It interprets the socialisation mechanism, links named thinkers and qualifies character-building with institutional reform.

## Q3(c). Dalai Lama on the moral cost of success
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → Ends, means and flourishing · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official instruction:** What does each of the following quotations mean to you?  
**Official quotation:** “Judge your success by what you had to give up in order to get it.” — Dalai Lama. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Human Values · Subtopic: Success, sacrifice and integrity · Proposed slug: `dalai-lama-cost-of-success` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Interpret and apply

### Model answer

**Concept:** The quotation proposes a moral balance sheet: achievement cannot be judged only by rank, wealth or output; its worth depends on the values, relationships and welfare sacrificed to obtain it.

**Thinkers:** Gandhi’s unity of means and ends and Aristotle’s idea of flourishing both reject success that destroys character.

**Application:** A civil servant who secures targets by fabricating data gives up integrity; a company that gains profit by unsafe work sacrifices human dignity; a student who succeeds through cheating loses self-respect and competence. Conversely, giving up comfort, leisure or privilege for disciplined public service may ennoble success.

Not every sacrifice is virtuous. Neglecting health, family or subordinates for prestige can reveal distorted priorities, while surrendering prejudice or short-term gain may mark growth. The test is whether the sacrifice protects or corrodes fundamental values.

**Conclusion:** Genuine success enlarges capability without impoverishing conscience; what we refuse to trade away defines us more than the prize acquired.

**Why this earns marks:** It avoids glorifying sacrifice, evaluates both harmful and worthy costs and applies the quotation across domains.

## Q4(a). Good governance and e-governance benefits
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Governance and International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/governance-and-international-ethics.md) → Good governance and digital delivery · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** What do you understand by the term ‘good governance’? How far recent initiatives in terms of e-Governance steps taken by the State have helped the beneficiaries? Discuss with suitable examples. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Ethical Governance · Subtopic: E-governance and beneficiary outcomes · Proposed slug: `good-governance-egovernance-beneficiaries` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Define, assess and illustrate

### Model answer

**Concept:** Good governance is lawful, participatory, transparent, accountable, responsive, equitable and effective exercise of public power.

**Thinker/framework:** The Second ARC’s citizen-centric approach treats technology as a means to better services, not an end.

**Application:** Direct Benefit Transfer reduces intermediaries and enables traceable payments; DigiLocker lowers documentary burdens; online tax and passport systems reduce discretion and waiting; GeM makes public procurement more transparent; telemedicine and digital land records can widen access.

Benefits are substantial but uneven. Authentication failure, poor connectivity, language barriers, inaccessible design, cyber fraud, privacy risks and weak grievance redress may convert “digital by default” into exclusion. Technology can also digitise a bad process rather than reform it.

Therefore services need assisted access, offline alternatives, data minimisation, audit trails, open standards, multilingual design and time-bound human appeal.

**Conclusion:** E-governance advances good governance when it reduces power asymmetry and transaction costs while preserving inclusion, privacy and answerability.

**Why this earns marks:** It defines good governance, assesses named initiatives and gives a balanced beneficiary-centred judgment.

## Q4(b). Ethical issues in online methods for vulnerable sections
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Service Delivery and Technology Ethics](../../topics/ethics/service-delivery-and-technology-ethics.md) → Digital inclusion and vulnerability · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** Online methodology is being used for day-to-day meetings, institutional approvals in the administration and for teaching and learning in education sector to the extent telemedicine in the health sector is getting popular with the approvals of the competent authority. No doubt, it has advantages and disadvantages for both the beneficiaries and the system at large. Describe and discuss the ethical issues involved in the use of online method particularly to the vulnerable section of the society. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Technology Ethics · Subtopic: Digital divide and vulnerable groups · Proposed slug: `online-method-vulnerable-sections` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Describe and discuss ethical issues

### Model answer

**Concept:** Online delivery improves reach and continuity, but justice requires not merely identical access rules but effective capability to use the service.

**Thinker:** Amartya Sen’s capability approach asks whether persons can actually convert digital resources into education, health or participation.

**Application:** Benefits include lower travel cost, continuity during emergencies, specialist teleconsultation and traceable approvals. Ethical risks are unequal devices and connectivity; gendered phone ownership; disability and language barriers; weak digital literacy; privacy breaches; algorithmic bias; impersonality in sensitive care; and exclusion through authentication failure. Children without quiet study space and elderly patients unable to navigate apps bear disproportionate burdens.

Consent may also be nominal when the online route is compulsory. Essential services therefore require assisted centres, accessible and multilingual design, offline alternatives, informed consent, data minimisation, cybersecurity, human review and grievance redress.

**Conclusion:** Digital efficiency is ethical only when no vulnerable citizen must trade dignity, privacy or entitlement for technological convenience.

**Why this earns marks:** It identifies benefits and distributional harms, uses a thinker and supplies concrete inclusion safeguards.

## Q5(a). Ethical issues in launching and continuing war
**Primary route:** Ethics → [International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/international-ethics.md) → Just war and civilian protection · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** Russia and Ukraine war has been going on for the last seven months. Different countries have taken independent stands and actions keeping in view their own national interests. We are all aware that war has its own impact on the different aspects of society, including human tragedy. What are those ethical issues that are crucial to be considered while launching the war and its continuation so far? Illustrate with justification the ethical issues involved in the given state of affair. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: International Ethics · Subtopic: Just-war ethics · Proposed slug: `russia-ukraine-war-ethical-issues` · Difficulty: High · Demand: Identify, illustrate and justify

### Model answer

**Concept:** Just-war reasoning separates *jus ad bellum*—just cause, lawful authority, right intention, last resort, proportionality and reasonable prospect—from *jus in bello*—distinction, necessity and proportionality in conduct.

**Thinkers:** Augustine and Aquinas supplied the classical moral restraints; contemporary international humanitarian law operationalises civilian distinction.

**Application:** Aggression violates sovereignty and political self-determination; self-defence may be legitimate, but no side acquires licence for indiscriminate attack. Continuing war must be reassessed against civilian deaths, displacement, food and energy insecurity, nuclear risk, environmental harm and prospects of negotiation. Parties must protect prisoners and humanitarian corridors. Third states face duties to avoid profiteering, proxy escalation and selective concern, while assistance to lawful self-defence cannot be equated automatically with prolongation.

National interest is legitimate but bounded by humanity, consistency and global justice.

**Conclusion:** The moral test is not victory alone, but whether force was necessary, discriminating, proportionate, accountable and continuously directed towards a just peace.

**Why this earns marks:** It uses the correct ethical framework, applies it without simplistic equivalence and evaluates continuation separately from initiation.

## Q6(a). Strengthening whistle-blower protection
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Corruption and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/corruption-and-administrative-ethics.md) → Whistle-blower protection · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** Whistle-blower, who reports corruption and illegal activities, wrongdoing and misconduct to the concerned authorities, runs the risk of being exposed to grave danger, physical harm and victimization by the vested interests, accused persons and his team. What policy measures would you suggest to strengthen protection mechanism to safeguard the whistle-blower? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Probity and anti-corruption · Subtopic: Whistle-blower safeguards · Proposed slug: `whistleblower-protection-policy` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Suggest policy measures

### Model answer

**Concept:** Whistle-blowing serves public accountability, but imposing extreme personal risk on truth-tellers creates moral hazard and deters disclosure.

**Thinker/framework:** The Second ARC’s integrity framework requires institutions that make honest conduct feasible, not heroic.

**Application—policy measures:**

- secure anonymous and confidential channels outside the immediate hierarchy;
- strict need-to-know identity protection, encrypted records and penalties for disclosure;
- time-bound threat assessment, police protection, relocation and emergency contact;
- anti-retaliation orders covering transfer, appraisal, suspension, harassment and blacklisting;
- interim relief, legal aid, counselling and compensation;
- independent inquiry with conflict-free investigators and periodic status communication;
- preservation of evidence and sanctions for obstruction or malicious complaints;
- judicial/oversight appeal and annual anonymised reporting;
- organisational speak-up culture and witness protection for associates and family where threatened.

Protection should not immunise knowingly false allegations, but good-faith reporting must not fail merely because wrongdoing is ultimately unproved.

**Conclusion:** A credible system protects identity, livelihood, safety, due process and the institutional use of the information disclosed.

**Why this earns marks:** It addresses physical, occupational, procedural and cultural risks while preserving safeguards against abuse.

## Q6(b). Whether CSR is efficient and sufficient
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Governance and International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/governance-and-international-ethics.md) → Corporate social responsibility · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** In contemporary world, corporate sector’s contribution in generating wealth and employment is increasing. In doing so, they are bringing in unprecedented onslaught on the climate, environmental sustainability and living conditions of human beings. In this background, do you find that Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) is efficient and sufficient enough to fulfill the social roles and responsibilities needed in the corporate world for which the CSR is mandated? Critically examine. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Corporate Governance · Subtopic: CSR limits and stakeholder responsibility · Proposed slug: `csr-efficient-sufficient` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Critically examine

### Model answer

**Concept:** CSR recognises that corporations owe responsibilities beyond shareholders because their operations affect workers, communities and ecosystems.

**Thinker:** Freeman’s stakeholder theory supports duties to all affected groups; the polluter-pays principle rejects shifting environmental costs to society.

**Application:** CSR can fund health, education, restoration and local capacity, while board oversight and mandatory disclosure can professionalise spending. Yet it is neither fully efficient nor sufficient. Projects may be fragmented, publicity-driven or geographically convenient; expenditure can coexist with unsafe labour, pollution, tax avoidance or community displacement. Philanthropy after harm cannot substitute for prevention and remedy within the core business model.

CSR must therefore complement—never replace—environmental compliance, labour rights, due diligence, transparent impact assessment, grievance mechanisms, responsible supply chains and director accountability. Independent evaluation and community participation improve efficiency.

**Conclusion:** CSR is valuable additional responsibility, but authentic corporate citizenship begins with doing no harm and internalising social and environmental costs.

**Why this earns marks:** It acknowledges CSR’s contribution, exposes structural limits and distinguishes philanthropy from responsible operations.

## Q7. Defence tender documents offered in exchange for employment
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Governance and International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/governance-and-international-ethics.md) → Corporate integrity and confidential information · **Secondary route:** Ethics → case-study method · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Case:** Prabhat, Vice President (Marketing) at Sterling Electric Ltd., faces falling sales, threatened division closure and serious family financial burdens. Subhash Verma, an otherwise strong candidate referred through the Defence Minister’s office, offers a rival company’s confidential defence-tender documents in exchange for suitable employment. **(250 words, 20 marks)**

**Official demands:** (a) Discuss the ethical issues involved in the case. (b) Critically examine the options available to Prabhat in the above situation. (c) Which of the above would be the most appropriate for Prabhat and why?

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Corporate Ethics · Subtopic: Trade secrets, bribery and recruitment integrity · Proposed slug: `defence-tender-documents-employment` · Difficulty: High · Demands: Ethical issues; options; justified choice

### Case framework

**Stakeholders:** Prabhat and dependants; Subhash; other candidates; Sterling and its employees/shareholders; rival company; Defence Ministry; taxpayers and national security.  
**Values/conflicts:** integrity versus self-preservation; merit versus quid pro quo; confidentiality and fair competition; loyalty versus legality; public security versus corporate gain.

<a id="2022-mains-gs4-q7a"></a>
### Q7(a). Ethical issues involved
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Governance and International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/governance-and-international-ethics.md) → Ethical issues involved · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  

Subhash proposes theft/misuse of confidential information, commercial espionage and a job-for-information quid pro quo. Acceptance would corrupt recruitment, deny equal opportunity to candidates, compromise tender fairness, expose sensitive procurement and reward disloyalty. Prabhat’s financial distress and pressure explain temptation but cannot justify using a rival or the state merely as a means.

<a id="2022-mains-gs4-q7b"></a>
### Q7(b). Options and consequences
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Governance and International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/governance-and-international-ethics.md) → Options and consequences · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  

1. **Accept:** may save jobs and Prabhat’s income, but is dishonest, legally risky, security-sensitive and reputationally ruinous; Subhash is also an enduring insider threat.
