## Q1. Passage cause inference
**Primary route:** CSAT → [Reading Comprehension](../../topics/csat/reading-comprehension.md) → Passage cause inference · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Passage:** The main threat to maintaining progress in human development comes from the increasingly evident unsustainability of production and consumption patterns. Current production models rely heavily on fossil fuels. We now know that this is unsustainable because the resources are finite. The close link between economic growth and greenhouse gas emissions needs to be severed for human development to become truly sustainable. Some developed countries have begun to alleviate the worst effects by expanding recycling and investing in public transport and infrastructure. But most developing countries are hampered by the high costs and low availability of clean energy sources. Developed countries need to support developing countries' transition to sustainable human development.

Unsustainability in production pattern is due to which of the following?
1. Heavy dependence on fossil fuels
2. Limited availability of resources
3. Expansion of recycling

Select the correct answer using the code given below.

**Options:** (a) 1 and 2 only · (b) 2 only · (c) 1 and 3 only · (d) 1, 2 and 3

**Metadata:** Family 01 · Reading Comprehension → Passage cause inference · Slug: `passage-cause-inference-2022-q01`  
**Canonical owner:** `CSAT/basic/01_Reading-Comprehension.md`

**FINAL answer: (a).**

**Full reasoning:** Statements 1 and 2 reproduce the passage's causal chain: production depends heavily on finite fossil fuels. Recycling is presented as a remedy used by developed countries, not a cause of unsustainability. Therefore only 1 and 2 are correct.

**Shortest safe method:** Separate causes from remedies: fossil-fuel dependence + finite resources, not recycling.

## Q2. Passage statement validity
**Primary route:** CSAT → [Reading Comprehension](../../topics/csat/reading-comprehension.md) → Passage statement validity · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Passage:** The main threat to maintaining progress in human development comes from the increasingly evident unsustainability of production and consumption patterns. Current production models rely heavily on fossil fuels. We now know that this is unsustainable because the resources are finite. The close link between economic growth and greenhouse gas emissions needs to be severed for human development to become truly sustainable. Some developed countries have begun to alleviate the worst effects by expanding recycling and investing in public transport and infrastructure. But most developing countries are hampered by the high costs and low availability of clean energy sources. Developed countries need to support developing countries' transition to sustainable human development.

Consider the following statements: Developed countries can support developing countries' transition to sustainable human development by
1. making clean energy sources available at low cost
2. providing loans for improving their public transport at nominal interest rates
3. encouraging them to change their production and consumption patterns

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

**Options:** (a) 1 only · (b) 1 and 2 only · (c) 2 and 3 only · (d) 1, 2 and 3

**Metadata:** Family 01 · Reading Comprehension → Passage statement validity · Slug: `passage-statement-validity-2022-q02`  
**Canonical owner:** `CSAT/basic/01_Reading-Comprehension.md`

**FINAL answer: (b).**

**Full reasoning:** Statement 1 directly addresses the passage's identified barriers: clean energy is costly and insufficiently available. Statement 2 is the practical support corresponding to the passage's positive example of investment in public transport. Statement 3 changes the object of the recommendation: the passage says developed countries should support the transition, but does not specifically say they should induce developing countries to change both production and consumption patterns. The official key therefore takes 1 and 2 only.

**Shortest safe method:** Link clean energy and public transport to the two express passage barriers/remedies; do not add statement 3.

## Q3. Passage consequence inference
**Primary route:** CSAT → [Reading Comprehension](../../topics/csat/reading-comprehension.md) → Passage consequence inference · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Passage:** Unless the forces and tendencies which are responsible for destroying the country's environment are checked in the near future and afforestation of denuded areas is taken up on a massive scale, the harshness of the climatic conditions and soil erosion by wind and water will increase to such an extent that agriculture, which is the mainstay of our people, will gradually become impossible. The desert countries of the world and our own desert areas in Rajasthan are a grim reminder of the consequences of large-scale deforestation. Pockets of desert-like landscape are now appearing in other parts of the country including the Sutlej-Ganga Plains and the Deccan Plateau. Where only a few decades back there used to be lush green forests with perennial streams and springs, there is only brown earth, bare of vegetation, without any water in the streams and springs except in the rainy season.

According to the passage given above, deforestation and denudation will ultimately lead to which of the following?
1. Depletion of soil resource
2. Shortage of land for the common man
3. Lack of water for cultivation

Select the correct answer using the code given below.

**FINAL answer: (c).**

**Full reasoning:** The passage expressly links denudation to soil erosion and disappearing perennial water, which establish 1 and 3. It does not say that land becomes unavailable to the common man; it says agriculture becomes impossible. Hence 2 is unsupported.

**Shortest safe method:** Passage gives soil loss and water loss, not a land-ownership shortage.

## Q5. Divisibility digit arrangement
**Primary route:** CSAT → [Number Systems and Number Sense](../../topics/csat/number-systems-and-number-sense.md) → Divisibility digit arrangement · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

An Identity Card has the number ABCDEFG, not necessarily in that order, where each letter represents a distinct digit (1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9 only). The number is divisible by 9. After deleting the first digit from the right, the resulting number is divisible by 6. After deleting two digits from the right of original number, the resulting number is divisible by 5. After deleting three digits from the right of original number, the resulting number is divisible by 4. After deleting four digits from the right of original number, the resulting number is divisible by 3. After deleting five digits from the right of original number, the resulting number is divisible by 2. Which of the following is a possible value for the sum of the middle three digits of the number?

**Options:** (a) 8 · (b) 9 · (c) 11 · (d) 12

**Metadata:** Family 02 · Number Systems and Number Sense → Divisibility digit arrangement · Slug: `divisibility-digit-arrangement-2022-q05`  
**Canonical owner:** `CSAT/basic/02_Number-Systems-and-Number-Sense.md`

**FINAL answer: (a).**

**Full reasoning:** The five-digit prefix must end in 5; the two-digit prefix must end even; the four-digit prefix must have its last two digits divisible by 4; and the six-digit prefix must be even with digit sum divisible by 3. Exhaustive checking of the \(7!\) distinct-digit orders leaves 1472589 and 7412589. Their middle-three sums are respectively \(7+2+5=14\) and \(1+2+5=8\). Only 8 is offered.

**Shortest safe method:** Apply the prefix divisibility chain and enumerate the few survivors; only option 8 occurs.

## Q6. Direction distance tracking
**Primary route:** CSAT → [Rates, Motion, Time and Geometry](../../topics/csat/rates-motion-time-and-geometry.md) → Direction distance tracking · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

Two friends X and Y start running and they run together for 50 m in the same direction and reach a point. X turns right and runs 60 m, while Y turns left and runs 40 m. Then X turns left and runs 50 m and stops, while Y turns right and runs 50 m and then stops. How far are the two friends from each other now?

**Options:** (a) 100 m · (b) 90 m · (c) 60 m · (d) 50 m

**Metadata:** Family 04 · Rates, Motion, Time and Geometry → Direction distance tracking · Slug: `direction-distance-tracking-2022-q06`  
**Canonical owner:** `CSAT/basic/04_Rates-Motion-Time-and-Geometry.md`

**FINAL answer: (a).**

**Full reasoning:** After the common 50 m, set that point as origin. X finishes 60 m to one side and 50 m forward; Y finishes 40 m to the opposite side and 50 m forward. Their forward coordinates match, while their lateral separation is \(60+40=100\) m.

**Shortest safe method:** Final forward movement is equal; opposite side offsets add: \(60+40=100\).

1. 25 notes of ₹50 are used and the remaining are in the denominations of ₹20 and ₹10.

Which of the above statements are not correct?

## Q10. Digit row arrangement count
**Primary route:** CSAT → [Logical Reasoning, Coding, Counting and Data Interpretation](../../topics/csat/logical-reasoning-coding-counting-and-di.md) → Digit row arrangement count · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

The digits 1 to 9 are arranged in three rows in such a way that each row contains three digits, and the number formed in the second row is twice the number formed in the first row; and the number formed in the third row is thrice the number formed in the first row. Repetition of digits is not allowed. If only three of the four digits 2, 3, 7 and 9 are allowed to use in the first row, how many such combinations are possible to be arranged in the three rows?

**Options:** (a) 4 · (b) 3 · (c) 2 · (d) 1

**Metadata:** Family 06 · Logical Reasoning, Coding, Counting and DI → Digit row arrangement count · Slug: `digit-row-arrangement-count-2022-q10`  
**Canonical owner:** `CSAT/basic/06_Logical-Reasoning-Coding-Counting-and-DI.md`

**FINAL answer: (c).**

**Shortest safe method:** Test the 24 permitted first rows; only 273 and 327 work.

## Q15. Circular track overtaking
**Primary route:** CSAT → [Rates, Motion, Time and Geometry](../../topics/csat/rates-motion-time-and-geometry.md) → Circular track overtaking · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

X and Y run a 3 km race along a circular course of length 300 m. Their speeds are in the ratio 3:2. If they start together in the same direction, how many times would the first one pass the other (the start-off is not counted as passing)?

**Options:** (a) 2 · (b) 3 · (c) 4 · (d) 5

**Metadata:** Family 04 · Rates, Motion, Time and Geometry → Circular track overtaking · Slug: `circular-track-overtaking-2022-q15`

**FINAL answer: (b).**

**Full reasoning:** While X runs 3 km, Y runs 2 km. X gains 1 km, equal to \(1000/300=3\frac13\) laps. Each complete 300 m relative gain is one pass, so X passes Y three times before finishing.

**Shortest safe method:** Faster runner gains 1,000 m; \(\lfloor1000/300\rfloor=3\) passes.
