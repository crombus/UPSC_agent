## Q1(a). Three universal values in civil services

**Official question:** State the three basic values, universal in nature, in the context of civil services and bring out their importance. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2018 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q1(a) · Directive: State and explain importance · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → Aptitude and foundational civil-service values → integrity, impartiality and compassion  
**Canonical owner:** [Ethics/basic/04_Aptitude-and-Foundational-Values-for-Civil-Service](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/04_Aptitude-and-Foundational-Values-for-Civil-Service.md)

### Demand decoding

Choose defensible universal values, define them in public-service terms, and show why each is institutionally important. The trap is merely listing virtues or treating “universal” as culturally identical expression.

### Model answer

**Concept.** Three foundational values are:

1. **Integrity**—coherence between public duty, declared principles and conduct. It prevents corruption, misuse of discretion and private capture of office.
2. **Impartiality and objectivity**—deciding on relevant evidence and law rather than identity, ideology or patronage. They secure equality, predictability and non-partisan administration.
3. **Compassion**—recognising the vulnerability and dignity of persons affected by state action. It humanises rules, improves last-mile access and directs discretion towards substantive justice.

**Thinker anchor.** Kantian respect for persons supports impartial treatment; Aristotle’s practical wisdom shows that rules require context-sensitive judgment; Gandhi’s concern for the weakest gives compassion a public test.

**Application.** An officer allocating disaster relief should publish criteria, apply them without favour and arrange assisted access for elderly or disabled citizens.

**Qualification.** Universality concerns their moral core, not mechanical application. Compassion cannot excuse illegality, objectivity is not value-neutrality, and integrity requires constitutional—not merely personal—conviction.

**Examiner takeaway:** The answer defines three values, connects each to administrative outcomes, and qualifies their use under constitutional public service.

## Q1(b). Code of ethics and code of conduct

**Official question:** Distinguish between “Code of ethics” and “Code of conduct” with suitable examples. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2018 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q1(b) · Directive: Distinguish with examples · Difficulty: Easy-Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → Codes → ethics versus conduct  
**Canonical owner:** [Ethics/basic/16_Codes-of-Ethics-and-Codes-of-Conduct](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/16_Codes-of-Ethics-and-Codes-of-Conduct.md)

### Demand decoding

Draw clear bases of distinction—nature, content, enforceability and function—then illustrate both. Do not claim that an ethics code has no accountability effect or that a conduct code settles every moral dilemma.

### Model answer

| Basis | Code of ethics | Code of conduct |
|---|---|---|
| Core | Aspirational values and principles | Specific permitted, required or prohibited acts |
| Question answered | “What kind of public servant should I be?” | “What may or must I do?” |
| Form | Broad: integrity, impartiality, public interest | Detailed: gifts, assets, political activity, confidentiality |
| Enforcement | Mainly internal, reputational and institutional | Usually disciplinary and rule-based |
| Use | Guides judgment in grey areas | Creates minimum behavioural compliance |

**Concept and example.** “Serve every citizen fairly and disclose conflicts” is ethical guidance. A rule fixing the gift-value threshold and requiring prior permission is conduct regulation.

**Thinker anchor.** Aristotle’s virtue ethics explains character-oriented ethics; deontological duty supports clear conduct rules.

**Application.** A procurement officer may technically comply with tender rules yet violate ethics by designing specifications to favour one firm. Conversely, an ethics code without reporting and sanctions may remain symbolic.

**Qualification.** The two are complementary: conduct provides an enforceable floor; ethics supplies the higher compass for discretion. Training, advice, disclosure systems and proportionate sanctions must connect both.

**Examiner takeaway:** The distinction is functional rather than absolute: values guide judgment, while rules discipline behaviour.

## Q2(a). Public interest: meaning, principles and procedure

**Official question:** What is meant by public interest? What are the principles and procedures to be followed by the civil servants in public interest? (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2018 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q2(a) · Directive: Define and prescribe · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → Public-service values → public interest and responsible discretion  
**Canonical owner:** [Ethics/basic/09_Public-Service-Values-Status-and-Ethical-Dilemmas](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/09_Public-Service-Values-Status-and-Ethical-Dilemmas.md)

### Demand decoding

Define public interest without equating it with majority preference or governmental convenience, then state both substantive principles and a defensible decision procedure.

### Model answer

**Concept.** Public interest is the constitutionally legitimate, evidence-based advancement of the community’s common welfare while protecting rights, dignity and especially vulnerable minorities. It is not the private interest of the ruler, bureaucracy or numerical majority.

**Principles:** legality; constitutional morality; equality and non-discrimination; necessity and proportionality; impartiality; economy and sustainability; transparency; participation; accountability; and inter-generational justice.

**Procedure:**  
`define lawful objective → collect verified facts → map stakeholders and rights → disclose conflicts → consult affected groups → compare options and distributional effects → record reasons → choose the least restrictive effective option → communicate, implement, audit and review`.

**Thinker anchor.** Rawls’s concern for the least advantaged qualifies utilitarian aggregation; Gandhi’s *antyodaya* asks how policy affects the weakest.

**Application.** Land acquisition for a hospital may serve public interest only after alternatives, fair compensation, rehabilitation and environmental costs are considered.

**Qualification.** Urgency may shorten consultation, but cannot erase legality, reasoned decision-making or later scrutiny. A public-purpose label never by itself proves public interest.

**Examiner takeaway:** Public interest is a reasoned constitutional process, not an official assertion.

## Q2(b). RTI and the redefinition of accountability

**Official question:** “The Right to Information Act is not all about citizens’ empowerment alone, it essentially redefines the concept of accountability.” Discuss. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2018 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q2(b) · Directive: Discuss · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → Transparency and RTI → answerability and enforceability  
**Canonical owner:** [Ethics/basic/15_Transparency-RTI-and-Information-Sharing](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/15_Transparency-RTI-and-Information-Sharing.md)

### Demand decoding

Go beyond empowerment. Explain how information changes the principal-agent relationship, administrative records, answerability and consequences, while recognising lawful exemptions.

### Model answer

**Concept.** Accountability combines **answerability**—the duty to explain—and **enforceability**—the possibility of correction or consequence. RTI shifts government records from bureaucratic possession towards a presumptively public trust.

**How RTI redefines accountability:**
- converts secrecy from the default into an exception requiring justification;
- makes file-based reasons, delay and expenditure externally testable;
- enables citizens, media and legislatures to audit implementation;
- encourages record management and proactive disclosure;
- makes responsibility traceable to offices and decisions;
- supports social audits, grievance redress and anti-corruption action.

**Thinker anchor.** Bentham’s “publicity principle” treats visibility as a restraint on official power.

**Application.** Disclosure of beneficiary lists or public-work measurements permits excluded citizens to contest decisions and exposes ghost entries.

**Qualification.** Transparency is not absolute: privacy, fiduciary interests and genuine security may justify narrowly construed exemptions. Information alone also does not guarantee sanctions; independent appeals, whistle-blower protection and responsive institutions remain necessary.

Thus RTI empowers citizens precisely by transforming accountability from periodic hierarchical supervision into continuous, reason-giving public scrutiny.

**Examiner takeaway:** RTI changes both who may demand reasons and how everyday administration must document them.

## Q3(a). Actual and potential conflicts of interest

**Official question:** What is meant by conflict of interest? Illustrate with examples, the difference between the actual and potential conflicts of interest. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2018 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q3(a) · Directive: Define, distinguish and illustrate · Difficulty: Easy-Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → Ethical dilemmas → conflict of interest  
**Canonical owner:** [Ethics/basic/09_Public-Service-Values-Status-and-Ethical-Dilemmas](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/09_Public-Service-Values-Status-and-Ethical-Dilemmas.md)

### Demand decoding

Explain that conflict concerns competing interests and impaired—or reasonably perceived impaired—judgment, not necessarily proven corruption. Distinguish present collision from foreseeable future collision.

### Model answer

**Concept.** A conflict of interest arises when a public official’s private, familial, financial or associational interest competes with the impartial discharge of public duty.

| Type | Meaning | Illustration |
|---|---|---|
| **Actual** | The official is presently deciding a matter that affects the competing interest | A tender-committee member evaluates a company owned by her sibling |
| **Potential** | Present duties do not yet collide, but a foreseeable change can create a collision | An officer negotiating regulation with a firm from which he is seeking post-retirement employment |

An **apparent** conflict, separately, exists where a reasonable observer may suspect impairment even if none actually exists.

**Thinker anchor.** Public office is a fiduciary trust: power must be exercised for beneficiaries, not the trustee.

**Application.** The safeguards are early disclosure, recusal, divestment or blind trust, reassignment, documented reasons and independent review.

**Qualification.** A conflict is a risk condition, not proof of misconduct. Concealment, participation despite conflict, or resulting favouritism converts manageable risk into an integrity breach.

**Examiner takeaway:** Ethical administration manages both actual bias and reasonably foreseeable threats to impartiality.

## Q3(b). Integrity before intelligence and energy

**Official question:** “In looking for people to hire, you look for three qualities: integrity, intelligence and energy. And if they do not have the first, the other two will kill you.” — Warren Buffett. What do you understand by this statement in the present-day scenario? Explain. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2018 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q3(b) · Directive: Interpret and apply · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → Foundational values → integrity as the governing value of competence  
**Canonical owner:** [Ethics/basic/04_Aptitude-and-Foundational-Values-for-Civil-Service](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/04_Aptitude-and-Foundational-Values-for-Civil-Service.md)

### Demand decoding

Interpret the hierarchy among the three qualities and apply it to modern high-capacity organisations. Avoid claiming integrity alone is sufficient.

### Model answer

**Concept.** Intelligence supplies capability and energy supplies execution; integrity determines the purpose for which both are used. Without integrity, competence magnifies harm: manipulation becomes sophisticated and wrongdoing becomes efficient.

**Thinker anchor.** Aristotle treats virtue as directing capacities towards the good; Kant similarly refuses to call talents unconditionally good because their moral worth depends on a good will.

**Present application:**  
- a skilled data scientist can improve welfare targeting or engineer invasive surveillance;  
- an energetic banker can widen credit or conceal risky lending;  
- an intelligent civil servant can simplify procurement or design collusive specifications.

Integrity therefore lowers agency costs, sustains trust, encourages truthful dissent and protects institutions where supervision is incomplete.

**Qualification.** Integrity is necessary but not sufficient. A well-intentioned but incompetent employee can still cause grave harm. Recruitment must test all three through verified past conduct, conflict disclosures, problem-solving ability and probation; organisations must reinforce them through ethical leadership, protected reporting and accountable incentives.

The quotation is thus a warning that morally unguided talent converts organisational strength into systemic risk.

**Examiner takeaway:** Integrity is the directional value that turns capacity and effort into public or organisational good.

## Q4(a). Are all non-prohibited means permitted in doing good?

**Official question:** “In doing a good thing, everything is permitted which is not prohibited expressly or by clear implication.” Examine the statement with suitable examples in the context of a public servant discharging his/her duties. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2018 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q4(a) · Directive: Examine · Difficulty: High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → Moral theories → legality, discretion and ethical means  
**Canonical owner:** [Ethics/basic/08_Moral-Theories-Deontology-Consequentialism-Virtue-Ethics](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/08_Moral-Theories-Deontology-Consequentialism-Virtue-Ethics.md)

### Demand decoding

Test the proposition, not merely reject it. Distinguish private liberty from public power and show why legality is a floor, while also preserving legitimate innovation.

### Model answer

**Concept.** The proposition is unsafe for public servants. Citizens may ordinarily do what law does not prohibit; public officials exercise entrusted power and therefore need lawful authority, proper purpose and fair procedure.

**Thinker anchor.** Kantian ethics forbids treating persons merely as means. Rule-of-law morality adds that benevolent ends do not create unlimited official jurisdiction.

**Application.**
- Secretly sharing beneficiary health data may improve coordination but violates privacy and purpose limitation.
- Diverting funds from a sanctioned head to urgent relief may appear beneficial but defeats legislative control.
- Conversely, arranging a voluntary interdepartmental help desk within delegated authority is legitimate innovation even if no rule specifically commands it.

An action should satisfy: competence, legality, constitutional rights, necessity, proportionality, transparency, impartiality and auditability.

**Qualification.** Rules cannot anticipate every emergency. Where law gives discretion, an officer may innovate for a good objective, but should choose the least rights-restrictive option, record reasons, consult competent authority where feasible and seek prompt ratification and review.

Hence absence of prohibition is insufficient; public action requires positive authority and ethically disciplined discretion.

**Examiner takeaway:** A good end expands neither jurisdiction nor permission; it must be pursued through lawful, proportionate means.

## Q4(b). Means and ends

**Official question:** With regard to morality of actions, one view is that means are of paramount importance and the other view is that the ends justify the means. Which view do you think is more appropriate? Justify your answer. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2018 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q4(b) · Directive: Choose and justify · Difficulty: Moderate-High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → Moral theories → deontology and consequentialism  
**Canonical owner:** [Ethics/basic/08_Moral-Theories-Deontology-Consequentialism-Virtue-Ethics](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/08_Moral-Theories-Deontology-Consequentialism-Virtue-Ethics.md)

### Demand decoding

Take a clear position while engaging both views. The best answer rejects both absolute rule fetishism and unrestricted consequentialism.

### Model answer

**Concept.** Means deserve presumptive primacy because they embody rights, fairness and the kind of institution being created. Unjust means contaminate ends by harming innocents, normalising abuse and destroying trust.

**Thinker anchor.** Gandhi held that means are to ends as seed is to tree; Kant bars using persons merely as instruments. Consequentialists rightly remind us, however, that foreseeable outcomes cannot be ignored.

**Application.** Torture cannot become ethical merely because information may be useful; fabricated evidence undermines justice even against a suspected criminal. Yet rigid procedure should not prevent a proportionate emergency evacuation undertaken under lawful necessity.

**Preferred view:** ethically permissible action needs both:
1. **side-constraints**—legality, dignity, basic rights, non-discrimination and due process; and
2. **consequence testing**—effectiveness, proportionality, distribution of harms and reversibility.

**Qualification.** In tragic choices every option may impose harm. The decision-maker should then choose the least harmful lawful option, document necessity, provide safeguards and accept review—not claim that a desirable end cleanses any means.

Thus good ends must be pursued through defensible means, with consequences used to select among morally permissible alternatives.

**Examiner takeaway:** Rights constrain the option set; consequences rank the options remaining within it.

## Q5(a). Rational policy for a dam affecting forests and ethnic communities

**Official question:** Suppose the Government of India is thinking of constructing a dam in a mountain valley bound by forests and inhabited by ethnic communities. What rational policy should it resort to in dealing with unforeseen contingencies? (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2018 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q5(a) · Directive: Suggest policy · Difficulty: High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → Environmental ethics → precaution, participation and just transition  
**Canonical owner:** [Ethics/basic/13_Emerging-Ethics-Technology-AI-and-Environment](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/13_Emerging-Ethics-Technology-AI-and-Environment.md)

### Demand decoding

Design a rational policy under uncertainty, not decide abstractly for or against the dam. Integrate environmental, cultural, livelihood, safety and procedural justice.

### Model answer

**Concept.** Government should adopt an **adaptive precautionary policy**: proceed only after transparent assessment, participation and readiness to revise or stop when new risks emerge.

**Policy architecture:**
- independent cumulative environmental and social-impact assessment, including downstream and disaster risks;
- genuine consultation with affected communities in accessible language and respect for applicable forest and community rights;
- compare no-project, alternative-site and lower-impact designs;
- publish costs, benefits, uncertainty and distributional effects;
- time-bound, land-for-land where feasible rehabilitation with livelihood, cultural and community safeguards;
- biodiversity offsets only after avoidance and minimisation;
- dam-safety, early-warning, emergency evacuation and climate-stress plans;
- staged approvals, independent monitoring, grievance redress, compensation reserve and periodic review with stop-work triggers.

**Thinker anchor.** Rawlsian justice protects those bearing concentrated costs; inter-generational ethics limits irreversible ecological transfer.

**Application.** Unexpected slope instability or livelihood loss should activate pre-declared review, relief and redesign protocols rather than be treated as sunk cost.

**Qualification.** Precaution is not “zero risk”; it requires proportionate action under uncertainty. Development is legitimate only when benefits cannot be achieved by less harmful means and affected people are not made sacrificial minorities.

**Examiner takeaway:** Rationality under uncertainty means adaptive, participatory and reversible governance.

## Q5(b). Resolving ethical dilemmas in public administration

**Official question:** Explain the process of resolving ethical dilemmas in Public Administration. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2018 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q5(b) · Directive: Explain a process · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → Public-service dilemmas → structured resolution  
**Canonical owner:** [Ethics/basic/09_Public-Service-Values-Status-and-Ethical-Dilemmas](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/09_Public-Service-Values-Status-and-Ethical-Dilemmas.md)

### Demand decoding

Provide an operational sequence that distinguishes fact, value, option and review. Generic virtue lists do not answer “process.”

### Model answer

**Concept and process:**

1. **Clarify facts and mandate:** verify evidence, law, urgency and decision authority.
2. **Frame the dilemma:** identify the genuinely conflicting duties or values, not personalities.
3. **Map stakeholders:** include silent, vulnerable and future stakeholders.
4. **Set hard limits:** reject illegality, corruption, discrimination and disproportionate rights violations.
5. **Generate options:** include consultation, recusal, sequencing, pilot or escalation—not only binary choices.
6. **Test options:** legality; public interest; duty and rights; consequences; virtue; publicity; reversibility; and precedent.
7. **Seek counsel:** consult rules, code, conscience, competent colleagues or ethics adviser while retaining responsibility.
8. **Decide and record reasons:** select the least harmful, proportionate and implementable option.
9. **Safeguard and review:** communicate, monitor outcomes, provide grievance redress and correct errors.

**Thinker anchor.** Aristotle’s *phronesis*—practical wisdom—integrates principle with context.

**Application.** In conflicting welfare claims, an officer should publish criteria, hear affected groups and document prioritisation.

**Qualification.** Conscience is a warning signal, not a private veto over law. In emergencies, abbreviated procedure requires stronger documentation and ex-post scrutiny.

**Examiner takeaway:** Ethical resolution is accountable practical reasoning, not intuition alone.

## Q6(a). Lincoln on weighing good and evil in public policy

**Official question:** What do each of the following quotations mean to you in the present context? (a) “The true rule, in determining to embrace, or reject any thing, is not whether it has any evil in it; but whether it has more evil than good. There are few things wholly evil or wholly good. Almost every thing, especially of governmental policy, is an inseparable compound of the two; so that our best judgment of the preponderance between them is continually demanded.” — Abraham Lincoln. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2018 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q6(a) · Directive: Interpret in present context · Difficulty: High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → Western thinkers and applied moral reasoning → proportionality and policy trade-offs  
**Canonical owner:** [Ethics/basic/07_Western-Moral-Philosophers-and-Thinkers](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/07_Western-Moral-Philosophers-and-Thinkers.md)

### Model answer

**Concept.** Lincoln rejects moral simplification in public policy. Most collective decisions distribute benefits, burdens and risks; responsible government must compare them rather than demand an impossible harm-free option.

**Thinker anchor.** This resembles rule-constrained consequential reasoning: outcomes matter, but judgment must remain bounded by rights and public justification.

**Present application.** A public-health restriction may protect life yet burden livelihood and liberty. The ethical task is not to deny either side, but to test necessity, proportionality, duration, distribution, alternatives and safeguards. Similarly, infrastructure may produce public benefits while displacing communities, requiring avoidance, compensation and rehabilitation.

Good judgment therefore asks: Who gains? Who bears harm? Is the harm reversible? Is a less restrictive option available? Can the burden be mitigated? Are reasons public and reviewable?

**Qualification.** “More good than evil” cannot legitimise torture, discrimination or sacrificing a powerless minority for aggregate gain. Some rights are side-constraints; uncertainty and unequal burdens require precaution.

The quotation calls for humility, evidence and continuous review: policy is ethical when trade-offs are acknowledged, justified and reduced—not hidden behind slogans.

**Examiner takeaway:** Mature policy ethics weighs consequences without allowing aggregate benefit to erase dignity and rights.

## Q6(b). Gandhi on anger, intolerance and understanding

**Official question:** What do each of the following quotations mean to you in the present context? (b) “Anger and intolerance are the enemies of correct understanding.” — Mahatma Gandhi. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2018 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q6(b) · Directive: Interpret in present context · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → Indian thinkers → non-violence, tolerance and epistemic humility  
**Canonical owner:** [Ethics/basic/06_Indian-Moral-Thinkers-and-Philosophers](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/06_Indian-Moral-Thinkers-and-Philosophers.md)

### Model answer

**Concept.** Anger narrows attention and encourages impulsive attribution; intolerance closes the mind to evidence and another person’s standpoint. Together they replace inquiry with certainty, making correct understanding—of facts, motives and remedies—unlikely.

**Thinker anchor.** Gandhi’s *ahimsa* is not passive avoidance but disciplined regard for the opponent’s humanity. His idea of relative truth demands humility because no individual possesses the whole truth.

**Present application.** In polarised social media, communal tension or a hostile public hearing, an administrator who reacts to provocation may generalise blame and use excessive force. Emotional regulation, verified information, patient listening and dialogue separate legitimate grievance from misinformation and enable proportionate action.

At an institutional level, reasoned debate, diverse teams, mediation and speaking orders reduce the cognitive errors produced by echo chambers.

**Qualification.** The quotation does not demand moral indifference. Anger can signal injustice and intolerance of cruelty may be justified. The ethical task is to transform moral outrage into non-violent, evidence-based action rather than permit hatred to control judgment.

Thus understanding requires empathy, self-restraint and willingness to revise one’s view.

**Examiner takeaway:** Emotion may alert conscience, but only disciplined empathy and evidence can guide public action.

## Q6(c). Tirukkural on falsehood and unblemished common good

**Official question:** What do each of the following quotations mean to you in the present context? (c) “Falsehood takes the place of truth when it results in unblemished common good.” — Tirukkural. (150 words, 10 marks)

**Metadata:** 2018 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q6(c) · Directive: Interpret in present context · Difficulty: High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → Indian thinkers → truth, intention and exceptional moral conflict  
**Canonical owner:** [Ethics/basic/06_Indian-Moral-Thinkers-and-Philosophers](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/06_Indian-Moral-Thinkers-and-Philosophers.md)

### Model answer

**Concept.** The couplet recognises a narrow conflict between truth-telling and prevention of grave harm. A false statement may be morally defensible only when it produces genuinely “unblemished” public good—without selfish motive, avoidable injury or injustice.

**Thinker anchor.** It qualifies rigid deontology through compassionate consequences, but its exceptionally high threshold protects truth as the normal rule.

**Application.** Misleading a violent pursuer about a victim’s hiding place may protect life. Temporarily withholding operational details during a rescue may prevent panic. By contrast, hiding policy failure, manipulating statistics or issuing convenient propaganda cannot qualify: these protect authority, impair autonomy and corrode trust.

A defensible exception should satisfy necessity, last resort, proportionality, good faith, absence of personal gain, minimal deception, temporary duration, and later accountability where disclosure becomes safe.

**Qualification.** Governments cannot self-certify “common good” to justify secrecy. Institutional checks, recorded reasons and independent review are essential because habitual noble lies destroy democratic consent.

The quotation therefore does not celebrate falsehood; it tests whether compassion in an extreme case can override literal truth without staining justice.

**Examiner takeaway:** Truth remains the rule; only necessary, selfless and harm-preventing deception can approach the couplet’s demanding exception.

## Q7. Rakesh and eligibility under a senior-citizen healthcare scheme

**Official case and demand (verified transcription):** Rakesh is a responsible district-level officer, who enjoys the trust of his higher officials. Knowing his honesty, the government entrusted him with the responsibility of identifying the beneficiaries under a healthcare scheme meant for senior citizens. The criteria to be a beneficiary are the following:

(a) 60 years of age or above.  
(b) Belonging to a reserved community.  
(c) Family income of less than ₹1 lakh per annum.  
(d) Post-treatment prognosis is likely to be high to make a positive difference to the quality of life of the beneficiary.

One day, an old couple visited Rakesh’s office with their application. They have been the residents of a village in his district since their birth. The old man is diagnosed with a rare condition that causes obstruction in the large intestine. As a consequence, he has severe abdominal pain frequently that prevents him from doing any physical labour. The couple have no children to support them. The expert surgeon whom they contacted is willing to do the surgery without charging any fee. However, the couple will have to bear the cost of incidental charges, such as medicines, hospitalization, etc., to the tune of ₹1 lakh. The couple fulfills all the criteria except criterion ‘b’. However, any financial aid would certainly make a significant difference in their quality of life. **How should Rakesh respond to the situation?** (250 words, 20 marks)

**Metadata:** 2018 · Mains · GS-IV · Section B · Official Q7 · Demand: resolve eligibility-versus-need dilemma · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → Case-study method → compassion within legality  
**Canonical owners:** [Case-study method](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/22_Case-Study-Method-and-Answer-Architecture.md) · [Public-service dilemmas](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/09_Public-Service-Values-Status-and-Ethical-Dilemmas.md)

### Stakeholders and values

- Couple: life, health, dignity and fair access.
- Eligible reserved-community applicants: distributive justice and protection from diversion.
- Rakesh and government: legality, fidelity to scheme design, compassion and accountability.
- Surgeon, hospital and community donors: professional beneficence and social solidarity.

**Conflict:** Individual need and substantive fairness versus rule-based eligibility and the rights of the intended beneficiary class.

### Options and consequences

1. **Illegally include the couple:** rapid relief, but falsifies eligibility, displaces a lawful claimant and creates an arbitrary precedent.
2. **Reject mechanically:** preserves the rule but abandons vulnerable citizens despite available administrative alternatives.
3. **Lawful bridge plus reform:** issue a reasoned ineligibility decision under this scheme, urgently mobilise another lawful source, and recommend a residual hardship window for future cases.

### Chosen action

Rakesh should not alter records or misuse the reserved scheme. He should:

1. verify age, income, diagnosis, urgency and estimated costs;
2. explain the decision empathetically and in writing;
3. obtain immediate referral under any general health, indigent-patient, district relief or hospital social-welfare mechanism lawfully available;
4. coordinate the surgeon’s waiver with generic medicines, public-hospital admission and transparent voluntary assistance, without coercing subordinates or contractors;
5. escalate a documented proposal for an emergency/discretionary hardship fund or revised scheme design, without delaying treatment.

### Safeguards

No fabrication, preferential queue-jumping or informal cash handling; written approvals, conflict-free fund mobilisation, receipts, audit and equal publication of alternate assistance channels.

**Examiner takeaway:** Compassion should activate lawful alternatives and policy feedback, not discriminatory rule-breaking.

## Q8. Ministerial pressure, road realignment and insider benefit

**Official case and demands (verified transcription):** As a senior officer in the Ministry, you have access to important policy decisions and upcoming big announcements such as road construction projects before they are notified in the public domain. The Ministry is about to announce a mega road project for which the drawings are already in place. Sufficient care was taken by the planners to make use of the government land with minimum land acquisition from private parties. Compensation rate for private parties was also finalized as per government rules. Care was also taken to minimize deforestation. Once the project is announced, it is expected that there will be a huge spurt in real estate prices in and around that area.

Meanwhile, the Minister concerned insists that you realign the road in such a way that it comes closer to his 20 acres farm house. He also suggests that he would facilitate purchase of a big plot of land in your wife’s name at the prevailing rate which is very nominal, in and around the proposed mega road project. He also tries to convince you by saying that there is no harm in it as he is buying the land legally. He even promises to supplement your savings in case you do not have sufficient funds to buy the land.

However, by the act of realignment, a lot of agricultural land has to be acquired, thereby causing considerable financial burden on the government, and also displacement of the farmers. As if this is not enough, it will involve cutting down of a large number of trees, denuding the area of its green cover. Faced with this situation, **what will you do? Critically examine various conflicts of interest and explain what are your responsibilities as a public servant.** (250 words, 20 marks)

**Metadata:** 2018 · Mains · GS-IV · Section B · Official Q8 · Demand: identify conflicts, duties and action · Difficulty: High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → Case-study method → corruption, confidential information and public trust  
**Canonical owners:** [Case-study method](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/22_Case-Study-Method-and-Answer-Architecture.md) · [Public funds and corruption](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/18_Utilization-of-Public-Funds-and-Challenges-of-Corruption.md)

### Stakeholders and competing values

Farmers, taxpayers, road users, environment, Ministry, Minister, officer and spouse. Values: integrity, impartiality, confidentiality, legality, economy, environmental stewardship and courage.

**Conflicts of interest:**
- Minister’s public duty versus private appreciation of his farmhouse.
- Officer’s official access versus prospective family gain.
- Confidential policy information versus land speculation.
- Political responsiveness versus professional, evidence-based advice.
- Development objective versus avoidable displacement, fiscal and ecological harm.

### Options and consequences

1. **Comply and buy land:** private gain and ministerial favour, but bribery-like inducement, abuse of confidential information, public loss and career/criminal exposure.
2. **Refuse orally and continue informally:** avoids immediate wrongdoing but leaves pressure and evidence unmanaged.
3. **Formal refusal, preserve process and report through lawful channels:** risks retaliation but protects the project and institution.

### Action

I would reject the offer unequivocally; make no purchase directly or through relatives; secure plans and restrict access; record the direction and my technical objections; require any proposed realignment to undergo fresh written appraisal on cost, acquisition, environment and public purpose; and place the original and altered options before the competent collective authority. If pressure persists, I would report it through the designated senior/vigilance channel and seek recusal or written directions without executing a manifestly improper order.

### Responsibilities and safeguards

Serve public interest, protect confidentiality, tender fearless advice, conserve funds and environment, disclose conflicts and maintain records. Safeguards include e-procurement/geospatial audit, independent technical review, asset disclosures, file tracking, farmer consultation and protection against retaliation.

**Examiner takeaway:** Legality of a land sale cannot cleanse misuse of insider information or policy manipulation for private enrichment.

## Q9. Illicit liquor in a prohibition district

**Official case and demand (verified transcription):** It is a State where prohibition is in force. You are recently appointed as the Superintendent of Police of a district notorious for illicit distillation of liquor. The illicit liquor leads to many deaths, reported and unreported, and causes a major problem for the district authorities. The approach till now had been to view it as a law-and-order problem and tackle it accordingly. Raids, arrests, police cases, criminal trials—all these had only limited impact. The problem remains as serious as ever.

Your inspections show that the parts of the district where the distillation flourishes are economically, industrially and educationally backward. Agriculture is badly affected by poor irrigation facilities. Frequent clashes among communities gave boost to illicit distillation. No major initiatives had taken place in the past either from the government’s side or from social organizations to improve the lot of the people. **Which new approach will you adopt to bring the problem under control?** (250 words, 20 marks)

**Metadata:** 2018 · Mains · GS-IV · Section B · Official Q9 · Demand: propose an integrated new approach · Difficulty: Moderate-High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → Case-study method → humane and preventive policing  
**Canonical owners:** [Case-study method](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/22_Case-Study-Method-and-Answer-Architecture.md) · [Public-service dilemmas](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/09_Public-Service-Values-Status-and-Ethical-Dilemmas.md)

### Stakeholders and values

Consumers and families, poor distillers, criminal financiers, local communities, police, excise and health authorities. Values: life, rule of law, livelihood, fairness, trust and effectiveness.

### Options and consequences

1. **More raids alone:** visible deterrence but repeats a failed strategy, may target the poorest and shift production.
2. **Tolerate illicit supply:** reduces confrontation but abandons law and exposes citizens to toxic liquor and organised crime.
3. **Integrated enforcement-development-public-health approach:** slower and coordination-intensive, but attacks both supply networks and structural demand.

### Action plan

- Map deaths, brewing clusters, supply chains, financiers, corrupt protection and vulnerable households.
- Prioritise intelligence-led action against organisers, toxic inputs, transport and proceeds; ensure lawful search, evidence integrity and police accountability.
- Create confidential reporting, witness protection, community liaison and cross-border coordination.
- Partner with health services for de-addiction, counselling, emergency toxicology response and honest risk communication.
- Convene district administration, irrigation, livelihoods, education, women’s groups and credible civil society for alternative employment, self-help groups, skill and credit support.
- Use peace committees and mediation to reduce communal conflict that sustains criminal dependence.
- Set measurable indicators: mortality, recurrence, network disruption, treatment uptake and livelihood transition—not arrest counts alone.

### Safeguards

No collective punishment, fabricated cases, extortion or harassment of families. Rotate sensitive staff, audit seizures, act against colluding personnel, publish anonymised outcomes and review unintended displacement of the trade.

**Examiner takeaway:** Ethical policing combines firm action against profiteers with prevention, health care and viable exits for vulnerable participants.

## Q10. Polluting chemical factory, closure and unemployment

**Official case and demand (verified transcription):** A big corporate house is engaged in manufacturing industrial chemicals on a large scale. It proposes to set up an additional unit. Many States rejected its proposal due to detrimental effect on the environment. But one State government acceded to the request and permitted the unit close to a city, brushing aside all opposition. The unit was set up 10 years ago and was in full swing till recently.

The pollution caused by the industrial effluents was affecting the land, water and crops in the area. It was also causing serious health problems to human beings and animals. This gave rise to a series of agitations demanding the closure of the plant. In a recent agitation thousands of people took part, creating a law-and-order problem necessitating stern police action. Following the public outcry, the State government ordered the closure of the factory.

The closure of the factory resulted in the unemployment of not only those workers who were engaged in the factory but also those who were working in the ancillary units. It also very badly affected those industries which depended on the chemicals manufactured by it. **As a senior officer entrusted with the responsibility of handling this issue, how are you going to address it?** (250 words, 20 marks)

**Metadata:** 2018 · Mains · GS-IV · Section B · Official Q10 · Demand: reconcile environment, health, livelihood and order · Difficulty: High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → Case-study method → environmental justice and just transition  
**Canonical owners:** [Case-study method](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/22_Case-Study-Method-and-Answer-Architecture.md) · [Environmental ethics](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/13_Emerging-Ethics-Technology-AI-and-Environment.md)

### Stakeholders and values

Residents, farmers, workers, ancillary firms, consumers, company, regulators, police, animals and future generations. Values: life and health, livelihood, rule of law, polluter accountability, procedural justice and peace.

### Options and consequences

1. **Permanent immediate closure only:** removes ongoing emissions but creates abrupt livelihood loss and may leave legacy contamination untreated.
2. **Unconditional reopening:** restores jobs but externalises grave health and ecological costs.
3. **Conditional, independently verified remediation with a just transition:** balances interests without bargaining away safety.

### Action

First maintain peace through dialogue, proportionate policing and medical outreach. Commission independent sampling and health/environment audits; publish results; identify regulatory failures; and require the company to stop discharge, fund treatment, compensation and site restoration under the polluter-pays principle.

Reopening, if technically and legally possible, should occur only in phases after best-available pollution controls, safe-waste systems, continuous monitoring, emergency plans and independently certified compliance. If compliance is impossible, closure remains, accompanied by retraining, wage/transition support, redeployment, assistance to ancillary units and a regional alternative-industry package. Farmers and residents should receive health surveillance, clean water, crop-loss assessment and accessible claims.

### Safeguards

Escrowed remediation funds, third-party monitoring with community representation, real-time disclosure, whistle-blower channel, time-bound milestones, automatic shutdown triggers and personal accountability for collusion. Review the original approval process.

**Examiner takeaway:** Jobs and ecology are not traded as absolutes; the ethical response internalises pollution costs and protects workers through a just transition.

## Q11. Dr X, substantial tax liability and technical defaults

**Official case and demand (verified transcription):** Dr X is a leading medical practitioner in a city. He has set up a charitable trust through which he plans to establish a super-speciality hospital in the city to cater to the medical needs of all sections of the society. Incidentally, that part of the State had been neglected over the years. The proposed hospital would be a boon for the region.

You are heading the tax investigation agency of that region. During an inspection of the doctor’s clinic, your officers have found out some major irregularities. A few of them are substantial which had resulted in considerable withholding of tax that should be paid by him now. The doctor is cooperative. He undertakes to pay the tax immediately.

However, there are certain other deficiencies in his tax compliance which are purely technical in nature. If these technical defaults are pursued by the agency, considerable time and energy of the doctor will be diverted to issues which are not so serious, urgent or even helpful to the tax collection process. Further, in all probability, it will hamper the prospects of the hospital coming up.

There are two options before you:

(i) Taking a broader view, ensure substantial tax compliance and ignore defaults that are merely technical in nature.  
(ii) Pursue the matter strictly and proceed on all fronts, whether substantial or merely technical.

**As the head of the tax agency, which course of action will you opt for and why?** (250 words, 20 marks)

**Metadata:** 2018 · Mains · GS-IV · Section B · Official Q11 · Demand: choose and justify between stated courses · Difficulty: High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → Case-study method → proportionality, consistency and reasoned discretion  
**Canonical owners:** [Case-study method](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/22_Case-Study-Method-and-Answer-Architecture.md) · [Probity](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/14_Probity-Concept-and-Philosophical-Basis-of-Governance.md)

### Stakeholders and values

Taxpayers, patients in the neglected region, Dr X and trust, tax agency and similarly situated assessees. Values: legality, revenue protection, equality, proportionality, administrative economy and social welfare.

### Options and consequences

1. **Ignore all technical defaults:** supports the hospital and economy of enforcement, but arbitrary waiver may exceed authority and create unequal treatment.
2. **Maximum action on every default:** formal consistency, but disproportionate use of power and resources may harm a public-benefit project without revenue gain.
3. **Substantial compliance plus lawful, proportionate correction:** best reconciles both options.

### Chosen course

I would recover the full substantial tax, interest and any legally required consequence after due process. Technical defaults should not be secretly “ignored”; I would use only authorised discretion—notice, correction opportunity, compounding, warning or proportionate penalty according to uniformly applicable law and circulars. Reasons must distinguish revenue loss, culpability, recurrence and curability. The hospital’s social value may inform sequencing and proportionality where law permits, but cannot purchase immunity.

### Safeguards

Obtain legal opinion, disclose any conflict, use a speaking order, apply the same matrix to comparable cases, collect payment before closure, require corrected filings, preserve appeal rights and subject the decision to supervisory/audit review. Separate the trust’s approvals from the doctor’s tax assessment.

**Qualification.** Beneficial intent does not erase liability; equally, enforcement is not ethical when punishment of harmless form defeats statutory purpose.

**Examiner takeaway:** Choose substantive compliance through transparent, rule-bound proportionality—not charity-driven favour or mechanical severity.

## Q12. Edward Snowden: legality, privacy and national security

**Official case and demands (verified transcription):** Edward Snowden, a computer expert and former CIA systems administrator, released confidential Government documents to the press about the existence of Government surveillance programmes. According to many legal experts and the US Government, his actions violated the Espionage Act of 1917, which identified the leak of State secrets as an act of treason.

Yet, despite the fact that he broke the law, Snowden argued that he had a moral obligation to act. He gave a justification for his “whistle-blowing” by stating that he had a duty “to inform the public as to that which is done in their name and that which is done against them.” According to Snowden, the Government’s violation of privacy had to be exposed regardless of legality since more substantive issues of social action and public morality were involved here.

Many agreed with Snowden. Few argued that he broke the law and compromised national security, for which he should be held accountable. **Do you agree that Snowden’s actions were ethically justified even if legally prohibited? Why or why not? Make an argument by weighing the competing values in this case.** (250 words, 20 marks)

**Metadata:** 2018 · Mains · GS-IV · Section B · Official Q12 · Demand: take a position by weighing competing values · Difficulty: High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → Case-study method → whistle-blowing, conscience, privacy and security  
**Canonical owners:** [Case-study method](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/22_Case-Study-Method-and-Answer-Architecture.md) · [Named cases](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/23_Comparative-and-Named-Real-Case-Studies.md) · [Law, rules and conscience](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/10_Sources-of-Ethical-Guidance-Laws-Rules-Conscience.md)

### Stakeholders and competing values

Citizens under surveillance, security personnel and operations, government, press, whistle-blower and democratic institutions.

- **For disclosure:** privacy, liberty, informed democratic consent, transparency, conscience and accountability for potentially unlawful mass surveillance.
- **Against indiscriminate disclosure:** national security, lawful confidentiality, institutional trust, safety of personnel and prevention of avoidable operational harm.

### Options and consequences

1. **Remain silent:** protects secrecy but permits possible rights violations without democratic correction.
2. **Release everything publicly:** maximises exposure but may reveal operationally sensitive material and harm innocents.
3. **Protected, minimised and evidence-led disclosure:** use credible oversight channels where effective; if they fail or are compromised, disclose only what is necessary through responsible intermediaries.

### Judgment and action standard

Snowden’s objective of exposing surveillance affecting public rights can be ethically defensible even where domestic law prohibits disclosure; legality and morality are related but not identical. Ethical justification, however, is not automatic. It depends on reasonable belief in serious wrongdoing, public-interest predominance, exhaustion or futility of safer channels, data minimisation, protection of identities and methods, absence of private gain, and proportionality between disclosure and harm.

### Safeguards and qualification

Independent public-interest review, redaction, responsible journalism, opportunity for official response, legislative/judicial oversight and legal protection for good-faith whistle-blowers reduce the forced choice between silence and mass leakage.

My conclusion is therefore **qualified justification**: exposing the existence and rights-impact of disproportionate surveillance may be ethical, but avoidable disclosure of unrelated security secrets would remain wrong and accountable.

**Examiner takeaway:** Conscience may override secrecy only through necessary, proportionate and harm-minimising public-interest disclosure.
