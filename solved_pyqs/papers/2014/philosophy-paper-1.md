### Q1(a). How are the synthetic a priori judgements justifiable according to Kant ? Explain.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Kant](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/kant.md) → synthetic *a priori* judgement · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Directive: Explain and justify · Syllabus unit: Kant—possibility of synthetic *a priori* judgements · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Kant](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Kant.md) · **Slug:** `kant-synthetic-a-priori-justification`

**Demand decoding:** Define the apparently paradoxical class, identify its transcendental conditions, and show why its validity is restricted to possible experience.

**Argument map:** analytic truth merely explicates concepts → empirical synthesis lacks necessity → mind supplies pure forms and categories → these organise every possible experience → judgements grounded in them are informative yet necessary → validity ends at appearances.

#### Model answer

Synthetic *a priori* judgements enlarge knowledge because their predicates are not contained in their subjects, yet they possess necessity and universality independently of particular observations. Kant justifies them through a **transcendental** question: what must the knowing mind contribute for experience itself to be possible?

Sensibility necessarily receives appearances through the pure forms of space and time. Hence geometrical and arithmetical judgements can be synthetic yet *a priori*. Understanding then unifies this manifold through categories such as substance and causality. “Every event has a cause” is not inductively derived; lawful succession is a condition under which occurrences can be experienced as objective events. The transcendental deduction connects the categories to the unity of apperception, while schematism gives them temporal application.

Their necessity is therefore neither Platonic intuition nor verbal definition: it rests on the constitutive structure of possible human experience.

**Qualification:** Such judgements apply only to appearances, not things-in-themselves. Kant secures objective experience, though critics dispute whether his logical table exhausts its conceptual conditions.

**Why this earns marks:** It explains synthesis, apriority, transcendental justification and the critical limit.

### Q1(b). Bring out the significance of ‘language games’ in Wittgenstein’s use theory of meaning.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Later Wittgenstein](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/later-wittgenstein.md) → language-games and meaning-as-use · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Directive: Bring out significance · Syllabus unit: Later Wittgenstein—meaning and use · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Later Wittgenstein](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Later-Wittgenstein.md) · **Slug:** `wittgenstein-language-games-use-meaning`

**Demand decoding:** Define language-games and show how they replace a single referential essence of meaning with public, rule-governed practice.

**Argument map:** Augustinian picture equates meaning with naming → actual speech performs many activities → word’s role is fixed within a practice → rules and criteria are publicly learned → philosophical confusion comes from wrenching words from their games.

#### Model answer

Wittgenstein calls the interwoven unity of speech and activity a **language-game**. Ordering, reporting, calculating, praying, joking and promising are different games: each has its own point, rules and standards of correct use. Thus “the meaning of a word is its use in the language” rejects the assumption that every meaningful expression names an object or corresponds to one mental image.

The concept is significant in four ways. First, it explains semantic diversity through **family resemblance**, not one essence common to all language. Second, meaning becomes public and normative: mastery is shown by rule-governed participation learned through training. Third, games are embedded in broader forms of life; “Slab!” functions as an order within a shared building practice. Fourth, philosophical therapy returns expressions from metaphysical abstraction to their ordinary homes—for example, “knowing” need not name a private inner state.

**Evaluation:** Language-games illuminate context and practice without making meaning arbitrary, because rules and shared criteria constrain use. Yet they may describe functioning language better than explain how practices themselves are critically assessed.

**Why this earns marks:** It connects language-games directly to use, rules, public criteria and philosophical method.

### Q1(c). Explain the significance of ‘bracketing’ in Husserl phenomenology.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Phenomenology](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/phenomenology.md) → epoché and reduction · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Directive: Explain significance · Syllabus unit: Husserl—phenomenological method · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Phenomenology](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Phenomenology-Husserl.md) · **Slug:** `husserl-bracketing-significance`

**Demand decoding:** Explain bracketing as suspension rather than denial and connect it to intentional description, reduction and transcendental inquiry.

**Argument map:** natural attitude posits an independently existing world → epoché suspends that positing → object is studied as given-to-consciousness → intentional correlations and essences become visible → reduction reaches constituting subjectivity.

#### Model answer

Husserl’s “bracketing” or epoché suspends the natural attitude’s unexamined commitment that the world simply exists in the way ordinarily assumed. It does not deny the world or doubt it in Cartesian fashion; it places the existential thesis “out of play” so inquiry can attend to how an object is given.

Its significance is methodological. Bracketing redirects attention from the tree as a natural object to the intentional correlation between perceiving and the tree-as-perceived. Different acts—perception, memory and imagination—present objects through distinct profiles and horizons. Phenomenological reduction thereby discloses structures such as intentionality, temporal synthesis and embodiment; eidetic variation seeks invariant essences. A further transcendental reduction examines subjectivity as the field in which meaning and objectivity are constituted.

Bracketing thus frees description from premature naturalism and metaphysical assumptions.

**Evaluation:** It made first-person experience philosophically rigorous, though critics question whether every background belief can be suspended and whether transcendental reduction moves toward idealism.

**Why this earns marks:** It states what is suspended, what is disclosed and why the method matters.

### Q1(d). Does Leibnitz’s theory of pre-established harmony necessarily lead to determinism ? Discuss.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Rationalism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/rationalism.md) → Leibniz’s pre-established harmony · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Directive: Discuss necessity of implication · Syllabus unit: Leibniz—monads and pre-established harmony · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Rationalism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Rationalism.md) · **Slug:** `leibniz-pre-established-harmony-determinism`

**Demand decoding:** Preserve the source spelling “Leibnitz”; explain harmony and distinguish causal, logical and moral necessity.

**Argument map:** monads have no causal windows → each unfolds an internally programmed series → God coordinates all series from creation → complete concepts contain every predicate → apparent determinism → Leibniz distinguishes certainty from compulsion and necessary from contingent truths.

#### Model answer

Leibniz’s pre-established harmony holds that monads do not causally interact. Each unfolds its perceptions from its own complete concept, while God originally coordinates all series so that mind and body agree like perfectly synchronised clocks. Every later state therefore follows from the monad’s preceding state, and God chooses the complete history of the best possible world.

This strongly suggests determinism: Caesar’s crossing the Rubicon and my future choice are already included in complete individual concepts. No event interrupts the established order.

Leibniz nevertheless denies **necessitarianism**. Necessary truths have opposites that are contradictory; contingent truths have reasons but their opposites remain logically possible. Human acts are free when they proceed spontaneously from the agent’s perceptions, reasons and inclinations rather than external coercion. God foreknows and coordinates them without compelling them.

**Evaluation:** Pre-established harmony does not entail logical or coercive necessity, but it does entail a providentially fixed sequence. Leibniz preserves compatibilist freedom—acting from one’s reasons—not libertarian power to do otherwise under identical conditions.

**Why this earns marks:** It separates determinism, necessity and compulsion before giving a qualified verdict.

### Q1(e). How far are Quine’s arguments in “Two Dogmas of Empiricism” justified ? Discuss.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Quine–Strawson](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/quine-strawson.md) → critique of empiricism’s dogmas · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Directive: Discuss and assess · Syllabus unit: Quine—analyticity and reductionism · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Quine–Strawson](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Quine-Strawson.md) · **Slug:** `quine-two-dogmas-assessment`

**Demand decoding:** Identify both dogmas, reconstruct the circularity and holism arguments, and evaluate their reach.

**Argument map:** analyticity is explained by synonymy/necessity/rules → each account presupposes cognate semantic notions → sentence-by-sentence reduction to experience fails → beliefs confront experience as a web → revisability is graded → critics defend framework-relative analyticity.

#### Model answer

Quine attacks two empiricist dogmas: a sharp analytic–synthetic distinction and the reduction of each meaningful statement to immediate experience. Definitions merely record synonymy; interchangeability *salva veritate* either permits accidental coextension or appeals to necessity; necessity and semantic rules in turn presuppose analyticity. Quine concludes that no non-circular behaviouristically acceptable account has been supplied.

Against reductionism, he argues that prediction depends on auxiliary assumptions. Experience tests a “web of belief,” not isolated sentences. Even logic is in principle revisable, though central beliefs are retained because revision costs differ. This yields confirmation holism and pragmatic naturalism.

The critique is justified in exposing simplistic definitions and atomistic verification. Scientific testing indeed underdetermines which assumption failed.

**Qualification:** Grice and Strawson argue that mutual dependence does not make semantic concepts meaningless; Carnap can stipulate analyticity relative to linguistic rules. Quine therefore weakens claims of an absolute, reductionist boundary more decisively than every contextual distinction between meaning and fact.

**Why this earns marks:** It treats both dogmas and gives a measured assessment.

### Q2(a). Distinguish between knowledge and belief according to Plato. How is it based on his metaphysics ? Explain.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Plato–Aristotle](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/plato-aristotle.md) → knowledge, opinion and Forms · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 20 marks · Directive: Distinguish and explain metaphysical basis · Syllabus unit: Plato—knowledge and opinion; Forms · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Plato–Aristotle](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Plato-Aristotle.md) · **Slug:** `plato-knowledge-belief-metaphysics`

**Demand decoding:** Compare *epistēmē* and *doxa*, connect their objects and faculties to Plato’s divided ontology, and address the apparent true-belief problem.

**Argument map:** knowledge is stable, explanatory and infallible → belief is fallible and tied to change → distinct faculties take distinct objects → Forms fully are, sensible particulars both are and are-not in respects → divided line and cave rank cognition → true belief becomes knowledge when tied by an account.

#### Model answer

Plato distinguishes knowledge (*epistēmē*) from belief or opinion (*doxa*) by their certainty, objects and explanatory structure. Knowledge is stable, truth-directed and, in the strongest Republic account, infallible; belief is corrigible and may be true or false. In the *Meno*, true belief guides action as successfully as knowledge, but becomes knowledge only when “tied down” by an explanatory account.

This epistemic hierarchy rests on Plato’s metaphysics:

| Cognitive state | Object | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Imagination (*eikasia*) | images and shadows | weakest opinion |
| Belief (*pistis*) | sensible things | opinion |
| Mathematical thought (*dianoia*) | intelligible structures via hypotheses | discursive knowledge |
| Understanding (*noēsis*) | Forms, culminating in the Good | highest knowledge |

Forms—Beauty itself, Equality itself and Justice itself—are eternal, unchanging and intelligible. They fully are what they are and therefore support definitions and necessary explanation. Sensible particulars change and combine opposites: one thing may appear beautiful in one relation and ugly in another. They occupy the intermediate domain of becoming and appropriately yield belief.

The divided line and cave dramatise ascent from images and sensible objects to dialectical apprehension of Forms. Recollection similarly presents learning as recovery of intelligible standards presupposed in judging sensible equality.

**Objection and reply:** The *Theaetetus* shows that merely adding any “account” to true belief does not easily define knowledge. Moreover, if Forms are separate, explaining knowledge of them and their relation to particulars becomes difficult. Plato can reply that dialectical understanding is not an arbitrary verbal addition but grasp of essence and explanatory relations under the Good.

**Evaluation:** Plato’s distinction insightfully links knowledge to stable explanation rather than lucky truth. Its strongest dualism depends, however, on the contested thesis that cognition requires separate immutable objects. A moderated reading retains the distinction between reasoned understanding and unstable appearance without requiring two wholly disconnected worlds.

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes faculties, objects and standards; uses the divided line, cave and *Meno*; and evaluates the metaphysical dependence.

### Q2(b). Explain the doctrine of Cartesian dualism and examine critically arguments in favour of it.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Rationalism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/rationalism.md) → Cartesian mind–body dualism · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Directive: Explain and critically examine · Syllabus unit: Descartes—mind–body dualism · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Rationalism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Rationalism.md) · **Slug:** `cartesian-dualism-critical-examination`

**Demand decoding:** State substance dualism, reconstruct conceivability, divisibility and cogito-based arguments, then test interaction and inference from concepts to reality.

**Argument map:** mind’s essence = thought → body’s essence = extension → each clearly conceived without the other → divisible body contrasts with apparently indivisible mind → therefore real distinction → interaction and dependence objections challenge separate substances.

#### Model answer

Cartesian dualism holds that mind and body are really distinct substances. Mind (*res cogitans*) is unextended, conscious and essentially thinking; body (*res extensa*) is extended, divisible and governed mechanically. A person is their intimate union, but neither essence includes the other.

Descartes’ principal arguments are:

1. **Clear-and-distinct conceivability:** he can clearly conceive mind without body and body without mind; what God can create separately is really distinct.
2. **Cogito and doubt:** bodily existence can be doubted, but the thinking self cannot be doubted while it thinks.
3. **Divisibility:** bodies are spatially divisible, whereas the “I” appears indivisible.
4. **Modal argument:** if thought is essential to mind and extension to body, their properties exclude identity.

These arguments capture first-person consciousness and the apparent irreducibility of reasons, intentionality and qualitative experience.

However, conceivability may reveal incomplete concepts rather than metaphysical possibility: water was once conceivable without H₂O. From “I cannot doubt thinking” but “can doubt body” identity does not follow, because epistemic contexts are intensional. Neural dependence also challenges separate substance. Princess Elisabeth’s decisive objection asks how an unextended mind moves an extended body; Descartes’ pineal-gland proposal locates rather than explains interaction.

**Evaluation:** Cartesian dualism rightly protects consciousness from crude reduction, but its substance ontology and interaction mechanism remain weak. Property dualism or non-reductive physicalism may preserve mental distinctiveness without two independently existing substances.

**Why this earns marks:** It reconstructs several arguments, presents named objections and reaches a discriminating conclusion.

### Q2(c). Evaluate critically Hume’s criticism of theory of causation.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Empiricism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/empiricism.md) → Hume on causation · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Directive: Critically evaluate · Syllabus unit: Hume—causation and induction · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Empiricism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Empiricism.md) · **Slug:** `hume-causation-critical-evaluation`

**Demand decoding:** Explain Hume’s attack on necessary connection, his regularity and psychological accounts, and assess sceptical and realist replies.

**Argument map:** every idea must derive from impression → observation gives contiguity, priority and conjunction, never power → necessity arises from habitual expectation → induction cannot be justified deductively or inductively → causal claims exceed observed instances → critics locate powers or transcendental conditions.

#### Model answer

Hume asks what impression grounds the idea of necessary causal connection. In observing one billiard ball strike another, we perceive spatial contiguity, temporal priority and repeated conjunction, but no force binding the second event to the first. Even perfect knowledge of an isolated cause cannot reveal its effect *a priori*. After repeated sequences, custom produces an expectation: on seeing A, the mind anticipates B. Necessity is therefore the felt determination of thought, not an observed tie between objects.

This supports two definitions: a cause is an object followed regularly by another, and an object whose appearance conveys thought to the other. It also generates the problem of induction. Deduction cannot prove future resemblance to the past without contradiction; induction cannot justify that uniformity without circularly assuming it.

Hume’s criticism powerfully distinguishes observed evidence from projected necessity and undermines rationalist claims to perceive causal power. It anticipates regularity theories and scientific fallibilism.

Yet simple regularity cannot distinguish cause from accidental correlation or common-cause relations. Causal realists posit dispositions whose manifestations explain regularities. Kant replies that causality is an *a priori* condition for experiencing objective succession, not an empirical impression.

**Evaluation:** Hume decisively refutes naïve perception of necessary connection and any non-circular proof of induction. He does not prove that causal powers are unreal; he establishes an epistemic limit. His enduring result is that causal inference requires either natural habit, constitutive principles or a richer explanatory realism than bare observation supplies.

**Why this earns marks:** It states Hume’s complete argument, the induction problem, rival replies and a qualified verdict.

### Q3(a). Are empirical statements conclusively verifiable ? Discuss the limitations of ‘verification theory of meaning’.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Logical Positivism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/logical-positivism.md) → verification principle · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 20 marks · Directive: Discuss and assess limitations · Syllabus unit: Logical positivism—verification theory of meaning · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Logical Positivism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Logical-Positivism.md) · **Slug:** `verification-theory-empirical-statements-limitations`

**Demand decoding:** Distinguish strong and weak verification, explain why universal empirical statements resist conclusiveness, and systematically evaluate the criterion.

**Argument map:** meaningful assertion must be analytic or empirically verifiable → finite observations verify particulars but not unrestricted universals → strong verification excludes science → weak verification admits probability but loses sharpness → criterion is self-referentially problematic and mishandles history, laws, dispositions and value.

#### Model answer

The verification theory of meaning, associated with logical positivism, says that a non-analytic statement is cognitively meaningful only if experience can verify it. **Strong verification** requires conclusive establishment; **weak verification**, developed by A. J. Ayer, requires only that experience count for or against a proposition.

Empirical statements are generally not conclusively verifiable. A finite observation may verify “this swan is white” under accepted conditions, but “all swans are white” ranges over unobserved cases and remains defeasible. Observation itself presupposes auxiliary assumptions about instruments, identity and background laws. Historical claims concern inaccessible events; theoretical entities such as electrons are tested indirectly; dispositions and counterfactuals exceed actual observations. Thus science relies on confirmation, prediction and interlocking theory, not decisive verification of isolated sentences.

The principle has further limitations:

1. **Self-application:** the verification principle is neither analytic nor empirically verifiable, so by its own test it appears meaningless.
2. **Excessive exclusion:** strong verification renders universal laws, much scientific theory, other minds and ordinary claims meaningless.
3. **Weak verification:** if any possible evidence confers probability, almost any statement may qualify.
4. **Theory-ladenness and holism:** observation is concept-dependent, and evidence bears on webs of belief.
5. **Metaphysics, ethics and religion:** their alleged non-cognitive status cannot be established merely by assuming the criterion.
6. **Falsification:** Popper notes that universal laws may be falsifiable although not verifiable, but tests still use auxiliary assumptions.

Carnap’s later framework-relative confirmation and probabilistic semantics soften these defects. Verification remains useful as an anti-dogmatic demand that factual claims specify evidential consequences.

**Evaluation:** Empirical statements are confirmable to degrees, not ordinarily conclusively verifiable. The theory fails as a universal criterion of meaning but survives as a methodological norm linking empirical assertion to possible evidence.

**Why this earns marks:** It answers the opening question, distinguishes versions and organises the major technical objections.

### Q3(b). Why does Wittgenstein disagree with Bertrand Russell’s interpretation of atomism in the philosophy of Tractatus ? Discuss.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Moore–Russell–Early Wittgenstein](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/moore-russell-early-wittgenstein.md) → logical atomism and the *Tractatus* · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Directive: Explain disagreement and discuss · Syllabus unit: Russell and early Wittgenstein—logical atomism · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Moore–Russell–Early Wittgenstein](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Moore-Russell-EarlyWittgenstein.md) · **Slug:** `wittgenstein-russell-tractatus-atomism`

**Demand decoding:** Compare their atoms, analysis, logic and metaphilosophy; avoid treating both projects as identical simply because Russell wrote the introduction.

**Argument map:** Russell seeks epistemically acquainted particulars and universals → analysis constructs facts and language → Wittgenstein posits formal simple objects as conditions of representation → logical form is shown, not named → philosophy clarifies rather than states a scientific metaphysics → Russell’s introduction misreads the ineffable and object theory.

#### Model answer

Russell interpreted the *Tractatus* through his own logical atomism: the world comprises atomic facts built from particulars and universals, and analysis should disclose logically proper names referring to objects known by acquaintance. He also treated Wittgenstein’s limits of language partly as a hierarchy problem solvable through a metalanguage.

Wittgenstein’s atomism differs fundamentally. The world is the totality of facts, not things, but *Tractarian* simple objects are not Russell’s sense-data, universals or empirically discovered atoms. Their simplicity is a **transcendental requirement**: propositions must have determinate sense, so analysis must terminate in names whose possible combinations mirror possible states of affairs. The picture relation depends on shared logical form.

Logical form, however, cannot itself be represented as another fact; it is **shown** in meaningful propositions, not said. Russell’s introduction, by trying to state what lies beyond ordinary language through a higher language, misses this saying/showing distinction. Nor is logic a body of general truths about logical objects: tautologies say nothing and display the scaffolding of the world.

Their aims also diverge. Russell uses analysis for scientific metaphysics; Wittgenstein treats philosophy as clarification marking the limits of sense, with his own propositions finally discarded like a ladder.

**Evaluation:** Both share truth-functional analysis, atomic propositions and opposition to idealist holism. Yet Russell makes atomism an epistemological-metaphysical theory, whereas Wittgenstein makes the atomistic structure a condition of representation combined with a therapeutic limit on doctrine. Russell’s reading is therefore illuminating historically but assimilates the *Tractatus* too closely to his programme.

**Why this earns marks:** It identifies specific disagreements over objects, logical form, hierarchy and philosophy’s task.

### Q3(c). Are G.E. Moore’s arguments in defence of common sense satisfactory ? Give reasons.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Moore–Russell–Early Wittgenstein](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/moore-russell-early-wittgenstein.md) → Moore’s common-sense realism · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Directive: Assess with reasons · Syllabus unit: G. E. Moore—defence of common sense · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Moore–Russell–Early Wittgenstein](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Moore-Russell-EarlyWittgenstein.md) · **Slug:** `moore-common-sense-defence-assessment`

**Demand decoding:** Present Moore’s truisms and “proof” of an external world, then distinguish dialectical force from ordinary certainty.

**Argument map:** ordinary propositions are known with greater certainty than sceptical premises → one hand + another hand entails external objects → proof is valid if premises are known → sceptic challenges whether Moore establishes knowledge rather than asserts it → hinge/common-sense approaches partly vindicate him.

#### Model answer

Moore defends propositions such as “there is now a living human body which is mine,” “the earth existed before my birth,” and “other human beings exist.” In “Proof of an External World” he raises one hand, then another: here is one hand; here is another; therefore at least two external objects exist. The inference is valid, the conclusion differs from the premises, and Moore claims to know the premises.

His deeper strategy is comparative certainty. It is more certain that he has hands than that any sceptical or idealist premise implying their non-existence is true. Philosophical reasoning should not overturn obvious knowledge through less credible abstractions. This usefully exposes the burden of proof and the oddity of demanding a proof stronger than ordinary knowledge.

Yet the argument is not fully dialectically satisfactory. The sceptic questions precisely whether perceptual appearance amounts to knowledge; Moore’s gesture assumes the reliability in dispute. He does not rule out dreaming or deception, nor provide a non-circular criterion connecting experience to external objects. His premise may be true and reasonably believed without answering radical scepticism on its own terms.

Wittgenstein’s later hinge analysis strengthens Moore: some certainties are not evidential conclusions but conditions of inquiry and doubt. However, Moore sometimes misleadingly treats hinges as ordinary propositions known through evidence.

**Evaluation:** Moore satisfactorily defends the rational priority of common sense and blocks easy philosophical revisionism. He does not furnish a premise-neutral refutation of radical scepticism. His achievement is methodological rather than a conclusive demonstrative proof.

**Why this earns marks:** It reconstructs both the proof and its epistemic strategy, then distinguishes validity from anti-sceptical success.

### Q4(a). Explain Kierkegaard’s concept of choice. How does the concept of choice in metaethics differ from normative ethics ? Explain.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Existentialism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/existentialism.md) → Kierkegaard on choice · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 20 marks · Directive: Explain and distinguish · Syllabus unit: Existentialism—Kierkegaard · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Existentialism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Existentialism.md) · **Slug:** `kierkegaard-choice-metaethics-normative-ethics`

**Demand decoding:** Explain existential choice as self-appropriation, then separately clarify metaethical and normative questions about choice.

**Argument map:** objective reflection cannot decide how one exists → choice commits and forms the self → aesthetic, ethical and religious stages embody different life-relations → anxiety accompanies possibility → normative ethics asks what to choose → metaethics asks what makes reasons/values binding or true.

#### Model answer

For Kierkegaard, choice is the passionate appropriation through which an individual becomes a self. Objective reflection can compare possibilities but cannot perform commitment.

In *Either/Or*, the **aesthetic** life disperses itself among immediate pleasures and avoids durable commitment. The **ethical** individual “chooses himself” by accepting responsibility, continuity and universal obligations, exemplified by marriage and vocation. Yet consciousness of guilt exposes the ethical self’s inability to ground or perfect itself. The **religious** stage relates the individual absolutely to God. Abraham in *Fear and Trembling* illustrates the paradoxical “teleological suspension of the ethical”: faith cannot be reduced to public ethical justification, though Kierkegaard does not offer private preference as a general moral licence.

Choice creates anxiety because possibility reveals freedom; despair arises when the self refuses to become itself. The “leap” is not caprice but commitment where objective certainty cannot substitute for existing.

The question’s second part requires a level distinction:

| Field | Question concerning choice | Typical issue |
|---|---|---|
| **Normative ethics** | What ought one choose or do? | duties, consequences, virtues, right action |
| **Metaethics** | What does “ought” mean, and what makes a choice morally justified? | objectivity, realism, freedom, reasons, motivation |

Normative ethics may say “choose truthfulness because it respects persons.” Metaethics asks whether that reason reports an objective moral fact, expresses an attitude, issues a prescription, or arises from rational construction. It also examines whether genuine responsibility presupposes free choice and whether reasons motivate intrinsically.

Kierkegaard crosses both levels: the ethical stage normatively endorses responsible commitment, while metaethically he denies that impersonal proof alone creates existential authority. Choice does not, however, create moral truth.

**Evaluation:** Kierkegaard reveals that moral agency requires lived commitment beyond abstract rule-recognition. Normative content still needs reasons and limits, while metaethics clarifies the status and authority of those reasons.

**Why this earns marks:** It gives Kierkegaard’s stages and concepts while accurately separating first-order ethics from second-order metaethics.

### Q4(b). State and evaluate Heidegger’s claim that temporality is the horizon against which the being of any entity is understood.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Existentialism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/existentialism.md) → Heidegger on temporality and Being · **Secondary route:** [Phenomenology](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/phenomenology.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Directive: State and evaluate · Syllabus unit: Heidegger—Dasein, temporality and Being · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Existentialism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Existentialism.md) · **Secondary classification:** [Phenomenology](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Phenomenology-Husserl.md) · **Slug:** `heidegger-temporality-horizon-being`

**Demand decoding:** Explain “horizon” through Dasein’s ecstatic temporality and show how different modes of temporal disclosure govern understanding of entities.

**Argument map:** Dasein already understands Being → its structure is care → care unifies projection, thrownness and involvement → future, having-been and present are ecstases → entities appear as usable, present or historical within this temporal field → evaluation tests universality and anthropocentrism.

#### Model answer

Heidegger calls temporality the horizon for understanding Being because entities become intelligible only within the temporal structure of *Dasein*, the being for whom Being is an issue. Temporality is not a sequence of neutral “nows.” It is **ecstatic**: Dasein projects itself toward possibilities (future), finds itself already thrown into a world (having-been), and makes entities present in practical engagement (present).

These ecstases unify the structure of **care**. Equipment is understood through future-directed projects—hammer as “for hammering”—against inherited practices and present concern. An object considered merely present-at-hand is itself disclosed through a derivative temporal stance. Being-toward-death individualises possibility; authentic anticipation retrieves inherited possibilities in resolute action. Hence permanence, change, presence and history are not intelligible apart from temporal horizons.

The thesis overcomes the static metaphysics that treats Being chiefly as enduring presence. It also integrates selfhood, action and historical situatedness.

Objections remain. If every being is understood through Dasein’s temporality, Being may appear anthropocentrically dependent on human disclosure. Natural entities existed before Dasein, and mathematical truths seem not essentially temporal. Heidegger replies that the claim concerns intelligibility, not causal existence: prehuman rocks can exist, but their mode of Being is articulated within a disclosed horizon.

**Evaluation:** Temporality convincingly explains lived significance and the historical character of understanding. It is less conclusive as the single exhaustive horizon of every entity; Heidegger’s later work accordingly stresses the historical event of disclosure rather than only Dasein’s temporal constitution.

**Why this earns marks:** It derives the claim from care and the three ecstases, applies it to entities and gives a qualified critique.

### Q4(c). Explain the significance of Aristotle’s doctrine of form and matter in his theory of causation.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Plato–Aristotle](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/plato-aristotle.md) → hylomorphism and four causes · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Directive: Explain significance · Syllabus unit: Aristotle—matter and form; causation · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Plato–Aristotle](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Plato-Aristotle.md) · **Slug:** `aristotle-form-matter-causation`

**Demand decoding:** Relate hylomorphism to material, formal, efficient and final explanation, change and actuality–potentiality.

**Argument map:** substance is form–matter composite → matter supplies potentiality and persistence → form specifies actuality and essence → efficient cause initiates actualisation → final cause is the realised form as end → causal explanation is integrated rather than mechanical only.

#### Model answer

Aristotle’s hylomorphism holds that ordinary substance is a composite of matter (*hulē*) and form (*eidos* or *morphē*). Matter is what can receive determination; form is the organisation or actuality by which a thing is the kind of thing it is. Their distinction makes change intelligible without reducing it either to creation from nothing or to Platonic separation.

It structures Aristotle’s four causes. For a bronze statue, bronze is the **material cause**, its defining shape the **formal cause**, the sculptor’s activity the **efficient cause**, and the intended statue the **final cause**. In natural generation these causes often converge: the mature organism’s form is transmitted by the generator and functions as the end toward which development proceeds.

Matter explains capacity, individuation and persistence through change; form explains essence, unity and characteristic activity. Change is the actualisation of what exists potentially, insofar as it is potential. Thus efficient causation is intelligible only relative to a material capacity and a formal-final outcome.

**Evaluation:** The doctrine provides richer explanation than event succession by integrating constitution, mechanism and purpose. Yet natural teleology may appear to project fixed ends onto evolutionary processes, and “prime matter” is difficult to conceive apart from all form. Its lasting strength is the insight that causal explanation asks both what something is made of and what organised activity makes it the thing it is.

**Why this earns marks:** It connects form and matter to all four causes, change, substance and a critical appraisal.

**Demand decoding:** Compare direct and representational realism, temporal ontology and scriptural authority without conflating either with Yogācāra.

#### Model answer

Vaibhāṣika and Sautrāntika are realist Buddhist schools: both reject a permanent self and analyse experience into momentary factors (*dharmas*), yet differ over how external objects are known.

Only present momentary entities strictly exist, while past seeds or dispositions explain continuity. Sautrāntikas privilege the sūtras over Vaibhāṣika Abhidharma.

The former secures direct objectivity but faces temporal-contact difficulty; the latter explains causal delay but must bridge representation and external cause.

**Why this earns marks:** It states the shared realism and the precise direct-versus-indirect epistemological contrast with temporal implications.

**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Vedānta](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/vedanta.md) → Śaṅkara on superimposition · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Directive: Bring out significance · Syllabus unit: Advaita Vedānta—adhyāsa, Brahman and world · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Vedānta](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Vedanta.md) · **Slug:** `shankara-adhyasa-metaphysics`

**Demand decoding:** Define superimposition and show how it explains subject–object confusion, bondage, levels of reality and liberation.

**Argument map:** self is pure consciousness → non-self attributes are mutually superimposed → “I am body/doer” and “this is mine” arise → ignorance generates plurality and bondage → sublation reveals non-dual Brahman without making experience sheer nothing.

#### Model answer

For Śaṅkara, superimposition (*adhyāsa*) is the false presentation of one thing and its attributes in another, classically like silver appearing in nacre. At the fundamental level, consciousness, permanence and subjecthood belonging to the self (*ātman*) are attributed to body–mind, while bodily agency, limitation and suffering are attributed to the self.

This beginningless mutual superimposition produces the empirical “I am the body,” doership, enjoyership and the subject–object world. It therefore supplies Advaita’s bridge from non-dual Brahman to experienced plurality without treating Brahman as actually transformed. The empirical world is transactionally valid (*vyāvahārika*) but sublatable, unlike absolute reality (*pāramārthika*); illusions possess merely apparent status (*prātibhāsika*).

Bondage is thus epistemic rather than a real alteration of the self. Scriptural inquiry and knowledge remove ignorance, revealing the identity of self and Brahman.

**Evaluation:** *Adhyāsa* unifies Advaita’s ontology, epistemology and liberation theory. Its difficulty is locating ignorance: it cannot ultimately belong to Brahman, yet no separate self finally exists to bear it.

**Why this earns marks:** It explains the mechanism and its complete metaphysical function.

### Q5(d). Are the arguments given in favour of existence of prakṛti adequate in Sāṁkhya philosophy ? Discuss.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Sāṃkhya](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/samkhya.md) → proofs of primordial nature · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

#### Model answer

Sāṃkhya infers primordial nature (*prakṛti*) through five considerations: finite and dependent effects require a cause (*bhedānāṃ parimāṇāt*); diverse effects share the three qualities (*guṇas*) and therefore need a common cause (*samanvayāt*); evolution requires an efficient potency (*śaktitaḥ pravṛtteḥ*); a distinction between manifest effect and unmanifest cause is necessary (*kāraṇa-kārya-vibhāgāt*); and differentiated things ultimately resolve into an undifferentiated source (*avibhāgād vaiśvarūpyasya*).

**Evaluation:** The proofs make *prakṛti* a coherent systematic postulate, not an empirically unavoidable conclusion.

**Why this earns marks:** It presents the full inferential set and evaluates its precise evidential reach.

### Q5(e). Can arthāpatti (postulation) be reduced to anumāna (inference) ? Discuss it from the Mīmāṁsā point of view.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Mīmāṃsā](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/mimamsa.md) → postulation as an independent means of knowledge · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Directive: Discuss from Mīmāṃsā viewpoint · Syllabus unit: Mīmāṃsā—means of knowledge · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Mīmāṃsā](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Mimamsa.md) · **Slug:** `mimamsa-arthapatti-anumana-reduction`

**Demand decoding:** Use the Devadatta example, distinguish explanatory necessity from known concomitance, and present the Nyāya reductionist reply.

**Argument map:** known facts conflict unless an unperceived fact is posited → postulation removes otherwise inexplicability → no prior vyāpti is consciously available → therefore independent *pramāṇa* → Nyāya reconstructs it as inference from absence of alternative.

#### Model answer

Postulation (*arthāpatti*) cognises an unperceived fact required to reconcile established facts. If Devadatta is alive, grows stout and does not eat by day, one postulates that he eats at night. The cognition arises from otherwise inexplicability (*anyathānupapatti*), not from direct perception.

Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā regards it as an independent means of knowledge (*pramāṇa*). In inference (*anumāna*), a known mark leads to a conclusion through previously established universal concomitance (*vyāpti*), as smoke leads to fire. In postulation, no such consciously known universal—“whoever is stout and does not eat by day eats at night”—precedes the conclusion; the explanatory posit is generated by tension among known facts. It may be perceptual or verbal, as when a sentence requires an implied condition.

Nyāya reduces the case to inference: Devadatta’s stoutness cannot occur without eating; day-eating is absent; therefore eating occurs elsewhere in time.

**Mīmāṃsā reply:** that reconstruction formulates a *vyāpti* only after postulation has disclosed the relevant necessity and thus mistakes analysis for the original cognition.

**Why this earns marks:** It gives mechanism, example, reduction and Mīmāṃsā’s anti-reduction argument.

### Q6(a). How is the theory of causation central to the theories of Reality in classical Indian tradition ? Discuss.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Sāṃkhya](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/samkhya.md) → causation across Indian schools · **Secondary routes:** [Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/nyaya-vaisesika.md), [Buddhism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/buddhism.md), [Vedānta](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/vedanta.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 20 marks · Directive: Discuss centrality · Syllabus unit: Indian philosophy—causation and metaphysics · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Causation across schools](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/_themes/Causation-across-schools.md) · **Slug:** `indian-causation-theories-reality`

**Demand decoding:** Show, through comparison, that causal theories encode each school’s ontology, continuity, change, world-origin and liberation—not merely list doctrines.

**Argument map:** account of effect–cause relation determines what persists through change → Sāṃkhya real transformation → Nyāya new production → Buddhism dependent momentariness → Advaita apparent transformation → Viśiṣṭādvaita qualified real transformation → causal disagreement maps rival realities.

#### Model answer

In classical Indian philosophy, causation is central because how an effect arises determines what exists, what persists through change, whether the world is ultimately real and what liberation removes.

**Sāṃkhya** defends effect-pre-existence (*satkāryavāda*): non-being cannot be produced, a specific effect needs an appropriate material, and effect shares the cause’s nature. Primordial nature (*prakṛti*) really transforms (*pariṇāmavāda*) into intellect, ego and elements. Reality is evolutionary continuity, while consciousness (*puruṣa*) remains inactive.

**Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika** accepts new production (*asatkāryavāda/ārambhavāda*). The pot does not pre-exist as pot in clay; conditions produce a new whole. This supports plural realism about substances, qualities, universals and relations.

**Buddhism** explains reality through dependent origination (*pratītyasamutpāda*): conditioned events arise without permanent substance or first cause. Causal efficacy supports momentariness. Nāgārjuna adds that dependently arisen cause and effect lack intrinsic nature (*svabhāva*); causation is conventionally valid but empty.

Vedānta’s causal positions directly express rival Brahman–world relations. **Advaita** uses apparent transformation (*vivartavāda*): Brahman grounds plurality without really changing, and the world is sublated by knowledge. **Rāmānuja** accepts real transformation of Brahman’s dependent modes—selves and matter—without impairment of the divine controller. **Madhva** preserves real differences and divine efficient governance.

**Mīmāṃsā’s** unseen potency (*apūrva*) connects ritual action with remote fruit, supporting Vedic moral order. **Jainism** combines continuity and change: substance persists while modes arise and perish, and karmic matter binds the soul.

Thus causal theory decides whether reality is transformation or novelty, substance or event, identity or difference, appearance or real manifestation. It also explains karmic continuity and liberation.

**Evaluation:** Each school’s causal criterion partly presupposes its ontology, yet every system must explain specificity, novelty and continuity. Causation is therefore the organising test of classical Indian theories of Reality.

**Why this earns marks:** It compares major schools and repeatedly shows what each causal doctrine proves about reality.

### Q6(b). Explain the concept of Karma and discuss its various types according to Jain philosophy.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Jainism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/jainism.md) → karmic matter and classification · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Directive: Explain and classify · Syllabus unit: Jainism—bondage and liberation · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Jainism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Jainism.md) · **Slug:** `jaina-karma-concept-types`

**Demand decoding:** Explain karma literally as subtle matter, give the eight principal types and organise them as destructive/non-destructive.

**Argument map:** activity plus passions causes influx → subtle karmic matter binds soul → karmas obscure intrinsic capacities and shape embodiment → four destructive + four non-destructive kinds → stoppage and shedding yield liberation.

#### Model answer

In Jain philosophy, karma is not merely a moral law or invisible merit. It is subtle material matter (*karma-pudgala*) that flows toward the soul (*jīva*) through activities of body, speech and mind. Passions—anger, pride, deceit and greed—make this influx (*āsrava*) adhere as bondage (*bandha*). Bound karma obscures the soul’s intrinsic infinite knowledge, perception, energy and bliss and determines embodied experience.

Eight principal karmas are classified into two groups:

**Destructive karmas (*ghātiyā*)**, directly obscuring essential qualities:
1. knowledge-obscuring (*jñānāvaraṇīya*);
2. perception-obscuring (*darśanāvaraṇīya*);
3. deluding (*mohanīya*);
4. obstructive (*antarāya*).

**Non-destructive karmas (*aghātiyā*)**, shaping empirical embodiment:
5. feeling-producing (*vedanīya*);
6. lifespan-determining (*āyuṣya*);
7. body-making/status-forming (*nāma*);
8. family or social-status determining (*gotra*).

Karma is further analysed by its nature, duration, intensity and quantity. Ethical and psychological differences thus produce determinate causal consequences without divine judgement.

Liberation requires stopping new influx (*saṃvara*) through right faith, knowledge and conduct, and shedding accumulated karma (*nirjarā*) through discipline and austerity. Destruction of the four *ghātiyā* karmas yields omniscience (*kevala-jñāna*); exhaustion of all eight releases the soul.

**Evaluation:** The theory closely joins ethics, psychology and cosmology, but its literal materialisation of moral causality is metaphysically demanding.

**Why this earns marks:** It gives the mechanism, complete eightfold taxonomy and liberative significance.

**Argument map:** qualified observer surveys qualified locus → pot is not apprehended → Bhāṭṭa: non-apprehension itself independently reveals absence → later Nyāya: absence is a real category directly perceived through locus contact → disagreement concerns extra *pramāṇa*, not practical cognition.

#### Model answer

Knowledge of absence (*abhāva*) requires more than failure to notice an object. The locus must be perceptible, the observer competent, and the conditions appropriate: seeing a well-lit empty floor warrants “there is no pot here,” whereas not seeing a microscopic germ does not.

When an object fit to be perceived is not apprehended in an apprehended locus, its absence is directly known. Earlier formulations sometimes analyse the knowledge inferentially from qualified non-perception. Nyāya distinguishes prior, posterior, mutual and absolute absence.

**Prābhākara Mīmāṃsā** is more parsimonious: cognition of the bare locus, together with memory of the expected object, is expressed negatively; absence need not be an independent positive entity or *pramāṇa*.

**Evaluation:** Bhāṭṭa theory captures the immediacy and defeasibility of absence-cognition. Nyāya supplies a detailed ontology and perceptual relation, but “contact” with a non-entity is difficult. Both rightly insist on fitness for perception; mere non-observation is not proof of absence.

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes ontology from epistemology and fairly presents three positions.

### Q7(a). Bring out the philosophical implications of introducing extraordinary (alaukika) perception in Nyāya philosophy.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/nyaya-vaisesika.md) → extraordinary perception · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 20 marks · Directive: Bring out implications · Syllabus unit: Nyāya—perception and extraordinary cognition · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Nyaya-Vaisesika.md) · **Slug:** `nyaya-alaukika-perception-implications`

**Demand decoding:** Define the three extraordinary contacts, explain why Nyāya needs them, and evaluate their effects on universals, illusion, induction and yogic knowledge.

**Argument map:** ordinary sense–object contact cannot explain universal cognition, recognition-based illusion or remote yogic awareness → Nyāya expands perceptual contact → sāmānyalakṣaṇa grounds class reach and induction → jñānalakṣaṇa explains cross-presented qualities/error → yogaja supports supersensible cognition → expansion threatens perception/inference boundary.

#### Model answer

Nyāya introduces extraordinary perception (*alaukika-pratyakṣa*) to explain cognitions that appear perceptual although ordinary sense–object contact is absent.

It recognises three forms:

1. **Universal-character perception (*sāmānyalakṣaṇa-pratyakṣa*):** by perceiving a cow together with cowness, cognition reaches other bearers of that universal. This supports classification and awareness of generality.
2. **Knowledge-mediated perception (*jñānalakṣaṇa-pratyakṣa*):** a present perception, aided by prior cognition, presents a quality ordinarily available to another sense or elsewhere. Seeing sandalwood as fragrant and mistaking nacre for silver are standard applications.
3. **Yogic perception (*yogaja-pratyakṣa*):** perfected yogins directly know remote, past, future or subtle objects through disciplined concentration.

Its implications are extensive. **First, realism about universals:** *sāmānyalakṣaṇa* makes universals objectively perceptible and helps explain how induction ranges beyond observed tokens. Critics reply that abstraction and inference suffice; a universal cannot literally connect a sense with every absent member.

**Second, error and cross-modal unity:** *jñānalakṣaṇa* explains why remembered silver appears “there” in nacre and why visible sandalwood seems fragrant. It preserves object-directedness, but risks redescribing memory-influenced judgement as perception.

**Third, expanded warrant:** yogic perception defends knowledge of remote, subtle and karmic facts. Its reliability is, however, difficult for non-yogins to test.

**Fourth, blurred means-of-knowledge boundaries:** perception now includes general, mnemonic and yogic mediation usually assigned to conception, inference or testimony. Explanatory reach increases at the cost of ordinary immediacy.

**Finally, metaphysical reinforcement:** the doctrine supports Nyāya’s realism about particulars, universals and relations. Buddhist nominalists can explain generality and illusion through conceptual construction without extraordinary contact.

**Evaluation:** The theory systematically addresses generality, recognition and expert cognition. Its first two forms are most defensible as concept-laden perception rather than literal sensory reach to absent entities; yogic perception remains internally coherent but publicly under-verified.

**Why this earns marks:** It defines all three forms and traces epistemological, metaphysical and methodological consequences with objections.

### Q7(b). Explain the nature and levels of samprajñāta samādhi. How does each level lead more towards asamprajñāta samādhi ?
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Yoga](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/yoga.md) → cognitive and seedless meditative integration · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Directive: Explain levels and progression · Syllabus unit: Yoga—samādhi and liberation · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Yoga](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Yoga.md) · **Slug:** `yoga-samprajnata-asamprajnata-samadhi`

**Demand decoding:** Give the fourfold progression, relevant subdivisions, and show how increasingly subtle objects and weakening seeds prepare objectless restraint.

**Argument map:** concentrated mind retains cognitive object → gross deliberative absorption → subtle reflective absorption → joy → bare I-am-ness → objects and conceptual admixture progressively cease → dispassion toward even refined cognition → only latent impressions remain → seedless integration.

#### Model answer

In Pātañjala Yoga, cognitive meditative integration (*samprajñāta-samādhi*) is absorption in which an object remains and lucid cognition operates. Yoga Sūtra I.17 describes a progression associated with deliberation, reflection, bliss and I-am-ness.

1. **Deliberative (*vitarka*) level:** concentration takes a gross object. In *savitarka*, word, object and conceptual knowledge are mixed; in *nirvitarka*, memory is purified and the object shines without verbal construction.
2. **Reflective (*vicāra*) level:** attention moves to subtle elements or principles. *Savicāra* retains spatial-temporal conditions; *nirvicāra* apprehends the subtle object without them.
3. **Bliss (*ānanda*) level:** with coarse and subtle discursiveness quieted, the clarity and joy of a predominantly luminous (*sāttvika*) mind become focal.
4. **I-am-ness (*asmitā*) level:** even bliss recedes; absorption rests on the transparent sense of individuality associated with pure “I-am,” short of discerning consciousness itself.

Each level reduces multiplicity, conceptual construction and attachment while making the object progressively subtler. Truth-bearing insight (*ṛtambharā prajñā*) arising from the highest cognitive absorption generates impressions that counter ordinary afflictive impressions. Yet even refined cognition has a seed—an object and latent disposition.

Through supreme dispassion (*para-vairāgya*), the yogin relinquishes attachment even to discriminative insight. In non-cognitive integration (*asamprajñāta-samādhi*), all active mental modifications are restrained and only residual impressions initially remain; seedless integration (*nirbīja-samādhi*) follows when these too lose causal force.

**Evaluation:** The sequence gives a psychologically coherent refinement from conceptual attention to objectless stillness. It must not be read as unconsciousness: awareness is not destroyed; mental objectification is suspended, preparing discriminative isolation (*kaivalya*).

**Why this earns marks:** It gives the complete hierarchy, subdivisions and the mechanism connecting every stage to seedlessness.

### Q7(c). Examine the Sāṁkhya view on liberation that “the self is neither bound nor liberates, nor does it transmigrate”.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Sāṃkhya](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/samkhya.md) → bondage, transmigration and isolation · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Directive: Examine · Syllabus unit: Sāṃkhya—puruṣa, bondage and liberation · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Sāṃkhya](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Samkhya.md) · **Slug:** `samkhya-self-neither-bound-nor-liberated`

**Demand decoding:** Resolve the paradox by distinguishing pure consciousness from the subtle body and nature, then test whether apparent bondage remains intelligible.

**Argument map:** puruṣa is changeless witness → bondage/change cannot literally qualify it → intellect misidentifies due to proximity and reflection → subtle body carries dispositions and transmigrates → discriminative knowledge ends nature’s activity for that witness → liberation is cessation of misidentification, not alteration of self.

#### Model answer

Sāṃkhya holds that consciousness (*puruṣa*) is eternal, inactive, changeless and beyond the three qualities (*guṇas*). Bondage, action, merit, suffering and movement are modifications of primordial nature (*prakṛti*), especially intellect (*buddhi*) and the subtle body. Therefore the self cannot literally become bound, undergo liberation or travel from body to body.

Bondage is an apparent conjunction caused by non-discrimination (*aviveka*). Intellect reflects consciousness and appears sentient; consciousness appears to appropriate intellect’s pleasure, pain and agency. Karmic dispositions belong to the subtle body, which transmigrates.

Liberation (*kaivalya*) arises through discriminative knowledge: “I am not nature or its evolutes; I am the witness.” Nature, having fulfilled its purposes of experience and liberation, ceases to manifest for that *puruṣa*, like a dancer withdrawing after being seen. The self does not acquire freedom; its ever-free isolation becomes evident.

This preserves consciousness’s immutability and explains why knowledge is decisive. However, if *puruṣa* is wholly inactive and unrelated to intellect, whose ignorance is removed and for whom does nature act? Unconscious intellect cannot itself suffer or seek release. The reflection metaphor describes but may not explain how proximity produces experience.

**Evaluation:** The statement is coherent within Sāṃkhya’s two-realities framework: the subtle body transmigrates, nature appears bound, and the self merely witnesses. Its philosophical cost is making bondage and liberation both somehow “for” a consciousness they never modify.

**Why this earns marks:** It resolves all three clauses and then examines the central interaction and ownership problem.

### Q8(a). How does the nature of Brahman differ in the philosophy of Sankara, Ramanuja and Madhva ? Discuss critically.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Vedānta](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/vedanta.md) → Advaita, Viśiṣṭādvaita and Dvaita · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 20 marks · Directive: Compare and critically discuss · Syllabus unit: Vedānta—Śaṅkara, Rāmānuja and Madhva · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Vedānta](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Vedanta.md) · **Slug:** `brahman-shankara-ramanuja-madhva`

**Demand decoding:** Compare Brahman’s attributes, unity, relation to selves/world, causality, liberation and scriptural interpretation; include reciprocal criticisms.

**Argument map:** shared Vedic ultimate and world-ground → Śaṅkara’s non-dual attributeless absolute → Rāmānuja’s personal qualified whole → Madhva’s independent Viṣṇu amid eternal differences → each balances unity, plurality and devotion differently → critical costs arise from ignorance, mutable modes or permanent duality/hierarchy.

#### Model answer

Śaṅkara, Rāmānuja and Madhva accept Brahman as the supreme reality disclosed by the Upaniṣads and as the ground of the world. They differ radically on whether Brahman excludes, includes or eternally confronts plurality.

| Dimension | Śaṅkara: Advaita | Rāmānuja: Viśiṣṭādvaita | Madhva: Dvaita |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ultimate nature | Non-dual existence-consciousness-infinity; ultimately without limiting attributes (*nirguṇa*) | Personal Nārāyaṇa with infinite auspicious attributes | Supreme personal Viṣṇu, absolutely independent (*svatantra*) |
| Selves and matter | Ultimately non-different from Brahman; distinction due to ignorance (*avidyā*) | Real dependent modes/body of Brahman—conscious (*cit*) and non-conscious (*acit*) | Distinct dependent realities (*paratantra*) |
| World | Transactionally real, ultimately sublatable appearance through *māyā* | Real manifestation of Brahman’s body | Fully real creation governed by God |
| Causation | Brahman is material and efficient cause without real change; apparent transformation (*vivarta*) | Brahman is material and efficient cause; real change belongs to dependent modes | God is chiefly efficient controller; matter and souls remain distinct |
| Liberation | Knowledge of identity of self and Brahman | Loving communion and service; individuality remains | Grace-supported knowledge and devotion; difference remains |

For **Śaṅkara**, Īśvara and causality belong to the empirical standpoint of Brahman associated with *māyā*. “That thou art” teaches identity once limiting adjuncts are negated.

For **Rāmānuja**, attributeless Brahman would be empty. Brahman is a concrete unity qualified by selves and matter as its body; identity means dependence, not numerical identity.

For **Madhva**, fivefold difference (*pañcabheda*) among God, souls and matter is eternal. Liberation is God-realisation, never identity.

Rāmānuja asks where Advaita’s ignorance resides. Advaita asks how changing, suffering modes form Brahman’s body without affecting perfection. Both criticise Madhva for weakening Upaniṣadic unity; Madhva reads unity as divine supremacy.

**Evaluation:** Śaṅkara secures unity but struggles with ignorance. Rāmānuja integrates realism and devotion but must reconcile perfection with dependent suffering. Madhva preserves theism and plurality but lacks deeper unity. Supremacy means, respectively, identity, organic qualification or eternal difference.

**Why this earns marks:** It compares every core axis, accurately stages reciprocal objections and gives a non-sectarian verdict.

### Q8(b). How does Nāgārjuna explain the concept of Śūnyatā ?
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Buddhism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/buddhism.md) → Madhyamaka emptiness · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Directive: Explain · Syllabus unit: Buddhism—Madhyamaka and emptiness · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Buddhism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Buddhism.md) · **Slug:** `nagarjuna-sunyata-explanation`

**Demand decoding:** Define emptiness as absence of intrinsic nature, derive it from dependent origination, explain the two truths and avoid nihilism.

**Argument map:** intrinsic nature would be independent, fixed and self-established → all phenomena arise dependently through causes, parts and designation → therefore they are empty → causal and conventional functioning remains → emptiness itself is empty → middle avoids eternalism and annihilationism.

#### Model answer

Nāgārjuna explains emptiness (*śūnyatā*) as the absence of intrinsic, independent and self-established nature (*svabhāva*). It is not a substance behind appearances and not sheer non-existence. His central equation is that whatever arises dependently is empty; being dependently designated, it is the Middle Path.

If something possessed *svabhāva*, it would exist from its own side, independent of causes and conditions, and could not genuinely arise, change or cease. Yet everything—self, chariot, cause, motion and even nirvāṇa—exists through causes, parts, relations and conceptual designation. The four-cornered analysis (*catuṣkoṭi*) shows that an effect cannot coherently arise from itself, from an wholly other cause, from both, or without cause when these alternatives are understood through intrinsic natures.

Nāgārjuna uses the **two truths**. Conventionally (*saṃvṛti-satya*), persons act, causes produce effects, language functions and the path ends suffering. Ultimately (*paramārtha-satya*), none of these has independent essence. Ultimate truth does not abolish convention; without relying on convention it cannot be taught, and without understanding it liberation is impossible.

The doctrine therefore rejects both eternalism—the belief in permanent substances—and annihilationism—the claim that nothing exists or matters. Indeed, emptiness itself is empty: reifying it would reproduce the error it cures.

**Evaluation:** Śūnyatā is a critical method dissolving attachment to views and making change possible. Critics charge self-refutation: if all theses are empty, so is Nāgārjuna’s. His reply is therapeutic—emptiness is not a final metaphysical thesis but the dependent correction of reification.

**Why this earns marks:** It links dependent origination, *svabhāva*, two truths, the Middle Path and anti-nihilism.

### Q8(c). How is Sri Aurobindo’s integral yoga an advancement over Pātañjala yoga ? Discuss.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Sri Aurobindo](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/sri-aurobindo.md) → Integral Yoga and Pātañjala Yoga · **Secondary route:** [Yoga](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/yoga.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 15 marks · Directive: Compare and discuss advancement claim · Syllabus unit: Sri Aurobindo—Integral Yoga; comparison with Yoga · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Sri Aurobindo](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Aurobindo.md) · **Secondary classification:** [Yoga](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Yoga.md) · **Slug:** `aurobindo-integral-yoga-advance-patanjali`

**Demand decoding:** Compare metaphysical aims, methods and world-relations, while qualifying “advancement” rather than simply declaring superiority.

**Argument map:** Pātañjala Yoga restrains mind for discriminative isolation → disciplined eight limbs and samādhi free puruṣa from nature → Aurobindo integrates knowledge, devotion and works → ascent is joined by supramental descent → psychic, spiritual and supramental transformations divinise nature → broader aim is advancement if world-transformation is accepted.

#### Model answer

Pātañjala Yoga defines Yoga as restraint of mental modifications (*yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ*). Through practice, dispassion, action-Yoga, the eight limbs and meditative integration (*samādhi*), discriminative knowledge separates consciousness (*puruṣa*) from nature (*prakṛti*). Its culmination is isolation (*kaivalya*): the qualities cease functioning for the liberated witness.

Sri Aurobindo accepts discipline and spiritual ascent but regards exclusive release from nature as incomplete. His Integral Yoga (*pūrṇa-yoga*) advances beyond it in four respects:

1. **Integral method:** it combines knowledge, devotion and works through aspiration, rejection of lower movements and surrender to the Divine Śakti.
2. **Whole-being transformation:** physical, vital, mental and spiritual nature must change, not merely cease to bind.
3. **Ascent and descent:** ascent is completed by descent of spiritual and supramental truth-consciousness into embodied life.
4. **World-affirming aim:** psychic, spiritual and supramental transformations prepare divine life rather than cosmic withdrawal.

The difference follows from metaphysics. Classical Yoga maintains an enduring dualism of many *puruṣas* and unconscious *prakṛti*. Aurobindo sees matter as involved consciousness and evolution as its progressive manifestation; nature is capable of divinisation.

Yet “advancement” is evaluative. Pātañjala Yoga offers precise psychology, ethics and graded practice. Aurobindo’s supramental transformation is broader but more speculative and difficult to verify. Pātañjala devotion to the Lord (*Īśvara-praṇidhāna*) also prevents describing it as merely negative escape.

**Evaluation:** Integral Yoga is an advancement if liberation must include transformation of life and world; it is not simply a correction within Patañjali’s own dualist goal. It broadens Yoga from isolation of consciousness to integration and divinisation of nature.

**Why this earns marks:** It compares doctrine, practice and goal, then critically qualifies the language of advancement.
