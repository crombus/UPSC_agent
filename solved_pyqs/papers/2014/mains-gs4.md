## Q1(a). Happiness and the good life

**Official question:** All human beings aspire for happiness. Do you agree? What does happiness mean to you? Explain with examples. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** 2014 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q1(a) · Directive: Take a position, define and explain with examples · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → happiness, flourishing and the good life · **Proposed slug:** `happiness-flourishing-good-life` · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Ethics and human interface](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/01_Ethics-and-Human-Interface.md) · [Moral theories](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/08_Moral-Theories-Deontology-Consequentialism-Virtue-Ethics.md)

### Demand decoding and framework

Answer whether the aspiration is universal, define happiness beyond momentary pleasure, illustrate its constituents and qualify the claim where desires are distorted or circumstances oppressive.

### Model answer

Broadly, all persons seek some perceived good, though they understand happiness differently and may pursue it through mistaken means. Happiness is not continuous pleasure or possession; it is durable well-being arising from meaning, relationships, self-respect and exercised capacities.

Aristotle's *eudaimonia* treats it as flourishing through virtue, while Amartya Sen stresses real freedom to live a valued life. A doctor serving an underserved district may accept hardship yet experience purpose; an officer refusing a bribe may lose advantage but preserve integrity.

Material security, health and liberty are indispensable because deprivation can crush agency. Yet wealth without trust, moderation or justice may produce anxiety rather than fulfilment. Happiness is therefore personal but not purely subjective: cruel domination cannot become ethical merely because it pleases someone.

**Conclusion:** Authentic happiness joins basic capabilities with virtue, belonging, meaningful work and a clear conscience.

**Why this earns marks:** It answers the universal-aspiration claim, distinguishes pleasure from flourishing, uses two conceptual anchors and qualifies subjective desire.

## Q1(b). What ethics promotes and why administration needs it

**Official question:** What does ethics seek to promote in human life? Why is it all the more important in public administration? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** 2014 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q1(b) · Directive: Explain purpose and administrative importance · Difficulty: Easy-Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → ethics and public power · **Proposed slug:** `ethics-purpose-public-administration` · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Ethics and human interface](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/01_Ethics-and-Human-Interface.md) · [Ethical governance](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/11_Accountability-and-Ethical-Governance.md)

### Demand decoding and framework

Define ethics functionally, identify the goods it promotes, then connect them to discretion, public resources, unequal power and citizen trust.

### Model answer

Ethics guides choice toward a good and just life. It promotes dignity, fairness, responsibility, compassion and self-restraint by asking whether an act's motive, means and consequences are justifiable.

It is especially important in public administration because officials:

- exercise coercive and distributive power over citizens;
- allocate scarce public resources;
- affect vulnerable groups and future generations; and
- act as trustees rather than owners of authority.

Rules cannot anticipate every case. A relief officer must interpret urgency impartially; a procurement officer must disclose a relative's bid; and a welfare official should correct exclusion rather than hide behind procedure. Kantian respect prevents citizens becoming mere files, while utilitarianism tests public consequences.

Ethics does not replace law or expertise. It gives them legitimate purpose and disciplines discretion through reasons, transparency and accountability.

**Conclusion:** Administrative ethics converts power into public trust by aligning lawful authority with constitutional morality and human welfare.

**Why this earns marks:** It links ethical purpose directly to the distinctive risks and fiduciary character of administration.

## Q2(a). Patriotism in everyday civil life

**Official question:** In the context of defence services, ‘patriotism’ demands readiness to even lay down one's life in protecting the nation. According to you, what does patriotism imply in everyday civil life? Explain with illustrations and justify your answer. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** 2014 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q2(a) · Directive: Interpret, illustrate and justify · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Indian Moral Thinkers](../../topics/ethics/indian-moral-thinkers.md) → constitutional patriotism and civic duty · **Proposed slug:** `patriotism-everyday-civic-duty` · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Human values](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/02_Human-Values-and-Lessons-from-Leaders.md) · [Foundational values](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/04_Aptitude-and-Foundational-Values-for-Civil-Service.md)

### Demand decoding and framework

Move patriotism from wartime sacrifice to daily constitutional citizenship; give illustrations and guard against performative or exclusionary nationalism.

### Model answer

Everyday patriotism is service to the constitutional community, not emotional display. It means respecting equal citizenship, performing civic duties, protecting public goods and placing legitimate public interest above narrow gain.

It appears when a taxpayer reports honestly, a citizen resists communal rumours, a professional serves during disaster, a voter rejects inducements and an official protects unpopular rights. Gandhi's constructive work shows service; Ambedkar's constitutional morality joins fraternity with liberty and equality.

Such conduct strengthens institutions, solidarity and common resources. Bribery, vandalism and suppression of criticism injure that inheritance.

Patriotism must not become blind obedience. Peaceful dissent against injustice can be patriotic when factual, non-violent and constitutionally pursued.

**Conclusion:** The civilian equivalent of sacrifice is disciplined, inclusive citizenship—doing one's duty when no applause or immediate reward follows.

**Why this earns marks:** It supplies a defensible definition, varied illustrations, Indian thinkers and an essential dissent qualification.

## Q2(b). Probity in public life

**Official question:** What do you understand by ‘probity’ in public life? What are the difficulties in practicing it in the present times? How can these difficulties be overcome? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** 2014 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q2(b) · Directive: Define, diagnose and prescribe · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Corruption and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/corruption-and-administrative-ethics.md) → probity and integrity systems · **Proposed slug:** `probity-public-life-difficulties-remedies` · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Probity](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/14_Probity-Concept-and-Philosophical-Basis-of-Governance.md) · [Protecting honest officials](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/21_Protecting-Honest-Officials-and-Vigilance-Administration.md)

### Demand decoding and framework

Define probity as more than absence of corruption; identify personal and systemic obstacles; offer mutually reinforcing ethical and institutional remedies.

### Model answer

**Probity** is demonstrable integrity in public office: honesty, impartiality, proper use of authority, conflict disclosure, reasoned decisions and readiness for scrutiny.

Difficulties include opaque discretion, political-business pressure, patronage, normalised gifts, weak whistle-blower protection, delayed investigation, frequent transfers and “everyone does it” rationalisation. Honesty may impose career costs.

Remedies should combine:

- clear codes of ethics and conflict-of-interest rules;
- transparent criteria, e-procurement and traceable files;
- asset, gift and beneficial-ownership disclosure;
- independent, time-bound vigilance and proportionate sanctions;
- protection and stable tenure for honest officials;
- citizen charters, social audits and grievance appeal;
- ethical leadership, training and rewards for documented good faith.

The Nolan principles offer a benchmark; surveillance without due process can create fear.

**Conclusion:** Probity survives when personal courage is supported by transparent systems that make honesty safe and misconduct costly.

**Why this earns marks:** It integrates definition, obstacles, institutional design and a proportionality qualification.

## Q3(a). Integrity and knowledge

**Official question:** “Integrity without knowledge is weak and useless, but knowledge without integrity is dangerous and dreadful.” What do you understand by this statement? Explain your stand with illustrations from the modern context. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** 2014 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q3(a) · Directive: Interpret, take a position and illustrate · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → competence and moral character · **Proposed slug:** `integrity-knowledge-competence-character` · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Foundational values](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/04_Aptitude-and-Foundational-Values-for-Civil-Service.md) · [Ethics and human interface](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/01_Ethics-and-Human-Interface.md)

### Demand decoding and framework

Explain both halves, show their complementarity through modern examples, and qualify integrity with competence and knowledge with accountable purpose.

### Model answer

Integrity aligns worthy values, words and conduct; knowledge enables sound understanding and effective means. Either alone is insufficient.

An honest but technically uninformed health administrator may purchase unsuitable equipment or spread misinformation: good intention cannot prevent avoidable harm. Conversely, a skilled data scientist who manipulates an algorithm to discriminate, or an engineer who conceals a safety defect, converts expertise into a powerful instrument of wrongdoing.

Aristotle's practical wisdom unites right ends with sound judgment. In administration, a district officer needs integrity to reject favouritism and knowledge to design lawful, workable relief. During a pandemic, scientific competence must be joined with truthful risk communication, privacy protection and equitable access.

Because knowledge is incomplete, integrity includes consulting experts, recording uncertainty and correcting error.

**Conclusion:** Public trust requires **ethical competence**: knowledge makes integrity effective, while integrity makes knowledge safe and socially purposeful.

**Why this earns marks:** It interprets both propositions symmetrically, applies a thinker and gives contemporary administrative and technological illustrations.

## Q3(b). Treating persons as ends in a techno-economic society

**Official question:** “Human beings should always be treated as ‘ends’ in themselves and never as merely ‘means’.” Explain the meaning and significance of this statement, giving its implications in the modern techno-economic society. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** 2014 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q3(b) · Directive: Explain meaning, significance and contemporary implications · Difficulty: High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → Kantian dignity and technology · **Proposed slug:** `kant-persons-ends-techno-economic-society` · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Western thinkers](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/07_Western-Moral-Philosophers-and-Thinkers.md) · [Emerging ethics](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/13_Emerging-Ethics-Technology-AI-and-Environment.md)

### Demand decoding and framework

State Kant's humanity formulation, distinguish using from merely using, then apply dignity, consent and autonomy to markets, labour and technology.

### Model answer

Kant's humanity formulation requires respect for every person's rational agency and intrinsic dignity. Cooperation inevitably uses others' services, but treating someone **merely** as a means involves deception, coercion or disregard of consent and welfare.

Modern implications include:

- workers need safety, fair conditions and voice;
- consumers must not be manipulated through dark patterns or concealed risks;
- personal data require lawful purpose, minimisation and remedy;
- AI-based welfare or recruitment must be explainable, bias-tested and appealable;
- patients and research participants require informed consent;
- development must involve and rehabilitate affected communities.

Extracting platform workers' data while shifting all risk to them violates autonomy. *K.S. Puttaswamy* reinforces privacy as dignity.

Respect is not absolute individual veto: public health may justify proportionate restrictions under law.

**Conclusion:** Technology and markets are ethical only when efficiency remains subordinate to agency, rights and fair participation.

**Why this earns marks:** It accurately explains “merely,” applies the principle across sectors and avoids making autonomy unlimited.

## Q4(a). An ethical exemplar and personal development

**Official question:** Which eminent personality has inspired you the most in the context of ethical conduct in life? Give the gist of his/her teachings. Giving specific examples, describe how you have been able to apply these teachings for your own ethical development. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** 2014 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q4(a) · Directive: Identify, summarise and personally apply · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Indian Moral Thinkers](../../topics/ethics/indian-moral-thinkers.md) → Gandhi and ethical self-cultivation · **Proposed slug:** `gandhi-ethical-exemplar-self-development` · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Indian moral thinkers](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/06_Indian-Moral-Thinkers-and-Philosophers.md) · [Human values](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/02_Human-Values-and-Lessons-from-Leaders.md)

### Demand decoding and framework

Choose one personality, state a coherent teaching rather than a biography, and provide concrete first-person applications with honest limitations.

### Model answer

Mahatma Gandhi inspires me because he joined moral ends with moral means. His teachings include *satya*, *ahimsa*, respect, simplicity, self-discipline and bearing personal cost rather than imposing unjust harm.

I apply them in small, verifiable ways. In group work, I disclose my error instead of shifting blame. During disagreement, I restate the other argument fairly and criticise the issue, not the person. I verify inflammatory messages before forwarding them and correct errors publicly. I also keep a decision diary: motive, affected persons and whether the means are publicly defensible.

These practices do not make me morally complete. Gandhi's idea of life as “experiments with truth” encourages continuous self-correction rather than self-righteousness.

**Conclusion:** His deepest influence is the habit of aligning everyday means with professed values.

**Why this earns marks:** It is personal without becoming biographical, states the thinker accurately and gives specific, credible applications plus self-evaluation.

### Demand decoding and framework

## Q5(a). Trust deficit and becoming trustworthy

**Official question:** The current society is plagued with widespread trust-deficit. What are the consequences of this situation for personal well-being and for societal well-being? What can you do at the personal level to make yourself trustworthy? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** 2014 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q5(a) · Directive: Analyse consequences and prescribe personal conduct · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Work Culture and Public Funds](../../topics/ethics/work-culture-and-public-funds.md) → trustworthiness and social capital · **Proposed slug:** `trust-deficit-personal-social-wellbeing` · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Human values](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/02_Human-Values-and-Lessons-from-Leaders.md) · [Foundational values](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/04_Aptitude-and-Foundational-Values-for-Civil-Service.md)

### Demand decoding and framework

Separate personal from societal consequences, then state behavioural foundations of warranted—not blind—trust.

### Model answer

Trust is a justified expectation that another will act competently, honestly and reliably. Its erosion damages both persons and institutions.

Personally, distrust produces anxiety, loneliness, defensiveness and constant verification; relationships become transactional. Socially, contracts and programmes become costlier, rumours spread, cooperation declines and citizens evade duties because they expect cheating. Polarisation impedes collective action. Putnam's social capital explains why trust enables cooperation.

I can become trustworthy by:

- keeping promises and warning early when I cannot;
- telling the truth, especially when inconvenient;
- matching competence to commitments;
- protecting confidences while refusing wrongful secrecy;
- disclosing conflicts and correcting errors;
- applying consistent criteria rather than favour;
- documenting decisions and accepting review.

Trust must be earned; transparency need not expose legitimate privacy.

**Conclusion:** Repeated congruence between word, capacity and action creates a small but replicable foundation for wider social trust.

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes two levels of harm and turns trustworthiness into observable conduct with a privacy qualification.

## Q5(b). Why affluent and powerful people become corrupt

**Official question:** It is often said that poverty leads to corruption. However, there is no dearth of instances where affluent and powerful people indulge in corruption in a big way. What are the basic causes of corruption among people? Support your answer with examples. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** 2014 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q5(b) · Directive: Diagnose basic causes with examples · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Corruption and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/corruption-and-administrative-ethics.md) → causes of corruption · **Proposed slug:** `causes-corruption-beyond-poverty` · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Corruption challenges](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/18_Utilization-of-Public-Funds-and-Challenges-of-Corruption.md) · [Moral theories](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/08_Moral-Theories-Deontology-Consequentialism-Virtue-Ethics.md)

### Demand decoding and framework

Reject a monocausal poverty thesis; organise causes across motive, opportunity, rationalisation and institutions, with examples.

### Model answer

Poverty may create survival pressure, but neither necessarily causes corruption nor explains grand corruption. Corruption arises when **motive, opportunity and rationalisation** converge.

Causes include greed, status competition, entitlement, monopoly, opaque discretion, weak detection, delayed punishment, patronage, political-business collusion, conflicts of interest and normalised “speed money.” Klitgaard's formulation—monopoly plus discretion minus accountability—captures opportunity.

A clerk may charge to move a file; a wealthy executive may bribe for regulatory advantage; an affluent official may manipulate procurement. Rationalisations such as “everyone does it” silence conscience.

Control alone can breed paralysis; simplification, transparency, enforcement and ethical leadership must reinforce one another.

**Conclusion:** Corruption is fundamentally a failure of character reinforced by profitable opportunity and weak accountability, not a condition of poverty.

**Why this earns marks:** It directly answers the paradox, uses a recognised framework and distinguishes petty from grand corruption.

## Q7. Individual and collective accountability

**Official question:** What does ‘accountability’ mean in the context of public service? What measures can be adopted to ensure individual and collective accountability of public servants? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** 2014 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q7 · Directive: Define and prescribe at two levels · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Work Culture and Public Funds](../../topics/ethics/work-culture-and-public-funds.md) → public-service accountability · **Proposed slug:** `individual-collective-public-accountability` · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

### Demand decoding and framework

Define answerability plus consequences, distinguish personal responsibility from organisational accountability, and provide preventive and corrective measures.

### Model answer

Public-service accountability means explaining entrusted authority, accepting scrutiny and facing proportionate correction. It combines **answerability** and **enforceability**.

**Individual accountability** requires clear duties, recorded reasons, conflict disclosure, standards, supervision, fair discipline and protection for lawful dissent. Officials cannot hide arbitrariness behind a file.

**Collective accountability** needs assigned institutional responsibility, service standards, legislative and CAG oversight, RTI, social audits, vigilance, appeals and outcome reviews. Digital trails locate delay.

However, collective responsibility must not become collective impunity, nor should one junior employee be scapegoated for systemic failure. Responsibility should track authority, knowledge and control. Honest error, negligence and corruption require different responses.

**Conclusion:** Accountability works when duties are clear, decisions traceable, citizens can challenge them and consequences are timely and proportionate.

**Why this earns marks:** It defines both elements, answers both levels and prevents scapegoating through a responsibility principle.

### Stakeholders and ethical issues

Present and future citizens; local and indigenous communities; workers and firms; government; non-human species and ecosystems. The conflicts are livelihood versus ecological security, short-term gain versus intergenerational justice, and aggregate benefit versus unequal local burdens.

### Options and consequences

1. **Unrestricted development:** may accelerate output and jobs, but externalises health, displacement and irreversible ecological costs.
2. **Blanket prohibition:** may protect ecosystems, but can freeze essential infrastructure and livelihoods irrespective of context.
3. **Development within ecological and justice limits:** requires more planning and cost initially, but preserves long-term capability and legitimacy.

### Justified strategy

Adopt the third course through a mitigation hierarchy: **avoid** critical habitats; **minimise** impacts through cleaner design; **restore** damaged systems; and use offsets only for genuinely residual, measurable harm. Apply strategic and cumulative assessment, not project-by-project clearance alone. Make alternatives, data and reasons public; secure meaningful participation of affected communities; recognise lawful rights; and ensure fair compensation, livelihood restoration and grievance appeal.

Use polluter-pays liability, extended producer responsibility, resource pricing, clean-technology incentives, public transport, circular production and independent compliance monitoring. Climate and biodiversity thresholds should be non-negotiable where harm is irreversible.

### Safeguards and qualification

Brundtland's sustainable-development principle joins present needs with future capacity; Rawlsian justice asks who bears the burden. Green approval must not become either ritual clearance or indefinite veto. Decisions require transparent evidence, periodic review and a just transition for workers.

**Examiner takeaway:** The conflict is managed by changing the location, technology, scale and distribution of development—not by choosing growth or nature in the abstract.

## Q10. Is individual ethical conduct practical?

**Official case and demand (verified transcription):** Suppose one of your close friends, who is also aspiring for civil services, comes to you for discussing some of the issues related to ethical conduct in public service. He raises the following points:

(i) In the present times, when unethical environment is quite prevalent, individual attempts to stick to ethical principles may cause a lot of problems in one's career. It may also cause hardship to the family members as well as risk to one's life. Why should we not be pragmatic and follow the path of least resistance, and be happy with doing whatever good we can?

(ii) When so many people are adopting wrong means and are grossly harming the system, what difference would it make if only a small minority tries to be ethical? They are going to be rather ineffective and are bound to get frustrated.

(iii) If we become fussy about ethical considerations, will it not hamper the economic progress of our country? After all, in the present age of high competition, we cannot afford to be left behind in the race of development.

(iv) It is understandable that we should not get involved in grossly unethical practices, but giving and accepting small gratifications and doing small favours increases everybody's motivation. It also makes the system more efficient. What is wrong in adopting such practices?

### Stakeholders and ethical conflicts

The aspirant and family; citizens affected by official choices; honest colleagues; the institution; future entrants. The conflicts are integrity versus self-preservation, individual agency versus systemic failure, ethical means versus speed, and reciprocity versus corruption.

### Critical analysis

1. **Path of least resistance:** concern for family and safety is legitimate; recklessness is not a duty. Yet routine surrender makes the official complicit and deepens the danger for others. Prudence should guide *how* one resists, not erase moral limits.
2. **Minority is ineffective:** one person cannot purify a system, but can protect a citizen, preserve evidence and create a precedent. Collective action often begins with credible minorities; visible integrity also changes expectations.
3. **Ethics hampers development:** arbitrary delay does, but corruption misallocates contracts, conceals unsafe work and destroys trust. Ethical rules improve durable efficiency; procedures should be simplified, not bypassed for favourites.
4. **Small gratification is harmless:** gifts create reciprocity, unequal access and escalating expectations. What looks like motivation becomes a private tax and compromises impartiality.

### Advice and safeguards

Adopt **principled pragmatism**: define non-negotiables; learn rules and use written orders; build alliances with honest peers; document decisions; disclose conflicts; simplify citizen processes; escalate through lawful channels; and use whistle-blower, vigilance or judicial remedies according to risk. Protect family through risk assessment rather than solitary heroics.

Aristotle's practical wisdom rejects both cowardly compliance and impulsive martyrdom. Kantian duty explains why universalised bribery defeats public office itself.

**Examiner takeaway:** Ethical conduct is neither naïve purity nor accommodation; it is lawful, strategic integrity supported by collective and institutional safeguards.

(iii) Brief your higher-ups, seek directions from them and act accordingly.

### Stakeholders and ethical issues

The complainant; accused officer and family; disciplined employees; other staff; Women's Commission/inquiry body; department; citizens served; media. ### Options and consequences

1. **Explain but soften discipline:** cooperates with inquiry, yet appears to trade official action for personal relief and rewards retaliation.
2. **Ignore the Commission:** keeps pressure on misconduct, but violates accountability, prejudices the complaint and damages legitimacy.
3. **Obey higher-ups mechanically:** gains institutional cover, but abdicates judgment and may permit influence.

### Justified course

I would promptly give a factual, documented response; request an independent, time-bound inquiry; preserve relevant communications, duty rosters and witness material; avoid contacting or retaliating against the complainant; and offer full cooperation. ### Safeguards

The stem characterises the complaint as instigated, but the officer must not treat that as an adjudicated fact. If malicious fabrication is independently established, action should follow law; if any misconduct is found, accountability must be accepted. Staff service conditions and grievance channels should also be reviewed.

**Examiner takeaway:** The strongest answer defends both gender-justice machinery and administrative integrity by insulating two fair processes from each other.

## Q12. Bribe demanded for a superior tender

**Official case and demand (verified transcription):** Suppose you are the CEO of a company that manufactures specialized electronic equipment used by a government department. You have submitted your bid for the supply of this equipment to the department. Both the quality and cost of your offer are better than those of the competitors. Yet the concerned officer is demanding a hefty bribe for approving the tender. Getting the order is important both for you and for your company. Not getting the order would mean closing a production line. It may also affect your own career. However, as a value-conscious person, you do not want to give bribe.

### Stakeholders and ethical issues

### Competing options and consequences

**Pay the bribe:** may save the line, jobs and career; the superior bid may serve government well. But it is illegal, rewards extortion, creates blackmail and accounting fraud, disadvantages honest competitors and normalises future demands.

2. activate the company's board-level anti-bribery protocol and obtain legal advice;

5. coordinate, where lawful, with industry bodies against extortion;
6. protect reporting staff and prepare temporary redeployment, alternative orders or finance for the production line.

This course integrates deontological integrity with consequential concern for employees. It may delay the order and trigger retaliation, so confidentiality, evidence security, board oversight and business-continuity planning are essential. Allegations must remain factual, not public-pressure tactics.

**Examiner takeaway:** The false choice between bribery and surrender is overcome by documented refusal, institutional escalation and livelihood safeguards.

## Q14. Rural-to-urban migration

**Official case and demand (verified transcription):** In our country, the migration of rural people to towns and cities is increasing drastically. This is causing serious problems both in the rural as well as in the urban areas. In fact, things are becoming really unmanageable. Can you analyze this problem in detail and indicate not only the socio-economic but also the emotional and attitudinal factors responsible for this problem? Also, distinctly bring out why—

(a) educated rural youth are trying to shift to urban areas;  
(b) landless poor people are migrating to urban slums;  
(c) even some farmers are selling off their land and trying to settle in urban areas taking up petty jobs.

What feasible steps can you suggest which will be effective in controlling this serious problem of our country? **(250 words, 20 marks)**

**Primary route:** Ethics → [Governance and International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/governance-and-international-ethics.md) → migration, dignity and balanced development · **Proposed slug:** `rural-urban-migration-ethical-governance` · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

### Stakeholders and ethical issues

Migrants and families; women, children and elderly left behind; farmers and landless labourers; rural communities; urban residents and local bodies; employers and governments. Values are freedom of movement, dignity, livelihood security, equal opportunity and fair distribution of development.

### Differentiated causes

- **Educated youth:** mismatch between education and rural jobs, better careers and services, status attached to urban life, autonomy from restrictive hierarchies and peer networks.
- **Landless poor:** seasonal unemployment, low wages, debt, caste exclusion and distress shocks; cities promise cash work and network-based entry even when housing is informal.
- **Farmers selling land:** fragmented holdings, volatile income, debt, climate and water stress, rising land value, weak services and declining social prestige of farming; petty urban work may appear more predictable.

Migration also reflects aspiration, exposure to urban lifestyles and a desire for anonymity and dignity—not only income.

### Options and consequences

Coercive controls violate liberty and drive migration underground. Laissez-faire movement without services produces slums and exploitation. The justified course is **balanced territorial development plus migrant inclusion**.

### Measures and safeguards

Build rural non-farm clusters, agro-processing, digital connectivity, quality schools and health care; align skills and credit with local enterprises; improve irrigation, risk protection, land leasing and farmer organisations; strengthen small towns as employment nodes. In cities, provide portable entitlements, rental housing, transport, worker registration and non-discriminatory services.

Migration policy should enable informed choice, not “control” people. Monitor outcomes by gender and caste, protect land sellers from fraud and support return migration.

**Examiner takeaway:** Reduce distress migration without suppressing mobility by making rural residence viable and urban migration safe, lawful and dignified.
