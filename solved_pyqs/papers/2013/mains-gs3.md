## Q1. Corporate social responsibility under the Companies Bill
**Primary route:** Economy → [Industrial Policy, 1991 Reforms, PSUs and Disinvestment](../../topics/economy/industrial-policy-1991-reforms-psus-and-disinvestment.md) → Corporate governance and CSR · **Secondary route:** [Poverty, Inequality, Social Sector and Inclusive Growth](../../topics/economy/poverty-inequality-social-sector-and-inclusive-growth.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2013 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 1  
**Official question:** With a consideration towards the strategy of inclusive growth, the new Companies Bill, 2013 has indirectly made CSR a mandatory obligation. Discuss the challenges expected in its implementation in right earnest. Also discuss other provisions in the Bill and their implications.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 200 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Corporate Sector → Governance and CSR  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/industrial-policy-1991-reforms-psus-and-disinvestment`  
**Difficulty / directive:** High · Discuss implementation challenges + Explain wider provisions and implications  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/16_Industrial-Policy-1991-Reforms-PSUs-and-Disinvestment.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/16_Industrial-Policy-1991-Reforms-PSUs-and-Disinvestment.md)

### Demand decoding
Explain the Bill’s “comply-or-explain” CSR design, diagnose implementation problems, and cover important non-CSR governance reforms with balanced implications.

### Compact framework
`board-level CSR duty + disclosure → social investment, but weak capacity/measurement may produce box-ticking; wider Bill strengthens accountability`

### Model answer
The Companies Bill, 2013 requires qualifying companies to constitute a Board CSR committee and spend at least 2% of average net profits of the preceding three years on specified social purposes, or explain non-spending. It therefore makes process and disclosure mandatory, while stopping short of an absolute spending command.

Implementation may face:

- choosing genuine local needs rather than publicity projects;
- shortage of credible implementing agencies and professional CSR capacity;
- inconsistent measurement of social outcomes and possible relabelling of normal business expenditure;
- concentration of funds near profitable urban or industrial centres;
- weak Board oversight, related-party diversion and superficial disclosure; and
- tension between shareholder accountability and government-directed philanthropy.

The Bill also introduces independent directors, woman-director requirements for prescribed companies, stronger auditor rotation and responsibility, vigil mechanisms, class-action suits, tighter related-party rules, one-person companies and National Company Law Tribunal arrangements. These can improve minority protection, transparency and entrepreneurship, but compliance costs may burden smaller firms and independence can remain nominal without enforcement.

Thus CSR can support inclusive growth only when additional to lawful wages, taxes and environmental compliance. Transparent project selection, impact reporting and accountable partnerships are essential.

### Examiner rationale
The answer accurately distinguishes mandatory governance from qualified spending, covers both limbs, and evaluates benefits against enforcement and compliance constraints.

## Q2. FRBM Act: rationale, design and effectiveness
**Primary route:** Economy → [Union Budget, Fiscal Policy and Deficit Indicators](../../topics/economy/union-budget-fiscal-policy-and-deficit-indicators.md) → Fiscal rules · **Secondary route:** [Taxation, GST, Finance Commission and Fiscal Federalism](../../topics/economy/taxation-gst-finance-commission-and-fiscal-federalism.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2013 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 2  
**Official question:** What were the reasons for the introduction of Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management (FRBM) Act, 2003? Discuss critically its salient features and their effectiveness.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 200 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Fiscal Policy → Fiscal Responsibility  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/union-budget-fiscal-policy-and-deficit-indicators`  
**Difficulty / directive:** High · Explain rationale + Critically assess features and outcomes  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/09_Union-Budget-Fiscal-Policy-and-Deficit-Indicators.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/09_Union-Budget-Fiscal-Policy-and-Deficit-Indicators.md)

### Demand decoding
Connect the 1990s fiscal deterioration to the Act’s rules, then judge achievements, design weaknesses and crisis-era suspension.

### Compact framework
`persistent deficits/debt/monetisation → numerical rules + transparency; discipline improved, but pro-cyclicality and off-budget escape weakened effectiveness`

### Model answer
The FRBM Act, 2003 responded to persistent revenue and fiscal deficits, rising public debt and interest burdens, and automatic monetisation that risked inflation and crowded out private investment. It sought inter-generational equity and macroeconomic credibility.

Its principal features were:

- time-bound elimination of the Union revenue deficit and reduction of fiscal deficit to 3% of GDP;
- annual reduction paths under the FRBM Rules, 2004;
- restrictions on Central borrowing from the RBI, except temporary advances and exceptional circumstances;
- presentation of medium-term fiscal, fiscal-policy strategy and macroeconomic framework statements; and
- disclosure of guarantees, liabilities and deviations, with escape for national security or calamity.

The framework improved fiscal vocabulary, transparency and pre-crisis consolidation: the fiscal deficit fell and direct RBI subscription to primary issues ended. However, buoyant growth and tax receipts also aided improvement. Targets encouraged expenditure compression, sometimes at the cost of capital creation, while off-budget bonds and optimistic assumptions obscured liabilities. The global financial crisis required relaxation from 2008–09, revealing inadequate counter-cyclical flexibility; statutory targets also lacked an independent enforcement mechanism.

FRBM was therefore useful as a transparency-and-discipline anchor, but numerical ceilings required credible accounting, protection of productive expenditure and well-defined cyclical escape clauses.

### Examiner rationale
The response links each feature to the problem addressed and gives a qualified outcome assessment rather than treating target compliance as sufficient.

## Q3. Tax expenditure and housing policy
**Primary route:** Economy → [Union Budget, Fiscal Policy and Deficit Indicators](../../topics/economy/union-budget-fiscal-policy-and-deficit-indicators.md) → Tax expenditure · **Secondary route:** [Taxation, GST, Finance Commission and Fiscal Federalism](../../topics/economy/taxation-gst-finance-commission-and-fiscal-federalism.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2013 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 3  
**Official question:** What is the meaning of the term ‘tax expenditure’? Taking housing sector as an example, discuss how it influences the budgetary policies of the government.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 200 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Public Finance → Tax Expenditure  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/union-budget-fiscal-policy-and-deficit-indicators`  
**Difficulty / directive:** Moderate · Define + Apply to housing and budget choices  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/09_Union-Budget-Fiscal-Policy-and-Deficit-Indicators.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/09_Union-Budget-Fiscal-Policy-and-Deficit-Indicators.md)

### Demand decoding
Define the revenue forgone through preferential tax treatment and show its fiscal, distributional and sectoral effects through housing.

### Compact framework
`departure from benchmark tax → implicit subsidy/revenue forgone → housing demand and construction, but fiscal cost and unequal incidence`

### Model answer
Tax expenditure is revenue forgone because the tax system grants exemptions, deductions, credits, concessional rates or deferrals relative to a benchmark tax. It is an implicit subsidy delivered through taxation rather than voted expenditure.

In housing, deductions for interest on housing loans and principal repayment, concessional treatment of specified housing activity and tax preferences for rental or construction investment can:

- lower the effective cost of owning a house and stimulate demand;
- encourage household savings to flow into housing;
- support construction, cement, steel and employment through multiplier effects;
- promote affordable housing when concessions are carefully targeted; and
- influence urban development and the formal mortgage market.

Budget policy must nevertheless record the opportunity cost. Revenue forgone narrows fiscal space for public housing, water, transport and slum upgrading. Benefits tied to taxable income and formal mortgages disproportionately reach middle- and higher-income households, while the poor may lack titles, credit or sufficient tax liability. Preferences may inflate land and house prices, encourage speculation and complicate the tax code.

Government should therefore publish tax-expenditure statements, compare each concession with direct expenditure, impose sunset reviews and target supply or affordable segments. A concession is justified only when its additional social benefit exceeds leakage and revenue cost.

### Examiner rationale
The answer defines the concept against a benchmark and traces both budgetary stimulus and hidden fiscal-distributional costs.

## Q4. Food security, implementation and WTO concerns
**Primary route:** Economy → [MSP, Procurement, Buffer Stocks, PDS and Food Security](../../topics/economy/msp-procurement-buffer-stocks-pds-and-food-security.md) → National Food Security Bill · **Secondary route:** [Direct and Indirect Farm Subsidies and WTO Rules](../../topics/economy/direct-and-indirect-farm-subsidies-and-wto-rules.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2013 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 4  
**Official question:** Food Security Bill is expected to eliminate hunger and malnutrition in India. Critically discuss various apprehensions in its effective implementation along with the concerns it has generated in WTO.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 200 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Food Security → NFSA and WTO  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/msp-procurement-buffer-stocks-pds-and-food-security`  
**Difficulty / directive:** High · Critically discuss domestic implementation + WTO concerns  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/12_MSP-Procurement-Buffer-Stocks-PDS-and-Food-Security.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/12_MSP-Procurement-Buffer-Stocks-PDS-and-Food-Security.md)

### Demand decoding
Assess whether legal grain entitlements can eliminate hunger, identify delivery and nutrition gaps, and explain the WTO subsidy controversy.

### Compact framework
`legal cereal entitlement → access floor; identification/procurement/PDS/nutrition constraints + WTO support calculation → qualified promise`

### Model answer
The 2013 Bill converts subsidised foodgrain access into a legal entitlement for roughly two-thirds of the population, with special maternity and child-nutrition provisions. It can reduce hunger, but cereals alone cannot eliminate malnutrition.

Implementation concerns include:

- inclusion and exclusion errors in identifying beneficiaries;
- leakages, ghost cards and weak last-mile PDS capacity;
- uneven State readiness and grievance redress;
- large procurement, storage, transport and fiscal requirements;
- rice-wheat bias that can stress water, crowd out pulses and distort cropping;
- inadequate attention to sanitation, health, dietary diversity and feeding quality; and
- possible erosion of local procurement or successful State-specific systems through rigid design.

At the WTO, public procurement at administered minimum support prices and sale at subsidised prices may count as trade-distorting domestic support. Developing countries object that the Agreement on Agriculture calculates support using fixed 1986–88 external reference prices, exaggerating current subsidy values through inflation. Large public stocks also raise concerns about export leakage and effects on world markets.

India should defend food-security policy while improving transparency, stock reporting and safeguards against commercial export. Domestically, end-to-end computerisation, doorstep delivery, local grains and nutrition convergence are essential. The law supplies an access floor, not a complete nutrition strategy.

### Examiner rationale
The response covers entitlements, implementation, nutrition and the precise WTO measurement issue, ending with a balanced verdict.

## Q5. Agricultural subsidies and distortions
**Primary route:** Economy → [Direct and Indirect Farm Subsidies and WTO Rules](../../topics/economy/direct-and-indirect-farm-subsidies-and-wto-rules.md) → Subsidy incidence and distortion · **Secondary route:** [Irrigation, Inputs, Credit, Insurance and Sustainable Agriculture](../../topics/economy/irrigation-inputs-credit-insurance-and-sustainable-agriculture.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2013 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 5  
**Official question:** What are the different types of agriculture subsidies given to farmers at the national and at state levels? Critically analyse the agricultural subsidy regime with reference to the distortions created by it.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 200 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Agriculture → Input and Price Subsidies  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/direct-and-indirect-farm-subsidies-and-wto-rules`  
**Difficulty / directive:** High · Enumerate + Critically analyse distortions  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/28_Direct-and-Indirect-Farm-Subsidies-and-WTO-Rules.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/28_Direct-and-Indirect-Farm-Subsidies-and-WTO-Rules.md)

### Demand decoding
Classify Union and State support, explain its protective rationale, and analyse economic, fiscal, ecological and distributional distortions.

### Compact framework
`risk protection and food security benefits ↔ price/input distortions, unequal capture, fiscal and ecological costs`

### Model answer
Agricultural support operates through both budgets and administered prices.

**Union-level support** includes fertiliser subsidy, food subsidy linked to MSP procurement, concessional crop credit and interest subvention, crop-insurance support, irrigation programmes, seeds, extension and tax concessions. **States** commonly subsidise or provide free electricity, canal water, local irrigation, seeds, credit relief and bonuses over MSP.

These measures protect farmers against weather and price risk, sustain food security and make modern inputs affordable. Yet their design creates distortions:

- MSP procurement concentrated in rice and wheat and in a few States discourages crop diversification;
- cheap urea disturbs the nitrogen-phosphorus-potassium balance and degrades soil;
- free or unmetered power promotes groundwater over-extraction and weakens electricity utilities;
- canal-water under-pricing reduces maintenance and favours head-reach farmers;
- benefits rise with landholding, input use and marketable surplus, so tenants and small rain-fed farmers gain less;
- recurring subsidies crowd out research, extension, storage and irrigation investment; and
- trade and administered-price support can create excess stocks and WTO disputes.

Reform should be gradual: better targeting, soil-based nutrient support, metered power with direct assistance, diversified procurement and greater investment in public goods. Removing support abruptly would expose vulnerable cultivators; the objective is to shift from input use to income, resilience and productivity.

### Examiner rationale
The answer distinguishes governmental levels and evaluates incidence, production incentives, resources and fiscal trade-offs without advocating indiscriminate withdrawal.

## Q6. Pink revolution, nutrition and health
**Primary route:** Economy → [Economics of Animal Rearing, Livestock, Dairy, Poultry and Fisheries](../../topics/economy/economics-of-animal-rearing-livestock-dairy-poultry-and-fisheries.md) → Meat and poultry value chains · **Secondary route:** [Food Processing, Cold Chains and Value Addition](../../topics/economy/food-processing-cold-chains-and-value-addition.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2013 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 6  
**Official question:** India needs to strengthen measures to promote the pink revolution in food industry for ensuring better nutrition and health. Critically elucidate the statement.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 200 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Animal Rearing and Food Processing → Meat and Poultry  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/economics-of-animal-rearing-livestock-dairy-poultry-and-fisheries`  
**Difficulty / directive:** Moderate-High · Critically explain proposition  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/30_Economics-of-Animal-Rearing-Livestock-Dairy-Poultry-and-Fisheries.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/30_Economics-of-Animal-Rearing-Livestock-Dairy-Poultry-and-Fisheries.md)

### Demand decoding
Relate safe meat/poultry development to protein nutrition, livelihoods and health, while addressing cultural, veterinary, hygiene and environmental limits.

### Compact framework
`livestock productivity + hygienic processing/cold chain → protein and incomes; health gains require safety, affordability and balanced diets`

### Model answer
The “pink revolution” denotes modern expansion of meat and poultry production, processing and marketing. It can improve access to high-quality protein, iron and micronutrients, while creating income for small livestock holders and employment in feed, veterinary care, slaughter, processing and cold transport.

However, greater output alone does not ensure health. India’s sector is constrained by low animal productivity, poor feed, weak disease surveillance, unhygienic slaughter, inadequate cold chains and fragmented markets. Zoonoses, antibiotic residues and food-borne infection can offset nutritional gains. High prices may exclude poor households; dietary choices also vary by culture. Intensive livestock production can generate waste, water pollution and welfare concerns.

Measures should include:

- breed improvement appropriate to local conditions and better fodder;
- vaccination, veterinary extension and traceability;
- modern, regulated abattoirs with ante- and post-mortem inspection;
- HACCP-based processing, refrigeration and testing;
- producer cooperatives, credit and market access for smallholders;
- scientific waste rendering and effluent treatment; and
- nutrition policy that also promotes pulses, milk and eggs rather than equating nutrition with meat alone.

Thus the pink revolution should mean safe, inclusive and resource-efficient value chains. It is one component of dietary diversity, not a stand-alone cure for malnutrition.

### Examiner rationale
The answer critically connects nutrition to the entire animal-health and processing chain and qualifies the central claim.

## Q7. Liberalisation and Indian-owned companies
**Primary route:** Economy → [Industrial Policy, 1991 Reforms, PSUs and Disinvestment](../../topics/economy/industrial-policy-1991-reforms-psus-and-disinvestment.md) → Post-reform competitiveness · **Secondary route:** [Foreign Trade, WTO, FTAs and Protectionism](../../topics/economy/foreign-trade-wto-ftas-and-protectionism.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2013 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 7  
**Official question:** Examine the impact of liberalization on companies owned by Indians. Are they competing with the MNCs satisfactorily? Discuss.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 200 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Liberalisation → Domestic Firm Competitiveness  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/industrial-policy-1991-reforms-psus-and-disinvestment`  
**Difficulty / directive:** High · Examine impact + Judge competitiveness  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/16_Industrial-Policy-1991-Reforms-PSUs-and-Disinvestment.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/16_Industrial-Policy-1991-Reforms-PSUs-and-Disinvestment.md)

### Demand decoding
Assess gains and pressures after 1991 and provide a sectorally qualified comparison with multinational corporations.

### Compact framework
`competition + technology/markets → stronger Indian firms; finance, R&D and scale gaps → uneven MNC parity`

### Model answer
Liberalisation dismantled industrial licensing, reduced tariffs, opened sectors to foreign investment and exposed Indian firms to competition. Its impact has been transformative but uneven.

Competition forced restructuring, quality improvement and adoption of technology. Access to foreign capital and markets enabled Indian groups to acquire overseas firms and build brands. Tata’s acquisition of Jaguar Land Rover, Bharti’s telecommunications expansion, pharmaceutical generics and Indian information-technology services illustrate global capability. Consumers gained wider choice and lower costs.

Conversely, inefficient firms lost protection; small enterprises faced imported competition and credit constraints. Some domestic companies accumulated debt through ambitious acquisitions. MNCs retained advantages in patents, research budgets, global supply chains, finance and established brands. Indian success was strongest in cost-sensitive engineering, automobiles, pharmaceuticals and IT services, but weaker in advanced electronics, capital goods and original research.

Therefore Indian-owned firms are competing satisfactorily in selected sectors, not uniformly. Some have become genuine multinationals; others survive through low costs, local knowledge or joint ventures without controlling core technology.

Policy should not restore indiscriminate protection. Reliable infrastructure, competition policy, skills, research support, patient finance and easier growth for smaller firms can convert market access into technological depth. Liberalisation is an opportunity framework; competitiveness depends on domestic capabilities.

### Examiner rationale
The answer uses period-appropriate firm examples, differentiates sectors, and reaches a qualified judgment rather than a binary verdict.

## Q8. Land reform, productivity and poverty
**Primary route:** Economy → [Land Reforms, Green Revolution and Cropping Systems](../../topics/economy/land-reforms-green-revolution-and-cropping-systems.md) → Agrarian structure · **Secondary route:** [Poverty, Inequality, Social Sector and Inclusive Growth](../../topics/economy/poverty-inequality-social-sector-and-inclusive-growth.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2013 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 8  
**Official question:** Establish relationship between land reforms, agriculture productivity and elimination of poverty in the Indian economy. Discuss the difficulties in designing and implementation of agriculture-friendly land reforms in India.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 200 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Agriculture → Land Reform and Poverty  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/land-reforms-green-revolution-and-cropping-systems`  
**Difficulty / directive:** High · Establish causal relationship + Diagnose design and implementation difficulties  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/11_Land-Reforms-Green-Revolution-and-Cropping-Systems.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/11_Land-Reforms-Green-Revolution-and-Cropping-Systems.md)

### Demand decoding
Show mechanisms connecting secure, equitable operational holdings to investment and poverty reduction, then explain why reform is politically and technically difficult.

### Compact framework
`secure access + efficient holdings → investment/productivity + bargaining power → poverty reduction; records, politics and fragmentation impede reform`

### Model answer
Land reform can join equity with productivity. Abolition of intermediaries gives cultivators direct incentives; secure tenancy encourages soil improvement; ceilings and redistribution provide assets and bargaining power to the landless; consolidation reduces boundary loss and permits efficient input use. West Bengal’s **Operation Barga** showed how recording sharecroppers and protecting tenure could improve incentives. Higher productivity raises food supply, wages and rural demand, reducing poverty.

Yet the relationship is not automatic. Very small fragmented plots may be unviable without credit, water, technology and markets. Poorly designed tenancy bans can drive leasing underground, denying tenants institutional support.

Major difficulties are:

- land being a State subject, producing uneven laws and political commitment;
- incomplete, outdated and disputed records;
- benami transfers, family partitions and litigation used to evade ceilings;
- resistance from landed interests embedded in local administration;
- balancing tenant security with owners’ fear of losing title;
- regional differences in tenure, tribal and customary rights;
- fragmentation through inheritance; and
- women’s weak recorded ownership.

Agriculture-friendly reform requires digitised and verified records, time-bound tribunals, legal and secure leasing, women’s titles, protection of commons and tribal land, consolidation or cooperative operation, and support services. Reform must enhance the cultivator’s security without freezing land into inefficient use.

### Examiner rationale
The response establishes the requested causal chain, supplies Operation Barga as evidence, and distinguishes land access from complementary productivity conditions.

**Primary route:** Environment → [Water Pollution and River Cleaning Missions](../../topics/environment/water-pollution-and-river-cleaning-missions.md) → Basin management and pollution control · **Secondary route:** [Multilateral Environmental Conventions: CBD, Basel, Stockholm and Montreal](../../topics/environment/multilateral-environmental-conventions-cbd-basel-stockholm-montreal.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2013 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 20  
**Official question:** Enumerate the National Water Policy of India. Taking river Ganges as an example, discuss the strategies which may be adopted for river water pollution control and management. What are the legal provisions of management and handling of hazardous wastes in India?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 200 words  
**Classification:** Environment → Water and Waste Governance → Integrated Pollution Control  
**Proposed topic slug:** `environment/water-pollution-and-river-cleaning-missions`  
**Difficulty / directive:** Very high · Enumerate policy + Apply river strategy + State hazardous-waste law  
**Canonical owner:** [`Environment-and-Ecology/basic/14_Water-Pollution-and-River-Cleaning-Missions.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Environment-and-Ecology/basic/14_Water-Pollution-and-River-Cleaning-Missions.md)

### Demand decoding
Allocate limited space across three explicit limbs: 2012 policy principles, Ganga-specific control measures and the then-operative hazardous-waste legal framework.

### Compact framework
`integrated basin policy → sewage/industrial/flow controls for Ganga; Environment Act rules govern hazardous waste cradle-to-grave`

### Model answer
The **National Water Policy, 2012** treats water as a scarce common-pool resource held under public trust. It advocates basin-level planning, ecological needs, drinking-water and sanitation priority, demand management, water-use efficiency, rational pricing with equity safeguards, aquifer mapping, recycling, participatory institutions and adaptation to climate variability.

For the Ganga, strategy should combine:

- basin-wide authority and pollution-load inventories;
- interception and treatment of municipal sewage, with operation-and-maintenance funding;
- strict consent, monitoring and common effluent treatment for tanneries and other industries under the **Water Act, 1974**;
- environmental flows, wetland and floodplain protection;
- sanitation, solid-waste control and improved cremation facilities;
- real-time quality monitoring, polluter-pays enforcement and public disclosure; and
- coordination across States rather than isolated town projects.  

Hazardous waste was governed principally by the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986** and the **Hazardous Wastes (Management, Handling and Transboundary Movement) Rules, 2008**. They require authorisation by Pollution Control Boards, safe packaging, labelling, transport manifests, records, accident reporting, approved storage/recycling/disposal and regulated import-export consistent with Basel controls. The Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991 provides immediate relief for accidents involving hazardous substances.

Policy must integrate water quantity, quality and waste enforcement; treatment assets without functioning institutions cannot restore a river.

### Examiner rationale
The response proportionally answers all three limbs with correct 2012 policy and 2013 legal anchors.

## Q21. Money laundering and economic sovereignty
**Primary route:** Internal Security → [Terror Financing, Money Laundering and FATF](../../topics/internal-security/terror-financing-money-laundering-and-fatf.md) → Anti-money-laundering architecture · **Secondary route:** [Organised Crime, Narco-terrorism and Trafficking](../../topics/internal-security/organised-crime-narco-terrorism-and-trafficking.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2013 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 21  
**Official question:** Money laundering poses a serious security threat to a country’s economic sovereignty. What is its significance for India and what steps are required to be taken to control this menace?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 200 words  
**Classification:** Internal Security → Financial Crime → Money Laundering  
**Proposed topic slug:** `internal-security/terror-financing-money-laundering-and-fatf`  
**Difficulty / directive:** High · Explain significance + Recommend controls  
**Canonical owner:** [`Internal-Security/basic/10_Terror-Financing-Money-Laundering-and-FATF.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Internal-Security/basic/10_Terror-Financing-Money-Laundering-and-FATF.md)

### Demand decoding
Connect placement-layering-integration to sovereignty, crime and terror, then assess India’s 2013 legal-institutional response and required improvements.

### Compact framework
`proceeds of crime → concealment/integration → criminal influence over markets and state; prevention + intelligence + confiscation + cooperation`

### Model answer
Money laundering disguises criminal proceeds through placement, layering and integration. It threatens economic sovereignty by evading taxation and regulation, distorting asset prices, corrupting institutions and financing organised crime, narcotics and terrorism.

India is vulnerable because of a large cash economy, informal remittance channels such as hawala, trade misinvoicing, gold and real-estate opacity, corruption proceeds, porous borders and increasingly complex cross-border transactions. Laundered funds can infiltrate businesses and politics, while terrorist financing may involve even small, apparently lawful sums.

Control requires:

- use of the **Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002**, including attachment, prosecution and confiscation with due process;
- Financial Intelligence Unit analysis of suspicious and cash-transaction reports;
- risk-based customer identification and beneficial-ownership verification by banks and intermediaries;
- regulation of money-service businesses, casinos, real estate and dealers in precious metals;
- integration of tax, customs, enforcement and police databases;
- investigation of predicate offences and parallel financial trails;
- controls against trade-based laundering and misuse of shell entities;
- faster mutual legal assistance, extradition and asset recovery; and
- compliance with **Financial Action Task Force** standards.

Success should be judged by credible convictions and recovered assets, not merely reporting volume. Strong safeguards and judicial oversight are necessary to prevent arbitrary seizure.

### Examiner rationale
The answer explains why laundering is a sovereignty issue and proposes a period-appropriate, rights-qualified enforcement chain.

## Q22. Social networking sites and security
**Primary route:** Internal Security → [Social Media, Encrypted Messaging and Information Warfare](../../topics/internal-security/social-media-encrypted-messaging-and-information-warfare.md) → Platform security risks · **Secondary route:** [Cyber Security, CII and Cybercrime](../../topics/internal-security/cyber-security-cii-and-cybercrime.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2013 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 22  
**Official question:** What are social networking sites and what security implications do these sites present?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 200 words  
**Classification:** Internal Security → Information Space → Social Networking Sites  
**Proposed topic slug:** `internal-security/social-media-encrypted-messaging-and-information-warfare`  
**Difficulty / directive:** Moderate · Define + Analyse security implications  
**Canonical owner:** [`Internal-Security/basic/09_Social-Media-Encrypted-Messaging-and-Information-Warfare.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Internal-Security/basic/09_Social-Media-Encrypted-Messaging-and-Information-Warfare.md)

### Demand decoding
Define networked user platforms and cover individual, communal, criminal, strategic and institutional risks with constitutional qualification.

### Compact framework
`rapid user-generated connectivity → participation and intelligence, but amplification, data exposure and manipulation risks`

### Model answer
Social networking sites are internet platforms on which users create profiles, form connections and rapidly produce, share and respond to text, images, video and links. Their low entry barriers and network effects make them valuable for communication but also security-sensitive.

Implications include:

- disclosure of personal, locational and institutional information enabling identity theft, stalking or operational targeting;
- phishing, malware and social engineering through trusted contacts;
- rapid circulation of rumours, doctored material and communal incitement, as demonstrated by online content surrounding the 2012 north-east exodus;
- recruitment, propaganda, fundraising and reconnaissance by extremist groups;
- criminal coordination and harassment;
- foreign influence, psychological operations and impersonation;
- profiling through large private datasets; and
- evidentiary and jurisdictional problems because servers, companies and users span countries.

Government also gains early warning, public communication and open-source intelligence, so blanket blocking is counterproductive.

India needs trained cyber cells, platform contact protocols, prompt preservation of lawful evidence, digital literacy, rapid factual counter-messaging, privacy and account-security practices, and narrowly tailored orders under law. Courts and oversight must protect legitimate speech and prevent surveillance abuse. Security policy should target unlawful conduct and coordinated harm, not disagreement. Resilience depends on credible institutions as much as content removal.

### Examiner rationale
The answer moves from definition to multi-level risks and supplies proportionate remedies without treating social media itself as criminal.

## Q23. Cyber warfare and India’s preparedness
**Primary route:** Internal Security → [Cyber Security, CII and Cybercrime](../../topics/internal-security/cyber-security-cii-and-cybercrime.md) → Cyber conflict and critical infrastructure · **Secondary route:** [Data Protection, DPDP Act and Cybersecurity](../../topics/science-and-technology/data-protection-dpdp-act-and-cybersecurity.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2013 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 23  
**Official question:** Cyber warfare is considered by some defense analysts to be a larger threat than even Al Qaeda or terrorism. What do you understand by Cyber warfare? Outline the cyber threats which India is vulnerable to and bring out the state of the country’s preparedness to deal with the same.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 200 words  
**Classification:** Internal Security → Cyber Security → Cyber Warfare and Preparedness  
**Proposed topic slug:** `internal-security/cyber-security-cii-and-cybercrime`  
**Difficulty / directive:** Very high · Define + Map threats + Assess preparedness  
**Canonical owner:** [`Internal-Security/basic/08_Cyber-Security-CII-and-Cybercrime.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Internal-Security/basic/08_Cyber-Security-CII-and-Cybercrime.md)

### Demand decoding
Define state-linked hostile cyber operations, identify India-specific vulnerabilities, and critically assess institutions as of the 2013 policy launch.

### Compact framework
`networked dependence + anonymous low-cost attack → espionage/disruption/sabotage; institutions exist but coordination and capability lag`

### Model answer
Cyber warfare is the use of network operations by, or on behalf of, a State to spy, disrupt, degrade or destroy an adversary’s information systems and critical functions. Unlike ordinary cybercrime, it pursues strategic objectives, though attribution is difficult and proxies blur the boundary.

India faces:

- espionage against government, defence, diplomatic and research networks;
- disruption of power, telecom, banking, transport and space-linked systems;
- theft of intellectual property and military data;
- website defacement, distributed denial-of-service and disinformation during crises;
- supply-chain compromise of imported hardware or software; and
- insider threats, weak passwords and unpatched systems.

Preparedness included the **Information Technology Act, 2000**, strengthened in 2008; CERT-In for incident response; sectoral teams; the National Technical Research Organisation; and protection functions for critical information infrastructure. The **National Cyber Security Policy, 2013** recognised capacity-building, information sharing, audits and public-private partnership.

However, responsibilities remained fragmented, skilled personnel scarce, reporting uneven and critical-infrastructure standards inconsistently enforced. India required a coordinated national command framework, regular red-teaming, indigenous cryptographic and hardware capability, mandatory incident sharing, resilient backups, cyber exercises and international cooperation.

Cyber threats can produce systemic disruption, but comparison with terrorism is context-dependent. Preparedness must integrate technology, intelligence, law, diplomacy and crisis management.

### Examiner rationale
The answer clearly distinguishes warfare from crime, uses institutions available in 2013, and critically identifies preparedness gaps.

## Q24. Fifth Schedule failures and Left Wing Extremism
**Primary route:** Internal Security → [Left Wing Extremism and Integrated Response](../../topics/internal-security/left-wing-extremism-and-integrated-response.md) → Tribal governance and alienation · **Secondary route:** [Forest Types and Forest Rights Act](../../topics/environment/forest-types-and-forest-rights-act.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2013 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 24  
**Official question:** Article 244 of the Indian Constitution relates to administration of scheduled areas and tribal areas. Analyse the impact of non-implementation of the provisions of the Fifth schedule on the growth of Left Wing extremism.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 200 words  
**Classification:** Internal Security → Left Wing Extremism → Fifth Schedule Governance  
**Proposed topic slug:** `internal-security/left-wing-extremism-and-integrated-response`  
**Difficulty / directive:** High · Analyse constitutional non-implementation as a causal factor  
**Canonical owner:** [`Internal-Security/basic/03_Left-Wing-Extremism-and-Integrated-Response.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Internal-Security/basic/03_Left-Wing-Extremism-and-Integrated-Response.md)

### Demand decoding
Identify Fifth Schedule safeguards and trace how institutional failure creates grievances exploitable by extremists, while avoiding monocausal explanation.

### Compact framework
`weak scheduled-area self-protection → land/resource alienation + legitimacy deficit → extremist mobilisation`

### Model answer
Under Article 244(1), the Fifth Schedule governs Scheduled Areas outside the north-east. It gives Governors special responsibility, requires annual reports to the President, establishes Tribal Advisory Councils and permits regulations restricting land transfer and moneylending or adapting laws to local conditions.

Weak implementation has aided Left Wing Extremism through:

- alienation of tribal land despite protective laws;
- mining and dams imposing displacement without credible rehabilitation or benefit sharing;
- exploitative credit, contractors and minor-forest-produce markets;
- poor use of Governor’s regulatory and reporting powers;
- Tribal Advisory Councils lacking influence;
- distant or predatory administration; and
- failure to coordinate the Schedule with **PESA, 1996** and the **Forest Rights Act, 2006**, weakening Gram Sabha control and forest rights.

These failures create a vacuum in which Maoists pose as defenders against dispossession, recruit aggrieved youth and obstruct the State. Extremist coercion, ideology and difficult terrain also matter; constitutional neglect is an enabling condition, not the sole cause.

An integrated response must enforce land protections, recognise community forest rights, empower Gram Sabhas, make mining transparent, deliver justice and combine development with precise security operations. Implementing the Fifth Schedule would restore legitimacy and reduce insurgent mobilisation.

### Examiner rationale
The response names the Schedule’s institutions and builds a direct causal chain from non-implementation to alienation and insurgent opportunity.

## Q25. Border management and internal security
**Primary route:** Internal Security → [Border Management and Border Area Development](../../topics/internal-security/border-management-and-border-area-development.md) → Porous borders and coordinated management · **Secondary route:** [Organised Crime, Narco-terrorism and Trafficking](../../topics/internal-security/organised-crime-narco-terrorism-and-trafficking.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2013 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 25  
**Official question:** How far are India’s internal security challenges linked with border management particularly in view of the long porous borders with most countries of South Asia and Myanmar?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 200 words  
**Classification:** Internal Security → Border Management → Transborder Threats  
**Proposed topic slug:** `internal-security/border-management-and-border-area-development`  
**Difficulty / directive:** High · Assess extent and qualify causation  
**Canonical owner:** [`Internal-Security/basic/06_Border-Management-and-Border-Area-Development.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Internal-Security/basic/06_Border-Management-and-Border-Area-Development.md)

### Demand decoding
Assess—not assume—the border-security linkage across varied frontiers, distinguish external channels from domestic causes, and indicate an integrated approach.

### Compact framework
`difficult terrain + social continuities + weak coordination → infiltration/contraband support; domestic grievances determine whether flows become insurgency`

### Model answer
India’s borders cross mountains, forests, rivers, deserts and settled communities. Social ties often predate boundaries, making complete sealing neither feasible nor desirable. Weak management nevertheless amplifies threats.

- The Pakistan frontier enables infiltration, arms and terror support, especially toward Jammu and Kashmir.
- Open movement and riverine stretches along Nepal and Bangladesh can enable counterfeit currency, trafficking, illegal migration and militant movement.
- The Myanmar frontier’s forests and cross-border ethnic links provide routes and sanctuaries for north-eastern insurgent groups and narcotics.
- Coastal gaps, informal trade and inadequate identification facilitate organised crime.

Yet borders are conduits, not complete causes. Insurgency also draws on exclusion, land conflict and governance failure. Treating every border community or migrant as a threat damages cooperation.

An effective approach requires terrain-specific fencing, roads and floodlighting, integrated check posts, coastal and riverine surveillance, communications, intelligence and joint work among border forces, customs and State police. Legal trade, identity systems, border-area development and community participation reduce dependence on smugglers. Diplomatic action against camps and trafficking networks is equally necessary.

Thus border management is a major force multiplier for internal security, but durable security also requires political inclusion and responsive governance within India.

### Examiner rationale
The answer assesses frontier-specific linkages, rejects a monocausal explanation and combines enforcement, development, community trust and diplomacy.
