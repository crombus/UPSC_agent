### Q1(a). Explain and evaluate Strawson’s arguments for his conception of the nature of a person.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Quine–Strawson](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/quine-strawson.md) → persons as basic particulars · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Directive: Explain and evaluate · Syllabus unit: Strawson—person and descriptive metaphysics · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Quine–Strawson](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Quine-Strawson.md) · **Slug:** `strawson-person-basic-particular`

**Demand decoding:** Reconstruct the anti-dualist argument, explain primitive ascription of mental and bodily predicates, and test whether conceptual priority establishes ontology.

**Argument map:** mental states require identifiable subjects → purely private egos cannot be publicly reidentified → bodies alone cannot own first-person predicates → “person” primitively bears both classes → self-ascription presupposes other-ascription → dualism is conceptually derivative.

#### Model answer

Strawson rejects the Cartesian picture of a person as two contingently connected substances. A **person** is a basic particular to which both bodily predicates—“weighs sixty kilograms”—and consciousness predicates—“is in pain,” “believes”—are primitively ascribed.

His central argument is that psychological concepts must permit both self-ascription and other-ascription. I can apply “pain” non-observationally to myself only because the same concept has behavioural criteria for others. A private ego could not be reidentified within a shared conceptual scheme; conversely, a mere body does not exhaust first-person agency and experience. Thus mind–body dualism abstracts two predicate-types from the prior unity of the person.

The view explains embodiment, interpersonal recognition and the asymmetry between first- and third-person knowledge without reducing consciousness to behaviour.

**Evaluation:** Strawson establishes the conceptual primacy of persons over Cartesian minds, but conceptual indispensability does not by itself prove that persons are ontologically irreducible. Physicalism may accept person-language while seeking neural explanation. His argument is strongest against separately identifiable disembodied selves.

**Why this earns marks:** It presents the primitive-ascription argument, its anti-dualist force and its ontological limit.

### Q1(b). Explain Wittgenstein’s arguments against the possibility of private language.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Later Wittgenstein](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/later-wittgenstein.md) → private-language argument · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Directive: Explain · Syllabus unit: Later Wittgenstein—private language · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Later Wittgenstein](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Later-Wittgenstein.md) · **Slug:** `wittgenstein-private-language-impossibility`

**Demand decoding:** Define genuinely private language and show why ostensive definition, memory and rule-following cannot provide correctness conditions.

**Argument map:** private sign names an introspectively accessible sensation → diarist associates “S” with it → no independent criterion distinguishes correct recurrence from seeming correct → memory cannot certify itself → rules require a practice with standards → sensation-talk is public without denying private experience.

#### Model answer

A private language would contain words referring to experiences knowable only to one speaker and in principle uncheckable by anyone else. Wittgenstein’s diary example imagines marking “S” whenever a private sensation occurs.

The diarist cannot privately fix “S” by ostensive definition. To use it later, she must distinguish correct memory from merely seeming to remember. Yet any private test appeals to another impression whose correctness is equally unchecked. “Whatever seems right is right” abolishes the distinction between correct and incorrect use; therefore no rule is being followed. A rule requires stable criteria embedded in a practice.

The argument does not deny that pain is felt first-personally or that one may invent personal shorthand. It denies a language whose meanings are constituted solely by inaccessible private objects. Ordinary sensation-language gains sense through public learning, expressive behaviour and shared circumstances, even though sincere first-person avowals are not observational reports.

**Evaluation:** The argument decisively challenges private ostension, though critics dispute whether all normativity must be socially grounded.

**Why this earns marks:** It explains the diary, correctness criterion and public-practice conclusion without behaviourist distortion.

### Q1(c). Distinguish necessary from empirical propositions. How is a necessary proposition justified ? Explain.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Logical Positivism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/logical-positivism.md) → necessity and analyticity · **Secondary route:** [Kant](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/kant.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Directive: Distinguish and explain justification · Syllabus unit: Analytic philosophy—necessary and empirical propositions · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Logical Positivism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Logical-Positivism.md) · **Secondary classification:** [Kant](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Kant.md) · **Slug:** `necessary-empirical-propositions-justification`

**Demand decoding:** Contrast modal status and methods of warrant, then distinguish major accounts of necessity rather than equating it uncritically with verbal truth.

**Argument map:** empirical propositions depend on how the world is → their denials remain possible → necessary propositions cannot be false under the relevant interpretation → justification may be logical/semantic proof or synthetic-a-priori grounding → experience occasions but does not establish necessity.

#### Model answer

An **empirical proposition**—“copper conducts electricity”—is justified through observation and experiment. It is contingent: its denial is intelligible, and evidence may revise it. A **necessary proposition**—“no bachelor is married” or “2 + 3 = 5”—cannot be false while its terms, logical rules and domain remain fixed.

On the analytic account, necessity is justified by definitions, meanings and transformation rules. “Every bachelor is unmarried” follows by substituting the definition; a contradiction results from its denial. Logical and mathematical proofs derive conclusions from axioms through truth-preserving rules, not inductive enumeration. Experience may teach symbols or prompt discovery but does not confer universality.

Kant complicates this identification: geometrical and causal principles are synthetic *a priori*, justified as conditions of possible experience rather than mere definitions. Later modal semantics explains necessity as truth in all relevant possible worlds, though it still requires an account of our access to them.

**Evaluation:** Necessary propositions demand rational demonstration or transcendental grounding; calling them necessary cannot itself justify them. Quine further challenges any absolute analytic–synthetic boundary.

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes status, denial and warrant while acknowledging rival theories of necessity.

### Q1(d). Discuss how by refuting the different concepts of substances Aristotle establishes his own theory of substance.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Plato–Aristotle](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/plato-aristotle.md) → substance and hylomorphism · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Directive: Discuss reconstructively · Syllabus unit: Aristotle—substance, form and matter · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Plato–Aristotle](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Plato-Aristotle.md) · **Slug:** `aristotle-substance-rival-conceptions`

**Demand decoding:** Show how Aristotle tests candidates—universal, matter, composite and form—and derives his qualified priority of form in concrete substance.

**Argument map:** substance must be subject and “what-it-is” → Platonic universal is predicated of many and separated → bare matter is indeterminate → composite is primary in ordinary existence → form explains unity, essence and actuality → substance is concrete form–matter unity with explanatory priority of form.

#### Model answer

Aristotle asks which candidate best satisfies substance (*ousia*): independent subjecthood and explanatory “what-it-is.”

He rejects Platonic Forms as separately existing substances of sensible things. A universal is predicable of many, whereas substance is a “this”; separation also fails to explain change in particulars. Yet bare matter cannot be primary substance, because matter without determination is neither identifiable nor actual. The concrete composite—this man or horse—is primary in the *Categories*, since qualities are predicated of it and it is not predicated of a subject.

In the *Metaphysics*, however, form gains explanatory priority. Form makes matter one determinate thing, supplies essence, actuality and the principle by which generation is intelligible. The soul, for example, is the form of a living body, not an independently inhabiting thing. The composite is the existing primary substance; form is substance in the sense of essence and cause.

**Evaluation:** Hylomorphism avoids both separated universal and indeterminate substrate. Its difficulty is whether individual or universal form grounds this particular’s identity.

**Why this earns marks:** It derives Aristotle’s position by eliminating rival candidates rather than merely defining substance.

### Q1(e). What is an antinomy ? Describe the major antinomies discussed by Kant.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Kant](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/kant.md) → antinomies of pure reason · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Directive: Define and describe · Syllabus unit: Kant—transcendental dialectic · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Kant](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Kant.md) · **Slug:** `kant-four-antinomies`

**Demand decoding:** Define reason’s contradiction and accurately state all four thesis–antithesis pairs with Kant’s critical diagnosis.

**Argument map:** reason seeks unconditioned totality → treats world-as-whole as knowable object → equally plausible proofs yield contradictions → mathematical antinomies dissolve for appearances → dynamical antinomies allow standpoint distinction → transcendental idealism diagnoses illusion.

#### Model answer

An **antinomy** is a conflict in which pure reason can apparently prove both a thesis and its contradictory antithesis when it treats the world as a completed totality beyond possible experience.

Kant’s four cosmological antinomies are:

1. **World:** it has a beginning in time and limits in space / it is infinite in time and space.
2. **Composition:** every composite ultimately consists of simple parts / no simple part exists; division is indefinite.
3. **Freedom:** causality through freedom is required / everything occurs solely according to natural causation.
4. **Necessary being:** an absolutely necessary being belongs to or grounds the world / no absolutely necessary being exists.

The first two are mathematical antinomies. Both sides falsely treat appearances as a completed thing-in-itself; the empirical series is indefinitely extendable. The dynamical antinomies permit a qualified reconciliation: an event may be naturally caused as appearance yet intelligibly free, and a necessary ground may be thinkable though not theoretically knowable.

**Why this earns marks:** It gives the four exact conflicts and Kant’s transcendental-idealist resolution.

### Q2(a). Explain Plato’s ontological theory of Forms. Is ‘knowledge’ one of the Forms ? Give reasons.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Plato–Aristotle](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/plato-aristotle.md) → Forms and knowledge · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 12½ marks · about 200 words · Directive: Explain and reasoned determination · Syllabus unit: Plato—Forms and knowledge · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Plato–Aristotle](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Plato-Aristotle.md) · **Slug:** `plato-forms-is-knowledge-a-form`

**Demand decoding:** Explain the ontology, then distinguish the Form that is an object of knowledge from knowledge as a cognitive state.

**Argument map:** many particulars share one character → stable definition requires intelligible Form → Forms are eternal, non-spatial and explanatory → particulars participate imperfectly → knowledge relates knower to Forms → relation or state is not thereby another separate Form → “knowledge itself” remains textually qualified.

#### Model answer

Plato’s Forms are eternal, intelligible, non-spatial realities such as Beauty itself, Equality itself and Justice itself. Sensible particulars are many, changing and compresent with opposites; a beautiful object may become ugly. A Form is one, stable and fully what it is. Particulars “participate in” or imitate it, while the Form supplies their character and the standard for definition. The divided line ranks intelligible Forms above sensible images and things; the Form of the Good finally grounds knowability and being.

Is knowledge itself a Form? Plato sometimes speaks of “knowledge itself,” and the *Parmenides* considers a Form of Knowledge. If every genuine one-over-many predicate requires a Form, distinct acts or kinds of knowledge may participate in Knowledge itself.

However, *epistēmē* primarily names a cognitive power or state whose objects are Forms, not an additional object required beside them. In the *Republic*, knowledge and opinion are faculties distinguished by their objects: what fully is versus what both is and is-not. Reifying the relation risks regress—knowledge of the Form of Knowledge would require further knowledge—and the *Parmenides* warns that a separate divine Knowledge could become disconnected from human knowing.

**Evaluation:** Plato’s ontology permits a Form corresponding to the essence of knowledge, but knowledge as the knower’s rational grasp is not simply one more Form. The safer distinction is between **Knowledge itself**, if admitted as an intelligible essence, and actual knowledge as a relational achievement directed toward Forms.

**Why this earns marks:** It explains participation and stability, then gives reasons on both sides before a precise distinction.

### Q2(b). State Kant’s view of causality. How far is Kant able to answer Hume’s objection that causal relation lacks logical necessity ?
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Kant](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/kant.md) → causality and Hume · **Secondary route:** [Empiricism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/empiricism.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 12½ marks · about 200 words · Directive: State and assess · Syllabus unit: Kant—causality; response to Hume · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Kant](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Kant.md) · **Secondary classification:** [Empiricism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Empiricism.md) · **Slug:** `kant-causality-answer-hume`

**Demand decoding:** Explain causality as an a priori category and Second Analogy, then judge whether transcendental necessity answers Hume’s demand for logical necessity.

**Argument map:** Hume finds only succession and habit → objective event requires irreversible order → category of cause supplies rule-governed succession → transcendental deduction validates it for possible experience → necessity is synthetic/transcendental, not analytic → no knowledge of things-in-themselves or particular laws follows.

#### Model answer

Hume argued that experience shows constant conjunction, never a necessary tie; expectation arises from custom. Kant agrees that causal necessity is neither perceived nor analytically extracted from the concept of an event.

For Kant, causality is a pure category of understanding. In the **Second Analogy of Experience**, merely subjective perceptions may occur in any order—I can scan a house from roof to door or reverse. An event, such as a ship moving downstream, is objectively successive only when the later state follows the earlier according to a rule. The principle “every alteration has a cause” is therefore synthetic *a priori*: it is a condition for experiencing an objective temporal sequence at all. The transcendental deduction connects such categories to the unity of apperception and restricts them to appearances.

Kant thus answers Hume by relocating necessity. It is not logical entailment between particular contents—“sunlight” does not analytically contain “warming the stone.” Nor does the category reveal which particular cause operates; empirical inquiry supplies laws. Its necessity is **transcendental**: any knowable event must stand within rule-governed causal order.

**Evaluation:** Kant successfully explains why objective experience presupposes causality and why habit alone seems insufficient. Hume may reply that Kant proves only the necessity of ordering experience, not mind-independent causal power. Kant accepts this limit: causality is valid for phenomena, not things-in-themselves.

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes logical, empirical and transcendental necessity and gives a graded verdict.

### Q2(c). Distinguish between atomic and general propositions. Show how they are justified true.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Moore–Russell–Early Wittgenstein](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/moore-russell-early-wittgenstein.md) → logical atomism and truth · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 12½ marks · about 200 words · Directive: Distinguish and demonstrate justification · Syllabus unit: Russell and early Wittgenstein—logical atomism · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Moore–Russell–Early Wittgenstein](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Moore-Russell-EarlyWittgenstein.md) · **Slug:** `atomic-general-propositions-truth`

**Demand decoding:** Explain logical form, truthmakers and quantification; avoid suggesting universal propositions are conclusively verified by finite enumeration.

**Argument map:** atomic proposition predicates relation of named particulars → true when corresponding atomic fact obtains → general proposition quantifies over a domain → universal true iff every substitution instance is true; existential iff at least one is → acquaintance/analysis grounds atomic claims → universals face epistemic limits.

#### Model answer

In logical atomism, an **atomic proposition** contains no proposition as a proper logical part. “a is red” or “a precedes b” combines names with a predicate or relation. It is true when the corresponding atomic fact—the particular possessing the quality or standing in the relation—obtains; otherwise it is false. Analysis and, for Russell, acquaintance with particulars and universals connect symbols to constituents.

A **general proposition** contains a quantifier rather than merely naming one fact. “All humans are mortal” has the form \((x)(Hx \rightarrow Mx)\); “some human is wise” has the form \((\exists x)(Hx \land Wx)\). A universal proposition is true if every value in the relevant domain makes its propositional function true. An existential proposition is true if at least one value does.

Their truth-conditions differ from their epistemic justification. A positive instance establishes an existential proposition, but finite observations cannot conclusively justify an unrestricted universal over unobserved cases. Russell sometimes treated general facts as truthmakers; Wittgenstein sought to construe generality through operations over elementary propositions, not a mysterious universal object.

**Evaluation:** Correspondence clearly explains atomic truth and formal semantics explains quantified truth. Our warrant for general empirical claims remains inductive and defeasible, whereas logical generalisations may be proven from rules and definitions.

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes syntax, truth-conditions and epistemic warrant.

### Q2(d). Write a short critical essay on Spinoza’s conception of freedom of the individual.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Rationalism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/rationalism.md) → Spinoza on freedom and necessity · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 12½ marks · about 200 words · Directive: Critical essay · Syllabus unit: Spinoza—substance, determinism and freedom · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Rationalism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Rationalism.md) · **Slug:** `spinoza-individual-freedom-critical`

**Demand decoding:** Explain freedom within strict necessity, connect adequate ideas to active affects, and assess whether self-determination survives substance monism.

**Argument map:** only God/Nature exists from its own necessity → finite modes are causally determined → ordinary free will is ignorance of causes → adequate understanding turns passive affects into action → freedom is rational self-determination, not uncaused choice → individuality and responsibility objections remain.

#### Model answer

Spinoza rejects libertarian free will. Only God or Nature (*Deus sive Natura*) is absolutely free because it exists and acts from the necessity of its own nature. Human beings are finite modes within the same causal order. We believe ourselves uncaused because we know our desires but not their determining causes.

Nevertheless, freedom admits degrees. An individual is **passive** when external causes and inadequate ideas determine the affects. One becomes **active** when adequate ideas explain oneself and one’s place in Nature. Reason organises the striving to persist (*conatus*), moderates destructive passions and enables cooperation. The highest freedom is the intellectual love of God: joyful understanding of things under the aspect of eternity. Freedom is therefore not exemption from necessity but action flowing from one’s understood nature.

This compatibilism insightfully links autonomy to self-knowledge rather than arbitrary choice. It anticipates the idea that compulsions diminish freedom even when actions are voluntary.

Yet if every idea and desire follows from the one substance with geometrical necessity, the individual seems only a local conduit of causes. Adequate understanding itself is determined, and alternatives, desert and moral responsibility become difficult to secure. Spinoza can answer that responsibility should be reformative and causal, not retributive.

**Evaluation:** He preserves rational autonomy but not the power to choose otherwise under identical conditions.

**Why this earns marks:** It relates metaphysical necessity, affects, adequate knowledge and a balanced freedom verdict.

### Q3(a). Explain Descartes’ method of doubt. Can this method be used to justify his belief in the existence of God ? Argue your case.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Rationalism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/rationalism.md) → Cartesian doubt and God · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 12½ marks · about 200 words · Directive: Explain and argue · Syllabus unit: Descartes—method, cogito and God · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Rationalism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Rationalism.md) · **Slug:** `descartes-doubt-justify-god`

**Demand decoding:** Present the stages and methodological purpose of doubt, then determine whether it independently validates the proofs of God.

**Argument map:** senses sometimes deceive → dream doubt destabilises external world → evil genius threatens mathematics → cogito survives → clear-and-distinct rule needs guarantee → causal/ontological proofs infer God → proofs rely on clarity before God guarantees clarity → Cartesian Circle.

#### Model answer

Descartes’ doubt is methodological, hyperbolic and temporary. He withholds assent from any belief admitting a ground of doubt: sensory beliefs because senses sometimes deceive; ordinary experience through the dream argument; and even mathematics through the hypothesis of an omnipotent deceiver. Doubt reaches an indubitable point: while deceived, “I think, therefore I am.” The thinking self and the contents of its ideas are immediately certain.

Can this route justify God? Descartes argues that the idea of an infinite perfect being cannot derive from a finite self; its objective reality requires an equally eminent cause. He also argues ontologically that necessary existence belongs to a supremely perfect being. A non-deceiving God then guarantees clear and distinct perceptions and restores knowledge of world and mathematics.

The method helps by purifying premises: the cogito and the presented idea of infinity survive doubt. Yet the justification is circular. Descartes trusts clear and distinct reasoning to prove God, while God is invoked to guarantee clear and distinct reasoning—the **Cartesian Circle**. His reply distinguishes momentary self-evidence from remembered demonstrations, but the proofs contain extended inferences already vulnerable to deception.

**Evaluation:** Doubt establishes the cogito more securely than God. It provides the setting and premises for the proofs, but cannot alone validate them without contested causal and ontological principles.

**Why this earns marks:** It traces every stage and argues a definite position through the Cartesian Circle.

### Q3(b). Comment : ‘Movement is contradiction itself.’ Examine, in this context, Hegel’s dialectical method.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Hegel](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/hegel.md) → contradiction and dialectic · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 12½ marks · about 200 words · Directive: Comment and examine · Syllabus unit: Hegel—dialectical method · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Hegel](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Hegel.md) · **Slug:** `hegel-movement-contradiction-dialectic`

**Demand decoding:** Interpret contradiction dynamically, explain determinate negation and sublation, and assess whether dialectic violates formal logic.

**Argument map:** static understanding isolates fixed predicates → movement requires being-here and not-being-here across a process → finite determination points beyond itself → contradiction drives determinate negation → sublation preserves and cancels → dialectic reconstructs conceptual development rather than asserting flat inconsistency.

#### Model answer

“Movement is contradiction itself” expresses Hegel’s claim that actuality is not exhausted by fixed, externally related predicates. A moving body is at a place and, through the same process, is ceasing to be there; life sustains identity only through continual negation and replacement. Contradiction is thus the inner instability of a finite determination, not merely our verbal error.

Hegel’s dialectic begins when the understanding fixes a category. Reflection discloses that the category depends upon or passes into its opposite. **Determinate negation** does not erase it abstractly; **sublation** (*Aufhebung*) cancels its one-sidedness, preserves its truth and raises both into a richer unity. In the *Logic*, pure Being, devoid of determination, is indistinguishable from Nothing; their truth is Becoming. Historical and social forms similarly generate tensions that disclose more adequate forms.

The familiar thesis–antithesis–synthesis formula is only a rough mnemonic, not Hegel’s universal terminology. Dialectic is immanent: the transition should arise from the concept’s own insufficiency, not an externally imposed triad.

**Evaluation:** Hegel powerfully explains process, relational identity and self-correcting concepts. Critics argue that movement involves different times, so no strict contradiction occurs, and that logical contradiction cannot exist in reality. A charitable reading treats contradiction as structured incompatibility within developing wholes, preserving formal non-contradiction for fixed propositions.

**Why this earns marks:** It connects movement to determinate negation, sublation and the logic objection.

### Q3(c). Examine John Locke’s theory of substance.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Empiricism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/empiricism.md) → Locke on substance · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 12½ marks · about 200 words · Directive: Examine · Syllabus unit: Locke—ideas and substance · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Empiricism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Empiricism.md) · **Slug:** `locke-substance-critical-examination`

**Demand decoding:** Explain substratum, real and nominal essence, material/spiritual substance, and expose the tension with empiricism.

**Argument map:** simple ideas occur together → mind supposes a bearer → substratum supports qualities but is unknown → nominal essence groups observable qualities → real essence is inner constitution → substance secures identity and causal explanation → no impression of support creates empiricist difficulty.

#### Model answer

Locke begins with simple ideas received through sensation and reflection. Because qualities such as colour, shape and solidity regularly occur together and cannot be imagined “subsisting by themselves,” the mind supposes an underlying support: substance in general, “something, I know not what.”

He distinguishes **particular substances**—gold, horse, person—from this obscure substratum. Their **nominal essence** is the abstract complex of observable qualities by which we classify them; their **real essence** is the unknown internal constitution producing those qualities. In bodies Locke conjectures corpuscular structure. Spiritual substance is likewise posited as the support of thinking, willing and perceiving; empiricism does not, he argues, justify denying it merely because its substratum is obscure.

The theory realistically acknowledges that observable properties have causal grounds and that nominal classifications may fail to track nature exactly. It also prevents substance from being reduced to one presently perceived quality.

However, Berkeley objects that an unknowable material support is meaningless: ideas require no inert bearer. Hume finds no impression of substance, only bundles of perceptions. Locke also appears inconsistent: if all meaningful ideas arise from experience, how is the idea of bare support acquired?

**Evaluation:** Locke’s substance is best read as a modest explanatory posit, not a positive idea. It marks the limits of empiricism but does not explain how support relates to qualities.

**Why this earns marks:** It integrates substratum, essences and the central empiricist objection.

### Q3(d). Examine Sartre’s distinction between Being-for-itself and Being-in-itself.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Existentialism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/existentialism.md) → Sartrean ontology · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 12½ marks · about 200 words · Directive: Examine · Syllabus unit: Sartre—being-in-itself and being-for-itself · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Existentialism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Existentialism.md) · **Slug:** `sartre-for-itself-in-itself`

**Demand decoding:** State both modes, explain nothingness, intentionality, freedom and bad faith, then examine the instability of their relation.

**Argument map:** in-itself is self-identical fullness → consciousness is always of something and not that thing → negation opens nothingness → for-itself surpasses facticity toward possibilities → freedom and lack define human existence → desire to become in-itself-for-itself is contradictory.

#### Model answer

For Sartre, **Being-in-itself** (*être-en-soi*) is the mode of things. It is massive, self-identical and simply what it is, without awareness, possibility or internal negation. A stone does not distance itself from its condition.

**Being-for-itself** (*être-pour-soi*) is consciousness. Because consciousness is intentional—always consciousness *of* something—it is not identical with its object or with any fixed description of itself. It introduces nothingness: I can negate the café’s fullness by expecting Pierre and apprehending his absence. The for-itself is what it is not yet and is not merely what it factually is. This gap grounds freedom, projects and temporality.

Human existence therefore combines **facticity**—body, past and situation—with **transcendence** toward possibilities. Bad faith arises when one reduces oneself to an in-itself, as the waiter who treats his role as an essence, or denies facticity by imagining pure freedom.

The distinction explains agency and the irreducibility of first-person consciousness. Yet an absolute ontological gulf makes embodied consciousness and causal interaction difficult to explain. Sartre’s claim that freedom is unavoidable can also understate social and psychological constraint.

**Evaluation:** The for-itself is not a second substance but the nihilation of in-itself. Its impossible desire to become an in-itself-for-itself—complete yet conscious—explains persistent lack, though the duality remains philosophically unstable.

**Why this earns marks:** It connects ontology to negation, freedom, facticity, bad faith and critique.

### Q4(c). Give a critical account of Leibnitz’s principle of the identity of indiscernibles.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Rationalism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/rationalism.md) → Leibniz on identity · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 12½ marks · about 200 words · Directive: Critical account · Syllabus unit: Leibniz—identity of indiscernibles · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Rationalism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/western/Rationalism.md) · **Slug:** `leibniz-identity-indiscernibles`

**Demand decoding:** State the principle, distinguish it from indiscernibility of identicals, explain its rationalist grounds and test counterexamples.

**Argument map:** no two substances can share every property → otherwise God lacks sufficient reason to place/name them differently → complete concept individuates each monad → symmetry cases challenge qualitative version → relational/spatiotemporal properties may differentiate → haecceity dispute remains.

#### Model answer

Leibniz’s **identity of indiscernibles** says that no two distinct things possess all the same properties: if for every property \(F\), \(Fx\) iff \(Fy\), then \(x=y\). It must be distinguished from the uncontroversial **indiscernibility of identicals**, according to which one thing cannot differ from itself.

The principle follows from sufficient reason. If two substances were qualitatively identical, God would have no reason to place or treat one differently; their supposed numerical difference would explain nothing. Each monad has a complete concept containing its entire perspective and history, so no two monads are perfectly alike. The principle supports anti-atomism about qualitatively identical ultimate particles and treats individuality as intelligible.

Critics construct symmetrical worlds: Max Black imagines two exactly similar spheres in an otherwise empty universe. They appear numerically distinct yet share every intrinsic qualitative property. Leibniz can include relational properties—being at a certain distance from *that* sphere—or perspectival differences, but in perfect symmetry these may be structurally alike. Quantum particles also pressure classical individuality, though quantum states are not straightforward Leibnizian substances.

**Evaluation:** The principle is plausible if all properties include relational or identity-involving properties, but then it risks triviality. Restricted to purely qualitative properties, counterexamples seem conceivable. It expresses Leibniz’s demand that numerical difference have a rational ground, not an uncontested law of logic.

**Why this earns marks:** It states both identity principles, gives Leibniz’s basis and tests the strongest symmetry objection.

#### Model answer

Hume applies his copy principle: every legitimate idea must derive from an impression. When he introspects, he encounters heat, pleasure, pain, thought or desire, never an impression of a simple, enduring self distinct from them. The mind is therefore “a bundle or collection of different perceptions” in perpetual flux, not a Cartesian substance.

Personal identity is a fiction produced when imagination mistakes connected succession for strict sameness. Hume acknowledged in the *Treatise* Appendix that he could not reconcile the distinct existence of perceptions with their real connection. Kant replies that the transcendental unity of apperception is a condition for experiences being mine, though not a perceived substance.

**Evaluation:** Hume undermines the self as observable substance but does not fully explain experiential unity.

**Why this earns marks:** It reconstructs bundle, identity-fiction and Hume’s own unresolved difficulty.

### Q5(a). Analyse the relation between the theory of saptabhanginaya and anekantavada.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Jainism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/jainism.md) → non-one-sidedness and sevenfold predication · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 10 marks · about 150 words · Directive: Analyse relation · Syllabus unit: Jainism—*anekāntavāda*, *syādvāda* and sevenfold predication · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Jainism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Jainism.md) · **Slug:** `jaina-saptabhangi-anekantavada-relation`

**Demand decoding:** Distinguish ontological plural-sidedness from its conditional logical-linguistic expression and list the seven modes accurately.

**Argument map:** real objects possess innumerable aspects → finite standpoints grasp partial truth → unconditional assertion becomes one-sided → “in some respect” qualifies predicates → seven combinations articulate affirmation, negation and inexpressibility → logic expresses ontology without relativism.

#### Model answer

Non-one-sidedness (*anekāntavāda*) is the Jain ontological thesis that reality has innumerable aspects and modes. A jar, for example, exists as present clay-jar from one standpoint but does not exist as cloth, at another time or in another place. No finite judgement exhausts the object.

Sevenfold predication (*saptabhaṅgī-naya*) is the logical-linguistic method expressing this ontology through conditional assertion (*syāt*, “in some respect”):

1. it is;
2. it is not;
3. it is and is not;
4. it is inexpressible;
5. it is and is inexpressible;
6. it is not and is inexpressible;
7. it is, is not and is inexpressible.

The predicates are not asserted in the same respect, time and relation; hence no formal contradiction follows. “Inexpressible” marks the inability to state successive, differently qualified aspects simultaneously.

Thus *anekāntavāda* supplies the many-sided structure of reality, standpoint theory (*nayavāda*) supplies partial perspectives, and sevenfold predication disciplines speech about them.

**Evaluation:** The relation supports intellectual non-absolutism, not sceptical relativism; critics question whether the doctrine itself is asserted absolutely.

**Why this earns marks:** It separates ontology, standpoint and predication while giving the complete sevenfold scheme.

#### Model answer

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes general impermanence from strict momentariness and supplies the causal derivation.

**Classification route:** [Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Nyaya-Vaisesika.md) · **Secondary classification:** [Mīmāṃsā](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Mimamsa.md) · **Slug:** `paratah-pramanyavada-validity`

#### Model answer

Nyāya replies that ordinary action may proceed from prima facie belief, while reflective knowledge of validity depends on later confirmation; not every verifier needs a further verifier in practice.

**Why this earns marks:** It gives correspondence, successful activity, regress objection and the intrinsic-validity contrast.

### Q6(b). What is samavaya ? What are the grounds for accepting samavaya as a distinct padartha ? Discuss.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/nyaya-vaisesika.md) → inherence · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Classification route:** [Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Nyaya-Vaisesika.md) · **Slug:** `samavaya-distinct-category`

**Demand decoding:** Define inseparable relation, identify its relata and explain why conjunction, identity or infinite relational regress are inadequate.

**Argument map:** qualities/actions/universals/parts require bearers → they are inseparable yet distinguishable → conjunction applies only to separable substances → identity erases predication and plurality → inherence explains whole–part and property–substrate unity → one eternal relation avoids regress, though critics challenge reification.

#### Model answer

Inherence (*samavāya*) is the Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika relation of inseparable connection between entities that cannot exist independently while remaining distinct. It relates:

- parts to a whole;
- quality and action to substance;
- universal to its instances;
- ultimate particularity to eternal substance;
- according to classical accounts, effect-substance to material causes.

It is accepted as a distinct category (*padārtha*) because conjunction (*saṃyoga*) cannot explain these cases. Conjoined substances, such as a book and table, can separate and exist independently; colour cannot detach from the rose as another substance. Nor can relation be reduced to identity: the cloth is not identical with each thread, and redness is not identical with every red object. Yet predication—“the rose is red”—requires a real tie between distinguishable terms.

Vaiśeṣika treats inherence as one, eternal and imperceptible, known through its indispensable explanatory role. If another relation connected inherence to its relata, regress would follow; inherence is therefore self-relating in its occurrence.

Buddhists deny wholes and universals as independent realities, removing the need for *samavāya*. Mīmāṃsā and later critics ask whether declaring a unique relation merely names, rather than explains, inseparability.

**Evaluation:** Inherence coherently unifies a realist category-system and distinguishes intimate dependence from contact. Its necessity depends on accepting wholes, universals and qualities as ontologically distinct entities.

**Why this earns marks:** It defines the relation, covers its major loci and tests the grounds against nominalist critique.

### Q6(c). Evaluate the relation, if any, between purusa and prakrti.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Sāṃkhya](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/samkhya.md) → consciousness–nature relation · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 12½ marks · about 200 words · Directive: Evaluate relation · Syllabus unit: Sāṃkhya—*puruṣa*, *prakṛti* and evolution · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Sāṃkhya](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Samkhya.md) · **Slug:** `samkhya-purusha-prakriti-relation`

**Demand decoding:** Explain ontological independence and functional conjunction, then evaluate interaction, experience and liberation problems.

**Argument map:** consciousness is inactive witness → nature is unconscious productive cause → neither transforms into nor inheres in the other → proximity produces apparent sentience and ownership → conjunction serves experience and liberation → analogies explain cooperation → radical difference makes causal connection obscure.

#### Model answer

Sāṃkhya posits two irreducible realities. Consciousness (*puruṣa*) is many, eternal, inactive, partless and beyond the three qualities. Primordial nature (*prakṛti*) is one, unconscious and constituted by luminosity, activity and inertia (*sattva, rajas, tamas*). Their relation is neither identity, transformation nor ordinary causal interaction.

Evolution begins through their conjunction (*saṃyoga*) or proximity. Nature becomes manifest for two purposes: experience (*bhoga*) and liberation (*apavarga*) of consciousness. Intellect reflects consciousness and appears sentient; consciousness, through non-discrimination, appears to appropriate intellect’s agency and pleasure-pain. Sāṃkhya illustrates cooperation through the blind and lame: active but unconscious nature carries seeing but inactive consciousness. The dancer performs until seen, then withdraws.

This relation explains why an unconscious world becomes experience without making consciousness mutable. Discriminative knowledge ends identification, though nature may continue for other consciousnesses.

The account faces a serious problem. If consciousness is wholly inactive and nature unconscious, what brings proximity about and whose purpose directs evolution? Mere presence seems causally effective despite the denial of interaction. If the two are utterly different, reflection and appropriation are metaphors rather than explanations.

**Evaluation:** The relation is functional and epistemic, not substantial: nature presents, consciousness witnesses. It preserves dualism but leaves the bridge between experience’s two poles under-explained.

**Why this earns marks:** It explains conjunction, purpose, analogies, bondage and the central interaction objection.

### Q6(d). How can Isvara (God) be distinguished from Brahman (Absolute) ? Which of the two concepts are philosophically better ?
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Vedānta](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/vedanta.md) → Īśvara and Brahman · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 12½ marks · about 200 words · Directive: Distinguish and evaluate · Syllabus unit: Vedānta—notions of God and Absolute · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Vedānta](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Vedanta.md) · **Slug:** `isvara-brahman-philosophical-comparison`

**Demand decoding:** Compare personal cosmic lord and ultimate absolute across attributes, causality, devotion and metaphysical ultimacy; avoid an unqualified winner.

**Argument map:** Īśvara is personal, omniscient creator and moral governor → Brahman is ultimate being-consciousness, beyond limiting predicates → Advaita ranks qualified God empirically and attributeless absolute ultimately → theism integrates agency and devotion → absolute avoids anthropomorphism and dependency → adequacy depends on explanatory demand.

#### Model answer

Īśvara is God conceived as personal, omniscient, omnipotent, purposive creator, sustainer and moral governor. Brahman is the Absolute: the ultimate ground whose nature is existence-consciousness-infinity and which is not one being among others.

In Advaita, Brahman associated with ignorance or cosmic appearance (*māyā*) is Īśvara, the intelligent and material cause of the empirical world. At the absolute level, attributeless Brahman (*nirguṇa Brahman*) is non-dual and beyond relational predicates. In Viśiṣṭādvaita and Dvaita, however, the contrast narrows: the supreme personal Lord is Brahman, endowed with unlimited auspicious attributes.

Īśvara is philosophically powerful for explaining purposive order, worship, grace, moral responsibility and a reciprocal religious relation. Yet personality, intention and action appear to involve limitation, change and relation; the problem of evil challenges perfect governance.

Brahman as Absolute explains the unity and non-dependence of reality while avoiding crude anthropomorphism. But an ineffable attributeless ground risks explanatory emptiness: how does it generate plurality, value or devotion?

**Evaluation:** Neither is simply “better.” The Absolute is stronger as a concept of metaphysical ultimacy; Īśvara is richer for religious, moral and cosmological explanation. A qualified non-dual account may integrate both, provided it explains rather than merely labels their relation.

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes levels and functions, compares strengths and reaches a reasoned conditional verdict.

### Q7(a). Analyse the Nyaya concept of vyapti and examine its relation to tarka.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/nyaya-vaisesika.md) → invariable concomitance and hypothetical reasoning · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 12½ marks · about 200 words · Directive: Analyse and examine relation · Syllabus unit: Nyāya—inference, *vyāpti* and *tarka* · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Nyaya-Vaisesika.md) · **Slug:** `nyaya-vyapti-tarka-relation`

**Demand decoding:** Define unconditional pervasion, explain its ascertainment, and show that hypothetical reasoning supports but does not independently produce knowledge.

**Argument map:** inference requires universal relation between reason and probandum → positive and negative instances establish regularity → removal of conditions secures unconditionality → doubt remains about hidden exception → hypothetical acceptance of contrary yields absurd consequence → *tarka* removes doubt and assists induction but is not a *pramāṇa*.

#### Model answer

Invariable concomitance (*vyāpti*) is the unconditional relation between the inferential reason (*hetu*) and what is to be proved (*sādhya*): wherever smoke exists, fire exists. It grounds inference (*anumāna*); observed smoke on the hill warrants fire only because smoke is pervaded by fire.

Nyāya establishes *vyāpti* through repeated observation of agreement in presence (*anvaya*)—smoky kitchens are fiery—and agreement in absence (*vyatireka*)—where fire is absent, smoke is absent—together with exclusion of a limiting condition (*upādhi*). Mere enumeration is insufficient because an unobserved exception may remain.

Hypothetical reasoning (*tarka*) assists at this point. Suppose smoke could occur without fire. Smoke would then lack the causal conditions known to produce it, generating an unacceptable consequence. Or if fire did not cause burning, ordinary causal practice would become unintelligible. By showing that the denial of a proposed pervasion entails absurdity, *tarka* removes doubt and strengthens the universal relation.

However, *tarka* is not an independent means of valid knowledge (*pramāṇa*). It does not directly establish its conclusion; it supports perception, testimony and inductive generalisation by defeating a rival supposition.

**Evaluation:** Their relation is complementary: *vyāpti* is inference’s objective ground, while *tarka* is a negative, doubt-removing method for securing it. Critics such as Cārvāka question whether either excludes every future exception.

**Why this earns marks:** It explains ascertainment, *upādhi*, hypothetical reasoning and its non-*pramāṇa* status.

### Q7(b). Evaluate Prabhakara Mimamsaka’s arguments for accepting sruti as pramana.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Mīmāṃsā](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/mimamsa.md) → Vedic testimony and intrinsic validity · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 12½ marks · about 200 words · Directive: Evaluate arguments · Syllabus unit: Mīmāṃsā—Veda and verbal testimony · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Mīmāṃsā](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Mimamsa.md) · **Slug:** `prabhakara-sruti-pramana`

**Demand decoding:** Explain why Vedic revelation is an autonomous source for duty, connect authorlessness and sentence meaning, then assess circularity and authority.

**Argument map:** duty concerns supersensible relation between act and result → perception/inference cannot disclose it → Vedic injunction supplies novel action-guiding cognition → Veda is authorless and therefore free from speaker defects → cognition is intrinsically valid unless defeated → objections challenge authorlessness, interpretation and moral sufficiency.

#### Model answer

Prābhākara Mīmāṃsā accepts Vedic revelation (*śruti*) as an autonomous means of valid knowledge (*pramāṇa*), especially concerning duty (*dharma*). Perception concerns present sensible objects; inference depends on previously known concomitance. Neither can reveal the supersensible obligatory relation between a sacrifice and its remote fruit. Vedic injunction—“one desiring heaven should sacrifice”—therefore supplies novel, action-guiding knowledge unavailable elsewhere.

Its authority does not rest on a divine or human author. The Veda is authorless (*apauruṣeya*) and beginningless; hence defects such as ignorance, deceit and carelessness that may corrupt ordinary testimony do not arise. Words and their significative relations are treated as enduring, and sentence meaning is grasped through connected words already oriented toward a unified action (*anvitābhidhāna*). Under intrinsic validity (*svataḥ-prāmāṇya*), cognition is accepted as true unless subsequently defeated.

The argument rightly identifies a domain not reducible to sensory description and avoids dependence on proving an infallible speaker.

However, authorlessness is difficult to establish historically, absence of an author does not itself guarantee truth, and incompatible interpretations require human hermeneutics. Further, moving from scriptural command to genuine moral obligation may appear circular to outsiders.

**Evaluation:** *Śruti* functions coherently as *pramāṇa* within Mīmāṃsā’s ritual-ethical framework. Its universal authority is not established unless the Veda’s authorlessness and independent normativity are separately defended.

**Why this earns marks:** It reconstructs novelty, authorlessness, intrinsic validity and the strongest external objections.

### Q7(c). Examine the Nyaya-Vaisesika arguments for the existence of jivatma (soul).
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/nyaya-vaisesika.md) → inference to the self · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 12½ marks · about 200 words · Directive: Examine arguments · Syllabus unit: Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika—self and liberation · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Nyaya-Vaisesika.md) · **Slug:** `nyaya-vaisesika-jivatma-arguments`

**Demand decoding:** Reconstruct the self as inferred substance underlying mental qualities, memory and coordinated agency, then confront Buddhist and materialist objections.

**Argument map:** cognition/desire/pleasure are qualities → qualities require substance → body, senses and mind cannot unify them → recognition and memory require enduring owner → coordinated action implies one agent → therefore enduring individual self → opponent explains continuity causally without substance.

#### Model answer

Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika regards the individual soul (*jīvātman*) as an eternal, all-pervasive substance whose adventitious qualities include cognition, pleasure, pain, desire, aversion, effort, merit and demerit. It is not directly perceived in ordinary experience but inferred.

First, qualities require a substratum. Cognitions and desires cannot inhere in the body, because the body may remain while consciousness is absent; nor in separate senses, since one subject coordinates sight and touch. The internal organ (*manas*) is atomic and instrumental, not the conscious owner.

Second, recognition—“this is the same object I saw”—and memory—“I experienced that”—connect past and present cognitions. A persisting subject must own both. Third, desire prompted by a remembered taste and bodily effort to obtain it show cross-modal coordination. Fourth, moral responsibility and karmic continuity require an agent enduring beyond bodily change.

Buddhist momentarists reply that a causal series of cognitions can explain memory without a substance; the later cognition inherits traces from the earlier. Cārvāka identifies consciousness with the living body. Nyāya counters that causal continuity does not explain first-person ownership and unified agency.

**Evaluation:** The arguments strongly support diachronic and synchronic unity of consciousness, but the inference to an eternal, all-pervasive substance exceeds what memory alone proves. A continuing embodied subject may explain the data more economically.

**Why this earns marks:** It presents several distinct inferences and evaluates the exact reach of their conclusion.

### Q7(d). Distinguish between Svarupa laksana and Tatastha laksana of Brahman after Sankara.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Vedānta](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/vedanta.md) → intrinsic and accidental indication of Brahman · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 12½ marks · about 200 words · Directive: Distinguish · Syllabus unit: Advaita Vedānta—nature of Brahman · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Classification route:** [Vedānta](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Vedanta.md) · **Slug:** `shankara-svarupa-tatastha-lakshana`

**Demand decoding:** Define both modes of indication, give scriptural examples, connect them to attributeless and qualified Brahman, and explain pedagogical hierarchy.

**Argument map:** Brahman cannot be defined as an object → intrinsic indication reveals inseparable nature → existence-consciousness-infinity is not a set of qualities → extrinsic indication points through world-causation → creatorhood applies from empirical standpoint → seeker moves from known effect to ground, then negates limiting predicates.

#### Model answer

For Śaṅkara, Brahman cannot be defined like an ordinary object because it is non-dual consciousness and the presupposition of all knowing. Scripture therefore employs two modes of indication (*lakṣaṇa*).

**Intrinsic indication (*svarūpa-lakṣaṇa*)** expresses Brahman’s inseparable nature. “Reality, knowledge, infinity is Brahman” (*satyam jñānam anantam brahma*) does not attach three qualifying properties to a substance. The terms mutually exclude non-being, insentience and limitation, indicating self-revealing existence-consciousness without internal difference. Existence-consciousness-bliss (*sat-cit-ānanda*) functions similarly.

**Extrinsic indication (*taṭastha-lakṣaṇa*)** identifies something through a contingent relation: “that from which the origin, sustenance and dissolution of the world proceed.” Creatorhood, omniscience and lordship identify Brahman through the empirical world. They belong to qualified Brahman or Īśvara associated with cosmic appearance (*māyā*), not to Brahman’s absolute nature.

The extrinsic indication is pedagogically indispensable: beginning from the experienced world, it directs inquiry toward its ground. Intrinsic indication then removes the relational limitations of the provisional account through “not this, not this.”

**Evaluation:** The distinction preserves both scriptural cosmology and non-duality. A critic may ask how an entirely relationless Brahman can genuinely ground the relational world; calling causality provisional risks leaving the appearance unexplained.

**Why this earns marks:** It supplies exact examples, logical function and the empirical-to-absolute progression.

### Q8(a). Comment : ‘Accepting sunyavada makes one indifferent to the pursuit of dharma.’ Examine, in this context, Nagarjuna’s arguments for sunyavada.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Buddhism](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/buddhism.md) → Madhyamaka emptiness and ethics · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 12½ marks · about 200 words · Directive: Comment and examine arguments · Syllabus unit: Buddhism—Nāgārjuna and emptiness · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Buddhism](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Buddhism.md) · **Slug:** `nagarjuna-sunyavada-dharma`

**Demand decoding:** Reconstruct emptiness from dependent origination and tetralemma, then answer the nihilism-and-moral-indifference charge through two truths.

**Argument map:** intrinsic nature would be independent and immutable → conditioned things arise through causes, parts and designation → therefore all are empty → fourfold causal analysis defeats self-existing production → conventional action and dharma remain functional → emptiness enables change and liberation rather than abolishing value.

#### Model answer

The objection assumes that emptiness (*śūnyatā*) means non-existence, so moral action, karma and liberation become pointless. Nāgārjuna rejects this interpretation. Emptiness is absence of intrinsic, independent nature (*svabhāva*), not annihilation.

His central argument identifies emptiness with dependent origination (*pratītyasamutpāda*). Whatever depends on causes, parts, relations and conceptual designation cannot exist from its own side. If things possessed immutable *svabhāva*, causal production and transformation would be impossible. The four-cornered analysis denies that an intrinsically existing effect arises from itself, from an wholly other cause, from both or without cause. The analysis applies even to cause, self, motion and nirvāṇa; emptiness itself must not be reified.

The **two truths** answer the ethical objection. Ultimately, persons and duties lack independent essence. Conventionally, suffering occurs, actions have consequences, teachings guide conduct and the path operates. Without conventional truth, ultimate truth cannot be taught; without realizing emptiness, liberation cannot occur.

Indeed, emptiness makes ethical cultivation possible: because anger and ignorance are conditioned rather than fixed essences, they can cease. Insight into interdependence can weaken egoistic attachment and deepen compassion.

**Evaluation:** *Śūnyavāda* undermines dogmatic foundations, not *dharma*. Moral indifference follows only from confusing emptiness with nihilism. Its practical success nevertheless requires a robust conventional account of reasons and consequences.

**Why this earns marks:** It presents Nāgārjuna’s arguments and directly resolves the moral-indifference charge.

### Q8(b). ‘Not karma, but knowledge alone leads to moksa.’ (Samkara). Do you agree ? Justify your answer.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Vedānta](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/vedanta.md) → knowledge and liberation in Advaita · **Secondary route:** [Mīmāṃsā](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/mimamsa.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 12½ marks · about 200 words · Directive: Take a position and justify · Syllabus unit: Advaita Vedānta—means to liberation · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Vedānta](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Vedanta.md) · **Secondary classification:** [Mīmāṃsā](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Mimamsa.md) · **Slug:** `shankara-knowledge-karma-moksha`

**Demand decoding:** Explain why action cannot remove ignorance, distinguish direct cause from preparatory means, and offer a qualified agreement.

**Argument map:** bondage is ignorance of ever-free self → action produces/modifies/attains/purifies an effect → liberation is not a produced result → self-knowledge directly removes ignorance → ritual and ethical action purify mind but cannot independently liberate → knowledge still requires disciplined preparation and embodied assimilation.

#### Model answer

I agree with Śaṅkara’s claim if “alone” identifies the **direct** means to liberation, not the whole preparatory path. Bondage is ignorance (*avidyā*): the ever-free self is mistaken for body, agent and enjoyer. Only knowledge—“self is non-dual Brahman”—can remove this error, as light removes darkness.

Action (*karma*) produces, modifies, obtains or purifies something. Liberation (*mokṣa*) cannot be produced, because whatever is produced is impermanent; nor can action transform the self, which is already complete. Vedic rituals operate within distinctions among agent, act, deity and result, whereas liberating knowledge sublates precisely this plurality. Therefore action and knowledge cannot jointly generate liberation as coordinate causes.

Yet action remains indispensable indirectly. Ethical duty, sacrifice performed without attachment, devotion and disciplined living purify the mind (*citta-śuddhi*) and cultivate discrimination, dispassion and concentration. Such fitness enables hearing, reflection and sustained contemplation of Upaniṣadic teaching. Already-fructifying karma may continue bodily life after knowledge.

Mīmāṃsā objects that scriptural practice, not mere cognition, transforms the agent. A further concern is whether propositional knowledge alone changes entrenched desire.

**Evaluation:** Śaṅkara is right that an epistemic bondage requires an epistemic remover. But liberating knowledge is realized insight prepared and stabilised by ethical-spiritual practice, not detached intellectual information.

**Why this earns marks:** It clearly distinguishes direct cause, preparatory action and a qualified agreement.

### Q8(c). Evaluate Ramanuja’s critique of Samkara’s theory of maya.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Vedānta](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/vedanta.md) → Rāmānuja’s critique of *māyā* · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 12½ marks · about 200 words · Directive: Evaluate critique · Syllabus unit: Advaita and Viśiṣṭādvaita—world and ignorance · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Vedānta](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Vedanta.md) · **Slug:** `ramanuja-critique-shankara-maya`

**Demand decoding:** Present the major “seven objections” family without mechanically listing labels, explain the alternative realism, and judge the critique’s force.

**Argument map:** Advaita explains plurality through beginningless indefinable ignorance → Rāmānuja asks its locus, object, nature, proof, concealment, removal and status → neither real nor unreal appears incoherent → self-luminous Brahman cannot be obscured → real world as Brahman’s body avoids illusion → Advaita replies that ignorance is experienced and sublatable.

#### Model answer

Rāmānuja attacks Advaita’s *māyā* or ignorance through objections traditionally grouped as seven “great untenabilities.” **Locus:** ignorance cannot reside in Brahman, pure knowledge, nor in the individual whose individuality it supposedly creates. **Object:** Brahman cannot be both what ignorance conceals and what remains self-luminous. **Nature:** describing ignorance as neither real nor unreal (*anirvacanīya*) seems to violate excluded alternatives. **Proof:** perception reveals a real world, while scripture need not teach illusion. **Concealment:** finite ignorance cannot cover self-revealing Brahman. **Removal:** knowledge cannot destroy a positive beginningless entity. **Status of the remover:** if scripture and liberating cognition belong to ignorance, their authority is threatened.

Rāmānuja instead treats souls and matter as real dependent modes—the body—of personal Brahman. Upaniṣadic unity signifies organic dependence, not numerical identity; plurality and devotion remain real.

The critique powerfully exposes Advaita’s difficulty in locating ignorance and explaining how non-duality appears as plurality. It also preserves ordinary knowledge and religious practice.

Advaita replies that ignorance is not a second substance but beginningless misapprehension, evident in error and removable by knowledge. “Indefinable” means neither absolutely real nor sheer non-being, as dream objects function before sublation. The empirical standpoint can provide valid instruction without ultimate independence.

**Evaluation:** Rāmānuja decisively pressures any reified *māyā*. He does not conclusively refute a phenomenological, non-substantial account of superimposition.

**Why this earns marks:** It reconstructs the central objections, alternative and Advaita reply before judging their reach.

### Q8(d). Give a critical account of the concept of cittavrtti in Yoga philosophy.
**Primary route:** Philosophy Paper I → [Yoga](../../topics/philosophy-paper-i/yoga.md) → mental modifications and restraint · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Metadata:** 12½ marks · about 200 words · Directive: Critical account · Syllabus unit: Yoga—mind, modifications and liberation · Difficulty: High  
**Classification route:** [Yoga](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Yoga.md) · **Slug:** `yoga-cittavrtti-critical-account`

**Demand decoding:** Define mind and modifications, classify the five, relate them to affliction and restraint, and assess whether cessation destroys cognition.

**Argument map:** mind-stuff transforms into object-shaped states → consciousness appears identified with them → five modifications may be afflicted or unafflicted → practice and dispassion restrain them → meditative integration progressively removes seeds → witness abides in own nature → dualist reflection model and cognition problem invite criticism.

#### Model answer

In Pātañjala Yoga, mind-stuff (*citta*) is a subtle product of nature (*prakṛti*) comprising intellect, ego-function and mind. A mental modification (*citta-vṛtti*) is its changing form when it cognises, imagines, remembers or sleeps. Consciousness (*puruṣa*) is changeless, but reflection in *citta* makes mental states appear conscious and consciousness appear modified.

Yoga Sūtra I.5–11 classifies five modifications, each afflicted or unafflicted:

1. valid cognition (*pramāṇa*): perception, inference and testimony;
2. error (*viparyaya*);
3. conceptual construction (*vikalpa*);
4. sleep (*nidrā*), a modification based on absence;
5. memory (*smṛti*).

Bondage arises through identification with these modifications under afflictions such as ignorance and egoity. Yoga is their restraint (*yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ*) through sustained practice and dispassion. Concentration and meditative integration (*samādhi*) progressively quiet gross, subtle and latent modifications until consciousness abides in its own nature and isolation (*kaivalya*) becomes possible.

The theory offers a nuanced functional psychology: even valid cognition can bind when appropriated, while restraint is disciplined clarity rather than mere blankness. Yet if all cognition belongs to unconscious *citta*, it is unclear how proximity to inactive consciousness produces awareness. Complete cessation also seems knowable only through a later mental state.

**Evaluation:** *Citta-vṛtti* effectively explains cognitive fluctuation and contemplative training, but its dualist illumination model remains metaphysically obscure.

**Why this earns marks:** It defines, classifies, connects to liberation and critically tests the consciousness relation.
