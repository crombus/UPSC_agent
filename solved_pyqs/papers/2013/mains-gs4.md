## Q1. Values, ethics and professional competence

**Official question:** What do you understand by ‘values’ and ‘ethics’? In what way is it important to be ethical along with being professionally competent? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** 2013 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q1 · Directive: Define and explain importance · Difficulty: Easy-Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → values, ethics and competence · **Proposed slug:** `values-ethics-professional-competence` · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Ethics and human interface](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/01_Ethics-and-Human-Interface.md) · [Foundational values](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/04_Aptitude-and-Foundational-Values-for-Civil-Service.md)

### Demand decoding and framework

Distinguish values from ethics, then show why competence without moral purpose is dangerous and good intention without competence is ineffective.

### Model answer

**Values** are enduring beliefs about what is desirable—such as honesty, dignity and compassion—that shape priorities and character. **Ethics** is the reasoned examination and application of such values to decide what ought to be done, especially where duties or interests conflict.

Professional competence supplies knowledge, skill and efficiency; ethics directs their ends and means. A technically skilled engineer who conceals a structural defect can magnify harm, while an honest but untrained officer may waste relief resources. Aristotle's practical wisdom joins good character with sound judgment. In administration, competence enables accurate beneficiary selection; impartiality and empathy ensure that discretion does not exclude the vulnerable.

Ethics also sustains legitimacy, trust and accountability where rules cannot anticipate every circumstance. Yet moral sincerity cannot substitute for evidence or expertise.

**Conclusion:** Public service requires ethical competence—capacity makes good intentions effective, while ethics makes professional power safe and publicly purposeful.

**Why this earns marks:** It gives precise definitions, establishes complementarity, uses administrative examples and includes a qualification.

## Q2(a). Five foundational public-service attributes

**Official question:** What do you understand by the following terms in the context of public service?  
(i) Integrity  
(ii) Perseverance  
(iii) Spirit of service  
(iv) Commitment  
(v) Courage of conviction **(250 words, 3 × 5 = 15 marks)**

**Metadata:** 2013 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q2(a) · Directive: Define in context · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Work Culture and Public Funds](../../topics/ethics/work-culture-and-public-funds.md) → foundational civil-service values · **Proposed slug:** `five-public-service-attributes` · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owner:** [Aptitude and foundational values](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/04_Aptitude-and-Foundational-Values-for-Civil-Service.md)

### Demand decoding and framework

For each term: concise definition → observable administrative conduct → boundary preventing misuse.

### Model answer

1. **Integrity:** Consistency among worthy values, words and conduct, with readiness for scrutiny. A procurement officer discloses a relative's bid and recuses. Integrity is not stubbornness; correction after evidence is part of it.
2. **Perseverance:** Sustained, disciplined effort despite difficulty or delayed results. A district team repeatedly reaches remote habitations to complete vaccination. It requires adaptation, not mechanically repeating a failed method.
3. **Spirit of service:** Treating authority as a trust for citizens, especially the vulnerable. It appears in accessible hearings and prompt relief rather than status-seeking. Service does not mean unlawful favour.
4. **Commitment:** Reliable devotion to constitutional goals, lawful duties and promised outcomes. An officer follows rehabilitation through after acquisition, instead of treating sanction as completion. Commitment must remain open to review.
5. **Courage of conviction:** Acting on a reasoned ethical judgment despite pressure or personal cost. An inspector records a safety violation by an influential firm. It differs from rashness because facts are verified, dissent documented and lawful channels used.

Together, these values make administration trustworthy: integrity fixes moral direction, perseverance sustains effort, service centres citizens, commitment secures delivery and courage protects principle when it becomes costly.

**Why this earns marks:** Each definition is behaviourally anchored and ethically qualified rather than reduced to a synonym.

## Q2(b). Two additional public-service attributes

**Official question:** Indicate two more attributes which you consider important for public service. Justify your answer. **(100 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** 2013 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q2(b) · Directive: Identify and justify · Difficulty: Easy-Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Emotional Intelligence and Ethical Dilemmas](../../topics/ethics/emotional-intelligence-and-ethical-dilemmas.md) → empathy and impartiality · **Proposed slug:** `empathy-impartiality-public-service` · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Foundational values](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/04_Aptitude-and-Foundational-Values-for-Civil-Service.md) · [Emotional intelligence](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/05_Emotional-Intelligence-in-Administration.md)

### Model answer

**Empathy** is the capacity to understand another person's situation and feelings without losing judgment. It helps an officer detect hidden barriers—for example, redesigning a hearing for persons with disabilities—thereby turning formal equality into responsive service.

**Impartiality** means deciding by relevant, publicly defensible criteria rather than kinship, prejudice or political pressure. Transparent disaster-relief lists illustrate it.

Empathy without impartiality may become favouritism; impartiality without empathy may become insensitive uniformity. Together they enable humane fairness: equal concern, context-sensitive treatment and reasons that every citizen can contest.

**Why this earns marks:** It selects distinct attributes, demonstrates administrative relevance and shows their necessary balance.

## Q3. Changing and universal human values

**Official question:** Some people feel that values keep changing with time and situation, while others strongly believe that there are certain universal and eternal human values. Give your perception in this regard with due justification. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** 2013 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q3 · Directive: Take a reasoned position and justify · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → relativism and universal values · **Proposed slug:** `changing-universal-human-values` · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owner:** [Ethics and human interface](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/01_Ethics-and-Human-Interface.md)

### Model answer

Values have both a durable core and a context-sensitive expression. Customs concerning dress, family roles or professional etiquette change with knowledge and social conditions. Even a stable value such as privacy is applied differently in a digital society.

Yet complete relativism would make slavery, torture or discrimination acceptable wherever normalised. Human dignity, non-cruelty, fairness, truthfulness and reciprocal respect have a universal moral claim because persons possess agency and vulnerability. Kant grounds dignity in persons as ends; the Universal Declaration of Human Rights institutionalises a shared minimum.

Application still requires practical judgment. Truthfulness does not require disclosing a victim's protected identity; equality may justify reasonable accommodation rather than identical treatment.

**Conclusion:** Ethical progress is neither abandonment of all tradition nor frozen rules. Universal values provide the compass; democratic deliberation, evidence and context determine their proportionate application.

**Why this earns marks:** It avoids both absolute relativism and rigid universalism while giving thinkers, examples and qualifications.

## Q4. Emotional intelligence and ethical decisions

**Official question:** What is ‘emotional intelligence’ and how can it be developed in people? How does it help an individual in taking ethical decisions? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** 2013 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q4 · Directive: Define, explain development and application · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Emotional Intelligence and Ethical Dilemmas](../../topics/ethics/emotional-intelligence-and-ethical-dilemmas.md) → emotional intelligence in decisions · **Proposed slug:** `emotional-intelligence-development-ethical-decisions` · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owner:** [Emotional intelligence](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/05_Emotional-Intelligence-in-Administration.md)

### Model answer

**Emotional intelligence (EI)** is the capacity to recognise, understand and regulate one's emotions and to perceive and respond constructively to others' emotions. In Goleman's formulation it includes self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy and social skills.

It can be developed through reflective journaling, feedback, mindfulness, perspective-taking, role-play, exposure to diverse communities and post-decision review. Institutions can add mentoring and conflict-resolution training.

EI supports ethical decisions by:

- preventing anger, fear or ego from hijacking judgment;
- revealing stakeholders' needs and power asymmetries;
- enabling respectful communication and de-escalation; and
- sustaining moral courage under pressure.

Thus a police officer handling a protest can acknowledge grievance and communicate calmly while enforcing proportionate legal limits.

EI is not morality itself: empathy may be manipulated and emotional control may serve wrongful ends. It must operate with law, evidence and constitutional values.

**Why this earns marks:** It covers all three demands and clearly separates emotional skill from ethical principle.

## Q5(a). Voice of conscience

**Official question:** What do you understand by the term ‘voice of conscience’? How do you prepare yourself to heed to the voice of conscience? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** 2013 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q5(a) · Directive: Define and explain self-preparation · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → conscience and moral reflection · **Proposed slug:** `voice-of-conscience-preparation` · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owner:** [Laws, rules and conscience](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/10_Sources-of-Ethical-Guidance-Laws-Rules-Conscience.md)

### Model answer

The **voice of conscience** is one's inward, reason-responsive moral judgment that approves or condemns a contemplated act. It is felt as an obligation to do what is right even without surveillance or reward.

I would prepare to heed it by:

- clarifying constitutional and professional values;
- practising daily reflection on motives, means and consequences;
- testing whether I could publicly justify the act and accept role reversal;
- seeking facts and disinterested advice to counter bias;
- cultivating small habits of truthfulness and self-restraint; and
- maintaining financial and emotional independence so pressure is resistible.

For example, before altering an unfavourable inspection note, I would pause, record evidence and ask whether the same concealment would be acceptable if public safety affected my family.

Conscience is not infallible; prejudice can masquerade as intuition. It must therefore be educated by reason, empathy, law and dialogue.

**Why this earns marks:** It defines conscience, gives an executable discipline and guards against subjectivism.

## Q5(b). Crisis of conscience

**Official question:** What is meant by ‘crisis of conscience’? Narrate one incident in your life when you were faced with such a crisis and how you resolved the same. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** 2013 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q5(b) · Directive: Define, narrate and explain resolution · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Emotional Intelligence and Ethical Dilemmas](../../topics/ethics/emotional-intelligence-and-ethical-dilemmas.md) → conscience conflict · **Proposed slug:** `crisis-of-conscience-resolution` · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Ethical dilemmas](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/09_Public-Service-Values-Status-and-Ethical-Dilemmas.md) · [Conscience](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/10_Sources-of-Ethical-Guidance-Laws-Rules-Conscience.md)

### Model answer

A **crisis of conscience** arises when competing duties or loyalties make every available choice carry moral cost, producing serious inner conflict.

In a university group project, a close friend copied substantial material shortly before submission. Reporting it could damage the friendship and the whole group's grade; silence would reward dishonesty and implicate us.

I first verified the overlap and spoke privately to the friend, distinguishing the person from the wrong. I asked that the copied portion be removed and offered help to rewrite it, while making clear that I would inform the supervisor if it remained. The friend agreed; we disclosed the delay, accepted a small penalty and submitted original work.

The resolution balanced loyalty with fairness through truth, dialogue and proportionate accountability. It taught me that conscience should neither rationalise friendship nor rush to punitive exposure when correction remains possible.

**Why this earns marks:** The incident is concrete, identifies the conflict and explains a proportionate resolution rather than claiming moral perfection.

## Q6(a). Need, greed and sustainability

**Official question:** “There is enough on this earth for every one's need but for no one's greed.” — Mahatma Gandhi. Bring out what it means to you in the present context. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** 2013 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q6(a) · Directive: Interpret in present context · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Indian Moral Thinkers](../../topics/ethics/indian-moral-thinkers.md) → Gandhi, restraint and trusteeship · **Proposed slug:** `gandhi-need-greed-sustainability` · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Indian thinkers](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/06_Indian-Moral-Thinkers-and-Philosophers.md) · [Environmental ethics](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/13_Emerging-Ethics-Technology-AI-and-Environment.md)

### Model answer

Gandhi distinguishes legitimate need from limitless accumulation. Nature can support dignified living when resources are fairly shared and prudently used, but not consumption driven by status, monopoly and indifference to ecological limits.

The present context is marked by food waste amid hunger, groundwater depletion, conspicuous consumption and climate burdens falling disproportionately on the poor. Gandhi's idea of **trusteeship** treats wealth and power as socially obligated rather than absolutely private.

Its application requires sustainable production, circular use, polluter responsibility, protection of commons and officials who refuse rent-seeking. Personally it supports moderation; institutionally it demands justice, because lecturing the poor about simplicity while permitting elite overconsumption is inequitable.

Need is not bare survival: dignity requires health, education and opportunity. The quotation therefore rejects both deprivation and greed.

**Conclusion:** Sufficiency with justice—not enforced poverty or limitless growth—is the ethical basis of sustainable development.

**Why this earns marks:** It interprets the quotation through Gandhian trusteeship, present ecological examples and a distributive-justice qualification.

## Q6(b). Power as a test of character

**Official question:** “Nearly all men can withstand adversity, but if you want to test a man's character, give him power.” — Abraham Lincoln. Bring out what it means to you in the present context. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** 2013 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q6(b) · Directive: Interpret in present context · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Work Culture and Public Funds](../../topics/ethics/work-culture-and-public-funds.md) → power, integrity and accountability · **Proposed slug:** `power-test-of-character-lincoln` · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Foundational values](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/04_Aptitude-and-Foundational-Values-for-Civil-Service.md) · [Ethical governance](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/11_Accountability-and-Ethical-Governance.md)

### Model answer

Adversity tests endurance; power tests self-restraint. Authority expands discretion, reduces dependence on others and creates opportunities for favouritism, impunity and self-glorification. Character is revealed by whether a person still respects limits and those unable to retaliate.

In administration, a district magistrate who gives a hearing to an unpopular group, records reasons and applies the same criteria to political allies demonstrates integrity. A superior who takes credit, suppresses dissent or uses transfers to punish honesty fails the test.

The insight supports constitutional checks: separation of powers, audit, RTI, reasoned orders and appeal. As Lord Acton's warning suggests, concentrated power increases corruption risk.

Yet institutions do not eliminate personal responsibility, and moral exhortation cannot replace controls. Ethical leadership must combine humility, empathy and willingness to be scrutinised with transparent systems.

**Conclusion:** The best evidence of character is not possessing power, but converting it into accountable service rather than privilege.

**Why this earns marks:** It connects the quotation to observable administrative conduct and balances character with institutional design.

## Q6(c). Mastery over desire as courage

**Official question:** “I count him braver who overcomes his desires than him who overcomes his enemies.” — Aristotle. Bring out what it means to you in the present context. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** 2013 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q6(c) · Directive: Interpret in present context · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → virtue, temperance and self-mastery · **Proposed slug:** `aristotle-self-mastery-courage` · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owner:** [Western thinkers](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/07_Western-Moral-Philosophers-and-Thinkers.md)

### Model answer

Aristotle treats moral courage as governance of the self, not merely victory over an external opponent. Desires for wealth, status, revenge or approval operate continuously and can make outwardly powerful persons internally dependent.

Present examples include an officer refusing an illicit gift, a leader accepting criticism instead of retaliating, and a citizen resisting addictive digital gratification. Such temperance protects impartiality and allows reason to choose the mean between indulgence and unhealthy suppression.

Self-mastery is harder than defeating an enemy because the adversary is recurring, rationalised and privately known. It is developed through habituation, ethical peers, disclosure of conflicts and institutions that reduce temptation.

The quotation does not require extinction of desire. Ambition, pleasure and material aspiration can support excellence when proportionate and justly pursued.

**Conclusion:** Genuine bravery is disciplined freedom—the capacity to govern desire so that power, talent and ambition remain servants of worthy ends.

**Why this earns marks:** It accurately applies Aristotelian temperance, gives contemporary illustrations and avoids equating virtue with ascetic denial.

## Q7. Individual good within the good of all

**Official question:** “The good of an individual is contained in the good of all.” What do you understand by this statement? How can this principle be implemented in public life? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** 2013 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q7 · Directive: Interpret and operationalise · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Indian Moral Thinkers](../../topics/ethics/indian-moral-thinkers.md) → sarvodaya and public good · **Proposed slug:** `individual-good-sarvodaya-public-life` · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Indian thinkers](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/06_Indian-Moral-Thinkers-and-Philosophers.md) · [Ethical governance](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/11_Accountability-and-Ethical-Governance.md)

### Model answer

The statement expresses interdependence: personal flourishing rests on social peace, public health, trust, justice and ecological security. Gandhi's **sarvodaya** links each person's welfare to the uplift of all, especially the weakest.

In public life it can be implemented through:

- universal basic services and targeted support where disadvantage is unequal;
- participatory decisions and social audits;
- progressive, transparent taxation and prudent public expenditure;
- protection of commons and future generations;
- conflict-of-interest rules and impartial recruitment; and
- the “last person” test when designing policy.

Vaccination illustrates mutual good: individual protection rises with community coverage. Accessible public transport enlarges opportunity for persons with disabilities while benefiting society.

Collective good must not become a pretext for sacrificing minorities. Rights, due process and proportionality constrain majoritarian utility.

**Conclusion:** The principle demands institutions that align legitimate self-interest with inclusive welfare while preserving each person's dignity.

**Why this earns marks:** It explains interdependence, provides implementable mechanisms and protects individual rights against collectivist excess.

## Q8. Politics and ethics

**Official question:** It is often said that ‘politics’ and ‘ethics’ do not go together. What is your opinion in this regard? Justify your answer with illustrations. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** 2013 · Mains · GS-IV · Section A · Official Q8 · Directive: Take a position and justify with illustrations · Difficulty: Moderate  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Governance and International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/governance-and-international-ethics.md) → political ethics · **Proposed slug:** `politics-and-ethics` · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Ethical governance](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/11_Accountability-and-Ethical-Governance.md) · [Indian thinkers](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/06_Indian-Moral-Thinkers-and-Philosophers.md)

### Model answer

Politics involves competition for and exercise of public power; ethics asks whether its ends, means and distribution are justifiable. They often diverge because electoral incentives, secrecy, money power and party loyalty reward expediency. But their separation cannot be accepted: unethical politics destroys legitimacy and converts representation into domination.

Gandhi insisted that means shape ends. Ethical politics appears in truthful campaigning, transparent finance, peaceful transfer of power, protection of dissent and resignation or correction after proven failure. Lal Bahadur Shastri's acceptance of ministerial responsibility after a railway accident illustrates accountability, while vote-buying shows how expediency corrupts citizenship.

Political ethics is not private moral purity. Negotiation and compromise are legitimate where they preserve constitutional values; deception, hatred and corruption are not.

**Conclusion:** Ethics may make politics harder in the short term, but it is what makes political authority legitimate, trustworthy and sustainable.

**Why this earns marks:** It recognises real incentives without normalising wrongdoing and distinguishes principled compromise from unethical expediency.

## Q9. RTI disclosure implicating the PIO

**Official case:** A Public Information Officer has received an application under RTI Act. Having gathered the information, the PIO discovers that the information pertains to some of the decisions taken by him, which were found to be not altogether right. There were other employees also who were party to these decisions. Disclosure of the information is likely to lead to disciplinary action with possibility of punishment against him as well as some of his colleagues. Non-disclosure or part disclosure or camouflaged disclosure of information will result into lesser punishment or no punishment.

The PIO is otherwise an honest and conscientious person but this particular decision, on which the RTI application has been filed, turned out to be wrong. He comes to you for advice.

The following are some suggested options. Please evaluate the merits and demerits of each of the options:

1. The PIO could refer the matter to his superior officer and seek his advice and act strictly in accordance with the advice, even though he is not completely in agreement with the advice of the superior.
2. The PIO could proceed on leave and leave the matter to be dealt by his successor in office or request for transfer of the application to another PIO.
3. The PIO could weigh the consequences of disclosing the information truthfully, including the effect on his career, and reply in a manner that would not place him or his career in jeopardy, but at the same time a little compromise can be made on the contents of the information.
4. The PIO could consult his other colleagues who are party to the decision and take action as per their advice.

Also please indicate (without necessarily restricting to the above options) what you would like to advise, giving proper reasons. **(250 words, 20 marks)**

**Metadata:** 2013 · Mains · GS-IV · Section B · Official Q9 · Directive: Evaluate options and advise · Difficulty: High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Service Delivery and Regulatory Ethics](../../topics/ethics/service-delivery-and-regulatory-ethics.md) → RTI, conflict of interest and accountability · **Proposed slug:** `rti-self-implicating-disclosure` · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [RTI and information sharing](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/15_Transparency-RTI-and-Information-Sharing.md) · [Case-study method](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/22_Case-Study-Method-and-Answer-Architecture.md)

### Stakeholders, issues and decision framework

**Stakeholders:** applicant and public; PIO; implicated colleagues; department; superior/appellate authority.  
**Issues:** statutory duty, truthfulness, conflict of interest, natural justice, accountability, proportional punishment and institutional trust.

### Model answer

The PIO's honest reputation does not permit distortion of a statutory response.

**Options and consequences**

1. **Seek superior advice:** useful for legal clarity, but blindly following an improper oral direction does not transfer the PIO's responsibility.
2. **Take leave/transfer:** may reduce perceived conflict, but evasion delays the applicant and can amount to dereliction. Transfer is proper only through a lawful competence provision, not to escape consequences.
3. **Compromise disclosure:** protects careers temporarily but is deceptive, violates RTI duty and compounds the original error through deliberate concealment.
4. **Follow implicated colleagues:** consultation may clarify facts, but they share the conflict and cannot determine disclosure.

**Advice:** The PIO should identify the records requested, apply only the exemptions expressly available under the RTI Act, and disclose all non-exempt material accurately within time. Where severability lawfully applies, he should give the remainder and record reasons. He may obtain written legal/RTI-cell advice and disclose his conflict to the competent superior, but must not camouflage facts.

Separately, he should report the erroneous decision through the appropriate administrative channel, preserve records, cooperate with an impartial inquiry and explain whether it was a good-faith error or misconduct. Responsibility should be individualised and punishment proportionate.

**Safeguards:** written file trail, no contact with the applicant to deter the request, protection against record tampering, and information on appeal.

**Decision:** Truthful lawful disclosure best protects the citizen's right, due process and long-term institutional trust.

**Why this earns marks:** It evaluates every printed option, distinguishes RTI disclosure from disciplinary liability and adds lawful safeguards.

## Q10. Unsafe flyover, hierarchy and deadline pressure

**Official case:** You are working as an Executive Engineer in the construction cell of a Municipal Corporation and are presently in-charge of the construction of a flyover. There are two Junior Engineers under you who have the responsibility of day-to-day inspection of the site and are reporting to you, while you are finally reporting to the Chief Engineer who heads the cell. While the construction is heading towards completion, the Junior Engineers have been regularly reporting that all construction is taking place as per design specifications. However, in one of your surprise inspections, you have noticed some serious deviations and lacunae which, in your opinion, are likely to affect the safety of the flyover. Rectification of these lacunae at this stage would require a substantial amount of demolition and rework which will cause a tangible loss to the contractor and will also delay completion. There is a lot of public pressure on the Corporation to get this construction completed because of heavy traffic congestion in the area. When you brought this matter to the notice of the Chief Engineer, he advised you that in his opinion it is not a very serious lapse and may be ignored. He advised for further expediting the project for completion in time. However, you are convinced that this was a serious matter which might affect public safety and should not be left unaddressed.

What will you do in such a situation? Some of the options are given below. Evaluate the merits and demerits of each of these options and finally suggest what course of action you would like to take, giving reasons:

1. Follow the advice of the Chief Engineer and go ahead.
2. Make an exhaustive report of the situation bringing out all facts and analysis along with your own viewpoints stated clearly and seek for written orders from the Chief Engineer.
3. Call for explanation from the Junior Engineers and issue orders to the contractor for necessary correction within targeted time.
4. Highlight the issue so that it reaches superiors above the Chief Engineer.
5. Considering the rigid attitude of the Chief Engineer, seek transfer from the project or report sick. **(250 words, 20 marks)**

## Q11. Child labour in a Sivakasi household unit

**Official case:** Sivakasi in Tamil Nadu is known for its manufacturing clusters on firecrackers and matches. The local economy of the area is largely dependent on firecrackers industry. It has led to tangible economic development and improved standard of living in the area.

So far as child labour norms for hazardous industries like firecrackers industry are concerned, International Labour Organization (ILO) has set the minimum age as 18 years. In India, however, this age is 14 years.

The units in industrial clusters of firecrackers can be classified into registered and non-registered entities. One typical unit is household-based work. Though the law is clear on the use of child labour employment norms in registered/non-registered units, it does not include household-based works. Household-based work means children working under the supervision of their parents/relatives. To evade child labour norms, several units project themselves as household-based works but employ children from outside. Needless to say that employing children saves the costs for these units leading to higher profits to the owners.

On your visit to one of the units at Sivakasi, the owner takes you around the unit which has about 10–15 children below 14 years of age. The owner tells you that in his household-based unit, the children are all his relatives. You notice that several children smirk, when the owner tells you this. On deeper enquiry, you figure out that neither the owner nor the children are able to satisfactorily establish their relationship with each other.

(a) Bring out and discuss the ethical issues involved in the above case.  
(b) What would be your reaction after your above visit? **(300 words, 25 marks)**

**Metadata:** 2013 · Mains · GS-IV · Section B · Official Q11 · Directive: Identify/discuss issues and state action · Difficulty: High  
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Service Delivery and Regulatory Ethics](../../topics/ethics/service-delivery-and-regulatory-ethics.md) → child labour and regulatory evasion · **Proposed slug:** `sivakasi-child-labour-regulatory-evasion` · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)  
**Canonical owners:** [Ethical governance](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/11_Accountability-and-Ethical-Governance.md) · [Case-study method](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Ethics/basic/22_Case-Study-Method-and-Answer-Architecture.md)

### Stakeholders and ethical issues

Children and families; owner and adult workers; consumers; local community; labour, police, education and child-welfare authorities; legitimate firms. Issues include child safety and dignity, exploitation, deception, poverty, education, regulatory gaps, unfair competition and livelihood dependence.

### Model answer

The children's age, hazardous environment and inability to establish kinship create a credible risk of disguised child labour. Economic development cannot be built by transferring injury, lost schooling and bargaining power onto children.

**Ethical issues**

- children are treated merely as low-cost means, contrary to dignity and best interests;
- danger is imposed on persons unable to give meaningful consent;
- false “household” claims evade law and disadvantage compliant firms;
- poverty may constrain families, requiring support rather than moral blame;
- abrupt closure without rehabilitation may push work further underground.

**Reaction:** I would not accept the owner's assertion or publicly interrogate children in his presence. Within my authority, I would document observations, prevent immediate hazardous exposure, and trigger a joint verification by the competent labour inspector and child-welfare/police authorities. Children should be interviewed separately by trained personnel, with privacy and protection from retaliation.

If violations are established, the unit should face lawful closure/prosecution and recovery of dues after due process; supply-chain principals should also be examined. Each child should receive health assessment, safe restoration through the Child Welfare Committee, school enrolment/bridge education and access to family social protection. Families need livelihood linkage and adults need safe employment or skilling.

**Systemic safeguards:** map household units; conduct risk-based surprise inspections; create confidential reporting; trace subcontracting; require purchaser due diligence; coordinate schools and local bodies to detect dropouts; and monitor rehabilitation to prevent re-entry.

The paper's stated legal ages are preserved as historical source wording; action must follow the law applicable at the time of decision.

**Decision:** Child protection is immediate, but enforcement must be evidence-based, survivor-sensitive and joined to rehabilitation and livelihood measures.

**Why this earns marks:** It answers both subparts, avoids unsupported summary punishment and addresses exploitation, enforcement and structural causes.

**Official case:** You are the Executive Director of an upcoming Infotech Company which is making a name for itself in the market.

Mr. A, who is a star performer, is heading the marketing team. In addition, he regularly sends indecent SMS's to all the team members including his female colleagues.

2. **Privately warn Mr. A:** may stop some conduct, but trivialises a serious complaint and risks intimidation or evidence loss.
3. **Dismiss him immediately:** signals seriousness but prejudges facts and may violate fair procedure.

**Safeguards:** anti-harassment policy, regular training, safe reporting, bystander responsibility, climate review and board oversight.

**Decision:** Performance never offsets violation of dignity; a survivor-centred, independent and fair process protects employees and sustainable corporate integrity.

**Why this earns marks:** It avoids both concealment and summary punishment while specifying immediate protection, lawful inquiry and organisational prevention.
