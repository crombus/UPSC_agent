## Q1. Digital Rupee and CBDC
**Primary route:** Economy → [Services, Digital Economy, Fintech and Platform Markets](../../topics/economy/services-digital-economy-fintech-and-platform-markets.md) → Central Bank Digital Currency · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2026 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 1  
**Official question:** What do you mean by Digital Rupee? In this context, explain the working and progress of India's Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC).  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Digital Economy and Fintech → Central Bank Digital Currency  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/digital-rupee-cbdc`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Define + Explain  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/24_Services-Digital-Economy-Fintech-and-Platform-Markets.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/24_Services-Digital-Economy-Fintech-and-Platform-Markets.md)

### Demand decoding

- Define the e₹ by issuer, liability and legal status—not merely as “digital money”.
- Explain the wallet-to-wallet flow and retail/wholesale variants.
- State dated pilot progress without presenting a pilot as nationwide replacement of cash or UPI.

### Compact framework

`RBI liability → CBDC-R/CBDC-W → wallet/settlement flow → dated pilot features → qualification`

### Model answer

The **Digital Rupee (e₹)** is India’s CBDC: sovereign legal tender issued as a direct liability of the RBI, convertible at par with cash and bank money.

In the **retail CBDC (CBDC-R)** pilot, users obtain e₹ tokens in wallets offered through participating banks or authorised providers. Payment transfers the token from the payer’s wallet to the payee’s wallet; unlike UPI, it is not merely an instruction to move commercial-bank deposits. **Wholesale CBDC (CBDC-W)** is designed for interbank and securities settlement.

**Progress, dated to 2026:** pilots begun in 2022 remain phased and voluntary. RBI has tested offline/NFC payments for low-connectivity areas, programmable transfers for specified uses, and wider wallet distribution, including fintech participation. Cross-border CBDC interoperability has also been discussed, including a proposed BRICS linkage.

Thus, e₹ can improve settlement finality, inclusion and targeted delivery, but remains a parallel pilot; privacy, cyber-resilience, interoperability and adoption must be resolved before scale-up.

### Evidence, analysis and qualification

- **Fact:** e₹ is an RBI liability; CBDC-R and CBDC-W have distinct users and purposes.
- **Analysis:** Offline and programmable features can reach gaps that ordinary online payment rails do not.
- **Qualification:** As of 2026, CBDC progress is pilot-stage; it has not replaced cash or UPI.

### Examiner rationale

This earns marks by distinguishing CBDC from UPI and cryptocurrency, explaining actual transaction architecture, and qualifying current progress.

## Q2. Financial inclusion and RBI’s Financial Inclusion Index
**Primary route:** Economy → [Poverty, Inequality, Social Sector and Inclusive Growth](../../topics/economy/poverty-inequality-social-sector-and-inclusive-growth.md) → Financial Inclusion · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2026 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 2  
**Official question:** Examine the view that financial inclusion is an integral part of social and economic inclusion in a country like India. Also throw light on the usefulness of the R.B.I.’s Financial Inclusion Index.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Inclusive Growth → Financial Inclusion  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/financial-inclusion-fi-index`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Examine  
**Primary owner:** [`Economy/basic/23_Poverty-Inequality-Social-Sector-and-Inclusive-Growth.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/23_Poverty-Inequality-Social-Sector-and-Inclusive-Growth.md)  
**Support owner:** [`Economy/basic/06_NPAs-Basel-Norms-Resolution-and-Financial-Inclusion.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/06_NPAs-Basel-Norms-Resolution-and-Financial-Inclusion.md)

### Demand decoding

Connect finance with welfare, agency and productive opportunity; then assess what the FI-Index measures and what it cannot prove.

### Compact framework

`Access → usage → quality → social/economic outcomes → FI-Index utility → limits`

### Model answer

Financial inclusion means affordable, reliable access to payments, savings, credit, insurance and pensions. In India it is integral to wider inclusion because a bank account enables **DBT and social-security receipt**, savings reduce vulnerability, formal credit supports enterprise and farm investment, insurance absorbs shocks, and digital payments lower transaction costs. Women’s SHGs and PMJDY accounts can also strengthen household agency.

However, opening accounts alone is insufficient: dormant accounts, unsuitable credit, digital exclusion, fraud and over-indebtedness can coexist with formal access.

The RBI’s annual **Financial Inclusion Index** is useful because it combines three dimensions—**Access, Usage and Quality**—across banking, investments, insurance, postal and pension services. It permits time-series monitoring, reveals whether infrastructure becomes actual use, and helps regulators identify weak dimensions and target policy.

Yet it is an aggregate diagnostic, not proof that inclusion caused better welfare. It can conceal gender, regional and income disparities. Therefore, it should be read with disaggregated outcome and consumer-protection indicators.

### Evidence, analysis and qualification

- **Fact:** RBI’s FI-Index uses Access, Usage and Quality and has no base year.
- **Analysis:** Inclusion becomes socioeconomic only when finance is usable, safe and productive.
- **Qualification:** An index score cannot independently establish causality or distributional fairness.

### Examiner rationale

The answer examines rather than celebrates inclusion and assesses both the index’s policy value and measurement limits.

## Q3. Agricultural Technology Missions and food security
**Primary route:** Economy → [Agricultural Technology Missions and Mission-Mode Policy](../../topics/economy/agricultural-technology-missions-and-mission-mode-policy.md) → Technology Missions · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2026 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 3  
**Official question:** Describe the various Technology Missions initiated in Indian agriculture and examine their role in food security.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Agriculture → Technology Missions  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/agricultural-technology-missions`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Describe + Examine  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/29_Agricultural-Technology-Missions-and-Mission-Mode-Policy.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/29_Agricultural-Technology-Missions-and-Mission-Mode-Policy.md)

### Demand decoding

Name representative mission families, show their research-to-market logic, and assess food-security gains and implementation limits.

### Compact framework

`Commodity missions + horticulture + cotton/jute → availability, access, nutrition, stability → limits`

### Model answer

Agricultural technology missions are coordinated, outcome-oriented programmes linking research, quality seed or planting material, demonstrations, extension, infrastructure, processing and markets.

Major examples include the **Technology Mission on Oilseeds (1986)** and successor edible-oil missions; **National Food Security Mission (2007-08)** for cereals, pulses and productivity; **National Horticulture Mission/MIDH** for planting material, protected cultivation and post-harvest systems; and Technology Missions on **Cotton** and **Jute**, which joined farm technology with grading, ginning/processing and markets. Current pulse, oilseed and cotton missions continue this mission-mode approach.

Their food-security contribution is multidimensional: higher yields expand availability; pulses and horticulture improve nutrition; domestic oilseed/pulse production reduces import vulnerability; climate-resilient varieties and water-saving practices improve stability; and processing/storage reduce losses.

However, gains remain uneven where extension, seed multiplication, irrigation, smallholder finance or assured markets are weak. Mission success therefore requires region-specific technology, ecological safeguards and production-to-market convergence—not merely distributing inputs.

### Examiner rationale

Representative missions are linked to all four food-security dimensions, with a clear mission-versus-scheme qualification.

## Q4. Inefficiency in agricultural marketing and e-commerce
**Primary route:** Economy → [APMC, e-NAM, FPOs and Agricultural Supply Chains](../../topics/economy/apmc-e-nam-fpos-and-agricultural-supply-chains.md) → Agricultural Marketing and e-NAM · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2026 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 4  
**Official question:** Explain the factors responsible for inefficiency of agri-produce marketing. How e-commerce helps to reduce inefficiency of agri-produce marketing? Explain.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Agriculture → Agricultural Marketing and e-NAM  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/agri-marketing-ecommerce`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Explain  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/13_APMC-e-NAM-FPOs-and-Agricultural-Supply-Chains.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/13_APMC-e-NAM-FPOs-and-Agricultural-Supply-Chains.md)

### Demand decoding

Diagnose market failures, then map each e-commerce function to a specific inefficiency while retaining physical-market constraints.

### Compact framework

`Fragmentation/information/logistics/finance → digital discovery/aggregation/trade/payment → physical complements`

### Model answer

Agri-marketing is inefficient because small, fragmented surpluses weaken bargaining power; farmers lack timely price and buyer information; multiple intermediaries and local market barriers segment trade; non-standard grading impedes comparison; and inadequate storage, cold chain, transport, credit and processing force distress sales. Delayed payment and weak contract enforcement add risk.

E-commerce can reduce these costs by:

- publishing competing bids and prices, improving **price discovery**;
- connecting farmers/FPOs to distant processors, retailers and institutional buyers;
- aggregating lots through FPOs and enabling assaying, grading and traceability;
- digitising auctions, contracts and payments, lowering search and settlement costs; and
- using demand and logistics data to plan harvest, storage and transport.

Platforms such as **e-NAM** can thereby widen the market beyond the local trader.

Yet a portal cannot move produce physically. Benefits depend on assaying facilities, warehouses, interoperable state rules, reliable connectivity, digital literacy, dispute resolution and affordable logistics. E-commerce must therefore complement—not substitute—market infrastructure and farmer collectivisation.

### Examiner rationale

The answer pairs each diagnosed inefficiency with a digital remedy and avoids equating online listing with completed market integration.

## Q5. Biotechnology in processing perishable crops
**Primary route:** Science and Technology → [Biotechnology Fundamentals and DBT Missions](../../topics/science-and-technology/biotechnology-fundamentals-and-dbt-missions.md) → Food Processing · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2026 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 5  
**Official question:** Explain by giving two examples, how biotechnology has helped the Indian farmers in processing their perishable crops.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Science and Technology → Biotechnology → Food Processing  
**Proposed topic slug:** `science-technology/biotechnology-perishable-crop-processing`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Explain with two examples  
**Primary owner:** [`Science-and-Technology/basic/13_Biotechnology-Fundamentals-and-DBT-Missions.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Science-and-Technology/basic/13_Biotechnology-Fundamentals-and-DBT-Missions.md)  
**Support owner:** [`Economy/basic/15_Food-Processing-Cold-Chains-and-Value-Addition.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/15_Food-Processing-Cold-Chains-and-Value-Addition.md)

### Demand decoding

Use exactly two processing-stage examples; explain biological mechanism and farmer benefit. Avoid unrelated GM cultivation traits.

### Compact framework

`Bioprocess → mechanism → processed product → loss/value effect`

### Model answer

Biotechnology helps convert rapidly spoilable produce into stable, higher-value products through biological enzymes and controlled microorganisms.

**1. Enzyme-assisted fruit processing:** Pectinase and cellulase produced by microbial fermentation break down pectin and fruit cell walls during processing of mango, guava and citrus. They improve juice/pulp recovery, reduce viscosity and clarification time, and permit surplus or lower-grade fruit to enter processing instead of being distress-sold.

**2. Microbial fermentation and preservation:** Selected lactic-acid bacteria or yeasts convert sugars in fruits and vegetables into acids or alcohol. The resulting low-pH or fermented environment suppresses spoilage organisms and produces pickles, beverages or vinegar with longer marketability.

Both routes reduce post-harvest losses, widen processing demand, improve price realisation and support local/FPO enterprises. Nevertheless, farmer gains require hygienic processing, quality control, cold-chain linkage, finance and assured markets. Biotechnology is therefore an enabling tool within a wider value chain, not a substitute for infrastructure.

### Examiner rationale

The response precisely answers “processing”, supplies two technically correct mechanisms, and connects them to farmer welfare.

## Q6. Fast Breeder Reactor, criticality and clean energy
**Primary route:** Science and Technology → [Nuclear Power and the Three-Stage Programme](../../topics/science-and-technology/nuclear-power-and-three-stage-programme.md) → Fast Breeder Reactors · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2026 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 6  
**Official question:** Distinguish between a Fast Breeder Reactor (FBR) and a thermal nuclear reactor. In the context of first indigenously developed prototype FBR at Kalpakkam, explain the term "criticality". What are its implications for clean energy future of our country?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Science and Technology → Nuclear Technology → Fast Breeder Reactors  
**Proposed topic slug:** `science-technology/fast-breeder-reactor-criticality`  
**Difficulty / demand:** High · Distinguish + Explain + Assess implications  
**Canonical owner:** [`Science-and-Technology/basic/04_Nuclear-Power-and-Three-Stage-Programme.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Science-and-Technology/basic/04_Nuclear-Power-and-Three-Stage-Programme.md)

### Demand decoding

Compare neutron spectrum, moderator, coolant and fuel-cycle role; define k=1; distinguish first criticality from commercial operation.

### Compact framework

`FBR-versus-thermal table → k=1 → dated PFBR milestone → clean-energy gains → engineering qualification`

### Model answer

| Thermal reactor | Fast Breeder Reactor |
|---|---|
| Uses a moderator to slow neutrons | Uses fast neutrons; no moderator |
| Commonly water/heavy-water cooled | PFBR uses liquid sodium coolant |
| Mainly consumes fissile fuel | Designed to breed more fissile material than it consumes |
| India’s Stage 1 PHWR pathway | Central to Stage 2 and the closed fuel cycle |

**Criticality** is the condition where the effective neutron multiplication factor **k = 1**: each fission generation sustains the next at a steady, controlled rate. The indigenous **500 MWe PFBR at Kalpakkam**, built by BHAVINI and designed by IGCAR, attained first criticality on **6 April 2026**.

Its significance lies in multiplying scarce fissile resources, converting U-238 into Pu-239, supporting eventual thorium/U-233 utilisation, and providing firm low-carbon power alongside renewables.

However, first criticality is neither grid synchronisation nor commercial operation. Sodium safety, reprocessing, waste, cost, regulatory capacity and long commissioning timelines qualify its clean-energy promise.

### Examiner rationale

The comparison is technically exact, “criticality” is correctly defined, and the dated milestone is not overstated.

## Q7. Infrastructure development and disaster-risk reduction

**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Disaster Management → Risk-Sensitive Infrastructure → Ecologically Sensitive Areas  
**Proposed topic slug:** `disaster-management/risk-sensitive-infrastructure`  
**Difficulty / demand:** High · Discuss  
**Primary owner:** [`Disaster-Management/basic/10_Landslides-Avalanches-and-GLOF-Risk.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Disaster-Management/basic/10_Landslides-Avalanches-and-GLOF-Risk.md)  
**Support owner:** [`Disaster-Management/basic/14_Urban-and-Critical-Infrastructure-Resilience.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Disaster-Management/basic/14_Urban-and-Critical-Infrastructure-Resilience.md)

### Demand decoding

Reject the false choice between development and safety; provide lifecycle governance, engineering and ecological measures with examples.

### Compact framework

`Avoid risk → assess cumulative impacts → resilient design → monitor/maintain → participate/enforce`

### Model answer

The contradiction is best managed through **risk-informed development**, because unsafe infrastructure ultimately destroys both lives and the investment itself.

First, use hazard-zonation, carrying-capacity studies and no-build buffers to avoid unstable slopes, floodplains and glacial-lake pathways. Second, require cumulative—not project-wise isolated—environmental and disaster-impact assessment for roads, dams, tunnels and tourism clusters. Fifth, monitor slopes, rainfall and glacial lakes through sensors/remote sensing, with threshold-based closures and evacuation plans. Local panchayats and communities should participate in siting and maintenance.

The **2021 Chamoli disaster** and recurring Himalayan landslides show cascading risks to hydropower and roads; **Joshimath subsidence** illustrates the cost of ignoring carrying capacity.

Fast approval must therefore be replaced by fast, transparent risk appraisal, independent compliance audits and lifecycle accountability.

### Examiner rationale

The answer supplies a governing principle, implementable measures, named examples and a balanced development-security conclusion.

## Q8. Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework
**Primary route:** Environment → [Multilateral Environmental Conventions CBD Basel Stockholm Montreal](../../topics/environment/multilateral-environmental-conventions-cbd-basel-stockholm-montreal.md) → KMGBF · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words

**Proposed topic slug:** `environment/kmgbf-india-commitments`

### Compact framework

`2030/2050 aim → Goals A-D → representative 23 targets → India’s NBSAP/actions → implementation gap`

### Model answer

The response covers the complete four-goal architecture, representative targets and India-specific implementation while qualifying output-based claims.

### Compact framework

### Model answer

Fake news becomes an internal-security threat when network speed and algorithmic amplification convert falsehood into panic, communal polarisation, vigilantism, electoral manipulation or hostile information warfare. The **2013 Muzaffarnagar video** and the **2012 North-East exodus panic** show online content producing physical disorder.

## Q11. India’s energy security
**Primary route:** Economy → [Energy Infrastructure Economics, Power, Fuels and Energy Security](../../topics/economy/energy-infrastructure-economics-power-fuels-and-energy-security.md) → Energy Security · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2026 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 11  
**Official question:** Explain the key challenges for India's energy security. What measures do you suggest for ensuring energy security along with economic growth and sustainability?  
**Marks / limit:** 15 / 250 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Infrastructure → Energy Security  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/energy-security-growth-sustainability`  
**Difficulty / demand:** High · Explain + Suggest  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/31_Energy-Infrastructure-Economics-Power-Fuels-and-Energy-Security.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/31_Energy-Infrastructure-Economics-Power-Fuels-and-Energy-Security.md)

### Demand decoding

Use availability, affordability, accessibility and sustainability as the diagnostic; propose a diversified transition rather than a single-fuel solution.

### Compact framework

`Import/geopolitical + affordability + grid/finance + transition risks → diversify + electrify + strengthen grid + efficiency + just transition`

### Model answer

Energy security is reliable, affordable and sustainable access to energy. India must expand supply for development while decarbonising a system still exposed to external and domestic vulnerabilities.

**Key challenges**

1. **Import exposure:** crude oil, gas and parts of clean-energy supply chains transmit wars, shipping disruptions, exchange-rate changes and price spikes.
2. **Demand growth:** urbanisation, manufacturing, cooling and mobility require rapid capacity addition.
3. **Coal dependence and pollution:** coal provides firm power and livelihoods but creates carbon, air-quality and water costs.
4. **Renewable variability:** solar/wind expansion requires storage, flexible generation, transmission and forecasting.
5. **Distribution weakness:** DISCOM losses, delayed payments and weak tariffs deter investment and impair reliability.
6. **Access and affordability:** clean cooking, reliable rural supply and industrial competitiveness remain distributional concerns.
7. **Critical minerals and technology:** batteries, solar modules and grid electronics create new import concentration.

**Measures**

- diversify oil/gas sources, strategic reserves and long-term contracts;
- accelerate domestic renewables, hydro, nuclear and sustainable bioenergy while retaining flexible transition capacity;
- build green-energy corridors, interstate transmission, storage, smart grids and demand response;
- improve efficiency through building codes, efficient appliances, rail/public transport and industrial electrification;

**Official question:** Discuss the different types of subsidies and supports provided by the Government of India to agricultural sector. Examine the related issues pertaining to Agreement on Agriculture of World Trade Organisation (WTO).  
**Marks / limit:** 15 / 250 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Agriculture → Farm Subsidies and WTO  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/farm-subsidies-wto-aoa`  
**Difficulty / demand:** High · Discuss + Examine  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/28_Direct-and-Indirect-Farm-Subsidies-and-WTO-Rules.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/28_Direct-and-Indirect-Farm-Subsidies-and-WTO-Rules.md)

### Demand decoding

Classify Indian support instruments, then examine AoA domestic-support rules, public-stockholding controversy and reform balance.

### Compact framework

`Support taxonomy → Amber/Green/Blue/Article 6.2 → stockholding/Peace Clause → asymmetric rules → sequenced reform`

### Model answer

- **price/output support:** MSP, procurement and buffer stocks;
- **input subsidies:** fertiliser, electricity, irrigation, seed and machinery;
- **credit support:** concessional crop loans and interest subvention;
- **income support:** PM-KISAN transfers;
- **risk support:** subsidised crop insurance and disaster relief;
- **public goods:** research, extension, roads, storage, irrigation and market infrastructure.

Under the WTO **Agreement on Agriculture (AoA)**, the principal issues are:

1. **Amber Box and de minimis:** trade-distorting support is disciplined. Developing countries generally have a 10% de minimis threshold, tested separately for product-specific and non-product-specific support.

Reform must protect small farmers and food security rather than abruptly withdraw support.

**Official question:** Mention salient features of "Mission Drishti". Discuss the imaging techniques used in the satellite launched on 3rd May 2026. Why it is being considered world's first satellite of its kind?  
**Marks / limit:** 15 / 250 words  
**Classification:** Science and Technology → Space Technology → Earth Observation and Sensor Fusion  
**Proposed topic slug:** `science-technology/mission-drishti-optosar`  
**Difficulty / demand:** High · Mention + Discuss + Explain  
**Canonical owner:** [`Science-and-Technology/basic/02_Satellites-NavIC-GAGAN-and-Applications.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Science-and-Technology/basic/02_Satellites-NavIC-GAGAN-and-Applications.md)

### Compact framework

`Mission facts → optical versus SAR → same-pass fusion → novelty basis → applications and claim qualification`

### Model answer

**Mission Drishti** is an Earth-observation satellite developed by Bengaluru-based private space startup **GalaxEye**. It was launched on **3 May 2026** aboard a SpaceX Falcon 9 rideshare mission. - a **multispectral optical imager**, which records reflected visible/infrared energy and provides intuitive, colour/spectral information but is constrained by darkness and cloud; and

### Model answer

**Agentic AI** denotes AI systems that pursue a high-level objective with limited step-by-step human direction by setting sub-goals, planning and taking actions. Unlike ordinary generative AI, which produces an output to a prompt, an agent can use tools and iterate until a task is completed.
