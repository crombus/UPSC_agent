## Q1(a). Ethical-trait matrix for civil-servant performance
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Work Culture and Public Funds](../../topics/ethics/work-culture-and-public-funds.md) → Foundational civil-service values · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** Identify five ethical traits on which one can plot the performance of a civil servant. Justify their inclusion in the matrix. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Foundational Values · Subtopic: Ethical performance assessment · Proposed slug: `civil-servant-ethical-trait-matrix` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Identify and justify

**Demand decoding:** Select five assessable traits, explain why each predicts ethical public performance, and show that the matrix complements—not replaces—objective outcomes.

### Model answer

**Concept:** An ethical-performance matrix should assess ends achieved and the character of the means used.

1. **Integrity:** consistency between duty and conduct; Kantian duty prevents manipulation of targets.  
2. **Impartiality:** decisions based on relevant criteria rather than identity, patronage or prejudice; Rawlsian fairness protects equal citizenship.  
3. **Empathy:** understanding citizens’ constraints; Sen’s capability approach exposes exclusions hidden by formally equal rules.  
4. **Accountability:** willingness to explain, document and correct decisions; it converts power into answerable power.  
5. **Moral courage:** readiness to resist unlawful pressure and report wrongdoing; it makes the other traits operational under adversity.

Each may be rated through behavioural indicators—reasoned orders, grievance disposal, conflict disclosures, treatment of vulnerable groups and response to pressure—alongside lawful outcomes.

**Qualification:** Ratings need multi-source evidence and appeal safeguards; otherwise subjective appraisal may punish principled dissent.

**Conclusion:** The matrix should reward public results achieved through integrity, fairness, humanity, answerability and courage.

**Why this earns marks:** It supplies exactly five traits, a justification and an administratively usable, qualified matrix.

## Q1(b). Essential values and prevention of unethical public conduct
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Corruption and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/corruption-and-administrative-ethics.md) → Integrity systems · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** Identify ten essential values that are needed to be an effective public servant. Describe the ways and means to prevent non-ethical behaviour in the public servants. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Foundational Values · Subtopic: Values and integrity management · Proposed slug: `public-service-values-prevent-unethical-conduct` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Identify and describe

**Demand decoding:** Name ten values and then provide preventive institutional, behavioural and accountability measures.

### Model answer

**Concept:** Public service joins competence with constitutional character.

**Ten essential values:** integrity, honesty, impartiality, non-partisanship, objectivity, empathy, compassion, dedication, accountability and moral courage.

**Thinker/framework:** Aristotle treats virtue as habituated conduct; the Second ARC favours an integrity system, not individual heroism.

**Prevention:** recruit and promote through transparent merit; give induction and dilemma-based ethics training; publish a practical code of ethics and conduct; require asset and conflict-of-interest disclosure; simplify procedures and reduce monopoly/discretion; digitise traceable workflows while retaining human appeal; rotate staff in vulnerable posts; protect whistle-blowers; conduct social and internal audits; ensure time-bound, proportionate disciplinary action; reward ethical leadership; and provide counselling where stress drives misconduct.

Citizen charters, grievance redress and speaking orders expose decisions.

**Qualification:** Surveillance alone breeds defensive compliance. Ethical example by seniors, fair workloads and protection for honest error are equally necessary.

**Conclusion:** Values become effective when culture, incentives, process design and accountability consistently support them.

**Why this earns marks:** It answers both numerical and preventive demands with individual and systemic measures.

## Q2(a). Digital technology as input for rational decisions
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Service Delivery and Technology Ethics](../../topics/ethics/service-delivery-and-technology-ethics.md) → Data-driven decision-making · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** Impact of digital technology as reliable source of input for rational decision making is a debatable issue. Critically evaluate with suitable example. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Technology Ethics · Subtopic: Reliability of digital evidence · Proposed slug: `digital-input-rational-decisions` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Critically evaluate with example

**Demand decoding:** Assess how technology improves rationality, expose reliability and ethical limits, and provide a concrete administrative example.

### Model answer

**Concept:** Digital technology can enlarge rationality through speed, scale, pattern detection and auditability, but data come from fallible institutions and are not self-interpreting.

**Thinker:** Herbert Simon’s bounded rationality explains why decision-support tools help; Sen’s capability approach asks whose reality the dataset omits.

**Application:** A district may use satellite imagery and geotagged records to detect illegal mining. This permits targeted inspection. Yet outdated images, wrong coordinates or manipulated entries can implicate innocent leaseholders. Ground verification and a hearing remain essential.

Digital inputs may reproduce historical discrimination, exclude people without devices, create opaque algorithmic authority, invade privacy and confuse correlation with causation. Conversely, rejecting them entirely preserves intuition, delay and discretion.

**Safeguards:** representative data, purpose limitation, independent validation, explainability, cybersecurity, error correction, human review and reasoned final orders.

**Conclusion:** Technology is a valuable evidentiary aid, not an autonomous ethical decision-maker; rationality requires reliable data plus contextual judgment and procedural justice.

**Why this earns marks:** It balances benefits and risks through a specific example and operational safeguards.

## Q2(b). Innovativeness and creativity in ethical dilemmas
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Emotional Intelligence and Ethical Dilemmas](../../topics/ethics/emotional-intelligence-and-ethical-dilemmas.md) → Creative ethical problem-solving · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** Besides domain knowledge, a public official needs innovativeness and creativity of a high order as well, while resolving ethical dilemmas. Discuss with suitable example. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Ethical Dilemmas · Subtopic: Innovative resolution · Proposed slug: `creativity-ethical-dilemmas` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Discuss with example

**Demand decoding:** Explain why technical knowledge is insufficient and how creativity can reconcile apparently competing ethical values without evading law.

### Model answer

**Concept:** Domain knowledge identifies lawful powers and feasibility; ethical creativity searches for a third path where binary choices sacrifice a legitimate value.

**Thinker:** Aristotle’s *phronesis* applies general principles sensitively to particulars, while Mary Parker Follett’s “integration” seeks solutions satisfying underlying interests rather than splitting the difference.

**Application:** During removal of hazardous street encroachments, an officer faces safety and mobility against livelihood and dignity. Eviction alone is harsh; inaction endangers citizens. Mapping vendors, consulting associations, creating nearby vending zones, phasing relocation and maintaining emergency lanes can protect both interests.

Creativity also improves disaster procurement, disability access and grievance redress. It must not become a label for bypassing tender rules, eligibility or equal treatment.

**Method:** identify stakeholders and non-negotiable rights; distinguish positions from interests; generate options; pilot the least harmful lawful alternative; document reasons and review outcomes.

**Conclusion:** Innovation is ethically valuable when it expands just choices under constitutional and evidentiary constraints.

**Why this earns marks:** It joins concept, thinker, administrative example, process and a legality qualification.

## Q3(a). Vivekananda on perseverance
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Indian Moral Thinkers](../../topics/ethics/indian-moral-thinkers.md) → Perseverance and disciplined action · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official instruction:** What does each of the following quotations mean to you?  
**Official quotation:** “Every work has got to pass through hundreds of difficulties before succeeding. Those that persevere will see the light, sooner or later.” — Swami Vivekananda. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Human Values · Subtopic: Perseverance · Proposed slug: `vivekananda-perseverance-difficulties` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Interpret and apply

### Model answer

**Concept:** Vivekananda presents perseverance as sustained, purposeful effort despite delay, resistance and repeated failure. Difficulty does not disprove a worthy goal; it tests commitment, learning and character.

**Thinker:** His practical Vedanta treats strength and disciplined action as expressions of human potential. Aristotle similarly sees virtue as a stable disposition built through repeated right action.

**Application:** A civil servant combating malnutrition may face poor records, vacancies and social prejudice. Perseverance means revising delivery methods, mobilising local workers, measuring outcomes and continuing lawful effort—not merely repeating a failed scheme. Scientists, social reformers and entrepreneurs likewise convert setbacks into feedback.

However, persistence is not obstinacy. An unethical end, disproved assumption or harmful method must be abandoned. Moral courage requires listening, adaptation and proportionality.

**Conclusion:** “Seeing the light” is not guaranteed instant success; it is the ethical achievement of remaining committed to a just purpose while learning from adversity.

**Why this earns marks:** It interprets the quotation, applies it and distinguishes resilient purpose from blind stubbornness.

## Q3(b). Dalai Lama on inner and outer peace
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Emotional Intelligence and Ethical Dilemmas](../../topics/ethics/emotional-intelligence-and-ethical-dilemmas.md) → Self-regulation and peace · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official instruction:** What does each of the following quotations mean to you?  
**Official quotation:** “We can never obtain peace in the outer world until and unless we obtain peace within ourselves.” — Dalai Lama. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Emotional Intelligence · Subtopic: Inner peace and social conduct · Proposed slug: `dalai-lama-inner-outer-peace` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Interpret and apply

### Model answer

**Concept:** Inner peace is freedom from uncontrolled fear, hatred, greed and ego. Such self-mastery shapes how individuals exercise power and respond to conflict.

**Thinker:** Buddhist ethics links mental intention to suffering, while Gandhi’s non-violence requires disciplining anger before seeking social reconciliation.

**Application:** A police officer provoked by a crowd needs emotional awareness and self-regulation to use dialogue and proportionate force rather than retaliatory violence. A negotiator who is not driven by humiliation can separate interests from personalities. At home, reflective listening prevents stress from becoming aggression.

Yet social peace cannot be reduced to private meditation. Injustice, exclusion and impunity require institutional reform; asking oppressed persons merely to become calm can legitimise structural violence.

Mindfulness, empathy and equanimity should therefore support—not replace—fair laws, dialogue and accountability.

**Conclusion:** Durable outer peace arises when inward self-command meets outward justice; neither psychological calm nor institutional order is sufficient alone.

**Why this earns marks:** It explains the causal link, uses administrative application and guards against depoliticising injustice.

## Q3(c). Erik Erikson on interdependence
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → Relational ethics and solidarity · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official instruction:** What does each of the following quotations mean to you?  
**Official quotation:** “Life doesn't make any sense without interdependence. We need each other, and the sooner we learn that, it is better for us all.” — Erik Erikson. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Human Values · Subtopic: Interdependence and solidarity · Proposed slug: `erikson-interdependence-solidarity` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Interpret and apply

### Model answer

**Concept:** Human autonomy is relational: language, identity, livelihood, care and security arise through families, communities, institutions and ecosystems. Interdependence converts isolated self-interest into reciprocity and shared responsibility.

**Thinker:** Erikson’s psychosocial account places development within relationships; Ubuntu’s “I am because we are” expresses the same moral insight.

**Application:** A pandemic shows that one person’s precautions protect strangers and that public health depends on workers, scientists, administrators and citizen trust. Cooperative federalism, self-help groups and community disaster response similarly pool capacities no individual possesses alone. In administration, departmental silos fail where nutrition, sanitation and education interact.

Interdependence does not erase individuality or justify oppressive conformity. Healthy cooperation protects autonomy, distributes burdens fairly and keeps institutions accountable. Dependence created by patronage is different from reciprocal solidarity.

**Conclusion:** Recognising mutual dependence produces humility, empathy and cooperation, enabling freedom to become socially sustainable rather than solitary.

**Why this earns marks:** It interprets the quote across personal, administrative and global levels with a clear qualification.

## Q4(a). Building a suitable public-service attitude
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Work Culture and Public Funds](../../topics/ethics/work-culture-and-public-funds.md) → Public-service attitude · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** Attitude is an important component that goes as input in the development of human being. How to build a suitable attitude needed for a public servant? **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Attitude · Subtopic: Formation of public-service attitude · Proposed slug: `building-public-service-attitude` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Explain how

**Demand decoding:** Define the desired attitude and specify formation mechanisms at individual, organisational and experiential levels.

### Model answer

**Concept:** Attitude is a learned tendency to think, feel and act. A public servant needs a constitutional, citizen-centric attitude marked by empathy, impartiality, optimism and accountability.

**Thinker/framework:** The cognitive-affective-behavioural model shows that information, emotion and repeated conduct must change together; Aristotle adds that virtue grows through habituation.

**Formation measures:**

- ethics education explaining dignity, equality and public trusteeship;
- field immersion with vulnerable communities to correct stereotypes;
- mentoring and ethical example by seniors;
- dilemma simulations, role-play and reflective journals;
- diverse teams and interdepartmental exposure;
- citizen feedback, audit and grievance hearings;
- reinforcement through fair appraisal, recognition and consequences;
- mindfulness and bias-awareness for emotional self-regulation.

For example, disability-access audits conducted with persons with disabilities transform abstract sympathy into informed administrative behaviour.

**Qualification:** Indoctrination or slogan-based training is insufficient; organisational incentives must not reward delay, secrecy or political compliance.

**Conclusion:** Suitable attitude develops when constitutional knowledge, humane contact, practice and ethical institutions align.

**Why this earns marks:** It explains both the desired attitude and credible mechanisms for building it.

## Q4(b). Emotional intelligence during a crisis of conscience
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Emotional Intelligence and Ethical Dilemmas](../../topics/ethics/emotional-intelligence-and-ethical-dilemmas.md) → Crisis of conscience · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** In case of crisis of conscience does emotional intelligence help to overcome the same without compromising the ethical or moral stand that you are likely to follow? Critically examine. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Emotional Intelligence · Subtopic: Conscience and ethical action · Proposed slug: `ei-crisis-of-conscience` · Difficulty: High · Demand: Critically examine

**Demand decoding:** Explain how EI assists moral action, but assess whether it can determine the ethically correct position.

### Model answer

**Concept:** A crisis of conscience occurs when duty, loyalty or consequences conflict. Emotional intelligence (EI) helps regulate emotions, understand others and communicate a principled decision.

**Thinker:** Daniel Goleman’s self-awareness, self-regulation, empathy and relationship management make moral judgment executable; Kant reminds us that emotional skill cannot replace duty.

**Application:** An officer ordered to conceal unsafe construction may fear transfer and empathise with colleagues facing deadlines. EI prevents panic, identifies fear and loyalty, anticipates stakeholders’ concerns, and enables calm documentation, dialogue and proportionate escalation while retaining the safety stand.

However, an emotionally skilled person may manipulate others or rationalise compromise. Conscience itself may reflect prejudice or misinformation. EI must therefore be guided by law, constitutional morality, rights, evidence and reasoned consequence analysis.

**Conclusion:** EI does not settle what is right; it helps a person discover biases, endure pressure and pursue the justified moral course without aggression or avoidable harm.

**Why this earns marks:** It critically separates moral compass from the emotional competence needed to follow it.

## Q5(a). Non-refoulement and democratic ethics
**Primary route:** Ethics → [International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/international-ethics.md) → Refugees and non-refoulement · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** “Refugees should not be turned back to the country where they would face persecution or human right violation.” Examine the statement with reference to ethical dimension being violated by the nation claiming to be democratic with open society. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: International Ethics · Subtopic: Refugee protection · Proposed slug: `refugees-non-refoulement-democratic-ethics` · Difficulty: High · Demand: Examine

**Demand decoding:** Explain the moral basis of non-refoulement, identify democratic values violated by return, and address legitimate state concerns.

### Model answer

**Concept:** Returning a refugee to persecution treats territorial control as superior to life and dignity. Non-refoulement embodies humanity and protection from grave harm.

**Thinkers/frameworks:** Kant’s hospitality limits hostility toward strangers; Rawlsian justice protects basic liberties; consequentialism recognises the potentially irreversible harm of forced return.

A democratic open society violates dignity, equality, liberty, compassion, rule of law and fidelity to its professed human-rights commitments when it returns people without individual assessment. Collective expulsion also ignores that vulnerability differs by political opinion, religion, ethnicity, gender or identity.

States nevertheless have legitimate security, capacity and fraud concerns. Ethical protection therefore requires fair screening, identity checks, temporary protection, reasoned decisions, appeal, family unity and international burden-sharing—not automatic admission or automatic rejection.

**Conclusion:** Sovereignty permits regulated borders, but democracy loses moral credibility when administrative convenience exposes a person to persecution. Security and compassion must meet through due process.

**Why this earns marks:** It identifies the violated values, supplies ethical frameworks and gives a balanced rights-respecting approach.

## Q5(b). Impartiality and non-partisanship
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Work Culture and Public Funds](../../topics/ethics/work-culture-and-public-funds.md) → Impartiality and non-partisanship · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** Should impartial and being non-partisan be considered as indispensable qualities to make a successful civil servant? Discuss with illustrations. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Foundational Values · Subtopic: Neutral public administration · Proposed slug: `impartiality-nonpartisanship-civil-service` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Discuss with illustrations

**Demand decoding:** Distinguish the two qualities, show why they are indispensable, illustrate their operation and qualify neutrality.

### Model answer

**Concept:** Impartiality means deciding on relevant criteria; non-partisanship means serving lawful government without party bias. Both uphold equality and state continuity.

**Thinker:** Weberian bureaucracy requires rule-bound neutrality, while constitutional morality fixes the values that neutrality must serve.

**Application:** A district officer must allocate drought relief by verified need, not caste or political recommendation. During elections, permissions and enforcement must apply equally to ruling and opposition parties. After a change of government, officials should implement the new lawful policy faithfully while preserving records and offering candid advice.

These qualities build trust, protect merit, prevent patronage and enable fearless counsel. Yet impartiality is not indifference to unequal circumstances: equal treatment may require disability accommodation or priority for the severely deprived. Non-partisanship is not silence before illegality.

**Conclusion:** A successful civil servant is politically neutral but constitutionally committed—fair among persons and parties, responsive to elected authority, and resistant to unlawful preference.

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes both values, illustrates them and rejects value-neutral passivity.

## Q6(a). Independent and empowered social audit
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Corruption and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/corruption-and-administrative-ethics.md) → Social accountability · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** An independent and empowered social audit mechanism is an absolute must in every sphere of public service, including judiciary, to ensure performance, accountability and ethical conduct. Elaborate. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Accountability · Subtopic: Social audit · Proposed slug: `independent-social-audit-public-services` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Elaborate

**Demand decoding:** Explain social audit, show how independence and empowerment improve performance and ethics, and address its application and limits including the judiciary.

### Model answer

**Concept:** Social audit publicly verifies whether authority, expenditure and service outcomes match law and entitlements. It supplements financial audit with lived evidence.

**Framework:** The Second ARC’s citizen-centric governance and Gandhi’s idea of public office as trusteeship support answerability to affected people.

Access to records, public hearings, retaliation protection and mandatory action-taken reports can expose ghost beneficiaries, delay and poor assets. MGNREGA social audits test muster rolls against workers’ testimony. In hospitals, schools and local bodies, scorecards reveal quality gaps.

For the judiciary, transparency in administration, case-management data, accessibility, complaint systems and reasoned institutional reporting can improve accountability without permitting popular pressure over adjudicatory outcomes.

**Qualification:** Social audit may be captured, politicised or breach privacy. It needs representative participation, data protection, independent follow-up and judicial/administrative review.

**Conclusion:** Empowered social audit makes public power visible and correctable, but must respect institutional independence and individual rights.

**Why this earns marks:** It explains mechanism, examples, judiciary-specific balance and safeguards.

## Q6(b). Integrity as an empowering value
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Moral Theories](../../topics/ethics/moral-theories.md) → Integrity and moral agency · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Official question:** “Integrity is a value that empowers the human being.” Justify with suitable illustration. **(150 words, 10 marks)**

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Human Values · Subtopic: Integrity and empowerment · Proposed slug: `integrity-empowers-human-being` · Difficulty: Moderate · Demand: Justify with illustration

**Demand decoding:** Explain the empowering mechanism of integrity, illustrate it and acknowledge the costs of principled conduct.

### Model answer

**Concept:** Integrity is adherence to justified values across thought, word and action. It frees a person from concealment, patronage and convenience.

**Thinker:** Kantian autonomy means acting from a principle one can rationally own; Gandhi’s unity of means and ends gives integrity social force.

An officer who records objections to an unsafe bridge may face transfer, but retains credibility, conscience and evidence for lawful oversight. Citizens trust predictable conduct; superiors find manipulation harder. A company recalling a defective product incurs loss yet protects consumers and earns legitimacy.

Integrity creates self-respect, clarity, courage, trust and moral authority. It reduces the burden of maintaining falsehoods.

**Qualification:** Integrity is not fidelity to uninformed belief. It requires evidence, prudence, empathy and competence, and may impose short-term costs.

**Conclusion:** Integrity empowers because it converts values into credible agency—the capacity to stand, speak and act without being owned by fear or favour.

**Why this earns marks:** It justifies empowerment causally, illustrates it and avoids romanticising integrity’s costs.

## Q7. Illegal sand-mining mafia threatens Sunil
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Environmental and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/environmental-and-administrative-ethics.md) → Integrity under criminal intimidation · **Secondary route:** Ethics → case-study method · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Case:** Sunil, an upright civil servant posted in a tribal district, acts against an illegal sand-mining mafia supported by compromised employees and local musclemen. The mafia threatens him, stalks his wife and elderly mother, and invokes the murder of a predecessor. **(250 words, 20 marks)**

**Official demands:** (a) Identify the different options available to Sunil in attending to this situation. (b) Critically evaluate each of the options listed by you. (c) Which of the above, do you think, would be the most appropriate for Sunil to adopt and why?

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Environmental and Administrative Ethics · Subtopic: Organised crime, official integrity and protection · Proposed slug: `sand-mining-mafia-threats` · Difficulty: High · Demands: Options; evaluation; justified choice

### Case framework

**Stakeholders:** Sunil and family; tribal communities; honest and compromised staff; lawful miners and transporters; river ecology; district administration, police and courts; citizens.  
**Values/conflicts:** duty and environmental justice versus personal safety; courage versus recklessness; tribal protection versus coercive enforcement; integrity versus corrupt nexus.

<a id="2021-mains-gs4-q7a"></a>
### Q7(a). Options available
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Environmental and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/environmental-and-administrative-ethics.md) → Options · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

1. Stop raids or seek transfer.  
2. Continue alone and secretly intensify enforcement.  
3. Negotiate or compromise with the mafia.  
4. Continue through a documented, multi-agency operation while seeking protection and institutional oversight.

<a id="2021-mains-gs4-q7b"></a>
### Q7(b). Critical evaluation
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Environmental and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/environmental-and-administrative-ethics.md) → Consequence analysis · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

Withdrawal protects family immediately but rewards intimidation, abandons communities and allows ecological theft. Acting alone shows courage but is reckless, leaks may defeat raids and evidence may collapse. Compromise is corrupt and deepens state capture. Institutional action distributes risk and improves legality, intelligence and continuity, though compromised insiders and delay remain hazards.

<a id="2021-mains-gs4-q7c"></a>
### Q7(c). Most appropriate course
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Environmental and Administrative Ethics](../../topics/ethics/environmental-and-administrative-ethics.md) → Justified action · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

Sunil should record threats; secure immediate police protection and safe accommodation for family; confidentially brief the district head, police leadership, mining and forest authorities; preserve digital and financial evidence; vet a small task force; rotate suspected staff through due process; use checkpoints, satellite/GPS evidence and lawful seizure; protect tribal witnesses; and seek judicial/vigilance oversight if local superiors are compromised.

**Safeguards:** intelligence-led rather than predictable raids, body cameras, asset and transport-chain investigation, community livelihood alternatives, environmental restoration, protected reporting and continuity plans if Sunil is transferred.

**Conclusion:** Courage is ethically sound when institutionalised: neither surrender nor solitary heroism, but lawful enforcement that protects people, evidence and ecology.

**Why this earns marks:** It covers stakeholders, values, options, consequences, action and safeguards while balancing duty with safety.

## Q8. Pressure to suppress examination malpractice
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Governance and International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/governance-and-international-ethics.md) → Institutional integrity in education · **Secondary route:** Ethics → case-study method · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Case:** As Vice Principal awaiting possible promotion, you face pressure from management to “resolve” a university flying squad’s detection of two students using unfair means with a senior lecturer’s help. The lecturer is close to management; the students are sons of an influential politician and major donor; students demand strict action. **(250 words, 20 marks)**

**Official demands:** (a) Discuss the ethical issues involved in the case. (b) Critically examine the options available with you as Vice Principal. What option will you adopt and why?

**Metadata:** Subject: Ethics · Topic: Institutional Ethics · Subtopic: Academic integrity and conflict of interest · Proposed slug: `examination-malpractice-pressure` · Difficulty: High · Demands: Ethical issues; options; justified choice

### Case framework

**Stakeholders:** students accused and student body; lecturer; honest examinees; Vice Principal; management; university; politician, donor and wider public.  
**Values/conflicts:** academic integrity and equality versus institutional reputation, funding and promotion; loyalty versus truth; due process versus public pressure.

<a id="2021-mains-gs4-q8a"></a>
### Q8(a). Ethical issues
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Governance and International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/governance-and-international-ethics.md) → Ethical issues · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

Cheating violates fairness and devalues honest students’ credentials. Lecturer assistance is abuse of authority. Management pressure creates conflict of interest, favouritism and obstruction of independent oversight; linking promotion to suppression is undue influence. Protesters’ demand is legitimate but cannot replace due process.

<a id="2021-mains-gs4-q8b"></a>
### Q8(b). Options and consequences
**Primary route:** Ethics → [Governance and International Ethics](../../topics/ethics/governance-and-international-ethics.md) → Options and consequences · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

1. **Suppress or bargain with the squad:** may avoid immediate scandal but is dishonest, unequal and likely to cause lasting reputational and legal harm.  
2. **Resign or withdraw:** preserves personal distance but abandons institutional responsibility.  
3. **Punish immediately:** appears firm but violates hearing and risks prejudgment.
