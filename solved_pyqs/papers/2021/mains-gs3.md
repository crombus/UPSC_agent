## Q1. GDP computation before and after 2015
**Primary route:** Economy → [National Income, GDP, GVA and Measurement](../../topics/economy/national-income-gdp-gva-and-measurement.md) → Methodology revision · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2021 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 1  
**Official question:** Explain the difference between computing methodology of India's Gross Domestic Product (GDP) before the year 2015 and after the year 2015.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → National Income → GDP Methodology  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/national-income-gdp-gva-and-measurement`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Explain and compare  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/01_National-Income-GDP-GVA-and-Measurement.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/01_National-Income-GDP-GVA-and-Measurement.md)

### Demand decoding
Compare the old and new series across base year, valuation, data coverage and production unit; do not confuse a statistical revision with a change in the meaning of output.

### Compact framework
`2004-05/factor cost/establishment data → 2011-12/basic and market prices/enterprise data → wider corporate coverage and SNA-2008 alignment`

### Model answer
In January 2015, India replaced the 2004–05 national-accounts series with a 2011–12 series.

- **Valuation:** the old headline was GDP at factor cost. The new series reports sectoral **GVA at basic prices** and GDP at market prices: `GDP = GVA + product taxes − product subsidies`.
- **Data:** private-corporate estimates earlier relied substantially on RBI samples and establishment-level information. The new series uses the MCA21 database of company filings, greatly widening coverage.
- **Unit and classification:** it adopts an enterprise approach for corporate activity and improved institutional-sector classification, rather than relying mainly on establishments.
- **Standards and coverage:** it incorporates SNA 2008 concepts, newer surveys and administrative datasets and captures previously under-covered activities.
- **Deflation:** sector-specific output and input indicators are used more extensively, although India does not apply universal double deflation.

Thus, post-2015 estimates are not obtained merely by changing prices; the base, valuation, coverage and databases changed, affecting sectoral shares and growth estimates.

### Examiner rationale
The answer distinguishes price-base revision from the deeper changes in valuation, unit of observation and corporate-data coverage.

## Q2. Capital Budget and Revenue Budget
**Primary route:** Economy → [Union Budget, Fiscal Policy and Deficit Indicators](../../topics/economy/union-budget-fiscal-policy-and-deficit-indicators.md) → Budget classification · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2021 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 2  
**Official question:** Distinguish between Capital Budget and Revenue Budget. Explain the components of both these Budgets.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Public Finance → Revenue and Capital Accounts  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/union-budget-fiscal-policy-and-deficit-indicators`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Easy-Moderate · Distinguish + Explain  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/09_Union-Budget-Fiscal-Policy-and-Deficit-Indicators.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/09_Union-Budget-Fiscal-Policy-and-Deficit-Indicators.md)

### Demand decoding
State the asset-liability test and classify both receipts and expenditure, including common exceptions such as grants for capital-asset creation.

### Compact framework
`recurring operations → revenue account; assets/liabilities → capital account`

### Model answer
The **Revenue Budget** records transactions that normally neither create assets nor reduce liabilities.

- **Revenue receipts:** tax revenue—income tax, corporation tax, GST and customs—and non-tax revenue such as dividends, fees and interest receipts. They neither create debt nor entail asset sale.
- **Revenue expenditure:** salaries, pensions, subsidies, interest, defence revenue expenditure and grants. It generally does not create government-owned assets.

The **Capital Budget** records transactions that alter government assets or liabilities.

- **Capital receipts:** borrowings, recovery of loans and disinvestment proceeds. Borrowing creates liabilities; recovery and disinvestment reduce financial assets.
- **Capital expenditure:** acquisition or construction of assets, equity infusion and loans to States, public enterprises or other bodies.

Classification depends on economic effect. A Union grant to another entity for creating an asset remains revenue expenditure in Union accounts because the Union does not own it. The distinction helps assess revenue deficit, asset creation and debt sustainability.

### Examiner rationale
It applies the accounting test consistently and explains every component rather than equating capital expenditure with all “development” spending.

## Q3. Land reforms and small farmers
**Primary route:** Economy → [Land Reforms, Green Revolution and Cropping Systems](../../topics/economy/land-reforms-green-revolution-and-cropping-systems.md) → Agrarian equity · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2021 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 3  
**Official question:** How did land reforms in some parts of the country help to improve the socio-economic conditions of marginal and small farmers?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Agriculture → Land Reforms  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/land-reforms-small-farmers-socioeconomic-outcomes`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Explain causal impact  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/11_Land-Reforms-Green-Revolution-and-Cropping-Systems.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/11_Land-Reforms-Green-Revolution-and-Cropping-Systems.md)

### Demand decoding
Explain mechanisms and regional evidence, while acknowledging uneven implementation and the limits of very small holdings.

### Compact framework
`abolish intermediaries + secure tenancy + redistribute/consolidate land → incentives, bargaining power and access → qualified welfare gains`

### Model answer
Where implemented seriously, land reforms changed both production incentives and rural power.

- **Abolition of intermediaries** made cultivators direct tenants of the State and reduced rent extraction.
- **Tenancy recording and security**, exemplified by West Bengal’s Operation Barga, protected sharecroppers from arbitrary eviction and improved their share of produce, encouraging investment.
- **Ceilings and redistribution**, though limited nationally, gave some landless and marginal households a productive asset and collateral-like security.
- **Consolidation**, notably in north-western India, reduced fragmented plots and enabled irrigation and mechanisation.
- Kerala’s tenancy reforms weakened landlord dominance and improved dignity, bargaining power and access to education and public programmes.

Ownership or secure possession also strengthened access to institutional credit, local political voice and resilience against exploitation. However, exemptions, benami transfers, poor records and litigation restricted redistribution; tiny plots alone cannot ensure viable incomes. Land reform works best with irrigation, credit, extension, markets and non-farm employment.

### Examiner rationale
The response links each reform instrument to an economic or social outcome and gives bounded state examples.

## Q4. Micro-irrigation and India's water crisis
**Primary route:** Economy → [Irrigation, Inputs, Credit, Insurance and Sustainable Agriculture](../../topics/economy/irrigation-inputs-credit-insurance-and-sustainable-agriculture.md) → Water-use efficiency · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2021 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 4  
**Official question:** How and to what extent would micro-irrigation help in solving India's water crisis?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Agriculture → Micro-irrigation  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/micro-irrigation-water-use-efficiency`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Mechanism + extent assessment  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/14_Irrigation-Inputs-Credit-Insurance-and-Sustainable-Agriculture.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/14_Irrigation-Inputs-Credit-Insurance-and-Sustainable-Agriculture.md)

### Demand decoding
Show how drip and sprinkler systems save water, then qualify the claim through crop choice, rebound effects, cost and aquifer governance.

### Compact framework
`controlled root-zone delivery → lower conveyance/evaporation loss → more crop per drop; but saved water ≠ aquifer recovery automatically`

### Model answer
Micro-irrigation applies water in controlled quantities: drip systems deliver it near roots, while sprinklers distribute it under pressure. It can:

- reduce conveyance, runoff and evaporation losses;
- improve uniformity, yields and fertiliser efficiency through fertigation;
- enable cultivation on uneven or water-scarce land;
- reduce waterlogging, salinity and pumping energy per unit of output;
- support precision scheduling under the **Per Drop More Crop** component.

Its contribution is nevertheless conditional. High initial costs, clogged emitters, unreliable power, fragmented holdings and weak extension constrain adoption. Sprinklers lose efficiency in wind and heat; drip is not equally suitable for every crop. Most importantly, farmers may use saved water to expand irrigated area or grow water-intensive crops, so basin-level extraction need not fall.

Micro-irrigation is therefore a major demand-management tool, not a complete solution. Metered or rational power, aquifer budgeting, crop diversification, rainwater harvesting, wastewater reuse and participatory groundwater regulation must complement it.

### Examiner rationale
The answer directly addresses “to what extent” by separating field-level efficiency from basin-level water saving.

## Q5. Technical strengths of the S-400
**Primary route:** Science and Technology → [Defence R&D, DRDO and Missile Systems](../../topics/science-and-technology/defence-randd-drdo-and-missile-systems.md) → Air defence · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2021 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 5  
**Official question:** How is S-400 air defence system technically superior to any other system presently available in the world?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Science and Technology → Defence Technology → Integrated Air Defence  
**Proposed topic slug:** `science-and-technology/s400-layered-air-defence`  
**Difficulty / demand:** High · Explain technical advantages with qualification  
**Canonical owner:** [`Science-and-Technology/basic/06_Defence-RandD-DRDO-and-Missile-Systems.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Science-and-Technology/basic/06_Defence-RandD-DRDO-and-Missile-Systems.md)

### Demand decoding
Explain system-level advantages—sensors, layered interceptors, target set and mobility—without repeating the stem's absolute superiority as an independently proven fact.

### Compact framework
`long-range surveillance + multi-band tracking + four interceptor envelopes + mobile command network → layered engagement`

### Model answer
The Russian S-400 Triumf is a mobile, networked surface-to-air system rather than a single missile. Its strength lies in:

- long-range surveillance and phased-array radars that can track numerous targets;
- multiple interceptor types, creating short-, medium-, long- and very-long-range engagement layers, with the 40N6 advertised up to about 400 km against suitable aerodynamic targets;
- ability to engage aircraft, UAVs, cruise missiles and some ballistic-missile threats at different altitudes;
- simultaneous tracking and engagement through an integrated command post;
- rapid road mobility, deployment and relocation, improving survivability;
- electronic counter-countermeasures and integration into a wider air-defence network.

This combination can deny airspace over a large radius and lets cheaper missiles address nearer targets. However, “superior to any other system” is context-dependent: actual performance varies with target type, terrain, radar horizon, crew proficiency, electronic warfare and integration. No air-defence system is impermeable; saturation and low-altitude masking remain challenges.

### Examiner rationale
It identifies why the system is technically formidable while avoiding an unverifiable absolute claim.

## Q6. Green Grids Initiative at COP26
**Primary route:** Environment and Ecology → [Renewable Energy and Green Hydrogen](../../topics/environment/renewable-energy-and-green-hydrogen.md) → Transnational solar grids · **Secondary route:** [Energy Infrastructure Economics](../../topics/economy/energy-infrastructure-economics-power-fuels-and-energy-security.md) · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2021 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 6  
**Official question:** Explain the purpose of the Green Grid Initiative launched at World Leaders Summit of the COP26 UN Climate Change Conference in Glasgow in November, 2021. When was this idea first floated in the International Solar Alliance (ISA)?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Environment and Ecology → Renewable Energy → OSOWOG  
**Proposed topic slug:** `environment/green-grids-initiative-osowog`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Explain + factual date  
**Canonical owner:** [`Environment-and-Ecology/basic/25_Renewable-Energy-and-Green-Hydrogen.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Environment-and-Ecology/basic/25_Renewable-Energy-and-Green-Hydrogen.md)

### Demand decoding
Explain the intermittency-and-geography problem solved by interconnected grids and correctly identify the ISA origin.

### Compact framework
`sun shines at different places/times → interconnected resilient grids → share surplus renewable power, storage and balancing`

### Model answer
The **Green Grids Initiative—One Sun One World One Grid (GGI-OSOWOG)**, launched by India and the United Kingdom at COP26, seeks to interconnect regional electricity grids so that renewable electricity can move across borders and time zones.

Its purposes are to:

- use the geographical complementarity of solar and wind resources;
- transmit surplus clean power to deficit regions, reducing curtailment and fossil backup;
- improve grid balancing, reliability and energy access;
- mobilise finance, technology, standards and institutional cooperation for cross-border interconnections;
- support a global transition consistent with the Paris Agreement.

The concept is ambitious because it requires harmonised grid codes, undersea and long-distance transmission, cyber-security, fair pricing and political trust; it therefore begins through regional integration rather than an instant single world grid.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi first publicly floated the **One Sun One World One Grid** idea at the **first Assembly of the International Solar Alliance in October 2018**.

### Examiner rationale
The answer supplies both the requested date and the grid mechanism, while identifying implementation constraints.

## Q7. WHO Air Quality Guidelines and NCAP
**Primary route:** Environment and Ecology → [Air Pollution and CPCB Standards](../../topics/environment/air-pollution-and-cpcb-standards.md) → Standards and policy response · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2021 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 7  
**Official question:** Describe the key points of the revised Global Air Quality Guidelines (AQGs) recently released by the World Health Organisation (WHO). How are these different from its last update in 2005? What changes in India's National Clean Air Programme are required to achieve these revised standards?  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Environment and Ecology → Air Pollution → WHO AQGs and NCAP  
**Proposed topic slug:** `environment/who-air-quality-guidelines-ncap`  
**Difficulty / demand:** High for word limit · Describe + Compare + Recommend  
**Canonical owner:** [`Environment-and-Ecology/basic/13_Air-Pollution-and-CPCB-Standards.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Environment-and-Ecology/basic/13_Air-Pollution-and-CPCB-Standards.md)

### Demand decoding
Present key tightened values, explain their health basis and convert a city-plan programme into a health-based, multi-pollutant airshed strategy.

### Compact framework
`lower safe thresholds → exposure reduction across pollutants → binding inventories, airsheds, enforcement and health tracking`

### Model answer
WHO's 2021 AQGs reflect harm even at low exposure. Annual guidelines were tightened from 2005 levels: **PM2.5 from 10 to 5 μg/m³, PM10 from 20 to 15, and NO2 from 40 to 10**. Short-term limits and staged interim targets were also specified for PM, ozone, NO2, SO2 and CO. These are health guidelines, not legally binding national standards.

India's NCAP then targeted a 20–30% reduction in PM by 2024 from the 2017 baseline. Alignment requires:

- revising NAAQS with interim milestones;
- shifting from city boundaries to regional **airshed** management;
- complete emission inventories and source-apportionment updates;
- enforceable sectoral action on transport, industry, power, dust, waste and household fuels;
- denser PM2.5, ozone and NO2 monitoring with public data and health indicators;
- performance-linked funding, independent verification and coordination across States.

The immediate aim should be continuous exposure reduction, not declaring cities safe merely on meeting an intermediate target.

### Examiner rationale
It answers all three parts with exact headline values and retains the legal distinction between WHO advice and Indian standards.

## Q8. India's earthquake vulnerability
**Primary route:** Environment and Ecology → [Air Pollution and CPCB Standards](../../topics/environment/air-pollution-and-cpcb-standards.md) → Q8. India's earthquake vulnerability · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Primary route:** Disaster Management → Earthquake Risk and Resilient Construction → Vulnerability and disaster evidence · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2021 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 8  
**Official question:** Discuss about the vulnerability of India to earthquake related hazards. Give examples including the salient features of major disasters caused by earthquakes in different parts of India during the last three decades.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Disaster Management → Earthquakes → Risk and Indian Events  
**Proposed topic slug:** `disaster-management/india-earthquake-vulnerability-events`  
**Difficulty / demand:** High for word limit · Discuss with examples  
**Canonical owner:** [`Disaster-Management/basic/05_Earthquake-Risk-and-Resilient-Construction.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Disaster-Management/basic/05_Earthquake-Risk-and-Resilient-Construction.md)

### Demand decoding
Combine tectonic exposure with built-environment vulnerability and geographically varied disaster examples; distinguish magnitude from damage.

### Compact framework
`hazard × exposure × structural/social vulnerability → shaking, liquefaction, landslide, fire and lifeline failure`

### Model answer
India is vulnerable because the Himalayan arc is an active continent-collision zone; the North-East and Andaman–Nicobar region are highly seismic; Kachchh contains active faults; and even the stable peninsular shield has damaging intraplate earthquakes. Dense cities, non-engineered masonry, soft-storey buildings, weak code enforcement and vulnerable lifelines convert shaking into disaster.

Examples illustrate different mechanisms:

- **Latur, 1993:** moderate intraplate event but severe rural mortality due to heavy stone-and-mud roofs and night-time collapse.
- **Jabalpur, 1997:** central-Indian intraplate earthquake damaged masonry and infrastructure.
- **Bhuj, 2001:** powerful Kachchh event caused widespread building collapse, liquefaction and lifeline disruption, including distant urban damage.
- **Sikkim, 2011:** Himalayan shaking triggered landslides and road isolation.
- **Nepal, 2015:** although centred outside India, it affected northern India and demonstrated transboundary Himalayan risk.

Risk reduction needs microzonation, code-compliant construction, retrofitting, trained masons, lifeline audits, drills and land-use control.

### Examiner rationale
The answer explains why similar hazards produce different losses and uses examples from distinct tectonic settings.

## Q9. Technology, globalisation and money laundering
**Primary route:** Internal Security → [Terror Financing, Money Laundering and FATF](../../topics/internal-security/terror-financing-money-laundering-and-fatf.md) → Laundering channels and controls · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2021 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 9  
**Official question:** Discuss how emerging technologies and globalisation contribute to money laundering. Elaborate measures to tackle the problem of money laundering both at national and international levels.  
**Marks / limit:** 10 / 150 words  
**Classification:** Internal Security → Financial Crime → Money Laundering  
**Proposed topic slug:** `internal-security/technology-globalisation-money-laundering`  
**Difficulty / demand:** High for word limit · Discuss + Measures  
**Canonical owner:** [`Internal-Security/basic/10_Terror-Financing-Money-Laundering-and-FATF.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Internal-Security/basic/10_Terror-Financing-Money-Laundering-and-FATF.md)

### Demand decoding
Trace technology and cross-border finance through placement, layering and integration, then provide matched domestic and international controls.

### Compact framework
`speed + anonymity/pseudonymity + jurisdictional arbitrage → layering → beneficial-ownership, analytics and cooperation`

### Model answer
Digital finance and globalisation lower the cost and time of layering illicit proceeds. Shell companies, trade misinvoicing, offshore centres and correspondent banking move funds across jurisdictions; online marketplaces, prepaid instruments, crypto-assets, mixers and mule accounts can obscure origin. Instant transfers and fragmented regulation permit rapid “jurisdiction shopping”. Technology itself is neutral: traceable ledgers can also aid investigation.

**National measures:** enforce the PMLA through predicate-offence investigation and proportionate attachment; strengthen FIU-IND suspicious-transaction analytics; verify beneficial ownership; regulate virtual-asset intermediaries on a risk basis; improve customs–tax–police–ED coordination; and protect due process while training investigators in digital forensics.

**International measures:** implement FATF standards and mutual evaluations; use Egmont Group FIU exchange, mutual legal-assistance treaties and extradition; share beneficial-ownership and tax information; supervise cross-border crypto transfers; and recover assets through time-bound cooperation.

Effective control follows the money across both technology and jurisdiction rather than banning an instrument without addressing the underlying network.

### Examiner rationale
The answer explains laundering mechanisms and assigns distinct functions to domestic enforcement and international cooperation.

Analyse national consequences beyond data theft and propose layered organisational, technical, legal and diplomatic defence.

Cross-border attacks exploit the internet's borderless architecture and attribution difficulty. ## Q11. India's V-shaped recovery
**Primary route:** Internal Security → [Cyber Security, Critical Information Infrastructure and Cybercrime](../../topics/internal-security/cyber-security-cii-and-cybercrime.md) → Q11. India's V-shaped recovery · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Primary route:** Economy → Economic Survey Synthesis and Macro Dashboard → Pandemic recovery · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2021 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 11  
**Official question:** Do you agree that the Indian economy has recently experienced V-shaped recovery? Give reasons in support of your answer.  
**Marks / limit:** 15 / 250 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Macroeconomy → Business-cycle Recovery  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/v-shaped-pandemic-recovery`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Take and defend a qualified position  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/26_Economic-Survey-Synthesis-and-Current-Macro-Dashboard.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/26_Economic-Survey-Synthesis-and-Current-Macro-Dashboard.md)

### Demand decoding
Define a V-shape, test it using output and high-frequency evidence available by January 2022, and distinguish aggregate rebound from complete, inclusive recovery.

### Compact framework
`lockdown-induced collapse → reopening + policy support + base effect → sharp aggregate rebound; but level, jobs and contact services lag`

### Model answer
A V-shaped recovery means a sharp output fall followed by a comparably rapid rebound. India broadly displayed this pattern in **aggregate activity**, though not uniformly across sectors or households.

Real GDP contracted by about 24% in Q1 of 2020–21 and 7.4% in Q2, then returned to positive growth in the second half. With reopening and a low base, growth rebounded strongly in the first two quarters of 2021–22. GST collections, e-way bills, electricity demand, rail freight, exports and the manufacturing PMI also recovered. Agriculture remained positive, while public capital expenditure, accommodative monetary policy, emergency credit guarantees, food support and vaccination aided demand and supply normalisation.

However, three qualifications matter:

1. high year-on-year growth partly reflected the exceptionally depressed base;
2. output recovery was uneven—contact-intensive services, informal firms and urban employment recovered more slowly;
3. repeated infection waves, learning losses, household debt and MSME stress meant welfare and jobs did not trace the same V.

Thus I agree with the Economic Survey's description for headline output and several high-frequency indicators, but not with an inference that all lost income or employment had been restored. Policy should consolidate recovery through vaccination, targeted social protection, MSME credit resolution, labour-intensive investment and sustained public capital expenditure.

### Examiner rationale
The answer takes a clear but qualified position, uses contemporaneous evidence and separates growth rates from levels and distribution.

## Q12. Infrastructure and inclusive growth
**Primary route:** Economy → [Infrastructure, PPPs, Logistics and Public Investment](../../topics/economy/infrastructure-ppps-logistics-and-public-investment.md) → Growth transmission · **Backlink:** [Paper-wise index](../../catalogue/PAPERWISE-INDEX.md)

**Identity:** 2021 · Mains · General Studies III · Compulsory Question 12  
**Official question:** “Investment in infrastructure is essential for more rapid and inclusive economic growth.” Discuss in the light of India's experience.  
**Marks / limit:** 15 / 250 words  
**Classification:** Economy → Infrastructure → Growth and Inclusion  
**Proposed topic slug:** `economy/infrastructure-rapid-inclusive-growth`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Moderate · Discuss  
**Canonical owner:** [`Economy/basic/18_Infrastructure-PPPs-Logistics-and-Public-Investment.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/18_Infrastructure-PPPs-Logistics-and-Public-Investment.md)

### Demand decoding
Explain productivity and inclusion channels, test them against Indian experience, and identify conditions under which infrastructure can exclude or create fiscal risk.

### Compact framework
`public capital → lower costs + crowd-in private investment + connect capabilities → jobs and inclusion, conditional on access and governance`

### Model answer
Infrastructure is both a productive input and an enabler of capabilities. Reliable power, roads, rail, ports and digital networks lower logistics and transaction costs, enlarge markets and crowd in private investment. Construction creates immediate jobs, while better connectivity raises long-run productivity.

India's experience shows the inclusive channels:

- rural roads connect farmers to mandis, schools and hospitals;
- electrification supports household welfare and rural enterprises;
- irrigation and storage reduce production risk and loss;
- telecom and digital public infrastructure enable payments, DBT and market access;
- metros, water, sanitation and affordable transport improve urban productivity and health.

The Golden Quadrilateral, PMGSY, renewable-energy expansion and digital connectivity illustrate network effects. The National Infrastructure Pipeline and National Monetisation Pipeline sought scale and lifecycle financing, while PPPs can bring expertise where risks are allocated credibly.

Land acquisition can displace communities; user charges can exclude the poor; prestige projects may crowd out maintenance; and weak appraisal produces cost overruns, stranded assets or contingent liabilities. Regional concentration can widen spatial inequality.

Therefore India needs transparent project selection, social and environmental assessment, multimodal planning through mechanisms such as PM Gati Shakti, competitive procurement, maintenance funding, universal-service safeguards and independent regulation. ### Model answer
Landslides occur when downslope forces exceed the shear strength of rock, soil or debris.

- preparedness, response, rehabilitation and reconstruction;
- institutional coordination, research and development.

Implementation should prioritise site-specific geotechnical investigation: a national susceptibility map cannot substitute for project-level design. Road agencies must manage drainage and spoil, audit slopes through the asset lifecycle and disclose risk to communities. ## Q20. Complexity and nexus of terrorism

**Official question:** Analyse the complexity and intensity of terrorism, its causes, linkages and obnoxious nexus. Also suggest measures required to be taken to eradicate the menace of terrorism.  
**Marks / limit:** 15 / 250 words  
**Classification:** Internal Security → Terrorism → Ecosystem and Counter-terrorism  
**Proposed topic slug:** `internal-security/terrorism-causes-nexus-response`  
**Difficulty / demand:** Broad · Analyse + Suggest  
**Canonical owner:** [`Internal-Security/basic/02_Terrorism-and-Counter-Terror-Architecture.md`](../../../upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Internal-Security/basic/02_Terrorism-and-Counter-Terror-Architecture.md)

Explain terrorism as an adaptive ecosystem rather than a single organisation, distinguish causes from justifications, map its criminal-state-digital nexus, and propose prevention, disruption and legitimacy.

### Compact framework

### Model answer
Terrorism is the planned use or threat of violence against civilians or symbolic targets to coerce a wider audience. Its complexity arises from diverse ideologies, cellular and lone-actor forms, transnational recruitment, cheap drones, encrypted communication and online propaganda. High-impact attacks can be mounted by small groups, while attribution is obscured through proxies.

- intelligence-led prevention and interoperable databases with Centre–State accountability;
- border, coastal, aviation and critical-infrastructure protection;
- financial investigation, beneficial-ownership transparency and FATF cooperation;
- specialised but rights-bound investigation by police and NIA, modern forensics, witness protection and speedy fair trials;
- removal of terrorist content with judicial safeguards, counter-messaging and digital literacy;
- community partnerships, early intervention, prison de-radicalisation and rehabilitation;
- diplomacy against sanctuary and sponsorship, plus MLAT and extradition cooperation;
- responsive governance and conflict resolution that deny recruiters exploitable grievances.

“Eradication” cannot mean force alone. Sustainable counter-terrorism continually reduces intent, capability and opportunity while preserving constitutional legitimacy—the source of public intelligence and democratic resilience.
