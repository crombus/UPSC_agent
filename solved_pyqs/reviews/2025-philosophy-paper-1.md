## Attribution and precision audit

1. Plato’s Forms are distinguished from spatially separate objects; the participation problem is retained.
2. Aristotle’s hylomorphism is limited to sensible natural substance, with prime matter and pure actuality qualified.
3. Sartre’s *pour-soi/en-soi* distinction is not reduced to Cartesian mind/body dualism.
4. Russell’s description analysis includes existence, uniqueness and predication; Strawson is treated as a later objection.
5. Hegel’s criticism targets the unknowable remainder while Kant’s negative-noumenon reply is preserved.
6. Spinoza’s dictum is traced to determination and finitude without making modes sheer unrealities.
7. Hume’s causal critique and Kant’s Second-Analogy response are kept distinct.
8. Wittgenstein’s transition includes the colour-exclusion pressure, language-games, forms of life and methodological therapy.
9. Strong and weak verification are distinguished without confusing verification with truth.
10. Moore’s act/object distinction is not overclaimed as a complete proof of direct realism.
11. Husserl’s transcendental ego is not treated as Cartesian mental substance; intersubjectivity is included.
12. Quine’s holism is constrained by pragmatic theory-choice and not represented as “anything goes.”
13. Cārvāka’s target is certainty-producing inference through unestablished *vyāpti*, with practical probability qualified.
14. Nyāya and Buddhist views of *pramāṇa/pramāṇaphala* are linked to realist and momentary ontologies without making all Buddhists external-world deniers.
15. Nyāya’s validity theory is extrinsic for both validity and invalidity.
16. Rāmānuja’s seven untenabilities are complete and correctly ordered.
17. Gautama’s perception definition analyses every clause and distinguishes ordinary from extraordinary perception.
18. Bhāṭṭa non-cognition is distinguished from Prābhākara cognition of the unqualified locus and Nyāya perception of absence.
19. Śaṅkara’s critique of Sāṃkhya includes why it is the chief rival and acknowledges Advaita’s own ignorance problem.
20. Yoga’s God is a special self and aid to liberation, not straightforwardly Nyāya’s creator or an object of final merger.
21. Jaina perspectivism is grounded in objective pluralistic realism, not subjective relativism.
22. Original–reflection theory is presented as a later Advaita explanatory model, not uniformly attributed to Śaṅkara.
23. Aurobindo’s integration is derived through involution, evolution, Supermind and transformation rather than vague compromise.
24. Buddhist nirvāṇa is reconciled with momentariness and no-self through conditioned cessation and causal continuity, with Madhyamaka qualification.
