## Scope and method

All eight English prompts were reviewed separately against the Essay standard in
`solved_pyqs/INSTRUCTIONS.md` and the canonical Essay framework. Checks covered:

- exact wording, American spelling in “color,” section and printed order;
- stable anchor, metadata, primary route, tags and proposed slug;
- interpretation, operative tension and likely misreadings;
- a qualified and contestable thesis;
- multidimensional progression with explicit transitions;
- a fair counter-view with a switching condition and altered synthesis;
- substantial continuous model treatment rather than a GS listicle;
- analytical use of examples through claim → evidence → analysis → qualification;
- direct, earned conclusion;
- absence of invented quotation, author attribution, statistics and false precision.

## Evidence review

- Constitutional references are limited to well-established provisions: Articles 14–17 and the
  Directive Principles at a general level.
- Statutory and institutional illustrations include the Right to Information Act, 2005, and
  ISRO’s Chandrayaan-2/Chandrayaan-3 learning sequence.
- Historical illustrations include *satyagraha*, constitutional accommodation and institutional
  learning from crisis.
- No uncertain numerical data or author attribution is used. Examples are qualified rather than
  presented as universal proof.

## Defects found and corrected

1. Absolute readings were bounded: objectivity versus standpoint, peace versus appeasement,
   creativity versus material constraint, adversity versus trauma, patience versus neglect, age
   versus wisdom, process versus outcomes, and contentment versus deprivation.
2. Every model was checked for prompt return in the opening, paragraph links and conclusion.
3. Repetitive GS-style dimension headings were kept in the planning apparatus but removed from
   the continuous model essays.
4. The source’s exact American spelling “color” and continuous numbering 1–8 were preserved.
