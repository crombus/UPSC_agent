## Attribution and argument-quality audit

1. Plato’s Forms connect degrees of reality to degrees of cognition and retain the participation/Third Man objection.
2. Russell’s logical analysis uses the theory of descriptions and correctly culminates in atomic propositions and facts.
3. Later Wittgenstein’s form of life is grounded in use, language-games and public rule-following without collapsing truth into consensus.
4. Husserl’s anti-psychologism distinguishes contingent acts from ideal validity; transcendental constitution is not treated as empirical creation.
5. Kantian apperception is the formal synthetic unity of consciousness, not a known Cartesian substance; space and time remain forms of intuition.
6. Heidegger’s Being-in-the-world includes equipment, being-with, care, the “they,” anxiety, death and resoluteness, with ethical and political limits.
7. Aristotle’s identity answer integrates hylomorphism, potentiality–actuality and four-causal process while acknowledging substantial change and replacement problems.
8. Spinoza’s substance monism distinguishes attributes and modes, immanent causation, *natura naturans/naturata* and qualified pantheism.
9. Kant’s four antinomies are complete and their mathematical/dynamical resolutions are restricted by transcendental idealism.
10. Hegelian dialectic uses determinate negation and sublation rather than a mechanical thesis–antithesis–synthesis formula.
11. Early Wittgenstein’s pictorial and logical forms are distinguished through structural isomorphism, bipolarity and showing.
12. Kierkegaard’s subjectivity is lived appropriation, not relativism; all three spheres and the leap are critically treated.
13. Descartes’ immediate cogito is distinguished from mediated world-knowledge; the Cartesian circle and substance overreach are retained.
14. Locke’s anti-innatism, sensation/reflection, construction of ideas and three degrees of knowledge are all present.
15. Sāṃkhya’s five classical proofs of consciousness (*puruṣa*) are complete and their limited inferential reach is evaluated.
16. Vaiśeṣika universal (*sāmānya*) is correctly treated as real, eternal, repeatable and related through inherence (*samavāya*), with regress and exclusion objections.
17. Pātañjala Yoga now precisely distinguishes cognitive absorption (*samprajñāta samādhi*), its object-focused attainments (*samāpattis*), non-cognitive absorption (*asamprajñāta samādhi*) and fully seedless absorption (*nirbīja samādhi*).
18. Jaina karma correctly runs from influx and bondage through stoppage and shedding; omniscience (*kevala-jñāna*) is distinguished from liberation (*mokṣa*).
19. Apparent transformation (*vivartavāda*) is presented as a qualified development, not a straightforward entailment, of real transformation (*pariṇāmavāda*).
20. Buddhist momentariness and karma are reconciled through causally continuous streams, with Nyāya, Mīmāṃsā/Vedānta and causal-transmission objections.
21. Jaina non-one-sidedness and conditional predication are distinguished from unrestricted relativism; the self-reference objection receives a direct reply.
22. Mīmāṃsā postulation (*arthāpatti*) is distinguished from inference through explanatory incompatibility and the absence of antecedently known pervasion.
23. Prabhākara’s non-discrimination theory and Kumārila’s contrary-apprehension theory are accurately related to their shared intrinsic-validity doctrine.
24. The Buddhist Three Jewels are defined relationally and reconciled with no-self through conventional designation and causal continuity.
25. Nyāya’s defence of inference includes positive and negative agreement, hidden conditions (*upādhi*), hypothetical reasoning (*tarka*) and the residual induction problem.
26. Advaita’s God, individual self and witness are distinguished through levels of reality and limiting adjuncts; lucidity (*sattva*) in cosmic appearance is stated precisely.
27. Aurobindo’s psychic, spiritual and supramental transformations are complete and connected to ascent, descent and whole-nature transformation.
28. Rāmānuja and Madhva are compared through ontology, means, liberated experience, eternal difference and graded bliss without assimilating either to Advaita.

## Defects found and corrected

1. The Yoga answer previously risked equating non-cognitive absorption (*asamprajñāta samādhi*) with fully seedless absorption (*nirbīja samādhi*) and blurred the four cognitive accompaniments with the object-focused attainments. The stages are now explicitly distinguished.
2. The Jain karma answer used the compressed expression “liberation (*mokṣa/kevala*)”, which could conflate omniscience with final release. It now distinguishes omniscience (*kevala-jñāna*) from complete liberation (*mokṣa*).
3. The Advaita answer referred vaguely to Māyā’s “pure quality.” It now identifies the predominance of lucidity (*sattva*) in the God standpoint.
4. Earlier broken topic-route filenames for Phenomenology and Sri Aurobindo had already been corrected in the paper; the final link audit confirms zero unresolved links.
