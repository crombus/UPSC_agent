## Scope and method

All eight English prompts were reviewed separately against the Essay standard in
`solved_pyqs/INSTRUCTIONS.md`. Checks covered:

- exact wording, punctuation, section, printed number and official order;
- one unique stable explicit anchor and one unique GitHub-safe proposed slug per prompt;
- paper metadata, primary topic route, tags and paper-index backlink;
- interpretation, operative tension, hidden assumptions and likely misreadings;
- a qualified, contestable and prompt-specific thesis;
- multidimensional progression with load-bearing transitions;
- a fair counter-view that materially changes the synthesis;
- substantial continuous model treatment rather than a GS-style listicle;
- evidence used through claim → named example/concept → analysis → qualification;
- a direct conclusion that returns to the prompt in refined form;
- absence of invented quotation, author attribution, unsupported statistics and false precision;
- filesystem resolution of every forward Markdown link and presence of exact reverse topic links.

## Evidence review

- Q1 uses ecosystem cycling, monoculture vulnerability, public goods and Chipko for defined
  analytical purposes; none is presented as universal proof that human economies are forests.
- Q2 uses Bhakti poetry, Kabir, `Vande Mataram`, Tagore, Subramania Bharati, Dalit writing and
  women’s poetry to demonstrate changes in moral vocabulary; the essay avoids attaching an author
  to the unattributed official prompt.
- Q3 uses vaccination, engineering, India’s space programme, colonial classification, the freedom
  movement and climate change to distinguish empirical authority from the ethical selection of
  ends.
- Q4 uses the Chandrayaan-2/Chandrayaan-3 learning sequence and Indian equality reforms as bounded
  illustrations of learning from failure and moral courage, not as proof that every risk is wise.
- Q5 relies on established preparedness mechanisms—maintenance, surveillance, primary care,
  fiscal buffers, institutional checks and climate adaptation—without unsupported numerical
  claims.
- Q6 uses constitutional amendment, judicial interpretation, scientific corrigibility and river
  ecology as examples of continuity maintained through change.
- Q7 uses ordinary social interaction, unequal workplace power, diplomacy, satire and the
  `Mona Lisa` to show context-dependent meaning while explicitly rejecting inferred consent.
- Q8 uses professional duty, emergency triage, consumer standards, digital choice architecture,
  constitutional rights and constrained labour choices to distinguish available options from
  morally justified options.
- No exact quotation is invented inside a model essay, no unsupported statistic or false precision
  is used, and no repository-authored treatment is represented as an official UPSC model answer.
