## Scope and method

All eight official English topics were reviewed separately against the Essay standard in
`solved_pyqs/INSTRUCTIONS.md` and the repository Essay workflow. The review checked:

- exact wording, section and official order;
- one stable anchor and one primary route per topic;
- interpretation, hidden tension and likely misreadings;
- a contestable, qualified thesis;
- a multidimensional outline with a logical progression;
- a fair counter-view and an altered synthesis;
- model-essay depth, prompt fidelity and direct conclusion;
- named examples used through claim → evidence → analysis → qualification;
- absence of fabricated quotations, generic GS checklists and broken OCR;
- tags, proposed slug and a “Why this earns marks” note.

## Evidence review

- Constitutional references used: Articles 14–16, 17, 19, 29–30, 32, 39A and 279A, plus the
  73rd Amendment’s minimum one-third reservation rule for panchayat seats.
- Statutory/institutional examples used: Right to Education Act, 2009; Right to Information Act,
  2005; GST Council; ISRO’s Chandrayaan-2/Chandrayaan-3 sequence.
- Historical/social illustrations used: Gandhian *satyagraha*, Bhoodan, Chipko, Santiniketan,
  Nalanda/Takshashila debate traditions and Amul’s cooperative history.
- Exact statistics and fabricated quotations were avoided. Examples are qualified so that no
  single case is treated as universal proof.

## Defects found and corrected

1. Initial model bodies were shorter than the intended exam-ready depth; each was expanded with
   prompt-specific analytical paragraphs rather than padding.
2. Potential metaphor traps were explicitly repaired: gratitude versus servility, resilience
   versus trauma-romanticisation, inquiry versus paralysis, responsiveness versus populism, and
   decisiveness versus haste.
3. Every topic was given an explicit stable anchor, classification, tags, proposed slug and
   examiner takeaway.
