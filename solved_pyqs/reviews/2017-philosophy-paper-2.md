## 4. Accuracy safeguards

The review specifically confirmed that:

1. Ambedkar's caste analysis is structural—graded inequality, endogamy and religious
   legitimation—not reduced to individual prejudice.
2. Kautilya's law of punishment (*daṇḍanīti*) and seven-limbed state (*saptāṅga*) are not equated
   with Bodin's juridical doctrine of absolute and perpetual sovereignty.
3. Marx's emancipatory ideal is distinguished from authoritarian party-state practice.
4. Capital punishment is assessed separately for murder, rape and corruption rather than through
   one undifferentiated emotional claim.
5. Buddhism is not assigned a permanent soul or creator: no-self (*anātman*), dependent
   origination (*pratītyasamutpāda*) and liberation (*nirvāṇa*) are accurately related.
6. Jain enduring soul (*jīva*) is distinguished from Buddhist causal continuity.
7. Patañjali's Lord (*Īśvara*) is a special person (*puruṣa-viśeṣa*), not imported as a standard
   creator God; devotion to God (*Īśvara-praṇidhāna*) is treated as soteriologically important
   without making creation its function.
8. The kalām, Thomistic sustaining-cause and Leibnizian contingency forms of the cosmological
   argument are compared by premises and explanatory target.
9. Aquinas's analogy and Tillich's symbol are kept conceptually distinct.
10. Voluntarism is limited to commitment, attention and practice rather than an implausible direct
    power to manufacture belief.

English concepts lead throughout; Sanskrit terms follow as accurate IAST glosses. Checked forms
include *matsyanyāya*, *daṇḍanīti*, *saptāṅga*, *sarvodaya*, *ātman*, *jīva*, *anātman*,
*pratītyasamutpāda*, *nirvāṇa*, *āryāṣṭāṅgamārga*, *puruṣa*, *puruṣa-viśeṣa*,
*Īśvara-praṇidhāna*, *samādhi*, *kaivalya*, *bhakti*, *prapatti*, *anekāntavāda*,
*Viśiṣṭādvaita* and *nirguṇa Brahman*.
