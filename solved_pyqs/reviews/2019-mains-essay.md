## Evidence and depth review

- Q1 preserves objective truth standards while treating humility, scientific correction, courts,
  journalism and dissent as wisdom-supporting practices.
- Q2 uses constitutional morality and anti-caste reform for bounded analysis and does not treat
  every inherited norm as a valid value.
- Q3 explains externalities across environment, health and digital life, then limits collective
  authority through rights, dissent and participatory definition of welfare.
- Q4 explicitly rejects passive acceptance, effort-only success and victim-blaming, and treats
  structural opportunity and ethical ends as necessary qualifications.
- Q5 recognises cultural continuities across borders while confronting caste, patriarchy,
  communal exclusion and the state’s role in protecting individual citizenship.
- Q6 avoids unsupported statistics and distinguishes access from quality, enrolment from learning,
  hospitals from primary care, and necessary foundations from a complete development strategy.
- Q7 does not presume viewpoint-free journalism and rejects a government arbiter of truth while
  defending transparency, correction, plurality and narrowly tailored legal accountability.
- Q8 distinguishes occupation from task, reskilling from upskilling and job quantity from quality,
  while avoiding both technological fatalism and automatic job-creation optimism.
