## 4. Accuracy safeguards

The review specifically confirmed that:

1. Caste is treated as Ambedkarite graded inequality, not merely individual prejudice.
2. Multicultural recognition is limited by member voice, dissent, exit and equal rights.
3. Marxist humanism is not equated with party-State collectivism.
4. Austin’s jurisprudential command theory is distinguished from Hobbes’s justificatory contract.
5. Racial supremacy is treated as a paradigmatic but not universal sufficient cause of genocide.
6. Restorative justice is not confused with leniency; proportionality and public censure remain.
7. Beauvoir’s social construction thesis is qualified by embodiment and intersectionality.
8. Alienation is neither the only injustice nor assumed wholly eliminable.
9. Gandhi’s evolving position is not reduced to simple defence of caste; Ambedkar’s structural
   disagreement and political safeguards remain central.
10. Q3(c) answers the printed *nīti*-based direction while accurately noting that Sen’s principal
    critique runs from realised *nyāya* against justice reduced to ideal institutions.
11. Collective rights cannot license internal oppression.
12. Democracy is distinguished from electoral majoritarianism.
13. Monotheism is not equated with exclusivism or violence.
14. The is–ought distinction is retained logically without denying that facts constrain ethics.
15. Immortality arguments are presented as non-demonstrative.
16. Hickian pluralism and Jain many-sidedness (*anekāntavāda*) are not made to erase contradictions.
17. Buddhism is not assigned an immortal transmigrating soul; rebirth is causal continuity under
    dependent origination (*pratītyasamutpāda*).
18. Non-cognitive accounts are not treated as exhaustive of believers’ ontological claims.
19. Understanding the idea of God is distinguished from accepting its instantiation or necessary
    existence.
20. Plantinga’s defence answers logical compatibility more successfully than evidential evil.
21. A first cosmological cause is not assumed to possess all attributes of the God of religion.
22. Advaita teaches self–*Brahman* identity, not dependence; Viśiṣṭādvaita preserves dependent
    individuality, devotion and grace.
23. Immanence and transcendence are related to human dignity, freedom and dependence rather than
    listed abstractly.
24. Reason supplies possible cumulative warrant, criticism and interpretation, not compulsory faith.
25. Buddhist no-self (*anātman*) and Jain enduring soul (*jīva*) remain genuinely conflicting
    interpretations of liberating experience.

English concepts lead throughout. Relevant accurate IAST includes *varṇa*, *ātman*, *Brahman*,
*jīva*, *anātman*, *pratītyasamutpāda*, *saṃsāra*, *mokṣa*, *avidyā*, *jñāna*, *śravaṇa*,
*manana*, *nididhyāsana*, *jīvanmukti*, *bhakti*, *prapatti*, *anitya*, *nirvāṇa*,
*kevala-jñāna*, *anekāntavāda*, *syādvāda*, *nīti* and *nyāya*.

## 7. Defects found and corrected

The independent review found and corrected:

1. Q3(c) initially privileged Sen’s familiar critique of institutional *nīti* from realised
   *nyāya* and did not sufficiently answer the paper’s reverse wording. The final answer now
   reconstructs *nīti*’s procedural critique before explaining Sen’s mutual correction.
2. Q7(c) initially described both schools as realising dependence on ultimate reality, which
   contradicted Advaita’s self–*Brahman* identity. The common ground is now limited to release from
   *saṃsāra*, followed by the correct metaphysical contrast.
3. The image-only source required visual restoration of marks and punctuation rather than reliance
   on absent embedded text.
4. The rebirth and religious-experience answers were checked to prevent attribution of a permanent
   self to Buddhism or denial of the Jain enduring *jīva*.
5. The power, democracy, multiculturalism and collective-rights answers were checked against
   unqualified majoritarian or communitarian conclusions.
