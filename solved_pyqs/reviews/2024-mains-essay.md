## Scope and method

All eight English prompts were reviewed separately against the Essay standard in
`solved_pyqs/INSTRUCTIONS.md`. Checks covered:

- exact wording, punctuation, printed defects and Section B’s restarted numbering;
- stable explicit anchor, metadata, route, tags and proposed slug;
- interpretation, operative tension and likely misreadings;
- a qualified and contestable thesis;
- multidimensional progression with explicit transitions;
- a fair counter-view that changes the synthesis;
- substantial continuous model treatment rather than a GS listicle;
- analytical examples through claim → evidence/example → analysis → qualification;
- a direct, earned conclusion;
- absence of invented quotation, author attribution, unsupported statistics and false precision.

## Evidence review

- Constitutional references are confined to well-established provisions: Articles 48A,
  51A(g), and 51A(h), plus general constitutional mechanisms such as judicial review and audit.
- Statutory/institutional illustrations use the Right to Information Act, 2005, Tele-MANAS,
  ISRO’s Chandrayaan-2/Chandrayaan-3 learning sequence and universal adult franchise.
- Historical or social illustrations such as Chipko and non-cooperation are used for a specific
  analytical function and are not presented as universal proof.
- No numerical mental-health, forest-cover, climate or economic claim is asserted.
- No author is attached to any unattributed official prompt.

## Defects found and corrected

1. Normalised coaching versions were rejected: “futures” remains plural; the happiness prompt
   retains its comma and capitalised second “Happiness”; the FOMO prompt retains no comma before
   “precipitating.”
2. Internal labels B5–B8 were separated from the paper’s printed Section B numbers 1–4.
3. Absolute readings were bounded: ecological decline versus regeneration, knowledge power versus
   domination, happiness versus deprivation, doubt versus denial, digital harm versus benefit,
   power versus corruption, simplicity versus simplism, and action versus irreversible error.
4. Planning headings were kept outside the continuous model essays; the essays themselves proceed
   through linked paragraphs rather than GS-style dimension lists.
5. Examples were checked for argument function and qualified where they could otherwise imply
   universal causation.
