## 7. Philosophical-quality safeguards

The review specifically confirmed that:

- Enlightenment humanism is not treated as the only historical humanism.
- Caste politics is evaluated as both hierarchy-reproduction and subordinated-group mobilisation.
- Systemic explanation of corruption does not erase personal culpability.
- Restrictions are not assumed either always to destroy or always to increase liberty.
- Bodin's “absolute” sovereignty is not equated with wholly arbitrary rule.
- Gandhi's changing position is not flattened, and the Ambedkarite structural objection is explicit.
- Marxian equity is grounded in formal equality, class relations and the two phases of communism.
- Gender construction is not equated with free individual choice or denial of embodiment.
- Multicultural description is separated from normative accommodation.
- Anti-propaganda remedies confront the censorship dilemma.
- Divine Right does not collapse monarchy and theocracy into one concept.
- Spinoza's God is not presented as a conventional personal creator.
- Buddhist causal continuity is distinguished from substantial identity.
- Non-cognitivism is not equated with meaningless speech.
- Aquinas' Five Ways are separately stated and do not by themselves establish every divine attribute.
- Rāmānuja's God–self relation preserves both dependence and numerical difference.
- Human responsibility for evil does not automatically discharge the creator's justificatory burden.
- Absolute truth is separated from an infallible monopoly upon its formulation.
- Advaita and Viśiṣṭādvaita are compared across ontology, agency, means and liberated state.
- James's private authority claim is not inflated into public proof.
- A posteriori arguments are preferred only as defeasible, shareable reasoning.
- Śaṅkara's empirical theism is distinguished from ultimate non-dual Brahman.
