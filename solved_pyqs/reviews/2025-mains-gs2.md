## High-risk facts specifically checked

- **Q1:** *Lok Prahari* (2018) was applied correctly: non-disclosure of assets and income sources can constitute Section 123(2) undue influence; disproportionate growth itself triggers scrutiny and action rather than bypassing proof through an election petition.
- **Q2:** *L. Chandra Kumar* review and the *Madras Bar Association* independence line were distinguished from Parliament’s 2021 rationalisation policy.
- **Q3:** India’s advised Article 72 power was separated from the US President’s personal federal-offence power; preemptive pardon was limited to completed conduct.
- **Q4:** J&K was described as a statutory UT legislature; public order and police were excluded, while land was not incorrectly imported from Delhi’s exclusion list.
- **Q11–Q13:** Constitutional morality, basic structure, the Judges Cases, NJAC and US Senate confirmation were assigned their correct legal roles.
- **Q14:** Planning Commission, NITI Aayog, Finance Commission and GST Council functions were not conflated; *Mohit Minerals* was used only for the recommendatory character of GST Council recommendations.
- **Q18:** NCPCR was treated as a statutory, recommendatory/monitoring body; prospective data-protection duties were not presented as a substitute for operative POCSO and intermediary-law remedies.
- **Q19:** I2U2 and the IMEC memorandum were not described as completed infrastructure.
- **Q20:** Active UN reform negotiations and the Pact for the Future were not presented as an adopted Charter amendment.
