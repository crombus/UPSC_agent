## 4. Accuracy safeguards

The review specifically confirmed that:

1. Bodin's sovereign is free from ordinary positive law but not simply released from divine,
   natural and covenantal restraints.
2. Rawls's well-ordered society is defined through public acceptance, common knowledge of the
   conception and institutional compliance, and an effective sense of justice—not social
   unanimity on comprehensive doctrines.
3. Laski's criticism distinguishes legal finality from unlimited moral and social authority.
4. Marxian instrumental, hegemonic and relative-autonomy accounts of the State are not collapsed
   into a single puppet theory.
5. Gandhi's emancipatory revaluation of women is distinguished from the feminist objection to
   idealised sacrifice and gender essence.
6. Ambedkar's historical endogamy thesis is distinguished from his social analysis of caste as
   graded inequality and a division of labourers.
7. Buddhism is not assigned an immortal transmigrating soul: no-self (*anātman*), five aggregates
   (*skandhas*), dependent origination (*pratītyasamutpāda*) and causal rebirth remain consistent.
8. Advaita, Viśiṣṭādvaita, Buddhism and Jainism are compared without being homogenised under
   “oriental religions”; the printed label is preserved but critically qualified.
9. Mystical experience is given defeasible evidential force, not treated as either automatic proof
   or pathology.
10. Kant's objection to the ontological argument is accurately stated as denying that existence is
    a real predicate; modal reformulation is separately assessed.
11. Free-will defence answers logical compatibility more successfully than the evidential weight
    of apparently gratuitous evil.
12. Aquinas's analogy is kept distinct from Tillichian symbol, Wittgensteinian use and
    verification/falsification critiques.
13. Darwinian natural selection undercuts Paley's biological inference without by itself settling
    every contemporary fine-tuning argument.

English concepts lead throughout. Accurate IAST forms include *ahiṃsā*, *anātman*, *skandha*,
*pratītyasamutpāda*, *nirvāṇa*, *karuṇā*, *anekāntavāda*, *ātman*, *māyā*, *mokṣa*,
*niṣkāma karma*, *āryāṣṭāṅgamārga*, *jīva* and *śūnyatā*.

2. OCR corruption of Q2(c)'s line breaks and Q7(b)'s `15` mark;
3. the risk of reading “unrestrained by law” as moral lawlessness rather than positive-law
   supremacy;
4. the risk of equating socialism with authoritarian State ownership;
5. the risk of presenting positive liberty without Berlin's paternalism objection;
6. the risk of making group rights protect internal domination;
7. the risk of reducing Ambedkar's caste analysis to prejudice or occupation;
8. the risk of attributing a permanent soul to Buddhist rebirth;
9. the risk of treating all Asian traditions as one world-denying doctrine;
10. the risk of presenting mystical experience, revelation or religious language as beyond all
    rational assessment;
11. the risk of claiming that free will fully explains natural evil;
12. the risk of treating Paley's analogy as untouched by Hume and Darwin;
13. omission of Rawls's effective sense of justice from the well-ordered-society definition;
14. an initially broad Q6(b) route, corrected to the exact “Relation to Man and the World”
    subproblem under Notions of God.
