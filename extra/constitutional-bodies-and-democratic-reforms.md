# Consolidated Summary

## 1. Constitutional and Statutory Bodies

| Body | Classification | Legal foundation |
|---|---|---|
| CAG | Constitutional body | Article 148 |
| CBI | Non-constitutional, non-statutory body | Executive resolution, 1963 |
| CVC | Statutory body | CVC Act, 2003 |
| RBI | Statutory body | RBI Act, 1934 |

A **constitutional body** is created directly by the Constitution. Examples include the CAG, Election Commission, UPSC and Finance Commission.

A **statutory body** is created through legislation. Examples include the RBI, CVC, NHRC and SEBI.

## 2. DSPE Act and CBI

The **Delhi Special Police Establishment Act, 1946** supplies the principal legal powers used by the CBI.

- It establishes the DSPE.
- It permits investigation of notified offences.
- CBI jurisdiction in a state normally requires that state's consent under Section 6.
- The CBI itself was created through an executive resolution in 1963.

Therefore, the CBI exercises statutory investigative powers but is not itself a statutory or constitutional body.

## 3. Amending the Constitution

Under **Article 368**, Parliament can amend the Constitution using the prescribed special majority and, for specified federal provisions, ratification by at least half the states.

However, Parliament cannot destroy the Constitution's **basic structure**, including:

- Constitutional supremacy
- Judicial review
- Rule of law
- Democracy
- Federalism
- Separation of powers
- Free and fair elections

The Supreme Court cannot pre-authorise destruction of the basic structure. It reviews enacted amendments and may invalidate them.

### Referendums and Plebiscites

India presently has no constitutional referendum procedure. A constitutional amendment could introduce referendums, but an amendment declaring plebiscites absolutely superior to courts, Parliament and the Constitution would probably violate the basic structure.

Countries such as Switzerland, Ireland and Australia use referendums as part of constitutional amendment, but generally combine popular approval with constitutional safeguards.

## 4. Constituent Assembly and a New Constitution

The present Constitution does not provide a procedure for establishing a new sovereign Constituent Assembly.

A lawful reform process could involve:

1. A constitutional commission or elected convention.
2. Public consultation and preparation of a draft.
3. Adoption through Article 368.
4. State ratification where required.
5. Judicial review for basic-structure compliance.

An assembly replacing the Constitution outside this process would create a new legal order through an **extra-constitutional act**, not a lawful constitutional amendment.

## 5. Democratic Accountability Reforms

The central concern is that citizens cannot adequately evaluate representatives or require answers from them.

An ACR for MPs and MLAs would be unsuitable because legislators are elected representatives, not government employees. A better solution is an objective public-information dashboard containing:

- Attendance
- Questions and debates
- Committee participation
- Constituency-fund utilisation
- Declared assets and liabilities
- Criminal cases
- Constituency grievances and responses

The ECI could facilitate verified candidate information, but it should not subjectively grade political performance.

Other possible reforms include:

- Multilingual candidate-comparison portals
- Verified constituency e-petitions
- Mandatory acknowledgment and referral of public petitions
- Campaign-finance transparency
- Stronger Question Hour and legislative committees
- RTI and CAG scrutiny
- Proportional or mixed-member representation discussions

These reforms require stronger democratic institutions, not dictatorship.

## 6. Bringing Reforms Through Civil Services

Civil servants cannot independently change election or constitutional law, but they can:

- Identify administrative problems.
- Collect baseline evidence.
- Design small, lawful pilots.
- Create grievance and transparency dashboards.
- Improve departmental response systems.
- Measure outcomes.
- Prepare cabinet notes and legislative proposals.
- Demonstrate successful models for wider adoption.

## Guiding Principle

> Give citizens reliable information, enforceable response mechanisms and stronger institutions - not an all-powerful ruler or institution.
