ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 2/31: Why the clause exists, and the only datable anchors
ORIGIN RAIL (conceptual, not calendar)
 1 morality is presented as the content of a divine will
   -> 2 Plato asks whether the divine will TRACKS goodness or CREATES it
        ==> the dilemma is OLDER than every theory built to escape it
     -> 3 escape UPWARD: goodness is the divine NATURE, not a decree
          (natural law; later, modified divine command)
       -> 4 escape SIDEWAYS: obligation is rational SELF-legislation (Kant)
         -> 5 escape OUTWARD: whole moral systems with NO commander at all
              (Mimamsa injunction, Buddhist intention, Jain vows, secular theories)
           -> 6 the question turns BACK on religion: does religiosity improve conduct,
                and can a religious premise ever license violence?
              ==> the clause exists because stages 3-5 succeed only PARTIALLY,
                  and stage 6 cannot be settled by any of them.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 3/31: Divine command theory, stated at full strength
THESIS: moral obligation CONSISTS IN being commanded by God. Modified versions ground the
  commands in an essentially LOVING divine nature.
  It is a claim about the CONSTITUTION of obligation - separable from, and much stronger than,
  the epistemic claim (revelation tells us our duty) and the motivational claim (love,
  judgement or sanction moves us to comply).
THE FOUR-STEP ARGUMENT
  1 obligation has AUTHORITY over mere preference
    -> 2 a supreme personal LEGISLATOR can issue universally binding commands
      -> 3 divine KNOWLEDGE and GOODNESS secure the right content
        -> 4 ACCOUNTABILITY supplies motivational seriousness
          ==> obligation is constituted by divine command.
THREE PRESUPPOSITIONS (name them; this is where marks are)
  [a] God exists
  [b] divine commands are KNOWABLE by fallible agents
  [c] AUTHORITY AS SUCH can generate moral reasons   <== KANT DENIES [c], GRANTS [a] AND [b]
WHAT THE THEORY BUYS (concede before objecting)
  objectivity | universality | authority over preference | motivational seriousness
CANONICAL TEST CASE: Abrahamic command cases - can obedience override ordinary moral judgement?
COSTS VISIBLE BEFORE EUTHYPHRO IS EVEN RAISED
  ACCESS COST     : no reliable access to the command = no access to the obligation
  HETERONOMY COST : obligation from another will, enforced by accountability, is obedience
                    rather than autonomy
TRAP: stating the theory as "religion teaches us to be good" describes moral EDUCATION and
  cannot be attacked by the Euthyphro at all.

TWO PARALLEL REPAIRS
  ALSTON'S STANDARD REPLY : God is the SUPREME STANDARD of goodness, not a conformer to a
    further one - as the standard metre bar was the standard of length without matching some
    prior length. No vicious regress, no arbitrariness.
  ZAGZEBSKI'S DIVINE MOTIVATION THEORY : God's MOTIVES, not God's commands, are the
    metaphysical ground of moral value ==> converts a command-ethic into a VIRTUE-ethic.
DEFENDER'S REPLY TO THE RESIDUE: every foundational theory borrows a concept it does not ground
  (the Kantian borrows "rational"). COUNTER: the divine command theorist's distinctive claim was
  that moral concepts are GROUNDED in God - it is the DIRECTION of grounding that is embarrassed.
TRAP: the standard-metre analogy is ALSTON's. Adams's contribution is the obligation/goodness split.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 6/31: Kantian autonomy - obligation without a commander
THE ARGUMENT
  1 moral worth requires action FROM DUTY, not merely in accordance with it
    -> 2 contingent DESIRE cannot yield NECESSITY, and obligation is necessary
      -> 3 rational agents test MAXIMS for universal law
        -> 4 autonomy = obedience to reason ONE SHARES AS RATIONAL = self-legislation
PRESUPPOSITION: practical reason can generate CATEGORICAL norms. (The divine command theorist
  denies exactly this - naming it keeps the exchange honest rather than rhetorical.)
THE FORK THAT DECIDES THE CLAUSE
  +-------------------------------+---------------------------------------+
  | AUTONOMY                      | HETERONOMY                            |
  | law given by reason to itself | law given by ANOTHER will, or by      |
  |                               | desire, reward, fear, punishment      |
  |-------------------------------|---------------------------------------|
  | obligation NECESSARY and      | obligation becomes CONDITIONAL:       |
  | unconditional                 | "if you want the reward, then..."     |
  |-------------------------------|---------------------------------------|
  | MORAL WORTH preserved         | moral worth LOST - the act is prudent |
  +-------------------------------+---------------------------------------+
  ==> divine command, IF it works through SANCTION, falls on the right-hand side.
      This is a DEEPER Kantian objection than the Euthyphro.
CANONICAL EXAMPLE: a truthful act motivated only by fear of divine punishment lacks the moral
  worth of the same act done from duty.
GOD, FREEDOM, IMMORTALITY = PRACTICAL POSTULATES: what reason must presuppose for the moral
  outlook to be coherent. NOT the source of the moral law. Religion is RELATED to morality.
OBJECTION: formalism - universalisation may UNDERDETERMINE content.
  REPLY: add HUMANITY AS AN END and the KINGDOM OF ENDS, which restore substantive content.
THE GAP TO BE NAMED IN ADVANCE: Kant explains why the law BINDS, not why THIS agent will act on
  it when inclination pulls hard. That gap is the whole subject of panels 18-20.
TRAP: autonomy is NOT "each person decides what is right". It is self-legislation of a
  UNIVERSAL law shared by all rational agents.

  STRONG DIVINE COMMAND ------- NATURAL LAW ------- KANTIAN AUTONOMY
  obligation = decree            order of goods      obligation = rational
                                 grounded in           self-legislation

  |                              |  DELIBERATELY      |
  |- revelation needed for       |- revelation NOT    |- revelation not needed
  |  moral knowledge             |  needed for much   |
THESIS: moral norms are rationally accessible PARTICIPATIONS in an objective order grounded
  ultimately in divine REASON, not in arbitrary decrees.
ARGUMENT: human goods and inclinations disclose basic ENDS -> practical reason DIRECTS conduct
  toward them -> divine grounding explains their OBJECTIVE order.
PRESUPPOSITION: human nature has intelligible normative ends.
MEDIATING CLAUSE TO WRITE: "God grounds the order, but reason knows much of it without revelation."
CANONICAL EXAMPLE: preservation of life and social cooperation are BASIC GOODS - not good merely
  because a text commands them.
OBJECTION: nature-to-norm / the naturalistic fallacy.
  REPLY: practical reason grasps goods AS REASONS, not as bare biological facts; the premise is
  normative from the start, so nothing is illegitimately derived from a description.
FURTHER OBJECTION: "this is divine command with better manners - the Euthyphro reappears as
  'is the order good because God grounds it?'"
  REPLY: the ground is divine REASON, not divine WILL, and the arbitrariness horn was generated
  by will. There is no moment at which the order could have been otherwise.
STRATEGIC VALUE: makes the debate THREE-CORNERED, so strong divine command can be refuted
  WITHOUT concluding that theism has nothing to say about grounding.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 9/31: How religion actually influences conduct - and the wall
FIVE MECHANISMS
  +---------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------+
  | 1 EXEMPLARS         | narrative models of a life worth imitating                |
  | 2 RITUAL FORMATION  | repeated practice forms habit and disposition             |
  | 3 COMMUNITY/SANCTION| visibility, belonging, approval and correction            |
  | 4 HOPE / REPENTANCE | failure is survivable, so effort is renewable             |
  | 5 WORLDVIEW         | a frame in which sustained moral effort is not absurd     |
  +---------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------+
CANONICAL EXAMPLE: a vow of non-violence may be STRENGTHENED by religious discipline, while its
  moral defensibility still requires reasons accessible BEYOND the community.
PRESUPPOSITION: belief and practice shape agency; they do not MECHANICALLY DETERMINE it.
================================ THE WALL ==========================================
  CAUSAL INFLUENCE  --X-->  LOGICAL DEPENDENCE
  CAUSAL INFLUENCE  --X-->  MORAL TRUTH
  "religious participation correlates with charitable giving"
     => religion MAY causally influence conduct
     =/=> religion MAKES charity right
     =/=> non-believers CANNOT KNOW charity is right
     =/=> divine command theory survives the Euthyphro
  Even the causal claim may require controls for community, selection and orientation.
====================================================================================
THE SYMMETRY THAT MUST BE STATED: the same five mechanisms can SANCTION EXCLUSION - exemplars
  can be partisan, ritual can substitute for substance, community sanction can police a
  boundary, hope can be promised selectively, a worldview can divide the world.
  ==> the influence is CONTINGENT: neither inherently moral nor inherently immoral.
OBJECTION: if it runs both ways it proves nothing. REPLY: contingency is not emptiness -
  traditions remain comparable by how far they build internal critique and moral universalism
  into the formation they perform.
2023 STEM: TWO DELIVERABLES, SEPARATELY MARKED - the influence AND the interaction model.

SIX TESTS ANY CLAIMED COMMAND MUST PASS
  +---+------------------+-------------------------------------------------------+
  | 1 | IDENTIFICATION   | is this really a command, and whose?                  |
  | 2 | INTERPRETATION   | does the text or tradition bear this reading?         |
  | 3 | CONSISTENCY      | does it cohere with the tradition's own norms?        |
  | 4 | PROPORTIONALITY  | is what is demanded proportionate?                    |
  | 5 | HUMAN DIGNITY    | does it treat persons as ends?                        |
  | 6 | AUTHORITY        | what is the moral standing of whoever invokes it?     |
  +---+------------------+-------------------------------------------------------+
  ==> moral criticism WITHIN AND ACROSS traditions, NOT automatic secular hostility.
NAMED INSTANCES: prophetic critique of ritualism; reform movements against caste and sati;
  the ETHICAL-FRUITS test (a religion is judged by its fruits).
PLURAL MORALITIES - THE INFERENCE THAT FAILS
  "religion supports morality"  --X-->  "all religions share one moral code"
  religious moralities conflict over: duties | authority | exceptions | interpretation of texts
IMMORAL RELIGION: a religion may preserve ritual while sanctioning injustice.
  REPLY: if morality is partly CONSTITUTIVE of worthy religion, this is INTERNAL CORRUPTION,
  not a counterexample - as a corrupt court fails BY the standard of law rather than refuting law.
OBJECTION: morality judging revelation is arrogance, the finite judging the infinite.
  REPLY: interpretation ALREADY uses moral concepts to identify the faithful reading, the
  legitimate authority and the proportionate application. What is judged is a HUMAN CLAIM
  ABOUT a command, not the divine as such.
WHY INTERNAL CRITIQUE IS THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE: external criticism can be met with the charge
  of imposing alien standards. Internal criticism cannot.

SANTIDEVA'S ARGUMENT - Bodhicaryavatara VIII (the strongest of the four)
  1 there is no substantial self (anatman)
    -> 2 therefore suffering is OWNERLESS; the boundary between persons is CONVENTIONAL
      -> 3 therefore the reason to relieve MY future suffering is EXACTLY AS STRONG as the
           reason to relieve ANOTHER'S
        ==> IMPARTIAL ALTRUISM on a METAPHYSICAL premise, with no theological premise anywhere.
        STANDING OBJECTION: the argument depends on anatman being true.
COMPARATIVE VERDICT: FOUR INDEPENDENTLY DEVELOPED non-theistic groundings, two of them Indian

  What theistic ethics distinctively supplies is NOT the CONTENT of morality but a claimed

  HONEST ANSWER: a THIN and CONTESTED morality, not NONE - rules about who may perform what,

2022 ASSESSMENT: the Euthyphro blocks strong divine command; religion nevertheless motivates

FOUR GENERAL REBUTTALS
  (a) EUTHYPHRO: if the act is wrong, calling it commanded does not make it right; if it is
      right, the command is not what makes it so.
  (b) EPISTEMIC FALLIBILITY: revelation-claims are contested even INSIDE a tradition, so no
      agent is entitled to the certainty the argument requires.
  (c) MEANS-END UNITY: Gandhi - means and ends are inseparable, "the means may be likened to a
      seed, the end to a tree" - so a sacred end cannot sanctify an unholy means.
  (d) INTERNAL CRITIQUE: every major tradition contains its own resources against violence -
      non-injury (ahimsa), the Buddhist first precept, the prophetic critique of sacrifice,
      the doctrine of the dignity of the person.

  | ONLY the ordinary, non-religious justification of DEFENSIVE FORCE - self-defence and  |

  |                                                                                       |
  | RELIGION ADDS NO INDEPENDENT LICENCE.                                                 |
  |                                                                                       |

  | defensive force STRICTER than secular law requires.                                   |

    creation of values.
  BUDDHISM: Nietzsche's "life-denying" charge is a real and repeated claim of his - and is
    standardly answered: NIRVANA is the cessation of CRAVING, not of life; KARUNA is an active,
    other-regarding energy; the middle way explicitly REJECTS self-mortification, which is
    precisely the ascetic ideal Nietzsche attacks. He in fact rates Buddhism ABOVE Christianity
    as "more realistic" while still classing it as nihilistic - SAY THIS PRECISELY.
  CARVAKA: closest Indian analogue in CONCLUSION (this-worldly value, rejection of the priestly
    order) but NOT in METHOD - empiricist where Nietzsche is genealogical.
VERDICT FORMULA: "Nietzsche's achievement is to make MORALITY ITSELF an object of historical
  and psychological suspicion, and to show that the death of God is a problem for ETHICS before
  it is a problem for THEOLOGY. His failure is the constructive half: the revaluation supplies
  no criterion by which one creation of values is better than another, which is why his
  critique is more durable than his solution."

FOUR STANDING CRITICISMS AND REPLIES
  1 EUTHYPHRO: arbitrary command or independent good. REPLY: necessary divine goodness.
    REJOINDER: the normativity of goodness still needs explanation.
  2 MOTIVATION GAP: secular reason may identify duty but fail to motivate. REPLY: motivation
    can arise from empathy, identity and respect; religion is one source, not a necessary one.
  3 HETERONOMY: reward and punishment corrupt moral worth. REPLY: mature theism motivates
    through love of the good, not prudential fear.
  4 IMMORAL RELIGION: a religion may preserve ritual while sanctioning injustice. REPLY: if
    morality is partly constitutive of worthy religion, this is INTERNAL CORRUPTION rather than
    a counterexample to ethical religion.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 31/31: The architectures, the formulations and the verdict
THE THREE ANSWER ARCHITECTURES
  10 MARKS (~150w): state the dependence claim -> Euthyphro/Kant discriminator -> ONE Indian
    illustration -> distinguish grounding from motivation -> verdict.
  15 MARKS (~250w): frame the obligation problem -> strongest theistic account -> autonomy or
    natural-law rival -> objection and reply -> interaction analysis -> defended conclusion.
  20 MARKS (~350w): separate rightness, obligation and motivation -> compare command, autonomy
    and dharma -> Euthyphro PLUS the immoral-religion objection -> replies -> graded relational
    thesis.
FOUR SAFE FORMULATIONS
  "God-reference may strengthen motivation without constituting obligation."
  "Religion and morality are conceptually distinguishable, historically interactive and
   normatively mutually critical."
  "Religion's distinctive contribution to the ethics of force is restraint, not licence."
  "Religiosity is an amplifier: it magnifies the moral content it is attached to."
THE QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
  The Euthyphro shows morality CANNOT BE WHOLLY REDUCIBLE TO divine command, so morality has
  AUTONOMY at the level of grounding; four independent non-theistic groundings - two Indian and
  ancient - confirm it empirically. Yet religion powerfully MOTIVATES and SUSTAINS morality
  through exemplars, ritual, community, hope and worldview, and morality reciprocally PURIFIES
  religion through prophetic and reformist criticism. Hence the INTERACTION view, not strict
  dependence in either direction, is soundest - and the interaction is itself CONTINGENT, since
  the same mechanisms that sustain care can sanction exclusion.
  WHAT WOULD CHANGE THIS VERDICT: a demonstration that some obligation cannot be grounded
  without a theistic premise. The four non-theistic groundings are precisely the evidence that
  no such demonstration is available.
