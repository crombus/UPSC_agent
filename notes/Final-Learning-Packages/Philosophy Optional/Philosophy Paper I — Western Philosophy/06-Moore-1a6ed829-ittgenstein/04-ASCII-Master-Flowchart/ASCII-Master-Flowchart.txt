ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 5/28: Moore 1925 - the list of truisms and the second-order claim
==============================================================================
"A DEFENCE OF COMMON SENSE" (in Contemporary British Philosophy, 1925)
        |
        v
THE LIST (Moore's own examples)
   "There exists at present a living human body, which is my body."
   "This body has existed continuously on or near the earth."
   "The earth has existed for many years past."
   "There have existed many other human beings, with bodies."
   "I am a human being who has had many experiences."
        |
        v
THE TWO CLAIMS MADE ABOUT THE LIST
   [1] FIRST-ORDER  I KNOW these propositions - not believe, KNOW
   [2] SECOND-ORDER very many other human beings KNOW propositions of
       the SAME KIND about themselves
   -> the second converts a report of personal confidence into a claim
      about a SHARED epistemic position, and it is what makes "COMMON
      sense" the right name
        |
        v
WHAT THE DOCTRINE IS NOT
   NOT the claim that whatever most people believe is true
   NOT an appeal to convention
   NOT a claim of infallibility - the list is a philosophical proposal
       and is CONTESTABLE
        |
        v
WHY A LIST RATHER THAN AN ARGUMENT
   Moore does not argue FOR the truisms from something more basic -
   that would concede they need support from outside. He ASSERTS them,
   and the size of the list displays how enormous the sceptic's task is:
   he must deny not one claim but a whole interlocking body of them.
        |
        v
ANSWER LINE: "Moore's list is not a survey of opinion but an inventory
   of what a sceptical argument would have to overturn."

     OR it says something different -> not an analysis of that concept

   a sceptic about the given, or anyone who ties understanding to
   analysis, will deny.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 7/28: Moore - comparative certainty and the reversal of the burden
==============================================================================
THE PRINCIPLE
   A philosophical argument overturns a truism ONLY IF every premiss of
   that argument is MORE CERTAIN than the truism it denies.
   Moore's empirical claim: NO SUCH PREMISS HAS EVER BEEN PRODUCED.
        |
        v
THE LOGIC BEHIND IT - a valid argument is a SET OF SCALES
   A valid argument transmits credibility BACKWARDS as well as forwards.
   If the conclusion is less credible than the premisses are credible,
   the argument has REFUTED A PREMISS rather than proved its conclusion.
        |
   THE SCEPTIC'S ARGUMENT              THE TRUISM
   P1 I cannot rule out dreaming       "I have a body."
   P2 if not, I do not know I have
      a body                           "The earth existed for many
   C  so I do not know                  years before I was born."
   abstract | technical | contested    as certain as anything I have
                                       ever been
        |
        v
RESULT: THE BURDEN OF PROOF IS REVERSED
   The realist no longer owes a proof of the external world from
   neutral ground; the SCEPTIC owes an explanation of why abstract
   premisses should be trusted more than ordinary perceptual certainty.
        |
        v
THE LIMIT THAT MUST BE WRITTEN
   The principle presupposes that certainty is COMPARABLE across
   ordinary and philosophical propositions, and Moore supplies NO
   NEUTRAL MEASURE. A sceptic who simply denies that Moore KNOWS the
   truism has been OUTWEIGHED, not shown to be irrational.
        |
        v
LABEL EXPLAINED (2019 Q3(c)): COMMON SENSE from the METHOD; REALISM
   from the CONTENT of the list - body, earth before my birth, other
   human beings. THE REALISM IS A RESULT, NOT A PREMISS.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 8/28: Moore 1939 - the proof, the gloss and the three conditions
==============================================================================
TARGET: KANT'S CHARGE that it is "a scandal to philosophy" that no
   satisfactory proof of things outside us has been given
        |
        v
THE GLOSS THAT MAKES THE INFERENCE VALID
   "EXTERNAL to our minds" = "THINGS MET WITH IN SPACE" - things whose
   existence does not consist in being experienced
        |
        v
THE ARGUMENT
   1 Here is one hand.        (present perceptual knowledge)
   2 And here is another.
   3 So at least two material things exist.
   4 So an external world exists.
   NOTE: the printed order is RIGHT hand first, THEN left. If unsure,
   write "holding up first one hand and then the other".
        |
        v
MOORE'S OWN THREE CONDITIONS FOR A RIGOROUS PROOF
   [1] the premiss must be DIFFERENT from the conclusion
       "here is a hand" != "an external world exists" -> NOT CIRCULAR
   [2] the premiss must be KNOWN, not merely believed or assumed
       "I did know it. It would have been absurd to suggest that I did
        not know it, but only believed it." -> THE CONTESTED CONDITION
   [3] the conclusion must FOLLOW
       hands are things met with in space -> VALID, given the gloss
        |
        v
THE CONCESSION, AND WHY IT IS NOT A DEFECT
   Moore CANNOT PROVE the premiss - to do so he would have to prove he
   is not dreaming, "and that I cannot do".
   A proof requires a KNOWN premiss, not a PROVED one; the contrary
   demand generates an INFINITE REGRESS in which nothing is proved.
        |
        v
ANSWER LINE: "The demand to prove every premiss is a demand that no
   proof of anything could ever meet."

==============================================================================
"THE REFUTATION OF IDEALISM", Mind, 1903
        |
        v
TARGET: ESSE EST PERCIPI - "to be is to be perceived"
   For the idealist inference to work, the object's BEING must be
   nothing over and above its BEING PERCEIVED. The sensation and its
   object must therefore be ONE THING, or the object a mere constituent
   of the act.
        |
        v
THE DISTINCTION
   +--------------------------------+-----------------------------+
   | ACT OF AWARENESS               | OBJECT OF AWARENESS         |
   | consciousness                  | blue, green, this sound     |
   | THE SAME in every sensation    | DIFFERENT in each sensation |
   | = THE COMMON ELEMENT           | = THE VARIABLE ELEMENT      |
   +--------------------------------+-----------------------------+
        |
        v
THE IDEALIST'S ERROR: conflating [1] and [2] - treating the object as
   IDENTICAL with, or CONSTITUTED BY, the act of awareness.
   If the sensation of blue IS just the blue, then blue cannot exist
   unperceived -> idealism.
        |
        v
WHY THE DISTINCTION IS NOT MERELY INTROSPECTIVE
   Moore does not simply report detecting two items. He argues that the
   identification makes EXPERIENCE UNINTELLIGIBLE: two sensations must
   have something IN COMMON (both are sensations) and must DIFFER in
   something (their objects differ). On the identity thesis there is
   only ONE item in each, so nothing is available to play both roles.
        |
        v
ANSWER LINE: "What varies cannot be what stays the same."

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 11/28: Moore - the two printed sentences, decoded
==============================================================================
THE 2025 SENTENCE (Q3(c))
   "Blue is one object of sensation and green is another, and
    consciousness, which both sensations have in common, is different
    from either."
   READ IT AS A LABELLING OF THE ARGUMENT:
     "blue ... green" ............ THE VARIABLE ELEMENT = the OBJECTS
     "which both sensations have in common" .... THE COMMON ELEMENT =
        consciousness
     "is different from either" .. THE CONCLUSION: act != object
        |
        v
THE 2020 SENTENCE (Q2(b))
   "If anyone tells us that to say 'Blue exists' is the same thing as
    to say that 'Both blue and consciousness exist', he makes a mistake
    and a SELF-CONTRADICTORY mistake."
        |
   WHY SELF-CONTRADICTORY - four steps, and this decoding is the mark
   [1] the claim is that the two statements say the SAME THING
   [2] the second statement is formed by CONJOINING TWO DISTINCT ITEMS,
       blue and consciousness
   [3] to assert the identity of content, the speaker must already
       POSSESS AND USE the very distinction whose significance he denies
   [4] therefore the identification is not merely false; it CANNOT BE
       STATED without presupposing its own falsity
        |
        v
THE RESIDUAL - state it, and the "critically" half is done
   The charge bites hardest against a strict IDENTITY formulation. An
   idealist who says instead that blue is DEPENDENT ON consciousness
   without being IDENTICAL with it has not been convicted of
   contradiction - he has been DEPRIVED OF THE ARGUMENT HE HAD.

TRANSPARENCY / DIAPHANOUSNESS
   Introspect the sensation of blue and what you find is THE BLUE. The
   act is elusive - "as if it were diaphanous". Attention passes
   THROUGH the act to the object.
   ROLE: a DIAGNOSIS of why the conflation is tempting.
   NOT A PREMISS of the refutation - the comparison argument does the
   refuting.
   CONTESTED: later philosophers of mind use the same observation to
   argue that there is NOTHING beyond the object to introspect. Moore's
   reply must be that ELUSIVENESS IS NOT ABSENCE.
        |
        v

   ACHIEVED ...... the IDENTITY route from object to act is BLOCKED, so
                   esse est percipi is NOT ESTABLISHED
   NOT ACHIEVED .. that any object exists UNPERCEIVED
   NOT ACHIEVED .. a positive THEORY OF PERCEPTION - the relation
                   between sense-data and material objects is left open
   NOT ACHIEVED .. a refutation of ABSOLUTE IDEALISM, which rests on
                   internal relations and the incompleteness of finite
                   determinations, NOT on the perceptual equation
   NOT ACHIEVED .. a refutation of Berkeley's whole philosophy - his

MOORE vs RUSSELL ON BERKELEY (the comparative half of 2018 Q3(a))
   MOORE     epistemic and DISTINCTION-DRAWING: denies the

   RUSSELL   logical and RECONSTRUCTIVE: accepts that what is given is

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 13/28: Russell - incomplete symbols and contextual definition
==============================================================================
DEFINITION
   An INCOMPLETE SYMBOL has NO MEANING IN ISOLATION, contributes
   systematically to the meaning of every sentence in which it occurs,
   and DISAPPEARS on analysis. It is given a CONTEXTUAL DEFINITION -
   a rule supplying the meaning of WHOLE SENTENCES containing it.
   SOURCE OF THE TECHNIQUE: contextual definition in Principia
   Mathematica (1910-13).
        |
        v
THE TEST TO APPLY IN THE EXAM
   Does the expression SURVIVE ANALYSIS as a CONSTITUENT of the
   proposition?
     "the author of Waverley" ....... does NOT survive -> incomplete
     "Scott", as a genuine name ..... would survive
        |
        v
THE COMMONEST ERROR
   "INCOMPLETE" DOES NOT MEAN "MEANINGLESS". The phrase is
   systematically meaning-CONTRIBUTING; it has no meaning ON ITS OWN.
   Write the words "in isolation" and the error cannot occur.
        |
        v
CONSEQUENCE FOR ONTOLOGY
   Because the phrase contributes NO CONSTITUENT, the truth of a
   sentence containing it commits us to nothing beyond the existence of
   things satisfying the PREDICATES. This is what makes the device an
   instrument of ONTOLOGICAL ECONOMY, and it is the bridge to
   CONSTRUCTIONS (panel 19) and ATOMISM (panels 20-22).
        |
        v
OPTIONAL BACKGROUND ONLY: Russell's PARADOX and the THEORY OF TYPES
   supply the habit behind the doctrine - a symbol may be ELIMINABLE
   rather than referential, and a well-formed-looking string may fail to
   express a proposition. NOT a printed limb. One sentence maximum.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 14/28: Russell - the theory of descriptions, in notation
==============================================================================
THE PROBLEM: "The present King of France is bald."
   Grammatically impeccable. Understood by every competent speaker.
   And there is no present King of France.
        |
   THREE ANSWERS RUSSELL REJECTS
   (a) THE SENTENCE IS MEANINGLESS ..... but we understand it, and we
       can argue about it
   (b) IT IS ABOUT A NON-EXISTENT OBJECT THAT SUBSISTS ..... MEINONG's
       route; it populates the world with golden mountains, and the
       ROUND SQUARE threatens non-contradiction
   (c) THE PHRASE DENOTES SOMETHING ELSE ..... no candidate survives
        |
        v
THE ANALYSIS - the phrase is ELIMINATED, not interpreted
        "The F is G"
             =
   (Ex)[ Fx  AND  (Ay)(Fy -> y = x)  AND  Gx ]
          ^             ^                   ^
      EXISTENCE     UNIQUENESS         PREDICATION
     at least one    at most one          it is G
        |
        v
WORKED CASE - "THE GOLDEN MOUNTAIN IS VERY HIGH" (2025 Q1(d))
   (Ex)[ Gx & (Ay)(Gy -> y=x) & Hx ]
   The EXISTENCE conjunct is FALSE -> the whole conjunction is FALSE
   -> NOT meaningless, and NO golden mountain need subsist
   The golden mountain is MEINONG'S OWN STOCK EXAMPLE - name him.
        |
        v
UNIQUENESS IS THE CONJUNCT MOST OFTEN DROPPED. Without it the analysis
   renders "the" as "a", and the definite article's entire contribution
   disappears.
        |
        v
THE GENERAL PRINCIPLE ESTABLISHED
   GRAMMATICAL FORM != LOGICAL FORM. The task of philosophy is to
   reveal the logical form hidden by language.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 16/28: Russell - scope, and the Strawson objection
==============================================================================
PRIMARY AND SECONDARY OCCURRENCE - "The King of France is not bald"
   PRIMARY (description takes WIDE scope):
      there is exactly one King of France AND he is not bald -> FALSE
   SECONDARY (negation takes WIDE scope):
      it is NOT the case that (there is exactly one King of France and
      he is bald) -> TRUE
   ONE sentence, TWO logical forms. Ambiguity of SCOPE, not of the
   meanings of the words.
        |
        v
THE STANDING OBJECTION
   STRAWSON, "On Referring", Mind, 1950
   Russell has confused a SENTENCE with a USE of a sentence. A speaker
   who says "The present King of France is bald" does not ASSERT that
   there is exactly one King of France; he PRESUPPOSES it. When the
   presupposition fails, THE QUESTION OF TRUTH OR FALSITY DOES NOT
   ARISE - the utterance is not false but VOID.
        |
        v
RUSSELL'S AVAILABLE REPLIES
   [1] Strawson describes conversational expectation; Russell gives
       TRUTH-CONDITIONS, and a logic requires them
   [2] presupposition failure needs a semantics of its own - typically
       a TRUTH-VALUE GAP - and gaps carry costs for classical logic
   [3] the analysis is a proposal about LOGICAL FORM for a scientifically
       regimented language, not ordinary-language lexicography
        |
        v
THE HONEST VERDICT
   A GENUINE RIVAL ANALYSIS, not a demonstration of error. Strawson
   describes ordinary USE better; Russell describes LOGICAL FORM better.
   OWNER BOUNDARY: Strawson appears here as ONE OBJECTION. His positive
   theory of reference and his descriptive metaphysics are TOPIC 11.

THE TWO-STOREY BUILDING
   UPPER: MOLECULAR PROPOSITIONS
     "this is red AND that is blue" | "... OR ..." | "NOT ..." |
     "IF ... THEN ..."

   GROUND: ATOMIC PROPOSITIONS
     "this is red" | "this is to the left of that" - no connectives

        |
        v
THE PAY-OFF OF TRUTH-FUNCTIONALITY
   NO ADDITIONAL FACT IN THE WORLD CORRESPONDS TO "and" OR "or".
   There is no conjunctive fact over and above the two conjuncts'
   facts. A large ONTOLOGICAL ECONOMY - state it, or you have
   described a truth-table instead of an argument.
        |
        v
THE ISOMORPHISM THESIS
   PROPOSITION   name1 ---- relation-word ---- name2
                   |              |              |
   FACT          object1 ------ relation ----- object2
   SAME LOGICAL STRUCTURE. Names correspond to objects; the ARRANGEMENT
   of names corresponds to the ARRANGEMENT of objects. This is the
   BRIDGE between language and world in Russell's atomism.
        |
        v
THE NEGATIVE-FACT PROBLEM
   "Socrates is not alive" is TRUE. What makes it true?
   RUSSELL: reluctantly, a NEGATIVE FACT - that a positive state does
     NOT obtain.
   COST: absences are added to the inventory of the world.
   WHY HE PAYS IT: to preserve a DIRECT TRUTH-MAKING relation - every
     truth is made true by something.
   WITTGENSTEIN'S DIFFERENT ROUTE: obtaining elementary states
     constitute the world, and their TOTALITY also determines which
     possible states do NOT obtain (2.05); negation is not a further
     object, and "the logical constants are not representatives"
     (4.0312). NO negative facts required.
        |
        v

   The programme requires that EVERY proposition be a truth-function of
   atomic ones. BELIEF-CONTEXTS ("George IV believes that p") and
   MODAL contexts resist this. Concede the difficulty; do not pretend
   the reduction is complete.

QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
   Three programmes, one method used for three incompatible purposes,
   each leaving something deliberately unfinished. MOORE redistributes
   the burden of proof and leaves the ANALYSIS open. RUSSELL secures
   ontological economy and leaves the INVENTORY open. EARLY
   WITTGENSTEIN fixes the conditions of sense with unmatched rigour and
   leaves THE STATUS OF HIS OWN BOOK open. Permanent achievements:
   the reversal of the sceptical burden | the quantificational analysis
   of definite descriptions | sense as determinate exclusion within a
   field of possibilities. Permanent costs: no neutral measure of
   certainty | no completed base | no consistent way of stating the
   boundary the Tractatus draws. State an achievement AND its matching
   cost in the same sentence, and the clause has been understood.
