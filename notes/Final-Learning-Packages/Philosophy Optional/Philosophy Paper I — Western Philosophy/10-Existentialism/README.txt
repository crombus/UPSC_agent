Scope note
----------
The printed clause names three thinkers and three limbs. Limb one removes the
blueprint, limb two asks what a person must then do, and limb three is
Heidegger's refusal to let either question be answered by a subject standing
over against a world — which is why the third limb is almost entirely his.
"Existentialism" is a retrospective family label: Kierkegaard died in 1855 and
never met the word, Heidegger denied the label in the "Letter on Humanism"
(written 1946, published 1947), and only Sartre accepted and defined it in the
lecture of October 1945 published in 1946. The clause owns 14 of 112 Western
Philosophy question-parts in the 2018-2025 corpus — joint-highest with
Rationalism and with Moore, Russell and Early Wittgenstein — and it owns at
least one part in every one of the eight years, split Sartre 8, Kierkegaard 4
and Heidegger 3. Husserl's suspension, theory of essences and avoidance of
psychologism belong to Topic 09 and appear here only as the method Heidegger
redirects and the position Sartre inherits; Hegel's dialectic and Absolute
Spirit belong to Topic 05 and are needed only as Kierkegaard's target;
Descartes' methodic doubt, cogito and dualism belong to Topic 02 and are the
position being-in-the-world dissolves; Kant on freedom and the antinomies
belongs to Topic 04; Nietzsche is not a printed thinker in this clause at all;
Camus, Jaspers, Marcel and de Beauvoir are bounded comparisons rather than
systems; and existentialist theology, faith and revelation, and death and
immortality belong to Paper II. The Look and being-for-others, and
Kierkegaard's indirect communication and pseudonymity, are marks-essential
core, the latter having been promoted out of optional status on 15 August 2026.
