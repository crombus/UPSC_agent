ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 12/48: Locke: abstraction - the eight numbered steps and the general representative
==============================================================================
THE PROBLEM: everything that EXISTS is particular; almost every WORD is
general. How can a mind furnished only with particular ideas use general
words?
        |
        v
THE ARGUMENT, NUMBERED                        Essay III.iii and IV.vii.9
 1 Everything that exists is PARTICULAR                     III.iii.1
 2 A separate name for every particular is impossible -
   memory could not hold them and communication would fail  III.iii.2-4
 3 THEREFORE language requires GENERAL TERMS
 4 Words are general ONLY by being signs of GENERAL IDEAS   III.iii.6
   (meaning is always mediated by an idea in the mind)
 5 Ideas become general when the mind SEPARATES them from
   circumstances of time, place and every concomitant idea  III.iii.6-9
 6 What remains serves as a "GENERAL REPRESENTATIVE" of all
   particulars of the same sort
 7 The abstract idea annexed to a sortal name IS the
   NOMINAL ESSENCE by which we classify                     III.iii.15
   -> classification is the WORKMANSHIP OF THE UNDERSTANDING,
      not a reading-off of real essences
 8 "General and universal belong not to the real existence of
   things; they are the inventions and creatures of the
   understanding, made by it for its own use, and concern
   only signs"                                              III.iii.11
        |
        v
POSITION NAMED: LOCKE IS A CONCEPTUALIST - neither a Platonic realist
about universals nor a bare nominalist. Naming the position is a
one-word gain in precision most scripts never make.
        |
        v
WHY THE DOCTRINE IS NOT OPTIONAL: without it Locke has no account of
classification, of definition, or of the nominal essences on which the
whole of Book III depends. Berkeley understood this exactly, which is
why he attacks ABSTRACTION FIRST, in the INTRODUCTION, before saying a
word about matter.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 22/48: Berkeley: the demolition of abstraction, the replacement and the payload

BERKELEY'S DEMOLITION, NUMBERED       Principles, Introduction Sec.6-25
 1 I CONCEDE I can imagine particular things I have perceived, and
   compound and divide such ideas - a hand without a body, a headless
   torso. This is SEPARATION OF PARTS THAT COULD EXIST APART.
 2 But ABSTRACTION PROPER requires separating what CANNOT exist apart:
   extension without any colour; motion without a determinate speed;
   "man" without any determinate stature or complexion.
 3 Locke's own triangle - neither equilateral nor scalene, yet ALL AND
   NONE at once - is SELF-CONTRADICTORY; no such idea can be framed.
   (Berkeley quotes the passage VERBATIM at Intro Sec.13.)
 4 THEREFORE there are no abstract general ideas.
 5 POSITIVE REPLACEMENT: an idea is general not by being abstract but
   "BY BEING MADE TO REPRESENT OR STAND FOR ALL OTHER PARTICULAR IDEAS
   OF THE SAME SORT" (Intro Sec.12). A determinate particular idea,
   SELECTIVELY ATTENDED TO, does duty for a class: the geometer draws
   THIS black inch-long line and proves a theorem about ALL lines.
 6 COROLLARY AGAINST THE WHOLE TRADITION: words do not always stand for
   ideas at all; language also RAISES PASSION, PROMPTS ACTION and
   DISPOSES THE MIND (Intro Sec.19-20). This is a genuine anticipation
   of the later claim that meaning is USE.
 7 METAPHYSICAL PAYLOAD: Locke's MATERIAL SUBSTANCE IN GENERAL and
   SUBSTRATUM are precisely abstract general ideas - the abstract idea
   of BEING plus the relative notion of SUPPORTING. If abstraction is
   impossible, "matter" is not a mysterious unknown but a MEANINGLESS
   EXPRESSION. IMMATERIALISM RESTS ON THE NOMINALISM, NOT THE REVERSE.
        |
        v
WHAT IS DENIED AND WHAT IS NOT
   DENIED: ABSTRACT general ideas.
   NOT DENIED: general ideas, general words, general knowledge. Geometry
   proceeds perfectly well on Berkeley's account.

   LIMIT 1 The argument works only against an IMAGISTIC reading of
     ideas. Locke's DETERMINABLE reply - undetermined, not contradictory
     - escapes step 3.

     particular idea represents its class? If the answer is a further

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 24/48: Berkeley: arguments 1 and 2 - the semantic argument and the likeness principle
==============================================================================
ARGUMENT 1 - SEMANTIC / ANALYTIC                    Principles Sec.1-4
 1 The objects of human knowledge are IDEAS: (a) imprinted on the
   senses, (b) perceived by attending to the passions and operations of
   the mind, (c) formed by memory and imagination.
 2 "SENSIBLE THINGS" MEANS things IMMEDIATELY perceived by sense.
   (This is the sense of the phrase quoted in PYQ 2021 Q1(c).)
 3 An apple, a stone, a tree, a book are NOTHING BUT COLLECTIONS of such
   immediately perceived ideas.
 4 It is impossible that ideas should exist unperceived - their ESSE is
   PERCIPI; existence for them CONSISTS IN being perceived.
 5 THEREFORE for sensible things ESSE EST PERCIPI (Sec.3), and to speak
   of their "absolute existence without relation to their being
   perceived" is UNINTELLIGIBLE.
   PRESUPPOSITION: step 3 is a DEFINITION, not a discovery. Grant it and
   immaterialism follows in four lines; deny it and nothing follows.
   THE WORD DOING THE WORK: "IMMEDIATELY". Locke held we perceive ideas
   that REPRESENT objects, so the object is reached MEDIATELY, by
   inference. Berkeley's definition removes the inference, and with it
   the veil and the sceptical problem the veil generated.
   BERKELEY'S OWN ILLUSTRATION (Sec.3): "The table I write on, I say,
   exists; that is, I see and feel it."
        |
ARGUMENT 2 - THE LIKENESS PRINCIPLE                    Principles Sec.8
 1 Locke says our ideas of primary qualities RESEMBLE qualities in
   bodies.
 2 But "AN IDEA CAN BE LIKE NOTHING BUT AN IDEA" - a colour or figure
   can be like nothing but another colour or figure.
 3 If the supposed originals are UNPERCEIVABLE, no comparison with them
   is even possible; a resemblance-claim we could NEVER IN PRINCIPLE
   CHECK is empty.
 4 THEREFORE the representative-realist relation of resemblance between
   idea and material quality is UNINTELLIGIBLE.
   PRESUPPOSITION: resemblance requires COMPARABILITY BY A PERCEIVER.
   STRONGEST OBJECTION: STRUCTURAL or MATHEMATICAL resemblance
   (ISOMORPHISM) does not require qualitative likeness - a map resembles
   terrain without being terrain-coloured. This is the standard modern
   RESCUE OF LOCKE, and it concedes the qualitative point while saving
   the representational relation.

ARGUMENT 3 - PARITY                     Principles Sec.9-15; Dialogues I
   LOGICAL FORM: a PARITY argument. Every relativity consideration Locke
   accepts for SECONDARY qualities - variation with observer, with
   organ, with medium - applies equally to PRIMARY ones:
      apparent SIZE varies with distance
      MOTION and rest are relative to the frame of reference
      SHAPE varies with the angle of view
      EXTENSION cannot be conceived without colour or some visual
        quality; FIGURE is inseparable from extension
   1 Locke ADMITS secondary qualities are mind-dependent.
   2 Primary qualities are EQUALLY mind-dependent by the same tests.
   3 THEREFORE all qualities are ideas.
   4 THEREFORE there is NOTHING LEFT for "material substance" to be.
   PRESUPPOSITION: perceptual relativity ENTAILS mind-dependence.
   OBJECTION: it does NOT. That a thing looks different from different
   positions is what one should EXPECT of a mind-INDEPENDENT thing with
   a real shape. So parity is suggestive, not decisive - which is why
   the 2024 answer must identify the LIKENESS principle as the weapon
   that actually does the work.
        |
ARGUMENT 4 - THE MASTER ARGUMENT                   Principles Sec.22-23
 1 Berkeley stakes everything on one challenge: CONCEIVE ONE EXTENDED
   MOVABLE BODY EXISTING UNCONCEIVED, and he will concede materialism.
 2 You reply that you can easily conceive a tree standing alone in a
   park with nobody by.
 3 But IN DOING SO YOU ARE CONCEIVING IT - so you have framed ideas in
   your own mind and have NOT conceived the tree UNCONCEIVED at all.
 4 THEREFORE the supposition is SELF-REFUTING; unperceived existence
   cannot even be entertained.
   PRESUPPOSITION (AND THE FATAL ONE): that conceiving "X EXISTING
   UNCONCEIVED" requires the ACT of conceiving to be part of the CONTENT
   conceived.
   STRONGEST OBJECTION (Russell; refined by Prior and Gallois): a SCOPE
   or USE-MENTION confusion. What is inconceivable is "I conceive OF X
   THAT IT IS NOT CONCEIVED BY ME"; what is required is only "I conceive
   THAT X exists and is not conceived" - perfectly coherent, exactly as
   I can THINK ABOUT an unthought-of number.
   BERKELEY'S BEST REPLY: for him there is no OBJECT over and above the
   IDEA, so the distinction the objector needs is precisely what is at

(A) THE NEGATIVE ARGUMENT ON NECESSARY CONNEXION
                                Treatise I.iii.14; Enquiry VII
 1 COPY PRINCIPLE: every simple idea derives from a simple impression.
 2 So if we have a genuine idea of NECESSARY CONNEXION, locate its
   SOURCE IMPRESSION.
 3 EXTERNAL SEARCH: in any SINGLE instance of causation - one billiard
   ball striking another - sense delivers only PRIORITY, CONTIGUITY and
   the SUCCESSION of qualities. No impression of a POWER or TIE.
 4 REPETITION ADDS NO NEW QUALITY TO THE OBJECTS: the hundredth
   collision is qualitatively no different from the first.
 5 INTERNAL SEARCH: volition does no better. I have NO IMPRESSION of HOW
   the will moves the limb; the actual mechanism - nerve, muscle - is
   unknown to consciousness. This rules out the CARTESIAN and the
   OCCASIONALIST appeal alike. (SCRIPTS OMIT THIS STEP; IT IS WHAT MAKES
   THE CONCLUSION GENERAL RATHER THAN ANECDOTAL.)
 6 But repetition DOES produce a change in THE MIND: after constant
   conjunction the mind is DETERMINED to pass from the idea of A to the
   idea of B. That felt determination is an IMPRESSION OF REFLECTION.
 7 THEREFORE the idea of necessary connexion is COPIED FROM THAT
   INTERNAL IMPRESSION. Necessity "IS SOMETHING THAT EXISTS IN THE MIND,
   NOT IN OBJECTS."
 8 THEREFORE TWO DEFINITIONS OF CAUSE FOLLOW:
+---------------------------------+-------------------------------+
| (i) OBJECTIVE / PHILOSOPHICAL   | (ii) SUBJECTIVE / NATURAL     |
| an object followed by another,  | an object followed by another,|
| where ALL OBJECTS SIMILAR to    | whose appearance CONVEYS THE  |
| the first are followed by       | THOUGHT to the other          |
| objects similar to the second   |                               |
| regularity-based, EXTERNAL      | psychological, INTERNAL       |
+---------------------------------+-------------------------------+
   NAMING BOTH IS THE SINGLE HIGHEST-VALUE MOVE IN ANY HUME-ON-CAUSATION
   ANSWER. TENSION TO FLAG: the two pull apart and Hume NEVER RECONCILES
   them - the origin of the modern REGULARITY vs PROJECTIVIST debate.
   MECHANISM: CUSTOM OR HABIT, "the great guide of human life" (Enq. V).
PRESUPPOSITIONS

   P2 single and repeated instances can be compared for ADDED content -
      i.e. causation must be visible in ONE instance if objective at all
   P3 impressions are ATOMIC and SEPARABLE, so no relation is given WITH

(B) THE PROBLEM OF INDUCTION AS A FORMAL DILEMMA          Enquiry IV
 1 All reasoning about matters of fact beyond present sense and memory
   rests on the relation of CAUSE AND EFFECT.
 2 Causal inference rests on the UNIFORMITY PRINCIPLE (UP): the future
   will resemble the past; unobserved cases resemble observed ones.
 3 UP can be established ONLY DEMONSTRATIVELY OR PROBABLY.
   <== THE VULNERABLE STEP: this is the FORK APPLIED REFLEXIVELY
 4 NOT DEMONSTRATIVELY: "the course of nature may change" implies no
   contradiction - it is perfectly CONCEIVABLE.
 5 NOT PROBABLY: every probable or experiential argument ALREADY
   PRESUPPOSES UP, so the argument would be CIRCULAR.
 6 THEREFORE UP has NO RATIONAL FOUNDATION.
 7 THEREFORE inductive inference is produced not by REASON but by
   CUSTOM OR HABIT.
   EVERY SERIOUS REPLY ATTACKS STEP 3:
     KANT     supplies a THIRD category - the SYNTHETIC A PRIORI
     POPPER   denies that science needs induction at all
     STRAWSON argues that demanding a justification of induction AS A
              WHOLE misapplies the very standards induction constitutes
   EXECUTABLE VERDICT: the sceptical conclusion depends on the
   EXHAUSTIVENESS of a dichotomy that IS the fork - so Hume's scepticism
   about induction is the SHADOW CAST BY HIS CLASSIFICATION OF
   PROPOSITIONS, and survives only as long as the fork does.

(C) KANT'S RESPONSE - THE ROUTED REPLY IN 2025 Q2(c)
   - Kant AGREES that necessary connection is not copied from a sensory
     impression. HE ACCEPTS THE NEGATIVE RESULT.
   - SECOND ANALOGY: the category of CAUSE is an A PRIORI RULE required

     POSSIBLE EXPERIENCE: the understanding supplies necessary temporal
     ordering to phenomena.

     knowledge of causality among THINGS IN THEMSELVES.

(D) MITIGATED SCEPTICISM - THE POSITIVE DOCTRINE       Enquiry XII
   EXCESSIVE / PYRRHONIAN: total suspension of judgment - UNLIVEABLE
   MITIGATED: limit enquiry to what experience can answer; avoid
     speculation beyond possible experience (substance, God, the soul);
     PROPORTION BELIEF TO EVIDENCE; rely on the natural beliefs custom
     compels

     groundlessness; both can be true without practical contradiction.
     RESIDUAL: stable ONLY IF natural beliefs are exempt from the demand
     for justification - which concedes that not all belief needs reason.
