MILL ('On Liberty', 1859) -- THE HARM PRINCIPLE
  coercion of a competent adult is legitimate ONLY to prevent HARM TO OTHERS
  SELF-REGARDING acts -> generally not coercible
  OTHER-REGARDING acts -> may be regulated or punished
  OFFENCE / DISAPPROVAL =/= HARM; heterodox opinion may not be suppressed
  OBJECTIONS -> few acts are cleanly self-regarding; "harm" is itself contested
  BEST DEFENCE -> a BURDEN-OF-PROOF rule: coercion must show injury, not distaste,
                  and paternalism stays exceptional

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 10/12: Justice - Plato, Aristotle, the criteria problem, compensation
PLATO      CITY: rulers rule | auxiliaries defend | producers provide
           SOUL: reason governs | spirit supports | appetite obeys
           JUSTICE = each part doing its OWN work without usurping another's role
           -> inner AND civic RIGHT-ORDER, not merely external distribution
           OBJ frozen hierarchy | REPLY a norm of competence and organic harmony
           VERDICT -> the idea survives the institution           -> 2024 Q1(a)
ARISTOTLE  DISTRIBUTIVE -> shares track RELEVANT desert -> PROPORTIONAL equality
                           (offices, honours)
           CORRECTIVE   -> restore the balance after a wrong -> ARITHMETIC equality
                           (compensation, restitution, punishment)
           RULE -> treat equals equally and unequals unequally ONLY where the basis
                   of comparison is RELEVANT.  GAP -> "relevant" is left unspecified
CRITERIA GRID   NEED -> subsistence, health care, basic education, disability
                DESERT -> honours; backward-looking and moralised
                MERIT -> offices; forward-looking and functional
                CONTRIBUTION / ENTITLEMENT -> wages, exchange, property titles
                EQUAL SHARE -> civil and political rights, one person one vote
                COMPENSATION -> rectification of unjustified prior loss
  THESIS -> justice is CRITERION-PLURAL but NOT ARBITRARY: each good has an internal
            logic fixing its proper basis.  MERIT =/= DESERT -> collapsing them
            yields the bad answer that "merit is a moral entitlement"
  INJUSTICE -> a criterion ESCAPING ITS SPHERE: wealth buying political influence;
               caste rank governing access to office
        v
COMPENSATORY PRINCIPLE -> corrective justice generalised from transactions
  1 systematic, legally sanctioned exclusion from land, education, office, honour
  2 exclusion TRANSMITS through wealth, networks, schooling, health, confidence
  3 formal equality from today RATIFIES the unequal starting line
  4 positive measures offset the transmitted disadvantage
  5 therefore compensation is demanded BY equality, not as an exception to it
  TERMINUS -> it lapses when the targeted transmission no longer operates
  RESIDUAL -> over-inclusion; the transmission premise is FACTUAL and must be argued
  ROUTING -> the affirmative-action policy debate belongs to the CASTE owner
