ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 9/22: Sen II -- freedom as end and means; the five instrumental freedoms

  1. equal resources yield UNEQUAL FREEDOM
  2. UTILITY CAN ADAPT to deprivation
  3. therefore assess REAL OPPORTUNITIES, not goods held or happiness reported
  4. the instrumental freedoms REINFORCE ONE ANOTHER
  5. FREEDOM IS BOTH THE END AND A MEANS OF DEVELOPMENT
        |
        v
THE FIVE INSTRUMENTAL FREEDOMS -- AND WHY THEY ARE A CIRCLE, NOT A LIST
  POLITICAL FREEDOMS ....... voice, dissent, scrutiny, forcing a public response
  ECONOMIC FACILITIES ...... access to resources, exchange, work, credit, markets
  SOCIAL OPPORTUNITIES ..... schooling, health care, public provision that
                             RAISES CONVERSION
  TRANSPARENCY GUARANTEES .. dealing with one another under disclosure -- trust

                             destitution
  EACH RAISES THE VALUE OF THE OTHERS: schooling raises the usefulness of voice;

  facility usable; protective security is what lets a person exercise any of them.

        v

  +-----------------------------+-------------------------------------------+

  public services and social stability.
  CAPABILITY BRIDGE -> if development is CAPABILITY not OUTPUT, then beyond the
  level at which output secures capability, further growth has NO AUTOMATIC
  DEVELOPMENTAL CLAIM. Sen reaches the same conclusion by a NON-ECOLOGICAL route.
BLOCK D -- INTERGENERATIONAL JUSTICE: duties of JUSTICE, not benevolence, not to
  FORECLOSE the capacity of the unborn to meet their needs. BRUNDTLAND (1987)
  builds it into the concept -- PARAPHRASE, NEVER QUOTE. THREE PROBLEMS: the
  NON-IDENTITY PROBLEM (different policies produce different people; the person
  born into a degraded world owes her existence to the degrading policy ->
  response: IMPERSONAL principles, or ground the duty in the RESOURCE BASE);
  MOTIVATION AND RECIPROCITY (the unborn cannot vote, bargain or sanction);
  DISCOUNTING (a PURE TIME PREFERENCE is arbitrary in the way that discounting
  DISTANCE is arbitrary). SPECIFICATION -> PRESERVE OPTIONS, AVOID
  IRREVERSIBILITY. Uncertainty then argues FOR PRECAUTION, not for inaction.
BLOCK E -- ENVIRONMENTALISM OF THE POOR (GUHA and MARTINEZ-ALIER, *VARIETIES OF
  ENVIRONMENTALISM*, 1997): in the global South environmental conflict is
  typically a struggle over LIVELIHOOD -- forests, water, grazing, fisheries,
  clean air -- not a POST-MATERIAL taste for wilderness. THEREFORE ENVIRONMENT
  AND DEVELOPMENT ARE THE SAME QUESTION for such communities, and "ENVIRONMENT
  VERSUS DEVELOPMENT" IS A FRAMING AVAILABLE ONLY TO THOSE WHOSE SUBSISTENCE IS
  NOT AT STAKE. THE REAL QUESTION IS DISTRIBUTIVE: WHO GAINS, WHO BEARS THE RISK,
  WHO DECIDES, ON WHAT TERMS. Objection: it ROMANTICISES the poor. Reply: it is
  about the STRUCTURE OF CONFLICT, not ecological virtue -- and SECURE RIGHTS
  convert DEPENDENCE INTO STEWARDSHIP.
BLOCK F -- ECO-AUTHORITARIANISM: hard limits + slow, short-horizoned democracy ->
  insulate decisions. FOUR REPLIES IN ASCENDING STRENGTH: EPISTEMIC (dispersed
  local knowledge of soils, water, seasons, species) | COMPLIANCE (imposed
  measures are evaded and reversed) | RECORD (insulation removes the FEEDBACK
  that detects damage) | DISTRIBUTIVE, THE DECISIVE ONE (a burden falling on
  those least able to bear it is neither JUST nor DURABLE -> JUST TRANSITION
  conditions restructuring on PRIOR GUARANTEES of livelihood, retraining,
  compensation and participation). VERDICT: THE ECOLOGICAL CASE **STRENGTHENS**
  THE ARGUMENT FOR PARTICIPATION.

SIX CRITICISMS AND REPLIES (with residual problems)
  capability is hard to measure -> evaluative richness beats false precision |
    residual: policy needs indicators
  universal capabilities are paternalistic -> protect opportunity, permit plural
    functionings | residual: who fixes the threshold?
  participation romanticises communities -> include dissent, rights, review |
    residual: elite capture
  sustainability blocks poor societies' growth -> affluent consumption and
    unequal burdens are the target | residual: transition costs
  Gandhianism is anti-modern -> a critique of scale, wants and domination |
    residual: complex infrastructure
  social progress is value-relative -> freedom from severe deprivation and
    humiliation have cross-cultural force | residual: reasonable disagreement
THE SIXTEEN TRAPS
  (1) growth, development, human development and progress are NOT synonyms.
  (2) never say economic development is simply "necessary and sufficient" --
      state the MATERIAL-THRESHOLD nuance. (3) Sen is NOT income plus welfare;
  capability is REAL FREEDOM and AGENCY matters. (4) capabilities are NOT
  achieved functionings. (5) a human-development composite is NOT a complete
  theory of progress. (6) technology does NOT create ethical progress. (7) skill
  education is NOT an employment statistic -- discuss capability, work quality
  and critical education. (8) do NOT romanticise tribal culture or the village.
  (9) enactment of PESA or FRA is NOT implementation or success. (10) a scheme,
  statute or judgment is NOT philosophical proof -- use it as a DATED
  INSTITUTIONAL ILLUSTRATION. (11) an indicator is NOT a theory of development --
  state its INFORMATIONAL SPACE and BLIND SPOT. (12) modernisation is NOT neutral
  convergence or an inevitable sequence -- name LINEARITY, DIFFUSION and
  EXPERT AUTHORITY. (13) not every social change is progress -- state the VALUED
  DIRECTION and permit regress, reversal and plural paths. (14) consultation is
  NOT empowerment -- empowerment requires DURABLE DECISION-POWER, RESOURCES,
  VOICE and REASONS. (15) do NOT use current social or economic figures; NO
  FIGURE IS REQUIRED. (16) do NOT import full liberalism, Marxism or Ambedkar's
  caste theory -- use the DEVELOPMENT CRITERION and ROUTE the doctrine.
