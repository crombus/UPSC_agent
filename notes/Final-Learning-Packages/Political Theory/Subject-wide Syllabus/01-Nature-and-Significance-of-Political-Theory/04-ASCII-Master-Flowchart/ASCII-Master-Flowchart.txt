ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 8/12: The Decline and Revival Debate
CENTRAL FOCUS
The Decline and Revival Debate
  [1] Alfred Cobban described a decline of political theory under both capitalist and
      communist systems in the mid-twentieth century, yet still insisted that politics
      required criteria of judgment that narrow social-science methodology alone could not
      supply (Gauba, PDF p.25).
        |
        v
  [2] Dante Germino identified revival with the recovery of political philosophy itself —
      critical inquiry into right political order, distinct both from reductionist
      behaviouralism and from ideology; on his reading, detachment is not the same thing
      as ethical neutrality (Gauba, PDF pp.26-27).
        |
        v
  [3] The debate operates on two levels at once: a methodological dispute over what counts
      as valid evidence (Easton 1953 vs Strauss/Germino), and a civilizational dispute
      over whether politics can ever avoid argument about ends (Strauss/Germino/Marcuse vs
      Lipset's "good society already found" thesis). Gauba's closing formula — science
      strengthens means, philosophy judges ends (Gauba, PDF p.27) — settles the first
      dispute in favour of integration without conceding the second, i.e. theory can be
      scientifically disciplined and normatively serious at the same time.
        |
        v

             causal logic, strongest limit and qualified verdict.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 9/12: Qualified Thesis and Directive-Specific Architecture
CENTRAL FOCUS
Qualified Thesis and Directive-Specific Architecture
  [1] Premises: (a) the political is the public/common sphere of authoritative rule
      (Gauba, PDF pp.15-16); (b) knowledge divides into empirical, logical and evaluative
      statements (Gauba, PDF p.16); (c) positivists/behaviouralists wish to restrict valid
      knowledge to the first two categories alone (Gauba, PDF pp.16-17).
        |
        v
  [2] Mechanism: political life generates factual questions ("why do riots occur?") and
      value questions ("what is a good society?") simultaneously; a theory that excludes
      evaluative statements cannot fully account for political phenomena, so political
      science supplies explanation while political philosophy supplies justification and
      concept-clarification — two angles on the same subject-matter (Gauba, PDF pp.17-20).
        |
        v
  [3] Conclusion: political theory as a whole must integrate both components; its
      significance lies in control of social life, social criticism/reconstruction,
      concept-clarification and cultivation of mutual respect and toleration (Gauba, PDF
      pp.21-23).
