Gandhism: Satyagraha, Trusteeship, Swaraj and Sarvodaya
  [1] Synthesis verdict: reading the four doctrines along these same four axes shows that
      fascism alone answers "equality" with hierarchy and "liberty" with subordination to
      the leader — this is precisely what places it outside the family of anti-capitalist,
      broadly egalitarian responses, even though socialism, anarchism and Gandhism
      themselves disagree sharply on how much coercive state power, if any, is legitimate
      en route to equality.
        |
        v

      concentration of power — a diagnosis shared by socialism, anarchism and Gandhism

      is the right instrument to fix this — socialism says yes if reformed, anarchism says
      no and seeks abolition, Gandhism says no and seeks moral transcendence (Gauba, PDF

      leader-rule as desirable rather than as a problem to be solved (Gauba, PDF p.66).

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 8/12: Conservatism I: Burke, Oakeshott and the Four Pillars
CENTRAL FOCUS
Conservatism I: Burke, Oakeshott and the Four Pillars
  [1] Relation to Burke: Oakeshott supplies the epistemological argument (why blueprint
      politics cannot know enough) for what Burke argues historically and prudentially
      (why blueprint politics destroys the accumulated capital of an order). They are
      complementary, not identical: Burke defends particular inherited institutions;
      Oakeshott is more sceptical about all political doctrine, including any doctrine of
      conservatism itself.
        |
        v
  [2] Working definition: conservatism is the political doctrine that treats inherited
      institutions, practices and settled expectations as a store of accumulated practical
      experience, and therefore places the burden of proof on those proposing to redesign
      them — favouring gradual, corrective, reversible reform over comprehensive
      reconstruction from first principles.
        |
        v
  [3] Genus/differentia line for a "Define" directive: conservatism belongs to the genus
      of doctrines about the warrant for political change; its differentia is that it
      locates warrant in inherited practice and demonstrated workability rather than in a
      rationally designed end-state.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 11/12: MCQ Remediation and Trap Repair
CENTRAL FOCUS
MCQ Remediation and Trap Repair
  [1] "Conservatism = fascism = reaction.": Reaction seeks to restore a superseded order;
      conservatism seeks to conserve and repair the existing one; fascism (§§2-9) seeks a
      leader-centred totalitarian reconstruction that overrides inherited constitutional
      restraint — which is precisely what a Burkean position exists to defend.
        |
        v
  [2] Trap: "Gandhi appealed to village tradition, therefore Gandhi is a conservative." ->
      Gandhi attacks entrenched social hierarchy and subordinates property to trusteeship
      (Gauba, PDF pp.81-82; §13 Equality and Property rows); appealing to indigenous
      practice against imported models is not the same as granting inherited hierarchy a
      presumption of legitimacy.
        |
        v
  [3] Prudential reform — the decisive point: prescription creates a presumption in favour
      of what exists, not an immunity. Burke's argument against the French revolutionaries
      is directed at method and scale — the simultaneous abolition of an entire
      institutional order on the authority of an abstract theory — while he defends
      timely, particular, remedial reform that repairs a defect without dismantling the
      frame that makes repair possible. The exam-usable formula is "reform in order to
      conserve": change is the instrument of continuity, not its opposite.
