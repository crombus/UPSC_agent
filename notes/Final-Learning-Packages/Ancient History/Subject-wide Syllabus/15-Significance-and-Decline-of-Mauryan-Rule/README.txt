Repair summary
--------------
The Core now teaches 24 visual-first sessions across Mauryan imperial scale,
differentiated political integration, administration and communication,
inscriptional governance, dhamma/public ethics, agriculture, routes, crafts,
urbanisation, scripts, languages, art and cultural interaction. It separates
imperial contraction, dynastic termination and regional transformation.

Post-Ashoka succession is source-qualified: Dasharatha is the secure
inscriptional anchor; Puranic, Buddhist and Jain lists remain divergent later
traditions; the Brihadratha-Pushyamitra army-review account is treated
cautiously; c. 187/185 BCE remains a conventional terminal range.

Decline is evaluated through claim, evidence, analysis and qualification:
succession instability, scale and communication, fiscal-military burden,
provincial agency and oppressive officials, north-western pressure, economic
and urban claims, Ashokan pacifism, Brahmanical reaction, material diffusion
and ecological constraints. H.C. Raychaudhuri, D.D. Kosambi, R.S. Sharma,
Romila Thapar and Upinder Singh are used only through bounded named positions.
No single-cause collapse is accepted.

Remaining evidence limits
-------------------------
Exact later-Maurya ruler order/reign lengths, annual provincial-loss dates,
routine depth of local control, realised taxation, army costs, treasury
condition, edict reception, Taxila revolt chronology, the motive and details
of Brihadratha's death, the scale of ritual grievance, monetary debasement,
all-India urban decline and any terminal ecological shock remain uncertain or
unproven. Shunga, Indo-Greek and Satavahana history is used only to establish
regional transition; detailed treatment remains with Topic 16 and later topics.
