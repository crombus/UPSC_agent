ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 1/12: Democratic audit purpose and accountability chain
LEGISLATURE AUTHORISES
tax, appropriation and public purposes
        |
EXECUTIVE COLLECTS / SPENDS / RECORDS
        |
CAG TESTS INDEPENDENT EVIDENCE
        |
President or Governor causes report to be laid
        |
PAC / CoPU / legislature scrutinises -> executive responds

LIMIT: audit informs control; it does not govern, punish or recover by itself.
MUST REMEMBER: Separate constitutional status under Articles 148-151 from the detailed audit
  mandate supplied by the CAG's DPC Act, 1971 and its amendments.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 2/12: Articles 148-151 constitutional spine
ARTICLE 148 -> office, oath, removal safeguards, IAAD and charged expenses
ARTICLE 149 -> duties and powers prescribed by or under parliamentary law
ARTICLE 150 -> President prescribes Union/State account form on CAG advice
ARTICLE 151 -> CAG -> President/Governor -> legislature

TRAP: the Constitution neither states the six-year/65 rule nor lists every audit type.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 3/12: Appointment tenure removal and independence
APPOINTMENT -> President, warrant under hand and seal
OATH -> President or nominee; Third Schedule
TENURE -> DPC Act section 4: six years or age 65, whichever earlier
REMOVAL -> same manner and grounds as Supreme Court Judge
INSULATION -> no disadvantageous variation; charged office expenses; no further office

LIMIT: strong post-appointment protection does not answer selection-process criticism.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 4/12: Audit accounts and the 1976 boundary
ACCOUNTS create and compile the financial record
AUDIT independently tests records and transactions
1976 UNION DEPARTMENTALISATION -> ministries assumed much Union compilation
DPC ACT SECTIONS 10-12 -> residual accounts roles where responsibility remains
DPC ACT SECTIONS 13-20 -> audit mandate continues

INDIA: predominantly ex-post auditor, not routine pre-withdrawal comptroller.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 6/12: Financial compliance propriety and performance audit
FINANCIAL -> is financial information fairly presented?
COMPLIANCE -> were law, sanction, rule and contract followed?
PROPRIETY -> was discretion faithful, prudent and economical?
PERFORMANCE -> economy + efficiency + effectiveness
RECEIPTS / IT / ENVIRONMENT -> specialised objects or methods within legal mandate

BOUNDARY: test implementation and results; do not replace a lawful policy choice.
CLOSE DISTINCTION: The CAG is an ex-post auditor and reports through legislatures; it does not
  pre-authorise expenditure, prosecute wrongdoing or enforce PAC action.

STATUTORY CORPORATION

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 8/12: Private entities PPPs and revenue nexus
PRIVATE STATUS DOES NOT BAR OR CREATE AUDIT
Sections 14 / 15 / 16 / 20 or a special law must supply the gateway
PPP audit follows grants, guarantees, assets, user fees or revenue share only as authorised
TELECOM CASE (2014) -> records needed to verify Union revenue share could be accessed

LIMIT: audit of the public nexus is not universal audit of unrelated private business.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 9/12: Local bodies and federal audit variation
ARTICLES 243J / 243Z -> State legislatures provide local-account audit arrangements
STATE LAW -> primary auditor and procedure may vary
CAG -> direct statutory/entrusted audit where applicable
TECHNICAL GUIDANCE AND SUPPORT -> capacity and standardisation route
FINANCE COMMISSION LINK -> local grants require traceable accounts and audit

TRAP: India has no single uniform constitutional CAG audit model for every local body.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 10/12: Reports PAC CoPU and legislative follow-up
UNION: CAG -> President -> both Houses
STATE: CAG -> Governor -> State legislature
PAC -> appropriation / finance accounts and relevant CAG reports
CoPU -> public undertakings and related CAG reports
COMMITTEE -> evidence -> finding -> action-taken response -> follow-up

CAG assists technically; report != committee conclusion != automatic liability.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 11/12: Judicial controls and legal limits
ARVIND GUPTA (2012) -> performance audit and the three Es supported
S. SUBRAMANIAM BALAJI (2013) -> post-expenditure legality, validity and propriety
ARUN KUMAR AGRAWAL (2013) -> CAG report respected but legislative scrutiny remains
Association of Unified Telecom Service Providers of India v. Union of India (2014) ->
  revenue-share access, not universal private audit

SYNTHESIS: broad evidence role + ex-post timing + no adjudicatory finality.
