ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 1/8: 1. Four relation models
RELIGION AND MORALITY
 +-> DEPENDENCE: morality requires God/revelation
 +-> INDEPENDENCE: moral truth is autonomous
 +-> SUPPORT: morality is independent but religion motivates/sustains it
 +-> TENSION: religious command or identity can conflict with moral judgement
                         |
 separate ontology (what makes right), epistemology (how known),
 motivation (why act) and sanction (reward/punishment); one answer need not settle all four.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 2/8: 2. Divine command theory
GOD COMMANDS X -> X IS OBLIGATORY
 attractions: objective authority | categorical demand | accountability | motivation
                         |
 versions:
 voluntarist: rightness constituted by command
 epistemic: revelation tells us independently grounded moral truth
 motivational: love/judgement strengthens compliance
                         |
 pressure: command identification, conflicting revelations, autonomy and arbitrariness.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 3/8: 3. Euthyphro and modified DCT
IS X GOOD BECAUSE GOD COMMANDS IT?
 +-> YES: morality appears arbitrary; cruelty could become right
 +-> NO: goodness is independent; God is not its foundation
                         |
 MODIFIED DIVINE COMMAND THEORY
 God's necessarily loving/good nature grounds value
 divine commands flowing from that nature ground obligation
                         |
 reply blocks arbitrary commands; objection asks whether “good nature” is intelligible
 without a standard logically prior to command.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 4/8: 4. Kant and natural law
KANT: moral law arises from rational autonomy, not heteronomous reward or threat
 categorical imperative -> universal law + humanity as end
 God and immortality are practical postulates of highest good, not sources of duty
                         |
 NATURAL LAW: practical reason reads goods and purposes in human nature;
 Aquinas joins rational participation in eternal law with divine order
                         |
 contrast: obey because rationally right, not merely because commanded;
 religion may complete moral hope without replacing moral deliberation.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 5/8: 5. Indian moral grounding
MĪMĀṂSĀ: dharma known through authorless Veda; apūrva links ritual act and result
 NYĀYA: God administers karma, but deserts arise from agents' acts
 BUDDHISM: intention, compassion and non-harm within dependent origination; no creator
 JAINISM: ahiṃsā and vows purify jīva by blocking karmic influx
 GĪTĀ/VEDĀNTA: dharma, niṣkāma karma, devotion and knowledge reshape agency
                         |
 Indian ethics shows objective discipline without one uniform divine-command model.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 6/8: 6. Motivation and conduct
WHY BE MORAL?
 fear of punishment -> hope of reward -> social belonging -> character/virtue
 -> respect for persons -> compassion -> love of good/God
                         |
 lower motives may secure compliance but not moral maturity
 religiosity can support self-control, service and forgiveness;
 it can also rationalise bias when identity outruns universal concern
 TEST: conduct and reasons, not ritual profession alone, reveal moral integration.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 7/8: 7. Violence, Nietzsche and moral critique
RELIGION WITHOUT MORALITY: ritual purity or orthodoxy detached from justice/compassion
 VIOLENCE PATH: exclusive truth + sacred authorisation + dehumanised out-group
              + political grievance -> moral disengagement
 safeguards: interpretation, conscience, rights, plural institutions, non-violence
                         |
 NIETZSCHE: genealogy exposes ressentiment and life-denying slave morality
 objection to Nietzsche: power may erase equality and protection of weak
 verdict: both religion and anti-religious critique need independent scrutiny of consequences.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 8/8: 8. PYQ answer architecture
STATE precise dependence claim -> separate ground/knowledge/motive/sanction
 -> DCT -> Euthyphro -> modified DCT -> Kant/natural law
 -> Indian non-theistic and theistic models -> conduct/violence test -> verdict
                         |
 CLOSE: morality need not depend on divine command for intelligibility,
 yet religion can deepen moral motivation, community and hope;
 its legitimacy depends on whether faith remains answerable to reason, dignity and justice.
