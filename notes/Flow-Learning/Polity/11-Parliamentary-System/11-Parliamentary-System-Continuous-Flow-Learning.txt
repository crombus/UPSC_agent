ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 1/9: Responsible government: the constitutional architecture
PARLIAMENTARY GOVERNMENT
  +-- real executive is drawn from and responsible to the legislature
  +-- survival depends on confidence of the popularly elected House

UNION ARCHITECTURE
Articles 74-75 -> PM-led Council; collective responsibility to Lok Sabha
Article 77     -> conduct of Union business
Article 78     -> PM's duties toward the President

STATE PARALLELS
Articles 163-164 -> CM-led Council; responsibility to Legislative Assembly
Articles 166-167 -> State business and CM-Governor information duties

SYSTEM LOGIC
popular election -> legislative majority -> ministry -> continuing confidence -> accountability

TRAP: Parliament does not elect every minister separately.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 2/9: Nominal and real executive: advice, office and limited discretion
PRESIDENT: CONSTITUTIONAL HEAD
  +-- formal executive authority is vested in the President
  +-- normal exercise follows Article 74 ministerial advice
        |
PRIME MINISTER + COUNCIL: REAL POLITICAL EXECUTIVE
  +-- formulates policy and controls administration
  +-- remains responsible to Lok Sabha
        |
ADVICE CONTROL
  +-- President may once require reconsideration
  +-- reconsidered advice binds
  +-- Article 74(2) protects inquiry into advice, not every underlying act

LIMITED DISCRETION / CONVENTION ZONES
  +-- appoint PM where no clear majority exists
  +-- test confidence on the floor
  +-- assess dissolution where an alternative government may be possible

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 3/9: The parliamentary mechanism: fusion, membership and responsibility
CLASSIC FEATURES
  +-- dual executive: nominal head + real ministry
  +-- majority-party / coalition government
  +-- collective responsibility to Lok Sabha
  +-- political homogeneity or negotiated common programme
  +-- double membership: executive drawn from legislature
  +-- PM leadership
  +-- lower-House dissolution possibility
  +-- secrecy and Cabinet solidarity

COLLECTIVE RESPONSIBILITY
Cabinet decision -> all ministers publicly support or resign
loss of Lok Sabha confidence -> entire ministry resigns or seeks lawful dissolution

INDIVIDUAL RESPONSIBILITY
personal misconduct / departmental failure may trigger resignation without government fall

FUSION != absence of checks; courts, bicameralism, committees and elections remain.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 4/9: Confidence, no-confidence, dissolution and caretaker government
GOVERNMENT FORMS
  +-- majority ministry
  +-- coalition ministry
  +-- minority ministry with outside support
        |
CONTINUING TEST: DOES THE MINISTRY COMMAND LOK SABHA CONFIDENCE?
  +-- no-confidence motion
  +-- confidence motion
  +-- defeat on an issue expressly treated as confidence
  +-- floor test where majority is disputed
        |
IF CONFIDENCE IS LOST
  +-- resign
  +-- or advise dissolution, subject to assessment of alternatives
        |
CARETAKER CONVENTION
  +-- continuity until successor takes office
  +-- restraint in major irreversible policy unless necessity demands

TRAP: an internal party setback is not automatically loss of House confidence.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 5/9: Cabinet, Council of Ministers and Prime Ministerial leadership
COUNCIL OF MINISTERS: THE WIDER CONSTITUTIONAL BODY
  +-- Cabinet Ministers
  +-- Ministers of State, independent charge or attached
  +-- all ranks share collective responsibility

CABINET: SMALLER INNER DECISION CORE
  +-- major policy, legislation, budget, appointments and crisis decisions
  +-- supported by Cabinet Committees and Secretariat coordination

PRIME MINISTER
  +-- selects / allocates portfolios through advice
  +-- chairs Cabinet and coordinates ministries
  +-- leads majority or coalition and government business in Parliament
  +-- communicates between President and ministry under Article 78

DOMINANCE RISKS
party control + agenda control + ordinance / delegated legislation + weak scrutiny

LIMIT: presidentialisation is an analytical label; responsibility remains parliamentary.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 6/9: Parliamentary accountability: the corrective ecosystem
DAILY / PERIODIC SCRUTINY
  +-- Question Hour, debates, calling-attention devices and motions
  +-- budget, demands for grants, cut motions and financial control
  +-- committee examination of Bills, ministries and delegated legislation

FINANCIAL ACCOUNTABILITY
CAG reports -> Public Accounts Committee / other financial committees -> government response

POLITICAL ACCOUNTABILITY
opposition + media + federal second chamber + constituency pressure + elections

LEGAL / CONSTITUTIONAL ACCOUNTABILITY
judicial review + constitutional offices + statutory transparency and audit

WHY CONTROL MAY WEAKEN
whip and anti-defection pressure -> limited dissent
compressed sittings / rushed Bills -> reduced deliberation
executive information advantage -> scrutiny gap

REFORM: time, information, committee capacity and opposition rights.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 7/9: Parliamentary and presidential systems compared precisely
AXIS                 PARLIAMENTARY                    PRESIDENTIAL
executive-legislature  fusion / interdependence         organisational separation
survival               continuing House confidence      fixed constitutional term
executive form         nominal + real executive         generally single real executive
membership             ministers usually legislators    executive normally outside legislature
removal                 political no-confidence          exceptional impeachment process
strength                continuous accountability        tenure stability
risk                    dominance / instability          deadlock / rigid mandate

INDIA
  +-- parliamentary responsibility + written supreme Constitution
  +-- federalism, judicial review and rights limit legislative majorities

UK contrast: orthodox parliamentary sovereignty and constitutional monarchy
US contrast: separately elected President and stronger personnel separation.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 8/9: Indian adaptation: stability, coalition and current design debate
WHY ADOPTED
familiar institutions + diversity accommodation + daily accountability + flexible change

MERITS                              DEMERITS
executive-legislature coordination  Cabinet / PM dominance
continuous responsibility           instability in fragmented Houses
adaptable coalition bargaining      short-term politics and secrecy
easy peaceful replacement           weak separation and limited expertise

INDIAN ADAPTATIONS
coalition agreements -> common programme -> coordination committees
minority government -> issue-based confidence -> bargaining and instability risks
anti-defection -> stability support, but reduced legislative independence

CURRENT CONTROL
129th Amendment Bill, 2024 remains a committee-stage proposal.
Proposed Article 82A is not current law; use only for stability-accountability analysis.
