  [1] Historical materialism restated as a mechanism: economic relations of production,

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 6/12: Class, State, Exploitation and Alienation
CENTRAL FOCUS
Class, State, Exploitation and Alienation
  [1] Read together: exploitation (surplus value), domination (state as class instrument)
      and dehumanization (alienation) are three faces of the same underlying property
      relation, not three unrelated Marxist claims — a strong answer connects them rather
      than listing them separately.
        |
        v
  [2] Class: private property in the means of production divides society into dominant
      (owning) and dependent classes with antagonistic interests, driving conflict across
      historical stages toward an expected socialist/classless resolution (Gauba, PDF
      pp.47, 49-52).
        |
        v
  [3] Alienation: the "Young Marx"/humanist strain of neo-Marxism centres on alienation,
      freedom and capitalism's dehumanizing effects on the worker (Gauba, PDF p.47). The
      mechanism: wage-labour separates the worker from the product of labour and from the
      labour process itself, and, in humanist readings, from a fully developed human
      nature and from fellow workers — a separation that operates even before any single
      surplus-value calculation is made.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 9/12: Objections, Qualified Thesis, Evidence and Doctrine Architecture
CENTRAL FOCUS
Objections, Qualified Thesis, Evidence and Doctrine Architecture
  [1] Premises: (a) private property in the means of production divides society into
      antagonistic classes (Gauba, PDF p.47); (b) the mode of production — forces plus
      relations of production — forms the base, shaping law, politics and culture as
      superstructure (Gauba, PDF p.48); (c) productive forces and relations of production
      can come into conflict (Gauba, PDF p.49).
        |
        v
  [2] Mechanism: when forces of production outgrow existing relations of production, the
      relations become a fetter that is broken by revolution, producing a new social
      formation (§12); this economic-class mechanism operates through and is reproduced by
      state power as a class instrument (§13) and is experienced by individuals as
      alienation (§13). Neo-Marxists (§14) extend the mechanism by adding culture/hegemony
      (Gramsci), critical theory (Frankfurt School), structural analysis (Althusser) and
      international core-periphery relations (dependency theory) as additional — not
      substitute — sites where the same underlying property-based conflict is reproduced
      or contested.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 12/12: PYQ Ownership and Answer Practice
CENTRAL FOCUS
PYQ Ownership and Answer Practice
  [1] The consistency argument. If the means of production are brought under genuinely
      social, democratic control, then the structural coercion of the wage-relation is
      removed and freedom is EXTENDED to those the market subordinated -- collective
      ownership is the condition, not the enemy, of real freedom.
        |
        v
  [2] Reframe "freedom". Marx rejects a purely negative liberty (non-interference) that
      leaves formal rights operating over radically unequal social conditions. His
      positive ideal is non-domination PLUS self-development: the capacity to engage in
      conscious, social, creative activity, of which alienated labour deprives the worker
      (evidence P3 -- estrangement from product, activity, species-being and others).
        |
        v
  [3] What the free market vindicates in Marx. Concentration of capital, commodification,
      precarity, recurrent crises and the ideological normalisation of market relations
      remain live phenomena the analysis still explains (evidence P2 -- the contradiction
      between developing forces and existing relations; P4 -- structural exploitation
      through surplus value, argued as a relation, not via the contested price theory).
        |
