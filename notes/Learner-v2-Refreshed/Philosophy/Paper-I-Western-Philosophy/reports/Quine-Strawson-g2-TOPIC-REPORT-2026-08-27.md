## Official syllabus coverage

> Quine and Strawson : Critique of Empiricism; Theory of Basic Particulars and Persons.

The clause wording is taken verbatim from

Core contains **10** independently complete sessions covering both named projects:
Quine's attack on the analytic/synthetic distinction, his rejection of reductionism and
his positive confirmation holism, the Duhem–Quine machinery, radical translation and
gavagai, ontological commitment, naturalized epistemology and empiricism without the
dogmas; Strawson's descriptive versus revisionary metaphysics, basic particulars and
re-identification, person as a primitive concept with M- and P-predicates, `On Referring`
against Russell, and his own five-line critique of empiricism; and a final comparative
synthesis. **10** Advanced sessions plus the optional Western Philosophy dossier follow
separately and are not required for a core answer.

## Semantic safeguards

- Quine and Strawson are kept as two distinct projects; Grice and Strawson's
  "In Defence of a Dogma" (1956) is stated so the pairing is never written as one front.
- Quine's conclusion is the absence of a principled, non-circular criterion of
  analyticity — a difference of degree of centrality, not the denial of analytic-seeming
  truths.
- The 2025 holism quotation is decoded through its conditional clause and the maxim of
  minimum mutilation, not as relativism.
- Duhem (1906, physics only, logic exempt, no *experimentum crucis*) is never merged with
  Quine (1951, whole of science, logic included, underdetermination).
- The bound-variable criterion is dated to "On What There Is" (1948), and naturalized
  epistemology to 1968/69 — never to "Two Dogmas" (1951).
- Strawson's "basic" is a claim of conceptual dependence through re-identification, not a
  proof that bodies exist; the auditory no-space world supports rather than defeats it.
- Person as primitive is derived through the self-ascription argument and shown blocking
  both Cartesian dualism and the no-ownership theory; behaviour stays criterial evidence
  rather than the meaning of P-predicates.
- On the empty subject Strawson gives a truth-value gap, never a falsehood; `On Referring`
  (1950) is kept apart from `On Denoting` (1905) and `Individuals` (1959) from
  `The Bounds of Sense` (1966).
- Obsolete `Progress X/Y` interactive navigation is stripped; only the `SESSION N`
  hierarchy is used.
