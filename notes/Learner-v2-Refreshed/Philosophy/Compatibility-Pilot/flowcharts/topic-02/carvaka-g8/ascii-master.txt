+-- SESSION 08: JAINISM IN INTER-SCHOOL COMPARISON
|   DEFINITION / TERMS : Jainism combines enduring substances with changing modes, thereby opposing Buddhist momentariness, Advaita's ultimate non-duality and Cārvāka's rejection of supersensible bondage.
|   MECHANISM / ARGUMENT: The comparison turns on whether reality contains enduring substances, whether a permanent self exists and whether karmic bondage is metaphysically real.
|   CONSEQUENCE / CONTRAST: Jainism preserves plurality and persistence while explaining change through modes, but rivals challenge the coherence of combining permanence and alteration.
|   TRAP / ANSWER-USE: Do not equate Jain non-absolutism with Buddhist emptiness or Advaita's claim that plurality is ultimately sublated.
|   EXAM OPENING: Jainism occupies a distinctive middle position: it is realist without absolutist finite predication and pluralist without denying the disciplined possibility of truth.

|   DEFINITION / TERMS : Jaina reply. ✅ Jainism replies through utpāda-vyaya-dhrauvya: origination, cessation, and permanence are simultaneously real features of every sat; the substance is permanent qua dravya and changing qua paryāya.
|   MECHANISM / ARGUMENT: The operative mechanism is that jaina reply. ✅ ✅ Opposed predicates are indexed to distinct standpoints.
|   CONSEQUENCE / CONTRAST: Jaina reply. ✅ The Jaina answer again uses standpoint-indexing: the same dravya persists while paryāyas arise and cease, so the opposition is between aspects, not within one unqualified predication.
|   TRAP / ANSWER-USE: Assessment. ⚠️ The reply is internally coherent within Jaina pluralism, but the interaction of conscious pradeśa-bearing jīva and matter remains a debated point.
|   EXAM OPENING: Jaina reply. ✅ ✅ Opposed predicates are indexed to distinct standpoints: dravya (substance), kṣetra (place), kāla (time), and bhāva (mode). ✅ There is no contradiction unless affirmation and negation are made in the same respect, place, time, and mode. ✅ Syādvāda is itself asserted syāt; this reflexive qualification is a virtue because the doctrine obeys its own rule. ⚠️ A residual objection remains: if syādvāda is always qualified, critics may say it loses final assertoric force.
