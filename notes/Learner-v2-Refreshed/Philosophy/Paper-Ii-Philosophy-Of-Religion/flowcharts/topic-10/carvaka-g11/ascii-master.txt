ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 1/8: 1. Two-axis control
RELIGIOUS LANGUAGE HAS TWO DISTINCT QUESTIONS
 AXIS 1 — MODE OF PREDICATION: univocal | equivocal | analogical | symbolic | negative
 AXIS 2 — LOGICAL STATUS: cognitive/factual | non-cognitive | performative | expressive
                         |
 “God is good” may be analogical AND cognitive;
 “I commit myself to God” may be meaningful without describing an observable object
 NEVER merge how words mean with whether utterances assert facts.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 2/8: 2. Univocal, equivocal and analogical
UNIVOCAL: same meaning for God and creatures -> clarity, but risks anthropomorphism
 EQUIVOCAL: wholly different meaning -> protects transcendence, but blocks knowledge/inference
 ANALOGICAL: related meaning grounded in causal participation
                         |
 AQUINAS: analogy of attribution/proportionality; creaturely goodness reflects divine source
 SCOTUS: some univocal concept of being is needed for valid reasoning about God
 tension: enough continuity for truth, enough difference for transcendence.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 3/8: 3. Symbol, negation and indication
TILLICH: symbol participates in the reality it discloses and opens levels of reality/self
 symbols are living and can die; they are not arbitrary signs
                         |
 VIA NEGATIVA: God is not finite, composite or dependent
 PSEUDO-DIONYSIUS: affirmation -> negation -> supereminent transcendence
 INDIAN “NETI NETI”: not this, not this; removes finite objectification
 LAKṢAṆĀ/indication: indirect meaning when literal sense fails
 limit: pure negation must retain some positive orientation or reference disappears.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 4/8: 4. Cognitive spectrum
COGNITIVE REALISM: God-talk states truths that may be warranted or false
 ANTI-REALISM/NON-REALISM: language expresses a form of life, orientation or construction
 EXPRESSIVISM: articulates attitude/commitment
 PERFORMATIVISM: prayer, vow, confession and blessing do something
 MYTHIC/NARRATIVE: forms identity and discloses a world of meaning
                         |
 utterances can have several functions; functional richness does not itself settle reference.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 5/8: 5. Verification and falsification
AYER: cognitively meaningful factual claims must be analytic or empirically verifiable
 -> traditional God-talk appears meaningless, not merely false
 HICK: eschatological verification — celestial-city parable; confirmation may occur after death
                         |
 FLEW: believers let “God loves us” die by a thousand qualifications
 HARE: religious blik is unfalsifiable orientation yet meaningful
 MITCHELL: partisan trusts stranger despite counter-evidence; commitment remains evidentially tested
 key test: what counts against the claim, and what kind of claim is it?

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 6/8: 6. Use, self-involvement and forms of life
WITTGENSTEIN: meaning is use within a language-game and form of life
 prayer, lament, confession and worship are not failed scientific hypotheses
 BRAITHWAITE: religious assertions express intention to follow an agapeistic way of life
                         |
 SAME WORD, DIFFERENT GRAMMAR
 “father” biological/secular | “Father” relational religious use
 “creation” temporal manufacture | dependence on ultimate source
 objection: use may explain intelligibility, but cannot by itself guarantee truth
 or protect critique.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 7/8: 7. Mysticism, Advaita and pluralism bridges
MYSTICISM: ineffability pressures literal description; paradox and symbol point beyond concepts
 ADVAITA: nirguṇa Brahman beyond limiting predicates; saguṇa language valid empirically
 mahāvākya interpretation uses implication to remove incompatible literal attributes
                         |
 PLURALISM: different conceptual schemes may refer to or construct diverse ultimates
 challenge: translation without erasing doctrinal difference
 answer: compare reference, predication, function and life-context separately.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 8/8: 8. PYQ answer architecture
IDENTIFY utterance -> analyse mode of predication -> classify logical status
 -> named theory (Aquinas/Tillich/Ayer/Flew/Hare/Mitchell/Wittgenstein)
 -> strongest objection/reply -> Indian analogy or negation bridge -> verdict
                         |
 CLOSE: religious language need not be literal to be meaningful,
 but analogy, symbol, use or commitment cannot automatically secure factual truth;
 adequacy requires semantic intelligibility, critical vulnerability and lived function.
