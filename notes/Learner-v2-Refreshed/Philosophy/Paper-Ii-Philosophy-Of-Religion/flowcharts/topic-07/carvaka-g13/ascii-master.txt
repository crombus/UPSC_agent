ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 1/8: 1. Definition and possibility test
CAN RELIGION EXIST WITHOUT A CREATOR GOD?
 religion may organise:
 ultimate concern | sacred value | ritual | community | moral discipline
 worldview | transformation | liberation
 SUFFICIENT CLUSTER: sacred order + disciplined practice + community + transformation
                         |
 THEISM is sufficient for many religions, not conceptually necessary for all
 test a tradition by doctrine + practice + soteriology, not by a Western creator template
 TRAP: non-theistic != atheistic; silence, irrelevance and denial are different positions.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 2/8: 2. Four negative positions
ATHEISM: denies that God exists
 AGNOSTICISM: suspends judgement because knowledge/evidence is insufficient
 NON-THEISM: God is absent or non-central to the path
 ANTI-THEISM: holds God-belief harmful or false in a stronger evaluative sense
                         |
 IGNOSTICISM: “God” must be made meaningful before existence is debated
 practical atheism: life organised without reference to God
 keep epistemic suspension separate from metaphysical denial.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 3/8: 3. Buddhism
FOUR NOBLE TRUTHS -> dependent origination -> eightfold path -> nirvāṇa
                         |
 no eternal creator is needed; suffering has conditioned causes, not divine decree
 devas may occur in cosmology but are impermanent and unsaving
 undeclared questions do not exhaust the case; creator-belief is non-central to the path
                         |
 objection: karma/order functions like hidden providence
 reply: causal-moral regularity is impersonal; no omnipotent allocator or creator follows.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 4/8: 4. Jainism, Mīmāṃsā and Sāṃkhya
JAINISM: eternal jīva/ajīva; karmic matter; tīrthaṅkara teaches, not creates
 MĪMĀṂSĀ: authorless eternal Veda; ritual action generates apūrva; no divine author needed
 SĀṂKHYA: prakṛti evolves for plural puruṣas; unconscious teleology without creator
                         |
 shared anti-creator pressure: if God is perfect, why create? if embodied, who made body?
 distinctions: Jainism retains worshipful exemplars; Mīmāṃsā centres dharma/ritual;
 Sāṃkhya centres discriminative metaphysics and kaivalya.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 5/8: 5. Cārvāka as limit case
CĀRVĀKA denies God, afterlife, karma, Vedic authority and mokṣa
 embodied this-worldly flourishing replaces salvation
                         |
 DOES IT COUNT AS RELIGION?
 doctrinal-historical member of Indian philosophical debate: YES
 sacred/transcendent/soteriological practice in robust sense: largely NO
                         |
 limit case shows that “philosophy without God” is easier than “religion without God”;
 Buddhism/Jainism better preserve ritual, community and liberation functions.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 6/8: 6. Nietzsche and post-theism
“GOD IS DEAD” = collapse of the Christian-metaphysical value horizon
                         |
 danger: nihilism, herd morality exposed, no transcendent guarantor
 task: revaluation of values -> self-overcoming -> life-affirmation
                         |
 not a simple empirical proof that no deity exists
 objection: self-created values risk arbitrariness or domination
 reply: genealogy tests inherited values; affirmative creation accepts responsibility
 POST-THEISM asks what religious depth survives after classical supernatural belief.

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 7/8: 7. Naturalism and fictionalism
RELIGIOUS NATURALISM: nature is ultimate; awe, gratitude, ritual and ethics without supernaturalism
 FICTIONALISM: participate in God-language/practice without literal ontological assent
 SEA OF FAITH / non-realism: religious symbols shape life rather than report a transcendent entity
                         |
 objections: worship without real object is make-believe; prayer loses address; truth is sacrificed
 replies: symbols and communal narratives can disclose value and transform conduct
 pressure remains: can function alone preserve religion's claim to truth and transcendence?

ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 8/8: 8. PYQ and qualified verdict
DEFINE religion by clustered features -> distinguish four negative positions
 -> demonstrate Buddhism/Jainism/Mīmāṃsā/Sāṃkhya -> use Cārvāka boundary
 -> Nietzsche/post-theism -> naturalism/fictionalism -> objection on truth/object
                         |
 VERDICT BY CRITERION
 creator-belief necessary? NO | ultimate concern/ritual/transformation possible? YES
 petition, grace and providence preserved? normally NO
 CLOSE: religion without God is coherent, but not religion without any ultimate value,
 disciplined practice, communal form or account of transformation.
