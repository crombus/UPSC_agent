|   DEFINITION / TERMS : ⚠️ Distinction to state in the answer: Anselm argues by reductio from a comparative ("greater than"); Descartes argues from essence containment ("existence is a perfection").
|   MECHANISM / ARGUMENT: ⚠️ Distinction to state in the answer: Anselm argues by reductio from a comparative ("greater than"); Descartes argues from essence containment ("existence is a perfection").
|   CONSEQUENCE / CONTRAST: It is the argument that has attracted the most sustained modern logical attention of all the proofs. ⚠️ Do not manufacture an "Indian ontological argument" out of Advaita.
|   TRAP / ANSWER-USE: Gaunilo offers a parody (the form proves too much); Kant offers a diagnosis (the form uses "existence" illegitimately). ⚠️ Do not attribute the island example to Kant, or the thaler example to Gaunilo. ⚠️ Descartes' ontological proof (Meditations V) must not be confused with his causal proof from the idea of the infinite (Meditations III). ⚠️ The modal version does not defeat Kant "cleanly"; it relocates the problem to the possibility premise. ⚠️ Do not say the ontological argument is "obviously absurd".
|   EXAM OPENING: The ontological argument is an a priori attempt to derive God's necessary existence from the concept of the greatest conceivable being.

+-- SESSION 05: KANT'S TWO-STOREY STRUCTURE
|   DEFINITION / TERMS : Technically, Kant'S Two-Storey Structure is analysed by relating logical inference to Newman's version, then testing the relationship through Hick's objection and sovereign claim.
|   MECHANISM / ARGUMENT: Kant is the destroyer of the theistic proofs and the author of a moral argument, and he sees no contradiction because the two operate on different storeys.
|   CONSEQUENCE / CONTRAST: The resulting consequence is that the basic assumption of all such arguments is that moral values are not capable...
|   TRAP / ANSWER-USE: This is the topic's most penalised error. ⚠️ Three postulates — freedom, immortality, God — not one. ⚠️ Newman's conscience argument is not Kant's; keep the two forms separate. ⚠️ God is not the source of moral obligation for Kant; the moral law is autonomous. ⚠️ Euthyphro refutes divine-command theories, not Kant's postulate. ⚠️ "Ought implies can" applies to promoting the highest good, not to achieving it single-handedly. ⚠️ Do not present the postulate as a reward-motive; that would be heteronomy and would destroy moral worth on Kant's own terms. ⚠️ Do not omit the Indian comparison; karmaphaladātā is the natural Indian counterpart.
|   EXAM OPENING: ✅ Hick's objection: the basic assumption of all such arguments is that moral values are not capable of naturalistic explanation — in terms of human needs, desires, ideals, self-interest, or the structure of human nature or society.

+-- SESSION 06: NYĀYA'S COSMIC DIVISION OF LABOUR
|   DEFINITION / TERMS : ⚠️ Nyāya's God is the efficient, never the material, cause.
|   MECHANISM / ARGUMENT: ⚠️ Nyāya's God is the efficient, never the material, cause.
|   CONSEQUENCE / CONTRAST: God is creator, preserver and destroyer — hence theism, not deism.
|   TRAP / ANSWER-USE: Atoms are the eternal material causes. ⚠️ Nyāya theism is theism, not deism — God preserves and destroys as well as creates. ⚠️ Eternal intelligence is an inseparable attribute of God in Nyāya, not His essence as in Advaita. ⚠️ Do not answer "the nature of Nyāya's God" merely by listing Udayana's proofs, or vice versa. ⚠️ Adṛṣṭa does not consciously distribute its own fruits — that unconsciousness is the whole premise of the argument. ⚠️ Enumerations of the proofs vary; say so and use the ones the question needs. ⚠️ The Veda–God circularity is answered by the order-of-existence / order-of-knowledge distinction; do not concede the circle. ⚠️ Do not treat Nyāya as "the Indian Aquinas" without flagging the material-causation divergence.
|   EXAM OPENING: Nyāya's Īśvara is an eternal, omniscient, omnipotent special self, never bound by karma.

+-- SESSION 08: PART I — JAINISM

+-- SESSION 09: PART II — BUDDHISM
|   DEFINITION / TERMS : ✅ If God is eternal and unchanging, genuine creation becomes difficult to explain, because willing or producing seems to involve change. ✅ If God creates from desire or purpose, that suggests lack, preference or incompleteness. ✅ The presence of suffering and evil sits uneasily with an omnipotent benevolent creator. ✅ Dependent origination (pratītyasamutpāda) already explains the arising of phenomena through conditions, making a creator unnecessary.
|   MECHANISM / ARGUMENT: ✅ If God is eternal and unchanging, genuine creation becomes difficult to explain, because willing or producing seems to involve change. ✅ If God creates from desire or purpose, that suggests lack, preference or incompleteness. ✅ The presence of suffering and evil sits uneasily with an omnipotent benevolent creator. ✅ Dependent origination (pratītyasamutpāda) already explains the arising of phenomena through conditions, making a creator unnecessary.
|   CONSEQUENCE / CONTRAST: If God determines all, karma and moral effort are meaningless; if God does not determine all, God is not the sufficient cause and the theistic explanation is redundant. ⚠️ The Dharmakīrti / Śāntarakṣita line.
|   TRAP / ANSWER-USE: ✅ Philosophical force, stated correctly: the Buddhist critique is not mere atheistic denial; it is a systematic refusal of explanatory surplus.
|   EXAM OPENING: Buddhism rejects a permanent creator and explains suffering through conditioned arising rather than divine production.

+-- SESSION 10: PART III — MĪMĀṂSĀ
|   DEFINITION / TERMS : ✅ And the direct counter to Nyāya's adṛṣṭa proof: apūrva is the unseen potency produced by correctly performed ritual; it persists after the act and matures into future fruit.
|   MECHANISM / ARGUMENT: ✅ And the direct counter to Nyāya's adṛṣṭa proof: apūrva is the unseen potency produced by correctly performed ritual; it persists after the act and matures into future fruit.
|   CONSEQUENCE / CONTRAST: Prābhākara adds niyoga: the injunction itself creates obligation, so one ought to act because the Veda commands, not because a deity rewards.
|   TRAP / ANSWER-USE: Answering the 2019 question with "Jainism is atheistic" misreads it. ⚠️ Jaina godhood is plural, achieved and non-creative; a siddha has no cosmic function. ⚠️ Jaina karma is material (pudgala); do not equate it with Nyāya's adṛṣṭa or a generic moral force. ⚠️ Nāstika means denier of Vedic authority, not denier of spirit. ⚠️ Buddhism's critique is a refusal of explanatory surplus, not a proof of atheism. ⚠️ The avyākṛta silence is a methodological judgement, not agnostic evasion. ⚠️ Mīmāṃsā's Veda is authorless — not God-authored; this is the exact contradictory of Nyāya's third proof. ⚠️ Apūrva is Mīmāṃsā's substitute for a fruit-dispensing God; do not call it "karma" loosely. ⚠️ Do not say Sāṃkhya "has no view"; classical Sāṃkhya is nirīśvara and also denies a creator. ⚠️ Do not present these schools as a single "Indian atheism".
|   EXAM OPENING: Mīmāṃsā (school of inquiry into ritual duty) treats the Veda (sacred revealed knowledge) as authorless and karma (morally consequential action) as self-fructifying, thereby displacing the need for God.
