+-- SESSION 05: KANT'S TWO-STOREY STRUCTURE
|   DEFINITION / TERMS : Kant is the destroyer of the theistic proofs and the author of a moral argument, and he sees no contradiction because the two operate on different storeys.
|   MECHANISM / ARGUMENT: Kant is the destroyer of the theistic proofs and the author of a moral argument, and he sees no contradiction because the two operate on different storeys.
|   CONSEQUENCE / CONTRAST: ✅ Hick's objection: the basic assumption of all such arguments is that moral values are not capable of naturalistic explanation — in terms of human needs, desires, ideals, self-interest, or the structure of human nature or society.
|   TRAP / ANSWER-USE: ⚠️ The whole examinable point is in the architecture, not the content.
|   EXAM OPENING: Kant rejects speculative demonstrations of God while postulating God within practical reason as a condition of the highest good.

+-- SESSION 06: NYĀYA'S COSMIC DIVISION OF LABOUR
|   DEFINITION / TERMS : ⚠️ Nyāya's God is the efficient, never the material, cause.
|   MECHANISM / ARGUMENT: ⚠️ Nyāya's God is the efficient, never the material, cause.
|   CONSEQUENCE / CONTRAST: God is creator, preserver and destroyer — hence theism, not deism.
|   TRAP / ANSWER-USE: ✅ God is the creator of the world in the sense of being the first efficient cause of the world and not its material cause — "a sort of demiurgus or a builder of the ordered universe". ✅ He is also the preserver, in so far as the world is kept in existence by the will of God, and the destroyer, who lets loose the forces of destruction when the exigencies of the moral world require it.

+-- SESSION 08: PART I — JAINISM
|   DEFINITION / TERMS : Jainism rejects a creator God but affirms liberated, omniscient souls as achieved godhead attained through self-effort.
|   MECHANISM / ARGUMENT: ✅ Jainism is "a religion without God" in the sense of a creator. ✅ There is no necessity of bringing in God to explain creation, for the world was never created: production, destruction and permanence characterise all substances; things have creation and dissolution because of their modes (paryāya), while the substances themselves are eternal.
|   CONSEQUENCE / CONTRAST: ✅ "Though Jainism denies God, it does not deny godhead.
|   TRAP / ANSWER-USE: In that Vedic sense Jainism and Buddhism are nāstika; but they are intensely spiritual and ethical, and denial of God does not amount to atheism in the Western sense — otherwise Sāṃkhya and Mīmāṃsā, which likewise do not believe in a creator God, could not have been counted orthodox.
|   EXAM OPENING: Jainism rejects a creator God but affirms liberated, omniscient souls as achieved godhead attained through self-effort.

+-- SESSION 09: PART II — BUDDHISM
|   DEFINITION / TERMS : Buddhism rejects a permanent creator and explains suffering through conditioned arising rather than divine production.
|   MECHANISM / ARGUMENT: ✅ If God is eternal and unchanging, genuine creation becomes difficult to explain, because willing or producing seems to involve change. ✅ If God creates from desire or purpose, that suggests lack, preference or incompleteness. ✅ The presence of suffering and evil sits uneasily with an omnipotent benevolent creator. ✅ Dependent origination (pratītyasamutpāda) already explains the arising of phenomena through conditions, making a creator unnecessary.
|   CONSEQUENCE / CONTRAST: If God determines all, karma and moral effort are meaningless; if God does not determine all, God is not the sufficient cause and the theistic explanation is redundant. ⚠️ The Dharmakīrti / Śāntarakṣita line.
|   TRAP / ANSWER-USE: ✅ Philosophical force, stated correctly: the Buddhist critique is not mere atheistic denial; it is a systematic refusal of explanatory surplus.
|   EXAM OPENING: Buddhism rejects a permanent creator and explains suffering through conditioned arising rather than divine production.

+-- SESSION 10: PART III — MĪMĀṂSĀ
|   DEFINITION / TERMS : ✅ The Veda is authoritative because it is apauruṣeya — eternal, authorless and self-valid.
|   MECHANISM / ARGUMENT: ✅ The Veda is authoritative because it is apauruṣeya — eternal, authorless and self-valid.
|   CONSEQUENCE / CONTRAST: They also record Max Müller's contention that if God were supposed to be the creator, He would be liable to the charges of cruelty and partiality; and Müller's further point that rejection of a creator-God is not necessarily rejection of God, since pantheisms such as Advaita and Spinoza's do not accept the reality of creation and yet are not fairly called atheistic. ⚠️ Their own verdict is more sceptical: judged by what the Mīmāṃsā itself does and says, the Vedic deities are not treated as objects of worship and are not believed to exist anywhere except in the mantras that describe them — "eternal and self-manifesting concepts", like the immortal characters of classical epics. ⚠️ In its anxiety to maintain the supremacy of the Vedas, the Mīmāṃsā "relegates God to an ambiguous position".
|   TRAP / ANSWER-USE: Prābhākara adds niyoga: the injunction itself creates obligation, so one ought to act because the Veda commands, not because a deity rewards.
|   EXAM OPENING: Mīmāṃsā (school of inquiry into ritual duty) treats the Veda (sacred revealed knowledge) as authorless and karma (morally consequential action) as self-fructifying, thereby displacing the need for God.
