# UPSC Mains Directive Words - Answer-Writing Guide

## 1. Why directive words matter

The directive tells you what intellectual operation the examiner expects.
Knowledge may be correct but still earn fewer marks if the answer performs the
wrong operation.

```text
QUESTION
   |
   +-- Subject: What is the topic?
   +-- Directive: What must I do with the topic?
   +-- Scope: Which period, region, dimension or claim?
   +-- Subparts: How many demands must I answer?
   `-- Limit: Marks, words and available time
```

## 2. The three commonly confused directives

### Discuss

**Meaning:** Examine the important dimensions of an issue and develop a
balanced, connected account.

**What the examiner expects:**

- explain the central issue;
- cover its principal dimensions;
- present relevant supporting and opposing considerations where they exist;
- show relationships among the dimensions;
- reach a balanced conclusion.

```text
DEFINE / CONTEXT
      |
      v
DIMENSION 1
      +
DIMENSION 2
      +
DIMENSION 3
      |
      v
LIMITATION / COUNTERVIEW
      |
      v
BALANCED CONCLUSION
```

**Do not:** merely list advantages and disadvantages without explaining them.

**Example:** "Discuss the role of social capital in economic development."

Answer through meaning -> mechanisms -> examples -> limitations -> judgement.

---

### Critically analyse

**Meaning:** Break the issue into its principal components, explain their
relationships, test the assumptions and evidence, and reach a reasoned
judgement after considering strengths and weaknesses.

**What the examiner expects:**

- identify the central claim;
- divide it into causal or conceptual components;
- explain how and why they operate;
- examine evidence supporting the claim;
- identify limitations, exceptions and counter-evidence;
- give a graded verdict.

```text
CLAIM
  |
  v
COMPONENTS / CAUSAL MECHANISM
  |
  +-- Supporting evidence
  +-- Assumptions
  +-- Limitations
  +-- Counter-evidence
  `-- Exceptions
  |
  v
REASONED, GRADED VERDICT
```

**"Critically" does not mean:** criticise everything or write equal positive
and negative points. It means test the claim rather than accept it
unquestioningly.

**Do not:** describe the topic and add one negative sentence at the end.

**Example:** "Critically analyse the view that economic growth automatically
produces human development."

The answer must trace the growth-to-development mechanism, identify conversion
conditions such as distribution and public services, present failures and
successes, and judge the extent to which the claim holds.

---

### Elucidate

**Meaning:** Make a difficult statement, doctrine or relationship clear through
systematic explanation.

**What the examiner expects:**

- state the meaning in simple terms;
- unpack every important phrase;
- explain the underlying logic;
- use an example, diagram or illustration;
- clarify the significance.

```text
STATEMENT / CONCEPT
       |
       v
PLAIN MEANING
       |
       v
STEP-BY-STEP LOGIC
       |
       v
EXAMPLE / ILLUSTRATION
       |
       v
SIGNIFICANCE
```

**Do not:** turn the answer into an extensive merits-versus-demerits evaluation
unless the question separately asks for criticism.

**Example:** "Elucidate Nyaya's theory of inference."

Explain its terms, five members, universal relation, standard example and
philosophical importance.

## 3. Core explanatory directives

| Directive | Exact demand | Recommended structure | Main mistake |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Define** | Give the precise meaning and boundary of a term. | Definition -> essential features -> boundary/example. | Giving a long history instead of a definition. |
| **Describe** | Present the principal features or sequence accurately. | Context -> features/stages -> brief significance. | Adding unsupported evaluation. |
| **Explain** | Show how or why something occurs. | Meaning -> mechanism/causes -> example -> consequence. | Merely restating the claim. |
| **Elucidate** | Make a complex idea clear by unpacking it. | Plain meaning -> components -> logic -> illustration -> significance. | Debating instead of clarifying. |
| **Illustrate** | Explain through relevant examples, diagrams or cases. | Claim -> example -> explain exactly how the example proves it. | Giving examples without connecting them to the claim. |
| **Enumerate/List** | State the requested points concisely. | One-line context -> numbered points -> brief closing line. | Writing long analysis when only identification is required. |
| **Outline** | Present the essential structure without exhaustive detail. | Background -> principal stages/features -> concise conclusion. | Treating it as a complete essay. |
| **Trace** | Follow development through time or stages. | Starting point -> chronological stages -> turning points -> final outcome. | Giving thematic points without chronology. |
| **Account for** | Explain the reasons behind an outcome or pattern. | Outcome -> major causes -> causal links -> relative importance -> conclusion. | Describing what happened without explaining why. |

## 4. Analytical and evaluative directives

| Directive | Exact demand | Recommended structure | Main mistake |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Analyse** | Break the issue into parts and explain their relationships. | Thesis -> components -> mechanisms/interrelations -> implications -> conclusion. | Fact-listing without linkage. |
| **Examine** | Investigate a claim carefully and test its validity. | Interpret claim -> evidence -> limitations -> finding. | Assuming the statement is completely true. |
| **Critically examine** | Examine and explicitly assess strengths, weaknesses and validity. | Claim -> supporting case -> objections/limits -> replies -> graded verdict. | Artificial 50:50 balance. |
| **Critically analyse** | Decompose, explain causal relationships and evaluate assumptions/evidence. | Components -> mechanisms -> evidence -> counter-evidence -> judgement. | Description followed by generic criticism. |
| **Evaluate** | Judge according to explicit criteria. | State criteria -> assess performance against each -> weigh results -> verdict. | Giving pros and cons without a standard of judgement. |
| **Assess** | Estimate importance, effectiveness, extent or impact. | Define benchmark -> evidence of achievement -> gaps -> degree/extent judgement. | Avoiding a clear statement of extent. |
| **Appraise** | Assess value or performance with informed judgement. | Purpose/standard -> achievements -> deficiencies -> overall appraisal. | Describing a policy without judging it. |
| **Review** | Survey developments or evidence and assess the present position. | Background -> major developments/views -> current status -> judgement. | Giving only chronology. |
| **Comment** | Give a reasoned opinion on a statement. | Interpret statement -> arguments/evidence -> qualification -> clear opinion. | Giving an unsupported personal view. |
| **Critique** | Identify assumptions, explanatory strengths and weaknesses. | Reconstruct position fairly -> strengths -> internal/external objections -> verdict. | Attacking a distorted version of the position. |

## 5. Argument and judgement directives

| Directive | Exact demand | Recommended structure | Main mistake |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Justify** | Provide reasons showing why a claim is valid or defensible. | Claim -> reasons -> evidence/examples -> acknowledge limitation -> affirmation. | Discussing both sides without defending the requested claim. |
| **Substantiate** | Support a claim with concrete evidence. | Claim -> named facts/data/cases -> explain evidentiary relevance -> conclusion. | Making additional assertions without evidence. |
| **Prove/Establish** | Construct a logically connected case for a proposition. | Proposition -> premises/evidence -> reasoning -> conclusion -> possible objection. | Treating a contested claim as already proved. |
| **Do you agree?** | Take a position and defend its degree. | Interpret claim -> "largely/partly/not entirely" thesis -> reasons -> counterpoint -> verdict. | Refusing to take a position. |
| **To what extent?** | Determine the degree to which a claim is true. | State degree -> where claim holds -> where it fails -> conditions -> final extent. | Writing a general discussion without measuring extent. |

## 6. Comparison directives

| Directive | Exact demand | Recommended structure | Main mistake |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Compare** | Identify significant similarities and differences using common criteria. | Common basis -> criterion-wise comparison -> overall relationship. | Describing X fully and then Y separately. |
| **Contrast** | Emphasise important differences using common criteria. | Shared context -> criterion-wise differences -> significance. | Mentioning similarities but neglecting differences. |
| **Compare and contrast** | Present both similarities and differences. | Common basis -> similarities -> differences -> analytical conclusion. | Two unrelated mini-essays. |
| **Distinguish/Differentiate** | Draw precise boundaries between related concepts. | Definition of each -> side-by-side criteria -> implication/example. | Giving vague differences without a comparison axis. |

## 7. Statement-based directive decoder

```text
"Discuss"
   -> cover major dimensions and arrive at balance.

"Examine"
   -> test whether the statement is valid.

"Critically examine"
   -> test it, expose limitations and give a graded verdict.

"Analyse"
   -> separate parts and explain relationships.

"Critically analyse"
   -> analyse parts and relationships, then test assumptions and evidence.

"Elucidate"
   -> make the statement clear; evaluation is secondary.

"Evaluate/Assess"
   -> judge against criteria and state the degree of success or validity.

"Comment"
   -> give a concise, evidence-based position.

"Justify/Substantiate"
   -> defend the claim with reasons or evidence.
```

## 8. One topic answered under different directives

Topic: Economic growth and human development.

### Discuss

Cover the relationship through income, employment, public revenue, health,
education, distribution and institutional conversion; add limitations and a
balanced conclusion.

### Analyse

Break the conversion mechanism into:

```text
Growth
  -> income and tax resources
  -> household/public expenditure
  -> health, education and capabilities
  -> human development
```

Then explain where each link may weaken.

### Critically analyse

Perform the analysis above, but also test the assumptions using inequality,
jobless growth, service quality, environmental costs and regional variation,
before giving a graded verdict.

### Examine

Investigate whether available logic and evidence support the proposition that
growth produces development.

### Elucidate

Clearly explain how growth can create resources for development, with a simple
mechanism and examples. Extensive evaluation is not the primary demand.

### Evaluate

First choose criteria such as health, education, employment, distribution and
sustainability. Judge growth's development performance against those criteria.

## 9. Universal answer-decoding procedure

Before writing, spend about two minutes on:

```text
1. CIRCLE THE DIRECTIVE
2. UNDERLINE THE SUBJECT
3. BOX THE SCOPE OR TIME PERIOD
4. NUMBER ALL SUBPARTS
5. WRITE THE REQUIRED OPERATION
6. FORM A ONE-SENTENCE THESIS
7. SELECT 3-5 BODY HEADINGS
8. DECIDE THE TYPE OF CONCLUSION
```

## 10. Conclusion matched to directive

| Directive | Appropriate conclusion |
|---|---|
| Explain/Elucidate | Summarise the clarified mechanism or significance. |
| Discuss | Give a balanced synthesis. |
| Analyse | State the principal relationship or causal finding. |
| Critically analyse/examine | Give a qualified, graded verdict. |
| Evaluate/Assess/Appraise | State performance or validity against criteria. |
| Compare/Contrast | State the most important comparative insight. |
| Justify/Substantiate | Reaffirm the claim in light of reasons/evidence. |
| To what extent | State the precise degree and conditions under which it holds. |

## 11. Golden rule

```text
CONTENT answers "What do I know?"
DIRECTIVE answers "What must I do with what I know?"
```

A strong UPSC answer must satisfy both.
