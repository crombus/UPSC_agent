Original, not verified PYQs; source abbreviations in `../COVERAGE.md`. Working draft.

1. **C.** A: names without interactions miss function. B: a biome is not a substitute for the local system. C: correct: Tansley's ecosystem links biota, abiotic conditions and functional relations (B§2; H145). D: one niche is narrower. **Trap:** counting components is not analysing their interactions.
2. **B.** A: reverses mineral/plant status. B: correct: minerals are abiotic; plants living (B§2; H145). C: air is abiotic, fungi living. D: rain abiotic, herbivores living. **Trap:** edaphic describes soil conditions, not organisms.
3. **B.** A: scavengers consume organic matter; they do not replace the lost autotrophs. B: correct: chemosynthetic bacteria are the food-web energy entry (B§7; L session 3). C: local loss is not global cessation. D: light increase is unsupported. **Trap:** a decomposer cannot serve as a replacement primary producer merely by being microbial.
4. **B.** A: being biotic does not erase the extra transfer and associated energy loss. B: correct: the bird can receive producer-derived energy directly from leaves or after a further transfer through the herbivorous insect; it therefore feeds across trophic levels (B§3; H146; L session 4). C: heat dissipates rather than cycling back to producers. D: leaf-eating makes the bird a consumer, not an autotroph. **Trap:** compare the length of each feeding path, not the species label alone.
5. **A.** A: correct: earthworm-mediated physical breakup can decline while surviving fungi still mineralise organic matter (B§6; L session 5). B: fungal breakdown does not automatically replace all animal fragmentation. C: fungi can mineralise even if earthworms are absent, although their rate may change. D: producer carbon fixation does not alone determine litter particle size. **Trap:** independent pathways may interact without becoming the same ecological task.
6. **C.** A: energy and matter have different paths. B: released nutrients can be taken up. C: correct: only nutrient recycling; dissipated energy does not return (B§3; A§3). D: reverses the processes. **Trap:** the OCR book H146 contains a contrary “non-directional” typo; follow physical mechanism.
7. **A.** A: correct: the two linear chains share algae and fish but have different herbivorous consumers (B§3; H146). B: a shared endpoint does not erase different intermediate links. C: the two routes draw ultimately from the same algae-based producer input. D: fish consuming two prey does not establish chemical nutrient recycling. **Trap:** a food web is connected chains, not a new energy entry point.
8. **D.** A: food resources overlap. B: consumers do not fix carbon. C: one tree does not make an abiotic sphere. D: correct: nesting heights differ, even if food niche overlaps (B§2; H146). **Trap:** sharing one niche axis need not mean complete niche identity.
9. **B.** A: transition defines ecotone, not a universal richness peak. B: correct: seasonal absence of edge effect does not erase the transitional zone (B§2; H146; L session 9). C: altitude is not established. D: niche is a role, not mapped transition. **Trap:** do not equate edge effect and ecotone.
10. **A.** A: correct: forest layers create microclimates and niches (B§5; A§3). B: response differs by taxon and site. C: a change of light does not establish stock. D: litter microbes can remain. **Trap:** an altered structural condition is not proof of a universal biodiversity outcome.
11. **C.** A: standing crop is a stock, NPP a rate. B: present stock does not fix gross annual input. C: correct: GPP and respiration can differ despite equal biomass (A§3; L sessions 11–12). D: respiration need not vanish. **Trap:** stock ≠ flow.
12. **A.** A: correct: gain over a year is a flow (L sessions 11–12). B: dry standing plant mass is stock. C: abiotic phosphate inventory is standing state. D: count is another standing-crop expression. **Trap:** state the time unit.
13. **C.** A: edge effect is species-density tendency, not shock response. B: standing state is nutrient inventory. C: correct: resistance and post-shock resilience differ (A§3; L session 13). D: monoculture may be shock-specific and fragile. **Trap:** specify disturbance and recovery interval.
14. **A.** A: correct: soil formation enables other services (A§2). B: fish are provisioning. C: flood moderation is regulating. D: nonmaterial value is cultural. **Trap:** value is not restricted to the marketed fish.
15. **D.** A: repeating the same marketed output adds no relational evidence. B: visitor counts can indicate a cultural benefit but do not establish knowledge or stewardship. C: nutrient mapping informs supporting processes rather than local relationships. D: correct: inquiry into local knowledge, stewardship and relations adds the distinctive IPBES NCP dimension to the existing MEA account (A§2; L final notes). **Trap:** MEA cultural value alone does not exhaust what NCP asks the assessor to elicit.
16. **B.** A: efficiency changes the condition, not a timeless capacity. B: correct: pollutant assimilation is one component of a sustainable load (B§13; L final notes). C: tree counts alone do not measure capacity. D: degradation cannot by itself justify doubling capacity. **Trap:** consumption, technology and governance modify but do not abolish limits.
17. **C.** A: cover and carbon stock measure different attributes, neither directly demonstrates both local functions. B: a designation and inland trees cannot establish juvenile-fish use or coastal wave effects. C: correct: observing juvenile-fish habitat use and site-specific surge attenuation tests the two proposed mangrove functions (B§5; A§2A; B§13). D: a species name and national area share cannot establish local nursery or storm effects. **Trap:** a proposed service is not an observed local outcome.
18. **D.** A: web links remain relevant. B: counts alone do not measure response to shocks. C: predators can have trophic effects. D: correct: umbrella effects require habitat/connectivity protection (A§6; B§5). **Trap:** animal count ≠ intact landscape.
19. **D.** A: particle retention does not establish removal of dissolved contaminants. B: protected-area status is not evidence of pollutant-specific removal. C: a sediment observation cannot by itself measure microbial transformation. D: correct: separately test retention and transformation for pollutant, hydrology and local mechanism (L session 14; B historical routing). **Trap:** one observed retention pathway does not measure every contaminant.
20. **D.** A: richness/recovery may differ. B: forest-cover data are extent measures. C: cycling is a separate functional measure. D: correct: canopy alone does not determine quality (A§5; B§8). **Trap:** ISFR extent ≠ ecosystem health.

22. **B.** A: an allotment and site proposal establish an intention, not a restored area. D: a suitability baseline locates potential intervention, not completed restoration. **Trap:** the Survey's envisaged MISHTI area is not an audited outcome, and neither a budget nor an estimate verifies delivery.
23. **C.** A: storey height distinguishes forest structure within a stand but does not establish a new altitude-linked climatic community. D: differing feeding times may distinguish niche use within one habitat but not a whole life zone. **Trap:** altitude difference alone is not ecological zonation; the climate–community association is the necessary further evidence.

25. **D.** A: geological, atmospheric and aquatic domains are nonliving, not feeding guilds. B: air and water are abiotic too. C: rocks and water are abiotic too. D: correct: lithosphere/atmosphere/hydrosphere (B§2; H145). **Trap:** the biosphere includes abiotic conditions, not only organisms.

30. **A.** A: correct: comparing suspended particles near live oysters with control sites directly probes the proposed filtering mechanism; NOAA documents oyster filter feeding and local water clarity (`https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/feature-story/oyster-reefs-provide-habitat-and-filter-water-findings-show`).
