**Q1 — B.** A: budget shares cannot establish practical access [M §§1,4]. B: ✅ intersecting disadvantages need coordinated frontline delivery and a check of usable access [M §§1,5; B §3]. C: separate group lists can conceal an intersectional gap [M §5]. D: mere referral is not delivery [M §1]. **Trap:** counted referrals are not proven capability gains.

**Q2 — A.** A: ✅ distribution can improve while identity-based denial of public-space access leaves recognitive injustice [B §§2,5; M §2]. B: aggregate consumption gains do not prove remedy for particular wrongs [A §3]. C: continuing stigma is compatible with an actual transfer [M §2]. D: exclusion by social identity directly implicates dignity and real freedom [B §§2,7]. **Trap:** redistribution and recognition are different evaluative dimensions.

**Q3 — D.** A: teacher attendance helps assess delivery but alone does not show each pupil's viable subsequent options [B §§3,10; M §4]. B: allocations show inputs, not genuinely accessible future choices [B §10; A §6]. C: older graduates' average earnings do not identify these pupils' actual alternative routes [B §2; A §6]. D: ✅ completion is an achieved functioning; accessible further-study routes evidence available capabilities for the pupils assessed [B §2; A §§2,6]. **Trap:** equal observed completion does not guarantee equal freedom to continue learning.

**Q4 — B.** A: reverses the characteristic risks [B §2; A §6]. B: ✅ universal provision may cover better-off people; targeting may miss eligible people [B §2; A §6]. C: neither design implies those results [A §6]. D: neither design alone specifies insurance or judicial remedy [B §2]. **Trap:** assess design contingently; do not claim all targeting fails or all universalism succeeds.

**Q5 — C.** A: aspirational rights language alone does not specify a constitutional cause of action [B §§2, semantic-completeness ownership]. B: a domestic executive circular is not international ratification [A §4]. C: ✅ legal force and remedy depend on the actual instrument [B §§2–4, semantic-completeness ownership]. D: breadth of coverage does not prove enforceability [B §2]. **Trap:** value, policy and actionable entitlement occupy different legal categories.

**Q6 — D.** A: children are a distinct income-security category, not only employed contributors [ILO, guarantees]. B: sickness is among cases of working-age inability to earn [ILO]. C: essential health care is part of the floor [ILO]. D: ✅ children and working-age persons unable to earn are named categories [ILO, guarantees; A §7]. **Trap:** R202 is guidance about nationally defined guarantees, not itself a domestic statute.

**Q7 — B.** A: identical application rules do not explain continued non-use after geographic outreach [B §§2–3]. B: ✅ removing one conversion barrier does not establish effective opportunity when observed use is unchanged; inspect remaining constraints before claiming substantive equality [B §7; A §§3,6; M §4]. C: access assistance need not narrow general service eligibility [B §7]. D: spending is an input; it does not establish realised health opportunities [M §4]. **Trap:** a plausible access correction needs evaluation and may leave other obstacles intact.

**Q8 — A.** A: ✅ voice, identity and protection from stigma address recognitive justice [B §2; M §2]. B: more allocation alone leaves voice unresolved [A §3]. C: reduced public voice deepens the recognitive gap [B §2]. D: aggregate income is a different evaluative object [B §§2,7]. **Trap:** material provision is not a substitute for dignity.

**Q9 — D.** A: routine public services typify institutional welfare, not last resort [A §2]. B: a hybrid includes universal provision as well as targeting [A §2]. C: a capability assessment is an evaluative standard, not this institutional model [A §§1–2]. D: ✅ residual welfare intervenes when family/market provision fails [A §2]. **Trap:** distinguish type of provision from method of evaluation.

**Q10 — C.** A: notices/offices count administrative activity [M §4]. B: meetings/registers do not show effective access [M §4]. C: ✅ pre/post effective access tests whether correction reaches an outcome [M §§1,4; A §3]. D: budget and circulars are inputs [B §10]. **Trap:** a fall in logged complaints could reflect barriers to filing, not resolved exclusion.

**Q11 — B.** A: Sen separates achieved functionings from capability freedoms [B §2]. B: ✅ Rawls's difference principle tests benefit to the least advantaged [A §§2,7]; OCR cross-check: `books\philosphy_books\An Introduction to Political Theory by O P Gauba www.upscpdf.com.pdf`, searchable PDF p. 448. C: Nussbaum specifies central capability thresholds [A §2]. D: Fraser's triad addresses recognition and representation as well as redistribution [B §11A]. **Trap:** adjacent justice theories ask distinct evaluative questions.

**Q12 — A.** A: ✅ inputs and measured achievements matter, but identical incomes and observed states cannot exhaust real freedoms [B §§2,7; A §§2,6]. B: social position and disability can affect conversion [B §7]. C: enrolment is not evidence of equal learning [A §6]. D: achieved states do not reveal unchosen feasible alternatives [A §6]. **Trap:** an outcome indicator is useful, not an exhaustive capability measure.

**Q13 — C.** A: poverty-based ranking distributes by need, not by the injury caused by the wrong [B §2; A §3]. B: identical recurring payments can provide support but do not measure the particular harm [B §2; A §3]. C: ✅ examine the specific injury for corrective redress and consider need-based provision separately as distribution [B §2; A §3]. D: aggregate job demand can guide future allocations but not determine compensation for an unlawful exclusion [B §2; M §4]. **Trap:** calibrating redress to need alone can erase the injury that makes the claim corrective.

**Q14 — A.** A: ✅ Part IV offers constitutional direction but the particular statutory claim is determined by the law [B §§3–4; A §4]. B: Part IV is not itself directly justiciable in that manner [B §7]. C: the Preamble does not prescribe scheme-level eligibility [B §§3–4]. D: a law can supply enforceable entitlements [B §4]. **Trap:** normative mandate and actionable legal instrument are different.

**Q15 — D.** A: identical register size can conceal both correction and fresh errors [A §2]. B: unchanged expenditure cannot identify wrongly included or excluded individuals [A §2; M §4]. C: substitutions alone do not disclose whether the people affected were eligible [A §2]. D: ✅ examine ineligible receipts among actual recipients and eligible omissions among all eligible people separately; both error rates can improve despite an unchanged aggregate count [A §2; M §4]. **Trap:** net caseload is not a measure of targeting accuracy.

**Q16 — B.** A: equal health does not entail equal opportunity in other areas [A §2]. B: ✅ equal opportunity to seek employment belongs to Nussbaum's *material* control over one's environment and is distinct from bodily health [Nussbaum, Appendix A, p. 24]. C: equally observed health does not establish income as an exhaustive metric [A §2]. D: a central capability list spans more than one achieved state [A §7]. **Trap:** employment access is material environmental control; reflective life planning is practical reason.

**Q17 — D.** A: claimant counts show formal take-up, not whether enrolled people can use services or gain choices [B §10; M §4]. B: funds and appointments establish inputs but not actual use or freedom [M §1; B §3]. C: a sound questionnaire and sample design cannot stand in for unreleased results [B §10; M §1]. D: ✅ published results must trace whether welfare aims became funded legal claims, accessible services and attainable choices, not infer outcomes from upstream inputs [B §§3,10; M §1; V session 14]. **Trap:** a survey's existence and methods cannot substitute for its actual findings.

**Q18 — A.** A: ✅ the independent Indian statute's operative provisions and remedy determine the enforceable domestic claim; R202 is guidance rather than a self-executing Indian law [B §§3–4; ILO, Objective/Principles]. B: an international recommendation's objective does not displace national entitlement terms [ILO, Objective/Principles]. C: R202 sets no globally enforceable national payment schedule [ILO, guarantees]. D: adoption of a Recommendation by the Conference does not itself enact an Indian statute [A §4; ILO, Objective]. **Trap:** identify the operative domestic source even when international guidance covers the same subject.

**Q19 — C.** A: housing is not the omitted R202 floor category [ILO, guarantees]. B: the recommendation fixes no global pension rate [ILO, guarantees]. C: ✅ essential health care, including maternity care, complements income security [ILO, guarantees; A §7]. D: mandatory public employment is not the omitted health guarantee [ILO, guarantees]. **Trap:** income transfers alone do not exhaust a social-protection floor.

**Q20 — B.** A: a formally uniform rule leaves the vertical disadvantage and creates inconsistent treatment among similar applicants [A §2]. **Q21 — D.** A: identity is already respected; a fresh label is not the missing influence [B §11A]. B: actual service provision need not be withdrawn to address rule-making exclusion [B §11A]. **Q22 — A.** A: ✅ prosecution answers one corrective need, but material transition and stigma still require distribution and recognition [B §5; A §5; M §2]. **Q23 — C.** A: a value does not by itself specify each statutory claimant's benefit [B §§3–4]. B: publicity is not a statutory claim [B §§2,4]. D: ILO guidance cannot cancel domestic entitlements [ILO, objective; A §4]. **Trap:** specialist scheme design remains in food and education topics.

**Q24 — B.** A: neither omission from the eligibility list nor non-arrival of the paid service is stated [M §4]. **Q25 — D.** A: a recommendation cannot prove actual access [A §§4,6]. B: advisory commission does not replace implementing authority [M §6]. C: complaint totals are not the remedy [M §4]. **Q26 — A.** A: ✅ the Preamble's socialist and social/economic/political justice ideals orient welfare without prescribing a single cash-only design; the framework permits general services alongside selective access support through actual law and policy [CoI, Preamble PDF p. 32; B §§2–4,7; A §§2,4]. **Q27 — C.** A: recorded votes do not cure material disadvantage [B §11A]. B: expenditure totals alone do not establish equal social standing [B §11A]. **Q28 — D.** A: last-resort means-testing of the core contradicts the stated shift to routine service; there is no basis to open the supplement to everyone [A §2; M §3]. **Q29 — B.** A: Nussbaum secures freedom thresholds rather than identical outcomes [A §2]. **Q30 — A.** A: ✅ material redistribution and decision-making representation are present, but Fraser's recognition component requires equal social standing as well [B §11A; V session 8]. **Q31 — C.** A: publicity does not remedy earlier unlawful conduct [B §5]. B: transfer tally does not establish a protective-law remedy [B §5]. **Q38 — D.** A: improved survival engages life, but school funding does not prove security of movement or freedom from assault [Nussbaum, Appendix A, p. 23]. **Q39 — C.** A: nutrition falls under bodily health, not life; freedom to grieve is emotions, not affiliation [Nussbaum, Appendix A, p. 23]. D: interchanges health/integrity and emotions/practical reason [Nussbaum, Appendix A, p. 23]. **Trap:** the ten names capture distinct freedoms, not four synonyms for welfare.

**Q40 — B.** A: animal/plant relations are other species and work access is *restricted* material control; recreation is supported, not restricted [Nussbaum, Appendix A, p. 24].
