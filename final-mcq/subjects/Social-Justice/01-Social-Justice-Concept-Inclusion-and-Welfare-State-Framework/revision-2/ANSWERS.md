**Q1 — D.** A: policy announcement is earlier than realised access [M §1]. B: publication of spending cannot resolve a physical barrier [M §§1,4]. C: a uniform code is not an access measure [M §§1,4]. D: ✅ accessibility and usability mediate formal provision and outcome [M §1; B §3]. **Trap:** service availability is not service accessibility.

**Q2 — D.** A: a uniform rule despite unequal barriers is formal sameness [A §§2–3]. B: the same form for persons in the same position exemplifies horizontal treatment [A §2]. C: averages cannot show differentiated support [A §2; B §10]. D: ✅ different assistance according to disadvantage expresses vertical equity [A §§2–3]. **Trap:** unequal support can be a route to equal effective opportunity.

**Q3 — C.** A: older persons' income is a different floor category [ILO, guarantees]. B: health care is part of the floor but does not replace income support in working-age incapacity [ILO, guarantees]. C: ✅ basic income security for active-age persons unable to earn sufficiently includes sickness [ILO, guarantees; A §7]. D: formal employment is not the sole qualifying logic in this guarantee [ILO, guarantees]. **Trap:** working age does not mean already employed or automatically eligible without inability to earn.

**Q4 — A.** A: ✅ a district mean can conceal people's failure to enjoy the named minimum opportunity for bodily integrity, including safe movement [Nussbaum, Appendix A, p. 23; A §§2,7]. B: an improved aggregate cannot establish whether the community crossed a named individual minimum [A §§2,7; Nussbaum, pp. 22–23]. C: income gains do not make a separate bodily-integrity freedom redundant [A §2; Nussbaum, Appendix A, p. 23]. D: equal observed behaviour is not the same as individual freedom to move safely [A §§2,7; Nussbaum, Appendix A, p. 23]. **Trap:** a threshold for a distinct capability must not be inferred from an average score or mandated identical behaviour.

**Q5 — B.** A: rights require institutions and resources to become lived access [B §3; A §6]. B: ✅ enforceable claims can remain paper promises without financed, functioning provision [A §6; B §2]. C: inputs cannot establish achieved learning [B §10]. D: a discretionary grant does not thereby acquire stronger legal force [B §2]. **Trap:** legal right ≠ realised outcome.

**Q6 — C.** A: the DPSP are not directly enforceable like Fundamental Rights [B §§3–4]. B: their relevance is not limited to absence of legislation [B §4; A §4]. C: ✅ Part IV gives policy direction and interpretive aid [B §§3–4; A §4]. D: the directives do not themselves specify identical individual payments [B §§2–3]. **Trap:** Social Justice uses the constitutional welfare lens; Polity owns detailed doctrine.

**Q7 — A.** A: ✅ livelihood assistance distributes resources/opportunities to affected workers [B §§2,5; M §2]. B: judicial enforcement of a prohibition is a separate corrective process [B §2]. C: assistance is material, not only symbolic recognition [M §2]. D: distribution alone cannot remedy particular wrongs [A §3]. **Trap:** the same policy bundle may require separate dimensions.

**Q8 — D.** A: only registered recipients cannot reveal missing eligible people [A §2]. B: payment totals to the list measure disbursement, not population coverage [B §3]. C: recipient satisfaction omits never-enrolled persons [M §§1,4]. D: ✅ comparing eligible people with the registry and receipts surfaces exclusion [A §2; M §4]. **Trap:** delivery success conditional on being listed is not overall inclusion.

**Q9 — C.** A: an offered place is an input, not the achieved state; spending is not freedom [B §2]. B: an offer and a classroom count do not express achieved state and freedom [B §2]. C: ✅ achieved literacy is functioning; a genuine opportunity to learn is capability [A §2; B §2]. D: staffing and advertising are inputs [B §§2–3]. **Trap:** observable achievement does not by itself show the opportunity set.

**Q10 — B.** A: a clinic may be formally open but physically unreachable [B §§2,7]. B: ✅ accessible travel can specifically remove the obstacle preventing conversion of a formally available service into an attainable health opportunity [B §7; A §6]. C: an access aid does not change the service into contributory insurance [B §7]. D: an intended improvement still needs an actual-use check [B §§3,10]. **Trap:** a transport input is valuable only insofar as it makes the service usable.

**Q11 — C.** A: meeting counts describe commission activity, not whether people gained service access [M §§4,6]. B: publicity can follow a complaint but does not prove a change in access [B §10; M §4]. C: ✅ examine subsequent duty-bearer action and actual use by affected eligible people; a complaint count is not an outcome denominator [M §§4,6; B §10]. D: recommendations alone cannot establish subsequent use [A §§4,6; M §4]. **Trap:** documented complaints identify a possible failure; neither their number nor the commission's activity measures restored access.

**Q12 — A.** A: ✅ targeting's fiscal concentration must be weighed against documentation/identification and exclusion [B §2; A §6]. B: targeted schemes still require expenditure [A §6]. C: India's framework permits hybrid designs [B §7; A §2]. D: greater spending alone does not establish expanded real freedoms [A §§2,6]. **Trap:** compare objectives and all costs, not only spending per beneficiary.

**Q13 — D.** A: slots reallocate opportunity rather than remedy this wrong [B §2]. B: transfers can distribute support without investigating particular harm [A §3]. C: spending reports cannot establish a victim's remedy [M §§1,4]. D: ✅ investigation and legally available redress target a specific injury [B §2; A §3]. **Trap:** a new benefit need not extinguish the prior claim.

**Q14 — B.** A: an undifferentiated transfer changes resources, not the degrading treatment [B §2]. B: ✅ despite recorded identity and participation in rules, equal standing and dignity at the service encounter remain recognitive concerns [B §2; M §2]. C: payments alone do not repair denied social respect [B §§2–3]. D: an expenditure dashboard cannot establish dignity in service access [M §2]. **Trap:** registry inclusion and rule-making voice are not proof of recognition of equal standing.

**Q15 — C.** A: children and older persons are among the floor's categories [ILO, guarantees]. B: Conference adoption of a Recommendation is not ratification of a Convention [ILO adoption; A §4]. C: ✅ the International Labour Conference adopted the Recommendation; enforceable claims depend on national law [ILO adoption; ILO, principles; A §4]. **Q16 — A.** A: ✅ when two feasible inequalities differ in their benefit to the least advantaged, the greater such benefit supports Y under the stated comparison [A §§2,7; Gauba, `books\philosphy_books\An Introduction to Political Theory by O P Gauba www.upscpdf.com.pdf`, searchable PDF p. 448]. B: greater total investment cannot substitute for greatest benefit to those least advantaged [A §7; Gauba p. 448]. C: merely improving their position under both plans does not make the two improvements equal [Gauba p. 448]. D: the difference principle permits inequality when it brings the greatest benefit to the least advantaged, subject to the other stated requirements [A §2; Gauba p. 448]. **Trap:** compare feasible outcomes for the least advantaged rather than stopping at a positive gain under either option.

**Q17 — C.** A: outreach may be checked, but publicizing the commitment does not establish whether *already enrolled* people could use the service [B §3; M §1]. B: the stem establishes a statutory claim and funds; the enactment date does not locate the unresolved conversion step [B §§3–4; M §1]. C: ✅ after law, funding and enrolment, investigate whether frontline services were usable and expanded actual choices; the survey challenges an inference of capability gain, not the fact of enactment [B §§3,10; M §§1,4; V session 14]. D: comparing projected registrations with enrolment still measures an output rather than explaining poor capability results [B §10]. **Trap:** diagnose the *unresolved link*, rather than reproduce the entire sequence.

**Q18 — A.** A: ✅ correct inclusion and restore access to the omitted eligible person [A §2]. B: leaving an eligible person off the list preserves exclusion [A §2]. C: a non-eligible payment is inclusion error, not proof of success [A §2]. D: aggregate amounts cannot classify household eligibility [A §2; M §4]. **Trap:** simultaneous leakage and exclusion are possible.

**Q19 — D.** A: the scenario specifies comparable need rather than additional differentiated disadvantage [A §2]. **Q20 — B.** A: representation alone does not distribute goods or eradicate stigma [B §11A]. B: ✅ voice, material redistribution and respect must be evaluated separately [B §11A; V session 8]. C: a seat is political representation, not a service transfer [B §11A]. **Q21 — C.** A: a larger transfer remains distributive, not necessarily corrective or recognitive [B §2]. B: a survey/registry does not supply harm-redress or dignity [M §§2,4]. **Q22 — D.** A: NFSA/RTE are Indian statutory frameworks, not ILO instruments [B §4]. B: rights-based welfare permits specific legislative claims [B §§2,4]. **Q23 — A.** A: ✅ asks after identification, legal enforcement, actual delivery, usable access and learned outcomes [M §4; V session 14]. **Q24 — B.** A: category counts can conceal compound access barriers [M §5]. B: ✅ intersectionality tests interaction of migration, gender and disability with service design [M §5; B §2]. **Q25 — C.** A: three separate spending headings still measure inputs rather than achieved justice in any domain [B §§3,10]. **Q26 — A.** A: ✅ evidence of usable social/educational access alongside formal political equality tests the persistence of the social and economic disadvantage in Ambedkar's warning [B §11A; V session 8; Delhi government-hosted CAD extract PDF p. 7]. **Q35 — B.** A: play and emotions/affiliation are distinct from the two aspects of environmental control [Nussbaum, Appendix A, pp. 23–24]. **Q36 — A.** A: ✅ adequate nutrition and shelter fall under bodily health, but freedom from assault while moving belongs to bodily integrity and remains impaired [Nussbaum, Appendix A, p. 23]. **Q37 — D.** A: grief is emotions rather than affiliation, dignity is affiliation rather than emotions, and species and play are reversed [Nussbaum, Appendix A, pp. 23–24]. **Q40 — A.** A: ✅ R202 couples financial, fiscal and economic sustainability (with due regard to social justice and equity) to transparent, accountable and sound financial management [ILO, Principles].
