C identifies a dynasty where the unread script and structures supply no ruler's name. **D** separates physical dams/reservoirs from the reasoned inference of planned water management and refuses to name the coordinating political house. **Sources:** B §§4,13.4,13.6,13.7A; A §§2,4; N PDF p. 166; [UNESCO Dholavira property 1645](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1645), accessed 2026-10-01. **Trap:** infrastructure testifies to organisation, not a deciphered record of its patron.
58. **A.** **A** contrasts the civic Harappan wells, reservoirs and drains with later descending stepwells for storage (some later works also commemorative) and temple-associated tanks. Different form and setting do not establish a direct architectural lineage; neither do they prove that no historical influence of any kind existed. B substitutes sewage conveyance for the stepwell's descent to stored water. C makes a temple tank a household-drain segment rather than a feature of a sacred precinct. D correctly distinguishes the built forms but wrongly treats a shared water problem as positive evidence of an established design descent. **Sources:** B §§13.7,13.7A; N PDF pp. 34,83,105,119,134,730–731. **Trap:** do not turn a lack of demonstrated lineage into a claim that all later waterworks were ritually used.
59. **A.** **A** distinguishes a modern descriptive name for an excavated bath complex from conventional functional names proposing storage or harbour use; none is a translated Harappan self-designation. B invents a translated Bath title and treats the other two functional names as merely neutral descriptions. C denies the Bath's excavated form and invents translations securing the two contested uses. D upgrades inferred ritual significance to a named office and invents administrative translations. **Sources:** B §§4,9,13.4,13.6; A §§4,9; N PDF pp. 33–34. **Trap:** knowing where a feature was excavated does not recover the original language used to name it.
