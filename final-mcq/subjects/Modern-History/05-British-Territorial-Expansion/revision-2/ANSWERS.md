### R2-001 — A

**Question focus:** Haidar's pressure on Madras forced the first-war peace and its mutual-defence promise. **Trap:** four successive settlements did not have interchangeable terms.

**Examiner trap:** four successive settlements did not have interchangeable terms.

- **A:** Correct. Madras ended Haidar's first war in 1769 and pledged mutual assistance against third-party attack; the Company failed to help when the Marathas attacked in 1771 (*Modern India*, local OCR PDF p. 78).
- **B:** Incorrect. The post-1799 partition followed Tipu's death in the fourth war; it did not promise mutual aid to Haidar, who had died in 1782 (*Modern India*, pp. 78–81, 84–85).
- **C:** Incorrect. Mangalore ended Tipu's second war in 1784 after Haidar's death, not the first-war advance toward Madras (*Modern India*, pp. 78–81).
- **D:** Incorrect. Seringapatam in 1792 followed Cornwallis's third-war coalition and required Tipu to cede territory, not a 1769 mutual-defence pact (*Modern India*, pp. 80–81).
