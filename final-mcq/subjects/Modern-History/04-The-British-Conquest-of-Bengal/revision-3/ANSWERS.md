# British Conquest of Bengal — revision 3 | ANSWERS

All items are original, not UPSC PYQs. ✅ denotes cited fact; ⚠️ denotes interpretation. Explanations address each option separately.

### Q1 — B

- **A:** ❌ Company-linked credit mattered but did not alone sustain Bengal's fiscal system.
- **B:** ✅ Bengal's trade, credit and working fiscal machinery made its receipts attractive to the Company.
- **C:** ❌ Mughal legitimacy coexisted with Nawabi control of local revenue administration.
- **D:** ❌ Bengal's textiles and agriculture were already part of Company commercial interests.

**Source:** Basic §§1,8.4A; Bipan Chandra, Modern India, ch. IV, PDF p. 71; Bandyopadhyay, From Plassey to Partition, PDF pp. 60–61 (printed 43–44).

**Examiner trap:** Successor-state autonomy need not erase imperial legitimacy or revenue machinery.

### Q2 — D

- **A:** ❌ The Company's own exemption did not automatically extend to servants' private goods.
- **B:** ❌ Other Europeans were not all immunised by the Company's imperial privilege.
- **C:** ❌ Nawabs contested private misuse of passes rather than surrendering all oversight.
- **D:** ✅ Earlier Nawabs challenged the extension of passes to private trade, a distinction Siraj inherited.

**Source:** Basic §§1,8.4A; Chandra PDF pp. 71–72; Bandyopadhyay PDF pp. 58–60.

**Examiner trap:** Corporate trade immunity did not make employees' private consignments exempt.

### Q3 — D

- **A:** ❌ Equal taxation would remove rather than illustrate the asymmetry specified.
- **B:** ❌ No imperial assignment to a Nawabi merchant occurred in this scenario.
- **C:** ❌ A later Diwani concerned territorial revenue, not this abuse of passes.
- **D:** ✅ The private trader borrowed the Company's corporate privilege, injuring revenue and competitors.

**Source:** Basic §§1,8.4A; Chandra PDF pp. 71,75; Bandyopadhyay PDF pp. 58–59.

**Examiner trap:** A pass may be legitimate for Company goods but not for its servant's private goods.

### Q4 — C

- **A:** ❌ Siraj disputed English fortification, not Nawabi command of the French enclave.
- **B:** ❌ Company directors could not confer Siraj's Nawabi title.
- **C:** ✅ Unauthorised defences and harbouring Krishna Ballabh challenged the Nawab's authority.
- **D:** ❌ Awadh's revenue was outside the 1756 Calcutta dispute.

**Source:** Basic §§1,8.4A; Chandra PDF pp. 71–72; Bandyopadhyay PDF p. 60 (printed 43).

**Examiner trap:** The asylum and fortification disputes both concern jurisdiction, not merely trade.

### Q5 — B

- **A:** ❌ Chinsurah was a Dutch settlement, not the English river refuge described.
- **B:** ✅ Fulta was the downriver shelter to which the English retreated.
- **C:** ❌ Chandernagore was French, not an English evacuation base.
- **D:** ❌ Kasimbazar had already been captured, so it was not the later haven.

**Source:** Chandra PDF pp. 72–73; Basic §1; Bandyopadhyay PDF p. 60.

**Examiner trap:** Distinguish the lost factory, occupied fort and evacuation refuge.

### Q6 — A

- **A:** ✅ Alinagar restored Company privileges; attacking Chandernagore then removed a possible French counterweight.
- **B:** ❌ Alinagar restored trade rights; the Company's Bengal Diwani came after Buxar.
- **C:** ❌ The English attacked the French factory; French privileges were not the result.
- **D:** ❌ Chandernagore did not become a Nawabi protectorate after its seizure.

**Source:** Chandra PDF p. 73; workbook Q3, Q11; Bandyopadhyay PDF p. 60.

**Examiner trap:** Alinagar restored commercial standing but did not settle sovereignty.

### Q7 — A

- **A:** ✅ Holwell was involved at Fort William; his numerical account should be attributed and qualified.
- **B:** ❌ Exchanging one unverified fixed number for another would not resolve disputed scale.
- **C:** ❌ The confinement narrative follows the fort's capture and cannot explain Siraj's earlier assault.
- **D:** ❌ Company publication gives a narrative provenance, not proof of exact totals.

**Source:** Basic §§1,5; Dictionary of National Biography, Holwell (1891), https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionary_of_National_Biography,_1885-1900/Holwell,_John_Zephaniah (interested survivor's account); Chandra PDF p. 72 (Calcutta attack).

**Examiner trap:** Older published numerical claims are evidence of what was claimed, not independently settled census data.

### Q8 — C

- **A:** ❌ The emperor supplied Diwani only after Buxar, not at Plassey.
- **B:** ❌ Shuja belongs to the later Awadh–Mughal alliance, not Siraj's succession settlement.
- **C:** ✅ Jagat Seth credit and disaffected officers enabled regime change despite limited fighting.
- **D:** ❌ Hastings' direct Company reform came in 1772, not before Plassey.

**Source:** Basic §§1,8.4B,8.6; Chandra PDF pp. 72–73; Bandyopadhyay PDF pp. 60–61.

**Examiner trap:** Banker credit and commander defections matter alongside Company force.

### Q9 — C

- **A:** ❌ The emperor's grant followed Buxar and dealt with revenue authority.
- **B:** ❌ The 24 Parganas grant was from Mir Jafar after Plassey.

**Examiner trap:** The red/white papers concerned private promises, not the imperial Diwani.

### Q10 — B

- **A:** ❌ Mir Jafar was a conspirator; Munro commanded Company forces at Buxar.
- **B:** ✅ Mir Madan and Mohan Lal resisted for Siraj even as other commanders held back.
- **C:** ❌ Shah Alam belonged to the later Buxar coalition.
- **D:** ❌ Shuja likewise belonged to the later coalition, not Siraj's artillery.

**Source:** Basic §§1,8.4B; Chandra PDF p. 73; workbook Q10.

**Examiner trap:** A political coup need not imply zero armed resistance.

### Q11 — D

- **A:** ❌ The 1757 grant was local; Mir Jafar did not confer imperial Diwani.
- **B:** ❌ Awadh was not the recipient of the 1757 Calcutta-area grant.
- **C:** ❌ Both grants affected revenue, not simply inland customs passes.
- **D:** ✅ Mir Jafar's 24 Parganas grant differed in grantor and scope from Shah Alam's Diwani.

**Source:** Basic §§2,8.4E; Chandra PDF pp. 73–74,76; workbook Q12, Q16.

**Examiner trap:** A local zamindari concession is not a three-province imperial revenue appointment.

### Q12 — D

- **A:** ❌ French invasion is not the explanation given for Jafar's 1760 removal.
- **B:** ❌ The cited Mir Jafar episode concerned Bengal, not an Awadh imperial revocation.
- **C:** ❌ No recorded peasant revolt replacing him is the episode's cause.
- **D:** ✅ Repeated Company payments and concessions made the client regime fiscally unsustainable.

**Source:** Basic §§1,8.4B; Chandra PDF pp. 74–75; Bandyopadhyay PDF p. 61 (printed 44).

**Examiner trap:** The Company repeatedly pressed its client for transfers.

### Q13 — D

- **A:** ❌ Jafar's first installation concerned the 24 Parganas and compensation.
- **B:** ❌ Siraj preceded the district grants and Munger reform.
- **C:** ❌ Nizam-ud-Daulah succeeded on Jafar's death, not in the 1760 exchange.
- **D:** ✅ Qasim succeeded Jafar, ceded the named districts and built capacity from Munger.

**Source:** Basic §4, Advanced §1; Chandra PDF p. 75; Britannica, https://www.britannica.com/biography/Mir-Qasim.

**Examiner trap:** Qasim's accession grants and later reforms form one sequence, not one simultaneous document.

### Q14 — A

- **A:** ✅ Ending inland duties for all checked servants' advantage but cost the Nawab revenue.
- **B:** ❌ Uniform taxation would preserve a levy; the policy abolished it.
- **C:** ❌ Court-merchant exclusivity would perpetuate rather than remove unequal access.
- **D:** ❌ The policy altered local taxes, not the emperor's three-province Diwani.

**Source:** Basic §§1,8.4C; Chandra PDF pp. 75–76; Bandyopadhyay PDF pp. 61–62.

**Examiner trap:** Equality by removing duties sacrificed receipts, not sovereignty's legal title.

### Q15 — B

- **A:** ❌ Chandernagore had already fallen before Qasim's reign and later flight.
- **B:** ✅ Following defeats, Qasim sought Shuja and Shah Alam for the Buxar coalition.
- **C:** ❌ Restored Jafar replaced Qasim and was not his place of asylum.

**Source:** Chandra PDF p. 76; Bandyopadhyay PDF p. 62 (printed 45); Basic §8.4D.

**Examiner trap:** The separate Patna prisoner-killing allegation remains held and is not needed here.

### Q16 — C

- **A:** ❌ Clive led at Plassey, not the Company command at Buxar.
- **B:** ❌ Siraj was killed in 1757; Qasim was the Bengal participant at Buxar.
- **C:** ✅ Munro faced Qasim, Awadh's Shuja and emperor Shah Alam II.

### Q17 — D

- **A:** ❌ Bandyopadhyay contrasts segmentary alliance and unitary Company command.

### Q18 — D

- **A:** ❌ The local 1757 zamindari grant was not the three-province Diwani.
- **B:** ❌ Qasim removed internal trade duties, not the emperor's revenue power.

### Q19 — A

- **B:** ❌ Shah Alam obtained a provision and Kora–Allahabad, not all Awadh.

### Q20 — B

### Q21 — B

### Q22 — A

### Q23 — A

### Q24 — C
