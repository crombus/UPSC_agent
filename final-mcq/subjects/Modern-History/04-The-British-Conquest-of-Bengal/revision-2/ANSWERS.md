# British Conquest of Bengal — revision 2 | ANSWERS

Original questions; these are not claimed as UPSC PYQs.

### Q1 — C

✅ **Fact / rationale:** Bengal wealth, successor Nawabs and Mughal legitimacy.

- **A:** Incorrect. The Diwani grant was later conferred on the Company.
- **B:** Incorrect. European trading factories were not the bankers' charge.
- **C:** Correct. Jagat Seth's banking network gave the court access to finance.
- **D:** Incorrect. Mir Jafar, not the Jagat Seth bankers, had military command.

**Source:** Basic §1, §8; Advanced §1; Chandra, *Modern India*, ch. IV, OCR PDF pp. 71, 73 (wealth and Jagat Seth); workbook Q1–2.
**Examiner trap:** Autonomous Nawabi Bengal was neither already annexed nor fiscally weak.

### Q2 — D

✅ **Fact / rationale:** 1717 farman, corporate trade scope and earlier Nawabi resistance.

- **A:** Incorrect. The contested issue was corporate versus private cargo, not a distinction restricted to ports.
- **B:** Incorrect. Servants' private use or resale of passes was abuse, not a universal merchant exemption.
- **C:** Incorrect. This reverses the farman's legal beneficiaries.
- **D:** Correct. Murshid Quli Khan and Alivardi Khan had resisted the expansion of scope.

**Source:** Basic §1–5, §8; Advanced §1–5; Chandra, ch. IV, PDF pp. 71–72; atlas Q1–2, Q65.
**Examiner trap:** Corporate exemption did not license duty-free servant commerce.

### Q3 — C

✅ **Fact / rationale:** Dastaks, servant/private trade and merchant-customs injury.

- **A:** Incorrect. Evasion reduced customs and advantaged the pass-holding trader.
- **B:** Incorrect. Traders lacking such passes remained at a competitive disadvantage.
- **C:** Correct. Chandra documents sale of passes and harm to honest merchants.
- **D:** Incorrect. Reselling a pass did not change the imperial scope of the farman.

**Source:** Basic §1–5, §8; Advanced §1–5; Chandra, ch. IV, PDF pp. 71, 75; atlas Q23, Q65.
**Examiner trap:** A pass on private goods did not grant either Diwani or equal competition.

### Q4 — C

- **B:** Incorrect. He ordered demolition, not regulated fortification.

**Source:** Basic §1–5, §8; Advanced §1–5; Chandra, ch. IV, PDF pp. 71–72; workbook Q2; atlas Q3, Q24.
**Examiner trap:** A fortification dispute was about territorial jurisdiction, not just rivalry.

- **D:** Incorrect. The Awadh alliance concerned paid military assistance, not duty-free Company exports.

**Source:** Basic §1 and §8 (annual payment); Chandra, *Modern India*, ch. IV, OCR PDF p. 71 (corporate trade privilege and passes); Advanced §1.
**Examiner trap:** Imperial commercial privilege does not include servants' private cargo or general revenue collection.

### Q42 — D

✅ **Fact / rationale:** Mir Jafar's grant combined a broad corporate trade concession with a geographically narrow zamindari, neither amounting to the Diwani.

- **A:** Incorrect. Free trade was not restricted to Awadh and the zamindari was not a three-province award.
- **B:** Incorrect. Awadh was treated separately after Buxar; the 1757 grant did not bestow an Awadh revenue title.
- **C:** Incorrect. Its three-province Diwani came later from Shah Alam II, not from Mir Jafar.
- **D:** Correct. The Company gained undisputed free trade in Bengal, Bihar and Orissa but zamindari specifically in the 24 Parganas.

**Source:** Chandra, *Modern India*, ch. IV, OCR PDF pp. 73–74, 76; Basic §2; Advanced §1–2.
**Examiner trap:** Do not confuse multi-province commercial concessions with the later multi-province Diwani.

### Q43 — B

✅ **Fact / rationale:** Bengal's fiscal subordination supplied exports and armed capacity to the expanding Company.

- **A:** Incorrect. Servant pass misuse injured rather than financed Nawabi military capacity.
- **B:** Correct. Chandra identifies Bengal revenue as enabling a strong Company army and the Company's export Investment.
- **C:** Incorrect. The imperial Diwani title financed Company, not French, power.
- **D:** Incorrect. The Nawab's customs were undermined and Awadh did not command Bengal Company forces.

**Source:** Chandra, *Modern India*, ch. IV, OCR PDF pp. 74, 77; Basic §8; Advanced §1, §5.
**Examiner trap:** The overseas-purchase mechanism and regional military finance are related but distinct uses of Bengal's resources.
