## Party Politics 1947-67 - The Congress System and the Opposition

### R2-001 - A

**[Original]**

- **A - correct:** Both statements capture the analytical model: Congress was dominant within a competitive multiparty order, and its internal factions processed conflicts that might otherwise have appeared wholly between parties.
- **B - incorrect:** Statement 2 is also correct; internal bargaining is central to the interpretation.
- **C - incorrect:** Statement 1 is also correct; dominance did not abolish lawful organised rivals.
- **D - incorrect:** Neither statement describes a one-party state or an official Congress self-description.

**Source:** `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Modern-Indian-History/advanced/33_Party-Politics-1947-67-Congress-System-and-Opposition.md`, §§1-2 and Historiography note.

**Examiner trap:** Treating “dominant party” as synonymous with legally exclusive party rule.

### R2-002 - A

**[Original]**

- **A - correct:** Congress combined national reach, inherited legitimacy and a social base extending across regions and classes.
- **B - incorrect:** Congress retained ideological flexibility, right and left minorities, and open internal debate.
- **C - incorrect:** Opposition parties competed legally and actively; dominance was not produced by constituency restrictions.
- **D - incorrect:** The Congress coalition included major economic interests, rural notables, middle groups and poorer voters rather than one narrow class.

**Source:** Bipan Chandra et al., *India After Independence*, Ch. 14 OCR, PDF pp. 234-237, especially pp. 235-237.

**Examiner trap:** Explaining Congress breadth by assigning it a single class base.
