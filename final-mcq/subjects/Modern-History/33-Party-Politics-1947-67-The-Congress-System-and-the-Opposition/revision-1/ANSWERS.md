# Topic 33 Revision 1 - Answers

## Party Politics 1947-67 - The Congress System and the Opposition

### R1-001 - D

**[Original]**

- **A - incorrect:** Congress had important urban support, but its social reach extended far beyond urban professionals and labour.
- **B - incorrect:** Princes and landlords were among many interests; they did not define the whole party base.
- **C - incorrect:** Congress possessed a national organisation rather than a base confined to former provincial governments.
- **D - correct:** The source describes a reach from metropolitan cities to remote villages and from big capitalists to the rural poor.

**Source:** Bipan Chandra et al., *India After Independence*, OCR PDF p. 235.

**Examiner trap:** Mistaking the presence of elite groups for an exclusively elite social base.

### R1-002 - C

**[Original]**

- **A - incorrect:** Congress competed for power after independence and did not revert to a protest movement without an electoral role.
- **B - incorrect:** Its district and provincial structures continued to convey viewpoints upward.
- **C - correct:** It became a distinct governing party but retained ideological flexibility, looseness and broad-coalition features.
- **D - incorrect:** Congress tolerated several viewpoints rather than enforcing one detailed doctrine.

**Source:** Bipan Chandra et al., *India After Independence*, OCR PDF pp. 235-237.

**Examiner trap:** Treating movement-to-party transformation as a complete organisational rupture.

### R1-003 - A

**[Original]**

- **A - correct:** The source locates Congress in the centre with a left orientation, alongside smaller right and left wings.
- **B - incorrect:** Propertied interests were represented, but Congress also pursued development, social justice and redistribution.
- **C - incorrect:** Its organisation and programme were not those of a revolutionary cadre party.
- **D - incorrect:** Candidate selection was one function; Congress also articulated broad national policies.

**Source:** Bipan Chandra et al., *India After Independence*, OCR PDF p. 237.

**Examiner trap:** Confusing left-of-centre breadth with a doctrinal socialist cadre party.

### R1-004 - D

**[Original]**

- **B - incorrect:** Courts and administration did not replace political bargaining within the party.
- **C - incorrect:** National and state leaders both participated in accommodation; it was not reserved to state leaders.
- **D - correct:** Congress worked as a medium of reconciliation, accommodation and adjustment among class, sectional and regional interests.

**Source:** Bipan Chandra et al., *India After Independence*, OCR PDF pp. 236-237.

**Examiner trap:** Reading accommodation as the absence of class and regional conflict.

### R1-005 - C

**Examiner trap:** Equating internal factionalism either with formal party federation or with no opposition.

### R1-006 - B

- **D - incorrect:** The Election Commission conducted polls; it did not select Congress candidates.

**Source:** Bipan Chandra et al., *India After Independence*, OCR PDF p. 237.

**Examiner trap:** Reducing co-option to electoral alliance rather than absorption and concession.

### R1-007 - A

### R1-008 - B

**Source:** Bipan Chandra et al., *India After Independence*, OCR PDF pp. 234-235; election OCR pp. 173-175.
