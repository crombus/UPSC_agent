## Complete owner ledger

Each revision independently covers:

1. India's wartime participation through men, money and materials; conditional nationalist support; expected constitutional reciprocity.
2. Wartime inflation, taxation, loans, shortages and recruitment pressure; uneven gains for selected industries and traders; democratic contradiction.
3. Tilak's June 1914 release, December 1915 readmission authorisation and December 1916 public Congress reunion without claiming ideological uniformity.
4. Tilak's April 1916 League: Maharashtra excluding Bombay city, Karnataka, Central Provinces and Berar; six branches; tours, vernacular pamphlets and meetings.
5. Besant's September 1916 League: Adyar/Madras anchor, wider complementary field including Bombay city, looser network, Arundale, Wadia and C.P. Ramaswami Aiyar, New India and Commonweal.
6. Shared constitutional Home Rule demand within the Empire; continuous branches, press, lectures, libraries and political education; socially uneven reach short of later mass mobilisation.
7. Tilak security/exclusion context, student restrictions and June 1917 internment of Besant, Wadia and Arundale; September release and Besant's December 1917 Congress presidency.
8. Lucknow's double unity: Moderate-Extremist reunion and Congress-League common scheme, with the two settlements kept institutionally distinct.
9. Pact architecture: four-fifths elected legislatures, proposed 150-member Imperial Council and one-third Muslim share among elected Indian central members.
10. Separate electorates, weightage and the three-fourths community safeguard kept distinct.
11. Verified provincial shares: Punjab 50%, Bengal 40%, Bombay one-third, United Provinces 30%, Bihar 25%, Central Provinces 15% and Madras 15%.
12. Pact significance as both enhanced anti-colonial bargaining and institutionalisation of community representation, without deterministic Partition claims.
13. Montagu Declaration of 20 August 1917 as a gradual, bounded promise of increasing Indian association and responsible government within the Empire.
14. 1918 decline through Moderate withdrawal, reform-response divisions, Besant's vacillation and Tilak's late-1918 departure; organisational absorption and transition without importing Topic 19.
