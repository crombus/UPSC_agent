### 1. D

**Answer: D.**

- **A:** Persian administrative practice persisted in various courts; language alone does not prove institutional origin.
- **B:** Both successor and new states could draw on merchants and local levies.
- **C:** Jat chieftains also held and bargained over agrarian rights; this does not establish a Mughal appointment.
- **D (correct):** Inherited provincial governorship identifies the successor route of Bengal, Awadh or Hyderabad.

**Sources:** B §§1–2, 8.4A–B; A §§1–2; P pp. 24–28, 32–33; W Q1, Q31.
**Examiner trap:** Classify by the state's origin, not by shared administrative tools or resources.

### 2. A

**Answer: A.**

- **A (correct):** Carnatic/Arcot belonged to the Mughal Deccan political hierarchy under Hyderabad; that pedigree does not imply the Nizam controlled it effectively at every later moment.
- **B:** Kerala chieftaincies belong to Travancore's consolidation, not Carnatic subordination.
- **C:** Mysore's Wodeyar succession arose from the Vijayanagara field, not Arcot's political origin.
- **D:** Awadh's wazirate was a northern imperial office, not the Carnatic's superior provincial line.

**Sources:** P p. 25 (Carnatic–Nizam), pp. 27–29 (Awadh/Mysore), p. 32 (Travancore); W Q3, Q50; B §2.
**Examiner trap:** A historical superior claim is not proof of uninterrupted day-to-day obedience.

### 3. B

**Answer: B.**

- **A:** Peshkash acknowledges a superior; chauth was a distinct Maratha quarter claim.
- **B (correct):** A chief's negotiated tribute and imperial symbolism fit layered regional power.
- **C:** An emperor's coin legend signals legitimacy; it cannot certify actual Delhi revenue collection.
- **D:** Retained imperial symbolism coexisted with the Nizam's army and local appointments.

**Sources:** P pp. 24–25, 37–39; W Q2, Q4–5, Q11, Q36; B §§2, 8.4A.
**Examiner trap:** Keep payment, coin legend and military command as separate kinds of evidence.

### 4. B

**Answer: B.**

- **A:** Jagirs were assignments, not a replacement office for the Bengal diwan.
- **B (correct):** Holding both nazim (executive) and diwan (revenue) concentrated provincial powers.
- **C:** The imperial wazirate was a court appointment, not the paired Bengal provincial offices.
- **D:** The mint mattered to finance and coinage but did not substitute for the nazim's executive office.

**Sources:** P pp. 25–27; W Q6, Q35, Q45; B §§2, 8.4A.
**Examiner trap:** Financial intermediaries and royal symbols should not be mistaken for the specific office merger.

### 5. C

**Answer: C.**

- **A:** Banker credit operated alongside continued zamindar collection and contestation.
- **B:** Influence over the mint did not make Bengal's succession uncontested.
- **C (correct):** Remittance and credit could finance a nawab without making bankers military commanders.
- **D:** Treasury and mint brokerage gave influence, not appointment as provincial nazim.

**Sources:** B §8.4D; A §2 (merchant-financier role); W Q7, Q37, Q55; P pp. 26–27 (provincial context, not the named-house mechanism).
**Examiner trap:** Financial leverage is neither legal sovereignty nor direct command of troops.

### 6. D

**Answer: D.**

- **A:** Alivardi followed, rather than preceded, Shuja-ud-din and his son Sarfaraz.
- **B:** Siraj succeeded Alivardi; he did not precede him.
- **C:** Sarfaraz succeeded his father Shuja-ud-din, not the reverse.
- **D (correct):** Murshid Quli was followed by Shuja-ud-din, then Sarfaraz; Alivardi displaced Sarfaraz and Siraj succeeded Alivardi.

**Sources:** P pp. 26–27; W Q8, Q50; B §§2, 4.
**Examiner trap:** Alivardi's accession interrupted the Shuja-ud-din–Sarfaraz line; Siraj came later.

### 7. D

**Answer: D.**

- **A:** Jagir assignments and zamindari rights were different arrangements; confirmation did not require wholesale conversion.
- **B:** Confirmation was conditional on dues, not an exemption from the nawab's fiscal demands.
- **C:** Ijara contracting was a fiscal mechanism, not evidence that local chiefs ceased mediating Awadh revenue.
- **D (correct):** Saadat Khan subdued zamindars but could confirm their landed rights if they submitted and paid.

**Sources:** P pp. 27–28, 43–45; W Q9–10, Q35; B §§2, 8.4A.
**Examiner trap:** Conquest, collection and conditional accommodation are compatible.

### 8. B

**Answer: B.**

- **A:** Imperial titles could be useful while the holder assembled a regional coalition.
- **B (correct):** His wazirate did not eliminate his Awadh fiscal and military influence.
- **C:** Safdar Jang's imperial service demonstrates, rather than disproves, this possibility.
- **D:** Local landed and military allies remained relevant to his provincial position.

**Sources:** B mini-timeline, §§2, 8.4A; P pp. 27–28; W Q9, Q49.
**Examiner trap:** The question asks what is **disproved**, not which other statements are true.

### 9. D

**Answer: D.**

- **A:** Mysore did not derive its authority from the Mughal nazimat, whereas Hyderabad's founder did hold provincial office.
- **B:** This reverses the genealogies: Hyderabad was a Mughal province, Mysore a Wodeyar kingdom.
- **C:** A Maratha fiscal claim is not Tipu's coinage strategy, and the Nizam's early imperial symbolism is reversed.
- **D (correct):** Tipu used stronger personal sovereignty symbolism; Asaf Jah retained the emperor's coin/khutba conventions.

**Sources:** P pp. 24–25, 29–31; W Q2, Q27, Q29; B §§2, 5.
**Examiner trap:** Compare whose sovereignty a coin asserts without assigning Mysore a Mughal provincial ancestry.

### 10. D

**Answer: D.**

- **A:** Travancore's army-building concerns Martanda Varma, not the source of Haidar's military expertise.
- **B:** Carnatic claimants' access to French-trained contingents is regional parallel evidence, not evidence about Haidar's arsenal.
- **C:** Ibrahim Khan Gardi's trained contingent demonstrates Maratha adaptation, not Mysore's specific practice.
- **D (correct):** Haidar's Dindigul arsenal with French technical assistance directly rebuts the stated claim.

**Sources:** P pp. 29–32, 39–41, 66–67 (Carnatic comparator); W Q28, Q30 and Mains 10M-2; B §§2, 8.4C.
**Examiner trap:** True examples of adaptation elsewhere are not evidence about the named ruler.

### 11. C

**Answer: C.**

- **A:** Bengal did inherit a revenue office, but Mughal diwani is not the genealogy of Travancore.
- **B:** Kerala's pepper setting does not describe Bengal; state credit alone cannot locate Travancore's distinctive strategy.
- **C (correct):** Bengal's governor–diwan framework contrasts with Martanda Varma's local army and pepper-trade consolidation.
- **D:** It swaps Bengal's Mughal diwani inheritance with Travancore's pepper-based Kerala consolidation.

**Sources:** P pp. 25–27, 32; W Q6, Q30; B §§2, 8.4A, 8.4C.
**Examiner trap:** A half-correct pairing is wrong if it projects Mughal provincial office onto Travancore.

### 12. B

**Answer: B.**

- **A:** Reverses the rulers' two known regional associations.
- **B (correct):** Suraj Mal strengthened Jat Bharatpur; Sawai Jai Singh II patronised Jaipur and its observatories.
- **C:** Suraj Mal's Bharatpur is right, but the Carnatic nawabate was not Jai Singh's Rajput court.
- **D:** Rohilla Afghan settlement is distinct from Suraj Mal's Jat base, though Jai Singh's Jaipur association is true.

**Sources:** P pp. 32–33; W Q31–33; B §§2, 4.
**Examiner trap:** Reject the partly right pair only after checking its other half.

### 13. B

**Answer: B.**

- **A:** Banda's officials and seals belong to his earlier revolt and state-making attempt.
- **B (correct):** The misls and collective Sikh action intervened before Ranjit Singh's unification.
- **C:** Guru Gobind Singh's Khalsa precedes Banda, not the intermediate phase after him.
- **D:** Banda's campaigns likewise belong before, not between, the misl and Ranjit Singh phases.

**Sources:** P pp. 34–36; W Q21–26; B §§4–5; A §4.
**Examiner trap:** All four choices are Sikh history; place each institution in its proper sequence.

### 14. C

**Answer: C.**

- **A:** Officials provide evidence of an attempt to administer captured territory.
- **B:** Coins and seals convey a claim to political authority.
- **C (correct):** Some Jat zamindars and Khatri interests opposed Banda; coinage or appointments cannot establish unanimous Sikh support.
- **D:** Agrarian participation is separately attested; the question expressly includes wider social evidence.

**Sources:** P pp. 34–36; W Q22–23 and Mains 15M-2; B §8.4B.
**Examiner trap:** Distinguish an administrative symbol from the extent of popular support.

### 15. D

**Answer: D.**

- **A:** Permanent single command is not established; joint action did not extinguish chiefs' autonomy.
- **B:** Misls were armed chiefly formations, not a replacement by Mughal district staff.
- **C:** Territorial shares could coexist with collective campaigning.
- **D (correct):** A fixed grid conceals shifting chiefly groupings and campaigning together through the Dal Khalsa.

**Sources:** P pp. 34–36; W Q24–25 and Mains 15M-2; B §§4–5.
**Examiner trap:** Twelve is a conventional count, not a warrant for inventing uniform bureaucratic districts.

### 16. A

**Answer: A.**

- **A (correct):** Sardeshmukhi was an additional superior-deshmukh claim, not a second name for chauth.
- **B:** The existence of a sanction did not guarantee collection across contested territory.
- **C:** Chauth was the Maratha quarter-share claim; it was not simply a village salary.
- **D:** The claim's asserted superior-right basis is a genuine distinction.

**Sources:** P pp. 37–39; W Q11–13, Q51; B §§2, 8.4B.
**Examiner trap:** For a rejection stem, the only wrong proposition is the equation of the two claims.

### 17. D

**Answer: D.**

- **A:** The sanads recognised Shahu's claims; they did not transfer Maratha collection rights to Hyderabad's diwan.
- **B:** Swarajya and Deccan fiscal claims were Maratha concerns, not Sikh territorial allotments.
- **C:** Balaji Vishwanath and subsequent Peshwas remained central to obtaining and pursuing these claims.
- **D (correct):** Imperial legal sanction did not compel compliance by all provincial authorities or chiefs.

**Sources:** P pp. 37–39; W Q13, Q20; B §§2, 8.4B; A §5.
**Examiner trap:** Legal recognition ≠ realised revenue: distinguish the document from its enforcement.

### 18. B

**Answer: B.**

- **A:** Recognition after a campaign did not end later contest over Deccan claims.
- **B (correct):** Baji Rao's manoeuvre against the Nizam reinforced recognition of Shahu's fiscal position.
- **C:** Safdar Jang's imperial wazirate belongs to Awadh's later politics, not Baji Rao's reward.
- **D:** Hyderabad survived Palkhed as an Asaf Jahi state; its territory was not annexed to Poona.

**Sources:** P pp. 38–39; W Q13–14; B §§2, 8.4B.
**Examiner trap:** Success in compelling recognition is not conquest or a final settlement.

### 19. C

**Answer: C.**

- **A:** Scindia–Gwalior and Gaekwad–Baroda are real pairings, but neither identifies the Indore house.
- **B:** Gaekwad–Baroda and Bhonsle–Nagpur are real pairings, not the house in the stem.
- **C (correct):** The Holkars were associated with Indore/Malwa; Scindias' base developed around Gwalior.
- **D:** Bhonsles centred on Nagpur, while the Gaekwads' base developed at Baroda.

**Sources:** P pp. 39–42; W Q15, Q20; B §§2, 4; A §4.
**Examiner trap:** Multiple options contain historically accurate house–base pairs; answer the specified **Indore** identification.

### 20. D

**Answer: D.**

- **A:** Ibrahim Khan Gardi's trained force rebuts a tactical myth but precedes the later revival.
- **B:** Suraj Mal's withdrawal pertains to the pre-battle coalition problem, not recovery.
- **C:** Abdali's Rohilla support helps explain the battle, not a subsequent Maratha recovery.
- **D (correct):** Maratha support for Shah Alam II's return to Delhi demonstrates later northern political reach.

**Sources:** P pp. 39–42; W Q17–19 and Mains 10M-2; B mini-timeline, §8.4B.
**Examiner trap:** A battle cause, a wartime capability and a later recovery event answer different questions.

### 21. A

**Answer: A.**

- **A (correct):** Bharatpur Jat agrarian chieftaincy differs from Afghan military settlement in Rohilkhand, which predated Abdali's later alliance.
- **B:** This reverses the agrarian-Jat and Afghan-Rohilla social bases.
- **C:** Jat chieftaincy fits, but Rohilkhand predated Abdali's later alliance; he did not grant its founding territories.
- **D:** Afghan settlement fits Rohillas; Jats did not originate under Mughal provincial governors.

**Sources:** P pp. 32–33, 39; W Q31, Q33; B §§2, 4; A §2.
**Examiner trap:** Do not backdate the Durrani alliance into Rohilkhand's foundation.

### 22. C

**Answer: C.**

- **A:** A jagir was a revenue assignment and peshkash acknowledged a superior; neither identifies this pair.
- **B:** The first describes peshkash and the second a hundi, reversing the instrument named first.
- **C (correct):** A hundi transfers/remits value through credit networks; ijara contracts revenue collection to a farmer.
- **D:** The first describes ijara more closely than hundi, while superior-deshmukh refers to sardeshmukhi.

**Sources:** P pp. 43–45; W Q35–38, Q51; B §§2, 8.4D.
**Examiner trap:** Keep revenue assignment, contracted collection, tribute and transferable credit distinct.

### 23. A

**Answer: A.**

- **A (correct):** Some inland trade suffered under raids while oceanic commerce continued or grew.
- **B:** European privileges could be politically damaging, but they did not erase local Bengal commerce wholesale.
- **C:** Local overland losses cannot establish that all lending and remittance ended.
- **D:** Export growth alone does not establish the prosperity of every inland trader.

**Sources:** P pp. 26–27, 43–45; W Q39–40; B §§1, 8.4D.
**Examiner trap:** Distinguish route-specific disruption from all-commerce collapse or universal prosperity.

### 24. C

**Answer: C.**

- **A:** Elite biography does not measure peasant women's property or labour conditions.
- **B:** Political agency in one court is not evidence of universal access to revenue office.
- **C (correct):** A woman's documented court authority cannot represent all castes, classes and villages.
- **D:** Inheritance practices varied across region and community; a single household cannot fix them.

**Sources:** P pp. 47–49; W Q41–42; B §8.4E; A §1 (social limits).
**Examiner trap:** Source visibility and social representativeness are different.

### 25. A

**Answer: A.**

- **A (correct):** Persianate continuity and vernacular regional adaptation could coexist.
- **B:** Commissioning attests patronage but cannot measure artisans' incomes across India.
- **C:** Regional languages flourished without the erasure of Persianate practice.
- **D:** Regional commissions show redistribution rather than extinction of elite support.

**Sources:** P pp. 49–51; W Q43–44; B §§1, 8.4E; A §§1–2.
**Examiner trap:** Court culture's survival is not evidence of either unchanged artistic geography or artisan equality.

### 26. B

**Answer: B.**

- **A:** Treasury capacity is not a series of peasant household incomes.
- **B (correct):** Banking could support armies even as war and collection raised costs for cultivators.
- **C:** Agrarian burdens and regional state-building were often simultaneous.
- **D:** Rural extraction did not entail the disappearance of transferable urban credit.

**Sources:** P pp. 43–49; W Q45–47, Q52; B §§1, 8.4D, 8.6; A §§1–2.
**Examiner trap:** Do not turn merchant/state capacity into an all-class welfare measure.

### 27. D

**Answer: D.**

- **A:** A Dindigul arsenal is evidence of reform, not automatic control of sea routes.
- **B:** Genealogy may matter for legitimacy but cannot settle logistics or battles on its own.
- **C:** A provincial title does not ensure cohesion of chiefs or field command.
- **D (correct):** Financing, provisioning and diplomatic coalitions affect whether improved troops can campaign successfully.

**Sources:** P pp. 29–31, 39–43; W Q47–48, Q51, Q56; B §8.4C, §8.6.
**Examiner trap:** This is a bounded comparison of capacities, not a claim that a later Company victory was inevitable.

### 28. A

**Answer: A.**

- **A (correct):** Court patronage and army pay are different, partial dimensions requiring corroboration.
- **B:** Arrears in a pay register do not show that patronage ended.
- **C:** An elite painting cannot measure rural household incomes.
- **D:** Two records from a setting cannot prove subcontinental uniformity.

**Sources:** P pp. 43–51; W Q55; B §§8.4D–E, 8.6.
**Examiner trap:** A source for one institution is not a universal census of welfare.

### 29. A

**Answer: A.**

- **A (correct):** Bengal production and a southern seaport illustrate regional craft/commercial geographies.
- **B:** Shared commercial significance does not entail identical commodities or exports.
- **C:** Dacca and Murshidabad were Bengal centres, but Masulipatnam lay on the Coromandel.
- **D:** Maratha fiscal pressure did not define all three centres or their only exchange route.

**Sources:** P pp. 43–45; W Q39–40; B §§1, 8.4D.
**Examiner trap:** Map the Coromandel location before treating the list as one province.

### 30. B

**Answer: B.**

- **A:** Village and urban examples support teaching outside a royal court.
- **B (correct):** The presence of teaching institutions cannot prove equal access by caste and gender.
- **C:** Schools and maktabs describe distinct local settings for some instruction.
- **D:** Reading, writing and arithmetic/accounts appear in the elementary-teaching account.

**Sources:** P pp. 46–47; W Q41–42; B §§1, 8.4E.
**Examiner trap:** For a *not established* question, distinguish provision from universal access.

### 31. C

**Answer: C.**

- **A:** Widowhood and remarriage varied with caste and region, independently of one Kerala example.
- **B:** Matrilineal descent is not identical to equal individual control over property or public power.
- **C (correct):** Kerala's variation defeats a claim that all Indian family systems were patrilineal.
- **D:** A particular inheritance custom does not establish all courtly women's landownership.

**Sources:** P pp. 47–49; W Q41–42; B §8.4E.
**Examiner trap:** A counterexample to universal patriliny is not proof of universal female equality.

### 32. C

**Answer: C.**

- **A:** Regional growth and peasant extraction could be simultaneous, not mutually cancelling.
- **B:** Local coercion does not establish an all-India collapse in commerce.
- **C (correct):** Regionalisation, credit and cultural vitality coexist with war-related and agrarian burdens.
- **D:** Bengal, Awadh and Hyderabad adapted enduring Mughal provincial institutions.

**Sources:** A historiography note and §§1–2; B §§1, 8.4, 8.6; P pp. 43–51; W Q45–47, Q52.
**Examiner trap:** Alam/Bayly's regional reordering and Chandra's social cautions are scale-dependent, not exclusive explanations.
