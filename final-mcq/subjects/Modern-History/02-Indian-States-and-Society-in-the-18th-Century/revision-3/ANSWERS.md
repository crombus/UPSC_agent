### 1. C

**Answer: C.**

- **A:** Incorrect. Bengal's institutional starting point was Mughal provincial office, not the Jat mobilisation route.
- **B:** Incorrect. Imperial titles did not imply Delhi's direct tax administration.
- **C:** Correct. A successor could use Mughal authority while controlling provincial taxation locally.
- **D:** Incorrect. Unlike Mysore, Bengal was an inherited Mughal province.

**Sources:** B §§1–2, 8.4A; A §§1–2; P pp. 24–27; W Q1–2.
**Examiner trap:** Neither imperial titles nor local decisions alone settle the form-versus-practice distinction.

### 2. A

**Answer: A.**

- **A:** Correct. A map of formal hierarchy cannot prove that every Arcot appointment was effectively controlled by Hyderabad.
- **B:** Incorrect. Arcot's Carnatic–Deccan genealogy is precisely what the hierarchy helps establish.
- **C:** Incorrect. Autonomy under nominal suzerainty is possible; this option makes no unproved claim about universal compliance.
- **D:** Incorrect. The mapped relationship places Carnatic politics within the Deccan provincial field.

**Sources:** B §§1–2; A §§1–2; P p. 25; W Q3, Q50.
**Examiner trap:** Distinguish a superior's recognised claim from day-to-day control.

### 3. D

**Answer: D.**

- **A:** Incorrect. A coin legend cannot demonstrate who actually appointed regional officials.
- **B:** Incorrect. Direct negotiation does not establish formal abandonment of Mughal legitimacy.
- **C:** Incorrect. A coin cannot prove individual chiefs' remittances reached Delhi.
- **D:** Correct. Asaf Jah combined imperial idiom with autonomous political action.

**Sources:** B §§2, 8.4A; A §§1–2; P pp. 24–25; W Q2, Q4–5.
**Examiner trap:** A monetary symbol is evidence of asserted legitimacy, not of effective fiscal or military command.

### 4. B

**Answer: B.**

- **A:** Incorrect. Combining nazim and diwan increased leverage over government and revenue.
- **B:** Correct. Even a powerful nawab depended on landed intermediaries for collection.
- **C:** Incorrect. Mughal office could furnish authority for locally controlled revenue administration.
- **D:** Incorrect. Bargains with zamindars were part of, not a contradiction to, provincial consolidation.

**Sources:** B §§2, 8.4A, 8.4D; A §§1–2; P pp. 25–27; W Q6, Q45.
**Examiner trap:** Office concentration does not imply elimination of intermediaries.

### 5. D

**Answer: D.**

- **A:** Incorrect. The stated banking house served both merchants and the nawab's treasury.
- **B:** Incorrect. A creditor's access and influence did not make that creditor the formal diwan.
- **C:** Incorrect. Loans and remittances moved funds; they did not replace land revenue as a fiscal base.
- **D:** Correct. Treasury and commercial links made the house influential in political bargaining, without conferring sovereign office.

**Sources:** B §8.4D; A §§1–2, 2 (merchant-financier role); W Q7, Q37, Q55; P pp. 26–27 (regional context, not a named banking-house mechanism).
**Examiner trap:** Separate credit brokerage, public office and the underlying source of revenue.

### 6. A

- **A:** Correct. Alivardi displaced Sarfaraz and was succeeded by Siraj-ud-Daula.
- **B:** Incorrect. Shuja-ud-din preceded Sarfaraz, while Safdar Jang belonged to Awadh.
- **C:** Incorrect. Murshid Quli preceded several Bengal nawabs; Shuja-ud-Daula belonged to Awadh.

### 7. C

**Answer: C.**

- **A:** Incorrect. Confirming collection rights did not hand over the provincial diwani.
- **B:** Incorrect. Conditional tribute is evidence against fully independent sovereignty.
- **C:** Correct. Coercion and negotiated retention of landed intermediaries could support collection.
- **D:** Incorrect. Confirming an existing zamindar is not, by itself, auctioning an ijara contract.

**Sources:** B §§2, 8.4A; A §§1–2; P pp. 27–28; W Q9–10, Q35.
**Examiner trap:** A confirmed zamindar and a revenue-farm contractor are not interchangeable.

### 8. B

### 9. A

**Answer: A.**

- **B:** Incorrect. Coins and drill concern legitimacy and military technique, not both states' institutional origins.

**Sources:** B §§2, 5, 8.8; A §§1–2, 5; P pp. 24–25, 29–31; W Q27–29, Q50.

### 10. D

**Answer: D.**

- **A:** Incorrect. Imported drill is consistent with locally manufactured firearms.
- **B:** Incorrect. Local manufacture did not prevent using selected European technical expertise.
- **C:** Incorrect. The existence of an arsenal alone says nothing about the advisers who assisted it.
- **D:** Correct. Haidar Ali's military adaptation drew on both local production and French expertise.

**Sources:** B §8.4C; A §§1–2; P pp. 29–31; W Q28.
**Examiner trap:** Adaptation is neither wholesale dependence nor complete insulation.

### 11. B

- **B:** Correct. Martanda Varma consolidated a Kerala monarchy, outside Mughal provincial succession.
- **C:** Incorrect. The Maratha confederacy was not Travancore's dynastic origin.
- **D:** Incorrect. Bengal's diwani was unrelated to Travancore's political genealogy.

**Sources:** B §§2, 4; A §§1–2; P p. 32; W Q30.

### 12. C

**Answer: C.**

- **A:** Incorrect. Suraj Mal's power centre was Bharatpur, not Rohilkhand.
- **B:** Incorrect. Suraj Mal was not a Jaipur Rajput and Jai Singh was not an Indore Maratha.
- **C:** Correct. Suraj Mal represents Jat Bharatpur; Sawai Jai Singh II belongs to Rajput Jaipur.
- **D:** Incorrect. Bharatpur was not the Mughal Deccan successor state Hyderabad.

**Examiner trap:** Similar eighteenth-century prominence does not make two leaders members of the same political type.

### 13. D

### 14. A

### 15. C

### 16. B

### 17. C

### 18. D
