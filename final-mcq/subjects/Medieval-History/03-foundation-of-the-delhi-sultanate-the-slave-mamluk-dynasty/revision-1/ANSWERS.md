### R1-01 — B

**Answer: B.** Mamluk denotes an owned, trained military-household servant; manumission could precede sovereign rule.

- **A (incorrect):** Provincial revenue assignments are *iqta* arrangements, not the meaning of *mamluk* (Basic §§9.1, 17.2).
- **B (correct):** Military-household status and training explain the designation without implying continued legal enslavement after manumission (Basic §9.1; Advanced §§1, 4; OCR p.87).
- **C (incorrect):** The Mamluk sequence was not a single biological lineage (Basic §§9.1, 11).
- **D (incorrect):** Caliphal investiture recognised political standing; it did not confer a military-slave identity (Basic §§9.1, 12.2).

**Trap:** Status and training are distinct from hereditary succession and caliphal legitimation.

### R1-02 — B

**Answer: B.** Different material classes can independently check *public claims*, not the effectiveness of rule in each locality.

- **A (incorrect):** A retelling may inherit rather than corroborate its source's picture of the frontier (Basic §9.2).
- **B (correct):** Dated local inscriptions and ruler-titled coins independently constrain chronology and public sovereignty claims; neither alone proves routine administration (Basic §§9.2, 17.1; Workbook MCQ 39).
- **C (incorrect):** Shared court records can transmit the same royal claim into both texts (Basic §9.2; Advanced §5).
- **D (incorrect):** Descent accounts establish claimed kinship, not local titulature or coin circulation (Basic §9.2).

**Trap:** The proposed checks cannot prove uninterrupted village-level taxation or garrison control.

### R1-03 — B

**Answer: B.** The three successor power bases differed even though control of Lahore was contested.

- **A (incorrect):** Uch was Qubacha's base; Yildiz held Ghazni (Basic §10; OCR pp.87–88).
- **B (correct):** Aibak's principal centre was Lahore, Yildiz's Ghazni and Qubacha's Multan–Uch sphere (Basic §§10–11; Advanced §1; OCR pp.87–88).
- **C (incorrect):** Aibak was centred in Lahore/Delhi, not the Ghazni claim held by Yildiz (Basic §10; OCR p.87).
- **D (incorrect):** Uch belonged to Qubacha; Aibak's main royal centre was Lahore (Basic §§10–11; OCR p.88).

**Trap:** A rival's later contest for Lahore does not make Lahore his principal initial base.

### R1-04 — B

**Answer: B.** Continuity after the Ghurid rupture and subsequent state consolidation are distinct achievements.

- **A (incorrect):** The Indus/Multan consolidation was chiefly Iltutmish's, not Aibak's finished frontier (Basic §§11–12; OCR pp.87–88).
- **B (correct):** Aibak sustained gains during a brief independent reign; Iltutmish subdued rivals and built durable Delhi-centred institutions (Basic §§10–12; Advanced §1).
- **C (incorrect):** Caliphal recognition followed Iltutmish's consolidation, while Yildiz lost his Ghazni-overlordship claim at Tarain (Basic §12).
- **D (incorrect):** Bengal remained intermittently subject even after Iltutmish's campaigns; he did not restore Ghurid dependency (Basic §§10, 12.1).

**Trap:** Neither independence nor a campaign is identical to a uniform administrative empire.

### R1-05 — A

**Answer: A.** Succession required alliances and force, not automatic inheritance.

- **A (correct):** Delhi elites invited Iltutmish; he defeated Aram Shah after Aibak's death (Basic §§11–12; Workbook MCQ 34).
- **B (incorrect):** Aram Shah's defeat belies an invariably operative primogeniture rule (Basic §§9.1, 11).
- **C (incorrect):** Iltutmish's caliphal investiture came after, not before, the Delhi accession struggle (Basic §12.2; Atlas hard MCQ 11).
- **D (incorrect):** An *iqta* assigned revenue for service, not an automatic succession title (Basic §§9.1, 17.2).

**Trap:** Aram Shah's precise parentage is disputed; it is unnecessary to establish his deposition.

### R1-06 — A

**Answer: A.** The Tarain encounter of Iltutmish's reign was not Muizz al-Din's earlier Tarain.

- **A (correct):** Iltutmish defeated Yildiz at Tarain in 1216, undermining the Ghazni-based claim to seniority (Basic §12.1; Workbook MCQ 35; OCR pp.87–88).
- **B (incorrect):** Qubacha was the rival in the Multan–Uch/Sindh theatre, not this Tarain encounter (Basic §12.1; OCR p.88).
- **C (incorrect):** Iwaz belongs to the Bengal contest rather than Yildiz's Ghazni claim (Basic §12.1; OCR p.88).
- **D (incorrect):** Aram Shah was the rival for Delhi's throne immediately after Aibak, not the Ghazni claimant (Basic §§11–12).

**Trap:** Do not collapse the two Tarain battles into a single event.

### R1-07 — C

**Answer: C.** Qubacha's western sphere is the Indus approach.

- **A (incorrect):** Yildiz, not Qubacha, claimed seniority from Ghazni (Basic §§10, 12.1; OCR p.87).
- **B (incorrect):** Lakhnauti and Bengal–Bihar were the eastern, Iwaz-related theatre (Basic §12.1; OCR pp.88, 99–100).
- **C (correct):** Qubacha's base lay at Multan and Uch, on Iltutmish's western line of competition (Basic §§10, 12.1; OCR pp.88, 96).
- **D (incorrect):** Lahore/Delhi describes Aibak's centres, though Qubacha later contested Lahore; "principal" distinguishes bases (Basic §§10–11; OCR p.88).

**Trap:** Differentiate a principal base from a city contested by multiple rulers.

### R1-08 — C

**Answer: C.** Iltutmish avoided commitment to the prince Chinggis was pursuing.

- **A (incorrect):** Delhi did not join Mangbarani in a war against Chinggis (Basic §12.1; OCR p.96).
- **B (incorrect):** Avoidance of a Khwarazmian alliance did not amount to surrendering Delhi's garrisons to Mongols (Basic §12.1; Advanced §4).
- **C (correct):** Declining alliance/asylum commitment limited Delhi's exposure to the Mongol–Khwarazm confrontation (Basic §12.1; Workbook MCQ 37; OCR p.96).
- **D (incorrect):** Iltutmish's policy was prudence, not conquest beyond the Indus (Basic §12.1; OCR p.96).

**Trap:** 1221 was neither a Delhi conquest by Chinggis nor a battlefield victory over him.

### R1-09 — B

**Answer: B.** Campaign, recognition and continued provincial obedience are different levels of authority.

- **A (incorrect):** The Basic owner distinguishes Bengal's intermittent suzerainty from continuous village revenue administration (Basic §§12.1, 17.1).
- **B (correct):** Iltutmish intervened repeatedly, but distance and regional governors made submission reversible (Basic §§12.1, 17.1; Workbook MCQ 43; OCR pp.88, 99–100).
- **C (incorrect):** Caliphal recognition did not make Bengal an Abbasid-administered province (Basic §§12.2, 17.1).
- **D (incorrect):** Iltutmish did assert claims and undertake expeditions toward Bengal (Basic §12.1; OCR p.88).

**Trap:** A shaded territorial map can conceal graded and changing sovereignty.

### R1-10 — B

**Answer: B.** The *iqta* was a service-linked revenue assignment, not a freehold estate.

- **A (incorrect):** The assignment did not confer unconditional inheritable ownership of the land (Basic §17.2; Workbook MCQ 23).
- **B (correct):** A *muqti* exercised revenue and military/administrative functions subject in principle to central control (Basic §§12.2, 17.2; Atlas hard MCQ 39).
- **C (incorrect):** Minting the sultan's tanka is distinct from holding a provincial *iqta* (Basic §§9.2, 12.2, 17.2).
- **D (incorrect):** Neither village election nor command of every Delhi cavalry unit defines the *muqti* (Basic §17.2).

**Trap:** Iltutmish adapted and extended *iqta* practice; he did not invent it from nothing.

### R1-11 — C

**Answer: C.** A coin records titulature and circulation, not fiscal reach at every nearby village.

- **A (incorrect):** Coin find-spots do not prove direct tax collection in their surrounding countryside (Basic §§9.2, 17.1; Workbook MCQ 39).
- **B (incorrect):** Caliphal recognition was symbolic; it did not put local mints under Baghdad's revenue administration (Basic §12.2; Atlas hard MCQ 11).
- **C (correct):** A titled tanka supplies material evidence of a sovereignty claim and circulation (Basic §§9.2, 12.2; Workbook MCQ 39).
- **D (incorrect):** Bengal's governors retained scope for rebellion despite the Delhi ruler's public claims (Basic §§12.1, 17.1).

**Trap:** Mint-place, find-place and jurisdiction of effective government are separate.

### R1-12 — A

**Answer: A.** Delhi became a Persianate court and urban centre as well as a garrison.

- **A (correct):** Refuge and patronage drew administrators, Sufis, artisans and scholars to Delhi (Basic §§12.2, 18; OCR pp.88, 95–96).
- **B (incorrect):** A complete compulsory transplantation of Lahore's population is not supported; regional nodes remained (Basic §§11, 18).
- **C (incorrect):** Lahore, Multan and other towns continued as regional centres (Basic §18).
- **D (incorrect):** Bengal's governors were not Delhi's exclusive builders or financiers (Basic §§12.1, 18).

**Trap:** Persianate indicates a linguistic-cultural sphere, not homogeneous Persian descent.

### R1-13 — B

**Answer: B.** This is the immediate post-1236 succession alignment.

- **A (incorrect):** Bahram, Masud and Nasiruddin belong to later phases after Raziya's accession and fall (Basic §13).
- **B (correct):** Ruknuddin's short reign, his mother Shah Turkan's influence and Raziya's accession frame the immediate struggle (Basic §13; Workbook MCQ 01).
- **C (incorrect):** Balban, Bughra Khan and Qaiqabad belong to later mid-/late-thirteenth-century politics (Basic §§15–16).
- **D (incorrect):** Aram Shah, Yildiz and Qubacha belong to the earlier 1210s–1220s succession/consolidation struggles (Basic §§11–12).

**Trap:** All are early-Sultanate actors; the qualifier is *immediate* after Iltutmish.

### R1-14 — A

**Answer: A.** Access to the ruler through the stables made Yaqut's office politically sensitive.

- **A (correct):** Raziya raised Jamal al-Din Yaqut to *amir-i-akhur*; this angered powerful Turkish nobles (Basic §13; Workbook MCQ 02; OCR pp.90–91).
- **B (incorrect):** Altunia was associated with the revolt at Tabarhinda, not Yaqut's stable appointment or leadership of the *barids* (Basic §13; OCR pp.90–91).
- **C (incorrect):** Raihan's prominence and contest with Balban arose during Nasiruddin's reign (Basic §§13–15; OCR p.92).
- **D (incorrect):** Iwaz belongs to Iltutmish's earlier Bengal campaigns, not a promotion by Raziya (Basic §§12.1, 13).

**Trap:** Noble rivalry, gender and provincial revolt matter; an alleged romance is not established by the contemporary account (OCR p.91).

### R1-15 — B

**Answer: B.** The conventional name of the Shamsi group does not describe a fixed representative institution.

- **A (incorrect):** “Forty” is not evidence for exactly forty permanent cabinet seats (Basic §14; Advanced §5).
- **B (correct):** The powerful Iltutmish-linked mamluk elite comprised changing factions with interests in offices and *iqtas* (Basic §14; Workbook MCQ 40; Advanced §1).
- **C (incorrect):** The elite did not represent all Turkish subjects, much less the population as a whole (Basic §14).
- **D (incorrect):** Bengal governors are regional actors, not the definition of the Shamsi court elite (Basic §§12.1, 14).

**Trap:** Barani's later label must not be mistaken for a literal membership register.

### R1-16 — D

**Answer: D.** Balban's deputyship under Nasiruddin was powerful but interrupted.

- **A (incorrect):** Iwaz was a Bengal rival under Iltutmish, not Balban's opponent at Bahram's court (Basic §§12.1–13; OCR pp.88, 92).
- **B (incorrect):** Yaqut's office and Raziya's deposition preceded Balban's ascendancy as Nasiruddin's *naib* (Basic §§13–15; OCR pp.90–92).
- **C (incorrect):** Yildiz was defeated under Iltutmish, decades before Balban's deputyship (Basic §§12.1, 15; OCR pp.87–88).
- **D (correct):** Imad al-Din Raihan displaced Balban in Nasiruddin's court before Balban recovered influence (Basic §§13–15; Workbook MCQs 06–07; OCR p.92).

**Trap:** “Dominant *naib*” must not imply uninterrupted authority or a powerless sultan.

### R1-17 — A

**Answer: A.** These were gestures of bodily submission at court.

- **A (correct):** *Sijda* (prostration) and *paibos* (foot-kissing) dramatised Balban's royal hierarchy (Basic §15.1; Workbook MCQ 11; OCR pp.93–94).
- **B (incorrect):** *Khutba* and *sikka* express public sovereignty through sermon and coin, not bodily audience gestures (Basic §§9.2, 21).
- **C (incorrect):** *Chatr* and *alam* are insignia, not acts of prostration or foot-kissing (Advanced §2; Basic §15.1).
- **D (incorrect):** *Iqta* and *muqti* refer to revenue-service arrangements and their holders (Basic §17.2).

**Trap:** Royal ceremony could meet theological objections; ritual was not a substitute for army or intelligence (Workbook MCQ 11).

### R1-18 — D

**Answer: D.** Intelligence helps the court scrutinise self-interested local reporting.

- **A (incorrect):** Cavalry/army inspection belongs to military administration, not the *barid*'s defining function (Basic §§15.2, 17.2).
- **B (incorrect):** Provincial fiscal assessment is a revenue/administrative task, distinct from intelligence reports (Basic §17.2).
- **C (incorrect):** A *qazi* exercised judicial/religious-law authority; this is not the reporting function of a *barid* (Basic §§17.2, 19).
- **D (correct):** Balban used informants to monitor governors, officers and conditions in distant provinces (Basic §15.2; Workbook MCQ 13; OCR pp.93–94).

**Trap:** *Barids* became particularly visible under Balban, but were not invented out of nothing (Basic §17.2).

### R1-19 — A

**Answer: A.** Balban's internal security measures targeted the capital's vulnerable land routes.

- **A (correct):** Campaigns in Mewat and Katehar used coercion to secure Delhi and surrounding routes; they had severe human costs (Basic §15.2; Workbook MCQ 16; OCR pp.101–102).
- **B (incorrect):** Fortification toward the Beas/Indus is the separate Mongol-facing northwest response (Basic §15.2; OCR p.97).
- **C (incorrect):** Tughril and later Bughra Khan belong to Bengal, not the Mewat–Katehar suppression (Basic §§15.2–16; OCR pp.99–102).
- **D (incorrect):** Court ceremony expressed hierarchy but did not replace these inland punitive operations (Basic §§15.1–15.2; OCR pp.93–94).

**Trap:** “Blood and iron” is later shorthand, not a verified self-description by Balban (Basic §15.2; Workbook MCQ 12).

### R1-20 — C

**Answer: C.** The fort group fits the Mongol-facing defence near the Beas.

- **A (incorrect):** Gwalior, Ranthambore and Bayana are Rajput-frontier settings, not this northwest fort line (Basic §§12.1, 17.1; OCR pp.97, 102).
- **B (incorrect):** Lakhnauti/Gaur/Bihar locate eastern Bengal politics rather than Beas defence (Basic §§12.1, 17.1).
- **C (correct):** Balban repaired Tabarhinda, Sunam and Samana and posted forces to hinder a Mongol crossing of the Beas (Basic §15.2; Workbook MCQ 18; OCR p.97).
- **D (incorrect):** Ajmer, Nagor and Badaun are not the specific fort triad in the Beas defence narrative (Basic §§12.1, 15.2; OCR p.97).

**Trap:** This defensive line is not a claim to uncontested control of all western Punjab.

### R1-21 — C

**Answer: C.** A successful punitive expedition did not erase the structural distance of Bengal.

- **A (incorrect):** Delegation continued; Bughra Khan's later position demonstrates no permanent abolition of governors (Basic §§15.2–16; OCR pp.99–100, 103).
- **B (incorrect):** Balban's campaign reasserted Delhi's claim, rather than proclaiming permanent autonomy as its result (Basic §15.2; OCR p.100).
- **C (correct):** Tughril's defeat restored authority temporarily; Bengal again had room for autonomy under Bughra Khan (Basic §§15.2–16; Workbook MCQ 22; OCR pp.99–100, 103).
- **D (incorrect):** Bengal is eastern; Balban's Mongol-facing western Punjab line was a separate theatre (Basic §§15.2, 17.1; OCR pp.97, 99–100).

**Trap:** Distinguish court claims, restored submission and durable revenue control.

### R1-22 — D

**Answer: D.** The frontier death removed a preferred heir; other listed events also matter to succession but do not answer the specified loss.

- **A (incorrect):** Bughra Khan's preference for Bengal later complicated succession, but is not the death of an heir in Mongol fighting (Basic §16; OCR p.103).
- **B (incorrect):** Court factions installed Qaiqabad after Balban's death; this was a consequence, not the frontier loss (Basic §16; OCR p.103).
- **C (incorrect):** Tughril's Bengal rebellion challenged royal authority in a different theatre, not the heir's northwest death (Basic §§15.2–16; OCR p.100).
- **D (correct):** Prince Muhammad, Balban's preferred heir, was killed fighting Mongols; the succession plan lost its intended successor (Basic §§15.2–16; Workbook MCQ 20; OCR pp.94, 103).

**Trap:** The son's death, Bughra's preference and Qaiqabad's accession are separate stages.

### R1-23 — D

**Answer: D.** A central court relied on layered regional and local cooperation.

- **A (incorrect):** Cultivators were not individually appointed by Delhi; local chiefs and headmen mediated revenue extraction (Basic §§17.2, 21).
- **B (incorrect):** Military and urban institutions mattered, but local fiscal connections also sustained the state (Basic §§17.2–18, 21).
- **C (incorrect):** Governors did not remove all existing intermediaries and scribes from village practice (Basic §17.2; Workbook MCQ 24).
- **D (correct):** Chiefs, headmen and scribal personnel helped link court demands to local revenue and coercive practice (Basic §§17.2–19, 21; Workbook MCQ 24).

**Trap:** Apex centralisation is compatible with uneven practical reach.

### R1-24 — B

**Answer: B.** The monument is an early royal tomb, not a mosque or minaret.

- **A (incorrect):** Balban's tomb belongs to the later Balban phase; Sultan Ghari, not Balban's tomb, commemorates Iltutmish's son (Basic §20).
- **B (correct):** Sultan Ghari commemorates Iltutmish's son Nasiruddin Mahmud, the prince who died before the later sultan's reign (Basic §§13, 20; Workbook MCQ 41; Atlas hard MCQ 48).
- **C (incorrect):** The first Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque phase began under Aibak, before Raziya's reign (Basic §20; Workbook MCQ 28).
- **D (incorrect):** Aibak began the Qutb Minar and Iltutmish continued it; it was not initiated by Balban (Basic §20; Workbook MCQ 29).

**Trap:** Identify patron, initial phase and subsequent alterations separately.

### R1-25 — D

**Answer: D.** Proximity in time and narrative genre distinguish the four writings.

- **A (incorrect):** Hasan Nizami's *Taj al-Ma'asir* is a relevant early conquest chronicle, but principally serves the earlier Aibak milieu (Basic §9.2; Atlas hard MCQ 03).
- **B (incorrect):** Fakhr-i Mudabbir's military-political advice is prescriptive rather than Minhaj's dynastic account of Raziya's court (Workbook 2020 Persian-sources Mains model answer; Basic §9.2).
- **C (incorrect):** Barani's *Tarikh-i Firuz Shahi* is retrospective, written later and shaped by aristocratic political concerns (Basic §9.2; Advanced §5; Atlas hard MCQ 04).
- **D (correct):** Minhaj-i Siraj's *Tabaqat-i Nasiri* is a near-contemporary account of Iltutmish, Raziya, nobles and succession (Basic §§9.2, 13; Workbook MCQ 07; Atlas hard MCQ 03).

**Trap:** A near-contemporary court text still requires scrutiny of its patronage and gendered judgements.

### R1-26 — B

**Answer: B.** The correspondence/chancery vocabulary differs from finance, army and stables.

- **A (incorrect):** *Wizarat* concerns finance under the wazir rather than the chancery (Basic §17.2; Workbook MCQ 25).
- **B (correct):** *Diwan-i-insha* is the royal correspondence office (Basic §17.2; Workbook MCQ 25; Atlas hard MCQ 41).
- **C (incorrect):** *Diwan-i-arz* concerns military administration, not official letters (Basic §17.2; Workbook MCQ 26; Atlas hard MCQ 42).
- **D (incorrect):** *Amir-i-akhur* means master of royal stables, the office associated with Yaqut (Basic §13; Workbook MCQ 02).

**Trap:** These are Sultanate-era terms, but the fully developed later diwan system should not be projected unchanged into Aibak's reign (Basic §17.2).

### R1-27 — D

**Answer: D.** Attribution of the mosque phase does not establish a single origin for all its fabric.

- **A (incorrect):** Balban's Delhi court belongs to a different setting from this Ajmer mosque (Basic §§15, 20).
- **B (incorrect):** Iltutmish's own Delhi tomb is not the early mosque at Ajmer (Basic §20).
- **C (incorrect):** Aibak is also associated with Delhi's Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque, but that mosque is not the Ajmer monument named here (Basic §20; Workbook MCQ 28).
- **D (correct):** The Ajmer mosque's early phase is associated with Aibak and incorporates earlier material (Basic §20; Workbook MCQ 30; Atlas hard MCQ 46).

**Trap:** Spolia/reuse is material evidence; one cannot infer every patron's motive or all construction dates from it alone.

### R1-28 — B

**Answer: B.** Chishti teachers had religious authority distinct from officials and chroniclers.

- **A (incorrect):** Minhaj and Hasan Nizami wrote court histories; they are not the requested Delhi–Punjab Chishti pair (Basic §§9.2, 19).
- **B (correct):** Qutb al-Din Bakhtiyar Kaki and Baba Farid exemplify early Delhi/Punjab Sufi networks (Basic §19).
- **C (incorrect):** Yaqut and Raihan were associated with court office and faction, not this Sufi teaching network (Basic §§13–15).
- **D (incorrect):** Yildiz and Qubacha were post-Ghurid rivals in the Ghazni and Multan–Uch theatres (Basic §§10, 12.1).

**Trap:** Neither Sufi authority nor *ulama* scholarship amounted to uniform control of royal policy (Basic §19).

### R1-29 — D

**Answer: D.** The same name refers to different individuals in separate episodes.

- **A (incorrect):** Balban's son Muhammad died on the Mongol frontier; Sultan Ghari's occupant was Iltutmish's son (Basic §§15.2, 20).
- **B (incorrect):** Iltutmish has a separate tomb; Sultan Ghari commemorates his son rather than the reigning father (Basic §20).
- **C (incorrect):** Sultan Ghari predates Nasiruddin Mahmud's later reign; its patron was Iltutmish, not the later sultan (Basic §§13, 20).
- **D (correct):** Sultan Ghari commemorates Iltutmish's deceased son Nasiruddin Mahmud; another Nasiruddin Mahmud later reigned while Balban rose as *naib* (Basic §§13, 20; Workbook MCQ 41; Atlas hard MCQ 48).

**Trap:** An undated occurrence of the shared name cannot identify the later sultan as the tomb's occupant.

### R1-30 — B

**Answer: B.** A military-fiscal review and frontier diplomacy served different purposes, and neither ensured a permanent settlement.

- **A (incorrect):** Balban questioned inherited retention without service; contacts did not replace Beas-line defence (Basic §§15.2, 17.2; Workbook MCQs 15, 18–19; OCR p.97).
- **B (correct):** Review aimed to reconnect village-revenue grants with military duty; Ilkhanid contacts helped manage the Mongol frontier alongside forts and troops (Basic §15.2; Workbook MCQs 15, 19; Atlas hard MCQs 31, 35; OCR pp.93, 97–98).
- **C (incorrect):** The inquiry did not abolish the *iqta* system, and diplomacy did not restore uncontested Delhi rule throughout western Punjab (Basic §§15.2, 17.2; Workbook MCQs 15, 18–19).
- **D (incorrect):** The review concerned soldiers' service grants, not Bengal governors; Mongol-facing defence stayed with Balban's commanders and garrisons (Basic §15.2; Workbook MCQs 15, 18–19).

**Trap:** Ilkhanid diplomatic contact did not end local Mongol raids; the service inquiry did not automatically prevail over vested interests (Workbook MCQs 15, 19; OCR pp.97–98).

### R1-31 — C

**Answer: C.** Aibak died after a fall while playing *changan* (polo); his brief reign was followed by a contested accession.

- **A (incorrect):** Iltutmish's death opened the Ruknuddin–Raziya contest, not a recorded *changan* accident (Basic §§11–13; OCR pp.88–89).
- **B (incorrect):** Aram Shah was displaced by Iltutmish, not recorded as dying from a polo fall (Basic §§11–12; OCR p.88).
- **C (correct):** Aibak's fall while playing *changan* preceded Aram Shah's contested succession and Iltutmish's accession (Basic §§11–12; Advanced mini-timeline; OCR p.88).
- **D (incorrect):** Ruknuddin's deposition brought Raziya to power; Aibak is the ruler associated with the *changan* fatality (Basic §§11, 13; OCR pp.88–89).

**Trap:** The sport is polo; distinguish the death from the later contest between Aram Shah and Iltutmish.

### R1-32 — A

**Answer: A.** Public presence and military command were instruments of Raziya's claim to govern in her own right.

- **A (correct):** Raziya appeared unveiled, led campaigns and tried to form a loyal base beyond dominant Turkish factions (Basic §13; Advanced §§1, 4; OCR p.91).
- **B (incorrect):** Contemporary narrative has Raziya holding public court and leading forces, rather than accepting seclusion and exclusive noble command (Basic §13; OCR p.91).
- **C (incorrect):** Her elevation of Yaqut and direct command challenged the Shamsi elite; she did not simply transfer all military leadership to it (Basic §§13–14; Advanced §1).
- **D (incorrect):** She held public court and led military operations herself; provincial rivals could nevertheless contest her authority (Basic §13; OCR pp.90–91).

**Trap:** Neither her public leadership nor Iltutmish's nomination made faction and gender barriers disappear.

### R1-33 — D

**Answer: D.** Altunia's revolt and Raziya's subsequent attempt to recover power belong to the same crisis, not a simplified romance narrative.

- **A (incorrect):** Iltutmish nominated Raziya before the later Tabarhinda uprising; Altunia did not contest that nomination as Delhi's army commander (Basic §13; OCR pp.89–91).
- **B (incorrect):** Tughril was Balban's later Bengal rebel; Altunia belonged to the Tabarhinda–Raziya contest (Basic §§13, 15.2; OCR pp.91, 99–100).
- **C (incorrect):** Yaqut, not Altunia, held the *amir-i-akhur* stables office; Altunia's revolt involved provincial military power (Basic §13; Workbook MCQs 02–04; OCR p.91).
- **D (correct):** Raziya was held at Tabarhinda after revolt; she then allied with Altunia in a renewed bid for Delhi (Basic §13; OCR p.91).

**Trap:** The later alliance does not erase Altunia's earlier revolt or explain her fall by romance alone.

### R1-34 — C

**Answer: C.** The shadow-of-God idiom exalted royal standing; it was not a literal claim to divinity or proof of automatic obedience.

- **A (incorrect):** *Ulama* provided legal/religious expertise but did not bestow Balban's crown through a caliphal vote (Basic §§15.1, 19; Workbook MCQ 10).
- **B (incorrect):** Balban asserted the sultan's superiority over noble peers, not a *muqti* collective's authority to appoint him (Basic §§15.1, 17.2; Advanced §§1–2).
- **C (correct):** *Zill-i ilahi* (“shadow of God”) cast the sultan as exalted above nobles within Persianate-Islamic kingship (Basic §15.1; Advanced §§2, 4; Workbook MCQ 10).
- **D (incorrect):** Balban strengthened the sultan's command and prestige rather than resting kingship entirely on Shamsi noble equality (Basic §15.1; Advanced §1).

**Trap:** Political theology and court ritual reinforced, but did not create from nothing, Balban's authority.

### R1-35 — A

**Answer: A.** Later exemplary justice narratives illuminate Balban's projected image without documenting identical treatment everywhere.

- **A (correct):** Barani's selected stories of punished officers reflect stern royal-justice ideals and episodes, not a universal judicial census (Basic §§9.2, 15.2; Workbook MCQ 14; OCR p.93).
- **B (incorrect):** A later chronicler's selection is not a record of every *qazi*'s judgments in each province (Basic §§9.2, 17.2; Workbook MCQ 14).
- **C (incorrect):** The stories specifically concern Balban punishing high officers rather than granting them blanket immunity (Basic §15.2; Workbook MCQ 14; OCR p.93).
- **D (incorrect):** Barani's anecdotes about discipline are not confined to the Mongol frontier; the court also projected justice within its realm (Basic §§15.2, 17.2; Workbook MCQ 14).

**Trap:** A selected later court anecdote is evidence of royal aspiration and particular punishment, not of uniformly impartial justice.

### R1-36 — C

**Answer: C.** Rajasthan's forts illustrate gradations between asserted suzerainty and effective district-level control.

- **A (incorrect):** Delhi undertook expeditions and sought submission at Rajput forts; coin circulation alone is not the whole relationship (Basic §§12.1, 17.1; OCR pp.89, 101–102).
- **B (incorrect):** A siege or tribute did not invariably create continuous garrisons and centrally collected revenue (Basic §§12.1, 17.1; Workbook MCQ 43).
- **C (correct):** Iltutmish addressed Rajput forts in phases; claim, campaign, tribute and direct control must not be treated as identical (Basic §§12.1, 17.1; Advanced §4; OCR p.89).
- **D (incorrect):** Ranthambore, Gwalior, Bayana and Ajmer did enter early Delhi's contest or claims, though control varied (Basic §§12.1, 17.1; OCR pp.89, 101–102).

**Trap:** OCR p.102 records Balban's failed attempt at Ranthambore/Gwalior: a coloured map cannot imply uniform continued occupation.

### R1-37 — A

**Answer: A.** The denomination and metal both matter, but material circulation does not prove complete monetisation.

- **A (correct):** Silver *tanka* and copper *jital* are the coinage anchors linked to Iltutmish, while economic practice stayed mixed (Basic §§9.2, 12.2, 18; Advanced §2; Workbook MCQ 38).
- **B (incorrect):** It swaps the metals of the tanka and jital (Basic §12.2; Workbook MCQ 38).
- **C (incorrect):** These coins were not restricted to court gifts: mint and circulation were wider indicators of monetary and sovereignty claims (Basic §§9.2, 12.2, 18; Atlas hard MCQ 13).
- **D (incorrect):** The same coinage pair cannot by itself prove direct tax control over every frontier village (Basic §§9.2, 17.1, 18; Workbook MCQ 39).

**Trap:** Distinguish denomination, political claim and the limits of a coin find.

### R1-38 — D

**Answer: D.** Investiture recognised established power; it was neither Baghdad's administration nor the source of Iltutmish's military victories.

- **A (incorrect):** Caliphal endorsement did not turn Delhi into an Abbasid-administered province (Basic §12.2; Atlas hard MCQ 11).
- **B (incorrect):** Iltutmish displaced Aram Shah before, not because of, later investiture (Basic §§11–12.2; Advanced §2).
- **C (incorrect):** Iltutmish defeated Yildiz at Tarain before receiving caliphal endorsement; the caliph supplied no Delhi army (Basic §§12.1–12.2; Workbook MCQ 38).
- **D (correct):** Recognition followed Iltutmish's own consolidation and enhanced legitimacy without constituting the power recognised (Basic §12.2; Advanced §§2, 5; Atlas hard MCQ 11).

**Trap:** Political symbolism was consequential, but investiture did not make the caliph an operational sovereign in Delhi.

### R1-39 — A

**Answer: A.** The Minar is a phased monument, not an achievement of one reign.

- **A (correct):** Aibak initiated the monument; Iltutmish added its major subsequent storeys; later rulers repaired or altered it (Basic §20; Advanced §4; Workbook MCQ 29).
- **B (incorrect):** Iltutmish's role included major construction, not merely superficial repairs to an Aibak-completed tower (Basic §20; Workbook MCQ 29).
- **C (incorrect):** Iltutmish continued a building begun by Aibak, and Balban was not responsible for its major storeys (Basic §20; Workbook MCQ 29).
- **D (incorrect):** Both Balban and Qaiqabad ruled after the Aibak–Iltutmish main construction phases (Basic §§16, 20; Workbook MCQ 29).

**Trap:** Separate initial construction from later repairs and do not assign an entire surviving monument to one patron.

### R1-40 — C

**Answer: C.** The attribution concerns a significant surviving structural phase, not the global invention of the arch.

- **A (incorrect):** An important true-arch example cannot establish that no earlier arches or arcuate forms existed anywhere in India (Basic §20; Workbook MCQ 42; Atlas hard MCQ 47).
- **B (incorrect):** The original dome is not a fully intact surviving dome that proves a complete tomb programme (Basic §20; Atlas hard MCQ 47).
- **C (correct):** Balban's tomb is associated with an important surviving true-arch phase; an absolute “first in India” requires separate architectural evidence (Basic §20; Advanced §6; Workbook MCQ 42; Atlas hard MCQ 47).
- **D (incorrect):** Balban's tomb is a later monument than Aibak's conquest-era Quwwat-ul-Islam, regardless of building-material reuse elsewhere (Basic §§11, 20; Workbook MCQs 28, 42).

**Trap:** “Early/surviving true arch” is narrower than “the first arch ever known in India.”

### R1-41 — B

**Answer: B.** Institutional and spiritual/religious influence had different social bases.

- **A (incorrect):** *Qazis* did not command every Chishti establishment, and Sufi identity is not a category of military *iqta* tenure (Basic §§17.2, 19).
- **B (correct):** A *qazi* exercised judicial/legal expertise; Chishti teachers such as Kaki and Baba Farid developed distinct Sufi authority (Basic §§17.2, 19).
- **C (incorrect):** *Amir-i-akhur* oversaw stables, whereas caliphal investiture was distinct from Chishti teaching (Basic §§12.2, 13, 19).
- **D (incorrect):** *Barids* reported intelligence; local revenue was not administered exclusively through Chishti teachers (Basic §§17.2, 19).

**Trap:** *Ulama*, judiciary and Sufi networks influenced the state but were not a single unified ruling office.

### R1-42 — D

**Answer: D.** Reused fabric records both appropriation and the participation of Indian craftspeople.

- **A (incorrect):** Earlier material was incorporated in a new mosque phase, not simply left in continuous temple use (Basic §20; Workbook MCQ 30).
- **B (incorrect):** Indian masons and artisans worked on early Sultanate structures, even where new forms and reused elements were involved (Basic §20; Atlas hard MCQ 46).
- **C (incorrect):** An earlier component does not date all phases of a reused structure or prove a single patron/motive for them (Basic §§9.2, 20; Workbook MCQ 30).
- **D (correct):** Quwwat-ul-Islam's reused temple fabric bears witness to material appropriation and local craftsmanship within phased building (Basic §20; Workbook MCQs 28–30; Atlas hard MCQ 46).

**Trap:** Neither a purely harmonious borrowing nor a denial of technical collaboration accounts for all the material evidence.

### R1-43 — C

**Answer: C.** Dynastic failure and the continuity of Delhi-centred state institutions must be distinguished.

- **A (incorrect):** Prince Muhammad was killed fighting Mongols, and Bughra preferred Bengal over Delhi; no Bughra Delhi kingship followed (Basic §§15.2–16; OCR pp.94, 103).
- **B (incorrect):** Bughra remained in Bengal; Qaiqabad's Delhi court inherited *iqtas* and military offices rather than simply extinguishing them (Basic §§16–17.2; Workbook MCQ 44).
- **C (correct):** The chosen heir's death, Bughra Khan's Bengal preference and faction-ridden Qaiqabad rule preceded Khalji ascendancy without dissolving the Delhi Sultanate (Basic §§15.2–16, 21; Workbook MCQ 44; OCR pp.103–104).
- **D (incorrect):** It reverses the locations of Bughra and Qaiqabad; Bughra was in Bengal and Qaiqabad reigned at Delhi (Basic §16; OCR p.103).

**Trap:** The 1290 Khalji takeover ended Mamluk primacy, not Delhi's inherited administration.

### R1-44 — A

**Answer: A.** Treat the vivid phrase as a historian's label and name the actual policies.

- **A (correct):** “Blood and iron” is later shorthand for Balban's coercion in Mewat/doab/Katehar and his security stance, not his recorded royal slogan (Basic §15.2; Advanced §5; Workbook MCQ 12).
- **B (incorrect):** The label was not an attested Balban court formula, and coercion was not confined to the Beas frontier (Basic §15.2; Workbook MCQ 12).
- **C (incorrect):** Neither hereditary *iqta* ownership nor a Bengal fiscal term is what the shorthand denotes (Basic §§15.2, 17.2; Workbook MCQs 12, 23).
- **D (incorrect):** The phrase describes punitive action, not a recorded promise to abandon it for general amnesty (Basic §15.2; OCR pp.93–94, 101–102).

**Trap:** Avoid presenting a later explanatory phrase as a quotation from Balban.

### R1-45 — D

**Answer: D.** Balban reorganised an existing military-administrative function; he did not invent all early Delhi offices.

- **A (incorrect):** *Diwan-i-wizarat* belonged to finance under the wazir, not army administration (Basic §17.2; Workbook MCQs 25–26).
- **B (incorrect):** *Diwan-i-insha* handled the ruler's correspondence; it was not the military office (Basic §17.2; Workbook MCQs 25–26).
- **C (incorrect):** *Amir-i-akhur* supervised royal stables, as in the Yaqut episode, rather than departmental military musters (Basic §§13, 17.2; Workbook MCQs 02, 26).
- **D (correct):** *Diwan-i-arz* and the *ariz* are associated with military administration, which Balban reorganised to strengthen the army (Basic §§15.2, 17.2; Advanced §4; Workbook MCQ 26; OCR p.93).

**Trap:** Do not substitute the later Mughal *Mir Bakshi*, or assume a mature later diwan system already existed in Aibak's form.

### R1-46 — C

**Answer: C.** These reigns followed Raziya in sequence while noble rivalry continued.

- **A (incorrect):** Nasiruddin Mahmud's long reign followed, not preceded, Bahram and Masud (Basic §13; OCR pp.91–92).
- **B (incorrect):** Bahram Shah preceded Ala al-Din Masud, not the reverse (Basic §13; OCR pp.91–92).
- **C (correct):** Bahram Shah was followed by Ala al-Din Masud and then Nasiruddin Mahmud, under whom Balban rose (Basic §§13–15; OCR pp.91–92).
- **D (incorrect):** Nasiruddin Mahmud succeeded Masud, so swapping those two reverses the order (Basic §13; OCR pp.91–92).

**Trap:** The sultan Nasiruddin Mahmud is not Iltutmish's earlier prince commemorated at Sultan Ghari (Basic §§13, 20).

### R1-47 — A

**Answer: A.** Two different Delhi tombs commemorate Iltutmish's son and Iltutmish himself.

- **A (correct):** Iltutmish commissioned Sultan Ghari for his son Nasiruddin Mahmud; Iltutmish's own Delhi tomb is a distinct funerary structure (Basic §20; Workbook MCQ 41).
- **B (incorrect):** Balban's tomb belongs to a later sultan, while Sultan Ghari is the son's, not the father's, tomb (Basic §§15–16, 20; Workbook MCQs 41–42).
- **C (incorrect):** Iltutmish's tomb commemorates Iltutmish, not his son; Balban's tomb belongs to Balban (Basic §20; Workbook MCQs 41–42).
- **D (incorrect):** Arhai Din ka Jhonpra is an Ajmer mosque, not the prince's Delhi tomb; Sultan Ghari commemorates the prince (Basic §20; Workbook MCQ 41).

**Trap:** Distinguish Iltutmish the patron of his son's mausoleum from Iltutmish the occupant of his own tomb.
