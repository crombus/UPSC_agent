### R3-31 — C

**Answer: C.** Aibak's fatal fall occurred while playing *changan* (also transliterated *chaugan*), the mounted game of polo, not in battle.

- **A (incorrect):** Injuries from a fall during a cavalry inspection — A fall is attested, but the activity was polo, not an army inspection. ([B03] §11; [OCR03] physical p.88)
- **B (incorrect):** Illness contracted while holding court in Lahore — Lahore was an important royal centre; the recorded cause was injury from a fall, not illness. ([B03] §11; [OCR03] physical p.88)
- **C (correct):** Injuries from a fall while playing changan (polo) — Both the Basic owner and the OCR history link the fatal fall to polo; spellings vary. ([B03] §11; [OCR03] physical p.88)
- **D (incorrect):** Wounds received during a siege of an Indus fort — Do not transfer Aibak's death to campaigns around Multan and Uch under Iltutmish. ([B03] §§11–12; [OCR03] physical p.88)

**Examiner trap:** Lahore as royal centre is not the *cause* of death; the exam discriminator is the mounted sport, not a speculative accident site.
**Sources:** [B03] §§10–11; [A03] §4; [OCR03] physical p.88 (prints *chaugen* for the sport).

### R3-32 — D

**Answer: D.** Razia personally led an expedition against Lahore; Altunia's revolt occurred at Tabarhinda and fed the coalition that toppled her.

- **A (incorrect):** She led troops towards Bengal; Altunia rose at Tabarhinda — The Tabarhinda theatre is right, but the documented personal march here was towards Lahore, not Bengal. ([B03] §13; [OCR03] physical p.91)
- **B (incorrect):** She led troops towards Lahore; Altunia rose at Lakhnauti — Lahore is right; Lakhnauti belongs to Bengal's political theatre, not Altunia's revolt. ([B03] §§12.1,13; [OCR03] physical p.91)
- **C (incorrect):** She led troops towards Ajmer; Altunia rose at Lakhnauti — Neither location gives the specified Lahore expedition–Tabarhinda revolt pairing. ([B03] §13; [OCR03] physical p.91)
- **D (correct):** She led troops towards Lahore; Altunia rose at Tabarhinda — This pairs Razia's own military leadership with the correct location of Altunia's revolt without relying on later romance. ([B03] §13; [OCR03] physical p.91)

**Examiner trap:** Razia was an active commander, not merely the object of noble politics; separate Lahore, Tabarhinda and Bengal.
**Sources:** [B03] §13; [A03] §§1,4–5; [OCR03] physical p.91.

### R3-33 — B

**Answer: B.** The shadow-of-God idiom sacralised the sultan's status and challenged the idea that powerful nobles were his equals.

- **A (incorrect):** A ruler who exercised authority only as the caliph's tax officer — The kingship claim exalted Delhi's monarch; it did not reduce him to a revenue delegate of Baghdad. ([B03] §§12.2,15.1; [A03] §2)
- **B (correct):** A sacralised kingship set above the parity claimed by nobles — Balban's reported shadow-of-God idiom and court deference reinforced hierarchical monarchy. ([B03] §15.1; [A03] §2)
- **C (incorrect):** A sovereign elected by every military officer holding an iqta — Iqta service did not create a general electorate empowered to appoint the ruler. ([B03] §§15.1,17.2; [A03] §2)
- **D (incorrect):** A judicial office holding power apart from the Delhi monarchy — Royal ideology concerns the sultan's authority, not a separate judicial magistracy. ([B03] §§15.1,17.2; [A03] §2)

**Examiner trap:** *Zill-i ilahi* means a legitimation idiom, not literal godhood or evidence of a functional caliphal bureaucracy at Delhi.
**Sources:** [B03] §15.1; [A03] §§1–2; [OCR03] physical pp.93–94 for Balban's court hierarchy and ceremony.

### R3-34 — A

**Answer: A.** The chronicled punishment of high officers' relatives illustrates Balban's claim to impartial discipline; exemplary cases cannot establish uniform day-to-day justice.

- **A (correct):** As exemplary royal punishment, not proof of uniform justice everywhere — The named punishments project royal impartiality, but the courtly narrative is selective. ([B03] §§9.2,15.2; [OCR03] physical p.93; [W03] MCQ 14)
- **B (incorrect):** As evidence that provincial qazis lost every judicial role in practice — Balban's royal punishments do not abolish the qazi or the multiple levels of adjudication. ([B03] §§15.2,17.2; [W03] MCQ 14)
- **C (incorrect):** As evidence that noble households could never retain royal offices — Punishing members of elite households is not evidence that their offices vanished altogether. ([B03] §§14–15.2; [OCR03] physical p.93)
- **D (incorrect):** As proof that all frontier provinces had identical court procedures — A few exemplary incidents near the court do not document identical provincial practice. ([B03] §§9.2,15.2,17.1; [W03] MCQ 14)

**Examiner trap:** Do not promote a chronicler's exemplary royal-justice stories into a claim of fully uniform, modern equality before law.
**Sources:** [B03] §§9.2,15.2,17.2; [OCR03] physical p.93 (relatives of the Badaun and Awadh governors); [W03] MCQ 14.

### R3-35 — C

**Answer: C.** The western Rajput forts and Ranthambore were approached in phases: recovery around Gwalior/Bayana, expeditions asserting suzerainty at Ranthambore.

- **A (incorrect):** Gwalior and Bayana were outside the campaigns; Ranthambore was annexed — The western forts were addressed; a Ranthambore expedition is not proof of permanent annexation. ([B03] §§12.1,17.1; [OCR03] physical p.89)
- **B (incorrect):** Gwalior and Bayana saw recovery drives; Ranthambore was fully audited — Recovery is supported, but an expedition does not establish audited local revenue collection. ([B03] §§12.1,17.1; [OCR03] physical p.89)
- **C (correct):** Gwalior and Bayana saw recovery drives; Ranthambore drew a suzerainty expedition — The book differentiates recovering Gwalior/Bayana from expeditions to reassert suzerainty at Ranthambore. ([B03] §§12.1,17.1; [OCR03] physical p.89)
- **D (incorrect):** Gwalior and Bayana were held by Yildiz; Ranthambore paid regular Delhi revenue — Yildiz's Ghazni claim is a different theatre; a regular Ranthambore revenue regime is not established. ([B03] §§12.1,17.1; [OCR03] physical pp.88–89)

**Examiner trap:** Fort recovery, expedition and suzerainty are not interchangeable with continuous district-level fiscal control.
**Sources:** [B03] §§12.1,17.1; [A03] §§1,4; [OCR03] physical pp.88–89.

### R3-36 — A

**Answer: A.** The jital was copper, while caliphal investiture later affirmed Iltutmish's established political position rather than supplying his army.

- **A (correct):** Copper jital; investiture recognised an already established Delhi ruler — Numismatic and diplomatic legitimacy reinforced a state built through prior consolidation. ([B03] §§9.2,12.2; [AT03] hard MCQ 11; [W03] MCQ 38)
- **B (incorrect):** Copper jital; investiture placed Delhi's army under a caliphal commander — The metal is right; investiture did not place Delhi's army under Baghdad's operational command. ([B03] §12.2; [AT03] hard MCQ 11)
- **C (incorrect):** Silver jital; investiture supplied the troops that overcame Yildiz — Silver identifies the tanka; the victory at Tarain preceded recognition and used Delhi's own resources. ([B03] §12.2; [AT03] hard MCQ 11; [W03] MCQ 38)
- **D (incorrect):** Silver jital; investiture administered Bengal's revenue from Baghdad — The denomination and the claimed caliphal fiscal control are both wrong. ([B03] §§12.2,17.1; [W03] MCQ 38)

**Examiner trap:** Do not swap silver *tanka* and copper *jital* or treat symbolic investiture as the source of Iltutmish's military sovereignty.
**Sources:** [B03] §12.2; [A03] §§2,5; [AT03] hard MCQ 11; [W03] MCQ 38.

### R3-37 — D

**Answer: D.** Aibak began and raised the first storey; Iltutmish added storeys; later rulers repaired or altered the minar.

- **A (incorrect):** Aibak's first storey; Iltutmish's repairs; Balban's added storeys — It swaps Iltutmish's principal extension for undocumented attribution of added storeys to Balban. ([B03] §20; [W03] MCQ 29)
- **B (incorrect):** Aibak's first storey; Balban's added storeys; later repairs — It moves Iltutmish's early storeys to Balban's reign. ([B03] §20; [W03] MCQ 29)
- **C (incorrect):** Aibak's first storey; Razia's added storeys; later repairs — It moves Iltutmish's early storeys to Razia's reign. ([B03] §20; [W03] MCQ 29)
- **D (correct):** Aibak's first storey; Iltutmish's added storeys; later repairs — The first storey, extensions and subsequent repairs represent distinct phases. ([B03] §20; [W03] MCQ 29)

**Examiner trap:** Early initiation and completion are distinct from later conservation or repair; do not give all minar storeys to one patron.
**Sources:** [B03] §20; [W03] MCQ 29; [AT03] hard MCQ 45.

### R3-38 — B

**Answer: B.** Balban's tomb is associated with an important phase of true-arch building, not proof of the first Indian arch of any form.

- **A (incorrect):** A true-arch phase, proving it India's first arch of any kind — The observed structural form does not justify an unqualified priority claim about all earlier arches. ([B03] §20; [W03] MCQ 42)
- **B (correct):** An important true-arch phase, but no absolute first claim — This states the material significance while leaving priority claims to comparative evidence. ([B03] §20; [W03] MCQ 42)
- **C (incorrect):** A corbelled-arch phase, with true arches arising only after Balban — A true arch and a corbelled approximation differ structurally; the monument is important for the former. ([B03] §20; [W03] MCQ 42)
- **D (incorrect):** A true-arch phase, proving no Indian craft entered its construction — Construction evidence does not establish that local masons were absent from early Delhi's building processes. ([B03] §20; [W03] MCQ 42)

**Examiner trap:** A qualified true-arch attribution is not an unqualified "first in India" or proof of an exclusively foreign workforce.
**Sources:** [B03] §20; [A03] §6; [W03] MCQ 42.
