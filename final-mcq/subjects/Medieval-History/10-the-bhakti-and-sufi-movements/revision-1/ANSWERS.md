# Revision 1 — Answers and option-level rationales

First original tranche; status `in_progress`. Page references identify PDF page indices (OCR), not printed pagination.

### Q1. C
**Source:** Basic owner §17; Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR p. 199.
- **A:** A silsilah is an order/lineage; a khanqah is its lodge, not the requested legal-observance contrast.
- **B:** Sama is musical audition and dhikr is remembrance; neither labels the two legal-observance groups.
- **C:** Ba-shara denotes law-observing and be-shara those outside that observance in this typology.
- **D:** Pir and murid distinguish guide and disciple, not the two groups.
**Examiner trap:** A lineage, lodge and master–disciple bond are different classifications.

### Q2. A
**Source:** Basic owner §§18–19; Chandra, OCR pp. 200–201; *Medieval India: From Sultanat to the Mughals*, Part I, OCR pp. 121–122.
- **A:** Ajmer, Delhi and Ajodhan match Muinuddin, Kaki and Farid respectively.
- **B:** The Delhi/Multan/Ajmer sequence misplaces all three; Multan is associated here with Zakariya.
- **C:** Ajmer fits Muinuddin, but Ajodhan belongs to Farid, not Kaki; Delhi fits Kaki.
- **D:** Multan does not fit Muinuddin; that centre is Zakariya's Suhrawardi field.
**Examiner trap:** Do not exchange Farid's Ajodhan with Zakariya's Multan.

### Q3. D
**Source:** Basic owner §§18–20; Part I, OCR pp. 121, 123–124.
- **A:** Ajmer is Muinuddin's centre; Zakariya did accept royal grants.
- **B:** Delhi was a major Chishti centre, not the location that identifies Zakariya.
- **C:** Ajodhan is associated with Farid; the option also wrongly assigns it to Zakariya.
- **D:** Zakariya's Suhrawardi base was Multan and he accepted royal grants.
**Examiner trap:** Patronage rules differed within the Sufi field.

### Q4. B
**Source:** Basic owner §§11–12; Chandra, OCR p. 201.
- **A:** The Shaiva/Vaishnava assignments are reversed.
- **B:** Tamil Alvars are Vaishnava and Nayanars Shaiva devotional poet-saints.
- **C:** Nayanars are Shaiva, not a Buddhist category.
- **D:** Neither name identifies a Persian-speaking Sufi lineage.
**Examiner trap:** Reverse-order labels disguise a simple denominational distinction.

### Q5. A
**Source:** Basic owner §12, teacher/doctrine table; Advanced owner, philosophical distinctions.
- **A:** Each of the three teacher–Vedanta pairings is correct.
- **B:** Ramanuja and Madhva's doctrines have been exchanged.
- **C:** Madhva and Nimbarka's doctrines have been exchanged.
- **D:** Ramanuja and Nimbarka's doctrines have been exchanged.
**Examiner trap:** Do not collapse three different Vaishnava Vedanta positions.

### Q6. C
**Source:** Basic owner §§12–13, 15; Chandra, OCR pp. 202–203.
- **A:** Kabir criticised reliance on ritual forms, rather than prescribing one exclusive ritual language.
- **B:** A modern legislative abolition programme cannot be inferred from Kabir's ethical criticism.
- **C:** His verses criticise external ritual and rank-based pride while privileging devotion.
- **D:** Household and artisanal settings are compatible with his teaching; monastic withdrawal was not mandatory.
**Examiner trap:** Ethical challenge is not proof of a modern legislative programme.

### Q7. B
**Source:** Basic owner §14; Chandra, OCR pp. 204–205.
- **A:** Nanak did not require withdrawal from the household as the prerequisite to devotion.
- **B:** Work and household responsibility could coexist with remembrance and ethical devotion.
- **C:** He did not make government service the central spiritual practice.
- **D:** Congregation and everyday labour were not mutually exclusive.
**Examiner trap:** Devotion need not imply renunciation of family or livelihood.

### Q8. D
**Source:** Basic owner §§12, 16; Chandra, OCR p. 205.
- **A:** Silent dhikr belongs to a Sufi-order context, not Chaitanya's Krishna congregations.
- **B:** A Persian malfuzat is a record of Sufi conversations, not this devotional form.
- **C:** His devotional movement used, rather than prohibited, collective singing.
- **D:** Chaitanya is particularly linked with congregational sankirtan in the Bengal–Puri field.
**Examiner trap:** Collective singing is not the same practice as Sufi dhikr.

### Q9. B
**Source:** Basic owner §§11, 13, 16.
- **A:** Kashmir and *vakhs* identify Lal Ded's regional memory, not Namdev's.
- **B:** Namdev is associated with Maharashtra, Varkari/Pandharpur devotion and Marathi *abhang* performance.
- **C:** *Eka Sarana* and *namghar* institutions belong to Shankaradeva's Assamese field.
- **D:** Kannada *vachanas* belong to the Virashaiva/Lingayat field; Udupi is associated with Madhva.
**Examiner trap:** Regional language, pilgrimage centre and teacher must belong to the same devotional network.

### Q10. B
**Source:** Basic owner §§11, 15–16.
- **A:** Lal Ded is associated with Kashmiri *vakhs*, not Kannada *vachanas*.
- **B:** Akka Mahadevi is a woman devotional voice within the Kannada Virashaiva/Lingayat *vachana* world.
- **C:** Andal belongs to the earlier Tamil Vaishnava Alvar bridge.
- **D:** Mirabai belongs to a Rajasthan/Gujarat-associated Krishna-devotional corpus.
**Examiner trap:** Do not collapse women devotional authors from different regions and languages into one tradition.

### Q11. A
**Source:** Basic owner §12, Vaishnava teacher table.
- **A:** Vallabha is linked with Shuddhadvaita and the grace-centred Pushtimarg.
- **B:** Dvaita and the Udupi lineage belong to Madhva, not Ramananda.
- **C:** Madhva taught Dvaita rather than Vallabha's Shuddhadvaita.
- **D:** Nimbarka belongs to a Vaishnava Bhedabheda/Dvaitadvaita tradition, not a Sufi order.
**Examiner trap:** Similar Krishna devotion does not make Vaishnava philosophical lineages interchangeable.

### Q12. C
**Source:** Basic owner §§18–19, order-centre matrix and named chronology.
- **A:** Zakariya's Multan centre represents the Suhrawardi network.
- **B:** Baqi Billah belongs to the later Naqshbandi establishment in Delhi.
- **C:** Gesu Daraz is associated with the Chishti movement into the Deccan and Gulbarga memory.
- **D:** Muhammad Ghawth at Gwalior is associated with the Shattari order.
**Examiner trap:** Match the order, figure and centre before inferring a regional transmission.

### Q13. A
**Source:** Basic owner §§13, 15.
- **A:** Ravidas is remembered in a north Indian leather-working caste context and in teaching on spiritual dignity and caste criticism.
- **B:** Braj Krishna lyric identifies Surdas rather than Ravidas.
- **C:** Kashmiri *vakhs* belong to Lal Ded's transmitted corpus.
- **D:** Assamese *namghar* and *sattra* institutions belong to Shankaradeva's field.
**Examiner trap:** Social background, region and literary form should not be exchanged among poet-saints.

### Q14. D
**Source:** Basic owner §§13, 15–16.
- **A:** Maharashtra, Pandharpur and Varkari pilgrimage identify Namdev and related Marathi traditions.
- **B:** A compiler of Nizamuddin's conversations belongs to the Sufi malfuzat field.
- **C:** Tamil Shaiva canonical memory belongs to Nayanar traditions.
- **D:** Mirabai is associated with Rajasthan/Gujarat Krishna devotion, female devotional agency and a layered attributed poem corpus.
**Examiner trap:** A remembered female voice does not make every attributed poem a lifetime autograph.

### Q15. A
**Source:** Basic owner §§13, 16.
- **A:** Surdas is associated with Braj Krishna lyric, while the received *Sursagar* grew through transmission.
- **B:** Awadhi Rama narrative identifies Tulsidas's *Ramcharitmanas*, not Surdas.
- **C:** Marathi *abhangs* and Varkari memory belong especially to Maharashtra.
- **D:** Kashmiri *vakhs* belong to Lal Ded and also carry oral/textual attribution cautions.
**Examiner trap:** A famous corpus name does not guarantee a fixed authorial text from one lifetime.

### Q16. A
**Source:** Basic owner §§13, 16.
- **A:** The Awadhi *Ramcharitmanas* widened Rama-devotional literary participation without proving one uniform social programme.
- **B:** Naqshbandi institutional history is unrelated to Tulsidas's authorship.
- **C:** The *Bijak* belongs to a Kabir corpus rather than Tulsidas.
- **D:** Assamese performance and *sattra* networks belong to Shankaradeva.
**Examiner trap:** Vernacular literary reach and a modern reform programme are different claims.

### Q17. B
**Source:** Basic owner §13.
- **A:** Pushtimarg belongs to Vallabha's saguna Krishna-devotional lineage.
- **B:** Dadu Dayal is associated with nirguna, non-sectarian *nipakh* teaching and a later Dadu Panth.
- **C:** Ajmer Chishti service belongs to Muinuddin and later shrine history.
- **D:** Dvaita and Udupi identify Madhva's lineage.
**Examiner trap:** A saint's teaching and the later institution bearing the name require chronological distinction.

### Q18. D
**Source:** Basic owner §18, order-centre-practice matrix.
- **A:** Muinuddin at Ajmer identifies the Chishti order.
- **B:** Zakariya at Multan identifies the Suhrawardi order.
- **C:** Muhammad Ghawth at Gwalior identifies the Shattari field.
- **D:** The Qadiri order developed important later-medieval/early-modern north Indian and Mughal-era connections, including Punjab/Lahore networks.
**Examiner trap:** Later Mughal links do not make every Qadiri centre politically identical.

### Q19. C
**Source:** Basic owner §17; Advanced owner §§1, 4.
- **A:** *Sama* is devotional audition and *futuh* an unsolicited contribution.
- **B:** Pir and murid are guide and disciple.
- **C:** *Fana* and *baqa* refer approximately to ego-effacement and abiding in or with God.
- **D:** Sharia is law/norm while waqf is an endowment category.
**Examiner trap:** Approximate mystical translations should not be confused with institutions or funding terms.

### Q20. C
**Source:** Basic owner §19, Baba Farid chronology note.
- **A:** Transmission of verse does not establish identical Chishti and Sikh theology.
- **B:** Later preservation does not make Farid the compiler of the final Sikh canon.
- **C:** The preservation illustrates movement of devotional verse across later language and institutional boundaries.
- **D:** Attribution in a later compilation is not proof of an autograph for every verse.
**Examiner trap:** Cross-community textual transmission does not erase distinct succession or doctrine.

### Q21. B
**Source:** Basic owner §14; Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR pp. 204–205.
- **A:** Nanak did not make monastic withdrawal or rejection of work the basis of devotion.
- **B:** Remembrance, honest work, sharing and disciplined household life belong together in the owner's summary.
- **C:** Territorial conquest and hereditary priesthood are not the core ethical programme described.
- **D:** The option substitutes taxation and withdrawal for congregation and ethical livelihood.
**Examiner trap:** A householder path is not lesser devotion or disguised monasticism.

### Q22. B
**Source:** Basic owner §§10, 14.
- **A:** The evidentiary relationship is reversed; Janamsakhis are later narratives.
- **B:** Hymns have stronger standing for teaching, while literal use of every later episode requires caution.
- **C:** Hymns and biographical narratives differ in genre and transmission.
- **D:** A later itinerary story does not automatically outrank preserved hymn evidence.
**Examiner trap:** Source genre determines what kind of claim can safely be made.

### Q23. A
**Source:** Basic owner §20.
- **A:** Khanqah support could include futuh, waqf, grants, patrons, disciples and local networks.
- **B:** Khanqah resources were not restricted to temple taxation.
- **C:** Funding varied by order and centre rather than one imperial salary system.
- **D:** Hospitality required multiple resources beyond a single pilgrim toll.
**Examiner trap:** An ethical refusal of office does not imply a resource-free institution.

### Q24. C
**Source:** Basic owner §20.
- **A:** A dargah is not produced by a sequence of military and fiscal offices.
- **B:** The option describes unrelated institutional processes.
- **C:** Burial, succession, pilgrimage, donation and later building/patronage can form a dargah.
- **D:** Musical practice does not create hereditary kingship or define shrine formation.
**Examiner trap:** Later shrine institutions should not be projected unchanged into the saint's lifetime.

### Q25. D
**Source:** Basic owner §21.
- **A:** Saints alone cannot explain varied regional conversion.
- **B:** The owner rejects a single uniform state-policy explanation.
- **C:** Later attendance does not date or measure formal conversion.
- **D:** Conversion outcomes could reflect settlement, marriage, mobility, patronage, persuasion and networks.
**Examiner trap:** Avoid both saint-only and state-only conversion models.

### Q26. B
**Source:** Advanced owner §§1–2, 5; Basic owner §22.
- **A:** The owner rejects wholly derivative, one-way borrowing.
- **B:** Symbiosis allows shared elements to receive renewed emphasis without erasing independent roots.
- **C:** South Indian Bhakti predates Chishti settlement in India.
- **D:** Similarity did not dissolve doctrinal boundaries.
**Examiner trap:** Shared vocabulary is evidence for comparison, not mechanical causation.

### Q27. A
**Source:** Basic owner §§21–22.
- **A:** Shared participation can demonstrate interaction while theology and social relations remain distinct.
- **B:** A shared site does not create one scripture or succession.
- **C:** Participation does not prove abolition of caste or gender hierarchy.
- **D:** Visiting a shrine is not equivalent to formal conversion.
**Examiner trap:** Participation, affiliation and theology are three different questions.

### Q28. C

- **B:** Social inequalities did not permanently disappear.
- **C:** Regional growth did not replace all learned-language traditions.

### Q40. C

- **A:** Later sects could develop boundaries despite access ideals.

**Examiner trap:** Avoid both triumphalist equality and cynical no-impact extremes.
