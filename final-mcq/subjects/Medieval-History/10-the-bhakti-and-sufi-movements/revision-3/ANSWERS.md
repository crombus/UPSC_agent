# Revision 3 — Answers and option-level rationales

First original tranche; status `in_progress`. Page references identify PDF page indices (OCR), not printed pagination.

### Q1. D
**Source:** Basic owner §§19–20; *Medieval India: From Sultanat to the Mughals*, Part I, OCR pp. 123–124.
- **A:** The order is reversed: a village assignment differs from an unsolicited gift.
- **B:** Sama and dhikr concern performance and remembrance, not the form of financial support.
- **C:** Murid and pir denote disciple and spiritual guide, not donations.
- **D:** Futuh denotes unsolicited gifts; an assigned village for upkeep is a different form of patronage.
**Examiner trap:** A gift and a standing revenue assignment are not equivalent.

### Q2. B
**Source:** Basic owner §§19–21; Part I, OCR p. 123.
- **A:** Zakariya's Suhrawardi grants do not exemplify an absolute Chishti prohibition of all gifts.
- **B:** Zakariya justified accepting resources partly by the capacity to assist people at his establishment.
- **C:** Funding form alone reveals nothing about the religion of each visitor.
- **D:** The cited account says Zakariya did not consider poverty indispensable to spiritual life.
**Examiner trap:** Wealth does not automatically negate a Sufi affiliation.

### Q3. C
**Source:** Basic owner §§9–10, 21; Advanced owner, source-critical distinctions.
- **A:** A second copy dependent on the same life need not be independent confirmation.
- **B:** Later shrine use cannot establish the wording of an earlier court exchange.
- **C:** Near-contemporary independent attestation would materially strengthen a verbatim claim.
- **D:** Repetition without provenance cannot establish a statement's historical wording.
**Examiner trap:** Independent near-contemporary evidence beats copies of one late story.

### Q4. A
**Source:** Basic owner §§16, 22–23; Advanced owner, convergence/theology distinction.
- **A:** Similar public forms can be compared without merging their doctrinal commitments.
- **B:** A performance overlap does not establish shared scriptures or identical conceptions of divinity.
- **C:** Neither movement was a single group with a mandatory shared repertoire.
- **D:** Music alone supplies no evidence of one institutional line of succession.
**Examiner trap:** Shared practice does not demonstrate shared theology.

### Q5. C
**Source:** Basic owner §§10–11, 13; Advanced owner, oral/textual-source qualifications.
- **A:** Kashmir's vernacular mystical verse is precisely part of the evidentiary field.
- **B:** Lal Ded belongs to a pre-Mughal Kashmiri context, not a Mughal court diary.
- **C:** Oral transmission and later textual layers complicate claims about the precise original wording.
- **D:** An authenticated lifetime autograph for the full received vakhs is not established.
**Examiner trap:** Later oral reception is not a dated autograph text.

### Q6. D
**Source:** Basic owner §§11, 13, 16; Advanced owner, regional institutional forms.
- **A:** Kashmir and Persian Chishti records do not identify Shankaradeva's Assamese network.
- **B:** Tamil Alvars and Nayanars belong to earlier southern devotional trajectories.
- **C:** Janamsakhis relate to Nanak; a Mughal mansab is a service rank, not Shankaradeva's institution.
- **D:** Shankaradeva's Assamese devotional field is associated with namghars and sattras.
**Examiner trap:** Assam's devotional institutions are not the same as its dynastic history.

### Q7. B
**Source:** Basic owner §§18–21; Advanced owner, living network versus posthumous memorial.
- **A:** A khanqah is not an iqta or village assignment; a dargah is not a decree.
- **B:** A lodge serving a living community differs from a saint's later tomb-shrine.
- **C:** Sama and dhikr name practices, not these two sites.
- **D:** Hindu monasteries and Sikh halls are not the definitions of these Sufi institutions.
**Examiner trap:** Living lodge and later tomb-shrine can occupy related but different histories.

### Q8. A
**Source:** Basic owner §§17, 19, terminology table; Advanced owner, doctrinal cautions.
- **A:** The expressions distinguish unity of being from unity of witnessing.
- **B:** This interchanges the two conventional translations.
- **C:** Royal service and communal dining name neither doctrinal expression.
- **D:** Sama and dhikr are practices, not the translations of these concepts.
**Examiner trap:** A doctrinal contrast should not be answered with practice names.

### Q9. C
**Source:** Basic owner §§20–21.
- **A:** Later ritual can preserve continuities but need not reproduce the founder's setting unchanged.
- **B:** Endowments and patronage around a later shrine do not establish that the saint held state office.
- **C:** Burial, successor management, pilgrimage, donations and architecture can create a dargah institution after the saint's death.
- **D:** A burial site does not attribute every later structure to the deceased saint.
**Examiner trap:** The history of a saint's lifetime and the later shrine institution are related but not identical.

### Q10. A
**Source:** Basic owner §21.
- **A:** The owner presents conversion as regionally variable and multi-causal, involving social, agrarian and political networks.
- **B:** Saints alone cannot account for conversion independently of wider historical processes.
- **C:** No single uniform state policy explains all regional outcomes.
- **D:** Later shared shrine attendance does not directly measure earlier conversion.
**Examiner trap:** Participation at a shrine and formal religious affiliation are different kinds of evidence.

### Q11. D
**Source:** Basic owner §20.
- **A:** Evidence of participation does not establish universal access to every formal office.
- **B:** The named roles do not make women's social position uniform across centres or periods.
- **C:** Participation in devotion, donation or service did not automatically eliminate gender hierarchy.
- **D:** The source permits several forms of participation while warning that evidence for women is uneven.
**Examiner trap:** Visible examples neither justify exclusion nor prove universal equality.

### Q12. D
**Source:** Basic owner §17, terminology table.
- **A:** The three terms are not separate religions or mutually exclusive orders.
- **B:** They do not denote fiscal or endowment categories.
- **C:** Dhikr and sama are practices, not the meanings of this three-part vocabulary.
- **D:** They refer approximately to law/norm, the disciplined path and realised truth.
**Examiner trap:** Translation-safe definitions should not turn related spiritual stages into competing religions.

### Q13. A
**Source:** Basic owner §22; Advanced owner §§1–2, 5.
- **A:** Guide–disciple comparison is useful, but lineage authority, scripture and theology remain distinct.
- **B:** Guru and pir belong to different institutional and theological settings rather than one legal office.
- **C:** Shared relational form does not produce one scripture or initiation.
- **D:** Similar vocabulary alone cannot prove the direction or mechanism of borrowing.
**Examiner trap:** Comparison is not identity, and resemblance is not proof of one-way causation.

### Q14. D
**Source:** Basic owner §§17, 22.
- **A:** Kirtan and sama do not carry identical ritual rules across every lineage.
- **B:** The institutional assignments are reversed and artificially exclusive.
- **C:** A shared musical dimension does not merge the organisations using it.
- **D:** Both can involve devotional performance, while theology and internal acceptability differ.
**Examiner trap:** Shared form should be separated from ritual authority and doctrine.

### Q15. B
**Source:** Basic owner §§15, 23.
- **A:** Later hierarchy cannot by itself erase an earlier access ideal.
- **B:** Institutionalisation can transmit inclusive memory while changing leadership and social practice.
- **C:** Remembered openness does not disprove later hereditary authority.
- **D:** Hereditary leadership does not automatically destroy every earlier text.
**Examiner trap:** Continuity of memory can coexist with institutional narrowing.

### Q16. C
**Source:** Basic owner §16, vernacularisation flow.
- **A:** The owner places oral/aural language before performance and manuscript compilation.
- **B:** Song did not automatically create fixed caste equality or end commentary.
- **C:** Performance and institutional networks helped oral idioms develop into compiled literary publics.
- **D:** Military orders and shrine conversion do not describe the literary process.
**Examiner trap:** Do not reverse the transmission sequence by placing a later canon before oral performance.

### Q17. D
**Source:** Basic owner §19.
- **A:** Preservation of verse does not turn all Chishti teaching into Sikh doctrine.
- **B:** Farid did not write the final compiled Sikh manuscript.
- **C:** Verse transmission does not merge Chishti and Sikh succession chains.
- **D:** The evidence shows cross-boundary textual movement while institutions remained distinct.
**Examiner trap:** Inclusion in a later canon is evidence of reception, not institutional identity.

### Q18. A
**Source:** Basic owner §§19, 21; Advanced owner §1.
- **A:** Ethical distance from office could increase moral prestige while rulers still sought interaction.
- **B:** Chishti ethics did not require acceptance of royal appointment.
- **C:** Nizamuddin is not described as a continuously salaried minister.
- **D:** Refusal of office could heighten, rather than eliminate, political relevance.
**Examiner trap:** Influence and office are different forms of relation to power.

### Q19. C
**Source:** Advanced owner §1; Basic owner §§18–19.
- **A:** The source explicitly rejects conversion-seeking as Muinuddin's primary object.
- **B:** His devotional and service field was not a provincial enforcement office.
- **C:** The advanced owner describes his primary object as helping Muslims live devotionally rather than pursuing conversions.
- **D:** Chishti service and counsel contradict a complete-isolation reading.
**Examiner trap:** Later conversion stories should not replace the source's stated devotional aim.

### Q20. B
**Source:** Basic owner §21.
- **A:** Patronage for a lodge does not establish endorsement of every state policy.
- **B:** It demonstrates a relationship of support, while the scope of political collaboration requires further evidence.
- **C:** Receiving patronage does not confer sovereign provincial power.
- **D:** One centre's court relation cannot be projected onto every branch of an order.
**Examiner trap:** Patronage, policy agreement and political office are separate claims.

### Q21. D
**Source:** Basic owner §14.
- **A:** Nanak did not require permanent withdrawal from household life.
- **B:** Territorial conquest is not the spiritual centre of the teaching.
- **C:** Ethical work, sharing and congregation are compatible with household responsibility.
- **D:** His teaching joins remembrance, livelihood and sharing without a modern state-founding programme.
**Examiner trap:** Do not recast a devotional community in later state-building categories.

### Q22. B
**Source:** Basic owner §§10, 14.
- **A:** A later biography is not an autograph overriding the hymns.
- **B:** Genre comparison supports stronger use of hymns for teaching and caution with literal itinerary stories.
- **C:** Narrative tradition can preserve memory even when not literal documentation.
- **D:** The two sources do not form one contemporary court chronicle.
**Examiner trap:** Critical comparison is better than automatic acceptance or total rejection.

### Q23. C
**Source:** Basic owner §14.
- **A:** Later procedures should not all be backdated unchanged.
- **B:** The practices were not simply created by Mughal court patronage.
- **C:** Community practices existed while organisation and procedure developed over time.
- **D:** Their presence does not prove caste vanished throughout society.
**Examiner trap:** Institution-building is a process, not a single founding moment.

### Q24. B
**Source:** Basic owner §§15, 23.
- **A:** Material inequality does not erase a documented spiritual claim.
- **B:** Equality before the divine and control of land, occupation or marriage are separate dimensions.
- **C:** Redistribution cannot be assumed without material evidence.
- **D:** Social contradiction does not prove absence of devotional teaching.
**Examiner trap:** Separate normative equality from measurable institutional power.

### Q25. A
**Source:** Basic owner §15.
- **A:** The comparison demonstrates varied female devotional agency under different regional conditions.
- **B:** The four figures span different regions, languages, centuries and institutions.
- **C:** Hagiographic prominence is not a census of universal authority.
- **D:** Their received corpora cannot all be treated as authenticated lifetime autographs.
**Examiner trap:** Comparative examples broaden history but do not justify universalisation.

### Q26. C
**Source:** Basic owner §16.
- **A:** Later copying can follow and preserve earlier oral performance.
- **B:** Manuscript production does not end textual or performed variation.
- **C:** Wider performance and later patronage/institutional preservation can coexist.
- **D:** Sponsorship does not make every song official doctrine.
**Examiner trap:** Circulation and canonisation are linked but distinct stages.

### Q27. D
**Source:** Basic owner §§21–22.
- **A:** Shared attendance does not establish one doctrine or scripture.
- **B:** A shared shrine does not guarantee permanent social peace.
- **C:** Attendance cannot be equated with formal conversion.
- **D:** It supports interaction while leaving theology and affiliation distinct.
**Examiner trap:** Shared sacred space is evidence of contact, not total merger.

### Q28. B
**Source:** Basic owner §§12, 19, 24.
- **A:** Sirhindi belongs later and did not instruct Nimbarka or Kabir.
- **B:** Nimbarka's Vaishnava lineage is earlier than Kabir, while Sirhindi is later than Kabir.
- **C:** Nimbarka neither followed Kabir nor founded Naqshbandi succession.
- **D:** The three are not one contemporary teacher–disciple chain.

### Q29. A
**Source:** Advanced owner §1; Basic owner §§11, 15.
- **A:** Early egalitarian access and later temple-centred hierarchy can belong to different institutional phases.
- **B:** Later hierarchy does not erase early devotional participation.
- **C:** Access ideals did not prevent all later Brahmanical authority.
- **D:** Institutional change did not eliminate the hymn traditions.
**Examiner trap:** Movements and institutions can change without making one phase fictitious.

### Q30. B
**Source:** Advanced owner §1; Basic owner §12.
- **A:** Chishti and Sikh institutions are unrelated to Ramanuja's bridging role.
- **B:** Ramanuja connected popular surrender/devotion with Vedic and Sri Vaishnava frameworks.
- **C:** Dvaita and Naqshbandi reform are separate traditions.
- **D:** Ramanuja remained within a saguna Vaishnava institutional field.
**Examiner trap:** “Bridge” describes doctrinal-social mediation, not merger of unrelated religions.

### Q31. C
**Source:** Advanced owner §§1–2, 5; Basic owner §§13, 15.
- **A:** Kabir's critique did not immediately abolish social boundaries.
- **B:** A modern legislative organisation cannot be projected from his teaching.
- **C:** His ritual and caste criticism could shape opinion without instantly transforming institutions.
- **D:** Ethical devotion, not royal power, is central to the attributed teaching.
**Examiner trap:** Powerful criticism is not identical to immediate structural revolution.

### Q32. D
**Source:** Advanced owner §§1–2, 5; Basic owner §22.
- **A:** Shared themes do not create complete theological identity.
- **B:** Earlier southern Bhakti prevents a one-way derivation model.
- **C:** The owner recognises contact and shared vocabulary.
- **D:** Symbiosis captures interaction and renewed emphasis without reducing one movement to the other.
**Examiner trap:** Avoid both total-merger and no-contact extremes.

### Q33. B

- **B:** Regional forms expanded rather than disappeared.

**Examiner trap:** Cultural integration does not require homogenisation.

### Q40. C
