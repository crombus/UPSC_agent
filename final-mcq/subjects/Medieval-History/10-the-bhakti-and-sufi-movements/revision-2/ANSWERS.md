# Revision 2 — Answers and option-level rationales

First original tranche; status `in_progress`. Page references identify PDF page indices (OCR), not printed pagination.

### Q1. B
**Source:** Basic owner §§9–10 (source-form table, *Fawa'id al-Fu'ad* entry), 19.
- **A:** The collection of conversations was compiled by a disciple, not an autograph by Nizamuddin.
- **B:** Amir Hasan Sijzi compiled Nizamuddin's conversations; compilation and transmission affect interpretation.
- **C:** A malfuzat is not an imperial order regulating a Sufi establishment.
- **D:** A Janamsakhi is a later narrative about Nanak, not this collection.
**Examiner trap:** Recorded teaching is not automatically an author's autograph.

### Q2. D
**Source:** Basic owner §§19–21; Part I, OCR pp. 123–124.
- **A:** Chishti reluctance toward grants of villages does not equal a prohibition on unsolicited gifts.
- **B:** Merchant and noble donations supported khanqahs; distance from courts was not resource-free existence.
- **C:** Regular village assignments were not the distinctive early Chishti funding preference.
- **D:** Separation from court service coexisted with acceptance of futuh, unsolicited contributions.
**Examiner trap:** Distance from the ruler is not a complete ban on donated resources.

### Q3. C
**Source:** Basic owner §§19–20; Part I, OCR p. 123.
- **A:** Occasional sama is attested; mandatory attendance for every visitor is not.
- **B:** Debate over musical audition was not confined to the Naqshbandis.
- **C:** Zakariya did not reject sama outright, but engaged in it occasionally.
- **D:** Sama refers here to musical audition, not a revenue assignment.
**Examiner trap:** "Occasional" differs from either mandatory or forbidden.

### Q4. A
**Source:** Basic owner §§9–10, 12; Advanced owner, hagiography/lineage cautions.
- **A:** A later biography records a received lineage, but not the independent date of every claimed encounter.
- **B:** A remembered discipleship list supplies no contemporary timestamp for each meeting.
- **C:** An attributed corpus is not made autograph by its appearance in a later list.
- **D:** A discipleship claim does not make distinct devotional positions doctrinally identical.
**Examiner trap:** Later hagiography cannot date every claimed lineage tie.

### Q5. D
**Source:** Basic owner §§9–10, 13; Advanced owner, manuscript/attribution cautions.

- **B:** Kabir's verse corpora have different collection and transmission histories, not one proven autograph.
- **C:** Variant recensions are pertinent to textual transmission and attribution.

**Examiner trap:** Attribution to Kabir is not proof of a lifetime autograph.

### Q6. C
**Source:** Basic owner §§10, 14; Advanced owner, source-critical cautions.
- **A:** Janamsakhis are later narratives, not an authenticated autograph diary.
- **B:** Nanak's hymns are devotional compositions, not a Delhi Sultanate court chronicle.

### Q7. A
**Source:** Basic owner §§12, 15, 22–23; Advanced owner, social-inclusion limits.
- **A:** Worship of a personal deity alone cannot establish membership rules or social equality in practice.
- **B:** Theological position does not imply the immediate abolition of caste across all communities.
- **C:** Devotional expression is not a population census or institutional membership record.
- **D:** Saguna devotion does not necessarily bar lower-caste participation either.
**Examiner trap:** Neither exclusion nor equality follows automatically from saguna/nirguna.

### Q8. B
**Source:** Basic owner §§14–16, esp. §14 on sangat/langar and historical development.
- **A:** Later procedures cannot all be projected back unchanged onto Nanak's lifetime.
- **B:** Recognising community practice alongside its subsequent organisation respects chronology.
- **C:** Sangat and food-sharing are not mutually exclusive.
- **D:** Early Sikh congregations were not confined to an imperial court.
**Examiner trap:** Do not backdate every mature institutional procedure.

### Q9. C
**Source:** Basic owner §12; Advanced owner §§1–2.
- **A:** The owner explicitly treats saguna and nirguna as useful tendencies, not sealed camps.
- **B:** Nirguna language does not automatically establish one social or political programme.
- **C:** The distinction helps classify devotional orientation, while image, caste, ritual and institutional positions require separate examination.
- **D:** Saguna devotion does not automatically establish institutional or social conservatism.
**Examiner trap:** A theology label cannot substitute for evidence about social practice.

### Q10. A
**Source:** Basic owner §§15, 23; Advanced owner §§1, 5.
- **A:** Spiritual access does not itself prove redistribution of material resources or political authority.
- **B:** Criticism of birth status can be established from the attributed teaching where the source supports it.
- **C:** Vernacular song and performance could widen access without abolishing every hierarchy.
- **D:** Later communities could preserve inclusive memory even while developing new hierarchy.
**Examiner trap:** Equality before the divine and structural redistribution are different historical claims.

### Q12. B

- **A:** Muinuddin at Ajmer is Chishti, and sankirtan belongs to a Bhakti performance field.
- **B:** Baqi Billah and Ahmad Sirhindi belong to the late-sixteenth/early-seventeenth-century Naqshbandi field, where silent dhikr was often emphasised.

- **D:** Gesu Daraz is Chishti and Shuddhadvaita belongs to Vallabha.
**Examiner trap:** Do not move later Mughal-era Naqshbandi figures into earlier Sultanate Chishti chronology.

### Q13. D
**Source:** Basic owner §§12, 15; Advanced owner §1.

- **B:** Later hierarchy does not erase the widened-access claim associated with *prapatti*.
- **C:** *Prapatti* belongs to Ramanuja's Vaishnava devotional vocabulary, not a direct Chishti borrowing.
- **D:** The teaching's access ideal and later institutional practice are separate historical questions.

### Q14. D
