## Fact and boundary controls

- Topic 21 retains the full 1657-58 succession war. Dara, Shuja and Murad appear only where accession legitimacy and sovereignty require them.
- Topic 23 retains Shivaji, the full post-1681 Deccan-Maratha war and the acute jagirdari crisis. Topic 22 uses only bounded alliance, timing and policy-variation bridges.
- Topic 25 retains the post-1707 decline narrative. Topic 22 assesses only reign-time legitimacy and alliance costs.
- Jizyah is treated as a differentiated poll tax, not land revenue or customs. Exemptions, separate treasury, corruption, crop-failure remissions and the 1704 southern suspension remain distinct.
- Temple policy separates formal old/new distinctions, the 1669 order, named Kashi/Mathura cases, wartime Marwar-Mewar destruction, grants and regional non-enforcement.
- Court singing is not converted into a claim that all music ended; naubat, instrumental and private patronage continuities are preserved.
- Continued Hindu/Rajput/Maratha recruitment is not used to erase discrimination; discrimination is not used to invent complete exclusion.
- Guru Tegh Bahadur's 1675 execution is source-qualified. The 1699 Khalsa is treated as a bounded consequence, not as mechanically caused by one event.
- Khalisa is crown-revenue administration, not automatic permanent annexation. Jaswant Singh's posthumous succession, Inder Singh, Ajit Singh, Durgadas, Mewar and the limited 1698 settlement remain separately tested.
