## Boundary and precision rules

- Topic 15 owns conquest, Rajput expansion and succession; only bounded integration prerequisites appear here.
- Topic 16 owns offices, mansab-jagir and revenue administration; service is mentioned only to distinguish it from discipleship.
- Topic 18 was not opened. Deccan policy is excluded.
- Early chronology is pilgrim tax 1563 and jizya 1564. Badauni's later narrative placement and failed attempts to undo the 1564 measure are preserved as a caution; no fiscal amount is invented.
- Ibadat Khana's Fatehpur Sikri setting, 1575 foundation, Thursday meetings and phased participation are secure. Exact archaeological identification of one standing structure remains unsettled.
- The Mahzar was signed by seven leading ulama in the bounded OCR. It allowed selection among existing juristic opinions for welfare and administration and retained the Quran/hadith or `nass` limit; practical ruler-centred effects do not make it blanket infallibility.
- Translation attributions are bureau/panel based unless a specific role is secure. Faizi's supervision of the Mahabharata project is retained; universal sole-translator claims are excluded.
- Encounter is not conversion. Ethical prescription is not uniform implementation.
- Tauhid-i-Ilahi/Din-i-Ilahi lacked a separate scripture, priesthood and congregational network, but reported initiation, `shast`, greetings and graded devotion prevent the literal claim that no practices existed.
- Blochmann's about-eighteen figure is a reconstruction of high-degree nobles, not a census. Birbal's description is confined to that list; Man Singh's refusal is used only to separate service from discipleship.
- Sulh-i-kul is inclusive imperial statecraft under sacral monarchy, not modern constitutional secularism.
