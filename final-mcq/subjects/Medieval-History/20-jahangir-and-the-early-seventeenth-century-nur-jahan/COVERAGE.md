## Precision and ownership rules

- Salim's dynastic position did not create a binding primogeniture rule; accession remained a political settlement with nobles and inherited institutions.
- Khusrau's 1606 revolt is a princely succession crisis using Punjab-Lahore routes and mixed followers, not a communal war.
- Jahangir's accusation against Guru Arjan is cited as an imperial claim. Sikh and Jesuit traditions, fine/confiscation language and the disputed punitive sequence remain separate.
- The 1606 episode is a major Mughal-Sikh turning point, not an instant creation of later militarised conflict.
- Mewar is suzerainty plus honour-sensitive accommodation, not equality, direct annexation or generosity without force.
- Bengal consolidation includes Dacca, Musa Khan, Usman Khan, selective restoration and Afghan induction; the failed Ahom attack limits the map.
- Kangra is a fort-level strategic and sovereignty success, not proof of complete Himalayan administration.
- Deccan content is restricted to Jahangir-period policy, Khurram's service and consequences of rebellion. Topic 18's full campaign system is not imported.
- Qandahar appears only as the bounded 1622 trigger for the internal court and succession crisis. Topic 19's foreign-policy narrative is preserved.
- Hawkins, Swally and Roe test commercial diplomacy within Mughal sovereignty. No permission is turned into territorial rule or an empire-wide monopoly.
- Nur Jahan's family, farmans, coins, access and patronage prove exceptional authority; they do not prove total sovereign replacement or general gender equality.
- Beni Prasad's fixed-junta thesis is presented as historiography. Nurul Hasan, Satish Chandra and Irfan Habib support a shifting-network, office-distribution and periodised reading.
- Khurram's rebellion remains multi-causal: succession, Qandahar command, jagirs/resources, court alignment and princely military capacity.
- Mahabat Khan's coup distinguishes custody of the emperor from durable control of treasury, administration, legitimacy and broad noble support.
- Jahangir's orders and justice chain are ruler-centred sovereignty claims; proclamation is not assumed to equal uniform implementation.
- Religious policy is broad Akbarian continuity plus contextual coercion, uneven implementation and reversals.
- Painting, natural history and Itimad-ud-Daulah patronage appear only as ruler/household-specific evidence. Topic 24's general culture survey is preserved.
- Alcohol, opium and health are used only through demonstrated mechanisms of access, mobility and succession anxiety.
- The `Jahangirnama/Tuzuk` is used for chronology, stated policy and self-fashioning with authorship/continuation and corroboration cautions.
- The topic stops after Jahangir's death, Asaf Khan's succession action and Nur Jahan's retirement. Shah Jahan's reign is not narrated.
