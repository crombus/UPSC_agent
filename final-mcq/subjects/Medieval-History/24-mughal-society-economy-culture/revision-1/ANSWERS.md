# Topic 24 Revision 1 - Answers

## Mughal Society, Economy & Culture

### Q1. C

- **A:** Incorrect. That describes a jagirdar's assignment, not a resident cultivator.
- **B:** Incorrect. Incoming settlers were commonly classed as pahi-kasht; their tenure could become durable.
- **C:** Correct. Khud-kasht combined residence, local right and direct cultivation, although wealth and caste still varied within the category.
- **D:** Incorrect. Bulk carriers were banjaras, not khud-kasht cultivators.

**Source:** `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/24_Mughal-Society-Economy-Culture.md` §§9.1-9.3; `topic24-part2-ocr.txt`, Satish Chandra Part II, PDF pp. 317-323.
**Examiner trap:** Equating every resident peasant with an undifferentiated owner-cultivator.

### Q2. A

- **A:** Correct. Riyayati marked privilege within resident society; it did not mean that all members had equal holdings or status.
- **B:** Incorrect. The company-linked broker-agent was commonly called a banian.
- **C:** Incorrect. Riyayati was an agrarian-social category, not a port office.
- **D:** Incorrect. Landless and service groups occupied different positions from privileged riyayatis.

**Source:** `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/24_Mughal-Society-Economy-Culture.md` §§9.1-9.3; `topic24-part2-ocr.txt`, Satish Chandra Part II, PDF pp. 317-323.
**Examiner trap:** Confusing riyayati, the privileged category, with raiyati, the ordinary category.

### Q3. D

- **A:** Incorrect. Workshop artisans belonged to craft production, not the muzarian agrarian category.
- **B:** Incorrect. Outsider or incoming cultivators were pahi-kasht, though categories could overlap over time.
- **C:** Incorrect. Zamindars were locally rooted claimants or chiefs, not the ordinary muzarian body.
- **D:** Correct. Muzarian corresponded broadly to raiyati cultivators, including owners and tenants under variable local conditions.

**Source:** `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/24_Mughal-Society-Economy-Culture.md` §§9.1-9.3; `topic24-part2-ocr.txt`, Satish Chandra Part II, PDF pp. 317-323.
**Examiner trap:** Turning a variable cultivator category into a single fixed tenancy form.

### Q4. C

- **A:** Incorrect. Pahi-kasht denoted residence and settlement position, not an Atlantic plantation status.
- **B:** Incorrect. The term concerned cultivation, not treasury administration.
- **C:** Correct. Pahis often settled surplus, new or abandoned land; some received seed, cattle or delayed full assessment.
- **D:** Incorrect. The evidence allows gradual absorption into the resident body over one or more generations.

**Source:** `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/24_Mughal-Society-Economy-Culture.md` §§9.1-9.3; `topic24-part2-ocr.txt`, Satish Chandra Part II, PDF pp. 317-323.
**Examiner trap:** Assuming that incoming status always meant temporary migrant labour.

### Q5. D

- **A:** Incorrect. Movement and resettlement occurred widely in inland agrarian society.
- **B:** Incorrect. Local initiative, credit and village negotiation were central to settlement.
- **C:** Incorrect. Mobility coexisted with strong resident privileges and layered customary claims.
- **D:** Correct. Migration could follow famine, war or oppression, but it also enabled cultivation of new or deserted land.

**Source:** `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/24_Mughal-Society-Economy-Culture.md` §§9.1-9.3; `topic24-part2-ocr.txt`, Satish Chandra Part II, PDF pp. 317-323.
**Examiner trap:** Reading every instance of flight as either free labour mobility or total agrarian collapse.
