### 1. D — FRAME
- **A ⚠️:** Delhi's provincial apparatus lost its hold; governors acting for Delhi did not secure the enduring autonomy of all three courts.
- **B ⚠️:** The western balance involved Gujarat and Malwa, but Bengal's delta polity was not built by a shared western alliance.
- **C ⚠️:** The courts depended on local revenue and elite intermediaries; routes did not organize themselves.
- **D ✅:** Regional courts connected distinct geographies to locally mobilised elites, revenue and political institutions.
- **Sources:** B §§1,9,17–19; A §§1–2; book pp. 174, 181–186; W M1. **Examiner trap:** A lost centre does not explain how successors worked.

### 2. A — B1
- **A ✅:** Delta circulation connected trade but impeded long-distance Delhi campaigning.
- **B ⚠️:** This inverts Delhi's logistical difficulty and Bengal's commerce.
- **C ⚠️:** Waterways aided Bengali trade, not merely hindered it.
- **D ⚠️:** Riverine routes facilitated trade as well as local movement; exchange was not chiefly forced onto roads.
- **Sources:** B §§11.1,11.2; A §1; book pp. 174–177; W Q1,M3. **Examiner trap:** Local mobility and outside invasion had different logistical costs.

### 3. C — B2
- **A ⚠️:** Firuz occupied Pandua; Ilyas retreated to Ekdala.
- **B ⚠️:** Karatoya was Bengal's eastern boundary, not its Kosi settlement with Delhi.
- **C ✅:** Ekdala sheltered Ilyas; the Kosi defined the Firuz-Ilyas settlement.
- **D ⚠️:** The Kosi concerned Delhi-Bengal, not Bengal-Kamata.
- **Sources:** B §11.1; A §4; book pp. 174–175; W Q1–2. **Examiner trap:** A half-correct fort or frontier fails the paired test.

### 4. D — B3
- **A ⚠️:** A Chinese mission is not a witness to the widow's legal case.
- **B ⚠️:** Hafiz's literary contact does not corroborate a particular trial.
- **C ⚠️:** Neither narrative nor diplomatic mission yields numerical trade volumes.
- **D ✅:** The anecdote describes a court ideal; foreign ties document external relations.
- **Sources:** B §§10,11.1; book pp. 175–176; W Q3. **Examiner trap:** Different genres do not independently prove the same event.

### 5. B — B4
- **A ⚠️:** Chittagong supported overseas re-export of imported goods too.
- **B ✅:** Bengal's exports and re-export of Chinese merchandise are both attested.
- **C ⚠️:** Re-export does not imply local manufacture of Chinese merchandise.
- **D ⚠️:** Port trade linked rather than displaced inland waterways and production.
- **Sources:** B §§11.2,18; A §4; book pp. 175–177; W Q19,M5. **Examiner trap:** Distinguish indigenous exports from re-exported imports.

### 6. D — B5
- **A ⚠️:** Other Hindu officials held the physician and mint posts, not these named brothers.
- **B ⚠️:** The brothers' high offices belong under Husain, not the earlier Ilyas.
- **C ⚠️:** The Kosi treaty preceded Husain by many decades.
- **D ✅:** Rupa and Sanatan held Husain's court offices; he sought Chittagong.
- **Sources:** B §§11.1–11.2,17; A §4; book pp. 175–177; W Q5. **Examiner trap:** Do not swap named officials with other officials of the same reign.

### 7. A — B6
- **A ✅:** Gaur/Pandua roofs, Mandu plinth/tiles and Jaunpur arches match in sequence.
- **B ⚠️:** This begins with Mandu rather than Bengal's roofs.
- **C ⚠️:** Jaunpur's arches cannot stand for the opening brick-roof observation.
- **D ⚠️:** Mandu's plinth/tiles and Jaunpur's arches are reversed.
- **Sources:** B §§11.2,12.2,13.2,14,20; A §§1–2; book pp. 176–177, 180–182, 186–187; W M2. **Examiner trap:** All cities are real; assign their characteristic forms accurately.

### 8. C — G1
- **A ⚠️:** Zafar took the title Muzaffar, but the later Ahmad, not Zafar, founded Ahmedabad.
- **B ⚠️:** Tatar Khan belongs to the earlier succession; Ahmedabad's founder was Ahmad Shah.
- **C ✅:** Zafar became Muzaffar; the later Ahmad founded Ahmedabad.
- **D ⚠️:** Begarha ruled later; the city's foundation belongs to Ahmad Shah.
- **Sources:** B §12.1; A §4; book pp. 178–180; W Q6,Q25. **Examiner trap:** Differentiate independence proclamation from capital-building.

### 9. D — G2
- **A ⚠️:** Ahmad founded Ahmedabad, but the book assigns this combined safe-road and caravanserai record to Begarha.
- **B ⚠️:** Begarha's road security is right; assigning his inns to the later Bahadur breaks the account.
- **C ⚠️:** Begarha's inns are right, but secure merchant roads also belong to his reign.
- **D ✅:** Satish Chandra credits Begarha with safe roads and with caravanserais and inns for travellers.
- **Sources:** B §§12.1–12.2; A §§1–2; book pp. 178–181; W M3,M5. **Examiner trap:** Do not isolate a trading port from overland support.

### 10. C — G3
- **A ⚠️:** Champaner's strategic significance included Khandesh as well as Malwa.
- **B ⚠️:** Malwa was also part of Begarha's strategic horizon at Champaner.
- **C ✅:** The fort's position and its ruler's Malwa ties made it strategic for Begarha's Malwa/Khandesh ambitions.
- **D ⚠️:** The position mattered to Gujarat's own expansion, not to securing Malwa's control of Khandesh.
- **Sources:** B §12.1; book pp. 179–181; W Q7. **Examiner trap:** Two names for Girnar are not two conquests.

### 11. B — G4
- **A ⚠️:** The Sidhpur attack contradicts shrine protection inferred from craft borrowing.
- **B ✅:** Jain-derived architectural forms coexisted with Ahmad's Sidhpur attack.
- **C ⚠️:** A documented campaign does not make the documented stonework fictitious.
- **D ⚠️:** Ahmad's architecture used local craft; it was not exclusively Begarha's innovation.
- **Sources:** B §§12.1–12.2,18; A §§1,4; book pp. 179–180; W Q18. **Examiner trap:** Building craft is not a universal religious-policy certificate.

### 12. C — G5
- **A ⚠️:** This reverses Begarha's resistance and Bahadur's concession.
- **B ⚠️:** Begarha did not defend Diu for Humayun; Champaner was his own conquest.
- **C ✅:** Begarha joined Egypt against Portuguese power; Bahadur later conceded.
- **D ⚠️:** The source has Egypt as Begarha's ally, not the adversary proposed.
- **Sources:** B §§4–5,12.1 (Bahadur), book p. 181 (Begarha only); W routed PYQ 1 (demand only), Q8,Q30. **Examiner trap:** Separate ruler, coastal pressure and inland pressure.

### 13. A — M1
- **A ✅:** Mandu's plateau routes attracted Gujarat, Mewar, Delhi and southern powers.
- **B ⚠️:** Malwa also controlled north-south communications, not merely Gujarat-north routes.
- **C ⚠️:** Its plateau mattered to Gujarat-north as well as north-south movement.
- **D ⚠️:** The Lodi rulers of Delhi were among the powers concerned with Malwa's position.
- **Sources:** B §§1,13.1; A §§1–2; book pp. 181–182; W Q9,M3. **Examiner trap:** Locate the inland capital before assigning route power.

### 14. D — M2
- **A ⚠️:** Dilawar preceded his successor Hushang.
- **B ⚠️:** The Khalji succession followed, rather than separated, the two Ghuri rulers.
- **C ⚠️:** The Khaljis were later than both early Ghuri phases.
- **D ✅:** Dilawar, Hushang, then Malwa Khaljis is the attested order.
- **Sources:** B §13.1; book pp. 181–183; W Q9–10. **Examiner trap:** Malwa Khaljis must not be equated with the Delhi Khaljis.

### 15. B — M3
- **A ⚠️:** Treasurer is right, but the text explicitly calls Nardeva one of Hushang's advisers.
- **B ✅:** The Jain merchant Nardeva Soni held the treasury and advised Hushang.
- **C ⚠️:** Adviser is right, but the text assigns no Chanderi governorship to Nardeva.
- **D ⚠️:** Nardeva is documented in Hushang's court, not as treasurer under the later Khalji ruler.
- **Sources:** B §§13.2,18; book p. 182; W Q10. **Examiner trap:** Do not conflate mercantile office and Rajput land grants.

### 16. B — M4
- **A ⚠️:** Hushang belongs to an earlier phase than Sanga.
- **B ✅:** Mahmud II's dispute with Medini Rai brought Sanga into the Malwa contest.
- **C ⚠️:** Kumbha was Mahmud Khalji's opponent, not Medini Rai's counterpart.
- **D ⚠️:** Ghiyasuddin's architecture is not the Medini Rai factional crisis.
- **Sources:** B §§13.1–13.2,19; book pp. 183–186; W M4. **Examiner trap:** Same name 'Mahmud' does not mean the same reign.

### 17. D — J1
- **A ⚠️:** Malik Sarwar, not Ibrahim, is associated with Malik-us-Sharq.
- **B ⚠️:** Husain's later struggle with Bahlul did not coin the dynastic title.
- **C ⚠️:** Bahlul conquered rather than founded the Sharqi dynasty.
- **D ✅:** Sarwar's Malik-us-Sharq title gave rise to the Sharqi ruling-house name.
- **Sources:** B §14; book pp. 186–187; W Q13. **Examiner trap:** Distinguish the founder's designation from later rulers.

### 18. C — J2
- **A ⚠️:** The learned epithet did not displace Sharqi territorial rule.
- **B ⚠️:** Bengal's rulers did not permanently occupy Jaunpur.
- **C ✅:** Learning accompanied substantial Sharqi Ganga-valley control.
- **D ⚠️:** Sharqi cultural patronage predated the Lodi conquest.
- **Sources:** B §14; A §§1–2; book pp. 186–187; W Q14. **Examiner trap:** Cultural reputation and political reach coexisted.

### 19. D — J3
- **A ⚠️:** Atala was built under the Sharqis, not first commissioned by their Lodi conqueror.
- **B ⚠️:** Architectural survival is not evidence that Sharqi rulers retained political sovereignty.
- **C ⚠️:** The building does not record the legal terms or any complete text of annexation.
- **D ✅:** An Atala monument in Jaunpur documents a built idiom that outlasted the Sharqi state.
- **Sources:** B §§14,19–20; book pp. 186–188; W Q15. **Examiner trap:** Do not transpose monuments between sultanates.

### 20. B — K1
- **A ⚠️:** Shah Mir preceded Sikandar; Sikandar did not reverse his own restrictions.
- **B ✅:** Shah Mir founded the line; Zainul reversed later restrictive measures.
- **C ⚠️:** Zainul did not precede Sikandar as dynastic founder.
- **D ⚠️:** Sriya Bhatt was an official, not founder.
- **Sources:** B §15.1; book pp. 189–191; W Q16. **Examiner trap:** Founder and reforming successor play different roles.

### 21. C — K2
- **A ⚠️:** Sikandar was associated with restrictions, not Zainul's later reversals.
- **B ⚠️:** Zainul's appointments accompanied, rather than replaced, restoration.
- **C ✅:** Zainul restored grants/temples, ended jizyah and employed Hindus.
- **D ⚠️:** The sequence of coercion and reconciliation is reversed.
- **Sources:** B §15.1; A §1; book pp. 190–191; W Q16–17. **Examiner trap:** Policy is reign-specific, not a permanent dynastic trait.

### 22. B — K3
- **A ⚠️:** Gujarat's Khambhat customs were not the authority organizing Kashmir's mountain crafts.
- **B ✅:** Valley routes, local crafts and Rishi-Sufi interaction describe Kashmir.
- **C ⚠️:** Zainul patronised Sanskrit scholars and translation as well as craft production.
- **D ⚠️:** Dams, canals and bridges form part of the same Kashmiri ruler's documented public works.
- **Sources:** B §§15.2,17–18; book pp. 189–191; W Q28. **Examiner trap:** Shared court craft patronage does not erase regional geography.

### 23. A — H1
- **A ✅:** UNESCO identifies both first capital and royal necropolis under Sukapha.
- **B ⚠️:** The UNESCO account expressly includes a necropolis.
- **C ⚠️:** The UNESCO account expressly calls Charaideo the first capital.
- **D ⚠️:** An earlier first centre contradicts UNESCO's description.
- **Sources:** B §16.1; https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1711/ (Brief synthesis); book p. 176 for Kamata. **Examiner trap:** Use UNESCO for its explicit claim, not an invented administrative history.

### 24. C — H2
- **A ⚠️:** Paik obligation was rotational labour/military service, not primarily coin payment.
- **B ⚠️:** Paik service included military mobilisation as well as labour.
- **C ✅:** Obligations rotated and developed across Ahom reigns.
- **D ⚠️:** A mature service system need not be projected unchanged to Sukapha.
- **Sources:** B §§16.1,17; A §2; W Q22,R6,M6; T learning MCQ 12 and Q22. **Examiner trap:** Institutional change matters to this service definition.

### 25. C — H3
- **A ⚠️:** Burial mounds do not establish locality-by-locality tax rates.
- **B ⚠️:** Burials do not date all surviving chronicle compilations.
- **C ✅:** First royal centre is relevant here; burial morphology has another owner.
- **D ⚠️:** Modern heritage boundaries do not map medieval sovereign borders.
- **Sources:** B §§16.1–16.2,20; A §2; https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1711/ (Brief synthesis, Authenticity); W Q21–24,M6. **Examiner trap:** Political context and art-historical form are separate claims.

### 26. B — SRC
- **A ⚠️:** Corroborating port notices helps, but a single court story does not establish routine verdicts.
- **B ✅:** Port reports require corroboration; the royal narrative conveys a courtly ideal, not case statistics.
- **C ⚠️:** Without evidence of fabrication, a royal narrative can still reveal what justice was meant to signify.
- **D ⚠️:** No single royal tale is a representative record of ordinary litigation.
- **Sources:** B §10; W Q3,Q18,Q27; T drill G. **Examiner trap:** Match the inference to each source's actual genre.

### 27. D — END
- **A ⚠️:** Atala was a Sharqi monument; Lodi annexation does not make Bahlul its original patron.
- **B ⚠️:** Jaunpur did not retain a separate sovereign Sharqi enclave under Bahlul.
- **C ⚠️:** Its learned reputation remained associated with Jaunpur, not transferred as a possession to Delhi.
- **D ✅:** Jaunpur's built and learned culture outlasted Sharqi sovereignty.
- **Sources:** B §§14,19; book pp. 186–188; W Q15,Q32,M1; T drill H. **Examiner trap:** Political end does not mean cultural erasure.

### 28. A — B7
- **A ✅:** Maladhar Basu's work and Gunaraja Khan title show patronage.
- **B ⚠️:** One title cannot date the whole Ganesh interlude.
- **C ⚠️:** The title does not chronicle Husain's eastern military campaigns.
- **D ⚠️:** The book says he was patronised by Bengal's sultans but does not identify Husain Shah as the specific patron who conferred the title.
- **Sources:** B §§11.1–11.2; book pp. 175–176; W M2. **Examiner trap:** Do not turn an honorific into authorship or an annal.

### 29. B — M5
- **A ⚠️:** Dhar district records Mahmud Khalji, not Hushang, completing Jami Mosque.
- **B ✅:** District records separate Hushang/Mahmud mosque phases from later Ghiyasuddin's Jahaz Mahal.
- **C ⚠️:** Mahmud II did not begin the mosque; Hushang was not Jahaz's recorded patron.
- **D ⚠️:** Dilawar did not complete the Jami Mosque; Jahaz belongs to a later phase.
- **Sources:** B §13.2; book p. 182 (Mandu grouping, including Hindola Mahal); https://dhar.nic.in/en/tourist-place/jami-mosque-a-fusion-of-brilliance-magnificence/; https://dhar.nic.in/en/tourist-place/jahaz-mahal-ship-palace/. **Examiner trap:** Same capital does not imply same builder.

### 30. A — K4
- **A ✅:** Sriya Bhatt was minister of justice and court physician.
- **B ⚠️:** The book does not assign him treasury or army command.
- **C ⚠️:** The ruler's translation/island projects are not Sriya's named offices.
- **D ⚠️:** Zainul developed irrigation, but Sriya had judicial/medical offices.
- **Sources:** B §15.1; book p. 191; W Q17. **Examiner trap:** Do not attribute all a ruler's projects to his minister.

### 31. C — M6
- **A ⚠️:** Medini Rai/Sanga belong to the later Mahmud II episode.
- **B ⚠️:** Hushang's grants and Khalji's Kumbha war belong to different rulers.
- **C ✅:** Mahmud Khalji fought Kumbha; Mahmud II faced Medini Rai/Sanga.
- **D ⚠️:** Sanga belongs to the later reign; Hushang's grants are earlier.
- **Sources:** B §§13.1–13.2,19; book pp. 182–186; W M4. **Examiner trap:** Separate two Mahmuds and Hushang's earlier policies.

### 32. A — H4
- **A ✅:** Ahom-backed success at Kamata did not annex Suhungmung's Ahom realm.
- **B ⚠️:** Alliance against Kamata is not subordination of Charaideo.
- **C ⚠️:** Suhungmung ruled the Ahoms and repulsed Bengal, not governed for it.
- **D ⚠️:** Kamtapur fell; the later Ahom attack failed.
- **Sources:** B §§11.2,16; book p. 176; W M6. **Examiner trap:** A western city and eastern Ahom polity are not identical.

### 33. C — COIN
- **A ⚠️:** Fanam is identified as gold, not silver.
- **B ⚠️:** A copper fraction would be another monetary category.
- **C ✅:** A small gold coin circulated in southern/coastal trade.
- **D ⚠️:** A coin is not the same as uncoined bullion weight.
- **Sources:** B §§4–5; W R3; T R3. **Examiner trap:** All rivals are monetary: verify metal and form.

### 34. A — B8
- **A ✅:** Statements 1 and 2 are supported: both sites are Bengal monuments, and Raja Ganesh marks a political interlude.
- **B ⚠️:** Statement 1 holds; statement 3 unjustifiably infers Raja Ganesh's patronage of both buildings.

### 35. A — G6

### 36. B — J4 (Sharqi legacy; Jayasi association held)

- **Sources:** B §14; book pp. 186–187; W Q14,M2. **Examiner trap:** A later author must not be invented as a Sharqi client.

### 37. D — K5
- **A ⚠️:** The Gwalior ruler sent Sanskrit music books, but the source does not identify their Persian translations.
- **B ⚠️:** A Gwalior gift is not the named translated second text, though Rajatarangini is.
- **C ⚠️:** Zainul wrote poetry, but his own Kashmiri verse is not a Sanskrit work translated into Persian.
- **D ✅:** Mahabharata and Kalhana's Rajatarangini were rendered into Persian.

### 38. B — M7
- **A ⚠️:** The inscription does not credit Hushang with personally restoring that temple.
- **B ✅:** A built temple suggests construction was not generally forbidden then.
- **C ⚠️:** Mahmud Khalji later destroyed temples in some campaigns.
- **D ⚠️:** One inscription is not a universal legal decree.
- **Sources:** B §§13.1–13.2; book pp. 182–183; W Q11. **Examiner trap:** Limit an inscription's inference to period and action.

### 39. A — M7 (Rajput grants)
- **A ✅:** The brothers of Rana Mokal held Malwa jagirs under Hushang.
- **B ⚠️:** A tenure grant does not hand sovereignty over Mandu to Mewar.

- **D ⚠️:** Hushang's dynasty did not become a Rajput dynasty through these grants.

### 40. D — G4 (court offices)
- **A ⚠️:** The trader ministers served Ahmad, not only Begarha.
- **B ⚠️:** The account names ministerial appointments, not battlefield commands at Sidhpur.
- **C ⚠️:** The source calls Manik Chand and Motichand ministers, not merely private advisers.
- **D ✅:** Manik Chand and Motichand were Ahmad's trader-community ministers.
- **Sources:** B §12.1; book p. 179. **Examiner trap:** Temple attack and minority appointments can coexist.

### 41. D — M4 (campaign nuance)
- **A ⚠️:** The recorded destruction belongs to Mahmud Khalji, not Hushang.
- **B ⚠️:** The author denies a general temple-destruction programme; the alleged building ban is not evidenced.

- **D ✅:** Some campaigns damaged temples, without proving a general programme.
- **Sources:** B §§13.1–13.2,18; book pp. 182–183; W Q11. **Examiner trap:** Do not erase violence or generalise beyond it.

### 42. B — K4 (public works)
- **A ⚠️:** Zaina Lanka's island in Wular was artificial, not natural.

### 43. A — H1/H4 (sequence)

### 44. C — SRC (coins)
- **A ⚠️:** A named mint cannot fix complete political borders around a coin's discovery place.
- **B ⚠️:** Neither mint name nor titulature reports an annual revenue total.

- **D ⚠️:** A single mint claim cannot fix the geographical circulation of a denomination.

### 45. B — B8 (Ganesh succession)
- **A ⚠️:** The book recounts Ghiyasuddin's Chinese contacts before the later Raja Ganesh interlude.
- **B ✅:** It reports that Raja Ganesh's sons preferred to rule as Muslims after his brief period of power.
- **C ⚠️:** Ilyas Shah's Kosi treaty preceded Raja Ganesh; his sons were not the negotiators.
- **D ⚠️:** Ilyas's refuge at Ekdala belongs to the earlier Firuz campaign, not Ganesh's sons.

### 46. A — H3 (chronicles)
- **A ✅:** Chronicles preserve sequences/terms but have court silences.
- **B ⚠️:** Buranjis are not complete household paik censuses.
- **C ⚠️:** Modern protected boundaries are not chronicle data.
- **D ⚠️:** Moidam construction dates require archaeological corroboration.
- **Sources:** B §§10,16.2; A §2; W Q23,R11,M6; https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1711/ (Authenticity). **Examiner trap:** Written royal memory and archaeology answer different questions.

### 47. C — B2 (diplomatic limit)
- **A ⚠️:** Gifts did not make Delhi appoint Bengal's successor.
- **B ⚠️:** Firuz did not retain Ekdala as a Delhi fort.
- **C ✅:** Gift exchange coexisted with independent Bengal rule.

### 48. A — H1 (incorporation)
