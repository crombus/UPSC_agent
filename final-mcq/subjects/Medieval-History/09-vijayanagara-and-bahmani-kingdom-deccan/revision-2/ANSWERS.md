### Q1. C

**Tested dimension:** 57 — Krishnadevaraya's direct rule at the Tungabhadra doab. **Source:** upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md §§12, 16; Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF pp. 161 (doab), 163 (continuing nayaka military service).

- **A:** Chandra states that the ruler ousted doab nayakas, not merely added a trade toll.
- **B:** A local doab intervention did not cancel nayakas' wider obligations to provide military service to the raya; the source describes continued troops and local revenue-service ties.
- **C:** The local intervention does not abolish the wider amaram/service relationship.
- **D:** Extrapolating one doab intervention to every province is unsupported.
- **Examiner trap:** A local exception to nayaka control is evidence of variable, not universally central, power.

### Q4. D

**Tested dimension:** 60 — an ayagar's local record/revenue service versus a tarafdar's provincial fiscal-military remit. R1 Q60 tests only the two offices' polity and territorial *scale*; R2 Q4 now requires the functional contrast rather than restating that scale. **Source:** `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md` §§16–17 (ayagar village functions, amaram/nayaka troop service, tarafdar provincial power, khalisa crown resources and royal oversight of forts); Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF pp. 159, 163 (Bahmani noble contingents, land revenue, provincial authority). Mature ayagar roles are not projected unchanged back to every Vijayanagara reign.

- **A:** Ayagar service involved village records and dues, but crown-reserved khalisa resources are a separate category from the provincial fiscal-military office of a tarafdar.
- **B:** Bahmani tarafdars exercised provincial fiscal-military powers; the troop-supported territorial assignment belongs to nayakas/amaram, not the ayagar village-service role.
- **C:** The village-office half is supported, but Gawan's effort to strengthen *royal* fort appointments is not itself the remit defining a provincial tarafdar.
- **D:** Ayagars served locally with record/revenue functions; tarafdars governed Bahmani provincial military-revenue arrangements. The same fiscal vocabulary does not make these offices functionally identical.
- **Examiner trap:** Distinguish village-service personnel, nayaka military assignments, provincial tarafdars and khalisa crown reserves; do not flatten them into one chain of command.
