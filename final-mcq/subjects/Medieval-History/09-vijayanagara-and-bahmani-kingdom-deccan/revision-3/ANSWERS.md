### Q1. A

**Tested dimension:** 21 — Tirumala's Aravidu line and regional nayaka continuation. **Source:** upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md §15 (Tirumala, Penukonda and three nayakas); Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF pp. 162–163 (continuing polity and Madurai/Tanjore nayakas; not independent evidence for Gingee).

- **A:** Tirumala's Aravidu line continued at Penukonda while local nayakas could exercise increasing power.
- **B:** A surviving court and grants contradict instant extinction of raya claims.
- **C:** Orders from the nayaka undercut an inference of exclusively direct royal administration there.
- **D:** Local orders from three nayakas cannot establish identical timing for every southern region.
- **Examiner trap:** Metropolitan destruction and continued dynastic/regional authority are separate claims.

### Q2. D

**Tested dimension:** 22 — Firuz Shah Bahmani's learning and Deccani officials. **Source:** upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md §§13, 22; Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF pp. 156–157.

- **A:** A single appointment cannot establish the composition of every office throughout the kingdom.
- **B:** Firuz's observatory near Daulatabad is consistent with, not contradicted by, the appointment.
- **C:** Firuz's court remained centred at Gulbarga; a revenue appointment does not challenge that.
- **D:** Local Brahman participation in revenue challenges an exclusionary reading of Persianate rule.
- **Examiner trap:** Do not infer universal equality merely from learned patronage and mixed staffing.

### Q3. C

**Tested dimension:** 23 — the *two specifically named overseas shipping regions* reaching Firuz's Chaul–Dabhol ports, not R1 Q23's Firuz-versus-Gawan port and Kherla attribution. **Source:** `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md` §§13–14 (Firuz and Chaul–Dabhol); Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF p. 157 (ships from the Persian Gulf and Red Sea, luxury imports). The options ask what the cited text **names**, not whether other connected waters were physically navigated.

- **A:** Chandra names the Persian Gulf, but pairs it with the *Red Sea* rather than the Gulf of Aden.
- **B:** The Red Sea is named, but the other named shipping origin is the *Persian Gulf*, not the Gulf of Oman.
- **C:** Chandra explicitly identifies ships from the Persian Gulf and Red Sea at Firuz's principal Chaul–Dabhol ports.
- **D:** Neither the Gulf of Oman nor the Gulf of Aden is the pair Chandra identifies; both lie near connected sea lanes but do not replace the text's named origins.
- **Examiner trap:** Neighbouring gulfs on a sea route do not automatically become the source's stated origins; R3 Q54 separately tests Kherla's subordinate relationship.

### Q4. C

- **B:** Firuz is wrongly credited with moving the capital to Bidar before Ahmad's Warangal campaign.
- **C:** Hasan founded the kingdom from Gulbarga in 1347; Ahmad Shah's Warangal expansion preceded his shift to Bidar.
- **D:** Firuz was a later Bahmani ruler, not the founder, although Ahmad's subsequent sequence is sound.
- **Examiner trap:** Religious memory and political chronology are not interchangeable evidence.

### Q5. A

**Tested dimension:** 01 — Recombination of pre-existing local jurisdictions and trade routes during Delhi's southern retreat, not R1 Q1's identification of four surviving polities. **Source:** upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md §11 (Karnataka nayakas and Tungabhadra–Krishna/coastal routes); Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF pp. 149–152 (Delhi's contested southern position and the regional political/economic field). R3 Q25 independently keys the Hoysala/Madurai and Reddi/Warangal roster as a southern-to-eastern chronological contrast.

- **A:** Local nayaka power and existing routes meant the rising courts had to contend with inherited jurisdictions and strategic corridors.
- **B:** Delhi's southern retreat did not transfer an intact, all-embracing provincial administration to either new kingdom.
- **C:** Delhi's weakness did not abolish local nayaka claims; the routes mattered alongside existing regional powers.
- **D:** Tungabhadra–Krishna connections and the coast remained strategic prizes, not irrelevant background to territorial competition.
- **Examiner trap:** Political recombination involves both existing claimants and the routes/revenue they contested; no source attests an empty frontier.

### Q6. D

**Tested dimension:** 02 — conventional Harihara–Bukka foundation c. 1336 versus the prolonged consolidation of the southern polity; not R1 Q2's test of Vidyaranya/Kampili origin biographies. **Source:** upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md §§11–12; Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF pp. 149–152 (p. 150 specifically mentions Hoysala contention and continued independent Madurai; its fall came in the later southern campaign).

- **A:** The founders' conventional date predates the defeat of surviving southern rivals; it is not a date of total territorial incorporation.
- **B:** Hoysala and Madurai resistance survived the foundation phase, but that does not invalidate a conventional starting date for the emergent polity.
- **C:** Bahmani fragmentation belongs much later; Madurai was eliminated during earlier Vijayanagara expansion.
- **D:** The c. 1336 Harihara–Bukka foundation and subsequent struggle against Hoysala and Madurai claimants describe different stages of state building.
- **Examiner trap:** The date assigned to a dynasty's emergence need not coincide with control of all later territories.

### Q7. C

**Tested dimension:** 03 — founder Hasan's 1347 Bahmani accession versus Gawan's later ministerial role; qualified retrospective Gangu story, not R1 Q3's sole founder/date-versus-legend verdict. **Source:** upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md §§11, 13, 17; Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF pp. 150–151 (Hasan and later Gangu explanations), 158–159 (Gawan's later ministry). The Basic owner supplies the caution that Firishta's Gangu narrative is retrospective; Chandra also relates that tradition without proving it.

- **A:** Hasan, not Gawan, founded the kingdom in 1347; Gawan belongs to a subsequent Bahmani ministry, regardless of the later Gangu story.
- **B:** Hasan's 1347 foundation and the story's evidentiary limit are right, but Gawan was not Hasan's contemporary minister.
- **C:** Founder Hasan and minister Gawan belong to different phases; Firishta's later patron explanation cannot document Hasan's ancestry.
- **D:** The offices and relative chronology are right, but a later patron narrative cannot establish a biological genealogy.
- **Examiner trap:** Keep founder, later minister and retrospective origin legend in separate evidentiary categories.

### Q8. A
