### Q1. B

**Tested dimension:** 01 — Regional recombination after Delhi's southern contraction. **Source:** upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md §§11; Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF pp. 149–150.

- **A:** Warangal remained a participant in the eastern field rather than vanishing before the two kingdoms emerged.
- **B:** Existing southern and eastern claimants made state formation a regional recombination.
- **C:** Madurai's independent sultanate was already active during the fourteenth-century southern realignment.
- **D:** The Reddis were already active before late Bahmani fragmentation, alongside Madurai and Warangal.
- **Examiner trap:** Delhi withdrawal did not erase Hoysala or Andhra claimants.

### Q2. C

**Tested dimension:** 02 — Harihara and Bukka's Vijayanagara foundation, c. 1336. **Source:** upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md §§11; Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF pp. 149–150.

- **A:** The foundation anchor is conventional, but the brothers' alleged Warangal service is disputed rather than securely documented.
- **B:** The earlier-service qualification is sound, but c. 1336 is not a directly attested contemporary coronation record.
- **C:** The conventional founding-phase date does not resolve competing traditions about the brothers' earlier affiliations.
- **D:** Doubt about earlier affiliations does not convert a conventional chronology into an authenticated contemporary coronation record.
- **Examiner trap:** Do not promote the debated Vidyaranya/conversion narrative to a dated document.

### Q3. A

**Tested dimension:** 03 — Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah's Bahmani foundation, 1347. **Source:** upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md §§11; Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF pp. 150–151.

- **A:** Hasan's accession anchors independence; Firishta's later story cannot prove ancestry.
- **B:** Hasan's assumed Bahman Shah title names the ruler and kingdom but does not authenticate a particular Gangu patron story.
- **C:** The earlier c. 1336 anchor belongs to Vijayanagara; Hasan's Bahmani independence dates to 1347.
- **D:** Firishta wrote retrospectively; he was not a contemporary observer of Hasan's accession.
- **Examiner trap:** Founder, retrospective Gangu genealogy and later minister are three different claims.

### Q4. B

**Tested dimension:** 04 — Madurai campaign during Bukka I's reign and Gangadevi's *Madhuravijayam*. **Source:** upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md §§10, 12; Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF pp. 150–152 (Chandra supports Madurai chronology; Gangadevi attribution is from Basic §10).

- **A:** Harihara I belongs to the earlier formation phase, not the poem's Madurai campaign.
- **B:** Gangadevi's Sanskrit poem commemorates the Madurai campaign during Bukka I's reign; court poetry is not a campaign diary.
- **C:** Harihara II expanded east after southern consolidation; this reverses the sequence.
- **D:** Deva Raya I's frontier wars with Firuz belong to a later reign and different theatre.
- **Examiner trap:** A campaign under a ruler is not evidence that the ruler personally led it; literary conquest memory is not a neutral campaign diary.

### Q5. A

**Tested dimension:** 05 — Harihara II's eastward policy, competing eastern claimants and separate Belgaum–Goa gains in the west. **Source:** upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md §§11–12, 14; Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF pp. 152–153 (Madurai consolidation followed by eastward policy; Reddi/Warangal/Orissa/Bahmani interests; distinct Belgaum–Goa success).

- **A:** All three statements are supported: the post-Madurai eastern drive met multiple rivals; western Belgaum–Goa gains do not establish eastern-port control.
- **B:** Statements 1 and 2 are sound, but this omits the independently attested western gain and the resulting east–west distinction in statement 3.
- **C:** Statements 1 and 3 are sound, but this omits the Reddi/Warangal and Orissa/Bahmani competition explicitly described in statement 2.
- **D:** Statements 2 and 3 are sound, but this omits Chandra's explicit sequence from Madurai consolidation to Harihara II's eastward policy in statement 1.
- **Examiner trap:** Distinguish an eastward *policy* from secure annexation of all eastern ports, and from the documented western Belgaum–Goa gain.

### Q6. C

**Tested dimension:** 06 — Bahmani–Warangal alliance around Golconda and Kaulas. **Source:** upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md §§14; Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF pp. 152–153.

- **A:** The Bahmani successor to Hasan seized both Kaulas and Golconda; Warangal did not retain the latter.
- **B:** Golconda was not restored: the sultan fixed it as his boundary after the seizure.
- **C:** Seizure, boundary-making and alliance altered Vijayanagara's eastern balance; the pact was not annexation of Warangal.
- **D:** A boundary undertaking and alliance did not annex Warangal as an ordinary Bahmani province.
- **Examiner trap:** An eastern alliance around Golconda/Kaulas was neither permanent annexation nor a doab settlement.

### Q7. C

**Tested dimension:** 07 — Raichur–Mudkal in the Tungabhadra doab. **Source:** upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md §§14, 19; Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF pp. 151–153.

- **A:** Chandra reports that Bukka's agreement restored shared doab arrangements, not permanent Bahmani control.
- **B:** The Tungabhadra doab's fertility mattered along with fortified routes and garrison supplies.
- **C:** The Tungabhadra doab's fertile land and defended inland routes gave these strongholds value.
- **D:** Deva Raya II's Mudkal/Bankapur fighting did not close this recurrent frontier dispute.
- **Examiner trap:** Doab forts and fertile routes are inland; do not transpose west-coast port logic.

### Q8. B

**Tested dimension:** 08 — Krishna–Godavari basin. **Source:** upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md §§14; Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF pp. 151–153.

- **A:** Eastern sea outlets belong to this basin, but the specified Tungabhadra doab cultivation is a different inland frontier, not Krishna–Godavari delta farmland.
- **B:** The fertile Krishna–Godavari delta and eastern sea outlets linked agriculture with coastal commerce within the same contested basin.
- **C:** Delta cultivation belongs here, but Goa's horse-import port lies on the western Konkan coast, not the Krishna–Godavari eastern outlet.
- **D:** Both the Tungabhadra doab's farmland and Goa's horse-import port concern other contested theatres, not this eastern basin's joined agrarian and maritime stakes.
- **Examiner trap:** The delta joins fields with ports; Konkan horse supply is a separate western issue.

### Q9. D

**Tested dimension:** 09 — Konkan–Goa horse corridor. **Source:** upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md §§14, 19; Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF pp. 151, 158.

- **A:** Horses came into Goa from Iran and Iraq; this option reverses the relevant direction.
- **B:** Goa was already a strategic horse-trade outlet well before Portugal captured it.
- **C:** Imported horse availability affected inland cavalry and therefore frontier warfare.
- **D:** Horses and overseas merchants made Goa strategically valuable before Portuguese conquest.
- **Examiner trap:** Goa was strategic for horse imports before Portuguese conquest; trade direction matters.

### Q10. D

**Tested dimension:** 10 — Bukka's Mudkal conflict and the 1367 agreement. **Source:** upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md §§14; Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF pp. 151–152.

- **A:** The reported future protection concerned helpless or unarmed inhabitants, not only captured military personnel.
- **B:** The account does not limit civilian protection to people within a defended town.
- **C:** The reported agreement looked forward to future wars; it was not solely a retrospective account of harm.
- **D:** Chandra records artillery in the conflict and a prospective pledge to spare helpless civilians; later observance is not thereby established.
- **Examiner trap:** Preserve the restraint's stated beneficiaries and future orientation without inventing a later record of compliance.

### Q11. C

**Tested dimension:** 11 — Deva Raya I and Firuz Shah Bahmani's marriage diplomacy. **Source:** upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md §§14; Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF pp. 153.

- **A:** Bankapur went to Firuz with Deva Raya I's daughter, not to the Raya.
- **B:** Bankapur passed to Firuz, but fighting resumed; the marriage was not a permanent peace.
- **C:** The dowry settlement did not make the rulers' political rivalry disappear.
- **D:** Deva Raya I's later eastern gains followed a changed Warangal alignment, not a gift of Krishna-mouth ports in the Bankapur marriage.
- **Examiner trap:** A dynastic marriage and Bankapur settlement did not prevent later fighting.

### Q12. A

**Tested dimension:** 12 — Deva Raya I's Tungabhadra and Haridra waterworks. **Source:** upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md §§12, 18; Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF pp. 154.

- **A:** Chandra names the capital-supplying Tungabhadra work and a separate Haridra irrigation dam.
- **B:** Bidar was a Bahmani court centre, not the destination of the Raya's capital canal.
- **C:** The separately identified irrigation work was on the Haridra, not the Krishna.
- **D:** The capital project was on the Tungabhadra; the separate dam was on the Haridra.
- **Examiner trap:** Haridra is an additional dam, not a synonym for the Tungabhadra canal.

### Q13. B

**Tested dimension:** 13 — Deva Raya II's cavalry and archery reorganisation. **Source:** upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md §§12, 19; Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF pp. 154–155.

- **A:** He attempted to recover disputed Mudkal and Bankapur with the reformed force rather than abandoning that frontier.
- **B:** Mounted recruitment and archery were adaptations, not evidence for any precise army total.
- **C:** Training aimed to strengthen mounted archery, not to replace cavalry with elephants.
- **D:** He did campaign to recover Mudkal and Bankapur, but Chandra reports three hard battles ending with the sides agreeing to their existing frontiers; the attempted recapture was not a lasting conquest.
- **Examiner trap:** Recruitment and archery reform under Deva Raya II does not validate inflated army totals.

### Q14. C

**Tested dimension:** 14 — Conti and Abdur Razzaq on Vijayanagara. **Source:** upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md §§10, 12; Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF pp. 155–156.

- **A:** Conti's earlier Italian visit preceded Razzaq's Timurid embassy.
- **B:** Razzaq visited before Paes's Tuluva-era observations, not after them.
- **C:** Conti preceded Razzaq (who described seven enclosures); Paes and then Nuniz belong to the Portuguese accounts.
- **D:** Paes's visit precedes Nuniz's later retrospective writing; the final pair is reversed.
- **Examiner trap:** Conti precedes Razzaq; neither is a Krishnadevaraya-era Portuguese visitor.

### Q15. B

**Tested dimension:** 15 — Saluva Narasimha and Vira Narasimha's dynastic transitions. **Source:** upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md §§12; Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF pp. 160.

- **A:** Saluva rule came between Sangama and Tuluva, not after Tuluva.
- **B:** The Saluva and Tuluva transitions preceded Krishnadevaraya's Tuluva reign.
- **C:** Aravidu emerged after 1565, not between Saluva and Tuluva.
- **D:** The Sangama phase preceded Vira Narasimha and Tirumala, not followed them.
- **Examiner trap:** Sangama, Saluva and Tuluva are successive dynasties; Aravidu follows Talikota.

### Q16. C

**Tested dimension:** 16 — Krishnadevaraya's Orissa and Raichur campaigns. **Source:** upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md §§12; Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF pp. 160–161.

- **A:** The Bahmani kingdom had fragmented before Krishnadevaraya; his Raichur opponent was the Bijapur ruler.
- **B:** Warangal chiefs belong to the earlier eastern balance, not Krishnadevaraya's Bijapur contest at Raichur.
- **C:** Eastern Orissa fighting and the 1520 Raichur battle show two distinct fronts.
- **D:** This swaps the fronts: Orissa's Gajapati was the eastern rival; Bijapur fought for Raichur.

### Q17. B

**Tested dimension:** 17 — Krishnadevaraya's tanks, towns and Amuktamalyada. **Source:** upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md §§12, 23; Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF pp. 161.

### Q19. D

**Tested dimension:** 19 — Achyuta, Sadashiva and Aliya Rama Raya. **Source:** upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md §§12; Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF pp. 162–163.

- **C:** Rama Raya, not Sadashiva, was the effective broker of later Deccan alliances.
- **D:** After Achyuta, nominal Sadashiva contrasted with Rama Raya's effective command.

### Q20. B

**Tested dimension:** 20 — Talikota/Rakshasa-Tangadi/Bannihatti, the leading shifting coalition and Rama Raya's fate. **Source:** `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md` §15; Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF p. 162 (three named leading powers combine after Rama Raya's earlier balancing; his immediate execution). - **A:** Bijapur was among the powers that combined against Rama Raya, not a Vijayanagara ally in this decisive alignment; the capture/execution alone is correct.
- **B:** Chandra names Bijapur, Golconda and Ahmadnagar as powers that combined to defeat Vijayanagara; Rama Raya was taken prisoner and immediately executed. Listing these three does not rule out the separately qualified participation of other powers.

### Q21. A

**Tested dimension:** 21 — Tirumala's Aravidu line and regional nayaka continuation. **Source:** upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/basic/09_Vijayanagara-and-Bahmani.md §§15; Satish Chandra, *A History of Medieval India*, OCR PDF pp. 162–163.

- **A:** Imperial primacy weakened but Aravidu continuation and Madurai/Tanjavur/Gingee nayakas persisted.
- **B:** Tirumala's Aravidu continuation used Penukonda and other centres; the old Hampi capital was devastated.
- **C:** Southern nayakas, including Madurai and Tanjavur, gained rather than lost autonomy after the metropolitan collapse.
- **D:** Bidar was a Bahmani centre, not the Aravidu refuge at Penukonda.
- **Examiner trap:** Sacking a capital ended imperial primacy, not every successor institution.

### Q22. B
