### 1 — A
(A) Correct: a historical transition can vary by region and institution. (B) The rise of Turkish courts cannot impose one chronology on every region. (C) Grants and agrarian relations changed unevenly, not in one shared shift. (D) Dynastic succession alone cannot date the social changes it intersects. **Source:** B §9. **Trap:** analytical period labels are not synchronised events.

### 2 — D
(A) This is a possible error about languages, but neither religious label is a court-language designation. (B) Dynastic politics still matters; the problem is using it as the whole history. (C) Merchant chronology is a separate problem, not the chief defect of religious periodisation. (D) Correct: rulers' religious affiliations do not classify heterogeneous societies or institutions. **Source:** B §9. **Trap:** ruler labels are not comprehensive social descriptions.

### 3 — A
(A) Correct: successor courts retained agrarian, military and political capacity. (B) The decline of the Pratiharas did not leave successors wholly dependent on their surviving treasury. (C) Competing Gahadavala and other centres operated in the Ganga plain. (D) Chola strength in the south did not transfer all north Indian resources to Chola control. **Source:** B §§1, 9; A §1. **Trap:** regionalisation is not a vacuum.

### 4 — B
(A) Gahadavala and Paramara centres are swapped; Chauhan presence at Delhi does not save the set. (B) Correct: Kannauj, Malwa and Ajmer respectively match Gahadavala, Paramara and Chauhan centres. (C) Gujarat was Chaulukya, Delhi Tomara/Chauhan and Kannauj Gahadavala. (D) Delhi had Tomara/Chauhan associations, Gujarat Chaulukya and Malwa Paramara. **Source:** B §§1, 9. **Trap:** judge the entire set, not one plausible pair.

### 5 — D
(A) This contrasts two frontier histories, not a south-versus-north-wide-collapse claim. (B) Both are north Indian military-political developments. (C) Successor strength does qualify a *north Indian vacuum* claim, but does not test the all-India thesis across north and south as directly. (D) Correct: Chola southern maritime strength persisted amid north-western reverses. **Source:** B §§1, 9. **Trap:** “most decisively” refers to a cross-subcontinental counterexample.

### 6 — B
(A) Temple patronage did not homogenise merchant networks; ports and regional courts varied. (B) Correct: grants and *samanta* relations reshaped authority differently by region. (C) Grant rights and cultivator obligations cannot be assumed identical everywhere. (D) Subordinate relations were regionally variable; a single uniform model is unwarranted. **Source:** B §9. **Trap:** an interpretive model is not evidence of uniform practice.

### 7 — A
(A) Correct: the permission supports negotiated Muslim merchant presence under a Rashtrakuta ruler. (B) It does not establish a transfer of port taxation. (C) A mosque does not confer jurisdiction over the host port. (D) A particular ruler's permission cannot demonstrate uniform rights at every inland court. **Source:** B §§1, 10; A §1; Part I PDF p. 8. **Trap:** accommodation does not prove sovereignty or fiscal privileges.

### 8 — B
(A) Seaborne Arab merchants did not conquer every port; Sind was a specific land-frontier conquest. (B) Correct: the Sind campaign established bounded political rule, whereas seaborne commerce crossed political frontiers. (C) Sind's conquest did not impose Arab rule on all coasts, and maritime circuits were not confined to Sind. (D) Sind was a military-political campaign rather than a courtly exchange, and shipping was not governed from Sind. **Source:** B §§10–11; A §1. **Trap:** separate agent, route and political effect.

### 9 — A
(A) Correct: Qasim served the Umayyads and defeated Dahir in Sind. (B) Jayapala belonged to the later Hindu Shahi–Ghaznavid frontier conflict, not Qasim's opponent. (C) Dahir and Sind fit, but Qasim operated under the Umayyads, not Abbasid authority. (D) Dahir and Sind fit, but Ghaznavid rule was a later and distinct formation. **Source:** B §§11, 13. **Trap:** matching two of three does not establish the authority.

### 10 — D
(A) Negotiated accommodation did not remove the use of garrisons. (B) Retaining local officers did not mean governors renounced taxes. (C) The evidence describes retained intermediaries, not their wholesale exclusion. (D) Correct: garrisons and taxation could operate alongside existing personnel. **Source:** B §11. **Trap:** neither seamless accommodation nor total displacement.

### 11 — A
(A) Correct: the later Persian narration must be read by genre and tested against independent evidence. (B) A claimed Arabic precursor does not survive to corroborate a particular speech. (C) Naming Dahir is narrative specificity, not contemporary transcription. (D) Agreement between later recensions is not independent eighth-century confirmation. **Source:** B §11; W semantic drill D. **Trap:** later narrative detail is not a verbatim record.

### 12 — B
(A) A later place-name alone does not securely locate an excavated settlement. (B) Correct: independently dated material and geographical agreement substantially strengthen a tentative identification. (C) A mosque without a secure archaeological sequence does not identify the eighth-century port. (D) Imported ceramics without a secure context cannot identify a named port. **Source:** B §§11, 17. **Trap:** Banbhore/Debal remains a debated identification, not a settled equation.

### 13 — D
(A) Direct Sanskrit-to-Arabic translation is too narrow: intermediaries and several scholarly channels were involved. (B) Arabic scholars adapted as well as preserved incoming methods. (C) The *Sindhind* label does not establish one verbatim surviving source for every passage. (D) Correct: translation and reconstruction took place across Sanskrit, Middle Persian and Arabic traditions. **Source:** B §12; A §1; Satish PDF p. 18 (attests translations, not an exact *Sindhind* chain). **Trap:** do not infer an audited one-text-to-one-text correspondence.

### 14 — C
(A) Muizzuddin belongs after Alp-tigin and Sabuktigin, not before them. (B) Sabuktigin cannot precede the Sind campaign or Alp-tigin. (C) Correct: Sind → Alp-tigin → Sabuktigin → Muizzuddin. (D) Sind predates Alp-tigin's Ghazni centre. **Source:** B §§11, 13–14; A mini-timeline. **Trap:** option text contains no date clues.

### 15 — C
(A) The Kabul/Jalalabad setback did not itself transfer the Punjab plain in the same campaign; Punjab incorporation was extended. (B) Hindu Shahi resistance continued after those reverses. (C) Correct: the losses reduced north-western strategic depth. (D) They did not establish Ghaznavid revenue administration across the Ganga plain. **Source:** B §§13, 15; A §2; Satish PDF p. 71. **Trap:** an enabling loss was not a complete or immediate conquest.

### 16 — A
(A) Correct: a raid did not require lasting administration at every target, unlike a maintained frontier base. (B) Punjab supported repeated operations and was not merely a single target yielding tribute. (C) Plundering a town did not by itself create a recurring revenue centre there. (D) Frontier control aided supply and revenue, not prestige alone. **Source:** B §§13–14; A §2. **Trap:** raid, tribute, occupation and durable rule differ.

### 17 — C
(A) A treasure claim cannot isolate motive: plunder, political prestige and religious symbolism could coexist. (B) Repetition among court chronicles alone does not independently audit a sum. (C) Correct: the precise figure requires corroboration before use as a quantitative fact. (D) A raid's claimed booty does not measure regular provincial revenue or expenses. **Source:** B §13; W MCQ 37 (practice only). **Trap:** do not convert triumphal figures into audited statistics.

### 18 — A
(A) Correct: Al-Biruni used Sanskrit learning comparatively, with elite and north-western access limits. (B) Textual informants cannot stand for every social group's practice. (C) His location and sources do not make his work a survey of all Indian regions. (D) The account benefits from comparison with material and other textual evidence. **Source:** B §§13, 17. **Trap:** neither reject nor universalise a learned outsider.

### 19 — C
(A) Chandawar came after both Tarain battles. (B) Second Tarain followed Gujarat and First Tarain. (C) Correct: Gujarat reverse → First Tarain → Second Tarain → Chandawar. (D) Gujarat preceded both battles at Tarain, not followed the first. **Source:** B §14. **Trap:** First and Second Tarain had different victors; the options omit dates.

### 20 — B
(A) Two successful defences do not establish permanent common command across Rajput courts. (B) Correct: Ghurid reverses show that later success was contingent rather than foreordained. (C) The later approach through Punjab contradicts an exclusively Gujarat route. (D) A frontier base could still facilitate subsequent campaigning despite defeats. **Source:** B §§14–16; A §2. **Trap:** structural advantages are not predestination.

### 21 — D
(A) A poem about an appointment cannot alone establish that an appointee exercised sustained power. (B) Victory and promised tribute are claims, not evidence of recurrent administration. (C) One title-bearing coin at a contested town alone does not establish broad, durable control. (D) Correct: garrisons, delegated command and regular revenues jointly indicate continuing enforcement, though not necessarily fully consolidated sovereignty. **Source:** B §§14, 17. **Trap:** a battlefield or titulature claim does not equal lasting jurisdiction.

### 22 — D
(A) Ghurid mounted warfare did not automatically yield victory in Gujarat; local tactics and supply mattered. (B) Bases mattered, but allies, opponents and decisions still shaped results. (C) Rivalry mattered, but tactics, logistics and resources also varied. (D) Correct: mobility worked in conjunction with logistics, command, finance and choices. **Source:** B §15; A §2. **Trap:** none of cavalry, bases or rivalry is sufficient alone.

### 23 — D
(A) Rajput lineages were diverse, without a permanent standing command over them all. (B) Regional courts did sometimes form alliances; these were not absent by definition. (C) Forts helped defence but did not substitute for coordinating states. (D) Correct: regional capacity coexisted with changing coalitions and coordination limits. **Source:** B §§9, 16. **Trap:** neither absent capacity nor imaginary permanent unity.

### 24 — C
(A) Persian language at court does not prove Iranian ethnic origin. (B) Islamic affiliation and Iranian court idioms were compatible. (C) Correct: Turkish elites could adopt Persian language and court practice. (D) Persianate denotes a cultural-administrative idiom, not a religion. **Source:** B §17; A §§1, 4; Satish PDF p. 70. **Trap:** ethnicity, religion and court language are distinct.

### 25 — D
(A) Narrative detail is not a reason to outrank nearer records without corroboration. (B) Surviving *Raso* recensions are later, not direct eyewitness reports of Tarain. (C) The time of the events narrated cannot date the surviving versions. (D) Correct: later Rajput memory can be tested against nearer inscriptions, coins and texts. **Source:** B §§16–17. **Trap:** even the nearer *Prithviraja Vijaya* is eulogistic, not a neutral transcript.

### 26 — B
(A) Hereditary, service-free ownership misdescribes a ruler-controlled military-revenue assignment. (B) Correct: assigned revenue supported service and troop maintenance under ruler authority. (C) The assignment concerned collection of state revenue, not transfer of proprietary title to land. (D) It conferred no unconditional rights over cultivators' persons or labour. **Source:** A §§1–2, 4; B §15; Part I PDF p. 9 (OCR imperfect). **Trap:** a revenue assignment is not ownership of cultivators.

### 27 — C
(A) A second court narrative may echo the first's triumphal rhetoric rather than establish the duration of rule. (B) An undated, unprovenanced coin cannot fix the period or extent of control. (C) Correct: local grants, dated forts and rival coinages can test regional continuity and contestation; their precise implication depends on context. (D) A later poem documents memory rather than independent administrative reach. **Source:** B §17. **Trap:** triangulate independent, dated kinds of evidence.

### 28 — C
(A) A Chaulukya victory at Gujarat does not prove all regional courts submitted to one command. (B) Separate courts defended fortified centres as well as campaigning in the field. (C) Correct: independent capacity and resistance continued amid regional rivalries. (D) A battlefield loss did not extinguish the revenues of all regional polities. **Source:** B §§14–16. **Trap:** military defeat does not imply wholesale institutional disappearance.
