### 1 — A
(A) Correct: dated grants and forts directly test whether successor states retained fiscal and defensive capacity. (B) Pratihara titulature concerns remembered imperial legitimacy, not successor administration. (C) Chola trade records test southern continuity, not the proposed northern vacuum. (D) Ghurid battle accounts test military encounters, not the intervening regional institutions. **Source:** B §§1, 9, 17. **Trap:** imperial fragmentation is not institutional extinction.

### 2 — D
(A) Chola sea power did not counterbalance the Ghaznavid land base in Punjab. (B) Southern commercial strength cannot quantify the revenues of northern courts. (C) No common Chola–Hindu Shahi frontier strategy is established by the southern campaigns. (D) Correct: strong southern agrarian and maritime networks defeat an all-India collapse inference without denying north-western reverses. **Source:** B §§1, 9. **Trap:** keep regional scales separate.

### 3 — C
(A) Grants from several places do not establish simultaneous or uniform change. (B) Subordinate chiefs could hold different obligations and rights; the dataset does not show identical powers. (C) Correct: compare local fiscal and agrarian patterns before proposing a general model. (D) Temples and chiefs could coexist in layered authority; replacement is not demonstrated. **Source:** B §9; A §2. **Trap:** neither universal “feudalism” nor uniform institutions follow from grants.

### 4 — D
(A) Worship with consent is what the report describes. (B) Residence under a local ruler is compatible with the report. (C) Mosque permission illustrates negotiated religious accommodation, not necessarily equality elsewhere. (D) Correct as the **unwarranted** inference: permission to worship does not transfer port jurisdiction. **Source:** B §§1, 10; A §1. **Trap:** the question asks what the evidence does NOT establish.

### 5 — B
(A) Diplomatic presence may signal talks or gifts; a continuing military alliance needs separate evidence. (B) Correct: collecting revenue under armed protection supports a territorial governing claim, though not complete control of every village. (C) Settlers may negotiate port residence; that alone does not establish fiscal jurisdiction. (D) Translation may occur at court without delegation of territorial command. **Source:** B §§10–11, 14. **Trap:** distinguish contact from enforceable rule.

### 6 — D
(A) A lower-Sind customs dispute might contribute to maritime tensions, but it excludes the campaign's wider territorial context. (B) The campaign reached Multan through lower Sind; a Multan-only tax quarrel cannot replace that sequence. (C) A specific pre-appointment succession dispute is not established as the campaign's trigger by this source. (D) Correct: maritime insecurity must be situated within Umayyad imperial, fiscal and frontier interests. **Source:** B §11. **Trap:** do not promote a plausible local trigger into an exhaustive causal account.

### 7 — C
(A) Some grants name or otherwise identify an authority; this is not an intrinsic limitation of the source type. (B) The grant can bear on the beneficiary's local legal or fiscal status, not merely a ruler's intentions. (C) Correct: one local arrangement does not demonstrate uniform treatment across Sind's communities. (D) A dated early grant can illuminate early policy, though its scope remains limited. **Source:** B §11. **Trap:** do not generalise from one community's negotiated terms.

### 8 — A
(A) Correct: Arab Sind precedes the Ghaznavid Punjab base, Ghurid delegation and Delhi's independent Sultanate. (B) Ghurid appointments follow, not precede, the Ghaznavid base. (C) Arab Sind precedes Ghaznavid Punjab. (D) Ghurid delegation precedes independent Sultanate authority at Delhi. **Source:** B §§11, 13–14; A timeline; `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/00_Master-Chronology.md` §§1–2. **Trap:** distinguish frontier, delegated and independent rule.

### 9 — A
(A) Correct: the *Chachnama* reveals a later Persian author's framing of conquest and authority. (B) Its purported eighth-century dialogue is not a verbatim court record. (C) Naming Debal does not independently identify an excavated site. (D) Narrative numbers are not audited garrison receipts. **Source:** B §11. **Trap:** distinguish what the narrative says from what it independently proves.

### 10 — A
(A) Correct: securely dated layers plus independent geographical fit strengthen a contested identification. (B) A later narrative shows Debal was remembered as a port, not where it stood. (C) Toponymic similarity alone cannot establish site identity. (D) A widely distributed coin type indicates exchange, not the port's name. **Source:** B §§11, 17; W MCQs 23, 39. **Trap:** Banbhore/Debal remains a debated attribution.

### 11 — C
(A) The *Sindhind* label alone does not fix a unique original or exact translation chain. (B) Arabic astronomy incorporated scholarly adaptation rather than verbatim preservation alone. (C) Correct: numerals and astronomy moved through multilingual, multistage translation and adaptation. (D) Scholars worked across Sind, Persian Gulf and Abbasid networks, not exclusively within Arab-ruled Sind. **Source:** B §12; Satish Chandra, `books/medival_history/Satish Chandra History of Medieval India.pdf`, p. 18. **Trap:** do not invent one-to-one textual lineages.

### 12 — D
(A) Persian was used beyond ethnically Iranian dynasties; court idiom does not identify dynasty descent. (B) Court usage does not show what language all soldiers spoke. (C) Language alone cannot establish which legal traditions governed the army. (D) Correct: Persianate court usage is compatible with Turkish ancestry. **Source:** A §§1, 4; B §17. **Trap:** cultural idiom is not an ethnic or institutional census.

### 13 — A
(A) Correct: losing frontier depth eased movement but campaigns, logistics and resistance still determined outcomes. (B) Kabul–Jalalabad road control had direct military-logistic implications as well as trade implications. (C) Kabul-area reverses preceded the extended process of gaining a Punjab base; the two were not one achievement. (D) Rajput rulers retained forts as defensive resources after frontier reverses. **Source:** B §§13–16; A §2; Part I PDF p. 9. **Trap:** strategic opportunity is not automatic conquest.

### 14 — B
(A) Sabuktigin's Shahi conflict is correctly placed, but Somnath was Mahmud's raid, not Muizzuddin's. (B) Correct: Sabuktigin's Shahi wars preceded Muizzuddin's Ghurid expansion. (C) Mahmud gained a Punjab base; Jayapala's opponent was Sabuktigin and then Mahmud, not the later Muizzuddin. (D) Alp-tigin established the Ghazni power centre, but Sabuktigin belonged to the Ghaznavid, not Ghurid, phase. **Source:** B §§11, 13–14; A timeline. **Trap:** each near-matched pair still needs both halves checked.

### 15 — C
(A) Whether a stated total counts endowments is a possible conjecture, not the demonstrated problem with the numerical claim. (B) The source does not establish that an assessed-tribute figure was substituted for booty. (C) Correct: precise booty figures in triumphal writing require independent accounting evidence before use as statistics. (D) Combining multiple raids is another possible but unverified explanation for a large number. **Source:** B §13; W MCQ 37. **Trap:** do not replace an uncorroborated total with an uncorroborated reconstruction of how it arose.

### 16 — D
(A) His setting and learned contacts gave real access to Indian textual traditions. (B) Sanskrit learning improved comparison but did not remove the need to test sources and sampling. (C) His learned account was not a village-by-village occupational enumeration. (D) Correct: elite informants and a limited geographical setting cannot establish the occupations of all cultivators. **Source:** B §13. **Trap:** textual social classification is not a demographic survey.

### 17 — C
(A) Reverses the two Tarain battles. (B) Places Chandawar before Second Tarain. (C) Correct: Gujarat defeat (1178), First Tarain (1191), Second Tarain (1192), Chandawar (1194). (D) Places the Gujarat defeat after First Tarain. **Source:** B §14; `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Medieval-Indian-History/00_Master-Chronology.md` §1. **Trap:** neither Tarain battle is interchangeable.

### 18 — B
(A) Statement 1 is supported, but 3 wrongly infers sustained inter-court command from separate victories. (B) Correct: 1 records resources and 2 correctly qualifies the effect of rivalry on alliances. (C) Includes unsupported 3 alongside true 1 and 2. (D) Includes unsupported 3 and omits true 1. **Source:** B §§9, 14–16. **Trap:** separate regional victories do not establish permanent coordination.

### 19 — C
(A) Earlier Ghaznavid mounted warfare confirms cavalry's use but does not isolate what made Ghurid campaigns succeed. (B) Horse imports show supply networks, but trading links alone cannot explain command or reverses. (C) Correct: organisation and provisioning matter, and Ghurid defeats show horses were not sufficient. (D) Rajput cavalry undercuts any claim of exclusive Turkish possession but alone does not explain particular outcomes. **Source:** B §§15–16; A §2. **Trap:** distinguish possession of horses from successful mounted operations.

### 20 — A
(A) Correct as the **unwarranted** claim: even victories required garrisons, revenue and negotiated control; universal immediate direct rule does not follow. (B) First Tarain was indeed an earlier Prithviraj-led victory, supported independently of the second battle. (C) Delegated commanders had to enforce claims after a battlefield success. (D) Second Tarain gave the Ghurids an important northern breakthrough. **Source:** B §14. **Trap:** victory is not district-by-district administration.

### 21 — C
(A) Inscriptions can preserve dated acts and titles despite praise; automatic priority of eulogy is unwarranted. (B) A later romance's dialogue is not independent confirmation of an earlier inscription. (C) Correct: date, genre, patronage and independent evidence constrain what each source can prove. (D) A fuller later narrative need not be more reliable than a narrower earlier record. **Source:** B §§16–17. **Trap:** neither inscriptional praise nor later romance is self-verifying.

### 22 — C
(A) A revenue assignment does not automatically transfer judicial sovereignty. (B) Hereditary landed property does not follow from an *iqta* assignment. (C) Correct: the service-linked assignment provides income for troops, not private ownership of land. (D) The ruler could control or reassign such revenues; perpetual locality is not entailed. **Source:** A §§1–2, 4; B §15. **Trap:** Topic 07 owns mature Sultanate administration; do not equate revenue rights with freehold.

### 23 — B
(A) Lahore was a Ghaznavid centre, but Aibak served the Ghurids, not its Ghaznavid defenders. (B) Correct: Aibak's delegated Ghurid command bridges military gains and their enforcement; independent rule belongs to Topic 03. (C) The Ghurid Gujarat campaign ended in defeat; it was not a pre-Tarain conquest by Aibak. (D) Alp-tigin established the Ghazni power centre before Aibak's time; Muizzuddin subsequently took Ghazni. **Source:** B §§13–14, 18; A timeline. **Trap:** keep dynasty, frontier base and campaign outcome distinct.

### 24 — B
(A) A court narrative is evidence of the claim it advances, not necessarily its implementation. (B) Correct as the **claim to withhold**: none of these sources surveys every rural locality or demonstrates direct administration throughout. (C) The ruler's title appears on the stipulated coins, but titulature alone cannot map administrative reach. (D) Later poetry may preserve political memory without proving all its events. **Source:** B §§16–17. **Trap:** a title or narrative claim is not a district-level governance map.

### 25 — B
(A) Afghan and Turk are distinct historical/ethnoregional terms; neither labels the entire Ghaznavid line in the same sense. (B) Correct: labels for origin, language and courtly culture can intersect without being synonymous. (C) Persianate describes a cultural-linguistic sphere, not reliably an official's descent. (D) Arab can describe lineage, language or political connection; it does not by itself fix every merchant's religion. **Source:** B §17; A §1. **Trap:** cultural practice, origin and religious affiliation answer different questions.

### 26 — A
(A) Correct: a Punjab base, military mobility and rival courts created opportunities realised through choices and enforcement. (B) Ghurid defeats show frontier advantages did not fix each outcome. (C) Rivalry mattered, but sustained campaigns also needed resources and a base. (D) Mobile victories did not make garrisons, revenue collection and delegated rule dispensable. **Source:** B §§14–16; A §2. **Trap:** explain the interaction, not a deterministic single cause.
