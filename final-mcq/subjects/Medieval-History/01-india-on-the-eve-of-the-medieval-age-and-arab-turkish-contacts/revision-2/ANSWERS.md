### 1 — B
(A) The Hindu Shahis resisted but did not retain Kabul throughout the later Ghaznavid campaigns. (B) Correct: Arab-ruled Sind, merchant settlement in Malabar and the later Ghaznavid frontier around Kabul had different histories. (C) Qasim's campaign established an Arab foothold in Sind toward Multan, not a conquest of Kabul. (D) Ghaznavid expansion ultimately reached the Kabul/Jalalabad frontier; it was not permanently beyond their reach. **Source:** B §§9–14; A mini-timeline. **Trap:** a true Sind/Malabar identification does not establish the wrong frontier chronology.

### 2 — D
(A) Statement 1 is true, but statement 2 is false: these were separate courts, not a single royal command. (B) Adds false statement 2 to true statements 1 and 3. (C) Includes false 2 and omits true 1. (D) Correct: Pratihara decline opened space for successors, and Chola vitality prevents an all-India decline inference. **Source:** B §§1, 9. **Trap:** a shared regional setting does not imply unified dynastic command.

### 3 — A
(A) Correct: a *samanta* title in one inscription cannot fix an all-India institutional model without comparable evidence elsewhere. (B) Dating the grant makes its local evidence stronger, not proof of a uniform feudal rank. (C) A grant's formula cannot establish identical revenue rights in distinct regions. (D) A donor's name identifies a local actor, not the office of every regional ruler. **Source:** B §§9, 17. **Trap:** generalise neither a title nor a grant formula beyond its evidentiary reach.

### 4 — A
(A) Correct: Indian professionals in Baghdad and Arab traders in Malabar demonstrate commercial and intellectual contacts beyond warfare. (B) A merchant community, even one allowed mosques by Rashtrakuta rulers, does not make a coast an Abbasid tax dependency. (C) Indian merchants remained active in Persian Gulf networks rather than being displaced by Arab settlement. (D) Maritime and scholarly circulation preceded the Ghaznavids' Indian frontier campaigns. **Source:** B §§10, 12; A §1; `books/medival_history/dokumen.pub_medieval-india-from-sultanat-to-the-mughals-part-i-8124119899-9788124119891.pdf` p. 8. **Trap:** movement of people and knowledge is not proof of fiscal subordination or conquest.

### 5 — C
(A) A shipowner's family and trading ties imply residence and commerce, not local rule. (B) Gift exchange is diplomacy, not administration of the neighbouring court. (C) Correct: a garrison imposing recurrent taxes in conquered towns most directly signals coercive state presence, though the wider region need not be fully governed. (D) Permission to finance a mosque indicates negotiated worship or residence, not sovereignty. **Source:** B §§10–11, 14. **Trap:** distinguish mercantile/diplomatic links from recurrent fiscal enforcement.

### 6 — B
(A) A security episode alone cannot explain the campaign's fiscal goals; revenue must be examined as an additional factor. (B) Correct: frontier politics, imperial aims and revenues belonged to the campaign's context alongside maritime insecurity. (C) A port incident did not eliminate the independent role of frontier politics. (D) Insecurity does not logically exclude Umayyad imperial expansion. **Source:** B §11. **Trap:** do not inflate one reported trigger into a complete causal explanation.

### 7 — A
(A) Correct: the early-thirteenth-century Persian redaction relates eighth-century Sind events, so its claims need corroboration. (B) Ali Kufi's claim to an Arabic antecedent does not provide an extant original against which its wording can be checked. (C) A narrated speech is not a verifiable contemporary court transcript merely because an earlier source is claimed. (D) Its Persian composition is centuries later than Qasim's campaign; the existence of later copies does not move that composition into the eighth century. **Source:** B §11. **Trap:** distinguish the reported event, claimed lost precursor and surviving redaction.

### 8 — D
(A) Titulature witnesses an assertion of authority, even where its practical reach is unknown. (B) Securely excavated coins reached that context, subject to provenance checks. (C) Coins can signal monetary circulation. (D) Correct as the inference *not* justified: coins in a frontier zone do not establish continuous revenue collection throughout it. **Source:** B §17. **Trap:** a ruler's coins are not a tax-office map.

### 9 — B
(A) No single regime across all towns and countryside follows from the varied arrangements reported in Sind. (B) Correct: local bargaining and group-specific fiscal terms make negotiation the safer procedure. (C) Appointed Arab officers could work alongside retained local intermediaries. (D) Payment could coexist with resistance or negotiated submission; a revenue record does not prove unopposed integration. **Source:** B §11. **Trap:** avoid turning a local fiscal trace into a universal social or political verdict.

### 10 — C
(A) Sanskrit-linked astronomy did circulate in Arabic scholarly settings. (B) Translators and scholars adapted rather than merely reproduced inherited calculations. (C) Correct as the claim needing verification: the *Sindhind* label does not identify an independently verified, exact rendering of one surviving Sanskrit manuscript. (D) Knowledge could pass through Sanskrit, Middle Persian and Arabic channels. **Source:** B §12; `books/medival_history/Satish Chandra History of Medieval India.pdf` p. 18. **Trap:** a transmission label does not prove exact text-to-text identity.

### 11 — D
(A) Fragmentation weakened rather than restored direct caliphal authority over regional commanders. (B) Regionalisation did not terminate Persian-language court and administrative culture. (C) Turkish regional military elites rose well before the twelfth-century Ghurid victories in India. (D) Correct: weakening Abbasid unity opened space for competing regional powers and Turkish military leaders. **Source:** A §§1–2; B §13; `books/medival_history/dokumen.pub_medieval-india-from-sultanat-to-the-mughals-part-i-8124119899-9788124119891.pdf` pp. 8–9. **Trap:** neither a restored unitary caliphate nor the end of Persianate culture followed fragmentation.

### 12 — C
(A) Jayapala belongs to the Hindu Shahi frontier wars, but Ghazni's later Ghurid seizure was Muizzuddin's, not Alp-tigin's. (B) Sabuktigin fought the Shahis, but Qasim led the earlier Arab campaign in Sind, not Mahmud. (C) Correct: Alp-tigin established a Ghazni power centre and Sabuktigin fought on the Hindu Shahi frontier. (D) Muizzuddin took Ghazni for the Ghurids; Alp-tigin is associated with the Ghazni power centre, not the Sabuktigin–Jayapala frontier-war pairing. **Source:** B §13; A mini-timeline; `books/medival_history/dokumen.pub_medieval-india-from-sultanat-to-the-mughals-part-i-8124119899-9788124119891.pdf` p. 9. **Trap:** one correct half of a pair does not rescue its misplaced second half.

### 13 — A
(A) Correct: improved road access facilitates reinforcement and movement, conditional on control and resources. (B) Roads helped campaigns but did not dispense with the contest for Shahi frontier towns. (C) Overland access did not immediately displace maritime traders. (D) Logistics could aid campaigns but never guaranteed submission across the plains. **Source:** A §2; B §§13, 15; `books/medival_history/dokumen.pub_medieval-india-from-sultanat-to-the-mughals-part-i-8124119899-9788124119891.pdf` p. 9. **Trap:** an operational advantage is neither automatic territorial control nor maritime displacement.

### 14 — B
(A) Religious legitimation could matter but cannot alone account for the route, economic incentives and political setting. (B) Correct: wealth, political symbolism and legitimation could interact in the campaign. (C) Revenue alone fails to explain symbolic and legitimating dimensions. (D) A raid did not establish enduring Ghaznavid fiscal administration of Gujarat's coast. **Source:** B §13. **Trap:** reject exclusive-motive explanations and distinguish a raid from recurring territorial taxation.

### 15 — C
(A) Learning Sanskrit improved Al-Biruni's access but did not make all regional experience equally represented. (B) He treated mathematical, religious and other intellectual subjects; his work was not confined to one domain. (C) Correct: scholarly informants and limited geographical access constrain the representativeness of a statement about caste. (D) His work belongs to the Ghaznavid milieu, not Qasim's eighth-century Sind. **Source:** B §13. **Trap:** a comparative scholar's textual classifications are not an all-India demographic census.

### 16 — A
(B) Second Tarain follows Gujarat and First Tarain. (D) Chandawar is after Second Tarain. (C) Gujarat is earlier than First Tarain. (A) Correct: 1178 → 1191 → 1192 → 1194.  **Source:** B §14. **Trap:** dates deliberately withheld from the ordering choices.

### 17 — C
(A) Ghurid victory at Second Tarain did not end resistance or establish administration throughout the upper Ganga plain. (B) The first encounter ended in Prithviraj's victory; the second in a Ghurid victory. (C) Correct: a Ghurid reverse at First Tarain preceded adaptation and the later breakthrough at Second Tarain.  (D) First Tarain was a Ghurid defeat, not the start of its direct rule at Delhi. **Source:** B §14; `books/medival_history/dokumen.pub_medieval-india-from-sultanat-to-the-mughals-part-i-8124119899-9788124119891.pdf` pp. 13–15. **Trap:** distinguish a battlefield reversal from later political consolidation.

### 18 — D
(A) Mounted archers could use coordinated movement for feints and pursuit; the report is consistent with tactical flexibility. (B) Cavalry manoeuvre could be supported by routes and supplies; it does not operate apart from logistics. (C) Keeping a reserve is a command choice that may affect the outcome. (D) Correct as the inference *not* established: Indian rulers also fielded cavalry, and one campaign cannot prove a universal lack of mounted forces or reserves. **Source:** B §§15–16; A §2. **Trap:** reported tactical advantage does not prove absence of the same military resource everywhere else.

### 19 — B
(A) Status was not fixed by a single descent line; political legitimation and region mattered. (B) Correct: diverse lineages and claims to Rajput status developed within changing political and agrarian contexts. (C) Formation preceded and outlasted the Ghurid victories; no single battle created the identity. (D) Agrarian expansion and temple networks could participate in, not be bypassed by, regional status formation. **Source:** B §§9, 16. **Trap:** do not mistake a historically evolving status for a timeless race or one-battle outcome.

### 20 — D
(A) Inscriptions can themselves be selective or panegyric; priority is not automatic. (B) A longer poetic narrative does not override an inscription simply by offering more detail; each has genre-specific limits. (C) Interested sources can still preserve claims testable against other evidence. (D) Correct: date and contextualise both sources before seeking corroboration. **Source:** B §§16–17. **Trap:** neither narrative length nor material form alone resolves a conflict.

### 21 — B
(A) Ruler authority structured revenue assignments linked to military service. (B) Correct as the claim *to reject*: an *iqta* revenue assignment did not necessarily turn into hereditary ownership of land. (C) An assignee could collect assigned revenue to support troops. (D) Service and troop maintenance were central to the institution. **Source:** A §§2, 4; B §§15, 18. **Trap:** an entitlement to collect revenue is not a necessary transfer of proprietary title.

### 22 — D
(A) Appointment alone does not establish settled administration; garrisons and recurrent enforcement must be checked. (B) Delegation does not prove each conquered district belonged to one functioning fiscal system. (C) Commanders still needed bases, armed enforcement and negotiation with local allies. (D) Correct: delegated command can mark expansion while control of each district remains to be demonstrated. **Source:** B §§11, 14. **Trap:** an appointment is evidence of a claim and a mechanism, not proof of full regional consolidation.

### 23 — D
(A) Persian-language court practice did not require rulers to be ethnically Persian. (B) Turkish and other non-Iranian-born rulers could adopt Persianate culture, so birthplace does not delimit it. (C) Persianate language and administration spread beyond Baghdad; it is not confined to one Abbasid centre. (D) Correct: the term concerns a Persian-language courtly and administrative idiom adopted across political lineages. **Source:** A §§1, 4; B §17. **Trap:** distinguish language and political culture from ethnicity or one dynasty's geography.

### 24 — A
(A) Correct: agent and court idiom, episodic raid and sustained rule, reported claim and independently tested evidence must be kept distinct. (B) Merchant and envoy are different roles, but a raid does not by itself prove lasting revenue control. (C) Court language need not establish ancestry, but payment of tax cannot establish total replacement of earlier institutions or groups. (D) Dynastic and religious categories differ, but victory does not itself establish complete control of the contested territory. **Source:** B §§10, 14, 17; A §§1–2. **Trap:** reject an otherwise sound distinction when its companion inference overstates what one trace proves.

### 25 — C
(A) Searchable words still do not authenticate speeches as contemporary Dahir-era transcripts. (B) Searchable place-names do not resolve a debated port identification without spatial and archaeological checks. (C) Correct: digitisation facilitates access, but a later copy is not thereby contemporary with the events it describes. (D) Searchability does not validate narrated dates without genre, provenance and independent comparison. **Source:** B §§11, 17; W MCQs 23, 26–27. **Trap:** digital discoverability is not historical corroboration.

### 26 — D
(A) One later chronicle's omission of a title cannot establish the absence of earlier regional institutions. (B) A patron's panegyric can attest political self-presentation but not by itself map wider institutional capacity. (C) Raids and their local consequences give a narrow frontier picture, not a survey of successor states. (D) Correct: successor courts, grants, forts and regional trade supply different kinds of evidence for continued political and economic capacity. **Source:** B §§1, 9, 17. **Trap:** investigate institutions across regions instead of treating one dynasty's decline as a vacuum.
