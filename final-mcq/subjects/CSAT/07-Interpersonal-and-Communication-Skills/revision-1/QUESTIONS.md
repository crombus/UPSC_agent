# csat-07 — Revision 1: Interpersonal and Communication Skills

All scenarios and options are original. Each item presents four courses of action or statements; choose the single most appropriate one for the public servant described. Use only the facts given in the item.

Practice budget: 30 questions in 36 minutes (about 72 seconds per question). Practice scoring: +2.5 for a correct answer, −0.83 for a wrong answer, 0 for a skipped question.

### R1-Q01
A block health officer has explained a revised vaccination schedule to a room of village health workers through a forty-minute lecture. No one asked questions, but two workers later tell the supervisor they are unsure which dose falls due on which day. What should the officer do next?

A. Treat the lecture as the completed instruction and circulate the same slide deck to every worker through the group messaging application.
B. Assume the workers understood because no questions were raised, and record the training as completed for the monthly reporting cycle.
C. Ask each worker to state the schedule in their own words, correct any gaps they reveal, and provide a one-page written version.
D. Schedule a repeat lecture for the following month so that workers can attend again and hear the full explanation at a slower pace.

### R1-Q02
A district collector must inform all block officers that the deadline for beneficiary verification has moved five days earlier. The change is binding, and the office may need proof of who was informed if a dispute arises later. Which mode of communication is most appropriate?

A. A formal written circular addressed to every block officer, stating the revised date, the reason for the change and the basis for it.
B. An informal message in the district officers' group chat, asking the senior officers to pass the revised date on to their colleagues.
C. A telephone call to the two most senior block officers, leaving them to decide how and when the remaining officers should be told.
D. A notice on the district office noticeboard, which any visitor in the coming week could read and which staff may check when convenient.

### R1-Q03
At a grievance hearing, a pensioner says that a delayed pension file reflects a pattern of officers treating applicants like nuisances. The hearing officer cannot tell whether the complaint concerns file handling, the delay or the conduct of staff. What is the most appropriate first response?

A. Explain that all files are processed in order of receipt, and that the complaint about staff behaviour lies outside the scope of the hearing.
B. Paraphrase the concern as a delayed pension, then ask which file step and which interaction the pensioner found difficult.
C. Agree that staff have been dismissive, and promise that the officers named will be moved to other desks before the next hearing.
D. Ask the pensioner to submit the complaint in writing and defer any discussion until the written complaint has been received and examined.

### R1-Q04
In a consultation on a new licensing rule, a trader says the rule is unfair to small sellers, but he also accepts that the underlying safety problem is real. A colleague suggests that the trader's objection is simply resistance to change. What should the hearing officer do?

A. Dismiss the objection as resistance to change, since the trader has accepted the safety problem and therefore has no ground left to dispute the rule's design.
B. Agree with the trader that the rule is unfair, so that he feels heard, and then confirm that the licence will still be issued under the existing procedure.
C. Ask the trader to withdraw the objection, since the safety problem is accepted and a stakeholder cannot change the rule once the consultation has opened.
D. Summarise the trader's view accurately, separate the safety fact from his concern about small sellers, and record the concern as an input to the decision.

### R1-Q05
A notice explaining a welfare scheme uses terms such as "eligibility convergence" and "provisional adjudication". The helpline reports that most calls concern the same confusion. What is the best remedy?

A. Rewrite the notice in plain language with a short list of eligibility steps and worked examples, then test it with a few applicants before reissue.
B. Add a glossary of the technical terms at the end of the notice and keep the wording unchanged, so that the official text stands for any later review.
C. Ask the helpline staff to handle every call by reading the official notice word for word, so that every applicant receives exactly the same explanation.
D. Post the notice on the scheme's website with a link to the detailed guidelines, so that applicants who need more detail can consult the full document.

### R1-Q06
Two section heads have issued conflicting written instructions to a clerk on the same file, and each has told the clerk that the other's order is obsolete. The clerk has stopped work on the file. What should the administrative officer do?

A. Instruct the clerk to follow whichever order came first, so that the file moves immediately and the question of which head is correct can be settled later.
B. Ask both heads to present their instructions at the next staff meeting, so that the group can decide which order will govern this file and future files.
C. Establish which authority controls the file, issue one corrected written instruction, withdraw the obsolete version and confirm it with the clerk.
D. Ask the clerk to follow the instruction from the head who issued the more recent order, so that work resumes today without a further review.

### R1-Q07
A ward committee meeting on a rural road project is planned online, but several members live where connectivity is unreliable, and one member has a visual impairment that makes the shared screen difficult to use. What should the convenor do?

A. Proceed with the online meeting, since members who cannot connect can read the minutes afterwards and send their comments by email later.
B. Cancel the meeting and reschedule it for a later date, when all members can attend in person at the same time and place.
C. Record the meeting for members to watch in their own time, and collect questions through a single email address one week after the meeting.
D. Offer telephone and in-person options, circulate an accessible agenda beforehand, and confirm each member's input in writing.

### R1-Q08
A junior engineer reported a minor measurement error to a colleague but not to the supervisor, saying he feared being blamed in front of the team. The supervisor now learns of the error and wants to deal with it. What is the best next step?

A. Call the engineer into a team meeting and ask him to explain the error in front of colleagues, so that everyone learns the correct procedure at the same time.
B. Warn the engineer that unreported errors can lead to disciplinary action, and ask him to submit a written explanation before the end of the day.
C. Leave the matter with the colleague who knows about it, since the colleague can pass on the correct procedure without involving the supervisor directly.
D. Speak with the engineer privately in a neutral tone, explain that the error can be corrected without blame, and agree on the reporting route he will use.

### R1-Q09
At a review meeting, a colleague repeatedly interrupts an officer while she presents a data set, and the chair has not intervened. What response is best?

A. Politely say she would like to finish the data point, propose her next step, and ask the chair to note the questions for later.
B. Stop speaking, let the colleague finish, and then say that she prefers not to present in future meetings where other members interrupt her.
C. Speak over the colleague in a sharper voice, and say that interruptions of this kind waste the time of everyone in the room.
D. Say nothing during the meeting, and later raise the matter informally with the colleague's supervisor in a short note.

### R1-Q10
A subordinate's file has missed a deadline because the task was never clearly assigned to anyone. Which response by the supervisor is assertive rather than passive or aggressive?

A. "It is not important, so let us move on and hope nothing similar happens again," said without raising the question of who held the task.
B. "Whoever is responsible has wasted the whole office's time, and it will not happen again on my watch," said in front of the team.
C. The deadline was missed because nobody held the task, so I need to confirm the owner today and agree how deadlines will be tracked.
D. "I suppose these things happen with this team, so I will leave the matter for someone else to sort out at the next review."

### R1-Q11
A citizen who has waited four hours for a certificate becomes loud and insults the counter staff, who feel threatened and angry. What is the most appropriate immediate response?

A. Match the citizen's volume to make clear that the abuse is unacceptable, and order the citizen to leave the counter area at once.
B. Pause, lower your voice, acknowledge the long wait, explain the next certificate step, and say service continues only if the abuse stops.
C. Refer the citizen to the supervisor without responding, so that the counter staff can continue serving the citizens who are waiting quietly.
D. Ask the citizen to complete a written complaint form before any further discussion, since spoken complaints cannot be recorded accurately.

### R1-Q12
Two departments both want the only conference room on the same afternoon. The first insists that it needs the room; the second insists that it also needs the room. Their underlying needs are a confidential interview and a video call with a foreign office. What is the most useful next step for the mediating officer?

A. Allocate the room to the department with the higher rank, since disputes over shared space between departments are settled by seniority.
B. Divide the room with a temporary partition, so that each department has an equal share of the same space for the afternoon.
C. Ask both departments to take turns on alternate afternoons, with the department that submitted its request first given precedence.
D. Ask each department why it needs the room, identify its privacy and connectivity needs, and then test alternatives that meet both.

### R1-Q13
A complaint alleges that a contractor skipped a required quality test. The contractor says the test was done but the register was lost. Which step should the inspecting officer take first?

A. Check the test records and site logs against the complaint, and verify whether the test was done before the next stage of work began.
B. Accept the contractor's explanation as the working fact, because the contractor who controls the site is more likely to know what was done.
C. Decide the penalty at once, since a missing register is itself evidence of non-compliance whether or not the test was done.
D. Ask the contractor to agree a settlement with the complainant, so that the dispute can be closed before any finding is made.

### R1-Q14
A supervisor suggests that, because the dispute is close, the department should pay half of the reimbursement a vendor has claimed, although the vendor's invoices do not meet the reimbursement rules. Which action is most appropriate?

A. Pay half the claim, since a split settlement ends the dispute quickly and keeps the vendor willing to supply the department in future.
B. Pay half the claim but record it as a provisional advance, so that the balance can be negotiated after the vendor corrects the invoices.
C. Reject the split because the invoices fail the reimbursement rules, state the grounds in writing and invite corrected invoices.
D. Refer the question to the vendor and let the vendor propose the amount, since the vendor knows best what it has spent on the work.

### R1-Q15
A junior officer tells a senior that a colleague has repeatedly made unwelcome sexual remarks in the office. The senior believes the junior is exaggerating and wants to resolve the matter informally. What is the best action?

A. Speak privately with the colleague first, ask for an explanation and an assurance that it will stop, and report the matter only if the remarks continue.
B. Record the complaint in writing, protect the complainant from retaliation, and refer it to the designated authority under the applicable process.
C. Arrange a joint meeting of the junior and the colleague to hear both accounts, so that the two can agree on how to work together in future.
D. Tell the junior to raise the issue with the colleague directly, since personal disputes between officers are best resolved between the people concerned.

### R1-Q16
A licence was wrongly cancelled because of a data-entry error in the department, and the applicant has been unable to work for three weeks. Which public communication is best?

A. Tell the applicant that the error was caused by the software vendor, and that the department will investigate the vendor before deciding on any compensation.
B. Wait until the correction is complete before writing to the applicant, so that the letter can state a settled outcome rather than an incomplete one.
C. Write to say the cancellation was an administrative error for which the department cannot be held responsible, and that a corrected licence will be considered.
D. Acknowledge the error, explain the known facts without speculation, state the date of correction, and give a named contact and an update schedule.

### R1-Q17
A message circulating on social media claims that a water supply will be cut for a week. The department has not decided on any cut. What should the department do?

A. Verify the facts internally, issue an official statement that no cut has been decided, and state when the next update will come.
B. Post a short denial on social media that the rumour is false, without giving any detail, so that the message does not gain more attention than necessary.
C. Wait until a cut is actually decided, since responding to rumours before a decision is made would create unnecessary alarm among residents.
D. Ask the originator of the message to remove it and to submit proof of the claim, before the department makes any statement about the water supply.

### R1-Q18
A welfare officer learns that a widow's pension is delayed because of a medical condition she disclosed in confidence. The next officer who handles the file needs to know its status in order to process it. What should the welfare officer do?

A. Send the full medical details to the next officer by email, so that the file is processed without delay and no information is lost in the handover.
B. Tell the next officer only that a medical verification is pending, and transfer the detail through the secure file channel with minimum content.
C. Keep the matter with the welfare officer alone, since the widow asked for confidentiality, and tell the next officer nothing about the file until she returns.
D. Post the status on the department's shared tracking board, so that every officer who handles pensions can see why the file is delayed and plan accordingly.

### R1-Q19
Residents oppose a proposed bus route because it passes their school. The planning officer has evidence that the alternative route increases travel time for some students. What is the best next step?

A. Announce the final route at the public meeting with its reasons, and consider any objections only after construction has begun.
B. Hear the residents' safety and access concerns, explain the travel-time evidence and constraints, and record unresolved objections on file.
C. Ask the school to choose which route is acceptable, since the school knows the needs of its students and their families best.
D. Conduct a survey that offers residents only the two routes the planning team prefers, so that the decision rests on a clear majority.

### R1-Q21
A department wants to measure public support for a new recycling scheme. Which question is best written for an unbiased survey?

C. Would you support a recycling scheme that the council has already approved and that wastes money on needless collection trips?
