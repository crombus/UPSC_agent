# csat-07 — Revision 1: answers and explanations

All items are original judgement items; none is a UPSC PYQ. Keys apply the owner principles in `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/CSAT/basic/07_Interpersonal-and-Communication-Skills.md` and `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/CSAT/advanced/07_Interpersonal-Communication-Negotiation-and-Public-Dealing.md`. Each entry states the principle behind the key, the error pattern in every option, the examiner trap, a shortcut and a time target.

**R1-Q01 — C.**
- **A:** Treats a one-way circulation of the same slides as completion; delivery is not comprehension.
- **B:** Reads silence as understanding and records completion that the workers' later questions contradict.
- **C (key):** Asks each worker to restate the schedule, which tests understanding, exposes gaps and gives a written reference.
- **D:** Repeats the one-way lecture with no confirmation step, so the same gap can recur.
- **Trap:** a message that was sent or heard is not a message that was understood.
- **Shortcut:** an option with no confirmation step cannot close a known gap.
- **Time target:** 50 seconds.

**R1-Q02 — A.**
- **A (key):** A formal written circular matches a binding instruction that needs a traceable record of who was told.
- **B:** An informal relay through a group chat has no record of receipt and depends on senior officers passing it on.
- **C:** A telephone call is oral, leaves the cascade to two people and produces no record.
- **D:** A public notice is non-targeted and cannot prove that each officer was informed.
- **Trap:** "formal" is chosen by purpose and accountability, not by habit.
- **Shortcut:** binding plus a need for proof of receipt means a traceable written channel.
- **Time target:** 45 seconds.

**R1-Q03 — B.**
- **A:** Dismisses a complaint about staff conduct that the office can examine, which skips the listening step.
- **B (key):** Paraphrasing the concern and asking for the specific step and interaction separates facts from the complaint and invites correction.
- **C:** Makes a promise about transfers before any facts are known, which is a false promise.
- **D:** Defers a conversation that can start now, and leaves the pensioner without any response.
- **Trap:** listening is not agreement, and a promise is not a finding.
- **Shortcut:** choose the option that clarifies before it acts.
- **Time target:** 50 seconds.

**R1-Q04 — D.**
- **A:** Labels the objection as resistance without testing it, although the trader has raised a concern about small sellers.
- **B:** Agrees with a claim the officer does not hold and pre-judges the outcome.
- **C:** Tells a stakeholder that consultation cannot change the rule, which contradicts the purpose of consultation.
- **D (key):** Summarises accurately, separates the safety fact from the concern about small sellers and records the concern as an input.
- **Trap:** accepting a valid fact does not oblige the officer to adopt the stakeholder's conclusion, nor to dismiss the concern.
- **Shortcut:** reject any option that ends listening before the concern is recorded.
- **Time target:** 60 seconds.

**R1-Q05 — A.**
- **A (key):** Rewrites the notice in plain language with examples and tests it with applicants, which removes the semantic barrier at source.
- **B:** A glossary keeps the confusing wording at the point of reading, so the barrier stays.
- **C:** Reading the official text word for word to each caller keeps the barrier in place for every caller.
- **D:** Sends applicants to a longer document, which adds burden and does not clarify the notice.
- **Trap:** a glossary helps a reader who already knows to use it; it does not repair the notice.
- **Shortcut:** fix the wording at the source, not at the point of complaint.
- **Time target:** 45 seconds.

**R1-Q06 — C.**
- **A:** Chooses by arrival order, which is not authority over the file.
- **B:** Hands an administrative decision to a meeting, so the controlling instruction remains unresolved in the meantime.
- **C (key):** Identifies the controlling authority, issues one corrected written instruction, withdraws the obsolete version and confirms it with the clerk.
- **D:** Chooses by recency, but a later order is not automatically controlling.
- **Trap:** "first" and "most recent" are tempting proxies for authority.
- **Shortcut:** find who controls the file, then remove the second instruction.
- **Time target:** 55 seconds.

**R1-Q07 — D.**
- **A:** Excludes members who cannot connect, and offers only a delayed written reply.
- **B:** Cancels the meeting for a problem that alternate channels can solve.
- **C:** A recording with a single delayed email channel is one-way and does not make participation real.
- **D (key):** Offers telephone and in-person options, an accessible agenda and written confirmation of each member's input.
- **Trap:** a recording is not an equal discussion, and a convenient single channel can exclude members.
- **Shortcut:** a fix that still leaves someone excluded is incomplete.
- **Time target:** 55 seconds.

**R1-Q08 — D.**
- **A:** Exposes the engineer in front of colleagues, which raises fear rather than reducing it.
- **B:** Threatens discipline and demands a written account the same day, which deepens fear and suppresses future reports.
- **C:** Routes the matter around the supervisor, which weakens accountability.
- **D (key):** A private, neutral conversation that removes blame, states the correction route and agrees how errors will be reported.
- **Trap:** punishment after a voluntary report discourages reporting.
- **Shortcut:** choose the option that changes the reporting incentive.
- **Time target:** 50 seconds.

**R1-Q09 — A.**
- **A (key):** Politely states the need to finish, proposes a next step and asks the chair to record the questions; this is assertive.
- **B:** Withdraws and then issues a conditional threat about future presentations, which is passive-aggressive.
- **C:** Speaks over the colleague and humiliates her, which is aggressive.
- **D:** Defers the matter to a later note, which is passive.
- **Trap:** not responding is not neutrality, and speaking louder is not assertion.
- **Shortcut:** an assertive response contains a specific request.
- **Time target:** 50 seconds.

**R1-Q10 — C.**
- **A:** Calls the missed deadline unimportant and leaves the cause unaddressed, which is passive.
- **B:** Blames the whole team in public, which is aggressive.
- **C (key):** States the fact, names the gap (no owner) and commits to a next step, which is assertive.
- **D:** Accepts the problem as normal and defers it, which is passive.
- **Trap:** a calm quotation can still be passive, so check whether it resolves anything.
- **Shortcut:** look for a fact, a need and a next step.
- **Time target:** 45 seconds.

**R1-Q11 — B.**
- **A:** Matches the citizen's volume and orders the citizen out, which escalates the incident.
- **B (key):** Pauses, lowers the voice, acknowledges the wait, explains the next step and keeps the boundary on abuse.
- **C:** Leaves the staff member to handle the abuse alone and does not address the citizen.
- **D:** Asks for a written form during the incident and wrongly states that spoken complaints cannot be recorded.
- **Trap:** a boundary and an acknowledgement are used together, not one instead of the other.
- **Shortcut:** choose the option that lowers tension and keeps service open.
- **Time target:** 50 seconds.

**R1-Q12 — D.**
- **A:** Allocates the room by rank, a positional rule that ignores the needs.
- **B:** Partitions the room, which fails both the confidentiality and the connectivity interests.
- **C:** Allocates by order of submission, which ignores the needs.
- **D (key):** Asks why each department needs the room, separates positions from interests and tests alternatives that meet both.
- **Trap:** a compromise is not automatically right; test it against interests first.
- **Shortcut:** if the option does not ask why, it is positional.
- **Time target:** 60 seconds.

**R1-Q13 — A.**
- **A (key):** Checks the test records and site logs against the complaint, which is the verifiable first step.
- **B:** Accepts a party's account as the fact before it is checked.
- **C:** Imposes a penalty before establishing whether the test happened.
- **D:** Seeks a settlement before any finding, which pre-empts the facts.
- **Trap:** an absent register is a documentation gap, not proof that the test was skipped.
- **Shortcut:** choose the verification step before any decision.
- **Time target:** 50 seconds.

**R1-Q14 — C.**
- **A:** Pays half a claim the rules do not support, to end the dispute.
- **B:** Pays the same amount under a provisional label, which keeps the unlawful split.
- **C (key):** Rejects the split because the invoices fail the rules, states the grounds in writing and invites corrected invoices.
- **D:** Lets the vendor set the amount, which hands the decision to the party with an interest in it.
- **Trap:** a "provisional" label does not change the entitlement.
- **Shortcut:** reject any option that splits a claim the rules do not support.
- **Time target:** 55 seconds.

**R1-Q15 — B.**
- **A:** Keeps the informal "talk first" route and waits for repetition, which the owner's trap rejects for a serious allegation.
- **B (key):** Records the complaint, protects the complainant and refers it to the designated authority under the applicable process.
- **C:** A joint meeting exposes the complainant to the accused.
- **D:** Pushes the complainant to confront the accused alone.
- **Trap:** informal mediation can harm the complainant when the allegation is serious.
- **Shortcut:** a serious allegation about conduct goes to the designated process.
- **Time target:** 55 seconds.

**R1-Q16 — D.**
- **A:** Blames a vendor without evidence and shifts responsibility away from the department.
- **B:** Waits until the correction is complete, which delays the remedy for a person who cannot work.
- **C:** Denies responsibility and offers only a vague future consideration.
- **D (key):** Acknowledges the error, explains known facts without speculation, gives the correction date and a named contact with an update schedule.
- **Trap:** a department error is not made smaller by moving the blame.
- **Shortcut:** choose the option with a date, a contact and a correction.
- **Time target:** 45 seconds.

**R1-Q17 — A.**
- **A (key):** Verifies internally, issues an official statement that nothing has been decided and gives the next update time.
- **B:** A denial with no detail does not correct the rumour.
- **C:** Waiting lets the false claim spread until a decision is made.
- **D:** Makes the originator's proof a precondition for any public statement, which leaves residents uninformed.
- **Trap:** a non-denial and a silent wait can amplify uncertainty.
- **Shortcut:** choose the option that is factual and dated.
- **Time target:** 50 seconds.

**R1-Q18 — B.**
- **A:** Sends full medical details by email, which exceeds the minimum needed and risks the channel.
- **B (key):** Shares only that a medical verification is pending, through the secure file channel, with the minimum content.
- **C:** Blocks processing and withholds information the next officer needs, which confuses confidentiality with secrecy.
- **D:** Places sensitive detail on a shared board that all pension staff can see.
- **Trap:** confidentiality limits what is shared; it does not stop the file from being processed.
- **Shortcut:** choose the option that shares the minimum through an approved channel.
- **Time target:** 55 seconds.

**R1-Q19 — B.**
- **A:** Announces the route and defers objections until after construction, which does not engage the concerns.
- **B (key):** Hears the safety and access concerns, explains the evidence and constraints and records unresolved objections.
- **C:** Hands the decision to one interested party.
- **D:** Rigs the survey to two preferred routes, so the majority it produces is not evidence of acceptance.
- **Trap:** a majority from a biased survey is not evidence of acceptance.
- **Shortcut:** choose the option that records objections and discloses evidence.
- **Time target:** 50 seconds.

**R1-Q20 — B.**
- **A:** Asks one thing (the number of visits).
- **B (key):** Asks two things in one item (speed of service and courtesy of staff), so a fast but discourteous experience cannot be answered.
- **C:** Asks one thing (the visiting day).
- **D:** Asks one thing (an opinion about evening hours).
- **Trap:** the defect is not the length of the item; a short item can still be double-barrelled.
- **Shortcut:** count the separate things a single answer must rate.
- **Time target:** 35 seconds.

**R1-Q21 — A.**
- **A (key):** Asks for a rating on a balanced scale without presupposing an answer.
- **B:** Leading; "every responsible household should welcome" pushes the answer.
- **C:** Loaded; it presupposes that the scheme wastes money.
- **D:** Presupposes that cost is a reason to oppose and asks for the strength of opposition.
- **Trap:** a neutral question can still contain an evaluative adjective or premise.
- **Shortcut:** reject any stem with a premise that the respondent did not supply.
- **Time target:** 45 seconds.

**R1-Q22 — C.**
- **A:** Earners and favourite destinations do not answer the feasibility decision.
- **B:** Earners and dietary needs do not answer the feasibility decision.
- **C (key):** Family size gives party composition and budget gives ability to pay, which are the data that change the decision.
- **D:** Family size is relevant, but dietary needs concern service design rather than feasibility.
- **Trap:** a relevant head paired with an irrelevant one is still defective.
- **Shortcut:** retain only the data that changes the decision.
- **Time target:** 55 seconds.

**R1-Q23 — C.**
- **A:** Moves the relative ahead of the queue quietly, which is the core ethical failure.
- **B:** Puts preferential treatment in writing, which does not make it lawful.
- **C (key):** Declines, applies the queue and records the request and the refusal for accountability.
- **D:** Hides the request from the supervisor, which weakens accountability.
- **Trap:** writing down a preference does not make it lawful.
- **Shortcut:** reject any option that moves a person ahead of a queue.
- **Time target:** 50 seconds.

**R1-Q24 — B.**
- **A:** Suspends the worker before the facts are known, which is coercive and disproportionate.
- **B (key):** Asks for an explanation of the two entries, checks the stock and records the outcome before any further step.
- **C:** Deducts the salary without a finding and treats a log entry as proof of theft.
- **D:** Publicises a suspicion, which harms the worker's dignity without establishing anything.
- **Trap:** a log entry shows a signature, not the destination of the stock.
- **Shortcut:** choose the option that verifies before imposing.
- **Time target:** 55 seconds.

**R1-Q25 — D.**
- **A:** Attacks character and makes an absolute claim about the colleague.
- **B:** Attributes motive ("not interested") and uses "always".
- **C:** Vague ("could improve in general") and gives no action.
- **D (key):** Names the dated pattern (three reports, two days late) and asks what would help.
- **Trap:** feedback can be accurate in content and still fail if it attributes motive.
- **Shortcut:** look for a dated behaviour and a question about help.
- **Time target:** 50 seconds.

**R1-Q26 — A.**
- **A (key):** An open question invites explanation and does not presume a cause.
- **B:** A closed question produces a fact but not the reason, so it is the wrong opener when the cause is unknown.
- **C:** Presumes a cause (vehicles) and leads the reply.
- **D:** Asks for judgement and assigns blame.
- **Trap:** a closed question is useful later for confirmation, but it is the wrong opener.
- **Shortcut:** the opener should not contain an assumed cause.
- **Time target:** 45 seconds.

**R1-Q27 — D.**
- **A:** Uses peer pressure from a neighbouring district.
- **B:** Uses a threat of funding loss, which is coercion.
- **C:** Shows only favourable data, which is selective and manipulative.
- **D (key):** Presents the full results, the costs and the reasons, invites questions and leaves the decision to the council.
- **Trap:** a full presentation with an invitation to question is more persuasive than selective data.
- **Shortcut:** reject pressure and selective disclosure.
- **Time target:** 50 seconds.

**R1-Q28 — B.**
- **A:** Hopes the date will be met and conceals a broken promise.
- **B (key):** Informs the group promptly of the facts, the revised date and the reason, and corrects the earlier statement openly.
- **C:** Blames the contractor publicly, which fails dignity and accountability.
- **D:** Apologises to one person and leaves the group uninformed.
- **Trap:** silence about a delay is not keeping a commitment.
- **Shortcut:** choose the option that corrects the promise openly.
- **Time target:** 50 seconds.

**R1-Q29 — A.**
- **A (key):** Clarifies the proposal in writing and tests it on a small sample before a team decision.
- **B:** Rejects an idea because of opposition, not merit.
- **C:** Adopts an unclear idea without evaluation.
- **D:** Refers the proposal away from the field team without comment.
- **Trap:** lack of agreement is not a reason to reject; lack of clarity is a reason to ask for detail.
- **Shortcut:** choose the option that clarifies and tests.
- **Time target:** 50 seconds.

**R1-Q30 — C.**
- **A:** Concentrates the hardest work on one officer and creates a bottleneck.
- **B:** Ignores skill and capacity by giving equal numbers.
- **C (key):** Allocates by skill and workload, pairs juniors with experienced officers and sets review points.
- **D:** Lets the most confident officers take the easiest work and leaves complex clusters unassigned.
- **Trap:** equal numbers are not equal workload.
- **Shortcut:** a fair allocation changes with the task and the people.
- **Time target:** 55 seconds.
