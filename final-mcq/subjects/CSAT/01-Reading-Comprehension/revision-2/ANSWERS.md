Method sources: B = `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/CSAT/basic/01_Reading-Comprehension.md` §§2–4, 10; A = `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/CSAT/advanced/01_Reading-Comprehension.md` §§2–4, 6–9. All evidence for these **ORIGINAL** items comes from the fictional passages in `QUESTIONS.md`; no official PYQ/key is asserted. ⚠️ Passage inference, not a real-world factual claim.

**R2-Q01 — C.** A: Space cannot waive the incomplete-registration exception. B: The officer's expectation is not a substitute for registration. C: “Issues a pass ... unless their registration is incomplete” excludes this grower under the rule. D: Publishing a list changes no registration condition. **Trap:** “unless” supplies a genuine stated exception; do not treat a necessary registration condition as enough for any grower who has it. Source: B §2; A §2.

**R2-Q02 — D.** A: The objection does not deny that there are eligible growers. B: The objection needs no claim about simultaneous use. C: It does not assume scarcity on every day, only that available space matters. D: If space could not affect admission independently, a registration list would suffice; negation defeats the objection. **Trap:** a necessary bridge can be modest; “always” scarcity is not required. Source: B §10; A §§2–3.

**R2-Q03 — B.** A: Time spent at cases is supporting evidence, not a reason to replace originals. B: Translations invite visitors in, while revisions visible in handwriting warrant preserving letters. C: Manuscript details can matter even to a reader of the translation. D: The curator opposes replacing handwriting, not exhibiting letters. **Trap:** retain both concession and principal claim. Source: B §§2–3; A §§2,6.

**R2-Q04 — D.** A: She expressly denies that print substitutes transparently for originals. B: She values translations as an invitation, so is not indifferent. C: She favours translations beside letters, so is not hostile. D: The invitation is welcome subject to the caution about lost crossed-out words. **Trap:** tone is qualified approval rather than unqualified approval or blanket rejection. Source: B §10; A §§2,7.

**R2-Q05 — A.** A: Unpredictable operation explains well use in P1; a long route limits usefulness in P2. B: It swaps the explicit locations of those two constraints. C: P1's walk was shortened, and P2 supplies the long route. D: Both constraints are stated, so “neither” fails. **Trap:** map each clause to its passage, not to a generic water-scheme narrative. Source: A §4.

**R2-Q06 — C.** A: P2 says the route remains long despite the repairs, so distance did not improve in both hamlets. B: P1 reports unpredictable operation despite a shorter walk, so reliability is not settled in both. C: P1 shows a reliability limit, P2 a proximity limit, so both can matter. D: P1 and P2 each show a useful improvement whose effect was limited by the other constraint; neither isolates one factor as determining use by itself. **Trap:** retain both distinct constraints without claiming either alone solves all. Source: A §§2,4.

**R2-Q07 — D.** A: The passage respects finished wares as well as the empty bowl. B: There is no discussion of declining demand. C: The narrative treats the child's imitation earnestly, not sarcastically. D: The repeated unpainted bowl evokes openness to learning while making finished work. **Trap:** tone is determined by narrated treatment, not by assuming empty means defective. Source: B §10; A §2.

**R2-Q08 — B.** A: The potter offers no pricing strategy. B: She names the “space for what I have not learned,” later recalled by the child. C: Neither potter calls it better than finished vessels. D: The original potter's death or departure is nowhere stated. **Trap:** phrase interpretation uses its local explanatory sentence, not a real-world history of pottery. Source: B §10; A §2.

**R2-Q09 — D.** Statement 1: supported by the afternoon's diverging readings. Statement 2: supported by the team's explicit refusal to make outside change a sufficient explanation for inside change. Statement 3: supported by the possible effect of vents. A: Omits supported 3. B: Omits supported 1. C: Omits supported 2. D: Includes all three supported statements. **Trap:** an outside reading can matter without sufficing to establish what happens inside. Source: B §10; A §§3,9.

**R2-Q10 — B.** A: Vents could alter the response, but no determination of *every* inside change is shown. B: The outside rose without an inside rise on the observed afternoon, directly establishing non-necessity of a matching rise. C: It describes no inside rise or delayed matching pattern. D: Neither the relative contribution of vents nor that of outside air is measured. **Trap:** sufficient vs relevant, and can vs must. Source: B §§2–4; A §2.

**R2-Q11 — A.** A: Negate transfer: discarded-fabric practice would not train cutting saleable cloth, defeating the proposal's aim; the manager's resemblance condition reinforces the bridge. B: The trainer expressly accepts that mistakes are part of learning. C: Mistakes on saleable fabric are called costly, with no inverse pricing claim. D: Existing error-free work would not be required for the proposal. **Trap:** the assumption must connect training material to actual work, not promise perfect outcomes. Source: B §10; A §3.

**R2-Q12 — D.** A: He agrees to a training trial, not its removal. B: The proposal still uses cutting machines. C: No sale occurs during discarded-fabric practice. D: Requiring resemblance makes experience on discarded pieces relevant to saleable cloth. **Trap:** identify the reason for the *condition*, not the trainer's broader aim of lowering losses. Source: B §10.

**R2-Q13 — B.** A: The final sentence says no required document was waived. B: Several applicants arrived without attachments although translation helped them read questions. C: The first clerk welcomes spoken help; it is not prohibited. D: The checklist names attachments without hiding conditions, not replacing them. **Trap:** stated-detail question; do not generalise from one intervention's partial success. Source: B §10.

**R2-Q14 — C.** A: No clerk proposes waiving written requirements. B: Spoken help is welcomed but variable, so cannot substitute for the checklist. C: Translation addresses comprehension and a stable checklist addresses missing attachments while preserving conditions. D: Missing attachments persist after translation, so translation alone is insufficient. **Trap:** the intended action incorporates two separate obstacles, not a one-step cure. Source: B §§2–3; A §2.

**R2-Q15 — B.** A: The first sentence mentions registration but does not settle whether it is sufficient. B: The additional space requirement prevents registration alone from predicting how many will receive a pass. C: Printing speed is outside the passage. D: The officers dispute predictive sufficiency, not whether registration exists. **Trap:** isolate the added “also” condition rather than reread the entire passage; if still unable to discriminate at the ceiling, park and log a necessary/sufficient reading error (**R**) or time error (**T**). Source: B §10; A §§3,8.

**R2-Q16 — C.** A: Resemblance is exactly the manager's condition, not an inconsistency. B: The trainer expressly accepts that mistakes are part of learning. C: The manager agrees to a conditional trial, contradicting outright rejection. D: Reducing costly mistakes is the trainer's stated aim. **Trap:** the negative stem calls for contradiction, not a claim merely absent from the passage. Source: B §10; A §7.

**R2-Q17 — C.** A: She accepts the shorter measured wait but does not deny that those seen may have benefited. B: The visitor asks about *both* departures and forms; neither party calls departures alone decisive. C: Both accept the narrower measurement, but the visitor disputes the inference to fair access for all. D: The visitor, not “only the clerk,” proposed tracking departures. **Trap:** distinguish disagreement over a measurement from disagreement over its scope. Source: B §§2–4,10; A §§2,6.

**R2-Q18 — A.** A: The ledger covers only people reaching consultation, so its shorter observed wait need not describe those who left. B: Departures are absent from the ledger, so their waits are not captured alongside those consulted. C: No comprehension measure is recorded; the visitor asks to add it. D: The ledger includes only those reaching the clerk, not everyone who tried to apply. **Trap:** “those consulted” is the denominator; no inference about those excluded from observation or later repairs is licensed. Source: B §§2–4; A §§2,6.

**R2-Q19 — D.** A: The visitor raises departures and form comprehension as different issues, not one exclusive cause. B: She accepts consultation times as a real, narrower measure. C: Returning is not required for an exit to be counted. D: Negate it: if not even some departures could be registered without a consultation, adding departures to a ledger that currently records only consultations would not work. **Trap:** this is the *unspoken feasibility bridge* for recording omitted people, not the stated claim that omitted people could matter. Source: B §10; A §3.

**R2-Q20 — B.** Statement 1: the passage expressly reports three mornings of rising readings at both points. Statement 2: false, because the bridge stayed shut after both gauges fell once. Statement 3: expressly stated function of the mark. A: Omits supported 3. B: Includes exactly 1 and 3. C: Includes contradicted 2 while omitting 1. D: Adds contradicted 2. **Trap:** a limited observed conjunction does not imply universal forecasting or bridge access. Source: B §10; A §§2,9.

**R2-Q21 — B.** A: The other volunteers reject removing useful but incomplete readings. B: Their notice uses the low reading but cautions that it cannot establish bridge access. C: Three observed mornings do not justify every future condition. D: Inspection was unfinished even when both gauges fell, so readings cannot replace it. **Trap:** the correct action preserves a partial indicator while stating what it cannot decide. Source: B §§2–4,10; A §2.

**R2-Q22 — D.** A: Only three mornings with upstream rises are identified. B: The bridge-open mark accompanies the readings. C: An unfinished inspection kept the bridge closed after both gauges fell. D: The passage explicitly says no one proposed the downstream gauge was faulty, let alone that it was discontinued. **Trap:** on a negative stem require a direct contradiction rather than a hypothetical outside the report. Source: B §10; A §7.

**R2-Q23 — A.** A: P2 alone says the caretakers want to preserve access while planning for upkeep. B: Only P1 supplies the count showing more midday visits after the gate was removed; P2 welcomes visitors but gives no before/after count. C: P2 rejects locking again as a substitute for keeping the garden usable. D: P2 expressly leaves open whether shade would change cleaning work; possibility is not established by the account. **Trap:** an observation from P1 cannot supply P2's evidence; an untested possibility is not a supported finding. Source: A §§2,4,7.

**R2-Q24 — C.** A: The visit count records frequency, not the identities of newly served residents. B: P2 reports litter without estimating who left it. C: P1 reports an increase in visits but expressly denies an account of each visitor's motives. D: Benches and shade are asked about, not certified adequate for all. **Trap:** a count is not a survey of motivations or comfort. Source: B §§2–4; A §4.

**R2-Q25 — C.** A: Welcoming visitors supports access but alone does not reject a proposed permanent closure. B: More litter explains the concern but does not settle the caretakers' response. C: The explicit rejection of locking as a substitute for upkeep rules out treating renewed closure as their plan. D: Volunteer absence might favour either rule unless their response is read. **Trap:** reread only the response to the resident's proposal; if still unresolved at the ceiling, park rather than substitute one's own garden policy. Source: B §10; A §§3–4,8.
