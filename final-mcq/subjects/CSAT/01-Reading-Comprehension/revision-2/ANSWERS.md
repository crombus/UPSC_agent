**R2-Q01 — A.** A: An eligible grower refused for lack of space may seek entry on another day; the notice does not promise space. B: The notice says the grower “does not promise space”; a guaranteed pass is not stated. C: The list records eligibility, not whether bays are occupied. D: The notice's second-day provision applies only to eligible growers, and incomplete registration makes a grower ineligible. **Trap:** a permission to seek entry is not a guarantee of admission. **Shortcut:** check whether the notice promises anything, or only permits a request. **Time:** ~60 s.

**R2-Q02 — C.** A: The objection does not deny that eligible growers exist. B: The objection needs no claim about simultaneous use. C: If space could not affect admission independently of registration, a list would suffice; negating this defeats the objection. D: The objection does not require scarcity on every day. **Trap:** a necessary bridge can be modest; “always” scarcity is not required. **Shortcut:** negate each option and see which one breaks the second officer's position. **Time:** ~60 s.

**R2-Q03 — B.** A: Time spent at cases is supporting evidence, not a reason that printed letters are better. B: Translations invite visitors in, while crossed-out details warrant keeping the handwriting. C: The curator's point is that manuscript details matter even to someone who reads the translation. D: She opposes replacing the handwriting, not displaying the letters. **Trap:** keep both the concession and the principal claim. **Shortcut:** the correct option holds the concession (“broaden entry”) and the claim (“retain value”). **Time:** ~60 s.

**R2-Q04 — D.** A: She expressly denies that print is a transparent substitute for originals. B: She values translations as an invitation, so she is not indifferent. C: She favours translations beside letters, so she is not hostile. D: The invitation is welcome subject to the caution about lost crossed-out words. **Trap:** tone is qualified approval, not unqualified praise or blanket rejection. **Shortcut:** look for a concession paired with a caution. **Time:** ~45 s.

**R2-Q05 — C.** A: Passage 1 describes only irregular operation; the long route is in Passage 2. B: This swaps the explicit locations of the two constraints. C: Passage 1 describes unpredictable operation; Passage 2 describes the long route. D: Both constraints are stated, so “neither” fails. **Trap:** map each clause to its own passage, not to a generic water-scheme narrative. **Shortcut:** check the passage label before the constraint. **Time:** ~50 s.

**R2-Q06 — A.** A: P1 shows a reliability limit despite a shorter walk; P2 shows a proximity limit despite repaired taps, so both can matter. B: P2 says the route stayed long; P1 shows that shortened routes do not settle the matter alone. C: P1 reports unpredictable operation despite a shorter walk, so reliability is not settled by route length. D: Each passage shows one factor limiting the other; neither alone determines use. **Trap:** retain both distinct constraints without claiming either alone solves all. **Shortcut:** keep the option that names both variables without “suffice” or “alone”. **Time:** ~60 s.

**R2-Q07 — D.** A: The narrator's account of the child does not express wistful regret about giving up a craft. B: The passage does not describe the potter as whimsical or amused. C: The narrator treats the child's imitation earnestly, not sceptically. D: The unpainted bowl and the potter's words evoke openness to learning while valuing finished work. **Trap:** tone is determined by narrated treatment, not by assuming an empty bowl is defective. **Shortcut:** identify the narrator's treatment of finished versus empty work. **Time:** ~60 s.

**R2-Q08 — B.** A: The potter offers no pricing strategy. B: She names the “space for what I have not learned,” which the child later recalls. C: Neither potter calls the empty bowl better than finished vessels. D: The original potter's departure from the fair is nowhere stated. **Trap:** interpret the phrase from its local explanatory sentence, not from a real-world history of pottery. **Shortcut:** find the sentence that explains the bowl. **Time:** ~45 s.

**R2-Q09 — C.** Statement 1: supported by the afternoon's diverging readings. Statement 2: supported by the team's refusal to make outside change a sufficient explanation for inside change. Statement 3: supported by the possible effect of vents. A: Omits supported 3. B: Omits supported 1. D: Omits supported 2. **Trap:** an outside reading can matter without sufficing to establish what happens inside. **Shortcut:** test each statement against its quoted clause before reading combinations. **Time:** ~60 s.

**R2-Q10 — A.** A: The outside air rose on the observed afternoon without a matching inside rise, so no matching rise is necessary. B: Vents could alter the response, but no determination of every inside change is shown. C: No delayed matching pattern is described. D: The relative contributions of vents and outside air are not measured. **Trap:** “need not” (not necessary) versus “will follow” (predictive); keep the claim the passage can license. **Shortcut:** find the option whose modal verb the passage actually supports. **Time:** ~55 s.

**R2-Q11 — D.** A: The trainer accepts that mistakes are part of learning; removing all of them is not assumed. B: No pricing claim about replacement costs is made. C: The trainer expects mistakes, so error-free operation is not required. D: The proposal depends on training on discarded fabric transferring to the cloth workers later cut. **Trap:** the assumption connects practice material to real work; it does not promise perfect outcomes. **Shortcut:** ask what the proposal would fail without. **Time:** ~70 s.

**R2-Q12 — B.** A: Shared machines make practice more relevant and so strengthen the proposal. B: If the discarded pieces differ in weight and weave, the manager's resemblance condition fails, so practice on them may not transfer; this weakens the proposal. C: Fewer errors on saleable cloth strengthen the proposal. D: Fewer mistakes in other units also strengthen it. **Trap:** a weakener must lower support for the proposal, not merely describe an alternative. **Shortcut:** find the option that breaks the transfer link. **Time:** ~70 s.

**R2-Q13 — A.** A: The clerks agreed to keep personal help and to test whether a stable written list reduces omitted attachments. B: They keep personal help; “alone, without personal help” misstates the test. C: The test concerns the list; translation is the existing condition, not what they test. D: They do not claim the list would eliminate every incomplete application. **Trap:** a close paraphrase can misstate one element. **Shortcut:** identify each element of the test (help kept, list tested, outcome measured) and check the option against all three. **Time:** ~70 s.

**R2-Q14 — C.** A: No clerk proposes waiving or removing written requirements. B: Spoken help is welcome but variable, so it cannot replace the checklist. C: Translation addresses comprehension; a stable list addresses missing attachments while keeping the form's conditions. D: Missing attachments persisted after translation, so translation alone is insufficient. **Trap:** the intended action has two components, not a one-step cure. **Shortcut:** pair the problem each element solves. **Time:** ~55 s.

**R2-Q15 — B.** A: Printing lists the evening before is irrelevant to whether a list alone predicts admissions. B: Passes fewer than registered growers, because the bays were full, show that space limits admission; the list alone does not predict it. C: Strengthens the first officer by showing that registration translates into use. D: Agreement on registration's importance is irrelevant to whether it predicts admissions. **Trap:** a weakener must target the claim (“alone predicts”), not the topic. **Shortcut:** find the finding that shows a second factor doing the work. **Time:** ~60 s.

**R2-Q16 — D.** A: Resemblance is exactly the manager's condition; it is consistent with the passage. B: The trainer expressly accepts that mistakes are part of learning. C: Reducing costly mistakes is the trainer's stated aim. D: The manager agrees to a conditional trial and does not reject it outright; the statement is inconsistent. **Trap:** the negative stem calls for contradiction, not a claim merely absent from the passage. **Shortcut:** look for the word “outright” and compare it with the manager's conditional agreement. **Time:** ~55 s.

**R2-Q17 — A.** A: Both accept the shorter recorded wait; the visitor asks what the measurement proves about fair access. B: The visitor says the recorded fall is not imaginary, so denying any benefit is inconsistent. C: The visitor, not the clerk, treats departures as needing examination; neither treats departures alone as decisive. D: The visitor, not only the clerk, proposed examining departures, and the office kept the tokens while examining missing observations. **Trap:** distinguish disagreement about a measurement from disagreement about its scope. **Shortcut:** check each party's stated position before comparing them. **Time:** ~70 s.

**R2-Q18 — C.** A: The ledger covers only consulted applicants, so it does not describe those who left. B: The ledger does not measure comprehension; the visitor asks that it be added. C: The ledger includes only consultations, so a lower wait need not describe those who left without consultation. D: The ledger includes only people who reached the clerk, not everyone who tried to apply. **Trap:** the consulted group is the denominator; no inference about those excluded from observation is licensed. **Shortcut:** identify the denominator first. **Time:** ~60 s.

**R2-Q19 — B.** A: The visitor raises departures and comprehension as distinct issues, not one exclusive cause. B: The unspoken bridge: some exits must be recordable without a completed consultation; otherwise adding departures would not work. C: The visitor accepts that consultation times remain a narrower measure. D: Returning later is not required for an exit to be counted. **Trap:** this is the feasibility bridge for recording omitted people, not the claim that omitted people matter. **Shortcut:** ask what must be possible for the proposal to work. **Time:** ~70 s.

**R2-Q20 — D.** Statement 1: the passage reports three mornings of rising readings at both points. Statement 2: false, because the bridge stayed closed once after both gauges fell. Statement 3: the mark's function is stated. A: Omits supported 3. B: Omits supported 1. C: Includes contradicted 2. **Trap:** a limited observed conjunction does not imply universal forecasting or bridge access. **Shortcut:** test each statement with a quotation before reading combinations. **Time:** ~60 s.

**R2-Q21 — C.** A: Rejected by the other volunteers, who prefer retaining useful but incomplete readings. B: Three observed mornings do not justify a forecast under every future condition. C: The notice uses the low reading but cautions that it cannot establish bridge access; this is the team's practical message. D: Inspection was unfinished even when both gauges fell, so readings cannot replace it. **Trap:** the correct action preserves a partial indicator while stating what it cannot decide. **Shortcut:** keep the option that both uses and limits the evidence. **Time:** ~50 s.

**R2-Q22 — A.** A: The passage says no one proposed the downstream gauge was faulty; it does not say the gauge was discontinued. B: Only three mornings with upstream rises are identified, so this is consistent. C: The bridge-open mark accompanies the readings, so this is consistent. D: An unfinished inspection kept the bridge closed after both gauges fell, so this is consistent. **Trap:** on a negative stem require a direct contradiction, not a claim that is merely unmentioned. **Shortcut:** check whether the option makes a claim about a fact the passage explicitly denies. **Time:** ~60 s.

**R2-Q23 — B.** A: The caretakers rejected locking the gate as a substitute for maintenance; the option reverses this. B: P2 alone says the caretakers want to preserve access while planning upkeep. C: The midday count is supplied only by Passage 1. D: P2 says whether a shaded bench would change cleaning work is not shown; the possibility is not a finding. **Trap:** a claim from Passage 1 cannot supply Passage 2's evidence; an untested possibility is not a supported finding. **Shortcut:** look for the phrase “Passage 2 alone” and check each option against Passage 2 only. **Time:** ~60 s.

**R2-Q24 — C.** A: The visit count shows frequency, not who the newly served residents are. B: P2 reports litter without estimating who left it. C: The visit count shows visits rose but expressly does not explain each visitor's reasons. D: The count does not show every visitor finds the garden comfortable. **Trap:** a count is not a survey of motivations or comfort. **Shortcut:** ask what the count measures and what it cannot. **Time:** ~55 s.

**R2-Q25 — D.** A: Locking the gate as a substitute for maintenance is explicitly rejected. B: P2 says whether a shaded bench would change cleaning work is not shown; “shown to reduce” is contradicted. C: P2 says the caretakers did not estimate who left litter; the claim is contradicted. D: The caretakers ask for upkeep for the newly opened space while the litter's cause is not yet measured, so this is consistent. **Trap:** the response to the resident's proposal and the absent evidence decide the item. **Shortcut:** remove any option that treats an unmeasured cause as established. **Time:** ~60 s.
