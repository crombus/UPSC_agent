**R1-Q02 — B.** A: Replacing all displays is neither proposed nor necessary. **R1-Q03 — C.** A: The volunteers propose comparing patches with similar tidal access, so useful comparison remains possible. B: Tide is an alternative explanation, not an established *greater* cause than trampling. C: Because tidal flow differs, bird return alone cannot separate fencing's contribution from tide's. Source: B §§2,4; A §2.

**R1-Q04 — A.** A: The passage explicitly says volunteers refused to attribute restoration to fences alone, so this is inconsistent. B: The varying tidal flow is their stated reservation. **R1-Q05 — D.** A: A further stock check is needed, so an in-range reading is insufficient. B: It cannot replace the explicitly required stock check. **R1-Q07 — A.** A: The narrator lingers on the lamp and the page of pencilled doubts, making the gesture gently meaningful. **R1-Q08 — C.** A: “Surrendered” concerns a habit of inquiry, not legal control of property. B: “Not quite” denies total loss of the habit. **R1-Q09 — D.** A: P1 says enquiries have little use if delivery dates cannot be honoured; P2's packing improvement does not validate an enquiries-only success test. **R1-Q12 — D.** A: A flag prompts inspection, but nothing establishes it is inaccurate *at the depth it measures*. **R1-Q13 — C.** A: The reviewer explicitly retains access to the minutes and asks for them to be checked. B: She warns a summary *might* omit something, not that every summary errs. **R1-Q14 — B.** A: She says summaries are not substitutes for the minutes. B: The “find relevant pages” role differs from “check the original minute before citing”. **R1-Q15 — A.** A: “Not to stand in for” excludes replacing the originals, leaving the qualified search-and-check option. **R1-Q16 — C.** A: Access may improve, but the reviewer calls for separate noise evidence, not giving complaints overriding weight. **R1-Q17 — B.** A: Some late-opening stallholders were indeed interviewed; the problem is incomplete coverage, not impossibility of testing.
