**R1-Q01 — A.** A: The passage says reliable access matters more than the device; the board is the remedy. B: Vehicle scheduling is a traders' benefit, not what the committee designs for. C: The text reports occasional failures; it does not rank gate notices as more accurate. D: A true benefit to growers, but narrower than the central idea. **Trap:** a true supporting detail is narrower than the central idea. **Shortcut:** find the sentence beginning “It argued that…”. **Time:** ~50 s.

**R1-Q02 — C.** A: Replacing all displays is neither proposed nor necessary. B: Growers may keep checking; the proposal needs no change in their habits. C: Negate it: if the board cannot carry timely figures, it cannot preserve access when the display fails, which undermines the recommendation. D: Trader priority is nowhere required for the board to work. **Trap:** negate the hidden bridge, not an attractive side effect. **Shortcut:** ask “what must be true for the board to work?” **Time:** ~70 s.

**R1-Q03 — B.** A: The volunteers propose comparing patches with similar tidal access, so useful comparison remains possible. C: Tide is an alternative explanation, not an established greater cause than trampling. B: Because tidal flow differs, bird return alone cannot separate fencing's contribution from tide's. D: The count alone cannot establish that trampling had no effect. **Trap:** do not treat correlation as proof of either competing cause. **Shortcut:** look for the option that claims only what the evidence can isolate. **Time:** ~60 s.

**R1-Q04 — D.** A: Tidal access as a reason for the return is consistent with the passage (the volunteers name tidal flow as a reservation). B: Comparable patches before wider advice is the group's proposal. C: Birds returned to that patch but not to every nearby patch. D: The volunteers expressly refused to attribute recovery to fences alone, so this is inconsistent. **Trap:** a negative stem has one contradicted claim; the other three are merely unfamiliar. **Shortcut:** mark any “alone” or “entire” claim first. **Time:** ~60 s.

**R1-Q05 — C.** A: A further stock check is needed, so an in-range reading is insufficient. B: It cannot replace the explicitly required stock check. C: “Only if” means dispatch implies an in-range reading, so the reading is a condition that any authorised dispatch must satisfy. D: No forecast of future cabinet performance is made. **Trap:** necessary is not sufficient; do not reverse the conditional arrow. **Shortcut:** rewrite “only if” as “dispatch → in range”. **Time:** ~45 s.

**R1-Q06 — A.** Statement 1 is supported: out-of-range means the delivery is held for review. Statement 2 is supported: an in-range indicator alone does not authorise delivery because the stock record must also be checked. Statement 3 is supported: the passage says the procedure specifies no repair method. B: Omits supported 3. C: Omits supported 2 and 3. D: Omits supported 1 while including 2 and 3. **Trap:** distinguish present dispatch conditions from an unprovided repair instruction. **Shortcut:** judge each statement as true or false before checking the combinations. **Time:** ~60 s.

**R1-Q07 — D.** A: The narrator's gesture is not presented as wistful regret; the notebook's new page of doubts ends on a positive note. B: The narrator voices no mockery of the lamp's purpose. C: The narrator does not urge reform; the lamp is described, not prescribed. D: The narrator lingers on the lamp and the page of pencilled doubts, appreciating the gesture without claiming it will draw readers. **Trap:** tone follows the narrator's framing, not a judgement about whether the lamp will change the town. **Shortcut:** identify the words that carry the attitude (“wanted”, “gained a page”). **Time:** ~60 s.

**R1-Q08 — B.** A: “Surrendered” refers to a habit of inquiry, not legal control of property. B: “Not quite” denies total loss of the habit: the town retains a fragile practice. C: The same phrase that negates the surrender denies the full abandonment that this option claims. D: The caretaker expected no other reader that evening, so a nightly crowd is not implied. **Trap:** interpret a metaphor in context, not literally or absolutely. **Shortcut:** the negation “not quite” flips “surrendered” to “partly kept”. **Time:** ~50 s.

**R1-Q09 — C.** A: P1 values enquiries only as far as delivery dates are honoured; P2 does not validate judging success by enquiries alone. B: P2 says packing did not bring new buyers, so marketing must extend beyond existing buyers. D: P2's packing improvement does not support offering dates without checking protection. C: Discovery is the shared demand; P1 adds dependable dates and P2 shows why packing and fulfilment matter. **Trap:** a recommendation supported by both must retain each passage's limiting claim. **Shortcut:** find the option that both passages could each support in their own terms. **Time:** ~70 s.

**R1-Q10 — A.** A: Both passages discuss finding buyers (P1: distant buyers discover the cloth; P2: the cloth must become visible), and only P1 discusses honoured dates. B: Passage 1 also discusses buyer discovery, so “Passage 2 alone” is wrong. C: Both passages discuss finding buyers; neither denies it. D: Passage 2 also seeks visibility, while P1 alone discusses honoured dates. **Trap:** do not equate damage reduction with the separate claim that delivery dates are honoured. **Shortcut:** tick each claim against the passage that makes it. **Time:** ~80 s.

**R1-Q11 — B.** A: A same-depth match, not a same-spot-different-location finding, says nothing about why inspecting two places helps. B: A discovered difference, in a location that was not flagged, shows that inspection reveals variation a single flag misses. C: Whole-field irrigation after one flag is the practice the station rejects, so it weakens the procedure. D: Sensor replacement says nothing about whether inspecting two locations reveals variation. **Trap:** a strengthener must show the procedure's distinctive benefit, not merely its existence. **Shortcut:** look for the option that shows a variation an unflagged location revealed. **Time:** ~75 s.

**R1-Q12 — D.** A: A flag prompts inspection, but nothing establishes that it is inaccurate at the depth it measures. B: Agreement at two locations does not establish conditions across the whole field. C: Inspection precedes any irrigation decision; no automatic decision follows. D: Refusing to generalise from one location and inspecting disagreements entails that a local reading may miss spatial variation. **Trap:** “may” preserves the scope of the evidence; a local agreement is not a field-wide proof. **Shortcut:** keep the option whose verb (“may hide”) matches the station's cautious scope. **Time:** ~60 s.

**R1-Q13 — A.** A: The reviewer keeps access to summaries and asks for originals to be checked before citation. B: She welcomes the easier search and asks that summaries remain, so she does not seek to end their display. C: She warns that a summary might omit something; she does not claim summaries are usually inaccurate. D: Rejecting summaries ignores the easier search she welcomes and asks to preserve. **Trap:** an author's intention can integrate approval and caution. **Shortcut:** look for the option that keeps both halves of her position. **Time:** ~50 s.

**R1-Q14 — C.** A: She says summaries are not substitutes for the minutes; “short” is not a licence. B: A possible omission is not an omission in every summary; she expressly makes no such claim. C: The “find relevant pages” role differs from “check the original minute before citing”; the passage draws this distinction. D: She welcomes easier search, although she insists on careful citation. **Trap:** match a qualified claim; neither dismiss the tool nor promote it into evidence. **Shortcut:** search for the word “citing” and its contrast. **Time:** ~50 s.

**R1-Q15 — B.** A: The archivist does not propose replacing minutes; summaries are for locating. B: “Needed a way to locate the meeting before studying it” reports the archivist's position exactly. C: The archivist never proposes withdrawal; the reviewer asks for the summaries to remain. D: The archivist makes no claim that summaries are accurate enough for citation; the reviewer demands checking. **Trap:** do not credit the archivist with the reviewer's views, or the reverse. **Shortcut:** identify who speaks before judging the claim. **Time:** ~50 s.

**R1-Q16 — D.** A: Access may improve, but the reviewer asks for separate noise evidence rather than giving complaints overriding weight. B: “All stalls” overstates interviews limited to late-opening stalls; admitting other causes does not cure the scope error. C: The performance may confound the result, but she explicitly does not call for withdrawal. D: She calls the sign encouraging but asks for all stallholders and separate recording of noise and access, so the trial is promising and evidence must be more complete. **Trap:** a central idea must retain encouraging evidence, selection limits and costs; a concern is not a verdict. **Shortcut:** the correct option holds both the promise and the limit. **Time:** ~60 s.

**R1-Q17 — A.** A: Some late-opening stallholders were interviewed; the universal claim covers early-closing stallholders who were not. B: Some stalls were open late and interviewed, so it was testable. C: No customer outcome for early closers is supplied, so loss cannot be inferred. D: The proportion of stalls interviewed is not stated, so “most” cannot be inferred. **Trap:** a universal claim's population must be compared with the sample. **Shortcut:** find the omitted group the universal word “every” covers. **Time:** ~60 s.

**R1-Q18 — C.** A: Comparison tests contributions; it does not presuppose that the performance mattered more than the bus. B: The reviewer wants all stallholders interviewed; a late-opening subset does not establish representation. C: Other conditions affecting visits must be comparable, or matched performance evenings cannot isolate the performance's contribution. D: Noise deserves separate recording; identical noise is not required to assess the performance's contribution to visits. **Trap:** the assumption is comparability for the inference, not a verdict about which intervention won. **Shortcut:** negate the comparison and check whether the inference still works. **Time:** ~70 s.

**R1-Q19 — D.** A: The narrator makes no claim that flawed cloth should be sold. B: The narrator's point is that the visible line helped teaching, not that visible errors beat hidden ones in general. C: The narrator treats the visible mistake as instructive, so a neutral stance is not supported. D: Folding the pieces and tracing the lapse treats the visible error as instructive without calling it a better pattern. **Trap:** the narrator's respectful tone does not endorse selling mistakes. **Shortcut:** keep the option that gives the teaching value and withholds praise. **Time:** ~55 s.

**R1-Q20 — B.** A: The sale of the cloth is not discussed; the story supplies no later account. C: The border hid the first error; the unfinished explanation concerns the account of the second lapse. D: The newcomer is shown both pieces; no record of which was made first is involved. B: She can identify where the thread entered but cannot explain how it escaped notice for so long; this is the unfinished explanation. **Trap:** the metaphor points to a specific gap in understanding, not to the cloth's commercial fate. **Shortcut:** find the sentence that begins “She could identify the place but not explain…”. **Time:** ~50 s.

**R1-Q21 — C.** Statement 1: supported by “could identify the place”. Statement 2: contradicted, because the apprentice disclaims that every error should be displayed. Statement 3: no subsequent fate of either cloth is supplied; sale cannot be inferred. A: Omits supported 1. B: Adds contradicted 2. D: Adds contradicted 2 and unsupported 3. **Trap:** “not established” is different from “sold” or “never sold”; judge the three statements separately. **Shortcut:** rule out any statement that has a counter-quote, then check for unsupported claims. **Time:** ~50 s.

**R1-Q22 — A.** A: P2 alone says several residents could identify the mapped path yet found its entrance hard to find in poor light. B: P1 alone calls prompt alert and intelligible route information separate parts of a usable warning. C: P1 alone cautions that a map must change when a path changes. D: P2 asks for testing with first-time users after dark, without reporting a completed test of a guide. **Trap:** do not turn a valid P1 claim into a P2 claim; distinguish a request for a test from a result. **Shortcut:** check each option for the phrase that only the passage named in it contains. **Time:** ~60 s.

**R1-Q23 — D.** A: P1 cautions that a map must be updated after path changes, so the assertion is consistent. B: P1 reports recipients asking about an alternative path, and P2 reports wanting directions beside the warning. C: P2 asks for tests with first-time users after dark. D: P1 says the exercise did not test whether a named path remained open after dark, so “established” is contradicted. **Trap:** the negative stem asks for an incompatible claim; “not tested” defeats “established”, not the proposition that the path might in reality stay open. **Shortcut:** look for “established” or “proved” beside a claim the passages say was not tested. **Time:** ~60 s.

**R1-Q24 — B.** A: Recipients asking about the path shows a need for directions, which strengthens the map proposal. B: The office's proposal assumes that the mapped path stays usable after dark, and the exercise did not test that. C: The reason for the proposal, not a weakener. D: Universal identification by every resident strengthens the map. **Trap:** a weakener must lower the support for the proposal, not merely describe a problem the proposal aims to address. **Shortcut:** ask whether the finding would make the map less useful for its purpose. **Time:** ~60 s.
