# csat-01 — Revision 3: solutions

Method sources: B = `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/CSAT/basic/01_Reading-Comprehension.md` §§1–4, 10 and semantic closure; A = `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/CSAT/advanced/01_Reading-Comprehension.md` §§2–4, 6–9. Every item is **ORIGINAL** and solved only from its own fictional passage in `QUESTIONS.md`. ⚠️ The passage's asserted facts are not external factual claims.

**R3-Q01 — C.** A: Payment may be recorded before the last seat is booked; no requested place is assured. B: Another departure can be sought but its seats are not assured. C: “No ticket ... without a recorded payment” directly entails ticket → recorded payment. D: The clerk distinguishes receipt from ticket and seat ledger; a receipt confirms payment, not reservation. **Trap:** necessary payment is not sufficient for a ticket. Source: B §2; A §2.

**R3-Q02 — B.** A: Returning a payment after the last seat is expressly allowed. B: The passage says no ticket without recorded payment, directly contradicting this claim. C: The notice says the traveller may seek another departure. D: This is expressly stated by the lack of a guarantee. **Trap:** in a negative stem prefer the contradicted claim; unknown seat availability on the next departure is not proof of availability. Source: B §§2,10; A §2.

**R3-Q03 — A.** A: P1 traces loans (not completed returns) without growth; P2 describes growth without establishing returns, so the notes answer different questions. B: P1 disclaims information on how varieties performed in plots. C: P2 expressly says plot reports cannot by themselves establish returns; they do not identify which borrower returned seed. D: Combining a traceable loan record with plot results does not add a record of completed returns. **Trap:** do not fuse two complementary measures into a single all-purpose measure. Source: A §4.

**R3-Q04 — B.** A: It reverses the passages' explicitly assigned roles. B: P2 describes growing results; P1 records who borrowed which varieties and thus makes the *loan* traceable. C: P2 describes plot results, while P1 records borrowing. D: P1 does not record growth, and P2 does not record who borrowed; neither passage proves every borrowed lot was actually returned. **Trap:** track the loans, not a return outcome the text does not establish. Source: A §§3–4.

**R3-Q05 — B.** A: The passage values speed conditionally, without ranking it generally above avoiding damage. B: Air movement speeds drying, but cracked pieces show why placement and monitoring also matter. C: Placement is to be checked, but the passage does not establish it *rather than* drying conditions as the cause. D: The instructor adjusts placement without requiring every piece to be far from a window. **Trap:** the central message includes both the benefit and its qualification. Source: B §§2–3; A §2.

**R3-Q06 — C.** A: Prior certainty about cracks is unnecessary for monitoring. B: The instructor does not ban moving air, let alone require its removal everywhere. C: Negate this bridge: if placement and monitoring cannot help manage damage, her proposed adjustments would not address her concern. D: She explicitly notes that moving air changes drying speed. **Trap:** the hidden premise supports the response, not an unrelated perfect prediction. Source: B §10; A §3.

**R3-Q07 — A.** A: “At least someone has begun” welcomes the effort, while the uneven notes and unknown future restrain confidence. B: No successful future is established. C: Neither sweeper nor narrator objects to the hour or cleanliness. D: The beginner is not ridiculed for the silence. **Trap:** infer attitude from the words, not from the novice's current ability. Source: B §10; A §2.

**R3-Q08 — D.** A: No later movements of the sweeper are reported. B: The passage refuses a prediction, negative as well as positive. C: It gives no forecast about the shop's activities. D: The uncertainty keeps the emphasis on the significance of starting. **Trap:** interpret narrative purpose without inventing an eventual result. Source: B §10; A §2.

**R3-Q09 — C.** Statement 1: supported, as advances can help purchase materials. Statement 2: false; an advance alone does not establish timely delivery. Statement 3: supported; recording delays is required before promising a date. A: Includes false 2 and omits 3. B: Includes false 2 and omits 1. C: Gives exactly 1 and 3. D: Includes false 2. **Trap:** treat “can help” and “sufficient to establish” separately. Source: B §§2,10; A §9.

**R3-Q10 — B.** A: Full coverage of material costs is unnecessary to estimate delays. B: If recorded delays could not inform dates, keeping them would not improve delivery promises; precise negation breaks the proposal. C: The register is expressly not a substitute for production. D: Planning can improve promises without guaranteeing universally early deliveries. **Trap:** assume only the needed bridge between evidence and planning, not success in every case. Source: B §10; A §3.

**R3-Q11 — D.** A: The school kept the descriptions and blank cards. B: The teacher rejected grading agreement with intention. C: The teacher refused a grading suggestion, which is not attributed to the student. D: She offered blank cards for visitors' responses, and the school kept them. **Trap:** track who said what; external exhibition conventions are irrelevant. Source: B §10.

**R3-Q12 — B.** A: The teacher refuses grading visitor conformity. B: The descriptions attract reading, and the blank cards protect alternative interpretations: both halves are represented. C: The school retains the descriptions, which some visitors read. D: The school keeps both kinds of card rather than removing drawings. **Trap:** a whole-passage idea must reconcile attention with interpretive openness. Source: B §§2–3; A §6.

**R3-Q13 — A.** A: The surveyor asks for measurements at several hours, recognising both helpful shade and wall heat. B: She observes that shade *already helps* some walkers, so it should not be discounted. C: She considers both planting and wall materials, not a tree-only measurement. D: A single noon reading cannot implement the several-hours comparison. **Trap:** translate qualified analysis into qualified action, not a one-factor survey. Source: B §10; A §2.

**R3-Q14 — C.** A: The councillor argues planting is pointless but does not establish wall effects everywhere. B: The surveyor, not the councillor, emphasises shade's benefit. C: The councillor moves from one hot wall-side stretch to a blanket claim of pointlessness. D: No change to walking hours is attributed to the councillor. **Trap:** identify the overgeneralisation rather than add a policy the speaker never advances. Source: B §4; A §3.

**R3-Q15 — D.** A: Planting's occurrence alone cannot determine its value. B: Growth in two stretches does not state whether anyone benefits. C: Persistent wall heat explains a limit but not whether benefits exist elsewhere. D: “Shade helped some walkers” directly rules out “no value” while fitting the qualified alternative. **Trap:** under a time ceiling reread the decisive clause, not the whole passage; log a wrong blanket claim as scope (**S**) or unsupported inference (**P**). Source: B §10; A §§3,8.

**R3-Q16 — A.** A: The sweeper values a tentative first effort while the narrator declines to forecast mastery. B: The later outcome is expressly left unknown. C: No claim that learning is finished follows from beginning. D: The sweeper returns to the road rather than quitting her own task. **Trap:** preserve the phrase's modest scope; do not read eventual achievement into a first note. Source: B §10; A §§2,6.

**R3-Q17 — D.** A: Separate inquiries are correctly retained, but listing conditional letters as bare support loses their waiting-area condition. B: Keeping the letters' conditions is right, but information seekers are *not* counted as supporters. C: The committee publishes conditions as well as counts, without making totals decisive. D: The separate category and letter qualifications preserve both direction and limits of each stance. **Trap:** the main message spans questions, conditional endorsements and the warning against an automatic tally. Source: B §§2–4; A §§2,6.

**R3-Q18 — A.** A: The letters endorse the move but require a waiting area first, which the bare label fails to convey. B: A requested waiting area is not reported as already provided. C: The letters are endorsements, so reclassifying them as opposition would misreport stance; the option's claim that concerns disappear from a tally is not the passage's conclusion. D: An endorsement with a condition is not a request for information or evidence against every move. **Trap:** a partly accurate label can lose a decisive “provided first” qualifier. Source: B §§2–4; A §§2–3.

**R3-Q19 — A.** A: Negate it: if relevant older residents' views were already adequately captured in submissions, visits could not *add voices* to the record as proposed. B: The proposal aims to add perspectives, not identify a universally preferred site. C: No evidence ranks the reliability of written versus in-person remarks. D: Other residents' questions about older people's access do not establish their concerns are identical to those residents' own views. **Trap:** test the bridge between additional visits and additional relevant voices, not whether visits settle the final vote. Source: B §10; A §3.

**R3-Q20 — B.** A: On the first evening, the counts differed; it is compatible. B: The next evening included a late arrival at the second light, making viewing periods unequal, so this contradicts the report. C: The late arrival is expressly described. D: The teacher kept the first observation while flagging the later limitation. **Trap:** do not let a flaw in the second evening retroactively invalidate the first observation. Source: B §10; A §7.

**R3-Q21 — D.** A: The teacher explicitly does not declare brightness irrelevant. B: Equal periods make the comparison fairer, but cannot *settle* brightness as the cause. C: First-evening counts differ, but no evidence establishes brightness as the chief cause. D: She says pooling totals without mentioning unequal periods makes the comparison seem more settled. **Trap:** methodological doubt is not a proven alternative causal explanation. Source: B §§2–4; A §§2–3.

**R3-Q22 — C.** A: The teacher preserves both evenings' counts. B: She declines the sole-cause inference from brightness. C: She values counts while demanding a fairer comparison and explicit caveat. D: Different periods are central to her criticism. **Trap:** a cautionary tone does not entail contempt for data. Source: B §10; A §§2,7.

**R3-Q23 — C.** A: P1 allows rest as a possibility but says no record shows how saved time was used. B: P1 raises other duties as a possibility, not an observed use of the saved time. C: P1 says the group had not recorded how freed time was used and agreed to ask members before describing the arrangement as increasing leisure; P2 is unnecessary. D: P1 postpones the leisure claim until after members are asked, rather than concluding that overall free time increased. **Trap:** neither an observed cooking-time saving nor a possible competing duty is a measured leisure outcome; do not borrow conditions from P2. Source: A §§2,4,7.

**R3-Q24 — D.** Statement 1: P1 reports shorter stove time for some but says no use-of-time record establishes leisure. Statement 2: P2 calls timely deliveries necessary but not enough, because the space may be occupied. Statement 3: P1 separates saved cooking time from leisure and P2 separates deliveries from implementation, so both support the distinction. A: Omits supported 2. B: Omits supported 3. C: Omits supported 1. D: Includes all three supported statements. **Trap:** judge the stated necessary-but-insufficient condition separately from the two-passage abstraction. Source: B §10; A §§2,4,9.

**R3-Q25 — D.** A: “Menus” marks the planning task but does not decide whether deliveries guarantee execution. B: “Agreed” specifies the delivery day, not sufficiency. C: “Several” identifies the observed days but does not explain why those arrivals alone could not initiate cooking. D: The text expressly calls reliable delivery “necessary ... yet not enough,” which rules out “guarantee.” **Trap:** reread the necessary/sufficient clause under the ceiling; a relevant input need not settle the outcome. Source: B §§2,10; A §§2–3,8.
