**R3-Q01 — C.** A: Payment may be recorded before the last seat is booked; no requested place is assured. B: Another departure can be sought, but its seats are not assured. C: “No ticket … without a recorded payment” entails ticket → recorded payment, i.e. a ticket needs a payment. D: The clerk distinguishes receipt from ticket; a receipt confirms payment, not reservation. **Trap:** necessary payment is not sufficient for a ticket. **Shortcut:** rewrite “no ticket without payment” as a conditional and check its direction. **Time:** ~60 s.

**R3-Q02 — A.** A: Contradicted: the passage says no ticket is issued without a recorded payment. B: Returning a payment after the last seat is booked is expressly stated. C: The notice says the traveller may seek another departure. D: The passage says payment does not guarantee a ticket, so it is consistent. **Trap:** in a negative stem prefer the contradicted claim; unknown seat availability is not proof of availability. **Shortcut:** look for “no … without”. **Time:** ~45 s.

**R3-Q03 — D.** A: Combining the records does not establish completed returns; P2 says reports cannot show that. B: P1 disclaims information on how varieties performed in plots. C: P2 says plot reports cannot by themselves establish returns; they do not identify returners. D: P1 records borrowing and P2 reports growing results, so they answer different questions. **Trap:** do not fuse two complementary measures into one all-purpose measure. **Shortcut:** name each note's question before combining. **Time:** ~70 s.

**R3-Q04 — B.** A: It reverses the passages' explicitly assigned roles. B: P2 describes growing results; P1 records who borrowed which varieties and so makes the loan traceable. C: P2 describes plot results, while P1 records borrowing, so “neither” fails. D: P1 does not record growth, and P2 does not record who borrowed; neither passage proves every borrowed lot was returned. **Trap:** track the loans, not a return outcome the text does not establish. **Shortcut:** match each measure to the note that names it. **Time:** ~60 s.

**R3-Q05 — A.** A: The passage values speed conditionally and makes damage and placement part of the message. B: The passage does not rank speed generally above monitored placement. C: Placement is to be checked, but the passage does not establish it rather than drying conditions as the cause. D: The instructor adjusts placement without requiring every piece to be far from a window. **Trap:** the central message includes both the benefit and its qualification. **Shortcut:** keep the option that holds both halves. **Time:** ~60 s.

**R3-Q06 — D.** A: Advance certainty about which pieces will crack is not needed for monitoring to help. B: The instructor does not ban moving air, let alone require its removal everywhere. C: Negate this bridge: if placement and monitoring cannot help manage damage, her proposed adjustments would not address her concern. D: Placement or monitoring is the premise that her proposal relies on. **Trap:** the hidden premise supports the response, not an unrelated perfect prediction. **Shortcut:** negate each option and see whether the advice survives. **Time:** ~70 s.

**R3-Q07 — B.** A: The narrator does not predict soon-fluent playing. B: “At least someone has begun” welcomes the effort; the uneven notes and unknown future restrain confidence. C: No regret is expressed about the timing of the start. D: The beginner is not ridiculed for the uneven notes. **Trap:** infer attitude from the words, not from the player's current ability. **Shortcut:** keep the option whose attitude includes a reservation. **Time:** ~55 s.

**R3-Q08 — C.** A: No later movements of the sweeper are reported. B: The passage refuses a prediction in both directions, so it does not imply that uneven notes cannot develop. C: The uncertainty keeps the emphasis on the significance of starting. D: The passage gives no forecast about the shop's activities. **Trap:** interpret narrative purpose without inventing an eventual result. **Shortcut:** ask what the narrator gains by withholding the outcome. **Time:** ~50 s.

**R3-Q09 — A.** Statement 1: supported, as advances can help purchase materials. Statement 2: false; an advance alone does not establish timely delivery. Statement 3: supported; recording delays comes before promising a date. B: Includes false 2. C: Includes false 2 and omits 1. D: Includes false 2. **Trap:** treat “can help” and “sufficient to establish” separately. **Shortcut:** test each statement against the sentence that governs it. **Time:** ~60 s.

**R3-Q10 — D.** A: Full coverage of material costs is unnecessary to use delay records for timing. B: The register is expressly not a substitute for production. C: The premise would be needed only if guaranteed early delivery were claimed, which the proposal does not do. D: If recorded delays could not inform dates, keeping them would not improve delivery promises; the negation breaks the proposal. **Trap:** assume only the bridge between evidence and planning, not success in every case. **Shortcut:** negate each option and check whether the register's purpose fails. **Time:** ~70 s.

**R3-Q11 — B.** A: The opposite of the teacher's reasoning: she refuses grading against the maker's intention. B: Her stated reason: a grading standard would turn a point of entry into a benchmark that permits only one way of looking. C: The teacher refuses to grade visitor cards at all, even conditionally. D: The passage does not separate cards from drawings this way. **Trap:** the principle explains the refusal; it is not a general rule about all visitor responses. **Shortcut:** find the teacher's own phrase “allowed only one way of looking” and match it. **Time:** ~70 s.

**R3-Q12 — C.** A: The teacher refuses to grade visitors' responses. B: The descriptions remain and are read, so makers' descriptions are not declared unnecessary. C: The descriptions attract reading, and the blank cards protect alternative interpretations: both halves are represented. D: The school keeps the drawings and both kinds of card; nothing is removed. **Trap:** a whole-passage idea must reconcile attention with interpretive openness. **Shortcut:** keep the option that names both the explanation and the openness. **Time:** ~55 s.

**R3-Q13 — D.** A: Giving less weight to observed shade opposes the surveyor, who says shade already helps some walkers. B: Leaving the wall out of the survey contradicts her reasoning, since she considers both. C: A single noon reading cannot implement the several-hours comparison. D: Her proposal asks for measurements at several hours, recognising both helpful shade and wall heat. **Trap:** translate qualified analysis into qualified action, not a one-factor survey. **Shortcut:** check which option keeps both factors and several hours. **Time:** ~60 s.

**R3-Q14 — A.** A: The councillor argues that planting is pointless because of one hot wall-side stretch, which dismisses the wider benefit. B: The surveyor, not the councillor, emphasises that shade helps some walkers. C: The councillor does not claim that the wall alone causes every hot stretch; the option overstates his argument. D: No change to walking hours is attributed to the councillor. **Trap:** identify the overgeneralisation rather than add a policy the speaker never advanced. **Shortcut:** compare the scope of the councillor's conclusion with the scope of his evidence. **Time:** ~60 s.

**R3-Q15 — B.** A: Councillors elsewhere planting trees says nothing about this lane's benefit. B: Less midday heat for walkers away from the wall, after planting, is evidence that the planting helps; it undermines “pointless”. C: How walking times are spread says nothing about whether the planting helps. D: Wall heat is the councillor's evidence; it supports his claim rather than weakening it. **Trap:** a weakener must lower support for the claim, not restate its premise. **Shortcut:** find the finding that shows a benefit where the councillor said there was none. **Time:** ~60 s.

**R3-Q16 — C.** A: The narrator does not say that a tentative start makes further practice unnecessary. B: The sweeper is encouraged by the effort; she does not doubt that progress will follow. C: “At least someone has begun” values a first effort without evidence of mastery. D: The sweeper returns to her road, which is not abandonment of her own work. **Trap:** preserve the phrase's modest scope; do not read eventual achievement into a first note. **Shortcut:** keep the option that values the start without predicting the result. **Time:** ~50 s.

**R3-Q17 — A.** A: The committee keeps each stance and its conditions in the record, separating inquiries from conditional endorsements. B: Listing conditional letters as plain support drops the conditions the committee insists on keeping; the separation of inquiries is right, but this option loses the conditions. C: Counting information requests as support contradicts the committee's refusal to count inquiries as supporters. D: Publishing only a final tally discards the reasons the committee decided to publish with each count. **Trap:** the main message spans three elements: stance, conditions and a warning against an automatic tally. **Shortcut:** check whether an option keeps the stance, the condition and the separation. **Time:** ~70 s.

**R3-Q18 — D.** A: The “support” label does not show that the waiting area was provided. B: Calling the letters opposition misreports their stance and loses their endorsement. C: Calling them questions misreports their endorsement. D: The letters endorse the move but require a waiting area first, which a bare label omits. **Trap:** a partly accurate label can lose a decisive “first” qualifier. **Shortcut:** identify the qualifier in the letters and see which option drops it. **Time:** ~60 s.

**R3-Q19 — B.** A: The proposal aims to add perspectives, not to identify a universally preferred site. B: Visits could add voices only if relevant older residents' views are missing from the submissions; this is the unstated premise. C: No reliability comparison is made. D: Other residents' questions do not establish that their concerns match the older residents'. **Trap:** test the bridge between additional visits and additional relevant voices, not whether visits settle the final vote. **Shortcut:** negate the option: if the older residents' views were already captured, visits would add nothing. **Time:** ~60 s.

**R3-Q20 — C.** A: The first evening's different counts are reported. B: The late arrival at the second light is reported. **R3-Q21 — A.** B: The teacher explicitly does not say brightness is irrelevant. C: Equal periods would make the comparison fairer, but the teacher does not say they would settle the cause. D: The first evening's counts differ, but no evidence establishes brightness as the chief cause. A: The teacher says pooling totals without the unequal periods makes the comparison appear more settled than it is. **Trap:** methodological doubt is not a proven alternative causal explanation. **Shortcut:** match the option's verb to the teacher's words (“appear more settled”). **Time:** ~60 s.

**R3-Q22 — D.** A: The teacher keeps both evenings' records and does not dismiss pre-experiment observations. B: She declines the sole-cause inference. C: Different periods are central to her criticism, so she is not indifferent. D: She values the counts while asking for a fairer comparison and an explicit caveat. **Trap:** a cautionary tone does not mean contempt for the data. **Shortcut:** find the option that holds both receptivity and caution. **Time:** ~50 s.

**R3-Q23 — B.** A: The group did not record how freed time was used, so a rest record is not supported. C: Other duties are a possibility, not an observed use of saved time.
