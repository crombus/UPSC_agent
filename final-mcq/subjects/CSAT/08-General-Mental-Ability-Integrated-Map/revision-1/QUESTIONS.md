**Passage for R1-Q01 to R1-Q03**

> Several cities have run "time banks", in which a volunteer earns one credit for each hour of help given and spends that credit on an hour of someone else's help. Supporters note that no cash changes hands, so the scheme does not compete with paid workers, and that it links neighbours who would otherwise never meet. Critics answer that treating every hour as equal ignores large differences in skill: an hour of specialist tutoring and an hour of lawn-mowing earn the same credit, which may leave people with scarce skills under-rewarded and less willing to join. A further worry is durability. The schemes rely on sustained goodwill, which tends to fade once the novelty wears off. Evaluations in two cities found that the most active members had typically been well connected before they joined.
>
