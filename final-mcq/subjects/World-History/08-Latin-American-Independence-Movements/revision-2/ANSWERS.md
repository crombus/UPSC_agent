### Q1. B

**Route:** Creole property did not confer the senior-office preference enjoyed by peninsulares.

- **A (incorrect):** Creole estates did not confer higher-office priority over Spanish-born peninsular candidates. [B§2,§8.4; F3]
- **B (correct):** Creole property could coexist with resentment of peninsular priority for senior crown appointments. [B§2,§8.4; F3]
- **C (incorrect):** Peninsulares enjoyed, rather than lacked, advantages in colonial crown appointment. [B§2,§8.4; F3]
- **D (incorrect):** Subordinate Indigenous and mixed-race groups did not inherit peninsular office privileges. [B§2,§8.4; F1]
- **Examiner trap:** Property and official appointment are distinct dimensions of creole privilege.
- **Sources:** B§2,§8.4; F1,F3.

### Q2. C

**Route:** Participation in Hidalgo's opening did not determine the social terms of Iturbide's settlement.

- **A (incorrect):** Iturbide's conservative guarantees did not simply carry forward Hidalgo's popular social programme. [B§2,§8.4; F1]
- **B (incorrect):** Morelos continued the insurgency, but the later Plan guaranteed Catholic and elite privileges, not a settled Indigenous land reform. [B§4,§8.5; F1]
- **C (correct):** Earlier Indigenous and mixed-race participation did not entail land redistribution by the eventual conservative coalition. [B§2,§8.4; F1]
- **D (incorrect):** Guerrero joined Iturbide's independence coalition; his later opposition to Iturbide's empire does not reverse that initial alliance. [B§4; F1]
- **Examiner trap:** Distinguish the sequence and the social beneficiaries of its different phases.
- **Sources:** B§2,§8.4; F1.

### Q3. C

**Route:** Emancipation in Saint-Domingue preceded independence; Brazil's legal slavery survived separation.

- **A (incorrect):** Louverture imposed forced plantation labour on legally free workers before independence; Dessalines later re-established plantation coercion. It did not begin with Dessalines. Brazil retained legal slavery. [F2 (Haiti /10.htm,/11.htm),F5]
- **B (incorrect):** Emancipation in French Saint-Domingue preceded, rather than began with, Haiti's declaration; Brazil did retain slavery. [F2,F5]
- **C (correct):** Colonial abolition during the Haitian revolution predates its independence; Pedro's Brazil still bought and sold enslaved people. [F2,F5]
- **D (incorrect):** Haitian emancipation developed before independence, not afterward; Brazil's restrictions on slave trading did not abolish slavery. [F2,F5]
- **Examiner trap:** Legal abolition, coerced labour, independence and slave-trade restrictions are four different milestones.
- **Sources:** B§2,§8.4; A§3; F2,F5.

### Q4. B

**Route:** Longstanding trade restraints did not themselves depose Ferdinand; Napoleon's intervention created the legitimacy crisis.

- **A (incorrect):** Spanish colonial trade restrictions were earlier grievances, not products of Ferdinand's captivity. [B§2–3; F3]
- **B (correct):** The trade grievance was older; the invasion and displacement of Spain's monarch raised the immediate authority question. [B§2–3; F3]
- **C (incorrect):** Juntas arose during the king's captivity, not because he had returned to impose new trading rules. [B§1,§3; F3]
- **D (incorrect):** The juntas' provisional invocation of captive Ferdinand responded to his displacement; it was not the exercise of a prior royal grant of autonomy. [B§3; F3]
- **Examiner trap:** Distinguish a longstanding trade grievance from the Spanish monarchy's immediate disruption.
- **Sources:** B§2–3; T5–8; F3.

### Q5. C

- **Sources:** B§1,§4,§8.5; A§3; F2.

### Q9. A
