### Q1. C
- **A:** A title did not convert an American-born creole into a person born in Spain for the office hierarchy. [B§2]
- **B:** Estate ownership did not change a creole's place of birth to Europe. [B§2]
- **C (correct):** American-born white creoles might own estates yet face peninsular preference for top appointments. [B§2,§8.4; I]
- **D:** Peninsular status mattered precisely to the upper appointments, not just trade connections. [B§2]
**Trap:** Keep office hierarchy separate from private landed wealth.

### Q2. D
- **A:** Continuing land pressure contradicts a supposed primary restoration of Indigenous land. [B§8.4]
- **B:** Replacing a peninsular official with a creole did not itself resolve Indigenous land tenure. [B§5,§8.4]
- **C:** Participation is not evidence of control over creole estates. [B§8.4; A§1]
- **D (correct):** Indigenous service in revolt did not establish an automatic transfer of land after independence. [B§8.4]
**Trap:** Political membership, land rights and actual redistribution are distinct.

### Q3. C
- **A:** The French commissioners' abolition belonged to the colonial revolutionary struggle, not an independent Haitian state. [H; U art. 3]
- **B:** Louverture's autonomy remained explicitly connected to the French Empire. [U arts. 1, 27–28]
- **C (correct):** Dessalines' declaration after renewed resistance to France marked an independent Haiti. [H; E]
- **D:** Louverture's constitution named him governor but still identified the colony with France. [U arts. 1, 27–28]
**Trap:** Emancipation, autonomous governance and independence are distinct stages.

### Q4. B
- **A:** Office resentment and trade restrictions predated the juntas rather than arising from them. [B§2–3]
- **B (correct):** Pre-existing economic controls and grievances were activated by the Napoleonic legitimacy crisis. [B§2–3; C]
- **C:** Juntas arose amid challenged royal legitimacy, not only after they had ended monopoly. [B§3]
- **D:** Colonial trade restraints antedated the Napoleonic invasion. [B§2–3]
**Trap:** A proximate trigger should not swallow the long-term causes.

### Q5. B
- **A:** Catholicism was indeed the sole publicly professed faith, but a colony's religious rules do not establish whether it is politically independent. [U art. 6; U art. 1]
- **B (correct):** The text explicitly calls Saint-Domingue part of the French Empire, notwithstanding its particular laws. [U art. 1]
- **C:** Eligibility for office regardless of colour is genuine, but civil equality does not by itself terminate French sovereignty. [U art. 4; U art. 1]
- **D:** The local assembly really could vote on the governor's proposed laws; its internal powers did not override the express imperial connection. [U arts. 19, 24; U art. 1]
**Trap:** All four clauses are real; distinguish civil status, local powers and formal sovereignty.

### Q6. D
- **A:** Continuing existing royal law does not itself constitute the local claim to new provisional authority. [B§3; C]
- **B:** Declared separation is a stronger claim than the initial response to a disputed crown. [B§3,§8.5]
- **C:** A viceroy's pre-existing delegated power is not the council's assertion of local provisional government. [B§3]
- **D (correct):** A junta could assert provisional local government during uncertainty about the Spanish crown. [B§3,§8.5; C]
**Trap:** Early autonomy claims need not begin as fully independent republics.

### Q7. C
- **A:** The court's move to Rio came before Lisbon's later Cortes summoned Pedro. [F]
- **B:** The Cortes sought to reverse Brazil's enhanced autonomy, not reaffirm its coequal standing. [F]
- **C (correct):** Court in Rio → constitutional pressure from Lisbon → Pedro's refusal to leave is the sequence. [F]
- **D:** João returned to Portugal; it was Pedro who remained in Brazil. [F]
**Trap:** João's residence and Pedro's refusal belong to different phases of the same crisis.

### Q8. D
- **A:** Hidalgo initiated the revolt before Morelos took up its leadership. [D; M]
- **B:** Iturbide's eventual pact followed, rather than preceded, Morelos's insurgency. [M; P]
- **C:** Iturbide was a former royalist at the later settlement, not the initial popular insurgent. [I; D]
- **D (correct):** Hidalgo's appeal, Morelos's insurgency and Iturbide's pact form the defensible sequence. [D; M; P]
**Trap:** Do not project Iturbide's conservative settlement back onto Hidalgo's initial movement.

### Q9. B
- **A:** The sea expedition to Peru followed the crossing and Chilean campaign, not preceded them. [J; B§4]
- **B (correct):** San Martín prepared in Cuyo, crossed to Chile and then sailed against Peru. [J; B§4]

- **D:** An Upper Peruvian march was not the Chile-and-sea strategy that San Martín followed. [J; B§4]
**Trap:** Distinguish the preparatory base, overland Chilean phase and Peruvian sea phase.

### Q10. A
- **A (correct):** The republic followed the victory in New Granada; Ecuador's incorporation was later. [G]

### Q11. A
- **A (correct):** Bolívar appointed Sucre; Sucre commanded the patriot army in the battle. [Y; S]
- **B:** Bolívar was senior to Sucre but did not command the field army there. [Y; S]

- **D:** La Serna commanded the opposing royalists; Bolívar appointed Sucre, not La Serna, to lead the liberation army. [Y]
**Trap:** An actual battle commander on the royalist side is not the patriot commander.

### Q12. D
- **A:** Upper Peru became a separate republic, not an Andean province of Gran Colombia. [S; G]
- **B:** Despite the adjoining Peruvian theatre, Upper Peru did not become a province of independent Peru. [S; B§1]

- **D (correct):** The territory formed the separate state named Bolivia after the final campaigns. [S; B§1]
**Trap:** Upper Peru's geographical name does not establish membership in Peru.

### Q13. B
- **A:** Pedro became emperor, but independence did not convert enslaved plantation labour to free wages. [F]
- **B (correct):** Pedro's empire separated politically while slavery endured. [F; B§8.5]
- **C:** João returned to Portugal; Pedro became the independent Brazilian emperor. [F]
- **D:** Pedro broke with Portugal and became emperor, rather than remaining its regent. [F]
**Trap:** The planter's desired continuity concerned labour, not continued Portuguese sovereignty.

### Q14. B
- **A:** Upper Peru was an endpoint of the broader Andean campaigns, not a substitute for the southern route. [B§4; S]
- **B (correct):** Haiti, Mexico, Brazil and northern/southern South America preserve five distinct comparative routes. [B§4,§8.5]
- **C:** Substituting Upper Peru for northern South America erases Bolívar's northern campaign. [B§4; G]
- **D:** Chile was a stage of San Martín's southern route, not its equivalent including Peru. [J; B§4]
**Trap:** A constituent theatre does not replace an entire regional campaign.

### Q15. D
- **A:** Enduring estates do not imply a redistribution to Indigenous farmers. [B§5,§8.4]
- **B:** Peninsular top-office preference was displaced by independence; creole land often remained. [B§8.4; A§1]
- **C:** Ending Spanish sovereignty, not preserving it, defines the stated change. [B§5]
- **D (correct):** Political sovereignty could change while creole landed power endured. [B§5,§8.4; A§1]
**Trap:** This is a qualified mainland tendency, not a claim about Haiti's antislavery outcome.

### Q16. C
- **A:** Gran Colombia fragmented and Central America's federation also dissolved; the latter did not remain intact. [G; C]
- **B:** Central America fragmented, but Gran Colombia did not preserve an enduring union either. [G; C]
- **C (correct):** Separate unions both encountered provincial interests and communication difficulties. [G; C; B§8.6]
- **D:** Neither union's dissolution meant restoration of Spanish rule. [G; C]
**Trap:** Parallel fragmentation is compatible with distinct geographies and political histories.

### Q17. B
- **A:** Election alone does not neutralise the private militia still loyal to its commander. [B§8.6; A§2]
- **B (correct):** Bringing private loyalties under civilian institutional control targets the caudillo mechanism. [B§8.6; A§2,§4]
- **C:** A life-appointed military superior centralises personal command without building accountable civilian control. [B§8.6; A§2]
- **D:** Estate financing leaves officers dependent on landed patrons rather than public institutions. [B§8.6; A§2]
**Trap:** Written rules without control over coercion do not resolve personal command.

### Q18. C
- **A:** Creole landed and war-made military authority could coexist; neither displacement follows from the other's rise. [B§8.4,§8.6; A§1–2]
- **B:** Wartime armed clients did not automatically become an elected civilian administration. [B§8.6; A§2]
- **C (correct):** Land, church and armed networks could constrain thin civilian institutions. [A§1–4]
- **D:** Colonial rule had not created a broadly representative civilian administrative class. [A§2]
**Trap:** Constitutional design and capacity to implement it are separate.

### Q19. A
- **A (correct):** Haiti challenges the blanket creole label; Ghana's negotiated transfer and Algeria's armed struggle challenge a blanket negotiated-withdrawal label. [H; B§8.7; A§5]
- **B:** Haiti's enslaved-led revolution cannot be reclassified as a creole-led secession. [H; A§3]
- **C:** Algeria's armed struggle cannot be assimilated to Ghana's negotiated transfer. [B§8.7; A§5]
- **D:** Both Haiti and Algeria defeat the respective generalisations. [H; B§8.7]
**Trap:** Qualify both halves of a cross-century comparison; one exception is insufficient.

### Q20. B
- **A:** Land survived but war-made officers did not thereby create durable nationwide civilian administrations. [B§8.4,§8.6; A§2]
- **B (correct):** Military clients, regional elite rivalry and exclusion all obstructed civic institutions. [B§8.6,§8.8; A§4]
- **C:** Military clients endured; battlefield victories did not extinguish regional competition. [G; B§8.6]
- **D:** Creole estates survived but broad civilian administration did not precede the wars. [B§8.4,§8.6; A§2]
**Trap:** Keep the true half of each rival explanation separate from its false institutional consequence.

### Q21. C

### Q22. B

### Q23. B
- **A:** The constitution prohibited slavery but still regulated plantation cultivation and labour. [U arts. 3, 14–16]
- **B (correct):** Legal abolition coexisted with regulated cultivation in a colony identified as French. [U arts. 1, 3, 14–16]
- **C:** French imperial status did not preclude local abolition or rules for cultivation. [U arts. 1, 3, 14–16]
- **D:** These cultivation provisions occur in Louverture's pre-independence constitution, not only after Dessalines' declaration. [U arts. 1, 14–16; E]
**Trap:** Distinguish legal abolition from freedom of labour movement and from political sovereignty.

### Q24. A
- **A (correct):** Morelos succeeded Hidalgo, convened Chilpancingo and urged abolition and popular sovereignty. [M]
- **B:** Hidalgo launched an earlier phase; the named congress and programme belong to his successor Morelos. [D; M]

**Trap:** Later insurgent participation by Guerrero does not make him the congress's author.

### Q25. D
- **A:** Morelos advocated popular sovereignty and emancipation, but those were not simply carried over into Iguala's monarchic settlement. [M; P]
- **B:** Iguala explicitly protected Catholicism and church position; it did not reduce church privilege. [P; I]

**Trap:** Similar independence aims did not mean identical institutions or church policy.

### Q26. C

### Q27. D
