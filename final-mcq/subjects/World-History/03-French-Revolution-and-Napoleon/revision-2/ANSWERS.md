### 1. A

- **A:** Yes: borrowing would not dissolve legally entrenched privilege.
- **B:** No: Borrowing changes financing, not estate exemptions.
- **C:** No: Subsistence relief does not decide voting and constituent authority.
- **D:** No: The loan does not alter hereditary access to office.
- **Evidence:** B §§3, 8.4; A §1. **Examiner trap:** A temporary financing device is not a reform of legal privilege.

### 2. D

- **A:** No: Property alone did not grant a commoner noble estate status.
- **B:** No: Privileged access was not simply a function of paying taxes.
- **C:** No: Legal status and exemption mattered even among property owners.
- **D:** Yes: social wealth and legal status were not equivalent.
- **Evidence:** B §§3, 8.4; A §2. **Examiner trap:** Distinguish property ownership from hereditary estate privilege.

### 3. C

- **A:** No: Deputies contested who could authorise reform for the nation.
- **B:** No: The Assembly's claim preceded the popular assault.
- **C:** Yes: the Third Estate's claim redirected the process.
- **D:** No: The summons opened a conflict, not an immediate republic.
- **Evidence:** B §§1, 4; A §3. **Examiner trap:** The institutional rupture preceded the Bastille.

### 4. B

- **A:** No: Both political changes belong to later phases.
- **B:** Yes: deputy claim and popular intervention differed.
- **C:** No: The institutional and popular forms have been reversed.
- **D:** No: They occurred in the revolution's opening, before the Directory.
- **Evidence:** B §§1, 4, 6. **Examiner trap:** Constitutional oath versus popular assault, neither a completed republic.

### 5. A

- **A:** Yes: legal transformation exceeded economic redistribution.
- **B:** No: Legal reform did not abolish unequal property.
- **C:** No: Women remained excluded from equal political citizenship.
- **D:** No: The declaration's sovereignty claim challenged dynastic monopoly.
- **Evidence:** B §§8.5–8.7; A §2. **Examiner trap:** Legal status and political principles are not equal property or suffrage.

### 6. B

- **A:** No: The monarchy's collapse involved war and political distrust.
- **B:** Yes: these pressures destabilised the compromise.
- **C:** No: A constitutional settlement did not eliminate royal mistrust.
- **D:** No: The republic preceded both Jacobin downfall and the Directory.
- **Evidence:** B §§4, 8.6. **Examiner trap:** Distinguish early subsistence crisis from the monarchy's wartime collapse.

### 7. A

- **A:** Yes: republic and universal citizenship were not synonymous.
- **B:** No: Women did not receive national suffrage.
- **C:** No: Broader male rights arose in a later radical phase.
- **D:** No: The propertied republic curtailed the radical political vision.
- **Evidence:** B §§8.5–8.6; A §§2–3. **Examiner trap:** Abolition of monarchy is not universal electoral participation.

### 8. A

- **A:** Yes: the three-part mechanism includes coercive costs.
- **B:** No: Social rights and republicanism do not logically mandate Terror.
- **C:** No: Internal political conflict mattered alongside war.
- **D:** No: The declaration predates the radical emergency government.
- **Evidence:** B §8.6; A §§1, 3. **Examiner trap:** Explain emergency politics without conflating cause and moral justification.

### 9. B

- **A:** No: Coercion was part of the Jacobin regime.
- **B:** Yes: mass politics and repression coexisted.
- **C:** No: Those institutions identify the later Directory.
- **D:** No: This is a later Napoleonic tension, not a Jacobin one.
- **Evidence:** A §3; B §§8.6–8.7. **Examiner trap:** Locate social and coercive contradictions within the same regime.

### 10. D

- **A:** No: Political inclusion did not solve subsistence needs.
- **B:** No: Ending feudal privilege was not guaranteed distributive equality.
- **C:** No: The propertied retreat frustrated rather than satisfied such aims.
- **D:** Yes: political inclusion differed from material equality.
- **Evidence:** A §§2–3; B §§8.5–8.7. **Examiner trap:** Legal/political change is not a wage or bread guarantee.

### 11. C

- **A:** No: Its regime marked a retreat yet remained fragile.
- **B:** No: Limited voting did not exclude military intervention.
- **C:** Yes: disorder increased leverage of successful generals.
- **D:** No: It was a republic, not a restored royal government.
- **Evidence:** B §§4, 8.6; A §4. **Examiner trap:** The army's leverage grew in an unstable post-Terror republic.

### 12. D

- **A:** No: The Jacobin republic preceded the Directory.
- **B:** No: Monarchy was abolished before the republican phase.
- **C:** No: The Directory preceded the coup and Consulate.
- **D:** Yes: these are distinct successive political forms.
- **Evidence:** B §§1, 4, 9; W Q45–56. **Examiner trap:** The Consulate began with the coup; empire came later.

### 13. C

- **A:** No: Napoleonic codification coexisted with censorship.
- **B:** No: The Code did not restore radical republican control.
- **C:** Yes: this is the central continuity/rupture distinction.
- **D:** No: Repression of dissent did not restore feudal law wholesale.
- **Evidence:** B §§5, 8.5; A §4. **Examiner trap:** Codified civil equality is not evidence of political liberty.

### 14. D

- **A:** No: National female suffrage had not been granted.
- **B:** No: The Code did not confer equal authority on married women.
- **C:** No: Political participation did not remove gendered family-law limits.
- **D:** Yes: legal equality was not gender-neutral in effect.
- **Evidence:** B §8.5; A §4. **Examiner trap:** Separate participation, franchise and civil family status.

### 15. C

- **A:** No: Rights language did not automatically end colonial slavery.
- **B:** No: The Napoleonic reversal followed earlier revolutionary abolition.
- **C:** Yes: principle and colonial practice diverged.
- **D:** No: Civil codification did not confer colonial political rights.
- **Evidence:** B §§8.5, 8.8; W Q69–72. **Examiner trap:** Keep revolutionary abolition, Napoleonic reversal and final abolition distinct.

### 16. A

- **A:** Yes: everyday political vocabulary changed too.
- **B:** No: It advanced citizens' rights against inherited authority.
- **C:** No: Revolutionary symbolic changes began earlier.
- **D:** No: Citizenship language did not ensure equal inclusion.
- **Evidence:** B §§2, 8.5; A §5. **Examiner trap:** Vocabulary changed beyond law but did not complete equality.

### 17. D

- **A:** No: Revolutionary nationhood also accompanied warfare.
- **B:** No: Colonial slavery exposed limits of the universal claim.
- **C:** No: Reforms and military conquest could be linked under Napoleon.
- **D:** Yes: liberation rhetoric and war-making could diverge.
- **Evidence:** B §§8.5, 8.7; A §5. **Examiner trap:** Nationhood can ground liberation claims and accompany expansion.

### 18. D

- **A:** No: Defeat did not reverse all social and legal reform.
- **B:** No: Legal equality persisted despite political regime change.
- **C:** No: Monarchy's return was not an extension of Jacobin government.
- **D:** Yes: political reversal did not equal total social restoration.
- **Evidence:** B §§5, 8.5; A §4. **Examiner trap:** Postwar political restoration is not total legal restoration.

### 19. C

- **A:** No: American settlers did not share France's estate structure.
- **B:** No: French domestic social conflict had its own mechanism.
- **C:** Yes: cross-Atlantic vocabulary did not mean identical social agendas.
- **D:** No: Rights vocabulary did not settle either exclusion.
- **Evidence:** B §§8.1, 8.8; A §§1, 5. **Examiner trap:** Compare social targets, not only ideological vocabulary.

### 20. D

- **A:** No: Subsequent electoral restriction refutes uninterrupted widening.
- **B:** No: Napoleon ended the republic.
- **C:** No: It marked a political retreat from the radical programme.
- **D:** Yes: the institutional legacy was non-linear.
- **Evidence:** B §§8.5–8.8; A §§1, 4. **Examiner trap:** Use post-1789 reversals rather than only opening events.

### 21. C

- **A:** No: The constitutional monarchy had preceded the republic.
- **B:** No: The Consulate belongs to a later regime.
- **C:** Yes: regicide followed the abolition of monarchy.
- **D:** No: It was summoned while Louis XVI still ruled.
- **Evidence:** B §§1, 9. **Examiner trap:** Date regime abolition before regicide.

### 22. A

- **A:** Yes: secular legitimacy and repression differ.
- **B:** No: Public law need not entail religious persecution.
- **C:** No: Revolutionary reform challenged that privilege.
- **D:** No: A political retreat is not total restoration of the Old Regime.
- **Evidence:** B §§3, 8.5; A §5. **Examiner trap:** An attack on institutional privilege is not a mandate to coerce belief.
