### 1. B

- **A:** No: The sources link popular distress to unequal burdens and fiscal crisis.
- **B:** Yes: the conjunction helped produce a legitimacy crisis.
- **C:** No: An inherited hierarchy is not by itself a sufficient crowd mechanism.
- **D:** No: Loans could postpone revenue pressure without ending privilege.
- **Evidence:** B §§3, 8.4; A §1. **Examiner trap:** A conjunction of structural and conjunctural causes is not a monocause.

### 2. B

- **A:** No: Wealth did not by itself change estate membership.
- **B:** Yes: a wealthy commoner could still lack noble exemption and office.
- **C:** No: The clergy were a separate privileged estate.
- **D:** No: Hereditary status made office access unequal.
- **Evidence:** B §3; A §2. **Examiner trap:** Wealth, occupation and estate status are separate axes.

### 3. C

- **A:** No: Summoning estates was still royal action, not the deputies' national claim.
- **B:** No: The assault was mass intervention, not the deputies' constituent act.
- **C:** Yes: the Third Estate asserted constituent authority.
- **D:** No: The oath instead committed deputies to making one.
- **Evidence:** B §§1, 4, 9. **Examiner trap:** National constituent authority was asserted by deputies against exclusive royal authority.

### 4. C

- **A:** No: Those reforms were legislated separately by deputies.
- **B:** No: That description fits the Tennis Court Oath, not the Bastille.
- **C:** Yes: its symbolic political role outran prisoner count.
- **D:** No: That description fits the later declaration, not the storming.
- **Evidence:** B §§1, 6; W Q21–24. **Examiner trap:** A popular assault is neither an oath nor an enacted reform.

### 5. B

- **A:** No: Rural revolt was a social pressure, not an Assembly ordinance.
- **B:** Yes: institutional and social pressures interacted.
- **C:** No: The Assembly made the institutional proclamations.
- **D:** No: Both contributed to challenges to privilege.
- **Evidence:** B §§3–4, 8.4; A §2. **Examiner trap:** Elite constituent action and rural mobilisation were different kinds of agency.

### 6. A

- **A:** Yes: norm and implementation are distinct.
- **B:** No: The rights language did not establish women's political franchise.
- **C:** No: End of estate privilege did not equal redistribution of all wealth.
- **D:** No: Abolition came later and was reversed.
- **Evidence:** B §§8.5, 8.8; A §5. **Examiner trap:** A legal norm does not guarantee suffrage or colonial emancipation.

### 7. D

- **A:** No: The Oath did not itself end monarchy.
- **B:** No: Republic and Directory came later.
- **C:** No: The Directory followed republican politics.
- **D:** Yes: the regime changed in stages, with republic in 1792.
- **Evidence:** B §§1, 4, 9. **Examiner trap:** 1791 monarchy and 1792 republic are distinct.

### 8. C

- **A:** No: Explanation of pressure is not justification of every coercive act.
- **B:** No: Debt cannot explain all war and factional mechanisms.
- **C:** Yes: context explains, not morally justifies, Terror.
- **D:** No: The Directory succeeded the Terror, not preceded it.
- **Evidence:** B §§4, 8.6; A §3. **Examiner trap:** War and fear are mechanisms, not moral absolution.

### 9. D

- **A:** No: Punishment of a royal figure does not establish distributive equality.
- **B:** No: One cannot infer disappearance of material distress.
- **C:** No: It did not prescribe later emergency coercion.
- **D:** Yes: concrete pressures shaped radical coercion.
- **Evidence:** B §8.6; A §§1, 3. **Examiner trap:** Identify the war/faction mechanism without claiming rights mandate repression.

### 10. D

- **A:** No: Broader male membership was part of the Jacobin political vision.
- **B:** No: A proposed public duty went beyond property protection.
- **C:** No: The constitutional vision joined political and social claims.
- **D:** Yes, this inference is unwarranted: a proposed duty to provide work did not guarantee equal income; the Jacobin constitution was not implemented.
- **Evidence:** B §§8.5–8.7; A §§2–3. **Examiner trap:** For a NOT-infer stem the correct option must be a genuinely unwarranted inference.

### 11. C

- **A:** No: It remained politically unstable and army-dependent.
- **B:** No: It represented a propertied retreat.
- **C:** Yes: that conjunction made a coup possible.
- **D:** No: It was a later republican government.
- **Evidence:** B §§4, 8.6; A §4. **Examiner trap:** Both anti-Terror preference and fear of generals can coexist.

### 12. C

- **A:** No: The Code followed the Napoleonic seizure of power.
- **B:** No: Consulate, Code and empire are distinct developments.
- **C:** Yes: 1799 and 1804 are distinct markers.
- **D:** No: Republican politics preceded Napoleonic codification.
- **Evidence:** B §§1, 9; A §4. **Examiner trap:** Do not collapse Brumaire, the Code and imperial coronation.

### 13. A

- **A:** Yes: Napoleon's legacy is contradictory.
- **B:** No: Civil reform did not guarantee political freedom.
- **C:** No: Napoleonic regimes could dismantle feudal restraints.
- **D:** No: Censorship did not prevent transmission of some reforms.
- **Evidence:** B §§5, 8.7; A §4. **Examiner trap:** Institutional reform abroad could accompany military subordination.

### 14. C

- **A:** No: Changes to slavery did not grant female national suffrage.
- **B:** No: Neither inclusion nor abolition was irreversible.
- **C:** Yes: sex and colonial status limited universalism.
- **D:** No: Family-law limits contradicted universal equality.
- **Evidence:** B §§2, 8.5; A §§2, 5. **Examiner trap:** Distinguish women's participation from colonial emancipation.

### 15. C

- **A:** No: The revolutionary proclamation did not irrevocably free colonies.
- **B:** No: Revolutionary abolition preceded the Code; it was reversed under Napoleon.
- **C:** Yes: principles did not enforce an irreversible colonial settlement.
- **D:** No: Final abolition occurred long after the Bastille.
- **Evidence:** B §§8.5, 8.8; W Q69–72. **Examiner trap:** The revolutionary abolition was reversed; final French abolition came in 1848.

### 16. D

- **A:** No: Waterloo ended the imperial phase; codification could persist.
- **B:** No: It ended decades before the postwar settlement.
- **C:** No: Complete restoration of Old Regime land burdens did not occur.
- **D:** Yes: transmission did not require continued military rule.
- **Evidence:** B §§5, 8.5; A §4. **Examiner trap:** Legal persistence need not depend on continued imperial rule.

### 17. C

- **A:** No: No fixed future programme was dictated by the declaration.
- **B:** No: Transmission of vocabulary is not wholesale institutional transplantation.
- **C:** Yes: influence is not identical to direct causation.
- **D:** No: Political restoration did not erase conceptual influence.
- **Evidence:** B §§8.5, 8.8; A §5. **Examiner trap:** Influence is neither command nor identical institutional survival.

### 18. A

- **A:** Yes: both contribute to modernity by different routes.
- **B:** No: The American Revolution had a distinct colonial target.
- **C:** No: Its constitutional foundations predate Napoleon's rule.
- **D:** No: Their rights claims coexisted with major exclusions.
- **Evidence:** B §§8.1, 8.5; A §7; W PYQ demand card 1. **Examiner trap:** 2019 GS-I asks a comparison, not a fabricated Prelims answer.

### 19. A

- **A:** Yes: this is the owner’s qualified answer route.
- **B:** No: Formal principles coexisted with restricted franchise.
- **C:** No: His rule concentrated power and ended republican government.
- **D:** No: Institutional succession was discontinuous.
- **Evidence:** B §§8.5, 9; A §§5, 7–8; W PYQ demand card 2. **Examiner trap:** 2025 GS-I asks lasting relevance, not literal survival of 1789's regimes.

### 20. B

- **A:** No: The Assembly claim preceded the 1791 constitutional monarchy.
- **B:** Yes: the progressive change of regime is correctly ordered.
- **C:** No: Constitutional monarchy preceded the republic.
- **D:** No: The republic preceded the Directory.
- **Evidence:** B §§1, 4, 9; A §4. **Examiner trap:** Sequence the deputy claim, monarchy, republic, Directory and empire.

### 21. D

- **A:** No: Regicide belongs to a later republican moment.
- **B:** No: The republic preceded the Napoleonic Code.
- **C:** No: His execution followed the abolition of monarchy.
- **D:** Yes: the two moments belong to different phases.
- **Evidence:** B §§1, 9. **Examiner trap:** Abolition and regicide followed the earlier declaration.

### 22. D

- **A:** No: Removal of privilege did not preclude coercive religious policy.
- **B:** No: Secular public authority did not require old clerical privilege.
- **C:** No: Feudal dues preceded the Directory and were not religious worship.
- **D:** Yes: reform and repression can coexist.
- **Evidence:** B §§3, 8.5; A §5. **Examiner trap:** Secular institutional law and liberty of religious practice are separable.
