### Q1. Which statement is best supported by the topic evidence regarding Latin America’s geographical and linguistic scope?
**Correct answer: D.**

**Option A — incorrect:** This option states: The Mexican Revolution began in 1910 and opened a century in which revolutionary legitimacy was later institutionalised through long PRI dominance. It is evidence for **Mexican Revolution**, not **Latin America’s geographical and linguistic scope**; it therefore fails the question's named-unit test.
**Option B — incorrect:** This option states: Oil wealth could fund populist politics while also intensifying vulnerability and instability; Chávez’s rise made Venezuela a major anti-US symbol. It is evidence for **Venezuela’s oil politics**, not **Latin America’s geographical and linguistic scope**; it therefore fails the question's named-unit test.
**Option C — incorrect:** This option states: The source lists US interventions or pressure in cases including Guatemala, Cuba, Brazil, the Dominican Republic, Chile, Nicaragua, Panama and Haiti. It is evidence for **US intervention pattern**, not **Latin America’s geographical and linguistic scope**; it therefore fails the question's named-unit test.
**Option D — correct:** This option states: Latin America includes South America, Central America including Mexico, and Caribbean islands; Spanish is predominant in most states while Portuguese is the main language in Brazil.

**Correct-option rationale (D):** Latin America includes South America, Central America including Mexico, and Caribbean islands; Spanish is predominant in most states while Portuguese is the main language in Brazil. The item tests the distinction between Latin America’s geographical and linguistic scope and closely related regional mechanisms.

**Evidence:** Basic §1; Complete package source audit; Norman Lowe, Ch.26, printed pp.608–609. This is an original question; no UPSC wording or options are reproduced.

**Examiner trap:** Do not reduce a regional category to Spanish-speaking South America alone.

**Coverage:** 1. Latin America’s geographical and linguistic scope
