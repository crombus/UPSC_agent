- **Italy franchise correction:** Lowe's statement that Italy's vote was still wealth-restricted in 1914 [L21] is not safe: [*Modern Italy*, “Italy between liberalism and democracy”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/modern-italy/article/italy-between-liberalism-and-democracy-universal-suffrage-and-the-1913-elections/2A8A082C5C90BB418FCF783405DD8458) describes the earlier suffrage reform and subsequent elections under quasi-universal male suffrage. Q66 tests this distinction; the older wealth-only claim appears only as a rejected distractor.
- **Convention primary text (F):** [US Department of State transcription](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1907p1/d415), Persia preamble, Afghanistan Article V and Tibet Article IV. The online heading prints 1901 in error; the signature line itself dates the convention to 1907. These three provisions differ from R2's north/south Persian concessions, Afghan diplomatic/frontier contacts and Tibet's Chinese intermediary/commercial exception.
- **US opening-war document (U):** [Wilson's neutrality proclamation](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1914Supp/d885), preamble and prohibited acts. It explicitly permits private expressions of sympathy and domestic arms manufacture while forbidding armed expeditions; cite for neutral policy, not later US participation.
- **Bounded source reconciliation (J, K):** [International Encyclopedia, July Crisis](https://encyclopedia.1914-1918-online.net/article/july-crisis-1914-1-1/) describes Grey's concern that promising France might embolden it and Russia, and *half-hearted* German support for mediation proposals without Vienna's uptake; IWM separately says Germany refused Grey's proposed **international conference**. J also quotes Tschirschky's caution against hasty action and Wilhelm's angry marginal rejection of his 2 July report (Q77); the emperor did not endorse the report. These are not evidence that a formal conference met or that no German mediation was ever attempted. [Richard C. Hall, Balkan Wars](https://encyclopedia.1914-1918-online.net/article/balkan-wars-1912-1913/) distinguishes Serbian auxiliaries withdrawing from Scutari under Austrian pressure *before* its capture from Montenegrins withdrawing *after* capture under Great Power flotilla pressure (Q76); Hall also links failed Russian mediation of Macedonia, Bulgaria's defeat and alienation from Russia to Serbia as Russia's remaining Balkan ally. Neither source establishes an official Prelims key.
- **Treaty check (G):** [GRIPS / University of Tokyo, 1902 Anglo-Japanese alliance, Articles II–III](https://worldjpn.net/documents/texts/pw/19020130.T1E.html) prescribes British neutrality if Japan fights one power and assistance if another joins against it. Current Q1 tests that conditional rule directly, not a purported automatic British war against Russia. This source is not an official UPSC PYQ or a substitute for the Lowe chapter.
- **Treaty check (Y):** [Yale Avalon transcription, Franco-Russian military convention (1892), Articles 1–2](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/19th_century/frrumil.asp) lists Germany, German-backed Italy/Austria and Triple Alliance mobilisation triggers, not Britain's alliance with Japan. Q4-D is thus a checked treaty-scope misreading, not an inference from absence of an unnamed treaty clause.
- **Triple Alliance primary text (TA):** [German History in Documents and Images, 1882 Triple Alliance treaty](https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/forging-an-empire-bismarckian-germany-1866-1890/triple-alliance-with-austria-and-italy-may-20-1882), preamble and Articles I, II, IV–V, full English transcription checked. The preamble's monarchy/social-order purpose (Q33) differs from Article I's consultation and Article II's conditional French-attack aid; Article IV reserves *each* partner's option to join while prescribing benevolent neutrality in its distinct threatened-ally scenario (Q28). Neither question asserts an Article IV casus foederis occurred in July 1914.
- **Date hold:** Lowe prints Russian **general** mobilisation **29 July** (PDF pp.25,28); IWM dates Russian forces' **preparation** to **30 July**. These are different descriptions and are not by themselves proof of conflicting dates for an identically defined measure. Detailed chronologies distinguish partial preparation, general authorisation and implementation. Q34 tests only robust order; no item demands an unsupported precise general-mobilisation date.
- **PYQ hold:** No local official Series-A paper plus corresponding FINAL Series-A answer-key pair has been confirmed for a Topic 09 objective question. Unverified candidate questions remain unkeyed and excluded; the topic-wide Prelims ownership census remains open. The 2024 GS-I Q12 is a 15-mark, 250-word Mains demand.
