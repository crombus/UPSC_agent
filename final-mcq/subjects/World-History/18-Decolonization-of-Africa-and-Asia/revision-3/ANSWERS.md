### Q1. Which option correctly pairs the decolonisation unit “European exhaustion after 1945” with its defining feature?
**Correct answer: B.**

**Option A — incorrect:** It pairs **Ghana after independence** with “Cocoa dependence, political closure and economic strain contributed to Nkrumah’s removal by a 1966 coup despite Ghana’s comparatively prepared transfer.”, but that feature is not the defining evidence for **European exhaustion after 1945**; it therefore fails the matching stem.
**Option B — correct:** This option gives the source-bounded proposition for **European exhaustion after 1945**: European exhaustion after 1945: The Second World War weakened European finances, coercive capacity and confidence, reducing the supply of imperial repression.
**Option C — incorrect:** It pairs **Malaya’s plural society** with “Malaya was a multiracial society of mostly Malays and Chinese, with Indians and Europeans, and an export economy based on rubber and tin.”, but that feature is not the defining evidence for **European exhaustion after 1945**; it therefore fails the matching stem.
**Option D — incorrect:** It pairs **Nigeria, 1960** with “Nigeria became independent in 1960 with a federal constitution of three regions and potentially important eastern oil resources.”, but that feature is not the defining evidence for **European exhaustion after 1945**; it therefore fails the matching stem.

**Correct-option rationale (B):** European exhaustion after 1945: The Second World War weakened European finances, coercive capacity and confidence, reducing the supply of imperial repression. The question tests the distinction between the named unit and close-option substitutes.

**Evidence:** ✅ Basic §2 and §7.4; Complete §7.4; Lowe Ch.24 §24.1, printed pp.531–533. This is an original question; no UPSC wording or options are reproduced.

**Examiner trap:** Do not treat exhaustion alone as sufficient; nationalist pressure and legitimacy loss also raised the cost of rule.

**Coverage:** 1. European exhaustion after 1945
