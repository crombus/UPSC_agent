### WH05-R3-34 — D

**Tested:** Central Italy. **Trap:** Genoa's addition to Piedmont does not imply direct Austrian annexation of Parma or the papal territories.

- **A — Incorrect:** Parma was assigned to Marie-Louise, not annexed directly to Austrian Lombardy-Venetia. **Source:** Britannica Vienna Decisions.
- **B — Incorrect:** Parma was not given to the Bourbon court in place of Marie-Louise, though Bourbons were restored elsewhere. **Source:** Britannica Vienna Decisions.
- **C — Incorrect:** Papal States were restored to the pope; Parma itself went to Marie-Louise. **Source:** Britannica Vienna Decisions.
- **D — Correct:** Genoa was added to Sardinia-Piedmont; Vienna ratified Marie-Louise's position in Parma, first awarded under Fontainebleau. Neither disposition annexed all Italy to Austria. **Source:** Britannica Vienna Decisions; Britannica Marie-Louise.

### WH05-R3-35 — D

**Tested:** Polish exceptions. **Trap:** Kraków's free-city status and Austria's Galicia contradict an all-Russian Polish map.

- **A — Incorrect:** Kraków was a free city, not permanently incorporated into Russia; Galicia was restored to Austria. **Source:** Britannica Vienna Decisions.
- **B — Incorrect:** Prussia gained Polish territory but not Kraków as a Prussian city; Galicia was not retained by Russia. **Source:** Britannica Vienna Decisions.
- **C — Incorrect:** Galicia was not the free-city unit; Kraków received that status, while Austria obtained Galicia. **Source:** Britannica Vienna Decisions.
- **D — Correct:** Kraków was made a free city and Galicia reverted to Austria rather than joining Congress Poland. **Source:** Britannica Vienna Decisions.

### WH05-R3-36 — B

**Tested:** Treaty limits. **Trap:** condemning a trade did not establish supranational police or a uniform immediate ban.

- **A — Incorrect:** The German federal Diet did not acquire powers to police transatlantic trafficking. **Source:** Britannica Vienna Decisions; B §3.
- **B — Correct:** The antislave-trade language was declaratory and lacked a workable enforcement scheme; river navigation had more concrete rules. **Source:** Britannica Vienna Decisions.
- **C — Incorrect:** The Holy Alliance was a separate moral pact, not the declaration's maritime enforcement service. **Source:** Britannica Vienna Decisions; Britannica Holy Alliance.
- **D — Incorrect:** The declaration did not bind every state to end the trade immediately; a declaration of principle is not a universal immediate ban. **Source:** Britannica Vienna Decisions.

### WH05-R3-37 — D

**Tested:** Aix settlement. **Trap:** the indemnity and evacuation of France precede the later intervention congresses.

- **A — Incorrect:** Troppau discussed the Neapolitan revolution and intervention doctrine, not the end of occupation in France. **Source:** Britannica Troppau; Britannica Aix.
- **B — Incorrect:** Verona treated French action against Spain after the occupation of France had already ended. **Source:** Britannica Verona; Britannica Aix.
- **C — Incorrect:** Laibach concerned an Austrian army in Naples, not allied troops withdrawing from France. **Source:** Britannica Laibach; Britannica Aix.
- **D — Correct:** An early indemnity settlement at Aix paved the way for the allied evacuation of France and its readmission to consultation. **Source:** Britannica Aix; B §7.5.
