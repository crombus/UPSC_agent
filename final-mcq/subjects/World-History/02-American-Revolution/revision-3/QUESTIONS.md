# American Revolution — Revision 3 | Original questions

Original conceptual MCQs; no exact official Prelims PYQ is included. Select one best answer.

### 1. A minister argues that postwar colonial duties are purely administrative. What is the strongest colonial constitutional reply?

A. Fiscal necessity outweighed objections to Parliament's right to tax.
B. Westminster's virtual representation supplied adequate colonial tax consent.
C. Taxes required colonists' representative consent, not just imperial need.
D. Colonial assemblies could tax locally, so parliamentary duties required no consent.

### 2. Two merchants disagree about British regulations: one profits, the other feels constrained. Which conclusion follows?

A. Gains from trade cancelled the grievance about Parliament's taxation powers.
B. Trade rules affected merchants, but never constrained colonial manufacturing.
C. Mercantilist rules could constrain autonomy even where some colonists profited.
D. Losses from trade rules affected all colonists in the same way and degree.

### 3. A frontier settler asks for freedom from London's settlement limits. Which interpretation avoids an exclusively settler perspective?

A. Removal of restrictions may intensify dispossession of Native communities.
B. Settler grievances against London prove Native people also favoured expansion.
C. Imperial limits protected Native land claims permanently from settler encroachment.
D. Removing restraints could benefit settlers without affecting Native territorial claims.

### 4. A historian claims local protest had no political infrastructure until the federal Constitution. Which evidence best challenges this?

A. Local assemblies organised resistance only after fighting began and imperial rule had ended.
B. Congress first created the colonies' representative assemblies after secession.
C. Pre-existing colonial assemblies and the first Continental Congress organised resistance.
D. Intercolonial coordination began only after adoption of the federal Constitution.

### 5. Which account best distinguishes the Boston Tea Party from the Declaration?

A. Tea protest claimed independence; the Declaration negotiated parliamentary duties.
B. Tea protest challenged policy; the Declaration designed the first federal tax system.
C. Tea protest challenged policy; the Declaration obtained Britain's legal recognition.
D. Tea protest challenged imperial policy; the Declaration claimed sovereignty.

### 6. A curator wants a causal caption for the move from protest to sovereignty. Which is sound?

A. Early tax protests gave way to a Declaration before any continental coordination.
B. Congress proclaimed sovereignty before military conflict turned protests into war.
C. Stamp Act agitation led to coordination, then fighting, then proclaimed independence.
D. The first military clashes prompted a Congress that initiated the Stamp Act protests.

### 7. A student attributes independence's intellectual argument, draft declaration and military command to one person. Which correction fits?

A. Paine argued for independence; Jefferson commanded; Washington drafted.
B. Paine argued for independence; Jefferson drafted; Washington commanded.
C. Paine argued for independence; Washington drafted; Jefferson commanded.
D. Jefferson drafted; Washington commanded; Paine authored the federal Constitution.

### 8. How should the Declaration's claim about equal rights be read in the setting of the early republic?

A. As evidence that property qualifications disappeared when independence was proclaimed.
B. As a legitimating ideal in tension with continuing slavery and exclusion.
C. As a legal measure immediately ending enslavement in the founding states.
D. As a state-design provision determining voting rules across all colonies.

### 9. A general seeks a reason to prioritise diplomatic outreach after a battlefield victory. Which pairing is strongest?

A. Saratoga enhanced American military credibility and helped draw France into formal alliance.
B. Saratoga settled British recognition without changing France's posture.
C. Yorktown persuaded France to enter only after Britain recognised independence.
D. Lexington persuaded France to intervene before Saratoga's strategic result.

### 10. Which feature makes 'Britain fought only colonial rebels' misleading after the war widened?

A. French entry supplemented American forces but ended their military command.
B. France allied with America as Spain and the Dutch also pressured Britain.
C. French aid was limited to trade and never affected the wider military balance.
D. Spanish and Dutch pressure replaced the Franco-American alliance as the main front.

### 11. Three exhibits show Saratoga, Yorktown and the Paris treaty. Which labelling matches their distinct functions?

A. Alliance catalyst; decisive surrender; British recognition.
B. Alliance catalyst; British recognition; the military capitulation.
C. British recognition; military capitulation; alliance catalyst.
D. Military capitulation; alliance catalyst; British recognition.

### 12. Why is the Articles-era union relevant to an account of the Constitution?

A. Its strong independent taxing authority made the later Constitution unnecessary.
B. It supplied a union with an executive as powerful as the later presidency.
C. It formalised the same federal power division as the later Constitution.
D. It was a weaker interstate union preceding the stronger federal Constitution.

### 13. Which hypothetical amendment is closest in function to the actual Bill of Rights?

A. One specifying safeguards for speech, religion and criminal procedure.
B. A clause conferring a veto over legislation on the national executive.
C. A clause distributing authority over trade between union and states.
D. A clause assigning the federal legislature authority over taxation.

### 14. A commentator equates a written federal republic with universal democracy. What is the decisive objection?

A. State-level franchise restrictions ended as soon as independence was declared.
B. A written constitution could not coexist with property restrictions in voting.
C. Federalism extended the franchise without ending enslavement.
D. Federalism did not end slavery or property-based limits on voting.

### 15. An exhibit presents independence as liberation for all residents alike. Which missing groups most directly complicate the claim?

A. Enslaved people and propertied settlers, both excluded from political power.
B. Enslaved people, women and Native Americans lacked equal political inclusion.
C. Native peoples and propertied white men, equally denied representation.
D. Women and male landowners, equally excluded from political participation.

### 16. A museum pairs an American constitution with France's abolition of estate privileges. What inference is warranted?

A. American institutional change and French social change were distinct.
B. French politics developed rights claims but preserved all inherited privileges.
C. Both societies made federalism the principal mechanism for ending privilege.
D. American constitutionalism directly removed French estate privileges.

### 17. Which statement about the Revolution as an anti-colonial precedent is most careful?

A. It showed empire could be broken, but later anticolonial routes differed.
B. It showed an alliance was necessary in every later struggle against empire.
C. It showed later movements could use republican ideals without new social conflicts.
D. It established that every later independence movement produced a federal republic.

### 18. Which verdict best integrates political outcome with a critical qualification?

A. The Declaration's equality claim meant all free residents could vote in the new republic.
B. The new republic broadened participation and abolished slavery in every state.
C. The peace treaty recognised independence and granted equal civic status nationwide.
D. A rights-based federal republic emerged while inherited hierarchy and exclusion persisted.
