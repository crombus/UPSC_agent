# American Revolution — Revision 2 | Original questions

Original conceptual MCQs; no exact official Prelims PYQ is included. Select one best answer.

### 1. Suppose a colonist benefits from Atlantic trade yet resists a new parliamentary tax. Which explanation avoids a false contradiction?

A. A merchant could oppose trade limits but had to accept every parliamentary tax.
B. Profits from trade implied consent to duties imposed without colonial representation.
C. Trade profits could coexist with opposition to new parliamentary taxes.
D. Profitable trade meant Parliament's tax authority was no longer contested.

### 2. Which proposition differentiates representation in the revolutionary slogan from modern universal suffrage?

A. Colonial assemblies claimed tax consent but did not represent all colonists.
B. Colonial assemblies claimed tax consent and gave all adult colonists equal suffrage.
C. Parliament's virtual representation satisfied the colonists' demand for local consent.
D. Local tax consent meant that assemblies could alone declare independence immediately.

### 3. Which policy conflict most directly exposed the tension between settler demands and Native American interests?

A. Duties imposed on imported tea in colonial ports.
B. Imperial limits on westward settlement.
C. Parliamentary limits on colonial tax assemblies.
D. British limits on colonial manufacturing.

### 4. How should the Stamp Act be placed within the causation of independence?

A. As a dispute over the level of a duty rather than Parliament's right to tax.
B. As a trade restriction that primarily forbade westward settlement.
C. As a direct decision by colonial assemblies to levy their own stamp tax.
D. As a flashpoint for opposition to taxes imposed without colonial consent.

### 5. A classroom chart labels the Tea Party 'proclamation of independence'. Which correction is best?

A. It was a congressional petition declaring secession.
B. It substituted a negotiated tariff settlement for intercolonial resistance.
C. It opened war before Congress could coordinate the colonies.
D. It preceded the first Congress and sharpened confrontation.

### 6. What relationship between the First Continental Congress and colonial assemblies is sound?

A. Congress coordinated resistance using political institutions of the colonies.
B. Congress coordinated resistance only after independence had been declared.
C. Congress asserted a local tax claim by replacing all colonial assemblies.
D. Congress chiefly designed the later federal Constitution before war began.

### 7. Which account correctly links collective protest, the onset of war and the sovereignty claim?

A. Continental coordination declared independence before any armed confrontation with British forces.
B. Boycotts and Congress preceded Lexington; the Declaration followed the onset of war.
C. The Tea Party proclaimed sovereignty after Congress had assembled an army of colonial militias.
D. Lexington secured Britain's recognition before a declaration could be issued.

### 8. Jefferson, Washington and Paine are mentioned together. Which allocation of roles is accurate?

A. Jefferson drafted; Washington authored the pamphlet; Paine commanded.
B. Jefferson drafted; Washington commanded; Paine argued for independence.
C. Jefferson drafted; Washington commanded; Paine organised the Articles.
D. Jefferson wrote Common Sense; Washington commanded; Paine drafted.

### 9. The Declaration's rights language is offered as proof that society was already equal. What is the central flaw?

A. It proclaimed equality after states had abolished property rules for voting.
B. The equality ideal outpaced the early republic's social practice.
C. Its equality claim itself guaranteed equal voting rights to women and enslaved people.
D. Its rights language governed the structure of federal taxation rather than secession.

### 10. A map highlights France and Spain alongside the thirteen colonies. Which interpretation is defensible?

A. European rivalry mattered but only after Britain had formally recognised independence.
B. French entry widened the war and replaced the American struggle altogether.
C. French intervention was an imperial economic grievance before hostilities began.
D. American resistance intersected with European rivalries in a widening imperial war.

### 11. Why is Saratoga a better marker for internationalisation than Yorktown?

A. It helped convince France to ally before the later decisive surrender.
B. It persuaded Britain to recognise independence before France joined the war.
C. It drew France into alliance only after the surrender at Yorktown.
D. It was decisive for French entry because it ended the entire war immediately.

### 12. If a timeline equates Yorktown with recognition by Britain, which correction should be made?

A. Recognition preceded the Declaration; Yorktown was a protest against taxes.
B. Yorktown proved a military victory; the Articles secured British recognition.
C. Yorktown was a major capitulation; the Treaty of Paris recognised independence.
D. Yorktown secured French entry; Paris brought battlefield surrender.

### 13. Which comparison between the Articles and the Constitution is historically sustainable?

A. The Articles enabled a central union with independent taxing powers equal to the later Congress.
B. An earlier and weaker union of states gave way to a stronger written federal constitution.
C. The Articles produced an enduringly weak centre even after the new Constitution.

### 14. What does adding a Bill of Rights to the constitutional framework illustrate?

A. Added civil-liberty safeguards within an already formed federal republic.
B. Added safeguards primarily to recognise independence from Britain in the constitutional order.
C. Added safeguards replaced the need to structure powers between federal and state governments.
D. Added safeguards extended universal suffrage throughout the republic.

### 15. What proposition would most seriously overstate the Revolution's political reach?

A. Independence brought equal voting rights to all colonists.
B. Declarations of rights justified resistance to imperial taxes and rule.
C. Local institutions helped organise claims against imperial taxation.
D. A written republican framework succeeded colonial rule.

### 16. A landholder describes the end of Britain's frontier restraints as universal freedom. Which response identifies an omitted perspective?

A. Ending frontier restraints secured both settler access and Native land titles in the west.
B. Britain had restricted migration westward only to protect colonial tax revenues.
C. Settler claims to new land did not conflict with Native territorial interests.
D. Opening land for settler expansion threatened the rights and land of Native peoples.

### 17. Which claim concerning American influence on the French Revolution avoids mechanical causation?

A. French rights claims followed the American model with identical social outcomes.
B. US independence ended France's fiscal crisis without internal French reform.
C. French politics adapted American rights language but did not contest privilege.
D. The US precedent spread rights ideas amid France's own fiscal and social conflicts.

### 18. Which synthesis best answers how revolutionary independence was?

A. It instituted a federal republic without changing the imperial source of political authority.
B. It changed international status but retained a British-appointed chief executive.
C. Independence changed sovereignty but did not erase social or political hierarchies.
D. It ended British sovereignty and gave all adults equal rights in the new republic.
