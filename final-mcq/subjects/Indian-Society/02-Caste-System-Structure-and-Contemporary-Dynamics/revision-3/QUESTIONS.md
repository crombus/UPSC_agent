### Q1. A district form offers only Brahmin, Kshatriya, Vaishya and Shudra as responses to 'Which local group arranges your marriage?' What design change is essential?
A. Recode every household into an occupation instead of a birth group.
B. Recode all marriages into a single districtwide purity hierarchy.
C. Collect locally self-identified jati/sub-jati separately from ideal varna.
D. Collect only an all-India varna rank to capture endogamy precisely.

### Q2. A longitudinal study records changes in job, birth membership and marriage choice. Which result most directly demonstrates unequal rates of change among caste's classical features?
A. Jobs and marriages change equally while birth membership is unrecorded.
B. Jobs diversify across jatis while within-jati marriages remain common.
C. Jobs remain inherited while every recorded marriage crosses jatis.
D. Jobs are unrecorded while local purity ranking changes completely.

### Q3. A marriage survey finds two respondents share a jati but not a gotra. How should an eligibility algorithm handle the evidence?
A. Reject by default because outside-gotra matches breach jati endogamy.
B. Record that two boundary tests differ; check any narrower sub-jati rule.
C. Approve by default because differing gotras remove all other barriers.
D. Treat their gotra difference as proof they belong to separate varnas.

### Q4. A fieldworker observes contrasting purity-based rankings of the same named jati in two districts. Which inference should guide a comparative table?
A. One district must have recorded the jati's legal notification incorrectly.
B. Record district-specific ranking instead of assigning one national score.
C. Take the landholding ranking as the caste's nationwide ritual position.
D. Average the two rankings and label that an unchanging textual varna.

### Q5. To test declining inherited service obligations without claiming caste has ended, which comparison is best?
A. Compare inherited barber/washerman/priest service ties with cash-wage alternatives.
B. Compare national religious counts with all-Panchayat election results.
C. Compare only the textual four varnas with every local legal category.
D. Compare two ritual diets without observing service or remuneration.

### Q6. A jati copies a higher-status group's diet and wedding customs. Which follow-up best tests the *limit* of the Sanskritisation claim?
A. Check only whether those customs were borrowed from British schools.
B. Check only whether the jati legally changed its census category.
C. Check whether neighbours accept its claimed rank and resources change.
D. Check whether a survey now reports a new all-India varna count.

### Q7. A local group copies higher-status rites while younger members invoke British-contact education and law for equality and rational inquiry. Which coding is most defensible?
A. Record Sanskritising emulation and Western-derived equality/rationality separately.
B. Record both changes only as imitation of the higher-ranked local jati.
C. Record both changes only as evidence of large landholding and office.
D. Record neither because they occurred in the same birth community.

### Q8. A public college replaces ritual eligibility with written qualifications. Which pair of dimensions should the researcher keep separate?
A. Impersonal institutional change and declining public ritual authority.
B. Hereditary occupation and automatic upward purity-rank acceptance.
C. Birth membership and automatic abolition of private endogamy.
D. Ritual emulation and automatic adoption of a new local jati.

### Q9. A researcher cites Rampura as proof that *every* Indian village ranks landowners above priests. Which repair is required?
A. Keep Srinivas's Rampura, Karnataka, case without imposing nationwide rankings.
B. Replace the village with a national all-jati survey never conducted.
C. Treat the village's local observation as a fourfold legal varna rule.
D. Transfer its rankings to all villages sharing one regional language.

### Q10. A study labels the largest jati 'dominant' without examining landholding or offices. Which additional evidence most directly closes the gap?
A. Its preferred marriage rite and clan prohibition.
B. Its place in a classical priestly purity hierarchy.
C. Its household language and devotional-sect affiliation.
D. Its share of local land and effective political influence.

### Q11. A data team combines caste, land and Panchayat office into one categorical 'high/low' field. What should a Béteille-informed reanalysis do?
A. Treat caste, class and office as interchangeable labels everywhere.
B. Use jati rank alone to impute each family's land and office.
C. Analyse inherited membership, class assets and power as separate fields.
D. Use election results alone to impute every family's marriage choice.

### Q12. A longitudinal panel wants to test persistence of caste when occupations diversify. Which variable most directly operationalises Ambedkar's reproduction mechanism?
A. Frequency of occupational switching within the birth group.
B. Variation in land rents between two ritual-status groups.
C. Turnout at caste-association election meetings.
D. Rates of within-jati marriage across consecutive cohorts.

### Q13. A study uses a purity-rank inventory to infer who blocks others from accessing Panchayat offices. Which caution is best?
A. Religious purity rank alone determines all votes and village offices.
B. Distinguish Dumont's ritual lens from land, numbers and political control.
C. Ritual rank measures household incomes without need for fieldwork.
D. Occupation determines purity ranking identically across every region.

### Q14. Entry-level jobs have opened to many jatis but one stigmatised trade remains inherited in a locality. Which result does this support?
A. Occupational decoupling has ended all unequal job inheritance.
B. Aggregate job diversification can coexist with residual caste stigma.
C. Occupational change necessarily eliminates marriage-group inheritance.
D. A single inherited trade proves every other job remains hereditary.

### Q15. An investigator observes a jati panchayat adjudicating a marriage dispute and a jati trust funding training and candidates. Which interpretation best handles both?
A. Both bodies are government census offices under different names.
B. Caste bodies can enforce marriage rules and pursue education, welfare and votes.
C. Neither body can function outside traditional occupational services.
D. Both bodies establish the same religious sect across all jatis.

### Q16. A city landlord prefers applicants referred by co-jati business contacts, even though no purity test is used. What should an access study measure?
A. Only ritual exclusion; referrals cannot shape city housing.
B. Network-based referral advantage distinct from ritual gatekeeping.
C. Only inherited occupations; networks cannot act without them.
D. Only the legal varna classification, independent of applicants.

### Q17. A political alliance calls itself one community, but its component jatis preserve ranked marriage boundaries. Which field should the analyst retain?
A. A single ethnic code replacing jati and marriage observations.
B. An income code replacing hierarchy and devotional sect.
C. Separate alliance identity and jati-level rank/endogamy variables.
D. A uniform regional code replacing all caste-specific records.

### Q18. A jati's local standing improves between surveys while marriage records remain predominantly within the group. Which interpretation follows?
A. The marriage boundary's persistence disproves any status shift.
B. The status shift proves marriage records must be inaccurate.
C. The status shift changes every member's legal caste category.
D. Status mobility and boundary reproduction can proceed together.

### Q19. An analyst must study shrine affiliation, marriage choice, electoral blocs and a territorial grievance. Which coding assigns the most relevant identity axes?
A. Jati, sect, region and varna respectively in every area.
B. Varna, region, sect and jati respectively in every area.
C. Region, jati, varna and religion respectively in every area.
D. Sect, jati, jati cluster and region respectively.

### Q20. An investigator samples only prosperous intercaste couples and concludes all interreligious unions will face the same reception. What is the strongest correction?
A. Match by income only; assume parity works alike across both boundaries.
B. Match by religion only; drop material standing from the caste comparison.
C. Match by locality only; infer parity is irrelevant to caste alliances.
D. Match by identity, parity and local context before comparing reception.

### Q21. Couples register civil marriages, but interviews show some kin still object. Which statement best reconciles these sources?
A. A civil route permits registration without guaranteeing social approval.
B. Registration formally dissolves all marriage-group ties for relatives.
C. Interview objections show registration is legally impossible.
D. Opposition alone establishes a national count of intercaste unions.

### Q22. A policy evaluation cites the 2025 caste-enumeration decision as if it were measured jati-level outcomes. Which evidence pipeline is correct?

B. Gazette date → automatic publication of complete caste microdata.
C. Inclusion decision → later enumeration/validation → possible analysis.
D. Historical SC category records → all current jati-level incomes.

### Q25. A study finds poor women and affluent men in one jati, while regional patterns differ. How should it describe the group's disadvantage?

### Q26. A district expands reserved-office access, yet cohort records still show within-jati marriages. Which evaluation separates a legal remedy from social reproduction?

B. Measure office entry and infer that marital boundaries have dissolved.

### Q27. An interviewee's shortlist follows a sub-jati, devotional life a sampradaya, faith affiliation a religion and petition a regional grievance. Which reading fits?

B. Devotional following determines statutory caste and all marriage choices.

### Q28. A council copies a locally prestigious jati's ceremony, starts impersonal hiring and removes religious tests for eligibility. Which conclusion survives if Western-contact equality rhetoric is *also* heard?

B. Hiring reform proves every household accepts the new ritual rank.
