### Q2. A survey finds engineers from traditionally service-linked jatis who still marry within them. Which pair of traits has changed most/least in this evidence?
A. Hereditary membership most; purity hierarchy least.
B. Occupation linkage most; endogamous boundary least.

### Q3. A family restricts matches to its sub-jati and bars a common gotra. How should these two screens be classified?

B. Exogamy outside a sub-jati; endogamy within a shared clan.
C. Narrow endogamy within a sect; exogamy outside a varna.
D. Exogamy outside a jati; endogamy within a regional language.

### Q4. One local priestly group ranks high ritually but cannot command land or votes. Which comparison avoids mixing distinct ladders?
A. Its purity standing automatically measures local land ownership.
B. Ritual rank is one hierarchy; land and votes are another axis.
C. Its rank determines whether elections are legally recognised.
D. Village vote share fixes its standing across all regions.

### Q5. A barber's family once performed inherited service for a landholding patron; its children now earn cash wages elsewhere. Which limited inference is warranted?
A. Jajmani service roles are identical to every non-farm wage relation.
B. The inherited service tie survives whenever a worker shares a jati.
C. A cash wage automatically dissolves the family's marriage boundary.
D. Barber, washerman and priest service ties can weaken without erasing jati.

### Q6. A community emulates a higher-status marriage rite and diet but has not gained local recognition. Which conclusion is defensible?
A. It has failed the definition of Sanskritisation until officially notified.
B. It has Sanskritised a claim; recognition is a separate empirical question.
C. Its economic class must already match the emulated community.
D. Its emulation qualifies as British-contact Westernisation instead.

### Q7. A student labels a new equality-based college rule 'Sanskritisation' because caste is its subject. What is the closer category?
A. Westernisation where British-contact education/law introduce equality and rationality.
B. Sanskritisation where one caste copies a locally higher caste's rites.
C. Dominant-caste formation where land and population converge.
D. Jajmani persistence where one patron funds the college.

### Q8. A ritual office loses influence over a public institution without any jati copying another's diet. Which distinction is being tested?
A. Any weakened ritual office proves every birth category has disappeared.
B. Reduced ritual influence is itself a claim to a higher purity rank.
C. Reduced ritual influence establishes numerical caste dominance.
D. Secularisation of a public arena is not Sanskritising emulation.

### Q9. A note credits Rampura-based observations to Dumont but uses them to explain Srinivas's status-mobility and local-power concepts. What must be corrected?
A. The village fieldwork attribution belongs to Srinivas in Karnataka.
B. The village fieldwork attribution belongs to Ambedkar in Karnataka.
C. The village fieldwork attribution belongs to Béteille in Karnataka.
D. The village fieldwork attribution belongs to a Census official.

### Q10. Two jatis are equally numerous; one has much local land and office access, the other has high ritual standing alone. Which observation matters most to dominant-caste analysis?
A. Numbers must be combined with land and political influence.
B. Equal numbers imply that both jatis are dominant castes.
C. Higher ritual position decides dominance when numbers tie.
D. Equal numbers mean neither can control village power.

### Q11. Two members of one jati own different amounts of land and wield different influence in the Panchayat. How should Béteille's distinction be used?
A. Distinguish caste membership, economic class and political power empirically.
B. Collapse jati membership, land value and voting power into one rank.
C. Treat land alone as proof that both have the same ritual status.
D. Treat office alone as proof that both have the same class position.

### Q12. If younger members change jobs but marry within their inherited group, which observation would most directly test Ambedkar's reproduction argument?
A. Whether an officeholder's wealth exceeds that of a neighbouring caste.
B. Whether marriage boundaries reproduce the birth group over generations.
C. Whether the new jobs were advertised in a regional language.
D. Whether a ritual elder publicly adopts Western clothing.

### Q13. A paper invokes Dumont to predict Panchayat seats from purity rank alone. Which limitation should a reviewer note?
A. A purity-hierarchy model is not by itself a land-and-office power model.
B. A purity-hierarchy model necessarily predicts every election winner.
C. A purity-hierarchy model is a theory only of British institutions.
D. A purity-hierarchy model measures each household's income directly.

### Q14. Which pair of observations would refute the blanket claim that occupational change has abolished caste inequality?
A. Non-farm hiring and the disappearance of all marriage preferences.
B. A change in diet and an automatic rise in every family's income.
C. Credential-based hiring and continued caste-linked job stigma.
D. Equal wages and the abolition of every household network.

### Q15. An education trust founded by a jati group helps members seek scholarships and organise candidates. Which account fits best?
A. A hereditary jajmani tie has become a constitutional census category.
B. A varna has replaced locally specific caste associations nationwide.
C. A jati trust aids education/welfare/votes; a panchayat arbitrates marriage.
D. A clan exogamy rule alone determines all scholarship recipients.

### Q16. A city's public canteen is open to all while prospective partners use co-jati matrimonial networks. Which conclusion follows?
A. Open canteens prove marriage endogamy has ceased in that city.
B. Public ritual relaxation can coexist with private caste networks.
C. Matrimonial networking proves all occupational choice is hereditary.
D. Citywide occupational equality establishes one ritual rank.

### Q17. An electoral coalition invokes shared identity, yet its member jatis retain different local rankings and endogamous rules. Why not simply relabel the coalition 'one ethnicity'?
A. Shared identity prevents any mobilisation based on caste.
B. Political aggregation does not erase jati hierarchy/endogamy.
C. Every coalition member must share the same birth-jati.
D. Ritual hierarchy alone determines the coalition's seat tally.

### Q18. A ritual-rank claim succeeds but the group's marriage circle remains narrow. How does this bear on 'caste is static and fluid'?
A. Rank change proves hereditary boundaries have also dissolved.
B. Marriage continuity proves rank could not have changed.
C. Ritual change guarantees class parity across the district.
D. Renegotiable rank and persistent endogamy concern different levels.

### Q19. At a shrine, two jatis follow the same sampradaya but restrict marriage separately. Which identity comparison holds?
A. Shared sect practice can cross jatis without merging endogamous groups.
B. Shared sect practice makes both jatis one birth-descent unit.
C. Shared sect practice makes each jati a regional language.
D. Shared sect practice fixes their numerical dominance equally.

### Q20. Two families with matched resources belong to different faiths; a third pair with matched resources belong to different jatis in one faith. Which caution fits the 2024 GS-I theme?
A. Matched resources assure the same social acceptance in both cases.
B. Faith boundaries prohibit civil marriage, unlike jati boundaries.
C. Different jatis cannot share economic parity in one locality.

### Q21. A survey reports legal registrations under the Special Marriage Act. Which inference best respects the limits of this evidence?

B. Registrations establish the number of families accepting each match.
C. Registrations reveal the civil route, not how families received the match.
