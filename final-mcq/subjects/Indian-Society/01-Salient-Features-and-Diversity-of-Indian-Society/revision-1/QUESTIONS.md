# Indian Society 01 — Revision 1: original Prelims practice

Answer-free original questions; none is represented as a UPSC Prelims PYQ.

### Q1. A multilingual neighbourhood association offers translated notices and open meetings, but long-term members choose which proposals reach a vote. Which comparison most directly tests whether newcomers' linguistic presence is matched by influence over association decisions?
A. Meeting attendance and translation use across language groups, separated by length of residence
B. Proposal access and adopted amendments across language groups, separated by length of residence
C. Voting turnout and committee-election results across language groups, separated by length of residence
D. Leadership tenure and membership renewals across language groups, separated by length of residence

### Q2. Two regions have similar faith compositions but markedly different school-access rates. Which interpretation respects the limits of that comparison?
C. Regional educational disparity, with school supply and household costs still open as explanations
B. Regional educational disparity, with school supply alone isolated by similar faith composition
D. Regional educational disparity, with household costs alone isolated by similar faith composition
A. Regional educational disparity, with language recognition alone isolated by similar faith composition

### Q3. Which list contains four commonly cited large Indian language families rather than four languages or scripts?
B. Indo-Aryan, Dravidian, Austroasiatic and Santali
A. Hindi, Dravidian, Austroasiatic and Tibeto-Burman
D. Indo-Aryan, Dravidian, Austroasiatic and Tibeto-Burman

### Q4. Which interpretation of the Census 2011 linguistic figures is sound?
B. 19,569 raw mother-tongue returns were rationalised into 121 scheduled languages.
C. 19,569 raw mother-tongue returns were rationalised into 121 language families.
A. 19,569 returned languages were reduced to 121 constitutionally scheduled mother tongues.

### Q5. The Eighth Schedule's 22 languages should be read as which kind of evidence?
C. A Census ranking of 22 high-frequency languages, later incorporated as a constitutional listing
B. A constitutional list of 22 language families, grouping many individual mother-tongue returns
A. A Census threshold list of 22 returned mother tongues, independent of constitutional notification
D. A constitutional list of 22 languages, distinct from the Census count of returned tongues

### Q6. Census 2011 estimates of about 8.6% ST and 16.6% SC describe what?
A. Shares of households counted in 2011, rather than of people in the total population
C. Shares of people in India's completed 2011 count, not current all-jati population shares
D. Shares of people facing poverty within each listed group in the 2011 population
B. Shares of SCs and STs in every state's 2011 population, treated as nationally fixed

### Q7. A researcher proposes classifying speakers of a Tibeto-Burman language as one inherited biological race. Which survey design preserves the linguistic evidence without collapsing distinct kinds of identity?
B. Record mother tongue and self-described community separately, then examine local histories independently.
D. Record mother tongue and assign one community name to everyone in its language family.
C. Record self-described community and infer each member's mother tongue from that name.
A. Record language family and assign its speakers the corresponding old racial label.

### Q8. A person's religion coincides with one set of peers, occupation with another and language with a third. Which effect is most defensible?
A. Occupational ties offset religious divisions only after each group attains equal income.
D. Distinct religious and occupational networks reinforce one group frontier even when their memberships cross.
C. Cross-cutting ties can ease aligned group polarisation without establishing equality of status.
B. Overlapping language and occupation ties establish parity of assets despite distinct religious affiliations.

### Q9. In one remote forest block, poor school access and insecure livelihoods fall disproportionately on a culturally distinct PVTG population. What does this illustrate?
C. Cultural identity explains both access barriers, making geographic and infrastructure differences irrelevant.
A. Identity, remoteness and low access coincide; the reinforcing pattern still needs causal investigation.
B. The example demonstrates cross-cutting advantages within the group rather than a stacked group disadvantage.

### Q10. Which pair most clearly illustrates syncretic practice rather than mere demographic coexistence?

D. An interfaith committee allocating space to Sufi and Bhakti gatherings without combining their rites
A. Shared-shrine worship and interacting Sufi–Bhakti idioms forming a blended local practice
C. Sufi and Bhakti events attended by neighbours while each retains an unchanged devotional repertoire

### Q11. A mixed-language public school admits families across groups but separates them systematically by caste in access to facilities. The strongest inference is:
A. Caste-skewed facilities rule out any integrative interaction despite the shared enrolment and location.
D. Cross-group enrolment makes the school integrative, so unequal facilities are solely household differences.
B. Cross-group enrolment signals linguistic recognition; facility segregation therefore reflects only cultural difference.
C. Cross-group enrolment can connect families while caste-skewed facilities sustain unequal institutional access.

### Q12. Why is linguistic state reorganisation an example of social accommodation?
C. It changed state administration but left linguistic demands primarily to the central constitutional language listing.
D. It accommodated linguistic identity chiefly through village self-government rather than state territorial boundaries.
B. It channelled some linguistic claims into state-level territorial governance without demanding national language uniformity.
A. It recognised linguistic majorities through new state boundaries and thereby resolved minority-language access within each state.

### Q13. A migrant takes part in shared city institutions while retaining a home language. Which process best fits?
B. Civic integration with language retention; loss of the inherited language is not established.
D. Civic assimilation through city participation; use of the inherited language must already be declining.
A. Linguistic accommodation by city authorities; the migrant's participation establishes formal protection of the language.
C. Civic segregation through home-language retention; shared participation cannot count as institutional integration.

### Q14. A newcomer adopts a dominant language over generations, and use of the inherited language declines. Which process does this best illustrate?
C. Multicultural recognition: public institutions protect the inherited tongue during the dominant-language shift.
D. Civic integration: institutional participation expands while the inherited home-language use remains stable.
A. Linguistic accommodation: authorities provide mother-tongue access without changing the language of home life.
B. Linguistic assimilation: dominant-language adoption displaces home-language use without demonstrating complete cultural erasure.

### Q15. A study identifies a prosperous linguistic-minority trading network in one city. Which inference has the correct logical scope?
C. It disproves inevitable poverty from minority identity, not a possible group-average income gap.
A. It disproves a group-average income gap, not possible poverty among non-network members.
D. It disproves historic exclusion elsewhere, not possible unequal access within the network.
B. It disproves language-service barriers locally, not possible low incomes outside the network.

### Q16. A landless worker from a locally dominant caste reports difficulty obtaining a loan. Which comparison best investigates household assets separately from group influence?

D. Compare borrowers with similar assets across castes, recording applications, decisions and loan terms.
C. Compare landholding distributions across castes without observing loan applications or decisions.
B. Compare caste leadership across villages without recording individual assets or credit decisions.

### Q17. A district announces cultural holidays, yet remote settlements report difficulty reaching schools. Which additional evidence best evaluates redistribution rather than recognition?
C. Settlement-wise reports of cultural respect and participation in the district celebrations.
D. District-wide enrolment totals alongside attendance at the newly announced holidays.
B. Aggregate school spending alongside the number of publicly recognised festivals.
A. Settlement-wise school travel times, provision and household costs across groups.

### Q18. A survey finds greater poverty among a named identity in remote blocks. What additional step is needed before attributing poverty to the identity itself?
A. Use a national mean instead of local variation when estimating an identity-linked poverty effect.
C. Stratify only by identity while pooling remote and well-connected households with unlike land access.
D. Examine historical exclusion, assets and geography before attributing any independent effect to identity.
B. Account for remoteness but assign any residual association to an intrinsic trait of the identity.

### Q20. A planner uses the official PVTG listing to design a study of service access. Which sampling strategy respects the listing's evidentiary limits?

### Q25. A district reports one combined participation total for seven named larger traditions and tribal-faith practitioners, then claims each sect has a voice on local school committees. What check is needed?
