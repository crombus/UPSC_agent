# Indian Society 01 — Revision 3: original application practice

Answer-free original questions; none is represented as a UPSC Prelims PYQ.

### Q1. A regional language becomes more prominent in public offices. Which comparison best tests whether users of smaller home tongues nonetheless face a service disadvantage?
A. Compare total office visits across wards and application completion by regional-language users.
C. Compare office signage languages and application filings by smaller-home-tongue users.
B. Compare application completion and assistance needed across home-language groups seeking similar services.
D. Compare ward home-tongue shares and the number of staff trained in the regional language.

### Q2. A multilingual resident reports one mother tongue to an enumerator. What further evidence would establish that resident's ability to converse in another language?
A. The reported mother tongue alone, since a Census return records every language a resident can speak.
C. The language family's name, since membership in one family establishes conversational fluency for each speaker.
B. The locality's dominant language, since residence establishes conversational ability in that language for each resident.
D. The resident's reported use or tested proficiency in that other language, rather than the mother-tongue entry.

### Q3. Households from two linguistic regions settle near a city market yet keep ties with their home localities. What would best test integration without assuming assimilation?
D. Whether residents join mixed market associations while retaining home languages and visiting home.
A. Whether neighbours share a ward number, without examining trade across language boundaries.
C. Whether remittances flow to home villages, without examining local intergroup participation.
B. Whether the city's dominant language is used on signs, without examining minority access.

### Q4. Consider two practices: 1. Multiple groups retain distinct rituals while sharing a civic forum. 2. Their devotional forms develop a blended shared ritual. Which sequence names them best?
A. Pluralism; accommodation
D. Multiculturalism; assimilation
B. Segregation; syncretism
C. Pluralism; syncretism

### Q5. Two district communities differ in faith and language. Which comparison tests whether material disadvantages *also stack in the same households*?
C. Cross-tab faith, language and school attendance, but omit households' assets.
A. Compare district-wide asset and schooling averages without community or household breakdown.
D. Cross-tab community, household assets and school access to observe their joint distribution.
B. Compare asset and school gaps separately by community without checking household overlap.

### Q6. A planner proposes one intervention for notified tribes in distant habitats with different home languages. Which comparison is needed before selecting delivery arrangements?
D. Compare local journey times and staffing, but omit consultation about language access.
C. Compare reported group preferences, but assume service routes work equally across habitats.
A. Compare regional mean incomes and group notification, but omit household service use.
B. Compare ecology, staffed service journeys and language access across the habitations.

### Q7. A school-access study samples only speakers of the Eighth Schedule's 22 languages, although Census 2011 reports 121 rationalised languages. Which sampling implication follows?
A. Scheduled-language speakers alone can represent every Census language group if the sample covers many states.
B. The schedule-only frame omits reported non-scheduled language groups; language access claims need a broader frame.
C. The 121 Census languages form 121 legally scheduled categories; school sampling can use either list interchangeably.
D. Scheduled-language speakers alone represent all raw mother-tongue returns whenever the study includes a large city.

### Q8. A locally influential caste contains large landowners and landless workers. Which table preserves *joint* within-caste variation in material position?
B. Cross-tab each household's tenure and assets within the caste, retaining the distribution.
A. Cross-tab tenure across castes but assign each caste's mean assets to its households.
C. Cross-tab assets within the caste but assign village-mean tenure to its households.
D. Cross-tab tenure and assets across the district, assigning district means to caste households.

### Q9. A school offers mother-tongue materials but teachers and progression into higher classes are unavailable. What does the example establish?
A. Printed materials suffice for learning access once the home tongue receives official recognition.
D. Teacher supply alone settles progression even if the next grade changes language without support.
C. Progression arrangements alone settle early learning even if teachers cannot use the materials.
B. Teacher capacity and accessible transitions complement materials in effective language accommodation.

### Q10. A shared festival attracts members of several groups, but one neighbourhood reports exclusion from its organising committee. Which evidence locates the barrier in *committee selection* rather than attendance?
D. Compare applications and admissions to the committee by neighbourhood, recording festival turnout separately.
A. Compare festival turnout by neighbourhood, inferring committee admission from each group's audience share.
C. Compare attendance at committee meetings among admitted members, omitting rejected applicants.
B. Compare committee applications by neighbourhood, inferring admission from applications alone.

### Q11. Suppose a Jain trading network and Jain wage workers live in the same city. Which comparison can test economic variation *within* that religious category?
C. Measure earnings and assets of both livelihoods in that city, separating traders from workers.
A. Measure earnings across Jain traders in several cities, imputing workers' income from city averages.
B. Measure traders and wage workers in different cities, pooling earnings without accounting for locality.
D. Measure wage workers across faiths in one city, imputing Jain traders' earnings from co-religionists.

### Q12. Two habitations show few health-centre visits despite different route lengths. Which comparison best separates travel barriers from service-delivery barriers?
B. Compare usable journey times and active staffing at the relevant centre for both habitations.
A. Compare aerial distances and active staffing at the centre, omitting usable journey conditions.
D. Compare usable journey times and the number of district centres, omitting local staffing.
C. Compare journey and staffing averages across the district, omitting habitation-specific access.

### Q13. Which pairing connects a response to its specific target?
B. Language respect — material redistribution only; accessible schools — material redistribution only
A. Language respect — material redistribution; accessible schools — symbolic recognition
C. Language respect — symbolic recognition; accessible schools — material redistribution
D. Language respect — symbolic recognition; accessible schools — symbolic recognition alone

### Q14. A group name appears in two state records, but Scheduled Tribe notification is confirmed in only one. Which inference respects both legal and social evidence?
B. Record notification state by state and assess livelihoods separately from that legal status.
D. Use similar household livelihoods in the two states to extend notification across them.
C. Use a common mother tongue in the two states to extend notification across them.
A. Use the matching group name to extend notification, then assess livelihoods separately.

### Q15. An analyst classifies a public-private work group by religion alone and misses variation by occupation and schooling. What corrective lens is most useful?
D. Cross-classify religious affiliation, occupation and schooling before interpreting group-level outcomes.
A. Cross-classify religion and occupation while leaving variation in schooling within occupations unmeasured.
B. Use regional education averages to substitute for households' occupation and religious affiliation.
C. Use each religious group's mean occupation to infer the schooling of its individual members.

### Q16. Which inference best links shared shrines, regional cuisine and public transport as integration evidence?
D. Shared sites ensure equivalent influence over shrines and transport decisions across communities.
C. Shared food and transit demonstrate blending of devotional rituals at the shrine itself.
A. Shared sites ensure equivalent affordability of transport and food for each community.
B. Shared sites and practices permit contact, while access and voice require separate assessment.

### Q17. A chart treats Census 2011 SC/ST shares as a baseline and the Gazette's later reference dates as a second observation. Which replacement could support a national population-share trend?
A. Combine 2011 shares with projected later state populations, treating projections as observed group shares.
C. Combine 2011 national shares with a later district sample, treating its composition as nationally representative.
D. Divide subsequently released national group counts by India's 2011 total population to obtain later shares.
B. Use subsequently released comparable national group counts and their own total-person denominator.

### Q18. Members of one linguistic minority trade freely in a city, while others in a remote settlement face poor market access. Which comparison can separate location effects from other possible group-linked barriers?
D. Compare market routes and entry rules across the localities, then examine access within each location.
A. Compare average trader incomes across the localities, taking income as a measure of route usability.
B. Compare reports of linguistic disrespect across the localities, treating these as measures of market entry.
C. Compare the minority's population shares across the localities, taking group size as market access.

### Q19. A scholar argues that conspicuous cultural difference is *necessary* for material marginality. Which observation would falsify that claim?
B. A prosperous minority neighbourhood retaining distinct traditions despite market integration.
C. A materially deprived household of the locally dominant caste without a conspicuous minority marker.
A. Poor households in two distinct minority communities facing different livelihood constraints.
D. An affluent locally dominant household alongside a deprived culturally distinct household.

### Q20. An inherited craft is passed through family and jati apprenticeship, but outsiders historically could not train. Which proposal supports continuity without treating exclusion as essential to heritage?
C. Preserve teaching by existing craftspeople while opening training to other residents and checking access.
D. Preserve teaching by existing craftspeople while retaining hereditary restrictions on who may enrol.
A. Replace teaching with a written archive alone and assume new learners can reproduce the living craft.
B. Permit outsiders to view performances but keep apprenticeship rights restricted to inherited insiders.

### Q21. An analyst says the ministry's 75-PVTG listing in 18 States excludes Andaman and Nicobar Islands. Which correction respects the geographic units?
D. The ministry reports 18 States with the island Union Territory counted as one of those States.
B. The ministry reports 18 States plus the island Union Territory, not an exclusion of islands.
C. The ministry reports 18 States plus the island Union Territory, but excludes its groups from 75.
A. The ministry reports 18 States, with island PVTGs counted only in the broader ST category.

### Q22. Which proposed observation would most directly test whether common markets bridge divisions rather than just reproduce segregation?
C. Track transactions and stall access across group boundaries, not merely shared market space.
A. Count communities present in the market without checking who trades with whom.
D. Record stall applications without testing whether approved traders trade outside their own group.
B. Record sales by trader group without testing who gains stalls and credit.

### Q23. A minority community transmits customary practices across cities, while remote households report unequal schooling. Which assessment preserves both dimensions without assigning a cause prematurely?
B. Trace ritual teaching, then take its continuity as proof that remote households can reach schools.
C. Trace ritual teaching and school access by region, assigning any residual gap to group stigma.
A. Trace ritual teaching and school access by assets, assigning any residual gap to remoteness.
D. Trace ritual teaching and school access by place and assets, investigating exclusion separately.

### Q24. A neighbourhood has everyday exchange across groups but also recurring inter-group tension. Which comparison tests the two observations without treating either as sufficient evidence of civic inclusion?

B. Map tensions and membership by faith while omitting who participates in everyday exchange.

### Q25. A survey records tribal-faith practice only for households holding ST certificates and merges all other faith descriptions into the seven named larger traditions. Which revision is warranted?

### Q26. A comparison finds different livelihoods in coastal and Himalayan settings. Which design tests geography's role without attributing all household variation to the setting?

B. Compare assets and occupations by setting, assigning one terrain exposure to each household.

### Q27. A shared city celebration borrows from regional Onam, Bihu and Pongal events. Which inference is safest?

### Q28. A surveyor sees a PVTG household using modern farm tools and concludes the group could not have met older identification criteria. Which correction follows?

### Q29. Two hypothetical groups have comparable population observations at two times: one is unchanged, the other declines. How do these observations relate to the legacy PVTG criterion?

### Q30. A school display names Hinduism, Islam, Christianity, Sikhism, Buddhism, Jainism, Zoroastrianism and tribal faiths. Which extension retains meaningful variation within these religious expressions?

### Q31. A researcher adds every locally reported jati name in two states and announces a settled all-India jati total and a matched ST total. Which step is missing?

### Q32. A migrant neighbourhood retains local shared-deity worship alongside regional festivals. Which interpretation best corrects a claim that migration makes their regional calendars interchangeable?
