**1 — C.** (A) Towns and rulers supported both traditions; the division of doctrinal causes is unwarranted; (B) surplus helped sustain patrons but cannot generate the two distinct teachings; (C) plural change widened the audience without dictating belief; (D) ritual debate was part of the setting, but its sequence and sufficiency are not established. Trap: context is not a sufficient cause. [B §§9–10; W Q1]

**2 — A.** (A) Identify transmitting school and independently test its chronology; (B) later site commemoration alone cannot establish the event's precise date; (C) council ordinals do not identify one shared historical sequence or settle school attribution; (D) naming a patron does not authenticate reported speeches. Trap: transmitted chronology is not an eyewitness minute. [B §§9, 14; W Q24–25]

**3 — D.** (A) The twenty-fourth position is traditional, but all preceding lives are not independently dated; (B) Pārśva precedes Mahavira, not the reverse; (C) no royal edict securely fixes Pārśva's life dates; (D) distinguishes traditional numbering from the plausible earlier stratum. Trap: a sacred sequence is not a securely dated chronology. [B §11.1; W Q2]

**4 — B.** (A) future Buddha is Maitreya; (B) Pali Nigantha Nataputta identifies Mahavira; (C) Buddha's epithets include Śākyamuni/Tathāgata; (D) Ashoka was a later patron. Trap: similar religious titles do not identify the same teacher. [B §§4, 11, 21; W Q3]

**5 — A.** (A) awakening at Bodh Gaya, first teaching at Sarnath; (B) birth is linked to Lumbini, death to Kushinagara; (C) reverses both sacred associations; (D) reverses awakening and teaching. Trap: biography and secure archaeological dates are different questions. [B §11.2; A §§1, 4]

**6 — C.** (A) A later biography's birth-year calculation is not fixed by the pillar; (B) a first recitation is remembered at Rājagṛha, but the pillar does not locate it; (C) the royal inscription attests the early Lumbini birthplace association; (D) an Ashokan record is not an eyewitness account of the founder's household or renunciation. Trap: distinguish epigraphic association from complete biography. [B §§9.1, 11.2; T Q39]

**7 — D.** (A) Right view replaces celibacy and is a Buddhist Eightfold Path factor; (B) right livelihood replaces non-possession and is a Buddhist path factor; (C) right speech replaces non-stealing and belongs to the Buddhist path; (D) lists Jain non-injury, truth, non-stealing, celibacy and non-possession. Trap: do not substitute a Buddhist path factor for a Jain vow. [B §12.1; W Q6]

**8 — B.** (A) Buddhist refuges; (B) Jain right faith, knowledge and conduct; (C) an analytical grouping of Buddhist path factors; (D) elements of Bodhisattva practice, not Jain three jewels. Trap: “three” alone is insufficient. [B §§12.1, 13.2; W Q6, Q32]

**9 — A.** (A) Jain *jīva* contrasts with Buddhist *anattā*; (B) Buddhism does not affirm the unchanged transmigration of Jain *jīva*; (C) Jainism affirms *jīva* while Buddhism does not treat karma as matter binding it; (D) ethical continuity remains possible in Buddhism without a permanent self. Trap: shared rebirth language hides ontological difference. [B §§12.1, 13.1, 17; W Q17–18]

**10 — D.** (A) Non-injury extends beyond humans in Jain ontology; (B) the concern includes subtle embodied life, not just plainly visible animals; (C) karmic bondage and strict non-injury are linked, not opposed; (D) wide classification of life motivates careful restraint. Trap: this is an ancient ontology, not a test of modern biology. [B §§12.1, 21 (2023 Q47); W PYQ 2023 Q47]

**11 — C.** (A) deva = gods; (B) manushya = humans; (C) yaksha is not one of four principal gatis; (D) tiryancha includes animals/plants. Trap: a familiar divine being need not be a gati category; the 2026 question's official key is unavailable. [B §§4, 12.1; W Q14]

**12 — B.** (A) arrest of influx cannot precede the influx whose effects must be addressed; (B) *āsrava* and *bandha* precede *saṃvara*, *nirjarā* and liberation; (C) shedding accumulated karma is misplaced before bondage; (D) bondage cannot precede the influx that brings it about, and shedding does not substitute for arresting fresh influx. Trap: stop new bondage and shed existing karma are distinct. [B §12.1; W Q5; T Q5]

**13 — A.** (A) *Dharma-dravya* is the medium of motion; (B) the rest-medium is *adharma-dravya*; (C) Jain *jīva* is the living self, not the motion-medium; (D) *āsrava* concerns karmic influx, not this technical medium. Trap: technical *dharma* need not mean moral law here. [W Q4; T Q4]

**14 — D.** (A) swaps the meanings of *anekānta* and *naya*; (B) swaps *naya* and *syād*; (C) swaps *anekānta* and *syād*; (D) assigns many-sidedness, standpoint and conditional predication respectively. Trap: mature systematisation was later, not a verbatim founder's lecture. [B §§9.1, 12.1; W Q7]

**15 — B.** (A) lay supporters were within the fourfold community, not outside it; (B) monks, nuns, laymen and laywomen had distinct roles; (C) wrongly excludes laywomen from the community; (D) lay *aṇuvratas* are scaled, not identical to ascetic *mahāvratas*. Trap: support does not require ordination. [B §12.2; W Q33; A §1]

**16 — C.** (A) Digambaras do not accept the surviving Śvetāmbara Agamas as the original canon; (B) reverses the received sectarian positions on liberation in a female body; (C) preserves the actual canonical difference without fixing one schism date; (D) reverses both traditions' scriptural claims. Trap: clothing is not the only sectarian issue. [B §12.3; W Q8]

**17 — A.** (A) Valabhi belongs to later Śvetāmbara redaction tradition; (B) Sthūlabhadra is associated instead with Jain Pataliputra preservation memory; (C) the Kaniṣka–Kashmir association is a northern Buddhist tradition; (D) Sri Lankan writing of a Pali canon is a distinct Theravāda memory. Trap: redaction is not founder authorship. [B §§12.3, 14.1; W Q10, Q38]

**18 — D.** (A) Sthānakavāsīs reject image worship rather than emphasizing it; (B) the later reform belongs to the Śvetāmbara, not Digambara, family; (C) neither its date nor its image practice is correct; (D) identifies the later non-image-worship Śvetāmbara reform. Trap: do not backdate Sthānakavāsī to Mahavira. [B §§12.3, 21 (2018 Q71); W Q9]

**19 — B.** (A) the southern memory cannot fix a precise schism date by itself; (B) compare the later story with dated material and epigraphic evidence; (C) a southern association cannot establish Jainism's origin in Karnataka; (D) a separate later redaction memory cannot be dated from this migration narrative. Trap: one migration memory did not instantly produce fixed sects. [B §§9.1, 12.3–12.4; W Q12]

**20 — C.** (A) Mathura votives are attested, not a Mathura canon; Hathigumpha concerns Kharavela rather than Ashoka; (B) Mathura votives are correctly placed but Valabhi redaction is not recorded at Hathigumpha; (C) Mathura votive tablets and Kharavela's Kalinga inscription match; (D) Kankali Tila is at Mathura, not Andhra. Trap: match each named source to its own region and use. [B §§12.4, 15.2; W Q11, Q13, Q39]

**21 — C.** (A) reverses the second and third truths, putting cessation ahead of cause; (B) reverses the third and fourth truths, putting the path ahead of the truth of cessation; (C) gives *dukkha*, its origin, cessation and the path, in order; (D) substitutes self-mortification for the Eightfold Path. Trap: know the sequence, not just its four themes. [B §13.1; W Q15]

**22 — D.** (A) attention to intention and ethical conduct are integral, not rejected extremes; (B) lay support and monastic discipline can coexist; (C) restraint and meditation are path components; (D) indulgence and severe self-mortification are the rejected extremes. Trap: Middle Path is not absence of discipline. [B §13.1; W Q15–16]

**23 — B.** (A) non-stealing is a Jain vow; (B) right mindfulness is an Eightfold Path factor; (C) celibacy is among Jain great vows; (D) non-possession is a Jain vow. Trap: similar ethical vocabulary does not make lists interchangeable. [B §§12.1, 13.1; W Q6]

**24 — A.** (A) dependent processes arise and cease with conditions; (B) a single unconditioned creator is not the teaching's source of dependent processes; (C) changing conditions permit cessation, contrary to this fixed-continuity claim; (D) a permanent transmitting self conflicts with non-self. Trap: conditionality is neither determinism nor nihilism. [B §13.1; W Q16]

**25 — A.** (A) intentional action has causal continuity without an eternal *jīva*; (B) particles binding a continuing *jīva* belong to Jain karma; (C) unchanged *ātman* conflicts with Buddhist non-self; (D) Buddhist rebirth does not depend on a permanent creator's judgement. Trap: rebirth need not imply an identical enduring entity. [B §§12.1, 13.1, 17; W Q18]

**26 — D.** (A) Buddhist non-self does not posit an eternal *ātman* to destroy; (B) retaining delusion contradicts the cessation of defilements; (C) bodily death alone does not define liberation; (D) ending greed, hatred and delusion and release from rebirth fits the teaching. Trap: nirvana is not simple annihilation. [B §13.1; W Q19]

**27 — D.** (A) Jain liberation's three jewels; (B) elements of Bodhisattva perfections, not refuges; (C) the Theravāda three canonical baskets; (D) the Buddha, teaching and community are the Buddhist refuges. Trap: identical “three” counts label different systems. [B §§12.1, 13.2, 14; T Q52]

**28 — C.** (A) Jain Agamas are not the third Theravāda basket; (B) Jain Agamas cannot replace Sutta in this triad; (C) disciplinary, discourse and analytical collections form the Theravāda Pali Tipiṭaka; (D) Jain Agamas cannot replace Vinaya. Trap: other Buddhist schools had their own canons. [B §14.1; W Q20, Q26]

**29 — A.** (A) nuns' remembered voices and hierarchical received discipline coexist; (B) literary agency does not establish equal institutional rank in every Vinaya; (C) received *garudhammas* have layering and contested founder attribution; (D) received restrictions do not erase the voices attributed to nuns in the *Therīgāthā*. Trap: neither universal equality nor absence of agency. [B §13.3; W Q23, Q34]

**30 — B.** (A) critiques of birth did not legislate pan-kingdom caste abolition; (B) challenges to birth superiority coexisted with surrounding hierarchy; (C) wider spiritual access did not confer uniform political equality; (D) crossing status lines was possible, while birth as the measure of spiritual worth was contested. Trap: ethical critique is not a modern legal revolution. [B §§13.2, 17; W Q22]

**31 — B.** (A) Mahāpajāpatī and the nuns' verses concern women's renunciant history, not the requested pair of lay patrons; (B) Anāthapiṇḍika and Visākhā are remembered lay supporters; (C) Sthūlabhadra and Bhadrabāhu figure in Jain traditions; (D) Aśoka and Kaniṣka are royal rather than lay patrons. Trap: textual exemplars are not a demographic survey. [B §§13.3, 15.1; W Q21]

**32 — A.** (A) gives the conventional Rājagṛha recitation and Vaiśālī discipline-dispute sequence; (B) reverses those events; (C) Valabhi, not Vaiśālī, is associated with later Śvetāmbara redaction; (D) Śvetāmbara redaction is Jain and associated with Valabhi, not the Buddhist first council. Trap: transmitted memories require school-specific dating. [B §§12.3, 14.1; W Q24]

**33 — A.** (A) identifies Theravāda Aśoka–Pāṭaliputra and northern Kaniṣka–Kashmir associations; (B) exchanges their rulers and misplaces both; (C) exchanges both locations; (D) Vaiśālī and Rājagṛha are remembered in earlier Buddhist council narratives, not these ruler-linked associations. Trap: “fourth council” is not univocal. [B §14.1; W Q25, Q38]

**34 — D.** (A) later Mahāyāna cannot be assigned wholesale to the early Rājagṛha council; (B) Lokottaravāda has a Mahāsāṃghika association, not Sarvāstivāda; (C) Theravāda is not a label for every early non-Mahāyāna school; (D) assigns Lokottaravāda and Theravāda to their relevant families. Trap: school ancestry ≠ synonym. [B §14.2; W Q28; T Q53–54]

**35 — D.** (A) the Bodhisattva path fits Mahāyāna, but Vajrayāna is not the Buddha's first sermon; (B) the Pali canon is Theravāda's, not Mahāyāna's exclusive collection; (C) swaps the characteristic emphases of the two later developments; (D) pairs Bodhisattva perfections and later tantric lineages. Trap: Mahāyāna and Vajrayāna have overlapping yet distinct histories. [B §§14.2, 18; T Q35]

**36 — C.** (A) austerity is substituted for wisdom; (B) right view is an Eightfold Path factor, not the listed perfection of wisdom; (C) gives *dāna, śīla, kṣānti, vīrya, dhyāna, prajñā*; (D) non-possession is a Jain vow, not the listed perfection of wisdom. Trap: a close sixth term changes the list. [B §21 (2020 Q31); W Q32]

**37 — A.** (A) expected future Buddha is attested beyond Mahāyāna; (B) restricts a wider expectation to Mahāyāna; (C) Avalokiteśvara is a distinct Bodhisattva, not a synonym for Maitreya; (D) Maitreya belongs to Buddhist, not exclusively Jain, tradition. Trap: Maitreya is not exclusively Mahāyāna. [B §§14.2, 21 (2018 Q74); W Q31]

**38 — C.** (A) Saṅghabhūti's Vinaya link is right, but Dhānyakaṭaka is not in Gandhāra; (B) Dhānyakaṭaka is in Andhra, but Saṅghabhūti's commentary belongs to Sarvāstivāda, not Theravāda; (C) matches Sarvāstivāda Vinaya and lower Krishna/Andhra; (D) the first match is right but Dhānyakaṭaka is not in Kashmir. Trap: identify both school and region before selecting the pair. [B §§14.2, 15.2, 21 (2024 Q64, 2023 Q41); W Q29–30]

**39 — B.** (A) selected recorded donors do not represent every regional follower; (B) particular gifts and patrons are attested, not a population census; (C) an inscribed gift does not by itself date every phase of construction; (D) one inscription identifies particular donors, not every adherent. Trap: material religious labels do not certify every adherent. [B §§9.1, 15.2; W Q37; T Q41, Q57]

**40 — B.** (A) a stupa is a mound rather than the *caitya* prayer hall; (B) *caitya* denotes shrine or congregational space and *vihāra* residential monastic space; (C) reverses these functions; (D) a *vihāra* is not the relic mound. Trap: a rock-cut complex can include both forms. [B §15.2; T Q42]

**41 — A.** (A) Aśokan inscriptions document specific support without equating all imperial *dhamma* with Buddhist doctrine; (B) reported council proceedings are not preserved as a contemporary Ashokan transcript; (C) Sanchi has nonroyal donors and later building phases; (D) the Lumbini inscription attests a birthplace association rather than reproducing the Pali canon. Trap: patron's ethics ≠ entire canonical programme. [B §§9.1, 15.1; W Q36]

**42 — D.** (A) translation establishes a latest, not earliest, possible composition date for the transmitted work; (B) it cannot fix the precise original year; (C) a dated Chinese witness cannot date every other surviving recension to that same moment; (D) the transmitted work existed by the date of translation. Trap: a translation gives a *terminus ante quem*, not an autograph. [B §§9.1, 13.4, 14.1; T Q27, Q47]

**43 — D.** (A) patronage matters but does not alone account for competition, economy and violence; (B) Jain institutions, sects and literary languages changed; (C) contraction was uneven, not simultaneous in every region; (D) plural regional patronage and institutional changes explain differential survival. Trap: “decline” is not sudden total extinction. [B §16.3–16.4; A §1; W Q40]

**44 — C.** (A) texts and practice changed with different routes and regional communities; (B) omits translators, merchants and other lay supporters; (C) multiple interacting networks and patrons explain expansion; (D) neither an identical school nor one route describes all Asian Buddhist transmission. Trap: transmission is not one identical canon exported everywhere. [B §§13.4, 15.3–16; A §1; T Q37]

**45 — B.** (A) Jain karmic matter belongs to Jain ontology, not Yogācāra's characteristic analyses; (B) later schools explore lack of inherent essence and consciousness-focused questions, respectively; (C) Madhyamaka does not posit a permanent void substance; (D) reverses the two schools' characteristic emphases. Trap: neither later philosophy can be assigned verbatim to the Buddha. [B §§9, 14.2, 18; T Q33–34]

**46 — A.** (A) Nālandā and Vikramaśīla were later Buddhist scholastic-monastic centres; (B) Sanchi and Bharhut provide stupa and donor evidence, not this later scholastic pairing; (C) Mathura and Shravanabelagola anchor Jain regional history; (D) Bodh Gaya and Sarnath are associated with the Buddha's remembered life rather than this later institutional network. Trap: enduring eastern centres refute universal early disappearance, not every regional decline. [B §§11.2, 12.4, 15.2–15.3, 16.3; T Q36]

**47 — D.** (A) both traditions later produced literature beyond these two language categories; (B) surviving canonical language does not certify either founder's exact speech; (C) Jain Prakrit and Buddhist Pali cannot be collapsed into one uniform personal dialect; (D) acknowledges parallel and plural literary/oral transmission. Trap: language of received canon ≠ exact spoken language of teacher. [B §§12.2, 13.4; W Q35]

**48 — A.** (A) places *Pariśiṣṭaparvan* in Jain literature and *Nettipakaraṇa* in Buddhist tradition; (B) reverses these affiliations; (C) *Pariśiṣṭaparvan* is Jain literary production rather than proof of founder-era canonical text, while *Nettipakaraṇa* is Buddhist; (D) reverses the Jain work's affiliation and mislabels the Buddhist work's genre. Trap: Jain literary affiliation does not itself establish narrow canonical status. [B §21 (2022 Q55); W PYQ 2022 Q55]

**49 — A.** (A) correctly pairs Jain Pataliputra–Sthūlabhadra preservation and later Śvetāmbara Valabhi redaction; (B) swaps these distinct Jain memories; (C) Valabhi is Śvetāmbara, not Digambara, canonical redaction; (D) Aśoka–Pataliputra is a Theravāda Buddhist council memory, not the Jain story sought. Trap: the shared city name does not make Jain and Buddhist council traditions identical. [B §§9.1, 12.3, 14.1; W Q10]
