**1 — B.** (A) a normative ban does not show universal compliance; (B) distinguishes a rule's ideal from a specific dated act; (C) a recorded gift does not prove monastics rejected a disciplinary rule; (D) an inscription cannot automatically date a separately transmitted prescription or make its audience identical. Trap: normative and material sources answer different questions. [B §9.1; W Q37]

**2 — A.** (A) merchant support did not exclude rulers, other householders or renouncers; (B) exchange routes connected travelling teachers; (C) surplus supported some monastic institutions; (D) rulers and households provided distinct patronage channels. Trap: one supporter group does not define the entire community. [B §§9–10, 15; A §1; W Q1]

**3 — B.** (A) sacred succession does not date this image independently; (B) the named teacher's presence and biography require more evidence than disputed visual resemblance; (C) later Jain images cannot identify an earlier Harappan figure's precise name and life; (D) a traditional teacher sequence cannot establish the object's identity without independent dated evidence. Trap: iconographic speculation is not a historical biography. [B §§9.1, 11.1; W Q2]

**4 — C.** (A) Siddhartha Buddha's epithets differ; (B) Maitreya is future Buddha; (C) Nigantha Nataputta identifies Mahavira; (D) Sanghabhuti is a much later monk. Trap: a Pali epithet may describe a rival teacher. [B §§4, 11, 21; W Q3]

**5 — C.** (A) Kushinagara, not Lumbini, is associated with passing; (B) the Lumbini record does not date Sarnath's first teaching; (C) it attests an early birthplace association, not every episode; (D) an inscription mentioning Lumbini does not fix the Bodh Gaya awakening date. Trap: inscriptional link ≠ all remembered episodes. [B §§9.1, 11.2; T Q39]

**6 — D.** (A), (B), (C) attach birth/passing/sermon/awakening to wrong places; (D) correctly matches enlightenment, first sermon and passing. Birth is remembered at Lumbini. Trap: places are traditional biographical associations, not independently fixed dates. [B §11.2; A §1]

**7 — B.** (A) lay vows have a scaled rather than identical ascetic severity; (B) *aṇuvratas* and *mahāvratas* differ in rigour; (C) monk and lay duties are not interchanged; (D) non-injury remains fundamental for laity as well as ascetics. Trap: donors need not be monks. [B §§12.1–12.2; W Q33]

**8 — C.** (A) reverses Jain path and Buddhist refuges; (B) Vinaya-Sutta-Abhidhamma is Buddhist canon, not Jain jewel; (C) correctly contrasts paths/refuges; (D) mixes perfections and the canon. Trap: “three jewels” is tradition-specific. [B §§12.1, 13.2–14.1; T Q52]

**9 — B.** (A) Buddhism rejects an eternal *ātman* as karma's bearer; (B) contrasts Jain material bondage of *jīva* and Buddhist non-self; (C) reverses Jain affirmation of *jīva* and imports karmic matter into Buddhist doctrine; (D) reverses the Jain-material/Buddhist-intentional distinction. Trap: similar terms do not erase distinct agent models. [B §§12.1, 13.1, 17; W Q17–18]

**10 — D.** (A) reverses the respective media of motion and rest; (B) *adharma-dravya* is the rest-medium rather than bondage; (C) karmic influx is *āsrava*, not *dharma-dravya*; (D) names Jain motion and rest media in order. Trap: technical term ≠ ordinary ethical usage. [W Q4–5; T Q4]

**11 — D.** (A) deva belongs to the four and yakṣa remains incorrectly listed; (B) nāraki belongs and yakṣa remains; (C) manuṣya belongs and yakṣa remains; (D) replacing yakṣa with tiryañca gives all four. Trap: exclude yakṣa, not deva, manuṣya or nāraki. [B §§4, 12.1; W Q14]

**12 — C.** (A) a qualified assertion is not one invariant answer regardless of standpoint; (B) differing respects are not unqualified contradictions in the same respect; (C) qualification by respect or standpoint answers the criticism; (D) conditioned predication is compatible with qualified truth claims, not blanket scepticism. Trap: many-sidedness is not nihilism. [B §12.1; W Q7]

**13 — A.** (A) influx/bondage followed by arrest of new influx and shedding of old karma precedes liberation; (B) allowing new influx defeats the proposed cessation of bondage; (C) liberation is wrongly placed ahead of arrest and shedding; (D) old karma is shed through *nirjarā*, not “arrested,” and fresh influx is not invited. Trap: ending new influx differs from shedding old karmic matter. [B §12.1; W Q5]

**14 — B.** (A) one recorded gift cannot establish female patronage at other sites; (B) it establishes a particular laywoman's participation; (C) her gift does not prove her school's regional predominance; (D) a laywoman is not an ordained nun. Trap: one inscription cannot quantify the wider community. [B §§9.1, 12.2–12.4; W Q33, Q37]

**15 — B.** (A) Valabhi redaction belongs to Śvetāmbara, not Digambara, canon memory; (B) later redaction has sect-specific acceptance; (C) Digambaras do not affirm the preserved Śvetāmbara Agamas as originals; (D) Valabhi is not a signed contemporary text from Mahavira. Trap: redaction ≠ autographed founder manuscript. [B §12.3; W Q10]

**16 — C.** (A) received Digambara positions do not accept liberation in a female body; (B) Śvetāmbara positions accept it; (C) distinguishes sectarian positions without denying historical Jain nuns; (D) doctrinal contrast does not date one instantaneous schism. Trap: female renunciant presence and sectarian salvation views are different. [B §§12.2–12.3; W Q8]

**17 — C.** (A) concerns another later Śvetāmbara phenomenon, canon redaction, rather than the guide's named sect; (B) assigns non-image practice to Digambaras rather than the named Śvetāmbara reform; (C) identifies the later Śvetāmbara non-image group backdated by the guide; (D) misassigns the later sect to the Digambara family. Trap: Sthānakavāsī is later and non-image-worship. [B §12.3; W Q9]

**18 — D.** (A) regional gifts cannot date all remote teacher biographies; (B) material patronage does not date the Digambara–Śvetāmbara split; (C) selected regional evidence cannot identify every follower's sect; (D) material anchors illuminate different patrons in different contexts, not a population census. Trap: distinct contexts ≠ universal census. [B §§9.1, 12.4; W Q11, Q13, Q39]

**19 — B.** (A) later southern memory cannot date the first Jain council precisely; (B) genre and chronology must be tested against southern material evidence; (C) the five vows are associated with earlier Jain teachers, not a southern origin; (D) a later migration story cannot independently date Valabhi's separate redaction tradition. Trap: migration memory and schism chronology are not identical. [B §§12.3–12.4; W Q12]

**20 — A.** (A) accurately corrects the reduction of *dukkha* to physical pain; (B) the phrase does not identify the cause, craving, with *dukkha* itself; (C) calling something pain does not treat it as an eternal substance; (D) the quoted claim does not confuse the diagnosis with the separate truth of cessation. Trap: unsatisfactoriness under clinging has wider scope. [B §13.1; W Q15]

**21 — A.** (A) right livelihood belongs to the path's ethical-conduct dimension; (B) right concentration is a path factor grouped with mental discipline; (C) right view belongs to the wisdom dimension; (D) right mindfulness is part of mental discipline. Trap: several path factors are genuine, but their traditional groupings differ. [B §13.1; W Q6]

**22 — A.** (A) dependent origination involves conditions without a fixed uncaused self; (B) an unchanging bearer is not needed for conditioned continuity; (C) processes can cease as conditions cease, contrary to fatalism; (D) Buddhist conditionality does not start with a creator's uncaused intervention. Trap: causality does not require a permanent bearer. [B §13.1; W Q16]

**23 — B.** (A) non-self does not cancel the ethical consequences of intentional action; (B) allows conditioned continuity without a permanent independent self; (C) importing a permanent self defeats the stated doctrine; (D) a Jain *jīva* is not required for Buddhist rebirth. Trap: non-self is not nihilism. [B §§13.1, 17; W Q17–18]

**24 — C.** (A) leaving delusion intact does not complete cessation; (B) bodily death with craving continuing is not liberation; (C) ending greed, hatred and delusion with release from rebirth fits the teaching; (D) Buddhist liberation does not posit an immortal soul to free. Trap: nirvana ≠ creator's heaven. [B §13.1; W Q19]

**25 — D.** (A) *upāsikā* is a female lay follower, not a nun (*bhikkhunī*); (B) Sutta, rather than Vinaya, refers to discourses; (C) *upāsikā* is female, not a male lay follower (*upāsaka*); (D) pairs Vinaya with discipline and *upāsikā* with a female lay adherent. Trap: lay supporters need not take Vinaya ordination. [B §§13.2, 14.1; W Q20, Q33]

**26 — D.** (A) no earliest Pali manuscript is an authenticated signed lifetime record; (B) oral transmission does not guarantee an unchanged dialect; (C) no Sanskrit founder autograph fixes the original speech of all other schools; (D) the received Theravāda canon's Pali language cannot establish the founder's precise spoken dialect. Trap: canonical language is not an audio record. [B §§13.4–14.1; W Q35]

**27 — C.** (A) reverses Vinaya and Sutta; (B) swaps the roles of Sutta and Abhidhamma; (C) maps discipline, discourses and analytical collection in order; (D) swaps Vinaya and Abhidhamma. Trap: Pali three baskets aren't every Buddhist canon. [B §14.1; W Q20, Q26]

**28 — C.** (A) the rules' verbatim founder attribution is debated; (B) ordination did not establish universal equal authority; (C) recognizes women's monastic agency and contested layers of subordination; (D) received *garudhammas* cannot be ignored. Trap: agency and subordination can coexist. [B §13.3; W Q23, Q34]

**29 — B.** (A) specific donations and ethical critique did not create universal caste-abolition laws; (B) bounded gifts and status criticism coexisted with social inequality; (C) one inscription is not a census of households; (D) status critiques did not restrict participation to royal households. Trap: salvific critique ≠ societal equality. [B §§9.1, 13.2–13.3; W Q22, Q37]

**30 — D.** (A) reverses Rajagriha's first recitation and Vaishali's disciplinary dispute; (B) Śvetāmbara redaction is later Jain Valabhi memory, not Rajagriha; (C) Vaishali is not the Śvetāmbara redaction site; (D) gives the qualified early recitation and later discipline-dispute memories. Trap: received accounts are not contemporary roll calls. [B §§12.3, 14.1; W Q24]

**31 — D.** (A) reverses the received school attributions; (B) Kashmir–Kaniṣka is a Sarvāstivāda-associated Buddhist memory, not Śvetāmbara; (C) Kaniṣka is not the patron of the Ashokan Pāṭaliputra tradition; (D) Theravāda and Sarvāstivāda-associated accounts refer to different periods and school-specific narratives. Trap: label transmitting tradition. [B §14.1; W Q25, Q38]

**32 — C.** (A) Lokottaravāda is Mahāsāṃghika-related, not a Sarvāstivāda lineage; (B) Theravāda has Sthavira connections, not Mahāsāṃghika ancestry; (C) correctly identifies both historical lineages and Maitreya as the future Buddha; (D) Maitreya is a future Buddha in Buddhist traditions, not a Jain teacher. Trap: Theravāda has Sthavira ancestry but is not every Sthavira school; Maitreya is not confined to one vehicle. [B §§14.2, 21; W Q28, Q31]

**33 — A.** (A) lists the six common Bodhisattva perfections; (B) non-possession replaces wisdom and belongs to Jain vows; (C) right mindfulness is an Eightfold Path factor, not the listed perfection of meditation; (D) concentration is an Eightfold factor but here replaces the perfection of effort. Trap: related Buddhist terms cannot substitute for any of the six. [B §§13.1, 21 (2020 Q31); W Q32]

**34 — C.** (A) later systematic philosophies are not fixed in the first council's recitation; (B) Vajrayāna did not begin at the Vaiśālī Vinaya dispute; (C) locates Madhyamaka, Yogācāra and tantric developments in later contexts; (D) Madhyamaka did not originate after a fully mature Vajrayāna tradition. Trap: do not project systematic school philosophy backwards. [B §§9.1, 14.2, 18; T Q33–35]

**35 — B.** (A) Buddhist patronage does not convert every edict into Vinaya; (B) documents bounded Buddhist support separately from wider ethical *dhamma*; (C) Sanchi also has later and nonroyal donors; (D) Buddhist institutions predated the emperor's support. Trap: royal patronage did not invent the religion. [B §15.1; W Q36]

**36 — C.** (A) not every stupa began as a relic repository; (B) independent sites have different construction phases; (C) *caitya* and *vihāra* can coexist while stupas have varied purposes and phases; (D) no single school decree originated all Buddhist images. Trap: architectural labels need excavation and context. [B §15.2; T Q40–42, Q57]

**37 — D.** (A) one translation does not date every other Indic recension; (B) it establishes a latest possible date for the transmitted work's existence, not its precise original composition year; (C) translation decisions can differ from source-language readings; (D) it provides a *terminus ante quem* without a founder-era composition certificate. Trap: a dated translation is a transmission witness. [B §§9.1, 13.4, 14.1; T Q27, Q47]

**38 — D.** (A) separate regions do not establish one shared patronage chronology; (B) Dhānyakaṭaka's school link does not assign Sanchi to the same school; (C) Sanchi donor evidence does not date construction at the lower Krishna site; (D) recognizes region-specific histories requiring separate sources. Trap: geographic association does not unify school/patron chronology. [B §§15.2, 21 (2023 Q41); W Q30, Q37]

**39 — A.** (A) variation in institutions and regions explains both Jain adaptation and uneven Buddhist contraction; (B) Jain practices and institutions evolved; (C) later violence cannot explain all earlier, regionally varied shifts; (D) eastern and Himalayan Buddhist continuities refute total disappearance. Trap: survival in some zones prevents a uniform “extinction” claim. [B §16.3–16.4; A §1; W Q40]

**40 — C.** (A) these were later centres, not pre-Buddha institutions; (B) later scholastic centres do not certify verbatim founder-era lectures; (C) Pala-era scholastic networks evidence continued Buddhism in parts of India; (D) their presence does not imply uniform flourishing across all regions. Trap: later monastic networks complicate a uniform disappearance claim. [B §§15.3, 16.3, 18; T Q36]

**41 — C.** (A) Saṅghabhūti is linked to Sarvāstivāda Vinaya, not Theravāda; (B) reverses Saṅghabhūti's Vinaya and Saṅghabhadra's Abhidharma associations; (C) keeps the Sarvāstivāda Vinaya-linked monk distinct from the later Abhidharma scholar; (D) Saṅghabhadra is a different person rather than another spelling. Trap: similar names do not establish identity. [B §§14.2, 21 (2024 Q64); W Q29]

**42 — A.** (A) both named works belong to Jain literary history; (B) both are Buddhist and can properly be assigned to Buddhism; (C) Vinaya and Sutta Piṭakas are Buddhist canonical collections; (D) *Therīgāthā* and *Dhammapada* belong to Buddhist literature. Trap: test each work independently and do not confuse literary affiliation with narrow canon status. [B §§13.3, 14.1, 21 (2022 Q55); W PYQ 2022 Q55]

**43 — D.** (A) a Pala regional centre does not imply equal flourishing elsewhere; (B) later political violence cannot cause earlier change; (C) Pala institutions cannot be projected unchanged onto the founder's sangha; (D) regional institutional survival and contraction differed by place and period. Trap: avoid uniform disappearance claims. [B §§15.3, 16.3; T Q36, Q43, Q58]

**44 — C.** (A) right mindfulness is an Eightfold Path factor replacing non-possession; (B) right livelihood is a Buddhist path factor replacing celibacy; (C) lists *ahiṃsā, satya, asteya, brahmacarya* and *aparigraha*; (D) right speech replaces truth and belongs to the Buddhist Eightfold Path. Trap: ethical overlap does not make formal lists interchangeable. [B §§1, 12.1, 13.1; W Q6]

**45 — D.** (A) the celibacy association is with Mahavira, not Ashoka; (B) reverses the traditional association between earlier teachers and Mahavira; (C) neglects the distinction of Mahavira's fifth vow; (D) gives the traditional earlier-four and added-celibacy formulation. Trap: this distinction is transmitted tradition, not a dated predecessor transcript. [B §§1, 11.1–12.1; W Q2, Q6]

**46 — C.** (A) the later Valabhi redaction cannot date an earlier Pāṭaliputra event by itself; (B) Digambara and Śvetāmbara views of surviving canon differ; (C) later sectarian transmission alone cannot supply a contemporary exact date; (D) one narrative does not precisely date the gradual sectarian split. Trap: a named ancient city does not turn sectarian narrative into contemporary minutes. [B §§9.1, 12.3; W Q10]

**47 — B.** (A) shared disciplinary concerns do not identify a single editorial lineage; (B) school affiliation, witnesses and translation sequence allow a grounded comparison; (C) the last translation does not date the first composition; (D) textual variation cannot be assigned to editors without comparing witnesses. Trap: Buddhist Vinaya traditions have distinct transmissions. [B §§9.1, 14.1–14.2; W Q29]. The separately authenticated 2024 Set-A Q64 is reproduction-held in `COVERAGE.md`.
