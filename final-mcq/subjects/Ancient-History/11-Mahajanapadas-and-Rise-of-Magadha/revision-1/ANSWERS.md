**1 — D.** (A) town/hinterland is not the lexical distinction; (B) lineage and subjects are not the two terms; (C) an assembled clan is closer to *jana* than to *janapada*; (D) territorial foothold contrasts with the collective people, though kin ties survive. Trap: territory is not yet a modern surveyed state. [B §9.1; W Q3]

**2 — B.** (A) *Mahāvastu* substitutions do not establish borders; (B) the *Aṅguttara* sixteen are conventional, not universal; (C) the Jain list has a different political horizon; (D) Puranic king lists do not harmonize Buddhist lists. Trap: mnemonic sixteen ≠ contemporaneous census. [B §§9.2, 11; A §§1–2; W Q1–2]

**3 — A.** (A) The conventional four major rival monarchies; (B) Vajji was an important confederacy, not Vatsa in this quartet; (C) Anga was an eastern rival but replaces Vatsa here; (D) Kāśi was absorbed by Kosala and replaces Kosala here. Trap: important polity ≠ member of the standard four. [B §§1, 11.1; W Q8]

**4 — C.** (A) Shurasena centres on Mathura; (B) Matsya belongs to eastern Rajasthan; (C) Gandhara and Kamboja occupy north-western routes/frontier; (D) Avanti is Malwa/Narmada-oriented. Trap: one north-western member does not make a north-western pair. [B §11; W Q33]

**5 — D.** (A) Avanti extended to the Narmada but is not the list's Godavari exception; (B) Chedi is a central Indian polity; (C) Vatsa belongs to lower Yamuna routes; (D) Ashmaka/Assaka is the Godavari exception south of the Vindhyas. Trap: Narmada links do not make Avanti Ashmaka. [B §§11–11.1; W Q5]

**6 — A.** (A) Kaushambi was Vatsa's lower-Yamuna centre; (B) Ujjayini was Avanti's, not Vatsa's; (C) Kaushambi was Vatsa's, not Avanti's; (D) Saketa was Kosalan, but Kosala's core is Sarayu-linked, not lower Yamuna. Trap: a correct capital alone cannot validate a wrong corridor. [B §11; W Q41]

**7 — C.** (A) Pava belongs to Malla, not Vajji; (B) Vaishali is Vajji's, Champa Anga's; (C) Varanasi, Champa and Vaishali match Kāśi, Anga and Vajji; (D) Rajagriha belongs to Magadha. Trap: distinguish close eastern centres across the Ganga. [B §11; W Q19, Q22]

**8 — B.** (A) Indraprastha belongs to Kuru, not Panchala; (B) the Kosalan, Malla and north/south Panchala centres fit; (C) Vaishali belongs to Vajji, not Malla; (D) Champa belongs to Anga, not Malla. Trap: multiple centres need not imply simultaneous dual capitals. [B §11; W Q41; T Q10]

**9 — A.** (A) Kuru—upper doab, Matsya—eastern Rajasthan, Shurasena—Mathura; (B) western Malwa is closer to Avanti than Matsya; (C) Kaushambi is Vatsa's, not Shurasena's; (D) Mathura is Shurasena's, not Matsya's. Trap: avoid swapping adjacent north-central route nodes. [B §11; A §2]

**10 — D.** (A) Kaushambi is Vatsa's; (B) Shuktimati is linked to Chedi; (C) Mathura belongs to Shurasena; (D) Ujjayini and Mahishmati are Avanti's Malwa/Narmada-linked centres. Trap: Narmada linkage does not extend to every western city. [B §11; W Q7, Q41]

**11 — B.** (A) reverses the political forms; (B) contrasts clan-confederate elites with dynastic royalty; (C) also reverses the forms; (D) no universal suffrage or fully direct appointment of village leaders is established. Trap: *gaṇa* is not modern mass democracy. [B §§9.1, 12; W Q9–11]

**12 — C.** (A) procedures cannot be shown identical in every polity; (B) a hall/token mention gives no complete electorate; (C) hall and later token vocabulary require separate chronological scrutiny; (D) assemblies do not imply uniform procedures. Trap: later procedure ≠ eyewitness Vajji constitution. [B §12; W Q12, Q42]

**13 — D.** (A) ruler narrative does not establish the site's urban functions; (B) ceramics and later capital lists lack sufficient functional convergence; (C) hoards and a court story omit settlement organization; (D) combined settlement, craft, exchange and functions support urban inference. Trap: artefact plus narrative is not automatically a city. [B §13.1; W Q13, Q34]

**14 — A.** (A) NBPW supplies a material horizon, subject to contextual dating; (B) urban identification needs multiple indicators; (C) even a fortified capital needs independent evidence; (D) pottery cannot identify a dynasty. Trap: NBPW alone does not establish a city. [B §§10, 13.1; W Q14, Q39]

**15 — C.** (A) burial need not date every coin's minting; (B) find-spot is not necessarily mint; (C) circulation is plausible, while issuer and deposition need separate study; (D) several issues can enter a hoard. Trap: coin hoard ≠ single named issuer. [B §§9.2, 13.1–13.3; W Q15]

**16 — D.** (A) neither word is a standard office for village governance or clan deliberation; (B) reverses the usual household/business emphasis; (C) neither denotes a corporate term or a corridor; (D) *gahapati* emphasizes substantial household production, *seṭṭhi* business/credit. Trap: positions overlap without becoming synonyms. [B §13.3; T Q24–25]

**17 — D.** (A) tools do not guarantee yields or victory; (B) river transport alone neither collects tax nor assures conquest; (C) ceramics and trade do not independently guarantee revenue; (D) access, labour, extraction and mobilization make a defensible conditional chain. Trap: opportunity is not sufficient causation. [B §§13.2, 17; W Q17–18]

**18 — A.** (A) Northern/southern corridors connected changing nodes; (B) uniform Haryanka highway tariffs are not established; (C) contemporary maps do not establish fixed routes; (D) goods and political movement also used them. Trap: “path” does not prove uniform paving. [B §13.3; T Q26]

**19 — C.** (A) corporate terms do not mean state tax offices; (B) later *Jātaka* guild details do not date every group to c. 600 BCE; (C) group terms indicate organization while later detail requires dating; (D) debris demonstrates crafts, not identical charters. Trap: organization ≠ a uniform legally incorporated guild. [B §§13.1, 13.3; T Q23, Q54]

**20 — B.** (A) the Malwa corridor belongs to Avanti; (B) Anga's annexation removed an eastern rival and opened Champa routes; (C) Vajji/Vaishali belongs to Ajatashatru's later war; (D) Vatsa/Kaushambi was another rival, not this annexation. Trap: keep Anga distinct from proximate and western rivals. [B §§11, 14–15; W Q19, Q37]

**21 — D.** (A) conflict over Kāśi/Kosala followed; (B) a Licchhavi marriage did not preclude Ajatashatru's Vajji conflict; (C) Madra diplomacy does not document control of Gandhara; (D) alliances are remembered without extant treaty documents. Trap: marriage stories do not establish permanent peace. [B §§14–15; W Q20]

**22 — A.** (A) War, a frontier fort, coalition politics and diplomacy are jointly relevant; (B) named siege devices cannot by themselves demonstrate a rapid victory; (C) blockade alone is not the transmitted sequence; (D) a confederate vote alone did not transfer authority. Trap: Vassakara narrative is not a battlefield log. [B §§12, 14–15; W Q21–22, Q31]

**23 — C.** (A) Ajatashatru is linked instead to Pataligrama's fortification; (B) Shishunaga is linked instead to Avanti's defeat; (C) Udayin/Udayibhadda is conventionally linked with making Pataliputra a capital; (D) Pradyota was an Avanti rival. Trap: fortification and capital establishment have different traditional attributions. [B §§15–16; W Q23]

**24 — C.** (A) Kosala's conflicts are attached especially to Ajatashatru; (B) Vatsa had its own rivalry with Avanti; (C) Shishunaga is credited in tradition with ending Avanti's independence; (D) Gandhara is not his conventional conquest. Trap: Shishunaga's Avanti victory is tradition-mediated, not a dated campaign archive. [B §§14–15, 17; W Q24, Q43]

**25 — D.** (A) exact founder/reign dates remain disputed; (B) fiscal concentration is central to Nanda traditions; (C) Shishunaga precedes rather than ends Nanda rule; (D) the pre-Mauryan Magadhan phase involves consolidation and conflicting origins. Trap: Greek force counts are not audited payrolls. [B §§10, 15, 18; W Q26, Q44]

**26 — A.** (A) Rajagriha's hill basin contrasts with Pataliputra's Ganga-system setting; (B) reverses topographies; (C) western passes do not describe Pataliputra; (D) upland/river placements are reversed. Trap: shifting channels preclude a fixed modern confluence. [B §§16–17; A §§1–2; W Q27–28]

**27 — C.** (A) the first occupation cannot be dated precisely to Bimbisara; (B) neither site securely identifies a Haryanka palace; (C) early historic settlement is evidenced, not a complete Haryanka city plan; (D) a Udayin-era street grid is not established. Trap: material horizon ≠ named builder. [B §§13.1, 16.1; A §1; W Q39]

**28 — B.** (A) ore proximity does not prove exclusive supply; (B) Magadha had a resource advantage but no proven sole monopoly; (C) Avanti could also exploit metal-bearing regions; (D) ore distribution alone cannot date an interstate victory. Trap: access still requires political conversion. [B §§1–2, 17; A §§1, 5; W Q36]

**29 — D.** (A) forest access does not guarantee elephants ready for war; (B) capture does not guarantee a trained army or its supply; (C) timber does not spontaneously become fortification; (D) capture, management, logistics and control turn potential into capacity. Trap: ecological possibility does not guarantee victory. [B §§2, 17; T Q44]

**30 — A.** (A) geography, institutions, alliances and victories explain cumulative gains; (B) agricultural advantage did not replace military and diplomatic action; (C) iron access was not a monopoly excluding rivals; (D) routes alone did not sustain fortification and extraction. Trap: winner-centred accounts can erase contingency. [B §§14–19; A §§1–5; W Q30, Q43]

**31 — C.** (A) this phase was not restricted to a revival in the Indus basin; (B) its towns predate Mauryan capital-building; (C) renewed early historic urbanization was especially Gangetic; (D) its onset predated Nanda consolidation. Trap: “second” compares broad South Asian urban episodes. [B §§1, 13.1; W Q31]

**32 — A.** (A) Kāśi passed into Kosala's sphere before Magadha's later contest; (B) puts absorption too late; (C) reverses the direction of absorption; (D) Kāśi was not the fourth major monarchy in place of Kosala. Trap: Kosala too was expansionist. [B §§11, 14; T Q11]

**33 — B.** (A) *seṭṭhi/gahapati* are economic/social designations, not royal offices; (B) high officials and village leadership are named in differing contexts; (C) *śreṇi/pūga* denotes corporate group terms, not village offices; (D) *santhāgāra/śalākā* concerns collective procedure, not tax offices. Trap: names do not establish later uniform departments. [B §16.2; W Q29; T Q13]

**34 — D.** (A) a prescription does not establish an observed mean; (B) tolls and tribute could also occur; (C) no audited Bimbisara returns survive; (D) fiscal ideals are not observed universal rates. Trap: collection also requires transport and personnel. [B §§13.2, 16.2; T Q30, Q53]

**35 — A.** (A) names survive in war accounts, not reliable specifications; (B) no securely recovered engine verifies use; (C) no contemporary plan documents exact mechanism; (D) devices do not independently date the war. Trap: a named machine is not archaeological proof. [B §15; W Q22]

**36 — A.** (A) cities and patrons altered audiences without determining doctrines; (B) merchant support did not create every teaching; (C) patronage included more than royal courts; (D) rural networks persisted. Trap: enabling setting differs from doctrinal content. [B §§13.3–13.4; T Q32]

**37 — B.** (A) reverses and misassigns the traditional roles; (B) Mahapadma figures in founder/conquest narratives, Dhana as the last Nanda; (C) Kalashoka, not Mahapadma, is council-linked; (D) Vajji wars and Kosalan diplomacy belong to earlier Haryankas. Trap: dynastic identification is safer than exact reign dates. [B §§10, 15; W Q26]

**38 — D.** (A) no contemporary council edict establishes the association; (B) it does not fix an accession year; (C) variant successor lists remain; (D) Kalashoka/Kakavarna's council/Vaishali link is traditional, with varying names/dates. Trap: council tradition is not contemporary minutes. [B §15; W Q25]

**39 — D.** (A) Kaushambi is Vatsa's, not Chedi's; (B) Mahishmati belongs to Avanti, not Ashmaka; (C) Ujjayini is Avanti's, not Chedi's; (D) Potana/Podana and Shuktimati are Ashmaka and Chedi traditions, with qualified identifications. Trap: traditional names have unequal archaeological security. [B §11]

**40 — B.** (A) coins do not prove royal issue; (B) ceramics, coins and walls together strengthen urban-exchange inference without naming a ruler; (C) an assemblage does not identify a polity; (D) the three finds do not fix a foundation date. Trap: combined evidence still has limits. [B §§9.2, 13.1; W Q13–15]

**41 — D.** (A) Kaushambi and Malwa links could sustain rivals; (B) Sarayu and Malwa routes offered competing exchange advantages; (C) rival commercial centres predated Mauryan rule; (D) Vatsa and Avanti had genuine route/resource alternatives. Trap: the winner's success was not predetermined. [B §§11.1, 17.1; A §§1, 5; W Q41, Q43]

**42 — D.** (A) Kaushambi's location supports a Yamuna connection, not a dated sovereignty claim. (B) An assignment to a political horizon is a contextual interpretation, not a claim about each layer. (C) Comparing independently dated sequences is methodologically appropriate. (D) Proving direct capital control of every layer needs independent, layer-specific political evidence. **Trap:** a named capital cannot date every occupation. [B §§7, 11, 17; W Q27]
