**1 — C.** (A) *Mahāvastu* supplies no fixed boundary survey; (B) substitutions preclude an unchanged horizon; (C) different textual horizons account for variable lists; (D) neither list neatly separates only monarchies from only clans. Trap: enumeration ≠ cadastral survey. [B §§9.2, 11; A §§1–2; W Q1–2]

**2 — A.** Statement 1 is true: territorial settlement did not erase kinship. Statement 2 is false: texts do not prescribe an identical constitutional grade. (A) selects the valid claim only; (B) accepts the false claim; (C) adds the false claim to the true one; (D) rejects the true claim. Trap: territoriality does not imply constitutional uniformity. [B §9.1; W Q3]

**3 — D.** (A) ware cannot name the first ruler of each capital; (B) material layers do not fix the composition date of all king lists; (C) pottery does not synchronize political expansion; (D) material and political transitions differed by region. Trap: c. 600 BCE is an organizing threshold. [B §§10, 13.1; W Q4, Q14]

**4 — B.** (A) Narmada belongs to Avanti's wider corridor, not Ashmaka's Godavari; (B) Taxila/Gandhara and Godavari/Ashmaka fit; (C) Malwa is Avanti's rather than Ashmaka's; (D) Kaushambi is Vatsa's, and Kamboja is a polity, not Gandhara's capital. Trap: north-west frontier and trans-Vindhyan exception differ. [B §11; W Q5, Q33]

**5 — C.** (A) Anga replaces Avanti; (B) Vajji replaces Vatsa; (C) Kosala, Magadha, Vatsa and Avanti are the conventional power quartet; (D) Kāśi replaces Avanti. Trap: historically important does not necessarily mean member of the quartet. [B §§1, 11.1; A §§1–2; W Q8]

**6 — A.** (A) Vaishali belongs to Vajji, but the north-west frontier does not; (B) Vatsa/Kaushambi/Yamuna fits; (C) Kosala/Shravasti/Sarayu fits; (D) Avanti's two centres are conventional. Trap: a correctly matched capital can conceal a false corridor. [B §11; W Q41]

**7 — B.** (A) Pava is Malla's, not Vajji's; (B) all four centres fit Kosala, Kāśi, Vajji and Anga; (C) Rajagriha is Magadhan, not Vajji's; (D) Mathura is Shurasena's, not Anga's. Trap: swap one eastern node and an otherwise plausible row fails. [B §11; T Q9–11, Q22]

**8 — B.** (A) Indraprastha belongs to Kuru; (B) Malla's two centres and Panchala's north/south centres fit; (C) Vaishali is Vajji's, not Malla's; (D) Kaushambi is Vatsa's, not Panchala's. Trap: multiple centres do not imply simultaneous equal capitals. [B §11; T Q10]

**9 — D.** (A) Kaushambi belongs to Vatsa, not Shurasena; (B) Ujjayini belongs to Avanti, not Matsya; (C) Kaushambi belongs to Vatsa, not Kuru; (D) Indraprastha/Kuru, Bairat/Matsya and Mathura/Shurasena fit. Trap: an approximately right upper-doab zone does not make neighbouring capitals interchangeable. [B §11; A §2]

**10 — C.** (A) secure excavated capitals are not established for both; (B) Rajapura is later/uncertain, not securely excavated; (C) Chedi is central and Kamboja's frontier limits remain uncertain; (D) Chedi is not north-western. Trap: proposed capitals have unequal evidential security. [B §§9.2, 11]

**11 — D.** (A) elite voting did not establish one permanently commanding elected monarch; (B) householders did not all vote equally; (C) Vajji was not simply a Magadhan dependency; (D) collective political membership was largely restricted to clan elites. Trap: “republic” is a loose translation. [B §§9.1, 12; W Q9–12]

**12 — A.** (A) a hall supports collective assembly space without a dated franchise; (B) does not establish universal participation; (C) later procedural vocabulary cannot precisely date voting rules; (D) all *gaṇas* did not necessarily share identical procedure. Trap: later *śalākā* usage is not a sixth-century voter register. [B §12; T Q15–16]

**13 — D.** (A) internal coordination was relevant; (B) diplomacy and war could both contribute; (C) surviving institutions might have changed; (D) one defeat does not establish universal monarchical inevitability. Trap: avoid evolutionary teleology. [B §§12, 14; W Q21, Q31]

**14 — C.** (A) walls alone do not identify a named ruler; (B) crafts do not prove a complete city government; (C) fortifications, crafts and settlement hierarchy give convergent support; (D) shared ware does not entail identical political status. Trap: ware ≠ city. [B §§10, 13.1; T Q18–19]

**15 — C.** (A) burial does not coincide necessarily with minting; (B) a hoard may mix issuing authorities; (C) finds suggest uneven monetary exchange, not a single named ruler; (D) exchange in kind continued. Trap: find-spot is not an issuer's signed mint. [B §§9.2, 13.1, 13.3; W Q15]

**16 — B.** (A) literary terms do not necessarily denote court offices; (B) composite *Jātaka* narratives cannot serve as contemporary occupational censuses; (C) corporate crafts are not automatically royal tax offices; (D) guild members cannot all be identified as landholders. Trap: later organized guild detail is not automatically sixth-century practice. [B §13.3; T Q23–25, Q54]

**17 — C.** (A) *seṭṭhi* is a business/credit term, not clan-assembly membership; (B) *Uttarāpatha* is northern, not southern; (C) both northern corridor and business-credit elite fit; (D) *Dakṣiṇāpatha* is southern, not northern. Trap: a correct social term can mask a reversed route. [B §13.3; T Q24–26]

**18 — A.** (A) a norm needs contextual testing alongside dues and actual extraction; (B) it is not an observed kingdom-wide rate; (C) it is not a mean household income; (D) all polities did not demonstrably collect identically. Trap: ideal fractions are not empirical rates. [B §13.2; T Q30, Q53]

**19 — D.** (A) Vajji dissension belongs to Ajatashatru's narrative; (B) Avanti's defeat is attributed to Shishunaga; (C) the capital shift is linked to Udayin, not Bimbisara; (D) Anga annexation and marriage links are both transmitted for Bimbisara. Trap: marriages did not preclude later wars. [B §§14–16; W Q19–20]

**20 — C.** (A) reverses the rulers; (B) reverses both rulers and conflicts; (C) Anga belongs to Bimbisara's expansion, Vajji to Ajatashatru's prolonged war; (D) no one Bimbisara campaign conquered both. Trap: do not compress cumulative expansion. [B §§14–15; W Q19, Q21]

**21 — B.** (A) reverses the conventional associations; (B) Ajatashatru/Pataligrama fort and Udayin/Pataliputra capital are different traditions; (C) Bimbisara is associated instead with Rajagriha and Anga; (D) Shishunaga is linked instead to Avanti's defeat. Trap: frontier fortification ≠ capital establishment. [B §§14–16; T Q35–37]

**22 — D.** (A) royal narratives cannot date structures by association; (B) architectural resemblance is insufficient for contemporaneity; (C) wall size does not identify a ruler; (D) separate archaeological phasing prevents backdating later Kumrahar remains. Trap: narrative attribution needs stratified corroboration. [B §§9.2, 16.1; W Q27, Q39]

**23 — C.** (A) Shishunaga follows, not precedes, Haryanka; (B) Nanda follows both earlier dynasties; (C) Haryanka → Shishunaga → Nanda is the broad conventional succession; (D) the sequence reverses all three phases. Trap: broad dynastic order is safer than exact regnal dates. [B §§10, 15; W Q38]

**24 — C.** (A) council association does not establish an accession year; (B) it does not reconcile every king list; (C) Vaishali/council link is transmitted rather than a contemporary record; (D) it cannot identify all dynastic capitals. Trap: traditional council attribution is not administrative minutes. [B §15; W Q25]

**25 — B.** (A) exact figures are not independently confirmed; (B) reports indicate perceived formidable strength without audited totals; (C) Indian traditions do not certify identical counts; (D) numbers do not establish precise regnal dates. Trap: distinguish perceived strength from audited rolls. [B §§9.2, 15; W Q26, Q40, Q44]

**26 — A.** (A) Avanti's access and institutional conversion challenge a monopoly thesis; (B) Avanti did have resources; (C) Kosala had fertile land and river access; (D) Vatsa had the Yamuna/Kaushambi corridor. Trap: proximity is not exclusive possession. [B §§13.2, 17; A §§1, 5; T Q43]

**27 — B.** (A) ancient channels did not remain fixed; (B) a strategic river-system shorthand need not claim unchanged channels; (C) no proven Ajatashatru canal blueprint is established; (D) no exact Udayin-era street grid is documented. Trap: map schematic ≠ fixed hydrology. [B §16.1; W Q28]

**28 — C.** (A) full provincial departments belong to later Mauryan evidence; (B) Ashoka follows Nanda rule; (C) Nanda consolidation prepares, but is not, Mauryan administration; (D) Nandas immediately preceded the Mauryas. Trap: do not backdate Mauryan institutions. [B §§16.2, 18; W Q29, Q32]

**29 — B.** (A) Kāśi's absorption was earlier than the end of Nanda expansion; (B) Kosala absorbed Kāśi before later rivalry with Magadha; (C) reverses the relationship; (D) contradicts the transmitted absorption. Trap: Magadha did not begin as the only expansionist polity. [B §§11, 14; T Q11]

**30 — D.** (A) social/business roles are not securely royal appointments; (B) hall/voting terms are not Magadhan local officials; (C) Mauryan titles do not establish Haryanka departments; (D) high and village-level roles appear in traditions, with variable functions. Trap: do not read later administrative organization backward. [B §16.2; W Q29]

**31 — C.** (A) no workshop is identified by the name alone; (B) named deployment does not independently prove effectiveness; (C) a named war device is transmitted but its design uncertain; (D) no exact victory year follows from the name. Trap: vivid narrative ≠ recovered engineering plan. [B §15; W Q22]

**32 — A.** (A) Mahapadma's conquests and Dhana's last-Nanda role are traditional; (B) Kalashoka, not Mahapadma, is council-linked; (C) Dhana is the last Nanda, not Avanti's conqueror; (D) the Haryankas and Kosalan marriage are earlier. Trap: dynastic narratives diverge on founder names. [B §§10, 15; W Q26]

**33 — B.** (A) capture does not guarantee trained regiments; (B) ecological resources required organization to become military capacity; (C) Magadha did not monopolize all northern timber; (D) access cannot validate exact Nanda counts. Trap: natural resources are not ready-made regiments. [B §§2, 17; T Q44]

**34 — D.** (A) urbanization alone did not determine doctrine; (B) merchant patronage was not the sole cause; (C) audiences included more than royal courts; (D) mobility and towns enlarged audiences without mechanically causing ideas. Trap: distinguish setting from doctrine. [B §13.4; T Q32, Q57]

**35 — D.** (A) Mathura is not Vatsa's; (B) Vaishali is not Kosala's; (C) Potana is linked to Ashmaka, not Avanti; (D) Ashmaka/Potana, Shurasena/Mathura and Vajji/Vaishali fit, with Potana qualified. Trap: not all centre-identifications have equal security. [B §11; W Q5]

**36 — D.** (A) a literary mention does not establish a full excavated ruler list; (B) it does not map exact modern borders; (C) it does not supply a sixth-century plan; (D) its identification depends on less secure literary geography than Vatsa's excavated Kaushambi. Trap: capital names carry unequal evidential weight. [B §§9.2, 11]

**37 — B.** (A) coin symbols alone do not securely name the ruler; (B) fortification, circulation and routes suggest exchange but not exact polity; (C) a wall does not prove monarchy; (D) a river does not date the site's founding. Trap: material convergence is not licence to name a ruler. [B §§9.2, 13.1, 17; W Q13–15]
