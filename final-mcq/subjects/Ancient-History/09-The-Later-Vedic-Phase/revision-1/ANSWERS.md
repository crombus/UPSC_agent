1. **A.** A correctly pairs ritual exposition with symbolic reading; B swaps Brahmana and Aranyaka emphases; C assigns early Upanishadic dialogue and Samavedic chant to the wrong genres; D assigns Atharvavedic charms and Yajurvedic procedures elsewhere. Trap: genre determines historical use. [B §1; W 1, 33]
2. **A.** A treats the rite as normative evidence; B derives actual control from prescription; C infers frequency from instruction; D dates excavated sites from vocabulary. Trap: a prescribed action is not an observed performance. [B §1; W 1, 5; T 51]
3. **B.** A exchanges Atharvavedic charms and Yajurvedic formulae; B pairs all three correctly; C exchanges Sama and Yajur functions; D exchanges Sama and Atharva functions. Trap: distinguish ritual use of each collection. [W 2–4; T 2–4; B §1]
4. **B.** A equates periodisation with each passage's date; B preserves internal regional/textual sequences; C equates local site dates; D assumes all eastern accounts are equally old. Trap: a working range is not a date stamp. [B §§1–2; W 6]
5. **B.** A is the earlier north-western focus; B is the Kuru-Panchala/upper-Ganga core; C is an eastern extension, not the core; D belongs more centrally to subsequent state formation. Trap: do not confuse core with earlier focus or later eastern reach. [B §2; A §§1–2; W 7]
6. **D.** A equates mention with direct rule; B assumes single-place composition of all layers; C wrongly ends Kuru-Panchala relevance; D distinguishes expanding geographical horizons from effective control. Trap: place-name ≠ border. [B §§1–2; W 34; T 8]
7. **A.** A identifies material and stratigraphic evidence; B guesses a recension from pots; C maps political allegiance from distribution; D infers epic-person identity. Trap: ceramic distribution cannot by itself establish speech or political identity. [B §3; W 8, 35; T 12]
8. **A.** A identifies all textual communities from pots without independent evidence; B uses stratigraphy locally; C correctly requires evidence beyond pottery for language; D permits a qualified spatial hypothesis. Trap: overlap ≠ identity. [B §3; A §§1, 5; W 35]
9. **A.** A fits the later dark-metal expression; B reads earlier generic *ayas* as always iron; C extrapolates one tool to all sites; D confuses metal with ceramics. Trap: earlier *ayas* need not mean iron. [B §4, §21; W 9]
10. **C.** A establishes dated use, not production; B supplies literary vocabulary; C gives site-specific metallurgical residues; D establishes dated tool use, not smelting. Trap: tool finds are not furnaces. [B §4; W 36]
11. **D.** A turns tools into sufficient causes; B eliminates regional variation; C extrapolates uniform yields; D recognises social and ecological mediation. Trap: capacity is not outcome. [B §§4–5; A §§1, 5; W 10, 48]
12. **D.** A denies continuing draught power; B wrongly makes rice displace other cereals everywhere; C erases seasonality; D recognises stronger cultivation alongside cattle. Trap: agriculture and pastoral value persisted together. [B §5; W 11; T 20]
13. **C.** A denies draught power; B erases rice evidence; C pairs mixed cereals with labour and animal traction; D assumes a common calendar. Trap: regional crop diversity is not one uniform regime. [B §5; W 11, 37; T 19]
14. **A.** A allows greater intensity without universalising; B assigns two crops to every household; C insists on iron shares; D excludes continuing cattle traction. Trap: a local two-crop pattern is not a regional census. [B §§4–5; A §1; W 37]
15. **B.** A equates textual roles with hereditary jatis; B identifies differentiation with a limit; C assumes household production vanished; D assumes exclusively urban wages. Trap: an occupational list is not a guild roll. [B §6; W 13; T 22]
16. **C.** A assumes a fixed universal currency; B assumes standardised local weights; C permits valuables and units without proof of minting; D projects struck coins onto all sites. Trap: metal/value vocabulary is not a coin find. [B §6; W 14; T 23]
17. **A.** A links people and territorial footing; B erases kin affiliation; C delays territorial change until mature cities; D invents simultaneously surveyed borders. Trap: *jana* and kinship did not disappear overnight. [B §7; A §§1–2; W 15; T 10]
18. **C.** A claims an excavated epic event; B relocates Kuru to a later eastern imperial setting; C follows Bharata-Puru traditions and political coalescence; D derives social identity from ceramics alone. Trap: political tradition is not literal epic verification. [B §7; W 16; T 9]
19. **B.** A imposes a salaried cabinet; B identifies significant ritual-political associates; C restricts all to priests; D infers elected household delegates. Trap: ritual lists do not prove permanent departments. [B §8; W 17; T 26–27]
20. **B.** A gives the *senani*'s war role to *suta*; B correctly pairs ruler's share and charioteer/herald; C gives the *gramani*'s group role to *bhagadugha*; D gives treasury and group roles to the wrong associates. Trap: terms identify roles, not a fixed bureaucracy. [B §8; W 38; T 28]
21. **C.** A denies increased obligation; B infers one uniform assessed rate; C allows regularisation without mature machinery; D mistakes tribute/share for exclusively priestly fees. Trap: *bhaga* has no secure universal rate here. [B §9; W 18; T 29–30]
22. **A.** A describes *sabha/samiti* persistence and *vidatha*'s reduced prominence; B erases the first two; C freezes all functions; D invents an eastward institutional sequence and ritual-fee meanings. Trap: change in weight is not extinction. [B §10; W 19, 39; T 31]
23. **D.** A treats the route as surveyed frontiers; B infers local tribute schedules; C turns claim into direct rule; D separates ritual sovereignty from actual reach. Trap: horse route ≠ enforceable frontier. [B §11; W 21, 40; T 34]

25. **A.** A separates fourfold normative ranking from later diverse *jatis*; B maps schema onto all endogamous groups; C claims status never changed; D mistakes ideology for a census of occupations. Trap: *varna* is not a complete map of lived groups. [B §12; W 22; T 39]

28. **D.** A excludes documented household participation; B universalises elite debaters; C lets the queen's role erase patriarchy; D combines stronger ideals and varied agency. Trap: exceptional presence neither proves equality nor universal decline. [B §§14–15; W 26, 46; T 43–44]
