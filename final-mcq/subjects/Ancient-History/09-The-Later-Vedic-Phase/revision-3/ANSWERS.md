1. **C.** A correctly describes the elite normative character of Brahmanas; B correctly associates Aranyakas with symbolic interpretation; C wrongly gives all early Upanishads one date and doctrine; D correctly identifies later Samhita evidence as layered. Trap: no complete single-date philosophical system. [B §1; W 1, 33]
2. **C.** A pairs chants correctly but misplaces formulas with Atharva; B reverses Sama and Yajur; C pairs Sama chant and Yajur action/formula correctly; D gives chants to Atharva. Trap: a Rigvedic verse can be reworked as a Samavedic chant. [B §1; W 2–3; T 2–3]
3. **D.** A focuses on royal ritual; B on sacrificial formulas; C on liturgical chant; D on domestic/healing charms. Trap: identify the collection by the passage's function, not merely its Vedic status. [W 2–4; T 2–4; B §1]
4. **B.** A repeats the error by dating the whole corpus from one early layer; B separates phase range from passage date; C assumes a different regional context dates another layer; D confuses oral survival with composition date. Trap: transmission and composition are distinct. [B §§1–2; W 6]
5. **A.** A orders 3 north-west, 2 Kuru-Panchala, 1 later eastern horizon; B moves Videha before the core; C moves the core before the Rigvedic baseline; D also puts the core first. Trap: the sequence describes broad textual foci, not dated migrations or one expanding kingdom. [B §2; A §§1–2; W 7, 34]
6. **B.** A reduces a narrative to ceramics; B reads eastward imagery and ritual legitimation; C infers direct Kuru administration; D extracts precise site dates absent from the account. Trap: narrative geography is not a migration ledger. [B §§1–2; T 8]
7. **D.** A assumes the same immediate predecessor despite the reported difference; B synchronises underlying levels without dates; C infers identical inhabitants; D respects variable local sequences. Trap: shared diagnostic ware does not erase distinct stratigraphies. [B §3; T 12, 14]
8. **B.** A dates narrative from ceramics; B keeps occupation and textual claims separate; C maps named combatants from pottery; D makes a site name identify occupants of each layer. Trap: epic association is not event verification. [B §3; T 49; W 35]
9. **D.** A makes a few bowls predominant; B extends their finish to all vessels; C mistakes a subset of sherds for a full occupational sequence; D recognises a diagnostic minority. Trap: diagnostic does not mean dominant. [B §3; T 13]
10. **D.** A conditionally concerns use; B conditionally concerns production; C requires dates for chronology; D infers simultaneous production everywhere from separate undated finds. Trap: secure provenience is indispensable. [B §4; W 10, 36]
11. **C.** A shows more use, not manufacture; B supplies textual vocabulary, not site production; C combines securely associated furnace and slag; D concerns storage and use without smelting residue. Trap: a tool is not a workshop. [B §4; W 36]
12. **A.** A identifies mediating inputs; B discounts traction; C wrongly treats distinct soils as yield-equivalent; D writes off continuing cattle wealth. Trap: technology enables but does not guarantee agrarian change. [B §§4–5; W 10, 48]
13. **D.** A turns one site into a regional yield census; B assumes urban coins from storage; C extends one site to all textual households; D makes a bounded inference. Trap: Jakhera illustrates potential, not the whole phase. [B §§4–5, 20; T 16]
14. **A.** A allows regional crop differences; B wrongly makes rice replace barley everywhere; C confines wheat to the earlier phase without evidence; D derives one calendar from two passages. Trap: crop mention does not quantify calendars. [B §5; T 19; W 11, 37]
15. **C.** A attributes cultivation to these references as an origin; B discounts cattle traction; C links labour and draught power to intensification; D universalises iron shares. Trap: ploughing need not imply identical implements. [B §§4–5; W 37]
16. **B.** A converts craft roles into excavated urban workshops; B allows rural household production alongside specialisation; C projects fixed guild membership; D infers wages and coinage. Trap: occupational differentiation is not an urban labour census. [B §6; W 13; T 22]
17. **B.** A overstates minted money and urban extent; B distinguishes value vocabulary from coinage and town vocabulary from urbanisation; C turns value into tax and towns into capitals; D assumes fixed wages and planned cities. Trap: neither lexical item alone establishes mature urban institutions. [B §§6, 20; T 23–24; W 14]
18. **A.** A reads a realm while leaving borders open; B equates PGW distribution with state boundaries; C assumes fixed provinces; D reduces the domain to a ritual lineage. Trap: *rashtra* does not independently map a bounded state. [B §7; T 11; W 15]
19. **B.** A excludes surviving ratification motifs; B allows growing kingship without assuming a standing army; C denies priestly and household associates; D infers regular salaries from tribute. Trap: strengthened monarchy does not imply later military organisation. [B §§8–10; W 20; T 25, 32]
20. **C.** A excludes the queen and other household links; B posits representative membership not evidenced by the rite; C identifies a staged royal network; D imposes uniform departments. Trap: *ratnins* are not a fixed cabinet. [B §8; W 17; T 26–27]
21. **C.** A makes the *senani* a herald; B substitutes a ritual supervisor for the *purohita* and a collector for the *gramani*; C correctly pairs adviser, war leader and village/group leader; D gives the *hotri*'s singing role to the *purohita* and the *suta*'s chariot role to the *gramani*. Trap: differentiated titles do not establish fixed ministries. [B §8; W 17]
22. **D.** A infers struck coins; B ends continuing gifts; C posits one assessed regional rate; D identifies growing appropriation without mature uniform bureaucracy. Trap: revenue terms cannot establish a precise rate. [B §9; W 18; T 29–30]
23. **C.** A universalises succession powers; B excludes hereditary tendencies; C allows assemblies to persist with altered influence; D assumes no change in their weight. Trap: persistence does not imply unchanged authority. [B §10; W 19, 39]
24. **B.** A describes reduced prominence, C recognises surviving collective vocabulary, D distinguishes silence from census; B overgeneralises absence in a passage to every community. Trap: textual silence cannot establish universal disappearance. [B §10; W 39]
25. **A.** A is a ritual sovereignty claim; B records a genealogy, not administrative reach; C establishes local occupation rather than royal frontiers; D, if authentic, records tribute in one place but not a purely ritual claim. Trap: horse-sacrifice ambition does not survey borders. [B §11; W 21, 40; T 34]
26. **B.** A exchanges *ashvamedha* and *vajapeya*; B correctly assigns consecration, horse/paramountcy and prestige/race; C exchanges *rajasuya* and *ashvamedha*; D exchanges *rajasuya* and *vajapeya*. Trap: none alone maps actual administrative reach. [B §11; T 33–35; W 40]
27. **C.** A is consistent with elite Brahmana–Kshatriya claims; B reflects producer/tribute roles; C projects every later *jati* backwards; D reflects the normative account of Shudra exclusion. Trap: ideological fourfold hierarchy does not enumerate lived groups. [B §§12–13; W 22–24]
28. **B.** A imposes one inherited status; B allows context-dependent servile relations; C invents a uniform cause; D equates a variable dependency term with Shudra rank. Trap: *dasa/dasi* is not one unchanging category. [B §13; W 41]
29. **D.** A captures the prescriptive household model; B captures son preference as an ideal; C correctly allows other evidence to challenge universality; D wrongly equates a text with uniform practice. Trap: prescription is not an inheritance census. [B §§14–15; W 42]
30. **D.** A universalises exogamy and four compulsory stages; B erases descent; C treats a prescription as a census; D identifies developing rather than fixed ideals. Trap: later systematisation must not be imposed on all households. [B §16; W 25, 27]
31. **C.** A projects universal ritual access; B treats debate and patriarchy as mutually exclusive; C makes a bounded elite-textual claim; D infers assembly participation frequency from a named dialogue. Trap: named participant does not establish a population norm. [B §15; W 26, 46]
32. **B.** A is supported by specialist recitation; B wrongly infers universally equal access; C describes oral teacher-pupil transmission; D allows differentiated training for ritual roles. Trap: skilled curriculum ≠ public schooling. [B §17; W 43]
33. **D.** A exchanges procedure and supervision; B gives Samavedic chant to *hotri* and recitation to *brahman*; C gives oversight to *hotri* and chant to *brahman*; D correctly pairs recitation, procedure and supervision. *Udgatri* specialised in Sama chanting. 34. **B.** A makes Prajapati's ascent exclusive; B permits changing emphases and diverse inquiry; C treats speculation as abolition of ritual; D backdates mature Puranic sects. Trap: emphasis does not imply exclusive worship. [B §§18–19; W 29–30]
35. **C.** A confuses philosophical terms with rite and fee; B assigns formula and kinship to self and ultimate reality; C correctly pairs self and ultimate reality; D mistakes priestly/royal roles for philosophical concepts. Trap: *brahman* as ultimate reality is distinct from the officiating *brahman*. [B §19; T 48; W 45]
36. **B.** A makes pottery proof of urban money; B uses contingent links and respects the doctrine boundary; C infers inevitable towns from ritual expenditure; D derives completed heterodox doctrine directly from early criticism. Trap: preconditions are not inevitable outcomes. [B §22; A §§1, 5; W 48]
