**1 — C.** (A) older trade and movement corridors predated both campaigns; (B) early contact was not confined to Darius's later claims; (C) they were different bounded north-western episodes within older networks; (D) neither extended durable rule into the Ganga plain. Trap: neither first contact nor pan-Indian conquest. [B §§9–10, 20–21; W Q28, Q32]

**2 — B.** (A) distant repeated claims do not show this town's extraction; (B) matching on-site fiscal/administrative evidence would be needed; (C) Greek tribute categories alone do not establish that town's fiscal practice; (D) a displaced coin cannot by itself show collection in this town. Trap: imperial claim vs direct administrative practice. [B §§11–13; W Q1, Q8]

**3 — A.** (A) royal eastern designation is real while Gandhara/Hindush extents differ and are debated; (B) a country-name does not fix an eastern border; (C) a royal land list is not evidence of town-by-town dues; (D) it does not survey one combined province. Trap: reading a royal list as a surveyed map. [B §§11–13; W Q35, Q41]

**4 — D.** (A) Darius's later inscriptions cannot retroactively map a Cyrus province; (B) no Cambyses Indian fiscal receipts establish annexation; (C) Herodotus's references do not settle exact eastern bounds; (D) late Cyrus reports and lack of clear Cambyses evidence require restraint. Trap: missing primary annexation evidence is not proof of complete prior control. [B §12.1; W Q4]

**5 — A.** (A) Herodotus conveys perceptions of a valuable eastern tribute zone, not modern audited returns; (B) no independently confirmed Persian receipt fixes the figure; (C) tribute district ≠ identical royal-land boundaries; (D) nothing in the number gives a per-person rate. Trap: 360 talents is a reported figure, not independently verified revenue. [B §§11, 13.2; W Q6, Q42]

**6 — C.** (A) Herodotus alone cannot supply independent royal evidence; (B) a royal name alone is no complete provincial survey; (C) texts and relevant material context converge on networks but not identical local control; (D) later script alone cannot fix a precise conquest date. Trap: use different source types to constrain each other. [B §§11–14; W Q1, Q7, Q33]

**7 — D.** (A) Herodotus and royal lists are distinct sources; (B) neither is an independent site map; (C) Herodotus's reported district cannot fix Hindush's farthest border; (D) Greek districts and royal names may not coincide. Trap: no one-to-one administrative map from unlike texts. [B §§11, 13.1–13.2; W Q42]

**8 — B.** (A) mixed assemblage cannot establish identical mint authority; (B) Chaman-i Hazouri/Bhir-type evidence supports circulation and contacts; (C) Persian coins' presence does not prove sole derivation; (D) burial date is not the minting date of every coin. Trap: coin type, mint authority and circulation are distinct. [B §§11, 14; W Q33; T Q8]

**9 — A.** (A) Kharoshthi is a north-western right-to-left script structurally associated with Imperial Aramaic; (B) Greek-only derivation does not fit the script's Aramaic relation; (C) regional adaptation is more plausible than identity with Aramaic; (D) exact inventor/year unknown. Trap: contact may explain scribal transmission without a royal foundation record. [B §§1, 14; W Q9, Q43]

**10 — C.** (A) neither lexical nor formal parallel proves institutional identity; (B) no court-artisan record links the Persian and Mauryan capitals directly; (C) selective resemblance is testable with attention to independent chronology; (D) shared terms cannot demonstrate one continuous workshop. Trap: influence ≠ origin of an entire institution. [B §§1, 14; A §§1, 5; W Q10, Q31]

**11 — B.** (A) Beas refusal followed rather than preceded the highland stages; (B) mountain valleys and river corridors made travel a staged, contested process; (C) Indus crossings and regional resistance mattered; (D) lower Indus return came after the Taxila encounter. Trap: single pass-name shorthand deletes geography. [B §§10, 15; W Q11]

**12 — D.** (A) neither site is fixed by independent Indian inscription; (B) they cannot be conflated into one secure fort identification; (C) Nysa is not a Persian tribute district; (D) Aornos as besieged stronghold, Nysa as Greek Dionysian mythic landscape; location caution applies to both. Trap: mythology is not equivalent to archaeological proof or disproof. [B §§11, 16; T Q22–23]

**13 — C.** (A) early Bhir occupation cannot backdate Sirkap's later grid; (B) the later Sirsukh phase does not backdate Sirkap; (C) later Sirkap grid cannot be dated to Alexander simply because he visited Taxila; (D) Hydaspes does not give the date of Sirkap's urban layout. Trap: one city-name covers stratified, distinct settlements. [B §§10, 20; T Q14, Q52]

**14 — A.** (A) different regional actors cooperated or resisted; (B) Porus opposed rather than endorsed Alexander's advance; (C) highland communities also resisted; (D) Ambhi's alliance could not bind every Punjab community. Trap: one ally is not a measure of universal acquiescence. [B §§10, 16; W Q12, Q44]

**15 — B.** (A) reverses battle and refusal; (B) Hydaspes=Jhelum against Porus, Hyphasis=Beas refusal; (C) Beas refusal is not the Indus descent; (D) Hydaspes was Jhelum, not Beas. Trap: examine place/event rather than guessing from Greek spelling. [B §15; W Q3]

**16 — D.** (A) elephant effects were complex, not proof of Porus's decisive battlefield success; (B) Greek numerical claims require independent caution; (C) tactical detail cannot establish exact casualty figures; (D) tactical reconstruction should be separated from uncertain quantitative detail. Trap: source-rich battle ≠ exact casualty audit. [B §§16, 18; W Q13–14]

**17 — A.** (A) reported Greek battlefield win followed by local political settlement is compatible; (B) reinstatement does not invert the claimed Greek battlefield result; (C) Porus was restored/enlarged; (D) the Greek accounts describe both battle and settlement. Trap: outcome of battle and shape of rule are distinct. [B §§1, 16, 19; W Q15, Q45]

**18 — C.** (A) Sangala was distinct from the Hydaspes battle; (B) highland siege episodes predate the Taxila reception; (C) Sangala is a distinct resistance episode after Porus in the Punjab campaign; (D) the army halted at Hyphasis/Beas, not Sangala. Trap: attention to Porus alone hides other regional communities. [B §§10, 15–16; T Q30]

**19 — C.** (A) Nanda counts are non-audited Greek reports, not sole cause; (B) monsoon alone does not explain prolonged fatigue/logistics; (C) repeated campaigning and season/logistical limits were interrelated; (D) a battle victory does not cancel troop exhaustion or supply costs. Trap: no single-cause Beas refusal. [B §17; W Q17–18]

**20 — B.** (A) no verbatim independent Indian participant transcript survives; (B) Coenus's reported argument shows later representation of troop morale, with speech wording uncertain; (C) rhetoric does not authenticate numerical eastern army reports; (D) no unchanged official vote record is preserved. Trap: later constructed orations should not be quoted as minutes. [B §§11, 17–18; T Q34]

**21 — A.** (A) serious wound during Indus descent indicates ongoing conflict; (B) refusal at Beas did not end fighting on the return; (C) Porus's arrangement did not bind all communities; (D) lower-Indus descent brought further resistance. Trap: refused eastern advance ≠ safe retreat. [B §§15–16; W Q19]

**22 — B.** (A) the fleet sailed on the return, not toward the Beas; (B) sea route under Nearchus and Gedrosian land difficulty belong to return; (C) Nearchus departed from the lower Indus rather than Taxila; (D) Alexander did not march to Magadha during the return. Trap: separate fleet/land strands after Patala. [B §§10, 15; W Q20–21, Q36]

**23 — C.** (A) none wrote a contemporaneous participant account; (B) genres differ between biography, military writing and geography; (C) Arrian's later military synthesis, Plutarch's biography and Strabo's critical geography have different functions; (D) none is a contemporary royal/satrapal archive. Trap: don't flatten classical source genres. [B §§11, 18; T Q18–21]

**24 — B.** (A) speeches in later sources cannot replace an indigenous archive; (B) compare and qualify genre-specific accounts and independent material/topography; (C) silence is not universal refutation; (D) biography does not give a complete audited casualty list. Trap: neither Greek triumphalism nor blanket rejection. [B §§11, 18; W Q38]

**25 — A.** (A) north-western alliances, limited satrapies and post-323 fragmentation contradict colour-wash maps; (B) Alexander never directly ruled the Ganga east; (C) Porus's authority was local, not all-subcontinent; (D) earlier Persian claims also had changing reach. Trap: passage is not province-wide rule. [B §§19, 21; A §1; W Q25–26]

**26 — D.** (A) Ambhi and Porus were retained local rulers, not two Macedonian officers; (B) Porus was a local king and Nearchus led the return fleet, not three uniform satraps; (C) Nearchus/Aristobulus/Ptolemy belong to Alexander's world rather than Persian governorships; (D) Philip/Peithon/Eudemus are associated with different changing frontier commands. Trap: named commanders ≠ one stable satrapy. [B §§18–19]

**27 — C.** (A) successor competition undermines uncontested command; (B) Achaemenid rule was not simply restored intact; (C) shifting governors and successor struggles limited continued control and enabled local agency; (D) local rulers could act amid fragmented imperial authority. Trap: political vacancy is not a guaranteed Mauryan “automatic succession.” [B §§19–20; W Q26–27]

**28 — B.** (A) names do not guarantee continuous Greek sovereignty; (B) settlement-name claims and later Indo-Greek state/coin formations belong to different phases; (C) later kingdoms were not simply the same administration; (D) a city name cannot prove continuous ethnic occupancy. Trap: diachronic contact ≠ uninterrupted imperial institution. [B §§19–20, 22; A §1; W Q30, Q46]

**29 — D.** (A) Alexander had died before the later agreement; (B) Gandhara art cannot be dated by a diplomatic event alone; (C) no evidence that Seleucid claims precisely copy Darius's lists; (D) later Seleucid diplomacy is relevant as boundary only. Trap: stop before Topic 14 Mauryan negotiations. [B §§9, 19; W Q40]

**30 — C.** (A) Indo-Greek issues belong to later trajectories; (B) Gandharan sculpture cannot be backdated to the campaign; (C) Indian coinage and Gandharan art have diverse regional/time pathways and are not single royal gifts; (D) Persian and Hellenistic contributions were different in time and medium. Trap: “influence” must specify medium, chronology and adaptation. [B §§14, 20, 22; A §§1, 4; W Q30, Q48]

**31 — C.** (A) Alexander's temporary local arrangements were not equally durable; (B) Persian expansion preceded Macedonian invasion; (C) Achaemenid incorporation was longer and Macedonian arrangements shorter; (D) reverses the duration comparison. Trap: a comparative duration claim isn't a map of uniform Iranian control. [B §§9, 21; A §§1–2; W Q32]

**32 — B.** (A) coin and city name do not independently fix an entire territory; (B) context, chronology and settlement/campaign control must each be evidenced; (C) a name does not guarantee continued successor rule; (D) both evidence classes can help if bounded. Trap: different objects answer different questions. [B §§11, 19–20; W Q16, Q46–47]

**33 — D.** (A) successor events did not make expansion inevitable; (B) Mauryan institutions did not simply inherit every satrapal post; (C) the frontier episode alone cannot unify Magadha; (D) frontier changes facilitated some later openings without causing unification by themselves. Trap: indirect condition ≠ sufficient cause. [B §§19–21; W Q27]

**34 — B.** (A) complete independent autographs do not survive; (B) both are linked with participant/near-participant reporting preserved via later texts; (C) neither is available only as an identical single Arrian account; (D) participant proximity does not audit all troop figures. Trap: participant proximity and surviving text's transmission are separate. [B §§11, 18; W Q21–22]

**35 — C.** (A) names alone do not fix a year of founding; (B) settlements do not establish uninterrupted sovereignty; (C) Greek naming indicates some military-settlement intention but durability needs archaeology; (D) later polities need their own evidence of occupation. Trap: a founder story is not uninterrupted urban occupation. [B §§19–20; A §1; W Q46]

**36 — B.** (A) later Greek sources do not prove uniform long-term rule; (B) inscriptions and later Greek/Roman texts are distinct evidence for bounded, variable local rule; (C) Achaemenid royal texts cannot independently date all Greek battle accounts; (D) later Seleucid claims do not map Alexander's collection in each town. Trap: contrast bounded control and long-run connections. [B §§11, 21–22; A §§1–5; W Q32]

**37 — C.** (A) the complete contemporaneous log is not available; (B) Nearchus belongs to Alexander's later return; (C) Herodotus's report associates Scylax with Darius's Indus reconnaissance; (D) no royal inscription provides Scylax's full itinerary. Trap: indirect literary report is evidence, not a signed expedition journal. [B §12.2; W Q5; T Q7]

**38 — A.** (A) Diodorus, Curtius Rufus, Justin's epitome and Arrian preserve different later versions of campaign material; (B) they were not expedition eyewitnesses; (C) their traditions cannot all be reduced to only Ptolemy's work; (D) they are later writers, not battle eyewitnesses. Trap: more surviving narratives do not mean more independent eyewitnesses. [B §§11, 18; A §1; W Q23, Q37; T Q17–21]
