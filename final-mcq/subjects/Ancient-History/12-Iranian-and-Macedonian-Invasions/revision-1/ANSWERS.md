**1 — B.** (A) Darius was the Achaemenid monarch, not a Macedonian officer; (B) separates Iranian Achaemenid and Macedonian campaigns; (C) Alexander displaced the Achaemenids rather than joining them; (D) Darius was Iranian and Alexander preceded the later Indo-Greek kingdoms. Trap: distinguish ruler, dynasty and later regional succession. [B §§9, 21; W Q32]

**2 — D.** (A) the lists do not delimit a single eastern province; (B) names do not verify taxation in each town; (C) eastern lands are named, so dismissing the claims is wrong; (D) inscriptional inclusion attests claims, not uniform on-site administration. Trap: royal land list ≠ satrapal register. [B §§11–13; W Q1, Q41–42]

**3 — A.** (A) Behistun gives Gandhara; later Darius lists also name Hindush; (B) reverses the textual order: Gandhara is already named at Behistun; (C) Herodotus's tribute grouping/number is not a Behistun inscription; (D) neither list surveys a satrapal frontier. Trap: distinguish named land, tribute district and mapped province. [B §12.2; W Q5, Q35]

**4 — C.** (A) Herodotus does not date and map that exact annexation; (B) Behistun itself names Gandhara; (C) annexation year and extent are reconstructed, not a complete contemporary border survey; (D) Behistun gives no precise day or surveyed eastern boundary. Trap: R.S. Sharma's 516 BCE and Advanced shorthand require Basic-owner qualification. [B §§1, 12–13; A mini-timeline; W Q2]

**5 — C.** (A) no such securely bounded province is attested by Cyrus's lists; (B) Darius's later lists cannot establish an earlier Cyrus takeover; (C) later Greek associations are possible antecedents, not secure Indian annexation; (D) no documented Cyrus annexation of the eastern Ganga basin. Trap: distinguish possible frontier contact from Darius's inscriptional claims. [B §12.1; T Q4]

**6 — B.** (A) neither an audited account nor identical with the royal Hindush boundary; (B) Herodotus reports Indian wealth and tribute within a Greek literary framing; (C) no per-capita count or known universal rate follows; (D) the number comes through Greek narrative, not an independent royal tax inscription. Trap: twentieth district and Hindush need not have identical borders. [B §§11, 13.2; W Q6, Q42]

**7 — D.** (A) levies may have involved local elites; (B) troops do not settle district boundaries; (C) troop participation need not mean a uniform standing army; (D) Herodotus associates Indian/Gandharan contingents with Xerxes. Trap: representation of troops does not prove uniform military administration. [B §§12.3, 13.3; W Q7]

**8 — A.** (A) combines satrapal administration with indirect local links and uneven control; (B) ignores the likely role of local intermediaries; (C) omits satrapal/imperial oversight attested as part of eastern networks; (D) turns a royal claim into proof of equally intensive administration everywhere. Trap: Persian subject land ≠ fixed fiscal province. [B §§11, 13.1–13.2; W Q8]

**9 — B.** (A) a complete first-person Scylax log or border survey is unavailable; (B) Herodotus recounts an Indus-to-sea reconnaissance commissioned by Darius; (C) Nearchus belongs to Alexander's later return, not the same Darius-era fleet; (D) the report does not itemise tolls for Indus ports. Trap: Herodotus reports the journey; no independent expedition log survives. [B §12.2; T Q7]

**10 — C.** (A) direction and Greek-only descent are unsupported; (B) north-western Kharoshthi is not left-to-right; (C) right-to-left north-western Kharoshthi has a structural Aramaic link; (D) its distribution was not subcontinent-wide and was not an unchanged Aramaic duplicate. Trap: geography, direction and transmission are distinct. [B §§1, 14; W Q9, Q43]

**11 — A.** (A) Aramaic use supplies a plausible scribal transmission context; (B) local Kharoshthi adaptation rules out asserting complete identity; (C) contact cannot date each later inscription precisely; (D) imperial Aramaic was relevant to the north-west, not confined to the Ganga. Trap: structural relationship is stronger than a total-identity or exact-date claim. [B §14; W Q9, Q43]

**12 — D.** (A) a mixed find cannot show one prototype for every Indian series; (B) no evidence assigns all bent-bar pieces to one Darius-era mint; (C) hoards have deposition and circulation histories distinct from each coin's origin; (D) Chaman-i Hazouri/Bhir-type mixed contexts support circulation and interactions. Trap: comparison ≠ single origin. [B §§11, 14; W Q33; T Q8]

**13 — B.** (A) lexical/form similarities do not reconstruct all Mauryan offices; (B) *dipi/lipi* and capital forms suggest selective adaptation with later chronology; (C) no single attested Persian workshop is responsible for every capital; (D) shared vocabulary does not prove identical fiscal administration. Trap: influence requires mechanism and dates, not visual similarity alone. [B §§1, 14; A §§1, 5; W Q10, Q31]

**14 — C.** (A) Taxila precedes the Hydaspes battle; (B) Aornos/highland fighting precedes the Taxila reception; (C) broadly correct approach via northwest highlands to Indus/Taxila and Jhelum; (D) Hindu Kush crossing precedes arrival at Taxila. Trap: “Khyber” alone obscures staged routes. [B §§10, 15; W Q11]

**15 — A.** (A) the siege is narrated but Pir-Sar identification debated; (B) proposed identification is not proven beyond doubt; (C) Herodotus does not map Alexander's Aornos siege; (D) a precise surviving plan is not attested. Trap: reported siege and identification confidence are separate. [B §§15–16; W Q34; T Q23]

**16 — D.** (A) no evidence identifies an Achaemenid governor ordering Ambhi's choice; (B) Porus was Ambhi's rival, but did not compel Ambhi's submission; (C) highland communities also resisted; (D) Ambhi's cooperation reflects local diplomacy and rivalries. Trap: agency includes choosing alliance as well as resistance. [B §§10, 16; W Q12, Q44]

**17 — D.** (A) swaps the two rivers; (B) Chenab/Sutlej are not these identifications; (C) Ravi/Indus are different rivers; (D) Hydaspes=Jhelum; Hyphasis=Beas. Trap: Hydaspes battle precedes Hyphasis refusal. [B §§15–17; W Q3]

**18 — B.** (A) Porus was retained/enlarged, not immediately replaced by annexation; (B) Greek battlefield success plus negotiated local rule is supported, with casualty caution; (C) the Greek sources claim Macedonian, not Porus, battlefield success; (D) no immediate full replacement by a satrap followed. Trap: do not invert tactical result merely because the ruler was restored. [B §§1, 16; W Q15, Q45]

**19 — A.** (A) restored local authority could mediate control without full direct occupation; (B) retention of rulers and the appointment of satraps coexisted; (C) restoration does not independently establish Porus's battlefield victory; (D) Porus's local authority does not equal direct Macedonian control over every Punjab state. Trap: battle and settlement answer different questions. [B §§16, 19; W Q15, Q25]

**20 — D.** (A) flood and distance raised costs, not strategic ease; (B) cumulative campaigning mattered alongside, not only, the Porus battle; (C) later reported Nanda figures are not an audited sole cause; (D) accumulated costs and morale explain the Hyphasis refusal. Trap: multicausal halt rather than “one frightening army.” [B §§15, 17; W Q17–18]

**21 — B.** (A) Hydaspes battle itself preceded the later Indus descent; (B) Malloi resistance during the descent seriously wounded Alexander; (C) Ambhi's alliance preceded Hydaspes; (D) highland Aornos siege preceded it too. Trap: post-Hydaspes did not mean pacified Punjab/Indus. [B §§15–16; W Q19]

**22 — A.** (A) correctly assigns halt/delta/desert-return stages; (B) Gedrosia lies on the return, not the initial approach; (C) Gedrosia was a return route rather than the eastward limit; (D) Hyphasis was the eastward limit, while Patala was the delta. Trap: distinguish Punjab halt, lower-Indus turn and overland return. [B §§10, 15; W Q20, Q36]

**23 — A.** (A) Nearchus's lost participant voyage material informs later narratives, notably Arrian; (B) no complete independent autograph of his log survives; (C) Nearchus's associated voyage was maritime, not Alexander's Gedrosian land march; (D) Scylax belongs to an earlier Darius-associated reconnaissance. Trap: participant account survives through later selection, not as complete autograph. [B §§15, 18; W Q21]

**24 — D.** (A) the participant works are not preserved intact inside Arrian; (B) he did not reproduce an independent Indian tally confirming Greek numbers; (C) Arrian used earlier traditions, including participant accounts; (D) later synthesis draws on Ptolemy/Aristobulus-type earlier accounts. Trap: access to participant traditions ≠ eyewitness status. [B §§11, 18; A §1; W Q23, Q37]

**25 — B.** (A) the wording is not guaranteed by near-contemporary Greek agreement; (B) biography shapes reported dialogues and attitudes; (C) a dialogue is not an archival boundary description; (D) Plutarch wrote later, not as an eyewitness. Trap: use Plutarch for portrayal, not verbatim quotation. [B §§11, 18; W Q24]

**26 — C.** (A) the absence of a matching Indian text cannot establish exact Greek speech wording; (B) textual asymmetry does not establish that all resistance was invented; (C) source asymmetry needs critical comparison; (D) material evidence remains relevant alongside Greek narratives. Trap: absence of an Indian mirror text ≠ non-event or unbiased Greek version. [B §§11, 18; W Q38]

**27 — A.** (A) Alexander combined local rulers with officers/garrisons and some settlements; (B) retained rulers did not exclude garrisons everywhere; (C) local kings, including Porus, were not all dismissed; (D) settlements did not replace all kings and officers. Trap: *satrap* or city name does not mean uniform sovereignty. [B §§16, 19; W Q25]

**28 — C.** (A) satraps were appointed after Hydaspes and the conquest; (B) succession disputes prevented a single stable north-west command; (C) succession conflict/limited control enabled local reassertion; (D) Porus's local authority did not mean command of Macedonian garrisons. Trap: fragility was political. [B §§1, 19; W Q26]

**29 — B.** (A) Rajagriha/Pataliputra are Magadhan capitals; (B) Boukephala/Nikaia are campaign-linked Greek settlement names, not proof of durable empire; (C) Ujjayini/Mahishmati are Avanti centres; (D) Kaushambi/Vaishali are other Indian urban centres. Trap: a founding claim must be separated from a lasting urban polity. [B §20; A §§1, 4; W Q46]

**30 — B.** (A) Alexander could not have personally directed a subsequent Seleucid negotiation; (B) Seleucus belongs after Alexander and fuller settlement belongs Topic 14; (C) later agreement cannot establish uninterrupted Alexander-era sovereignty; (D) neither the negotiations nor the Mauryan state was Alexander's satrapy. Trap: successor kingdom ≠ Alexander's immediate satrapy. [B §§9, 19; W Q40]

**31 — A.** (A) later Indo-Greek and Gandhara phenomena have later political and workshop contexts; (B) these are not immediate products of Alexander's temporary presence; (C) they cannot be wholly backdated to the pre-Alexander Persian phase; (D) connections through time do not prove uninterrupted rule. Trap: contact history is longer than the 326 BCE campaign. [B §§20, 22; A §§1, 4; W Q30, Q48]

**32 — C.** (A) Alexander's local-king arrangements and brief garrisons differ from Achaemenid satrapal networks; (B) Persian presence was longer than Alexander's; (C) Persian eastern incorporation was longer-lived than Alexander's transient arrangements; (D) reverses the two modes of contact and misattributes Hindush taxation. Trap: sources and durations differ even though both affected northwest networks. [B §§9, 21; A §§1–5; W Q32]

**33 — B.** (A) myth does not fix an excavated site; (B) Nysa–Dionysus is Greek landscape interpretation with uncertain location/historicity; (C) an associated myth alone is not proof of local cult practice; (D) Nysa and Aornos need independent topographic identifications. Trap: a mythic association need not prove the entire settlement imaginary either. [B §§11, 16; T Q22]

**34 — A.** (A) victory iconography is a possible interpretive source under attribution caution; (B) an image cannot count a whole battle's troops; (C) dating/attribution debated; (D) pictorial imagery cannot establish the tactical crossing on its own. Trap: imagery is not a complete battle narrative. [B §§11, 16; W Q16, Q47; T Q28]

**35 — A.** (A) Bhir is earlier, Sirkap later Indo-Greek, Sirsukh Kushana in heritage periodization; (B) Sirkap's later grid is not Alexander's; (C) Bhir precedes Indo-Greek Sirkap; (D) reverses Sirkap and Sirsukh. Trap: Alexander at Taxila does not mean he founded later Sirkap. [B §§10, 20; T Q14, Q52]

**36 — C.** (A) Ambhi's alliance did not bind independent communities; (B) routes predated the campaigns; (C) Ambhi, Porus and Malloi demonstrate plural responses; connections outlasted shallow control; (D) later place names do not establish continuous state control. Trap: neither universal conquest nor “no impact.” [B §§10, 19–21; A §§1–5; W Q32, Q44]

**37 — D.** (A) earlier participant traditions are mostly lost, not five surviving accounts; (B) only Arrian is a surviving later writer among four earlier participant traditions; (C) Plutarch and Curtius are later but Ptolemy, Nearchus and Cleitarchus are earlier/lost traditions; (D) five surviving late Graeco-Roman narrative authors. Trap: the original participant sources behind later syntheses have largely disappeared. [B §§11, 18; A §§1, 4; W Q23–24, Q37; T Q17–21]
