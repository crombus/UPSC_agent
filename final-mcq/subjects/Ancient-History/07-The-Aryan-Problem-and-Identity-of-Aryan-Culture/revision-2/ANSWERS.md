**1 — B.** (A) Comparing independently dated sources is legitimate. (B) Pottery alone provides neither speech nor ancestry. (C) Separating mobility and speech is sound. (D) Burial context can date a sample without decoding its speech. **Trap:** co-location is not an equation. [B §§1, 7, 11; W Q1, Q23]

**2 — D.** (A) Alliance and ritual boundaries may shift. (B) Rivalry may be politically significant. (C) Speaker and genre affect labels. (D) Neither term names a stable biological descent group. **Trap:** contextual terms are not genetic markers. [B §§2, 8; W Q33]

**3 — A.** (A) Indo-Aryan is nested within Indo-Iranian and Indo-European. (B) Iranian is a sister branch, not Indo-Aryan's parent. (C) Indo-Iranian is nested within Indo-European, not the reverse. (D) Indo-Aryan is within Indo-Iranian, not its parent. **Trap:** classification is not material ethnicity. [B §4; W Q5]

**4 — C.** (A) Elite technical language may be restricted, but that does not directly address dating every hymn. (B) Oral layering complicates verse dates; the decisive error is using Mitanni as a universal clock. (C) External forms cannot date every oral textual layer. (D) Attestation dates the external record, not each parallel verse. **Trap:** language attestation ≠ composition date. [B §§5, 8; W Q6, Q35]

**5 — C.** (A) Movement need not homogenise ancestry across destinations. (B) Prestige can shift speech without rapidly replacing a community's inherited ancestry. (C) Bilingual prestige may spread speech in communities also receiving newcomers, without uniform genetic replacement. (D) Borrowed words do not directly imply community-wide language replacement. **Trap:** language shift need not equal replacement. [B §§1, 16; W Q30, Q39]

**6 — A.** (A) Systematic philology is independent of biological rankings. (B) An unmixed native race repeats essentialism. (C) Skull measurements cannot identify speech. (D) Colonial misuse does not invalidate the comparative method. **Trap:** critique misuse, not evidence itself. [B §§4, 6, 17; W Q3, Q40]

**7 — D.** (A) A river mention does not date its channel. (B) Placement cannot fix all family-book composition dates. (C) River-names do not identify speech of burial users. (D) North-western textual clues have broad value when layered transmission is considered. **Trap:** family-book antiquity is relative. [B §§8–9; W Q35]

**8 — B.** (A) Changing references do not fix settlement range for every layer. (B) Textual focus changes without recording an itinerary. (C) An exact route for each community needs independent evidence. (D) Texts cannot date every north-western site abandonment. **Trap:** textual focus is not a route map. [B §§8–9; A §4; W Q9]

**9 — A.** (A) Without independent dates the proposed river cannot be linked to that occupation and passage. (B) Palaeohydrology can investigate channel history. (C) Archaeology can date occupations. (D) Layered poetry may preserve older memories without establishing contemporaneity. **Trap:** geography alone is not synchrony. [B §§7, 9; W Q36]

**10 — C.** (A) Local continuity and weakened integration are observable. (B) Sites can change differently. (C) Conquest causing contraction at most cities requires broad supporting destruction evidence absent here. (D) Later contact remains plausible. **Trap:** reject mass assault, not all migration. [B §§10, 14; A §1; W Q17]

**11 — B.** (A) Objects cannot establish unchanged speech. (B) Material ties persist alongside changed funerary forms in the stipulated context. (C) A changed rite cannot establish total replacement. (D) Ritual identity cannot be assumed unchanged from select continued artefacts. **Trap:** Cemetery H is an archaeological horizon. [B §11; W Q18]

**12 — D.** (A) Deposits do not document spoken language change. (B) PGW is later than the Mature urban phase. (C) Pottery overlap does not prove biological identity. (D) Local material succession can be investigated archaeologically, without assigning the users' language. **Trap:** overlap ≠ a Vedic identity tag. [B §11; W Mains 4]

**13 — D.** (A) Grave goods can be compared but their resemblance does not establish an assault. (B) Some mobility is possible, yet burial change does not date a single destructive wave. (C) Contact is a plausible comparative angle, not a record of a subcontinent-wide assault. (D) Differing mortuary forms across phases undercut the one-short-event claim. **Trap:** a corridor is not a marching path. [B §11; W Q19]

**14 — B.** (A) Craft style does not establish speech. (B) Oasis interactions with mobile networks can be investigated. (C) An archaeological complex does not name a single descent group. (D) Interaction does not require permanent steppe settlement. **Trap:** BMAC is archaeology, not a language. [B §11; W Q22]

**15 — C.** (A) An undated attribution cannot supply context. (B) Similar forms elsewhere do not secure this object's association with the OCP find. (C) Secure provenance is the missing first control; even secure dating cannot identify a Vedic clan without independent evidence. (D) A metal reference cannot assign a specific unstratified hoard to one clan. **Trap:** unstratified hoards cannot date a speech community. [B §11; W Q20]

**16 — A.** (A) PGW belongs chiefly to a later comparison and cannot label early hymn-makers. (B) Partial overlap at some sites does not make PGW an early Rigvedic identity marker. (C) Distributional variation also does not identify speech. (D) Mixed assemblages require local dating, not ethnic assumptions. **Trap:** do not retroject later material into early hymns. [B §11; W Q21]

**17 — D.** (A) Wheel construction is directly observable. (B) Context can be dated archaeologically within uncertainties. (C) Vedic's linguistic classification rests on separate language evidence. (D) Attribution of vehicle users' speech is inferential. **Trap:** spoked wheels do not talk. [B §§4, 11–12; W Q15]

**18 — B.** (A) Mortuary context does not determine spoke construction. (B) Solid wheel construction directly undermines the precise light-spoked claim. (C) Associated OCP ceramics do not establish wheel form. (D) Chariot vocabulary cannot establish the construction of this find. **Trap:** compare form, date and draught. [B §12; W Q16]

**19 — A.** (A) Species identification needs checking before claims about regional significance; archaeological phase remains a separate control. (B) A contested local fragment cannot represent all Harappan sites. (C) It cannot establish the horse's economic centrality. (D) Species, even if secured, does not determine speech. **Trap:** a debated bone is not a population marker. [B §12; W Q14]

**20 — C.** (A) Later admixture cannot be excluded by this older sample. (B) DNA does not record a specified language. (C) The published result constrains one individual. (D) One sample cannot represent all contemporary settlements. **Trap:** Rakhigarhi ≠ every Harappan. [B §13; A §1; W Q27]

**21 — B.** (A) Genetic affinities are within DNA's remit. (B) Ancestry patterns cannot independently disclose the language of each ancient migrant. (C) Dated samples may constrain approximate chronology. (D) Mixture may coexist with local ancestry. **Trap:** “Steppe-related” names affinity, not a language. [B §13; A §1; W Q28]

**22 — D.** (A) Shared affinity alone cannot identify recent migrants; that wording wrongly makes them necessary. (B) An ancestry model cannot give this person's immediate birthplace. (C) Affinity does not date a farmer colony. (D) A deep relationship across reference populations is not by itself evidence of recent farmer migration. **Trap:** distinguish related from recently migrated. [B §13; A §1; W Q37]

**23 — C.** (A) Local burials alone cannot establish a homeland for the whole branch. (B) Continued pottery does not explain external language forms. (C) A full outward dispersal model must explain Indo-Iranian branching and Mitanni attestations. (D) Crop continuity is compatible with multiple language histories. **Trap:** local continuity cannot settle language homeland. [B §15; W Q25]

**24 — A.** (A) Ancient evidence cannot certify exclusive modern ownership. (B) Wheel dating does not reveal users' speech. (C) One genome does not represent every social group. (D) Pottery continuity cannot establish unchanged modern community names. **Trap:** ancient evidence is not a presentist ownership deed. [B §§1, 17; W Q40]

**25 — D.** (A) Broad similarity cannot assign all proposed loans to one donor. (B) Retroflexion alone cannot identify a migrant tongue. (C) Modern pronunciations do not directly date ancient loans. (D) Test each proposed loan's comparative forms and plausible borrowing path. **Trap:** retroflexion does not name a donor. [B §16; W Q39]

**26 — B.** (A) Later place-name resemblance supplies no local mechanism. (B) Dated stress, plausible response and linked occupation support—but do not prove—causation. (C) Climate alone cannot demonstrate a settlement response. (D) A conflict account outside the relevant zone/time cannot establish the proposed local cause. **Trap:** climate does not identify speech. [B §§7, 14; W Q8, Q29]

**27 — C.** (A) Simultaneity alone does not prove a shared cause. (B) Co-located finds cannot automatically identify speech. (C) First test temporal and social-scale compatibility, then geography and source independence before inferring convergence. (D) Discordant datasets cannot simply be subordinated to the text. **Trap:** triangulation is not forced agreement. [B §7; W Q8, Q32]

**28 — B.** (A) Harvest does not prove herding was replaced. (B) Pastoral values and cultivation can coexist. (C) One harvest term cannot date a wholly agrarian phase. (D) Poetic animal imagery is not a census of all diets. **Trap:** avoid a nomad-versus-farmer binary. [B §8; W Q11]

**29 — D.** (A) Later iron meanings cannot simply be imposed on early *ayas*. (B) Later PGW contexts do not determine lexical meaning in every verse. (C) One metal mention does not date all books. (D) First examine the term's meaning and the verse's textual layer before drawing chronological conclusions. **Trap:** early *ayas* is not automatically iron. [B §§8, 11; W Q12]

**30 — A.** (A) Language shift through mobility or bilingualism can coexist with local settlement continuities. (B) Continued pottery does not freeze speech. (C) Shift need not require population replacement. (D) Contact features do not require one mass migration. **Trap:** settlement continuity does not prohibit bilingualism. [B §§10, 14, 16; W Q24, Q30]

**31 — A.** (A) Mature integration precedes Late localisation, mid-second-millennium Mitanni forms and later PGW; the treaty records no Harappan assault. (B) Mitanni attestation does not precede the onset of Late Harappan localisation. (C) Late localisation follows Mature urban integration. (D) The PGW comparison is later than the Mitanni external attestation. **Trap:** relative broad horizons are not exact synchronies; an external treaty is no conquest report. [B §§5, 10–11; A mini-timeline; W Q6, Q17]

**32 — D.** (A) Shared terms do not guarantee identical later meanings. (B) Cognates do not locate a rite's origin on their own. (C) A cognate cannot date individual verses. (D) In systematic comparison the forms support linguistic inheritance without identical societies. **Trap:** shared speech history ≠ identical ritual practice. [B §4; W Q34]

**33 — C.** (A) Conflict may leave no burn layer; this alone does not justify a subcontinent-wide conclusion. (B) A qualified local negative is reasonable but cannot generalise automatically. (C) A representative regional survey with expected detectability calibrates the negative inference. (D) A local absence cannot simply be projected across a region. **Trap:** absence has a sampling denominator. [B §§7, 10, 14; W Q29]
