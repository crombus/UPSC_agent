**1 — D.** (A) A sampled genome cannot profile every user of a pottery type, despite valid material and poetic observations. (B) Pottery cannot date a separate oral hymn merely because both are displayed together. (C) Hymns cannot identify the speech of particular makers of an excavated vessel. (D) Pots inform material custom, DNA modelled ancestry and hymns their poets' textual world; no category automatically identifies another. **Trap:** the four options have the same three-clause shape, so test each asserted source-to-claim link. [B §§1, 7; W Q7–8]

**2 — B.** (A) Speaker/opponent context matters. (B) *Dasyu* and *ārya* are not stable biological descent groups. (C) Political and ritual boundaries are reasonable interpretive possibilities. (D) Genre-bound clues retain historical value. **Trap:** neither word is a race marker. [B §§2, 8; W Q33]

**3 — C.** (A) Shared ritual terms alone may be borrowed. (B) Similar translated names are insufficient to distinguish inheritance from borrowing. (C) Repeated sound patterns across related grammatical forms support inherited descent. (D) Neighbouring trade vocabulary can transfer between unrelated languages. **Trap:** borrowing versus inherited pattern. [B §4; W Q4]

**4 — A.** (A) Indo-Aryan and Indo-Iranian are nested linguistic branches within Indo-European, not genes or material types. (B) Indo-Aryan is not a gene pool and Indo-Iranian is broader than one biological group. (C) Indo-Iranian is not an ancestry component and pots do not identify Indo-Aryan speech. (D) Neither text labels nor excavated speakers' speech can be assigned by that pairing. **Trap:** BMAC ≠ Indo-Iranian speech. [B §§1, 4, 11; W Q5]

**5 — A.** (A) Treaty names attest bounded Indo-Aryan-related forms beyond South Asia. (B) Court vocabulary is no census of ordinary speech. (C) External attestation cannot date the family books. (D) It does not trace a route into South Asia. **Trap:** Mitanni evidence is not an invasion itinerary. [B §5; W Q6]

**6 — C.** (A) Neither approach rests on independently dated burials. (B) Neither is distinguished by rigorous sound-law comparison. (C) Opposite political stories can share essentialist race method. (D) The claims differ in direction and establish no common dated route. **Trap:** replacing one pure race with another is not source criticism. [B §§6, 17; W Q40]

**7 — B.** (A) Ritual-poetic references do not represent a census of ordinary life. (B) Layered verses provide qualified cultural clues. (C) Verse imagery cannot date each river or archaeological vehicle find. (D) Prominent imagery does not prove uniform livelihoods. **Trap:** texts are valuable without being annals. [B §8; W Q9]

**8 — D.** (A) Early north-western clusters are documented in the text. (B) Later Kuru-Panchala focus is identifiable. (C) River references have broad, qualified geographical value. (D) Neither focus supplies the precise itinerary behind the changing textual emphasis. **Trap:** textual layers ≠ field diaries. [B §§8–9; A §§1, 4; W Q35]

**9 — C.** (A) Proximity cannot date composition of the passage. (B) Hydrological history does not establish when riverside sites became Vedic. (C) Independently establish channel, occupation and textual chronologies before correlating them. (D) A disputed river match cannot establish settlers' speech. **Trap:** matching locations is not dating a text. [B §9; W Q36]

**10 — A.** (A) Material continuity does not determine whether later entrants interacted with local populations. (B) Some local practice can continue. (C) Sites can follow varied paths. (D) Urban integration can recede. **Trap:** continuity and movement can coexist. [B §§10, 14; W Q17–18]

**11 — D.** (A) Rites can change despite material continuity. (B) Stratigraphy helps place phases in relative time. (C) Continued local finds warrant comparison. (D) Mortuary change alone cannot assign incoming speech. **Trap:** Cemetery H is not an ethnic name-tag. [B §11; W Q18]

**12 — B.** (A) Material change cannot document a dated language transition. (B) Local succession or overlap of assemblages can be investigated. (C) Pots alone cannot establish an ethnic boundary. (D) Deposits do not date composition of individual oral hymns. **Trap:** Bhagwanpura gives context, not translation. [B §§8, 11; W Mains 4]

**13 — B.** (A) Steppe contacts cannot independently establish common speech across oasis sites. (B) Excavated assemblages cannot identify residents' speech directly. (C) Shared material styles do not establish an incoming linguistic community. (D) Mobile interaction does not mean oasis residents adopted pastoralists' speech. **Trap:** do not turn a corridor node into a single ethnic caravan. [B §11; W Q22]

**14 — A.** (A) Diverse burial phases fit changing frontier interaction. (B) Variation makes one uniformly transmitted style and cohort insecure. (C) Chronological spread contradicts a short phase shared by most cemetery horizons. (D) Related grave goods cannot establish shared speech. **Trap:** broad chronology defeats one-wave certainty. [B §11; W Q19]

**15 — D.** (A) Partial archaeological associations can be studied, but do not identify speech. (B) PGW is a useful later comparison, not an ethnic marker. (C) Shared crafts can cross linguistic boundaries. (D) Varying dates and contexts cannot alone establish a common language. **Trap:** OCP/hoards and later PGW must not be compressed into one people. [B §11; W Q20–21]

**16 — C.** (A) Wheel construction and draught evidence do not establish the same vehicle design. (B) Reverses the attested wheel distinction. (C) Early Sintashta spoked-wheel technology differs from Sinauli solid-wheel vehicles. (D) Technical similarities cannot identify identical users. **Trap:** horse/chariot imagery is no vehicle typology. [B §§11–12; W Q15–16]

**17 — A.** (A) Neither horse draught nor Vedic identification follows from the excavated vehicles alone. (B) Wheel form is observable. (C) Burial context can inform chronology. (D) Both draught and linguistic links require separate evidence. **Trap:** “vehicle” does not mean Vedic horse-drawn war chariot. [B §12; W Q16]

**18 — D.** (A) Comparative analysis is needed to establish species. (B) Horse imagery in hymns is independently observable. (C) The find's phase needs checking. (D) One disputed equid cannot identify a widespread horse-based Harappan economy or Vedic-speaking owners. **Trap:** local bone, general livelihood and textual identity are three claims. [B §12; W Q13–14]

**19 — C.** (A) The one dated genome is informative within its sampling limits. (B) It may be compared with models from other sites. (C) Inputs into other periods and regions require distinct samples. (D) Publication and analysis of this individual are already established. **Trap:** do not generalise one sample to later periods. [B §13; A §1; W Q27–28]

**20 — B.** (A) Speech and ancestry are distinct, but that does not resolve whether the affinity implies recent migration. (B) Deep Iranian-related affinity need not equal recent Iranian farmer migration. (C) Sampling matters but is not the missing temporal distinction. (D) Reference populations are proxies, but that alone does not distinguish deep from recent affinity. **Trap:** relatedness ≠ recent migration from a named modern country. [B §13; A §1; W Q37]

**21 — D.** (A) Genetic affinities can be investigated. (B) Mixture can coexist with local continuity. (C) Dated samples constrain broad chronology. (D) Ancestry movement alone cannot establish each moving group's linguistic affiliation. **Trap:** even broadly supported movement does not identify every speaker. [B §13; A §1; W Q28]

**22 — A.** (A) Report the sample locally, withholding regional generalisation pending wider comparisons. (B) Extending results to other strata without testing is unjustified. (C) Nearby valleys still need independent sampling. (D) Preservation does not guarantee representativeness. **Trap:** who, when, where and whose graves? [B §13; W Q38]

**23 — B.** (A) Textual conflict can coexist with many local encounters, not prove a civilisation-wide assault. (B) Asynchronous urban change and lack of a general destruction horizon directly weaken the single-event model. (C) Later genetic movement can occur without a destructive invasion. (D) Regional continuities also matter, but alone do not directly test a common assault horizon. **Trap:** absence of mass assault does not rule out gradual movement. [B §§10, 14; A §1; W Q17, Q29]

**24 — C.** (A) Uniform language across Harappan settlements cannot be established from continuity. (B) Continued settlement does not imply unchanged speech. (C) External Indo-Iranian history includes shared innovations and Mitanni attestation; an origin model must explain both. (D) The Harappan script remains undeciphered. **Trap:** cultural continuity ≠ proof of language origin. [B §§3, 15; W Q25]

**25 — C.** (A) These features do not name a Harappan donor language. (B) Even a likely loan needs independent dating. (C) Item-level linguistic work can test possible contact and areal features. (D) Contact evidence does not specify a single route. **Trap:** loanwords do not decode an entire civilisation. [B §16; W Q39]

**26 — B.** (A) A distant conflict text cannot independently date the local dry phase. (B) Test the proposed mechanism using independently dated and geographically matched evidence. (C) Broad similarity is not proof of flight. (D) Conflict imagery cannot disclose ancestry. **Trap:** correlation needs chronological and geographical fit. [B §§7, 9, 14; W Q8, Q29]

**27 — A.** (A) Grade each model against independently dated evidence, distinguishing secure relationships from inferential mechanisms. (B) A single explanatory event can be neat yet unsupported across regions. (C) One vehicle cannot establish a population's itinerary. (D) Linguistics alone cannot resolve archaeological destruction or ancestry. **Trap:** distinguish secure linguistic descent, probable movement and debated routes. [B §§14, 17–18; W Q31–32, Q40]

**28 — C.** (A) Genre caution by itself supplies no contrary farming evidence. (B) Metaphorical cattle references do not prove farming. (C) Harvest vocabulary directly qualifies an exclusively pastoral reading. (D) Farming in a different Harappan period cannot establish Rigvedic farming. **Trap:** literary prominence is not exclusive livelihood. [B §8; W Q11]

**29 — B.** (A) Later PGW chronology matters but does not address the first lexical leap. (B) Early *ayas* requires context-sensitive translation, not automatic equation with iron. (C) Archaeological comparison follows, rather than resolves, translation. (D) Pottery-speaker attribution is another unjustified step, after the first error. **Trap:** first check the word, then avoid calling pottery a people. [B §§8, 11; W Q12, Q21]

**30 — D.** (A) Foodways do not prove inherited biology of newly adopted speech. (B) Language spread does not require abandoning local names. (C) Name retention is compatible with language shift. (D) Prestige-driven language shift can coexist with regional foodways and names. **Trap:** acculturation is not biological purity. [B §§16–17; W Q30, Q39]

**31 — D.** (A) Phase overlap cannot identify either group's speech. (B) Textual chronology does not date every individual occupation. (C) Contemporaneity does not require shared material culture. (D) Overlap gives no direct identification of the users' language. **Trap:** shared time is not proof of shared speech. [B §§8, 10; A mini-timeline; W Q17, Q31]

**32 — B.** (A) Shared forms do not guarantee continuous identical ritual practice. (B) Linguistic kinship in systematic comparison allows divergent later practices. (C) Sound resemblance alone cannot date a borrowing. (D) Related terms do not specify identical ritual rules. **Trap:** shared heritage ≠ identical later ritual life. [B §§4, 8; W Q34]

**33 — D.** (A) Unexcavated levels do not supply positive battle evidence. (B) An incomplete site cannot establish absence at unsurveyed sites. (C) Unearthed horizons do not settle unexcavated phases. (D) Qualify the local result by expected preservation and coverage. **Trap:** absence is informative in proportion to expected detectability. [B §§7, 10, 14; W Q29]
