**1 — C.** (A) Co-location alone does not establish a common homeland. (B) An ancestry component cannot date pottery adoption. (C) Archaeology, linguistic descent and sampled ancestry measure different things. (D) A language family does not establish site-specific movement. **Trap:** geographic co-location is not identity. [B §§1, 7; W Q1, Q7]

**2 — A.** (A) Competing alliances can change who receives an opponent label. (B) The labels do not consistently mark migration status. (C) Internal conflict can coexist with ritual distinctions across groups. (D) Opponent labels cannot identify a burial horizon. **Trap:** do not fossilise relational labels. [B §2; W Q33]

**3 — D.** (A) Ritual vocabulary can spread by borrowing. (B) A few sound resemblances may be accidental or borrowed. (C) Shared ancestry among modern speakers does not prove inherited ancient phonology. (D) Regular phonological patterns across inflectional forms are stronger evidence of inheritance. **Trap:** isolated resemblance is not systematic comparison. [B §4; W Q4]

**4 — B.** (A) Iranian and Indo-Aryan are related branches, not a direct parent-child pair. (B) In wider systematic comparison *soma/haoma* supports Indo-Iranian linguistic descent, not identical later rites. (C) Cognates cannot establish identical ritual sequences. (D) These terms cannot locate ancestral speech at one site. **Trap:** Indo-Aryan ⊂ Indo-Iranian ⊂ Indo-European. [B §4; W Q5, Q34]

**5 — B.** (A) External terminology cannot locate early family-book composition. (B) Mitanni treaty names and technical terms attest Indo-Aryan-related forms in a Near Eastern context. (C) Technical or elite terms do not census everyday speech. (D) They do not trace a continuous route to the Indus. **Trap:** external attestation is not a travel diary. [B §5; W Q6]

**6 — D.** (A) Grammar does not establish biological descent. (B) Rejection of race theory does not exclude migration. (C) Phenotype cannot independently confirm linguistic kinship. (D) A linguistic genealogy does not imply a ranked racial genealogy. **Trap:** two distinct claims have different evidentiary grades. [B §§4, 6, 17; W Q3, Q40]

**7 — A.** (A) Examine the river hymn's own textual layer, not merely its book label, before dating. (B) Book membership alone cannot establish its oldest layer. (C) Current channel geography cannot date orally transmitted poetry. (D) A river-name may carry memory or undergo reinterpretation; it is not an unchanged composition record. **Trap:** older textual stratum ≠ exact date. [B §§8–9; W Q9, Q35]

**8 — C.** (A) Proximity alone cannot prove the river's ancient textual identity. (B) A surviving name cannot date the passage. (C) Watercourse, occupation and passage need separate chronologies before correlation. (D) Mapped channels cannot automatically date all neighbouring occupations. **Trap:** spatial match is not independent synchrony. [B §9; W Q36]

**9 — D.** (A) A single conquest lacks general destruction evidence. (B) Continuity and later mobility can coexist. (C) Continuity does not establish full Harappan–Vedic identity. (D) Regional transformation with continuing practices weighs against universal conquest. **Trap:** no invasion ≠ no migration. [B §§10, 14; A §1; W Q17]

**10 — A.** (A) Finds allow a study of transitions among local material horizons, not the users' language. (B) Shared horizons cannot establish language continuity. (C) Shared material practices cannot establish political unity. (D) Deposits cannot date oral verse composition precisely. **Trap:** Bhagwanpura illustrates sequence, not a speaker label. [B §§8, 11; W Q18, Mains 4]

**11 — C.** (A) An oasis network is not an excavated one-way route into Swat. (B) Neither context establishes a uniform demographic replacement phase. (C) BMAC's oasis settings differ from Swat's varied frontier burials. (D) BMAC material cannot identify speech in north-western graves. **Trap:** corridor nodes are not named peoples. [B §11; W Q19, Q22]

**12 — B.** (A) Broad overlap is insufficient to identify speakers; OCP/PGW are not simply the same horizon. (B) Independently dated language or self-identification evidence would be needed to link specific assemblages with speech; pottery alone cannot. (C) Similar copper objects cannot supply a linguistic identity for PGW users. (D) Ceramic resemblance can result from exchange or imitation rather than common speech. **Trap:** PGW is a later comparison, not an early Rigvedic ethnic tag. [B §§7, 11; W Q20–21, Q23]

**13 — A.** (A) Sintashta spoked-wheel evidence concerns technology, not an excavated language in India. (B) Technology may spread by several mechanisms. (C) Vehicle parallels do not establish identical rites. (D) Technological resemblance does not specify a route. **Trap:** technical chronology and linguistic attribution need different proofs. [B §§11–12; W Q15]

**14 — D.** (A) Vehicles and burials are observable. (B) Stratigraphy is a legitimate control. (C) Solid versus spoked wheels matters. (D) Vehicle presence cannot prove Vedic speech. **Trap:** “chariot” is an interpretation, not a translation of burial goods. [B §§11–12; W Q16]

**15 — B.** (A) Species identification remains a local technical question. (B) Even a securely identified local horse cannot establish economic centrality across Harappan regions. (C) Stratigraphic phase needs checking independently. (D) A verified specimen could establish local presence, but not wider prominence. **Trap:** presence ≠ economic centrality. [B §12; W Q13–14]

**16 — C.** (A) Genetic relatedness does not establish even adjacent speakers' languages. (B) Grave goods cannot disclose a sampled person's social identity through DNA. (C) Genetic affinities can be modelled against reference samples. (D) A textual affiliation cannot be recovered genetically. **Trap:** ancestry evidence is sampled and model-dependent. [B §§7, 13; W Q26]

**17 — C.** (A) The label describes modelled statistical affinities. (B) Deep relatedness is a possible reading, not a direct migration record. (C) The affinity does not establish recent Iranian farmer migration into this burial. (D) Its interpretation depends on reference comparisons. **Trap:** affinity label is not a migration certificate. [B §13; A §1; W Q37]

**18 — A.** (A) One local Rakhigarhi genome cannot establish later movement; the broader regional study must be assessed separately. (B) One genome cannot exclude later inward movement. (C) Admixture cannot reveal the earlier individual's speech. (D) Movement need not cause uniform regional replacement. **Trap:** distinguish studies, dates and sampling scales. [B §13; A §1; W Q27–28]

**19 — D.** (A) Preservation is not a guarantee of representativeness. (B) Neighbouring populations can have different ancestry histories. (C) One phase cannot represent all ancient periods. (D) Social burial selection may bias comparisons across regions. **Trap:** whose remains survived and were sampled? [B §13; W Q38]

**20 — B.** (A) A shift in speech need not entail wholesale cultural replacement. (B) Gradual mobility need not produce the universal destruction horizon posited by the invasion claim. (C) A military headcount does not define the difference; invasion implies conquest. (D) Migration may leave varied material traces rather than one regional signature. **Trap:** reject invasion without denying contact. [B §14; A §§1–2; W Q24]

**21 — A.** (A) A complete origin model must explain external Indo-Iranian history, including Mitanni forms and branching. (B) A single site's urban continuity cannot answer outward language spread. (C) Harappan signs are undeciphered and cannot independently assign speech. (D) Material continuity cannot exclude inward movement. **Trap:** apply the same evidence standard to every model. [B §§3, 15; W Q25]

**22 — C.** (A) Not every loan has the same known donor. (B) Synchronising suspected loans with one assumed incoming group would presuppose the contact date and mechanism. (C) Compare proposed areal phonology with individual loan histories before assigning donors. (D) Inherited development alone has not been established for all such areal features. **Trap:** substrate proposals do not automatically identify Harappan speech. [B §16; W Q39]

**23 — D.** (A) Climate evidence can inform pressures, not identity. (B) Independent occupation dating allows comparison, not ethnic naming. (C) Environmental stress may affect mobility but need not. (D) Correlation cannot identify the ethnicity of supposed migrants. **Trap:** causal mechanism and self-identity require separate evidence. [B §§7, 13–14; W Q8, Q29]

**24 — B.** (A) Linguistic kinship alone does not specify a route. (B) The kinship is more securely established than precise routes or ethnic identities. (C) Absence of general assault does not exclude smaller movements. (D) DNA cannot assign named languages. **Trap:** grade confidence by claim. [B §§17–18; W Q31, Q40]

**25 — C.** (A) Herding receives marked emphasis. (B) Cultivation vocabulary contradicts exclusive pastoralism. (C) Cattle-centred values and cultivation can coexist. (D) One poem cannot establish a uniform farming economy. **Trap:** poetic emphasis is not livelihood exclusivity. [B §8; W Q11]

**26 — A.** (A) The meaning of early *ayas* warrants scrutiny before using the term to date a verse. (B) Generic “metal” does not establish which metal. (C) Later iron usage cannot settle metal identity in earlier verses. (D) Regional iron horizons cannot date a verse merely mentioning ambiguous *ayas*. **Trap:** do not date a corpus by presumed translation. [B §§8, 11; W Q12]

**27 — D.** (A) Elite borrowing alone does not explain community-level adoption without assessing bilingualism. (B) Retained foodways weaken complete replacement. (C) Speech and ancestry do not spread in lockstep. (D) Mixed communities can adopt new speech while retaining local foodways. **Trap:** mobility and continuity need not be opposites. [B §§14, 16; W Q30]

**28 — C.** (A) PGW is a later comparison than Mature urbanism. (B) Late localisation follows Mature integration. (C) Late Harappan phases overlap the conventional Early Vedic window in some regions, without implying uniform change. (D) Mitanni attestation postdates Mature urban onset. **Trap:** overlap ≠ shared ethnicity. [B §§5, 10–11; A mini-timeline; W Q17, Q31]

**29 — A.** (A) Regionally extensive survey with expected detectability gives negative evidence weight. (B) Omitted high-visibility areas weaken negative inference. (C) One urban core cannot represent a region. (D) Selective attention to burn layers misses other indicators and contexts. **Trap:** no general assault horizon does not exclude local conflict. [B §§7, 10, 14; W Q29]
