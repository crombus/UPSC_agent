**Sources:** https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2024/253724.pdf (Finance Ministry, S.O. 1722(E), 16 April 2024, pp. 3–4, serial 12.2: 49% automatic, government route beyond; effective on publication); https://www.dpiit.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/07/12e3884759ec5ad29cb2ad37e6a525e0.pdf (DPIIT Press Note 1/2024). No claim of October 2026 legal consolidation.
**Examiner trap:** An automatic-entry ceiling is not the total FDI sectoral cap.

### 36. D — First experimental and intermediate lineage

- A: ❌ SLV-3, not ASLV, is the first experimental satellite launcher.
- B: ❌ PSLV came after SLV-3/ASLV, and ASLV did not follow LVM3.
- C: ❌ GSLV is a later launcher; SLV-3 was the first experimental vehicle.
- D: ✅ SLV-3 began experimental satellite-launch capability; ASLV was an intermediate learning step.

**Sources:** https://www.isro.gov.in/SLV.html; https://www.isro.gov.in/ASLV.html; upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Science-and-Technology/basic/01_Space-Programme-ISRO-Launch-Vehicles.md.
**Examiner trap:** ASLV was not the first experimental satellite launcher.

### 37. C — GSLV payload and propulsion

- A: ❌ SSLV is a small-satellite launcher, not a cryogenic GTO workhorse.
- B: ❌ PSLV does not fly a semi-cryogenic upper stage for heavier GTO missions.
- C: ✅ ISRO links GSLV's indigenous cryogenic upper stage to heavier communication payloads.
- D: ❌ ASLV was an experimental intermediate vehicle, not a cryogenic GTO launcher.

**Sources:** https://www.isro.gov.in/Launchers.html; upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Science-and-Technology/basic/01_Space-Programme-ISRO-Launch-Vehicles.md.
**Examiner trap:** GSLV cryogenic GTO capacity should not be confused with a polar name or future semi-cryogenic upgrade.

### 38. A — Insurance and State responsibility

- A: ⚠️ Financial risk can be contractually allocated without displacing the launching State's absolute treaty liability for surface damage.
- B: ❌ An insurer is not made a launching State by a domestic indemnity clause.
- C: ❌ The Article II treaty standard for surface damage does not change with a private contract.
- D: ❌ Insurance and indemnity do not waive Article VI continuing State supervision.

**Sources:** https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/626736/files/st_space_11rev2E.pdf (Liability Convention Article II, Outer Space Treaty Article VI); upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Science-and-Technology/advanced/01_Space-Programme-ISRO-Launch-Vehicles.md.
**Examiner trap:** Insured operator ≠ insurer substitutes for the launching State.

### 39. C — Liability away from Earth's surface

- A: ❌ Registration determines treaty recording and links the object to a State, but it does not alone make every orbital collision subject to absolute liability.
- B: ❌ Article II's absolute standard covers Earth's surface and aircraft in flight, not every collision between space objects in orbit.
- C: ✅ Liability Convention Article III requires fault of the launching State, or persons for whom it is responsible, for damage caused elsewhere than Earth's surface to a space object of another launching State or persons/property aboard it.
- D: ❌ Ownership of insurance does not replace the Convention's launching-State fault test for the specified orbital collision.

**Sources:** https://www.unoosa.org/oosa/en/ourwork/spacelaw/treaties/liability-convention.html (Liability Convention Articles II–III, UN primary treaty text); upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Science-and-Technology/advanced/01_Space-Programme-ISRO-Launch-Vehicles.md.
**Examiner trap:** Do not apply absolute liability for surface or aircraft damage automatically to a collision between States' orbiting objects.
### 40. B — Different FDI routes for launch vehicles and specified components

- A: ❌ The notification does not make every space activity automatically eligible for 100% foreign participation; launch vehicles and associated systems have a separate 49% automatic ceiling.
- B: ✅ A 60% launch-vehicle proposal is above the automatic ceiling and uses the government route, while specified satellite, ground and user components are listed under the 100% automatic category.
- C: ❌ The specified-component category is not the lower-ceiling category in the notified triplet.
- D: ❌ The 2024 amendment covers more than satellites in orbit, including launch vehicles, spaceports and specified components.

**Sources:** https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2024/253724.pdf (Finance Ministry Gazette S.O. 1722(E), 16 April 2024, pp. 3–4); https://www.dpiit.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/07/12e3884759ec5ad29cb2ad37e6a525e0.pdf (DPIIT Press Note 1/2024, policy context); upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Science-and-Technology/basic/01_Space-Programme-ISRO-Launch-Vehicles.md.
**Examiner trap:** Do not apply the 100% specified-component category to launch vehicles or confuse the automatic ceiling with the government route above that ceiling.
