### 44. A — Fault prerequisite in orbit

- A: ✅ Under Liability Convention Article III, damage elsewhere than Earth's surface to another launching State's space object requires fault of the damaging object's launching State or those for whom it is responsible; if neither is at fault, that Article imposes no liability for the described damage.
- B: ❌ Article II's absolute liability governs damage on Earth's surface or to aircraft in flight, not every orbital collision.
- C: ❌ Article III specifies a fault-based launching-State standard, not strict insurer liability; private insurance does not alter the treaty.
- D: ❌ Outer Space Treaty Article VI continuing supervision does not change the distinct Article III liability standard into automatic compensation.

**Sources:** https://www.unoosa.org/oosa/en/ourwork/spacelaw/treaties/liability-convention.html (Liability Convention Articles II–III, UN primary treaty text); https://www.unoosa.org/oosa/en/ourwork/spacelaw/treaties/outerspacetreaty.html (Outer Space Treaty Article VI); upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Science-and-Technology/advanced/01_Space-Programme-ISRO-Launch-Vehicles.md.
**Examiner trap:** A State's Article VI duty to supervise is not proof of fault under Liability Convention Article III.
