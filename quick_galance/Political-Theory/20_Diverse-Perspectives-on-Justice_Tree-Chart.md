                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Justice theories disagree because they identify different primary wrongs and different
objects of distribution. Rawls protects fair basic institutions and the least
advantaged; Nozick protects self-ownership and historically valid holdings; Marxism
targets exploitation and productive property; democratic socialism combines welfare
with civil liberty; anarchism targets coercive domination; feminism and subaltern
theory expose structured exclusion; communitarianism protects shared meanings and
sphere boundaries. Capability and recognition approaches further ask what people can
actually do and whether they can participate as equals.
