                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Theories of the state select and universalise different dimensions of political order.
Organic theory sees an ethical whole; contract theory a human device for security,
rights or self-rule; laissez-faire liberalism a minimal protector; welfare liberalism
an enabler of real freedom; Marxism a class structure; communitarianism an expression
of shared life; post-colonial theory an inherited and developmental formation;
Gandhism a coercive machine to be morally transcended; feminism a gendered structure;
pluralism a coordinator among associations. No perspective alone exhausts the state.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
No single image of the state is adequate in every context. The state can protect
rights, enable welfare and coordinate plural associations, yet also reproduce class,
colonial and patriarchal power. Community and ethical purposes matter, but cannot
justify absorption of conscience; minimal government protects against coercion, but
cannot guarantee substantive freedom. A defensible state is therefore limited,
socially enabling, pluralist and accountable, with its claims continuously tested
against the power relations each critical perspective reveals.
