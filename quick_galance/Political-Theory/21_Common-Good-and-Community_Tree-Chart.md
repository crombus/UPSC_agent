                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The common good is a normative ideal of conditions and purposes beneficial to the
political community as a whole. It is not the state's interest, majority preference,
the sum of private gains, an administrative claim of public interest or an economic
public good. Liberalism derives it from fair reconciliation of plural interests;
republicanism links it to non-domination and civic self-government; communitarianism
grounds it in shared practices and embedded identities; Marxism denies its full
possibility under class domination; Gandhism seeks sarvodaya through non-violence,
trusteeship and bread labour. Rights and minority voice remain indispensable tests.
