                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Rights are justified moral or legal positions that protect persons against arbitrary
power and secure conditions for dignified agency. Human rights attach to persons as
human beings and form the broadest moral framework; civil liberties protect legal
freedom from arbitrary interference; democratic rights organise participation in
public power. These categories overlap without forming a rigid universal hierarchy.
Rights require both restraint and positive protection, and their moral grounding,
legal recognition, institutional enforcement, limitation and effective enjoyment must
be analysed separately.
