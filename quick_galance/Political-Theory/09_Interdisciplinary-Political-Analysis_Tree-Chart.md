                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Politics is embedded in historical sequences, material distribution, social
structures, identities, psychology, territory, law and moral argument. Political
analysis therefore needs purposeful borrowing from relevant disciplines to explain,
verify and evaluate claims. Interdisciplinarity does not dissolve political science:
the organising question remains political authority, conflict, allocation, legitimacy
or collective choice. Integration is necessary because every single-discipline lens is
partial; merger and reductionism are neither necessary nor desirable.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Interdisciplinary analysis is indispensable because political causes and consequences
cross disciplinary boundaries. History tests sequence, economics reveals distribution,
sociology maps groups, psychology explains attitudes, law fixes formal authority,
geography locates conflict, anthropology widens comparison and philosophy judges ends.
Yet none can replace political analysis. The defensible method is selective integration:
borrow what the political question requires, test the scale and limits of each lens, and
return to authority, power, legitimacy and collective choice.
