                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Citizenship is full and responsible membership of a political community, joining legal
status with rights, duties, allegiance and participation. Its democratic promise is
equal standing, but formal membership may coexist with exclusion from effective voice,
welfare, security or recognition. Liberal, libertarian, republican, communitarian,
Marxist and pluralist accounts identify different citizen roles; feminist, subaltern,
postcolonial, multicultural and migration critiques show why universal citizenship may
require differentiated remedies while retaining common membership and individual rights.
