                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Political theory is systematic knowledge of political phenomena. It integrates
empirical observation, logical reasoning and evaluative judgment because political
life raises questions about facts, causes, concepts, means and justified ends at the
same time. Political science explains political reality; political philosophy
clarifies concepts, criticises arrangements and justifies goals. Neither evidence-free
moralism nor value-blind scientism is sufficient.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Political theory did not disappear when behaviouralism challenged traditional
speculation. What declined was the credibility of theory that ignored evidence, while
facts-only inquiry also proved unable to judge justice, legitimacy and collective ends.
Its continuing significance lies in disciplined integration: empirical inquiry explains
political reality, logical analysis tests coherence, and political philosophy clarifies
and justifies the ends by which institutions and policies should be assessed.
