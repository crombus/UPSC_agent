                              |
                              v
                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Ideology is both an action-oriented set of beliefs that mobilises groups and a field
of inquiry into how ideas arise, become distorted and serve power. It can sustain
domination, express emancipatory claims or close politics around an alleged final
truth. Political theory therefore tests ideology rather than treating it as either
pure falsehood or unquestionable faith. The end-of-ideology thesis was a historically
bounded claim about affluent industrial societies, not proof that ideological conflict
disappeared.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Ideology remains indispensable to political mobilisation and dangerous when it hides
interest, exempts itself from criticism or becomes a state-backed monopoly of truth.
Industrial affluence and technocratic administration may narrow older doctrinal
conflicts, but they do not remove value choice or power. The defensible conclusion is
not the end of ideology, but the continuing need for political theory to expose,
compare and critically test ideological claims, including claims of neutrality.
