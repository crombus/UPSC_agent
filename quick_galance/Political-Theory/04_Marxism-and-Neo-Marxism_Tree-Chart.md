                              v
                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Marxism explains domination and historical change through material production,
property relations, class conflict and exploitation. Its causal core is the tension
between developing productive forces and existing relations of production, not a
mechanical slogan that economics instantaneously determines everything. Neo-Marxism
extends this structural critique into alienation, culture, communication, civil
society, ideology and international dependence. These extensions revise economism
without abandoning the material and class foundations of Marxist analysis.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Marxism remains a powerful account of how property, production and class structure
shape political power and exploitation. It becomes weakest when converted into a
mechanical economic determinism or a single-cleavage explanation of all domination.
Neo-Marxist analysis strengthens the tradition by showing how consent, culture,
communication, state institutions and international dependence reproduce material
power. The defensible legacy is therefore structural but non-reductionist.
