                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Socialism, anarchism and Gandhism are rival responses to inequality, capitalism and
coercive power: democratic socialism reforms ownership through the state, anarchism
seeks voluntary order beyond the coercive state, and Gandhism pursues ethical self-rule
through truth, non-violence, trusteeship and labour. Fascism is not a fourth egalitarian
alternative: it rejects equality, liberty, pluralism and reasoned criticism in favour
of hierarchy, leader rule, mythic nationalism and totalitarian power. Conservatism,
owned here as a supplementary fifth doctrine, prefers inherited order and prudential,
reversible reform to abstract reconstruction and must not be confused with fascism.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The doctrines cannot be grouped merely because each criticises some feature of
liberal-capitalist modernity. Democratic socialism trusts public power for equality;
anarchism distrusts coercive authority; Gandhism seeks moral self-rule and non-violent
social reconstruction; conservatism distrusts abstract total redesign. Fascism alone
turns crisis into a justification for hierarchy, leader supremacy and suppression of
liberty and pluralism. Sound comparison must therefore follow values, institutions,
methods and ownership of power, not superficial labels.
