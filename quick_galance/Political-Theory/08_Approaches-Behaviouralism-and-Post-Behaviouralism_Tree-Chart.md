                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
An approach decides which political questions, units and evidence matter; a method is
the procedure used to investigate them. Traditional approaches illuminate concepts,
history, law and institutions. Behaviouralism added observation, verification,
regularities, technique, quantification and interdisciplinary science. Its narrow
micro-focus and claim to value-neutrality prompted post-behaviouralism, which retained
rigour but demanded relevance, action and responsibility. Political analysis is
therefore plural: systems, structural-functional, communications, decision-making and
Marxian models each reveal some mechanisms and obscure others.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Behaviouralism permanently improved political inquiry by demanding evidence,
verification, explicit technique and attention to actual conduct. It became inadequate
when method displaced important questions and neutrality became indifference to public
crisis. Post-behaviouralism's defensible contribution was not to reverse science but to
connect it with relevance and responsibility. The wider lesson is methodological
pluralism: approaches and models should be chosen by the problem and checked against
one another's blind spots.
