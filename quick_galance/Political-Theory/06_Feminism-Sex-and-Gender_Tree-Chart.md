                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Feminism exposes women's subordination as a political relation rather than a natural
consequence of bodily difference. The sex-gender distinction separates biological
features from culturally produced roles, status and power, while later feminism also
questions whether sex itself is interpreted through social discourse. Feminist schools
disagree over the main structure of domination and the remedy: formal barriers,
patriarchy, capitalism, racial-caste-class intersections, cultural recognition or the
devaluation of care. A complete analysis therefore joins equality, difference,
representation, bodily agency and transformation of public and private institutions.
