                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The United Nations improved on the League by keeping the major powers inside a
wider organisation, enlarging economic, social and human-rights work, and
creating procedures for deliberation, peacekeeping and authorised enforcement.
Yet its central strength and weakness are the same design choice: great-power
participation was purchased through the Security Council veto, so the UN is
strongest where major powers agree or remain indifferent and weakest where
their vital interests collide. Judged as world government it disappoints;
judged as a permanent forum, norm-setter, mediator and humanitarian-development
network, it is a durable and historically significant system of governance.
