                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The Second World War arose from two regional expansionist wars, Japanese in
Asia and German in Europe, fused into a single global conflict by the decisions
of 1941. Axis operational speed initially overwhelmed divided opponents, but
success produced strategic overreach. Britain survived, the USSR absorbed and
reversed invasion, the United States added unmatched resources, Allied sea and
air power matured, and brutal occupation generated resistance. The war was
also a social and moral catastrophe: civilians became strategic targets,
occupation enabled genocide, and societies faced resistance-collaboration
choices. Victory ended European primacy, created bipolar and nuclear politics,
strengthened demands for decolonisation and produced the United Nations.
