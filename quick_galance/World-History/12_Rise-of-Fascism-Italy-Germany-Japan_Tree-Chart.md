                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Interwar authoritarianism emerged from a common crisis of war grievance,
economic insecurity, weak parliamentary government, class fear, political
violence and a demand for national restoration, but it did not produce one
uniform regime. Italian fascism created the first mass-party authoritarian
model; Nazism radicalised dictatorship through biological race and systematic
terror; Japan developed army dominance within surviving imperial-constitutional
forms; and Franco's Spain became a civil-war-forged, clerical-authoritarian
state. Democracy was challenged both from below by movements and violence and
from above when monarchs, conservatives and legal institutions surrendered it.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Fascist and authoritarian movements benefited from crises that liberal

elites and institutions treated democracy as dispensable. Their family
resemblance lay in nationalism, anti-communism, coercion and expansion, while
