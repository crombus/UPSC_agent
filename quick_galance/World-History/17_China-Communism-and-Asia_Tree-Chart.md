                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
China's twentieth-century revolution solved a state-building crisis that the
1911 Revolution had exposed but not resolved. The CCP triumphed in 1949 not
through doctrine or foreign help alone, but because Mao adapted revolution to a
peasant society, the Long March preserved and legitimised leadership, wartime
resistance enhanced patriotic standing, and KMT corruption, inflation and weak
administration destroyed its mandate. Mao unified China and transformed rural
power, but the Great Leap and Cultural Revolution showed the catastrophic
capacity of campaign politics. Deng separated market reform from political
liberalisation. Across Asia, communist regimes followed distinct national
routes, and the Sino-Soviet split disproved any monolithic-bloc interpretation.
