                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Russia's revolutions formed a sequence, not one event. The unresolved
constitutional, agrarian, labour and dynastic crisis exposed in 1905 was
converted into state collapse by the First World War. February 1917 was a
broad uprising and elite abandonment of the Tsar; October was an organised
Bolshevik seizure made possible by Provisional Government failure. Lenin
consolidated power through popular decrees, withdrawal from war, civil-war
victory, party monopoly, coercion and the tactical retreat of NEP. Stalin then
used party organisation to build personal supremacy and imposed a second
revolution from above: rapid industrial capacity was achieved through
collectivisation, famine, labour discipline, purge and one-party terror.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The Russian Revolution was neither a predetermined social law nor a simple
minority conspiracy. War broke a vulnerable autocracy; February joined mass
uprising to elite abandonment, and October joined disciplined organisation to
the collapse of provisional legitimacy. Lenin secured the new state while
embedding party monopoly and coercion. Stalin inherited that framework but
transformed its scale, remaking economy and society through forced
industrialisation, collectivisation and terror. The resulting USSR gained the
capacity to survive invasion and become a superpower, but did so through famine,
poor consumption, destroyed expertise and repression that cannot be separated
from the achievement.
