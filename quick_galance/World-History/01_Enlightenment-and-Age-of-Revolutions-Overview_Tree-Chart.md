                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The Enlightenment did not itself cause revolution. It changed the language in
which inherited authority could be challenged: rulers had to answer claims of
reason, natural rights, consent, divided power and popular sovereignty. Fiscal
and social crises supplied timing, state weakness supplied opportunity, and
mobilised groups supplied force. The Atlantic revolutions translated this
intellectual grammar into republican and citizenship projects while preserving
major exclusions. Their aftermath generated liberal, conservative, nationalist,
capitalist and socialist responses that structured modern political society.
