                              v
                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The American Revolution began as a dispute over the terms of empire and became
a revolution over the source of political authority. British post-war fiscal
and mercantilist tightening collided with colonial assemblies and rights-
language, converting taxation into a sovereignty question. Armed resistance
survived through inter-colonial organisation and became internationally viable
after French intervention. Its durable achievement was a written federal
republic with entrenched liberties; its defining contradiction was that
slavery, Native dispossession, property restrictions and women's exclusion
survived the claim of universal rights.

                 QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The American Revolution decisively changed political legitimacy by proving
that colonial subjects could reject empire and bind a republic through a
written constitution. Its success depended on institutions already present in
the colonies and on international alliance as much as on abstract rights. The
new order was therefore a major constitutional revolution but an incomplete
social one. That contradiction made the American example both globally
influential and internally unfinished.
