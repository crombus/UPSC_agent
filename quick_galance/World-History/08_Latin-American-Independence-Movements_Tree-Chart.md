                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Latin American independence grew from colonial hierarchy, commercial
restriction and Atlantic revolutionary ideas, but became possible when the
Iberian monarchies lost legitimacy after 1808. It followed five distinct paths:
Haiti's anti-slavery social revolution, Bolivar's northern campaigns, San
Martin's southern campaigns, Mexico's shift from popular revolt to conservative
settlement, and Brazil's monarchical separation. Sovereignty changed faster
than society. In much of the mainland, creole elites displaced peninsular
officials while land, racial hierarchy, slavery and regional inequality
survived, producing states before stable nations and armies before institutions.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Latin American independence ended Iberian sovereignty through multiple wars
and settlements rather than one continental revolution. The 1808 legitimacy
crisis transformed old grievances into political opportunity, but social
leadership shaped the result: Haiti united emancipation and sovereignty,
