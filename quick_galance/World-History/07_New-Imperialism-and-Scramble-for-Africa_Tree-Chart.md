                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
New Imperialism was not one motive or one conference. Industrial demand,
strategic routes and great-power competition created incentives; technology
made inland conquest more feasible; racial and civilising ideologies supplied
permission; Berlin standardised claims; and armed occupation converted claims
into rule. African societies were never passive: they resisted, negotiated,
collaborated and adapted under radically unequal power. Colonial extraction,
coercive administration, hardened identities and inherited borders outlasted
formal empire, but none alone mechanically explains every later African crisis.
