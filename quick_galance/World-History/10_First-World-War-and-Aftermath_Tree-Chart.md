                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The First World War became a prolonged industrial conflict because quick
victory failed, defensive firepower exceeded offensive mobility, two large
coalitions could continually mobilise new resources, and neither accepted
compromise. The Central Powers lost cumulatively through two-front strain,
blockade, a failed submarine gamble, American resources, improved Allied
coordination and collapse of weaker allies. Total war fused battlefield, state,
factory, household and empire. The peace settlement destroyed old continental
empires and created new states and the League, but applied self-determination
unevenly, preserved imperial control through mandates and created grievance
without a durable enforcement or reconciliation mechanism.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The First World War was won through the endurance of states, economies,
societies and coalitions rather than by one decisive battlefield event. It
destroyed continental empires, enlarged interventionist government, shifted
relative power beyond Europe and made civilians and colonies central to modern
war. The 1919-23 settlement achieved formal peace and institutional innovation,
but combined selective self-determination with mandates, minorities and
revisionist resentment. It made later conflict easier to mobilise politically,
yet the Depression, authoritarian choices and interwar enforcement failures,
not Versailles alone, determined whether instability became another world war.
