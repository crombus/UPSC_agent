+-- I. THE SIX SURVIVAL TERMS (fix these in the first two lines)
|   +-- Immortality: a subject is deathless — Plato, Vedānta, Nyāya, Jainism.
|   +-- Survival: something continues after death — the weaker neutral claim.
|   +-- Rebirth (punarjanma / punarbhava): the WIDER term; the only correct one for Buddhism.
|   +-- Reincarnation: a SUBSTANTIAL soul re-enters flesh — Vedānta, Nyāya, Jainism only.
|   +-- Resurrection: God re-constitutes the embodied person; ONE life; final judgement.
|   +-- Liberation: exit from the series — SEVEN non-identical destinations.
|   +-- Sanskrit range: punarjanma · punarāvṛtti · punarbhava · saṃsāra.
|
+-- II. PERSON-MODELS AND IDENTITY CRITERIA
|   +-- Models: substance dualism · hylomorphism · bundle/no-self · embodied person ·
|       Indian substantial self (ātman / jīva).
|   +-- Criteria: bodily · psychological-memory · soul-substance · causal continuity.
|       +-- Bodily and psychological answer HOW WE TELL; substance and causal answer
|           WHAT MAKES IT SO. Memory presupposes identity and cannot define it.
|   +-- Hick's replica: a UNIQUE psychophysical duplicate with memory and character,
|       appearing in a resurrection world, IS the person for all purposes that matter.
|       +-- Duplication objection: one replica implies the possibility of two; identity is
|           one-to-one, so the criterion tracks similarity, not numerical identity.
|       +-- Status: a coherence defence of resurrection identity, never evidence of survival.
|
+-- III. PLATO'S FOUR A PRIORI PROOFS (Phaedo) — 2023 Q6(a)
|   +-- 1 Opposites/cyclical: living and dead arise from each other, so souls persist.
|       +-- Critique: degree-opposites are not being and non-being; gives a cosmic balance.
|   +-- 2 Recollection (anamnesis): we judge sticks DEFICIENTLY equal by a standard sense
|       cannot supply. +-- Establishes PRE-EXISTENCE ONLY, not post-mortem survival.
|   +-- 3 Affinity: the soul resembles the invisible, stable and non-composite.
|       +-- Critique: likeness is not identity; simplicity is assumed, not proved.
|   +-- 4 Form of Life: what essentially brings Life cannot admit death.
|       +-- Critique: "cannot admit death" is not "cannot cease"; fire does not become cold,
|           but fire goes out.
|   +-- Simmias (inside the dialogue): the soul is an attunement (harmonia) of the body.
|       +-- Plato's twofold reply: an attunement cannot pre-exist its instrument, and the
|           soul RULES the body, opposing appetite, which no attunement does.
|   +-- Kant's paralogism: the unity of apperception is a formal condition of thought, not
|       knowledge of a substance. Immortality returns only as a POSTULATE of practical reason.
|
+-- IV. FIVE ARGUMENT FAMILIES AND THE BURDEN SHIFT — 2020 Q5(b)
|   +-- Simplicity · identity/continuity · moral order · desire/fulfilment · testimony.
|   +-- Each fails in its own way: simplicity unproved; continuity suffices; hope not proof;
|       longing does not entail an object; interpretation and diversity.
|   +-- Body/brain dependence does NOT deductively refute survival; it SHIFTS the burden to
|       accounts of interaction, individuation and identity.
|   +-- Claim-levels available: coherence, presupposition, postulate — never demonstration.
|
+-- V. HINDU IMMORTALITY — VERSE SPINE AND FOUR SCHOOLS — 2021 Q6(a), 2025 Q7(c)
|   +-- Gītā 2.22 worn-out garments (rebirth); 2.23 weapons/fire/water/wind fail
|       (indestructibility); 9.21 return when merit is exhausted — heaven is INSIDE saṃsāra.
|   +-- Subtle body (sūkṣma-śarīra) carries dispositions (saṃskāra/vāsanā): it is the VEHICLE,
|       never the continuant.
|   +-- Advaita: non-dual awareness | Viśiṣṭādvaita: eternal jīva, a mode (prakāra) of God |
|       Nyāya: eternal substance, consciousness ADVENTITIOUS | Sāṃkhya: plural contentless witness.
|   +-- Supports: continuity of the knower · moral bookkeeping · scriptural testimony (śabda) ·
|       beginninglessness (anādi), which blocks "when did the soul first fall?".
|
+-- VI. KARMA — LEDGER, ADMINISTRATION, POSTULATE — 2019 Q8(a), 2021 Q8(b)
|   +-- Act PLUS unseen potency: adṛṣṭa (Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika) / apūrva (Mīmāṃsā).
|   +-- Sañcita (stored; destroyed by knowledge) · prārabdha (fructifying; runs its course) ·
|       āgāmi (future; where effort acts). Effort = puruṣakāra WITHIN daiva.
|   +-- Administration fork: SELF-REGULATING (Buddhism, Jainism, Mīmāṃsā) vs SUPERINTENDENT
|       God (karmādhyakṣa: Nyāya, theistic Vedāntas) who RATIFIES desert, never authors it.
|   +-- Postulate test: remove karma and distribution, rebirth, liberation and ritual efficacy
|       all fail. Costs: unverifiability · victim-blaming risk · the God-and-law problem.
|   +-- Niṣkāma-karma USES the law rather than escaping it.
|
+-- VII. REBIRTH — THE FIT-PROBLEM — 2023 Q7(b)
|   +-- Claim: every act matures. Fact: many do not, within one life.
|   +-- Three exits: (a) abandon the claim; (b) one post-mortem judgement — the SEMITIC route;
|       (c) WIDEN the field across many lives — the INDIAN route. Rebirth is exit (c).
|   +-- Contributions: exceptionless law preserved · initial inequality explained ·
|       beginninglessness secured · liberation given content.
|   +-- Costs: infinite regress explains each birth but never the series; no past-life memory;
|       moral hazard of reading suffering as fault.
|
+-- VIII. BUDDHISM — THE COUNTER-EXAMPLE — 2022 Q5(c), 2024 Q6(a), 2025 Q7(c)
|   +-- Four steps: all conditioned factors impermanent -> no permanent self (anātman);
|       no aggregate (skandha) is a self ->
|       craving (tṛṣṇā) and formations condition future consciousness -> continuity with NO owner.
|   +-- Denied: a permanent, independent substance-self. NOT denied: the conventional person,
|       moral consequence, causal descent, rebirth itself. Anātman is NOT nihilism.
|   +-- Ownership objection: who receives the fruit? Reply: causal descent, "neither the same
|       nor wholly other". Residue to concede: descent may be thinner than ownership.
|
+-- IX. THE LIBERATION PARITY BENCH — SEVEN DESTINATIONS
|   +-- Mokṣa (Advaita): ignorance ends | Brahman alone | consciousness YES, self-luminous.
|   +-- Mokṣa (Viśiṣṭādvaita): karmic bondage ends | eternal jīva WITH God | YES, personal.
|   +-- Apavarga (Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika): pain AND its conditions end | the self-substance |

|   +-- Kaivalya (Sāṃkhya-Yoga): non-discrimination ends | puruṣa isolated | YES, contentless.
|   +-- Liberation/siddhahood (Jainism): karmic matter ends | perfected omniscient jīva |

|   +-- Nirvāṇa (Buddhism): craving, ignorance and suffering end | not stated as a self |
|       neither survival- nor annihilation-language.
|   +-- Resurrection (Semitic): death ends | the embodied person | YES, before God.
|   +-- TRAP: Jain kevala = OMNISCIENCE; Sāṃkhya kaivalya = CONTENTLESS ISOLATION.

+-- X. VEDĀNTA — ONE FORK — 2021 Q6(b), 2022 Q7(b), 2025 Q5(d)
|   +-- Advaita: bondage = ignorance (avidyā) -> REMOVAL model -> knowledge is the SOLE
|       operative cause -> individuality SUBLATED (not annihilated) -> jīvanmukti NOW.
|       +-- Body continues by prārabdha; potter's wheel image; videhamukti at its fall.
|       +-- Knowledge destroys sañcita and āgāmi, NEVER prārabdha.
|       +-- Preparation: citta-śuddhi, sādhana-catuṣṭaya, śravaṇa-manana-nididhyāsana.

|   +-- Viśiṣṭādvaita: bondage = karma and dependence -> ATTAINMENT model -> knowledge,
|       devotion (dhruvānusmṛti), self-surrender (prapatti), grace -> individuality PRESERVED.
|       +-- The soul REACHES God; it never BECOMES God.
|   +-- Real-agent question: Advaita says liberation without a real agent is possible and
|       necessary; Viśiṣṭādvaita says an unreal agent cannot strive, love or be rewarded.

+-- XI. JAINISM — SOUL AND BONDAGE — 2022 Q8(b)
|   +-- Jīva: eternal, conscious, uncreated, no creator God; intrinsic ananta-catuṣṭaya;
|       CO-EXTENSIVE with the body it occupies (extension is a MODE during bondage).
|   +-- Ladder: passions (kaṣāya) -> inflow (āsrava) -> bondage (bandha) -> stoppage (saṃvara)

|   +-- Karma is quasi-material (karma-pudgala): nature, duration, intensity, quantity.

|   +-- Interaction objection answered by modal extension; cost: a substance neither cleanly
|       material nor cleanly spiritual. Liberation PERFECTS individuality.

|   +-- Sāṃkhya: puruṣa (plural, inactive, contentless witness) vs prakṛti (one, unconscious,

+-- QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
    +-- Immortality, rebirth and liberation form no single package. A deathless substance

        Buddhism shows that causal continuity preserves karma and rebirth without any
        substance, and Sāṃkhya-kārikā 62-63 denies transmigration even to an eternal
