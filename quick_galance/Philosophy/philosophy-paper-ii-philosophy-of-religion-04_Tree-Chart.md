# Philosophy Optional — Paper II — Philosophy of Religion 04 — Soul: Immortality, Rebirth and Liberation

## Quick-Glance Tree Chart

```text
SOUL, IMMORTALITY, REBIRTH AND LIBERATION
|
+-- CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
|   +-- Immortality asks whether a person/self survives death; rebirth asks how a new
|       embodiment is causally continuous with an earlier life; liberation asks how
|       bondage, ignorance or suffering finally ends.
|   +-- The doctrines are separable: Buddhism affirms rebirth without immortal substance,
|       Nyāya affirms an enduring self but defines release as cessation, and Vedānta/Jainism
|       offer distinct fulfilment-rich accounts.
|
+-- SCOPE / OWNERSHIP FIREWALL
|   +-- Owns: soul/self/person distinctions; Plato's immortality arguments; Hindu karma,
|       rebirth/reincarnation; Buddhist continuity without self; Jaina jīva; Nyāya apavarga;
|       Sāṃkhya-Yoga kaivalya; Advaita jīvanmukti; Viśiṣṭādvaita/Dvaita liberation;
|       bhakti, jñāna and karma paths; resurrection comparison.
|   +-- Detailed school metaphysics remain with Paper I owners; religious experience and
|       God arguments are separate.
|
+-- SOURCE / ATTRIBUTION CAUTION
|   +-- Plato: Phaedo; Bhagavad Gītā; Upaniṣadic/Vedānta traditions; Buddhist dependent
|       origination; Jaina Tattvārtha tradition; Nyāya and Sāṃkhya-Yoga sources.
|   +-- Hinduism has no single identical rebirth/liberation model.
|   +-- Reincarnation, rebirth, resurrection and immortality must not be used interchangeably.
|   +-- Paranormal reports are not needed and must not substitute for philosophical argument.
|
+-- I. CONCEPTUAL MAP
|   +-- Soul:
|       +-- Substantial, enduring bearer of consciousness/identity in many traditions.
|   +-- Self:
|       +-- May mean witness, empirical ego, stream or personal subject.
|   +-- Person:
|       +-- Moral/social identity, not always metaphysical substance.
|   +-- Continuity-bearer:
|       +-- Soul, memory, causal stream, subtle body or divine reconstitution.
|
+-- II. IMMORTALITY
|   +-- Personal survival after bodily death.
|   +-- Numerical identity must be distinguished from:
|       +-- Similar successor.
|       +-- Influence/reputation.
|       +-- Species/cosmic continuity.
|   +-- Religious functions:
|       +-- Moral accountability.
|       +-- Hope/meaning.
|       +-- Communion or liberation.
|   +-- It is not a necessary presupposition of every religion because Buddhism and some
|       non-theistic traditions organise religion without an eternal soul.
|
+-- III. PLATO'S PHAEDO ARGUMENTS
|   +-- Cyclical/opposites:
|       +-- Living comes from dead as waking from sleep.
|       +-- Critique: transition does not prove same soul survives.
|   +-- Recollection:
|       +-- Knowledge of equality itself exceeds imperfect sensibles; soul knew Forms before birth.
|       +-- Critique: conceptual capacity need not prove prenatal existence.
|   +-- Affinity:
|       +-- Soul resembles invisible, intelligible, stable Forms more than divisible body.
|       +-- Critique: resemblance does not establish indestructibility.
|   +-- Final/life argument:
|       +-- Soul essentially brings life and cannot admit death.
|       +-- Critique: carrier of life may cease rather than instantiate opposite.
|   +-- Arguments are a priori/metaphysical, not empirical survival proof.
|
+-- IV. KARMA–SAṂSĀRA
|   +-- Intentional action has morally structured consequence.
|   +-- Saṃsāra: repeated birth/death under ignorance/desire/action.
|   +-- Rebirth connects lives through:
|       +-- Enduring self plus subtle body in many Hindu systems.
|       +-- Causal continuum without self in Buddhism.
|       +-- Jīva with material karma in Jainism.
|   +-- Karma is not fate:
|       +-- Present action can alter future conditions.
|   +-- Nor does it justify social indifference/victim-blaming.
|
+-- V. HINDU REBIRTH / REINCARNATION
|   +-- Bhagavad Gītā:
|       +-- Embodied self passes through bodily stages and bodies; ātman not destroyed.
|   +-- Subtle body/dispositions mediate continuity in several systems.
|   +-- Reincarnation often implies same enduring self in another body.
|   +-- Rebirth is broader and can occur without substance identity.
|   +-- Exact mechanisms differ across Sāṃkhya, Nyāya and Vedānta.
|
+-- VI. BUDDHISM
|   +-- No permanent ātman.
|   +-- Five-aggregate continuum conditioned by craving/karma.
|   +-- Neither strictly same nor wholly different successor.
|   +-- Flame/seed causal analogies.
|   +-- Immortal soul is not necessary for rebirth if causal continuity suffices.
|   +-- Objection: responsibility without identity.
|   +-- Reply: later experiencer inherits uniquely connected causal stream.
|   +-- Residual: whether causal continuity supports "my" survival remains disputed.
|
+-- VII. JAINISM
|   +-- Many eternal conscious jīvas.
|   +-- Karmic pudgala binds and obscures intrinsic capacities.
|   +-- Saṃvara stops influx; nirjarā sheds accumulated karma.
|   +-- Liberation yields siddha with individuality, infinite knowledge/perception/bliss/energy.
|   +-- No creator needed.
|
+-- VIII. NYĀYA APAVARGA
|   +-- Enduring plural self bears cognition, desire, karma and memory.
|   +-- True knowledge ends defect/action/rebirth chain.
|   +-- Apavarga: complete cessation of suffering.
|   +-- Classical liberated self lacks embodied pleasure/pain/cognition.
|   +-- Stone-like objection:
|       +-- Cessation appears empty.
|   +-- Reply:
|       +-- Absolute freedom from suffering is intrinsically desirable.
|
+-- IX. SĀṂKHYA–YOGA KAIVALYA
|   +-- Puruṣa is eternally pure witness.
|   +-- Bondage is identification with Prakṛti/citta.
|   +-- Viveka-khyāti removes confusion.
|   +-- Guṇas cease activity for liberated Puruṣa.
|   +-- Kaivalya: isolation, not merger or heavenly continuation.
|
+-- X. ADVAITA MOKṢA
|   +-- Ātman is Brahman; bondage is avidyā/adhyāsa.
|   +-- Jñāna directly liberates.
|   +-- Prāptasya prāpti: attaining what is already attained.
|   +-- Jīvanmukti:
|       +-- Liberated while embodied.
|       +-- Prārabdha karma sustains body until death.
|   +-- Videhamukti:
|       +-- No further embodied individuality after residual karma.
|   +-- Objection: if ignorance gone, why embodiment?
|   +-- Reply: released-arrow/potter-wheel momentum of prārabdha.
|
+-- XI. VIŚIṢṬĀDVAITA / DVAITA
|   +-- Viśiṣṭādvaita:
|       +-- Real dependent jīva retains identity.
|       +-- Devotion/surrender/grace; communion and service to Brahman.
|   +-- Dvaita:
|       +-- Eternal difference from God.
|       +-- Bhakti/grace and blissful service; possible tāratamya.
|   +-- Liberation without loss of agent is coherent here because self is real.
|   +-- Contrast Advaita:
|       +-- Empirical agent sublated in non-dual knowledge.
|
+-- XII. JÑĀNA / KARMA / BHAKTI
|   +-- Jñāna:
|       +-- Transformative knowledge of self/ultimate.
|   +-- Karma:
|       +-- Duty/action purifies and orders life; often indirect preparation.
|   +-- Bhakti:
|       +-- Loving devotion, surrender and grace.
|   +-- Advaita:
|       +-- Knowledge direct; action/devotion prepare mind.
|   +-- Viśiṣṭādvaita/Dvaita:
|       +-- Devotion and grace constitutive, with knowledge/action integrated.
|   +-- Bhagavad Gītā:
|       +-- Multiple yogas coordinated rather than mechanically identical.
|
+-- XIII. RESURRECTION / REBIRTH
|   +-- Resurrection:
|       +-- Divine restoration/reconstitution, typically one earthly life and eschatological event.
|   +-- Rebirth:
|       +-- Repeated embodiment through karma.
|   +-- Immortality:
|       +-- Continued existence, potentially disembodied.
|   +-- Reincarnation:
|       +-- Same soul taking another body in common usage.
|   +-- These answer different identity and agency questions.
|
+-- OBJECTIONS / REPLIES
|   +-- No memory means no same person -> memory is neither necessary nor sufficient for
|       identity; causal/substantial criteria proposed.
|   +-- Karma is unverifiable -> traditions treat it as metaphysical-moral explanation,
|       not simple observed law.
|   +-- Liberation without agent incoherent -> Buddhism distinguishes conventional stream
|       and absence of permanent substance.
|   +-- Cessation-only liberation unattractive -> freedom from suffering is not worldly pleasure.
|   +-- Jīvanmukti contradicts continued action -> prārabdha/body momentum distinction.
|
+-- VERIFIED THINKER / TEXT / EXAMPLE BANK
|   +-- Plato: Phaedo; Bhagavad Gītā; Śaṅkara; Rāmānuja; Madhva; Nyāya, Jaina,
|       Buddhist and Sāṃkhya-Yoga canonical traditions.
|   +-- Examples: flame transfer, seed/fruit, worn garments, released arrow, potter's wheel.
|   +-- Analogies do not independently prove survival; no fabricated quotations.
|
+-- HIGH-RISK CONCEPTUAL TRAPS
|   +-- Soul, self, person and stream are not synonyms.
|   +-- Immortality is not rebirth; rebirth is not resurrection.
|   +-- Buddhism does not teach immortal soul.
|   +-- No-self does not mean no causal/moral continuity.
|   +-- Karma is not fatalism or licence for victim-blaming.
|   +-- Jaina jīva is not Advaita ātman.
|   +-- Apavarga is not positive sensory bliss.
|   +-- Kaivalya is not divine union.
|   +-- Jīvanmukti is not bodily immortality.
|   +-- Bhakti has different metaphysical meaning across schools.

+-- QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
    +-- Immortality, rebirth and liberation form no single package. An enduring self makes
        reincarnation intuitive but inherits evidence and identity problems; Buddhism shows
        how causal continuity can preserve karma without substance; Indian liberation
        theories range from cessation and isolation to identity and communion. A strong
        evaluation therefore asks separately what survives, what transmits consequence,
        what bondage is and whether freedom requires a persisting agent.
```
