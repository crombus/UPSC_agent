# Philosophy Optional — Paper I — Indian Philosophy 06 — Yoga

## Quick-Glance Tree Chart

```text
PĀTAÑJALA YOGA: CITTA, DISCIPLINE, SAMĀDHI, ĪŚVARA AND KAIVALYA
|
+-- CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
|   +-- Yoga is the disciplined cessation of citta-vṛttis so that Puruṣa abides in its
|       own nature rather than identifying with mental modifications.
|   +-- It shares Sāṃkhya's Puruṣa–Prakṛti ontology but adds a systematic psychology,
|       ethical-meditative practice and Īśvara as a special Puruṣa and privileged aid.
|   +-- Samādhi weakens seeds of affliction, but stable discriminative knowledge finally
|       ends misidentification and returns the guṇas to quiescence for that Puruṣa.
|
+-- SCOPE / OWNERSHIP FIREWALL
|   +-- Owns: citta; vṛtti; nirodha; five citta-bhūmis; five vṛttis; abhyāsa/vairāgya;
|   |   five kleśas; karma-vipāka; kriyā-yoga; aṣṭāṅga-yoga; yama/niyama; dhāraṇā,
|   |   dhyāna, samādhi; saṃyama; samprajñāta/asamprajñāta/nirbīja samādhi;
|   |   samāpatti; ṛtambharā prajñā; dharmamegha; viveka-khyāti; pratiprasava;
|   |   Īśvara as puruṣa-viśeṣa; praṇava; kaivalya.
|   +-- Sāṃkhya owns full 25-tattva derivation, guṇa metaphysics, satkāryavāda and its
|   |   independent proofs.
|   +-- Vedānta owns Brahman-union; Buddhist schools own no-self and śūnyatā.
|   +-- Modern postural yoga, wellness claims and sectarian devotional overlays are
|       outside unless explicitly compared.
|
+-- SOURCE / ATTRIBUTION CAUTION
|   +-- Patañjali: Yoga-sūtra, organised in Samādhi, Sādhana, Vibhūti and Kaivalya pādas.
|   +-- Vyāsa's Yoga-bhāṣya is foundational for classical interpretation.
|   +-- Vācaspati Miśra's Tattvavaiśāradī and Vijñānabhikṣu represent later commentary.
|   +-- "Yoga is cessation of mental modifications" is a careful translation of the
|       foundational formula, not licence to equate nirodha with destruction of mind.
|   +-- Samādhi taxonomies derive from different sūtra sequences; do not collapse the
|       YS 1.17 progression into the savitarka-to-nirvicāra samāpatti list.
|
+-- I. YOGA AS SEŚVARA-SĀṂKHYA
|   +-- Shared:
|   |   +-- Many Puruṣas.
|   |   +-- One Prakṛti with three guṇas.
|   |   +-- Tattva evolution.
|   |   +-- Bondage through non-discrimination.
|   |   +-- Kaivalya through viveka.
|   +-- Distinct:
|       +-- Īśvara as puruṣa-viśeṣa.
|       +-- Fine psychology of citta and saṃskāra.
|       +-- Eight-limbed practical method.
|       +-- Detailed samādhi map.
|
+-- II. CITTA
|   +-- Inner psychomental apparatus dominated by sattva but constituted by all guṇas.
|   +-- Functional convergence of buddhi, ahaṃkāra and manas.
|   +-- Receives object-forms and becomes modified.
|   +-- Not self-luminous:
|   |   +-- As a Prakṛti product, it is unconscious.
|   |   +-- It appears conscious by reflecting Puruṣa.
|   +-- Like a transparent crystal, it takes the colour of:
|   |   +-- External object.
|   |   +-- Puruṣa's reflected consciousness.
|   +-- Misidentification:
|       +-- Puruṣa appears to think, desire and suffer.
|       +-- Citta appears to be conscious subject.
|
+-- III. FIVE CITTA-BHŪMIS
|   +-- Kṣipta:
|   |   +-- Restless, scattered, rajas-dominated.
|   +-- Mūḍha:
|   |   +-- Dull, inert, tamas-dominated.
|   +-- Vikṣipta:
|   |   +-- Occasionally steady but repeatedly distracted.
|   +-- Ekāgra:
|   |   +-- One-pointed; sustained concentration possible.
|   +-- Niruddha:
|       +-- Modifications restrained; seedless culmination approached.
|   +-- Yoga in strict meditative sense becomes stable in ekāgra and niruddha.
|   +-- Application:
|       +-- Scientist may display ekāgra concentration without liberation.
|       +-- Devotee may attain one-pointed God-orientation.
|       +-- Self-realised yogi requires discriminative knowledge beyond concentration.
|
+-- IV. CITTA-VṚTTI-NIRODHA
|   +-- Vṛtti:
|   |   +-- Functional modification by which citta takes an object/form.
|   +-- Nirodha:
|   |   +-- Restraint, stilling or cessation of operative fluctuations.
|   |   +-- Not annihilation of citta as a substance.
|   +-- When restrained:
|   |   +-- Seer abides in own form.
|   +-- Otherwise:
|       +-- Seer appears identified with vṛttis.
|   +-- Even valid cognition is a vṛtti:
|       +-- It may be akliṣṭa and instrumentally liberating.
|       +-- It must finally cease because every mental form is not Puruṣa.
|
+-- V. FIVE VṚTTIS
|   +-- Pramāṇa:
|   |   +-- Valid cognition through perception, inference and testimony.
|   +-- Viparyaya:
|   |   +-- False cognition not based on object's real form.
|   +-- Vikalpa:
|   |   +-- Verbal/conceptual construction lacking corresponding object.
|   +-- Nidrā:
|   |   +-- Sleep modification based on cognition of absence.
|   +-- Smṛti:
|       +-- Non-loss and re-presentation of experienced content.
|   +-- Each may be:
|       +-- Kliṣṭa: afflicted, bondage-producing.
|       +-- Akliṣṭa: non-afflicted, conducive to clarity.
|
+-- VI. ABHYĀSA AND VAIRĀGYA
|   +-- Abhyāsa:
|   |   +-- Sustained effort to remain in steadiness.
|   |   +-- Firm when practised long, uninterruptedly and reverentially.
|   +-- Vairāgya:
|   |   +-- Mastery over thirst for seen and scripturally promised objects.
|   +-- Lower dispassion:
|   |   +-- Freedom from ordinary objects.
|   +-- Para-vairāgya:
|       +-- Non-attachment even to guṇas through Puruṣa-knowledge.
|   +-- Practice stabilises; dispassion removes fuel.
|
+-- VII. CITTA-PARIṆĀMA
|   +-- Nirodha-pariṇāma:
|   |   +-- Emergence of restraint-impressions over outgoing impressions.
|   +-- Samādhi-pariṇāma:
|   |   +-- Distraction declines and one-pointedness rises.
|   +-- Ekāgratā-pariṇāma:
|       +-- Similar subsiding and arising cognitions maintain one object.
|   +-- Transformation is lawful restructuring of citta, not Puruṣa's transformation.
|
+-- VIII. FIVE KLEŚAS
|   +-- Avidyā:
|   |   +-- Root confusion of impermanent/permanent, impure/pure, painful/pleasant,
|   |       non-self/self.
|   +-- Asmitā:
|   |   +-- Identification of seer with instrument of seeing.
|   +-- Rāga:
|   |   +-- Attachment following pleasure.
|   +-- Dveṣa:
|   |   +-- Aversion following pain.
|   +-- Abhiniveśa:
|       +-- Clinging to life/fear of death, present even in learned persons.
|   +-- Avidyā conditions all four.
|
+-- IX. STATES OF KLEŚAS
|   +-- Prasupta:
|   |   +-- Dormant.
|   +-- Tanu:
|   |   +-- Attenuated.
|   +-- Vicchinna:
|   |   +-- Interrupted by an opposing affliction.
|   +-- Udāra:
|       +-- Fully active.
|   +-- Suppression is not eradication.
|   +-- Meditation burns subtle seeds; discriminative knowledge prevents recurrence.
|
+-- X. BONDAGE CHAIN
|   +-- Avidyā -> asmitā -> attachment/aversion/clinging.
|   +-- Kleśa -> karma.
|   +-- Karma deposits saṃskāra in citta.
|   +-- Karmāśaya -> birth, lifespan and experience.
|   +-- Experience triggers further reaction.
|   +-- Vṛtti and saṃskāra reinforce each other.
|   +-- Nirodha and viveka break the cycle at psychological and cognitive roots.
|
+-- XI. KRIYĀ-YOGA
|   +-- Tapas:
|   |   +-- Disciplined effort / purification.
|   +-- Svādhyāya:
|   |   +-- Study, recitation and reflective self-study.
|   +-- Īśvara-praṇidhāna:
|       +-- Dedication/surrender to Īśvara.
|   +-- Purposes:
|       +-- Cultivate samādhi.
|       +-- Attenuate kleśas.
|   +-- It is preparatory and integrated, not a substitute for discriminative insight.
|
+-- XII. AṢṬĀṄGA-YOGA
|   +-- 1. Yama:
|   |   +-- Ahiṃsā, satya, asteya, brahmacarya, aparigraha.
|   |   +-- Universal great vows, not local etiquette.
|   +-- 2. Niyama:
|   |   +-- Śauca, santoṣa, tapas, svādhyāya, Īśvara-praṇidhāna.
|   +-- 3. Āsana:
|   |   +-- Stable and comfortable posture.
|   +-- 4. Prāṇāyāma:
|   |   +-- Regulation of inhalation, exhalation and retention.
|   +-- 5. Pratyāhāra:
|   |   +-- Withdrawal of senses from independent object-following.
|   +-- 6. Dhāraṇā:
|   |   +-- Binding citta to one locus.
|   +-- 7. Dhyāna:
|   |   +-- Continuous flow of cognition toward that object.
|   +-- 8. Samādhi:
|       +-- Object alone shines forth; self-referential awareness recedes.
|   +-- First five are bahiraṅga relative to final three.
|   +-- Dhāraṇā + dhyāna + samādhi on one object = saṃyama.
|   +-- Ethical limbs are constitutive safeguards, not optional preliminaries.
|
+-- XIII. SAMPRAJÑĀTA SAMĀDHI
|   +-- Cognitive / object-supported absorption.
|   +-- Seed remains.
|   +-- YS 1.17 fourfold character:
|   |   +-- Vitarka: gross-object engagement.
|   |   +-- Vicāra: subtle-object engagement.
|   |   +-- Ānanda: joy associated with refined sattva.
|   |   +-- Asmitā: absorption in pure I-am-ness of refined citta.
|   +-- Do not call these four wholly separate unrelated samādhis without qualification.
|
+-- XIV. SAMĀPATTI TAXONOMY
|   +-- Savitarka:
|   |   +-- Gross object with word, meaning and knowledge mixed.
|   +-- Nirvitarka:
|   |   +-- Gross object free from discursive admixture.
|   +-- Savicāra:
|   |   +-- Subtle object with reflective construction.
|   +-- Nirvicāra:
|       +-- Subtle object free from such construction.
|   +-- These concern object-refinement.
|   +-- They are not simply identical to the vitarka-vicāra-ānanda-asmitā sequence,
|       though commentators relate them.
|
+-- XV. ṚTAMBHARĀ PRAJÑĀ
|   +-- Truth-bearing wisdom arising from purified nirvicāra absorption.
|   +-- Differs from verbal and inferential knowledge:
|   |   +-- Directly discloses particular object.
|   |   +-- Not mediated by general concepts.
|   +-- Produces a saṃskāra that checks ordinary saṃskāras.
|   +-- Its own seed must eventually cease for nirbīja samādhi.
|   +-- It is not Aurobindo's Supermind; resemblance is only controlled comparison.
|
+-- XVI. ASAMPRAJÑĀTA AND NIRBĪJA
|   +-- Asamprajñāta:
|   |   +-- Supra-cognitive absorption beyond object-presenting awareness.
|   |   +-- Arises through cessation-practice and supreme dispassion.
|   |   +-- Residual saṃskāras may remain in some commentarial readings.
|   +-- Nirbīja:
|       +-- Seedless samādhi when even restraint-producing seed ceases.
|   +-- Not sleep:
|       +-- Sleep is a tamasic vṛtti with absence-content.
|       +-- Asamprajñāta follows disciplined clarity and dispassion.
|   +-- Bhava-pratyaya route:
|       +-- Associated with certain disembodied/nature-merged beings; not secure final
|           discriminative release.
|   +-- Upāya-pratyaya route:
|       +-- Faith, energy, memory, samādhi and wisdom.
|
+-- XVII. DHARMAMEGHA-SAMĀDHI
|   +-- "Cloud of dharma" culmination.
|   +-- Arises when yogin remains disinterested even in highest discriminative knowledge
|       and powers.
|   +-- Kleśas and karmas cease.
|   +-- Knowledge becomes effectively unbounded relative to little remaining knowable.
|   +-- Guṇas complete their purpose.
|   +-- Not identical by definition with every use of asamprajñāta or nirbīja.
|   +-- It marks final transition within Kaivalya-pāda's liberation account.
|
+-- XVIII. SIDDHIS
|   +-- Extraordinary capacities arise from birth, herbs, mantra, austerity or samādhi.
|   +-- Saṃyama produces specialised knowledge/powers.
|   +-- They are achievements relative to ordinary mind.
|   +-- They are obstacles if appropriated by ego or mistaken for liberation.
|   +-- Demonstrative supernatural claims are text-owned but not independently verified;
|       distinguish doctrinal reporting from empirical endorsement.
|
+-- XIX. ĪŚVARA
|   +-- Puruṣa-viśeṣa:
|   |   +-- A special Puruṣa.
|   +-- Untouched by:
|   |   +-- Kleśa.
|   |   +-- Karma.
|   |   +-- Vipāka.
|   |   +-- Latent karmic deposits.
|   +-- Seed of unsurpassed omniscience reaches limit in Īśvara.
|   +-- Teacher even of ancient teachers because unconditioned by time.
|   +-- Praṇava / Oṃ is its designator.
|   +-- Practice:
|       +-- Repetition and contemplation of meaning.
|       +-- Īśvara-praṇidhāna helps remove obstacles and attain samādhi.
|
+-- XX. WHAT YOGA'S ĪŚVARA IS NOT
|   +-- Not one of ordinary bound Puruṣas.
|   +-- Not Prakṛti.
|   +-- Not material cause of world.
|   +-- Not necessarily creator/atom-arranger in Nyāya's sense.
|   +-- Not a Vaiṣṇava personal saviour by definition of the classical system.
|   +-- Does not erase the need for practice, dispassion and viveka-khyāti.
|
+-- XXI. GROUNDS AND PHILOSOPHICAL ROLE OF ĪŚVARA
|   +-- Gradation of knowledge points to unsurpassed omniscience.
|   +-- Timeless teacher anchors transmission.
|   +-- Devotional concentration supplies exceptionally powerful object.
|   +-- Objection:
|   |   +-- If ontology and liberation work through Sāṃkhya principles, Īśvara is redundant.
|   +-- Reply:
|   |   +-- Instrumentally optional does not mean philosophically meaningless.
|   |   +-- Īśvara is a uniquely effective means and exemplar of unbound consciousness.
|   +-- Residual:
|       +-- Text permits multiple means; necessity of Īśvara remains debated.
|
+-- XXII. KAIVALYA
|   +-- Isolation of Puruṣa from guṇa-identification.
|   +-- Puruṣa is already pure; bondage is reflected misidentification.
|   +-- Process:
|   |   +-- Ethical purification.
|   |   +-- Attenuation of kleśas.
|   |   +-- Concentration and samādhi.
|   |   +-- Viveka-khyāti: uninterrupted discrimination between sattva/citta and Puruṣa.
|   |   +-- Para-vairāgya.
|   |   +-- Dharmamegha.
|   |   +-- Pratiprasava: guṇa effects return toward source after purpose ends.
|   +-- Svarūpa-pratiṣṭhā:
|   |   +-- Consciousness-power established in own nature.
|   +-- Not:
|       +-- Brahman-union.
|       +-- Creation of a new self.
|       +-- Mere trance.
|       +-- Annihilation.
|
+-- XXIII. FARMER, BURNT SEED AND POTTER'S WHEEL
|   +-- Farmer:
|   |   +-- Does not manufacture water; removes barrier.
|   |   +-- Practice removes obstacles to Prakṛti's purified unfolding.
|   +-- Burnt seed:
|   |   +-- Kleśa/karma seed deprived of future germination.
|   +-- Potter's wheel:
|       +-- Residual body-mind process continues after liberating insight until momentum ends.
|
+-- XXIV. INTER-SCHOOL COMPARISONS
|   +-- Yoga versus Sāṃkhya:
|   |   +-- Shared metaphysics; Yoga adds disciplined method and special Puruṣa.
|   +-- Yoga versus Nyāya:
|   |   +-- Both realist and theistic in later forms.
|   |   +-- Nyāya God orders world; Yoga Īśvara chiefly models/aids liberation.
|   +-- Yoga versus Advaita:
|   |   +-- Plural Puruṣas and real Prakṛti versus non-dual Brahman.
|   |   +-- Kaivalya versus identity-realisation.
|   +-- Yoga versus Buddhism:
|   |   +-- Both analyse mental conditioning and meditation.
|   |   +-- Yoga affirms permanent witness; Buddhism denies substantial self.
|   +-- Yoga versus Aurobindo:
|       +-- Classical isolation and cessation versus integral transformation and divine
|           descent into life.
|
+-- XXV. CLASSIC OBJECTIONS / REPLIES
|   +-- Pure Puruṣa cannot become bound.
|   |   +-- Reply: bondage is misidentification in citta, not real alteration.
|   +-- Unconscious citta cannot cognise.
|   |   +-- Reply: it becomes illuminated by reflected consciousness.
|   |   +-- Residual: reflection relation remains difficult.
|   +-- Valid cognition should not be suppressed.
|   |   +-- Reply: it removes error but remains a non-self mental form.
|   +-- Asamprajñāta is unconsciousness.
|   |   +-- Reply: its cause, clarity and aftermath differ from sleep.
|   +-- Siddhis validate fantasy.
|   |   +-- Reply: classical text subordinates powers to liberation.
|   +-- Īśvara is redundant.
|   |   +-- Reply: practical indispensability for some need not mean material causality.
|   +-- Kaivalya is negative isolation.
|       +-- Reply: absence of suffering and establishment in pure consciousness is not a
|           privation from Yoga's standpoint.
|

+-- QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
    +-- Yoga's distinct achievement is to turn Sāṃkhya's dualism into a disciplined
        psychology of liberation: ethics, attention, absorption and insight progressively
        weaken the causal machinery of bondage. Its account of citta and saṃskāra explains
        why intellectual assent alone is insufficient. Yet reflected consciousness, the
        precise relation among highest samādhis and the necessity of Īśvara remain debated.
        Kaivalya therefore rests not on trance alone but on irreversible discrimination
        between the luminous witness and every transformation of nature.
```
