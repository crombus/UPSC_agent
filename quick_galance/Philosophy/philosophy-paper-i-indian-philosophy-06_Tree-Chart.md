PĀTAÑJALA YOGA: CITTA, DISCIPLINE, SAMĀDHI, ĪŚVARA AND KAIVALYA
|
+-- CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
|   +-- Yoga is the disciplined restraint of citta-vṛttis so that Puruṣa abides in its
|       own nature rather than identifying with mental modifications.
|   +-- It shares Sāṃkhya's Puruṣa–Prakṛti ontology but adds a systematic psychology,
|       ethical-meditative practice and Īśvara as a special Puruṣa and privileged aid.
|   +-- Samādhi matures rather than replaces knowledge: stable viveka-khyāti finally
|       ends misidentification and returns the guṇas to quiescence for that Puruṣa.
|
+-- SCOPE / OWNERSHIP FIREWALL
|   +-- Owns: citta; vṛtti; nirodha; five citta-bhūmis; five vṛttis; kliṣṭa/akliṣṭa;
|   |   abhyāsa/vairāgya; citta-pariṇāma; five kleśas and their four states;
|   |   karmāśaya, vipāka, saṃskāra, vāsanā; kriyā-yoga; aṣṭāṅga-yoga; yama/niyama;
|   |   saṃyama; antarāyas; samprajñāta/asamprajñāta/nirbīja; samāpatti;
|   |   ṛtambharā prajñā; dharmamegha; siddhis; Īśvara as puruṣa-viśeṣa; praṇava;
|   |   viveka-khyāti; pratiprasava; kaivalya; Kaivalya-pāda realism.
|   +-- Sāṃkhya owns the full 25-tattva derivation, guṇa metaphysics, satkāryavāda and
|   |   its independent proofs. Yoga restates only what its psychology needs.
|   +-- Vedānta owns Brahman-union; Buddhist schools own no-self and śūnyatā;
|   |   Nyāya owns the creator-God proof-set and the category scheme.
|   +-- Modern postural yoga, wellness claims and sectarian devotional overlays are
|       outside the topic unless explicitly compared.
|
+-- SOURCE / ATTRIBUTION CAUTION
|   +-- Patañjali: Yoga-sūtra, in Samādhi, Sādhana, Vibhūti and Kaivalya pādas.
|   +-- Vyāsa's Yoga-bhāṣya is the classical interpretive baseline.
|   +-- Vācaspati Miśra's Tattvavaiśāradī and Vijñānabhikṣu's Yogavārttika are later
|   |   commentary.
|   +-- COMMENTARIAL, NOT A SŪTRA LIST:
|   |   +-- the five citta-bhūmis (standard from Vyāsa onward);
|   |   +-- the four stages of lower vairāgya (yatamāna, vyatireka, ekendriya,
|   |   |   vaśīkāra);
|   |   +-- the expanded theoretical proof-set for Īśvara.
|   +-- "Yoga is cessation of mental modifications" is a careful translation of the
|   |   foundational formula, not licence to equate nirodha with destruction of mind.
|   +-- Samādhi taxonomies come from two different sūtra sequences; never collapse the
|   |   YS 1.17 factor-list into the savitarkā-to-nirvicārā samāpatti list.
|   +-- Historical layering and dating of the sūtra tradition remain disputed. Identify
|       the received Pātañjala system; never build an answer on a date.
|
+-- I. YOGA AS SEŚVARA-SĀṂKHYA
|   +-- Also called sāṃkhya-pravacana, Sāṃkhya-exposition.
|   +-- Definition and its two companions:
|   |   +-- YS 1.2 yogaś-citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ — Yoga is restraint of the modifications
|   |   |   of citta. A STATE, not an activity.
|   |   +-- YS 1.3 tadā draṣṭuḥ svarūpe'vasthānam — then the seer abides in its own form.
|   |   +-- YS 1.4 otherwise the seer takes the form of the vṛttis. That is bondage.
|   +-- Shared with Sāṃkhya:
|   |   +-- Many Puruṣas; one Prakṛti with three guṇas; 25 tattvas.
|   |   +-- Satkāryavāda in the form of pariṇāmavāda.
|   |   +-- Bondage through non-discrimination; kaivalya through viveka.
|   +-- Distinct to Yoga:
|       +-- Īśvara as puruṣa-viśeṣa (hence seśvara against Sāṃkhya's nirīśvara).
|       +-- Fine psychology of citta, vṛtti and saṃskāra.
|       +-- Eight-limbed practical method and kriyā-yoga.
|       +-- Detailed samādhi map and yogi-pratyakṣa.
|
+-- II. TEXTUAL MAP — THE FOUR PĀDAS
|   +-- Samādhi-pāda: definition, five vṛttis, abhyāsa/vairāgya, Īśvara, sabīja and
|   |   nirbīja absorption, samāpatti, ṛtambharā.
|   +-- Sādhana-pāda: kriyā-yoga, five kleśas and four states, karma and vipāka,
|   |   the seen and the seer, the eight limbs.
|   +-- Vibhūti-pāda: saṃyama, the three citta-pariṇāmas, siddhis and their demotion.
|   +-- Kaivalya-pāda: mind/object realism, vāsanās, viveka-khyāti, dharmamegha,
|       pratiprasava, kaivalya.
|
+-- III. EPISTEMOLOGICAL FRAME
|   +-- Three pramāṇas only, as in Sāṃkhya:
|   |   +-- Pratyakṣa: perception.
|   |   +-- Anumāna: inference.
|   |   +-- Āgama / śabda: authoritative verbal testimony.
|   +-- Counts to keep apart: Yoga 3 · Sāṃkhya 3 · Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika 4 · Buddhist
|   |   epistemologists commonly 2 · Advaita commonly 6.
|   +-- Pramāṇa is itself one of the five vṛttis: valid cognition is still a mental form.
|   +-- Ṛtambharā prajñā is NOT a fourth pramāṇa. It is best classified as perfected
|       extraordinary yogic perception (yogi-pratyakṣa), and it too must finally cease.
|
+-- IV. CITTA
|   +-- Inner psychomental apparatus: buddhi + ahaṃkāra + manas as one operative
|   |   continuum, not manas alone.
|   +-- A product of Prakṛti and therefore jaḍa (insentient) in itself.
|   +-- Sattva-predominant AS A WORKING WHOLE, hence transparent and able to take
|   |   object-form and to mirror consciousness.
|   +-- Derivation precision:
|   |   +-- Prakṛti -> buddhi -> ahaṃkāra -> manas from the sattva-predominant side of
|   |   |   ahaṃkāra.
|   |   +-- ONLY manas derives directly from sattva-ahaṃkāra. Do not derive all three
|   |       components from sattva-predominant ahaṃkāra.
|   +-- Contrasts:
|   |   +-- Sāṃkhya keeps buddhi, ahaṃkāra and manas analytically distinct.
|   |   +-- Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika has an atomic, partless manas plus a separate self-substance.
|   |   +-- Advaita has antaḥkaraṇa under avidyā inside a Brahman framework; Yoga has
|   |       many Puruṣas and no Brahman doctrine.
|   +-- Soteriological point: bondage runs through citta, so release must be worked
|       there. Puruṣa is unmodifiable and offers no surface for work.
|
+-- V. REFLECTION, PROXIMITY AND ASMITĀ
|   +-- Double borrowing:
|   |   +-- citta takes the COLOUR of objects (uparāga) — that is cognition.
|   |   +-- citta takes the LIGHT of Puruṣa (pratibimba / chāyā) — that is the sense of
|   |       being someone.
|   +-- Bondage = borrowed light read as owned light + witnesshood read as agency.
|   |   +-- Technical name: asmitā; described as sva-svāmi-śakti confusion.
|   +-- Protective clause: sannidhi-mātra (mere proximity). ALL transformation is on the
|   |   citta side; Puruṣa is apariṇāmin and neither acts nor changes.
|   +-- Images: sphaṭika (clear crystal) beside a red flower; magnet moving iron by
|   |   mere presence.
|   +-- Objection: reflection normally presupposes a real relation; disjoint principles
|   |   have none.
|   +-- Reply and residue: all change is on citta's side, but mere proximity NAMES the
|       relation more clearly than it EXPLAINS it. Advaita presses by collapsing the
|       duality; Nyāya by making consciousness an adventitious property of a substance.
|
+-- VI. FIVE CITTA-BHŪMIS
|   +-- Kṣipta: restless and scattered; rajas-dominated; activity without integration.
|   +-- Mūḍha: dull and inert; tamas-dominated; OBSCURATION, not calm.
|   +-- Vikṣipta: attends, infers, analyses and briefly concentrates, but cannot remain
|   |   inwardly collected; sattva flashes within a rajas-governed life.
|   +-- Ekāgra: one-pointed on a chosen support; sattva-dominant; yoga-fit.
|   +-- Niruddha: all modifications restrained; the culmination, not blankness.
|   +-- Only ekāgra and niruddha are fully fit for authentic Yoga; the first three are
|   |   pre-yogic or only partially prepared.
|   +-- Decisive step: vikṣipta -> ekāgra, from occasional concentration to sustained
|   |   one-pointedness.
|   +-- 2019 APPLICATION (justify from ruling guṇa and stability, not from focus-strength):
|   |   +-- Scientist = VIKṢIPTA. Intense but outward-directed, result-attached and
|   |   |   interrupted. High intellectual focus is not yogic restraint.
|   |   +-- God-realised devotee = EKĀGRA. One-pointed on Īśvara; īśvara-praṇidhāna
|   |   |   stabilises citta and removes antarāyas.
|   |   +-- Self-realised yogin = NIRUDDHA. All vṛttis stilled; correlated with the
|   |       highest samādhi and the approach to kaivalya.
|   +-- Attribution: the fivefold scheme is Vyāsa-onward commentarial exposition.
|
+-- VII. CITTA-VṚTTI-NIRODHA
|   +-- Vṛtti: the operative modification by which citta takes an object or form.
|   +-- Nirodha: restraint or cessation of those operative fluctuations.
|   |   +-- NOT annihilation of citta as a substance.
|   |   +-- Revelatory, not destructive: it removes concealment, not consciousness.
|   +-- When restrained, the seer abides in its own form; otherwise the seer appears
|   |   identified with the vṛttis.
|   +-- Even valid cognition is a vṛtti:
|       +-- It may be akliṣṭa and instrumentally liberating.
|       +-- It must finally cease because what binds is FORM, not falsity.
|
+-- VIII. FIVE VṚTTIS
|   +-- Pramāṇa: valid cognition through perception, inference and testimony.
|   |   +-- Example: smoke seen, fire correctly inferred.
|   +-- Viparyaya: false cognition not corresponding to the object as it is.
|   |   +-- Example: nacre taken for silver. Typically kliṣṭa.
|   +-- Vikalpa: cognition from words alone with no corresponding object.
|   |   +-- Example: "the horn of a hare". Intelligible without a referent.
|   +-- Nidrā: sleep as a POSITIVE mode grounded on absence-content.
|   |   +-- Evidence: the waking report "I slept and knew nothing".
|   +-- Smṛti: non-loss and re-presentation of experienced content.
|   +-- Each of the five may be:
|   |   +-- Kliṣṭa: coloured by avidyā and the other kleśas; bondage-producing.
|   |   +-- Akliṣṭa: purified and redirected toward discrimination and detachment.
|   +-- Ladder rule: akliṣṭa vṛttis cancel kliṣṭa ones and then themselves subside.
|   +-- The kliṣṭa/akliṣṭa division qualifies VṚTTIS ONLY. Kleśas are inherently
|       afflictive; there is no "akliṣṭa avidyā".
|
+-- IX. WHY CESSATION IS PRESCRIBED
|   +-- 1. Citta takes the form of whatever it attends to.
|   +-- 2. Puruṣa's consciousness is reflected in that form.
|   +-- 3. In ignorance the changing form is appropriated: "I know", "I suffer",
|   |      "I desire", "I remember".
|   +-- 4. Therefore the stream of vṛttis SUSTAINS bondage; it is the running mechanism,
|   |      not a symptom.
|   +-- 5. Therefore restraint UNCOVERS the already-free seer.
|   +-- Image: when ripples settle, the pool reflects one clear sky. Nothing was added to
|       the water and nothing was taken from the sky.
|
+-- X. ABHYĀSA AND VAIRĀGYA
|   +-- Jointly responsible for nirodha (YS 1.12); neither alone suffices.
|   +-- Abhyāsa: effort to remain stable in restraint.
|   |   +-- Firm only when practised for a LONG TIME, WITHOUT INTERRUPTION and WITH
|   |   |   DEVOTED SERIOUSNESS.
|   |   +-- Function: continuity against old saṃskāras.
|   +-- Apara-vairāgya, also vaśīkāra-vairāgya (YS 1.15): mastery over thirst for SEEN and
|   |   objects SCRIPTURALLY PROMISED. Heavenly reward is included.
|   +-- Para-vairāgya (YS 1.16): non-thirst even for the guṇas, following knowledge of
|   |   Puruṣa; releases attachment to subtle states, insight and siddhis.
|   +-- Commentarial ladder inside lower vairāgya: yatamāna -> vyatireka -> ekendriya
|   |   -> vaśīkāra.
|   +-- Failure modes: practice without dispassion is strain; dispassion without practice
|       is mood without capacity.
|
+-- XI. CITTA-PARIṆĀMA
|   +-- Three specifically yogic transformations:
|   |   +-- Nirodha-pariṇāma: impressions of restraint overpower impressions of emergence.
|   |   +-- Samādhi-pariṇāma: many-pointedness declines as one object becomes dominant.
|   |   +-- Ekāgratā-pariṇāma: similar cognitions arise and subside toward one object.
|   +-- General threefold transformation of any substrate:
|   |   +-- Dharma-pariṇāma (property), lakṣaṇa-pariṇāma (temporal phase),
|   |       avasthā-pariṇāma (condition), all in a persisting dharmin.
|   +-- Why a bearer is needed: practice accumulates, saṃskāras are deposited and
|   |   retrieved, and stable meditative acquisition is not a feature of a mere series.
|   +-- Against Buddhist kṣaṇikavāda: change needs a bearer.
|   |   +-- Residual force: a rich causal series may reproduce much of what Yoga assigns
|   |       to a substrate.
|   +-- Against Advaita vivartavāda: the change in citta and Prakṛti is REAL.
|   +-- Guard: the persisting citta is a substrate of NATURE, not a self. Puruṣa stands
|       outside every transformation.
|
+-- XII. FIVE KLEŚAS
|   +-- Avidyā is the ROOT and the field in which the other four grow, not merely item one.
|   |   +-- Four reversals: non-eternal taken as eternal; impure as pure; painful as
|   |       pleasant; non-self as self.
|   +-- Asmitā: identification of the seer with the instrument of seeing (Puruṣa with
|   |   buddhi/citta).
|   +-- Rāga: attachment following remembered pleasure.
|   +-- Dveṣa: aversion following remembered pain.
|   +-- Abhiniveśa: clinging to life and fear of death; instinctive and PRESENT EVEN IN
|       THE LEARNED, because it is carried by saṃskāras rather than by opinions.
|
+-- XIII. FOUR STATES OF THE KLEŚAS
|   +-- Prasupta: dormant and seed-like. DORMANCY IS NOT DESTRUCTION.
|   +-- Tanu: attenuated by practice, austerity, meditation and discernment; the only
|   |   state that represents genuine progress.
|   +-- Vicchinna: interrupted, temporarily overridden by an opposing tendency —
|   |   attachment may look absent only because fear is presently stronger.
|   +-- Udāra: fully active and visibly governing speech, action and affect.
|   +-- Diagnostic rule: "I no longer feel it" is compatible with prasupta, tanu AND
|   |   vicchinna. Only destruction of the seed removes a kleśa.
|   +-- Therapy ladder: gross expression by ethical and practical discipline; sūkṣma
|       forms burned by dhyāna; the seed destroyed by viveka-khyāti.
|
+-- XIV. BONDAGE CHAIN, KARMA AND VIPĀKA
|   +-- Kleśa -> karma -> karmāśaya -> vipāka -> punarjanma; experience generates fresh
|   |   reaction and the loop closes.
|   +-- Karmāśaya is kleśa-mūla: action binds because it is coloured by the afflictions.
|   +-- Vipāka is THREEFOLD: jāti (birth or species-condition), āyus (life-span),
|   |   bhoga (experience).
|   +-- Carriers of continuity: saṃskāra (impression) and vāsanā (latent tendency).
|   +-- Four colours of karma:
|   |   +-- Śukla (white), kṛṣṇa (black), śukla-kṛṣṇa (mixed).
|   |   +-- Aśukla-akṛṣṇa (neither): the yogin whose action no longer produces ordinary
|   |       bondage. The goal is not the cessation of action but of its colouring.
|   +-- Scale objection: exhausting an unsurveyable stock would postpone release
|   |   indefinitely.
|   +-- Reply: liberating knowledge and samādhi burn the seeds of FUTURE bondage; only
|       already-fructifying momentum runs out.
|
+-- XV. KRIYĀ-YOGA
|   +-- Tapas: disciplined, heat-generating effort and purification.
|   +-- Svādhyāya: study and recitation of sacred teaching with reflective self-study.
|   +-- Īśvara-praṇidhāna: dedication or surrender to Īśvara.
|   +-- Stated purpose: ATTENUATE the kleśas and cultivate samādhi.
|   +-- Preparatory and integrated; not a substitute for viveka-khyāti, and not the same
|       as saṃyama or the full eight limbs.
|
+-- XVI. AṢṬĀṄGA-YOGA
|   +-- 1. Yama: ahiṃsā, satya, asteya, brahmacarya, aparigraha.
|   |   +-- YS 2.31: unrestricted by class/birth, place, time or circumstance they form
|   |       the sārvabhauma-mahāvrata, the universal great vow.
|   +-- 2. Niyama: śauca, santoṣa, tapas, svādhyāya, īśvara-praṇidhāna.
|   |   +-- The last three overlap with kriyā-yoga; that overlap is a structural link.
|   +-- 3. Āsana: a steady and comfortable seat; freedom from bodily distraction.
|   +-- 4. Prāṇāyāma: regulation of breath and the psycho-physical economy.
|   +-- 5. Pratyāhāra: withdrawal of the senses from compulsive extroversion.
|   +-- 6. Dhāraṇā: binding citta to one locus; first clear sign of ekāgratā.
|   +-- 7. Dhyāna: uninterrupted flow of cognition toward that same object; also the
|   |   burner of subtle kleśas.
|   +-- 8. Samādhi: the object alone shines and discursiveness recedes.
|   +-- First five are bahiraṅga, last three antaraṅga. "External" means PREPARATORY,
|   |   not unimportant.
|   +-- Dhāraṇā + dhyāna + samādhi on ONE object = saṃyama.
|   +-- Dhāraṇā, dhyāna and samādhi are graded, NOT synonymous.
|   +-- Ethical limbs are constitutive safeguards: without them meditative power can
|       coexist with affliction and egoity.
|
+-- XVII. ANTARĀYAS AND THEIR REMOVAL
|   +-- Nine obstacles: vyādhi (disease), styāna (mental stagnation), saṃśaya (doubt),
|   |   pramāda (carelessness), ālasya (laziness), avirati (sensual non-restraint),
|   |   bhrānti-darśana (false vision), alabdha-bhūmikatva (failure to attain a stage),
|   |   anavasthitatva (instability in an attained stage).
|   +-- Four accompanying symptoms: duḥkha, daurmanasya, aṅgamejayatva,
|   |   śvāsa-praśvāsa.
|   +-- Remedies: īśvara-praṇidhāna; practice of one principle; friendliness, compassion,
|   |   gladness and equanimity; breath regulation; suitable meditative supports.
|   +-- This is why questions on samādhi open into the role of God: Yoga's Īśvara is a
|       practice-enabling doctrine, not a creation-first one.
|
+-- XVIII. SAMPRAJÑĀTA SAMĀDHI — THE YS 1.17 GRID
|   +-- Cognitive, object-supported absorption; a seed of cognition remains.
|   +-- Called SABĪJA at YS 1.46 because an object-support is present.
|   +-- YS 1.17 fourfold characterisation:
|   |   +-- Vitarka: gross object-level deliberative grasp.
|   |   +-- Vicāra: subtle object-level reflection.
|   |   +-- Ānanda: refined sattvic joy — NOT ordinary pleasure.
|   |   +-- Asmitā: bare I-am-ness — NOT the binding kleśa, but an extremely subtle
|   |       objectification of the I-sense.
|   +-- Ānanda and asmitā are therefore NOT a contested later addition; they stand in the
|   |   sūtra's own list. What is disputed is only how commentators map them onto the
|   |   samāpatti sequence.
|
+-- XIX. SAMĀPATTI TAXONOMY — YS 1.42-1.46
|   +-- Savitarkā: gross (sthūla) object, mixed with śabda-artha-jñāna-vikalpa.
|   +-- Nirvitarkā: gross object, memory and verbal construction purified.
|   +-- Savicārā: subtle (sūkṣma) object, subtle reflective consideration remains.
|   +-- Nirvicārā: subtle object, even subtle reflective overlay stilled.
|   +-- YS 1.45 extends the subtle domain up to aliṅga, the undifferentiated condition.
|   +-- YS 1.46 declares all of these sabīja.
|   +-- The sa-/nir- prefix marks conceptual-verbal overlay; the vitarka/vicāra pair marks
|   |   object-level. These are DIFFERENT CRITERIA from the YS 1.17 factor-list.
|   +-- Commentators MAP the two grids; they do not IDENTIFY them.
|
+-- XX. ṚTAMBHARĀ PRAJÑĀ
|   +-- Truth-bearing insight arising with nirvicāra-vaiśāradya and adhyātma-prasāda
|   |   (YS 1.47-1.48).
|   +-- Field: a PARTICULAR object (viśeṣa-artha), where śruta and anumāna reach
|   |   universals and general relations.
|   +-- Its saṃskāra obstructs other saṃskāras (YS 1.50).
|   +-- Status: NOT a fourth pramāṇa; perfected yogi-pratyakṣa, and still a vṛtti.
|   +-- Its own seed must eventually cease for nirbīja samādhi.
|   +-- Caution: do not equate it with Aurobindo's knowledge by identity or supermind.
|       The resemblance is thematic only; here it must finally cease.
|
+-- XXI. ASAMPRAJÑĀTA AND NIRBĪJA
|   +-- Asamprajñāta (YS 1.18): preceded by the practice of the idea of cessation
|   |   (virāma-pratyaya) AND DEFINED BY SAṂSKĀRA-ŚEṢA, residual impressions.
|   |   +-- The residue is the sūtra's own defining mark, not a mere commentarial option.
|   +-- Nirbīja (YS 1.51): when THAT residue too is restrained, everything being
|   |   restrained, absorption is seedless.
|   +-- TWO SENSES OF "SEED": sabīja at 1.46 = with OBJECT-SUPPORT; nirbīja at 1.51 =
|   |   without the residual IMPRESSION. Losing the object-support does not by itself
|   |   make an absorption seedless.
|   +-- Safe formula: asamprajñāta = vṛttis stopped, saṃskāra-śeṣa remains;
|   |   nirbīja = even that residue restrained.
|   +-- Not sleep: nidrā is itself one of the five vṛttis; asamprajñāta is disciplined
|   |   cessation of vṛttis. Genesis and value both differ.
|   +-- Bhava-pratyaya route (YS 1.19): condition-based, associated with videha and
|   |   prakṛti-laya beings; nature-mergence need not equal final discriminative release.
|   +-- Upāya-pratyaya route (YS 1.20): means-based, through śraddhā, vīrya, smṛti,
|       samādhi and prajñā. These are routes or bases, NOT a third main classification.
|
+-- XXII. DHARMAMEGHA-SAMĀDHI
|   +-- The "cloud of dharma" raining down the fullness of discriminative knowledge.
|   +-- The highest samādhi immediately preceding kaivalya.
|   +-- Reached only when even fascination with extraordinary knowledge or powers is
|   |   relinquished; the yogin no longer seeks omniscience, attainment or display.
|   +-- Kleśas and binding karma cease; the guṇas complete their purpose.
|   +-- Not a divine grace replacing effort; the ripened summit of perfected discipline.
|   +-- Do not flatten it into every use of asamprajñāta or nirbīja.
|
+-- XXIII. SIDDHIS
|   +-- Higher concentrations and saṃyama may yield extraordinary capacities.
|   +-- YS 3.37: they are attainments in the outward-turned state but OBSTACLES TO
|   |   ABSORPTION (samādhāv upasargāḥ) when they attract appropriation.
|   +-- They reactivate egoity, attachment and pride, and belong to the side-effects of
|   |   discipline rather than to the essence of kaivalya.
|   +-- Para-vairāgya is what releases attachment to subtle states, knowledge and powers.
|   +-- Reporting them is doctrinal exposition, not empirical endorsement; distinguish
|       what the text owns from what has been independently verified.
|
+-- XXIV. ĪŚVARA
|   +-- Puruṣa-viśeṣa: a SPECIAL Puruṣa, special but not different in kind. He belongs to
|   |   the category of witness; his distinction is that he was never entangled.
|   +-- YS 1.24: untouched by kleśa, karma, vipāka and āśaya.
|   +-- YS 1.25: in him lies the unsurpassed seed of omniscience.
|   +-- YS 1.26: teacher even of the ancients, being unconditioned by time.
|   +-- YS 1.27-1.28: denoted by praṇava (OṂ); its repetition with contemplation of its
|   |   meaning is canonical practice.
|   +-- Five positive roles:
|       +-- Object of meditation; aid through īśvara-praṇidhāna; remover of antarāyas;
|           perfect model of untouched status; timeless teacher-principle.
|
+-- XXV. WHAT YOGA'S ĪŚVARA IS NOT
|   +-- Not one of the ordinary bound Puruṣas.
|   +-- Not Prakṛti and not the material cause of the world.
|   +-- Not the Nyāya creator, efficient cause and moral governor of the world-order.
|   +-- Not a devotional saviour who grants liberation arbitrarily.
|   +-- Not a substitute for abhyāsa, vairāgya and viveka-khyāti.
|
+-- XXVI. GROUNDS AND PHILOSOPHICAL ROLE OF ĪŚVARA
|   +-- Patañjali's own grounds are compact and practice-facing: a special witness,
|   |   unsurpassed omniscience, timeless teacherhood and praṇava-mediated contemplation.
|   +-- Later commentators add scripture, maximal degrees of knowledge and power, and the
|   |   need for intelligent coordination of witness and nature. DO NOT project that
|   |   proof-set back onto Patañjali.
|   +-- Objection from nirīśvara Sāṃkhya: if the ontology already explains liberation,
|   |   Īśvara is redundant.
|   +-- Reply: pragmatic and soteriological — a unique focus of devotion, obstacle-removal
|   |   and support for samādhi. The addition is justified by practical efficacy.
|   +-- Objection from redundancy: if viveka-khyāti liberates, is he dispensable?
|   +-- Reply: NOT redundant, but NON-FINAL.
|   +-- Exam formula: Īśvara is INSTRUMENTAL, NOT CONSTITUTIVE, in liberation.
|   +-- Residual: the traditional Yoga case for Īśvara's NECESSITY is weaker than Nyāya's
|       creator-argument, since Prakṛti remains the material basis of evolution.
|
+-- XXVII. KAIVALYA
|   +-- Aloneness, isolation, absolute distinctness: the isolation of Puruṣa from Prakṛti
|   |   and its evolutes.
|   +-- Puruṣa is already pure; bondage is reflected misidentification, so nothing is
|   |   achieved IN Puruṣa. What ends is a false relation.
|   +-- Process:
|   |   +-- Abhyāsa and vairāgya -> kriyā-yoga and aṣṭāṅga-yoga.
|   |   +-- Attenuation and destruction of the kleśas -> stable samādhi.
|   |   +-- Viveka-khyāti: uninterrupted discernment of the absolute difference between
|   |   |   sattva/citta and Puruṣa. Yoga stays cognitivist at the summit.
|   |   +-- Para-vairāgya -> dharmamegha-samādhi.
|   |   +-- Pratiprasava: the guṇas cease to function for that Puruṣa and resolve back
|   |       toward their source; citta's function is exhausted.
|   +-- Svarūpa-pratiṣṭhā: consciousness established in its own nature, completing YS 1.3.
|   +-- FOUR NEGATIONS:
|   |   +-- Not union with God in the devotional sense.
|   |   +-- Not identity with Brahman in the Advaita sense.
|   |   +-- Not annihilation of consciousness.
|   |   +-- Not mere painlessness abstracted from the self's own luminosity.
|   |   +-- And not destruction of mind as a substance; its binding vṛttis cease.
|   +-- Īśvara assists but does NOT grant kaivalya; liberation follows viveka-khyāti.
|   +-- Contested: whether kaivalya is austere aloneness or blissful self-abidance.
|
+-- XXVIII. FARMER, BURNT SEED AND POTTER'S WHEEL
|   +-- Kṣetrika (farmer): he does not push the water; he breaches the embankment and
|   |   water flows by its own nature. Discipline removes obstruction; it does not
|   |   manufacture Puruṣa's freedom.
|   +-- Dagdha-bīja (burnt seed): destroyed kleśas keep their shape and cannot germinate.
|   +-- Potter's wheel: after the push stops the wheel turns until momentum is spent.
|       This is the careful basis for jīvanmukti language — Puruṣa is unbound while
|       bodily and mental processes run out their residual cause.
|
+-- XXIX. KAIVALYA-PĀDA REALISM AGAINST VIJÑĀNAVĀDA / YOGĀCĀRA
|   +-- Pūrvapakṣa: what is taken as an external object is explicable as cognition-only.
|   +-- Siddhānta — Sāṃkhya-realism in four moves:
|   |   +-- Object-independence: an object is not ekacitta-tantra; it would otherwise
|   |   |   vanish when that mind ceased to cognise it.
|   |   +-- The mind's knownness: citta is na tat svābhāsam (not self-illuminating)
|   |   |   because it is itself dṛśya for Puruṣa; mind-only struggles to terminate the
|   |   |   regress of cognitions cognising cognitions.
|   |   +-- No simultaneous self-cognition: citta cannot be both lamp and illuminated
|   |   |   object in one act.
|   |   +-- Uparāga: an object is known when citta is uparakta by it, which implies the
|   |       object is not identical with the cognition.
|   +-- Three-term realism: viṣaya (object), citta-vṛtti (modification), Puruṣa (witness).
|   +-- Counter: shared karmic traces explain intersubjective appearance without external
|   |   objects.
|   +-- Rejoinder and residue: a common object plus many cittas explains shared appearance
|       better, but Yoga must still say HOW an external object colours an insentient citta.
|
+-- XXX. INTER-SCHOOL COMPARISONS
|   +-- Yoga versus Sāṃkhya:
|   |   +-- Same ontology and same 25 tattvas; nirīśvara there, seśvara here.
|   |   +-- Explanatory metaphysics there; practical psychology, samādhi and
|   |       yogi-pratyakṣa here. Yoga operationalises rather than rejects Sāṃkhya.
|   +-- Yoga versus Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika:
|   |   +-- Broad citta versus atomic manas plus a self-substance.
|   |   +-- Meditative Īśvara versus creator, efficient cause and moral governor.
|   |   +-- Kaivalya as isolation versus apavarga as cessation of suffering.
|   |   +-- Three pramāṇas versus four.
|   +-- Yoga versus Advaita:
|   |   +-- Many Puruṣas and real Prakṛti versus non-dual Brahman.
|   |   +-- Kaivalya as disentanglement versus identity-realisation.
|   |   +-- Pariṇāmavāda versus vivartavāda at the highest level.
|   +-- Yoga versus Buddhism:
|   |   +-- Enduring Puruṣa versus nairātmyavāda; persisting citta versus kṣaṇikavāda.
|   |   +-- Meditation aimed at Puruṣa-Prakṛti discrimination versus insight into
|   |   |   impermanence, non-self and dependent origination.
|   |   +-- Kaivalya versus nirvāṇa.
|   +-- Yoga versus Mīmāṃsā:
|   |   +-- Mīmāṃsā: a non-creator Īśvara is otiose and ritual action secures dharma.
|   |   +-- Yoga: ritual cannot by itself remove avidyā and asmitā.
|   +-- Yoga versus Cārvāka:
|   |   +-- Cārvāka: Puruṣa, karma, rebirth and kaivalya are unverifiable beyond
|   |   |   perception.
|   |   +-- Yoga: disciplined yogi-pratyakṣa and pragmatic transformation; public
|   |       verification nonetheless remains difficult.
|   +-- Yoga versus Aurobindo:
|       +-- Classical isolation and cessation versus integral transformation and divine
|           descent into life.
|
+-- XXXI. CLASSIC OBJECTIONS / REPLIES
|   +-- Pure Puruṣa cannot become bound.
|   |   +-- Reply: bondage is misidentification through reflection, not real alteration.
|   +-- Insentient citta cannot cognise.
|   |   +-- Reply: it becomes luminous by reflecting Puruṣa.
|   |   +-- Residual: the reflection relation remains difficult.
|   +-- Valid cognition should not be suppressed.
|   |   +-- Reply: it removes error but remains a non-self mental form; form binds, not
|   |       falsity.
|   +-- Asamprajñāta is unconsciousness.
|   |   +-- Reply: its cause, clarity and aftermath differ from nidrā.
|   +-- Siddhis validate fantasy.
|   |   +-- Reply: the classical text subordinates powers to liberation.
|   +-- Īśvara is redundant.
|   |   +-- Reply: non-final rather than redundant; instrumentally uniquely powerful.
|   +-- Kaivalya is negative isolation.
|   |   +-- Reply: svarūpa-pratiṣṭhā is its positive side — unconfused self-abidance
|   |       rather than ecstatic union.
|   +-- Dhyāna suffices without ethics.
|       +-- Reply: no; without yama and niyama, meditative power coexists with affliction
|           and egoity.

+-- QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
    +-- Yoga's distinct achievement is to turn Sāṃkhya's dualism into a disciplined
        psychology of liberation: ethics, attention, absorption and insight progressively
        weaken the causal machinery of bondage. Its account of citta and saṃskāra explains
        why intellectual assent alone is insufficient, and its Kaivalya-pāda arguments show
        that it is metaphysically committed rather than a mere praxis manual. Yet reflected
        consciousness, the precise relation among the highest samādhis and the necessity of
        Īśvara remain debated. Kaivalya therefore rests not on trance but on irreversible
        discrimination between the luminous witness and every transformation of nature.
