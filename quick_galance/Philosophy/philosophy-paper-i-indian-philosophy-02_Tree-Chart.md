                              |
                              v
                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Reality is anekānta: every real dravya possesses enduring identity through guṇa while
undergoing origination and cessation through paryāya. Finite knowledge grasps only a
standpoint; nayavāda disciplines partial description, syādvāda marks its conditions and
saptabhaṅgī maps seven predicative possibilities. This pluralist realism supports a
soteriology in which conscious jīva is obscured by material karma, stops influx through
ethical restraint and sheds bondage until omniscience and liberation.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Jainism offers a remarkably integrated pluralist realism: change does not destroy
identity, partial cognition need not become error, and ethical discipline follows from
a metaphysics of living plurality and karmic interdependence. Its conditional logic
avoids crude contradiction by indexing predicates to respects, while its soteriology
turns knowledge into disciplined transformation. Yet the system relies on demanding
commitments to omniscience, subtle material karma and a soul both enduring and mutable.
Its enduring force lies in combining objective truth with epistemic humility, not in
reducing truth to preference.
