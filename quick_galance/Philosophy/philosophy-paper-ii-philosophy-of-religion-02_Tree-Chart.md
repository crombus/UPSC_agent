# Philosophy Optional — Paper II — Philosophy of Religion 02 — Proofs for the Existence of God

## Quick-Glance Tree Chart

```text
PROOFS FOR GOD: ONTOLOGICAL, COSMOLOGICAL, DESIGN, MORAL, NYĀYA — AND THEIR CRITIQUE
|
+-- CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
|   +-- Arguments move from the concept, from change/contingency, from purposive order,
|       from moral experience, or from Indian causal-linguistic-karmic order, to an
|       ultimate explanatory reality.
|   +-- Every argument is graded on THREE SEPARATE TIERS:
|       +-- VALIDITY  -> does the conclusion follow?
|       +-- SOUNDNESS -> are the premises true? (most critics attack here)
|       +-- ADEQUACY  -> is the being reached one, necessary, personal, good, worship-worthy?
|   +-- "Proof" in the printed clause does NOT guarantee mathematical certainty.
|
+-- SCOPE / OWNERSHIP FIREWALL
|   +-- Owns: Anselm/Descartes ontological; Aquinas' Ways, Leibniz, kalām; Paley/Hume/Darwin/
|       fine-tuning; Kant's postulate and the three moral routes; a priori vs a posteriori;
|       Nyāya–Udayana; Jaina, Buddhist, Mīmāṃsā, Cārvāka anti-creator cases; cumulative-case logic.
|   +-- Notions of God owns attributes and God–world relation; Problem of Evil owns theodicy;
|       Religious Experience owns phenomenology; Reason–Revelation–Faith owns non-proof warrant.
|   +-- Pascal's wager and Jamesian permission are classified only: they are NOT existence-proofs.
|
+-- SOURCE / ATTRIBUTION CAUTION
|   +-- Anselm, Proslogion 2 and 3 (1077-78); Gaunilo, In Behalf of the Fool.
|   +-- Descartes, Meditations V (1641) — NOT the causal proof of Meditations III.
|   +-- Aquinas, Summa Theologiae I q.2 a.3 (Five Ways).
|   +-- Hume, Dialogues Concerning Natural Religion (1779) — Philo, not Cleanthes, objects.
|   +-- Paley, Natural Theology (1802) — the watch is Paley's, never Aquinas'.
|   +-- Kant, Critique of Pure Reason (1781/87) destroys; Critique of Practical Reason (1788) postulates.
|   +-- Darwin, Origin of Species (1859).
|   +-- Udayana, Nyāyakusumāñjali, 5th stabaka (11th c.); earlier Vātsyāyana, Uddyotakara, Jayanta.
|   +-- Śāntarakṣita, Tattvasaṃgraha with Kamalaśīla's Pañjikā; Dharmakīrti.
|   +-- Jaina case assembled in Jinasena's Mahāpurāṇa and Hemacandra.

|       Flew, God and Philosophy.

|
+-- I. PROOF-FORCE AND BURDEN
|   +-- Deductive (Anselm) | inductive-abductive (design, fine-tuning) | cumulative
|       (Udayana, Swinburne) | practical postulate (Kant) | explanatory termination (Leibniz).
|   +-- Burden splits three ways: starting premise -> inference -> theological identification.
|   +-- A finite explanatory gap is NOT evidence for God: "science has not explained X" is no argument.
|   +-- Allowing a brute contingent totality REJECTS sufficient reason; it does not refute the argument.
|
+-- II. A PRIORI vs A POSTERIORI  [2022 Q8(a), 20]
|   +-- A priori: concept alone -> ontological only. Strength: necessity. Weakness: Kant's predicate objection.
|   +-- A posteriori: facts of the world -> cosmological, teleological, moral, Nyāya.
|       Strength: conceded premises. Weakness: at best a probable, finite, possibly plural cause.
|   +-- CLASSIFY BY SOURCE OF PREMISES, never by modality of the conclusion.
|   +-- Verdict formula: evidential contact -> a posteriori; modal strength -> a priori;
|       religious adequacy -> neither alone.
|
+-- III. ONTOLOGICAL ARGUMENT  [2018 Q6(c) 15 | 2020 Q6(a) 20 | 2024 Q7(c) 15 (10+5)]
|   +-- Anselm, Proslogion 2 — a REDUCTIO:
|       +-- God =def "that than which nothing greater can be conceived"; greater = more perfect.
|       +-- NOT "the most perfect being that there is" — the stronger notion is required.
|       +-- Existing in reality is greater than existing in the understanding alone.
|       +-- Assume mind-only existence -> a greater is conceivable -> contradiction -> God exists.
|   +-- Anselm, Proslogion 3 — the serious form:
|       +-- A being that cannot be thought not to exist is greater -> NECESSARY existence, aseity.
|   +-- Descartes, Meditations V: existence belongs to the essence of a supremely perfect being
|       as three angles to a triangle, a valley to a mountain.
|
+-- IV. ONTOLOGICAL CRITIQUE AND REPAIR
|   +-- Gaunilo: most perfect island parody.
|       +-- PRECISION: he said "most perfect OF islands"; correcting it to "most perfect
|           CONCEIVABLE island" strengthens the parody.
|       +-- Anselm's reply: an island is a contingent piece of the physical world and CAN be
|           thought not to exist; only God is defined with necessary existence.
|       +-- Residue: protects form 2, exposes form 1, shifts burden to COHERENCE of maximal greatness.
|   +-- Kant: "existence is not a real predicate" (CPR A598/B626 region).
|       +-- 100 real thalers contain no more concept than 100 possible thalers.
|       +-- Chatterjee–Datta's idiom: 100 rupees imagined in the pocket.
|       +-- Only a CONDITIONAL follows; nothing can be defined into existence.
|   +-- Post-Fregean: "exists" is second-order (instantiation), so it is no perfection at all.
|   +-- Modal repair (Malcolm, Hartshorne, Plantinga): possible maximal greatness -> necessary
|       existence in every world. BURDEN MIGRATES to the possibility premise; "possibly not"
|       reverses the result by the same logic.
|   +-- 2018 verdict: conceptual entailment YES, instantiation NO.
|   +-- No classical Indian analogue — Indian pramāṇas are world-involving and anumāna needs vyāpti.
|
+-- V. COSMOLOGICAL FAMILY — THREE FORMS, NEVER MERGED
|   +-- Aquinas Ways 1-3: present change, ordered efficient causation, contingency ->
|       unmoved actualiser, first efficient cause, necessary being.
|   +-- Leibniz: the contingent totality + principle of sufficient reason -> a necessary reason.
|   +-- Kalām: whatever begins has a cause; the universe began; therefore a cause of the beginning.
|   +-- THE DECIDING DISTINCTION:
|       +-- ESSENTIALLY ORDERED (per se): hand -> stick -> stone. Simultaneous; intermediates
|           have only DERIVATIVE power; removal stops the series NOW. Aquinas terminates THIS.
|       +-- ACCIDENTALLY ORDERED (per accidens): grandfather -> father -> son. Aquinas need not stop it.
|       +-- "First" = EXPLANATORY priority here and now, not the earliest event in time.
|       +-- Aquinas holds a temporal beginning is known by FAITH, not demonstration.
|   +-- THE FIVE WAYS MAPPED: 1 motion/change | 2 ordered efficient causes | 3 contingency
|       (all cosmological) | 4 observed degrees -> maximum (henological) | 5 regular
|       directedness -> intelligence (teleological). Each closes "and this everyone
|       understands to be God".
|   +-- Way 3's weak steps: the quantifier shift at "at some time nothing existed";
|       and necessary-through-another (ab alio) vs necessary-of-itself (a se).
|
+-- VI. COSMOLOGICAL CRITIQUE
|   +-- Hume: why not an infinite series? explaining each part may explain the whole;
|       the cause need not be one, personal, infinite or good.
|   +-- Russell: the universe is "just there" — a brute fact; composition fallacy.
|   +-- Composition reply: the argument concerns DEPENDENCE, not property-transfer.
|   +-- Kant: the move from "a necessary being" to "the most real being" is the ontological
|       argument in disguise — an ADEQUACY-tier objection, not a causal one.
|
+-- VII. DESIGN — THREE TELEOLOGIES  [2025 Q6(a), 20]
|   +-- Aquinas Way 5: regular directedness of NON-COGNITIVE nature; archer and arrow.
|       A GOVERNANCE argument, not an artefact analogy.
|   +-- Paley (1802): ANALOGY. Watch on a heath — wheels, cogs, axles, springs, balances
|       adjusted for regular timekeeping; a stone is not.
|       +-- Three riders: the inference survives (i) never having seen a watch made,
|           (ii) imperfect working, (iii) parts whose function we cannot discover.
|       +-- Cumulative evidence: eye as self-adjusting optical instrument, bird's wing,
|           fish's fin, planetary rotation, the seasons.
|   +-- Fine-tuning: probabilistic and pre-biological, about constants — not an analogy.
|
+-- VIII. HUME ON DESIGN — FIVE FRONTS  (A-A-P-P-E)
|   +-- ADAPTED: any persisting universe must look adapted; there could not be birds with
|       wings unable to live in air. EPICUREAN HYPOTHESIS: finite particles in random motion
|       pass through every combination; one is a stable order.
|   +-- ANALOGY: the universe resembles a vegetable or a crustacean at least as much as a machine.
|   +-- PROPORTION: proportion cause to effect — a finite world never yields an infinite creator.
|   +-- PLURALITY: many designers, an inferior, infant or senile deity, an abandoned first attempt.
|   +-- EVIL: disorder and suffering count against a good and powerful maker.
|   +-- LIMIT: Hume does NOT disprove a designer; he attacks inference-strength and attributes.
|   +-- CHRONOLOGY TRAP: the Dialogues (1779) PRECEDE Paley (1802).
|   +-- Darwin's EXACT SCOPE: defeats Paley's biological premise; does NOT touch cosmic
|       law-governedness or fine-tuning. Ozone illustration: life fitted itself to the filter.
|   +-- Fine-tuning's live rivals: design | physical necessity | multiverse + observation
|       selection | unknown deeper law.
|
+-- IX. MORAL ARGUMENT — THREE ROUTES  [2021 Q7(b), 15]
|   +-- Route 1, conscience (Newman, Grammar of Assent): duty and shame imply One before whom
|       we are responsible. Hick: it BEGS the question by assuming values resist naturalism.
|   +-- Route 2, objective value: stance-independent moral truths need a transcendent ground.
|       EUTHYPHRO presses this route: good because willed, or willed because good?
|   +-- Route 3, Kant's highest good:
|       +-- Moral law obligates the summum bonum (virtue with proportionate happiness).
|       +-- Finite agents cannot secure the proportion; nature does not supply it.
|       +-- "Ought implies can" licenses postulating its conditions.
|       +-- THREE POSTULATES: FREEDOM, IMMORTALITY, GOD. Rational faith (Vernunftglaube):
|           subjectively sufficient, objectively insufficient.
|   +-- Kant does NOT prove God by morality; Euthyphro does not touch Kant, whose moral law
|       is autonomous and whose God guarantees PROPORTION, not obligation.
|   +-- Indian counterpart: Nyāya's fruit-giver (karmaphaladātā). Kant postulates a God who
|       COULD proportion; Nyāya infers a God who DOES — bolder, more exposed.
|
+-- X. NYĀYA–UDAYANA  [2018 Q8(a) 20 | 2023 Q7(a) 20 | 2025 Q7(b) 15]
|   +-- Īśvara is the FIRST EFFICIENT cause — a builder, NEVER the material cause; also
|       preserver and destroyer; one, infinite, eternal, omniscient; six perfections (ṣaḍ-aiśvarya).
|   +-- Eternal intelligence is an INSEPARABLE ATTRIBUTE of God, not His essence (contra Advaita).
|   +-- THEISM, NOT DEISM: continuous relation as creator, maintainer, destroyer.
|   +-- prayojaka kartā (directive cause of human action) | karmaphaladātā (dispenser of fruits).
|   +-- UDAYANA'S EIGHT GROUNDS — a mnemonic verse; glosses of the last four VARY, so give the
|       term plus one clause and say so:
|       +-- kāryāt: the world is an effect (made of parts, sāvayava; intermediate magnitude,
|           avāntara-mahattva) -> an omniscient maker who knows atoms.
|       +-- āyojanāt: inert atoms cannot self-combine; initial conjunction needs volition.
|       +-- dhṛtyādeḥ: support, maintenance and dissolution need a governing will.
|       +-- padāt: transmission of language and arts presupposes an original teacher.
|       +-- pratyayataḥ: the Veda's validity (prāmāṇya) needs a trustworthy (āpta) author.
|       +-- śruteḥ: the Veda's own testimony to Īśvara.
|       +-- vākyāt: the Veda consists of sentences, and sentences are person-produced.
|       +-- saṃkhyāviśeṣāt: the first dyad needs the numerical cognition (apekṣā-buddhi) of a
|           conscious being — state it only if stated correctly.
|   +-- ADṚṢṬA ARGUMENT (the philosophical core): differences of lot -> karma -> a persisting
|       stock of merit and demerit -> adṛṣṭa is UNCONSCIOUS and cannot allot determinate fruit
|       -> an omniscient superintendent (karmādhyakṣa).
|   +-- Internal answers: circularity is dissolved by two ORDERS (existence vs knowledge);
|       the bodiless-agent objection is met by a dilemma.
|   +-- Pressure: Śaṅkara's atomism dilemma; Sāṃkhya's unconscious prakṛti; C. D. Sharma's
|       REDUNDANCY charge that God is "reduced to the status of a supervisor" chained by adṛṣṭa.
|   +-- Chatterjee–Datta's verdict: God is the highest premise and cannot be deduced from an
|       anterior one; God must be known by direct experience. Lotze: proofs are "pleas put
|       forward in justification of our faith."
|
+-- XI. TWO SENSES OF "GOD" IN INDIA
|   +-- Sense A, WORLD-MAKER (jagat-kartā / Īśvara-kāraṇa-vāda): affirmed by Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika,
|       theistic Vedānta, Yoga; denied by Jainism, Buddhism, Mīmāṃsā, Cārvāka, classical Sāṃkhya.
|   +-- Sense B, PERFECTED BEING: omniscient, passion-free, exemplary, non-creative — affirmed
|       by Jainism and Buddhism.
|   +-- Nāstika = denier of VEDIC AUTHORITY, not denier of spirit.
|   +-- Denying a creator is not denying divinity.
|
+-- XII. JAINA CRITIQUE  [2019 Q5(e), 10 — a trap-shaped stem]
|   +-- FIRST LINE: Jainism propounds NO proof of a creator; it argues AGAINST one and
|       relocates divinity in the perfected soul.
|   +-- World uncreated, beginningless; six substances (jīva, pudgala, dharma, adharma,
|       ākāśa, kāla); reality is utpāda–vyaya–dhrauvya; change belongs to MODES.
|   +-- Six arguments: MOTIVE (a perfect being has no want, so no reason to create) | REGRESS |
|       INSTRUMENT (no matter, space, body before creation) | MORAL (suffering refutes
|       compassion; sport is cruel) | REDUNDANCY (karma + six dravyas suffice — lāghava) |
|       SVABHĀVA (fire burns, water flows).
|   +-- What is affirmed instead:
|       +-- ARIHANTA/KEVALIN: destroyed the four obscuring (ghātiyā) karmas; omniscient WHILE EMBODIED.
|       +-- TĪRTHAṄKARA: an arihanta who RE-FOUNDS the fourfold order and teaches; 24 per
|           half-cycle; Mahāvīra is the 24th. Defined by FUNCTION, not rank.
|       +-- SIDDHA: shed the four non-obscuring (aghātiyā) karmas at death; DISEMBODIED at the
|           siddhaśilā with the four infinitudes (ananta-catuṣṭaya).
|       +-- EVERY TĪRTHAṄKARA BECOMES A SIDDHA; NOT EVERY SIDDHA WAS A TĪRTHAṄKARA.
|       +-- Vītarāga: worship is COMMEMORATIVE — the object does not and cannot respond.
|   +-- Nyāya objection: unconscious karmic matter cannot allot determinate fruits.
|       Jain reply: maturation is law-like, as poison acts without a poisoner.
|       Counter (highest-yield paragraph): "no motive" assumes creation is need-driven;
|       classical theism replies creation is a non-needy diffusion of goodness.
|
+-- XIII. BUDDHIST CRITIQUE — THREE STRATA  [2018 Q8(b), 15]
|   +-- (a) CANONICAL/GENETIC: Brahmajāla Sutta (DN 1) — Brahmā's honest mistake;
|       Aggañña Sutta (DN 27) — cyclic naturalistic account; avyākata + poisoned arrow.
|       LIMIT: diagnosis of the belief, not refutation of the proposition.
|   +-- (b) CAUSAL-METAPHYSICAL (Dharmakīrti; Śāntarakṣita/Kamalaśīla) — THE EXAMINABLE CORE:
|       +-- To be real is to be causally efficacious (arthakriyā-kāritva).
|       +-- KRAMA-AKRAMA-VIRODHA: a permanent unchanging cause must produce all at once
|           (cosmos in one instant) or successively (God acquires an auxiliary, hence changes).
|       +-- Auxiliary regress: with auxiliaries God is not sole cause; without them the
|           temporal order is unexplained.
|       +-- The vyāpti "effect -> conscious maker" holds only for artefacts.
|       +-- Pratītyasamutpāda already explains arising and cessation — a creator is an idle wheel.
|   +-- (c) MORAL: this quantum of suffering counts against a benevolent omnipotent maker;
|       Devadaha Sutta (MN 101) — any doctrine voiding present effort destroys the moral life.
|   +-- Buddhism denies a CREATOR, not devas, who are impermanent beings within saṃsāra.
|   +-- Nyāya reply: eternal will, effects timed by adṛṣṭa; demanding perception begs the question.
|       Buddhist rejoinder: a will timed by another is not the sole cause claimed.
|   +-- Verdict: strongest as metaphysics, weakest as genealogy.
|
+-- XIV. MĪMĀṂSĀ, CĀRVĀKA AND ADVAITA'S LEVELLING
|   +-- Mīmāṃsā: the Veda is APAURUṢEYA — eternal, authorless, self-valid; no human and no
|       divine author. Authorlessness blocks an author's deceit or ignorance and avoids
|       Nyāya's three-stage dependence (God -> omniscience -> truthfulness).
|       +-- APŪRVA matures into fruit WITHOUT a dispenser; Prābhākara's niyoga grounds
|           obligation in the injunction itself.
|       +-- This is the exact contradictory of Udayana's padāt/pratyayataḥ/śruteḥ/vākyāt
|           AND of the adṛṣṭa argument.
|   +-- Cārvāka: only perception is a pramāṇa; inference cannot reach the supersensible —
|       if this stands, EVERY anumāna-based proof falls at once.
|   +-- Advaita: a DEMOTION, not a denial — Īśvara is saguṇa and vyāvahārika, not nirguṇa Brahman.
|   +-- Classification key: every Indian anti-creator argument is ECONOMY, COHERENCE
|       or EPISTEMIC RESTRICTION.
|
+-- XV. CUMULATIVE CASE
|   +-- C-INDUCTIVE: premises RAISE probability without tipping it past one half.
|   +-- P-INDUCTIVE: premises make the conclusion MORE PROBABLE THAN NOT.
|   +-- Swinburne: independent C-inductive strands can jointly yield a P-inductive result,
|       given a non-negligible prior and genuine independence; SIMPLICITY supplies the prior.
|   +-- Mitchell: the jury analogy — converging circumstantial evidence.
|   +-- IMAGE CONTROL: rope or legal brief, NEVER a chain; and not the same as inference to
|       the best explanation, which compares rival total hypotheses.
|   +-- Flew's TEN LEAKY BUCKETS: zero plus zero is zero. Reply: a C-inductive argument is
|       not zero-valued by definition.
|   +-- Residue: underdetermination (finite designer, committee of gods, value-principle);
|       simplicity is contested; and the case must be run as a NET balance including suffering.
|   +-- Udayana's eight-fold collection is structurally the SAME move, eight centuries earlier.
|
+-- OBJECTIONS / REPLIES
|   +-- Proofs define rather than discover God -> some infer from world and order.
|   +-- The God of the proofs is not the God of religion -> only a cumulative bridge is offered.
|   +-- A brute fact stops the regress -> that rejects sufficient reason, it does not refute the argument.
|   +-- Rival explanations underdetermine design -> compare scope and simplicity, conceding
|       non-demonstrative force.
|   +-- Karma operates without God -> intelligent allocation explains DETERMINATE desert;
|       the dispute tracks rival metaphysics.

+-- QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
    +-- The proofs do not share one fate. Ontological reasoning depends on a disputed bridge
        from possibility to actuality; cosmological reasoning on sufficient reason and on an
        essentially ordered series; design on comparative explanation after Darwin; moral
        reasoning on a postulate that may be declined; Nyāya on a cumulative realist order
        that Mīmāṃsā and Buddhism contest from inside India. None alone yields every classical
        attribute, and even aggregation delivers a probability rather than the personal,
        morally perfect God of worship. The proofs therefore fail as demonstrations and
        succeed as diagnostics — which is why every tradition eventually supplements proof
        with experience, testimony or faith.
```
