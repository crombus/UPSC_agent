# Philosophy Optional — Paper II — Philosophy of Religion 02 — Proofs for the Existence of God

## Quick-Glance Tree Chart

```text
PROOFS FOR GOD: ONTOLOGICAL, COSMOLOGICAL, DESIGN, MORAL AND NYĀYA
|
+-- CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
|   +-- Arguments for God move from concept, contingency/change, purposive order, moral
|       experience or Indian causal-linguistic order to an ultimate explanatory reality.
|   +-- Their force depends on premises about possibility, sufficient reason, causation,
|       design and moral objectivity; most support a limited divine profile unless combined.
|
+-- SCOPE / OWNERSHIP FIREWALL
|   +-- Owns: Anselm/Descartes ontological argument; Aquinas Ways and cosmological families;
|       Paley/design/Hume; Kantian moral routes; a priori/a posteriori; Nyāya/Udayana;
|       Jain/Buddhist anti-creator responses and cumulative-case logic.
|   +-- Notions of God owns attributes; Evil owns theodicy; faith/revelation owns non-proof warrant.
|
+-- SOURCE / ATTRIBUTION CAUTION
|   +-- Anselm: Proslogion; Aquinas: Summa Theologiae; Paley: Natural Theology; Hume:
|       Dialogues; Kant: Critique of Pure Reason/Practical Reason; Udayana: Nyāyakusumāñjali.
|   +-- Aquinas's Five Ways are not all one cosmological proof; Kant rejects speculative
|       proofs but uses moral postulation, not a theoretical demonstration.
|
+-- I. PROOF-FORCE
|   +-- Deductive proof: conclusion follows if premises true.
|   +-- Inductive/abductive: God proposed as best explanation.
|   +-- Demonstration of a first cause does not automatically prove omniscience, goodness
|       or one worship-worthy personal God.
|   +-- Burden: validity, premise truth, explanatory adequacy and attribute bridge.
|
+-- II. ONTOLOGICAL ARGUMENT
|   +-- A priori: begins from concept, not observed world.
|   +-- Anselm:
|       +-- God is that than which none greater can be conceived.
|       +-- Existing in reality is greater than existing in understanding alone.
|       +-- If God existed only in thought, greater could be conceived.
|       +-- Therefore God exists.
|   +-- Descartes:
|       +-- Necessary existence belongs to supremely perfect being as triangle properties
|           belong to its essence.
|
+-- III. ONTOLOGICAL CRITIQUES
|   +-- Gaunilo:
|       +-- Parallel perfect-island reasoning suggests illicit move from concept to reality.
|   +-- Anselm reply:
|       +-- Only necessary/maximally great being, not contingent island, fits.
|   +-- Kant:
|       +-- Existence is not a real predicate adding perfection to concept.
|       +-- Conceptual necessity yields conditional existence only if instantiated.
|   +-- Modal repair:
|       +-- If maximally great being is possible, necessary existence follows in relevant modal logic.
|   +-- Objection:
|       +-- Possibility premise carries the dispute.
|
+-- IV. COSMOLOGICAL FAMILY
|   +-- A posteriori: begins from change, causes or contingency.
|   +-- Aquinas Way 1: change requires actualiser; no essentially ordered infinite regress;
|       unmoved mover.
|   +-- Way 2: efficient causes require first cause.
|   +-- Way 3: contingent beings require necessary being.
|   +-- Distinguish:
|       +-- Temporal first event.
|       +-- Present sustaining/explanatory dependence.
|   +-- Leibniz/PSR:
|       +-- Contingent totality needs sufficient reason outside itself.
|   +-- Kalām:
|       +-- Whatever begins has cause; universe began; therefore cause.
|
+-- V. COSMOLOGICAL CRITIQUES
|   +-- Hume:
|       +-- Causal principle derived from experience cannot automatically exceed it.
|       +-- Explaining each member may leave no additional whole to explain.
|   +-- Russell/brute fact:
|       +-- Universe may simply exist.
|   +-- Infinite regress:
|       +-- Why impossible?
|   +-- Special pleading:
|       +-- If everything needs cause, why not God?
|   +-- Reply:
|       +-- Argument concerns contingent/changed beings, not necessary being.
|   +-- Residual:
|       +-- Coherence and uniqueness of necessary terminus remain contested.
|
+-- VI. DESIGN
|   +-- Order, adaptation and purposive complexity suggest intelligence.
|   +-- Aquinas Fifth Way:
|       +-- Non-rational things regularly act toward ends; directing intelligence inferred.
|   +-- Paley:
|       +-- Watch's functional complexity implies watchmaker; organism/world analogously imply designer.
|   +-- Modern fine-tuning:
|       +-- Life-permitting conditions presented probabilistically; use only as bounded extension.
|
+-- VII. HUME ON DESIGN
|   +-- Weak analogy between artefact and universe.
|   +-- Finite effect warrants at most finite/imperfect cause.
|   +-- Order may arise through generation, necessity or unknown principles.
|   +-- Many designers possible.
|   +-- Evil/disorder weaken perfect designer inference.
|   +-- Unique universe gives no comparison class.
|   +-- Darwinian explanation weakens biological design inference without settling cosmic order.
|   +-- Reply: design is inference to best explanation, not strict analogy; underdetermination remains.
|
+-- VIII. MORAL ARGUMENTS
|   +-- Moral lawgiver route:
|       +-- Objective obligation implies transcendent authoritative source.
|   +-- Kant's highest-good route:
|       +-- Virtue deserves happiness.
|       +-- Full harmony not secured naturally.
|       +-- Practical reason postulates God and immortality as conditions of highest good.
|   +-- Moral experience/conscience route:
|       +-- Binding moral awareness points beyond preference.
|   +-- Critiques:
|       +-- Euthyphro dilemma.
|       +-- Secular moral realism.
|       +-- Postulate is not theoretical proof.
|   +-- Reply: God may ground goodness by divine nature rather than arbitrary command.
|
+-- IX. NYĀYA / UDAYANA
|   +-- Kāryāt: world as effect implies intelligent maker.
|   +-- Āyojanāt: inert atoms require initiator/combiner.
|   +-- Dhṛtyādeḥ: support, dissolution and ordered maintenance require governor.
|   +-- Padāt: conventional word-use requires authoritative origin.
|   +-- Pratyayataḥ: Vedic reliability requires omniscient source.
|   +-- Śruteḥ: scripture supports Īśvara.
|   +-- Vākyāt: sentence composition points to intelligent author.
|   +-- Saṃkhyāviśeṣāt: precise numerical combination of atoms requires cognition.
|   +-- Adṛṣṭāt: karma needs intelligent allocator.
|   +-- Udayana's case is cumulative; lists/translations vary by exposition.
|
+-- X. INDIAN CRITIQUES
|   +-- Mīmāṃsā:
|       +-- Veda authorless; apūrva/karma distributes fruit; creator unnecessary.
|   +-- Sāṃkhya:
|       +-- Prakṛti evolves through guṇas; perfect God lacks motive.
|   +-- Buddhism:
|       +-- Dependent origination needs no first creator; eternal unchanging agent cannot act.
|   +-- Jainism:
|       +-- Eternal jīvas/matter/karma; perfected beings are not creators.
|   +-- Nyāya reply:
|       +-- Unconscious causes and moral potencies cannot explain coordinated order alone.
|
+-- XI. CUMULATIVE CASE
|   +-- Ontological: necessary possibility.
|   +-- Cosmological: explanatory ground.
|   +-- Design: intelligence.
|   +-- Moral: goodness/practical postulate.
|   +-- Religious experience/revelation: personal warrant, owned elsewhere.
|   +-- Strength: independent lines may mutually reinforce.
|   +-- Weakness: accumulating weak arguments does not automatically yield strong proof;
|       tensions among inferred attributes remain.
|
+-- OBJECTIONS / REPLIES
|   +-- Proofs define rather than discover God -> reply: some infer from world/order.
|   +-- God of proof not God of religion -> reply: cumulative bridges are needed.
|   +-- Brute fact stops regress -> reply: explanatory rationalism rejects arbitrary termination.
|   +-- Alternative explanations underdetermine design -> reply: compare scope/simplicity,
|       while conceding non-demonstrative force.
|

        together they show that theism is an argued explanatory option rather than a bare
        assertion, while leaving rational non-theistic alternatives open.
```
