# Philosophy Optional — Paper II — Philosophy of Religion 07 — Religion without God

## Quick-Glance Tree Chart

```text
RELIGION WITHOUT GOD
|
+-- CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
|   +-- Religion need not require a creator deity if it supplies an ultimate orientation,
|       diagnosis of the human condition, disciplined practice, community, moral transformation
|       and a goal such as awakening or liberation.
|
+-- SCOPE / OWNERSHIP FIREWALL
|   +-- Owns substantive/functional definitions; Buddhism, Jainism, Mīmāṃsā, Sāṃkhya,
|       Cārvāka boundary, agnosticism, Nietzsche, religious naturalism/non-realism.
|   +-- Proofs and morality have separate owners.
|
+-- SOURCE / ATTRIBUTION CAUTION
|   +-- Buddhism and Jainism reject creator, not all transcendent/soteriological claims.
|   +-- Mīmāṃsā defends Veda without divine author; Sāṃkhya explains cosmos without creator.
|   +-- Nietzsche critiques Christianity/morality in specific contexts; avoid sloganisation.
|
+-- I. DEFINING RELIGION
|   +-- Substantive: relation to God/gods/sacred reality.
|   +-- Functional: meaning, ritual, community, moral formation and ultimate concern.
|   +-- Family-resemblance: no single necessary feature, overlapping practices.
|   +-- Too narrow deity-definition excludes Buddhism/Jainism.
|   +-- Too broad function-definition makes nationalism/therapy religion.
|   +-- Best account combines ultimate concern, practices, community and transformative horizon.
|
+-- II. FUNCTIONS REQUIRING / NOT REQUIRING DEITY
|   +-- Personal prayer/providence/creation require deity-profile.
|   +-- Ethics, ritual, contemplation, community, salvation and sacred orientation need not.
|   +-- Liberation may mean cessation, isolation or perfected knowledge rather than divine union.
|
+-- III. BUDDHISM
|   +-- No creator; dependent origination explains conditioned existence.
|   +-- Four Truths diagnose suffering and prescribe path.
|   +-- Karma/rebirth without permanent self.
|   +-- Buddha is awakened teacher, not creator.
|   +-- Nirvāṇa supplies ultimate soteriological end.
|   +-- Religious dimensions: refuge, monastic/community discipline, meditation, ethics and ritual.
|   +-- Objection: later devotional forms function theistically.
|   +-- Reply: devotional objects need not become omnipotent creator.
|
+-- IV. JAINISM / MĪMĀṂSĀ / SĀṂKHYA
|   +-- Jainism: eternal jīvas/matter/karma; Tīrthaṅkaras perfected guides, not creators.
|   +-- Mīmāṃsā: authorless Veda, dharma, ritual and apūrva without divine legislator.
|   +-- Sāṃkhya: Prakṛti evolution and Puruṣa liberation without creator.
|   +-- These show non-theism can coexist with metaphysics, ethics and liberation.
|
+-- V. CĀRVĀKA
|   +-- Rejects supersensible God, soul, karma and afterlife through perception-centred critique.
|   +-- Boundary question:
|       +-- A worldview rejecting ritual/transcendence may be philosophy without being religion.
|   +-- Thus absence of God is not sufficient to classify something as religion.
|
+-- VI. AGNOSTICISM
|   +-- Suspends judgement because God's existence/nature is unknown or unknowable.
|   +-- Weak: currently insufficient evidence.
|   +-- Strong: human cognition cannot know.
|   +-- Agnostic religious life may preserve reverence, practice, moral community or openness.
|   +-- Distinct from atheism and indifference.
|
+-- VII. NIETZSCHE
|   +-- "Death of God" diagnoses collapse of authoritative transcendent value horizon.
|   +-- Genealogy exposes morality's resentment and life-denial in targeted traditions.
|   +-- Nihilism follows when inherited values lose force.
|   +-- Task: revaluation and value-creation, not simple hedonistic atheism.
|   +-- Criticism: self-created values risk domination.
|
+-- VIII. RELIGIOUS NATURALISM / NON-REALISM
|   +-- Religious naturalism locates awe, value and belonging within nature without supernatural deity.
|   +-- Non-realism interprets God-language as moral/spiritual practice rather than external entity report.
|   +-- Fictionalism may use religious narratives without literal assent.
|   +-- Objection: retains functions by changing truth-claims beyond recognition.
|   +-- Reply: practice/meaning may be religious without metaphysical realism.
|
+-- OBJECTIONS / REPLIES
|   +-- No God means no worship -> sacred/ultimate orientation can structure devotion.
|   +-- No divine command means no morality -> compassion, flourishing and rational duty suffice.
|   +-- No providence means no hope -> liberation/transformation can replace rescue by agent.
|   +-- Functional definition is over-broad -> combine functions with ultimate orientation/tradition.
|
+-- VERIFIED THINKER / TEXT / EXAMPLE BANK
|   +-- Buddha/early Buddhist teaching; Jaina Tattvārtha tradition; Jaimini/Kumārila;
|       Sāṃkhyakārikā; Cārvāka reconstruction; Nietzsche; religious-naturalist debate.
|   +-- No fabricated quotations.
|
+-- HIGH-RISK CONCEPTUAL TRAPS
|   +-- Non-theism is not atheism; atheism is not agnosticism.
|   +-- Buddhism has no creator, not no religious objects/practices.
|   +-- Jina/Buddha are not creator Gods.
|   +-- Mīmāṃsā Veda is not divine speech.
|   +-- Cārvāka is not automatically religion.
|   +-- Functional religion cannot be unlimited.
|

|   +-- Status: wording/marks verified from local official Paper II corpus.

|
+-- REVISION CHAIN
|   +-- Definition -> functions -> Buddhism -> Jainism/Mīmāṃsā/Sāṃkhya ->
|       Cārvāka boundary -> agnosticism -> Nietzsche -> naturalism/non-realism.
|
+-- OPTIONAL-PAPER ANSWER SPINE
|   +-- Define religion without begging the question; distinguish deity from ultimate concern.
|   +-- Test a non-theistic tradition across belief, practice, community and liberation.
|   +-- Present theistic objection and functional/substantive reply; conclude conditionally.
|
+-- QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
    +-- A creator God is sufficient for many religious forms but not necessary for religion
        as disciplined orientation to ultimacy and transformation. Buddhism most clearly
        establishes the possibility, while Jainism and Mīmāṃsā widen it. Yet definitions
        must remain bounded so that every meaning-producing practice does not become religion.
```
