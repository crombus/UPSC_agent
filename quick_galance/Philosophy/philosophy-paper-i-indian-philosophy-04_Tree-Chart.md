                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika is a pluralist realism: a mind-independent world contains substances,
qualities, motions, universals, ultimate particularities, inherence and absences, and it
is knowable through disciplined perception, inference, comparison and testimony. Error
is a misplacement of something real; effect is newly produced from real causes; enduring
selves bear cognition and karma; and later Nyāya infers an omniscient Īśvara who orders
atoms and moral consequences without creating eternal entities from nothing.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika provides Indian philosophy's most systematic defence of common-sense
realism: distinctions in the world answer to distinctions in thought, valid cognition
is causally and publicly testable, and inferential discipline can move beyond immediate
perception. Its categories, relation of inherence, universals, selves and God give this
realism explanatory range, but also create pressures of regress, ontological expansion
and contested induction. Its enduring achievement is not an infallible inventory of
reality but a rigorous programme connecting ontology, epistemology, debate and release.
