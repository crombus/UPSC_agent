                      CENTRAL THESIS
A pluralist realism in which a mind-independent world comes in a fixed number of kinds
and is reachable by disciplined knowing, so that logic itself becomes a path to release.

universal-mediated perception and a karma-bound God -- are stipulated, not derived.

                    QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Nyaya-Vaisesika gives Indian philosophy its most rigorous realism, its most developed
theory of inference and fallacy, and its most sustained natural theology. Its ontology is
regulated rather than promiscuous: the six blockers refuse universals, postulation and
non-cognition are reduced to existing instruments, and absence is admitted as one
category with four kinds. Yet the three devices that complete the system -- inherence
declared self-linking, particularity declared self-differentiating, and the universal
declared perceptually given -- are termini named rather than derived, and God's justice is
purchased by binding him to a karmic order he did not make. The fair verdict is therefore
conditional and graded: the system is compelling for anyone who grants realism about
universals and absences, so its long quarrels with Buddhism, Advaita, Mimamsa and Samkhya
are disagreements about starting-points rather than about internal consistency.
