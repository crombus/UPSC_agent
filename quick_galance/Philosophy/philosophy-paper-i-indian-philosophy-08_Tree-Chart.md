# Philosophy Optional — Paper I — Indian Philosophy 08 — Schools of Vedanta

## Quick-Glance Tree Chart

```text
SCHOOLS OF VEDĀNTA: BRAHMAN, SELF, WORLD, BONDAGE AND LIBERATION
|
+-- CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
|   +-- Vedānta interprets the Upaniṣads, Bhagavad Gītā and Brahma-sūtra to explain the
|       relation among Brahman, selves and world.
|   +-- Advaita affirms non-dual Brahman and treats plurality as dependent appearance;
|       Viśiṣṭādvaita affirms one qualified Brahman whose real body is selves and matter;
|       Dvaita affirms an independent personal God and irreducibly dependent but real
|       selves/world; later schools map further forms of difference and non-difference.
|   +-- Their disagreements are not over whether scripture liberates, but over what is
|       ultimately real, how causation works and whether mokṣa is identity, communion,
|       proximity, participation or blissful service.
|
+-- SCOPE / OWNERSHIP FIREWALL
|   +-- Owns: prasthāna-traya; Brahman; Īśvara; ātman/jīva/sākṣī; jagat; māyā/avidyā;
|   |   adhyāsa; three levels of reality; vivartavāda; mahāvākya interpretation;
|   |   anirvacanīya-khyāti; mokṣa; jīvanmukti; prārabdha; Advaita six pramāṇas;
|   |   Viśiṣṭādvaita apṛthaksiddhi/body-soul model; Dvaita pañcabheda/tāratamya;
|   |   Nimbārka, Vallabha and Caitanya comparisons; bimba-pratibimba and other
|   |   individuality models; Rāmānuja's seven māyā objections.
|   +-- Sāṃkhya owns independent Prakṛti/Puruṣa and pariṇāma.
|   +-- Mīmāṃsā owns injunction-centred Vedic authority.
|   +-- Nyāya owns atomistic creation and anyathākhyāti.
|   +-- Bhakti history/theology beyond philosophical comparison is outside.
|
+-- SOURCE / ATTRIBUTION CAUTION
|   +-- Shared prasthāna-traya:
|   |   +-- Upaniṣads.
|   |   +-- Bhagavad Gītā.
|   |   +-- Brahma-sūtra of Bādarāyaṇa.
|   +-- School identity arises through commentarial interpretation:
|   |   +-- Śaṅkara: Advaita.
|   |   +-- Rāmānuja: Viśiṣṭādvaita.
|   |   +-- Madhva: Dvaita.
|   |   +-- Nimbārka: Dvaitādvaita.
|   |   +-- Vallabha: Śuddhādvaita.
|   |   +-- Caitanya tradition: Acintyabhedābheda.
|   +-- Do not attribute all later Advaita sub-school models directly to Śaṅkara.
|   +-- Famous formulas must be identified as traditional summaries unless verified as
|       exact wording in a named primary source.
|
+-- I. SHARED TEXTUAL PROBLEM
|   +-- Upaniṣadic passages teach:
|   |   +-- Unity/identity.
|   |   +-- Difference, devotion and lordship.
|   |   +-- Brahman as source of world.
|   +-- Schools resolve apparent tension differently.
|   +-- Hermeneutic axes:
|       +-- Primary versus secondary meaning.
|       +-- Nirguṇa versus saguṇa.
|       +-- Identity versus qualification versus difference.
|       +-- Real transformation versus appearance versus dependent creation.
|
+-- II. BRAHMAN: TWO DEFINITIONS
|   +-- Svarūpa-lakṣaṇa:
|   |   +-- Defines intrinsic nature.
|   |   +-- Sat-cit-ānanda / reality-consciousness-limitlessness in Advaita usage.
|   +-- Taṭastha-lakṣaṇa:
|       +-- Relational indicator.
|       +-- Source, sustainer and dissolver of world.
|   +-- Advaita:
|       +-- Intrinsically nirguṇa, non-dual and beyond limiting predicates.
|   +-- Viśiṣṭādvaita:
|       +-- Brahman is necessarily qualified by auspicious attributes and real modes.
|   +-- Dvaita:
|       +-- Viṣṇu is supreme independent personal reality with infinite perfections.
|
+-- III. ADVAITA BRAHMAN
|   +-- One without a second.
|   +-- Nirguṇa:
|   |   +-- Free from limiting material qualities and internal differentiation.
|   |   +-- Not a blank nonentity.
|   +-- Nirviśeṣa:
|   |   +-- Without ultimate part/attribute/substance structure.
|   +-- Self-revealing consciousness.
|   +-- Ātman is Brahman.
|   +-- Language reaches Brahman indirectly:
|       +-- Negation of limiting predicates.
|       +-- Lakṣaṇā in mahāvākyas.
|       +-- Scripture removes ignorance rather than producing Brahman.
|
+-- IV. RĀMĀNUJA'S BRAHMAN
|   +-- Saguṇa only:
|   |   +-- Infinite auspicious qualities are essential, not lower appearance.
|   +-- Brahman/Nārāyaṇa is:
|   |   +-- Conscious.
|   |   +-- Personal.
|   |   +-- Omniscient.
|   |   +-- Omnipotent.
|   |   +-- Material and efficient cause.
|   +-- Cit and acit are real modes/attributes/body of Brahman.
|   +-- Unity is organic and qualified, not featureless identity.
|   +-- Nirguṇa passages deny bad/material qualities, not all qualities.
|
+-- V. MADHVA'S BRAHMAN
|   +-- Viṣṇu alone svatantra:
|   |   +-- Independent.
|   +-- Souls and matter are paratantra:
|   |   +-- Dependent but real.
|   +-- Brahman is efficient ruler, not transformed into world.
|   +-- Qualities are real and perfect.
|   +-- Difference is real eternally.
|   +-- Scripture's identity language is reinterpreted through dependence/similarity,
|       never numerical identity.
|
+-- VI. BRAHMAN AS CAUSE
|   +-- Advaita:
|   |   +-- Brahman is both nimitta and upādāna cause through māyā.
|   |   +-- As material cause, no real modification occurs in Brahman.
|   |   +-- Spider/web and clay/pot analogies are qualified; vivarta avoids transformation.
|   +-- Viśiṣṭādvaita:
|   |   +-- Brahman is intelligent and material cause.
|   |   +-- Subtle cit/acit form its body before manifestation.
|   |   +-- World is real transformation of Brahman's modes, not essence corrupted.
|   +-- Dvaita:
|       +-- God is efficient cause/controller.
|       +-- Matter remains distinct dependent material cause.
|   +-- Sāṃkhya:
|       +-- Unconscious Prakṛti material cause, no creator.
|   +-- Nyāya:
|       +-- God efficient cause; eternal atoms material cause.
|
+-- VII. ADVAITA PRAMĀṆAS
|   +-- Six:
|   |   +-- Perception.
|   |   +-- Inference.
|   |   +-- Comparison.
|   |   +-- Postulation.
|   |   +-- Non-cognition.
|   |   +-- Testimony.
|   +-- All operate within vyavahāra.
|   +-- Brahman not an object of perception/inference because it is subject-ground.
|   +-- Upaniṣadic śabda is decisive:
|       +-- Reveals/removes ignorance concerning already-present self.
|   +-- Experience/anubhava stabilises understanding but does not replace śruti.
|
+-- VIII. MAHĀVĀKYA: TAT TVAM ASI
|   +-- Direct meanings:
|   |   +-- Tat: omniscient cause, Īśvara with māyā-upādhi.
|   |   +-- Tvam: embodied limited knower with mind-body upādhi.
|   +-- Direct identity appears contradictory.
|   +-- Bhāga-tyāga-lakṣaṇā:
|       +-- Abandon mutually incompatible limiting adjuncts.
|       +-- Retain common consciousness.
|       +-- Identity is of underlying consciousness, not empirical roles.
|   +-- Devadatta illustration:
|       +-- "This is that Devadatta": discard incompatible time/place qualifications,
|           retain person.
|   +-- Rāmānuja:
|       +-- "Thou art that" expresses body/dependence relation, not absolute identity.
|   +-- Madhva:
|       +-- No identity; text interpreted consistently with eternal difference.
|
+-- IX. ĪŚVARA IN ADVAITA
|   +-- Brahman conditioned by māyā:
|   |   +-- Māyā-upahita Brahman.
|   +-- Omniscient, creator, ruler within empirical reality.
|   +-- Not false in the sense of sheer nonexistence.
|   +-- Ultimately non-different from nirguṇa Brahman when limiting standpoint removed.
|   +-- Soteriological role:
|       +-- Worship purifies mind.
|       +-- Grace/scripture/teacher aid knowledge.
|       +-- Final liberation is non-dual knowledge, not eternal dual relation.
|
+-- X. ĀTMAN, JĪVA AND SĀKṢĪ IN ADVAITA
|   +-- Ātman:
|   |   +-- Pure self-luminous consciousness identical with Brahman.
|   +-- Sākṣī:
|   |   +-- Witness of waking, dream, deep sleep and mental states.
|   |   +-- Not an agent or changing observer.
|   +-- Jīva:
|       +-- Consciousness apparently limited/reflected in internal organ.
|       +-- Empirical knower, doer and enjoyer.
|   +-- Three explanatory models:
|       +-- Avacchedavāda: space-in-pot model of apparent limitation.
|       +-- Pratibimbavāda: one consciousness reflected in many minds.
|       +-- Ābhāsavāda: individuality as appearance of consciousness.
|   +-- These are later explanatory models with sub-school differences, not three
|       independently real selves.
|
+-- XI. BIMBA-PRATIBIMBAVĀDA
|   +-- Bimba:
|   |   +-- Original consciousness.
|   +-- Pratibimba:
|   |   +-- Reflection in mind/avidyā.
|   +-- One sun appears as many reflections in water.
|   +-- Multiplicity depends on reflecting media, not original.
|   +-- Distortions belong to medium/reflection, not original consciousness.
|   +-- Soteriological significance:
|       +-- Purification clarifies reflection.
|       +-- Knowledge removes mistaken independence.
|       +-- Liberation recognises non-difference from original.
|   +-- Objection:
|       +-- Formless consciousness cannot literally be reflected.
|   +-- Reply:
|       +-- Analogy marks dependent manifestation, not optical mechanism.
|
+-- XII. THREE STATES AND WITNESS
|   +-- Waking:
|   |   +-- External objects through gross body/senses.
|   +-- Dream:
|   |   +-- Internal mental projection without waking objects.
|   +-- Deep sleep:
|       +-- Differentiated cognition absent; later recollection "I slept" indicates continuity.
|   +-- Sākṣī persists through changing states.
|   +-- Māṇḍūkya framework may add turīya:
|       +-- Not a fourth temporal state but non-dual ground.
|
+-- XIII. JĪVA IN VIŚIṢṬĀDVAITA
|   +-- Real eternal self.
|   +-- Atomic in size, consciousness as attributive knowledge that can contract/expand.
|   +-- Distinct from body and Brahman.
|   +-- Inseparable from and absolutely dependent on Brahman.
|   +-- Agent/enjoyer under divine dependence.
|   +-- Retains individuality in mokṣa.
|   +-- Does not become numerically identical with God.
|
+-- XIV. JĪVA IN DVAITA
|   +-- Eternal real dependent substance.
|   +-- Always different from God and other souls.
|   +-- Differences persist in liberation.
|   +-- Tāratamya:
|       +-- Hierarchy among souls and liberated enjoyments in fuller doctrine.
|   +-- Normative criticism:
|       +-- Essential hierarchy can appear incompatible with equal spiritual worth.
|   +-- Dvaita response:
|       +-- Hierarchy is metaphysical order, not historically imposed injustice.
|
+-- XV. JAGAT IN ADVAITA
|   +-- World is mithyā:
|   |   +-- Empirically experienced and causally functional.
|   |   +-- Not ultimately independent.
|   |   +-- Sublated by Brahman-knowledge.
|   +-- Not tuccha:
|       +-- Not sheer non-being like hare's horn.
|   +-- Vivartavāda:
|       +-- Apparent transformation of Brahman through māyā.
|       +-- Brahman remains unchanged.
|   +-- Rope-snake:
|       +-- Substratum real, superimposed snake empirically experienced, error sublated.
|
+-- XVI. THREE LEVELS OF REALITY
|   +-- Pāramārthika:
|   |   +-- Ultimate non-dual Brahman.
|   +-- Vyāvahārika:
|   |   +-- Shared empirical world, persons, ethics, karma, God and pramāṇas.
|   +-- Prātibhāsika:
|       +-- Private/temporary illusion such as dream or shell-silver.
|   +-- Empirical is not equated with private illusion.
|   +-- Sublation hierarchy:
|       +-- Dream corrected by waking.
|       +-- Waking plurality corrected by Brahman-knowledge.
|
+-- XVII. JAGAT IN VIŚIṢṬĀDVAITA
|   +-- Real.
|   +-- Acit is Brahman's body/mode.
|   +-- Subtle state before creation; gross manifest state afterward.
|   +-- Transformation belongs to dependent modes, not loss of divine perfection.
|   +-- Body-soul relation:
|       +-- World is controlled, supported and used by Brahman for divine purpose.
|   +-- No māyā as indeterminable ontological illusion.
|
+-- XVIII. JAGAT IN DVAITA
|   +-- Real, plural and dependent.
|   +-- Matter differs from God and souls.
|   +-- Creation is real ordering of dependent matter.
|   +-- No ontological identity with Brahman.
|   +-- Difference does not restrict God because dependence is asymmetric.
|
+-- XIX. MĀYĀ
|   +-- Advaita explanatory principle of appearance.
|   +-- Beginningless but terminable through knowledge.
|   +-- Neither absolutely real nor absolutely unreal:
|       +-- Anirvacanīya.
|   +-- Two powers:
|       +-- Āvaraṇa: concealment of non-dual reality.
|       +-- Vikṣepa: projection of names/forms and plurality.
|   +-- Macro/micro vocabulary:
|       +-- Māyā often associated with cosmic causal power/Īśvara.
|       +-- Avidyā often associated with individual ignorance/jīva.
|   +-- Identity versus distinction between māyā and avidyā varies across Advaita.
|
+-- XX. TWO SENSES OF INDESCRIBABILITY
|   +-- Brahman:
|   |   +-- Beyond objectifying conceptual predicates because it is non-dual subject-ground.
|   |   +-- Indescribable from plenitude/transcendence, not logical defect.
|   +-- Māyā:
|       +-- Indeterminable as real, unreal or both because experienced yet sublated.
|       +-- Indescribable from unstable ontological status.
|   +-- Trap:
|       +-- Same term anirvacanīya does not imply same reason/status.
|
+-- XXI. AVIDYĀ AND ITS LOCUS
|   +-- Beginningless ignorance of ātman-Brahman identity.
|   +-- Terminates through knowledge.
|   +-- Produces/grounds adhyāsa and empirical individuality.
|   +-- Locus problem:
|       +-- If Brahman is pure consciousness, ignorance cannot belong to it.
|       +-- If jīva is produced by ignorance, jīva cannot straightforwardly be prior locus.
|   +-- Later lines:
|       +-- Bhāmatī emphasis on jīva as locus.
|       +-- Vivaraṇa association with Brahman under qualification.
|   +-- Keep dispute secondary unless explicitly asked.
|
+-- XXII. ADHYĀSA
|   +-- Superimposition:
|       +-- Appearance elsewhere of what was previously experienced, in standard
|           Śaṅkara formulation.
|   +-- Mutual superimposition:
|       +-- Self's consciousness attributed to body/mind.
|       +-- Body/mind's limitation and agency attributed to self.
|   +-- Natural and beginningless.
|   +-- Grounds subject-object, agent-action, mine/not-mine and ritual transaction.
|   +-- Scripture begins from empirical superimposition to remove it.
|
+-- XXIII. ANIRVACANĪYA-KHYĀTI
|   +-- Illusory object is neither:
|   |   +-- Real, because sublated.
|   |   +-- Unreal, because experienced.
|   +-- Nor both in same sense.
|   +-- Shell-silver:
|       +-- Shell is substratum.
|       +-- Silver appearance projected through avidyā.
|       +-- Correct cognition sublates it.
|   +-- Against Nyāya anyathākhyāti:
|       +-- Elsewhere real silver does not explain immediate "this silver" presentation.
|   +-- Nyāya objection:
|       +-- "Indeterminable" creates third ontological category and violates logic.
|   +-- Advaita reply:
|       +-- It records graded experience/sublation, not a permanent third substance.
|
+-- XXIV. RĀMĀNUJA'S SAPTA-ANUPAPATTI
|   +-- 1. Āśraya-anupapatti:
|   |   +-- No coherent locus of avidyā.
|   +-- 2. Tirodhāna-anupapatti:
|   |   +-- Ignorance cannot conceal self-luminous Brahman.
|   +-- 3. Svarūpa-anupapatti:
|   |   +-- Avidyā's nature as positive/negative is unclear.
|   +-- 4. Anirvacanīyatva-anupapatti:
|   |   +-- Neither real nor unreal is incoherent.
|   +-- 5. Pramāṇa-anupapatti:
|   |   +-- No valid means establishes avidyā.
|   +-- 6. Nivartaka-anupapatti:
|   |   +-- Knowledge cannot remove a positive beginningless entity as claimed.
|   +-- 7. Nivṛtti-anupapatti:
|       +-- Final cessation of avidyā is unintelligible.
|   +-- Positive alternative:
|       +-- Real Brahman, real selves/world, ignorance as real misapprehension rather than
|           inexplicable cosmic stuff.
|
+-- XXV. APṚTHAKSIDDHI
|   +-- Viśiṣṭādvaita relation of inseparable dependence.
|   +-- Cit and acit cannot exist apart from Brahman.
|   +-- Yet they are not numerically identical with Brahman.
|   +-- Aprthaksiddha-viśeṣaṇa:
|       +-- Inseparable qualifier.
|   +-- Śarīra-śarīrī-bhāva:
|       +-- Selves/world as body; Brahman as indwelling self.
|   +-- Body is controlled, supported and exists for self's purpose.
|   +-- Avoid:
|       +-- Samavāya equivalence.
|       +-- Advaita identity.
|       +-- Dvaita external relation.
|
+-- XXVI. PAÑCAVIDHA-BHEDA
|   +-- Madhva's five real differences:
|   |   +-- God–soul.
|   |   +-- God–matter.
|   |   +-- Soul–soul.
|   |   +-- Soul–matter.
|   |   +-- Matter–matter.
|   +-- Difference is constitutive of reality and knowledge.
|   +-- If distinctions illusory, scripture, devotion and liberation lose determinate meaning.
|   +-- Dependence does not cancel difference.
|
+-- XXVII. NIMBĀRKA
|   +-- Dvaitādvaita / difference-and-non-difference.
|   +-- Jīva and matter:
|       +-- Different as dependent and limited.
|       +-- Non-different as powers/modes inseparable from Brahman.
|   +-- Matter:
|       +-- Prākṛta: material nature.
|       +-- Aprākṛta: non-material divine stuff in doctrinal accounts.
|       +-- Kāla: time.
|   +-- Exact sub-classification should follow source; do not substitute Sāṃkhya categories.
|   +-- Liberation retains devotion and distinction.
|
+-- XXVIII. VALLABHA
|   +-- Śuddhādvaita / pure non-dualism.
|   +-- Brahman manifests world really without māyā-based falsification.
|   +-- World is Brahman's real expression.
|   +-- Saṃsāra is bondage-producing mistaken attachment, not world as sheer illusion.
|   +-- Grace and loving participation are central.
|   +-- Mokṣa:
|       +-- Participation in divine bliss, not erasure of manifestation.
|
+-- XXIX. CAITANYA TRADITION
|   +-- Acintyabhedābheda:
|   |   +-- Inconceivable simultaneous difference and non-difference.
|   +-- Jīva/world are energies of God:
|       +-- Non-different as dependent power.
|       +-- Different as finite manifestations.
|   +-- Acintya marks limitation of discursive reconciliation, not arbitrary contradiction.
|   +-- Liberation centres loving devotion while individuality persists.
|
+-- XXX. MOKṢA IN ADVAITA
|   +-- Prāptasya prāptiḥ:
|       +-- Attainment of what is already attained.
|   +-- Self is always Brahman; ignorance alone obstructs recognition.
|   +-- Preparation:
|       +-- Sādhana-catuṣṭaya.
|       +-- Śravaṇa.
|       +-- Manana.
|       +-- Nididhyāsana.
|   +-- Jñāna, not action, directly liberates.
|   +-- Jīvanmukti:
|       +-- Knowledge while body continues.
|   +-- Prārabdha:
|       +-- Already-fructifying karma sustains body like released arrow.
|   +-- Videhamukti:
|       +-- On bodily fall, no further embodiment.
|   +-- No real individual travels to become Brahman.
|
+-- XXXI. MOKṢA IN VIŚIṢṬĀDVAITA
|   +-- Release through knowledge, devotion, surrender and divine grace.
|   +-- Jīva reaches communion/service in divine presence.
|   +-- Individuality remains.
|   +-- Obstructing karma removed.
|   +-- Consciousness expands to intrinsic fullness.
|   +-- Equality with God in bliss does not mean equality in lordship/creation.
|
+-- XXXII. MOKṢA IN DVAITA
|   +-- Eternal distinction from God persists.
|   +-- Loving knowledge and grace yield blissful service.
|   +-- No merger.
|   +-- Tāratamya may imply graded bliss/status among liberated souls.
|   +-- Differs from Rāmānuja:
|       +-- Rāmānuja stresses organic body-soul unity and shared divine enjoyment.
|       +-- Madhva stresses irreducible difference and hierarchy.
|
+-- XXXIII. FOUR-SCHOOL LIBERATION MATRIX
|   +-- Viśiṣṭādvaita:
|   |   +-- Communion with real difference-in-qualified-unity.
|   +-- Dvaita:
|   |   +-- Eternal dependent service with fivefold difference.
|   +-- Śuddhādvaita:
|   |   +-- Grace-filled participation in manifest Brahman.
|   +-- Acintyabhedābheda:
|       +-- Loving relation preserving inconceivable difference/non-difference.
|
+-- XXXIV. LOGICAL CONSEQUENCES OF ADVAITA ABSOLUTISM
|   +-- World becomes dependently real, not ultimate.
|   +-- Individuality and agency become empirical.
|   +-- Ethics/karma valid within vyavahāra.
|   +-- Personal God and devotion are preparatory/empirically real.
|   +-- Pramāṇas operate until self-knowledge sublates duality.
|   +-- Liberation cannot be a newly produced state.
|   +-- Self-referential pressure:
|       +-- If all distinctions empirical, teaching and teacher are also empirical.
|   +-- Advaita reply:
|       +-- A provisional means can remove error and then be relinquished, like thorn
|           removing thorn.
|
+-- XXXV. ŚAṄKARA VERSUS RĀMĀNUJA
|   +-- Brahman:
|   |   +-- Nirguṇa ultimate versus necessarily qualified.
|   +-- World:
|   |   +-- Mithyā/vivarta versus real body/mode.
|   +-- Self:
|   |   +-- Ultimately identical versus real dependent part/mode.
|   +-- Māyā:
|   |   +-- Essential explanatory principle versus incoherent posit.
|   +-- Liberation:
|       +-- Non-dual knowledge versus eternal communion/service.
|   +-- Scriptural method:
|       +-- Identity passages govern versus unity-with-qualification harmonisation.
|
+-- XXXVI. ŚAṄKARA VERSUS MADHVA
|   +-- One ultimate reality versus five eternal differences.
|   +-- Jīva as apparent limitation versus eternally distinct dependent substance.
|   +-- World as mithyā versus fully real.
|   +-- Liberation as identity-recognition versus devotion/service.
|   +-- Madhva objection:
|       +-- Illusory difference undermines scripture and liberation.
|   +-- Advaita reply:
|       +-- Empirical validity remains until non-dual knowledge.
|
+-- XXXVII. MAJOR OBJECTIONS / REPLIES
|   +-- Nirguṇa Brahman is unknowable/nonentity.
|   |   +-- Advaita: it is self-revealing subject, indicated by negation.
|   +-- Māyā is self-contradictory.
|   |   +-- Advaita: anirvacanīya names sublatable experience, not formal contradiction.
|   +-- Mithyā world destroys ethics.
|   |   +-- Advaita: empirical law and obligation remain binding before knowledge.
|   +-- If Brahman is self, bondage is inexplicable.
|   |   +-- Advaita: beginningless adhyāsa has no first event but can terminate.
|   +-- If cit/acit are real modes, Brahman changes.
|   |   +-- Viśiṣṭādvaita: modes transform while essential perfection remains.
|   +-- Eternal difference limits divine unity.
|   |   +-- Dvaita: absolute dependence secures sovereignty without identity.
|   +-- Tāratamya compromises equality.
|       +-- Dvaita: difference belongs to intrinsic nature; criticism remains normatively strong.
|
```
