# Philosophy Optional — Paper II — Philosophy of Religion 09 — Religious Pluralism and Absolute Truth

## Quick-Glance Tree Chart

```text
RELIGIOUS PLURALISM AND THE PROBLEM OF ABSOLUTE TRUTH
|
+-- 0. SYLLABUS BOUNDARY / CENTRAL QUESTION
|   +-- Diversity becomes a philosophical problem when traditions make:
|   |   determinate truth-claims + determinate salvation/liberation claims + demand consistency.
|   +-- Question: can several traditions be authentic without relativism, and can one affirm
|       Absolute Truth without granting one finite tradition exhaustive possession of it?
|   +-- Firewall: social tolerance, epistemic humility, alethic pluralism and
|       soteriological pluralism are FOUR different theses.
|
+-- 1. FOUR AXES — NAME THE AXIS IN SENTENCE ONE
|   +-- TRUTH / ALETHIC       -> which propositions are true, false, symbolic or partly adequate?
|   +-- SALVATION             -> can another path genuinely save, liberate or transform?
|   +-- EPISTEMIC ACCESS      -> what should disagreement do to confidence and warrant?
|   +-- POLITICAL COEXISTENCE -> how should citizens/institutions treat deep disagreement?
|   +-- Illicit slide A: peaceful coexistence => all religions equally true.          [FALSE]
|   +-- Illicit slide B: one Absolute Truth => my finite formulation is exhaustive.  [FALSE]
|
+-- 2. EXCLUSIVISM -> INCLUSIVISM -> PLURALISM
|   +-- EXCLUSIVISM
|   |   +-- One revelation/tradition has uniquely decisive truth and/or saving authority.
|   |   +-- Strength: determinate content + non-contradiction.
|   |   +-- Burden: privileged warrant; contingency of birth.
|   |   +-- Does NOT entail coercion: Locke's incoercibility argument is available to it.
|   |   +-- Attribution chain:
|   |       Cyprian (d. 258), Epistle 73: salus extra ecclesiam non est
|   |       -> later tag extra ecclesiam nulla salus
|   |       -> Lateran IV 1215 / Unam Sanctam 1302 / Florence 1442
|   |       -> Vatican II 1965, Lumen Gentium 16 + Nostra Aetate: move toward inclusivism.
|   +-- INCLUSIVISM
|   |   +-- Others have partial truth / may be saved, but through the home tradition's norm.
|   |   +-- Rahner: “anonymous Christians” = response to grace, not secret membership.
|   |   +-- Differentia: others are included ON TERMS SET BY THE PRIVILEGED TRADITION.
|   |   +-- Cost: asymmetry and patronising redescription.
|   +-- PLURALISM
|       +-- Several traditions are authentic responses to ultimate reality OR attain
|           genuinely different real ends.
|       +-- Denies exhaustive possession; does NOT mean relativism, indifferentism,
|           syncretism, or “all religions say the same thing”.
|
+-- 3. JOHN HICK — COPERNICAN TURN
|   +-- Shift: one church/incarnation at centre -> THE REAL at centre.
|   +-- Four-step argument:
|   |   1 birth-linked persistent diversity
|   |   2 transformation/saintliness across traditions
|   |   3 equal divine concern resists arbitrary birthplace-based salvation
|   |   4 therefore one Real may be experienced through different schemes.
|   +-- Status: possibility / best-explanation hypothesis, not demonstration.
|   +-- Kantian apparatus:
|   |   Real an sich (transcategorial) -> conceptual schemes -> personal/non-personal
|   |   manifestations (personae / impersonae).
|   +-- Departure from Kant: Hick makes the noumenal Real causally related to experience.
|   +-- Criterion: self-centredness -> Reality-centredness.
|       +-- Tests tradition-level authenticity, NOT joint truth of contradictory doctrines.
|   +-- Objections:
|       +-- NOUMENAL: if beyond categories, existence/causality/goodness look illicit.
|       +-- PARITY: pluralism itself is a contested superior meta-belief.
|       +-- IDENTITY/MISSION: traditions are redescribed against self-understanding.
|       +-- FLATTENING: communion, non-dual realisation and cessation may not be one end.
|
+-- 4. EXCLUSIVISM DEFENDED / PLURALISM ACCUSED
|   +-- Plantinga — “A Defense of Religious Exclusivism”
|   |   +-- Arbitrariness: pluralist too holds a contested belief.
|   |   +-- Arrogance: believing p despite dissent is ordinary philosophical practice.
|   |   +-- Irrationality: disagreement may reduce warrant, not extinguish it.
|   |   +-- Limit: establishes permissibility, not truth; birthplace contingency remains.
|   +-- D'Costa
|       +-- Pluralism is covert exclusivism: its second-order theory is treated as uniquely true.
|       +-- Hick's “hypothesis, not creed” reply does not fully remove adjudicating authority.
|
+-- 5. PLURALISMS — DO NOT TREAT AS ONE POSITION
|   +-- Hick / identist        -> one Real, many conditioned responses.
|   +-- S. Mark Heim / differential -> Salvations (1995): different real ends;
|   |                              communion != non-dual realisation != cessation.
|   +-- Panikkar / Cobb        -> mutual transformation through dialogue.
|   +-- Griffin / deep pluralism -> process-based plural ultimates.
|   +-- Verdict: Hick best explains unity but risks revisionism; Heim protects
|       self-description but gives up one final destination.
|
+-- 6. INDIAN RESOURCES — ENGLISH FIRST, EXACTLY QUALIFIED
|   +-- ṚGVEDA 1.164.46: ekaṃ sad viprā bahudhā vadanti.
|   |   +-- Asya vāmasya riddle-hymn; attributed to Dīrghatamas.
|   |   +-- Native subject = naming VEDIC DEITIES (Indra, Mitra, Varuṇa, Agni,
|   |       Garutmān, Yama, Mātariśvan), NOT a ready-made charter of world religions.
|   |   +-- Inter-religious extension is modern and must be argued.
|   +-- Vivekananda — UNIVERSAL RELIGION
|   |   +-- Different temperaments -> knowledge (jñāna), devotion (bhakti), action (karma),
|   |       meditative discipline (rāja) -> realisation, not doctrinal uniformity.
|   |   +-- Chicago 1893: acceptance, not mere toleration.
|   |   +-- Objection: neo-Vedāntic assimilation; status remains contested.
|   +-- Radhakrishnan
|   |   +-- Diverse doctrinal interpretations of common spiritual experience (anubhava).
|   |   +-- Risk: perennialist reduction of genuine difference.
|   +-- Gandhi
|   |   +-- Equal respect + fallibilism; sarva-dharma-samabhāva is a modern summary,
|   |       not an unverified verbatim slogan.
|   +-- Jain many-sidedness
|       +-- reality is many-sided (anekāntavāda)
|       +-- name standpoint (nayavāda)
|       +-- qualify “in a certain respect” (syādvāda; syāt)
|       +-- sevenfold predication (saptabhaṅgī)
|       +-- NOT relativism: respects are stated and checkable; contradiction in the
|           SAME respect is not licensed.
|
+-- 7. ABSOLUTE TRUTH WITHOUT EPISTEMIC ABSOLUTISM
|   +-- Four separations: REFERENT -> FORMULATION -> ACCESS -> SALVATION.
|   +-- Suppressed premise in 2022 stem: “a finite formulation can exhaust the absolute”.
|   +-- Denials of premise:
|   |   +-- Advaita: Brahman without qualities (nirguṇa) beyond exhaustive predication;
|   |       with-qualities (saguṇa) forms structure real devotion.
|   |   +-- Jain conditional predication indexes claims to standpoints.
|   |   +-- Hick's Real is transcategorial.
|   +-- Therefore: ontological absolutism != epistemic monopoly != political privilege.
|   +-- Answer line: “Pluralism need not deny absolute reality; it denies exhaustive
|       possession of it by a finite perspective.”
|
+-- 8. HARD-CONTRADICTION CONTROL
|   +-- Legitimate routing moves: LEVEL / SYMBOL / STANDPOINT / DIFFERENT SOTERIOLOGICAL END.
|   +-- Residue: a personal creator cannot both exist and not exist in the same respect;
|       a permanent self cannot both exist and be denied in the same sense.
|   +-- Alethic exclusivity on one proposition can coexist with soteriological or
|       political pluralism. ACKNOWLEDGE residue; do not harmonise rhetorically.
|
+-- 9. PEER AND DEEP DISAGREEMENT
|   +-- Peer = roughly equal evidence + reasoning ability.
|   +-- Conciliationism / equal weight: Feldman, Christensen -> reduce confidence.
|   +-- Steadfastness: Kelly, van Inwagen -> one's reasoning remains part of evidence.
|   +-- Independence principle = hinge of dispute.
|   +-- Fogelin 1985: deep disagreement lacks shared framework propositions;
|       Wittgenstein's hinge commitments in On Certainty; Lynch extends to epistemic principles.
|   +-- Religion differs over SOURCES / CRITERIA / FRAMEWORK.
|   +-- Consequences:
|       +-- persistence does not prove relativism
|       +-- exclusivism not thereby irrational
|       +-- pluralism not established merely by diversity
|       +-- humility, charity, fallibilism and framework-explicitness become duties.
|   +-- Answer line: unresolvability is EPISTEMIC, not ALETHIC.
|
+-- 10. TOLERATION / RELIGIOUS FREEDOM
|   +-- Toleration = objection + acceptance reasons + power to interfere but restraint.
|   +-- Forst ladder: PERMISSION -> COEXISTENCE -> RESPECT -> ESTEEM.
|   |   Only respect/esteem fit equal citizenship; most historical tolerance was permission.
|   +-- Western stock:
|   |   Locke 1689 incoercibility | Mill 1859 harm + epistemic cost | Rawls overlapping
|   |   consensus | Popper 1945 limited to those rejecting argument and answering with violence.
|   +-- Indian stock:
|   |   Aśoka Rock Edict XII, c. 3rd c. BCE -> do not disparage; concord (samavāya)
|   |   Akbar's ṣulḥ-i kull = policy | Gandhi | Vivekananda | Jain epistemic ground.
|   +-- Freedom bundle: conscience / profess / practise / propagate / change or leave /
|       freedom from compelled observance / associational autonomy.
|   +-- Hard problems:
|       belief-practice asymmetry | coercion/fraud vs persuasion | Shachar's multicultural
|       vulnerability | wall of separation vs Bhargava's principled distance |
|       essential-religious-practices doctrine makes state an arbiter of theology.
|
+-- 11. CONFLICT AND UNIFICATION
|   +-- “History of religions = history of conflicts” — grade the claim:
|   |   involvement [trivial] -> significant cause [arguable] -> defining engine [false].
|   +-- Analytical roles: CAUSE / MARKER / MOBILISER / LEGITIMISER.
|   +-- For: exclusivist certainty + sacralised authority + singular identity;
|   |   Kimball's five markers = typology, not law.
|   +-- Against: Cavanaugh 2009 (religious/secular construction), Armstrong 2014
|   |   (anachronism), Sen 2006 (singular-affiliation fallacy).
|   +-- Counter-evidence: Aśoka, Bhakti/Sufi universalism, abolition, civil rights,
|       satyāgraha, relief and interfaith movements.
|   +-- Religion as global unifier:
|       Durkheim collective effervescence + 1893/1993 Parliaments + Hans Küng
|       AGAINST in-group demarcation + Huntington thesis under criticism + Roy.
|   +-- Three conditions: UNIVERSALIST ETHICS + FALLIBILIST EPISTEMOLOGY +
|       NON-MONOPOLISED IDENTITY.
|
+-- 12. EXAMINER TRAPS
|   +-- Pluralism != relativism; inclusivism != pluralism; tolerance != equal truth.
|   +-- Do not quote RV 1.164.46 without deity-naming context and the modern bridge.
|   +-- Do not attribute extra ecclesiam nulla salus verbatim to Cyprian.
|   +-- Do not omit Vatican II 1965.
|   +-- Do not present exclusivism only as bigotry; state Plantinga, then birthplace cost.
|   +-- Do not treat pluralism as one position; compare Hick and Heim.
|   +-- Do not answer conflict-history with wars; use four roles.
|   +-- Cite Huntington as a criticised thesis, never as a finding.
|   +-- Do not turn deep disagreement into relativism.

        equal religious freedom and dialogue. Hick secures parity at the cost of an elusive

        Jain many-sidedness disciplines claims without abolishing truth. Pluralism succeeds
        only when it preserves non-contradiction and refuses to infer civic respect from,
        or reduce it to, metaphysical agreement.
```
