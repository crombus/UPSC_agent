# Philosophy Optional — Paper II — Philosophy of Religion 09 — Religious Pluralism and Absolute Truth

## Quick-Glance Tree Chart

```text
RELIGIOUS PLURALISM AND ABSOLUTE TRUTH
|
+-- CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
|   +-- Religious diversity creates an epistemic problem because traditions make partly
|       conflicting truth and salvation claims. Exclusivism, inclusivism and pluralism
|       differ over access to truth, not merely social tolerance.
|   +-- Absolute truth need not entail epistemic absolutism: reality may be one while finite,
|       historically situated apprehensions remain partial and corrigible.
|
+-- SCOPE / OWNERSHIP FIREWALL
|   +-- Owns exclusivism/inclusivism/pluralism; Hick's Real/Copernican turn; Vivekananda,
|       Radhakrishnan, Gandhi and Jaina many-sidedness; absolute truth; toleration,
|       religious freedom, conflict/dialogue and deep disagreement.
|   +-- Secularism/multiculturalism and religious language are adjacent owners.
|
+-- SOURCE / ATTRIBUTION CAUTION
|   +-- Hick: An Interpretation of Religion; Vivekananda's universal-religion writings;
|       Radhakrishnan and Gandhi; Jaina anekāntavāda.
|   +-- Popular "truth is one" formulas have multiple textual histories; do not fabricate
|       wording or collapse Vedānta, Jainism and Hick.
|
+-- I. FOUR AXES
|   +-- Ontological: one ultimate reality or many?
|   +-- Epistemic: one tradition uniquely true or all partial?
|   +-- Soteriological: who can attain salvation/liberation?
|   +-- Political: how should diverse citizens coexist?
|   +-- Tolerance does not itself settle truth; pluralism does not automatically dictate policy.
|
+-- II. EXCLUSIVISM
|   +-- One tradition possesses uniquely final truth/saving path.
|   +-- Strength: doctrinal integrity and determinate commitment.
|   +-- Objection: epistemic luck, sincere outsiders and conflict.
|   +-- Reply: truth does not become false because access is unequal; revelation may be particular.
|
+-- III. INCLUSIVISM
|   +-- One tradition normatively final, but outsiders may participate anonymously/partially.
|   +-- Strength: widens salvation while preserving centre.
|   +-- Objection: absorbs others under categories they reject.
|   +-- Reply: shared fulfilment can exceed explicit self-understanding.
|
+-- IV. PLURALISM
|   +-- Multiple traditions are valid responses to ultimate reality.
|   +-- Does not require every doctrine equally true.
|   +-- May be:
|       +-- Common-core/perennial.
|       +-- Hickian noumenal.
|       +-- Complementary.
|       +-- Dialogical.
|       +-- Contextual/pragmatic.
|
+-- V. HICK
|   +-- Copernican revolution:
|       +-- Shift from one's religion at centre to the Real at centre.
|   +-- Real an sich:
|       +-- Ultimate beyond human categories.
|   +-- Real as experienced:
|       +-- Personal/non-personal manifestations shaped by conceptual schemes.
|   +-- Traditions assessed by transformation from self-centredness to Reality-centredness.
|   +-- Objections:
|       +-- Real becomes unknowable.
|       +-- Contradictions are redescribed, not resolved.
|       +-- Hick imposes one meta-theory over traditions.
|   +-- Reply:
|       +-- Distinguish ultimate transcendence from phenomenal religious apprehensions.
|
+-- VI. ABSOLUTE TRUTH
|   +-- Ontic absoluteness: reality is determinate independently of belief.
|   +-- Propositional absoluteness: a claim is unconditionally true.
|   +-- Epistemic absolutism: claimant possesses exhaustive certainty.
|   +-- Political absolutism: claimed truth authorises coercion.
|   +-- First does not entail latter three.
|   +-- Fallibilism permits committed truth claims open to correction.
|
+-- VII. VEDĀNTIC / VIVEKANANDA MODEL
|   +-- One ultimate spiritual reality approached through diverse temperaments/paths.
|   +-- Jñāna, bhakti, karma and rāja-yoga address differing capacities.
|   +-- Universal religion is unity that preserves plurality, not one homogenised creed.
|   +-- Religions can be partial expressions/developments of truth.
|   +-- Objection: non-dual Vedānta becomes hidden superior framework.
|   +-- Reply: practical acceptance and experiential verification aim beyond mere toleration;
|       residual asymmetry should be acknowledged.
|
+-- VIII. RADHAKRISHNAN / GANDHI / JAINISM
|   +-- Radhakrishnan: experiential spiritual core with doctrinal forms.
|   +-- Gandhi: truth is ultimate; human grasp partial, requiring humility/nonviolence.
|   +-- Jain anekāntavāda: reality many-sided; standpoints condition finite judgement.
|   +-- Differences:
|       +-- Jaina pluralist realism is not Vedāntic one-spirit perennialism.
|       +-- Gandhi's ethical humility is not Hick's noumenal model.
|
+-- IX. TOLERATION / FREEDOM / DIALOGUE
|   +-- Toleration: restraint toward disapproved belief; may retain superiority.
|   +-- Religious freedom: conscience, practice, change/exit and equal citizenship.
|   +-- Dialogue: reciprocal learning without requiring doctrinal surrender.
|   +-- Paradox of tolerance: unlimited tolerance of coercive intolerance can destroy freedom.
|   +-- Limits require harm, rights and public justification, not dislike.
|
+-- X. CONFLICT / GLOBALISATION
|   +-- Religion can unite through solidarity, moral universals and transnational community.
|   +-- It can divide through exclusive identity, sacred territory, political mobilisation
|       and dehumanisation.
|   +-- History of religions is not only conflict: coexistence, exchange and reform coexist.
|   +-- Globalisation expands encounter and hybrid identity but can provoke defensive absolutism.
|
+-- XI. DEEP DISAGREEMENT
|   +-- Traditions differ in evidence standards, authorities and concepts of ultimate reality.
|   +-- Rational convergence may not follow from more argument.
|   +-- Responses:
|       +-- Epistemic humility.
|       +-- Internal criticism.
|       +-- Shared practical reasons.
|       +-- Peaceful constitutional coexistence.
|   +-- Disagreement does not prove all claims equally true or rationally incomparable.
|
+-- OBJECTIONS / REPLIES
|   +-- Pluralism destroys truth -> reply: distinguishes ultimate reality from finite access.
|   +-- Pluralism is self-refuting absolute claim -> reply: formulate fallibly/contextually.
|   +-- Exclusivism inevitably violent -> reply: firm belief can coexist with rights/humility.
|   +-- Dialogue dilutes commitment -> reply: understanding is not surrender.
|   +-- Universal religion homogenises -> reply: unity must preserve irreducible differences.
|
+-- VERIFIED THINKER / TEXT / EXAMPLE BANK
|   +-- Hick, Vivekananda, Radhakrishnan, Gandhi and Jaina anekāntavāda.
|   +-- Examples: one Reality/many responses, standpoint-conditioned elephant analogy,
|       interfaith dialogue and conscientious dissent.
|   +-- No fabricated quotations.
|
+-- HIGH-RISK CONCEPTUAL TRAPS
|   +-- Plurality is not pluralism; toleration is not recognition.
|   +-- Inclusivism is not pluralism; pluralism is not relativism.
|   +-- Absolute truth is not exhaustive certainty.
|   +-- Hick's Real is not simply one religion's God.
|   +-- Vivekananda does not advocate identical doctrines.
|   +-- Anekāntavāda is not "all views true".
|   +-- Religious freedom includes exit/non-belief.
```
