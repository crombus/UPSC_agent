|   +-- Integral philosophy seeks to reconcile:
|   |   +-- Spirit and matter.
|   |   +-- One and many.
|   |   +-- Transcendence and immanence.
|   |   +-- Liberation and world-transformation.
|   |   +-- Knowledge, devotion and works.

|   +-- Sat:
|   |   +-- Infinite existence.
|   +-- Cit:
|   |   +-- Consciousness-force, cit-śakti; awareness and power are not ultimately split.

|   +-- Shared:
|   |   +-- Ultimate spiritual reality.
|   |   +-- Essential unity.
|   |   +-- Ignorance as source of divided consciousness.
|   +-- Difference:
|       +-- World is real manifestation, not merely sublatable appearance.
|       +-- Divine is dynamic as consciousness-force.
|       +-- Individual liberation is not final goal.
|       +-- Matter can be transformed, not simply transcended.
|   +-- Aurobindo's anti-illusionism:

|   +-- Absolute does not lose infinity by manifestation.
|   +-- Unity differentiates without fragmentation.
|   +-- Multiplicity is real expression within unity.
|   +-- Creation is not ex nihilo.
|   +-- The involved Divine becomes explicit through graded forms.
|   +-- Analogy:
|       +-- White light differentiating colours can illustrate unity-in-multiplicity,

|   +-- Vijñāna / Truth-Consciousness.
|   +-- Creative mediating principle between Saccidānanda and divided mind.
|   +-- Knows by identity:
|       +-- Knower, knowledge and known are internally one.
|   +-- Holds unity and multiplicity simultaneously.
|   +-- Contains real-idea:
|       +-- Truth that is inherently effective power of manifestation.
|   +-- Self-determination without ignorance.
|   +-- Makes real manifestation intelligible:
|       +-- Absolute does not directly leap into error.
|       +-- Supermind deploys truth into ordered multiplicity.
|   +-- Not ordinary intellect, intuition-flash or highest human reason.
|   +-- Not identical with Saccidānanda:
|       +-- It is first creative self-determination/mediating truth-plane.
|

|   |   +-- Comprehensive unity; multiplicity held implicitly in oneness.
|   +-- Second poise:
|   |   +-- One consciousness distributes itself into centres while retaining unity.
|   +-- Third poise:
|       +-- Individualised standpoint becomes pronounced without loss of truth-unity.
|   +-- These are conceptual descriptions of one truth-conscious operation, not three
|       separate substances or chronological universes.
|

|   +-- Below Supermind, above intuitive mind.
|   +-- Releases multiple divine powers and truths into large separate formations.
|   +-- Still luminous and cosmic, but no longer holds indivisible truth-unity with
|       supramental exactness.
|   +-- Makes division possible without itself being ordinary ignorance.
|   +-- Deities/religious truth-formations can express overmental powers.
|   +-- Trap:
|       +-- Overmind is not a weaker synonym for Supermind.

+-- IX. KNOWLEDGE BY IDENTITY
|   +-- Highest form:
|       +-- Knower knows because it is one with known.
|   +-- Self-knowledge is paradigmatic.
|   +-- Supermind integrates identity with differentiation.
|   +-- Knowledge by intimate contact:
|       +-- Partial recovery of unity.
|   +-- Knowledge by direct cognition/intuition:
|       +-- Truth grasped immediately but may still be limited.
|   +-- Separative knowledge:
|       +-- Subject treats object as external and reconstructs through sense/reason.
|   +-- Lower knowledge is not useless:
|       +-- It is appropriate under division but partial and corrigible.

+-- X. EPISTEMIC ARGUMENT
|   +-- Premise 1: complete self-knowledge is possible only through identity.
|   +-- Premise 2: finite cognition contains intimations of direct insight, sympathy and
|       participation beyond external representation.
|   +-- Premise 3: a consciousness fundamentally one with reality could know multiplicity
|       without ignorance.
|   +-- Conclusion: separative mind is not the final possible mode of knowledge.
|   +-- Objection:
|       +-- Identity may erase critical distance and verification.
|   +-- Reply:
|       +-- Supramental identity includes differentiation and effective truth, not blank fusion.
|   +-- Residual:
|       +-- Public criteria for claimed higher cognition remain difficult.
|
+-- XI. INVOLUTION
|   +-- Prior ontological self-concealment of consciousness in lower forms.
|   +-- Necessary because evolution cannot manifest what is wholly absent.
|   +-- Descent:
|       +-- Saccidānanda -> Supermind -> Overmind/mind -> life -> matter -> Inconscient.
|   +-- Each lower level:
|       +-- Conceals higher powers.
|       +-- Retains them involved as potential.
|   +-- Involution is not a past physical journey in clock time.
|   +-- It is the ontological condition that makes ascending evolution intelligible.
|
+-- XII. MATTER AND THE INCONSCIENT
|   +-- Inconscient:
|       +-- Consciousness maximally involved and self-obscured.
|   +-- Matter is not independent opposite of Spirit.
|   +-- It is Spirit's densest self-formulation.
|   +-- Therefore consciousness need not emerge from absolute non-conscious nothing.
|   +-- Materialist objection:
|       +-- Consciousness can emerge from complex matter without prior involution.
|   +-- Aurobindo reply:
|       +-- Emergence of radically novel consciousness is more intelligible if consciousness
|           is latent in reality's ground.
|   +-- Residual:
|       +-- "Involved" may restate rather than empirically explain emergence.
|
+-- XIII. EVOLUTION
|   +-- Ascending manifestation:
|       +-- Matter -> Life -> Mind -> higher mind ranges -> Supermind.
|   +-- Life exceeds yet depends on matter.
|   +-- Mind exceeds yet depends on life.
|   +-- Supermind is next principle capable of transforming mind/life/body.
|   +-- Evolution has:
|       +-- Emergent novelty.
|       +-- Continuity because higher is involved in lower.
|       +-- Teleological direction toward fuller manifestation.
|   +-- Not a denial of biological evolution:
|       +-- It proposes metaphysical depth and future consciousness beyond biological account.
|
+-- XIV. DARWIN AND AUROBINDO
|   +-- Darwinian theory:
|       +-- Explains biological variation, selection and adaptation through empirical mechanisms.
|   +-- Aurobindo:
|       +-- Asks why matter can support life, life mind, and mind spiritual consciousness.
|   +-- Compatible level claim:
|       +-- Biological mechanisms can mediate a wider evolution.
|   +-- Difference:
|       +-- Darwin does not require cosmic teleology.
|       +-- Aurobindo posits involved consciousness and purposive direction.
|   +-- Do not present supramental evolution as established biological science.

+-- XV. MAN AS TRANSITIONAL BEING
|   +-- Mental consciousness is not evolutionary endpoint.
|   +-- Human capacity for self-reflection enables conscious collaboration.
|   +-- Mind:
|       +-- Divides, analyses and reconstructs.
