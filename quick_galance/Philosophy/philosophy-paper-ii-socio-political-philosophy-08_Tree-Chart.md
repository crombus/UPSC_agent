|       valuable lives without destroying ecological conditions or subordinating tribal

|
+-- I. GROWTH / DEVELOPMENT / PROGRESS
|   +-- Growth:
|       +-- Quantitative expansion of output/income.
|   +-- Development:
|       +-- Structural and qualitative enlargement of capabilities, health, education,
|           institutions and opportunities.
|   +-- Social progress:
|       +-- Normative improvement in justice, freedom, dignity, inclusion and sustainability.
|   +-- Growth can occur:
|       +-- With inequality.
|       +-- Without public health.
|       +-- Through ecological destruction.
|   +-- Therefore indicators are means, not complete moral verdict.
|
+-- II. MEASURES
|   +-- Income/resources.
|   +-- Basic needs.
|   +-- Welfare/utility.
|   +-- Quality of life.
|   +-- Capabilities/functionings.
|   +-- Agency/participation.
|   +-- Distribution/recognition.
|   +-- Ecological resilience.
|   +-- No single scalar captures all dimensions without value judgement.
|
+-- III. ECONOMISTIC MODEL
|   +-- Assumes rising production/income drives welfare.
|   +-- Strengths:
|       +-- Resources fund health, education and infrastructure.
|       +-- Employment/productivity can reduce deprivation.
|   +-- Limits:
|       +-- Distribution ignored.
|       +-- Unpaid/care work omitted.
|       +-- Ecological costs externalised.
|       +-- Agency and dignity reduced to consumption.
|       +-- Growth may coexist with displacement/exclusion.
|
+-- IV. MODERNISATION
|   +-- Linear movement from traditional to industrial, urban, rational-bureaucratic society.
|   +-- Assumptions:
|       +-- Western historical path as universal.
|       +-- Tradition as obstacle.
|       +-- Technology/investment as main driver.
|   +-- Contributions:
|       +-- Structural transformation, education and institutional capacity.
|   +-- Criticisms:
|       +-- Eurocentrism.
|       +-- Homogenisation.
|       +-- Colonial power neglected.
|       +-- Local knowledge undervalued.
|
+-- V. HUMAN DEVELOPMENT
|   +-- People are ends, not instruments of production.
|   +-- Core dimensions:
|       +-- Long healthy life.
|       +-- Knowledge.
|       +-- Decent material conditions.
|       +-- Agency and participation.
|   +-- Development asks what people can actually be and do.
|   +-- Aggregate achievement must be disaggregated for distribution/exclusion.
|
+-- VI. SEN: DEVELOPMENT AS FREEDOM
|   +-- Development = expansion of substantive freedoms.
|   +-- Constitutive role:
|       +-- Freedom is part of development.
|   +-- Instrumental role:
|       +-- Freedoms reinforce one another.
|   +-- Instrumental freedoms:
|       +-- Political freedoms.
|       +-- Economic facilities.
|       +-- Social opportunities.
|       +-- Transparency guarantees.
|       +-- Protective security.
|   +-- Capability:
|       +-- Real opportunity set.
|   +-- Functioning:
|       +-- Achieved being/doing.
|   +-- Agency:
|       +-- Pursuing goals one values, beyond personal welfare alone.
|
+-- VII. CAPABILITY CONVERSION
|   +-- Same resources yield different freedoms because of:
|       +-- Disability/age/health.
|       +-- Gender norms.
|       +-- Environment/location.
|       +-- Public services.
|       +-- Social discrimination.
|   +-- Formal access is not effective capability.
|   +-- Adaptive preference:
|       +-- Deprived persons may lower aspirations.
|   +-- Therefore subjective satisfaction alone under-measures injustice.
|
+-- VIII. NUSSBAUM
|   +-- Defends threshold of central human capabilities.
|   +-- Political goal:
|       +-- Each person secured minimum dignity-compatible opportunity.
|   +-- More determinate than Sen's open-ended comparative approach.
|   +-- Strength:
|       +-- Protects basic claims against local neglect.
|   +-- Objection:
|       +-- Universal list risks paternalism/cultural imposition.
|   +-- Reply:
|       +-- Capabilities are opportunities, not compelled functionings; list can be
|           politically specified/revised.
|
+-- IX. DEPENDENCY / POST-DEVELOPMENT
|   +-- Dependency:
|       +-- Global centre-periphery relations structure underdevelopment.
|       +-- Trade/capital patterns can extract surplus and constrain autonomy.
|   +-- Post-development:
|       +-- "Development" discourse can classify communities as deficient and legitimise
|           expert control.
|       +-- Values local autonomy/plural ways of living.
|   +-- Objection:
|       +-- Romanticises locality and may deny infrastructure/rights sought by poor.
|   +-- Reply:
|       +-- Strongest critique targets top-down domination, not health/education as such.
|
+-- X. SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT
|   +-- Present well-being must not destroy future generations' capacity.
|   +-- Integrates:
|       +-- Economic viability.
|       +-- Social justice.
|       +-- Ecological limits.
|   +-- Weak sustainability:
|       +-- Natural capital may be substituted by human/manufactured capital.
|   +-- Strong sustainability:
|       +-- Critical ecosystems are non-substitutable.
|   +-- Intergenerational justice:
|       +-- Future persons lack voice yet bear current costs.
|   +-- Intragenerational justice:
|       +-- Poor communities often bear transition/conservation burdens.
|
+-- XI. SOCIAL PROGRESS
|   +-- Change is not automatically progress.
|   +-- Linear view:
|       +-- History advances through reason/science/institutions.
|   +-- Cyclical view:
|       +-- Civilisations rise/decline.
|   +-- Plural view:
|       +-- Gains in one dimension may accompany losses in another.
|   +-- Criteria:
|       +-- Reduced avoidable suffering.
|       +-- Expanded capabilities.
|       +-- Equal status.
|       +-- Democratic agency.
|       +-- Ecological viability.
|       +-- Peace and social trust.
|
+-- XII. NECESSARY / SUFFICIENT CONDITIONS
|   +-- Economic development often necessary:
|       +-- Resources for public goods and poverty reduction.
|   +-- Not strictly necessary for every moral/institutional improvement:
|       +-- Rights reform may occur without prior high income.
|   +-- Never sufficient:
|       +-- Distribution, institutions, recognition and sustainability mediate outcomes.
|   +-- Best verdict:
|       +-- Material adequacy is enabling condition within a larger normative/institutional set.
|
+-- XIII. DISTRIBUTION / RECOGNITION / PARTICIPATION
|   +-- Distribution:
|       +-- Who receives resources/opportunities?
|   +-- Recognition:
|       +-- Whose identity/knowledge/status counts?
|   +-- Participation:
|       +-- Who defines needs and decides projects?
|   +-- Development imposed on passive recipients reproduces domination.
|   +-- Empowerment means effective voice plus capability to influence outcomes.
|
+-- XIV. DEMOCRACY AND PROGRESS
|   +-- Public reasoning reveals needs and trade-offs.
|   +-- Accountability reduces catastrophic neglect.
|   +-- Participation gives procedural legitimacy.
|   +-- But elections alone do not ensure long-term/ecological justice.
|   +-- Institutions need minority voice, expertise and intergenerational representation.
|
+-- XV. TECHNOLOGY AND ETHICAL STANDARDS
|   +-- Technology expands means, information and control.
|   +-- It can improve transparency, health and education.
|   +-- It can also intensify surveillance, inequality, violence and manipulation.
|   +-- No automatic moral progress:
|       +-- Technical capacity does not supply worthy ends.
|   +-- Value-laden design:
|       +-- Technologies embody choices about access, risk and power.
|   +-- Ethical progress requires institutions, education and accountability.
|
+-- XVI. SKILL EDUCATION
|   +-- Enhances employability, productivity and practical agency.
|   +-- Development gains depend on:
|       +-- Quality.
|       +-- Labour demand.
|       +-- Mobility.
|       +-- Equal access.
|       +-- Worker rights.
|       +-- Capacity for lifelong adaptation.
|   +-- Narrow skilling can reduce education to market utility.
|   +-- Balanced model joins skill with critical citizenship and general capability.
|
+-- XVII. GANDHIAN DEVELOPMENT
|   +-- Swaraj:
|       +-- Self-rule of person/community.
|   +-- Swadeshi:
|       +-- Local responsibility and production.
|   +-- Village decentralisation.
|   +-- Bread labour and dignity of work.
|   +-- Trusteeship and limits to accumulation.
|   +-- Simplicity/need-limitation.
|   +-- Ahiṃsā in means and institutions.
|   +-- Development measured by moral autonomy and weakest person's welfare, not output alone.

|       +-- Displacement.

|       +-- Communities are diverse, changing and internally unequal.

|       +-- Human interests central.

|       +-- Challenges human-centred exploitation.
|   +-- Criticism:

|       +-- Social success equated with ever-rising production/consumption.
|   +-- Degrowth:

|   +-- Objection:
|       +-- Poor societies need growth.

|       +-- Democratic planning and differentiated responsibility.
