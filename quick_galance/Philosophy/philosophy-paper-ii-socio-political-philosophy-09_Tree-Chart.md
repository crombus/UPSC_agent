|   +-- Sex: bodily/reproductive traits, themselves more varied than crude binary summaries.

|   +-- Distinction exposes naturalisation of hierarchy.
|   +-- Interaction matters: bodies are socially interpreted; social conditions affect bodies.

|   +-- Socialisation assigns work, conduct, dress, mobility and aspiration.
|   +-- Institutions repeat and reward norms until they appear natural.

|   +-- Liberal: unequal rights/opportunities; remedy legal equality and access.

|   +-- Socialist: capitalism and patriarchy interact but neither reduces wholly to other.
|   +-- Care ethics: abstract justice overlooks dependence, relationship and care.
|   +-- Intersectional: gender is mediated by caste, class, race, religion, disability and region.

|   +-- Family is not pre-political: labour, inheritance, violence and socialisation shape citizenship.
|   +-- Pateman: social contract can conceal sexual contract.
|   +-- Okin: unjust family reproduces unequal opportunity.
|   +-- Young: harms can arise from many ordinary actions and institutions without one sole culprit.
|   +-- Responsibility is forward-looking and shared according to power, privilege and capacity.

|   +-- Sameness approach: identical standards prevent paternalism.
|   +-- Difference approach: pregnancy/care/social disadvantage require accommodation.
|   +-- Risk of sameness: male norm treated as neutral.
|   +-- Risk of difference: stereotypes become permanent.
|   +-- Resolution: equal status with context-sensitive capability and anti-subordination.
|
+-- VI. FEMALE FOETICIDE
|   +-- Sex-selective elimination expresses unequal valuation before birth.
|   +-- Causal structure:
|       +-- Son preference + patrilineal inheritance + dowry/status norms +
|           old-age insecurity + small-family preference + technology misuse.
|   +-- Technology is enabling means, not sole cause.
|   +-- Moral wrong:
|       +-- Discrimination, bodily coercion, commodification and demographic/social harm.
|   +-- Sen's missing-women frame identifies cumulative excess female mortality/absence;
|       exact contemporary numbers require verification.
|   +-- Social imbalance can intensify trafficking, forced marriage and violence, but avoid
|       treating women instrumentally as demographic resources.
|
+-- VII. LAND / PROPERTY RIGHTS
|   +-- Property supplies bargaining power, security, credit, exit and public status.
|   +-- Distinguish:
|       +-- Legal title.
|       +-- Effective possession.
|       +-- Control over use/income.
|       +-- Inheritance.
|       +-- Joint versus independent ownership.
|   +-- Formal law may fail through family pressure, records, awareness and social sanctions.
|   +-- Property is powerful but insufficient without bodily autonomy, education, safety
|       and decision-making power.
|
+-- VIII. EMPOWERMENT
|   +-- Power over: control/domination.
|   +-- Power to: capacity to act.
|   +-- Power with: collective action.
|   +-- Power within: self-respect/critical consciousness.
|   +-- Resources -> agency -> achievements.
|   +-- Agency is not mere receipt of welfare.
|   +-- Adaptive preference: apparent consent may reflect narrowed options.
|   +-- Capability approach asks what women are genuinely able to do and be.
|
+-- IX. CARE ETHICS
|   +-- Gilligan foregrounds responsibility, relationship and responsiveness.
|   +-- Corrects abstract atomistic moral reasoning.
|   +-- Risk: associating women naturally with care reinforces unpaid burden.
|   +-- Best synthesis:
|       +-- Socialise and fairly distribute care.
|       +-- Join care with rights and justice rather than oppose them.
|

|   +-- Redistribution: income, land, education, time and services.
|   +-- Recognition: equal status and freedom from degrading norms.
|   +-- Representation: voice in decisions and agenda-setting.
|   +-- Descriptive presence can aid voice but does not guarantee substantive transformation.
|   +-- Empowerment can reduce discrimination, yet institutions and backlash may reproduce it.

|   +-- Equality is not automatic:
|       +-- Domestic labour, patriarchal culture and political leadership may remain unequal.
