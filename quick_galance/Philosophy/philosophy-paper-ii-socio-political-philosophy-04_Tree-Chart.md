+-- I. PLATO - THE DESCENT, AND THE CRITIQUE OF DEMOCRACY
|   +-- SEQUENCE: aristocracy of knowledge -> TIMOCRACY (honour) -> OLIGARCHY (wealth;
|   |   the city splits rich/poor) -> DEMOCRACY (freedom and equality of desires) ->
|   |   TYRANNY (one demagogue).
|   +-- ENGINE: the ordering principle slips from REASON to APPETITE; the city is the soul
|   |   written large, so political decay is moral decay.
|   +-- NOT a historical prediction; NOT a defence of hereditary monarchy; democracy is
|   |   SECOND-worst, and its vice is that it PRODUCES tyranny.
|   +-- THREE ARGUMENTS AGAINST DEMOCRACY: (1) rule is a CRAFT requiring knowledge - the
|   |   SHIP-OF-STATE analogy; (2) democracy mistakes LIBERTY for QUALIFICATION, levelling
|   |   knowledge and ignorance; (3) unregulated liberty makes restraint look oppressive
|   |   and breeds the demagogue.
|   +-- DEMOCRATIC REPLY on four axes: COMPETENCE (expertise informs, citizens authorise);
|   |   FREEDOM (constitutional liberty ordered by rights and accountability); DESIRE
|   |   (plural media, opposition, review expose manipulation); UNITY (legitimate
|   |   plurality beats enforced unity).
|   +-- ASYMMETRIC VERDICT: the DIAGNOSIS survives; the REMEDY fails - philosopher-rule
|       supplies neither consent nor external correction of the guardians' own error.
|
+-- II. ARISTOTLE - TWO VARIABLES, SIX FORMS, AND POLITY
|   +-- CRITERIA: how many rule x whether rule serves the COMMON or a PRIVATE advantage.
|   +-- ONE: monarchy / tyranny. FEW: aristocracy / oligarchy. MANY: POLITY / democracy in
|   |   the classical DEVIANT sense.
|   +-- Members of a pair are STRUCTURALLY IDENTICAL and differ ONLY in purpose - so no
|   |   institutional description alone delivers a normative verdict.
|   +-- TRAP: the good many-form is POLITY. Naming democracy as the good form collapses the
|   |   purpose axis; reading his "democracy" as the modern kind is the standing anachronism.
|   +-- NUMBER TRACKS CLASS: oligarchy = the wealthy few; democracy = the poor many. Hence
|   |   POLITY - the mixed constitution, the many under law, moderated by property, virtue
|   |   and participation, resting on a large MIDDLE stratum.
|   +-- Middle course in three directions: against STATISM, against INDIVIDUALISM, for
|   |   CIVIC MODERATION. Plato asks who ought ideally to rule; Aristotle asks what
|   |   constitution can SUSTAIN good political life under real social conditions.
|   +-- LIMIT: polity is an institutional PRECURSOR of constitutional democracy, never an
|       identity (Aristotle excluded most residents), and it argues STABILITY, not JUSTICE.
|
+-- III. REGIME GRAMMAR BEYOND THE PRINTED THREE
|   +-- CONSTITUTIONAL: limited powers under general law, rights, review, removal. TEST -
|   |   a constitution in NAME is insufficient if limits cannot be ENFORCED.
|   +-- ABSOLUTE: final power not institutionally answerable. Decisiveness supplies neither
|   |   legitimacy nor error-correction.
|   +-- DICTATORSHIP names CONCENTRATION of decision; AUTHORITARIANISM names SUPPRESSION of
|   |   contestation with residual plurality; TOTALITARIANISM names PENETRATION of the whole
|   |   of social life. NOT synonyms; real regimes are mixtures that change over time.
|   +-- Source control: Gauba local PDF pp. 510-514; the authoritarian/totalitarian
|       distinction is a Linz/Arendt reconstruction, not a printed syllabus limb.
|
+-- IV. MONARCHY - THE SPLIT THAT DECIDES BOTH OWNED STEMS
|   +-- DEFINITION: government by ONE ruler, usually holding office by HEREDITY.
|   +-- ABSOLUTE: rules directly; concentrates executive, legislative and sometimes
|   |   judicial functions; weak institutional accountability; efficient but normatively
|   |   risky.
|   +-- CONSTITUTIONAL: REIGNS but does not GOVERN; historic continuity PLUS constitutional
|   |   limitation; accountability SHIFTS to cabinet and parliament; acceptable as
|   |   SYMBOLIC HEADSHIP.
|   +-- FOR: unity and continuity; decision against faction; the ABOVE-POLITICS symbol.
|   +-- AGAINST: EQUALITY (office closed to equal claimants); MERIT (birth guarantees
|   |   neither wisdom nor virtue - Plato's ruler is chosen by TRAINING); ACCOUNTABILITY
|   |   (concentration invites arbitrariness); FREEDOM (survives only where law, rights and
|   |   institutions restrain the crown).
|   +-- ASYMMETRY: defences are PRUDENTIAL and SYMBOLIC; objections are NORMATIVE.
|   |   Usefulness is not entitlement.
|   +-- MERIT -> REMOVABILITY SHIFT: the strong objection is that NO regular non-violent
|   |   procedure exists to act on the finding that a ruler is incompetent.
|   +-- WEBER: monarchy is the paradigm of TRADITIONAL authority; ROUTINISATION explains why
|       hereditary succession is SYSTEMATIC - and why that solves SUCCESSION, not
|       ACCOUNTABILITY.
|
+-- V. DIVINE RIGHT - THE BRIDGE, NOT AN IDENTITY
|   +-- DOCTRINE: the monarch rules by GOD'S WILL and is answerable PRIMARILY TO GOD, not
|   |   to the people. It SACRALISED authority and strengthened absolutism.
|   +-- FORMULA: divine right makes monarchy THEOLOGICALLY LEGITIMATED; theocracy makes
|   |   theology POLITICALLY CONSTITUTIVE.
|   +-- CHAINS: divine-right monarchy = God -> KING -> subjects (the king is decisive);
|   |   theocracy = God -> sacred law -> INTERPRETERS -> subjects (the interpreter is
|   |   decisive, since sacred law does not apply itself).
|   +-- CRISES DIFFER: monarchy breaks at SUCCESSION and personal conduct; theocracy breaks
|   |   at INTERPRETATION and rival interpreters.
|   +-- Divine right is a THEORY OF LEGITIMATION attached to monarchy, never a fourth form.
|   +-- CONCEDE: the distinction weakens where the ruler is ALSO the chief interpreter.
|
+-- VI. THEOCRACY - FEATURES, DEFECT AND THE SECULARISM LINE
|   +-- DEFINITION: political authority claimed in the NAME OF GOD, divine law, or
|   |   AUTHORISED INTERPRETERS. Not a religious society - a polity whose ULTIMATE
|   |   SOVEREIGNTY is divine rather than popular.
|   +-- FIVE FEATURES: sovereignty of God; sacred law guiding or determining civil law;
|   |   clerical mediation; limited pluralism (dissent as theological deviance); fusion of
|   |   religious and political legitimacy.
|   +-- CORE CLAIM: norms are RECEIVED, not created - which places rulers under a higher law
|   |   they cannot rewrite. This is the STRONGEST thing that can be said for it.
|   +-- STRUCTURAL DEFECT: divine law must still be APPLIED, so the INTERPRETER acquires
|   |   power insulated from criticism, because objection to him can be recast as IMPIETY.
|   |   The defect arises EVEN WHERE EVERY INTERPRETER IS SINCERE.
|   +-- THE LINE THAT DECIDES EVERY STEM: religiously informed PUBLIC REASONING (compatible
|   |   with constitutional democracy) vs THEOCRATIC SOVEREIGNTY (constitutive authority for
|   |   religious law - not compatible). DISSENTER TEST: ordinary dissent, or impiety?
|   +-- FOUR-POINT TENSION WITH SECULARISM: privileges one framework; constrains dissent and
|   |   apostasy; makes citizenship ASYMMETRICAL; narrows the public basis of law from
|   |   SHARED REASON to SECTARIAN AUTHORITY.
|   +-- INDIA (illustration only): Articles 25-28 - freedom of conscience and religion
|       SUBJECT TO public order, morality and health; no religious instruction in wholly
|       State-funded institutions. A dated legal fact; it proves no philosophical thesis.
|
+-- VII. DEMOCRACY - THE DISTINCTIONS THAT PRECEDE EVERY VERDICT
|   +-- CORE IDEA: rule of the people, implying not only majority rule but POLITICAL
|   |   EQUALITY, PARTICIPATION, ACCOUNTABILITY, PUBLIC JUSTIFICATION and some PROTECTION
|   |   OF RIGHTS.
|   +-- DIRECT (participation; poor scaling) vs REPRESENTATIVE (scale; distance). Modern
|   |   democracies are mainly representative, with referenda and local assemblies as
|   |   direct residues.
|   +-- PROCEDURAL (are rulers chosen fairly?) vs SUBSTANTIVE (do people actually govern
|   |   under fair social conditions?). A polity may hold elections and remain deeply
|   |   unequal, exclusionary or manipulative.
|   +-- REPRESENTATION TOOLS: DELEGATE (instructions) / TRUSTEE (judgment) / MANDATE
|   |   (programme); DESCRIPTIVE (who they are) vs SUBSTANTIVE (what they do).
|   +-- FIVE MODELS with their pathologies: MAJORITARIAN (tyranny of the majority);
|   |   REPRESENTATIVE (distance, elite capture); LIBERAL (formal safeguards with social
|   |   exclusion); PARTICIPATORY (fatigue, unequal voice); DELIBERATIVE (demanding
|   |   conditions of time and equality).
|   +-- HABERMAS: legitimacy grows where laws emerge through COMMUNICATION approximating
|   |   FREEDOM, RECIPROCITY and ABSENCE OF DOMINATION. Deliberation DEEPENS voting.
|   +-- EPISTEMIC DEFENCE, stated correctly: not that opinions track truth, but that
|   |   democratic PROCEDURES allow ERROR-DETECTION, REVISION and access to DISPERSED
|   |   SOCIAL KNOWLEDGE.
|   +-- EXPERTISE UNDER DEMOCRATIC CONTROL: authorise goals, demand reasons, hold experts
|   |   accountable - neither expertise enthroned nor democracy replaced by polling.
|   +-- THREE BOUNDED ANCHORS, ONE JOB EACH: ROUSSEAU - representation is INCOMPLETE;
|       MILL - participation EDUCATES (with a competence-weighting residue); TOCQUEVILLE -
|       majority rule needs ASSOCIATIONS, LOCAL SELF-GOVERNMENT, FREE PRESS and REVIEW.
|
+-- VIII. DESIGN AXES - TRADE-OFFS, NOT TESTS OF DEMOCRACY
|   +-- PARLIAMENTARY (fusion; executive answerable to legislature) vs PRESIDENTIAL
|   |   (separation; separate mandate and fixed tenure) - responsibility and removability
|   |   against stability and deadlock.
|   +-- UNITARY (delegated powers) vs FEDERAL (constitutionally divided competences) -
|   |   uniformity and decisiveness against autonomy and diversity-management.
|   +-- FUSION vs SEPARATION OF POWERS - coordination against prevention of arbitrary
|   |   concentration. CENTRALISATION vs DECENTRALISATION under SUBSIDIARITY - uniform
|   |   standards against participation and local accountability.
|   +-- CONTROL: fusion need not mean absence of checks, separation need not mean perfect
|   |   independence. Ask whether each organ can EXPLAIN, CONTEST and CORRECT the others
|   |   without PARALYSING government.
|   +-- INDIA: parliamentary government with a HOLDING-TOGETHER federal structure under
|       constitutional supremacy and judicial review; Parts IX and IX-A inserted by the
|       Constitution (Seventy-third and Seventy-fourth Amendment) Acts, 1992. NO AXIS BY
|       ITSELF proves a regime democratic.
|
+-- IX. LIBERAL DEMOCRACY, MINORITIES, LIBERTY AND EQUALITY
|   +-- LIBERAL DEMOCRACY: popular rule + constitutional limits + rights + rule of law +
|   |   minority protection, so that the majority governs WITHOUT CONVERTING NUMBER INTO
|   |   UNRESTRICTED POWER and electoral defeat remains SURVIVABLE.
|   +-- WHY PROTECTION IS CONSTITUTIVE: democracy without rights becomes majoritarianism;
|   |   citizenship requires EQUAL MORAL STANDING, not counting heads.
|   +-- ACHIEVEMENTS (institutional): legal guarantees, representation, judicial review,
|   |   civil liberties, protected dissent, constitutional accommodation.
|   +-- LIMITS (social): prejudice outlasting formal equality; majoritarian electoral
|   |   incentive; minorities TOLERATED FORMALLY but EXCLUDED SUBSTANTIVELY.
|   +-- ASYMMETRY: achievements are INSTITUTIONAL, limits are SOCIAL - institutions may work
|   |   as designed while the protection they promise fails.
|   +-- VERDICT: normatively COMMITTED; actual success depends on CONSTITUTIONAL CULTURE,
|   |   SOCIAL EQUALITY and INSTITUTIONAL INDEPENDENCE.
|   +-- LIBERTY vs EQUALITY: held in INSTITUTIONALISED TENSION. Liberty without equality
|   |   entrenches domination by wealth and status; equality without liberty flattens
|   |   individuality and dissent.
|   +-- DISTINCTIVENESS TEST: rivals can secure both values - a limited monarchy protects
|   |   liberties, a benevolent absolutism formalises legal equality. Only democracy
|   |   institutionalises EQUAL AUTHORISATION and PEACEFUL REPLACEMENT.
|   +-- AMBEDKAR: political equality cannot long survive amidst deep social and economic
|       inequality. Fraternity and constitutional morality are the COHESION side - a
|       COMPLEMENT to rights, never a SUBSTITUTE.
|
+-- X. LEGITIMACY, ELITE COMPETITION AND DEMOCRATIC DECAY
|   +-- WEBER: POWER = compliance despite resistance; AUTHORITY = power believed RIGHTFUL.
|   |   TRADITIONAL (immemorial custom; patrimonial staff; succession by inheritance);
|   |   CHARISMATIC (devotion to an exceptional person; no career structure; acute
|   |   succession crisis); LEGAL-RATIONAL (belief in enacted rules; BUREAUCRACY;
|   |   succession solved impersonally by rule).
|   +-- PRESUPPOSITION: legitimacy is SOCIOLOGICAL BELIEF, not normative justification.
|   |   NEVER equate "legitimate" with "justified".
|   +-- ROUTINISATION: charisma must convert into traditional or legal-rational form -
|   |   movement -> party -> bureaucracy.
|   +-- THE CROSSING: a democracy is normally LEGAL-RATIONAL yet may be captured by a
|   |   PLEBISCITARY-CHARISMATIC leader WHILE RETAINING ITS LEGAL FORMS.
|   +-- SCHUMPETER: democracy = the COMPETITIVE STRUGGLE FOR THE PEOPLE'S VOTE; the people
|   |   PRODUCE A GOVERNMENT, they do not GOVERN. A FLOOR, not a CEILING.
|   +-- MICHELS (1911): scale, specialisation and control of information entrench a
|   |   permanent leadership - defensibly a RESISTIBLE TENDENCY, not an IRON law.
|   +-- THE SEQUENCE THAT MAKES AN ARGUMENT: PLATO objects on COMPETENCE -> MICHELS on
|   |   ORGANISATION -> SCHUMPETER concedes both and REDEFINES democracy.
|   +-- POPULISM, CONTESTED: MUDDE 2004 thin-centred, pure people vs corrupt elite, attaches
|   |   to a HOST ideology; MULLER 2016 EXCLUSIVE MORAL REPRESENTATION - anti-elitism is
|   |   compatible with democracy, ANTI-PLURALISM IS NOT; LACLAU 2005 a logic of
|   |   ARTICULATION, potentially democratising. State the CONTEST, then choose with reasons.
|   +-- THE SLIDE: representation deficit -> a leader embodies "the real people" ->
|   |   disagreement becomes BETRAYAL -> checks recast as ELITE OBSTRUCTION -> weakened,
|   |   captured or bypassed WHILE ELECTIONS CONTINUE -> ILLIBERAL DEMOCRACY.
|   +-- FORM RETAINED / SUBSTANCE ERODED: elections vs FAIRNESS of contest; majority rule vs
|   |   LIMITS on majorities; courts vs INDEPENDENCE; press vs PLURALITY of ownership;
|   |   opposition vs REALISTIC ALTERNATION.
|   +-- PROPAGANDA: a judgment is the citizen's OWN only under ACCESS, EXPOSURE and FREEDOM
|   |   FROM MANIPULATION. Consent so produced is MANUFACTURED, so an election may confer
|   |   SOCIOLOGICAL legitimacy without NORMATIVE authorisation.
|   +-- REGULATOR'S DILEMMA: every content-based remedy hands the state POWER OVER PUBLIC
|   |   TRUTH. Defensible remedies are STRUCTURAL - plural ownership, transparency of
|   |   political finance and paid messaging, independent electoral adjudication, protected
|   |   journalism, civic education, rights of reply.
|   +-- SIX PATHOLOGIES: tyranny of the majority; elite capture; short-termism; populist
|       anti-pluralism; manufactured consent; bureaucratic domination - each with its
|       institutional reply and its residue.
|
+-- XI. EXAMINER TRAPS - FINAL PRE-SUBMISSION CHECK

|   +-- "Theocracy means religion influences politics" -> it makes divine law or authorised
|   |   interpreters the GOVERNING SOURCE of political authority.
|   +-- "Democracy is periodic election" -> electoral, liberal, participatory, deliberative
|   |   and substantive democracy are different claims.
|   +-- "Aristotle's polity = modern democracy" -> a mixed-government precursor only.
|   +-- "Plato hated freedom" -> reconstruct competence, order and demagoguery first.
|   +-- "Secularism excludes every religious voice" -> it concerns EQUAL CITIZENSHIP and the
|   |   institutional relation of state and religion.
|   +-- "Dictatorship = authoritarianism = totalitarianism" -> concentration, suppression,
|   |   penetration.
|   +-- "Parliamentary = democratic, presidential = authoritarian"; "federalism = democracy"

|   +-- "Legitimate = justified" in Weber -> legitimacy there is BELIEF about rightfulness.
