# Philosophy Optional — Paper II — Philosophy of Religion 03 — Problem of Evil

## Quick-Glance Tree Chart

```text
PROBLEM OF EVIL: DIVINE PERFECTION, FREEDOM, SUFFERING AND THEODICY
|
+-- CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
|   +-- Evil becomes a philosophical problem when God is conceived as omnipotent,
|       omniscient and perfectly good: such a being knows, can prevent and opposes evil.
|   +-- Logical defences seek consistency; evidential replies address probability;
|       theodicies offer positive reasons. Their adequacy must face moral, natural,
|       horrendous and animal suffering without turning victims into instruments.
|
+-- SCOPE / OWNERSHIP FIREWALL
|   +-- Owns: logical/evidential evil; moral/natural evil; free-will defence; Augustine;
|       Irenaean/Hick soul-making; process theodicy; karma; greater-good, best-world,
|       mystery, eschatology, anti-theodicy; horrendous evils; animal suffering;
|       skeptical theism; evil/profane as religion-forming.
|   +-- Divine attributes belong to Notions of God; proof assessment to Proofs for God.
|
+-- SOURCE / ATTRIBUTION CAUTION
|   +-- Augustine, Plantinga, Hick, Whitehead/process tradition, Rowe, Wykstra, Bergmann,
|       Marilyn McCord Adams and Indian karma traditions.
|   +-- Defence shows possible consistency; theodicy claims actual reason.
|   +-- Historical suffering must never be used decoratively or described with invented facts.
|
+-- I. LOGICAL PROBLEM
|   +-- God is omnipotent.
|   +-- God is omniscient.
|   +-- God is perfectly good.
|   +-- Evil exists.
|   +-- Additional premises:
|       +-- Good always eliminates every evil it can.
|       +-- No limits on logically possible prevention.
|   +-- Alleged contradiction depends on these bridging premises.
|
+-- II. FREE-WILL DEFENCE
|   +-- Significant freedom includes possibility of moral wrong.
|   +-- A world with freely chosen good may be more valuable than programmed innocence.
|   +-- God cannot causally determine libertarian choices and leave them free.
|   +-- Plantinga's aim: show possible scenario, defeating strict contradiction.
|   +-- Strength: addresses moral evil.
|   +-- Limits: natural evil, scale, distribution and why freedom is not better protected.
|
+-- III. EVIDENTIAL PROBLEM
|   +-- Some intense suffering appears gratuitous: no outweighing good or necessary prevention
|       of worse evil is visible.
|   +-- Rowe-style inference:
|       +-- If perfect God existed, apparently pointless suffering would be unlikely.
|       +-- Such suffering appears present.
|       +-- Therefore God's existence is less probable.
|   +-- Not a formal contradiction; comparative probability/judgement.
|
+-- IV. AUGUSTINIAN THEODICY
|   +-- Evil is privatio boni: privation/corruption of good, not independent substance.
|   +-- Creation as such is good.
|   +-- Moral evil arises from created will turning from higher to lower goods.
|   +-- Punitive/natural disorder linked to sin in theological scheme.
|   +-- "All evil is sin or punishment for sin" captures the structure but faces:
|       +-- Innocent/animal suffering.
|       +-- Collective/inherited punishment.
|       +-- Historical/scientific difficulties of a fall account.
|
+-- V. SOUL-MAKING
|   +-- Irenaean/Hick line:
|       +-- Humans created immature and develop through challenge.
|       +-- Epistemic distance permits free moral response.
|       +-- Courage, compassion and forgiveness require adversity.
|   +-- Strength: gives developmental purpose to stable law-governed world.
|   +-- Objection: suffering often destroys rather than builds; unequal burden.
|   +-- Reply: eschatological completion/ultimate healing may be required.
|   +-- Residual: persons must not be mere instruments for others' virtues.
|
+-- VI. HORRENDOUS EVILS
|   +-- Adams: some evils give participants prima facie reason to doubt whether life can be
|       a great good to them.
|   +-- Balancing-off:
|       +-- More good than evil overall is insufficient.
|   +-- Defeat:
|       +-- Evil must be integrated/overcome within sufferer's own life and relation to God.
|   +-- Goodness-to-persons constraint:
|       +-- Theodicy must address victim, not only cosmic aggregate.
|
+-- VII. PROCESS THEODICY
|   +-- God is not omnipotent coercive creator.
|   +-- Reality includes genuine becoming and creaturely causal power.
|   +-- God persuades toward value but cannot unilaterally prevent every evil.
|   +-- God prehends/shares suffering and preserves achieved value.
|   +-- Strength: avoids attributing preventable evil to unlimited control.
|   +-- Objection: finite God may not deserve worship or guarantee final victory.
|   +-- Reply: persuasive love and solidarity may be greater perfection than coercive control.
|
+-- VIII. KARMA
|   +-- Moral actions causally condition future experience across lives.
|   +-- Reduces arbitrary divine distribution and can preserve moral order without creator.
|   +-- Traditions differ:
|       +-- Nyāya/Vedānta may include divine governance.
|       +-- Jainism treats karma as subtle matter.
|       +-- Buddhism has causal continuity without permanent soul.
|   +-- Objections:
|       +-- Beginningless explanation.
|       +-- Memory/evidence.
|       +-- Victim-blaming and social indifference.
|   +-- Reply:
|       +-- Karma is not permission to withhold compassion; present duties remain.
|
+-- IX. NATURAL / ANIMAL EVIL
|   +-- Free will directly explains only agent-caused evil.
|   +-- Natural-law defence:
|       +-- Stable regularities enable action, responsibility and knowledge.
|   +-- Soul-making:
|       +-- Danger permits courage/care.
|   +-- Animal/pre-human suffering:
|       +-- Predates human moral choice and often lacks developmental benefit.
|   +-- This remains one of strongest evidential pressures.
|
+-- X. OTHER RESPONSES
|   +-- Greater-good: evil permitted for outweighing good.
|   +-- Best-possible-world: any creatable optimal order includes limitations.
|   +-- Eschatological: suffering redeemed in final fulfilment.
|   +-- Mystery/apophatic: divine reasons exceed finite grasp.
|   +-- Anti-theodicy: explaining suffering may morally appropriate it; priority is protest,
|       solidarity and resistance.
|
+-- XI. SKEPTICAL THEISM
|   +-- CORNEA:
|       +-- Absence of a discernible reason warrants absence only if one should reasonably
|           expect to discern it.
|   +-- Representativeness caution:
|       +-- Human grasp of possible goods, evils and entailments may be radically limited.
|   +-- Strength: blocks inference from "seems pointless" to "is pointless".
|   +-- Objection: spills into moral scepticism and trust in revelation/providence.
|   +-- Reply: restrict humility to total divine governance, not local duty.
|   +-- Do not combine skeptical suspension and confident detailed theodicy uncritically.
|
+-- XII. EVIL / PROFANE / RELIGION
|   +-- Sacred/profane distinction organises ultimate meaning and boundaries.
|   +-- Evil can intensify:
|       +-- Need for salvation.
|       +-- Moral community.
|       +-- Ritual purification.
|       +-- Theodicy/protest.
|   +-- But religion can also label outsiders profane and legitimate violence.
|   +-- Explanatory role in religion does not establish truth of any doctrine.
|
+-- OBJECTIONS / REPLIES
|   +-- Free will does not explain natural evil -> combine only cautiously with law/soul-making.
|   +-- Soul-making instrumentalises victims -> require proportionality and goodness-to-persons.
|   +-- Process God is not sovereign -> reply revises perfection toward persuasive love.
|   +-- Karma blames victim -> separate causal doctrine from present compassionate duty.
|   +-- Mystery defeats every argument -> reply limits inference but must not erase moral reason.
|
+-- VERIFIED THINKER / TEXT / EXAMPLE BANK
|   +-- Augustine: privation/free will; Plantinga: logical defence; Hick: soul-making;
|       Whitehead/process thought; Rowe: evidential evil; Wykstra/Bergmann: skeptical theism;
|       Adams: horrendous evils.
|   +-- Examples: freely chosen harm, stable natural law, suffering animal, apparently
|       pointless intense suffering. No invented cases or quotations.
|
+-- HIGH-RISK CONCEPTUAL TRAPS
|   +-- Logical evil is not evidential evil.
|   +-- Defence is not theodicy.
|   +-- Privation does not mean suffering unreal.
|   +-- Freedom does not explain every natural evil.
|   +-- Soul-making does not show every victim benefits.
|   +-- Process theodicy revises omnipotence.
|   +-- Karma is not one uniform Indian doctrine.
|   +-- Skeptical theism is not proof of hidden reason.
|   +-- Horrendous evil cannot be answered by aggregate balancing alone.

+-- QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
    +-- The logical contradiction can be resisted if freedom or other goods are possibly
        compatible with permitted evil, but evidential pressure remains because much
        suffering appears excessive, destructive and unrelated to growth. Process and
        karmic models redistribute responsibility by revising divine power or moral
        causation, yet inherit new costs. The most responsible philosophy combines logical
        clarity with epistemic humility and refuses explanations that silence victims.
```
