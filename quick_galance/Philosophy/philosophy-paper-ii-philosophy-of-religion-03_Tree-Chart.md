+-- I. THE FOUR VERSIONS AND THEIR BURDENS
|   +-- LOGICAL (Mackie 1955): the propositions form an inconsistent set.
|       +-- BURDEN: exhibit a POSSIBLE morally sufficient reason. REPLY-TYPE: defence.
|   +-- EVIDENTIAL (Rowe 1979): the amount and kinds of suffering make God improbable.
|       +-- BURDEN: restore PROBABILITY. REPLY-TYPE: theodicy, skeptical theism, revision.
|   +-- EXISTENTIAL (Adams 1999): can THIS life be a great good TO THIS PERSON?
|       +-- BURDEN: DEFEAT the evil within the individual life. REPLY-TYPE: goodness to persons.
|   +-- DISTRIBUTIONAL (karma): why THIS person rather than that one?
|       +-- BURDEN: explain ALLOCATION. REPLY-TYPE: karma; the West has no standard answer.
|   +-- MISMATCH = ERROR. Plantinga answers Mackie; deploying him against Rowe loses marks.
|
+-- II. REPLY TYPOLOGY — THE DISTINCTION THAT SEPARATES BANDS
|   +-- DEFENCE: God COULD have a morally sufficient reason. Success = bare logical possibility.
|       Refuted only by showing the proposed story impossible. Plantinga; skeptical theism.
|   +-- THEODICY: this IS God's actual reason. Success = plausibility and truth. Refuted if the
|       reason is false or insufficient. Augustine; Hick; Leibniz; process theodicy.
|   +-- REVISION: change the God-concept so the charge lapses. Process; finite God; Advaita.
|   +-- DISSOLUTION: remove the defendant. Buddhism; Jainism; Mīmāṃsā; karma without a creator.
|   +-- ETHICS CONTROL: causal explanation is NEVER moral permission to withhold aid.
|       Protest/anti-theodicy diagnoses the ethics of explanation but settles no argument.
|
+-- III. KINDS OF EVIL AND WHAT EACH REPLY CAN REACH
|   +-- MORAL EVIL — cruelty, injustice, betrayal, oppression; source = misused free will.
|       +-- Free-will defence APPLIES | Augustine (sin) APPLIES | soul-making APPLIES.
|   +-- NATURAL EVIL — earthquake, disease, famine, congenital harm; source = law-governed nature.
|       +-- Free-will defence DOES NOT APPLY | Augustine reaches it only via a FALLEN ORDER
|           (historically and scientifically strained) | soul-making APPLIES as environment.
|   +-- HORRENDOUS EVIL — Adams' technical sense; needs DEFEAT within the life.
|   +-- ANIMAL / PRE-HUMAN EVIL — no choice, no growth, no desert; only nomic regularity survives.
|   +-- Three complaints hide inside "too much evil": AMOUNT | KIND | DISTRIBUTION.
|       +-- Distribution is the West's weakest point and karma's structural advantage.
|
+-- IV. THE LOGICAL PROBLEM  [2020 Q6(b), 15]
|   +-- THE SET: God is omnipotent; God is omniscient; God is perfectly good; evil exists.
|   +-- NOT a formal contradiction: nothing in the set plus logic alone entails p and not-p.
|   +-- BRIDGE A (goodness): a perfectly good being eliminates every evil it can.
|   +-- BRIDGE B (power): omnipotence has no relevant limit on preventing evil whose
|       prevention is logically possible.
|   +-- Mackie's taxonomy: ADEQUATE solutions abandon a proposition; FALLACIOUS solutions
|       only appear to keep all three ("good cannot exist without evil"; "evil is a necessary
|       means"; "the universe is better with some evil"; "evil is due to free will").
|   +-- THREE EXITS: deny Bridge A (morally sufficient reason) | deny Bridge B (Plantinga or
|       process) | deny the force of "evil exists" (privation, Advaita) at the cost of
|       taking suffering seriously.
|   +-- VERDICT: widely regarded as ANSWERED; the live debate has MIGRATED to Rowe.
|
+-- V. THE PARADOX OF OMNIPOTENCE  [2021 Q5(e), 10]
|   +-- Can an omnipotent being create a thing it cannot afterwards control?
|       +-- YES -> first-order power (over things) is limited.
|       +-- NO  -> second-order power (over what powers exist) is limited.
|   +-- Unrestricted omnipotence in BOTH senses is incoherent.
|   +-- RESTRICTIONS AND COSTS:
|       +-- Logical possibility only — cheap and near-universal; settles nothing further.
|       +-- Self-binding sovereignty — restraint becomes VOLUNTARY, re-opening intervention.
|       +-- Abandonment (process) — no guarantee of final victory over evil.
|   +-- LINK TO EVIL (mandatory in the stem): the free-will defence REQUIRES a restriction.
|       +-- PLANTINGA: God CHOOSES not to override. GRIFFIN: God CANNOT coerce.
|   +-- Do not reduce this to the heavy-stone puzzle; that is a riddle, not an analysis.
|
+-- VI. PLANTINGA'S FREE-WILL DEFENCE
|   +-- (1) A world with significantly free creatures is more valuable than one with none.
|   +-- (2) Significant freedom entails genuinely available alternatives.
|   +-- (3) God cannot CAUSALLY DETERMINE a creature to FREELY choose rightly — self-cancelling.
|   +-- (4) So God can create free persons only by accepting the possibility of misuse.
|   +-- (5) So moral evil is COMPATIBLE with omnipotence and perfect goodness.
|   +-- Mackie's objection: why not free agents who ALWAYS freely choose good?
|       +-- REPLY — TRANSWORLD DEPRAVITY: POSSIBLY every creaturely essence God could
|           instantiate goes wrong in some circumstance. POSSIBILITY ONLY, never actuality.
|       +-- Attacking it as "empirically unsupported" misses the modal point entirely.
|   +-- PRESUPPOSITION: libertarian freedom; a compatibilist denies premise (3).
|   +-- RESIDUE: natural evil untouched. ANSWER LINE: programmed beneficence lacks moral authorship.
|
+-- VII. ABSOLUTE CREATION AND RESPONSIBILITY  [2022 Q6(c), 15]
|   +-- Scope fix first: the stem is about MORAL evil and human agency.
|   +-- (a) Free-will defence: God creates the CAPACITY, the agent supplies the ACT.
|   +-- (b) Privation: God creates natures, not the defect in them.
|   +-- (c) Foreknowledge is NOT causation: knowing what will freely occur does not cause it.
|   +-- (d) Process: rejects the ANTECEDENT — God lacks the unilateral power assumed.
|   +-- RETAINED REJOINDER: knowingly instantiating the WHOLE SYSTEM, foreseeing its outcomes,
|       remains morally relevant. Concede it; do not suppress it.
|   +-- VERDICT: false as stated, but not empty — it identifies residual responsibility for
|       the system, which the free-will defence prices rather than denies.
|
+-- VIII. AUGUSTINE — PRIVATION, SIN AND PUNISHMENT  [2023 Q8(a), 20]
|   +-- METAPHYSICS FIRST (the directive demands the system before the objection):
|       +-- Being insofar as it is being is GOOD.
|       +-- God creates BEINGS, not evil substances.
|       +-- Corruption is PARASITIC: it depends on a good nature to corrupt.
|       +-- Moral fault is DISORDERED WILLING — turning from a greater good to a lesser.
|       +-- => EVIL = PRIVATIO BONI, a privation of a due good.
|   +-- CANONICAL EXAMPLE: rot is not a rival substance to wood but the loss of its integrity;
|       blindness is a REAL lack in a creature whose nature calls for sight.
|       +-- PRIVATION DOES NOT MAKE PAIN IMAGINARY — the commonest misreading.
|   +-- THE PARTITION: moral evil = SIN; natural evil = PUNISHMENT through a fallen order.
|   +-- FOUR OBJECTIONS:
|       +-- Innocent suffering — infants and the congenitally afflicted fit retribution poorly.
|       +-- Animal and pre-human suffering — no choice, no sin, no growth.
|       +-- Privation is strained for intense POSITIVE pain.
|       +-- INHERITED GUILT vs INHERITED CONDITION: personal guilt for an ancestor's act is
|           the harder claim; an inherited damaged condition is defensible BUT still owes an
|           account of why the condition and its unequal effects are permitted.
|   +-- COMPARATORS: Hick replaces punishment with DEVELOPMENT; karma replaces a single Fall
|       with a BEGINNINGLESS series and answers DISTRIBUTION.
|   +-- VERDICT: preserves divine goodness for moral evil; incomplete for natural, innocent
|       and animal suffering.
|
+-- IX. HICK'S IRENAEAN SOUL-MAKING
|   +-- (1) Ready-made virtue is not ACHIEVED virtue.
|   +-- (2) Courage requires danger; compassion requires another's need.
|   +-- (3) Such virtues require a LAW-GOVERNED world, which must permit harm.
|   +-- (4) Humans are created IMMATURE at an EPISTEMIC DISTANCE — the world is religiously
|       ambiguous so that faith and love are freely reached, not compelled.
|   +-- TYPOLOGY: Augustinian = backward-looking FALL, retributive. Irenaean = forward-looking
|       GROWTH from image into likeness, developmental.
|   +-- ATTRIBUTION CHAIN: Irenaeus = theology | Keats 1819 = phrase | Hick 1966 = theodicy.
|   +-- COVERAGE: moral AND natural evil — its advantage over the free-will defence.
|   +-- COSTS: proportionality (much suffering builds nothing); animals do not morally grow;
|       the reply is a UNIVERSAL ESCHATOLOGICAL FULFILMENT resting on contested commitments.
|   +-- TRAP: "suffering makes us stronger" is NOT the theodicy; the three components are.
|
+-- X. ROWE'S EVIDENTIAL PROBLEM
|   +-- (1) There exist instances of intense suffering an omnipotent, omniscient being could
|       have prevented without losing a greater good or permitting an equally bad or worse evil.
|   +-- (2) A wholly good being would prevent any intense suffering it could, absent such a reason.
|   +-- (3) Therefore no omnipotent, omniscient, wholly good being exists.
|   +-- THE FAWN: trapped in a lightning-started fire, severely burned, suffering unobserved
|       for days. EVIDENTIAL, not deductive.
|       +-- Built to remove free will, moral growth, desert and observer benefit AT ONCE.
|   +-- THE INDUCTIVE STEP: "no justifying reason SEEN" -> "no justifying reason THERE".
|       +-- Not automatically fallacious: such inferences are sound where we WOULD expect
|           to see the thing if it were present.
|   +-- THREE THEIST ROUTES: attack the inference | supply a reason | revise the God-concept.
|       +-- Routes 1 and 2 sit AWKWARDLY together; noticing the tension earns marks.
|   +-- Rowe claims EVIDENTIAL FORCE, not demonstration, and allows the theist may stay rational.
|
+-- XI. SKEPTICAL THEISM AND CORNEA
|   +-- CORNEA (Wykstra 1984), Condition of ReasoNable Epistemic Access: one may claim it
|       APPEARS that p is absent only if it is reasonable to believe that, given one's
|       cognitive situation, p WOULD LIKELY BE DISCERNIBLE if it were there.
|   +-- BERGMANN'S REPRESENTATIVENESS THESES: our sample of possible goods, possible evils and
|       the entailment relations between them is NOT representative of the total space.
|   +-- TARGET: the NOSEEUM inference — "no see 'um, so none there".
|   +-- ANALOGIES: Alston's novice and the chess grandmaster; Wykstra's infant and the procedure.
|   +-- It is a DEFENCE, not a theodicy, and it targets the EVIDENTIAL, not the logical, problem.
|   +-- THREE COSTS:
|       +-- MORAL SCEPTICISM (Almeida, Russell, Wielenberg): threatens the duty to rescue;
|           the "obligation is fixed by what we know" reply is found unstable.
|       +-- PARITY: undercuts confidence in divine promises; restricting it looks ad hoc.
|       +-- BRACKETING: extended, no evidence could ever count against theism.
|   +-- INDIAN PARITY: adṛṣṭa is atīndriya, known only through śabda; karmic ripening is among
|       the Buddhist acinteyya. Genuine parallel — and it inherits the SAME objection.
|   +-- TRAP: "God's ways are mysterious" is NOT skeptical theism. State CORNEA or Bergmann.
|
+-- XII. HORRENDOUS EVILS  (Adams)
|   +-- DEFINITION (technical): an evil whose participation — as PERPETRATOR or VICTIM —
|       gives the participant PRIMA FACIE reason to doubt whether their life could be
|       A GREAT GOOD TO THEM ON THE WHOLE.
|   +-- ARGUMENT: standard theodicies balance GLOBALLY; a global balance can be struck while
|       a particular life remains, for that person, a net disaster; a God good TO PERSONS must
|       DEFEAT the evil WITHIN that life; only religion-specific resources — divine
|       identification, beatific intimacy as an INCOMMENSURABLE good — can do so.
|   +-- BALANCING OFF = the good outweighs the evil in a sum; evil stays EXTERNAL.
|   +-- DEFEAT (Chisholm's term) = the evil becomes an ORGANIC PART of a whole whose value it
|       contributes to. COMPENSATION IS NOT DEFEAT.
|   +-- OBJECTIONS/REPLIES: eschatological and seemingly instrumentalising -> Adams DENIES
|       instrumentality (the evil need not be REQUIRED, only DEFEATED); religion-specific
|       resources abandon neutral ground -> the atheological argument targets a PARTICULAR theism.
|   +-- INDIAN PARITY: karma answers DISTRIBUTION, not personal worth; Advaita's sublation and
|       Buddhist liberation dissolve the evaluating standpoint — evasive or therapeutic.
|       +-- CONTRAST: DEFEAT WITHIN the life vs EXIT FROM the life-series.
|
+-- XIII. ANIMAL AND PRE-HUMAN SUFFERING
|   +-- Pain predates moral agency -> not misused human freedom -> the animal does not morally
|       grow -> it cannot be retributive -> standard theodicies leave a MAJOR REMAINDER.
|   +-- FIVE REPLIES, EACH PRICED:
|       +-- Neo-Cartesian (canvassed by Murray 2008) — contested premise about awareness.
|       +-- Nomic regularity — explains the KIND of world, not the AMOUNT of pain.
|       +-- Soul-making extended — instrumentalises animals for human virtue.
|       +-- Eschatological animal fulfilment — unverifiable and undeveloped.
|       +-- Fallen order — historically and scientifically strained.
|   +-- INDIAN PARITY: karma in animal births (tiryak-yoni); Jain graded jīva grounding ahiṃsā;
|       Buddhist duḥkha and karuṇā extended to sentient beings. Cost: unverifiable; never
|       infer that a suffering creature deserved it.
|   +-- OBJECTION: an animal cannot LEARN from a fruit it cannot understand.
|       +-- REPLY: karmic maturation is CAUSAL, not PEDAGOGICAL; the compassion it demands stands.
|   +-- Do NOT answer this with the free-will defence — saying so explicitly earns marks.
|
+-- XIV. PROCESS THEODICY  [2025 Q6(b), 15]
|   +-- SIX TENETS (the directive is an ENUMERATION — number them):
|       +-- (1) CREATIVITY, not God, is ultimate; actual entities are self-creative occasions.
|       +-- (2) God is DIPOLAR: PRIMORDIAL nature supplies each occasion its INITIAL AIM;
|           CONSEQUENT nature receives and preserves what the world becomes.
|       +-- (3) POWER IS INTRINSICALLY DISTRIBUTED: to be actual is to have self-determination.
|       +-- (4) Divine power is PERSUASIVE BY METAPHYSICAL NECESSITY (Griffin), not by
|           self-limitation — this separates process theodicy from the free-will defence.
|       +-- (5) EVIL = finite occasions DEVIATING from the divine lure; God is not culpable.
|       +-- (6) God is "the FELLOW-SUFFERER WHO UNDERSTANDS" (Whitehead).
|   +-- NAME THE LAYER: Whitehead (dipolarity, initial aim, consequent nature, fellow-sufferer) |
|       Hartshorne (classical omnipotence INCOHERENT; surrelativism/panentheism) |
|       Griffin (metaphysical necessity) | Cobb (systematisation).
|   +-- It is a THEODICY, not a defence: it claims God's ACTUAL situation and is refutable.
|   +-- CRITICISM: a God who cannot guarantee victory may be unworthy of worship; the
|       metaphysics is contested. REPLY: solidarity and the OBJECTIVE IMMORTALITY of achieved
|       value in the consequent nature — the objection does not disappear.
|   +-- VERDICT: it purchases GOD'S INNOCENCE at the price of GOD'S SOVEREIGNTY.
|
+-- XV. THE REPLY-SPACE AROUND THE CLASSICAL THEODICIES
|   +-- GREATER-GOOD / STABLE ORDER: risk and regular law are needed for freedom and virtue.
|       +-- COST: explains the KIND of world, not the AMOUNT or DISTRIBUTION.
|   +-- LEIBNIZIAN BEST WORLD (Théodicée 1710): the best total COMPOSSIBLE order.
|       +-- COST: there may be no unique best world; it can neglect goodness to persons.
|   +-- ESCHATOLOGICAL FULFILMENT: no finite suffering is final.
|       +-- COST: compensation is not automatically justification for permitting it.
|   +-- DIVINE MYSTERY: finite minds may fail to grasp providential reasons.
|       +-- COST: unfalsifiable without an access principle; skeptical theism is the
|           disciplined evidential version.
|   +-- PROTEST / ANTI-THEODICY: refusing to justify protects truthfulness and resistance.
|       +-- COST: settles neither the logical nor the evidential argument.
|
+-- XVI. THE INDIAN RESPONSE
|   +-- KARMA-SAṂSĀRA: suffering is the fruit of one's own past deeds across lives; the moral
|       law is SELF-OPERATING; so evil raises no problem for a creator's goodness.
|   +-- KARMA IS NOT ONE DOCTRINE:
|       +-- NON-THEISTIC — Buddhism, Jainism, Mīmāṃsā: no divine distributor; NO DEFENDANT.
|       +-- THEISTIC — Nyāya, Rāmānuja: God allots karmic fruit justly as KARMĀDHYAKṢA,
|           a JUDGE and not an arbitrary cause of evil.
|       +-- ADVAITA: evil is real at the EMPIRICAL level (vyāvahārika) and sublated at the
|           ULTIMATE level (pāramārthika). COST: risk of minimising present suffering.
|   +-- BEGINNINGLESSNESS: no first wrongful act need be identified. It blocks ONE regress
|       question and VERIFIES NOTHING about any claimed past-life cause.
|   +-- ORIGINATION VOCABULARY: avidyā | āsrava | taṇhā. The West asks about PERMISSION;
|       India about the ORIGINATION OF BONDAGE.
|   +-- MORAL CONTROLS (non-negotiable):
|       +-- The inference from "this person suffers" to "this person deserves it for a
|           specific past act" is UNAVAILABLE to ordinary observers -> VICTIM-BLAMING.
|       +-- Natural suffering, collective harms and unequal starting conditions still press
|           the fairness and verification of the mechanism.
|       +-- Karma does NOT cancel present duties of compassion, protection and relief.
|
+-- XVII. INDIA–WEST PARITY BENCH
|   +-- Why evil at all?  West: free will, privation, soul-making | India: avidyā, āsrava, taṇhā
|   +-- Why THIS person?      West: no standard answer | India: karma across lives.
|   +-- Why so MUCH?      West: nomic regularity, skeptical theism | India: beginningless saṃsāra
|   +-- Is God culpable?      West: theodicies, process | India: Nyāya allots per karma; others
|                             have no defendant at all.
|   +-- What defeats evil?    West: Adams' beatific intimacy, eschatology | India: mokṣa/nirvāṇa/
|                             kaivalya ending the standpoint of suffering.
|   +-- Animal suffering      West: nomic regularity, neo-Cartesian | India: tiryak-yoni, graded
|                             jīva, ahiṃsā, karuṇā.
|   +-- DIVERGENCE: India can DROP THE DEFENDANT; the West cannot.

+-- XVIII. EVIL AND THE PROFANE AS RELIGION-FORMING  [2019 Q6(b), 15]
|   +-- FUNCTIONAL, not theological: what do evil and the profane DO?
|   +-- DURKHEIM, The Elementary Forms of Religious Life (1912) — SOCIAL side: all known
|       religious beliefs presuppose a classification of things into PROFANE and SACRED;
|       religion = beliefs and practices relative to sacred things, uniting a moral community,
|       a CHURCH. The definition is deliberately GOD-FREE.
|   +-- OTTO, The Idea of the Holy (1917) — OBJECT side: the NUMINOUS, mysterium tremendum
|       et fascinans.
|   +-- ELIADE, The Sacred and the Profane (1957): HIEROPHANY; sacred SPACE around an
|       AXIS MUNDI; recoverable sacred TIME.
|   +-- FOUNDING SEQUENCE: PREDICAMENT -> BOUNDARY -> INSTITUTION -> SOTERIOLOGY.
|   +-- FOUR CATEGORIES, NEVER MERGED: evil (harm) | profane (the ORDINARY) | impure (ritually
|       polluting) | demonic (actively anti-sacred). DURKHEIM'S PROFANE IS NOT EVIL.
|   +-- INDIAN PARITY: Buddhism's FIRST NOBLE TRUTH is the clearest world case; Jain āsrava/
|       bandha with saṃvara/nirjarā; Advaita's avidyā; śuddhi/aśuddhi and śauca; maṅgala/
|       amaṅgala; pāpa/puṇya; Mīmāṃsā ritual purity.
|       +-- RIDER: India's founding defect is more often IGNORANCE or BONDAGE than MORAL EVIL —
|           diagnosis-and-insight rather than sin-and-forgiveness.
|   +-- OBJECTIONS: the binary is a Durkheimian imposition and Eliade's archaic ontology is
|       unfalsifiable -> treat it as an IDEAL TYPE; religion also grows from wonder and gratitude.
|   +-- VERDICT: they found religion STRUCTURALLY rather than DOCTRINALLY, and they found
|       RELIGION, NOT THEISM.
|
+-- XIX. CONCEPT-DEPENDENCE  [2024 Q6(c), 15]
|   +-- CLASSICAL THEISM: full force; all burdens live; the theodicies must do the work.
|   +-- FINITE / PROCESS GOD: dissolved at the logical level. LOSS: sovereignty and any
|       guarantee of final victory over evil.
|   +-- IMPERSONAL ABSOLUTE (Advaita, nirguṇa): relocated to the empirical level. LOSS: a
|       personal God to address; risk of minimising suffering.
|   +-- NO CREATOR (Buddhism, Jainism, Mīmāṃsā): removed; no defendant. LOSS: providence,
|       divine rescue, a cosmic guarantor of justice.
|   +-- CONCEPT-DEPENDENCE IS NOT A SOLUTION: it relocates the cost from the ARGUMENT into
|       the THEOLOGY. The theodicies exist to KEEP ALL THE ATTRIBUTES.
|
+-- XX. ASSESSMENT GRID AND STANDING CRITICISMS
|   +-- Free-will: explains moral; preserves omnipotence + goodness; natural evil left over.
|   +-- Augustinian privation: moral via the Fall; preserves all attributes; privation strained,
|       innocence and animals unresolved.
|   +-- Soul-making: moral + natural; preserves purposive goodness; scale, animals, eschatology.
|   +-- Process: all; preserves goodness NOT omnipotence; sacrifices classical sovereignty.
|   +-- Karma: all including distribution; cosmic justice with no creator-blame; victim-blaming,
|       verification, regress.
|   +-- Advaita: empirical-level evil; Brahman untouched; risk of minimising suffering.
|   +-- Skeptical theism: explains nothing (a defence); preserves all attributes; moral
|       scepticism and unfalsifiability.
|   +-- FOUR CRITICISMS, EACH WITH REPLY AND REJOINDER:
|       +-- Excess evil -> epistemic limits and long-range goods -> unknown reasons are no licence.
|       +-- Natural evil -> stable law enables agency -> stability does not explain extremes.
|       +-- Divine responsibility -> foreknowledge does not cause -> choosing the system counts.
|       +-- Karma and compassion -> compassion remains obligatory -> the misuse is real and common.
