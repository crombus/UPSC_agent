+-- I. TWO PICTURES OF THE RELATION - THE AXIS IS EXPLANATORY PRIORITY
|   +-- MECHANISTIC / ARTIFICIAL: persons are logically prior; the state is an INSTRUMENT;
|   |   freedom = a protected sphere; rights AGAINST the state are intelligible.
|   |   Names: Hobbes, Locke, contractarian liberalism. Risk: ATOMISM.
|   +-- ORGANIC / ETHICAL: the whole is partly CONSTITUTIVE of the person's good; freedom =
|   |   participation in rational institutions; rights mature WITHIN an order.
|   |   Names: Aristotle, Hegel. Risk: STATISM.
|   +-- CONTROL: rival LOGICAL metaphors about priority - NOT literal biology.
|   +-- The other four relation-models: WELFARE (removes hindrances; risk paternalism),
|   |   MARXIST (class state universalises a partial interest; risk civil liberty),
|   |   ANARCHIST (no proven right to rule; risk coordination), COMMUNITARIAN (embedded
|   |   self; risk conformity and silenced minorities).
|   +-- SYNTHESIS: constitutional democracy is MECHANISTIC in insisting institutions serve
|       persons, ORGANIC in recognising that citizenship is socially formed.
|
+-- II. RIGHTS - WHAT THEY ARE AND WHAT GROUNDS THEM
|   +-- DEFINITION: a JUSTIFIED claim, liberty, power or immunity recognised within a moral
|   |   or legal order - a protected normative position, not a preference.
|   +-- FOUR DEMANDS of every claim: HOLDER, CONTENT, BEARER, GROUND.
|   +-- THREE OPENING DISTINCTIONS: need is not yet a right; liberty is not a claim-right;
|   |   moral right is not legal right.
|   +-- SIX GROUNDS
|   |   +-- NATURE (Locke): rights held in the state of nature - life, liberty, property;
|   |   |   government is a FIDUCIARY TRUST; violation justifies RESISTANCE.
|   |   |   Locke is NOT Hobbes: trust vs irreversible transfer; resistance vs none.
|   |   +-- LAW (Bentham): natural rights are "nonsense upon stilts"; a right exists where
|   |   |   law supplies CLAIMANT + DUTY-BEARER + REMEDY. He rejects NATURAL rights only.
|   |   +-- MORALITY: rights justified by ethical reasoning BEFORE enactment - which is why
|   |   |   anti-slavery and anti-colonial claims were intelligible. Legal VALIDITY and
|   |   |   moral LEGITIMACY are not identical.
|   |   +-- WILL: rights are normative CONTROL over another's duty; fits autonomy; breaks
|   |   |   on infants and the severely disabled.
|   |   +-- INTEREST: rights protect IMPORTANT INTERESTS; fits welfare and protective
|   |   |   rights; breaks on lawful waiver by a competent adult.
|   |   +-- DIGNITY: rights belong to all human beings AS HUMAN (human-rights doctrine).
|   +-- HOHFELD'S FOUR INCIDENTS - THE PRECISION INSTRUMENT
|   |   +-- CLAIM-RIGHT <-> DUTY (opposite: no-right)
|   |   +-- LIBERTY / PRIVILEGE <-> NO-RIGHT (opposite: duty)
|   |   +-- POWER <-> LIABILITY (opposite: disability)
|   |   +-- IMMUNITY <-> DISABILITY (opposite: liability)
|   |   +-- Ordinary rights BUNDLE incidents: free speech = liberty + contextual claim-right
|   |   |   against censorship + immunity, but NO general claim-right to a platform.
|   |   +-- LIMIT: an ANALYTIC scheme, not a moral justification - it fixes form, not content.
|   +-- HUMAN RIGHTS: UNIVERSALITY vs CULTURAL RELATIVISM [2018 Q4(a)]
|   |   +-- Universalist: rights belong to all humans; ground is dignity, equal worth,
|   |   |   protection from cruelty, arbitrariness and degradation.
|   |   +-- Relativist: conceptions of personhood differ; the claim-rights vocabulary is
|   |   |   historically Western in FORMULATION; cultures may prioritise role and duty.
|   |   +-- BALANCED LINE: universality of ASPIRATION, plurality of ARTICULATION; core
|   |   |   protections universal, institutional expression variable; rights readable
|   |   |   RELATIONALLY.
|   |   +-- NEVER CONCEDE: that the critique of cruelty and arbitrary power is merely local.
|   |   +-- RESIDUAL: the BOUNDARY of the minimum core, and who fixes it, stay disputed.
|   +-- INDIAN TRADITION AND RIGHTS [2020 Q2(c)]
|   |   +-- "Antagonistic" fails: the tradition neither defends cruelty nor denies worth.
|   |   +-- "Identical" fails: it asserts no claim-rights against political authority.
|   |   +-- ANSWER: a DIFFERENT IDIOM - dharma: role-based obligation, reciprocity,
|   |   |   self-restraint, truth, order; the person EMBEDDED in relations.
|   |   +-- GANDHI'S BRIDGE: rights grow out of WELL-PERFORMED DUTIES - a thesis about the
|   |   |   MORAL SOURCE of rights, not their denial. Duty-first is not hierarchy-first.
|   |   +-- CONCESSION: the idiom can sacralise hierarchy and leave protest without
|   |       standing - AMBEDKAR's capture objection. Verdict: ANTECEDENT, not antagonistic;
|   |       synthesise duty-language with constitutional rights and moral equality.
|   +-- UNCONDITIONAL RIGHTS AND ANARCHY [2022 Q4(b)]
|       +-- Attack both words: "unconditional" = NO limit whatever; "necessarily" = a MODAL
|       |   claim about conceptual necessity.
|       +-- COLLISION: unlimited claims cannot be jointly satisfied, and any resolving
|       |   procedure would itself BE a condition.
|       +-- FOUR BOUNDARY PRINCIPLES: others' equal rights; harm constraints; public order;
|       |   institutional adjudication.
|       +-- LIMITATION IS NOT NULLIFICATION - the abuse to fear is ARBITRARY restriction.
|       +-- OBJECTION: admitted limits enable steady EROSION. REPLY: close on
|       |   ACCOUNTABILITY - each restriction justified, proportionate, reviewable.
|       +-- VERDICT: SELF-DEFEATING rather than necessarily anarchy-producing.
|
+-- III. DUTIES
|   +-- DEFINITION: an obligation to DO, AVOID, RESPECT or SUPPORT, owed to others,
|   |   institutions, community or oneself.
|   +-- KINDS: legal, moral, civic, political, professional, spiritual. Do NOT reduce duty
|   |   to legal duty.
|   +-- LEGAL DUTY: law and procedure; courts, penalties, sanction.
|   |   MORAL DUTY: conscience and ethical reason; praise, blame, self-judgment.
|   +-- CONTROLLING ARGUMENT: law cannot codify the whole of ethical life, so democratic
|   |   citizenship rests on TOLERANCE, HONESTY, RESTRAINT and RESPECT FOR DISAGREEMENT -
|   |   habits law cannot manufacture. Some duties SHOULD remain moral.
|   +-- GANDHI: duty EDUCATES the moral self; SWARAJ = ethical self-rule; neither hard
|   |   positivist nor atomistic liberal nor centralising statist.
|   +-- ARTICLE 51A: inserted 1976 (Forty-second Amendment); NON-JUSTICIABLE; read
|   |   philosophically as balancing rights-bearing with responsibility-bearing
|   |   citizenship. Non-justiciability settles NO moral thesis.
|   +-- STANDING DANGER: duty invoked BY AUTHORITY TO SILENCE DISSENT. Test is DIRECTIONAL -
|       does the duty-talk bind the POWERFUL too?
|
+-- IV. CORRELATIVITY AND PRIORITY - THE TWO STEMS THAT DECIDE THIS CLAUSE
|   +-- 2025 Q4(c) IS MODAL: "no NECESSARY connection".
|   |   +-- CONCEDE THE PREMISE: obligation and entitlement ARE analytically distinguishable.
|   |   +-- REFUSE THE INFERENCE: distinguishable is not SEPARABLE.
|   |   +-- HOHFELDIAN SPLIT: STRICT correlativity for CLAIM-RIGHTS; ABSENT for LIBERTIES.
|   |   |   So the connection is CONCEPTUAL for one class and INSTITUTIONAL for the rest.
|   |   +-- CONSEQUENCE: entitlements without obligations are EMPTY; obligations without
|   |       claims risk AUTHORITARIANISM.
|   +-- 2023 Q2(b) IS COMPARATIVE: duty and accountability prior to rights?
|       +-- NAME THE CRITERION FIRST. ETHICAL priority = what motivates conduct;
|       |   POLITICAL priority = what protects persons against abuse.
|       +-- GRID: rights-first (shields minorities / risks atomised entitlement);
|       |   duty-first (stresses responsibility / risks paternalism); balanced (best fit /
|       |   requires vigilance).
|       +-- ACCOUNTABILITY is the term that stops duty-priority becoming SUBORDINATION.
|       +-- VERDICT: duty has ETHICAL priority, rights retain POLITICAL priority - so
|           EMPHASIS, not PRECEDENCE.
|
+-- V. ACCOUNTABILITY
|   +-- DEFINITION: those exercising public power must EXPLAIN, JUSTIFY and BEAR
|   |   CONSEQUENCES. The bridge from AUTHORITY to LEGITIMACY.
|   +-- THREE DIMENSIONS (all three, or none)
|   |   +-- ANSWERABILITY: information + public justification. Isolated -> RITUAL.
|   |   +-- ENFORCEABILITY: review, remedy, sanction, removal. Isolated -> ARBITRARINESS.
|   |   +-- RESPONSIVENESS: change conduct, repair harm, learn. Isolated -> correction
|   |       without prospective rules.
|   +-- THREE ARENAS
|   |   +-- POLITICAL: to the people - and NOT reducible to periodic voting; includes
|   |   |   opposition, deliberation, publicity, criticism.
|   |   +-- LEGAL: to law and review. A legally wrongful act does NOT become right because
|   |   |   it is ELECTORALLY SUPPORTED.
|   |   +-- MORAL: to conscience, civil society, history. LEGALITY DOES NOT EXHAUST
|   |       LEGITIMACY.
|   +-- TWO NEGATIONS: accountability is NOT efficiency (a state may be efficient yet
|   |   unaccountable), and it does NOT contradict finality - it governs its exercise.
|   +-- DWORKIN, 1977 - WHY ADJUDICATION IS ACCOUNTABILITY
|   |   +-- RULES apply ALL-OR-NOTHING; PRINCIPLES have WEIGHT and survive being outweighed.
|   |   +-- Rules rest on PEDIGREE; principles on the argument that best JUSTIFIES the
|   |   |   practice. Judges APPLY rules; they INTERPRET and WEIGH principles.
|   |   +-- Illustration: no one may profit from his own wrong - the murderer loses the
|   |   |   inheritance a valid will conferred, WITHOUT the rule being invalid.
|   |   +-- UPSHOT: RIGHTS ARE TRUMPS against collective goals.
|   |   +-- RESIDUAL: the RIGHT-ANSWER THESIS is hard to sustain under permanent reasonable
|   |       disagreement (Hartian bounded discretion).
|   +-- DO RIGHTS MAKE CITIZENS ACCOUNTABLE TO THE STATE? [2019 Q2(a)]
|       +-- AUTHORITARIAN READING: rights come WITH obedience - grantor is also judge.
|       +-- DEMOCRATIC READING: rights EMBED both parties in MUTUAL answerability.
|       +-- VERDICT: RECIPROCAL, NOT ONE-DIRECTIONAL. Citizens accountable FOR law-abiding,
|           participation and respect for others' rights; the state accountable FOR
|           freedom, equality and dignity. RELATIONAL, not FEUDAL. Reciprocity is not
|           symmetry.
|
+-- VI. THE RELATION-MODELS IN DETAIL
|   +-- LIBERAL: the individual is MORALLY PRIOR; the state INSTRUMENTALLY justified.
|   |   +-- NEGATIVE freedom = a protected sphere; POSITIVE freedom = effective CAPACITY,
|   |   |   which justifies education, health or a social minimum WITHOUT directing life.
|   |   +-- MILL'S HARM PRINCIPLE: coerce a competent adult to prevent HARM TO OTHERS, not
|   |   |   because officials think the choice unwise.
|   |   +-- PATERNALISM: SOFT checks whether the choice is informed and voluntary; HARD
|   |   |   overrides a competent voluntary choice.
|   |   +-- NEUTRALITY vs PERFECTIONISM: fair terms without one comprehensive ideal, against
|   |   |   public cultivation of genuine goods. Controls: promotion stays RIGHTS-BOUND and
|   |   |   CONTESTABLE; privacy CANNOT immunise harm or domination; dissenters keep EQUAL
|   |   |   CIVIC STANDING.
|   |   +-- POROSITY OBJECTION: the self/other harm boundary leaks. REPLY: harm is a
|   |       BURDEN-OF-PROOF test, not a claim of social isolation.
|   +-- IDEALIST: the state as an ETHICAL INSTITUTION through which freedom is realised.
|   |   +-- HEGEL: the RATIONAL state within ETHICAL LIFE (family, civil society, state);
|   |   |   freedom becomes CONCRETE. NOT the sanctification of every existing government.
|   |   +-- GREEN: the state is a MEANS to the common good; it REMOVES REMEDIABLE
|   |   |   HINDRANCES and leaves moral agency with citizens; obligation is CONDITIONAL.
|   |   +-- OBJECTION: once the state defines the higher self, coercion becomes liberation.
|   |       REPLY: keep enabling action rights-bound, decentralised, publicly challengeable.
|   +-- WELFARE: formal rights are HOLLOW where poverty, illness, ignorance or dependency
|   |   makes choice unusable. LIMIT: paternalism and dependency.
|   +-- COMMUNITARIAN: the self is EMBEDDED; common goods are not derivable from isolated
|   |   choice. LIMIT: tradition conceals hierarchy; community silences internal minorities.
|   |   REJOINDER: neutrality itself privileges an AUTONOMOUS CHOOSER.
|   +-- ANARCHIST: targets the LEGITIMACY OF COERCION AS SUCH; forces theory to JUSTIFY
|   |   coercion rather than assume it. Philosophical anarchism need NOT endorse chaos.
|   |   UNANSWERED OBJECTION: large-scale rights security, adjudication and defence.
|   +-- MARXIST: targets the CLASS FORM of the state and the FORMALITY of its rights -
|       formally equal, materially unequal. It does NOT deny all rights; it criticises
|       BOURGEOIS-RIGHTS frameworks. UNANSWERED OBJECTION: civil liberty in transition.
|
+-- VII. SOCIAL CONTRACT [2024 Q1(b); 2021 Q4(c)]
|   +-- HOBBES: insecurity and fear -> peace by an IRREVERSIBLE TRANSFER to a strong
|   |   sovereign; broad surrender for self-preservation.
|   +-- LOCKE adds TRUST, LIMITS and a RIGHT OF RESISTANCE; natural rights RETAINED.
|   +-- ROUSSEAU relocates sovereignty in the GENERAL WILL; obedience becomes
|   |   SELF-LEGISLATION; civil freedom as moral autonomy.
|   +-- COMMON AIM: obligation from the STANDPOINT OF INDIVIDUALS, not divine right or
|   |   conquest. "Development" means what each successor CHANGES.
|   +-- FICTION OBJECTION: no actual contract occurred. REPLY: the value is NORMATIVE -
|   |   what terms could free and equal persons reasonably accept?
|   +-- ADEQUACY FOR HUMAN RIGHTS: benchmark = universality BEYOND citizenship and consent.
|       Struggles with persons OUTSIDE the contract, FUTURE GENERATIONS, and those LACKING
|       BARGAINING POWER. VERDICT: POWERFUL BUT INCOMPLETE - contract explains POLITICAL
|       LEGITIMACY; human rights also need a theory of DIGNITY or PERSONHOOD.
|
+-- VIII. PLATO AGAINST ARISTOTLE [2024 Q3(a)]
|   +-- TEST OF A MIDDLE COURSE: does an INTERMEDIATE SPHERE survive between person and
|   |   polis?
|   +-- PLATO: justice as FUNCTIONAL HARMONY under philosopher-rule; the guardian class
|   |   gives up PRIVATE PROPERTY and PRIVATE FAMILY to prevent faction -> the private
|   |   domain is SUBORDINATED. Nearer the STATIST pole.
|   +-- ARISTOTLE: the polis is NATURAL and exists for the good life, but he criticises
|   |   EXCESSIVE UNITY - a polis is a PLURALITY, not one household or one person.
|   |   Household and private property REMAIN; citizenship is ACTIVITY within a
|   |   DIFFERENTIATED CONSTITUTION.
|   +-- COMPARATIVE JUDGMENT: Aristotle preserves plurality and the intermediate sphere,
|   |   and RELATES rather than ABSORBS person and polis.
|   +-- QUALIFICATION: BOTH are PERFECTIONISTS - neither treats the state as neutral among
|   |   conceptions of the good - and Aristotle's SLAVERY, GENDER EXCLUSION and RESTRICTED
|   |   CITIZENSHIP block modern endorsement.
|   +-- VERDICT LINE: "Aristotle's success is COMPARATIVE, not LIBERAL."
|
+-- IX. POLITICAL OBLIGATION AND THE DISOBEDIENCE LADDER
|   +-- DEFINITION: a MORAL duty to obey the law of one's OWN state BECAUSE IT IS THE LAW.
|   |   NOT fear (being obliged vs having an obligation), NOT agreement with content, NOT
|   |   legal validity.
|   +-- FOUR TESTS: GENERALITY - PARTICULARITY - CONTENT-INDEPENDENCE - DEFEASIBILITY.
|   +-- SIX FAMILIES AND THEIR FAILURES
|   |   +-- CONSENT (Locke, Hobbes, Rousseau): express consent almost never given; tacit
|   |   |   consent from residence is not voluntary.
|   |   +-- FAIR PLAY (Hart, Rawls): public order is NON-EXCLUDABLE, so benefits are
|   |   |   RECEIVED not ACCEPTED; the repaired version binds only those who least need it.
|   |   +-- NATURAL DUTY OF JUSTICE (Rawls): fails PARTICULARITY.
|   |   +-- ASSOCIATIVE (Dworkin, communitarians): mere membership, however unjust.
|   |   +-- GRATITUDE: some reciprocity, not general obedience; an oppressor can claim it.
|   |   +-- ANTI-OBLIGATION (Wolff, SIMMONS): cannot distinguish a legitimate state from a
|   |       private agency.
|   +-- RAZ'S SERVICE CONCEPTION: authority is legitimate when its directives help subjects
|   |   CONFORM BETTER to reasons that ALREADY APPLY; directives act as PRE-EMPTIVE reasons
|   |   that REPLACE private calculation. LIMIT: instrumental, therefore BOUNDED.
|   +-- THE FOUR RUNGS - NEVER MERGE THEM
|   |   +-- CONSCIENTIOUS OBJECTION: avoid complicity; may be private; seeks exemption;
|   |   |   accepts penalty; own act; ACCEPTS the system.
|   |   +-- CIVIL DISOBEDIENCE: persuade the majority; ESSENTIALLY PUBLIC; breach WITH
|   |   |   ACCEPTANCE OF PENALTY; a specific law; ACCEPTS the system and appeals to its own
|   |   |   principles.
|   |   +-- RESISTANCE: obstruct a grave injustice; may be covert; may refuse the penalty;
|   |   |   particular acts; DENIES their legitimacy.
|   |   +-- REVOLUTION: replace the order; organised; rejects the legal order wholesale.
|   +-- TWO DEFINING FEATURES OF CIVIL DISOBEDIENCE: PUBLICITY with appeal to shared
|   |   principles, and ACCEPTANCE OF THE LEGAL PENALTY. Non-violence is the standard third
|   |   condition and is CONSTITUTIVE on the Gandhian account.
|   +-- THREE DATED ANCHORS: Thoreau 1849 (individual refusal); Gandhi - salt law broken
|   |   6 April 1930 (public, non-violent, penalty-accepting); Ambedkar 25 November 1949
|   |   ("grammar of anarchy").
|   +-- GANDHI vs AMBEDKAR: they disagree about the AVAILABILITY CONDITION - whether
|   |   constitutional channels are genuinely open TO THE GROUP IN QUESTION. Ambedkar's
|   |   deeper worry: agitation in a caste-stratified society can be CAPTURED by dominant
|   |   groups.
|   +-- INDIA: Articles 19(1)(a) and 19(1)(b) protect PROTEST subject to reasonable
|       restrictions under 19(2) and 19(3) - NOT law-breaking. Article 51A, 1976,
|       NON-JUSTICIABLE. Legal permission is not moral justification; legal prohibition is
|       not moral refutation. NEVER claim Indian law "recognises a right to civil
|       disobedience".
|
+-- X. EXAMINER TRAPS - FINAL CHECK
|   +-- Bentham rejected ALL rights. NO - natural rights only.
|   +-- Correlativity holds for every right. NO - strict for claim-rights, absent for
|   |   liberties.
|   +-- Gandhi is anti-rights. NO - he reorders their MORAL SOURCE.
|   +-- Accountability = elections. NO - three dimensions and three arenas.
|   +-- Indian tradition is "antagonistic". NO - a duty-centred idiom, filtered.
|   +-- Limitation = destruction. NO - principled limits are the condition of coexistence.
|   +-- Hobbes = Locke = Rousseau. NO - transfer vs trust vs general will.
|   +-- Organic theory as literal biology. NO - a logical metaphor about priority.
|   +-- Hegel defends every actual state. NO - RATIONAL ethical institutions.
|   +-- Positive freedom = paternalism. NO - enabling provision stays rights-bound.
|   +-- Aristotle is a modern individualist. NO - a COMPARATIVE middle only.
|   +-- Simmons and Raz swapped. NO - sceptic vs service conception.
|   +-- Article 51A used as moral proof, or mis-dated. NO - 1976, non-justiciable, settles
|       nothing moral.
|

|   +-- 2018 Q4(a) [20] dignity as a COMMON HUMAN ASPIRATION -> aspiration vs articulation;

|   +-- 2022 Q4(b) [15] do UNCONDITIONAL rights NECESSARILY lead to anarchy -> define both
|   |   words; collision; four boundary principles; close on accountability.
|   +-- 2023 Q2(b) [15] DUTY and ACCOUNTABILITY prior to rights -> criterion first; the

    +-- QUALIFIED CONCLUSION: a mature polity does not CHOOSE between rights and duties; it
        CONSTITUTIONALISES rights, CULTIVATES duties and INSTITUTIONALISES accountability -
        because the individual is neither an ATOM OUTSIDE SOCIETY nor an EXPENDABLE PART OF
        THE STATE, and the task of political philosophy is to specify a justified relation
        between DIGNITY and AUTHORITY.
