POLITICAL IDEOLOGIES: ANARCHISM, MARXISM AND SOCIALISM
|
+-- CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
|   +-- Anarchism treats coercive hierarchy, especially the state, as presumptively
|       illegitimate and seeks voluntary self-organisation.
|   +-- Marxism explains political domination through material production, class
|       exploitation and alienation and seeks revolutionary supersession of capitalism.
|   +-- Socialism is the wider family seeking social control of productive power,
|       substantive equality and cooperative freedom through revolutionary, democratic,
|       market, planning or Gandhian paths.
|

|   |   Lenin, Gramsci, Althusser, Miliband/Poulantzas; varieties of socialism;

|   +-- Sovereignty owns Bodin/Austin/Laski/Kautilya.
|   +-- Individual and State owns political obligation and rights.
|   +-- Social Ideals owns detailed equality/liberty/justice.

|
+-- SOURCE / ATTRIBUTION CAUTION
|   +-- Proudhon: What Is Property?; Bakunin: Statism and Anarchy; Kropotkin: Mutual Aid

|   +-- Marx: Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts of 1844, German Ideology, Capital,
|       Critique of the Gotha Programme; Marx/Engels: Communist Manifesto.
|   +-- "Dialectical materialism" is more explicitly systematised in Engels/later Marxism;

|   +-- Gandhi's relation to anarchism/socialism is qualified; he did not simply join a
|       European ideological school.
|   +-- Soviet, Chinese or contemporary policy outcomes cannot be equated automatically
|       with all Marxism/socialism.
|
+-- I. WHAT MAKES AN IDEOLOGY?
|   +-- Diagnosis of existing order.
|   +-- Account of human agency/social structure.
|   +-- Normative vision of good society.
|   +-- Theory of power/state/property.
|   +-- Strategy of transition.
|   +-- Institutional model after transition.
|   +-- Comparison axes:
|       +-- Freedom.
|       +-- Equality.
|       +-- Property.
|       +-- State.
|       +-- Violence/change.
|       +-- Individual/community.
|
+-- II. ANARCHISM
|   +-- An-archē:
|       +-- Against rule/domination, not necessarily absence of order.
|   +-- Central thesis:
|       +-- Coercive political authority lacks adequate justification.
|   +-- Distinguish:
|       +-- Philosophical anarchism: no general duty to obey state.
|       +-- Political anarchism: seeks stateless social order.
|       +-- Anomie/chaos: absence of norms, not anarchist ideal.
|
+-- III. ANARCHIST ARGUMENT AGAINST STATE
|   +-- Premise 1:
|       +-- Persons are morally autonomous.
|   +-- Premise 2:
|       +-- State claims monopoly of coercion and general right to command.
|   +-- Premise 3:
|       +-- Coercion requires specific justification; office does not create moral truth.
|   +-- Premise 4:
|       +-- States reproduce hierarchy, war, bureaucracy and property domination.
|   +-- Conclusion:
|       +-- Replace compulsory hierarchy with voluntary, decentralised cooperation.
|   +-- Strong form:
|       +-- All states always illegitimate.
|   +-- Modest form:
|       +-- State authority bears continuing justificatory burden.
|
+-- IV. ANARCHIST STRANDS
|   +-- Individualist anarchism:
|       +-- Maximal individual sovereignty and voluntary exchange/association.
|       +-- Stirner and some American traditions differ sharply from socialist anarchism.
|   +-- Mutualism:
|       +-- Proudhon; possession, reciprocity and federated exchange.
|   +-- Collectivist anarchism:
|       +-- Bakunin; collective ownership with distribution linked to labour in common summaries.
|   +-- Anarchist communism:
|       +-- Kropotkin; common ownership and distribution by need.
|   +-- Anarcho-syndicalism:
|       +-- Worker unions/direct action as organs of transformation and administration.
|   +-- Pacifist/Gandhian affinity:
|       +-- Nonviolent decentralised self-rule, but Gandhi's spiritual/duty framework is distinct.
|
+-- V. NATURAL ORDER / MUTUAL AID
|   +-- Society precedes/exceeds state.
|   +-- Cooperation can arise through:
|       +-- Reciprocity.
|       +-- Custom.
|       +-- Federation.
|       +-- Mutual aid.
|       +-- Voluntary communes/workplaces.
|   +-- Kropotkin:
|       +-- Cooperation is a significant evolutionary/social factor.
|   +-- Institutions need not be absent:
|       +-- Anarchists reject coercive hierarchy, not coordination as such.
|
+-- VI. ANARCHIST OBJECTIONS / REPLIES
|   +-- Disorder/security:
|       +-- Reply: decentralised federations and mutual defence can coordinate.
|   +-- Free-riding/public goods:
|       +-- Reply: reciprocity, local accountability and cooperative norms.
|   +-- Private domination:
|       +-- Reply: social anarchism abolishes concentrated property, not only state.
|   +-- Scale/complexity:
|       +-- Reply: federations and delegated, recallable functions.
|   +-- Coercive minorities:
|       +-- Reply: community defence without permanent monopolistic state.
|   +-- Residual:
|       +-- Stable adjudication and protection of dissent remain difficult.
|
+-- VII. GANDHI AS QUALIFIED POLITICAL ANARCHIST
|   +-- Swaraj:
|       +-- Self-rule through self-discipline.
|   +-- State:
|       +-- Concentrated violence and soulless machinery in critical formulations.
|   +-- Ideal:
|       +-- Decentralised self-governing communities.
|   +-- Method:
|       +-- Ahiṃsā, satyāgraha, non-cooperation and constructive programme.
|   +-- Difference from classical anarchists:
|       +-- Spiritual moral law.
|       +-- Trusteeship.
|       +-- Gradual ethical transformation.
|       +-- Practical acceptance of minimal state under imperfect conditions.
|   +-- Therefore "qualified" or "philosophical" anarchist, not doctrinal identity.
|
+-- VIII. ANARCHISM AND PANDEMIC / CRISIS
|   +-- Crisis can produce:
|       +-- State expansion, emergency command and surveillance.
|       +-- Mutual aid and local self-organisation.
|       +-- Breakdown where legitimacy/capacity fail.
|   +-- Disorder is not anarchism.
|   +-- Voluntary solidarity may display anarchist principle.
|   +-- Durable crisis governance still raises coordination/equality questions.
|   +-- Specific pandemic facts are historical/date-bound and must not replace conceptual analysis.
|
+-- IX. MARXISM: CORE DOCTRINE
|   +-- Material production structures social relations.
|   +-- Class conflict drives historical change.
|   +-- Capitalism generalises commodity production and wage labour.
|   +-- Labour's social power confronts workers as private capital.
|   +-- Emancipation requires transforming ownership and production, not only legal equality.
|
+-- X. DIALECTICAL MATERIALISM
|   +-- Reality/process understood through contradiction, relation and transformation.
|   +-- Material conditions are primary over independent ideal forces.
|   +-- Quantity/quality, opposition and development formulations belong especially to
|       Engels/later Marxist systematisation.
|   +-- Use cautiously:
|       +-- Marx's historical method is not a mechanical universal three-step formula.
|   +-- Human practice mediates structure and change.
|
+-- XI. HISTORICAL MATERIALISM
|   +-- Productive forces:
|       +-- Labour power, tools, technology and knowledge.
|   +-- Relations of production:
|       +-- Property/control/class relations.
|   +-- Mode of production:
|       +-- Structured unity of forces and relations.
|   +-- Base:
|       +-- Material-economic structure.
|   +-- Superstructure:
|       +-- Legal, political and ideological forms.
|   +-- Contradiction:
|       +-- Developing forces conflict with existing relations.
|   +-- Class struggle transforms social formation.
|   +-- Not crude one-way determinism:
|       +-- Politics, law and ideas have relative effect within material limits.
|
+-- XII. ALIENATION
|   +-- Under capitalist wage labour worker is alienated from:
|       +-- Product: belongs to capital.
|       +-- Activity: labour externally compelled.
|       +-- Species-being: creative social capacity reduced to survival.
|       +-- Other persons: competition and class antagonism.
|   +-- Also self-alienation:
|       +-- Human power appears as alien power of capital/market.
|   +-- Alienation is structural, not only dissatisfaction.
|   +-- Automation may reduce drudgery or deepen loss of control depending on ownership.
|
+-- XIII. EXPLOITATION / SURPLUS VALUE
|   +-- Labour-power purchased as commodity.
|   +-- Worker creates value beyond value of wages required to reproduce labour-power.
|   +-- Surplus value appropriated by capitalist.
|   +-- Absolute surplus value:
|       +-- Extend/intensify work.
|   +-- Relative surplus value:
|       +-- Productivity lowers necessary labour-time.
|   +-- Exploitation is class relation of production, not merely low wage or immoral employer.
|   +-- Criticisms:
|       +-- Labour theory/value transformation controversies.
|       +-- Entrepreneurship/capital/risk contributions.
|   +-- Marxist reply:
|       +-- Core concern is control of surplus/social labour, not moral blame alone.
|
+-- XIV. IDEOLOGY
|   +-- Ideas can represent sectional interests as universal.
|   +-- Commodity fetishism:
|       +-- Social relations between people appear as relations between things.
|   +-- Legal equality can obscure unequal productive dependence.
|   +-- Ideology is not simply deliberate lying.
|   +-- It is socially generated misrecognition tied to material practices.
|
+-- XV. MARXIST STATE
|   +-- State maintains conditions of dominant class order in classical formulation.
|   +-- Relative autonomy:
|       +-- State need not obey each capitalist directly.
|       +-- It may regulate particular capitals to preserve system.
|   +-- Political emancipation:
|       +-- Equal citizenship valuable but incomplete.
|   +-- Human emancipation:
|       +-- Ends structural domination in civil society/production.
|
+-- XVI. REVOLUTION AND COMMUNISM
|   +-- Capitalism socialises production but privately appropriates product.
|   +-- Proletarian organisation contests class power.
|   +-- Transitional socialist phase:
|       +-- Social control of productive resources.
|       +-- Suppression of old class domination.
|   +-- Higher communism:
|       +-- Classes disappear.
|       +-- State as coercive class instrument withers.
|       +-- Distribution according to need.
|       +-- Free development of each compatible with all.
|   +-- Historical regimes must not be treated as automatic fulfilment of concept.
|
+-- XVII. MARXISM AND FREEDOM
|   +-- Formal freedom:
|       +-- Contract/property/legal equality.
|   +-- Material unfreedom:
|       +-- Need compels sale of labour-power.
|   +-- Marxian freedom:
|       +-- Collective control of social conditions.
|       +-- Reduction of necessary labour.
|       +-- Development of diverse capacities.
|   +-- Objection:
|       +-- Collective ownership/state transition can suppress individual liberty.
|   +-- Reply:
|       +-- Capitalist private power already restricts substantive freedom.
|   +-- Residual:
|       +-- Institutional safeguards against party/state concentration are indispensable.
|
+-- XVIII. LENIN
|   +-- Imperialism:
|       +-- Monopoly/finance capital and global rivalry reshape capitalism.
|   +-- Weakest link:
|       +-- Revolution may occur outside most industrially advanced state.
|   +-- Vanguard party:
|       +-- Organises revolutionary consciousness/action.
|   +-- Criticism:
|       +-- Vanguard substitutes party for class and fosters authoritarianism.
|   +-- Reply:
|       +-- Repression and fragmented consciousness require disciplined organisation.
|
+-- XIX. GRAMSCI
|   +-- Hegemony:
|       +-- Ruling class leads through consent as well as coercion.
|   +-- Civil society:
|       +-- Institutions where common sense and consent are organised.
|   +-- War of manoeuvre:
|       +-- Direct assault.
|   +-- War of position:
|       +-- Long struggle over institutions, culture and ideas.
|   +-- Organic intellectuals:
|       +-- Articulate social group's experience/project.
|   +-- Corrects reduction of power to state coercion/economic ownership alone.
|
+-- XX. ALTHUSSER
|   +-- Repressive State Apparatus:
|       +-- Government, police, military, courts; primarily coercion.
|   +-- Ideological State Apparatuses:
|       +-- School, family, media, religion and related institutions; primarily ideology.
|   +-- Interpellation:
|       +-- Ideology constitutes individuals as subjects.
|   +-- Structural causality / relative autonomy.
|   +-- Humanist criticism:
|       +-- Agency and lived alienation disappear.
|   +-- Reply:
|       +-- Structural account explains reproduction beyond conscious intention.
|
+-- XXI. MILIBAND / POULANTZAS
|   +-- Miliband:
|       +-- Instrumentalist emphasis on elite recruitment, networks and class ties.
|   +-- Poulantzas:
|       +-- State has relative autonomy and structurally secures capitalist relations.
|   +-- Debate:
|       +-- Who controls state versus how state form reproduces system.
|   +-- Synthesis:
|       +-- Personnel, institutions and structural dependence can jointly matter.
|
+-- XXII. MARXISM IN INDIA / PRESENT CONTEXT
|   +-- Concept-led uses:
|       +-- Informal labour and precarity.
|       +-- Agrarian class relations.
|       +-- Commodity power.
|       +-- Concentration/platform control.
|       +-- Welfare and social reproduction.
|   +-- Class-reduction criticism:
|       +-- Caste, gender, race and ecology have partially autonomous structures.
|   +-- Reply:
|       +-- Extend material analysis through alliance with Ambedkarite, feminist and
|           ecological perspectives rather than claim explanatory monopoly.
|   +-- Current empirical examples require fresh verification.
|
+-- XXIII. SOCIALISM
|   +-- Broad commitment:
|       +-- Social control of productive resources.
|       +-- Equality/substantive freedom.
|       +-- Cooperation over domination.
|       +-- Production oriented to social need.
|   +-- Not one doctrine.
|   +-- May retain:
|       +-- State.
|       +-- Markets.
|       +-- Mixed ownership.
|       +-- Parliamentary democracy.
|   +-- Or seek revolutionary/common ownership.
|
+-- XXIV. VARIETIES
|   +-- Utopian socialism:
|       +-- Owen, Fourier, Saint-Simon; model communities/plans.
|   +-- Scientific socialism:
|       +-- Marx/Engels claim grounding in material contradictions/class struggle.
|   +-- Democratic socialism:
|       +-- Democratic institutions plus social ownership/economic democracy.
|   +-- Social democracy:
|       +-- Regulated capitalism, welfare, redistribution and labour rights.
|   +-- Market socialism:
|       +-- Social ownership combined with market coordination.
|   +-- Guild/syndical socialism:
|       +-- Worker/functional self-government.
|   +-- Gandhian socialism:
|       +-- Decentralisation, trusteeship, nonviolence and need-limited economy.
|
+-- XXV. SOCIALISM / COMMUNISM / SOCIAL DEMOCRACY
|   +-- Socialism:
|       +-- Umbrella family and/or transitional social ownership.
|   +-- Communism:
|       +-- Classless, stateless higher association with common control.
|   +-- Social democracy:
|       +-- Parliamentary reform, welfare and regulated markets, often without abolishing
|           private ownership generally.
|   +-- Do not use terms interchangeably.
|
+-- XXVI. GANDHIAN SOCIALISM
|   +-- Sarvodaya:
|       +-- Welfare/uplift of all.
|   +-- Antyodaya:
|       +-- Priority to last person.
|   +-- Trusteeship:
|       +-- Wealth-holders morally hold surplus for society.
|   +-- Bread labour.
|   +-- Aparigraha:
|       +-- Non-possession/limited wants.
|   +-- Decentralised village-oriented economy.
|   +-- Nonviolent transformation and class cooperation.
|   +-- Marxist criticism:
|       +-- Trusteeship moralises exploitation and leaves ownership power intact.
|   +-- Gandhian reply:
|       +-- Violent centralisation reproduces domination; means shape ends.
|
+-- XXVII. SOCIALIST OBJECTIONS / REPLIES
|   +-- Incentive/innovation:
|       +-- Reply: democratic workplaces, social motivation and limited differentials.
|   +-- Calculation/knowledge:
|       +-- Reply: plural planning, markets and decentralised information can combine.
|   +-- Bureaucracy:
|       +-- Reply: social ownership must be democratic, not state ownership alone.
|   +-- Coercion:
|       +-- Reply: capitalist property also coercively structures options.
|   +-- Uniformity:
|       +-- Reply: equality of power/conditions can expand diverse life-plans.
|   +-- Historical authoritarianism:
|       +-- Reply: critique must shape institutional safeguards, not be dismissed as
|           accidental to every socialist project.
|
+-- XXVIII. ANARCHISM VERSUS MARXISM
|   +-- Shared:
|       +-- Critique of capitalist property, class domination and coercive state.
|   +-- State transition:
|       +-- Marxism permits proletarian transitional state.
|       +-- Anarchism fears transition will reproduce domination.
|   +-- Organisation:
|       +-- Party/class strategy versus decentralised federation/direct action.
|   +-- Historical sequence:
|       +-- Marxism expects withering after class abolition.
|       +-- Anarchism demands dismantling hierarchical state through transition itself.
|
+-- XXIX. MARX VERSUS DEMOCRATIC SOCIALISM
|   +-- Marxian revolutionary line:
|       +-- Capitalist state/property structure cannot simply be administered toward socialism.
|   +-- Democratic socialist line:
|       +-- Elections, unions and reforms can transform ownership and power.
|   +-- Marxist objection:
|       +-- Capital constrains reform and can reverse gains.
|   +-- Democratic reply:
|       +-- Revolution risks authoritarian concentration; institutions preserve plural freedom.
|
+-- XXX. MARX VERSUS GANDHI
|   +-- Shared:
|       +-- Critique exploitation, possessiveness and dehumanising industrial order.
|   +-- Marx:
|       +-- Class conflict, structural ownership, revolution.
|   +-- Gandhi:
|       +-- Nonviolence, self-restraint, trusteeship, decentralisation.
|   +-- Marxist criticism:
|       +-- Moral conversion insufficient against class power.
|   +-- Gandhian criticism:
|       +-- Violent means and centralised industrialism corrupt emancipatory ends.
|
+-- XXXI. M. N. ROY'S RADICAL-HUMANIST CRITIQUE
|   +-- Rejects deterministic/authoritarian Marxism.
|   +-- Centres rational, free individual.
|   +-- Party dictatorship cannot emancipate.
|   +-- Decentralised democracy and ethical humanism.
|   +-- Criticism:
|       +-- May understate structural class power.
|   +-- Contribution:
|       +-- Freedom must be protected during, not postponed until after, transformation.
|
+-- XXXII. FUTURE / RELEVANCE
|   +-- Marxism remains diagnostically relevant to:
|       +-- Concentration.
|       +-- Labour insecurity.
|       +-- Commodification.
|       +-- Ideology and platform power.
|   +-- Needs revision regarding:
|       +-- Caste/gender/race/ecology.
|       +-- Democratic safeguards.
|       +-- Service/digital economy.
|   +-- Anarchism remains relevant as:
|       +-- Critique of unaccountable authority.
|       +-- Mutual aid/decentralisation.
|   +-- Socialism remains relevant where:
|       +-- Formal liberty lacks material capacity.
|       +-- Public goods and economic democracy are central.
