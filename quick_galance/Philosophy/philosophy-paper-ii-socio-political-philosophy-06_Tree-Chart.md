+-- I. HUMANISM
|   +-- Human beings possess dignity and capacities for reason, freedom, creativity and
|       ethical responsibility.
|   +-- Human flourishing is a central evaluative end.
|   +-- Authority must answer to human well-being rather than inherited dogma alone.
|   +-- Humanism need not mean atheism, individualism or anthropocentric mastery.
|
+-- II. FORMS OF HUMANISM
|   +-- Renaissance: recovery of classical learning, human agency and worldly achievement.
|   +-- Enlightenment: autonomy, reason, criticism, universal rights and scientific inquiry.
|   +-- Secular/naturalistic: ethics grounded without supernatural revelation.
|   +-- Religious: divine/spiritual orientation expressed through human dignity and service.
|   +-- Marxist: emancipation from alienation and exploitative relations.
|   +-- Existential: self-making, freedom and responsibility without fixed essence.
|   +-- Radical Humanism: Roy's reason, freedom, ethical individual and decentralised democracy.
|   +-- Integral/universal: Tagore's reconciliation of person, nature and universal humanity.
|
+-- III. ENLIGHTENMENT ARGUMENT
|   +-- Critique inherited authority -> public reason -> autonomy.
|   +-- Scientific inquiry expands confidence in human understanding.
|   +-- Natural-right and equal-citizenship languages displace status privilege.
|   +-- Religious toleration separates civic standing from orthodoxy.
|   +-- Limits: colonialism, instrumental reason and exclusion show Enlightenment ideals
|       were incompletely realised, not automatically worthless.
|
+-- IV. TAGORE
|   +-- Human personality fulfils itself through relation, creativity and universal spirit.
|   +-- Reconciles:
|       +-- Individual and universal.
|       +-- Freedom and harmony.
|       +-- Nature and culture.
|       +-- East and West without civilisational imitation.
|       +-- Spirituality and reason.
|   +-- Critiques aggressive nationalism when machine-like state power eclipses humanity.
|   +-- Difference is fulfilled within relation, not erased into uniformity.
|
+-- V. GANDHI / VIVEKANANDA / ROY
|   +-- Gandhi:
|       +-- Truth, ahiṃsā, dignity, service, self-rule and interreligious respect.
|       +-- Humanism is spiritual and duty-centred.
|   +-- Vivekananda:
|       +-- Divine potential of each person; service as practical spirituality.
|   +-- M. N. Roy:
|       +-- Rejects supernaturalism and authoritarian communism.
|       +-- Rational ethics, freedom, scientific temper and decentralised democracy.
|   +-- Roy versus Gandhi:
|       +-- Secular reason/autonomous individual versus spiritual truth/self-restraint.
|       +-- Both oppose domination and value decentralised moral agency.
|
+-- VI. CAN HUMANISM SUBSTITUTE FOR RELIGION?
|   +-- Yes:
|       +-- Dignity, empathy, reason and shared vulnerability can ground ethics.
|       +-- Public morality need not depend on disputed revelation.
|   +-- No:
|       +-- Religion supplies transcendence, community, ritual, hope and motivational depth.
|   +-- Qualified answer:
|       +-- Humanism can ground civic ethics but may coexist with non-dominating religion.
|       +-- It should replace coercive dogma, not necessarily all spiritual meaning.
|
+-- VII. HUMANISM OBJECTIONS / REPLIES
|   +-- Anthropocentrism:
|       +-- Reply: ecological/posthuman correction can extend dignity beyond mastery.
|   +-- Abstract universal "man":
|       +-- Reply: embodied, gendered, cultural and unequal locations must shape humanism.
|   +-- Rationalism neglects affect/tradition:
|       +-- Reply: dialogic humanism includes sympathy, art and community.
|   +-- Secular humanism lacks motivation:
|       +-- Reply: solidarity and reciprocal dignity can motivate without divine sanction.
|
+-- VIII. SECULARISM
|   +-- Political principle governing religion-state relation under freedom and equality.
|   +-- Protects:
|       +-- Freedom of religion.
|       +-- Freedom from religious coercion.
|       +-- Equal citizenship across belief/non-belief.
|       +-- State legitimacy independent of one faith's authority.
|   +-- Not rejection of religion.
|   +-- Not necessarily strict institutional separation in every context.
|
+-- IX. MODELS
|   +-- Strict separation:
|       +-- Institutional wall; neither establishment nor religious control.
|   +-- Neutrality:
|       +-- State neither favours nor disadvantages religion.
|   +-- Equal respect:
|       +-- Comparable concern across communities.
|   +-- Principled distance:
|       +-- Context-sensitive engagement or disengagement to advance liberty/equality.
|   +-- Establishment with toleration:
|       +-- Official religion but protected minorities; weaker equality.
|   +-- Laïcité:
|       +-- Strong public institutional separation; applications differ historically.
|
+-- X. INDIAN SECULARISM
|   +-- Conditions:
|       +-- Deep religious diversity.
|       +-- Community institutions/personal practices.
|       +-- Need for reform against internal oppression.
|   +-- Principled distance:
|       +-- State may support, regulate or abstain depending on equal freedom.
|       +-- Not equidistance mechanically.
|   +-- Constitutional orientation:
|       +-- Conscience, equality, non-discrimination and reform.
|   +-- Tensions:
|       +-- Minority protection versus gender equality.
|       +-- Accommodation versus favouritism.
|       +-- Reform versus paternalism.
|   +-- Legal status/examples must be checked when used.
|
+-- XI. GANDHIAN SECULARISM
|   +-- Sarva-dharma-sambhāva:
|       +-- Equal regard/interfaith respect in common paraphrase.
|   +-- Religion as ethical truth cannot be expelled from public life.
|   +-- State must not establish coercive denominational rule.
|   +-- Politics requires morality, but no community may dominate.
|   +-- Criticism:
|       +-- Religious public vocabulary may disadvantage non-believers/minorities.
|   +-- Reply:
|       +-- Gandhi's truth is plural, fallible and disciplined by nonviolence.
|
+-- XII. SECULARISM / PLURALISM
|   +-- Religious pluralism:
|       +-- Social fact/normative acceptance of multiple faiths.
|   +-- Secularism:
|       +-- Political rules securing equal citizenship among them and non-believers.
|   +-- Related but not identical:
|       +-- Homogeneous society can adopt secular state.
|       +-- Plural society can remain non-secular/hierarchical.
|   +-- Acceptance of all religions is insufficient if dissent and nonreligion lack equality.
|
+-- XIII. SECULARISM OBJECTIONS / REPLIES
|   +-- Western import:
|       +-- Reply: institutional form varies; equal conscience addresses Indian problems too.
|   +-- Anti-religious:
|       +-- Reply: it regulates coercive power, not personal faith.
|   +-- Minority appeasement:
|       +-- Reply: accommodation is justified only by equal citizenship and non-domination.

+-- XIV. MULTICULTURALISM
|   +-- Descriptive:
|       +-- Presence of cultural, linguistic, religious and ethnic diversity.
|   +-- Normative:
|       +-- Principles/institutions recognising diversity fairly.
|   +-- Beyond toleration:
|       +-- Public identity and institutions may need reform, not mere private permission.
|   +-- Beyond relativism:
|       +-- Cultures can be respected while practices remain criticisable.

|   +-- Cross-cutting identities and internal diversity.
|   +-- Migration/global flows.
|   +-- Need for both common citizenship and differentiated accommodation.

+-- XVIII. PAREKH

|   +-- Human capacities are culturally mediated.
|   +-- Intercultural dialogue tests practices through shared public reasoning.
|   +-- Common political culture should be negotiated, not majority culture universalised.
|   +-- Neither liberal uniformity nor cultural enclosure is adequate.
|
+-- XIX. TOLERATION / COEXISTENCE / RECOGNITION
|   +-- Toleration:
|       +-- Refrain from suppressing what one disapproves.
|   +-- Coexistence:
|       +-- Peaceful shared space and institutions.
|   +-- Recognition:
|       +-- Equal public standing and identity-respect.
|   +-- Progression is not automatic:
|       +-- Toleration may preserve hierarchy.
|       +-- Recognition may over-fix identities.
|   +-- Ethical society needs dialogue, rights and reciprocity.
|
+-- XX. MULTICULTURALISM / GLOBALISATION
|   +-- Globalisation increases migration, communication and hybrid identities.
|   +-- It can:
|       +-- Enrich cultural exchange.
|       +-- Standardise consumer culture.
|       +-- Intensify identity reaction.
|       +-- Create diasporic/transnational belonging.
|   +-- Multicultural policy mediates change through language, representation and rights.
|   +-- Avoid assuming culture is static before globalisation.
