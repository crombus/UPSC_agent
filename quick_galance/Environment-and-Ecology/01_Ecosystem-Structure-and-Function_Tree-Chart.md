                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
An ecosystem is a functional unit in which organisms and the physical
environment exchange energy and matter. Structure asks which components,
species, trophic levels and spatial layers are present; function asks how
primary production, consumption, decomposition, nutrient cycling and recovery
operate. Energy flows one way and dissipates as heat, while matter cycles.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Ecosystem protection is effective when policy preserves the relationships that
generate production, decomposition, regulation and resilience rather than
counting isolated species or canopy alone. Scientific uncertainty does not
remove ecological limits; it requires monitoring, precaution and adaptive
management across landscape and watershed scales.
