                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Biosphere Reserves and Ramsar Sites are distinct international-recognition and
planning systems. A Biosphere Reserve uses zonation to reconcile conservation,
sustainable development and scientific support across a large landscape. Ramsar
uses wise use, site designation, ecological-character maintenance and cooperation
for wetlands. Neither label automatically creates a new Indian statutory land
category or proves effective management; domestic law, institutions, plans,
community participation and ecological monitoring determine outcomes.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
International designation can focus attention, reporting and cooperation, but
wetland and landscape resilience emerges only when zonation or wise use is tied
to domestic legal protection, functioning State Wetland Authorities, funded
management plans, catchment-scale restoration and community legitimacy. The
correct success metric is maintained ecological character and livelihood-compatible
resilience, not the number of labels on a map.
