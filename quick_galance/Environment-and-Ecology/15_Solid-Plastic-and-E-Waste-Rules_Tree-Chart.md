                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Waste governance begins by identifying the material stream, legal vintage,
responsible actor and stage in the lifecycle. Municipal solid waste, plastic waste
and e-waste use separate rule frameworks under the Environment Protection Act.
Extended Producer Responsibility shifts end-of-life responsibility upstream, but
registrations, targets and certificates become environmental outcomes only when
physical collection, authorised processing, residue control and material recovery
are independently verified and informal workers are safely integrated.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
India's waste rules increasingly place lifecycle responsibility on producers,
but circularity cannot be established through portal entries or certificates
alone. A credible system must prevent waste, preserve segregation, audit material
throughput and residues, integrate informal collection livelihoods with safe
authorised processing, and remediate legacy contamination separately. The final
test is verified material and pollution reduction, not nominal compliance.
