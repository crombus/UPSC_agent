                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Four forest vocabularies must remain separate: ecological forest type, legal
forest category, recorded forest area, and remotely sensed forest or tree cover.
The Forest Rights Act, 2006 is a rights-recognition statute correcting historical
injustice; it recognises differentiated individual, community, resource-management
and habitat rights through a Gram Sabha-centred process. Rights recognition can
support conservation, but neither a title nor canopy gain proves ecological success.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
India's forests cannot be understood through one map or one number. Ecological
type, legal status, recorded area, canopy cover and community rights answer
different questions. A durable forest regime must protect biome-specific
ecological processes while completing fair FRA recognition, strengthening CFR
institutions and measuring whether secure tenure and management actually improve
forest condition, biodiversity and livelihoods.
