                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
CAMPA and the Green India Mission are related but distinct. CAMPA is a statutory,
diversion-triggered finance-and-planning system funded by user-agency payments;
GIM is a wider NAPCC mission for forest quality, ecosystem services, adaptation,
mitigation and livelihoods. Neither money accrued, money spent, seedlings planted,
canopy detected nor green credits proves replacement of a mature natural forest.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Compensatory finance is necessary when lawful diversion is unavoidable, but it
cannot make ecologically unequal landscapes interchangeable. Forest governance
must prioritise avoidance, apply the current statute-and-judgment framework,
protect tenure, spend transparently and align CAMPA with GIM's native-biome,
ecosystem-service and livelihood objectives. Success begins only where financial
accounting ends and independently monitored ecological recovery begins.
