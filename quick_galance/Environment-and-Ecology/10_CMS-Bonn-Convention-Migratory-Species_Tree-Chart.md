                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Migratory-species conservation is a complete-range and weakest-link problem.
A species may breed, feed, rest, pass, winter or move beyond national jurisdiction
across several legal systems; failure at one critical route segment can negate
protection elsewhere. CMS supplies Appendix duties and a graduated cooperation
toolkit, but each listing, Agreement, MOU, Action Plan or Concerted Action has a
different legal status and none proves implementation or species recovery.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
CMS is necessary because migratory species connect jurisdictions, habitats and
threats that no single national statute can govern alone. Its effectiveness,
however, depends on using the correct instrument for the correct problem and on
implementing habitat, obstacle, taking and monitoring measures at every critical
route segment. India can convert flyway leadership into conservation only when
international coordination is matched by durable local institutions and verified
population and habitat outcomes.
