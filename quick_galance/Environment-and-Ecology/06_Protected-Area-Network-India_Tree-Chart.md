                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
India's protected-area network is a graded legal and spatial system, not one
uniform fortress category. National Parks, Wildlife Sanctuaries, Conservation
Reserves and Community Reserves create different rights-and-use settlements
under wildlife law; Tiger Reserves, Elephant Reserves, Eco-Sensitive Zones,
corridors and OECMs add distinct overlays or surrounding conservation functions.
Ecological success depends on habitat quality, connectivity, rights-respecting
governance, enforcement and adaptive management, not notification count alone.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
India needs both secure core habitats and permeable, rights-respecting landscapes.
The legal category must match the ecological task: strict protection where essential,
community or conservation reserves where tenure and connectivity require partnership,
species overlays where focused management helps, and site-specific ESZs and corridors
beyond the boundary. Expansion is credible only when dated notification is followed by
rights settlement, funded management and measurable ecological connectivity.
