                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The IPCC is an intergovernmental scientific-assessment body: it evaluates
published evidence, uncertainty, risks and response options but neither conducts
primary research nor negotiates climate treaties or national targets. Its authority
comes from expert assessment, transparent review, calibrated uncertainty and
government consideration of summaries. Every finding must retain its report,
working group, scale, scenario, calibrated language, literature cut-off and status.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The IPCC's value lies not in issuing policy commands but in creating a traceable,
reviewed and calibrated common evidence base across competing national interests.
Its findings gain authority through scientific synthesis and intergovernmental
scrutiny, yet remain time-, scale- and scenario-bound. Sound use therefore cites
the exact report and uncertainty language, while leaving negotiation and domestic
choice to the institutions that legally own them.
