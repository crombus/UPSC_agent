                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
CITES regulates international trade in listed specimens of wild fauna and flora.
It does not itself prohibit domestic hunting, protect habitat or assess extinction
risk. Its operative logic is appendix classification plus specimen, source, purpose
and transaction checks, scientific findings, permits or certificates and border
verification. Listing matters only when the complete science-to-document-to-
enforcement chain works and domestic conservation addresses non-trade threats.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
CITES is strongest when treated precisely as a science-gated international-trade
regime. Its Appendices classify the control pathway, but conservation depends on
correct specimen identification, credible non-detriment and legal-acquisition
findings, authentic documents, capable border enforcement and complementary
domestic habitat and anti-poaching action. Appendix listing is therefore a legal
gateway, not proof that trade is lawful, trafficking has ended or a species is secure.
