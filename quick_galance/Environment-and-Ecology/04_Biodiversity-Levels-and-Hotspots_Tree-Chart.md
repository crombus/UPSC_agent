                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Biodiversity exists at genetic, species and ecosystem levels, each sustaining
different dimensions of adaptability and ecological function. A biodiversity
hotspot is a scientific prioritisation category requiring both exceptional
vascular-plant endemism and severe historical habitat loss. It is neither a
synonym for species richness nor a domestic legal protection category.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Hotspots help direct scarce conservation resources toward irreplaceable and
highly threatened regions, but they cannot substitute for nationwide protection
of genetic resources, habitats and ecological processes. India needs stronger
local biodiversity institutions, credible benefit sharing and landscape
connectivity, with global targets translated into ecologically effective and
socially legitimate action.
