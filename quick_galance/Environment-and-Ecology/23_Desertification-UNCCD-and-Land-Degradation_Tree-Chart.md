                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Land degradation is the broad loss of land productivity and ecosystem function;
desertification is that process within drylands; drought is a temporary water-
deficit hazard. UNCCD and Land Degradation Neutrality organise cooperation,
national action and avoid-reduce-reverse accounting, but neutrality is credible
only with a baseline, scale, indicators, land-user rights and proof of durable
soil, water, vegetation, biodiversity and livelihood recovery.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Combating desertification requires protecting healthy drylands before offsetting
loss, managing soil and water together, and recognising pastoral and common-land
institutions. LDN is useful only when neutrality is more than arithmetic: gains
must be durable, ecologically appropriate and socially legitimate. India should
measure recovery through long-term soil, water, vegetation and livelihood evidence,
not hectares treated or trees planted alone.
