                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Air-quality governance must connect source, pollutant, atmospheric transformation,
ambient concentration, human exposure and health or ecological effect without
collapsing them. NAAQS, source-emission standards, AQI, NCAP, GRAP and CAQM serve
different legal, scientific and temporal functions. Effective policy requires
representative monitoring, place-and-season source apportionment, enforceable
source control and separate proof of target attainment and health outcomes.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Clean-air governance succeeds when standards, monitoring and source-specific
abatement operate as one evidence chain. AQI can warn, GRAP can reduce acute
exposure, NCAP can organise structural action and CAQM can coordinate a region,
but none substitutes for credible emissions control, representative data and
measured attainment. The correct policy unit is the pollutant-source-place-season
combination, not a single national blame narrative.
