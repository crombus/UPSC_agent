                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The natural greenhouse effect is a life-enabling energy-balance process; the
current climate problem is its anthropogenic enhancement through increased
greenhouse-gas concentrations and other forcing agents. Scientific reasoning must
separate emissions from concentrations and stocks, forcing from feedback, weather
from climate, detection from attribution, and observations from conditional
projections. Gas, time horizon, scale and confidence govern every valid claim.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Climate science is strongest when causal claims preserve the full evidence chain:
human activity changes emissions, atmospheric stocks alter radiative forcing,
feedbacks shape the response, and exposure and vulnerability shape impacts.
Policy must cut long-lived carbon dioxide and short-lived warming agents while
adapting to unavoidable change. Every numerical or event claim must retain its
assessment, baseline, scale, time horizon and confidence.
