                 QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Jainism and Buddhism transformed the religious and social
history of ancient India by joining ethical discipline and
liberation doctrines to durable ascetic-lay institutions.
They emerged in a favourable urban, political and intellectual
ecology but were not merely economic protests. Jainism's jiva,
radical ahimsa and rigorous vows differed fundamentally from
Buddhism's non-self, Middle Path and conditioned causation.
Their councils, schools, languages, donors and material networks
changed over centuries: Jainism built resilient regional
continuities, while Buddhism became a trans-Asian tradition even
as many Indian monasteries contracted through multiple causes.
