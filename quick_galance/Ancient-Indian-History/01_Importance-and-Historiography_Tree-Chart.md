                PERIODISATION DEBATE
                         │
          HINDU–MUSLIM–BRITISH MODEL FAILS
 • Criterion shifts from religion to nationality
 • Ruler’s religion cannot represent all social life
 • Buddhist, Jaina and non-Muslim polities disappear
 • Social continuity and change become communal blocks
                         │
                         ▼
         COMMON PROVISIONAL PERIODISATION
Ancient/early     — earliest times to c. 6th century CE
Early medieval   — c. 6th to 12th/13th centuries
Medieval         — c. 13th to 18th centuries
Modern           — 18th century onward
                         │
                         ▼
 Boundaries vary; period labels are arguments, not neutral facts.
                         │
                         ▼
            PROCESS-BASED PERIODISATION
Subsistence change + urbanisation/de-urbanisation
+ state formation + agrarian expansion and land rights
+ monetisation and exchange + social stratification
+ cultural and regional transformation
                         │
                         ▼
 Period boundary depends on the historical question.
                         │
                         ▼
                HIGH-YIELD TRAPS
 • Material evidence is not automatically objective
 • A normative text does not directly describe society
 • A royal inscription is not popular opinion
 • Myth is evidence of memory/value before it is proof of event
 • Nationalist history is not identical to communal history
 • Marxist history is not simply economic history
 • One civilisation-wide explanation erases regional variation
 • Ancient institutions cannot be projected unchanged into today
 • No surviving record does not automatically prove non-existence

                 QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Ancient Indian history is neither a neutral collection of facts
nor an arbitrary story. It is a provisional but disciplined
reconstruction in which multiple interpretations are possible,
yet evidence, source criticism and transparent reasoning allow
better interpretations to be distinguished from weaker ones.
