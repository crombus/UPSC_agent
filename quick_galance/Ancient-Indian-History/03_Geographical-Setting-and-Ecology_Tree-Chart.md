### High-yield site-river-region pairs

| Pair | Use in answer | Mandatory qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Mohenjo-daro — Indus floodplain | Floodplain urban adaptation | Not the whole Harappan ecology |
| Dholavira — Khadir island | Reservoir-based water management | Adaptation, not environmental immunity |
| Kalibangan — Ghaggar zone | Settlement in changing river landscape | Does not alone prove textual identity |
| Atranjikhera — upper Ganga | Settlement and iron sequence | Iron is not a sole cause |
| Rajgir/Pataliputra — Magadha | Defence, rivers and strategy | Institutions and labour were decisive |
| Arikamedu — Coromandel | Port-hinterland and overseas contact | Imports do not measure total trade |

### Seven examiner traps

1. Himalayas were neither an absolute wall nor merely invasion routes.
2. Harappan civilisation was wider than the Indus valley.
3. Palaeochannel, archaeological occupation and textual river name are separate claims.
4. Forests were inhabited resource zones, not empty land.
5. Iron, wet rice, rivers and elephants require a multi-causal state-formation explanation.
6. A local climate proxy cannot establish an all-India collapse.
7. Modern borders do not define ancient ecological or cultural regions.
