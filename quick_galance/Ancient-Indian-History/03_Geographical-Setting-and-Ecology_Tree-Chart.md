              HIGH-YIELD PRELIMS TRAPS
• Himalayas did not completely isolate India
• Indo-Gangetic plain was not historically uniform
• Harappan distribution was not confined to the Indus
• Palaeochannel does not prove a named Vedic river
• Thar did not necessarily always have present aridity
• Eastern and western coasts are not morphologically identical
• Modern state borders do not map ancient eco-regions
• Ore deposit does not prove political control
• Rivers mattered beyond irrigation
• A map cannot substitute for causal explanation

                MAINS PARAGRAPH FORMULA

→ source/scale limitation
                         │
                         ▼
                 QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Ancient India developed through regionally differentiated
interactions among mountains, plains, rivers, monsoons,
resources, routes and coasts. These features created fields
of possibility rather than predetermined outcomes. Technology,
labour, institutions and political power converted ecological
opportunities into settlements, networks and states, while
societies simultaneously transformed the landscapes on which
they depended.
