                   CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
From the sixth to mid-eighth century, Pallava and Badami
Chalukya state formation combined warfare, alliances, subordinate
chiefs, agrarian expansion, land grants, irrigation, temple
patronage, Sanskrit court culture, regional languages and Bhakti.

produced uniform territorial control across the peninsula.

                 QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Pallava and Chalukya history is best understood as regional state
formation through conflict, negotiation and cultural institution-
building. Their courts exercised real military and fiscal power, yet
authority beyond core zones depended on chiefs, local bodies,
beneficiaries and shifting alliances. Land grants, irrigation and
settlement extension linked agrarian growth with hierarchy and peasant
obligations. Brahmanization worked through resources, ritual and caste
claims, but regional languages, local cults and Bhakti reshaped rather
than merely received Sanskritic forms. Mamallapuram, Kanchi, Badami,
Aihole and Pattadakal transformed rivalry into sacred geography and
craft experimentation, while bilingual inscriptions and works such as
Mattavilasa-prahasana made literature part of statecraft. The period
thus created durable political, devotional, linguistic and architectural
templates without producing uniform rule, cultural homogeneity or a
single linear transition to the early medieval order.
