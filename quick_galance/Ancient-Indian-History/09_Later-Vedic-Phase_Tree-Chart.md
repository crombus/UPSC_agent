                 QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The Later Vedic phase was a bridge rather than a completed
territorial-state or urban order. Expansion into Kuru-Panchala
and the upper Ganga, stronger plough agriculture, iron use,
surplus extraction and ritual kingship encouraged territorial
and social consolidation. Yet pastoralism, kinship, assemblies,
older deities and regional diversity persisted. Sharper varna
and patriarchal prescriptions coexisted with uneven practice,
while the Upanishads transformed Vedic ritualism from within.
Together these changes prepared—but did not mechanically
produce—the Mahajanapada, urban and shramana world.
