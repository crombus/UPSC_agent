THE LATER VEDIC PHASE (c. 1000-600 BCE)
|
+-- 1. SOURCE + SCOPE
|   +-- later Saṃhitās: Sāma, Yajur, Atharva and later Rigvedic layers
|   +-- Brāhmaṇas -> ritual, kingship, status and political theology
|   +-- Āraṇyakas -> symbolic/reflective transition
|   +-- early Upaniṣads -> ātman, brahman, karma, rebirth, liberation
|   `-- text layer + genre + prescription/practice must be separated
|       -> Topic 08 owns Early/Rig baseline; Topics 10-13 own later outcomes
|
+-- 2. GEOGRAPHICAL REORIENTATION
|   north-west/Sapta-Sindhu
|       -> Kuru-Pañcāla and Indo-Gangetic divide
|       -> upper Ganga-Yamuna doab
|       -> gradual Kosala/Videha and middle-Ganga extension
|   `-- settlement + interaction + forest-edge use + agriculture
|       -> not one empty-land conquest or one uniform migration
|
+-- 3. PGW + IRON: CORRELATION, NOT IDENTITY
|   +-- Painted Grey Ware -> fine grey bowls/dishes with painted designs
|   |   -> settlement distribution, relative chronology, associated tools
|   |   -> not proof of Vedic language, Aryan ethnicity, one state or epic event
|   +-- dark/black metal (kṛṣṇa/śyāma ayas) commonly linked to iron
|   +-- iron tools/weapons/slag -> regional adoption and possible clearance
|   `-- iron + fuel + labour + oxen + crops + ecology + institutions
|       -> mediated change; iron alone did not create states
|
+-- 4. AGRARIAN POLITICAL ECONOMY
|   settlement + plough/oxen + rice/barley/wheat/pulses + storage
|       -> more regular production and surplus
|       -> tribute/gifts + priests/warriors/specialists
|       -> territorial institutions and hierarchy
|   +-- agriculture intensified; it did not begin only now
|   +-- cattle remained important for draught, food, wealth, ritual and prestige
|   +-- crafts: carpentry, chariot-making, metal, pottery, weaving, leather
|   +-- niṣka/śatamāna/kṛṣṇala -> valuables/value measures
|   `-- no automatic coinage, mature guilds or fully monetised town economy
|
+-- 5. JANA -> JANAPADA
|   +-- Bharata/Puru traditions -> Kuru consolidation
|   +-- Pañcāla -> major political-cultural centre
|   +-- kin identity gains territorial attachment
|   `-- lineage bodies persist within emerging territorial polities
|       -> gradual territorialisation, not overnight state revolution
|
+-- 6. KINGSHIP + RATNINS
|   +-- rājan becomes more hereditary, territorial and ritually exalted
|   +-- ritual-political support network (ratnins):
|   |   purohita, senānī, grāmaṇī, sūta, bhāgadugha,
|   |   saṃgrahītṛ, queen and other key household figures
|   +-- bhāga = ruler's share; bali becomes more regular/obligatory
|   |   while dāna/dakṣiṇā and booty continue
|   `-- transitional roles, not modern cabinet, provinces or Mauryan bureaucracy
|
+-- 7. ASSEMBLIES + MILITARY CHANGE
|   +-- sabhā -> narrower/elite deliberative, social or adjudicative weight
|   +-- samiti -> continues with rising royal authority; exact role debated
|   +-- vidatha -> recedes from later prominence
|   +-- territorial kingship + tribute + retainers expand
|   `-- assemblies do not vanish everywhere; no universal standing army
|
+-- 8. RITUAL SOVEREIGNTY
|   +-- rājasūya -> consecration/renewal, alliance and redistribution
|   +-- aśvamedha -> paramountcy/military reach claim
|   +-- vājapeya -> strength, abundance and chariot prestige
|   `-- king + priest + surplus -> sacred legitimacy and political theatre
|       -> ritual claim is not proof of actual borders or effective control
|
+-- 9. VARṆA, HOUSEHOLD + INEQUALITY
|   +-- sharper Brahmana-Kshatriya cooperation and status competition
|   +-- Vaishya -> production, herding, agriculture and tribute
|   +-- Shudra -> subordination and restricted ritual access in texts
|   +-- varṇa = broad ideological schema
|   |   -> not the complete later jati/untouchability system
|   +-- gṛhapati -> production, property, ritual and lineage head
|   +-- patriliny, sons, inheritance and ancestor rites strengthen
|   `-- dāsa/dāsī -> servile/captive categories can expand in meaning;
|       do not reduce the term to one race

+-- 10. WOMEN, GOTRA + LIFE STAGES
|   +-- stronger patriarchal textual norms and unequal ritual access
|   +-- wives/queens retain economic, ritual and political roles in contexts
|   +-- Gargi and Maitreyi show elite philosophical participation
|   +-- gotra descent/exogamy develops; later detailed system not automatic
|   +-- student/householder/forest/renouncer stages develop gradually
|   `-- safe claim: stronger patriarchy with status/regional variation,
|       not either total equality or total loss for all women

+-- 11. RELIGIOUS SPECIALISATION
|   +-- hotṛ -> recites; udgātṛ -> chants
|   +-- adhvaryu -> performs physical procedure/formula
|   +-- brahman -> supervises/corrects
|   +-- Indra/Agni remain; Prajāpati, Viṣṇu and Rudra gain prominence
|   `-- specialised ritual knowledge + surplus -> priestly authority
|       -> older gods and practices continue

+-- 12. UPANISHADIC TURN
|   dialogue/analogy/debate (Bṛhadāraṇyaka, Chāndogya)
|       -> brahman + ātman + karma + saṃsāra
|       -> knowledge/internalised sacrifice + mokṣa
|   `-- critique/transformation from within the Vedic world
|       -> not one doctrine, total ritual rejection or completed Vedānta

+-- 13. EARLY -> LATER COMPARISON
|   +-- geography: north-west -> Kuru-Pañcāla/upper Ganga
|   +-- economy: cattle-valued mixed -> stronger settled agrarian surplus
|   +-- settlement: kin/mobile -> persistent villages and PGW contexts
|   +-- polity: jana chiefship -> janapada/territorial kingship
|   +-- society: emerging rank -> sharper varṇa and household patriarchy
|   +-- religion: hymn/offering -> elaborate sacrifice and royal ritual
|   +-- thought: cosmological questioning -> karma/rebirth/internalisation
|   `-- transition = gradual reweighting; pastoralism, kinship and assemblies
|       do not simply disappear

+-- 14. TRANSITION TO NEXT PHASE
|   agrarian surplus + territoriality + extraction + occupational differentiation
|       + ritual critique and hierarchy
|       -> preconditions for Mahajanapadas/second urbanisation
|   `-- no mechanical single cause:
|       Topics 10-13 own Jainism/Buddhism, Mahajanapadas and Magadha

+-- 15. VERIFIED PYQ ROUTE
|   +-- 2024 GS-I Q1 (10 marks/150 words; direct):
|   |   "Underline" explicit society/economy changes:
|   |   cattle/mobility -> settled plough agriculture/surplus
|   |   kin/mobile settlement -> stable villages
|   |   jana -> janapada; gifts/booty -> more regular appropriation
|   |   emerging varṇa -> sharper hierarchy; stronger patriarchal norms
|   +-- 2023 GS-I Q11 (15 marks; cross-cutting):
|   |   later varṇa, household/gender trends, sacrifice and priestly roles,
|   |   karma/rebirth and transformed—not unchanged—continuities
|   +-- adjacent 2024 Prelims:
|   |   Upanishads/Puranas relative chronology; do not claim Upanishads lack
|   |   parables; use as supporting source route, not an owner replacement
|   `-- answer formula:
|       paired change -> named term/text/archaeological correlation
|       -> mechanism -> qualification (prescription != uniform practice)

`-- 16. CLOSE-OPTION TRAPS + QUALIFIED VERDICT
    PGW = Vedic people/language -> no, material horizon
    iron alone caused states -> no, mediated multi-causal change
    agriculture began only later -> no, scale/stability intensified
    cattle ceased to matter -> no, relative role changed
    ratnins = modern cabinet -> no, ritual-political persons/roles
    assemblies disappeared -> no, relative weight changed unevenly
    rājasūya/aśvamedha = real borders -> no, sovereignty claims/theatre
    varṇa = complete later caste -> no, distinguish jati and later systems
    Upanishads rejected Veda -> no, internal transformation and critique
    verdict:
      Later Vedic India was a regionally uneven bridge from kin-centred,
      cattle-valued society toward agrarian surplus, territorial kingship,
      sharper hierarchy and philosophical internalisation—before, not identical
      with, the fully urban Mahajanapada world.
