                 QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The age of the Buddha cannot be understood through varna texts,
urban archaeology or religious doctrine in isolation. Agrarian
production, extraction, fortified states, towns, crafts, coins
and credit generated new capacities and inequalities. Varna
ideology sharpened, but actual society also turned on wealth,
occupation, kinship, gender and dependence, visible in gahapatis,
setthis, artisans, cultivators, labourers, women and excluded
groups. Monarchies and clan oligarchies remained elite systems,
while Jain and Buddhist institutions offered ethical critique
and alternative identities without instantly abolishing social
hierarchy. It was a regionally uneven bridge from lineage-based
orders to the more urban, fiscal and imperial Mauryan world.
