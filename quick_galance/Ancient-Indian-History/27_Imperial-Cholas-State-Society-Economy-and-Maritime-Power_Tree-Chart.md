                       CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The Imperial Cholas were a Kaveri-centred early-medieval regional
empire whose military and maritime reach rested on irrigated agrarian
surplus, land survey and taxation, layered territorial administration,
powerful but socially bounded local corporations, temple-centred
institutions, merchant networks and a rich inscriptional culture.

                 QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The Imperial Cholas built one of early-medieval India's strongest
regional empires by joining Kaveri-delta surplus to military command,
survey, layered administration, local corporate institutions, temple
patronage and merchant networks. Their inscriptions reveal substantial
royal capacity, but also show that authority worked through nadus,
sabhas, urs, nagarams, temples, chiefs and socially restricted elites.
Great temples and bronzes converted agrarian resources into ritual,
labour, archive and imperial memory. Overseas commerce and campaigns
linked the Tamil coast with Sri Lanka, Srivijaya and China, yet only
Sri Lanka saw significant territorial incorporation; the 1025 strike
against Srivijaya was coercive projection, not colonization. Chola
primacy declined through cumulative regional pressures, while irrigation,
temples, Tamil culture, merchant connections and artistic memory endured.
The Chola achievement therefore lay in a negotiated agrarian-commercial
empire whose maritime reach was impressive but bounded.
