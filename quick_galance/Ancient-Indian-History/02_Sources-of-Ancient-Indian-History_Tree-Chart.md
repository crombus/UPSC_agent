1. Match chronology
2. Match geography
3. Match scale—household, settlement, region or polity
4. Test independence—copied accounts are not new witnesses
5. Ask what each source was designed to record
6. Preserve productive contradiction
7. State residual uncertainty

              HIGH-YIELD EXAMINER TRAPS
• Text is not a transparent chronicle
• Contemporary inscription is not automatically unbiased
• Archaeological culture is not an ethnic identity
• Coin distribution is not an imperial boundary
• Coin absence is not proof of absent trade
• Manuscript date is not composition date
• Traveller distance does not create objectivity
• Radiocarbon result is not an exact calendar year
• aDNA does not identify language, caste or religion
• More sources do not create certainty unless independent
  and matched in chronology, geography and scale

                 QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Ancient Indian history is reconstructed neither by treating
texts as chronicles nor objects as self-explanatory facts.
Reliable reconstruction comes from matching each source to
the question it can answer, controlling its context, purpose,
date and transmission, and triangulating independent evidence
while preserving uncertainty and contradiction.
