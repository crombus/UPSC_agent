                 QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Achaemenid and Macedonian penetration affected connected parts
of the north-west, not India as a whole. Achaemenid rule was the
longer imperial frontier relationship and supplied a strong
Aramaic context for Kharoshthi and later selective adaptations.
Alexander's campaign was spectacular but brief: alliances,
Hydaspes, resistance and the Beas refusal exposed a fragmented
yet active political landscape, followed by rapidly unstable
satrapies. The episodes neither transformed the whole
subcontinent nor lacked significance. Their best summary is:
bounded political control, intensified transregional contact,
and an indirect—never sufficient—context for Mauryan expansion.
