          ARCHAEOLOGICAL LIMITS
• Stone tools do not reveal language, race or full identity
• Surface finds show distribution better than chronology
• River gravels can redeposit artefacts
• Caves and shelters are over-represented
• Organic and plant remains preserve unevenly
• Site function requires complete assemblage/context
• Scientific dates apply to particular samples/events
• Domestication needs morphology + context + chronology
• Ethnographic analogy suggests possibility, not continuity
• Social organisation and ritual remain graded interpretations

                 QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The Palaeolithic and Mesolithic were not crude preliminary
stages awaiting agriculture. They contain regionally diverse
technologies, mobility systems, food strategies, symbolic
practices and mortuary traditions. Their reconstruction depends
on context and scientific dating, while the transition to food
production is best understood as uneven experimentation and
coexistence rather than a simultaneous evolutionary leap.
