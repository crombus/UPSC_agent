                 QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Sangam Tamilakam was neither an isolated literary realm nor a
ready-made centralized empire. Its history emerged from megalithic
and early historic transitions, complementary ecological zones,
agrarian and craft production, warrior-chiefly redistribution,
lineage competition, caravans and port-hinterland exchange. Poems
preserve an unmatched vocabulary of landscape, love, labour, war,
gift and fame, while Tamil-Brahmi records, burials, settlements,
coins and port archaeology control their chronology and scale.
Mediterranean demand intensified an already diversified Indian Ocean
economy rather than creating it. The Chera, Chola and Pandya lineages
developed durable political idioms within a region of Velir chiefs,
uneven institutions and shifting centres. The strongest reconstruction
therefore triangulates literature and material evidence, recognizes
social and gender inequality alongside agency, and treats the later
transition as regional transformation rather than abrupt collapse.
