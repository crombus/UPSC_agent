              HIGH-YIELD UPSC TRAPS
• Gupta homeland/social origin is not certain
• Gupta era's exact founding event is debated
• Lichchhavi marriage did not automatically annex all lands
• prashasti is evidence plus imperial rhetoric
• all defeated rulers were not directly annexed
• Dakshinapatha campaign was not a provincial map
• empire was large but not pan-Indian
• Vakatakas were allies, not Gupta provinces
• Ramagupta story is not fully settled
• Mehrauli “Chandra” attribution is common, not incontestable
• Kumaragupta as sole Nalanda founder is unsafe
• administrative terms varied regionally
• district elite boards were not democratic municipalities
• every land grant did not transfer full sovereignty
• “feudalism” requires rights, region and chronology

                 QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The Gupta Empire was a powerful but layered north Indian hegemony.
Its Ganga core, army, taxation, provinces and imperial titles gave it
substantial coercive capacity, while marriage alliances, Sanskrit
prashastis, Ashvamedha claims, coin images, tributary arrangements
and local intermediaries expanded its reach beyond direct rule.
Samudragupta's differentiated treatment of Aryavarta, southern,
forest, frontier and distant rulers is the clearest evidence that
Gupta sovereignty was graded rather than uniform. Chandragupta II's
western expansion, Udayagiri programme and Vakataka alliance further
joined military power with symbolic and diplomatic integration.
Land grants and subordinate structures strengthened expansion but
could also encourage regional autonomy. Huna pressure aggravated,
rather than singly caused, a multicausal contraction. Gupta history
is therefore best understood as major imperial and classical
florescence with genuine political achievement, flexible territorial
incorporation and unequal regional and social distribution.
