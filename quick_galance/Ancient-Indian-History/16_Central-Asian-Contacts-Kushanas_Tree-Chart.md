                 QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Central Asian contacts transformed the post-Mauryan north-west
into a connected arena of overlapping states and mobile peoples.
Indo-Greek bilingual portrait coins, Shaka satrapal formations,
Indo-Parthian regional rule and Kushana control of Bactrian,
Gandharan and Mathuran corridors linked India more intensively
with China, Iran, Central Asia and the Mediterranean. Their
multilingual coins, plural pantheons, Buddhist networks and
Gandhara–Mathura workshops reveal adaptation and co-creation,
not foreign culture imposed on a passive society. Political
control remained tiered and regional, trade had multiple bases,
and later contraction produced successor formations rather than
a single civilisational break.
