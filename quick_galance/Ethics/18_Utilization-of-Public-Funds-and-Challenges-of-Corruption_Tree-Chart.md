                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Public money is a fiduciary trust collected under law for authorised public purpose.
Ethical utilisation requires economy, efficiency, effectiveness and equity, but a high
expenditure ratio proves none of them. Under-utilisation may reflect delay or prudent
saving; full utilisation may conceal waste, diversion or poor outcomes. Corruption can
be a transaction, a network or an institutional equilibrium involving coercion,
collusion, omission, squandering, regulatory capture and state capture. Control must
cover the entire budget-procurement-delivery-outcome chain through segregation,
reconciliation, audit, transparency, citizen verification, protected reporting,
proportionate enforcement and remedy.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Ethical public finance asks not whether money was exhausted but whether authorised
resources produced durable and equitable public value. India needs controls across the
whole lifecycle, from realistic budgeting and competitive procurement to physical
verification, social audit and outcome evaluation. Digital traceability can expose
anomalies, but it must be paired with human review and citizen remedy. Anti-corruption
systems should punish wilful abuse while protecting documented, good-faith decisions
from hindsight-driven suspicion.
