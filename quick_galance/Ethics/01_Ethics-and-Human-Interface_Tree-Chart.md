### Central thesis

```text
ETHICS AND HUMAN INTERFACE
        |
        v
Ethical conduct at the point where public power meets citizen need is produced
NEITHER by values alone NOR by institutions alone, but by values durably CONTAINED
within institutions that reduce discretion, increase transparency and empower the
citizen - subject to the political ceiling the Commission itself names at 1.12.
```

### 1. ESSENCE  (ARC 1.1-1.2)

```text
ETHICS = "a set of standards that society places on itself"
         guiding "behaviour, choices and actions"
   |
   +-- standards alone DO NOT ensure ethical behaviour; the crux is:
   |      ADOPTION IN ACTION -> SANCTIONS -> COMPETENT DISCIPLINARY BODIES
   |      ACTING QUICKLY -> A CULTURE OF INTEGRITY
   |
   +-- ethikos (Greek) = "arising from habit"
   +-- corruptus (Latin) = "to break or destroy"
   +-- CORRUPTION = "an important manifestation of the failure of ethics"
   +-- scale: GRAND (high places) vs RETAIL ("everyday life of common people")
```

### 3. DIMENSIONS OF ETHICS - TWO LAYERS

```text
LAYER 1 - FOUR LEVELS OF ENQUIRY
   DESCRIPTIVE  what is in fact believed        -> one line in GS-IV
   NORMATIVE    what ought to be done, and why  -> HIGH weight
   META-ETHICS  what moral words mean           -> at most one line
   APPLIED      what THIS field must do here    -> HIGH weight
   RULE: never cross the IS -> OUGHT gap. "Everyone does it" settles nothing.

LAYER 2 - SIX WORKING DIMENSIONS OF ONE DECISION   (2024 GS-IV Q1(b))
   DUTY / RIGHTS        catches: using a citizen as a means
   CONSEQUENCES         catches: good intent with foreseeable harm
   JUSTICE / FAIRNESS   catches: silent unexplained unequal treatment
   VIRTUE / CHARACTER   catches: situational courage, selective honesty
   CARE / EMPATHY       catches: procedure that is humanly blind
   ACCOUNTABILITY       catches: decisions that survive only unexamined
   VERDICT: they are MUTUAL CHECKS. Name the conflict; justify the trade-off.
```

### 4. THE PRECISION GRID - CLOSE-OPTION DISTINCTIONS

```text
ETHICS      reasoned standard        "justified by argument?"
MORALITY    actually held belief     "what does this community hold right?"
VALUES      enduring commitments     "what do I consistently prize?"
LAW         enacted, enforceable     "does a rule permit or forbid this?"
PROPRIETY   fit for office/occasion  "would a reasonable citizen accept it?"
CONSCIENCE  reflective judgment      "can I answer for this on reflection?"

          LEGAL?  MORAL?  PROPER?
  A         yes     yes     yes    routine decision on published criteria
  B         yes     yes     NO     lavish dinner hosted by a bidder
  C         yes     NO      NO     harsh but lawful eviction
  D         NO      yes     --     conscientious refusal (Topic 10)

ARC BOX 2.3 (Fulbright; Senate Ethics Manual 2003 ed., p.6):
  favours "under the guise of friendship, which offend the spirit of the law but
  do not violate its letter"; "morality has become identical with legality".
REMEDY MAP: illegality -> sanction | immorality -> argument |
            impropriety -> disclosure, recusal, avoidance.
```

### 5. DETERMINANTS

```text
INDIVIDUAL LAYERS
   SOCIETY > ORGANISATION > FAMILY + EDUCATION > THE PERSON -> the act on the file
   ARC 1.5: values "exist in abundance"; "a sense of right and wrong is intrinsic
      to our culture and civilization"  => the scarce good is not values
   ARC 1.4 INCENTIVE RULE: reward good + punish bad -> people "follow the straight
      and narrow path"; good "fraught with difficulties" + bad "extravagantly
      rewarded" -> people "stray from the honourable path"

STRUCTURAL
   THE BUNDLE (1.5) AUTHORITY + DISCRETION + CONTROL OF THE PUBLIC PURSE
      -> "enormous temptation and opportunity to promote private gain at public cost"
   AGGRAVATOR 1 (1.6) colonial legacy of "unchallenged authority"
   AGGRAVATOR 2 (1.6) asymmetry of power - ~90% unorganized sector; ~70% of
      organized workers with security employed by the state  [2007 FIGURES ONLY]
      -> "reduces societal pressure to conform to ethical behaviour"
   AGGRAVATOR 3 (1.7) over-regulation + scarcity + subsidies under asymmetry
      -> "patron and master" vs "citizens into mendicants"; cut the "capacity to
         resist extortionary demands"   [expressly an UNINTENDED consequence]
   MULTIPLIER 1 (1.8) monopoly + discretion INCREASE; competition + transparency
      REDUCE.  Sectors: telephones, steel, cement, sugar, two-wheelers.
      Services: railway reservation, driving licences (computerisation).
   MULTIPLIER 2 (1.9) over-centralisation widens the distance between AUTHORITY
      and ACCOUNTABILITY; "smallest number of final decision makers".
   CEILING (1.12) "quality of politics" is "perhaps the most important
      determinant"; unmet demand for illegitimate funds -> "other avenues will be
      forcibly opened up" -> corruption MIGRATES to NATURAL-MONOPOLY sectors.
```

### 6. CONSEQUENCES

```text
SIX CHANNELS
   [1] transactional  delays, harassment, lost wages, "danger of loss of life or limb"
   [2] distributive   retail corruption hits "everyday life of common people"
   [3] relational     patron-and-master vs mendicant  (1.7)
   [4] systemic       black money, economic offences, money laundering "leading even
                      to the funding of terrorist activities against the State" (1.16)
   [5] legitimacy     cynicism that "bodes ill for our democratic system itself" (1.3)
   [6] developmental  Preface: non-expropriation + contract enforcement + less delay
                      and corruption "can raise the GDP growth rate significantly"
POSITIVE MIRROR: predictability -> lower risk | equal treatment -> social harmony |
   reviewable reasons -> fewer disputes and faster disposal.

COERCIVE vs COLLUSIVE  (1.13)
   COERCIVE   citizen entitled to the service; "an unwilling victim"; only the
              official gains; the victim can complain
              -> time-bound guarantee, published fee, single window, automation,
                 safe complaint channel, protection of the complainant
   COLLUSIVE  giver wants the irregular outcome; "both parties benefit at immense
              cost to society"; NO complaining witness
              examples: public works contracts, procurement, recruitment, tax
                 evasion, substandard projects, collusive violation of regulations,
                 adulteration of foods and drugs, obstruction of justice, doctoring
                 evidence
              -> procurement transparency, third-party audit, asset and beneficial-
                 interest scrutiny, whistle-blower protection, investigate BOTH sides
   TREND: as controls are removed, extortionary corruption DECLINES and collusive
          corruption INCREASES.
```

### 7. PRIVATE AND PUBLIC RELATIONSHIPS  (ARC 1.17)

```text
PRIVATE partiality is legitimate  ||  PUBLIC office is FIDUCIARY: partiality is a breach
   crossing points: NEPOTISM | CONFLICT OF INTEREST | GIFTS AND HOSPITALITY
   conflict-of-interest remedy: DISCLOSE -> RECUSE -> REALLOCATE, in writing
   public test: not "did I feel biased" but "can I state this reason publicly, and
      would the citizen who LOST accept it?"
ARC 1.17 extends the duty beyond government to:
   corporate ethics - "paradigm shift from the pejorative 'business ethics' to
      'ethics in business'"
   "every profession, voluntary organization and civil society structure"
   "ethics in citizen behaviour because such behaviour impinges directly on ethics
      in government and administration"
LIMIT: citizen duty binds fully in COLLUSION, weakly where the citizen is COERCED.
```

### 8. THE THREE CORRECTIVE LEVERS

```text
LEVER 1 STRUCTURAL  close the ALTERNATIVE gap (competition), the INFORMATION gap
   (transparency) and the DISCRETION gap (automation).
   ARC 1.15 named: SC ruling on filing wealth + educational qualifications +
   criminal antecedents with nomination papers; RTI Act; ICT/e-governance.
LEVER 2 CITIZEN EMPOWERMENT (1.10) - six instruments in one sentence:
   RTI | effective citizens' charters | opportunity and incentives for proactive
   citizens | stakeholders' involvement in delivery | public consultation in
   decision making | social auditing.
   ARC: they "dramatically curbed corruption and promoted integrity and quality of
   decision making".   LIMIT: elite capture where literacy/organisation are absent.
LEVER 3 PREVENTION *AND* PUNISHMENT (1.11, 1.16)
   "enforcement of rule of law and deterrent punishment ... are critical"
   also demanded: analysis of "the causes of their failure"
   RECOMMENDED in Jan 2007 (not then law): benami and illegal-asset forfeiture;
   whistleblower legislation "against retribution"; stronger investigation;
   exemplary punishment "raising the risk associated with corrupt behaviour".
   ARITHMETIC: prevention lowers OPPORTUNITY, punishment raises RISK.
```

### 10. EXAMINER TRAPS

```text
1 defining ethics as "moral values" instead of ARC 1.1's social-operational definition
2 ethics and morality as always identical - or over-corrected to never overlapping
3 "if it is legal, no ethical question arises"        (rejected by Box 2.3)
4 attributing a single "decline of values" cause      (rejected at 1.4-1.5)
5 blaming the coerced citizen for paying              (contradicted at 1.13)
6 quoting the 90% / 70% figures as current statistics (2007 illustrative framing)
7 claiming liberalisation ended corruption            (capped by 1.12 migration)
8 equating Comprehensive National Power with military strength alone
```
