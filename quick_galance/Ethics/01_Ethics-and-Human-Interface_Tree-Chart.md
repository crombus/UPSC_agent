                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Ethics is the reasoned and practised standard by which persons and institutions
distinguish right conduct from mere convenience, legality or self-interest. The human
interface is where private motive, public duty, citizen dignity and institutional
power meet. Ethical conduct depends neither on good character alone nor on rules
alone: values need institutions that reduce arbitrary discretion, disclose reasons,
enable citizen voice, reward integrity and sanction abuse. Every public decision
should therefore be tested through legality, constitutional values, duty, rights,
consequences, virtue, justice, transparency and public trust.
