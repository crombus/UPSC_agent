                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Corporate ethics is how value is created, not a charitable layer added after profit.
Corporate governance supplies structures through which boards, managers, auditors and
owners remain accountable to shareholders and wider stakeholders. CSR is a statutory
social-spending obligation for qualifying companies, distinct from ethical core
operations. International ethics applies sovereignty, justice, civilian protection,
humanitarian neutrality, anti-corruption cooperation and recipient ownership across
borders. Compliance is a floor: transparent boards, independent assurance, stakeholder
voice and verifiable environmental and social performance are needed to prevent
greenwashing, fraud and externalisation of harm.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Corporate legitimacy depends on ethical core operations, not the visibility of CSR
spending. Boards must align profit with honest accounting, fair labour, environmental
responsibility, stakeholder voice and independent assurance. International instruments
can coordinate anti-corruption and humanitarian standards, but they remain effective
only through domestic enforcement and recipient ownership. The governing test is
whether value creation avoids shifting concealed costs onto workers, communities,
future generations or weaker states.
