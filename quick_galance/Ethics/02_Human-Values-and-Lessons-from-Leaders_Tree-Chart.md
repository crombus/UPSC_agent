                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Values are enduring standards of what is worthwhile; they are learned through family,
education, culture, institutions, experience and exemplars, then tested under pressure.
Leader-based questions are not biography tests. They require accurate attribution,
translation of a teaching into a concrete administrative rule, an Indian application
and a limitation. Exemplars inspire moral imagination, but their lessons must be
combined with constitutional values and institutional safeguards: character without
systems is fragile, while systems without internalised values invite mechanical
compliance and evasion.
