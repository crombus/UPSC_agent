                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Probity is upright, impartial and public-purpose conduct in entrusted public office,
made capable of reasoned public justification and verification. It overlaps with
honesty, integrity, propriety, transparency and accountability but is reducible to none.
Its philosophical foundation combines duty to the public trust, virtue as habituated
uprightness, justice and constitutional morality. Probity is not paperwork or absence
of conviction; it requires clean motives, proper means, disclosed interests, fair
decisions, reliable records, independent scrutiny and correction. Good governance
results when individual integrity is institutionally visible and enforceable.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Probity is public integrity made visible and answerable. It demands upright motives,
proper means, impartial decisions and records capable of independent scrutiny. Its
philosophical basis in duty, virtue, trusteeship and constitutional morality must be
translated into disclosure, recusal, reasoned orders, audit and remedy. At the same
time, probity systems must distinguish mala fide conduct from honest error so that
accountability deters corruption without paralysing lawful initiative and development.
