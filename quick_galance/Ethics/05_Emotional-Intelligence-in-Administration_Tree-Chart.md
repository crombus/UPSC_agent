                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Emotional intelligence is the ability to perceive, use, understand and manage emotions
in oneself and others so that thought and action improve under pressure. In public
administration it supports composure, empathy, motivation, team leadership, conflict
resolution and communication. EI is necessary but ethically neutral: a skilled reader
of emotion can serve citizens or manipulate them. It must therefore be paired with
integrity, law, technical competence and institutional support. Self-regulation is not
emotional suppression, empathy is not favouritism, and individual resilience cannot
permanently compensate for unsafe workload or weak delegation.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Emotional intelligence improves administrative judgment by making emotions visible,
interpretable and governable rather than suppressed or obeyed blindly. Its highest
public value lies in calm crisis leadership, humane service and trustworthy teamwork.
But EI cannot determine what is right and can sharpen manipulation when divorced from
integrity. Ethical administration therefore combines emotional capability with
constitutional standards, technical competence, transparent processes and institutions
that protect both citizens and officials from chronic emotional strain.
