                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
A Code of Ethics states the public purpose and broad values an institution expects;
a Code of Conduct translates those values into specific behavioural duties and
prohibitions. The distinction is a spectrum, not a watertight binary, and sanctions
arise from the applicable legal or service framework rather than the document's title.
An effective ethics system joins values, prospective advice, conflict management,
training, reporting, fair investigation, proportionate discipline and periodic review.
Rules without ethical understanding invite loophole-seeking; aspirations without precise
standards make enforcement arbitrary.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
An ethics code gives public service its constitutional identity; conduct rules give
that identity behavioural precision. India should preserve enforceable service rules
while adding a coherent civil-service ethics framework supported by confidential advice,
conflict management, realistic case training and fair discipline. The aim is neither
an unenforceable declaration nor an ever-growing prohibition list, but a living system
that helps officials recognise, prevent, explain and correct ethical risk.
