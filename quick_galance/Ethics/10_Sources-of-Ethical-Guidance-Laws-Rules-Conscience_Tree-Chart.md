                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Law is a necessary ethical floor but not a sufficient ceiling; conscience can expose
a wrong the rulebook misses but can also reflect bias or self-righteousness. A public
servant should therefore avoid both blind legalism and unconstrained personal morality.
The defensible approach identifies legal authority and constitutional limits, tests
conscience against facts and public reason, and uses accountable channels such as
interpretation, written dissent, recusal, escalation, review and protected disclosure.
Exceptional defiance demands compelling necessity, proportionality, evidence and
willingness to accept scrutiny.
