                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Corruption law must distinguish extorted citizens from collusive beneficiaries,
unethical conduct from criminal offences, honest error from mala fide abuse, and
departmental, criminal, civil and asset-recovery proceedings. The 2018 Prevention of
Corruption amendment criminalised bribe-giving and commercial-organisational bribery
while protecting a compelled giver only through a narrow seven-day reporting defence;
it also narrowed criminal misconduct and introduced prior approval for investigation
of official-duty decisions. The framework must deter corruption without converting
good-faith administrative judgment into automatic criminal suspicion, and must protect
those who report wrongdoing through credible channels.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
India's anti-corruption law is stronger where it names the bribe giver, company and
illicit enrichment, but more contested where prior approval and narrowed misconduct
definitions may shield or protect official decision-making. The ethical balance requires
fast, reasoned approvals, independent investigation, modern whistleblower protection and
clear separation of honest error from mala fide abuse. Legal precision should protect
public servants who decide in good faith while ensuring that power, influence and
beneficial ownership cannot conceal public loss.
