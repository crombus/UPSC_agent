                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Responsibility assigns a duty; accountability requires an identifiable actor to explain
and justify conduct against a known standard before a forum able to investigate, judge,
correct, remedy or sanction. Transparency supplies information but does not by itself
create accountability. Ethical governance therefore combines ex-ante standards and
records with ex-post review across internal vigilance, external constitutional and
judicial oversight, political answerability and social control. No single layer is
sufficient: internal systems can collude, external audit can be delayed and social
accountability can be captured or uninformed.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Accountability becomes ethical governance only when power is traceable, reasons are
contestable and wrongdoing or service failure produces correction and remedy. India's
internal, external and social control layers should reinforce rather than replace one
another. Concurrent digital assurance can shorten delay, but constitutional audit,
independent review, citizen participation and due process remain essential. The final
test is whether affected citizens receive both an explanation and a just correction.
