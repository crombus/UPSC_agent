                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Public status creates stricter ethical duties because officials exercise asymmetric
power, use public resources, possess privileged information and act in citizens' names.
The Nolan principles convert this status into checkable standards. An ethical dilemma
exists only when legitimate values or duties genuinely conflict; personal gain versus
a clear public duty is usually temptation or misconduct, not a dilemma. Resolution
requires a legality floor, precise stakeholder and value mapping, at least three
realistic options, duty-consequence-virtue-justice analysis, a reasoned decision,
transparent implementation and mitigation of the value that remains compromised.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Public-service ethics is stricter than private preference because public status carries
power, trust and fiduciary responsibility. The Nolan principles make this responsibility
visible, while dilemma analysis prevents either moralistic simplification or convenient
relabelling of misconduct. Ethical officials disclose conflicts, resist coercion and
partisan pressure, give reasons and accept scrutiny. Ethical institutions make such
