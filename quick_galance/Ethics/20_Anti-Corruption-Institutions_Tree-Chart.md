                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Anti-corruption institutions are a chain, not interchangeable agencies. Departmental
vigilance and CVOs prevent and screen; the CVC supervises and advises within its
statutory remit; the CBI investigates through DSPE powers and territorial gates;
prosecutors and courts adjudicate; the Lokpal handles complaints within a broad central
jurisdiction and may direct referred investigation; State Lokayuktas vary. Multiplicity
becomes weakness when referrals, records, timelines and responsibility are unclear.
Independence must coexist with legality, federalism, due process, representative
legitimacy and protection of honest decisions.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
India does not lack anti-corruption bodies; it lacks complete coherence across their
mandates, territorial gates, referrals and follow-up. The CVC, CBI, Lokpal, departmental
vigilance and State institutions should remain specialised but interoperable, with
independent resources, secure records, time-bound reasoned decisions and due process.
Broader jurisdiction or more complaints is not sufficient: credibility depends on
protecting informants and honest officials while converting substantiated cases into
timely remedy, sanction and systemic prevention.
