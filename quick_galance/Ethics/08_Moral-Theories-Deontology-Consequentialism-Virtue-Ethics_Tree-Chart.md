                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Normative theories answer different questions. Deontology asks whether an act respects
duty, rights and persons; consequentialism asks which option produces the best expected
overall outcome; virtue ethics asks what a practically wise and good public servant
would do in context. Care ethics adds relationships and vulnerability, while justice
tests distribution and procedure. Mature reasoning triangulates these lenses: rights
form a floor, consequences guide optimisation within it, and practical wisdom resolves
contextual conflict. Outcomes matter, but moral luck means officials must also be judged
by intention, information, diligence and process available when they decided.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
No single theory can carry every administrative dilemma. Duties and rights prevent
persons from becoming instruments; consequences reveal hidden and long-term harms;
virtue and care supply contextual judgment; justice tests the rule and its distribution.
Theory triangulation does not eliminate tragic choices, but it makes the final choice
publicly reasoned and reviewable. Officials should be evaluated by both outcomes and
the quality of judgment possible with the information and control they possessed.
