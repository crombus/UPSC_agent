# Indian Art and Culture 07 - Painting Traditions

## Quick-Glance Tree Chart

```text
INDIAN PAINTING TRADITIONS
|
+-- MASTER IDEA
|   Indian painting changes with support, scale, patronage and audience.
|   Wall mural, manuscript folio, album page, bazaar sheet and ritual wall
|   painting belong to different viewing worlds.
|
+-- READING METHOD
|   support -> technique -> patron/workshop -> audience/function
|   -> theme/iconography -> stylistic comparison -> evidence limit
|
+-- TECHNIQUE / FORMAT VOCABULARY
|   Shadanga: Roopbheda / Pramana / Bhava / Lavanya Yojana /
|   Sadrishya / Varnikabhanga
|   mural != miniature
|   fresco secco / tempera safer than blanket "true fresco"
|   manuscript != album
|
+-- EARLY CHRONOLOGY
|   Prehistoric rock art: Bhimbetka, Lakhudiyar
|   Great mural pair: Ajanta + Bagh
|   South Indian mural line: Sittanavasal -> Pallava sites -> Armamalai
|   -> Brihadisvara -> Lepakshi
|
+-- AJANTA
|   multi-phase chronology
|   Buddhist caves
|   Jataka / Buddha / Bodhisattva / court scenes
|   Padmapani anchor
|   Vakataka-Harisena in major later phase
|   technique caution: prepared plaster, dry-surface execution
|
+-- BAGH
|   Dhar district, on Baghni river
|   Gupta-era mural anchor
|   stronger outlines, more earthly register
|   Cave 4 = Rang Mahal
|   Hallisalasya route: keep only source-safe wording
|
+-- SOUTH INDIAN MURAL RAIL
|   Sittanavasal lotus-pond / Jain register
|   Pallava evidence bank: Kanchipuram, Panamalai, Mamandur
|   Armamalai bridge
|   Chola mural memory: Brihadisvara, Rajaraja with Karuvur Devar
|   Vijayanagara-Nayaka continuation: Lepakshi
|   RULE: survival gaps != production gaps
|
+-- PORTABLE PAINTING SHIFT
|   Pala -> Buddhist illustrated manuscripts on palm leaf
|   Western Indian / Apabhramsa -> Jain-dominant manuscript line
|   red / yellow / ochre, angular faces, fish-shaped eyes
|   Kalpasutra / Kalakacharya Katha
|
+-- SULTANATE TO MUGHAL
|   Sultanate synthesis: Persianate + indigenous manuscript habits
|   Nimatnama at Mandu
|   Humayun -> Mir Sayyid Ali + Abdus Samad
|   Akbar -> Tasvir Khana / Hamzanama / Razmnama / Akbarnama
|   Jahangir -> album / portrait / flora-fauna / Ustad Mansur
|   Shah Jahan -> court polish
|   Aurangzeb -> reduced patronage, artist dispersal
|
+-- REGIONAL MINIATURE FAMILIES
|   Deccani -> saturated colour / elongated figures / fantastic landscape
|   Rajasthani -> Mewar / Bundi / Kota / Jaipur / Kishangarh
|   Bani Thani -> Kishangarh, not Kangra
|   Pahari -> Basohli -> Guler -> Kangra
|   RULE: shared resources do not erase regional distinction
|
+-- DEVOTIONAL / OBJECT SURFACES
|   Tanjore -> gold-rich devotional panel
|   Mysore -> gesso and fine line
|   Ganjifa -> painted playing-card tradition
|
+-- COLONIAL / MODERN TURN
|   Company painting -> colonial patron + documentation + hybrid taste
|   Kalighat / bazaar -> urban market sheets
|   Ravi Varma -> oil + print public
|   Bengal School -> Abanindranath / Bharat Mata / wash idiom
|   Santiniketan -> experimentation and pedagogy
|   Progressive Artists Group -> post-1947 modern turn
|
+-- FOLK / TRIBAL CONTINUITY
|   Madhubani -> Mithila / ritual / bold colour
|   Pattachitra -> Odisha / treated cloth / Jagannath world
|   Kalamkari -> treated cloth / pen or block process
|   Warli -> white on ochre / geometric ritual wall
|   Gond -> patterned sacred-natural world
|   Phad / Cheriyal -> scroll and performance linkage
|
+-- PRELIMS TRAPS
|   Ajanta is not a single-phase event.
|   Bagh is not Ajanta's clone.
|   Hallisalasya must not be tied to a modern named classical form.
|   Bani Thani belongs to Kishangarh.
|   Jahangir, not Akbar, marks the album/individual-portrait shift.
|   Sanchi is not a mural-site answer.
|
+-- MAINS CONCLUSION
    Indian painting shows continuity through adaptation of support,
    technique, patronage and audience - from rock shelter to mural,
    manuscript, album, market sheet and living ritual wall - not through
    one unchanged style.
```
