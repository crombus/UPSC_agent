                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
A craft tradition is an ecology, not merely a decorated object:
raw material
-> specialised technique
-> embodied skill
-> producing community
-> patron / market
-> regional name
-> transmission
-> legal recognition
-> conservation.
Continuity never means stasis. Court, temple, guild, merchant, colonial,
tourist, export and digital markets change motif, scale, labour and meaning.
A Geographical Indication protects an origin-linked product name; it does not
by itself guarantee authenticity, fair income, raw-material access, ecological
sustainability or intergenerational skill transmission.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Indian crafts survive not because they are frozen survivals but because
communities continually adapt material, motif, product and market while
retaining specialised knowledge. Legal recognition can protect a regional
name, and public awards can recognise skill, but neither substitutes for
producer power, ecological raw materials, fair remuneration and transmission.
Safeguarding must therefore protect the whole craft ecology while allowing
community-led innovation, and every identification claim must still pass the
region-community-technique-product test.
