                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Indian performance is a composite archive of text, music, movement, image,
ritual and community memory. Classical Sanskrit theatre formalised
dramaturgical modes, characters, language registers and stage conventions;
regional folk and modern theatres repeatedly adapted performance to devotional,
commercial, nationalist and social purposes. Puppetry preserved epic,
philosophical and local narratives through four distinct control mechanisms,
while martial forms moved between combat training, ritual and staged display.
Their histories require a crucial distinction: Sanskrit theatre underwent a
long historical contraction yet survives through Kutiyattam, whereas puppetry
faces a present, documented crisis of audience and funding requiring active
livelihood and transmission support.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Indian performance traditions survive by changing medium, patron and audience.
Sanskrit dramaturgy supplied a precise language of representation, but its
courtly dominance contracted as vernacular, devotional and popular forms
expanded. Modern theatre turned the colonial proscenium into commercial,
nationalist and political space. Puppetry remained a multi-art archive of epic
and community memory, yet today requires active support for performers and
audiences rather than object preservation alone. The strongest historical
account therefore distinguishes prescription from practice, contraction from
extinction and living safeguarding from retrospective documentation.
