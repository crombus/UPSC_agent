                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Heritage safeguarding is a threat-to-instrument matching problem. Monument
fabric needs conservation law and technical capacity; a living practice needs
community transmission; a manuscript needs custody, cataloguing and material
care; an origin-linked craft name needs intellectual-property protection.
World Heritage, Intangible Cultural Heritage, Memory of the World and
Geographical Indications are therefore non-interchangeable systems. Recognition
begins an obligation; it does not transfer sovereignty, guarantee funding,
prevent damage, sustain practitioners or make status permanent. Every changing
count, inscription, scheme and institutional mandate must carry a date and an
authoritative source.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
India possesses an extensive constitutional, statutory, institutional and
international architecture for heritage, yet loss often occurs because the
wrong instrument is applied, capacity is thin, or communities are treated as
spectators. UNESCO inscription, GI registration and national protection are
valuable only when converted into preventive maintenance, viable transmission,
documentary custody, accountable tourism and local participation. Safeguarding
must therefore be category-specific, evidence-led and continuously monitored,
with every live status claim dated and every intervention judged by
authenticity, integrity, reversibility and community rights.
