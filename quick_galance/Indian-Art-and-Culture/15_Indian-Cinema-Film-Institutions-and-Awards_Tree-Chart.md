                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Cinema is simultaneously a composite art, reproducible mass medium, cultural
industry, regulatory object, social representation and fragile archive. Camera,
lighting, framing, mise-en-scene, acting, sound, music and editing create
meaning; finance, distribution, language markets, certification and platforms
shape who sees it. A certificate, festival selection, nomination, award and
archival designation are different statuses. International recognition records
the decision of one institution, category and jury in one year; it neither
proves universal artistic superiority nor substitutes for India's multilingual
publics and preservation responsibilities.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Indian cinema's cultural importance lies not in a single language industry,
box-office total or international prize, but in the way an imported technology
was rapidly indigenised into many regional narrative, musical and visual
traditions. Its reproducibility gives it unusual reach and therefore unusual
regulation, while the material fragility of film makes preservation urgent.
A mature policy must classify audiences without reducing certification to
arbitrary suppression, protect specialist archival functions amid institutional
consolidation, and judge global recognition precisely by institution, category
and year while keeping India's multilingual cultural field at the centre.
