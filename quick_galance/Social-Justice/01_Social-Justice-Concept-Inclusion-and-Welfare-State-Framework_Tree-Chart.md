                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Social justice converts formal equality into substantive freedom by combining
fair distribution, remedy for harm, recognition of dignity and representation
in decision-making. The welfare state supplies institutions and resources; the
capability approach asks whether people can actually be healthy, educated,
secure and agentic. Growth, legislation or scheme expenditure are enabling
inputs, not proof of justice unless entitlement survives identification,
access, quality, grievance and outcome gaps.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Social justice is realised when equal citizenship is supported by the material
conditions and social recognition needed to exercise freedom. The welfare state
must guarantee universal floors, correct historically structured disadvantage
and make remedies accessible without reducing citizens to beneficiaries.
Growth remains necessary, but it becomes human development only when public
institutions convert resources into health, education, security, dignity and
voice across intersecting forms of exclusion.
