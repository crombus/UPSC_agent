                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Education becomes human-resource development only when legal access converts
into attendance, foundational learning, progression, higher-order capability,
skills and dignified work. Article 21A and the RTE Act establish a 6-14
entitlement; NEP 2020 supplies a wider policy architecture; Samagra Shiksha and
NIPUN Bharat support delivery; NCVET and apprenticeship connect learning to
work. Enrolment, infrastructure and certificates are outputs, not proof of
learning, employability or equal agency.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
India has constitutionalised elementary access and built a broad policy and
scheme architecture, but education produces human development only when every
learner acquires foundational competence, progresses without exclusion and can
translate learning into agency and productive work. The reform priority is to
align teachers, language, assessment, digital access, vocational pathways and
employer participation around learning outcomes rather than institutional
enrolment or certificate counts.
