                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Children are rights-holders whose evolving capacities require protection,
provision, participation and rehabilitation. India's architecture combines
constitutional guarantees, POCSO, juvenile justice, child-labour and
child-marriage law, welfare through Mission Vatsalya and oversight by NCPCR.
Digital platforms create new exposure to grooming, sexual-abuse material,
cyberbullying, profiling and addictive design; protection must combine criminal
law, intermediary responsibility, child-friendly reporting, privacy, literacy
and accessible psychosocial support.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Child protection requires more than criminalisation after harm. It must combine
safe families and institutions, accessible reporting, specialised justice,
education and social protection with digital design that prevents exploitation
and preserves privacy. NCPCR can coordinate and monitor this system, but
effective protection ultimately depends on trained local institutions, timely
rehabilitation, platform accountability and the child's own informed voice.
