                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Social-sector effectiveness requires a complete targeting-delivery-outcome-
correction cycle. A database identifies potential eligibility; administrative
machinery delivers; horizontal and vertical convergence meet multidimensional
household needs; surveys and evaluations test capability outcomes; grievances
and policy review correct error. Coverage, registration and expenditure are
outputs, not proof of justice. Data integration must improve portability and
coordination without creating unchecked profiling or automated exclusion.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Welfare schemes are necessarily selective where they address distinct
disadvantage, but legitimate differentiation requires accurate, reviewable and
non-stigmatising criteria. No database can replace current need or human
correction, and no collection of schemes becomes convergence by proximity.
Social-sector governance succeeds when portable entitlements, coordinated
frontline institutions and triangulated outcome evidence continuously correct
both wrongful inclusion and rights-denying exclusion.
