                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Ageing policy must protect dignity, autonomy, income, health, care,
participation and protection from abuse across the life cycle. India's 2007
Act creates maintenance duties, summary Tribunals and a conditional
property-revocation remedy; AVYAY, RVY, Elderline and SAGE add welfare and
innovation. Family support remains important but cannot substitute for public
social protection, geriatric health systems, accessible communities and
accountable long-term care.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Elderly welfare cannot rest exclusively on family morality or a summary
maintenance proceeding. The 2007 Act provides an important legal floor, while
AVYAY and health systems address care and participation. An ageing society
requires adequate portable income, accessible primary and long-term care,
caregiver support, age-friendly local environments and protection from abuse,
with older persons retaining autonomy and voice rather than being treated only
as dependants.
