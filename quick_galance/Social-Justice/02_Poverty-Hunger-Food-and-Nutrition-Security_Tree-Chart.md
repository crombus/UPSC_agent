                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Poverty is a multidimensional deprivation of income, assets, services, voice
and resilience. Hunger is inadequate food access; nutrition insecurity also
reflects dietary quality, care, disease, water and sanitation. Poverty and
malnutrition reinforce each other through impaired health, learning,
productivity and intergenerational capability. Breaking the cycle requires
statutory food entitlement, maternal-child nutrition, school meals, portability,
women's agency, WASH, livelihoods and reliable grievance correction.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
India's food-entitlement architecture protects millions from acute deprivation,
but grain access alone cannot eliminate malnutrition or intergenerational
poverty. Nutrition security requires maternal and child care, diverse diets,
school meals, health services, safe water, sanitation, women's agency and
livelihood assets. The governing test is not tonnes distributed or transactions
recorded, but whether every household can convert an enforceable food right into
healthy growth and resilient capability.
