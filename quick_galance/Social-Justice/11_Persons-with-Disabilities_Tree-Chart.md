                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Disability is produced by the interaction between impairment and physical,
digital, institutional and attitudinal barriers. A rights-based model replaces
charity and segregation with equality, autonomy, accessibility, reasonable
accommodation, community living and participation. The RPwD Act 2016 broadens
recognition and creates duties, but rights become capabilities only when built
environments, education, work, information, transport and grievance systems are
accessible in practice.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Disability justice requires the State and society to remove barriers rather
than asking individuals to overcome exclusion through extraordinary effort.
The RPwD Act provides a rights framework, but accessibility and reasonable
accommodation must become routine requirements in budgets, procurement,
education, employment and digital services. Sensitisation matters when it
changes institutional behaviour and is backed by standards, resources,
participation and enforceable remedies.
