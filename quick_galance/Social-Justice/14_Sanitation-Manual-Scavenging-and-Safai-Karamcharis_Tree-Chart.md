                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Eradicating manual scavenging requires four linked levers: prohibition of
degrading labour, complete mechanisation of hazardous sanitation work,
rehabilitation into durable livelihoods, and prompt compensation and
accountability after death or injury. The caste-labour nexus makes this a
dignity, equality and forced-labour issue rather than merely an engineering
problem. Outsourcing cannot erase the responsibility of local authorities,
principal employers and the State.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Manual scavenging persists because infrastructure failure, contractual
fragmentation, caste hierarchy and livelihood dependence reinforce one another.
The 2013 Act must be enforced together with universal mechanisation, worker-led
identification, durable rehabilitation and the Supreme Court's compensation
directions. Sanitation becomes just only when no worker's life or dignity is
treated as an expendable substitute for safe engineering and accountable public
management.
