                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Urban poverty is a housing-livelihood-portability nexus. A worker may find
employment in a large city yet remain excluded from secure tenure, shelter,
food, health, childcare and labour protection. Homeless persons, slum residents,
street vendors and circular migrants need different but connected responses:
place-based housing and services, livelihood rights, and person-based portable
entitlements. Smart urban development is just only when benefits reach informal
settlements and peripheral workers rather than showcase zones alone.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Urban inclusion requires both person-based portability and place-based public
investment. A migrant may carry food or worker registration across States yet
still lack safe shelter, affordable rent, childcare and a protected livelihood
at destination. Cities should therefore combine in-situ upgrading, rental
housing, quality shelters, vendor rights, portable entitlements and
distribution-sensitive planning, with ULBs given the authority and resources
to serve populations who may not appear in conventional resident records.
