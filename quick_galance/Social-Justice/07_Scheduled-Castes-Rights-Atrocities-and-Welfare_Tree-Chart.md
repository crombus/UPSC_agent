                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Scheduled Caste justice requires simultaneous abolition of caste status,
prevention and punishment of atrocities, political and service representation,
access to assets, education and livelihoods, and restoration of dignity.
Protective criminal law and welfare schemes solve different problems. The 2024
Davinder Singh judgment permits evidence-based sub-classification for fair
distribution within Scheduled Castes but does not alter the Presidential List,
create a sub-quota or establish a universal creamy-layer rule.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Scheduled Caste justice requires a credible guarantee against violence and
untouchability alongside the assets and capabilities needed to exit inherited
occupational and social dependence. The PoA framework must deliver timely
investigation, witness safety and relief, while welfare programmes create
education, livelihood and housing mobility. Sub-classification may improve
distribution when evidence-based, but it cannot substitute for list integrity,
anti-discrimination enforcement or solidarity against caste hierarchy.
