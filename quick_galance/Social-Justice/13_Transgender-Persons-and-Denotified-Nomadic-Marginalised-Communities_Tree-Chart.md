                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Transgender persons and Denotified, Nomadic and Semi-Nomadic communities face
different histories but a shared recognitive injustice: identity is denied,
stigmatised or administratively invisible, which then blocks education, housing,
health, work, documents and voice. Legal recognition must therefore connect to
anti-discrimination, representation and material capability. Certification or
scheme enrolment alone cannot overcome policing stigma, family exclusion,
documentation gaps or market discrimination.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Recognition is the first public act of justice for communities historically
misnamed, criminalised or administratively erased. NALSA and the 2019 Act
provide a transgender-rights foundation, while DWBDNC and SEED begin addressing
DNT invisibility. Durable inclusion, however, requires portable documents,
enforceable anti-discrimination, safe housing, health, education, livelihoods,
representation and policing reform so that identity recognition produces
material freedom.
