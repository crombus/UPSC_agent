                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Tribal justice requires protection of land, forest, habitat, culture and
self-government while securing health, education, connectivity and livelihoods.
Displacement and rehabilitation are central axes, but development is incomplete
without sustainable market access and cultural autonomy. FRA and PESA recognise
community authority; PM-JANMAN targets PVTG service gaps; Van Dhan and EMRS build
livelihood and education capability. Cash compensation cannot replace commons,
identity or consent.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Tribal development cannot be reduced to compensating households after land is
taken. Justice begins with recognition of individual, community and habitat
rights, meaningful local authority and avoidance of unnecessary displacement.
Where change is unavoidable, rehabilitation must restore livelihoods, commons,
culture and political agency. PM-JANMAN, Van Dhan and EMRS can expand capability
only when they strengthen rather than replace forest rights and self-government.
