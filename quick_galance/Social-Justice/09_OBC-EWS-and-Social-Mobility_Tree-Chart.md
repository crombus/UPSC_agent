                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
OBC reservation addresses social and educational backwardness and inadequate
representation; EWS reservation addresses economic disadvantage among citizens
outside the SC, ST and OBC reservation categories. They rest on different
constitutional clauses, identification methods and internal-equity rules.
Reservation opens institutional entry, but durable social mobility also requires
quality education, retention, promotion, assets, credit, employment and freedom
from discrimination.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
OBC and EWS measures pursue substantive equality through different
constitutional diagnoses and must not be merged into a single poverty test.
Creamy-layer and sub-categorisation tools can improve internal distribution
only when grounded in lawful, transparent data. The long-term measure of success
is not the number of reserved entries but whether beneficiaries retain
education and employment gains, acquire assets and transmit greater freedom to
the next generation.
