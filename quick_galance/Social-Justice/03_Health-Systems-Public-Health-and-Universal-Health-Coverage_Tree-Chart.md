                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Universal Health Coverage means that every person can obtain needed promotive,
preventive, curative, rehabilitative and palliative services of sufficient
quality without financial hardship. India combines a State-delivered public
health system, NHM support, comprehensive primary care through Ayushman Arogya
Mandirs and hospital protection through PM-JAY. Insurance cannot substitute for
primary care, public-health capacity, affordable medicines, regulation and a
functional referral system.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Universal health coverage is achieved when people receive appropriate care
through a trusted primary and referral system without financial hardship.
India's insurance expansion is important, but lasting equity requires stronger
public primary care, medicines, diagnostics, workforce, public-health capacity
and private-sector regulation. The State may purchase from plural providers,
yet it cannot become a residual provider or surrender responsibility for
quality, prevention and universal access.
