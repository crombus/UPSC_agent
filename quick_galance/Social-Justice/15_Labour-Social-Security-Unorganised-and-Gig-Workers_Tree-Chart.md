                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Social security pools life-cycle risks that an individual worker cannot
reliably bear through wages alone. The Code on Social Security 2020 recognises
unorganised, gig and platform workers and provides enabling financing and scheme
architecture, but legal recognition is not a delivered benefit. Effective
protection requires worker identification, portable registration, notified
schemes, actual contributions, accessible claims, grievance redress and
benefits adequate for sickness, accident, maternity, disability and old age.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The Code's recognition of gig and platform work is a major legal step, but
social protection begins only when a worker can enrol, contributions are
collected, risks are covered and claims are paid across jobs and States.
Registration databases and statutory ranges are enabling architecture, not
benefits. India needs a portable minimum floor, occupation-specific institutions,
platform accountability and simple grievance systems that make flexibility
compatible with security and dignity.
