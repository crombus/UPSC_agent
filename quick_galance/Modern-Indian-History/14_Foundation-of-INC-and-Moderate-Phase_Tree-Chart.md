                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The Congress organised an already developing Indian political nation.
Its Moderate leaders used constitutional agitation to educate public
opinion, expose colonial economic exploitation and create an all-India
platform. They won little executive power, but their organisational,
economic and ideological work supplied the grammar and institutions
from which militant and mass nationalism later grew.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The Moderates did not secure responsible government, but they changed
the terms on which British rule could be defended. By connecting poverty
to colonial policy, training a cross-regional leadership and making
rights, budgets and representation national questions, they built the
intellectual and organisational base that later movements transformed
from constitutional pressure into boycott and mass mobilisation.
