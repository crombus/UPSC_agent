                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The Janata victory proved that opposition unity could defeat Congress and
restore constitutional safeguards, but its collapse showed that negative
anti-Congress unity could not replace a shared programme or organisational
culture. Indira returned largely through Janata's failure, even as state
politics became more autonomous. Regional parties deepened federal democracy;
crises escalated where legitimate demands met delayed accommodation, central
interference, competitive outbidding and ultimately coercive security policy.

coherently. Indira's return did not restore the old Congress system: autonomous
regional coalitions increasingly defined state politics. Electoral assertions
in Tamil Nadu, Andhra and West Bengal strengthened federal democracy, while
delayed accommodation in Assam, Kashmir and Punjab corroded trust. The Punjab
tragedy showed the cost of allowing a negotiable federal question to become a
militarised security crisis.
