                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The uprising began as a Bengal Army mutiny but became a broad civil
rebellion in major parts of north and central India where sepoy,
taluqdar, peasant, artisan and dispossessed-dynasty grievances converged.
It was anti-colonial and often inter-communal, yet regionally uneven,
restoration-oriented and without a modern national programme or unified
command. Its defeat transformed the colonial state.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
1857 was a military mutiny that became a major anti-colonial civil
rebellion wherever sepoy networks met recent annexation, agrarian
dispossession and recoverable political leadership. Its fragmented
command and restorationist horizons helped the British defeat it, but
its social breadth forced the end of Company rule, reorganisation of
the army and a new Crown alliance with princes and landed magnates.
