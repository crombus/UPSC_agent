                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
World War I widened the gap between Indian loyalty and imperial reward.
The Home Rule Leagues turned annual elite politics into continuous
branch-based agitation, repaired the Moderate-Extremist split and made
self-government a normal demand. Lucknow produced double unity but
accepted communal representation; Montagu promised gradual responsible
government, while the 1919 scheme later conceded portfolios without
effective power.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Home Rule supplied the continuous organisation and public demand that
Congress had lacked after Surat, while Lucknow restored unity across
two political divides. Yet the Pact embedded communal representation,
and the Montagu promise translated pressure into a deliberately limited
dyarchy. The phase therefore bridged elite constitutionalism and mass
nationalism while carrying forward unresolved questions of power and
representation.
