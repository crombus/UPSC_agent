                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Swadeshi converted nationalist politics from elite petition into
boycott, self-reliance, national education, cultural mobilisation and
passive resistance. It grew from Moderate frustration and Curzonian
provocation, widened participation beyond annual Congress sessions and
created the repertoire of later mass politics; yet repression, Congress
division, uneven peasant-Muslim reach and fragile enterprise limited
its immediate durability.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Swadeshi's immediate organisation weakened after repression and Surat,
but its historical achievement outlived that retreat. It turned
consumption, education, culture and non-cooperation into political acts,
forced the reversal of Bengal's partition and demonstrated both the
power and limits of mobilisation without a durable inclusive mass
organisation. Gandhi later nationalised a repertoire first tested here.
