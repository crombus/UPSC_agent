                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Independent India rejected both museum-like isolation and forced assimilation
of tribal communities. Its stated model was integration with a preserved
identity: development had to occur, but along the tribes' own genius. The
constitutional design varied with demography, using autonomy where tribal
communities were territorially concentrated and protective regulation where
they were dispersed. Political accommodation and statehood often worked;
material delivery remained weak because land alienation, forest restriction,
displacement, administrative neglect and elite capture persisted.
