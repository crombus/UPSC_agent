                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Congress dominance was not one-party dictatorship. It rested on freedom-
movement legitimacy, nationwide organisation, a broad social coalition,
internal factions, policy co-option and first-past-the-post arithmetic. Much
opposition therefore operated inside Congress, while external parties acted as
pressure groups and remained divided across socialist, communist, cultural-
nationalist, market-liberal and regional lines. The system endured while
Congress could arbitrate factions and absorb demands; its erosion made the
1967 state-level breakthrough possible.

                 QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The Congress system combined authentic democratic competition with profoundly
unequal organisational power. Congress's national legitimacy, internal
pluralism and co-option made it a coalition before elections began, while
first-past-the-post punished a fragmented opposition. The opposition was not
irrelevant: it governed Kerala, shaped policy and developed the coalition logic
of non-Congressism. By 1967 declining vote strength, factional autonomy and
opposition coordination converted long-term pressure into a state-level break.
