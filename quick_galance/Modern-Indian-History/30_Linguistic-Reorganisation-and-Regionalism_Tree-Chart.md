                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
India strengthened unity by accommodating linguistic identity within the
federal Union rather than treating every regional demand as secession. The
leadership's post-Partition delay concerned timing, not rejection of a
principle Congress had accepted in 1921. Andhra forced the first concession;
the States Reorganisation Commission generalised it; Bombay, Punjab and the
official-language crisis showed its limits. Accommodation deepened democracy,
while nativist exclusion and minority-border disputes remained real costs.

The leadership was justified in fearing identity-based territorial politics
after Partition, but experience showed that refusing linguistic claims was more
