                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The war exposed the contradiction between Britain's claim to defend democracy
and its refusal to grant Indians control over their own government. Deferred
offers, minority vetoes and the failure of Cripps exhausted constitutional
patience. Quit India demanded immediate withdrawal; pre-emptive arrests made
the movement leaderless but not purposeless, producing local insurgency,
underground organisation and parallel authority. It was suppressed militarily
yet made durable colonial rule appear dependent on permanent coercion.
