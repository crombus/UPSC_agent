                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The 1935 Act enlarged representative government while retaining imperial
sovereignty through an unrealised federation, central reserved subjects,
governor safeguards, residuary authority and emergency powers. Congress used
the operative provincial half as a new arena of struggle: its ministries
proved an Indian capacity to govern and delivered limited reforms, but also
revealed fiscal, social and constitutional constraints. Their wartime
resignation widened Congress-League conflict on the road to 1940.

                 QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The Act's provincial autonomy was a genuine expansion of elected government,
and the Congress ministries used it to widen liberties, deliver partial reform
and gain administrative experience. Yet the federation never operated, the
Governor-General retained the central security core and residuary authority,
and governors could restrain or suspend provincial responsibility. The 1939
resignations therefore exposed the decisive truth: Indian ministers could
administer provinces, but they could not decide whether India went to war.
