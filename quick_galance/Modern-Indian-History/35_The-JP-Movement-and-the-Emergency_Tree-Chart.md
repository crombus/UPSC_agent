                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The Emergency resulted from the convergence of economic distress, a political
movement that moved beyond normal electoral opposition, a judicial threat to
the Prime Minister personally, and a ruling party already weakened by
centralisation. The regime used constitutional form to impose authoritarian
practice through detention, censorship, amendment and extra-constitutional
power. Indian institutions largely failed to prevent that turn, but the
electorate reversed it in 1977, demonstrating the depth of democratic norms.
