                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Non-Cooperation was India's first sustained all-India mass movement.
It fused Punjab outrage, post-war distress, disappointment with reforms
and the Khilafat grievance into a graded withdrawal of consent from the
colonial state. Nagpur gave Congress a mass organisation; local groups
translated the programme through their own agrarian and social demands.
Chauri Chaura exposed the gap between mobilisation and non-violent
discipline, making withdrawal principled within Gandhism but politically
costly.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Non-Cooperation transformed nationalism by turning everyday cooperation
with schools, courts, cloth, titles and councils into a political
choice, while Nagpur supplied the organisation to sustain that choice.
Its local translations revealed genuine popular agency and weak central
discipline. Withdrawal after Chauri Chaura preserved Gandhi's moral
method but sacrificed momentum; the mass Congress and constructive
institutions it left behind made future civil disobedience possible.
