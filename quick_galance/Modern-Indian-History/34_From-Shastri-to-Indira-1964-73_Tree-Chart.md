                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Between 1964 and 1973 Indian democracy survived two unplanned successions, war,
economic crisis and the collapse of Congress dominance in many states. Indira
Gandhi answered the weakened Congress system by bypassing party bosses through
left-populist measures and a direct national mandate. Bank nationalisation,
privy-purse abolition and Garibi Hatao had substantive policy content and
served as instruments of intra-party power. The resulting centralisation
restored electoral dominance while weakening organisational and institutional
checks, preparing the crisis of 1973-75.
