# The British Conquest of Bengal — Quick Glance

## Central thesis

```text
LICENSED TRADER -> POLITICAL KINGMAKER -> MILITARY SUPREMACY
-> LEGAL DIWAN -> DUAL GOVERNMENT -> DIRECT ADMINISTRATION
```

Bengal was conquered through a sequence, not one battle:
Plassey changed the ruler; Buxar established wider military predominance;
the two Allahabad treaties converted victory into legal-fiscal and strategic
arrangements; Dual Government operationalised Company power until 1772.

## Chronology rail

| Date | Event and significance |
|---|---|
| 1717 | Farrukhsiyar's farman protects the Company's registered trade; private dastak abuse remains unauthorised. |
| 20 June 1756 | Fort William falls during the sovereignty and fortification crisis. |
| 23 June 1757 | Plassey installs Mir Jafar and makes the Company a political kingmaker. |
| 1760-63 | Mir Qasim shifts to Munger, reforms administration and army, and pursues fiscal parity. |
| 22 October 1764 | Buxar defeats Mir Qasim, Shuja-ud-Daula and Shah Alam II. |
| 12 August 1765 | Shah Alam II grants the Diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa. |
| 16 August 1765 | Shuja-ud-Daula receives restored Awadh under indemnity and alliance terms. |
| 1765-72 | Dual Government separates effective revenue power from visible responsibility. |
| 1770 | Famine combines ecological shock with administrative and revenue vulnerability. |
| 1772 | Warren Hastings ends the Dual Government. |

## Mechanism map

| Stage | Mechanism | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Plassey | Conspiracy, finance and inactive commanders produce regime change. | No legal revenue title yet. |
| Client regime | Payments, concessions, 24 Parganas, Clive's jagir | Mir Jafar fiscally exhausted |
| Mir Qasim | State recovery from Munger challenges unequal commercial privilege. | War widens beyond Bengal. |
| Buxar | Unified Company command defeats a segmented coalition. | Victory still needs legal settlement. |
| Allahabad | Mughal grant supplies Diwani; Awadh becomes a buffer. | Two treaties, dated 12 and 16 August. |
| Dual Government | Company revenue via Indian deputies; Nawab nominally responsible | Power and accountability split |

## Named actors

- Siraj-ud-Daulah: defended Nawabi authority against unauthorised fortification.
- Jagat Seth: credit power and regime-change interest.
- Mir Jafar: client Nawab after Plassey.
- Mir Qasim: recovery programme built around Munger, army reform and fiscal parity.
- Hector Munro: unified Company command at Buxar.
- Shah Alam II: source of the Diwani grant on 12 August 1765.
- Shuja-ud-Daula: Awadh restored by the separate treaty of 16 August 1765.
- Muhammad Reza Khan and Raja Shitab Rai: revenue deputies under the Dual Government.

## Prelims traps

- Farman privilege is not private dastak abuse.
- Plassey was political entry; Buxar was open military supremacy.
- Alivardi's Giria battle of 1740 and Mir Qasim's Giria defeat of 1763 are different events.
- Diwani means revenue and civil authority; Nizamat retained nominal criminal and military functions.
- The two Allahabad treaties were four days apart, not one combined same-day document.
- Dual Government did not mean equal joint rule.
- The 1770 famine was multicausal; avoid both policy-only and drought-only explanations.

## Mains answer spine

Define conquest as a process -> trace privilege conflict and Calcutta crisis
-> explain Plassey through conspiracy and finance -> show Mir Qasim's recovery
attempt -> compare Buxar's command structures -> distinguish the two
Allahabad treaties -> analyse Dual Government's accountability paradox
-> qualify collaboration, resistance and famine causation
-> conclude with the 1772 transition.

## PYQ application

No direct question is claimed for this chapter. The 2022 GS-I questions on
Company military success and colonial famines are bounded applications:
Buxar illustrates command integration, while Bengal in 1770 illustrates
ecological shock interacting with extractive governance.
