                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
British constitutional development in India began as an attempt to
control a constitutional anomaly: a profit-seeking chartered company
had become a territorial sovereign. From 1773 to 1858 Parliament and
the Crown centralised executive, legislative, judicial and bureaucratic
authority, but this was imperial accountability and efficient extraction,
not Indian representation or democratic self-government.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The constitutional acts from 1773 to 1858 did not democratise India;
they nationalised and rationalised British control over a corporate
empire. Parliament imposed supervision, the Governor-General acquired
all-India authority, law and recruitment became more standardised, and
the Crown inherited this machinery in 1858. Administrative order grew,
but Indian consent and representation remained outside its design.
