# Modern Indian History 07 - Economic Impact of British Rule

## Quick-Glance Revision Chart

### Central thesis

**Colonial rule modernised circulation without creating autonomous development:**
goods, information and people moved faster, while tariff policy, surplus
direction, investment priorities and risk allocation remained externally
controlled.

```text
CONQUEST PLUNDER
  -> INSTITUTIONALISED DRAIN
  -> LAND / MARKET / CRAFT RESTRUCTURING
  -> INFRASTRUCTURE + DEPENDENT INDUSTRY
  -> FAMINE VULNERABILITY
  -> STRUCTURAL UNDERDEVELOPMENT
```

### Core mechanisms

- **Commercialisation:** cash demand -> advance/credit -> cash crop
  -> price/harvest shock -> debt and land alienation.
- **Deindustrialisation:** machine production + tariff asymmetry + lost court
  patronage + railway penetration; uneven decline, not total extinction.
- **Drain:** home charges, remittances, interest/profit, stores and imperial
  expenditure; name channels, never invent a total.
- **Council Bills:** export surplus serviced sterling obligations; it did not
  automatically return as imports or investment.
- **Infrastructure:** imperial purpose and guaranteed British returns, with genuine unintended integration.
- **Forests:** 1864 Department -> 1865 Act -> 1878 reserved/protected/unclassified categories.
- **Famines:** climatic trigger plus revenue, commercialisation, price-directed
  transport and restrictive relief amplifiers.

### Named evidence

- Naoroji — *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India*.
- R.C. Dutt — *The Economic History of India*.
- Indigo: planters and refusal to sow; Pabna: zamindars and legal resistance;
  Deccan: moneylenders and destruction of debt bonds.
- Guarantee System: minimum return to British capital, shortfall met from Indian revenues.
- Strachey Commission 1880 followed the 1876-78 famine; severe famine recurred in 1896-1900.

### Standing traps

- Permanent Settlement fixed the state's demand on the zamindar, not the ryot's rent.
- Ryotwari was not automatically pro-peasant.
- Drain is not deindustrialisation.
- Deindustrialisation is not total disappearance of industry.
- Climate triggered famine; colonial institutions amplified it.
- Do not assert a drain total, famine mortality total, craft-employment share or exact Hilton-Young adopted rate.
