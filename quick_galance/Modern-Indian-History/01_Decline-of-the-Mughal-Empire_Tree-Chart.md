DECLINE / DECENTRALISATION / REGIONALISATION / DISINTEGRATION / CONTINUITY
DECLINE: reduced ability to appoint, tax, command, transfer, protect.

REGIONALISATION: provincial office becomes durable regional state structure.
DISINTEGRATION: loss of one enforceable imperial system.

CHRONOLOGY RAIL (core window)
1707: Aurangzeb dies at Ahmadnagar; Muazzam, Azam, Kam Bakhsh contest
  succession.
1707-1712: Muazzam rules as Bahadur Shah I — conciliatory policy toward
  Rajputs, Marathas (sardeshmukhi conceded, chauth/succession unresolved) and
  Sikhs (Banda Bahadur conflict); temporary balance, no structural repair.
1712-1713: Jahandar Shah, with Zulfiqar Khan dominant.
1713-1719: Farrukhsiyar raised by Sayyid Brothers (Abdullah Khan, Husain Ali
  Khan); 1717 farman grants the English Company Bengal trade privileges
  (commercial concession only — not territory, not Plassey's cause);
  1718-19 Maratha settlement negotiations (chauth, sardeshmukhi); 1719
  Farrukhsiyar deposed/killed, rapid enthronements, Muhammad Shah takes the
  throne.
1719-1748: Muhammad Shah's long reign. 1720: Sayyid dominance ends. 1722:
  Saadat Khan founds the Awadh power base. 1724: Nizam-ul-Mulk leaves the
  wazirat for autonomous Hyderabad. 1717 (Bengal)/1722 (Awadh)/1724
  (Hyderabad): provincial office converts into hereditary autonomy in form
  continuous, substance regional.
1738-1739: Nadir Shah invades, defeats the fragmented Mughal command at
  Karnal, sacks Delhi; Peacock Throne and Koh-i-Noor carried off; Muhammad
  Shah retained as emperor, territories west of the Indus ceded.
1740s: regional states and Maratha power reshape the political field.
BOUNDARY: 1748 onward — Ahmad Shah Abdali's repeated invasions, and Panipat
  III (1761), are consequences of the fragmented order and belong to Medieval
  Topic 25 / Modern Topic 02, NOT the 1707-1740s core.

IMPERIAL STRUCTURE INHERITED IN 1707
Emperor (appointment/rank/legitimacy) -> Mansab (status + service obligation)
-> Jagir (transferable revenue assignment, not ownership) -> Khalisa (direct
imperial treasury reserve) -> Subah -> Sarkar -> Pargana -> Village -> Subahdar
(executive) + provincial Diwan (fiscal check) -> Zamindar (local rooted
power).
STRENGTH: large agrarian base, prestige, composite elite, roads/cities,
  administrative vocabulary, recognised coinage/court.
VULNERABILITY: rank depended on assignments; assignments depended on
  collection; collection depended on local cooperation; expansion/war raised
  claimants; succession could redirect the whole network.
=> 1707 inherited a powerful but high-maintenance imperial machine.
                              |
                              v
JAGIRDARI-MANSABDARI MECHANISM (the causal engine — use these terms exactly)
Mansabdars grow in number/rank -> Paibaqi (assignable reserve) shrinks ->
Be-jagiri (shortage/delay of suitable jagir, NOT ordinary corruption) spreads
-> Jama (assessed revenue) exceeds Hasil (realised collection) -> Khalisa
pressure vs alienated service nobility -> frequent transfer + Ijara (revenue
farming) raise short-term extraction -> zamindar/peasant resistance
(Irfan Habib's agrarian-crisis link) -> falling hasil -> weaker troop
maintenance -> weaker imperial discipline -> further regional autonomy.
This is a STRUCTURAL failure of the reward-and-service mechanism, not simple
individual corruption or moral failure of nobles.
                              |
                              v
DECCAN LEGACY, MARATHAS, JAT/SIKH/RAJPUT/BUNDELA — EVIDENCE OF CONTRACTION ONLY
Aurangzeb's Deccan war exhausts treasury/army and exposes communications.
Chauth (~1/4 revenue claim) and Sardeshmukhi (~1/10 additional claim) are
fiscal-political instruments, not religious taxes; Maratha collection inside
Mughal areas creates a parallel claim and lowers imperial hasil; 1718-19
settlement uses imperial sanction for Maratha claims; from 1720 Baji Rao
converts settled claims into offensive reach.
Jats (agrarian-zamindar, Bharatpur), Sikhs (Banda Bahadur phase; later misl
organisation lies beyond this period), Rajputs (dynastic autonomy within
imperial rank) and Bundelas (local military-landed assertion) each convert
weakening imperial enforcement into regional authority through distinct
social bases — do not collapse them into one undifferentiated "rebellion."
Comparative development of these powers is handed to Modern Topic 02; full
1707-1761 synthesis is handed to Medieval Topic 25.
                              |
                              v
BENGAL / AWADH / HYDERABAD AS EVIDENCE (successor-state formula)
Bengal (Murshid Quli Khan), Awadh (Saadat Khan), Hyderabad (Nizam-ul-Mulk):
Mughal office + regional revenue + local military-patronage network =
autonomy in substance, continuity in form. They did not all issue immediate
declarations of independence — coin, khutba, title and imperial sanction
persist even as appointments, army, revenue, succession and diplomacy turn
regional.
                              |
                              v
NADIR SHAH: EXPOSURE AND ACCELERANT, NOT ORIGIN
Frontier weakness + Persian military capacity -> Karnal, 1739: numerical
Mughal resources without unified command lose to disciplined, unified Nadir
Shah -> Delhi sacked; Peacock Throne and Koh-i-Noor removed (do not state
unverified plunder/casualty totals) -> treasury and prestige loss, frontier
concession, invitation to future invasion. Nadir did NOT stay to govern India.
BOUNDED ABDALI BRIDGE: Ahmad Shah Abdali's invasions from 1748 and Panipat III
(1761) destabilise Punjab/Delhi further but sit outside Topic 01's core and
must not be used as the cause of a decline that began in 1707.
                              |
                              v
WEIGHTED MULTI-CAUSAL HIERARCHY
1. Structural: jagirdari-mansabdari mismatch + agrarian/zamindar pressure.
2. Political: succession + faction + weakened appointment/transfer capacity.
3. Imperial overstretch: Deccan war + Maratha parallel extraction.
4. Regional: provincial offices become autonomous states.
5. Military: poor coordination, contingent under-maintenance, north-west
   defence decay.
6. External: Nadir Shah and (bounded) Abdali accelerate and expose.
Personal rule (Aurangzeb's choices, Muhammad Shah's reign) shapes timing but
cannot replace this structural explanation.

PRELIMS TRAPS
- Aurangzeb dies 1707; the DYNASTY does not end in 1707.
- Bahadur Shah I: 1707-1712 (not a full "settlement" reign).
- Sayyid Brothers: king-makers, not emperors.
- Farrukhsiyar 1713-1719; 1717 Company farman is a trade concession only.
- Muhammad Shah: 1719-1748; "Rangila" is a sobriquet, not a causal
  explanation.
- Nizam-ul-Mulk: Hyderabad autonomy dates to 1724.
- Jagir = revenue assignment, never ownership; Paibaqi = assignable reserve;
  Be-jagiri = shortage/delay of suitable jagir; Jama = assessment,
  Hasil = realisation — never interchange these terms.
- Nadir Shah: Karnal/Delhi 1739, plundered and withdrew (did not annex or
  rule India).
- Abdali: invasions begin 1748; Panipat III is 1761 — a later, bounded
  bridge, not part of the causal core.

QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Between 1707 and the 1740s Mughal authority contracted because the
fiscal-military institutions that had tied rank, revenue and service to the
emperor ceased to produce reliable income, disciplined nobles or coordinated
defence. Succession contests and Nadir Shah's 1739 invasion accelerated this
failure, while provincial regimes captured Mughal offices and symbols to
build new states. The period was therefore both imperial decline and
regional transformation — creating an opportunity, but not a predetermined
victory, for the English East India Company.
