                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Communalism is a modern political ideology, not religion or timeless
religious hostility. It converts a faith community into a presumed
community of secular interests, then treats those interests as separate
and finally antagonistic. Colonial classification and electorates,
uneven development, scarce offices, elite competition and political
strategy institutionalised this logic; nationalism challenged it but
also sometimes negotiated within its categories.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Communalism became powerful when political actors translated uneven
social competition into religious representation and colonial
institutions rewarded that translation. The League's path from loyalist
elite body to Pakistan claimant was historical, not predetermined.
Nationalism repeatedly created unity but could not permanently escape
the communal constitutional framework or the regional interests it had
helped make politically rational.
