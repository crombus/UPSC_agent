                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
1858 transferred sovereignty from a corporation to the Crown while
retaining the centralised purposes and much of the machinery of colonial
government. The Raj then rebuilt security through princes, landlords and
a reorganised army, and moved from exclusion to controlled association:
Indians could advise, discuss and train politically, but could not hold
the executive responsible or exercise representative sovereignty.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Post-1858 Britain made Indian rule more directly imperial, socially
conciliatory and administratively consultative without making it
representative. Princes and landlords secured the Raj, councils and
local bodies channelled elite opinion, and the executive retained final
authority. These reforms strengthened Crown rule while unintentionally
training the political class that would demand responsibility rather
than nomination.
