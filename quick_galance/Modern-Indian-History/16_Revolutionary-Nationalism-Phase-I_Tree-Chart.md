                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Early revolutionary nationalism rested on a theory of exemplary action:
a disciplined minority would attack symbols of colonial power, break
fear, inspire sacrifice and prepare a wider rising. Repression after
Swadeshi drove secret organisation; migration and racial exclusion made
the movement transnational; wartime opportunity encouraged mutiny plans.
Its secrecy produced courage and surprise but prevented durable mass
organisation and made informer penetration devastating.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Phase-I revolutionary nationalism made sacrifice and armed defiance
central to anti-colonial memory and turned the Indian cause into an
international problem. Yet its cell structure, weak mass linkage and
susceptibility to intelligence prevented sustained revolt. Its durable
legacy lay in morale, martyrdom and networks, while later Gandhians and
socialist revolutionaries reworked its relationship with the masses.
