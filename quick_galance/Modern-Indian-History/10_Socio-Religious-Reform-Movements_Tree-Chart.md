                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Nineteenth- and early-twentieth-century reform arose from a dialogue
among colonial criticism, modern education, print, Indian scripture,
regional social conflict and the agency of subordinated groups.
Movements attacked selected customs and rebuilt cultural confidence,
but differed sharply over reason, revelation, caste, gender, community
and nationalism. "Reformist" and "revivalist" are analytical tendencies,
not moral rankings or airtight boxes.
