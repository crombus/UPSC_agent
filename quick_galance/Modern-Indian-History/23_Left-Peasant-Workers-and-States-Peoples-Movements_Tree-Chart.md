                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The social movements surrounding the 1930s widened nationalism from a
transfer-of-power struggle into a debate over labour, land, tenancy,
planning, social rights and democratic rule in princely India. Their
organisations often worked with Congress but retained distinct constituencies
and demands. Congress absorbed much of their programme without becoming a
single-class or communist party, creating both wider legitimacy and persistent
limits in implementation.
