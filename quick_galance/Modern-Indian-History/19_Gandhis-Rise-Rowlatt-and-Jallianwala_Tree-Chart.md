                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Gandhi became a national leader by first proving satyagraha in three
bounded, local and materially specific struggles. He joined factual
inquiry, moral self-suffering, disciplined non-cooperation and negotiated
settlement. Rowlatt then tested the method nationally; violence exposed
its organisational limits, while Jallianwala Bagh destroyed residual
faith in British justice and made mass non-cooperation politically viable.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Gandhi's rise rested on a repeatable political technology: investigate
a concrete grievance, build disciplined collective action, accept
suffering and negotiate without surrendering moral initiative.
Champaran, Ahmedabad and Kheda established credibility; Rowlatt exposed
the danger of untrained mass action; Jallianwala converted that lesson
and imperial violence into the moral basis of nationwide non-cooperation.
