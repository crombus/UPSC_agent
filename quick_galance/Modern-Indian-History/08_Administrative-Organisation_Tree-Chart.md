                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Colonial administration was modern in technique and despotic in
purpose. A small, highly paid and racially exclusive civil service,
an Indian sepoy army under European command, a district-centred police
and codified courts made revenue extraction and coercive order durable.
Uniform rules created real institutional continuity, but equality,
representation and public accountability remained sharply limited.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The colonial administrative machine succeeded because its four pillars
reinforced one another: civil servants directed revenue, police enforced
district order, courts converted power into legal procedure and the army
guaranteed the system. Professionalism and codification were real, but
their racial exclusions and coercive purposes made this a legalistic
imperial state rather than government by or for its Indian subjects.
