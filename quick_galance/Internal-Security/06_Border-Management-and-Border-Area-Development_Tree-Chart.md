                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Border management is broader than border defence. It combines guarding, lawful
regulation of movement and trade, development of border communities, infrastructure,
technology, intelligence, inter-agency coordination and bilateral dispute-management.
Different borders require different strategies: a disputed, undemarcated LAC is not
the same problem as a settled but porous and densely populated boundary or an open
border. Fencing, a sensor system, an agreement or a scheme is a capability input,
not proof of a sealed border or durable security outcome.
