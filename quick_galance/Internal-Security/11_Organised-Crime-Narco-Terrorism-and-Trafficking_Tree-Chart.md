                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Organised crime primarily seeks financial or material benefit and may build a
parallel economy while coexisting with the State. Terrorism primarily seeks
political, ideological or religious coercion and alteration of the status quo.
Their ends differ, but they can share routes, finance, corruption, weapons,
technology and protection networks. Narco-terrorism is the intersection where
narcotics revenue or territorial control funds or enables political violence.
Effective policy follows both the route and the money while protecting trafficked
persons as victims rather than treating every migrant or drug user as a syndicate.

Organised crime and terrorism must be distinguished by purpose while their shared
financial, logistical and corrupt networks are attacked together. India's current
NCORD-ANTF-NCB architecture should combine route interdiction with financial and
digital investigation, international cooperation and timely prosecution. A credible
narco-terror response also protects trafficked persons, supports treatment and
rehabilitation, and measures success through dismantled networks and reduced harm,
