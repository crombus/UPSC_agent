                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Cybersecurity protects confidentiality, integrity, availability, authenticity and
resilience of digital systems. A cyber incident is a technical event, a cybercrime
is a penal offence, cyber terrorism carries terror intent, cyber warfare implies
state-linked strategic action, and an information operation targets perception.
These may overlap but require different evidence, institutions and remedies. Critical
Information Infrastructure is consequence-defined and formally protected under law;
sector membership alone does not make every system notified CII.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
India's cyber resilience depends on correctly routing technical incidents, crimes,
CII threats and information operations to the institutions and laws designed for
them. CERT-In and NCIIPC provide complementary incident and critical-infrastructure
functions, while police, I4C and sector operators complete the chain. Because
attribution and supply-chain dependence cannot be eliminated, secure design,
continuous audits, rapid recovery, private-sector accountability and rights-based
oversight must remain as important as detection and deterrence.
