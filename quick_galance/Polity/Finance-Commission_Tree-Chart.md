                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Article 280 creates a periodic expert commission to reconcile India's vertical
fiscal imbalance between Union revenues and State responsibilities and its
horizontal imbalance among States with unequal needs and capacities. The
Commission recommends rather than commands, but Article 281's public report and
action memorandum make its advice politically consequential. Its central task
is distributive judgment: balance equalisation and national cohesion against
tax effort, demographic performance, ecological costs and economic
contribution, while recognising that cesses and discretionary transfers shape
fiscal autonomy outside the headline devolution percentage.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The Finance Commission is India's principal constitutional mechanism for
reconciling unequal fiscal capacity with a common standard of citizenship. Its
periodic and advisory form permits adaptation while Article 281 makes departure
publicly accountable. Yet a devolution percentage cannot secure autonomy if the
shareable pool shrinks or discretionary conditions dominate. A credible fiscal
federation requires transparent tax bases, balanced horizontal criteria, timely
local-finance institutions and predictable space for State priorities.
