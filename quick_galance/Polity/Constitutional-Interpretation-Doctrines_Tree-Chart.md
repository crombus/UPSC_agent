# Polity 55 - Constitutional Interpretation Doctrines

## Quick-Glance Tree Chart

```text
CONSTITUTIONAL INTERPRETATION DOCTRINES
|
+-- 1. FOUNDATION: WHY DOCTRINES EXIST
|   |-- Constitution is supreme; public power must trace to constitutional authority.
|   |-- Article 13: law inconsistent with Fundamental Rights is void to the extent.
|   |-- Article 32: guaranteed Supreme Court remedy for enforcement of Fundamental Rights.
|   |-- Article 226: High Court writs for Fundamental Rights and any other purpose.
|   |-- Article 141: law declared by Supreme Court binds all courts in India.
|   '-- Doctrine = judicial test operating through text; it is not a separate Article.
|
+-- 2. INTERPRETIVE METHOD
|   |-- Text -> words, definitions, provisos, Part and Schedule in context.
|   |-- Structure -> institutions, federal fields, rights and checks.
|   |-- Purpose -> make guarantee effective; Maneka Gandhi (1978).
|   |-- Precedent -> controlling ratio and bench strength.
|   |-- History/original materials -> Constituent Assembly context, persuasive not enacted.
|   |-- Living Constitution -> enduring text applied to new conditions; Puttaswamy (2017).
|   '-- LIMIT -> no text-free purpose, historical freezing or personal judicial policy.
|
+-- 3. ARTICLE 13 VALIDITY
|   |-- SEVERABILITY
|   |   |-- Trigger: part of law contravenes a Fundamental Right.
|   |   |-- Test: separation + legislative intent + workable remainder + scheme unity.
|   |   |-- Holding: R.M.D. Chamarbaugwala v Union of India (1957).
|   |   |-- Effect: invalid part falls; valid independent remainder survives.
|   |   '-- Limit: deletion cannot redesign the legislative scheme.
|   |-- ECLIPSE
|   |   |-- Trigger: classically, pre-Constitution law inconsistent under Article 13(1).
|   |   |-- Test: right, protected claimant and exact inconsistency.
|   |   |-- Holding: Bhikaji Narain Dhakras (1955).
|   |   |-- Effect: dormant/unenforceable to extent; possible revival if impediment removed.
|   |   '-- Limit: not repeal; post-Constitution and citizen/non-citizen contexts need
|   |       right-specific precedent, including Ambica Mills (1974).
|   '-- NON-WAIVER
|       |-- Trigger: person consents to unconstitutional State action.
|       |-- Holding: Basheshar Nath v CIT (1958).
|       |-- Effect: consent generally cannot validate Part III violation.
|       '-- Limit: personal, statutory and procedural rights are not all non-waivable.
|
+-- 4. FEDERAL COMPETENCE: ARTICLES 245-246 + SEVENTH SCHEDULE
|   |-- Pith and substance -> true nature: purpose, scope and effects.
|   |   |-- Prafulla Kumar Mukherjee (PC, 1947); F.N. Balsara (1951).
|   |   |-- Effect: dominant valid field controls; incidental encroachment tolerated.
|   |   '-- Limit: dominant trespass cannot be called incidental.
|   |-- Incidental/ancillary power -> reasonably necessary support for valid entry.
|   |   '-- Limit: cannot create independent forbidden competence.
|   |-- Colourable legislation -> K.C. Gajapati (1953).
|   |   |-- Test: does form disguise substance outside power?
|   |   '-- Limit: competence, not political motive; fraud on Constitution != mala fides.
|   '-- Territorial nexus -> Article 245; R.M.D. Chamarbaugwala (1957).
|       |-- Test: real, sufficient and liability-relevant State connection.
|       '-- Limit: remote/illusory nexus fails; Article 245(2) specifically protects Parliament.
|
+-- 5. REPUGNANCY: ARTICLE 254
|   |-- Preconditions: valid Union + State laws in same Concurrent List field.
|   |-- Tests: direct collision / impossible simultaneous obedience / exhaustive occupation.
|   |-- Holdings: Deep Chand (1959); M. Karunanidhi (1979).
|   |-- Effect: Union law prevails; State law void only to extent of conflict.
|   |-- Article 254(2): reserved + Presidential assent -> State law may prevail in that State.
|   |-- Parliament may later add to, amend, vary or repeal the assented State law.
|   |-- Occupied field = demonstrated exhaustive Parliamentary intent, not mere central law.
|   '-- Limit: assent cannot cure State incompetence; List I/List II overlap is not Article 254.
|
+-- 6. RECONCILIATION AND REMEDY
|   |-- Harmonious construction -> both provisions retain meaningful operation.
|   |   |-- Kerala Education Bill (1958); Venkataramana Devaru (1958).
|   |   '-- Limit: cannot erase express priority/prohibition.
|   |-- Federal supremacy -> final response after reconciliation fails under Arts 246/254.
|   |-- Reading down -> valid narrower meaning text already bears; Kedar Nath Singh (1962).
|   |-- Reading into -> necessary safeguard from rights/structure; Vishaka (1997).
|   |-- Shreya Singhal (2015) -> vague Section 66A could not be judicially reconstructed.
|   '-- Remedy sequence -> read down if possible -> sever if workable -> strike if infected.
|
+-- 7. ARTICLE 141 PRECEDENT + TIME
|   |-- Ratio decidendi = necessary legal principle; obiter = persuasive observation.
|   |-- Per incuriam = ignorance of binding statute/precedent; narrow exception.
|   |-- Sub silentio = point not consciously determined.
|   |-- Larger bench controls; coordinate doubt -> reference; Dawoodi Bohra (2005).
|   |-- Prospective overruling -> Golaknath (1967), court-fashioned temporal remedy.
|   '-- Limit: not automatic and never permission for lower courts to ignore precedent.
|
+-- 8. BASIC STRUCTURE: ARTICLE 368 + ARTICLE 13 RELATIONSHIP
|   |-- 1st Amendment Act, 1951 -> Article 31B + Ninth Schedule.
|   |-- 24th Amendment Act, 1971 -> Articles 13(4), 368(3); constituent power affirmed.
|   |-- 25th Amendment Act, 1971 -> original Article 31C protection.
|   |-- 29th Amendment Act, 1972 -> specified Kerala laws added to Ninth Schedule.
|   |-- Kesavananda Bharati (24 April 1973) -> wide amendment power, no basic-structure damage.
|   |-- 39th Amendment Act, 1975 -> Article 329A(4); struck in Indira Gandhi (1975).
|   |-- 42nd Amendment Act, 1976 -> expanded Article 31C + Arts 368(4)-(5);
|   |   Minerva Mills (1980) invalidated them and protected review/limited power/FR-DPSP harmony.
|   |-- 44th Amendment Act, 1978 -> property removed from Part III; Article 300A protection.
|   |-- Waman Rao (1981) -> 24 April 1973 Ninth Schedule cut-off.
|   |-- I.R. Coelho (2007) -> post-cut-off insertions reviewable for basic-structure damage.
|   |-- 99th Amendment Act, 2014 + NJAC Act -> invalidated in 2015; judicial independence.
|   '-- LIMIT -> amendment review, not ordinary policy veto; no closed exhaustive feature list.
|
+-- 9. RIGHTS-REVIEW STANDARDS
|   |-- Presumption of constitutionality -> rebuttable; Ram Krishna Dalmia (1958).
|   |-- Classification -> intelligible differentia + rational nexus.
|   |-- Arbitrariness -> Royappa; manifest arbitrariness -> Shayara Bano (2017).
|   |-- Proportionality -> authority + aim + connection + necessity + balance.
|   |   Modern Dental (2016); Puttaswamy (2017); Anuradha Bhasin (2020).
|   '-- LIMIT -> structured justification review, not preferred-policy substitution.
|
+-- 10. MORALITY, TRANSFORMATION AND RELIGION
|   |-- Constitutional morality -> text, procedure, equal citizenship, role obligations;
|   |   NCT Delhi and Navtej (2018); not social/personal morality.
|   |-- Transformative constitutionalism -> lawful anti-hierarchy project through
|   |   liberty, equality, dignity, fraternity, democracy and remedies.
|   |-- Living/dynamic interpretation -> new application, not new uncontrolled power.
|   |-- ERP -> Articles 25-26; Shirur Mutt (1954): religion versus secular activity.
|   |-- Apply public order, morality, health, social reform and competing Part III rights.
|   '-- Status: nine-judge reference judgment reserved 14 May 2026; no new final holding.
|
+-- 11. ANSWER SPINE
|   |-- 1 Governing Article and institution.
|   |-- 2 Trigger: meaning / competence / conflict / right / amendment / precedent / time.
|   |-- 3 Exact test and must-write elements.
|   |-- 4 Leading holding with year.
|   |-- 5 Fact application.
|   |-- 6 Legal effect: coexist / prevail / read down / sever / strike / future effect.
|   '-- 7 Limit + narrow qualified conclusion.
|
'-- QUALIFIED VERDICT
    Doctrines make broad constitutional commitments legally repeatable. Their legitimacy
    depends on correct routing: competence doctrines cannot replace rights review, Article 254
    cannot replace field analysis, saving interpretation cannot become rewriting, and morality
    or transformation cannot float free of text, precedent and institutional competence.
```
