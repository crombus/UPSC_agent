                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The Constituent Assembly was not elected directly on universal adult
franchise, but it acquired constitutional legitimacy through political
evolution, social breadth, deliberative committee work and full legal
sovereignty after the Indian Independence Act 1947. Constitution-making was
distributed among the Assembly, specialised committees, Constitutional Adviser
B.N. Rau, the Drafting Committee chaired by B.R. Ambedkar and the Assembly's
clause-by-clause votes. Its achievement lay not in inventing every institution
but in adapting comparative and colonial material to popular sovereignty,
universal franchise, rights, federalism and social revolution.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The Constituent Assembly lacked the direct universal mandate that the
Constitution would later guarantee, and its Congress dominance and post-
Partition composition require honest acknowledgement. Yet it became legally
sovereign, worked through specialised and public deliberation, accommodated
substantial ideological diversity and consciously transferred authority from
empire to the people. Its legitimacy rests on that reasoned transformation and
on the democratic order it created, not on a claim of perfect representation.
