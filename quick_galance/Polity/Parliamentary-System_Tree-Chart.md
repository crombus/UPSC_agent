                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
A parliamentary government is defined by the political responsibility of the
real executive to the legislature. India combines a nominal constitutional
head with a Prime Minister-led Council of Ministers drawn from Parliament and
collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha. This fusion promotes coordination
and daily accountability, but a disciplined majority, anti-defection rules,
agenda control, delegated legislation and Prime Ministerial concentration may
turn Parliament from controller into ratifier. The constitutional answer is
stronger scrutiny within responsible government, not confusion with British
parliamentary sovereignty or presidential separation.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
India's parliamentary system knowingly privileges continuous responsibility
over fixed-term stability. Its recurring weakness is not the fusion of powers
alone but the conversion of party majority into executive control over debate,
finance and legislative time. Collective responsibility remains meaningful
only when Parliament has information, committee capacity, adequate sittings and
space for members to scrutinise. Reform should revitalise those institutions
while preserving the plural accountability for which the system was chosen.
