                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
A cooperative is a member-owned enterprise organised around democratic control
and mutual service rather than capital dominance. The 97th Amendment added a
Fundamental Right, a Directive Principle and Part IXB, but it attempted to
constitutionalise governance in a State-List field without State ratification.
Rajendra N. Shah preserved Articles 19(1)(c), 43B and Part IXB for multi-State
cooperatives while invalidating Part IXB's application to ordinary State
cooperatives. National reform must therefore proceed through multi-State law,
finance and cooperative federalism, not uniform central command.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The constitutional value of cooperation survives the partial invalidation of
Part IXB. Citizens retain the right to form cooperatives, and the State remains
directed to promote voluntary, democratic and professional institutions.
Rajendra N. Shah correctly preserves the federal boundary: ordinary cooperatives
remain State-governed, while Parliament regulates the multi-State field.
National revival must therefore strengthen member autonomy, clean governance
and market capacity through partnership with States rather than constitutional
centralisation.
