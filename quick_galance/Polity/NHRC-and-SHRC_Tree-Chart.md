                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The NHRC and SHRCs are statutory watchdogs under the Protection of Human Rights
Act 1993. They investigate violations, review safeguards, inspect institutions,
promote awareness and recommend relief, prosecution or institutional reform.
They possess civil-court inquiry powers but not binding adjudicatory or punitive
authority. Their effectiveness therefore depends on independence, plural
composition, credible investigation, government compliance, timely inquiry and
public reporting. The one-year bar, limited armed-forces procedure and reliance
on government staff remain structural constraints.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Human Rights Commissions add accessible investigation, institutional scrutiny
and public visibility to India's rights system, but they do not replace courts,
prosecutors or democratic government. Their recommendations acquire force
through credible evidence, publication and government response rather than
coercive decree. Stronger independence, plural appointments, investigative
capacity, timely follow-up and carefully expanded jurisdiction are necessary if
the watchdog is to protect dignity without becoming either symbolic or an
unaccountable parallel judiciary.
