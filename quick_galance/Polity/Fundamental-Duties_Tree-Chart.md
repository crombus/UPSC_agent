                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Article 51A is a constitutional grammar of responsible citizenship. Fundamental
Duties are addressed to citizens, are non-justiciable and do not create
standalone offences, yet they carry constitutional weight through civic
education, interpretation and precise legislation enacted by a competent
legislature. Duties complement rather than cancel rights: the State may invoke
them to support a proportionate law, but cannot use vague appeals to patriotism,
culture or public order to bypass liberty, equality, legality or judicial
review. Responsible freedom, not compelled conformity, is their governing idea.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Fundamental Duties complete the Constitution's idea of citizenship by asking
individuals to sustain the institutional, plural, cultural and ecological
conditions in which rights can flourish. Their weakness is also a safeguard:
because they are non-justiciable, punishment must come through precise,
reviewable law rather than open-ended moral command. Their best implementation
is constitutional education, public example and proportionate legislation, not
compelled conformity or a vague judicial code of approved citizenship.
