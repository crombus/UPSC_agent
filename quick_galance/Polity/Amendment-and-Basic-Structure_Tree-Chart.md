                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Article 368 gives Parliament constituent power to amend the Constitution by
addition, variation or repeal, but not a power to replace its constitutional
identity. The amendment design combines flexibility and rigidity through three
routes: simple-majority changes outside Article 368, special-majority
amendments, and federal amendments needing ratification by at least half the
states. The Supreme Court's basic-structure doctrine adds a substantive ceiling:
Parliament may alter every provision, including Fundamental Rights, but cannot
damage or destroy the Constitution's essential features.
