                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Political parties are not expressly created by the Constitution, yet they are
the indispensable link between citizens, elections, legislatures and executive
government. Indian law regulates registration, symbols, disclosure, finance
and defection but largely leaves internal organisation to parties. Democratic
legitimacy therefore requires more than competitive elections: transparent
money, fair candidate selection, internal accountability, representative
leadership and adjudication that neither captures parties nor freezes them.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Parties transform social claims into public authority, so their private
associational autonomy has unavoidable public consequences. India should not
allow the state to prescribe ideology or manage internal politics, but it can
require verifiable membership, periodic elections, transparent finance,
reasoned candidate selection and fair dispute procedures. Democratic elections
cannot remain fully democratic when the organisations controlling nominations,
money and legislative careers are opaque within.
