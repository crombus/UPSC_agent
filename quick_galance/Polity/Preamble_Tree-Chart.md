                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The Preamble is the Constitution's authoritative statement of source, identity,
objectives and adoption. It is legally part of the Constitution and guides
interpretation, but it is non-justiciable, creates no independent power or cause
of action, and does not prohibit legislation by itself. It may be amended under
Article 368, but its basic features cannot be destroyed. Its force is therefore
indirect yet foundational: it supplies the value grammar through which the
Constitution's text, structure and limits are understood.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The Preamble is neither an ornamental aspiration nor a self-executing bill of
rights. It is the Constitution's interpretive charter: enacted by the people,
expressing the State's identity and ordering justice, liberty, equality and
fraternity into a common project. Courts may use it to resolve ambiguity and
protect constitutional identity, while respecting its non-justiciable and
non-conferring character. Its amendment history and the 2024 judgment confirm
that its words may evolve, but its basic democratic promise cannot be erased.
