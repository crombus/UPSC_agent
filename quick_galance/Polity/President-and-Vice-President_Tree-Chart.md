# President and Vice-President — Self-Contained Quick-Glance Tree

## 1. Core constitutional design

```text
PARLIAMENTARY REPUBLIC
|
+-- PRESIDENT: constitutional Head of State
|   +-- Article 52: there shall be a President of India
|   +-- Article 53: Union executive power is formally vested in the President
|   +-- Article 74: Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister aids and advises
|   +-- Article 75: Council is collectively responsible to Lok Sabha
|   `-- Result: formal presidential authority + politically accountable ministry
|
`-- VICE-PRESIDENT: parliamentary presiding office plus continuity bridge
    +-- Article 63: there shall be a Vice-President of India
    +-- Article 64: ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha; not a member of that House
    `-- Article 65: acts as President during vacancy or discharges functions during inability
```

**Answer line:** The President is a constitutional sentinel, not an alternative executive; the Vice-President is principally Rajya Sabha's presiding officer, not an ordinary deputy executive.

## 3. President: election and disputes

```text
ARTICLE 54 — WHO VOTES
|-- elected Lok Sabha members
|-- elected Rajya Sabha members
|-- elected MLAs of States
|-- elected MLAs of Delhi and Puducherry
`-- excludes nominated MPs/MLAs, all MLCs and J&K UT MLAs

ARTICLE 55 — HOW VOTES ARE WEIGHTED AND COUNTED
|-- MLA value = 1971 population / elected MLAs / 1000, with constitutional rounding
|-- MP value = total value of elected MLA votes / elected MPs of both Houses
|-- every elected MP has the same vote value
`-- PR-STV + secret ranked ballot + transfer until majority quota

ARTICLE 71 — WHO DECIDES DISPUTES
|-- Supreme Court finally decides President and Vice-President election disputes
|-- vacancy in electoral college does not invalidate election
`-- acts done before an election is declared void remain valid
```

**Trap firewall:** Nominated MPs do not elect the President but may participate in impeachment.

## 4. President: qualification, tenure, vacancy and impeachment

```text
ARTICLE 58 — QUALIFICATION
|-- Indian citizen; 35 years
|-- qualified for Lok Sabha
`-- no disqualifying office of profit

ARTICLE 60 — OATH
`-- administered by CJI; senior-most available Supreme Court judge substitutes

ARTICLES 56-57 — TERM
|-- five years; continues until successor enters office
|-- resignation addressed to Vice-President
`-- re-election allowed without a numerical cap

ARTICLE 62 — VACANCY
|-- expiry election completed before term ends
|-- casual vacancy election within six months
`-- elected successor receives a fresh five-year term

ARTICLE 61 — IMPEACHMENT
|-- sole ground: violation of Constitution
|-- either House initiates; one-fourth signs; fourteen days' notice
|-- initiating House: two-thirds of total membership
|-- other House investigates; President may appear or be represented
`-- investigating House: two-thirds of total membership -> removal
```

## 5. Continuity and immunity

```text
ARTICLE 65
|-- Vice-President acts when President's office is vacant
`-- Vice-President discharges functions during temporary inability

ARTICLE 70 + PRESIDENT (DISCHARGE OF FUNCTIONS) ACT, 1969
`-- if both President and Vice-President are unavailable, CJI, or senior-most
    available Supreme Court judge, discharges presidential functions

ARTICLE 361
|-- President not personally answerable to court for official acts
|-- no criminal proceeding or arrest during tenure
|-- specified personal civil proceeding needs two months' prior notice
`-- government action remains judicially reviewable: immunity is not impunity
```

## 6. Advice and bounded situational judgment

```text
NORMAL RULE
|-- Article 74(1): ministerial advice
|-- 42nd Amendment (1976): binding character made explicit in Article 74(1)
|-- 44th Amendment (1978): Article 74(1) proviso permits one reconsideration
`-- reiterated advice binds

CASES / PRACTICE
|-- Shamsher Singh (1974): constitutional head acts through responsible government
|-- U. N. R. Rao (1971): Council continues even after Lok Sabha dissolution
|-- hung House: invite person most likely to command Lok Sabha confidence
|-- disputed majority: require prompt floor test
`-- K. R. Narayanan: returned President's-Rule advice for reconsideration
```

**Answer spine:** normal binding advice -> one reconsideration -> narrow contingency -> floor confidence -> no personal policy programme.

```text
LEGISLATIVE

|-- Articles 86-87: messages and addresses

FINANCIAL

`-- Finance Commission and Contingency Fund functions

JUDICIAL / EXTERNAL / EMERGENCY
|-- Article 72: clemency

|-- formal diplomatic and Supreme Commander roles
`-- Articles 352, 356 and 360: separate emergency triggers and safeguards
```

## 8. Veto: decode by Bill type

| Route | Presidential option | Re-passage consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Article 111 ordinary Union Bill | assent, withhold, or return once | after re-passage, assent is mandatory |
| Article 111 Money Bill | assent or withhold; cannot return | no return route |
| Article 368 amendment Bill | assent obligatory after 24th Amendment (1971) | no veto |
| Article 201 reserved State non-Money Bill | assent, withhold, or direct return through Governor | State re-passage does not textually compel assent |

- **Pocket veto:** conventional label arising from Article 111's lack of a fixed time limit.
- **Zail Singh / Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill, 1986:** standard illustration of prolonged non-decision.
- **2025 Article 143 opinion:** rejected court-made fixed clocks and deemed assent under Articles 200/201, while permitting limited intervention against glaring, prolonged and unexplained inaction.

## 9. Ordinance under Article 123

```text
TRIGGER
|-- both Houses are not simultaneously in session
`-- circumstances require immediate action
        |
        v
FORCE AND LIMIT
|-- same force as an Act within Parliament's competence
|-- may amend Central law and operate retrospectively within constitutional limits
|-- cannot amend Constitution or abridge Fundamental Rights
        |
        v
EXIT
|-- must be laid before both Houses
|-- ceases six weeks after later reassembly, unless earlier disapproved/withdrawn
        |
        v
JUDICIAL CONTROL
|-- R. C. Cooper (1970): satisfaction not wholly immune from review
|-- D. C. Wadhwa (1986): routine re-promulgation is fraud on Constitution
```

## 10. Clemency under Article 72

```text
FIVE FORMS
|-- pardon: removes sentence and conviction consequences
|-- reprieve: temporary stay
|-- respite: lesser sentence due to special circumstances
|-- remission: reduces duration without changing character
`-- commutation: substitutes a lighter form of punishment

PRESIDENT'S FIELD
|-- court-martial cases
|-- Union executive-field offences
`-- every death sentence

ARTICLE 161 GOVERNOR CONTRAST
|-- State executive-field offences
|-- no court-martial power
`-- cannot pardon death sentence, but may suspend, remit or commute it

CASE CONTROL
|-- Maru Ram (1980): clemency exercised on binding ministerial advice
|-- Kehar Singh (1988): merits may be examined afresh; not a judicial appeal
`-- Epuru Sudhakar (2006): limited review for mala fides, arbitrariness,
    irrelevant material or non-application of mind
```

## 11. Vice-President

```text
ARTICLE 66 — ELECTION
|-- all elected and nominated MPs of both Houses
|-- no State or UT legislator
|-- equal vote value; PR-STV; secret ballot
`-- citizen, 35, qualified for Rajya Sabha; 20 proposers + 20 seconders

ARTICLE 67 — TERM / REMOVAL
|-- five years; resignation to President
|-- Rajya Sabha alone initiates after fourteen days' notice
|-- majority of all then members in Rajya Sabha
|-- Lok Sabha agrees by ordinary voting rule
`-- no stated ground; removal is not impeachment

ARTICLE 68 — VACANCY
|-- election before expiry
`-- casual vacancy filled as soon as possible; no six-month text; fresh term
```

## 12. Rajya Sabha Chair and acting President

```text
ARTICLE 64: Vice-President is ex-officio Chairman, not a Rajya Sabha member
ARTICLE 100: no first vote; casting vote on equality
TENTH SCHEDULE: decides Rajya Sabha defection cases
KIHOTO HOLLOHAN (1992): presiding-officer defection decisions are judicially reviewable
ARTICLE 92: during own removal consideration, may speak but cannot preside or vote
ARTICLE 65: when acting as President, does not discharge Chairman's duties
DEPUTY CHAIRMAN: performs Chair duties during the acting-presidential period
```

## 13. President versus Vice-President

| Test | President | Vice-President |
|---|---|---|
| Electoral college | elected MPs + specified elected MLAs | all MPs, including nominees |
| Vote value | weighted | equal |
| House qualification | Lok Sabha | Rajya Sabha |
| Oath administered by | CJI / senior-most available SC judge | President / appointee |
| Resignation addressed to | Vice-President | President |
| Removal | Article 61 impeachment; either House; 2/3 total in both | Article 67(b); Rajya Sabha effective majority + Lok Sabha agreement |
| Casual vacancy | election within six months | election as soon as possible |
| Regular role | constitutional Head of State on advice | Rajya Sabha Chairman |
| Contingency role | office to be continuously discharged | acts as President temporarily |

## 15. Final answer spine

```text
Exact Article
   -> constitutional mechanism
```

**Final verdict:** The two offices secure continuity without displacing parliamentary responsibility: presidential dignity and Vice-Presidential procedure remain constitutionally bounded.
