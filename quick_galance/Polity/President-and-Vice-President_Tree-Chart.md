                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The President is the constitutional head of the Union: executive power is
formally vested in the office, but ordinarily exercised on binding ministerial
advice. The office is therefore neither a rival elected executive nor a nullity;
its influence appears through warnings, reconsideration, formal safeguards and
carefully bounded situational judgment when parliamentary majorities are
uncertain. The Vice-President is designed principally as the ex-officio Chairman
of the Rajya Sabha and only contingently as acting President. Election,
removal, veto, ordinance and clemency rules reflect these distinct purposes.

The President supplies constitutional continuity, dignity and formal legality
