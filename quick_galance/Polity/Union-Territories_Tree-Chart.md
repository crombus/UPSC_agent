                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
A Union Territory is constitutionally administered by the President through an
Administrator who acts as the President's agent, not as a State's independent
constitutional head. Parliament retains plenary power to legislate for every
UT, including on State List subjects. Yet UTs are not institutionally uniform:
some have no legislature, Puducherry has a statutory legislature, Delhi has a
special constitutional legislature, and J&K has a legislature under the 2019
Reorganisation Act. The resulting asymmetry balances national, strategic and
capital interests against local representative government.

single constitutional category in operation. Directly administered islands and
frontiers, statutory Puducherry and J&K legislatures, and constitutional Delhi
combine Union responsibility with varying local democracy. Where an elected
government exists, legitimacy requires the Administrator to respect its lawful
sphere and use reference or override powers exceptionally. Strategic control
must not turn representative government into administrative symbolism.
