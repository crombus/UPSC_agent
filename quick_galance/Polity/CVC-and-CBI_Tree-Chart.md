                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
India's Union anti-corruption system separates vigilance supervision from
criminal investigation. The CVC is a statutory, mainly advisory integrity body;
the CBI is an executive-created police organisation exercising powers under the
DSPE Act 1946. The CVC superintends CBI corruption investigations under the
Prevention of Corruption Act, not every CBI case. State consent limits ordinary
CBI operation because police is a State subject, while constitutional courts may
direct exceptional investigations without consent. Independence requires a
statutory charter, secure leadership, lawful federal coordination and due process.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The CVC and CBI are complementary, not interchangeable. Vigilance prevention
and disciplinary advice cannot replace independent criminal investigation, and
investigation cannot ignore federal consent or due process. The present system
has acquired safeguards through Vineet Narain, selection committees and court
review, yet the CBI's borrowed statutory foundation and the CVC's advisory
ceiling remain structural weaknesses. A dedicated charter and transparent
federal protocols would protect both autonomy and accountability.
