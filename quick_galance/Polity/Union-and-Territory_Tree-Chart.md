                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Articles 1 to 4 make the Union territorially indestructible but its constituent
units constitutionally alterable. Article 1 names India, that is Bharat, and
describes it as a Union of States; Article 2 concerns admission or establishment
of new States; Article 3 enables Parliament to reorganise existing Indian units
after consultation but without state consent; Article 4 allows the necessary
schedule and consequential changes through ordinary legislation. The design
combines territorial flexibility with national permanence, but its legitimate
use requires federal consultation, viable transition and minority safeguards.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Articles 1 to 4 secure a permanent Union through a flexible map. This design
enabled the integration of princely states, linguistic accommodation and later
responses to regional, administrative and developmental claims without granting
any unit a secession right or boundary veto. Yet constitutional legality is only
the minimum. Sustainable reorganisation requires transparent evidence, genuine
consultation, negotiated division of assets and liabilities, fiscal viability
and protection of minorities within both old and new units.
