                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Part IV directs the State to convert political democracy into social and
economic democracy. Directive Principles are non-justiciable under Article 37,
but they are fundamental in governance and impose a constitutional duty on the
State in law-making. Their force is political, legislative and interpretive,
not a direct judicial command. The settled relationship with Fundamental Rights
is harmony rather than abstract hierarchy: enforceable rights ordinarily control
ordinary law, while the State may pursue Part IV through valid legislation and
constitutional amendment without damaging the basic structure.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Directive Principles are constitutional commitments without direct judicial
execution. Their democratic force comes from Article 37's duty, electoral
accountability, legislation and their interpretive relationship with rights.
Their record is neither empty aspiration nor complete fulfilment: legal aid,
local government, education and environmental law show real conversion, while
resource, capacity and equality gaps remain. The constitutional method is to
advance Part IV without sacrificing Part III, making social revolution and
limited government mutually reinforcing.
