# Polity 32 - Comptroller and Auditor General of India

## Quick-Glance Tree Chart

```text
CAG: CONSTITUTIONAL AUDIT, NOT EXECUTIVE COMMAND
|
+-- 1. CORE CONCEPT
|   +-- Independent constitutional auditor and head of IAAD.
|   +-- Converts legislative authorisation into evidence-based ex-post accountability.
|   +-- Cannot prosecute, recover, invalidate policy, pre-authorise every payment,
|       compel PAC action or audit every private entity automatically.
|
+-- 2. ORIGIN AND TIMELINE
|   +-- 1950 Constitution: Articles 148-151 constitutionalised the office and report route.
|   +-- DPC Act 56 of 1971: service conditions plus statutory duties and powers.
|   +-- DPC Amendment Act 45 of 1976: Sections 10, 11 and 22 changed; relieving
|   |   orders enabled departmentalisation of Union accounts and separation from audit.
|   +-- Departmentalisation of Union Accounts Act 59 of 1976: transferred personnel
|   |   into executive accounting organisations, supporting the CGA system.
|   +-- DPC Amendment Act 2 of 1984: Section 6 pension provisions revised.
|   +-- DPC Amendment Act 50 of 1987: Section 6 revised, Section 7 omitted and
|   |   Section 9 revised; retirement/service benefits linked to SC Judge framework.
|   +-- DPC Amendment Act 51 of 1994: Sections 3 and 6 salary/pension adjustments;
|       no expansion of general audit jurisdiction.
|
+-- 3. DECODED CONSTITUTIONAL SPINE
|   +-- Art 148(1): President appoints by warrant; removal only like an SC Judge.
|   +-- Art 148(2): Third Schedule oath before President or nominee.
|   +-- Art 148(3): Parliament sets salary/service conditions; protected rights cannot
|   |   be varied to disadvantage after appointment.
|   +-- Art 148(4): no further office under Union or State government.
|   +-- Art 148(5): President makes IAAD staff/administration rules after CAG consultation.
|   +-- Art 148(6): salary and specified office/IAAD expenses charged on CFI.
|   +-- Art 149: duties/powers prescribed by or under parliamentary law; DPC Act is core.
|   +-- Art 150: President prescribes Union/State account form on CAG advice.
|   |   42nd Amendment, 1976 changed Art 150 to the present President-on-advice form.
|   +-- Art 151(1): Union reports -> President -> each House of Parliament.
|   +-- Art 151(2): State reports -> Governor -> State legislature.
|   +-- Art 279: CAG ascertains/certifies net proceeds; certificate is final.
|
+-- 4. OFFICE MECHANISM AND INDEPENDENCE
|   +-- Section 4: six years or age 65, whichever earlier; resignation to President.
|   +-- Removal: proved misbehaviour/incapacity + Article 124(4) special-majority address.
|   +-- Safeguards: hard removal, no disadvantage, charged finance, post-office bar,
|   |   consultation on IAAD administration.
|   +-- Limitation: no constitutional collegium or confirmation hearing; practical
|       autonomy also needs staff, records, timeliness and follow-up.
|
+-- 5. ACCOUNTS, AUDIT AND DPC ACT MECHANISM
|   +-- Accounts create/compile records; audit independently tests them.
|   +-- 1976 departmentalisation shifted much Union compilation to ministries/CGA;
|   |   Sections 10-12 preserve residual CAG duties where not relieved.
|   +-- s13: Consolidated Fund expenditure; Contingency/Public Account transactions;
|   |   departmental commercial accounts; legality-purpose-authority test.
|   +-- s14: substantially financed bodies; Rs 25 lakh + 75% test; separate Rs 1 crore
|   |   approval route; two-year continuation.
|   +-- s15: sanctioning procedures for specific-purpose grants/loans.
|   +-- s16: receipts payable into Consolidated Funds; assessment/collection/allocation.
|   +-- s17 stores/stock; s18 relevant access; s23 scope regulations; s24 limited checks.
|
+-- 6. ENTITY-SPECIFIC BOUNDARIES
|   +-- Government company: DPC s19 + Companies Act s2(45), s139 and s143;
|   |   CAG appoints/directs auditor and may supplement, comment or test audit.
|   +-- Parliamentary statutory corporation: establishing law + s19(2).
|   +-- State corporation: qualified s19(3) request route.
|   +-- Otherwise unaudited body: s20 entrustment with consultation, public interest
|   |   and opportunity to represent.
|   +-- Local body: Arts 243J/243Z make State law primary; CAG needs a legal basis,
|   |   entrustment or Technical Guidance and Support arrangement.
|   +-- Private/PPP entity: grant, receipt, guarantee, public asset, company control,
|       special law or contract may create bounded access; status alone does not.
|
+-- 7. AUDIT TYPES
|   +-- Financial: fair presentation of financial statements.
|   +-- Compliance: law, appropriation, sanction, rules, orders and contracts.
|   +-- Propriety: prudence, faithfulness and economy; no policy substitution.
|   +-- Performance: economy, efficiency and effectiveness; implementation/results.
|   +-- Receipts/IT/environment: specialised object or method within existing mandate.
|   +-- Finding != guilt, binding recovery, invalid policy or final adjudication.
|
+-- 8. REPORT AND FOLLOW-UP MECHANISM
|   +-- CAG function: evidence, auditee response and final report.
|   +-- President/Governor function: cause legislative laying under Art 151.
|   +-- PAC function: examine Appropriation/Finance Accounts and selected CAG reports.
|   +-- CoPU function: examine specified public undertakings and related CAG reports.
|   +-- Executive function: reply and take recovery/discipline/prosecution only under
|   |   separate lawful powers.
|   +-- CAG assists committees technically; it is not a member and cannot compel action.
|
+-- 9. JUDGMENTS AND BOUNDED EXAMPLES
|   +-- Arvind Gupta v Union of India (2012): three-E performance audit is in-built
|   |   in the 1971 Act; the challenge to regulations failed.
|   +-- CPIL v Union of India (2012): 2008 FCFS 2G allocation arbitrary under Art 14;
|   |   122 licences quashed; not judicial adoption of CAG loss estimates.
|   +-- Arun Kumar Agrawal v Union of India (2013): CAG report is subject to
|   |   parliamentary/PAC scrutiny and government response, not automatic adjudication.
|   +-- S. Subramaniam Balaji v State of Tamil Nadu (2013): legality, validity and
|   |   propriety audit arises after expenditure; illustrates ex-post timing.
|   +-- Association of Unified Telecom Service Providers v Union of India (2014):
|   |   records needed to verify Union telecom revenue share auditable under s16;
|   |   no roving audit of unrelated private business.
|   +-- Vinod Rai-era 2G/coal reports: scrutiny examples only; no headline figures;
|       audit counterfactual is not judicially proved loss or criminal guilt.
|
+-- 10. INSTITUTIONAL COMPARISON
|   +-- CAG/IAAD: constitutional audit and field work.
|   +-- CGA: executive Union civil accounting and financial reporting.
|   +-- Finance Commission: Art 280 fiscal recommendations; CAG Art 279 certification.
|   +-- Election Commission: Art 324 election administration, not public audit.
|   +-- UPSC/SPSC: public-service examinations/advice; Art 319 post-office rules differ.
|   +-- PAC/CoPU: elected legislative scrutiny bodies, separate from the auditor.
|
+-- 11. PRELIMS TRAPS
|   +-- Six years/65 is statutory, not text of Art 148.
|   +-- President appoints; removal is not at pleasure.
|   +-- Charged expenditure is non-votable, not beyond discussion.
|   +-- Art 150: President on CAG advice; Art 151: President/Governor report route.
|   +-- s18 access follows jurisdiction; s15 is not universal grantee audit.
|   +-- CAG does not automatically audit every local body, PPP or private entity.
|   +-- CAG report != PAC report != executive order != court judgment.
|
+-- 12. MAINS ANSWER SPINE
    +-- Define independent, predominantly ex-post constitutional auditor.
    +-- Art 148 safeguards and appointment gap.
    +-- Art 149 + exact DPC/Companies Act/entity route.
    +-- Audit type and one precise named case.
    +-- Art 150/151 account-form and report chain.
    +-- Limit: no prosecution, recovery, policy veto or automatic private audit.
    +-- Reform: selection credibility + auditability + timeliness + committee follow-up.
    +-- Verdict: protects legislative power of the purse through evidence, not command.
```

## Qualified conclusion

The CAG is constitutionally insulated and legally powerful in gathering and reporting audit evidence, but deliberately non-punitive and predominantly ex post. Its effectiveness depends on a precise public-money gateway, timely records, balanced findings, legislative scrutiny and reasoned executive follow-up.
