                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The CAG converts legislative control of public money into evidence-based
accountability. It independently audits whether revenue and expenditure follow
law, authorisation, economy, efficiency and intended purpose, then reports to
the legislature through the President or Governor. The CAG does not itself
recover money, punish officials or ordinarily control withdrawal from the
Consolidated Fund. Its effectiveness depends on lawful audit reach, access to
records, professional independence and timely committee follow-up.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The CAG is indispensable because legislative control over expenditure would be
nominal without independent audit. Its authority is evidentiary and
constitutional, not executive or punitive: it reveals illegality, waste and
performance failure so elected legislatures can demand correction. To remain
the guardian of the public purse in a changing fiscal State, audit law must
cover new public-private and off-budget forms, while appointment, data access
and committee follow-up preserve both independence and practical consequence.
