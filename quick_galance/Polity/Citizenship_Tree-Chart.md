                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Citizenship is full legal membership of the Indian political community. Part II
of the Constitution did not create a permanent citizenship code; Articles 5 to
11 identified citizens at commencement and preserved Parliament's power to
legislate, producing the Citizenship Act 1955. India combines a federal polity
with single citizenship: political membership is national, while carefully
bounded residence, tribal and regional protections may survive without becoming
separate state citizenship. Citizenship, domicile, residence, nationality and
OCI status must therefore be kept analytically and legally distinct.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
India's citizenship architecture places national political membership above
state membership while permitting constitutionally bounded local protection.
Its legitimacy depends on precise statutory administration: dates, parentage,
residence, registration and proof cannot be replaced by slogans or identity
documents. Contemporary disputes over Assam and the CAA show that citizenship
law must reconcile sovereign border control and humanitarian concern with
equality, secular constitutionalism, due process and the fraternity promised to
all existing citizens.
