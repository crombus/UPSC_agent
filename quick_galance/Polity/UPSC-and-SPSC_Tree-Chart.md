                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Articles 315 to 323 constitutionalise impartial recruitment advice so permanent
public services are selected through merit rather than political patronage.
The UPSC and State PSCs are independent constitutional commissions, while a
Joint State PSC is created by parliamentary law. Their advice is generally
advisory, not binding, and selection creates no indefeasible right to
appointment. Institutional authority therefore rests on secure removal,
charged expenditure, transparent examinations, public reasons for rejecting
advice and judicial control of arbitrariness without appellate re-marking.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Public Service Commissions protect merit by separating competitive selection
from day-to-day political patronage, but they do not govern the civil services
or bind the elected executive. Their authority rests on difficult removal,
charged expenditure, fair procedure and public explanation when advice is
rejected. Recruitment integrity now also requires secure examinations,
transparent verification and stable rules. Reform should strengthen these
capacities without converting an advisory commission into an unaccountable
personnel executive.
