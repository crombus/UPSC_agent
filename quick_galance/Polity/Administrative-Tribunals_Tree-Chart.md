                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Tribunals are constitutionally permitted specialist adjudicatory institutions,
not executive departments and not constitutional courts. Their justification
is expertise, procedural flexibility, access and speed; their legitimacy
depends on judicial independence, fair procedure, adequate capacity and review
by constitutional courts. After L. Chandra Kumar, tribunalisation may redirect
first-instance jurisdiction but cannot extinguish the High Courts' Articles
226/227 supervision or the Supreme Court's constitutional role.
