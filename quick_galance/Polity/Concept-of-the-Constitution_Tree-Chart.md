                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
A constitution both creates public power and restrains it. It identifies the
political community, establishes institutions, allocates authority, protects
rights, states public purposes and provides peaceful methods of change.
Constitutionalism is the deeper condition in which every constituted authority,
including a democratic majority and the courts, remains limited by law,
procedure, rights, accountability and effective remedies. India's design is a
legal, political and transformative order, not merely a written document.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
A constitution is successful not when it describes every institution in
detail, but when authorised power remains legitimate, limited and capable of
peaceful correction. India's text combines democratic government, enforceable
rights, federal accommodation and social transformation while permitting
change through amendment and interpretation. Its enduring challenge is to
close the gap between constitutional form and constitutional practice without
turning either electoral mandate or judicial doctrine into unlimited power.
