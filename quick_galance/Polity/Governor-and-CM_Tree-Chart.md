                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The State executive has two identities: the Governor is the formal
constitutional head, while the Chief Minister-led Council of Ministers is the
real executive responsible to the Legislative Assembly. The Governor's Union
appointment, pleasure tenure and limited discretionary functions create a
federal bridge but also recurring partisan friction. Shamsher Singh makes aid
and advice the rule; Bommai, Rameshwar Prasad, Nabam Rebia and Subhash Desai
confine discretion through objective material, floor tests and judicial review.
The office is a constitutional trustee, not an agent of either party.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The Governor is indispensable as a constitutional State head and federal link,
but the appointment and pleasure design creates incentives for partisan use.
The cure is not an elected rival executive or unlimited judicial substitution.
It is a narrower, reasoned and reviewable discretion: majority on the House
floor, assent without indefinite obstruction, ordinances returned to the
legislature, and appointments and tenure guided by transparent federal
conventions. The Chief Minister must remain the real executive while confidence
survives.
