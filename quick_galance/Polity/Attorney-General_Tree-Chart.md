                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The Attorney General and Advocate General are constitutional law officers who
advise and represent the elected governments of the Union and States while owing
professional duties to courts and the legal order. They are not judges, Cabinet
members or ordinary civil servants. Their tenure is at constitutional pleasure,
their advice is not binding, and private practice is permitted within conflict
restrictions. The offices work only when confidential government counsel,
professional independence, candour to court and public accountability are held
in balance.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The Attorney General and Advocate General make authoritative legal advice
available at the heart of executive government without converting counsel into
judge or minister. Their flexible tenure and private practice preserve
professional character but create dependence and conflict risks. The offices
serve constitutional government best when appointments are merit-based,
conflicts transparent, advice candid and advocacy faithful both to the elected
client and to the duty of fairness owed to courts and the Constitution.
