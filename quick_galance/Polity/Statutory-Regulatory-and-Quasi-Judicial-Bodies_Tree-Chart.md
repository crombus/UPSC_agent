                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Modern government regulates complex markets and rights through specialised
statutory bodies that may make subordinate rules, supervise conduct, investigate
breaches and adjudicate individual cases. These labels describe different
dimensions: a statutory body is defined by legal source, a regulator by
function, and a quasi-judicial body by the character of a particular decision.
Delegated expertise is legitimate only when bounded by statute, transparent
procedure, reasoned orders, independent appeal, legislative accountability and
constitutional judicial review.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The regulatory state is not a retreat of government but a change in how public
power is exercised. Specialist bodies can outperform departments and ordinary
courts only when their expertise is matched by legal discipline. Parliament
must define objectives, regulators must consult and give reasons, adjudicators
must be insulated from investigators and ministries, and appeals must remain
clear. The goal is neither ministerial control nor unaccountable technocracy,
but transparent, reviewable and outcome-tested regulation.
