                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The Indian Constitution is a deliberately composite design: written and
detailed, federal but centre-weighted, parliamentary but constitutionally
limited, rights-based yet welfare-oriented, judicially reviewable without
judicial sovereignty, and nationally integrating while accommodating
territorial, linguistic and social asymmetry. Borrowed institutional techniques
were adapted rather than copied. Its operative identity lies in the interaction
of popular authority, limited government, responsible executive, constitutional
supremacy, judicial review, social transformation and plural federalism.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The Constitution's distinctiveness does not come from institutional isolation
or textual novelty. It comes from combining borrowed techniques with Indian
choices: a responsible executive under a supreme Constitution, federal
division with national integration, rights with welfare, judicial review with
democratic government, and common citizenship with plural accommodation. Its
strength is this calibrated balance; its continuing risk is that concentration
of political power can convert flexible coordination into institutional
dominance unless constitutional morality and accountability remain effective.
