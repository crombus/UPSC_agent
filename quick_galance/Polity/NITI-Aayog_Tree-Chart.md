                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
NITI Aayog is an executive-created policy think tank, not a constitutional or
statutory authority and not a fund-allocation commission. It replaced the
Planning Commission's command-and-control and plan-transfer role with strategic
advice, monitoring, innovation and a Prime Minister-Chief Ministers platform
for cooperative and competitive federalism. Its flexibility allows rapid
policy coordination, but its influence without legal power, executive control
over composition and reliance on rankings create accountability, federal trust
and measurement risks.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
NITI Aayog replaced command planning with policy coordination, State dialogue
and outcome monitoring, but it also surrendered the fiscal authority that made
the Planning Commission powerful. Its value lies in persuasion, evidence and
networked problem-solving rather than allocation or legal command. To make that
influence democratically credible, it needs transparent data, systematic
evaluation, stronger State agenda-setting and clearer accountability to
Parliament and the public.
