                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The 101st Amendment created a shared constitutional taxing space rather than
transferring indirect taxation wholly to either the Union or the States.
Articles 246A, 269A and 279A combine simultaneous legislative competence,
inter-state apportionment and a permanent intergovernmental Council. Its voting
formula prevents either side from carrying a decision alone, while Mohit
Minerals confirms that recommendations remain persuasive rather than binding.
GST is therefore pooled fiscal sovereignty: cooperative by design, asymmetric
in bargaining power and dependent on legislation and notifications for effect.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The GST Council is India's most continuous experiment in negotiated fiscal
federalism. Its design compels coalition, while Article 246A and Mohit Minerals
preserve legislative authority outside the conference room. The compact remains
credible only when recommendations are translated transparently into law,
revenue effects are shared honestly, compensation commitments are distinguished
from later cess servicing, and States participate as constitutional partners
rather than implementing agencies.
