                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Part XVI uses distinct constitutional techniques to remedy historical
exclusion: reserved legislative representation, consideration of SC/ST claims
in services, constitutional commissions and legally controlled identification
of beneficiary classes. These mechanisms must not be collapsed. A constitutional
list identifies a class; an equality provision authorises a benefit; a reserved
seat secures representation; a commission monitors safeguards; and a welfare
scheme requires its own legal and fiscal authority.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Part XVI is best understood as a constitutional portfolio rather than a single
quota rule. It secures presence in legislatures, recognises service claims,
creates safeguard institutions and protects list integrity through different
procedures. The modern challenge is to combine stable recognition with
evidence-based distribution and federal responsiveness. Substantive equality
requires that benefits reach the most excluded, but neither States nor courts
may use redistribution to rewrite constitutional lists outside the prescribed
process.
