                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The Prime Minister-led Council of Ministers is India's real Union executive.
Articles 74 and 75 make ministerial advice, Lok Sabha confidence and collective
responsibility the constitutional foundations of executive authority. The Prime
Minister is the hinge connecting President, ministry, Parliament, party and
administration. This creates effective leadership but also permits power to
shift from collective Cabinet government toward prime-ministerial government.
The constitutional test is whether leadership remains collegial, answerable to
the Lok Sabha and constrained by law, courts, federalism and internal process.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The Prime Minister is the organising centre of Union government, but the office
derives authority from a collectively responsible Council and a Lok Sabha
majority rather than an independent presidential mandate. Strong leadership can
coordinate a complex State; unchecked concentration can empty Cabinet and
Parliament of deliberation. The durable constitutional balance is leadership
through collective process, transparent business rules, parliamentary scrutiny,
federal consultation and judicially enforceable limits.
