                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
India deliberately has an official-language framework without a national
language. Part XVII makes Hindi in Devanagari the Union's official language,
continues English through parliamentary statute, permits States to choose their
own official languages, protects linguistic minorities and preserves English
for authoritative higher-court and legislative texts. This is a federal
accommodation, not a hierarchy of citizenship. Eighth-Schedule recognition,
classical-language status and education's three-language formula are distinct
constitutional, executive and policy categories.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
India's language Constitution is deliberately plural: Hindi carries Union
official status, English supplies continuity and inter-regional neutrality,
States retain linguistic autonomy and minorities receive access safeguards.
This settlement works through accommodation rather than a national-language
hierarchy. Its future lies in high-quality translation, mother-tongue capacity,
federal consultation and transparent recognition criteria, so multilingualism
becomes administrative capability rather than political suspicion.
