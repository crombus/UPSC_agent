                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Indian statecraft joins two forms of constitutional autonomy. Internally,
national integration means shared citizenship and fraternity without erasing
linguistic, religious, tribal or regional diversity. Externally, strategic
autonomy means independent judgment without isolation from partnerships.
Accommodation, rights, federal consultation and development build domestic
legitimacy; executive flexibility, parliamentary law, treaty accountability
and diversified cooperation convert that legitimacy into external capacity.
Uniformity is not unity, and autonomy is not equidistance or disengagement.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
India remains integrated not because difference disappears, but because
constitutional institutions allow difference to seek dignity, resources and
autonomy within a common republic. The same principle animates foreign policy:
independence is sustained by diverse partnerships, domestic capability and
lawful accountability rather than diplomatic solitude. Durable statecraft
therefore combines fraternity with pluralism at home and strategic autonomy
with responsible cooperation abroad. Legitimacy supplies capacity; capacity
