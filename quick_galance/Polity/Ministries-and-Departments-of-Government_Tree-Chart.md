                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Union executive power is formally expressed in the President's name but is
politically exercised through a Council of Ministers and administratively
processed through ministries, departments, the Central Secretariat and
executive agencies. Article 77(3) creates the operating bridge: Allocation of
Business Rules decide who handles a subject, while Transaction of Business
Rules decide how important proposals are consulted, escalated and approved.
Sound machinery combines ministerial responsibility, professional advice,
inter-departmental coordination and implementation autonomy.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The machinery of government succeeds when authority, advice, implementation
and answerability remain traceable. Article 77 allows flexible organisation,
but moving boxes on an organogram cannot by itself create policy capability.
The Central Secretariat must provide evidence and institutional memory,
executive agencies need bounded autonomy, and the Cabinet Secretariat and PMO
should resolve cross-government priorities without absorbing routine
departmental responsibility. Coordination should enable ministers and
departments, not blur who decided and who must answer.
