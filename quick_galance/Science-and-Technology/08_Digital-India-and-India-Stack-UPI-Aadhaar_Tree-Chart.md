                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Digital public infrastructure is a set of interoperable rails for identity,
payments, data and documents. It is not one application or database. Public value
depends on open interfaces, regulated institutions, user consent, privacy, security,
competition, accessibility and reliable offline alternatives. Aadhaar establishes
identity/authentication within its legal scope; UPI authorises and routes payments;
neither by itself proves citizenship, solvency, data accuracy or freedom from fraud.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
India's digital public infrastructure shows how reusable identity, payment, document
and consent rails can lower transaction costs and widen access. Its legitimacy,
however, rests on more than scale: lawful purpose, security, competition, meaningful
user control, accessibility, reliable redress and non-digital fallback are essential
to ensure that digital efficiency strengthens rather than conditions citizenship.
