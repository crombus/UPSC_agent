                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Intellectual property is a differentiated legal architecture: patents protect
qualifying inventions, copyright protects original expression, trademarks protect
commercial source identifiers, geographical indications protect collective
place-linked reputation, designs protect visual appearance, plant-variety law
protects varieties with farmers' rights, and trade secrets protect confidential
know-how. Exclusive rights are territorial, limited by subject matter and public
interest, and valuable only when disclosure, examination, enforcement, licensing
and commercialisation function.
