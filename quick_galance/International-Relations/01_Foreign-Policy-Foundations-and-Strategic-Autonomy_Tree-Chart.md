                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
India's foreign-policy labels have changed from non-alignment to strategic
autonomy and multi-alignment, but the durable principle is freedom to decide
each issue through India's own interest calculus. Autonomy is not neutrality,
equidistance or absence of commitments. It requires domestic economic,
technological, military and institutional capacity, diversified partnerships,
normative credibility and the ability to bear the cost of saying yes, no or
conditional yes to competing powers.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Strategic autonomy is the capacity to make consequential choices, not the
language used to describe them. India's non-aligned inheritance remains
relevant because contemporary multi-alignment still seeks freedom from
exclusive bloc discipline. That freedom will endure only if economic,
technological, defence and diplomatic capabilities expand faster than external
dependencies, allowing principles and interests to be reconciled through
credible, cost-bearing choices.
