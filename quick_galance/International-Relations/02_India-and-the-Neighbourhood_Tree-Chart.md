India's neighbourhood policy must manage extreme asymmetry without producing
fear of domination. Geography makes neighbouring stability integral to India's
security, growth, connectivity and social cohesion. Neighbourhood First and the
Gujral Doctrine combine non-reciprocal accommodation, development partnership,

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
India cannot choose its geography, but it can choose whether its asymmetry is
experienced as reassurance or dominance. Non-reciprocal support, connectivity
and first-responder capacity create influence only when projects are delivered,
sovereignty is respected and engagement survives regime change. A stable
neighbourhood requires India to combine security vigilance with people-centred
diplomacy, federal consultation and development partnerships that expand the
neighbour's choices as well as India's own.
