                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
For India, the Indo-Pacific is an inclusive strategic geography joining the
Indian and Pacific Oceans, while the Indian Ocean remains the primary security
and development theatre. Maritime order rests on international law, open sea
lanes, sovereign equality, cooperative institutions, domain awareness and
capacity building. India seeks a free, open, inclusive and rules-based region
without treating the concept as a closed military alliance or a synonym for
containment of one country.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
India's maritime strategy succeeds when strategic deterrence, international law
and regional public goods reinforce one another. The Quad and stronger naval
capacity matter, but neither can substitute for ASEAN centrality, IORA, IONS,
domain awareness, island partnerships and sustainable ocean governance.
SAGAR and MAHASAGAR therefore require measurable implementation while BBNJ and
UNCLOS statuses must be described with exact legal and temporal caution.
