                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
India simultaneously manages strategic mistrust and economic dependence with
China, technology and defence convergence with the United States, and installed-
base defence and energy complementarity with Russia, while diversifying through
Japan and the European Union. Supply-chain resilience means de-risking critical
dependencies through domestic capacity and trusted partnerships, not replacing
China instantly or entering an exclusive bloc. Strategic autonomy is tested by
the cost, reliability and political conditions attached to each partnership.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
India's major-power strategy is not a choice between Washington, Moscow and
Beijing but a management problem involving different benefits and dependencies.
Western supply-chain interest creates opportunity, yet tariff volatility and
technology controls show that partnership is not insurance. Russia's installed
base cannot be replaced abruptly, and China cannot be economically decoupled
without high cost. Resilience therefore requires diversified partnerships plus
competitive domestic production, technology and logistics.
