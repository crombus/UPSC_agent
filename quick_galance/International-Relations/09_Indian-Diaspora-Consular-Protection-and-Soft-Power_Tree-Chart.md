                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The overseas Indian community is simultaneously a human constituency requiring
protection, a bridge of knowledge and goodwill, and a possible source of
political friction. Diaspora policy must therefore separate legal categories,
routine consular work, migrant-worker protection, crisis evacuation, economic
linkages and cultural influence. Soft power cannot be commanded like coercive
power: it depends on credibility, pluralism, host-country agency and sustained
institutional coordination.
