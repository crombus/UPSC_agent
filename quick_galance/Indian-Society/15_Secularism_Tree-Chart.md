                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Secularism is a normative and institutional arrangement for equal citizenship,
freedom of conscience, non-domination and peaceful coexistence in a religiously
diverse society. Indian secularism is not adequately described as either strict
state-religion separation or equal respect alone. Its operative logic is
principled distance: the state may abstain, engage, regulate or support
religious institutions according to liberty, equality, dignity, reform and
minority protection. The central test is not whether religion is visible in
public, but whether public power protects freedom without establishing
religious hierarchy or allowing internal domination.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Indian secularism is sustained neither by privatising religion completely nor
by treating every practice as beyond public scrutiny. It requires principled,
consistent state distance; protection of conscience and minority identity;
reform of domination within communities; and everyday institutions that make
equal citizenship real. Its durable social foundation is pluralism joined to
rights and fraternity, not coerced assimilation or passive tolerance.
