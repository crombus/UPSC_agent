                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
"Tribe" is not a single timeless sociological type. Scheduled Tribe is a
constitutional notification under Article 342, while communities differ in
language, kinship, livelihood, religion, territory, political organisation and
market connection. Isolation, assimilation and integration are competing
historical approaches, not stages all communities follow. Contemporary tribal
development is powerfully shaped by land alienation, project displacement and
the quality of rehabilitation, but health, education, self-government, cultural
recognition and control over resources remain indispensable.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Tribal development cannot be reduced to welfare delivery or to a single
administrative category. India's policy history moved from protection and
absorption toward integration, yet projects and markets often expanded faster
than rights and agency. Displacement and rehabilitation remain the principal
arena of conflict, but the outcome depends on land, livelihood, community
continuity, self-government, health, education and cultural recognition.
Aggregation is necessary for rights and dangerous for diagnosis.
