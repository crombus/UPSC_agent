                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Family, household, marriage and kinship are distinct institutions organising
care, reproduction, residence, descent, alliance, inheritance, authority and
identity. Separate nuclear residence does not necessarily end extended-family
obligation; matriliny does not automatically mean matriarchy; legal capacity to
marry does not equal social acceptance. Contemporary change redistributes
family functions among kin, market, state, workplace and digital systems rather
than simply destroying family.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Indian family change is multi-track. Urbanisation and technology favour
separate residence, mediated interaction and individual choice, but financial,
ritual and care obligations keep kinship consequential. Marriage can become
more companionate without losing every sacramental or endogamous function, and
matrilineal exceptions demonstrate institutional diversity without proving
female dominance. Analysis must separate form from function, law from
acceptance and demographic indicators from lived family relations.
