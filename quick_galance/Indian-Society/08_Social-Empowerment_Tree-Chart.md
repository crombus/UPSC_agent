                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Social empowerment is the expansion of real capability, agency, resources,
recognition, representation and institutional voice. Welfare input, legal
entitlement, capability-conversion factor and lived outcome are distinct
stages. Equal entitlement can yield unequal outcomes where schooling, health,
mobility, information, accessibility or stigma differ. Formal mobility in
income or occupation becomes substantive empowerment only when accompanied by
dignity, decision power and freedom from residual social closure.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Empowerment is completed only when a formal right becomes usable capability,
recognised mobility and independent voice. Phule's cross-cutting reform and
Ambedkar's graded-inequality diagnosis explain why legal equality can coexist
with unequal conversion. More entitlement may be necessary, but equal schooling,
accessibility, information, freedom from stigma and participatory institutions
determine whether underprivileged groups obtain substantive benefit.
