                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Caste is lived through locally ranked, birth-ascribed and mainly endogamous
jatis, not directly through the four-varna textual ideal. Its occupational and
public ritual functions have weakened unevenly, while marriage boundaries,
discrimination, associations, electoral mobilisation and network capital
persist. Caste is therefore fluid in rank, occupation and public register yet
static in hereditary membership and endogamous reproduction. Ritual rank,
economic class and political power overlap without being identical.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Caste has changed its register more than its reproductive boundary. Education,
markets and urban work loosen occupational allocation and status rank, while
associations and electoral networks repurpose caste as collective capital.
Endogamy, hereditary identity and unequal dignity persist unevenly. Caste is
therefore neither a relic nor an unchanged total system: it is a multi-arena
institution whose ritual, material, political and intimate dimensions change at
different speeds.
