                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Population change is a social process mediated by education, health, gender
agency, marriage timing, child-survival expectations, son preference, work,
urban costs and contraceptive access. Fertility decline is neither merely
technical nor a coercive population-control project. A national average hides
states at different stages: India can retain a youth-dividend opportunity while
some regions already face ageing and below-replacement-fertility pressures.
Replacement fertility does not produce immediate zero growth because population
momentum depends on inherited age structure.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
India's population question has shifted from a single concern with growth to
uneven fertility, reproductive agency, youth employment and ageing. One
national TFR cannot diagnose every state, and replacement fertility does not
stop growth immediately. Policy should expand informed voluntary choice,
prepare regionally for different age structures and use Census, SRS and NFHS
for their proper purposes. Demography supplies constraints and opportunities;
social capability determines their outcome.
