                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Indian society is a layered, plural and unequal social formation. Language,
religion, region, caste, tribe, class, gender and rural-urban location overlap
rather than run in parallel. Diversity is the fact of difference; plurality is
coexistence with recognition; disparity is unequal outcome; marginality is
exclusion from resources, opportunity or voice. Diversity correlates with
marginality only where historical exclusion, geographic remoteness and low
assets reinforce the same identity. Difference is a marker, not an automatic
cause, of deprivation.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Indian society sustains culture through decentralised transmission,
accommodative absorption and territorial accommodation rather than insulation.
The same institutions can preserve hierarchy, so continuity cannot be
romanticised. Cultural diversity becomes socio-economic marginality only when
identity aligns with exclusion, remoteness and asset poverty. Policy must
therefore protect difference while disaggregating disadvantage and matching
recognition, redistribution and voice to the actual mechanism of exclusion.
