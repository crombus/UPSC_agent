                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Social change is any significant alteration in social structure, institution,
relation, norm, value or behaviour over time. Modernisation is one historically
specific pathway associated with differentiation, rationalisation, scientific
orientation, mobility, markets, bureaucracy, education, communication and
individual choice. It is neither synonymous with westernisation nor a linear
replacement of tradition. In India, constitutional reform, democracy,
industrialisation, urbanisation, education, social movements, technology and
globalisation interact with caste, kinship, religion and regional diversity,
creating selective adoption, resistance, reinterpretation and hybrid outcomes.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Indian modernisation is plural, contested and incomplete. Modern institutions
do not simply erase tradition; they can weaken, recast or even amplify older
identities and hierarchies. Social change becomes substantively progressive
only when constitutional equality, public capability, scientific temper and
democratic participation convert technological and economic transformation
into dignity, agency and security across caste, class, gender and region.
