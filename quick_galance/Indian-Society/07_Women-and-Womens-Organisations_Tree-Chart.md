                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Women's status must be assessed through rights, resources, unpaid care, paid
work, property, bodily autonomy, mobility, safety, representation and voice.
Equality establishes a common rights baseline; equity removes unequal
constraints; empowerment is the lived capability to make and act upon choices.
Indian women's organising is layered—reform associations, national bodies,
rights campaigns, labour unions, autonomous movements and SHG federations
overlap. Membership, account ownership, labour-force entry or legal protection
does not alone prove agency.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Women's empowerment is reached not when a right, account, job or group
membership exists, but when women can exercise recognised control over
resources, time, mobility and decisions. Indian women's organising has expanded
from reform and legal advocacy to labour and grassroots collective action, yet
different women encounter different conversion barriers. Programme design must
therefore combine equality with intersectional equity and measure substantive
voice rather than institutional coverage alone.
