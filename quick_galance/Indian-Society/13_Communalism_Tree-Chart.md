                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Communalism is a political-social ideology and mobilisation that treats a
religious community as a homogeneous political interest, assumes its interests
are distinct from or hostile to those of other communities, and converts
difference into boundary, fear, competition or violence. Religion itself is
not communalism; religiosity, inter-community disagreement and an isolated
crime are not automatically communal. Communal outcomes arise through the
interaction of identity entrepreneurs, historical narratives, institutional
incentives, relative deprivation, segregation, rumours, impunity and triggering
events. Prevention therefore requires both security and structural inclusion.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Communalism is neither an automatic product of religious diversity nor a
spontaneous expression of ancient hostility. It is constructed when real or
perceived grievances are framed through exclusive identities and rewarded by
political or institutional incentives. A durable response combines impartial
law enforcement and justice with inclusive development, shared civic
institutions, critical public communication and constitutional fraternity.
