                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Rural society is a changing relation among caste, class, land, labour, credit,
kinship, Panchayat, market and state; rural is not identical to agricultural.
Jajmani ties have often given way to cash and non-farm work, yet personalised
and ritual relations can persist. Agrarian technology can raise output while
deepening differentiation where land, irrigation, credit and procurement are
unequal. Dominant-caste power can be challenged by reservation and migration
without automatically disappearing. A rural-urban continuum is more accurate
than an isolated-village model.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Rural India is neither a timeless village order nor an urban society waiting
to emerge. Markets, technology, migration and local democracy have altered
customary service and agrarian relations, while land, caste, gender and
informal networks continue to organise power. The decisive question is which
resource now carries dominance—land, office, credit, network or non-farm
income—and whether formal inclusion translates into substantive control over
livelihood and public decisions.
