                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Poverty is not exhausted by low income. It can be monetary, multidimensional,
chronic, transient, relative, socially excluded or vulnerable-to-shock.
Amartya Sen's capability approach asks whether people can actually be healthy,
educated, secure and socially participating. Coping behaviours observed among
the poor may be adaptations to insecurity rather than an autonomous culture
causing poverty. Development and conservation can genuinely conflict where
resource-dependent communities lose livelihood access; design and power decide
whether sustainability is exclusionary or participatory.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Poverty persists when income, assets, services, security, recognition and voice
fail together. It cannot be corrected by blaming coping behaviour, citing one
headcount or expanding schemes without improving conversion and accountability.
Sustainable development may impose real costs on resource-dependent poor
communities, but participatory design, rights, livelihood restoration and clear
institutional roles can reduce the conflict. Development succeeds when people
gain durable capability, not merely temporary programme contact.
