                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Globalisation is the intensified cross-border flow of goods, services, capital,
people, information and culture. It is not identical to liberalisation,
privatisation, westernisation or consumerism. Each channel produces uneven and
sometimes contradictory effects: markets may homogenise brands while
glocalisation adapts them; urban work may enlarge women's freedom while kinship
ties persist; technology may expand access while widening digital and material
inequality. Outcomes depend on skill, class, caste, gender, region, household
bargaining, firm strategy and public regulation.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Globalisation is not one force with one social result. It standardises some
markets and aspirations while local actors adapt products, language and
practice; it enlarges choice while exposing workers and families to new
precarity; it connects people while reproducing digital divides. Indian society
is neither passively homogenised nor untouched. Outcomes depend on bargaining
power, public regulation and the capacity of local institutions to convert
global flows into secure work, plural voice and sustainable consumption.
