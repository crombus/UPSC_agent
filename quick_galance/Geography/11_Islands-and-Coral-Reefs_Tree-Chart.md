                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
An island's origin governs its relief, resources, hazards and ecological
limits. Continental fragments, volcanic arcs, hotspot islands, coral atolls
and depositional islands must not be conflated. Reefs are living carbonate
factories whose survival depends on a narrow light-temperature-salinity-
sediment window. Low reef islands depend on healthy reefs for sediment and on
thin freshwater lenses for habitability. Strategic development must therefore
be tested cumulatively against geology, tsunami exposure, reef-rainforest
connectivity, water, carrying capacity and indigenous rights.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Indian islands cannot be reduced to strategic dots or scenic reefs. Andaman-
Nicobar is an active tectonic arc with volcanic and tsunami risk, while
Lakshadweep is a low coral system dependent on living carbonate production and
a fragile freshwater lens. Reefs can recover from short bleaching but repeated
heat, acidification and local damage reduce resilience. Development is
defensible only where alternatives, cumulative carrying capacity, indigenous
rights, disaster exposure and monitored ecological thresholds are treated as
core design conditions rather than post-clearance additions.
