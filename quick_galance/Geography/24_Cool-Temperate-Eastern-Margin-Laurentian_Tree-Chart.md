                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The Laurentian climate is the cool eastern-margin intermediate between the
maritime British and continental Siberian types. Westerlies arrive from the
continental interior in winter, bringing severe cold and relative dryness;
summer maritime easterlies and storms create a rainfall maximum. Offshore
warm-cold current convergence over broad shelves produces both fog and
productive fishing grounds. India has no true Laurentian region; the cool,
humid Eastern Himalaya is an altitude-controlled analogue.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Laurentian geography shows how continental outflow and maritime summer inflow
can make an eastern coast climatically severe yet economically oriented toward
forest and sea. Current convergence supplies a productive possibility and a
fog hazard, but governance decides whether fisheries endure. The Eastern
Himalaya offers a useful cool-humid comparison through altitude and monsoon,
while its tea and transport economy is inseparable from slope, sediment and
Teesta-basin risk. Analogy must preserve these causal differences.
