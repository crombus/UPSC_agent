                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Weathering creates regolith in situ; erosion removes it by an external agent;
mass movement transfers it downslope chiefly under gravity; groundwater moves
through pores, fractures and solution openings. These systems interact through
rock, climate, slope, vegetation, time and land use. Rainfall is often a trigger,
not a complete landslide cause; pumping is a stress, not proof of one depletion
mechanism. Sound analysis therefore follows material and structure -> antecedent
moisture -> trigger -> movement or aquifer response -> exposure and consequence.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Weathering, slope movement, soil and groundwater are one connected near-surface
system. Rock breakdown supplies soil and debris; slope and water decide whether
it remains, creeps or fails; infiltration can replenish an aquifer or rapidly
carry pollution into it. India's policy challenge is therefore not solved by a
single retaining wall, recharge pit or national average. It requires
site-specific geology, drainage and aquifer mapping, maintenance, demand
control, community-scale monitoring and honest attribution that separates
long-term susceptibility from the immediate trigger.
