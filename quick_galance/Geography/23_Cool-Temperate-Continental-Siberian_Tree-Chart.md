                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The Siberian climate is the high-latitude continental interior regime of the
Northern Hemisphere: weak maritime moderation produces long severe winters,
short cool summers, low summer-maximum precipitation and a great annual range.
Taiga conifers fit the short season and snow load, while cold slows
decomposition and stores carbon in soil, peat and permafrost. India has no
continental taiga; its subalpine-conifer-alpine sequence is an altitudinal
analogue.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The Siberian climate exists because broad northern continents permit extreme
continentality at high latitude. Its cold creates both commercially uniform
softwood forests and enormous vulnerable below-ground carbon stores. Himalayan
subalpine forests and alpine meadows parallel the vertical sequence but are
controlled by monsoon, aspect and altitude rather than continental latitude.
Management must distinguish taiga, treeline, meadow and permafrost processes
and avoid turning one seasonal observation into a biome-wide trend.
