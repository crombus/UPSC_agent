                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Climate classification compresses long-period temperature, precipitation and
seasonality into comparable regions. Koppen is empirical and vegetation-linked:
it describes patterns efficiently but does not explain circulation. A code is
not a biome, crop system, hazard zone or administrative unit. Boundaries are
thresholds across real gradients and can move when the underlying climate
normal changes, so every modern map requires dataset, baseline and resolution.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Koppen endures because it converts routine climate data into a simple,
comparable map, not because it captures every cause. India demonstrates both
its utility and its limits: relief separates wet Ghats from dry Deccan at the
same latitude, monsoon seasonality creates adjacent codes, and altitude
compresses cold climates into the Himalaya. Classification should therefore be
used as a descriptive first layer, supplemented by circulation and water-
balance analysis, with every boundary treated as scale-, convention- and
baseline-dependent.
