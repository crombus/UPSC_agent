                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Regional diversity is unranked difference; regional disparity is measurable
inequality that disadvantages one territory. Development clusters because
initial advantage attracts capital, skills, infrastructure and markets.
Planning seeks to strengthen spread effects and restrain backwash, but a growth
node becomes an enclave when local supplier, labour, fiscal and transport links
remain weak. India's planning style shifted from central plan outlays toward
mission, index, district and block targeting; the geography problem persists.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Regional disparity is not India's diversity but the unequal conversion of
location, resources and public investment into capabilities and opportunity.
Markets may spread growth, yet cumulative advantage often strengthens cores and
drains peripheries. Plans, corridors and dashboards matter only when nodes build
local linkages and administrative capacity turns allocations into outcomes.
Balanced development therefore means reducing avoidable disadvantage through
place-specific strategy, not making every region economically identical.
