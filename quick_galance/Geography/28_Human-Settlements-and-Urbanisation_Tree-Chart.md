                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Settlement form is the spatial expression of site, situation, livelihood,
transport, security, history and planning. Urbanisation is not merely more
people or buildings: it is a shift toward non-primary functions, density,
networks and regional command. India's distinctive problem is mismatch between
functional urban regions and administrative boundaries, especially census
towns and peri-urban belts. Urban growth generates productivity while weak
housing, drainage, mobility and municipal capacity externalise its costs.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Urbanisation is an engine of productivity and non-farm opportunity, but its
quality depends on whether governance follows the functional city. Census towns,
outgrowths and peri-urban belts reveal development beyond municipal capacity,
while impermeable surfaces and wetland loss turn growth into flood and heat
risk. The solution is not to resist cities but to align boundaries, finance,
housing, transit and blue-green infrastructure with actual settlement systems
and to measure service outcomes rather than project announcements.
