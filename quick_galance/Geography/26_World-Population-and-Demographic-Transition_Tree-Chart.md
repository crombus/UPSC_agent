                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Population geography studies where people live, how numbers change and how
age, sex, health, education and work composition vary spatially. Growth is the
balance of births, deaths and migration, not the birth rate alone. Demographic
transition describes the lagged fall of mortality and fertility; it is a
generalisation, not a universal timetable. A working-age bulge is only a
potential dividend, and below-replacement fertility does not immediately stop
growth because age structure creates momentum.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Demographic change is not a simple movement from high to low population growth
but a spatially uneven restructuring of ages, households and labour. Mortality
and fertility decline create momentum, opportunity and eventually ageing.
India's national averages conceal states at different points in that sequence.
The dividend is therefore conditional and time-bound, while demographic winter
is a warning description rather than a formal stage. Reliable answers must
separate census counts, survey estimates and projections before drawing policy.
