                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Savanna is a tropical transition between equatorial forest and desert. Seasonal
ITCZ migration creates a wet summer and dry winter; moisture duration limits
tree recruitment while fire, grazing, herbivory, soil and land use maintain a
grass-tree mosaic. It is therefore neither climatic climax alone nor simply
degraded forest. India's closest analogue grades from moist deciduous to dry
deciduous, thorn scrub and distinct grasslands whose hydrology and management
cannot be reduced to one rainfall threshold.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Savanna is a coupled climate-disturbance landscape. Seasonal moisture permits
grass and scattered trees, while fire and herbivory regulate their balance.
India's deciduous, thorn and grassland systems echo that logic but differ
sharply in hydrology and management. Conservation must reject both the myth
that all open land is degraded forest and the opposite claim that disturbance
is always benign. The goal is a native, connected mosaic with fire, grazing
and livelihoods governed at the correct ecological scale.
