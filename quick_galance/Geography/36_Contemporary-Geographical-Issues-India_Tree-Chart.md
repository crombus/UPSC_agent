                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
A current issue becomes geographical when its location, distribution, terrain,
climate, basin, network, settlement or resource structure explains why it
occurs there and how effects spread. News is only an example; the answer must
identify process, pattern, natural and human drivers, scale, exposure,
vulnerability, trade-off and a mechanism-matched response. Most persistent
failures arise because aquifers, basins, catchments, airsheds, sediment cells
and labour markets cross the administrative boundaries expected to govern them.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
India's contemporary geographical issues are rarely failures of physical
knowledge alone. They occur where natural systems and administrative systems
do not share boundaries, where development concentrates exposure, and where
costs fall on groups with least adaptive capacity. Geography contributes by
locating the mechanism and choosing the scale of response. Durable solutions
therefore combine functional-region institutions, transparent dated data,
demand management, resilient land use and explicit treatment of competing
interests rather than headline-driven generic remedies.
