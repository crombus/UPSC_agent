                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The equatorial Af system is produced by persistent high solar input,
convergence, humidity and convection, giving uniform heat, year-round rainfall
and a multilayer evergreen forest. Its paradox is that immense biomass often
stands on strongly leached, nutrient-poor soil because nutrients cycle rapidly
through living matter. Human response is not environmentally predetermined:
technology, capital, access, disease, tenure and markets explain why some
equatorial regions remain sparsely settled while volcanic islands and urban-
plantation corridors support dense populations.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The equatorial climate sustains continuous biological production through
persistent heat and moisture, yet the same conditions accelerate decomposition
and leaching, leaving nutrients concentrated in biomass. Its rainforest is
therefore productive but vulnerable to clearance and repeated disturbance.
India's evergreen belts reproduce much of this structure through monsoon,
orography and insularity rather than a uniform Af climate. Their future depends
on maintaining moisture recycling, connectivity and viable community land use,
not on climatic labels or exclusionary protection alone.
