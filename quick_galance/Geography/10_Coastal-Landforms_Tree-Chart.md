                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
A coast is a moving interface where wave energy, sediment supply, tides,
currents, storms, relative sea level, rock structure and human works interact.
Erosion and accretion can occur simultaneously within one sediment cell.
Relative sea level, not global mean alone, determines local inundation.
Protection of one reach can transfer erosion downdrift, so regulation and
engineering must work at cell, estuary and delta scale rather than attempting
to freeze every shoreline.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
India's coast is both a resource frontier and a moving hazard boundary. Its
western estuaries and backwaters and eastern deltas and lagoons reflect
different relief and sediment histories, while local risk depends on relative
sea level, storms, subsidence and exposure. Hard protection remains necessary
for some assets but cannot be the universal answer because it transfers
sediment deficits. Coastal governance must integrate CRZ maps, sediment cells,
ecosystem buffers, warning and planned accommodation or retreat, with every
shoreline statistic tied to its assessment period.
