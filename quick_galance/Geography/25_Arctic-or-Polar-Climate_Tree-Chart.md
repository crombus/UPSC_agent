                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Polar regions are cold deserts governed by low solar angle, polar night, high
albedo and low atmospheric moisture. Tundra and ice cap are distinct, as are
Arctic sea ice, Greenland grounded ice, Antarctic grounded ice and floating ice
shelves. Their losses have different sea-level effects. Arctic amplification
arises from reinforcing feedbacks, while links to midlatitude weather remain
less certain than direct albedo, sea-level, permafrost and ecosystem effects.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Polar change cannot be discussed as one undifferentiated melting process.
Arctic sea ice controls albedo and access, Greenland and Antarctic grounded ice
control direct sea-level addition, ice shelves buttress land ice, and
permafrost links climate to carbon and infrastructure. Ladakh offers an Indian
cold-desert comparison but not a polar climate. Policy must separate these
mechanisms, grade uncertain weather teleconnections honestly and sustain
long-term observation through India's polar and Himalayan research systems.
