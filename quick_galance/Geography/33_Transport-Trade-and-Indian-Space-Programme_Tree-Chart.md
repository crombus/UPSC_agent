                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Transport changes relative accessibility; trade converts differences in
resources and productivity into flows. Modal choice depends on terminal cost,
distance, bulk, value, urgency and reliability, while modern freight is an
intermodal chain. Ports succeed through both site and hinterland situation;
chokepoints compress global movement into vulnerable passages. Space systems
are applied geographic infrastructure for navigation, communication, weather,
mapping and disaster support. Connectivity is necessary, never sufficient, for
regional development.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Transport and space systems compress distance but distribute advantage
unevenly. A corridor, port or satellite creates potential only when last-mile
links, local capacity and operational reliability convert it into access.
Chokepoints remain valuable because they shorten routes and dangerous because
they concentrate flows. India's integrated strategy must connect inland
production to ports, public transport to land use and satellite observation to
ground institutions while separating announcements, construction and outcomes.
