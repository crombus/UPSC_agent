                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
A boundary is a legal line; a frontier is a wider zone of transition; a
borderland is the lived space of movement, trade, kinship and security around
them. India's borders cross glaciated mountains, settled plains, shifting
rivers, forests, deserts and seas, so no uniform management instrument fits all.
Boundary morphology and genesis explain recurring problems but do not settle
sovereignty. Disputed alignments must use official maps and exact dated legal
language rather than confident reconstruction.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
India's boundary geography is plural: glaciated crest lines, settled plains,
mobile rivers, forests, deserts and shallow seas each create different legal,
logistical and human problems. Security cannot rely on one template, and
infrastructure can integrate communities while also signalling strategic
change. Effective management combines precise legal categories, official
cartography, terrain-specific technology and borderland livelihoods, while
preserving the distinction between claimed boundary, actual control and
negotiated settlement.
