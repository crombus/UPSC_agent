                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Deserts are moisture-deficit climates, not necessarily hot landscapes.
Subtropical subsidence, offshore flow, cold currents, continentality and rain
shadow produce distinct hot and mid-latitude regimes. Clear skies create large
diurnal ranges; continental interiors add severe winter cold and large annual
ranges. Human adaptation is organised around water access, while modern canals,
groundwater and infrastructure relax one constraint but can create salinity,
depletion, habitat fragmentation and unequal access.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Hot and mid-latitude deserts share moisture deficit but differ sharply in
thermal regime and geographic cause. The Thar is a monsoon-margin desert whose
parallel Aravalli orientation matters alongside subsidence, continentality and
weak moisture penetration. Water engineering and renewable infrastructure have
expanded opportunity but also redistributed salinity, depletion and habitat
risk. Sustainable desert development must preserve mobility, traditional water
systems and open ecosystems while placing new infrastructure through
landscape-scale, evidence-led planning.
