                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The ocean redistributes heat, freshwater, nutrients, carbon and oxygen.
Surface currents arise mainly from wind stress, Coriolis deflection, pressure
gradients, basin shape and friction; deep circulation arises substantially
from density contrasts created by temperature and salinity. Tides are
astronomical oscillations, not currents or seasons. The North Indian Ocean is
exceptional because monsoon winds reverse its surface circulation. ENSO and
IOD alter probabilities through coupled ocean-atmosphere feedbacks; neither
determines India's monsoon alone.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Ocean circulation is a coupled heat, freshwater and nutrient system rather than
a list of arrows. Wind and Coriolis organise the surface, density helps ventilate
the depth, and basin geometry modifies both. The monsoon reverses northern
Indian Ocean currents, while ENSO and IOD redistribute convection across
different basins and timescales. Their influence on rainfall and fisheries is
powerful but probabilistic. Strong answers therefore attach season to every
current, mechanism to every fishery claim and date to every observed phase.
