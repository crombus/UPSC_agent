                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Resource geography begins with geology but ends with economics and strategy.
An occurrence becomes a resource only when grade, depth, technology, access,
price, law and environmental limits permit use; a reserve is the recoverable
subset under a stated classification and date. Shields favour many metallic
ores, basins host fuels, weathering can concentrate bauxite and coasts sort
placers, but no setting guarantees a deposit. The energy transition overlays
coal-oil geography with solar-wind corridors and critical-mineral processing
chokepoints.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Mineral and energy maps are inherited from Earth history but continually
rewritten by technology, infrastructure and strategic demand. India's shield,
Gondwana basins, sedimentary margins and renewable corridors provide different
advantages, none of which automatically becomes an economic reserve or secure
supply. The critical-mineral era shifts attention from extraction toward
processing, recycling and diversified chains, while the energy transition must
distinguish installed capacity from dependable generation and account for land,
water, ecosystem and community costs.
