                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Warm-temperate eastern margins combine hot wet summers with cooler drier
winters because seasonal continental pressure reversal draws warm maritime air
onshore in summer and permits continental outflow in winter. The same summer
moisture that supports intensive rice-tea agriculture also brings typhoon,
flood and erosion hazard. China, Gulf and Natal subtypes share eastern-margin
position but differ in winter severity because landmass size and maritime
moderation differ.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The China type is an eastern-margin summer-rain system whose agricultural
advantage and cyclone exposure arise from the same maritime mechanism. Its
regional variants differ because continental scale changes winter outflow.
India's Bengal-Northeast belt is a useful analogue, not one uniform copied
climate class. Analysis must connect Bay moisture with relief, river sediment,
settlement and institutions, thereby treating flood as both geomorphic renewal
and hazard rather than as rainfall alone.
