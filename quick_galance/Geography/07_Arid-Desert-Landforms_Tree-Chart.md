                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
A desert is defined by persistent moisture deficit, not heat or sand.
Atmospheric subsidence, continentality, rain shadow, cold currents or polar
cold create aridity. Sparse cover exposes sediment to wind, but episodic
flash floods shape much desert relief. Desertification is different: it is
degradation of productive drylands through climatic variation and human
pressure. The Thar is therefore a mosaic of dune fields, rocky surfaces,
playas, irrigated tracts and semi-arid margins, not a uniform advancing sea
of sand.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Arid landscapes are produced by atmospheric and geographic controls but shaped
jointly by wind and episodic water. The Thar's dunes, rocky plains, playas,
pastoral systems and irrigated tracts make it a changing mosaic. Desertification
is not an inevitable march of sand; it is a potentially reversible decline in
dryland function whose causes vary by place. Restoration must therefore match
the process, protect commons and water budgets, and distinguish greater
satellite greenness from durable ecological and livelihood recovery.
