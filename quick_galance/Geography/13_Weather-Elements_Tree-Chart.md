                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Weather is a chain: unequal radiation creates temperature contrasts; these
create pressure gradients; pressure-gradient, Coriolis and friction forces
organise winds; winds transport heat and moisture; uplift and cooling produce
cloud and precipitation. Surface weather is embedded in upper-air waves and
jets. A western disturbance is not a monsoon branch, a heat dome is not a
cyclone, and a severe event becomes disaster through terrain and exposure.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Weather emerges from linked radiative, thermodynamic and dynamic processes
across surface and upper air. India's mountains, continental interior and warm
seas then convert those processes into distinctive WDs, Loo winds, cyclones
and cloudbursts. Better observation and high-resolution forecasting reduce
uncertainty but do not abolish it. The strongest explanation identifies energy,
force, moisture, uplift and terrain separately, and the strongest policy answer
links forecasts to land use, resilient construction and last-mile action.
