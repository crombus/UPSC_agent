                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Regional geography explains how a distinctive combination of location, relief,
climate, water, resources, population, economy and connectivity produces a
regional personality. Continents, statistical regions, cultural labels,
economic blocs and states are different units. Map questions are solved by
placing a feature inside a physical system and a route network, not by
memorising isolated country names. Geography constrains development and power;
history, institutions and connectivity determine outcomes.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Regional geography is valuable because it converts a map into an explanation:
physical structure defines possibilities, situation connects or isolates, and
institutions transform both. India's outward geography is maritime-heavy to
west and east and barrier-heavy to the north. Yet no regional label is neutral
or timeless. Strong answers declare the unit being used, compare through common
axes, distinguish country from region and treat routes, classifications and
political status as dated relationships rather than memorised names.
