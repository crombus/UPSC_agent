                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Migration is selective movement that changes usual residence across a meaningful
boundary. It is shaped not by push and pull alone but by distance, expected
income, intervening opportunities, networks, household strategy, gender,
law and shocks. Migration can be opportunity and distress in the same corridor.
It redistributes labour and dependency between origin and destination, while
welfare systems tied to residence often lag behind mobile lives.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Migration is neither pathology nor automatic development. It is a selective
household and individual response to uneven opportunity, risk and social ties.
Large cities dominate because their labour markets and networks offer several
chances rather than one guaranteed job. Migration can reduce poverty and supply
urban labour while transferring housing, care and welfare costs. Policy should
make movement safer and benefits portable while creating credible intermediate-
town and source-region opportunities, not restrict mobility itself.
