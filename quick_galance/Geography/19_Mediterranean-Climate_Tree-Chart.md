                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Mediterranean climate is the warm-temperate western-margin regime produced by
seasonal pressure-belt migration: subtropical-high subsidence gives dry sunny
summers, while equatorward-shifted westerlies and travelling depressions bring
mild winter rain. Summer drought shapes sclerophyll vegetation, orchard-vine
agriculture, irrigation demand and wildfire risk. India's Himalayan orchards
are a functional temperate-horticulture analogue, not proof that Kashmir or
Himachal possesses a true Mediterranean climate.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Mediterranean regions convert summer drought and winter rain into specialised
orchard, vine and cereal systems, but only through water storage, market access
and fire management. India's Himalayan fruit belt shares the horticultural
logic without sharing the climate classification. Its future cannot be inferred
from warming alone: chilling, altitude, frost, hail, water, cultivar and supply
chains interact. Climatic analogy is therefore useful only when its boundaries
are stated explicitly.
