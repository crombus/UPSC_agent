                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Vulcanism and earthquakes express the release of internal heat and accumulated
strain through specific tectonic and thermal settings. Hazard becomes disaster
only through the interaction of source, path, site condition, exposure,
vulnerability and capacity. The same magnitude can produce unequal loss because
depth, distance, alluvium, saturation, slope and construction differ. Seismic
zones are broad design-risk generalisations, monitoring detects events, and
warning estimates consequences; none predicts the exact time of an earthquake.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Volcanoes, earthquakes and tsunamis are not random events but spatial
expressions of plate boundaries, intraplate thermal or structural anomalies and
the transfer of energy through rock and water. Their consequences are never
fixed by magnitude alone: depth, sediment, saturation, slope, coast form,
construction and preparedness determine loss. India's collision belt,
intraplate pockets and island-arc margin require different maps and designs.
Because exact prediction remains unavailable, the defensible strategy is
mechanism-based monitoring, microzonation, compliant construction, protected
lifelines and warning systems that convert geophysical knowledge into action.
