                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
A lake is water occupying a terrestrial depression; its origin, water budget,
mixing, salinity, nutrient state and legal status are separate classifications.
Lakes are temporary on geomorphic time because outlets incise, sediment fills
basins and hydrological balances change. Wetland restoration must begin with
catchment, inflow, groundwater and connectivity, not cosmetic waterfront work.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Lakes and wetlands are basin-scale outcomes rather than isolated blue patches.
Their origin determines initial form, while inflow, groundwater, evaporation,
sediment, nutrients and human abstraction determine survival. Legal recognition
can focus attention but cannot replace hydrological connectivity or pollution
control. Effective conservation must therefore classify the basin correctly,
protect its catchment and seasonal regime, and treat every count, area and
status as dated evidence rather than a permanent attribute.
