                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Agricultural geography explains where production occurs through climate,
relief, soil, water, labour, tenure, technology, transport, market and policy.
Physical suitability creates possibility, not realised output. Crop seasons
express rainfall and temperature rhythms; agricultural systems combine labour
and market orientation; procurement geography converts local surplus into
national food security. Non-farm primary activities remain tightly tied to
physiography because fish, forests and minerals cannot be relocated.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Primary activities reveal the strongest connection between physical geography
and economic life, yet nature supplies only the map of possibility. Irrigation,
technology, transport, labour institutions and procurement convert suitability
into production and can also lock regions into ecologically costly paths.
India's next agricultural transition must retain food-security capacity while
diversifying water-stressed belts, strengthening rain-fed regions and matching
district interventions to soil, water, market and holding structure.
