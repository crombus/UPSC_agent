                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Temperate steppe is a mid-latitude continental-interior grassland shaped by
weak maritime influence, large annual temperature range and low-to-moderate
summer rainfall. Moisture deficit limits trees while grass-root turnover and
low leaching build deep humus-rich soils. These regions became world granaries
only when railways, mechanisation and markets converted physical potential into
commercial production. India's Punjab-Haryana-western UP wheat belt is an
economic analogue on alluvium, not a true chernozem steppe.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Temperate grasslands became granaries through a conjunction of fertile
root-built soils, level relief, suitable seasonal climate, mechanisation and
transport. Removing the protective sod also created erosion and soil-carbon
costs. India's wheat core reproduces the economic function through alluvium,
irrigation and procurement rather than steppe climate. Its durability now
depends on aligning price and market signals with groundwater, soil, residue
and heat adaptation rather than assuming the original production package can
expand indefinitely.
