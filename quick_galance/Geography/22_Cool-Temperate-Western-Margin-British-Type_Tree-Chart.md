                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The British or marine-west-coast climate is controlled by year-round onshore
westerlies, warm-current influence and travelling depressions. Maritime air
produces mild winters, cool summers, a small annual range and precipitation in
all seasons. The same latitude on an eastern margin receives continental air
and becomes colder and more seasonal. India's Himalayan temperate forests are
an altitudinal ecological analogue, not a true British maritime climate.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The British type demonstrates how prevailing wind direction and maritime
exposure can make western margins mild and reliable at latitudes where eastern
interiors are severe. Its economic value follows from climatic continuity but
also ports, markets and institutions. Himalayan temperate forests reproduce
some ecological features through altitude, yet their rainfall, fire and water
systems are monsoonal and topographic. Comparison is useful only when analogy
is not mistaken for climatic identity.
