                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Monitoring tracks whether implementation is proceeding; evaluation judges
whether a policy was relevant, effective, efficient, equitable, coherent,
sustainable and causally useful. Expenditure and outputs are necessary but do
not prove outcomes. A credible results system links a theory of change to
baselines, indicators, disaggregated data, independent verification,
counterfactual reasoning and an institutional decision to adapt, scale or stop.
Evidence that is collected but cannot change policy leaves the learning gap
intact.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Monitoring makes implementation visible; evaluation makes claims about success
contestable. Neither improves governance unless evidence is disaggregated,
independently tested and connected to a decision on budget, design or
continuation. India's OOMF, DMEO and dashboard systems provide an important
architecture, but the decisive shift is cultural: from rewarding expenditure
and countable activity to learning openly from outcomes, distribution and
failure.
