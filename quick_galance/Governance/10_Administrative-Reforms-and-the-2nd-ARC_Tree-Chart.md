                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Administrative reform changes the structures, rules, capabilities, incentives
and accountability through which public authority works. The First ARC reviewed
the early post-Independence administrative State; the Second ARC supplied
fifteen reports for a liberalised, technology-enabled and rights-oriented era.
Commission recommendations are expert, dated and non-binding. Their value lies
in precise diagnosis and routing; implementation must be proved separately
through an Act, rule, order, functioning institution and evaluated outcome.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The Second ARC remains the most useful map of contemporary Indian
administrative reform, but it is not a code of current law. Its reports should
be used to diagnose a precise failure, identify a proposed institutional
response and then test what was actually enacted, staffed and evaluated.
Reform succeeds when recommendations are sequenced through legal authority,
capacity, incentives and federal ownership rather than being cited as a
substitute for implementation evidence.
