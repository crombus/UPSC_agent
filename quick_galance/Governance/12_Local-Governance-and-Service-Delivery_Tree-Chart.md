                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Local governments are constitutionally recognised institutions of self-
government, but their service performance depends on actual devolution of
functions, finances and functionaries. Proximity improves information,
participation and accountability; it does not automatically overcome weak
capacity, State control, parastatal overlap or local elite capture. Functional
decentralisation requires clear activity mapping, predictable revenue, staff
control, integrated rural-urban planning and enforceable citizen accountability.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Local government improves service delivery only when constitutional recognition
is translated into operational control over the full service chain. Proximity
creates information and accountability advantages, but State-dependent finance,
deputed staff and parastatal fragmentation often leave elected bodies responsible
without authority. The next stage of decentralisation is therefore functionality:
clear activity maps, predictable funds, professional local cadres, regional
planning and citizen oversight.
