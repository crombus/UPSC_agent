                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Independent regulation delegates technical rule-making, supervision,
enforcement and sometimes adjudication to specialised statutory bodies.
Independence protects long-term public objectives from day-to-day political or
industry pressure; accountability prevents insulated technocracy and capture.
Legitimate regulation requires a clear legislative mandate, transparent
appointments, adequate expertise and finance, open consultation, functional
separation, reasoned orders, statutory appeal and constitutional review.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Independent regulation is justified by expertise and credible long-term rules,
not by insulation for its own sake. A regulator gains legitimacy when its
statutory objectives are clear, its personnel and data are protected from both
ministerial and industry capture, and affected citizens can participate,
understand reasons and appeal. The durable balance is operational autonomy
inside a transparent chain of legislative, judicial and public accountability.
