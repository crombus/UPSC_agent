                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Development is produced through an ecosystem of public authorities, communities,
multilateral and bilateral agencies, charitable trusts, foundations, CSR
programmes, NGOs and firms. Each actor carries a different legal form, funding
source and accountability chain. Non-state finance can add expertise,
flexibility and reach, but inclusion is not inherent in charity or expenditure:
it depends on who defines need, who participates, how benefits are distributed,
which institution can be compelled, and whether the intervention remains
sustainable after the project or donor exits.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The development industry expands public problem-solving capacity but also
relocates power over priorities, evidence and accountability. Its legitimacy
cannot be inferred from a charitable form or a statutory CSR outlay. Non-state
finance is most valuable when it is additional to public duty, responsive to

for institutional handover. Inclusion depends on the governance of development,
