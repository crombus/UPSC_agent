                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Citizen-centric administration organises public institutions around the
rights, tasks and lived experience of the citizen rather than departmental
convenience. Sevottam joins a clear service promise, an effective grievance
mechanism and the capability to deliver. A Citizens' Charter is useful but
usually non-enforceable; State Right to Public Services laws convert notified
standards into legal timelines and penalties. Portals and disposal targets
matter only when the citizen receives a reasoned, accessible and correctable
outcome.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Citizen-centric administration is achieved not by publishing a charter or
launching a portal, but by making the public authority's promise specific,
capable and correctable. RTS laws can add enforceability, while CPGRAMS and
single-window systems reduce navigation costs, yet neither works without
frontline capacity and inclusive access. The decisive test is whether the
citizen receives the service, understands the decision and can obtain a timely
remedy when administration fails.
