                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Civil society occupies the space between household, market and State. NGOs,
SHGs, associations and movements mobilise citizens, generate social capital,
deliver services, produce knowledge and scrutinise authority. Their non-State
status is structural, not evidence of being anti-State. Their democratic value
depends on internal governance, transparent finance, representative voice,
lawful advocacy and downward accountability; State regulation must secure
integrity and sovereignty without converting disagreement into illegitimacy.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Civil society strengthens governance when it converts dispersed citizens into
organised voice, mutual support and accountable partnership. Its legitimacy
does not require agreement with government, but it does require lawful conduct,
transparent resources and answerability to members and beneficiaries. SHGs and
NGOs should complement public systems, not become excuses for State withdrawal.
The democratic objective is a confident State that regulates genuine risk while
treating independent civic scrutiny as a source of correction.
