                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Government identifies the formal institutions that hold public authority;
governance describes the wider process through which the State, market, civil
society and citizens make and implement collective decisions; good governance
judges that process against rule of law, transparency, accountability,
participation, responsiveness, equity and effectiveness. Norms become outcomes
only when a clear legal duty, capable institution, accessible procedure,
adequate capacity, enforceable remedy and learning loop connect authority to
the citizen's lived result.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Good governance is neither a decorative list of virtues nor a single ranking.
It is the disciplined conversion of constitutional authority into lawful,
inclusive and effective outcomes through identifiable duties and correctable
failure. India already possesses many norms and instruments; the recurrent gap
lies between disclosure and remedy, announcement and delivery, and measurement
and learning. Governance improves when evidence reveals where the chain breaks
and institutional responsibility is redesigned at that precise point.
