                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
JAM, DBT, PFMS, SNA-SPARSH and the General Financial Rules form distinct
layers of India's delivery-finance architecture. JAM supplies identity,
accounts and communication; DBT transfers a benefit; PFMS tracks public funds;
SNA-SPARSH disciplines Centrally Sponsored Scheme releases; GFR governs
financial procedure and procurement. These tools can reduce identity-based
leakage and idle balances, but cannot by themselves correct faulty eligibility,
inadequate benefits, poor service quality or authentication-based exclusion.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Digital financial tools have made the path of public money more visible and
direct, but the shortest payment chain is not necessarily the fairest service
system. DBT must be judged alongside eligibility accuracy, payment recovery and
benefit adequacy; PFMS and SNA-SPARSH alongside State integration and service
continuity. Public financial management becomes citizen-centric only when every
failed transaction has an identifiable cause, responsible authority and timely
restoration route.
