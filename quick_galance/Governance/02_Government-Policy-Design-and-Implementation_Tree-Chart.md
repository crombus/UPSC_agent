                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
A policy is not implemented when it is announced or enacted; it is implemented
when lawful authority, rules, responsible agencies, finance, personnel,
technology, frontline discretion, grievance systems and evaluation convert the
decision into an accessible outcome. The implementation gap emerges from both
bad design and delivery failure. Effective policy therefore requires accurate
problem diagnosis, consultation, coherent incentives, convergence across levels,
guided street-level discretion and feedback that changes the next policy cycle.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Public policy succeeds when design anticipates implementation rather than

uniform central control or unlimited local discretion, but a clear national

is therefore the continuing constitutional and administrative life of policy,
