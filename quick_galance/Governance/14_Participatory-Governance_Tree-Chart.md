                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Participation improves governance when decision authority is located near
relevant information and those who will implement or bear the result. The
subsidiarity principle supports local decision-making but permits higher-level
action for scale, redistribution, rights and capacity. Attendance, consultation
or a digital comment box are not sufficient: meaningful participation requires
timely information, inclusive voice, defined influence over resources, a public
response and safeguards against elite capture.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Participation is valuable not because citizens are always correct, but because
they possess information, experience and legitimacy that distant institutions
cannot reproduce. Subsidiarity must therefore place authority near execution
while preserving higher-level standards, redistribution and technical support.
The test of participation is not how many meetings or comments occur, but
whether diverse citizens shape priorities and receive a reasoned, reviewable
response.
