                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Akbar converted Humayun's restored but vulnerable throne into a durable
empire by defeating Afghan and noble alternatives, ending regency,
building a composite Rajput-Mughal elite and linking conquest to
garrisons, jagirs, routes and administration. Accommodation and
coercion operated together; neither tolerant hero nor conqueror alone
explains the process.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Akbar's expansion endured because it was political and institutional,
not merely military. He neutralised rival claimants, diversified the
ruling class and converted regional conquest into service and revenue
relationships; yet massacres, sieges and continuing Mewar and eastern
resistance show that the composite empire remained coercive and uneven.
