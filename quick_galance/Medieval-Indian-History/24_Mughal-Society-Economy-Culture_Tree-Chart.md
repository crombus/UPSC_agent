                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Mughal India combined agrarian expansion, monetisation, Indian-led
commercial networks, large cities and institutional artistic production
with a sharply hierarchical rural and court order. Composite culture
emerged through patronage, translation and artisan collaboration, but
it neither erased caste/gender inequality nor converted commercial
dynamism into modern industrialisation.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Mughal India was neither a stagnant despotism nor a modern capitalist
economy. Agrarian surplus, Indian merchants, global trade and courtly
institutions sustained major urban and cultural production, while
technology, social mobility and integration remained constrained by
hierarchical property, caste, gender and weak links between learned
science and productive technique.
