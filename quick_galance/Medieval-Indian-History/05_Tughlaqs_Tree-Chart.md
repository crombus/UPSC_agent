                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The Tughlaq experience reveals two contrasting answers to the problem
of governing a vast Sultanate: Muhammad bin Tughlaq pursued ambitious
centralisation and experimentation beyond reliable administrative
capacity, while Firuz Shah exchanged coercive control for conciliation,
heredity and public works, buying peace but weakening the centre.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Muhammad bin Tughlaq exposed the gap between imperial imagination and
verification, logistics and regional consent; Firuz Shah reduced that
gap by accepting a narrower bargain and investing in the core, but
hereditary privilege, weaker audits and factional institutions shifted
the cost into the future. Together they show why Tughlaq expansion and
stability remained administratively fragile.
