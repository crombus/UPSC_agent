                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Alauddin's state linked Mongol defence, a large standing cavalry,
central revenue extraction and Delhi-centred market regulation.
Its impressive registers, supply controls, intelligence and coercion
show strong core capacity, but not an empire-wide welfare economy;
the integrated system depended heavily on one ruler and did not last.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Alauddin created the Sultanate's most tightly integrated core of
revenue, army, supply and price control. It demonstrated exceptional
Delhi-centred administrative reach under external pressure, but its
heavy agrarian cost, reliance on surveillance and personal coercion,
uneven geography and rapid post-1316 collapse prevent its description
as welfare, socialism or a durable empire-wide economic order.
