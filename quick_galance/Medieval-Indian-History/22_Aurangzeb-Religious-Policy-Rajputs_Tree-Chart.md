                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Aurangzeb was an orthodox Timurid ruler who invoked sharia yet retained
zawabit and political discretion. His jizya and temple policies carried
real symbolic and social costs, but religion alone cannot explain
rebellion or decline. The Marwar-Mewar rupture arose from succession,
khalisa, honour and clumsy imperial intervention as well as religious
context, damaging trust in Mughal guarantees.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Aurangzeb's religious turn had genuine exclusionary and symbolic
effects, but its political damage became greatest where it intersected
with succession, honour and imperial coercion. The mishandled
Marwar-Mewar crisis weakened a tested alliance; it contributed to,
rather than single-handedly caused, the empire's wider late-century
strain.
