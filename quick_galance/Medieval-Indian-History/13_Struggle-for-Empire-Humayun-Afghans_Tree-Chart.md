                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Humayun inherited a conquest, not a consolidated empire. His failures
in Gujarat and Bengal arose from the mismatch between imperial ambition
and a shallow Mughal ruling class, while Sher Khan converted Bihar,
Afghan networks, finance, fort strategy and battlefield judgement into
a credible alternative north Indian empire.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Humayun's fall was the failure of premature imperial consolidation,
not simply of character. Sher Khan succeeded because he joined Afghan
mobilisation to a Bihar-Bengal resource base and better strategy; yet
Humayun's Persian-backed return preserved the Mughal claim that Akbar
would later turn into a deeper and more locally rooted empire.
