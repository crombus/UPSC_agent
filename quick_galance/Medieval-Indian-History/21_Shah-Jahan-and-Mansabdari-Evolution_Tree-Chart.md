                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Shah Jahan presided over imperial consolidation and courtly grandeur
while the mansab-jagir system developed a widening mismatch between
rank claims and realizable revenue. The month-scale kept the machinery
working, but Deccan shortfalls, mansab inflation, frontier campaigns
and succession competition incubated later jagirdari strain.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Shah Jahan's empire remained administratively formidable, but its
expanding service elite generated claims faster than realizable jagir
income. The month-scale managed rather than solved this imbalance.
Courtly grandeur and campaigns therefore expressed real capacity while
also revealing the fiscal and political pressures inherited by the
late-seventeenth-century empire.
