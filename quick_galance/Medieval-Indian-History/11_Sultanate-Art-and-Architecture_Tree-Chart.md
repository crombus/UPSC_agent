                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Sultanate architecture records conquest, appropriation, collaboration
and technical adaptation. West and Central Asian arcuate forms,
Persianate patronage, Indian trabeate knowledge, local materials and
craft labour interacted unevenly, producing Delhi phases and distinct
regional styles rather than a pure imported or unchanged indigenous art.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Sultanate culture emerged through asymmetrical encounter: conquest and
reuse changed sacred and political landscapes, while Indian artisans,
local materials and regional courts transformed imported requirements
into new technical and aesthetic languages. Its monuments are strongest
when read as evidence of power, labour and adaptation, not as simple
symbols of either rupture or harmony.
