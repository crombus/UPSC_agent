                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Mughal decline was a multi-causal transformation of imperial authority,
not sudden civilizational collapse. Jagir scarcity, jama-hasil gaps,
agrarian and zamindar resistance, Deccan war, noble faction, weak
succession and external shocks fragmented the centre, while successor
states, Marathas, Sikhs, merchants and regional economies reorganised
power and preserved substantial continuity.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The Mughal Empire declined because its military-fiscal bargains ceased
to convert paper claims into governable revenue and reliable service.
Aurangzeb's wars and choices intensified that structural crisis, while
succession conflict and invasions exposed it. The eighteenth century
therefore marks both imperial fragmentation and creative regional
reordering, the setting into which Company power later expanded.
