                     CENTRAL THESIS
Mughal decline was a multi-causal transformation of imperial authority, not
sudden civilisational collapse. Jagir scarcity, jama-hasil gaps, agrarian and
zamindar resistance, Deccan-war exhaustion, noble faction, no settled
succession principle, and external shocks (Nadir Shah, Abdali) fragmented the
centre, while successor states, Marathas, Jats, Sikhs, merchants and regional
economies reorganised power and preserved substantial continuity.

                JAGIRDARI CRISIS MECHANISM
Mansabdar numbers grow + territorial expansion stalls + paibaqi (assignable
land) turns scarce + jama (assessed revenue) exceeds hasil (realised revenue)
+ frequent jagir transfer rewards short-term extraction + zamindar/peasant
resistance + Deccan-war disruption
   = BE-JAGIRI (shortage of suitable assignment) + noble faction + troop
     shortfall + agrarian pressure — a self-reinforcing loop, not a single
     accounting failure.

                              v
                  AURANGZEB FACTOR (balanced)
Long Deccan war (distance, supply, Maratha raids) + Bijapur/Golconda
annexation (added claims without proportionate revenue) + succession left
unsettled. Verdict: choices deepened an inherited structural crisis; he did
not single-handedly cause or end the empire.
                              |
                              v
             POST-1707 SUCCESSION AND THE SAYYID WIZARAT
No primogeniture -> every succession is a contested coalition bid (province +
army + noble/Rajput backing). Sayyid Abdullah Khan (wazir) and Husain Ali Khan
(mir bakhshi) make and unmake emperors; Farrukhsiyar deposed/killed 1719;
their dominance ends by 1720. Policy record: jizya abolished 1713; Maratha
chauth/sardeshmukhi settlement negotiated with Shahu. Emperor remains the
source of title/legitimacy even as coercive reach shrinks.
                              |
                              v
              SUCCESSOR-STATE REORGANISATION
HYDERABAD: Nizam-ul-Mulk (Asaf Jah) converts personal military strength into
Deccan autonomy (1724 Shakar Kheda), keeps Mughal titles.
AWADH: Saadat Khan builds autonomy via zamindar/taluqdar accommodation, not
fiscal overhaul.
BENGAL: Murshid Quli Khan's revenue-farming (ijaradari) settlement; Alivardi
Khan later contains Maratha bargi raids.
Shared pattern: provincial office -> local army/patronage -> practical
autonomy -> nominal Mughal forms retained (not independence by decree).
                              |
                              v
                MARATHA BID AND OTHER REGIONAL POWERS
Shahu-Tarabai succession dispute resolved in Shahu's favour -> Peshwa-led
confederate expansion. Confederacy: Peshwa + named houses Scindia, Holkar,
Gaekwad, Bhonsle, each with its own regional base. Instruments: chauth
(~1/4 claim) + sardeshmukhi (~10% claim) — fiscal claims, not religious
taxes. Strength: mobile armies + revenue claims + Mughal-legitimacy use.
Weakness: divergent chief interests + supply/coalition limits.
OTHER POWERS: Jats (Churaman-Badan Singh-Suraj Mal, Bharatpur) + Sikhs
(Banda Bahadur 1708-1716, later Dal Khalsa misls) + Rohillas — redistribution
of power along existing local/agrarian fault lines, not anarchy.

                              v
              NADIR SHAH (1739) AND ABDALI/PANIPAT III (1748-1767)
Nadir Shah: north-west defence weakness + court faction + fiscal strain ->
Karnal defeat -> Delhi sack -> severe, lasting fiscal shock (exact plunder
figures not independently verifiable) -> accelerates, does not originate,
decline.
Abdali: repeated invasions 1748-1767 show a contested, not vacant, frontier;
Maratha northward bid meets his coalition at Third Panipat (1761) -> severe
Maratha defeat but Maratha power revives and Abdali builds no durable Indian
empire. Result: checks one bid for supremacy; does not restore Mughal power
or hand India to the Company.

          ECONOMY, CULTIVATION/TRADE ROUTES AND CONTINUITY
Imperial contraction (war, plunder, insecurity in some zones) coexists with
continuing agriculture/cultivation, craft production and inland/overseas
trade routes redirected toward regional courts (Hyderabad, Lucknow,
Murshidabad). Banking/credit houses (e.g. Jagat Seth) persist and expand.
Political fragmentation != universal economic collapse, though war and
revenue-farming could still raise peasant/artisan burdens.
BOUNDED COMPANY INVOLVEMENT: before 1757, the Company already fights the
inland, land-based Carnatic Wars (from the 1740s) alongside rival regional
claimants — not merely coastal trading; but formal Company territorial rule
(Bengal Diwani 1765 onward) is Modern History's own later sequence, not this
topic's.

             CULTURAL CONTINUITY AND HISTORIOGRAPHY
Symbolic legitimacy (khutba, sikka) persists; mansab/jagir/Persian
administrative forms and court etiquette are adapted by successor states;
artists and scholars relocate to regional courts (Lucknow, Hyderabad).

                   QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The Mughal Empire declined because its military-fiscal bargains stopped
converting paper claims into governable revenue and reliable service. No
settled succession principle, Sayyid kingmaking, Aurangzeb's Deccan-war
legacy, and Nadir Shah's and Abdali's invasions exposed and deepened that
structural crisis, while Bengal, Awadh, Hyderabad, the Marathas, Jats and
Sikhs redistributed power and much of the agrarian-commercial economy
persisted. This is the setting into which the Company - already fighting the
inland Carnatic Wars from the 1740s, though still short of territorial rule
before 1757 - later expanded, not a foreordained handover.
