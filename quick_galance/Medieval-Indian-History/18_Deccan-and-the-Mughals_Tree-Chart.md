                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Mughal Deccan policy sought graded all-India suzerainty before direct
annexation. Khandesh routes, Ahmadnagar succession, Deccani rivalry,
Portuguese pressure and Maratha mobility drew the empire south; Malik
Ambar exposed Mughal logistical limits, while Shah Jahan's balanced
1636 settlement was undermined by renewed intervention in 1656-57.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The Mughal-Deccan contest was a problem of hierarchy, logistics and
coalition rather than a simple north-south war. Malik Ambar proved that
mobile regional alliances could defeat imperial scale; Shah Jahan's
1636 settlement briefly matched ambition to capacity, while the renewed
interventions of 1656-57 recreated the instability later inherited by
Aurangzeb.
