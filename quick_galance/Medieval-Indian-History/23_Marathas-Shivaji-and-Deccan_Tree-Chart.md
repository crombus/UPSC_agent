                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Shivaji built sovereignty by combining hill-fort geography, mobile
warfare, local landed networks, revenue reform, sea power and ritual
legitimation. Aurangzeb's failure to secure a durable settlement,
followed by Bijapur-Golconda annexation and Sambhaji's execution,
decentralised Maratha resistance and fused Deccan war with the Mughal
jagirdari crisis.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Shivaji's power rested on far more than raids: forts, revenue, local
alliances, maritime capacity and sovereign institutions sustained a
regional state. Aurangzeb's annexations enlarged territory but reduced
governability; decentralised Maratha resistance then converted military
war into a fiscal and jagirdari crisis that outlasted individual leaders.
