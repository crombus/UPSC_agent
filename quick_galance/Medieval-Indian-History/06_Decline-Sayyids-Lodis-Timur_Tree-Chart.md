                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Delhi's decline was a long internal fragmentation accelerated, not
created, by Timur. The fifteenth century was not an empty interlude:
regional states formed a balance of power, the Sayyids preserved a
weak Delhi core, and the Lodis rebuilt it through Afghan networks while
remaining constrained by noble autonomy and succession conflict.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The Sultanate ended not through one invasion or weak ruler but through
succession failure, fragmented fiscal-military authority, provincial
state capacity and the unresolved tension between monarchy and armed
nobility. Timur deepened the rupture; the Lodis partially reversed it;
Babur exploited, rather than created, the final coalition crisis.
