                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The Mamluk century transformed fragmented Ghurid military commands into
an independent, Delhi-centred military-fiscal monarchy. Its history is
the struggle among sultan, service nobility, provincial power and the
Mongol frontier, mediated by iqta, coinage, court ideology, garrisons,
local intermediaries and uneven territorial control.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The Mamluk rulers did not inherit a finished Sultanate. Through
sequenced warfare, revenue assignments, Delhi-centred institutions,
coinage, court ideology and frontier defence they built a durable
military-fiscal monarchy; yet noble autonomy, provincial distance,
Mongol pressure, social mediation and failed succession kept authority
uneven and ultimately enabled the Khalji transition.
