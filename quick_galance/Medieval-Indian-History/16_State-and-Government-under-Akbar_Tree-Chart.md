                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Akbar's state integrated a diverse empire through ruler-centred checks,
a graded mansab service, transferable jagirs, provincial duplication of
central offices and plural revenue methods. It was a sophisticated
military-fiscal monarchy, not a modern bureaucracy: paper assessment,
actual collection, troop obligation and local power never matched
perfectly.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Akbar's administrative achievement lay in making rank, revenue,
territory and service mutually accountable without eliminating local
power. Mansabdari and dahsala improved imperial legibility and elite
control, but dependence on jagir realization, zamindars and ruler-led
checks kept the system negotiated, unequal and vulnerable to later
fiscal strain.
