                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Bhakti and Sufi currents created overlapping devotional, ethical,
vernacular and social spaces, but they were not one merged movement.
Each had distinct scriptures, theology, institutions and genealogies;
their historical significance lay in renewed personal devotion, the
guide-disciple bond, performance, service and criticism of empty ritual,
while caste, gender and political hierarchy persisted.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Bhakti and Sufi traditions widened devotional participation and
reoriented language, performance and ethical imagination, but their
similarities emerged through contact within distinct religious worlds.
They created durable communities and critiques rather than a single
programme of social revolution, and their emancipatory language must
be balanced against later institutional and social continuities.
