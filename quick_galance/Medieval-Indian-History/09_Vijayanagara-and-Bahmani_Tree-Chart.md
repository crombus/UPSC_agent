                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The Deccan was shaped by rivalry and borrowing: Vijayanagara and the
Bahmani state competed for the Raichur doab, ports, horses and eastern
routes, while adapting military, fiscal and cultural practices from
one another. Both combined central ambition with powerful territorial
service elites; neither fits a communal civilizational-war model.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Vijayanagara and Bahmani power grew from the same Deccan strategic
system of agrarian cores, imported horses, ports, forts and service
elites. Their rivalry stimulated adaptation and cultural formation,
but centralising projects repeatedly collided with nayak, taraf and
factional autonomy, producing successor states rather than abrupt
civilizational breaks.
