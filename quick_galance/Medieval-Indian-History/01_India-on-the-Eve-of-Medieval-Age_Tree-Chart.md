                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
India before the Delhi Sultanate was neither isolated nor in an
all-India civilizational decline. Regional states, agrarian expansion,
ports, temples, merchants and intellectual networks remained vigorous,
while political rivalry and the gradual loss of north-western strategic
depth created openings for later Turkish conquest.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The medieval transition emerged from interaction rather than a sudden
civilizational rupture: commercial and intellectual contact long
preceded conquest, while the Ghurid breakthrough became durable only
when frontier geography was converted into power through logistics,
military-fiscal organization, political opportunity and delegated
state formation amid continuing Indian resistance and regional vitality.
