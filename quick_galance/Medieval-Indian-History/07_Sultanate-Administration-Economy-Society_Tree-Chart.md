                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The Delhi Sultanate was a hierarchical military-fiscal network:
the sultan and central departments redirected agrarian surplus through
iqta, khalisa, taxation, armies and towns, but operated through older
chiefs, village intermediaries, merchants, artisans and religious
institutions rather than abolishing local society.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The Sultanate increased the capacity to mobilise revenue, cavalry,
credit, urban demand and specialised production, but its authority
remained layered and socially unequal. Central institutions mattered
because they worked through, disciplined and transformed older local
structures, not because they replaced them with a uniform bureaucracy.
