                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Sher Shah converted Afghan regional power into an effective north
Indian military-fiscal empire through revenue measurement, roads,
sarais, coinage, customs simplification, forts and strict justice.
His reforms were powerful adaptations within an older process, not a
modern administrative revolution, and excessive personal supervision
made the Sur achievement fragile after Sher Shah and Islam Shah.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Sher Shah's achievement was to connect agrarian assessment, mobility,
security, currency and military supervision into a functioning empire.
Its reliance on existing local elites and a masterful ruler makes it
neither modern nor wholly novel; the rapid Sur fragmentation shows that
administrative instruments required a durable political coalition.
