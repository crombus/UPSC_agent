                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Jahangir's reign was consolidation under factional and frontier
pressure, not automatic decline. Akbar's composite nobility and broad
tolerance continued; Mewar was settled honourably, while Qandahar,
the Deccan, Khurram and Mahabat Khan exposed limits. Nur Jahan exercised
exceptional influence, but the fixed "junta" and personal-rule models
overstate stable control.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Jahangir preserved Akbar's institutional and composite framework while
using conciliation in Mewar and force elsewhere. Nur Jahan became an
important co-actor in patronage and crisis politics, but shifting
alliances, Qandahar, the Deccan and succession conflict show a court
system under strain rather than a state captured by one permanent junta.
