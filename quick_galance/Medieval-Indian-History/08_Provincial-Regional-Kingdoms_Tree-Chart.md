                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The fifteenth century was an age of regional state formation, not a
vacuum after Delhi. Ecology, agrarian hinterlands, routes, ports,
service elites, fortified capitals and cultural patronage enabled
durable but contested kingdoms within eastern, western and northern
balances of power.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Regional kingdoms converted ecological position, agrarian and
commercial resources, service networks and cultural patronage into
state capacity. Their rise demonstrates that Delhi's contraction
redistributed sovereignty rather than extinguishing political life,
although every regional order remained internally hierarchical,
externally contested and vulnerable to succession and imperial change.
