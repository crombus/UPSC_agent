                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Landslides, snow avalanches and glacial-lake outburst floods are related mountain
hazards but not interchangeable. A landslide is gravity-driven movement of rock,
debris or earth; an avalanche is rapid downslope movement of unstable snow and ice;
a GLOF is sudden release of water and debris after failure or overtopping of a
glacial-lake dam. Mountain disaster risk is created when fragile geology, steep
relief, intense rain, snow and cryosphere change interact with road cutting,
deforestation, hydropower, tourism, mining, unsafe settlement and weak warning.

Mountain risk cannot be managed through retaining walls, satellite maps or emergency
rescue in isolation. India must translate susceptibility maps into land-use and
project decisions, expand carefully validated forecasting, maintain route and
community preparedness, and assess cascading earthquake, rainfall, snow and glacial
risks together. Development remains necessary, but roads, tourism and hydropower must
be designed around mountain limits rather than transferring avoidable danger to
