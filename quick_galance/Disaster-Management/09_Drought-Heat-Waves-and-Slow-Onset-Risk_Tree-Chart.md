                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Slow-onset hazards accumulate harm before a dramatic emergency threshold appears.
Drought can cascade from rainfall deficit to soil-moisture loss, crop stress, depleted
surface and groundwater, ecological decline, food insecurity, debt and migration.
Heat becomes disastrous when temperature and humidity overwhelm physiological cooling,
especially for exposed workers and households without water, shade, ventilation or
healthcare. Management must therefore use multi-indicator monitoring, anticipatory
action, Heat Action Plans, water and livelihood governance, occupational protection,
social protection and long-term climate-sensitive development.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Drought and heat expose the limits of disaster systems built around sudden visible
events. India needs thresholds that trigger support before households exhaust assets,
while also changing the water, crop, urban and labour systems that reproduce risk.
National relief eligibility and expanding Heat Action Plans are important inputs, but
success must be judged through timely water and health protection, reduced unequal
exposure, protected livelihoods and recovery of aquifers, ecosystems and human
capability across several seasons.
