                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Disaster finance must change risk, not merely reimburse loss. India now has a
four-fund architecture: State and national response funds for event-triggered relief
and recovery, and State and national mitigation funds for plan-triggered ex-ante risk
reduction. Insurance and capital-market instruments transfer financial loss but do
not lower physical exposure. A resilient portfolio layers prevention, reserves,
contingent liquidity, insurance and reconstruction finance by frequency and severity,
then subjects every rupee to transparent damage, needs, expenditure and outcome
assessment. Build Back Better means safer systems and fairer recovery, not replacing
the same vulnerable asset.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
India's four-fund architecture is a major advance over relief-only financing, but its
80:20 balance and project-dependent mitigation window still reveal an ex-post bias.
Resilience requires predictable local prevention budgets, layered fiscal and insurance
instruments, transparent assessment and protection for households that markets cannot
cover. Finance achieves Build Back Better only when it produces safer locations,
stronger services, restored livelihoods and ecosystems, and measurable risk reduction
rather than faster replacement of vulnerable assets.
