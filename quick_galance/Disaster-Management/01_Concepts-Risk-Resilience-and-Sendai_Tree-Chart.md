                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
A hazard is not automatically a disaster. Disaster risk emerges when a hazardous
event intersects with exposed people or assets, multiple vulnerabilities and
insufficient coping or adaptive capacity. Development choices can create or reduce
risk before the event. Disaster management therefore spans prevention, mitigation,
preparedness, response, recovery and Build Back Better. Resilience is the ability
to resist, absorb, accommodate, recover and adapt without rebuilding the same risk.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Disasters are produced by the interaction of hazards with exposed and vulnerable
development, not by nature alone. Resilience therefore requires preventing new risk,
reducing existing risk, preparing for residual risk and recovering through safer,
inclusive transformation. Sendai provides a common global architecture, but its
non-binding targets become meaningful only when national and local plans are funded,
measured and revised through evidence from actual outcomes.
