                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Forest fire is an ecological process in some ecosystems but becomes a disaster when
its timing, intensity, extent and exposure exceed ecological and human coping capacity.
Fuel and oxygen are normally present; ignition supplies heat, while weather, terrain,
fuel condition and management determine spread. India's satellite detection layer is
comparatively mature, but an alert is not suppression. Durable risk reduction requires
community partnership, fuel and land-use governance, trained and equipped State crews,
wildland-urban-interface planning, health protection, post-fire erosion control and
ecological restoration suited to the local fire regime.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
India can increasingly see forest fires from space, but resilience is determined on
the ground by prevention incentives, trained crews, access, maintenance and trusted
community institutions. Fire policy must distinguish ecological fire from destructive
fire, target high-risk landscapes without excluding forest-dependent livelihoods, and
carry management beyond flame suppression into smoke protection, erosion control and
ecosystem recovery. Technology should shorten action time, not become a substitute
for sustained forest governance.
