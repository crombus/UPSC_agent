                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
India's Disaster Management Act creates a cascading national-State-district-local
system. NDMA sets policy and plans, executive committees coordinate implementation,
SDMAs adapt State policy, DDMAs organise district action, local authorities deliver
the last mile, NIDM builds capacity and NDRF provides specialist response. The
architecture is only as strong as its weakest operational tier. Statutory creation,
notification, staffing, funding, planning, exercise and demonstrated outcome are
different stages.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
India's disaster law has moved governance beyond ad hoc relief by assigning policy,
coordination, response and planning across national, State, district and local tiers.
The 2025 amendment strengthens statutory coordination and data architecture, but
several provisions still require State-level constitution and operational capacity.
The decisive reform is to give DDMAs and local bodies skilled staff, predictable
finance, tested plans and transparent data so national guidance becomes measurable
last-mile risk reduction.
