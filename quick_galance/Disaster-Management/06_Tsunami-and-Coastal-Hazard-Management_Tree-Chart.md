                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
A tsunami is a displacement wave, not a storm wave or an unusually large tide.
Its disaster potential emerges from source mechanism, ocean depth, bathymetry,
coastal geometry, run-up, dense exposure, weak evacuation capacity and degradation
of coastal buffers. India has a capable regional detection and advisory architecture,
but detection is not evacuation and an advisory is not an all-clear. Low-frequency,
high-consequence risk demands maintained drills, risk-sensitive land use, mixed
ecosystem and engineered protection, accessible warning and livelihood-sensitive
recovery between rare events.

India's post-2004 warning architecture sharply improves detection and regional

recovery. Because rare hazards invite readiness decay, repeated community verification
