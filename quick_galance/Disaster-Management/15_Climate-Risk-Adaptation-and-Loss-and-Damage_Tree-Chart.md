                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Climate change modifies the probability, intensity, timing, distribution and baseline
conditions of many hazards, but it does not cause every disaster and does not replace
exposure, vulnerability and capacity analysis. Climate-disaster governance follows an
avoid-adapt-address sequence: mitigation limits future risk creation; adaptation and
DRR reduce present and expected harm; loss and damage addresses residual impacts that
cannot be avoided or adapted to. Effective policy combines climate services,
ecosystem-based and structural adaptation, anticipatory finance, social protection,
planned mobility, just recovery and precise event-attribution and fund-status cautions.

Climate-resilient disaster management must reduce the hazards society can still avoid,

infrastructure can unite Sendai, Paris and the SDGs, but each intervention must be
tested for maladaptation and unequal burden. Loss-and-damage institutions matter only
