                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Trade policy changes landed prices, market access, sourcing, investment,
production, employment and consumer welfare through tariffs and non-tariff
rules. Multilateral commitments, preferential agreements and trade remedies
have different legal purposes. The correct evaluation asks who gains, who bears
adjustment costs, whether trade is created or diverted and whether domestic
capability improves rather than reading gross trade growth as proof of success.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Trade openness can expand scale, productivity and consumer choice, but poorly
sequenced liberalisation or permanent protection can each weaken capability.
India should combine multilateral rules, selective high-quality agreements,
credible trade remedies and domestic adjustment support. Every claim must
distinguish legal status from implementation and gross trade change from causal
welfare gain.
