                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
International financial institutions must be compared by mandate, client,
capital and voting structure, instrument, conditionality and development
outcome. The IMF primarily supports macroeconomic and external stability,
whereas multilateral development banks finance sovereign or private development
projects and policy programmes. Newer banks widen finance and voice but
complement rather than automatically replace Bretton Woods institutions.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
The IMF and development banks address different failures and should not be
collapsed into one category. India benefits from engaging older and newer
institutions simultaneously, but financing quality depends on governance,
currency risk, safeguards, policy ownership and measurable outcomes. Reform of
quota and voting structures should accompany, not replace, sound project
appraisal and responsible debt management.
