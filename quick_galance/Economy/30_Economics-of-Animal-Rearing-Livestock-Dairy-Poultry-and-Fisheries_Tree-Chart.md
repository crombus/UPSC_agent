                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
An animal is both a biological asset and a recurring production unit. Enterprise
viability therefore depends on lifetime output, offspring and terminal value
minus feed, health, labour, mortality, finance and market losses. Livestock,
dairy, poultry and fisheries diversify smallholder income and nutrition, but
productivity requires complementary genetics, feed, health, management, climate
resilience, biosecurity and fair value chains.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Animal agriculture can stabilise smallholder income because it produces frequent
cash flows and uses crop residues, but biological and market risks are tightly
linked. Productivity policy must combine feed, genetics, veterinary systems,
biosecurity, cold chains and fair producer institutions. Sustainable fisheries
and integrated farming should maximise lifetime and ecosystem value rather than
animal numbers or harvest alone.
