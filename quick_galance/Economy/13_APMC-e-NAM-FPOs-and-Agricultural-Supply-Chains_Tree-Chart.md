                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Agricultural marketing converts dispersed, perishable and quality-variable farm
output into graded, financed and delivered produce. Market reform is not merely
removing intermediaries: it must improve competition, aggregation, assaying,
storage, logistics, payment, information and bargaining power. A digital portal
cannot integrate markets without physical and institutional interoperability.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Agricultural-market reform succeeds when farmers can aggregate produce, verify
quality, discover prices, obtain finance and complete delivery across competing
channels. Neither mandis nor intermediaries should be judged by label alone,
and e-NAM cannot substitute for assaying, logistics and payment enforcement.
Strong FPOs and transparent regulation are the bridge between small farms and
larger markets.
