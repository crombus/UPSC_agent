                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Farm support must be classified by objective, delivery channel, economic
incidence and WTO treatment. A subsidy may improve income, food security or
risk absorption while distorting crops, water, nutrients, trade or fiscal
priorities. Direct and indirect, explicit and implicit, and Green, Amber, Blue
or Article 6.2 categories are different classifications; a domestic policy
label does not determine its WTO legal treatment.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Farm subsidies remain justified where they correct risk, liquidity and food-
security failures, but their incidence and ecological effects must be measured
rather than assumed. India should shift gradually toward better-targeted income,
investment and risk support while preserving vulnerable farmers and pursuing a
permanent, development-sensitive WTO solution for public stockholding.
