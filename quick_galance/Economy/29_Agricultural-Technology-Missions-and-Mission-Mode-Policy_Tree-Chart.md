                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
A mission-mode programme starts with a defined bottleneck, coordinates research,
inputs, extension, finance and markets, and uses feedback to pursue a measurable
outcome. Technology is broader than machinery: it includes biological,
agronomic, water, digital, post-harvest and institutional innovation. Mission
success must be judged from adoption and outcomes, not budget, distribution or
headline production alone.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Mission-mode agricultural policy is valuable when it coordinates the complete
innovation system around a measurable bottleneck. India's experience shows that
research or subsidy alone is insufficient: local adaptation, trusted extension,
finance, water, repair and markets determine repeated use. Missions should be
judged by attributable income, resilience and ecological outcomes, not by
targets or inputs distributed.
