                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Growth expands economic resources; development asks whether people can convert
those resources into long, healthy, knowledgeable and dignified lives. Average
achievement, inequality and overlapping deprivation are separate questions.
HDI measures average capability achievement, IHDI discounts it for inequality,
and MPI measures the incidence and intensity of simultaneous household
deprivations.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Development measurement must distinguish the size of the resource envelope from
its conversion into capabilities and its distribution across people. HDI, IHDI,
MPI and income poverty illuminate different layers. Policy should therefore use
disaggregated dashboards, improve service quality and combine productive growth,
social protection and equal access rather than treating any single rank as the
development verdict.
