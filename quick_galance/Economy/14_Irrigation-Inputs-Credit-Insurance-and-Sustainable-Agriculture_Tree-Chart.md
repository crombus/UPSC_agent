                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Farm resilience depends on a system, not one input: reliable and efficient
water, balanced nutrients, suitable seed and technology, timely credit, credible
insurance, extension and markets. Increasing irrigation potential is different
from utilising it, micro-irrigation can save water per unit but induce expansion,
and insurance protects income only when enrolment, yield data, loss assessment
and payment work together.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Sustainable agriculture requires simultaneous reform of water incentives,
input use, credit access and risk protection. Micro-irrigation and insurance
are valuable but can fail through rebound, poor maintenance, basis risk or
tenant exclusion. The durable approach combines aquifer governance, suitable
cropping, balanced nutrients, reliable data and diversified farm livelihoods.
