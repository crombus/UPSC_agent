                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Inflation is a sustained rise in the general price level, not a rise in one
price. Diagnosis requires the index, basket, weight, base, frequency, cause,
persistence and output context. Demand, supply, expectations and administered
prices transmit differently, so monetary, fiscal, supply-side and protection
responses must be assigned to the mechanism rather than applied mechanically.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Inflation control is strongest when policy distinguishes a broad demand
overheating from a temporary supply shock and a persistent expectations loop.
India needs credible monetary anchoring, prudent demand management, resilient
food and energy systems and targeted protection. No index or instrument alone
captures the distributional and cyclical reality.
