                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Food processing is a chain-coordination industry. Farmer income, loss reduction,
nutrition, employment and exports improve only when aggregation, grading,
pre-cooling, storage, transport, processing, testing, packaging, contracting and
market access work continuously. Installed capacity or scheme sanction does not
prove operational throughput, fair value capture or quality employment.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Food processing can stabilise demand, reduce loss and create rural non-farm
employment, but factories alone cannot deliver those outcomes. India needs
continuous cold logistics, reliable standards, viable small processors and
farmer organisations able to negotiate fair contracts. Policy success should
be measured through throughput, farmer value capture, nutrition and job quality,
not sanctioned capacity alone.
