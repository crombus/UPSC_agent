                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
The balance of payments is a double-entry flow account of transactions between
residents and non-residents; foreign-exchange reserves are a stock on the
central bank's balance sheet. A current-account deficit must be financed through
net financial inflows, reserve use and recorded errors or omissions. Exchange-
rate analysis must state the quotation, distinguish market movement from
official parity change and evaluate reserve adequacy through composition and
liabilities rather than a headline total alone.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
External stability cannot be inferred from a balanced accounting statement or a
large reserve headline. India must finance current-account needs through durable
flows, limit short-term and unhedged foreign-currency exposure and preserve
exchange-rate flexibility. Reserves are insurance against volatility, not a
substitute for export competitiveness, energy resilience or sound domestic
finance.
