                     CENTRAL PRINCIPLE
Agritech creates welfare only when reliable data are converted into a correct
decision, timely farm action and measurable net-income, risk or resource gains.
A registry, device, message or portal is an activity, not an outcome. Digital
tools require physical inputs, extension, markets, repair services, data rights,
assisted access and human appeal so that owner-cultivator gaps and algorithmic
errors do not become exclusion.

                  QUALIFIED CONCLUSION
Digital agriculture should be judged by better farm decisions and net welfare,
not by devices or registrations. India needs interoperable public architecture,
trusted extension and shared services that reach tenants, women and smallholders
without sacrificing data rights. Human correction and physical delivery remain
essential because algorithms and portals cannot sow, insure, store or sell a
crop by themselves.
