## Lesson 1 — What kind of philosophy is Yoga?

**Progress:** 1/12 · **Stage:** Foundation · **Coverage:** I17-01–I17-02

### Visual first: Yoga’s division of labour

```text
SĀṂKHYA                                PĀTAÑJALA YOGA
metaphysical map                       operational path
Puruṣa / Prakṛti                       discipline of citta
25 principles                          affliction therapy
discrimination                         absorption
no necessary God            +          special Puruṣa (Īśvara)
             \                          /
              \________________________/
                 “theistic Sāṃkhya”
                 (seśvara-Sāṃkhya)
```

*Yoga retains Sāṃkhya’s dualist grammar but turns it into a disciplined psychology of release.*

### Simple start

Yoga does not primarily mean physical flexibility or mystical union with a universal substance.
It asks a stricter question: **How can an already pure witness cease mistaking the changing mind
for itself?** Its answer is to diagnose the mind, its habits and afflictions, train them through a
graded practice, mature discriminative knowledge in absorption, and end false identification.

### Core doctrine

1. **Textual identity.** Patañjali is the traditional systematiser of classical Yoga through the
   *Yoga Sūtra*, read with Vyāsa’s *Yoga Bhāṣya* and later commentators such as Vācaspati Miśra
   and Vijñānabhikṣu. Dating and textual layering are debated; UPSC answers need the received
   Pātañjala system, not speculative chronology.
2. **Four chapters (pādas).**

| Chapter | Governing concern | Exam retrieval |
|---|---|---|
| Absorption chapter (Samādhi-pāda) | definition, modifications, practice/dispassion, God, absorption | mind and meditative cognition |
| Practice chapter (Sādhana-pāda) | afflictions, karma, eight limbs, ending conjunction | diagnosis and therapy |
| Powers chapter (Vibhūti-pāda) | combined concentration, transformations, extraordinary powers | advanced practice and its danger |
| Isolation chapter (Kaivalya-pāda) | realism, latent traces, discernment, final isolation | metaphysics of liberation |

3. **Source precision.** The five mind-levels are standard in Vyāsa’s commentarial exposition;
   they are not a five-item list in Patañjali’s sūtras.
4. **Metaphysical base.** Yoga accepts the plurality of conscious witnesses (puruṣas), primordial
   nature (prakṛti), its three qualities (guṇas) and the twenty-five Sāṃkhya principles.
5. **Distinctive addition.** Yoga adds a detailed method and admits God (Īśvara) as a special
   Puruṣa (puruṣa-viśeṣa), hence “theistic Sāṃkhya” (seśvara-Sāṃkhya).
6. **Governing definition.** Yoga is restraint of the modifications of the mind
   (citta-vṛtti-nirodha), not union with Brahman.

### Argument anatomy

```text
If Puruṣa is pure, why bondage?
        |
        v
Bondage is not a change in Puruṣa.
It is misidentification through citta.
        |
        v
Therefore liberation need not manufacture freedom.
It must remove the cognitive-affective obstruction.
        |
        v
Yoga requires both metaphysics (what is distinct?)
and practice (how is the confusion ended?).
```

### Example with limit

A cinema screen remains unchanged while many scenes appear on it. The image helps distinguish
the witness from changing contents. Its limit is equally important: unlike a physical screen,
Puruṣa is consciousness itself, while citta—not Puruṣa—undergoes the changing presentations.

### Objection, reply, residual

**Objection:** If Sāṃkhya already provides Puruṣa, Prakṛti and discrimination, Yoga’s added
practice and God are redundant.

**Reply:** Metaphysical distinction does not automatically remove ingrained affliction. Yoga
supplies an operational technology—practice, ethics, breath, concentration and absorption—and a
privileged contemplative support in Īśvara.

**Residual:** God remains instrumental rather than logically constitutive of liberation. This
will make Yoga’s theism both distinctive and vulnerable.

### Directive, retrieval and application

| If the question says… | Retrieve | Do |
|---|---|---|
| Explain Yoga | dualism + method + Īśvara + kaivalya | define, locate, state causal spine |
| Compare Yoga and Sāṃkhya | shared ontology; different method/God | compare on fixed axes |
| Critically examine Yoga | operational strength; interaction and God problems | objection → reply → residual |

**Retrieval cue:** **Map–Method–Mediator–Release**: Sāṃkhya map; yogic method; Īśvara as mediator;
kaivalya as release.

### Concept-check MCQ 1

Which statement most accurately identifies classical Pātañjala Yoga?

A. It retains Sāṃkhya dualism, adds a disciplined path, and admits Īśvara as a special Puruṣa.
B. It replaces Prakṛti with Brahman and treats liberation as realization of a single non-dual self.
C. It accepts Sāṃkhya ontology but reduces liberation to mastery of posture, breath, and bodily control.
D. It retains Prakṛti yet makes a creator God the material cause of the world and the giver of release.

#### Answer and explanation

**Correct answer: A**

- **A — Correct:** It preserves the Sāṃkhya base while adding systematic practice and a special
  Puruṣa.
- **B — Incorrect:** That imports an Advaita identity thesis rejected by Yoga’s plural dualism.
- **C — Incorrect:** Posture and breath are two limbs within a much wider soteriology.
- **D — Incorrect:** Prakṛti remains the material basis; Patañjali does not make God the Nyāya-style
  creator.

## Lesson 2 — Mind-complex, reflection and the five mind-levels

**Progress:** 2/12 · **Stage:** Foundation/Core · **Coverage:** I17-03–I17-15

### Visual first: the three-term experience model

```text
EXTERNAL OBJECT
      |
      | colours / shapes
      v
MIND-COMPLEX (citta)
buddhi + ahaṃkāra + manas
active, changing, insentient, sattva-predominant
      ^
      | reflects the light of
      |
WITNESS (Puruṣa)
conscious, inactive, unchanging

ERROR: “the changing cognition is I”
RELEASE: “the cognition is seen; I am the seer”
```

### Simple start

When a learner says “my mind knows,” Yoga divides that sentence. The mind-complex changes into
the form of an object; the witness supplies consciousness; egoic appropriation says “I know.”
Bondage begins when these functions are collapsed into one entity.

### What mind-complex (citta) includes

| Component | English function | Error if isolated |
|---|---|---|
| Determinative intellect (buddhi) | judges, decides, discriminates | citta is not intellect alone |
| Ego-maker (ahaṃkāra) | appropriates a state as “I” or “mine” | ego is not the true witness |
| Coordinating mind (manas) | attends, doubts and coordinates senses | citta is broader than manas |

The evolutionary order is **intellect → ego-maker → coordinating mind**. Only coordinating mind
arises directly from the sattva-predominant side of ego-maker; it is inaccurate to say that all
three arise directly from sattvic ego. The functioning citta as a whole is sattva-predominant and
therefore transparent enough to mirror consciousness.

### Reflection and object-colouring

```text
flower -> clear crystal seems red
object -> transparent citta assumes object-form
Puruṣa -> citta appears conscious

No literal merger:
flower ≠ crystal
object ≠ cognition
Puruṣa ≠ citta
```

Citta is a product of Prakṛti and is intrinsically insentient. Yet it appears conscious through
the reflection or shadow-image (pratibimba/chāyā) of Puruṣa. Conversely, because consciousness is
reflected in changing citta, Puruṣa appears to think, act and suffer. All transformation remains
on the citta side; Puruṣa is non-transforming (apariṇāmin).

### The relation problem

**Objection:** Reflection normally requires a relation between mirror and original. If Puruṣa and
Prakṛti are radically independent—and Puruṣa is inactive—how can proximity generate one lived
experience?

**Yoga’s reply:** Mere proximity (sannidhi-mātra) suffices. Like a magnet whose presence occasions
movement in iron without itself changing, Puruṣa’s presence makes cognitive illumination possible
while citta alone transforms.

**Residual:** The analogy protects Puruṣa’s immutability but does not fully explain the ontological
bridge. Advaita, Nyāya and Buddhist critics can still ask whether “reflection” names rather than
solves the relation.

### Five mind-levels (citta-bhūmis)

```text
PRE-YOGIC / UNSTABLE                              YOGA-FIT

scattered        stupefied       intermittent     one-pointed       restrained
(kṣipta)   ->    (mūḍha)    ->   (vikṣipta)  ->  (ekāgra)     ->   (niruddha)
rajas            tamas            mixed            sattva             vṛttis held
```

| Level | Exact character | Crucial distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Scattered (kṣipta) | restless outward movement under rajas | activity is not concentration |
| Stupefied (mūḍha) | torpor, delusion and inertia under tamas | dullness is not meditative stillness |
| Intermittently focused (vikṣipta) | capable bursts amid distraction | intelligence does not equal yogic integration |
| One-pointed (ekāgra) | sustained attention on one support | support remains; not final restraint |
| Restrained (niruddha) | modifications are held in restraint | not sleep and not destruction of awareness |

Only one-pointed and restrained mind are fully fit for Yoga. The decisive transition is from
occasional capacity to stable meditative orientation.

### Applied 2019 classification

| Description in the PYQ | Best reasoned placement | Qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Scientist | usually intermittently focused (vikṣipta) | intense task-focus may resemble ordinary one-pointedness; title alone proves nothing |
| God-realized devotee | one-pointed (ekāgra) when devotion retains Īśvara as support | if all modifications truly cease, restrained is more exact |
| Self-realized yogi | restrained (niruddha) when all modifications are arrested | Puruṣa itself never moves through mental levels |

The classifications are conclusions from operative states, not stereotypes attached to social
labels.

### Argument anatomy

1. Citta is a persisting but changing inner instrument.
2. Its transparency permits object-colouring and reflected consciousness.
3. Ego-maker appropriates the illuminated state.
4. Different quality-patterns produce different levels of stability.
5. Only stable one-pointedness and restraint can mature liberating discernment.

### Directive, retrieval and application

| Demand | Kernel | Application |
|---|---|---|
| Define citta | three faculties + material subtlety + reflected consciousness | never equate it with manas or Puruṣa |
| Explain reflection | object-form + borrowed luminosity + false ownership | add relation objection and mere-proximity reply |
| Apply mind-levels | state → quality-pattern → level → qualification | reason from condition, not title |

**Retrieval cue:** **Instrument–Illumination–Identification–Integration**.

### Concept-check MCQ 2

Why is a brilliant scientist not automatically placed in the one-pointed yogic level?

A. Because even valid scientific cognition is always afflicted and cannot support stable liberation-oriented attention.
B. Because task-focus may coexist with wider distraction; yogic one-pointedness is stably liberation-oriented.
C. Because only devotional concentration on Īśvara can produce a genuinely stable, liberation-oriented citta.
D. Because Puruṣa must become intellectually active before directing citta toward a stable liberating object.

#### Answer and explanation

**Correct answer: B**

- **A — Incorrect:** Valid scientific cognition may be a non-afflicted modification.
- **B — Correct:** The classification concerns the stable condition and orientation of citta, not
  occupational competence.
- **C — Incorrect:** Devotion is one powerful support, not the sole route to concentration.
- **D — Incorrect:** Puruṣa remains inactive; citta develops the capacity.

## Lesson 3 — Mental modifications, restraint, practice and dispassion

**Progress:** 3/12 · **Stage:** Core · **Coverage:** I17-16–I17-33

### Visual first: why restrain even a true thought?

```text
OBJECT / WORD / MEMORY / ABSENCE
                 |
                 v
        a mental mode (vṛtti)
                 |
      +----------+----------+
      |                     |
affliction-feeding      liberation-aiding
(kliṣṭa)               (akliṣṭa)
      |                     |
bondage                  useful ladder
                            |
                            v
                    finally transcended too
```

### The foundational pair

> **Yoga is restraint of the modifications of the mind
> (yogaś-citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ). Then the seer abides in its own nature
> (tadā draṣṭuḥ svarūpe’vasthānam).**

Restraint (nirodha) is revelatory, not annihilative. It does not destroy consciousness; it ends
the misreading of changing mental contents as the witness.

### Five mental modifications

| Modification | English meaning | Example | Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Valid cognition (pramāṇa) | cognition corresponding to its object | perceiving smoke and inferring fire | truth is still a mental change |
| Misapprehension (viparyaya) | cognition contrary to the object | nacre taken as silver | not word-only fiction |
| Conceptual construction (vikalpa) | word-based cognition without corresponding object | “hare’s horn” | linguistically intelligible, ontically empty |
| Sleep (nidrā) | mode grounded in absence-content | later recollection “I slept” | blankness is not samādhi |
| Memory (smṛti) | preserved re-presentation of past experience | recalled pleasure driving craving | links impressions to present conduct |

Yoga accepts perception (pratyakṣa), inference (anumāna) and authoritative testimony
(āgama/śabda) as valid cognition. All five modification-types can be either afflicted (kliṣṭa) or
non-afflicted (akliṣṭa). Do not confuse this division with the separate list of five afflictions.

### Why valid cognition must eventually cease

```text
false cognition -> must be corrected
true cognition  -> corrects and discriminates
true cognition  -> remains a mode of citta
final freedom   -> no appropriation of any mode
```

Non-afflicted knowledge is a ladder. It is indispensable before release but is not itself the
unchanging witness. Yoga therefore avoids both anti-intellectualism and endless conceptualism.

### Joint means: practice and dispassion

| Means | Exact force | Why the partner is needed |
|---|---|---|
| Practice (abhyāsa) | long, uninterrupted, committed effort to stabilise citta | repetition without dispassion may strengthen desire |
| Lower dispassion (vairāgya) | mastery over craving for seen and promised goods | abstinence without training leaves attention unstable |
| Higher dispassion (para-vairāgya) | relinquishment even of subtle qualities, insight and powers through Puruṣa-discernment | prevents refined egoity from owning attainment |

The four familiar lower stages of dispassion belong to commentarial exposition; do not present
them as Patañjali’s own four-item sūtra list. The governing sūtra joins practice **and**
dispassion because each corrects the one-sided danger of the other.

### Transformations of citta

Yoga treats citta as a persisting substrate capable of real transformation (pariṇāma):

1. **Restraint-transformation (nirodha-pariṇāma):** restraint-impressions check outgoing modes.
2. **Absorption-transformation (samādhi-pariṇāma):** many-pointedness gives way to one object.
3. **One-pointedness-transformation (ekāgratā-pariṇāma):** similar cognitions flow without break.

The same change can be analysed as change in **property (dharma)**, **temporal phase
(lakṣaṇa)** and **state (avasthā)**. This retains a substrate across change and therefore differs
from a Buddhist series of wholly momentary cognitions.

### The 2023 and 2024 demand routes

**2023 route:** define the whole citta-field; name and distinguish all five modes; explain
afflicted/non-afflicted status; then show why reflection and appropriation make cessation
liberating rather than merely calming.

**2024 route:** unpack the quoted reflection thesis: citta changes; its consciousness is borrowed;
ignorance attributes its states to Puruṣa; discipline refines citta toward discrimination. Then
appraise the interaction problem and the ladder-problem of useful cognition.

### Objection, reply, residual

**Objection:** If valid knowledge is restrained, Yoga seems to prefer blankness to truth.

**Reply:** Non-afflicted valid cognition prepares discrimination and removes error. Its eventual
cessation occurs only after it has completed that task; the witness is not a blank mental state.

**Residual:** Yoga must articulate how a cognitive discernment can terminate cognition without
becoming self-defeating. Its answer is sequential: discriminative knowledge removes confusion,
then higher dispassion removes appropriation of even that knowledge.

### Directive, retrieval and application

| Directive | Structure |
|---|---|
| Explain | definition → five modes → afflicted/non-afflicted → purpose of restraint |
| Give reasons | reflection → appropriation → bondage → self-abiding |
| Appraise | doctrine → practical strength → relation objection → graded verdict |

**Retrieval cue:** **Five–Two–Two–Three**: five modes; two moral statuses; practice plus
dispassion; three citta-transformations.

### Concept-check MCQ 3

Which claim best explains why valid cognition is counted as a mental modification?

A. Because Yoga treats perception and inference as useful but ultimately incapable of yielding truth.
B. Because every true cognition inevitably creates attachment and therefore strengthens bondage.
C. Because truth may aid discrimination while remaining a changing citta-form that is finally transcended.
D. Because valid cognition is an activity of Puruṣa that temporarily appears as a change in citta.

#### Answer and explanation

**Correct answer: C**

- **A — Incorrect:** Yoga accepts three means of valid cognition.
- **B — Incorrect:** Valid cognition can be non-afflicted and liberating in function.
- **C — Correct:** Epistemic truth and ontological changeability are different questions.
- **D — Incorrect:** Puruṣa witnesses; the cognitive modification belongs to citta.

## Lesson 4 — Afflictions, karma and the causal engine of bondage

**Progress:** 4/12 · **Stage:** Core · **Coverage:** I17-34–I17-45

### Visual first: from misseeing to rebirth

```text
IGNORANCE (avidyā)
mistakes impermanent/purely instrumental/non-self
                 |
                 v
EGOIC IDENTIFICATION (asmitā)
seer confused with seeing instrument
                 |
          +------+------+
          |             |
 ATTACHMENT        AVERSION
   (rāga)          (dveṣa)
          \             /
           \           /
        LIFE-CLINGING (abhiniveśa)
                 |
                 v
afflicted action -> karmic deposit -> ripening
                  (karmāśaya)        (vipāka)
                                      |
                         birth + lifespan + experience
```

### Five afflictions (kleśas)

| Affliction | Exact meaning | Typical error |
|---|---|---|
| Ignorance (avidyā) | takes impermanent as eternal, impure as pure, painful as pleasant, non-self as self | reducing it to missing information |
| Egoic identification (asmitā) | confuses witnessing consciousness with the cognitive instrument | equating it only with social pride |
| Attachment (rāga) | craving rooted in remembered pleasure | treating all preference as identical bondage |
| Aversion (dveṣa) | rejection rooted in remembered pain | defining it as mere absence of attraction |
| Life-clinging (abhiniveśa) | deep fear of death/self-preservative adhesion, even in the learned | treating it as a consciously held proposition only |

Binding egoic identification must be distinguished from the refined “I-am” factor that can remain
in cognitive absorption. The same term can function at different levels; context controls meaning.

### Four conditions of an affliction

| Condition | Meaning | Diagnostic test |
|---|---|---|
| Dormant (prasupta) | latent and presently unmanifest | can favourable conditions reactivate it? |
| Attenuated (tanu) | weakened through discipline | has its intensity genuinely reduced? |
| Interrupted (vicchinna) | temporarily displaced by another tendency | does it return when the opposing force fades? |
| Active (udāra) | fully behaviour-shaping | is it governing present thought and conduct? |

Suppression is not eradication. A temporarily interrupted or dormant affliction can revive.

### First therapy: action-oriented yoga (kriyā-yoga)

```text
disciplined austerity (tapas)
          +
self-study / sacred recitation (svādhyāya)
          +
surrender to God (Īśvara-praṇidhāna)
          |
          v
attenuate afflictions + prepare absorption
```

Gross conduct can be re-educated through discipline, but subtle causal traces require meditation
and discriminative insight. Ethical compliance without inner transformation may merely conceal the
seed.

### Karmic deposit and ripening

1. Afflictions colour intention and action.
2. Action leaves a karmic deposit (karmāśaya).
3. Latent tendencies (vāsanās) and impressions (saṃskāras) dispose future cognition and conduct.
4. The deposit ripens (vipāka) into:
   - **birth (jāti)**,
   - **lifespan (āyus)**,
   - **experienced pleasure and pain (bhoga)**.
5. Yoga classifies action as white, black, mixed, and neither-white-nor-black. The last describes
   the yogin’s non-binding action.

Never reverse the sequence: **affliction → action → deposit → ripening**.

### Burnt seed and residual momentum

A burnt seed can retain its shape yet cannot germinate. Similarly, an exhausted affliction loses
karmic fertility. The potter’s wheel explains why already-fructifying embodiment may continue
after the production of fresh bondage ends: the wheel turns briefly after the push stops.

### How removal leads to isolation

```text
action-oriented yoga -> gross afflictions attenuated
meditation             -> subtle traces weakened
absorption             -> citta clarified
discrimination         -> seer/seen confusion severed
higher dispassion      -> no ownership of attainment
qualities withdraw     -> service to this Puruṣa ends
isolation              -> witness abides in itself
```

### Objection, reply, residual

**Objection:** If karmic deposits accumulated across lives are innumerable, liberation appears
indefinitely postponed.

**Reply:** Yoga does not require sequential enjoyment of every dormant seed. Liberating
discernment and absorption burn the causal fertility of future-producing traces; only
already-fructifying momentum continues.

**Residual:** The reply depends on a morally ordered trans-life causal mechanism not publicly
observable in ordinary experience. Yoga appeals to disciplined transformation and yogic insight;
a materialist critic can still dispute the metaphysical extension.

### Directive, retrieval and application

| Question burden | Required move |
|---|---|
| Nature of afflictions | define all five + four conditions |
| How removal leads to kaivalya | causal chain + therapies + discrimination + quality-withdrawal |
| Critically examine | temporary suppression objection + burnt-seed reply + metaphysical residual |

**Retrieval cue:** **5–4–3–3**: five afflictions; four conditions; threefold action-oriented yoga;
three results of ripening.

### Concept-check MCQ 4

Which sequence correctly represents Yoga’s moral-causal account?

A. Ripening → affliction → karmic deposit → action → future birth, lifespan and experience
B. Action → karmic deposit → ripening → ignorance → birth, lifespan and experience
C. Karmic deposit → attachment → Puruṣa → ripening → birth, lifespan and experience
D. Affliction → action → karmic deposit → ripening → birth, lifespan and experience

#### Answer and explanation

**Correct answer: D**

- **A — Incorrect:** Ripening is an effect, not the initiating root.
- **B — Incorrect:** Ignorance precedes afflicted action, and deposit mediates ripening.
- **C — Incorrect:** Puruṣa is not a link produced inside the karmic sequence.
- **D — Correct:** It preserves both causal order and the three results of ripening.

## Lesson 5 — Eight-limbed therapy: from conduct to absorption

**Progress:** 5/12 · **Stage:** Core · **Coverage:** I17-46–I17-54

### Visual first: an outer-to-inner bridge

```text
OUTER LIMBS (bahiraṅga)
restraints -> observances -> posture -> breath -> sense withdrawal
     ethics       self-work      body       energy       bridge
                                                        |
                                                        v
INNER LIMBS (antaraṅga)
concentration -> meditation -> absorption
 fixing           continuous       object shines
                  flow

combined on one object = integrated discipline (saṃyama)
```

### The eight limbs (aṣṭāṅga-yoga)

| Limb | English-first meaning | Philosophical function |
|---|---|---|
| Restraints (yama) | non-injury, truth, non-stealing, disciplined sexuality, non-grasping | removes socially enacted turbulence |
| Observances (niyama) | purity, contentment, austerity, study, God-surrender | cultivates inward order |
| Posture (āsana) | steady ease | makes embodiment non-distracting |
| Breath regulation (prāṇāyāma) | regulation of breath/life-force | steadies the psychophysical field |
| Sense withdrawal (pratyāhāra) | release from compulsive object-following | bridges outer and inner discipline |
| Concentration (dhāraṇā) | binding citta to one locus | initial fixing |
| Meditation (dhyāna) | uninterrupted one-object flow | sustained continuity |
| Absorption (samādhi) | object shines with minimal discursive interference | culmination of inner integration |

The restraints become the **universal great vow (sārvabhauma-mahāvrata)** when unrestricted by
class, place, time or circumstance. Ethics is therefore not an optional wellness preface.

### Why the sequence matters

```text
unethical conduct -> agitation, concealment, fear
unstable body/breath -> attentional interruption
uncontrolled senses -> dispersion
dispersion -> no stable concentration
no concentration -> no meditation
no meditation -> no clarifying absorption
```

Posture is not the whole of Yoga; it creates steady ease for attention. Breath is not liberation;
it is a psychophysical hinge. Sense withdrawal is not sensory mutilation; it ends compulsion.
Concentration is not meditation; it fixes. Meditation is not yet final absorption; it sustains a
continuous stream toward the object.

### Integrated discipline (saṃyama)

Concentration, meditation and absorption performed together on the same object form integrated
discipline (saṃyama). It progressively removes interference until cognition becomes transparent
to the object. Powers may arise from this mastery, but they do not prove freedom.

### Example with limit

Learning music illustrates the progression: ethical reliability enables practice; posture and
breath support steadiness; distractions are withdrawn; a phrase is fixed, continuously attended
to and finally played without disruptive self-commentary. The limit is that yogic practice aims
not at expert performance but at ending Puruṣa–citta confusion.

### Objection, reply, residual

**Objection:** A highly concentrated but unethical person shows that meditation can develop without
the first limbs.

**Reply:** Technical concentration can occur, but Yoga asks whether it is non-afflicted and
liberation-oriented. Without restraints and observances, powers may strengthen ego, attachment and
harm.

**Residual:** Ethical conduct alone also does not guarantee metaphysical discernment. Yoga’s
distinctive claim is integration: morality is necessary preparation, not a substitute for
meditative knowledge.

### Directive, retrieval and application

| Demand | Fast structure |
|---|---|
| Explain eight limbs | 5 outer + 3 inner + one function each |
| Distinguish dhāraṇā/dhyāna/samādhi | fix → flow → absorption |
| Assess ethics and meditation | ethics necessary, not sufficient; meditation powerful, not self-validating |

**Retrieval cue:** **Restrain–Cultivate–Steady–Regulate–Withdraw–Fix–Flow–Absorb**.

## Lesson 6 — Cognitive absorption: two grids that must not be conflated

**Progress:** 6/12 · **Stage:** Core · **Coverage:** I17-55–I17-64

### Visual first: two questions, two taxonomies

```text
GRID A — YS 1.17                         GRID B — YS 1.42–1.44
What accompanies cognitive              How does object-coalescence vary?
absorption (samprajñāta)?

gross grasp (vitarka)                    gross + conceptual overlay (savitarkā)
subtle reflection (vicāra)               gross - overlay (nirvitarkā)
refined joy (ānanda)                     subtle + reflection (savicārā)
bare I-am-ness (asmitā)                  subtle - reflection (nirvicārā)

RELATED, NOT ONE FOUR-ITEM LIST
```

### Nature of absorption (samādhi)

Absorption is radically unified and transparent attention. It is not ordinary trance, sleep or
mere relaxation. Its states prepare liberating knowledge but no single state automatically equals
isolation.

### Cognitive absorption (samprajñāta samādhi)

Cognitive absorption retains an object-support and determinate awareness. It is “with seed”
(sabīja) in the sense that a support remains. Do not confuse this support-seed with the residual
impression denied by final seedless restraint.

#### Grid A: four factors in YS 1.17

| Factor | Level/tone | Qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Gross-object grasp (vitarka) | deliberative engagement with gross objects | not identical with savitarkā alone |
| Subtle reflection (vicāra) | attention to subtle principles | not the whole savicārā/nirvicārā grid |
| Refined joy (ānanda) | sattvic meditative joy | not ordinary pleasure or kaivalya |
| Bare I-am-ness (asmitā) | very subtle objectified I-sense | not ordinary binding egoity |

#### Grid B: object-coalescences (samāpattis)

| Variety | Object | Overlay | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| With gross conceptualisation (savitarkā) | gross | word–object–knowledge mixture remains | name and meaning are not purified |
| Without gross conceptualisation (nirvitarkā) | gross | memory/verbal construction purified | gross object shines more directly |
| With subtle reflection (savicārā) | subtle | subtle reflective overlay remains | inquiry reaches subtle principles |
| Without subtle reflection (nirvicārā) | subtle | subtle overlay stilled | luminous subtle-object clarity |

The subtle range extends up to the unmanifest signless condition (aliṅga). Lucidity
(vaiśāradya) in non-reflective subtle coalescence yields inner clarity and truth-bearing insight
(ṛtambharā prajñā).

### Truth-bearing insight and yogic perception

Truth-bearing insight is particular, non-discursive clarity arising from refined absorption. It
counteracts ordinary impressions. It should not be casually added as a fourth independent means
of knowledge; it is a yogically intensified form of direct insight within the system’s meditative
epistemology.

### The seed distinction

```text
“WITH SEED” IN OBJECT-SUPPORTED ABSORPTION
= a cognitive support/object remains

“SEED” IN FINAL SEEDLESS RESTRAINT
= even the residual impression capable of renewed cognition is restrained

objectlessness alone does not automatically establish final seedlessness
```

### Objection, reply, residual

**Objection:** The two fourfold lists show textual inconsistency.

**Reply:** They answer different analytic questions. YS 1.17 describes factors accompanying
cognitive absorption; YS 1.42–1.44 distinguishes modes of object-coalescence by object-level and
conceptual overlay. Commentators can relate them without erasing the distinction.

**Residual:** Exact mappings vary across commentators, especially around joy and I-am-ness.
UPSC-safe writing states the two grids first and treats the mapping as interpretive.

### Directive, retrieval and application

| Directive | Required execution |
|---|---|
| Explain stages | nature first; Grid A; Grid B; culminating clarity |
| Distinguish | use axes: object/support, cognitive determination, residual impressions |
| Examine | add non-equivalence of the grids and one interpretive residual |

**Retrieval cue:** **17 = factors; 42–44 = overlays**.

### Concept-check MCQ 6

C. YS 1.17 entirely excludes refined joy and bare I-am-ness from factors accompanying cognitive absorption.

#### Answer and explanation

**Correct answer: B**

- **A — Incorrect:** Commentarial mappings do not cancel the difference in analytic question.

- **D — Incorrect:** In object-supported absorption, “seed” refers to the support.

## Lesson 7 — Beyond cognitive support: residual impressions, seedlessness and culmination

**Progress:** 7/12 · **Stage:** Core · **Coverage:** I17-65–I17-72

### Visual first: do not collapse the summit

```text
cognitive absorption (samprajñāta)
object/support and determinate knowledge remain
                  |
                  v
supra-cognitive absorption (asamprajñāta)
active objectified cognition ceases; impressions may remain
                  |
                  v
further restraint
even the residual impression is checked
                  |
                  v
seedless absorption (nirbīja)

parallel late culmination:
stable discrimination + non-attachment
                  |
                  v
cloud-of-dharma absorption (dharmamegha)
                  |
                  v
isolation (kaivalya)
```

### Supra-cognitive absorption (asamprajñāta samādhi)

In supra-cognitive absorption, objectified cognition ceases through prior discipline, while
residual impressions may remain (saṃskāra-śeṣa). It is not sleep: sleep is itself a mental
modification grounded in absence-content, whereas this state is intentionally produced restraint.
It is also not automatically final liberation, because latent potential can remain.

### Seedless absorption (nirbīja samādhi)

Final seedless absorption names further restraint in which even the residual impression generated
by the highest insight is checked. Therefore:

- supra-cognitive absorption and seedless absorption are closely related;
- they should not be used as unqualified synonyms;
- neither word should be equated casually with isolation;
- the decisive soteriological test is stable discrimination and exhaustion of binding causality.

### Two routes beyond ordinary cognition

| Route | Meaning | Risk/qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Condition-based (bhava-pratyaya) | disembodied or nature-merged beings enter an extraordinary condition due to prior existential state | nature-mergence is not final Puruṣa–Prakṛti discrimination |
| Means-based (upāya-pratyaya) | faith, energy, memory, absorption and insight deliberately cultivate the state | philosophically safer path because it integrates knowledge and discipline |

### Cloud-of-dharma absorption (dharmamegha-samādhi)

When discriminative insight is continuous and even attachment to omniscience or powers is absent,
cloud-of-dharma absorption marks the final ripening before isolation. It is not a miraculous divine
gift replacing effort. It is the culmination of discernment and higher dispassion.

### Powers as obstacles

Extraordinary capacities may arise through integrated discipline. Their existence within the
system does not make them liberation. If appropriated by ego, they revive attachment and block
higher dispassion. **Attainment is therefore evidence of concentration, not proof of freedom.**

### Nine obstacles and associated symptoms

| Obstacles (antarāyas) | Associated symptoms |
|---|---|
| illness, mental dullness, doubt, carelessness, laziness, sensuality, false perception, failure to attain a stage, instability after attainment | pain, dejection, bodily tremor, disturbed inhalation/exhalation |

Remedies include one-principle practice, cultivation of friendliness/compassion/joy/equanimity,
breath regulation, stabilising contemplations and God-surrender. God is a powerful route, not the
only remedy named in the practical field.

### 2020/2026 difference table

| Axis | Cognitive absorption (samprajñāta) | Supra-cognitive absorption (asamprajñāta) |
|---|---|---|
| Object/support | remains | active objectification ceases |
| Knowledge | determinate meditative cognition | no active determinate cognition |
| Canonical marker | gross grasp, subtle reflection, joy, I-am-ness | practice-produced cessation with residual impressions |
| Seed | object-support remains | residual impressions may remain |
| Danger | attachment to clarity/joy/I-am-ness | confusion with sleep or automatic liberation |
| Relation to kaivalya | prepares discrimination | deepens restraint; still requires exhaustion of ignorance |

### 2022 proportional route

For a ten-marker on stages and God:

1. define absorption;
2. state cognitive versus supra-cognitive;
3. briefly separate the two internal grids;
4. mention seedless and cloud-of-dharma culmination without overloading;
5. give God’s role as contemplative aid, not substitute for discrimination.

### Objection, reply, residual

**Objection:** A state without objectified cognition is indistinguishable from unconsciousness.

**Reply:** Genesis and causal role differ. Sleep arises naturally as a modification and leaves
ignorance intact; supra-cognitive absorption follows disciplined attenuation, carries the force of
prior insight and suppresses the active stream.

**Residual:** The state is difficult to verify from an external standpoint. Its soteriological
meaning depends on the wider causal sequence, not on phenomenological blankness alone.

### Directive, retrieval and application

**Retrieval cue:** **Support–Residue–Seed–Cloud**: support in cognitive absorption; residue after
supra-cognitive restraint; seedless further restraint; cloud-of-dharma culmination.

**Correct answer: C**

## Lesson 8 — God in Yoga: real, powerful, limited

**Progress:** 8/12 · **Stage:** Core · **Coverage:** I17-73–I17-78

### Visual first: the exact divine profile

```text
GOD (Īśvara) = special Puruṣa (puruṣa-viśeṣa)
                   |
       untouched by four bondage-markers
 affliction + action + ripening + karmic deposit
                   |
       unsurpassed seed of omniscience
       teacher of the ancients beyond time
       designated by OṂ (praṇava)
                   |
              PRACTICAL ROLE
 meditation support -> ego-surrender -> obstacle reduction
                   |
              IMPORTANT LIMIT
 no Nyāya-style creator proof in Patañjali
 no arbitrary gift of kaivalya
 discernment remains decisive
```

### Nature of God (Īśvara)

God is a **special Puruṣa**, eternally untouched by affliction, action, karmic ripening and latent
deposit. In God lies the unsurpassed seed of omniscience. God is the timeless teacher of earlier
teachers and is designated by the sacred syllable OṂ (praṇava).

These predicates distinguish God from ordinary Puruṣas without changing the basic dualism. Later
commentators may expand arguments from maximal knowledge, scripture or cosmic coordination; do not
attribute the full later creator-administrator proof-set directly to Patañjali.

### God-surrender (Īśvara-praṇidhāna)

God-surrender appears in three linked settings:

1. as an observance (niyama);
2. as an element of action-oriented yoga (kriyā-yoga);
3. as an independent and powerful aid to absorption.

Meditation on OṂ and its meaning stabilises citta, weakens egoic ownership and counters obstacles.

### What God is not

| Not this | Why |
|---|---|
| Nyāya’s efficient creator and moral governor | Prakṛti remains the material-evolutionary basis; Patañjali foregrounds contemplative function |
| material cause of the world | Yoga retains Sāṃkhya’s Prakṛti |
| Vaiṣṇava-style saviour who grants liberation by fiat | isolation depends on discriminative discernment |
| decorative addition | God supplies distinctive devotional and psychological resources |

The most exact formula is: **God is instrumental, not constitutive, in liberation.**

### If God is optional, why retain God?

**Pragmatic-soteriological case:**

- a perfectly unbound focus is uniquely stabilising;
- surrender reduces egoic appropriation;
- sacred sound links theology and attention;
- timeless teacherhood grounds an ideal of transmission;
- obstacle-removal accelerates the means-based path.

**Sāṃkhya objection:** the same discrimination can occur without God.

**Yoga reply:** logical dispensability does not imply practical redundancy. A means may be
non-exclusive yet privileged.

**Nyāya objection:** a God who neither creates nor finally grants release is metaphysically thin.

**Yoga reply:** God’s role is not world-production but contemplative efficacy. The question is
whether such efficacy warrants the special ontological category.

**Residual:** Yoga can show usefulness more easily than necessity. This limited theism remains a
live philosophical pressure.

### 2025 answer route

The question requires **nature and role**, so a list of divine names is inadequate:

1. define special Puruṣa;
2. state the four untouched bondage-markers;
3. add omniscience, timeless teacherhood and OṂ;
4. explain surrender, concentration and obstacle-removal;
5. limit the claim: no creator-first doctrine and no divine fiat;
6. conclude that discernment remains the proximate cause of isolation.

### Directive, retrieval and application

**Retrieval cue:** **Special–Untouched–Teacher–Sound–Support–Limit**.

## Lesson 9 — Discrimination and isolation: what liberation means

**Progress:** 9/12 · **Stage:** Core · **Coverage:** I17-79–I17-85

### Visual first: liberation removes a mistake, not consciousness

```text
CONJUNCTION (saṃyoga)
Puruṣa appears to own citta’s states
             |
      ignorance (avidyā)
             |
             v
practice -> absorption -> discriminative discernment
                         (viveka-khyāti)
             |
             v
higher dispassion -> cloud-of-dharma absorption
             |
             v
qualities return toward source (pratiprasava)
their service to this Puruṣa is complete
             |
             v
ISOLATION (kaivalya)
abiding in own nature (svarūpa-pratiṣṭhā)
```

### Why liberation is needed if Puruṣa is eternally pure

Puruṣa is never intrinsically modified or bound. Bondage is false ownership mediated by ignorance
and reflection in citta. Liberation therefore does not manufacture a new property in
consciousness; it ends the experienced conjunction and its karmic consequences.

### Discriminative discernment (viveka-khyāti)

Discriminative discernment is stable recognition of the absolute difference between conscious
seer and every quality-made object, including the most refined citta. It is not a conceptual
slogan or a temporary trance. Absorption matters because it makes the distinction continuous and
existentially effective.

### Higher dispassion and final transition

The yogin relinquishes not only ordinary pleasures but also subtle clarity, knowledge and powers.
Without higher dispassion, refined egoity could appropriate enlightenment. Cloud-of-dharma
absorption marks the final maturation; the qualities (guṇas) then cease serving the liberated
Puruṣa and return toward their source (pratiprasava).

This withdrawal is indexed to **this Puruṣa**. The cosmos need not disappear for every other
witness.

### Three transition images

| Image | What it explains | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Farmer breaches an embankment | discipline removes obstruction; it does not create Puruṣa’s freedom | does not itself explain reflection |
| Burnt seed | destroyed affliction cannot generate future bondage | residual embodiment still needs explanation |
| Potter’s wheel | already-fructifying embodiment may continue after fresh propulsion ends | analogy should not import another school’s full karma theory |

### Exact meaning of isolation (kaivalya)

Isolation means the absolute distinctness of Puruṣa from Prakṛti and its evolutes. Positively, it is
abiding in one’s own nature (svarūpa-pratiṣṭhā). It is:

- not union with God;
- not identity with Brahman;
- not annihilation of consciousness;
- not sleep;
- not merely negative painlessness;
- not physical destruction of mind as a substance.

### Liberation contrasts

| Axis | Yoga isolation | Advaita liberation | Nyāya release |
|---|---|---|---|
| Self | many Puruṣas | one non-dual self/Brahman | enduring substantial selves |
| Error | seer confused with citta/Prakṛti | superimposition under ignorance | false knowledge of categories/self |
| End | separation and self-abiding | identity-realisation | cessation of suffering and qualities associated with bondage |
| God | contemplative aid | qualified Lord at empirical level | creator/moral governor in developed Nyāya |

### Objection, reply, residual

**Objection:** Isolation sounds negative, empty and relationally impoverished.

**Reply:** Yoga describes a positive self-abiding of pure consciousness free from alien
appropriation. Its positivity is clarity, not merger or activity.

**Residual:** Traditions that define fulfilment through bliss, knowledge-of-an-object, communion or
creative agency may still find contentless witnesshood austere.

### Directive, retrieval and application

**Retrieval cue:** **Already free–Discern–Detach–Withdraw–Abide**.

## Lesson 10 — Realism, rival schools and the hardest objections

**Progress:** 10/12 · **Stage:** Core evaluation · **Coverage:** I17-86–I17-87

### Visual first: Yoga’s three-term realism

```text
REAL OBJECT (viṣaya)
exists independently of one mind
        |
        v
MENTAL MODIFICATION (citta-vṛtti)
is coloured by the object; itself knowable
        |
        v
WITNESS (Puruṣa)
knows citta without becoming its modification

Against mind-only: object ≠ cognition
Against momentariness: citta bears traces through real transformations
Against self-denial: witness terminates the regress of known cognitions
```

### Buddhist challenges

#### Momentariness

**Challenge:** A causally connected series of momentary cognitions can explain continuity without a
persisting citta or Puruṣa.

**Yoga’s reply:** Impressions, memories and transformations require a continuing Prakṛtic
substrate; a stable witness is required because citta itself is known.

**Residual:** A Buddhist can reconstruct continuity through causal dependence rather than
substance. Yoga must show why bearing a trace requires numerical persistence.

#### Cognition-only

**Challenge:** Shared karmic impressions can explain common appearances without mind-independent
objects.

**Yoga’s reply:**

1. an object does not vanish when one citta ceases to cognise it;
2. many cittas can encounter the same object;
3. citta is not self-luminous because it is itself seen;
4. one act cannot fully illuminate itself and its object in the same respect;
5. object-colouring (uparāga) is better explained by a real object.

**Residual:** The Buddhist shared-impression model remains a serious alternative; Yoga’s realism
is coherent but not established by assertion alone.

### Master objection–reply–residual matrix

| Objection | Yoga reply | Residual force |
|---|---|---|
| Pure inactive Puruṣa cannot be bound | bondage is misidentification in citta, not alteration of Puruṣa | how do radically distinct orders form one experience? |
| Insentient citta cannot cognise | citta borrows luminosity through reflection | reflection/proximity may rename interaction |
| Valid cognition should not cease | use non-afflicted truth as a ladder, then transcend it | how does knowledge extinguish itself coherently? |
| Supra-cognitive absorption is sleep | different genesis, causal history and soteriological role | external verification is difficult |
| God is unnecessary | privileged practical support, not exclusive logical cause | usefulness does not prove ontological necessity |
| Isolation is merely negative | positive self-abiding of unconfused consciousness | relational traditions may find it thin |
| Meditation without ethics can succeed technically | technical power is not non-afflicted liberation | ethics remains necessary but not sufficient |
| Karma and rebirth exceed perception | yogic insight and explanatory moral continuity | public evidential access remains contested |

### Cross-school positioning

| Axis | Yoga | Sāṃkhya | Advaita | Nyāya | Buddhism |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| self | many witnessing Puruṣas | same base | one non-dual self | substantial selves | no permanent self |
| mind | persisting subtle citta | inner organ distinctions | inner organ under ignorance | atomic manas | causal stream |
| God | special Puruṣa, aid | no necessary God | Lord at empirical level | creator/efficient cause | no creator |
| liberation | isolation | discrimination/isolation | identity-realisation | release from suffering | nirvāṇa |
| world | realist Prakṛti evolution | realist | empirically real/dependently apparent | plural realism | school-dependent; momentary/dependent |

### Controlled evaluation

**Philosophical strengths**

- integrated account of attention, memory, habit, emotion, conduct and liberation;
- graded method rather than bare speculation;
- ethics and meditation mutually constrain each other;
- realism and causal psychology make the system more than posture-practice;
- objections can be met without sacrificing Puruṣa’s immutability.

**Philosophical weaknesses**

- reflection does not conclusively solve the interaction problem;
- God’s practical role exceeds the argument for God’s necessity;
- karmic trans-life causation and extraordinary cognition resist public verification;
- isolation may appear normatively thin compared with communion or non-dual fullness.

**Graded verdict:** Yoga is strongest as an operational phenomenology and moral psychology of
attention, and weaker where its dualist relation and limited theism require independent
metaphysical proof.

### Directive, retrieval and application

**Retrieval cue:** **Object–Mind–Witness; then four residuals: relation, evidence, God, value**.

## PYQ 1 — 2019 Q7(b), 15 marks

**Exact question:** “How would Yoga philosophy comprehend the Citta-levels of a Scientist, a
God-realized Devotee and a Self-realized Yogi? Justify your answer.”

### Demand decode

The directive requires applied classification **and justification**. The answer must reason from
the operative condition of citta, not assign levels merely from occupational or honorific labels.

### Model answer

Yoga’s commentarial psychology classifies citta into scattered (kṣipta), stupefied (mūḍha),
intermittently focused (vikṣipta), one-pointed (ekāgra) and restrained (niruddha) conditions.
The first three remain pre-yogic; one-pointedness and restraint are genuinely Yoga-fit.

A scientist capable of intense inquiry is best placed, with qualification, in the intermittently
focused condition. Sattva enables sustained bursts of analysis, but ordinary research competence
may coexist with attachment, ambition and wider distraction. A particular scientist can display
task-specific one-pointedness, yet this does not automatically establish liberation-oriented
ekāgra-bhūmi.

A God-realized devotee whose attention flows steadily toward God (Īśvara) exemplifies the
one-pointed condition, because a stable support remains. However, if “realized” means that every
modification has actually been restrained, the more exact classification is restrained
(niruddha), not merely one-pointed.

A self-realized yogi whose mental modifications are fully held in restraint exemplifies the
restrained condition. The qualification is metaphysically crucial: citta moves through these
levels; Puruṣa does not. Puruṣa remains an unchanging witness.

Thus Yoga classifies states by stability, quality-pattern and relation to mental modification.
The labels “scientist,” “devotee” and “yogi” provide evidence only when their actual mental
condition is specified.

## PYQ 2 — 2020 Q5(b), 10 marks

**Exact question:** “Explain the difference between Samprajñāta Samādhi and Asamprajñāta Samādhi.”

### Demand decode

“Difference” requires a controlled axis-based contrast, not two disconnected definitions.

### Model answer

Cognitive absorption (samprajñāta samādhi) is object-supported and cognitively determinate.
A meditative support remains, so it is “with seed” (sabīja). Yoga Sūtra 1.17 identifies gross
grasp (vitarka), subtle reflection (vicāra), refined joy (ānanda) and bare I-am-ness (asmitā) as
its factors. Its object-coalescences may also be distinguished as gross/subtle and with/without
conceptual overlay.

Supra-cognitive absorption (asamprajñāta samādhi) follows disciplined restraint in which active
objectified cognition ceases. Yet residual impressions may remain (saṃskāra-śeṣa); hence it should
not be identified automatically with final seedless absorption or isolation.

The central differences are therefore:

| Axis | Samprajñāta | Asamprajñāta |
|---|---|---|
| support | remains | active support ceases |
| cognition | determinate meditative knowledge | no active determinate cognition |
| residue | object-seed present | latent impressions may remain |
| danger | attachment to refined cognition | confusion with sleep/final release |

Unlike sleep, supra-cognitive absorption is the outcome of prior practice and insight. Both are
stages in Yoga’s path; neither by itself guarantees isolation without stable discrimination and
exhaustion of binding causes.

## PYQ 3 — 2021 Q6(a), 20 marks

**Exact question:** “Explain with reference to Yoga Philosophy, the nature of kleśas. How does the
removal of these lead to kaivalya?”

### Demand decode

This is a two-part causal question: complete diagnosis of afflictions, followed by the mechanism
linking their removal to isolation.

### Model answer

Yoga calls the deep afflictive colourings of citta afflictions (kleśas). They are not merely
passing emotions; they generate binding action and reproduce saṃsāra. Ignorance (avidyā) is their
field: it mistakes the impermanent for eternal, impure for pure, painful for pleasant and non-self
for self. From it arise egoic identification (asmitā), which confuses the seer with the cognitive
instrument; attachment (rāga), rooted in remembered pleasure; aversion (dveṣa), rooted in
remembered pain; and life-clinging (abhiniveśa), present even among the learned.

Each affliction may be dormant, attenuated, interrupted or active. This distinction prevents a
temporary suppression from being mistaken for destruction. Afflictions motivate action; action
leaves a karmic deposit (karmāśaya); that deposit ripens (vipāka) as birth, lifespan and
pleasure-pain experience. The system therefore joins moral psychology to a theory of rebirth.

Removal is graded. Action-oriented yoga—austerity, study and God-surrender—attenuates gross
afflictions. Restraints and observances purify conduct; posture, breath and sense withdrawal reduce
disturbance; concentration, meditation and absorption attack subtler traces. Non-afflicted
cognition matures into stable discriminative discernment (viveka-khyāti), which distinguishes
Puruṣa from every modification of Prakṛti.

When ignorance and egoic appropriation cease, actions no longer create fresh binding deposits.
The burnt-seed image explains why exhausted afflictions cannot germinate, while the potter’s-wheel
image allows already-fructifying embodiment to continue briefly. Higher dispassion relinquishes
even insight and powers; cloud-of-dharma absorption completes the transition. The qualities then
cease their service to this Puruṣa and return toward their source.

Isolation (kaivalya) is thus not produced as a new property. It is the absolute distinctness and
self-abiding of the eternally pure witness after the causal machinery of false ownership is
exhausted. A critic may object that latent karma is unverifiable and suppression can recur.
Yoga replies by distinguishing interrupted/dormant affliction from genuinely burnt causal seeds.
Its account is strong as an integrated therapy of habit and attention, though its trans-life
causal metaphysics remains contestable.

## PYQ 4 — 2022 Q5(c), 10 marks

**Exact question:** “Discuss the nature and different stages of Samādhi as per Pātañjala yoga and
examine the role of Īśvara in it.”

### Demand decode

The answer must balance nature, stages and God within ten marks; God cannot displace the absorption
taxonomy.

### Model answer

Absorption (samādhi) is the radical unification and transparency of citta in which the object
shines with minimal discursive interference. It is neither sleep nor ordinary concentration.

Pātañjala Yoga first distinguishes cognitive absorption (samprajñāta), where an object and
determinate cognition remain, from supra-cognitive absorption (asamprajñāta), where active
objectification ceases though residual impressions may survive. Within cognitive absorption,
Yoga Sūtra 1.17 names gross grasp, subtle reflection, refined joy and bare I-am-ness. A second,
non-identical grid distinguishes gross and subtle object-coalescence with or without conceptual
overlay: savitarkā/nirvitarkā and savicārā/nirvicārā. Refined non-reflective clarity yields
truth-bearing insight. Further restraint checks residual impressions in seedless absorption;
cloud-of-dharma absorption precedes isolation.

God (Īśvara) is a special Puruṣa untouched by affliction, action, ripening and deposits. God-
surrender and meditation on OṂ stabilise attention, reduce egoity and counter obstacles. Yet God
does not replace practice or discriminative discernment and is not primarily Patañjali’s
Nyāya-style creator. God is therefore a privileged contemplative aid, while isolation finally
depends on stable seer–seen discrimination.

## PYQ 5 — 2023 Q7(c), 15 marks

**Exact question:** “Explain Citta and its modifications in the philosophy of Yoga. Why does Yoga
philosophy prescribe cessation of modifications of Citta? Give reasons in support of your answer.”

### Demand decode

The answer has three obligations: define citta, classify its modifications, and give reasons—not
mere assertion—for restraint.

### Model answer

Mind-complex (citta) is Yoga’s unified inner instrument comprising determinative intellect
(buddhi), ego-maker (ahaṃkāra) and coordinating mind (manas). It is a subtle, sattva-predominant
product of Prakṛti: active and changing but intrinsically insentient. It appears conscious because
it reflects Puruṣa and assumes the form of encountered objects.

Patañjali classifies its modifications (vṛttis) into five: valid cognition through perception,
inference and testimony; misapprehension; word-based conceptual construction without a
corresponding object; sleep as an absence-content mode; and memory as preservation of prior
experience. Each can be afflicted or non-afflicted depending on whether it strengthens bondage or
aids discrimination.

Yoga prescribes restraint because ignorance converts reflection into identity. When citta changes,
the empirical “I” appropriates cognition, pleasure, pain and memory as states of the witness.
Afflicted modifications reinforce attachment, aversion, latent impressions, action and karmic
deposit. Even valid cognition remains a changing form; it can serve as a non-afflicted ladder but
cannot be the unchanging self.

Practice stabilises attention and dispassion removes craving for its objects. As modifications are
restrained, the seer no longer appears identical with the seen and abides in its own nature. Thus
restraint is revelatory rather than destructive: it ends false ownership, not consciousness.

The strongest objection is that restraining truth appears anti-intellectual. Yoga replies that
valid cognition first corrects error and matures discrimination; only after completing this task is
its modification transcended. The account coherently links epistemology to liberation, though the
reflection relation between inactive Puruṣa and insentient citta remains metaphysically difficult.

## PYQ 6 — 2024 Q7(b), 15 marks

**Exact question:** “So long as there are changes and modifications in citta, the self is
reflected therein, and, in the absence of discriminative knowledge, identifies itself with them.”
Present an appraisal of Yoga Soteriology in the light of the above statement.

### Demand decode

“Appraisal” demands reconstruction, strengths, objection, reply, residual and a graded verdict.

### Model answer

The statement condenses Yoga’s soteriology. Citta is a transparent but insentient evolute of
Prakṛti. It assumes object-forms and reflects the light of the unchanging Puruṣa. Under ignorance,
egoic identification confuses the instrument of seeing with the seer; thinking, acting, pleasure
and suffering are attributed to pure consciousness.

This misidentification produces a causal sequence. Mental modifications become afflicted;
attachment, aversion and life-clinging motivate action; action deposits karmic traces; their
ripening perpetuates embodied experience. Since Puruṣa is never intrinsically altered, salvation
must transform citta rather than the witness.

Yoga’s practical route is correspondingly integrated. Practice and dispassion stabilise and
detach the mind. Action-oriented yoga attenuates afflictions; the eight limbs join ethics, body,
breath, senses and attention; absorption clarifies non-afflicted cognition. Stable discriminative
discernment distinguishes Puruṣa from every mental state. Higher dispassion prevents appropriation
of insight itself, and isolation follows when the qualities cease functioning for this Puruṣa.

The theory’s strength is that it links a precise phenomenology of attention and habit to an
operational path. It explains liberation as removal of false attribution rather than production of
a new self. However, an ontological problem survives: if Puruṣa and citta are radically distinct,
mere proximity or reflection may not explain their apparent unity. Further, useful discriminative
cognition must arise before being restrained.

Yoga replies that all change and error occur only in citta; Puruṣa is never literally bound.
Non-afflicted knowledge is a ladder whose final transcendence is coherent once confusion is
removed. The soteriology is therefore powerful as disciplined psychology, though its reflection
model remains the vulnerable metaphysical hinge.

## PYQ 7 — 2025 Q7(b), 15 marks

**Exact question:** “Explain the nature of God and its role in Kaivalya in yoga philosophy.”

### Demand decode

Both divine profile and causal role in liberation are required. A creator-God essay would miss
the owner doctrine.

### Model answer

Yoga defines God (Īśvara) as a special Puruṣa (puruṣa-viśeṣa), eternally untouched by affliction,
action, karmic ripening and latent deposit. In God lies the unsurpassed seed of omniscience; God is
the teacher of the ancients beyond temporal limitation and is designated by OṂ.

This account must be distinguished from developed Nyāya. Patañjali does not primarily present God
as the efficient creator and karmic administrator of a world materially constituted by atoms.
Prakṛti remains the basis of evolution. Later commentators may enlarge God’s cosmic role, but the
Yoga Sūtra foregrounds contemplative and pedagogic functions.

God’s role in the path is nevertheless real. God-surrender is an element of action-oriented yoga
and observance, and an independent aid to absorption. Meditation on OṂ provides a uniquely
unafflicted support; surrender weakens egoic ownership; devotion counters obstacles and stabilises
one-pointed attention. God is also the ideal of freedom and timeless instruction.

Yet isolation is not an arbitrary divine gift. It occurs through stable discriminative discernment
between Puruṣa and Prakṛti, followed by higher dispassion and cessation of the qualities’ service.
God accelerates and supports this process but does not substitute for it. Hence God is
**instrumental, not constitutive**, in isolation.

The Sāṃkhya objection is that an optional aid is metaphysically redundant. Yoga answers that
non-exclusive means can still be uniquely efficacious. The residual difficulty is that practical
usefulness does not by itself establish ontological necessity. Yoga’s theism is therefore genuine
but deliberately limited: God is the supreme contemplative support within a Sāṃkhyan frame.

## PYQ 8 — 2026 Q5(e), 10 marks

**Exact question:** “Explain, as per Yoga philosophy, the nature of Samadhi with special reference
to the difference between Samprajnata Samadhi and Asamprajnata Samadhi.”

### Demand decode

Unlike the 2020 wording, this question explicitly requires the nature of absorption before the
special-reference comparison.

### Model answer

Absorption (samādhi) is the culmination of meditative integration in which citta becomes
one-pointed and transparent, allowing the object to shine with minimal discursive interference.
It is not sleep, passivity or liberation by itself; its soteriological purpose is to mature
discriminative discernment.

Cognitive absorption (samprajñāta samādhi) retains an object-support and determinate meditative
knowledge. Yoga Sūtra 1.17 describes it through gross grasp, subtle reflection, refined joy and
bare I-am-ness. The related gross/subtle coalescences with or without conceptual overlay should be
stated as a separate grid.

Supra-cognitive absorption (asamprajñāta samādhi) follows further restraint: active objectified
cognition ceases, but residual impressions may remain. It therefore differs from sleep by its
disciplined origin and liberating causal history, and from final seedless absorption because the
impression-seed may not yet be exhausted.

Thus the primary contrast is object-supported knowledge versus cessation of active objectification
with possible residue. Both belong to the graded path, but neither automatically equals isolation,
which requires stable discrimination, higher dispassion and exhaustion of binding causes.

## A1 — Commentarial mapping without taxonomy collapse

The two absorption grids can be related without being identified. A commentator may map gross

I-am-ness as progressively subtler supports. The gain is a developmental sequence; the price is

**Deployment:** use after accurately stating both grids in a question on “different stages.”
**Stop rule:** one sentence on interpretive mapping is enough; do not turn a ten-marker into a

## A2 — Meditative epistemology and the status of truth-bearing insight

Truth-bearing insight (ṛtambharā prajñā) is not merely intense conviction. It emerges from the
lucidity of non-reflective subtle coalescence and is claimed to disclose particulars beyond the
generality of testimony and inference. Its new impression checks ordinary impressions; final
restraint then checks even this liberating impression.

**Philosophical gain:** Yoga explains how disciplined attention can transform what and how one
knows, rather than treating meditation as affective calm.

**Pressure:** private contemplative certainty can be mistaken for error. The system needs
discipline, non-affliction and coherence with the causal path as internal controls, but public
replicability remains limited.

**Deployment:** useful in a twenty-marker on Yoga as philosophy, not necessary in a basic
samprajñāta/asamprajñāta distinction.

## A3 — Reflection as a philosophy-of-mind model

Yoga separates:

```text
intentional content -> citta’s object-form
subjective illumination -> reflected Puruṣa
ownership -> ahaṃkāra/asmitā
```

This tripartite analysis prevents both material reduction and idealist collapse. It also resembles
a distinction between mental content, awareness and self-ascription.

**Gain:** it explains how cognition can have content without making matter intrinsically
conscious, and how awareness can be present without making Puruṣa an agent.

**Price:** the model inherits an interaction problem. If reflection has no real relation, it
explains too little; if it has a real relation, radical dualism is qualified.

**Deployment:** excellent as one evaluative paragraph in the 2024 soteriology question.

## A4 — Habit, affect and moral agency

Yoga’s sequence of memory → pleasure/pain trace → attachment/aversion → action anticipates a
philosophy of habit in which cognition and affect are inseparable. Practice creates counter-
impressions; dispassion removes incentive; ethics alters the situations that feed turbulence;
meditation reaches the latent source.

**Gain:** the theory avoids the intellectualist claim that merely knowing a proposition dissolves
a habit.

**Price:** explaining every entrenched tendency through inherited karmic deposits risks making the
account too expansive to test.

**Deployment:** useful in questions on afflictions, ethics or the practical superiority of Yoga
to a merely theoretical discrimination.

# ORIGINAL SOLVED MAINS PRACTICE

## Original 10-marker

**Question:** Distinguish restraint (nirodha) from suppression and sleep in Yoga philosophy.
Answer in about 150 words.

### Model answer

Restraint (nirodha) is the disciplined checking of mental modifications so that the witness no
longer identifies with them and abides in its own nature. It arises through sustained practice,
dispassion, ethical purification and meditative integration. It is therefore a soteriologically
ordered transformation of citta.

Suppression is weaker and unstable. An affliction may be dormant or interrupted because another
tendency presently dominates; its causal seed can revive. Yoga consequently distinguishes
temporary non-manifestation from attenuation and final loss of karmic fertility.

Sleep is itself one of the five modifications. It is grounded in absence-content and leaves a
recollectable trace—“I slept.” It does not by itself produce discriminative discernment. Supra-
cognitive absorption may lack active objectification, but it differs from sleep in origin,
preparation and causal function.

Thus stillness is not a sufficient criterion. Restraint is luminous, trained and discrimination-
oriented; suppression is reversible; sleep is ordinary obscuration. The distinction protects Yoga
from equating liberation with blankness.

## Original 15-marker

**Question:** “Yoga’s admission of God is practically significant but metaphysically under-
determined.” Critically examine. Answer in about 200 words.

### Model answer

Yoga’s God (Īśvara) is a special Puruṣa, untouched by affliction, action, ripening and karmic
deposit. God possesses the unsurpassed seed of omniscience, teaches beyond temporal limitation and
is designated by OṂ. The doctrine is therefore more than a devotional ornament.

Its practical significance is clear. God-surrender belongs to action-oriented yoga and the
observances; meditation on OṂ provides an exceptionally stable support; surrender weakens egoic
appropriation and helps overcome obstacles. God embodies the ideal of freedom and anchors
contemplative transmission. In this sense, Yoga is genuinely theistic and differs from classical
non-theistic Sāṃkhya.

Yet God is not the material cause, is not foregrounded as Nyāya’s efficient creator, and does not
grant isolation by fiat. Stable discriminative discernment remains the decisive cause of release.
The Sāṃkhya critic can therefore ask why a non-necessary being should be added; the Nyāya critic
can ask whether contemplative usefulness proves divine reality.

Yoga can answer that non-exclusive means may still be uniquely efficacious and that a perfectly
unafflicted teacher-support has coherent soteriological value. Nevertheless, practical efficacy
does not independently establish ontological necessity. The judgement is therefore accurate:
Yoga’s God is doctrinally real and practically powerful, but metaphysically limited and less fully
argued than Nyāya’s creator.

## Original 20-marker

**Question:** Evaluate Yoga as an integrated philosophy of mind, moral causation and liberation.
Answer in about 300 words.

### Model answer

Yoga integrates philosophy of mind, moral psychology and soteriology within Sāṃkhya dualism.
Puruṣa is conscious, inactive and unchanging; citta is an active, subtle and insentient complex of
intellect, ego-maker and coordinating mind. Objects colour citta and Puruṣa’s light is reflected
in it. Egoic identification then attributes thought and suffering to the witness.

Its philosophy of mind is unusually differentiated. Five mental modifications—valid cognition,
error, construction, sleep and memory—can be afflicted or non-afflicted. Practice stabilises
attention; dispassion removes incentive; real transformations explain continuity of habit.
However, the reflection model faces an interaction difficulty: mere proximity between radically
different principles may name rather than explain unified experience.

Yoga’s moral causation deepens this psychology. Ignorance generates egoity, attachment, aversion
and life-clinging. Afflicted action deposits karmic force, which ripens as birth, lifespan and
pleasure-pain experience. The four conditions of affliction distinguish dormancy from destruction.
The burnt-seed account explains cessation of future bondage, though trans-life deposits remain
difficult to verify publicly.

The path is equally integrated. Action-oriented yoga and the first five limbs reshape conduct,
body, breath and sense-orientation. Concentration, meditation and absorption clarify citta.
Cognitive and supra-cognitive absorptions are stages, not liberation itself. God supplies a
privileged support, while stable discriminative discernment distinguishes Puruṣa from every
quality-made state. Higher dispassion, cloud-of-dharma absorption and withdrawal of the qualities
culminate in isolation.

Yoga’s strength is operational completeness: ethical practice, affect, attention, knowledge and
liberation form one causal architecture. Its weaknesses are the dualist relation, limited proof of
God, contested karma metaphysics and austere ideal of isolation. Overall, it is philosophically
strongest as a disciplined phenomenology and therapy of false ownership, while its metaphysical
supports remain open to Advaita, Nyāya and Buddhist criticism.

## MCQ 13

A learner equates restrained mind (niruddha) with the stupefied level (mūḍha) because both can look
inactive. What is the best repair?

A. Stupefaction is tamasic obscuration; restraint is trained cessation serving luminous discernment.
B. Both are equivalent cessations of mental activity, distinguished only by occurring in different bodies.
C. Restrained mind is a deeper form of sleep in which cognition and discriminative capacity disappear.
D. Stupefaction is the final contemplative stage reached after one-pointed attention is perfected.

### Answer and explanation

**Correct answer: A**

- **A — Correct:** It distinguishes quality, genesis and soteriological value.
- **B — Incorrect:** They are opposed conditions, not bodily variants.
- **C — Incorrect:** Sleep is itself a modification; restraint is not deeper sleep.
- **D — Incorrect:** Stupefaction is pre-yogic.

## MCQ 14

A learner writes, “Supra-cognitive absorption is seedless because no object remains.” Which
correction is most exact?

A. Every objectless condition is liberating because absence of support proves that all causal seeds are exhausted.
B. Supra-cognitive absorption may retain residual impressions; final seedlessness restrains that remaining seed.
C. The remaining seed refers only to physical matter, never to an impression capable of renewing cognition.
D. Cognitive absorption lacks object-support, whereas supra-cognitive absorption retains a determinate support.

### Answer and explanation

**Correct answer: B**

- **A — Incorrect:** Objectlessness does not establish exhaustion of causal residue.
- **B — Correct:** It preserves the YS 1.18/1.51 distinction.
- **C — Incorrect:** “Seed” is technical and context-sensitive.
- **D — Incorrect:** Cognitive absorption is object-supported.

## MCQ 15

A learner claims that God directly produces isolation. Which doctrinal sequence repairs the claim?

A. God creates Prakṛti, governs its evolution, and finally merges every finite Puruṣa into himself.
B. God replaces ethical discipline and meditation, so surrender alone produces isolation without discernment.
C. God aids concentration and removes obstacles; discrimination and higher dispassion end false ownership.
D. God transforms insentient citta into a conscious substance that can liberate itself without Puruṣa.

### Answer and explanation

**Correct answer: C**

- **A — Incorrect:** Yoga preserves plural Puruṣas and Prakṛti.
- **B — Incorrect:** God-surrender functions inside a wider discipline.
- **C — Correct:** It states the instrumental, non-constitutive divine role.
- **D — Incorrect:** Citta remains an insentient Prakṛtic instrument.

## MCQ 16

Which concluding verdict best appraises Yoga’s reflection theory?

A. It resolves dualism’s interaction problem because reflection establishes a literal relation between its terms.
B. It is unnecessary because citta is intrinsically conscious and needs no reflected illumination from Puruṣa.
C. It entails that Puruṣa transforms into each cognition while citta remains an unchanging material instrument.
D. It preserves an immutable witness and explains false ownership, but mere proximity leaves a relation problem.

### Answer and explanation

**Correct answer: D**

- **A — Incorrect:** The relation problem remains the chief residual.
- **B — Incorrect:** Yoga denies citta intrinsic consciousness.
- **C — Incorrect:** All transformation occurs on the citta side.
- **D — Correct:** It gives both the strongest achievement and the surviving weakness.

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

## 1. Identity and governing thesis

- Pātañjala Yoga is the received *Yoga Sūtra* tradition read with Vyāsa’s *Yoga Bhāṣya*.
- Four chapters: absorption, practice, powers and isolation.
- Five mind-levels are a standard commentarial classification, not Patañjali’s five-item sūtra.
- Yoga is “theistic Sāṃkhya” (seśvara-Sāṃkhya): Puruṣa–Prakṛti dualism and 25 principles, plus
  a systematic path and God as special Puruṣa.
- Governing pair: restraint of mental modifications → seer abides in its own nature.
- Do not write: posture-only Yoga, Brahman-union, God-created Prakṛti or consciousness-destruction.

## 2. Mind-complex and reflected consciousness

```text
object -> citta assumes object-form
Puruṣa -> citta borrows luminosity
ego-maker -> “this cognition/action/suffering is I”
```

- Mind-complex (citta) = intellect (buddhi) + ego-maker (ahaṃkāra) + coordinating mind (manas).
- Correct evolution: intellect → ego-maker → coordinating mind; the whole field is
  sattva-predominant, but not all components arise directly from sattvic ego.
- Citta is changing and insentient; Puruṣa is conscious and unchanging.
- Crystal analogy: object colours citta; Puruṣa’s light is reflected; no literal merger.
- Mere-proximity reply preserves Puruṣa’s inactivity; residual problem is relation between two
  irreducible orders.

### Five mind-levels

| Level | Quality/condition | Recall |
|---|---|---|
| scattered (kṣipta) | rajas | active but dispersed |
| stupefied (mūḍha) | tamas | dull, not restrained |
| intermittently focused (vikṣipta) | mixed | bursts, not stable |
| one-pointed (ekāgra) | sattva | sustained support |
| restrained (niruddha) | modes held | highest Yoga-fit condition |

- Only one-pointed and restrained are fully Yoga-fit.
- 2019 route: scientist usually intermittent; God-focused devotee one-pointed; self-realized yogi
  restrained—always qualify by actual state, not title.

## 3. Mental modifications and restraint

| Modification | Kernel |
|---|---|
| valid cognition (pramāṇa) | perception, inference, testimony; true but still changing |
| misapprehension (viparyaya) | contrary to object |
| conceptual construction (vikalpa) | word-based, no corresponding object |
| sleep (nidrā) | absence-content with recollected trace |
| memory (smṛti) | preserved prior experience |

- Every modification may be afflicted or non-afflicted.
- Non-afflicted truth is a ladder: it corrects error and matures discrimination, then is
  transcended.
- Practice = long, uninterrupted, committed stabilisation.
- Lower dispassion = mastery over craving for seen/promised goods.
- Higher dispassion = no craving even for qualities, insight or powers.
- Practice without dispassion can strengthen desire; dispassion without practice leaves citta
  unstable.
- Three citta-transformations: restraint, absorption and one-pointedness.
- Change can be analysed as property, temporal phase and state; a persisting substrate remains.

## 4. Afflictions and moral causation

### Five afflictions

1. ignorance: impermanent/impure/painful/non-self misread as their opposites;
2. egoic identification: seer confused with instrument;
3. attachment: remembered pleasure;
4. aversion: remembered pain;
5. life-clinging: deep fear of death even among the learned.

### Four conditions

```text
dormant -> attenuated -> interrupted -> active
```

Do not confuse dormancy or interruption with eradication.

### Causal sequence

```text
affliction -> afflicted action -> karmic deposit -> ripening
                                              -> birth
                                              -> lifespan
                                              -> pleasure/pain experience
```

- Action types: white, black, mixed, neither-white-nor-black.
- Action-oriented Yoga: austerity + study + God-surrender.
- Gross affliction responds to disciplined conduct; subtle seeds require meditation and
  discernment.
- Burnt seed = no future germination; potter’s wheel = residual embodiment; farmer = obstruction
  removed, freedom not manufactured.

## 5. Eight-limbed route

```text
restraints -> observances -> posture -> breath -> sense withdrawal
     -> concentration -> meditation -> absorption
```

- Five restraints: non-injury, truth, non-stealing, disciplined sexuality, non-grasping.
- Universal great vow: not limited by class, place, time or circumstance.
- Five observances: purity, contentment, austerity, study, God-surrender.
- Posture = steady ease; breath = psychophysical hinge; withdrawal = freedom from compulsive
  extroversion.
- Concentration fixes; meditation flows; absorption integrates.
- Five outer limbs; three inner limbs.
- Concentration + meditation + absorption on one object = integrated discipline (saṃyama).
- Ethics is necessary because meditative power can coexist with ego; ethics alone remains
  insufficient for discrimination.

## 6. Absorption taxonomy

### Nature

- Absorption is radically unified, transparent attention.
- Not ordinary sleep, trance, relaxation or automatic isolation.

### Grid A: YS 1.17 factors of cognitive absorption

```text
gross grasp -> subtle reflection -> refined joy -> bare I-am-ness
```

### Grid B: YS 1.42–1.44 object-coalescences

```text
gross with overlay / gross without overlay
subtle with reflection / subtle without reflection
```

- The grids are related but not identical.
- Non-reflective subtle lucidity yields truth-bearing insight.
- “With seed” in object-supported absorption means support remains.
- Truth-bearing insight is not casually a fourth ordinary means of knowledge.

### Main contrast

| Axis | Cognitive (samprajñāta) | Supra-cognitive (asamprajñāta) |
|---|---|---|
| support | remains | active objectification ceases |
| cognition | determinate | no active determinate cognition |
| residue | object-support | impressions may remain |
| trap | attachment to refinement | sleep/final-release confusion |

- Seedless absorption requires further restraint of residual impression.
- Condition-based route can include disembodied/nature-merged states; means-based route deliberately
  uses faith, energy, memory, absorption and insight.
- Cloud-of-dharma absorption follows stable discrimination and non-attachment.
- Powers are obstacles if appropriated.
- Nine obstacles: illness, dullness, doubt, carelessness, laziness, sensuality, false perception,
  non-attainment and instability; symptoms include pain, dejection, tremor and disturbed breath.

## 7. God: nature, function and limit

```text
special Puruṣa
untouched by affliction/action/ripening/deposit
unsurpassed seed of omniscience
timeless teacher
designated by OṂ
```

- God-surrender functions as observance, action-oriented Yoga and independent absorption aid.
- Roles: meditative support, ego-reduction, obstacle-removal, perfect model, teacher principle.
- Not Patañjali’s Nyāya-style creator-first God; Prakṛti remains material basis.
- Later commentators may expand God’s cosmic role.
- God does not bestow isolation by arbitrary fiat.
- Best formula: **instrumental, not constitutive**.
- Main objection: optional aid appears redundant.
- Reply: non-exclusive means can be uniquely efficacious.
- Residual: usefulness does not prove ontological necessity.

## 8. Isolation and final transition

```text
practice/dispassion
-> action-oriented and eight-limbed Yoga
-> absorption
-> stable discriminative discernment
-> higher dispassion
-> cloud-of-dharma absorption
-> return of qualities for this Puruṣa
-> isolation and self-abiding
```

- Discriminative discernment = seer absolutely distinguished from all quality-made seen.
- Puruṣa was never intrinsically bound; liberation ends false ownership and its causes.
- Return of qualities does not mean the cosmos vanishes for all witnesses.
- Isolation = absolute distinctness and self-abiding.
- Not union with God, identity with Brahman, sleep, annihilation, mere painlessness or physical
  destruction of mind.

### Liberation contrast

| Yoga | Advaita | Nyāya |
|---|---|---|
| many Puruṣas | one Brahman/self | many substantial selves |
| separation | identity-realisation | release/cessation of suffering |
| citta/Prakṛti confusion | superimposition | false knowledge |

## 9. Realism and objections

### Three-term realism

```text
real object -> object-shaped mental mode -> witnessing Puruṣa
```

- Against cognition-only: object is not dependent on one mind; citta is itself known; object-
  colouring points to an external cause.
- Against momentariness: citta bears impressions and undergoes real transformations.
- Buddhist replies: causal series can explain continuity; shared karmic traces can explain common
  appearance.

### Four residuals for any critical conclusion

1. **Relation:** how can inactive witness and insentient citta form one experience?
2. **Evidence:** how publicly test yogic perception, karma and supra-cognitive states?
3. **God:** does contemplative efficacy establish a special Puruṣa?
4. **Value:** is isolated witnesshood sufficiently positive?

**Ready verdict:** Yoga is philosophically strongest as an integrated, operational psychology of
attention, habit, affect, ethics and liberation; it is weaker where reflection, trans-life
causation and limited theism require independent metaphysical support.
