## 🖼️ Yoga’s philosophical structure

```text
SĀṂKHYA
Puruṣa–Prakṛti dualism
25 principles + three qualities
Effect pre-existence
Liberation through discrimination
             │
             │ accepted by Yoga
             ↓
           YOGA
      ┌──────┴────────┐
      ↓               ↓
Detailed discipline   Īśvara
of the mind           as special Puruṣa
      ↓
Citta-vṛtti-nirodha
      ↓
Discriminative knowledge
      ↓
Kaivalya
```

> **Sāṃkhya supplies the metaphysical map; Yoga supplies the psychological discipline and practical path.**

## 1. What is classical Yoga?

Classical Yoga is traditionally systematised by **Patañjali** in the *Yoga Sūtra*.

It is often called:

```text
Seśvara-Sāṃkhya
= Sāṃkhya “with Īśvara”
```

| Sāṃkhya | Classical Yoga |
|---|---|
| Generally non-theistic | Accepts Īśvara |
| Explains reality through 25 principles | Accepts the same basic ontology |
| Emphasises discriminative knowledge | Develops a graded practical discipline |
| Analyses intellect, ego and mind separately | Treats them functionally as **citta** |
| Kaivalya through Puruṣa–Prakṛti discrimination | Same goal through mental restraint and discrimination |

Yoga is not simply Sāṃkhya plus devotional belief. It develops a detailed account of:

- the mind;
- mental modifications;
- afflictions;
- moral discipline;
- meditation;
- absorption;
- liberation.

## 2. Patañjali’s definition

The foundational statement is:

> **Yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ**
> Yoga is the restraint or cessation of the modifications of citta.

```text
Citta         = mind-complex or mental field
Vṛtti         = modification, movement or mental pattern
Nirodha       = disciplined restraint or cessation
```

It does not mean destroying the mind through physical force.

It means bringing its movements under such complete control that the distinction between **Puruṣa**, the witness, and **citta**, the witnessed instrument, becomes evident.

## 3. What happens after restraint?

Patañjali continues:

> **Tadā draṣṭuḥ svarūpe’vasthānam**
> Then the seer abides in its own nature.

```text
Ordinary condition
Puruṣa identifies with thoughts, feelings and memories
                         ↓
Citta-vṛtti-nirodha
                         ↓
Misidentification ends
                         ↓
Puruṣa abides as pure witness
```

When restraint is absent:

```text
“I am angry”
“I am afraid”
“I am successful”
```

Yoga interprets these as identification of the seer with conditions of citta.

A more discriminating formulation is:

```text
Anger is present in citta.
Fear is a modification of citta.
Puruṣa is the witness of these states.
```

## 4. Does Yoga mean union?

In popular usage, Yoga is frequently translated as “union.”

But in classical Pātañjala philosophy, its final goal is not:

- union of the individual soul with Brahman;
- merger with God;
- destruction of individuality;
- merely physical health.

Its goal is **kaivalya**:

```text
Puruṣa isolated from false identification with Prakṛti
```

> 🔑 **UPSC distinction:** Vedāntic union and Yogic kaivalya must not be treated as identical.

## 5. Why is Yoga needed if Puruṣa is already pure?

Sāṃkhya and Yoga hold that Puruṣa never literally becomes impure or modified.

The problem is false identification:

```text
Puruṣa = conscious but inactive
Citta  = active but insentient
              ↓
Consciousness appears reflected in citta
              ↓
Citta appears conscious
Puruṣa appears to act and suffer
              ↓
Bondage
```

Yoga does not transform Puruṣa. It purifies and restrains citta so that misidentification ends.

### Analogy

```text
Clear crystal beside a red flower
                ↓
Crystal appears red
                ↓
But its essential nature has not changed
```

Similarly, Puruṣa appears coloured by mental states without actually undergoing them.

## 6. Shared metaphysical foundation

Yoga accepts from Sāṃkhya:

1. many Puruṣas;
2. one primordial Prakṛti;
3. the three qualities;
4. twenty-five principles;
5. real transformation;
6. subtle-body transmigration;
7. liberation through discriminative knowledge;
8. kaivalya as Puruṣa–Prakṛti separation.

Yoga therefore continues directly from what you learned in the previous session.

## 7. What does Yoga add?

```text
Sāṃkhya question:
“What is the difference between Puruṣa and Prakṛti?”

Yoga question:
“How can that difference become directly realised?”
```

Yoga adds:

- a detailed theory of citta;
- five mental modifications;
- five afflictions;
- practice and dispassion;
- action-oriented Yoga;
- eight-limbed discipline;
- stages of samādhi;
- Īśvara-praṇidhāna;
- a precise psychological route to kaivalya.

## 8. Īśvara at this stage

Yoga defines Īśvara as a **Puruṣa-viśeṣa**, or special Puruṣa.

He is untouched by:

- afflictions;
- karma;
- karmic results;
- latent karmic deposits.

At this introductory stage, remember:

```text
Īśvara assists concentration and liberation
                  ≠
Puruṣa merges into Īśvara
```

Yoga does not clearly make Īśvara the creator-God in the Nyāya sense. His exact nature and role will be studied in Subtopic 7.

## 9. Four chapters of the Yoga Sūtra

| Chapter | Main concern |
|---|---|
| Samādhi-pāda | Definition of Yoga, mental restraint and absorption |
| Sādhana-pāda | Afflictions, karma and practical discipline |
| Vibhūti-pāda | Combined meditation and extraordinary capacities |
| Kaivalya-pāda | Discriminative knowledge and final liberation |

Extraordinary powers are not the final goal. Attachment to them can obstruct liberation.

### Mastery MCQ 2

Why does Yoga prescribe **citta-vṛtti-nirodha** if Puruṣa is eternally pure?

A. Puruṣa must acquire consciousness through purified citta.

Puruṣa does not acquire consciousness—it is **consciousness itself**. Citta is an insentient but transparent instrument that appears conscious by reflecting Puruṣa.

```text
Citta: active, modifiable, insentient
                   ↓
False identification creates apparent bondage
                   ↓
Citta-vṛtti-nirodha ends that identification
```

## 🖼️ What is citta?

```text
                 CITTA
        Internal psychical instrument
                      │
       ┌──────────────┼──────────────┐
       ↓              ↓              ↓
    Buddhi         Ahaṃkāra         Manas
Determines       Appropriates     Coordinates
“This is X”      “I know X”       sense-data
```

Yoga functionally combines the three internal faculties distinguished in Sāṃkhya under the term **citta**.

Citta is:

- a product of Prakṛti;
- subtle and sattva-predominant;
- capable of taking the form of an object;
- the field in which thoughts, emotions and memories occur;
- not the conscious self.

## 1. How cognition occurs

```text
Object
   ↓
Senses
   ↓
Manas coordinates
   ↓
Ahaṃkāra appropriates: “I experience”
   ↓
Buddhi determines
   ↓
Citta assumes the object’s form
   ↓
Puruṣa illuminates the cognition
```

Citta performs the mental operation, but Puruṣa supplies consciousness.

## 2. Is citta conscious?

No. Citta is intrinsically insentient because it belongs to Prakṛti.

It appears conscious because it reflects Puruṣa.

### Crystal analogy

```text
Red flower beside a clear crystal
                 ↓
Crystal appears red
                 ↓
Crystal has not become the flower
```

Likewise:

```text
Puruṣa reflected in citta
              ↓
Citta appears conscious
              ↓
Puruṣa appears to think and act
```

The actual transformation occurs only in citta. Puruṣa remains unchanged.

## 3. How bondage arises

```text
Citta experiences anger
          ↓
Consciousness illuminates that state
          ↓
Ahaṃkāra appropriates it
          ↓
“I am angry”
          ↓
Puruṣa is confused with citta
```

Yoga replaces identification with discrimination:

```text
Wrong: “I, the conscious self, am anger.”

Discriminating view:
“Anger is a modification occurring in citta;
Puruṣa is its witness.”
```

## 4. Five citta-bhūmis

The citta-bhūmis are five operative conditions of the mind.

```text
SCATTERED                              RESTRAINED
    │                                      │
Kṣipta → Mūḍha → Vikṣipta → Ekāgra → Niruddha
 rajas    tamas     unstable     one-pointed   restrained
```

This classification comes explicitly from Vyāsa’s commentarial exposition rather than a five-item sūtra stated verbatim by Patañjali.

## 5. Kṣipta: restless

**Dominant quality:** rajas

```text
Thought A → desire → worry → sensation → thought B
```

Characteristics:

- agitated;
- outward-running;
- impulsive;
- unable to sustain attention.

It contains abundant activity but little integration.

## 6. Mūḍha: dull

**Dominant quality:** tamas

Characteristics:

- lethargy;
- confusion;
- delusion;
- mental heaviness;
- lack of discrimination.

> 🔑 Mūḍha is not meditative calmness. Both may appear inactive, but mūḍha is obscured while meditation is alert.

## 7. Vikṣipta: intermittently focused

The mind can concentrate effectively for limited periods, but remains unstable and attached to outward purposes.

```text
Focus → distraction → focus → ambition → focus
```

It may:

- conduct scientific analysis;
- solve difficult problems;
- display intellectual brilliance;
- sustain temporary concentration.

But intellectual concentration alone is not yogic integration.

## 8. Ekāgra: one-pointed

**Dominant quality:** sattva

```text
Many mental streams
        ↓
One uninterrupted stream toward one object
```

Characteristics:

- sustained concentration;
- mental clarity;
- stability;
- suitability for meditation;
- readiness for samādhi.

Ekāgra is the first fully Yoga-fit condition.

## 9. Niruddha: fully restrained

In niruddha:

- mental modifications are fully restrained;
- binding impressions no longer generate ordinary fluctuations;
- discriminative knowledge reaches culmination;
- citta approaches its final soteriological function.

```text
Ekāgra:
one modification remains continuously focused

Niruddha:
even modifications are fully restrained
```

Niruddha is not sleep, coma or unconscious blankness. It is the culmination of disciplined awareness.

## 10. Which conditions are Yoga-fit?

| Condition | Yoga-fit? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Kṣipta | No | Attention is scattered |
| Mūḍha | No | Awareness is obscured |
| Vikṣipta | Not fully | Concentration remains intermittent |
| Ekāgra | Yes | Mind is continuously one-pointed |
| Niruddha | Yes | Modifications are fully restrained |

The decisive transition is:

```text
Vikṣipta → Ekāgra
Occasional focus → sustained one-pointedness
```

## 11. Applied 2019 PYQ

The verified PYQ asks how Yoga would classify the citta-levels of:

| Person | Citta-level | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Scientist | Vikṣipta | Powerful outward concentration, but not necessarily stable yogic restraint |
| God-realised devotee | Ekāgra | Mind remains one-pointedly centred on Īśvara |
| Self-realised yogi | Niruddha | Mental modifications have been fully restrained |

### Important qualification

A particular scientist could develop an ekāgra mind. The PYQ’s conventional mapping assumes a scientist whose concentration remains empirical, outward and purpose-dependent.

The distinction is not intelligence versus spirituality. It is **intermittent outward focus versus sustained yogic one-pointedness**.

## 🖼️ Five modifications of citta

```text
                         CITTA-VṚTTIS
                               │
       ┌──────────┬────────────┼─────────┬─────────┐
       ↓          ↓            ↓         ↓         ↓
   Pramāṇa    Viparyaya     Vikalpa    Nidrā     Smṛti
 valid          error       verbal      sleep     memory
knowledge                  construction
```

A **vṛtti** is a form or modification assumed by citta.

```text
Object, word, absence or past experience
                    ↓
Citta assumes a corresponding form
                    ↓
Puruṣa illuminates that modification
                    ↓
The person says: “I know, imagine, sleep or remember”
```

## 1. Pramāṇa: valid cognition

Pramāṇa is cognition corresponding to its object.

Yoga accepts three sources:

| Pramāṇa | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Pratyakṣa | Perception | Seeing smoke |
| Anumāna | Inference | Inferring fire from smoke |
| Āgama/śabda | Reliable testimony | Learning from an authoritative source |

### Why restrain valid knowledge?

Valid cognition is useful and can promote liberation. Nevertheless, it remains a modification of citta.

```text
False cognition → replace with valid cognition
Valid cognition → develop discrimination
Discriminative cognition → eventually transcended
```

Yoga therefore does not reject knowledge. It uses non-afflicted knowledge therapeutically before transcending all modifications.

## 2. Viparyaya: erroneous cognition

Viparyaya is false knowledge that does not correspond to the object.

```text
Nacre seen at a distance
          ↓
Mistaken for silver
```

A more important Yogic error is:

```text
Citta performs mental activity
          ↓
Puruṣa illuminates it
          ↓
“I, Puruṣa, am acting and suffering”
```

This basic confusion culminates in **avidyā**, studied in the next subtopic.

## 3. Vikalpa: verbal construction

Vikalpa arises from words or concepts without a corresponding real object.

Examples:

- “hare’s horn”;
- “square circle”;
- imaginary identities or fears;
- conceptual combinations without actual referents.

```text
Words are meaningful
         ↓
They generate a mental form
         ↓
But no corresponding object exists
```

Vikalpa is not meaningless noise. It has linguistic intelligibility without objective reference.

## 4. Nidrā: sleep

Patañjali treats sleep as a positive mental modification based upon absence-content.

Why?

```text
During sleep: ordinary objects are absent
                     ↓
After waking: “I slept and knew nothing”
                     ↓
Sleep left a mental impression
                     ↓
It was a determinate condition of citta
```

### Critical distinction

| Sleep | Niruddha/samādhi |
|---|---|
| Tamas and absence dominate | Alert restraint and discrimination |
| Afflictions remain latent | Afflictions are progressively destroyed |
| One wakes with ignorance intact | Practice moves toward liberation |
| Involuntary | Disciplined attainment |

> 🔑 **Mental inactivity is not necessarily spiritual freedom.**

## 5. Smṛti: memory

Smṛti is the re-presentation of an earlier experience without losing its essential content.

```text
Past experience
      ↓
Impression or saṃskāra
      ↓
Appropriate stimulus
      ↓
Memory arises
```

Memory can sustain:

- attachment to past pleasure;
- aversion based on pain;
- fear;
- identity narratives;
- habits.

It may also assist liberation by recalling teachings, previous insight and disciplined commitments.

## 6. Kliṣṭa and akliṣṭa

Each of the five vṛttis can be either:

| Type | Meaning | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Kliṣṭa | Coloured by afflictions | Reinforces bondage |
| Akliṣṭa | Non-afflicted and discriminative | Supports liberation |

### Examples

```text
Pramāṇa + pride        → kliṣṭa
Pramāṇa + discrimination → akliṣṭa

Memory + attachment    → kliṣṭa
Memory + recollection of teaching → akliṣṭa

Vikalpa + irrational fear → kliṣṭa
Conceptual meditation used skilfully → akliṣṭa
```

Thus, Yoga does not classify one whole vṛtti category as invariably good or bad.

## 7. Why restrain all five?

Even an accurate modification is still:

- changing;
- object-directed;
- part of Prakṛti;
- distinct from Puruṣa.

```text
Vṛtti arises
      ↓
Puruṣa’s consciousness is reflected
      ↓
Ahaṃkāra appropriates the experience
      ↓
“I am the knower/doer/sufferer”
      ↓
Bondage continues
```

Restraint removes misidentification:

```text
Vṛttis restrained
        ↓
Citta no longer obscures the distinction
        ↓
Seer abides in its own nature
```

Nirodha is therefore **revelatory**, not destructive. It reveals Puruṣa by ending concealment.

## 8. Method of restraint

Patañjali identifies two foundational means:

```text
ABHYĀSA                         VAIRĀGYA
Sustained practice              Dispassion
       │                             │
Stabilises attention          Removes attachment
       └──────────────┬──────────────┘
                      ↓
             Citta-vṛtti-nirodha
```

### Abhyāsa

Practice must be:

- prolonged;
- uninterrupted;
- undertaken with commitment;
- directed toward stability.

### Vairāgya

Dispassion is mastery over thirst for:

- experienced sensory objects;
- promised heavenly objects;
- later, even subtle insight and the qualities of Prakṛti.

Practice without dispassion can strengthen ambition. Dispassion without practice may remain merely intellectual.

## 9. Clear-pool analogy

```text
Disturbed pool:
ripples distort reflection

Still pool:
reflection becomes clear
```

Similarly:

```text
Changing citta → Puruṣa appears entangled
Restrained citta → Puruṣa is recognised as distinct
```

The analogy does not mean Puruṣa itself becomes clearer. The obstruction in citta is removed.

## 🖼️ The afflictive chain

```text
                         AVIDYĀ
                Fundamental misapprehension
                           ↓
                         ASMITĀ
                  Identification as “I”
                    ┌──────┴──────┐
                    ↓             ↓
                  RĀGA          DVEṢA
               Attachment       Aversion
                    └──────┬──────┘
                           ↓
                     ABHINIVEŚA
                Clinging to embodied life
                           ↓
                Afflicted action—karma
                           ↓
              Karmic deposit—karmāśaya
                           ↓
                    Birth and experience
```

**Kleśas** are deep afflictions that colour citta, motivate karmically binding action and perpetuate rebirth.

They are not merely temporary emotions. They are causal tendencies underlying ordinary cognition and conduct.

## 1. Avidyā: root ignorance

Avidyā is the field from which the remaining afflictions grow.

Patañjali defines it through four inversions:

| Reality | Avidyā mistakes it for |
|---|---|
| Non-eternal | Eternal |
| Impure | Pure |
| Painful | Pleasurable |
| Non-self | Self |

### Central philosophical error

```text
Citta changes, acts and experiences
              ↓
Puruṣa illuminates these processes
              ↓
The two appear identified
              ↓
Citta is mistaken for the self
```

Avidyā is therefore not simply absence of information. It is a fundamental existential misorientation.

A person may possess extensive intellectual knowledge while still confusing:

- body with self;
- citta with consciousness;
- temporary pleasure with lasting fulfilment;
- changeable conditions with permanent identity.

## 2. Asmitā: egoity

Asmitā is the identification of the **seer** with the **instrument of seeing**.

```text
Puruṣa              Buddhi/citta
pure witness         cognitive instrument
      └──────── mistaken identity ────────┘
                         ↓
                     “I am this”
```

Examples:

```text
Citta thinks     → “I am the thinker”
Citta suffers    → “I am suffering”
Buddhi decides   → “I am the independent agent”
```

### Asmitā versus ahaṃkāra

| Ahaṃkāra | Asmitā |
|---|---|
| Ontological principle evolved from buddhi | Afflictive identification |
| A component of the internal organ | Confusion of Puruṣa with that organ |
| Part of Sāṃkhya cosmology | A kleśa in Yoga psychology |

They are closely related but not strictly interchangeable.

## 3. Rāga: attachment

Rāga arises from remembered pleasure.

```text
Pleasant experience
       ↓
Memory of pleasure
       ↓
Desire to repeat or possess it
       ↓
Attachment
```

Rāga does not mean merely enjoying something. It means dependence upon its continuation:

> “Without this object, status or experience, I cannot be fulfilled.”

Attachment generates further action and karmic impressions.

## 4. Dveṣa: aversion

Dveṣa arises from remembered pain.

```text
Painful experience
       ↓
Memory of suffering
       ↓
Avoidance, hostility or repulsion
       ↓
Aversion
```

Rāga and dveṣa organise reactive life:

```text
Pleasant → pursue and possess
Painful  → avoid and resist
```

Both prevent equanimity because citta remains controlled by past experience.

## 5. Abhiniveśa: clinging to life

Abhiniveśa is deep attachment to continued embodied existence, commonly manifested as fear of death.

Patañjali says it exists even among the learned.

Why?

```text
Intellectual knowledge:
“All embodied things are impermanent”

Deep instinct:
“I must continue as this embodied identity”
```

Abhiniveśa is not removed merely by reading philosophical arguments. It is rooted in latent impressions carried through the mental continuum.

## 6. How one kleśa produces another

```text
Avidyā:
I mistake citta/body for the self
               ↓
Asmitā:
I appropriate its activity as “mine”
               ↓
Rāga:
I cling to what pleases this identity
               ↓
Dveṣa:
I resist what threatens or pains it
               ↓
Abhiniveśa:
I fear the destruction of this identity
```

The five are analytically distinct but function as an interconnected system.

## 7. Four states of kleśas

Kleśas do not always appear with equal intensity.

| State | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Prasupta** | Dormant | Anger remains latent until provoked |
| **Tanu** | Attenuated | Attachment weakened by discipline |
| **Vicchinna** | Interrupted | Attachment temporarily hidden by fear |
| **Udāra** | Fully active | Anger openly controls conduct |

### Visual distinction

```text
PRASUPTA       TANU          VICCHINNA        UDĀRA
Dormant        Weakened      Interrupted      Active
seed           thin seed     covered seed     germinating seed
```

### Critical distinctions

- Dormant does not mean destroyed.
- Interrupted does not mean attenuated.
- An opposing kleśa may temporarily suppress another.
- The desirable result of preliminary discipline is **tanu**, or attenuation.

## 8. Kleśa–karma cycle

```text
Kleśa
afflictive motive
   ↓
Karma
action
   ↓
Saṃskāra
impression
   ↓
Karmāśaya
accumulated karmic deposit
   ↓
Vipāka
fruition
   ↓
Jāti + āyus + bhoga
birth + lifespan + experience
   ↓
Further reactions and kleśas
```

### Three fruits of karma

| Fruit | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Jāti | Kind or condition of birth |
| Āyus | Lifespan |
| Bhoga | Experiences of pleasure and suffering |

Action binds because it is rooted in affliction. Mere outward activity is not automatically binding in exactly the same way.

## 9. Four types of karma

| Karma | Character |
|---|---|
| Śukla | White or meritorious |
| Kṛṣṇa | Black or harmful |
| Śukla-kṛṣṇa | Mixed |
| Aśukla-akṛṣṇa | Neither white nor black; non-binding Yogic action |

Ordinary good karma can produce pleasant results but still sustains saṃsāra.

Liberation requires ending karmic production, not merely replacing painful rebirth with pleasurable rebirth.

## 10. Kriyā-yoga: preliminary remedy

Kriyā-yoga consists of:

```text
TAPAS
Disciplined effort
       +
SVĀDHYĀYA
Study and self-examination
       +
ĪŚVARA-PRAṆIDHĀNA
Dedication to Īśvara
       ↓
Attenuation of kleśas
+ preparation for samādhi
```

Kriyā-yoga weakens gross afflictive tendencies. It does not by itself necessarily destroy their most subtle seeds.

## 11. Meditation and the burnt-seed analogy

```text
Ordinary seed
retains power to germinate

Burnt seed
retains an apparent form
but cannot produce a plant
```

Likewise:

```text
Dormant kleśa → can become active again
Burnt kleśa-seed → cannot produce binding karma
```

Ethical discipline restrains gross expression; meditation and discriminative knowledge destroy the subtle causal seed.

## 12. From kleśa-removal to kaivalya

```text
Kleśas attenuated and destroyed
              ↓
Binding action ceases
              ↓
New karmic deposits cease
              ↓
Existing karmic momentum is exhausted
              ↓
Citta becomes purified
              ↓
Discriminative knowledge becomes uninterrupted
              ↓
Puruṣa is no longer identified with Prakṛti
              ↓
Kaivalya
```

Puruṣa is not newly purified. The mechanism generating apparent bondage has ceased.

## 13. Comparison across schools

| School | Root problem | Distinctive remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Yoga | Five kleśas rooted in avidyā | Discipline, meditation and discrimination |
| Sāṃkhya | Non-discrimination | Knowledge of Puruṣa–Prakṛti difference |
| Buddhism | Ignorance and craving | Eightfold path and insight |
| Advaita | Avidyā and superimposition | Knowledge of Brahman–Ātman identity |
| Nyāya | False knowledge | True knowledge of categories |

Yoga shares terminology with other schools, but its Puruṣa–citta dualism remains distinctive.

## 14. Current-affairs anchor

✅ Modern Yoga protocols may help manage stress, anxiety and behavioural reactivity.

⚠️ Classical kleśa theory makes a stronger claim:

```text
Modern therapeutic aim:
reduce distress and improve functioning

Classical Yogic aim:
destroy the roots producing karma and rebirth
```

Reduced anxiety does not by itself establish the removal of avidyā or attainment of kaivalya.

## 🖼️ The graded path

```text
ETHICAL LIFE          BODY–BREATH          SENSE CONTROL
Yama + Niyama  →  Āsana + Prāṇāyāma  →  Pratyāhāra
                                                ↓
                                      INTERNAL DISCIPLINE
                              Dhāraṇā → Dhyāna → Samādhi
                                                ↓
                                             Saṃyama
                                                ↓
                                  Discriminative knowledge
                                                ↓
                                            Kaivalya
```

Aṣṭāṅga-yoga operationalises **citta-vṛtti-nirodha**. Each limb removes a different source of mental disturbance.

## 1. Kriyā-yoga

Before presenting the eight limbs, Patañjali introduces **kriyā-yoga**:

```text
TAPAS + SVĀDHYĀYA + ĪŚVARA-PRAṆIDHĀNA
```

| Component | Meaning | Psychological role |
|---|---|---|
| Tapas | Disciplined effort or austerity | Weakens comfort-driven habits |
| Svādhyāya | Self-study and sacred study/recitation | Corrects ignorance and directs reflection |
| Īśvara-praṇidhāna | Dedication or surrender to Īśvara | Weakens egoistic appropriation |

Its two explicit purposes are:

```text
Attenuation of kleśas
          +
Preparation for samādhi
```

### Kriyā-yoga versus aṣṭāṅga-yoga

Kriyā-yoga is not a competing path. Its three practices reappear within **niyama** and prepare the practitioner for the complete eight-limbed discipline.

## 2. Yama: ethical restraints

The five yamas regulate conduct toward other beings and worldly objects.

| Yama | Meaning | Affliction weakened |
|---|---|---|
| Ahiṃsā | Non-violence | Hostility and aversion |
| Satya | Truthfulness | Deception and ego-defence |
| Asteya | Non-stealing | Acquisition driven by craving |
| Brahmacarya | Disciplined sexuality/energy | Sensory attachment |
| Aparigraha | Non-possessiveness | Accumulation and insecurity |

### Why ethics comes first

```text
Violence, deception and greed
             ↓
Fear, guilt, hostility and planning
             ↓
Repeated mental agitation
             ↓
Meditative instability
```

Ethics is therefore a psychological condition for concentration, not an optional moral decoration.

### Universal great vow

When yamas are unrestricted by:

- social class;
- place;
- time;
- circumstance;

they become the **universal great vow—sārvabhauma-mahāvrata**.

A convenient action cannot suspend the Yogic ethical commitment.

## 3. Niyama: positive observances

| Niyama | Meaning | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Śauca | Purity | Reduces bodily and mental disorder |
| Santoṣa | Contentment | Weakens restless acquisition |
| Tapas | Discipline | Strengthens mastery over habit |
| Svādhyāya | Study and self-examination | Directs citta toward insight |
| Īśvara-praṇidhāna | Dedication to Īśvara | Reduces ego-centred agency |

### Yama versus niyama

```text
Yama:
“What must I restrain?”

Niyama:
“What qualities must I cultivate?”
```

Yama regulates harmful conduct; niyama positively reshapes the practitioner.

## 4. Āsana: steady posture

Patañjali characterises āsana as:

```text
Steady + comfortable
```

Its philosophical purpose is not gymnastic achievement.

```text
Physical discomfort
       ↓
Attention repeatedly returns to the body
       ↓
Meditative continuity breaks
```

Āsana creates embodied stability so the body no longer dominates attention.

> 🔑 Classical āsana is a means to mental discipline, not the final definition of Yoga.

## 5. Prāṇāyāma: regulation of breath

Prāṇāyāma regulates the movement of inhalation, exhalation and retention.

```text
Breath becomes irregular
          ↕
Citta becomes agitated

Breath becomes regulated
          ↕
Citta becomes steadier
```

Breath functions as a bridge between voluntary discipline and involuntary psycho-physical processes.

Prāṇāyāma:

- reduces agitation;
- prepares concentration;
- refines bodily energy;
- helps weaken sensory dependence.

It is not identical to meditation.

## 6. Pratyāhāra: withdrawal of senses

Pratyāhāra means withdrawing the senses from compulsive pursuit of their objects.

```text
Ordinary condition:
Object → sense captured → citta follows

Pratyāhāra:
Object present → sense no longer compels citta
```

It does not mean damaging or permanently suppressing the senses. It means freedom from automatic extroversion.

Pratyāhāra is the bridge between external and internal Yoga.

## 7. Dhāraṇā: concentration

Dhāraṇā is fixing citta upon one chosen locus.

Examples include:

- a bodily centre;
- an image;
- a sound;
- breath;
- Īśvara or praṇava;
- a subtle object.

```text
Distraction occurs
       ↓
Attention is deliberately returned
       ↓
One locus is repeatedly maintained
```

Dhāraṇā remains relatively effortful.

## 8. Dhyāna: meditation

Dhyāna is an uninterrupted flow of cognition toward the same object.

### Oil-stream analogy

```text
Oil poured continuously
without separate drops
```

Likewise, the meditative stream becomes continuous rather than repeatedly broken.

| Dhāraṇā | Dhyāna |
|---|---|
| Attention is repeatedly fixed | Attention flows continuously |
| Distraction and return remain visible | Breaks become minimal |
| Effortful concentration | Sustained meditative continuity |

## 9. Samādhi: absorption

In samādhi, the object alone shines forth with minimal interference from the ordinary subjective structure.

```text
Dhāraṇā:
“I am holding attention on this object.”

Dhyāna:
“Attention flows continuously toward it.”

Samādhi:
The object predominates; self-referential interference recedes.
```

Samādhi does not automatically mean final kaivalya. It has different stages, seeds and objects, which form Subtopic 6.

## 10. External and internal limbs

| Group | Limbs |
|---|---|
| Bahiraṅga—external | Yama, niyama, āsana, prāṇāyāma and pratyāhāra |
| Antaraṅga—internal | Dhāraṇā, dhyāna and samādhi |

“External” does not mean dispensable. These limbs are external only relative to the subtler meditative operations.

Without ethical and bodily preparation, internal concentration remains unstable or can reinforce egoism.

## 11. Saṃyama

Saṃyama is the integrated application of:

```text
Dhāraṇā + Dhyāna + Samādhi
```

```text
Fixing attention
       ↓
Uninterrupted flow
       ↓
Absorption
       ↓
Saṃyama on the chosen object
```

Saṃyama can produce extraordinary knowledge and capacities. However, attachment to these **siddhis** can obstruct liberation.

## 12. How eight limbs treat the kleśas

| Path stage | Primary therapeutic role |
|---|---|
| Yama | Checks harmful conduct generated by kleśas |
| Niyama | Cultivates disciplined counter-tendencies |
| Āsana | Removes bodily disturbance |
| Prāṇāyāma | Calms psycho-physical agitation |
| Pratyāhāra | Weakens sensory attachment |
| Dhāraṇā | Reverses mental dispersion |
| Dhyāna | Attenuates subtle afflictive patterns |
| Samādhi | Enables transformative discriminative insight |

## 13. Is the path strictly linear?

The limbs form a graded order, but they also reinforce one another.

```text
Ethics improves meditation
Meditation reveals subtle ethical defects
Breath steadies concentration
Concentration improves self-observation
```

A practitioner does not abandon ahiṃsā after beginning meditation.

## 14. Why posture alone is incomplete

```text
Postural proficiency
without yama and niyama
             ↓
Body may become controlled
while ego, attachment and aversion continue
             ↓
Kleśa–karma chain remains active
```

Therefore, physical practice may yield health benefits without necessarily producing classical Yogic liberation.

## 15. Current-affairs anchor

✅ Contemporary public programmes use accessible combinations of posture, breathing, relaxation and meditation for preventive healthcare.

⚠️ Classical aṣṭāṅga-yoga is broader:

```text
Public-health protocol
→ improved physical and mental functioning

Pātañjala aṣṭāṅga-yoga
→ ethical purification + restraint of citta
  + discriminative liberation
```

Therapeutic benefits can support practice, but they are not equivalent to kaivalya.

## UPSC traps

- Kriyā-yoga has three components, not eight.
- Tapas, svādhyāya and Īśvara-praṇidhāna appear in both kriyā-yoga and niyama.
- Yama is universal ethical restraint.
- Āsana is steady and comfortable posture, not gymnastic display.
- Prāṇāyāma is not identical to dhyāna.
- Pratyāhāra withdraws sensory compulsion, not sensory capacity.
- Dhāraṇā, dhyāna and samādhi are distinct stages.
- Saṃyama combines the final three limbs.
- Siddhis are not identical to liberation.
- External limbs remain necessary after internal practice begins.

## Revision notes

1. Kriyā-yoga attenuates kleśas and prepares samādhi.
2. It consists of tapas, svādhyāya and Īśvara-praṇidhāna.
3. Yama governs restraint; niyama governs positive cultivation.
4. The five yamas form a universal great vow.
5. Āsana establishes steady, comfortable embodiment.
6. Prāṇāyāma regulates breath and psycho-physical agitation.
7. Pratyāhāra frees citta from compulsory sensory pursuit.
8. Dhāraṇā fixes attention upon one locus.
9. Dhyāna is uninterrupted cognition toward that locus.
10. Samādhi minimises self-referential interference.
11. The first five limbs are external; the final three are internal.
12. Saṃyama combines dhāraṇā, dhyāna and samādhi.
13. Ethics is constitutive of Yoga, not optional preparation.
14. Aṣṭāṅga-yoga transforms conduct, embodiment, attention and cognition.

## 🖼️ Complete progression

```text
SAMPRAJÑĀTA / SABĪJA
Object-supported cognition remains
              ↓
Ṛtambharā prajñā
Truth-bearing insight
              ↓
ASAMPRAJÑĀTA
Active cognition ceases,
but residual impressions remain
              ↓
NIRBĪJA
Even residual seed is restrained
              ↓
Dharmamegha-samādhi
Complete dispassion toward attainments
              ↓
Kaivalya
```

## 1. What is samādhi?

Samādhi is absorption in which citta becomes radically unified and transparent.

It is not:

- sleep;
- unconsciousness;
- emotional excitement;
- mere relaxation;
- necessarily final liberation.

```text
Dhāraṇā → attention fixed
Dhyāna  → attention flows continuously
Samādhi → object predominates with minimal self-interference
```

## 2. Two principal forms

| Samprajñāta | Asamprajñāta |
|---|---|
| Cognitive absorption | Beyond active cognitive objectification |
| An object or support remains | No ordinary object-support remains |
| A refined vṛtti remains | Active vṛttis cease |
| Object-supported or sabīja | Residual saṃskāras may remain |
| Produces higher insight | Deepens restraint beyond cognition |

## 3. Samprajñāta samādhi

Samprajñāta is object-oriented absorption accompanied by refined awareness of a support.

Patañjali characterises it through four factors:

```text
Gross                          Subtle
  │                               │
Vitarka → Vicāra → Ānanda → Asmitā
```

| Factor | Character |
|---|---|
| Vitarka | Engagement with a gross object |
| Vicāra | Reflection upon a subtle object |
| Ānanda | Refined sattvic joy |
| Asmitā | Extremely subtle bare I-am-ness |

### Asmitā trap

The asmitā accompanying higher samprajñāta is a subtle objectification of I-am-ness. It should not be simplistically equated with gross egoism, though it has not yet been completely transcended.

## 4. A separate samāpatti classification

Patañjali also analyses object-coalescence through:

| Samāpatti | Object | Conceptual overlay |
|---|---|---|
| Savitarkā | Gross | Word, object and idea remain mixed |
| Nirvitarkā | Gross | Gross verbal-conceptual overlay is purified |
| Savicārā | Subtle | Subtle reflective consideration remains |
| Nirvicārā | Subtle | Subtle reflective overlay is stilled |

### Visual distinction

```text
GROSS OBJECT
Savitarkā ──conceptual purification──→ Nirvitarkā

SUBTLE OBJECT
Savicārā ──reflective purification───→ Nirvicārā
```

## 5. The major UPSC trap

These are two related but distinct schemes:

```text
YS 1.17:
Vitarka, vicāra, ānanda, asmitā
= factors characterising samprajñāta

YS 1.42–1.44:
Savitarkā, nirvitarkā,
savicārā, nirvicārā
= varieties of samāpatti
```

Do not write that Patañjali’s four factors of samprajñāta are savitarkā, nirvitarkā, savicārā and nirvicārā.

The schemes overlap in meditative development but classify it by different criteria.

## 6. Why samprajñāta is sabīja

Sabīja means “with seed.”

In object-supported absorption:

```text
Object or meditative support remains
                 ↓
A refined cognitive modification remains
                 ↓
Possibility of further mental formation remains
```

Every samāpatti through nirvicārā remains sabīja.

But the word **seed** is also used for residual impressions. These meanings must be distinguished:

| Context | Meaning of seed |
|---|---|
| Sabīja object-absorption | An object-support remains |
| Nirbīja culmination | Residual saṃskāra itself has ceased |

## 7. Ṛtambharā prajñā

Perfection of nirvicārā produces **ṛtambharā prajñā**, or truth-bearing insight.

It differs from ordinary inference and testimony because it discloses its particular object with exceptional clarity.

```text
Ordinary impressions
          ↓ opposed by
Truth-bearing impression
          ↓
Lower impressions become ineffective
```

It is not a fourth pramāṇa. It is best understood as extraordinary Yogic perception.

### Ladder principle

Ṛtambharā is liberating but remains a purified citta-vṛtti:

```text
Use insight to overcome lower impressions
                   ↓
Eventually restrain the insight’s own impression
```

The ladder is useful but cannot be carried after reaching the roof.

## 8. Asamprajñāta samādhi

Asamprajñāta involves cessation of active cognitive modifications.

However, Patañjali describes it as:

```text
Active vṛttis cease
        +
Saṃskāra-śeṣa remains
Residual impressions continue
```

Therefore:

❌ Asamprajñāta does not necessarily mean that every seed has already been destroyed.

❌ It is not synonymous with nirbīja.

## 9. Nirbīja samādhi

Nirbīja is attained when even the residual impression sustaining restraint is itself restrained.

```text
Asamprajñāta:
vṛttis cease, residual saṃskāra remains

Nirbīja:
even residual saṃskāra ceases
```

> 🔑 **Safe formula:** Asamprajñāta has saṃskāra-śeṣa; nirbīja restrains even that residue.

This is one of the most important close distinctions in the topic.

## 10. Why samādhi is not sleep

| Sleep | Asamprajñāta samādhi |
|---|---|
| Nidrā-vṛtti operates | Active vṛttis are methodically restrained |
| Tamas dominates | Sattvic discipline has been refined |
| Afflictions remain intact | Afflictive seeds are progressively neutralised |
| Involuntary ordinary condition | Culmination of sustained practice |
| No discriminative achievement | Supports liberating discrimination |

Both may lack ordinary object-awareness, but their causes, mental quality and consequences differ.

## 11. Dharmamegha-samādhi

Dharmamegha means the “cloud of dharma.”

It occurs when the yogin relinquishes attachment even to:

- extraordinary knowledge;
- meditative attainments;
- discriminative insight as a possession;
- supernatural powers.

```text
Complete discrimination
          +
No desire to possess its fruits
          ↓
Dharmamegha-samādhi
          ↓
Kleśa and karma cease
          ↓
Kaivalya
```

## 12. Samādhi and siddhis

Saṃyama may generate extraordinary capacities.

But siddhis can reactivate:

- asmitā;
- pride;
- rāga;
- desire for recognition;
- attachment to power.

```text
Siddhi treated as instrument → possible aid
Siddhi appropriated as “mine” → obstacle
```

Patañjali therefore treats powers as attainments in worldly functioning but potential obstacles to samādhi.

## 13. Role of Īśvara

Īśvara-praṇidhāna assists samādhi by:

- reducing egoistic agency;
- stabilising attention;
- providing an unsurpassed meditative support;
- helping overcome obstacles;
- linking concentration with praṇava—OṂ.

However:

```text
Īśvara assists samādhi
          ≠
Īśvara automatically grants kaivalya
```

Discriminative knowledge remains necessary.

## 14. PYQ-ready answer structure

For “Differentiate samprajñāta and asamprajñāta samādhi”:

1. Define samādhi.
2. Explain object-supported samprajñāta.
3. Give its four factors.
4. Mention the separate samāpatti classification.
5. Explain saṃskāra-śeṣa in asamprajñāta.
6. Distinguish asamprajñāta from nirbīja.
7. Conclude with their progressive role toward kaivalya.

- Nirvicārā remains sabīja.

## 🖼️ Īśvara’s position

```text
                   PURUṢAS
                      │
       ┌──────────────┴──────────────┐
       ↓                             ↓
Ordinary individual Puruṣas       ĪŚVARA
Pure in essence but              Special Puruṣa
apparently bound through         eternally untouched by
identification                   affliction and karma
       │                             │
Practice and discrimination     Meditative support,
needed for kaivalya             teacher and ideal
```

Patañjali defines Īśvara as **Puruṣa-viśeṣa**, a special Puruṣa.

## 1. Why “special” Puruṣa?

Īśvara is untouched by four things:

```text
KLEŚA  → afflictions
KARMA   → action
VIPĀKA  → results of action
ĀŚAYA   → accumulated karmic deposits
```

Ordinary Puruṣas are also intrinsically pure. However, their association with citta creates apparent bondage.

Īśvara has never undergone even this apparent bondage.

| Ordinary Puruṣa | Īśvara |
|---|---|
| Appears bound through citta | Eternally free |
| Associated with karmic embodiment | Untouched by karmic deposits |
| Requires discrimination for release | Never lacks freedom |
| Possesses limited empirical knowledge through citta | Bears unsurpassed seed of omniscience |

## 2. Īśvara is not Brahman

Yoga retains:

- many Puruṣas;
- independent Prakṛti;
- real plurality;
- liberation as isolation.

It does not teach:

```text
Ātman = Brahman
or
all Puruṣas merge into one God
```

Thus, Īśvara is not Advaita’s non-dual Brahman.

## 3. Is Īśvara the creator?

Patañjali does not primarily define Īśvara as the world’s creator.

```text
Nyāya:
God is the efficient cause coordinating atoms

Yoga:
Prakṛti evolves through its own qualities;
Īśvara is primarily a special Puruṣa
and meditative–pedagogical principle
```

Later commentators may attribute wider cosmic functions to Īśvara. An exam answer should distinguish such developments from Patañjali’s compact account.

### Safe formulation

> Yoga affirms a real Īśvara, but Patañjali primarily presents him as an eternally free special Puruṣa rather than systematically proving him as the creator of the universe.

## 4. Unsurpassed omniscience

Patañjali states that in Īśvara lies the unsurpassed seed of omniscience.

```text
Ordinary knowledge
limited by citta, karma and time
                 ↓
Īśvara’s knowledge
unsurpassed and unconditioned
```

This distinguishes Īśvara from liberated individual Puruṣas in his practical and pedagogical role.

## 5. Teacher beyond time

Īśvara is the teacher even of the ancient teachers because he is not limited by time.

```text
Ordinary teacher
learns within a temporal tradition

Īśvara
is not preceded by ignorance or instruction
```

He functions as the timeless source and ideal of spiritual guidance.

## 6. Praṇava—OṂ

Patañjali identifies **praṇava**, OṂ, as Īśvara’s designation.

The discipline involves:

```text
Repetition of OṂ
        +
Contemplation of its meaning
        ↓
Citta becomes one-pointed
        ↓
Obstacles weaken
        ↓
Inward consciousness develops
```

Mechanical repetition alone is insufficient. Recitation must be joined with contemplation.

## 7. Īśvara-praṇidhāna

Īśvara-praṇidhāna means dedication, surrender or orientation toward Īśvara.

It appears as:

- a component of kriyā-yoga;
- one of the five niyamas;
- an independent means conducive to samādhi.

### Psychological mechanism

```text
“I am the independent achiever”
              ↓
Dedication of action and result
              ↓
Egoistic appropriation weakens
              ↓
Anxiety and attachment reduce
              ↓
Citta becomes steadier
```

Surrender does not mean inactivity. It means performing discipline without possessive identification.

## 8. How Īśvara assists samādhi

| Role | Effect |
|---|---|
| Meditative object | Gives citta a stable support |
| Perfect model | Represents freedom from kleśa and karma |
| Teacher | Grounds spiritual guidance |
| Obstacle-remover | Assists concentration and perseverance |
| Focus of surrender | Weakens asmitā and attachment |
| Praṇava’s referent | Unites sound, meaning and meditation |

## 9. Nine obstacles

Yoga identifies obstacles including:

- disease;
- mental stagnation;
- doubt;
- carelessness;
- laziness;
- sensory non-restraint;
- false perception;
- failure to attain a stage;
- instability after attainment.

Their symptoms include:

```text
Suffering + dejection + bodily disturbance
+ irregular inhalation and exhalation
```

Īśvara-praṇidhāna is one important means of overcoming them, though Yoga also prescribes breath regulation, one-principle practice and cultivation of friendliness, compassion, joy and equanimity.

## 10. Does Īśvara grant kaivalya?

Not directly or arbitrarily.

```text
Īśvara-praṇidhāna
        ↓
Citta stabilises
Obstacles weaken
Egoity diminishes
        ↓
Samādhi deepens
        ↓
Viveka-khyāti develops
        ↓
Kaivalya
```

The indispensable liberating condition remains continuous discrimination between Puruṣa and Prakṛti.

> 🔑 **Īśvara is instrumental, not constitutive, in kaivalya.**

- **Instrumental:** he assists the path.
- **Not constitutive:** liberation is not merger with him or an arbitrary divine gift.

## 11. Why is Yoga called Seśvara-Sāṃkhya?

```text
Sāṃkhya ontology
Puruṣa + Prakṛti + 25 principles
              +
Real Īśvara as special Puruṣa
              +
Devotional aid to meditative discipline
              ↓
Seśvara-Sāṃkhya
```

The addition is practically significant but does not replace Sāṃkhya’s basic metaphysics.

## 12. Is Īśvara philosophically redundant?

### Objection

If discrimination alone produces kaivalya, Īśvara appears unnecessary.

### Yogic reply

Not every aspirant can immediately attain pure discrimination through unaided analysis.

Īśvara supplies:

- an ideal of perfect freedom;
- an emotionally powerful focus;
- reduction of egoity;
- concentration;
- confidence and continuity;
- assistance in overcoming obstacles.

### Balanced assessment

Īśvara is not logically indispensable to explain Prakṛti’s evolution, but he is soteriologically important within Patañjali’s practical discipline.

## 13. Comparison

| School | Nature of God |
|---|---|
| Sāṃkhya | Classical system generally does not require Īśvara |
| Yoga | Special Puruṣa and meditative support |
| Nyāya | Omniscient efficient cause coordinating world order |
| Advaita | Īśvara is Brahman associated with māyā at the empirical level |
| Viśiṣṭādvaita | Supreme personal Brahman; souls and world are dependent modes |

```text
          ↓
Pratiprasava of the guṇas
          ↓
KAIVALYA
Puruṣa abides in its own nature
```

## 1. Meaning of kaivalya

**Kaivalya** means:

- aloneness;
- isolation;
- absolute distinctness;
- independence of Puruṣa from Prakṛti.

```text
Puruṣa:
conscious, inactive and unchanging

Prakṛti:
insentient, active and changing

Kaivalya:
complete termination of their false identification
```

It does not mean loneliness in an emotional sense. It means metaphysical and experiential independence.

## 2. If Puruṣa is already free, what is liberated?

Puruṣa never literally becomes bound.

```text
Citta changes
      ↓
Puruṣa illuminates those changes
      ↓
Avidyā identifies witness with instrument
      ↓
Puruṣa appears to think, act and suffer
```

Liberation therefore does not manufacture freedom.

```text
Bondage = false identification
Kaivalya = cessation of that identification
```

> 🔑 Yoga removes an obstruction; it does not create Puruṣa’s purity.

## 3. Viveka-khyāti

Viveka-khyāti is uninterrupted discriminative discernment between Puruṣa and Prakṛti.

It distinguishes:

| Puruṣa | Citta/Prakṛti |
|---|---|
| Conscious | Insentient |
| Unchanging | Continuously changing |
| Witness | Instrument |
| Non-agent | Active |
| Non-object | Knowable object |
| Many individual witnesses | One evolving material principle |

### Knowledge versus intellectual assertion

Simply repeating “Puruṣa is different from Prakṛti” is insufficient.

```text
Conceptual knowledge
        ↓
Meditatively confirmed discrimination
        ↓
Continuous viveka-khyāti
```

Yoga is cognitivist at its summit, but its liberating cognition is matured through disciplined transformation of citta.

## 4. Why samādhi alone is insufficient

A practitioner may attain absorption yet retain:

- subtle attachment;
- pride;
- fascination with bliss;
- identification with sattvic citta;
- desire for extraordinary powers.

Therefore:

```text
Samādhi without discrimination
may remain a refined condition of Prakṛti

Samādhi producing continuous discrimination
moves toward kaivalya
```

The decisive issue is not intensity of experience but elimination of misidentification.

## 5. Para-vairāgya

Para-vairāgya is supreme dispassion.

At this stage the practitioner relinquishes attachment even to:

- sattvic joy;
- extraordinary knowledge;
- samādhi experiences;
- siddhis;
- the status of being a spiritual achiever;
- the qualities of Prakṛti themselves.

```text
Ordinary vairāgya:
no craving for sensory or heavenly objects

Para-vairāgya:
no craving even for subtle Yogic attainments
```

Without para-vairāgya, asmitā can survive in refined form:

> “I am an advanced yogin possessing knowledge and power.”

## 6. Dharmamegha-samādhi

Dharmamegha means the “cloud of dharma.”

The image suggests a cloud pouring down the fullness of liberating discernment.

It arises when:

```text
Viveka-khyāti is complete
            +
No attachment remains even toward omniscience
            ↓
Dharmamegha-samādhi
```

Its result is the final cessation of:

- kleśas;
- karmic production;
- obstacles to isolation.

Dharmamegha is not arbitrary divine grace. It is the matured culmination of discipline, discrimination and complete detachment.

## 7. Pratiprasava

Pratiprasava means counter-evolution, return or reabsorption into the cause.

### Evolution

```text
Prakṛti → mahat → ahaṃkāra → mind, senses and elements
```

### Liberating reversal

```text
Binding functions of citta and the guṇas
                 ↓
Their purpose for this Puruṣa is completed
                 ↓
They return toward causal quiescence
```

The guṇas had two purposes:

1. **bhoga** — experience for Puruṣa;
2. **apavarga/kaivalya** — enabling eventual liberation.

When both purposes are completed, they cease functioning **for that liberated Puruṣa**.

### Important qualification

Prakṛti is not universally destroyed.

```text
For liberated Puruṣa:
the guṇas’ binding function ends

For other Puruṣas:
Prakṛti and saṃsāra continue
```

This follows from Yoga’s acceptance of many Puruṣas.

## 8. Three analogies

### Farmer analogy

```text
Water naturally tends to flow
           ↓
Embankment blocks it
           ↓
Farmer removes the obstruction
           ↓
Water flows without being manufactured
```

Likewise, discipline removes obstructions but does not manufacture Puruṣa’s freedom.

### Burnt-seed analogy

```text
Ordinary seed → can germinate
Burnt seed    → retains form but cannot germinate
```

Destroyed kleśas lose the capacity to produce future karma.

### Potter’s-wheel analogy

```text
Potter stops applying force
            ↓
Wheel continues briefly through momentum
            ↓
Eventually stops
```

After liberating knowledge, the body–citta complex may continue through already-fructifying karmic momentum without creating renewed bondage.

## 9. Liberation while embodied

Yoga can cautiously accommodate liberation while bodily processes continue:

```text
Discrimination complete
          ↓
No fresh binding karma
          ↓
Existing embodiment continues temporarily
          ↓
Residual momentum is exhausted
```

The term **jīvanmukti** is more strongly associated with Vedānta, so use it cautiously in a Yoga answer. Explain the mechanism rather than relying only on the label.

## 10. Svarūpa-pratiṣṭhā

Kaivalya is also described as consciousness established in its own nature.

This completes the opening logic:

```text
When vṛttis operate:
seer appears identified with them

When restraint and discrimination mature:
seer abides in its own nature
```

Puruṣa does not acquire a new property. Its eternal nature is no longer misrepresented through citta.

## 11. Kaivalya versus Advaita mokṣa

| Yoga | Advaita |
|---|---|
| Many Puruṣas | One ultimate Brahman |
| Prakṛti is real | World ultimately dependent upon māyā |
| Liberation is isolation | Liberation is non-dual realisation |
| Puruṣa remains distinct | Ātman is realised as Brahman |
| No universal merger | Apparent plurality is sublated |

> Yoga seeks **disentanglement**; Advaita teaches **non-dual identity**.

## 12. Kaivalya versus Nyāya apavarga

| Yoga kaivalya | Nyāya apavarga |
|---|---|
| Isolation of Puruṣa from Prakṛti | Release through true categorical knowledge |
| Consciousness abides in its nature | Often characterised through cessation of pain |
| Meditative discrimination is central | Logical and metaphysical knowledge is central |
| Citta’s binding purpose ends | Defects, activity and rebirth cease |

A useful contrast is that Yoga gives a more explicitly meditative account of the mind’s transformation.

## 13. Īśvara’s role

Īśvara:

- provides a meditative support;
- reduces egoistic appropriation;
- helps overcome obstacles;
- facilitates samādhi.

But:

```text
Īśvara-praṇidhāna assists the path
                ↓
Viveka-khyāti directly removes confusion
                ↓
Kaivalya follows
```

Kaivalya is not a gift granted through divine preference and does not mean union with Īśvara.

## 14. Is kaivalya merely negative?

### Criticism

Isolation appears to be merely the absence of experience, relation and positive fulfilment.

### Yogic reply

Kaivalya is not annihilation. It is:

- freedom from misidentification;
- independence from karmic conditioning;
- cessation of suffering;
- Puruṣa’s establishment in its intrinsic consciousness.

### Remaining concern

Because Puruṣa is inactive and qualityless, critics may still ask what positively meaningful content liberated existence possesses.

A balanced UPSC answer should acknowledge this concern instead of describing kaivalya as ordinary happiness or Vedāntic bliss.

⚠️ Public programmes understandably emphasise measurable health outcomes.

```text
better bodily and mental functioning

```

Health is compatible with Yoga but does not exhaust its final philosophical goal.

```text
        ↓
```

## 1. Yoga’s philosophical strengths

### Integrated psychology

Yoga systematically connects:

```text
Vṛtti → memory → impression → habit
→ affliction → action → karmic result
```

It explains not merely what the mind contains, but how mental patterns become dispositions and bondage.

### Operational method

Unlike a purely speculative metaphysics, Yoga provides:

- ethical restraints;
- positive observances;
- bodily stability;
- breath regulation;
- sensory discipline;
- concentration;
- meditation;
- absorption.

### Ethics and meditation

Yoga recognises that meditative ability without moral purification can magnify pride, attachment and power-seeking.

### Graded epistemology

It distinguishes:

- ordinary cognition;
- concentrated cognition;
- object-supported absorption;
- truth-bearing insight;
- residual impressions;
- seedless restraint.

## 2. Puruṣa–Prakṛti interaction problem

### Objection

```text
Puruṣa:
conscious but completely inactive

Citta:
active but completely insentient
```

How can two radically different principles produce one unified experience?

### Yoga’s reply

Citta reflects Puruṣa through proximity:

```text
Clear crystal near a red flower
appears red without becoming the flower
```

All transformation occurs in citta; Puruṣa remains unchanged.

### Residual difficulty

A reflection normally requires some intelligible relation. “Mere proximity” may describe the relation without fully explaining how it is possible.

> **Evaluation:** Yoga is phenomenologically powerful but metaphysically vulnerable at the interaction point.

## 3. Is citta capable of cognition?

### Objection

If citta is insentient, how can it know anything?

### Reply

Citta:

- takes the form of an object;
- reflects Puruṣa’s consciousness;
- thereby appears luminous.

Cognition requires three distinguishable terms:

```text
External object
      ↓
Citta-vṛtti corresponding to it
      ↓
Puruṣa witnessing the modification
```

The unresolved question is whether reflected consciousness adequately explains genuine awareness.

## 4. Why restrain valid cognition?

### Objection

If pramāṇa is true, restraining it appears irrational.

### Reply

Valid cognition performs a therapeutic function:

```text
Error
 ↓ replaced by
Valid cognition
 ↓ refined into
Discriminative knowledge
 ↓ finally transcended
```

Pramāṇa is an **akliṣṭa ladder**. It removes error but remains a modification of citta and must eventually subside.

## 5. Is samādhi merely sleep?

| Sleep | Samādhi |
|---|---|
| Nidrā-vṛtti operates | Vṛttis are methodically restrained |
| Tamas predominates | Sattvic lucidity is refined |
| Afflictions remain fertile | Afflictive seeds are neutralised |
| No liberating discrimination | Viveka-khyāti develops |

Both may lack ordinary external awareness, but their cause, quality and result differ.

## 6. Is Īśvara redundant?

### Objection

If Sāṃkhya ontology and viveka-khyāti already explain liberation, why add God?

### Yoga’s reply

Īśvara provides:

- an ideal of eternal freedom;
- an object of meditation;
- ego-reducing surrender;
- obstacle removal;
- timeless instruction.

### Assessment

Īśvara is practically useful but has a limited metaphysical role:

```text
Not material cause
Not clearly Patañjali’s creator
Not final giver of liberation
```

This makes Yoga’s God soteriologically important but less explanatory than Nyāya’s creator-God.

## 7. Is kaivalya merely negative?

### Objection

Isolation may appear empty, inactive and devoid of relationship.

### Yoga’s reply

Kaivalya is positively described as:

- freedom from karmic conditioning;
- cessation of suffering;
- pure consciousness established in itself;
- complete absence of misidentification.

### Residual issue

Yoga must avoid importing ordinary pleasure or Advaitic bliss into a Puruṣa defined as inactive and qualityless.

A balanced verdict is:

> Kaivalya is positive as unconditioned self-abidance, though its experiential content remains philosophically austere.
