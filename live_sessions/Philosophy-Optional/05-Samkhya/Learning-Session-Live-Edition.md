# MUST-NEEDED / CORE LEARNING SESSION

## Lesson 1 — What kind of system is Sāṃkhya?

**Progress:** 1/12 · **Stage:** Foundation · **Coverage:** system identity, source control,
three means of valid knowledge, imperceptibility and general-correlation inference

### Visual first: the explanatory division of labour

```text
QUESTION: what changes, and what is aware of change?

PRIMORDIAL MATERIAL NATURE                     CONSCIOUS WITNESS
(Prakṛti)                                      (Puruṣa)
unconscious · active · one                     conscious · inactive · many
three qualities · material source              self-luminous · quality-transcendent
mind AND matter evolve from it                 world never evolves from it
                     \                        /
                      \---- empirical life ---/
                         proximity + reflection

NOT Cartesian interaction:
mind belongs on the Prakṛti side; Puruṣa is not a thinking substance that pushes a body.
```

### Simple start

Sāṃkhya begins with a distinction ordinary language blurs. Thoughts, decisions, pleasure and pain
all change and can be noticed. The noticing principle must therefore not simply be another noticed
change. Sāṃkhya assigns every changing physical and mental event to primordial material nature
(Prakṛti), while pure awareness belongs to the conscious witness (Puruṣa). Its philosophy is
dualist, realist, evolutionary and classically non-theistic.

### Core doctrine

1. **Textual identity.** [C] Tradition associates the school with Kapila, followed by Āsuri and
   Pañcaśikha. The earliest extant systematic classical statement is Īśvarakṛṣṇa’s
   *Sāṃkhyakārikā*. Later Sūtra and theistic materials must not be back-projected into every
   classical claim.
2. **Enumeration and discrimination.** [A] The name suits both its enumeration of twenty-five
   principles and its liberating discrimination between nature and witness.
3. **Three means of valid knowledge (pramāṇas).**

| Means | English function | Controlled definition / use |
|---|---|---|
| Perception (pratyakṣa / dṛṣṭa) | ascertain a presented object | object-directed ascertainment through functioning sense-capacities |
| Inference (anumāna) | know what is not directly presented | antecedent-to-effect, effect-to-ground, or general-correlation reasoning |
| Reliable testimony (āptavacana / śabda) | know through a trustworthy source | especially relevant where the object is not easily available otherwise |

4. **Reduced candidates.** [C] Comparison is handled through testimony plus inference; postulation
   and inclusion through inference; non-apprehension through failed perceptual conditions rather
   than an independent instrument; traditional report counts only when reliable.
5. **Imperceptibility is not non-existence.** Excessive distance or proximity, organ-defect,
   inattention, subtlety, obstruction, overpowering and admixture can block perception. Root nature
   and witness are beyond the senses (atīndriya), not thereby unreal.
6. **General-correlation inference (sāmānyato-dṛṣṭa anumāna).** It moves from the public structure
   of dependent, ordered and objectifiable experience to an unobserved material ground and a
   non-object witness. The later proof chains discharge this epistemic burden.

### Argument anatomy

```text
Perception reaches only manifest objects
                  |
                  v
root nature and witness are not sense-objects
                  |
       testimony alone is dialectically weak
                  |
                  v
general-correlation inference is required
        /                              \
manifest dependent order               objectifiable experience
        |                              |
        v                              v
unmanifest material ground             conscious witness
```

### Example with limit

A person sees a moving shadow and infers an unseen source of movement. The example clarifies
inference beyond immediate presentation. Its limit is that Sāṃkhya is not inferring another
ordinary hidden object; it is inferring principles of a different explanatory order.

### Objection, reply, residual

**Objection — Cārvāka:** an invariable relation cannot be established for a wholly imperceptible
term; root nature and witness are speculative.

**Reply:** strict perceptualism cannot explain ordinary causal reasoning or the contrast between a
changing cognition and awareness of that cognition. The two inferences form a cumulative
explanation, not an isolated leap.

**Residual:** inference to *some* ground or witness does not by itself prove every Sāṃkhya
predicate. The school must separately establish one root, three qualities, inactivity and plurality.

### Directive, retrieval and application

| Directive | Retrieve | Execute |
|---|---|---|
| Explain Sāṃkhya | two principles + real evolution + discrimination | define the division of labour and causal spine |
| Examine its epistemology | three means + imperceptibility + inference | expose the inferential presupposition |
| Critically discuss | Cārvāka and Nyāya pressure | objection → reply → residual |

**Retrieval cue:** **Three–Two–One:** three means; two inferred principles; one liberating
discrimination.

### Concept-check MCQ 1

Which statement most accurately represents classical Sāṃkhya?

A. Mind and matter evolve from one unconscious nature, while many inactive witnesses make
experience possible.
B. Mind and matter remain independent substances, while each witness actively directs bodily and
mental change.
C. Mind evolves from a conscious witness, while primordial nature supplies only the visible
material elements.
D. Perception alone reveals primordial nature, while inference and reliable testimony apply only
to ordinary objects.

#### Answer and explanation

**Correct answer: A**

- **A — Correct:** it preserves one material source, many witnesses and the inclusion of mind
  within nature.
- **B — Incorrect:** mind belongs to nature, and witness does not direct productive change.
- **C — Incorrect:** witness is not a productive source, and nature includes mental as well as
  visible material functions.
- **D — Incorrect:** Sāṃkhya accepts perception, inference and reliable testimony; root nature is
  inferentially established.

## Lesson 2 — Primordial material nature and the five proofs

**Progress:** 2/12 · **Stage:** Foundation/Core · **Coverage:** definition, synonyms, need,
five proofs, one-root inference and one-nature/many-witness distinction

### Visual first: from manifest diversity to an unmanifest root

```text
FINITE FORMS + SHARED ORDER + DETERMINATE POWER
          + CAUSE/EFFECT LEVELS + COSMIC CONTINUITY
                              |
                              v
       PRIMORDIAL MATERIAL NATURE (Prakṛti)
     root nature (mūla-prakṛti) · chief (pradhāna)
                 unmanifest (avyakta)
                              |
      material source of intellect, ego, mind, senses,
              subtle potentials and gross elements
```

### Simple start

The changing world cannot arise from the changeless witness. Sāṃkhya therefore posits a second
ultimate: an uncreated, unconscious and productive material source. “Material” here is broad. It
includes the roots of psychological functions as well as the physical world.

### The five proofs

| Proof | Exact argumentative kernel | What it does **not** mean |
|---|---|---|
| From finite determinateness (bhedānāṃ parimāṇāt) | delimited diversity points to a subtler material ground | not merely “everything has a cause” |
| From coordination (samanvayāt) | shared constitution and ordered diversity suggest a common matrix | not proof by resemblance alone |
| From operation due to power (śaktitaḥ pravṛtteś ca) | determinate effects require adequate productive capacity | not conscious planning |
| From cause–effect distinction (kāraṇa-kārya-vibhāgāt) | manifest products imply a subtler unmanifest causal level | not total separation of cause and effect |
| From an undivided source of diversity (avibhāgāt vaiśvarūpyasya) | one interconnected, quality-structured cosmos points to an undivided root | not an observed count of invisible roots |

The exact third formulation matters: the argument is from productive power and operation, not from
intelligent intention.

### Why one Prakṛti?

The fifth proof is the direct route. Diverse effects form one coordinated material order and share
the three-quality constitution; several independent ultimate matrices would leave that common
structure and coordination unexplained. Economy therefore favours one root.

**Critical qualification:** “undivided” does not deductively exclude several internally undivided
roots. The conclusion requires the additional premise that the cosmos constitutes one material
continuum. Thus it is a strong explanatory inference, not perceptual head-counting.

### Argument anatomy

```text
many determinate effects
        |
share causal pattern and three-quality structure
        |
manifest/unmanifest stages remain materially continuous
        |
one root explains commonality without coordinating many ultimates
        |
qualified verdict: explanatory unity, not strict deduction
```

### Distinction that saves two doctrines

| Question | Sāṃkhya answer |
|---|---|
| How many ultimate material roots? | one Prakṛti |
| How many conscious centres? | many Puruṣas |
| Why no contradiction? | unity concerns the material continuum; plurality concerns non-transferable experience |

### Objection, reply, residual

**Objection — Nyāya:** atoms plus an intelligent organiser can explain plurality and order without
one imperceptible all-inclusive material root.

**Reply:** atomism still owes an account of the pervasive qualitative and material continuity
between cause and effect; adding an organiser does not supply the material identity Sāṃkhya seeks.

**Residual:** the inference depends on treating the world’s diverse processes as one explanatory
order rather than several ultimately independent systems.

### Directive, retrieval and application

| Demand | Minimum answer kernel | Evaluative addition |
|---|---|---|
| Prove Prakṛti | define it; explain all five reasons | show the inferential form |
| Which proof establishes one root? | fifth proof + shared quality/material order | multiple-root objection |
| Distinguish Prakṛti from Puruṣa | active/unconscious/one versus inactive/conscious/many | avoid mind–body analogy |

**Retrieval cue:** **Limit–Link–Power–Levels–Unity.**

### Concept-check MCQ 2

Which proof is directly used to support the claim that primordial material nature is one?

A. The instrumental order of body, senses and mind exists for a conscious beneficiary beyond them.
B. The diversified cosmos proceeds from one undivided, quality-structured material source.
C. Liberation-seeking activity presupposes a witness distinct from material nature.
D. Experienced pleasure, pain and delusion require a knower beyond the qualities.

#### Answer and explanation

**Correct answer: B**

- **A — Incorrect:** aggregate-instrumentality belongs to the witness-proof family.
- **B — Correct:** the fifth material-nature proof supplies the one-root route, qualified by the
  premise of one shared material order.
- **C — Incorrect:** striving for liberation is a witness-proof.
- **D — Incorrect:** the distinction between knower and experienced qualities is also a
  witness-proof.

## Lesson 3 — Three qualities and the engine of transformation

**Progress:** 3/12 · **Stage:** Core · **Coverage:** three qualities, equilibrium, same-form and
different-form transformation, and the energy role of activity

### Visual first: equilibrium to manifestation

```text
UNMANIFEST EQUILIBRIUM (sāmyāvasthā)

illumination (sattva) = activity (rajas) = obstruction (tamas)
                   same-form change continues
                         |
             proximity of conscious witness
                         |
                         v
DISEQUILIBRIUM
illumination / activity / obstruction become asymmetrical
                         |
                         v
different-form transformation (virūpa-pariṇāma)
                         |
                         v
intellect -> ego-maker -> faculties / subtle potentials -> gross world
```

### Simple start

Primordial nature is not inert stuff. It is constituted by three inseparable tendencies. Their
balance is the unmanifest condition; their changing proportions explain both psychological moods
and cosmological diversity.

### Three-quality matrix

| Quality | English-first character | Affective tone | Function | Trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| illumination (sattva) | lightness, clarity, disclosure | pleasure | cognition and manifestation | not a separate luminous substance |
| activity (rajas) | motion, stimulation, propulsion | pain | energy and mediation | produces no independent tattva-set |
| obstruction (tamas) | heaviness, concealment, resistance | delusion | inertia and density | not simply moral evil |

The qualities are mutually supporting, opposing and dominating tendencies within one nature. No
empirical product contains only one.

### Two transformations

1. **Same-form transformation (sarūpa-pariṇāma):** each quality changes within its own character
   while equilibrium remains; no differentiated principle is manifested.
2. **Different-form transformation (virūpa-pariṇāma):** the qualities enter unequal relations and
   generate differentiated evolutes. Actual cosmological unfolding belongs here.

### Argument anatomy

```text
experience shows pleasure, pain and delusion
                     |
the world displays disclosure, motion and resistance
                     |
one threefold constitution explains mind and matter together
                     |
changing proportions explain difference without new ultimate substances
```

### Example with limit

A lamp’s flame, oil and wick cooperate while performing different roles; likewise the qualities
oppose and support one another. The analogy is limited because qualities are not three detachable
objects assembled from outside.

### Objection, reply, residual

**Objection:** reducing every qualitative difference to three tendencies is too schematic.

**Reply:** the theory does not deny complex differences; it explains them through innumerable
proportional configurations and stages of transformation.

**Residual:** explanatory flexibility risks unfalsifiability unless a writer shows the precise
role of each quality rather than labelling every case after the fact.

### Directive, retrieval and application

| Directive | Required route |
|---|---|
| Explain the three qualities | character → affect → function → inseparability |
| Explain their modifications | equilibrium → same-form change → disequilibrium → different-form evolution |
| Examine | add why activity energises both branches without a third product-list |

**Retrieval cue:** **Light–Move–Block; Same–Different.**

### Concept-check MCQ 3

Which statement is correct?

A. Same-form transformation produces gross and subtle principles while the qualities remain in
equilibrium.
B. Obstruction (tamas) is a purely moral evil with no cognitive or material function.
C. Activity (rajas) energises both cognitive and material branches without generating a third
product-list.
D. Illumination (sattva) alone constitutes mind, ego and intellect, while the other qualities
remain external.

#### Answer and explanation

**Correct answer: C**

- **A — Incorrect:** differentiated gross and subtle products belong to different-form
  transformation.
- **B — Incorrect:** obstruction includes heaviness, concealment and resistance; it is not merely
  a moral category.
- **C — Correct:** activity mediates both branches.
- **D — Incorrect:** mind, ego and intellect contain all three qualities in varying proportions.

## Lesson 4 — Conscious witness and the five proofs

**Progress:** 4/12 · **Stage:** Core · **Coverage:** nature of witness, five proofs, cumulative
evaluation and non-productive enjoyment

### Visual first: object and witness

```text
body · senses · mind · intellect · pleasure · pain · delusion
                       |
              all are presented / changing
                       |
                       v
          CONSCIOUS WITNESS (Puruṣa)
 self-luminous · unchanging · beyond the three qualities
 witness / experiencer, but not productive agent
```

### Simple start

Sāṃkhya distinguishes consciousness from every mental operation. Intellect judges, mind
coordinates and ego appropriates; none is conscious by itself. The witness is not a hidden mental
object but the condition under which any object or cognition appears.

### The five proofs

| Proof | Argument | Pressure point |
|---|---|---|
| Aggregates serve another (saṅghāta-parārthatvāt) | body–mind complexes function as instruments for a beneficiary | purpose may not require a separate user |
| Opposite of the three qualities (triguṇādi-viparyayāt) | known pleasure, pain and delusion imply a non-quality knower | a Buddhist can deny a substantial knower |
| Presiding locus (adhiṣṭhānāt) | coordinated psychophysical presentation requires a conscious locus | not a proof of God or productive controller |
| Experiencerhood (bhoktṛ-bhāvāt) | an insentient presented complex cannot itself be the subject of experience | “experiencer” must not become causal agent |
| Striving for isolation (kaivalyārthaṃ pravṛtteḥ) | release-seeking presupposes something distinct from the bondage-producing complex | motive alone may not prove an eternal self |

### Cumulative evaluation

No single proof is decisive in isolation. Together they argue:

```text
instrumentality + objectifiability + experiential unity + liberation-orientation
                                      |
                                      v
                         a non-object conscious witness
```

Their form is general-correlation inference. The strongest move is the subject–object distinction:
whatever can be presented as changing cannot exhaust the awareness to which it is presented.

### Objection, reply, residual

**Objection — Buddhist:** a permanent inactive witness has no causal efficacy and therefore adds
nothing to the stream.

**Reply:** witness-efficacy is not production. It is manifestation-for-experience: without
awareness, a causal stream may occur but cannot be an experienced stream.

**Residual:** calling witness “necessary for experience” can restate the datum unless Sāṃkhya shows
why momentary awareness-events cannot perform the role.

**Objection — Nyāya:** a self must bear cognition, desire, effort and merit; a wholly inactive self
cannot ground responsibility.

**Reply:** those changing properties belong to the empirical apparatus. Making pure awareness an
agent turns bondage into its real modification and threatens liberation.

### Directive, retrieval and application

| Directive | Do | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Explain proofs | five named reasons with one-line logic | bare Sanskrit list |
| Examine and evaluate | cumulative force + Buddhist/Nyāya objections + verdict | declaring every proof deductive |
| Define witness | essence-consciousness, changeless, quality-transcendent, non-productive experiencer | “mind” or “soul-substance with qualities” |

**Retrieval cue:** **Instrument–Other–Preside–Experience–Release.**

### Concept-check MCQ 4

What is the best evaluation of the five witness-proofs?

A. Each proof independently demonstrates every attribute of an eternal witness with strict
deductive certainty.
B. The presiding-locus proof demonstrates a creator who coordinates material nature from outside.
C. Together the proofs establish intellect as a permanently conscious material faculty.
D. Together the proofs infer a non-object witness from instrumental and objectifiable experience.

#### Answer and explanation

**Correct answer: D**

- **A — Incorrect:** the proofs mutually reinforce an inferential case rather than independently
  proving every attribute.
- **B — Incorrect:** the target is witness, not God.
- **C — Incorrect:** intellect belongs to unconscious nature.
- **D — Correct:** it states both the method and conclusion without overstating certainty.

## Lesson 5 — Many witnesses, empirical self and apparent agency

**Progress:** 5/12 · **Stage:** Core · **Coverage:** plurality, objections, empirical self,
inner instrument, reflection, thirteen instruments and unconscious teleology

### Visual first: how the inactive witness appears active

```text
OBJECT -> senses -> coordinating mind -> ego-maker -> determinative intellect
                                                     |
                                      reflects conscious witness
                                                     |
                                                     v
                                  “I know / act / enjoy”

ACTUAL DIVISION:
intellect determines · ego appropriates · mind coordinates
witness illuminates but does not produce
empirical self (jīva) = this conditioned appearance, not a 26th principle
```

### Why witnesses are many

The first three grounds belong directly to the classical plurality formula; separate liberation is
a supplementary inference.

| Ground | Reasoning | Qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Different births, deaths and faculties | one body’s death or blindness does not spread to all | differences first occur in embodied streams |
| Non-simultaneous activity | living complexes do not act together | productive action belongs to intellect, not witness |
| Different quality-configurations | temperaments vary as illuminating, active or obstructed | qualities belong to nature, not pure witness |
| Some bound, some released | one release does not release everyone | supplementary, not a fourth phrase of the classical verse |

### Advaita objection and Sāṃkhya reply

**Objection:** these facts prove many bodies and minds, not many pure consciousnesses. An
attributeless, partless and non-spatial awareness lacks a principle of individuation; limiting
adjuncts (upādhis) can make one awareness appear many.

**Reply:** non-transferable experience and particular liberation become unintelligible if there is
only one witness. Distinct reflective loci in distinct intellect-streams require numerically
distinct experiencers.

**Residual:** the reply protects individual ownership but does not fully explain how qualitatively
identical, attributeless witnesses are numerically individuated.

### Empirical self and the thirteen instruments

| Unit | Function |
|---|---|
| Determinative intellect (buddhi) | judgement, ascertainment, discrimination |
| Ego-maker (ahaṃkāra) | “I” and “mine” appropriation |
| Coordinating mind (manas) | attention and synthesis |
| Five cognitive capacities | hearing, touch, sight, taste and smell |
| Five action capacities | speech, grasping, locomotion, excretion and reproduction |

The external capacities are “doors”; the inner instrument is the “doorkeeper.” The empirical self
(jīva) is witness as apparently conditioned by intellect, ego, mind, body and karmic stream. It is
not another ultimate principle.

### Teleology without a planner

Nature functions for witness-purpose (puruṣārtha): experience and eventual release. Milk for the
calf, the lame-and-blind pair and the retiring dancer depict non-deliberative service.

**Objection:** purpose requires intelligence.

**Reply:** ordered end-serving operation need not involve conscious planning; a natural structure
can serve an end.

**Residual:** the analogies illustrate but do not establish why one unconscious nature serves many
inactive witnesses with individually timed bondage and release.

### Directive, retrieval and application

| Demand | Retrieval | Verdict move |
|---|---|---|
| One or many? | three classical grounds, then separate liberation | add Advaita objection and individuation residual |
| Empirical self versus witness | conditioned complex versus pure metaphysical consciousness | empirical self is not a 26th principle |
| Explain cognition | doors → inner instrument → reflection → appropriation | mind appears conscious; witness appears agent |

**Retrieval cue:** **Bodies–Acts–Qualities–Liberations; Determine–Appropriate–Coordinate.**

### Concept-check MCQ 5

Which claim is doctrinally safest?

A. The classical verse gives three plurality grounds; separate liberation supplies a supplementary
inference.
B. The empirical self is a twenty-sixth principle, distinct from both the material apparatus and
pure witness.
C. Productive agency belongs to witness, while material intellect merely receives its commands.
D. Different quality-configurations are intrinsic properties that individuate otherwise pure
witnesses.

#### Answer and explanation

**Correct answer: A**

- **A — Correct:** it distinguishes the three classical grounds from a supplementary inference.
- **B — Incorrect:** the empirical self is a conditioned appearance, not an added principle.
- **C — Incorrect:** productive agency belongs to nature’s evolutes; witness does not issue
  commands.
- **D — Incorrect:** quality-configurations belong to the material apparatus, never to pure
  witness.

## Lesson 6 — The twenty-five principles and the architecture of experience

**Progress:** 6/12 · **Stage:** Core · **Coverage:** full sequence, causal classes, branches,
faculties, subtle and gross elements, subtle body, transmigration and moral-continuity pressure

### Visual first: the complete evolutionary tree

```text
ROOT ONLY
Primordial material nature (1)
        |
        v
BOTH CAUSE AND EFFECT
cosmic / determinative intellect (2)
        |
        v
ego-maker (3)
   +----------------------+-----------------------+
   |                      |                       |
illumination branch     activity factor       obstruction branch
   |                    energises both             |
   v                                              v
coordinating mind (4)                         5 subtle potentials (15–19)
5 cognitive capacities (5–9)                        |
5 action capacities (10–14)                        v
                                             5 gross elements (20–24)

NEITHER CAUSE NOR EFFECT
conscious witness (25) — counted, never evolved
```

### Four causal-status classes

| Status | Members | Count |
|---|---|---:|
| cause, not effect | primordial material nature | 1 |
| cause and effect | intellect, ego-maker, five subtle potentials | 7 |
| effects only | mind, ten capacities, five gross elements | 16 |
| neither | conscious witness | 1 |

### The distinction tested most often

**Cosmic intellect (mahat) and determinative intellect (buddhi) are one principle.** “Mahat”
expresses its cosmic scale; “buddhi” its psychological function. Ego-maker (ahaṃkāra) is the next
principle and appropriates experience as “I.” Never write three sequential stages.

### Branches and members

| Family | Members | Function |
|---|---|---|
| cognitive capacities | hearing, touch, sight, taste, smell | receive their corresponding fields |
| action capacities | speech, grasping, locomotion, excretion, reproduction | outward operation |
| subtle potentials (tanmātras) | sound, touch, form/colour, taste, smell | subtle conditions of gross elements |
| gross elements (mahābhūtas) | ether, air, fire/light, water, earth | gross material world |

Correspondence: sound–ether; touch–air; form–fire; taste–water; smell–earth. Do not import Advaita’s
fivefold grossification into the classical Sāṃkhya sequence.

### Why cosmology is also psychology

The same nature produces the object, the capacities that receive it, the mind that coordinates it,
the ego that claims it and the intellect that judges it. Sāṃkhya therefore explains experience
without turning consciousness into a changing faculty.

### Subtle body and transmigration

The subtle body (liṅga-śarīra / sūkṣma-śarīra) is commonly counted as eighteen: intellect,
ego-maker, eleven capacities including mind, and five subtle potentials. It carries dispositions
and karmic tendencies when the gross body falls. The witness neither acts nor literally migrates.

**Moral-continuity pressure:** if pure witness is inactive, why is a particular karmic stream tied

## Lesson 7 — Pre-existence of the effect and real transformation

**Progress:** 7/12 · **Stage:** Core · **Coverage:** pre-existence thesis, five arguments,
real transformation, objections and manifestation replies

### Visual first: what production means

```text
BEFORE PRODUCTION                    CAUSAL CONDITIONS                 AFTER PRODUCTION
effect-form is latent        +       remove obstruction       ->       effect is manifest
in a specific material               and alter configuration           in a new state

NOT: being from sheer non-being
NOT: a miniature gross product hidden inside the cause
YES: determinate material continuity through real transformation
```

### Simple start

The doctrine of pre-existence of the effect (satkāryavāda) says that production does not create
being from nothing. A determinate effect is present in its material cause in an unmanifest state.
Sāṃkhya adds real transformation (pariṇāmavāda): the material cause genuinely changes into the
effect.

### The five causal arguments

| Argument | Core reasoning | Rival pressure and Sāṃkhya response |
|---|---|---|
| Non-being cannot be produced (asadakaraṇāt) | a sheer non-entity cannot become an entity | Nyāya distinguishes absolute nothing from prior absence of the effect; Sāṃkhya asks what fixes the determinate terminus |
| Selection of a material cause (upādāna-grahaṇāt) | curd-seekers take milk, cloth-makers take threads | a rival invokes capacity; Sāṃkhya identifies determinate capacity with latent effect |
| Not everything arises from everything (sarvasambhavābhāvāt) | undifferentiated non-being cannot explain selective production | a rival invokes causal constitution; Sāṃkhya asks why that constitution is effect-specific |
| A cause makes only what lies within its power (śaktasya śakya-karaṇāt) | milk can become curd, not cloth | Nyāya treats power as a cause-property; Sāṃkhya says its directedness presupposes effect-content |
| The effect has the being of the cause (kāraṇa-bhāvāt) | cloth is threads configured; ornament remains gold | Nyāya posits a new whole; Sāṃkhya treats novelty as arrangement, not new substance |

### Why this is real transformation

The three qualities genuinely alter their proportions and relations. Cause and effect are
materially continuous but differ by state (avasthā): latent/unmanifest versus patent/manifest.
Production is manifestation (abhivyakti), not mere verbal relabelling.

### Three standard objections

1. **Effort becomes redundant.** If the effect exists, why act?
   - **Reply:** effort supplies conditions and removes obstruction.
2. **Cause and effect become identical.**
   - **Reply:** they are materially non-different but configurationally and functionally distinct.
3. **The gross world must be eternally manifest.**
   - **Reply:** the determinate effect-form is latent; the gross product is not spatially present.

### Argument anatomy

```text
specific effect
   requires specific material + specific power + material continuity
                                 |
                                 v
                 effect is latent, not absolutely absent
                                 |
                                 v
                    production = real manifestation
```

### Directive, retrieval and application

| Directive | Required route |
|---|---|
| Explain pre-existence | thesis + all five reasons + latent/manifest distinction |
| Distinguish from real transformation | satkāryavāda states pre-existence; pariṇāmavāda states how it appears |
| Critically examine | effort, identity and novelty objections; reply and residual |

**Retrieval cue:** **Cannot–Select–Not-anything–Power–Continuity.**

## Lesson 8 — Rival causation and the mango-seed test

**Progress:** 8/12 · **Stage:** Core/Application · **Coverage:** Nyāya prior absence, Advaita
appearance, Buddhist contrast, worked seed–tree reasoning and bilateral rejection

### Visual first: one example, three ontologies

```text
MANGO SEED -> soil + water + warmth + time -> MANGO TREE

SĀṂKHYA                         NYĀYA–VAIŚEṢIKA                 ADVAITA
latent effect-form              tree-as-new-whole was           empirical change valid,
specific power                  previously absent               ultimately name/form
material continuity             specific causes produce it      dependent on Brahman
real transformation             beginning/new production        apparent transformation
```

### Sāṃkhya explanation

A mango seed is selected because its material constitution and powers are determinate. Under
appropriate conditions, its quality-structured material continuum genuinely changes until the
tree-form is manifest. The gross seed may cease while causal continuity remains. The theory does
not say a tiny visible tree was packed inside the seed.

### Rival 1: Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika

**Strong formulation:** the tree as a numerically new organised whole was absent before production
(prāgabhāva). The seed, auxiliary causes and causal powers existed, but the tree did not. This is
not creation from sheer nothing.

**Sāṃkhya reply:** prior non-appearance does not prove total non-being. Specific selection,
effect-directed power and material continuity show the result was grounded in the cause.

**Nyāya rejoinder:** cause-specific power explains selectivity without identifying power with a
pre-existing effect. Novel whole-properties are not reducible to a mere rearrangement.

**Residual:** the dispute turns on whether determinate capacity logically entails latent
effect-being.

### Rival 2: Advaita

**Strong formulation:** seed–tree transformation is empirically valid, but no changing,
independent unconscious nature is ultimate. Name and form appear through dependent superimposition
on changeless conscious Brahman; ultimate causation is appearance (vivarta).

**Sāṃkhya reply:** real material continuity better explains the specificity and practical
difference of seed and tree. A purely apparent ultimate change risks under-describing empirical
causal order.

**Advaita rejoinder:** empirical efficacy does not prove independent ultimate reality. Sāṃkhya has
not shown why its inferred material root outranks non-dual consciousness.

### Bounded Buddhist contrast

Dependent origination explains a conditioned transient series without a single enduring primordial
material substance. Use this only when comparison is demanded; the owned mango questions require
Nyāya and Advaita as the principal rivals.

### Twenty-mark answer mechanism

```text
define pre-existence + real transformation
             |
apply five causal reasons selectively to mango seed
             |
reconstruct Nyāya accurately -> Sāṃkhya reply -> residual
             |
reconstruct Advaita accurately -> Sāṃkhya reply -> residual
             |
graded conclusion: specificity supports continuity,
but does not by itself settle the identity criterion
```

### Directive, retrieval and application

| Phrase | Non-negotiable response |
|---|---|
| “through theory of causation” | state pre-existence and real transformation |
| “by rejecting rival perspectives” | reconstruct both rivals before replying |
| “mango tree from mango seed” | latent determinate form + conditions + continuity; no miniature tree |

**Retrieval cue:** **Seed–Conditions–Continuity; New Whole–Appearance.**

## Lesson 9 — The relation problem and Śaṃkara’s chief opponent

**Progress:** 9/12 · **Stage:** Core/Critical · **Coverage:** proximity, reflection, timing,
individual association, Advaita reaction, chief-opponent rationale, arguments and verdict

### Visual first: the unstable bridge

```text
CONSCIOUS WITNESS                         PRIMORDIAL MATERIAL NATURE
inactive · changeless                     unconscious · active
          \                               /
           \ mere proximity (sānnidhya) /
                    |
        +-----------+-----------+
        |                       |
magnet / iron                crystal / flower
activation image             attribution image
        |                       |
magnet exerts force          colour-appearance does not
                             initiate real evolution
                    |
                    v
ŚAṂKARA: analogy illustrates; it does not explain the relation
```

### Sāṃkhya defence

The relation is not physical contact or mutual modification. Nature transforms through its own
qualities in witness-presence. Reflection explains why unconscious intellect appears conscious and
why changeless witness appears affected.

### Four sharpened problems

1. **Activation:** if witness does nothing, how can presence make nature act?
2. **Timing:** if witness and nature are eternally proximate, why does disequilibrium occur now
   rather than always?
3. **Association:** why is this attributeless witness linked to this intellect-stream?
4. **Termination:** why does nature cease binding display for one witness while continuing for
   others?

The magnet is imperfect because a magnet has causal power. The crystal explains apparent
attribution but not initiation.

### Why “chief opponent” (pradhāna-malla)?

Sāṃkhya is not a minor error but a comprehensive rival. It offers one material ground, a full
cosmology, psychology, causation theory and liberation without Brahman or creator. It is close
enough to Advaita in explanatory ambition and pre-existence orientation to demand sustained
refutation.

### Śaṃkara’s principal arguments

| Advaita pressure | Sāṃkhya reply | Residual |
|---|---|---|
| Upaniṣadic cause is conscious and knowing, not unconscious chief nature | inference from material continuity establishes an unconscious material cause | Sāṃkhya does not grant Advaita’s scriptural interpretation as neutral evidence |
| purposive order cannot arise from unconscious qualities | natural teleology can be structural, like milk serving a calf | analogy does not fully explain individual end-directedness |
| inactive witness and unconscious nature cannot relate | proximity and reflection are non-physical | naming non-physical relation may not explain activation |
| a second independent ultimate defeats non-dual Brahman | dualism preserves real world and changeless awareness | this is a clash of first principles, not a local correction |
| real transformation threatens ultimacy | witness does not transform; only material nature does | Advaita still denies the independent ultimacy of that nature |

### Bilateral evaluation

Śaṃkara is strongest when he exposes unexplained linkage and teleology. He is less immediately
decisive when he assumes that conscious-source scripture is already binding on Sāṃkhya’s
inferential method. Sāṃkhya preserves realism and witness-immutability, but its bridge between two
independent ultimates remains its weakest joint.

### Directive, retrieval and application

| Demand | Structure |
|---|---|
| Present Śaṃkara’s criticism briefly | state dualism → relation dilemma → analogy limits → verdict |
| Advaita reaction and Sāṃkhya defence | conscious cause/appearance/relation → material continuity/real quality-change/changeless witness → rejoinder |
| Why chief opponent? | explanatory proximity + comprehensive rivalry, then examine each argument |

**Retrieval cue:** **Activate–Time–Associate–Stop; Conscious–Purpose–Relation–Non-duality.**

## Lesson 10 — Suffering, bondage, liberation and non-theism

**Progress:** 10/12 · **Stage:** Core/Soteriology · **Coverage:** threefold suffering,
non-discrimination, discriminative knowledge, analogies, embodied release, non-theism and the
“never bound” objection

### Visual first: liberation as correction, not production

```text
THREEFOLD SUFFERING
internal · other beings/material · cosmic/unseen
                  |
                  v
false identification (aviveka)
“mental quality, agency and pain are mine as witness”
                  |
                  v
discriminative knowledge (viveka-jñāna)
“every changing state belongs to nature; I am the witness”
                  |
                  v
isolation (kaivalya)
not merger · not God-union · not physical death
                  |
          prior momentum continues
                  |
living release -> final disembodiment
```

### Threefold suffering

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| internal (ādhyātmika) | bodily or mental affliction | illness, grief, confusion |
| other-being/material (ādhibhautika) | injury from beings or surroundings | violence, animals, environmental harm |
| cosmic/unseen (ādhidaivika) | elemental, cosmic or traditionally unseen agency | extraordinary natural or unseen affliction |

Temporary remedies do not remove the root confusion between witness and nature.

### Bondage and liberation

Bondage is non-discrimination (aviveka), expressed psychologically as false appropriation
(abhimāna). Consciousness reflected in intellect makes the intellect seem conscious and the
witness seem to know, act and suffer. Liberation is steady discriminative knowledge
(viveka-jñāna) and culminates in isolation (kaivalya).

The dancer withdraws after being seen; the potter’s wheel continues after the initiating impulse
has ceased. These images explain why binding display ends while a liberated body may continue
through prior momentum. Strictly, witness was never modified, migrated, bound or newly liberated.

### The soteriological objection

**Objection:** if witness is eternally free and never suffers, liberation solves nothing.

**Reply:** suffering is real at the empirical intellect–body level, and false attribution structures
experience. Knowledge removes the error and ends the production of further bondage.

**Residual:** the distinction between an eternally unaffected witness and a genuinely suffering
empirical stream makes liberation look like an event in nature rather than an achievement of the
witness. Sāṃkhya accepts this: nature ceases its binding function for that witness.

### Classical non-theism

Classical Sāṃkhya needs no creator or grace. Its standard pressures against a creator are:

1. desire-driven creation compromises perfection;
2. a fulfilled being lacks motive to create a painful world;
3. karma either constrains divine freedom or arbitrary distribution threatens justice;
4. immutable consciousness cannot be the material source of a changing unconscious world under
   material-continuity causation;
5. nature and witnesses already perform the explanatory work.

The safe conclusion is “God-unproved or unnecessary,” not an overbroad proof that no divine being
could exist. Later theistic interpretations must be qualified; Yoga owns the special-witness God.

### Directive, retrieval and application

| Demand | Non-negotiable distinctions |
|---|---|
| Explain liberation | suffering → false identification → discrimination → isolation |
| Examine | add “never really bound” objection and event-in-nature reply |
| Is Sāṃkhya atheistic? | classical non-theism; no required creator; later qualifications |

**Retrieval cue:** **Three Sufferings–False “I”–Discriminate–Isolate–Momentum.**

### PYQ 1 — 2018 Q5(e) · 10 marks

> **Is Puruṣa one or many? Explain the Sāṃkhya position in this regard and give arguments in
> support of your answer.**

**Directive decoding:** “Explain” requires the position and its intelligible basis; “give
arguments” requires the classical grounds, not a bare assertion.

#### Model answer

Sāṃkhya accepts **many conscious witnesses (Puruṣas)**. They are qualitatively alike—pure,
inactive, changeless awareness—but numerically plural. This contrasts with one primordial material
nature (Prakṛti), whose unity concerns the material continuum rather than conscious ownership.

The *Sāṃkhyakārikā* route gives three principal grounds. First, births, deaths and allotments of
sense-capacities are distinct: one person’s death or blindness does not spread to all. Second,
embodied streams do not act simultaneously; their activities differ in time and form. Third,
quality-configurations differ, producing illuminating, active and obstructed temperaments. A
supplementary argument adds that the liberation of one does not liberate everyone.

These facts support non-transferable experience. If there were only one witness, Sāṃkhya argues,
individual bondage and release would become unintelligible. However, Advaita objects that the
evidence establishes many bodies and minds—limiting adjuncts—not many attributeless
consciousnesses. Sāṃkhya replies that distinct reflective intellect-streams and particular
experience require distinct experiencers.

Thus plurality is well motivated by experiential individuality, though the ultimate principle
that numerically differentiates qualitatively identical witnesses remains a residual difficulty.

**Why this earns marks:** it states the doctrine, prioritises the three classical grounds, adds the
supplementary inference and gives a qualified verdict.

### PYQ 2 — 2018 Q7(b) · 15 marks

> **How do the Advaita Vedāntins react to the Prakṛtipariṇāmavāda of the Sāṃkhya philosophy? How
> do the Sāṃkhyas defend their own position in this regard? Discuss.**

**Directive decoding:** the answer must be bilateral: Advaita reaction, Sāṃkhya defence and a
reasoned close.

#### Model answer

Sāṃkhya’s nature-transformation doctrine (Prakṛti-pariṇāmavāda) combines pre-existence of the
effect with **real transformation**. The quality-structured material cause genuinely evolves into
intellect, ego, faculties, subtle potentials and the gross world, while conscious witness remains
unchanged.

Advaita raises three pressures. First, Upaniṣadic causal passages describe the source as conscious
and knowing, not an independent unconscious chief nature. Second, if ultimate reality truly
transforms, it appears mutable; Advaita instead treats world-production as appearance (vivarta) of
name and form dependent on changeless Brahman. Third, inactive witness and unconscious nature
cannot intelligibly relate, so Sāṃkhya’s evolutionary trigger remains obscure.

Sāṃkhya replies that it does **not** transform conscious witness. It posits a distinct unconscious
material cause precisely to preserve awareness as changeless. Specific material selection, causal
power and continuity—milk to curd, seed to tree—support genuine transformation. Calling empirical
change ultimately apparent does not itself explain why only determinate causes produce determinate
effects. Proximity and reflection, rather than physical interaction, explain experience and false
attribution.

Advaita can rejoin that empirical efficacy is compatible with ultimate appearance and that
“proximity” does not solve activation. The Sāṃkhya defence is strongest as causal realism and
weakest at the relation between its independent ultimates.

**Why this earns marks:** it does not reduce the debate to “real versus unreal”; it reconstructs
scriptural, causal and relation arguments on both sides.

### PYQ 3 — 2019 Q5(d) · 10 marks

> **Critically discuss the metaphysical status of a Jīva and a Puruṣa according to Sāṃkhya
> philosophy.**

**Directive decoding:** define both, show their relation, then test the explanatory cost.

#### Model answer

Conscious witness (Puruṣa) is Sāṃkhya’s ultimate conscious reality: self-luminous, changeless,
inactive and beyond the three qualities. Consciousness is its essence, not a changing property.
The empirical self (jīva), by contrast, is **not a twenty-sixth principle**. It is witness as
apparently conditioned through determinative intellect, ego-maker, mind, body and karmic stream.

The inner instrument is unconscious. Intellect determines, ego appropriates and mind coordinates.
Because intellect is transparent and illumination-predominant, it reflects witness-consciousness.
The material complex then appears conscious, while witness appears to know, act and enjoy.
False appropriation—“I think, act and suffer”—constitutes bondage.

This distinction protects the immutability of witness and locates moral and psychological change
in nature. Yet it creates two problems. If witness never acts, moral ownership belongs to the
empirical stream rather than the ultimate self. Further, if witnesses are attributeless, their
particular association with distinct karmic streams is under-explained.

Hence jīva is the conditioned person-complex and Puruṣa its conscious metaphysical pole. The
distinction is soteriologically powerful, though the bridge between personal continuity and an
inactive ultimate witness remains difficult.

**Why this earns marks:** it answers “metaphysical status,” explains reflection and includes two
direct critical pressures.

### PYQ 4 — 2019 Q8(c) · 15 marks

> **Which Sāṃkhya proof for the existence of Prakṛti actually shows that there can be only one
> Prakṛti? Justify your answer.**

**Directive decoding:** “which” demands selection; “justify” demands the inferential bridge and its
qualification.

#### Model answer

The relevant proof is **from the undivided source of the diversified universe**
(*avibhāgāt vaiśvarūpyasya*). The manifest world is extraordinarily diverse, yet its effects form
an interconnected material order and display the same three-quality constitution. Sāṃkhya infers
an unmanifest, undivided material root from which later differentiations arise.

This proof bears on **number**, unlike the other arguments, which primarily establish the need for
a material ground. Finite determinateness, coordination, productive power and cause–effect levels
show why an unmanifest cause is needed. The fifth adds that the total diversified order returns to
one undifferentiated source. Positing several independent primordial matrices would leave their
shared quality-structure and coordinated effects unexplained; one root is more economical.

The justification must nevertheless be qualified. “Each source is undivided” does not logically
exclude several such sources. The inference to exactly one Prakṛti depends on an added premise:
the cosmos is one materially continuous, quality-structured explanatory order. A pluralist can
challenge that premise.

Therefore the fifth proof provides Sāṃkhya’s strongest one-root argument, but as explanatory
inference rather than deductive or perceptual enumeration. It must not be confused with the
separate claim that conscious witnesses are many.

**Why this earns marks:** it selects the exact proof, distinguishes its function from the other
four and supplies both justification and logical limit.

### PYQ 5 — 2020 Q7(a) · 20 marks

> **“A mango tree is grown out of a mango seed.” How will Sāṃkhya system explain this process
> through their theory of causation by rejecting their rival perspectives?**

**Directive decoding:** apply Sāṃkhya causation to the example and reject, without caricature,
Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika and Advaita.

#### Model answer

Sāṃkhya explains the mango tree through pre-existence of the effect (satkāryavāda) in the form of
real transformation (pariṇāmavāda). The tree is latent in the seed’s determinate material
constitution—not as a miniature gross tree, but as an effect-specific capacity and form. Soil,
water, warmth and time remove obstacles and enable the quality-structured material continuum to
transform until the tree becomes manifest.

The five causal reasons support this account. Sheer non-being cannot be produced; a specific
material cause is selected; not everything comes from everything; a cause produces only what lies
within its power; and the effect retains the being of its material cause through changed
configuration. Thus production is manifestation, not creation from absolute nothing.

Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika replies that the tree as a new organised whole was previously absent
(prāgabhāva). Seed, conditions and powers were real, but the effect did not pre-exist. Novel
whole-properties justify new production. Sāṃkhya answers that non-appearance does not establish
non-being and that effect-directed power and continuity are best understood as latent effect.
Nyāya can still insist that capacity does not entail identity.

Advaita accepts seed–tree causation empirically but denies an independent unconscious material
ultimate. Change is ultimately appearance of name and form dependent on Brahman. Sāṃkhya answers
that real transformation better explains determinate material specificity; Advaita rejoins that
empirical efficacy does not establish ultimate independence.

The example strongly supports restricted, continuous production. It does not by itself settle
whether causal power is identical with a latent effect or whether empirical transformation is
ultimately real.

**Why this earns marks:** it applies all essential causal logic, reconstructs both rivals fairly
and ends with the precise unresolved issue.

### PYQ 6 — 2021 Q6(b) · 15 marks

> **Explain the Sāṃkhya view on three guṇas (guṇa-traya) and their modifications.**

**Directive decoding:** both the three qualities and their two modes of modification are compulsory.

#### Model answer

Primordial material nature is constituted by three inseparable qualities. Illumination (sattva)
is light, clear and revealing, associated with pleasure and cognition. Activity (rajas) is
restless, propulsive and mediating, associated with pain and effort. Obstruction (tamas) is heavy,
resistant and concealing, associated with delusion and material density. They are not detachable
substances; every empirical product contains all three in varying proportions.

In the unmanifest condition, the three remain in equilibrium (sāmyāvasthā). Nature is still
dynamic, but no differentiated principle emerges. This is explained through **same-form
transformation (sarūpa-pariṇāma)**: each quality changes within its own character while balance
persists.

In witness-proximity, balance is disturbed. Unequal relations among the qualities generate
intellect, ego, faculties, subtle potentials and gross elements. This is **different-form
transformation (virūpa-pariṇāma)**, the actual mechanism of cosmological evolution. Activity
supplies operative energy to the illumination-predominant and obstruction-predominant branches; it
does not generate an independent third list of products.

The doctrine unifies psychology and cosmology: clarity, motion and concealment explain both mental
states and the material world. Its economy is impressive, although its flexibility can become
over-general unless the role of each quality is specified.

**Why this earns marks:** it covers nature, affect, function, equilibrium and both modifications,
including the frequent activity-branch trap.

### PYQ 7 — 2022 Q5(a) · 10 marks

> **Examine and evaluate the proofs given by Sāṃkhya philosophy to prove the existence of Puruṣa.**

**Directive decoding:** listing is insufficient; explain the inferential logic and weigh it.

#### Model answer

Sāṃkhya offers five cumulative reasons for a conscious witness distinct from unconscious nature.
First, body–mind aggregates function as instruments for another
(*saṅghāta-parārthatvāt*). Second, mutable pleasure, pain and delusion are objects of awareness, so
their knower must be other than the qualities (*triguṇādi-viparyayāt*). Third, coordinated
psychophysical presentation requires a conscious presiding locus (*adhiṣṭhānāt*). Fourth,
experience requires an experiencer (*bhoktṛ-bhāvāt*). Fifth, striving for isolation presupposes a
liberable principle distinct from bondage-producing nature (*kaivalyārthaṃ pravṛtteḥ*).

Their strength is cumulative and phenomenological: the objectifiability of mental states appears
to require a non-object pole of awareness. However, a Buddhist can deny that this pole is a
permanent substance, while Nyāya argues that a wholly inactive self cannot bear moral agency.
Further, purposive aggregates may not logically require a separate beneficiary.

Sāṃkhya replies that witness is required for manifestation-as-experience, not productive
causation; agency belongs to the empirical apparatus. The proofs therefore make a serious case for
consciousness irreducible to mental function, but do not conclusively establish every predicate of
an eternal, plural and inactive Puruṣa.

**Why this earns marks:** all five proofs are explained and the conclusion precisely calibrates
what their cumulative force establishes.

### PYQ 8 — 2023 Q5(b) · 10 marks

> **“If Puruṣa and Prakṛti are two completely independent realities, then no relation between the
> two is possible.” In the light of this statement make a brief presentation of Śaṅkara’s criticism
> of Sāṃkhya dualism.**

**Directive decoding:** keep it brief and centre Śaṃkara’s relation criticism.

#### Model answer

Sāṃkhya posits inactive conscious witnesses and one active unconscious nature. Yet evolution,
experience, bondage and liberation require them to be related. The school invokes mere proximity
(sānnidhya) and reflection rather than physical interaction.

Śaṃkara argues that this response is insufficient. If witness is wholly inactive, it cannot
disturb quality-equilibrium; if nature is wholly unconscious, it cannot purposively evolve for
witness-experience and release. The magnet analogy is defective because a magnet exerts force,
which would compromise witness-inactivity. The crystal analogy explains only why awareness appears
coloured by nearby mental states; it does not explain initiation of a real world-process.

The difficulty also concerns timing and association. Eternal proximity cannot explain why
evolution begins now rather than always, and an attributeless witness cannot easily be attached to
this rather than that intellect-stream. A third mediator would generate regress; denying real
relation makes proximity merely verbal.

Sāṃkhya can preserve non-physical reflection and material self-transformation, but Śaṃkara’s
criticism exposes the system’s central weakness: two independent ultimates are required to
cooperate without an intelligible connecting principle.

**Why this earns marks:** it stays on the quoted relation problem, tests both analogies and adds the
timing/association dilemma.

### PYQ 9 — 2024 Q7(a) · 20 marks (10+10)

> **Present an account of evolution of Prakṛti as propounded in Sāṃkhyakārikā. In this context,
> also explain the difference between buddhi, mahat and ahaṃkāra.**

**Directive decoding:** honour the exact **10+10** structure. Give each half an independent,
balanced treatment.

#### Model answer — Part I: evolution of primordial material nature (10 marks)

In its unmanifest state, primordial material nature is the equilibrium of illumination, activity
and obstruction. Witness-proximity occasions different-form transformation. The first evolute is
cosmic/determinative intellect. From it arises ego-maker. Ego differentiates into two product
branches, both energised by activity: the illumination-predominant branch yields coordinating mind,
five cognitive capacities and five action capacities; the obstruction-predominant branch yields
five subtle potentials—sound, touch, form, taste and smell—from which arise ether, air, fire,
water and earth.

The complete enumeration is one root, seven principles that are both cause and effect, sixteen
effects only, and witness as neither cause nor effect. Witness is counted as the twenty-fifth
principle but never evolves from nature. The sequence unifies cosmology and psychology because the
same source produces objects and the apparatus through which they are experienced.

#### Model answer — Part II: intellect, cosmic intellect and ego-maker (10 marks)

Cosmic intellect (mahat) and determinative intellect (buddhi) are **not two successive principles**.
They are two aspects or names of the first evolute: “mahat” stresses its cosmic scale, while
“buddhi” stresses determination, judgement and discrimination in the individual apparatus.

Ego-maker (ahaṃkāra) is the next evolute. It appropriates determinations as “I” and “mine” and
grounds individuation. Thus the correct sequence is:

```text
primordial nature -> cosmic/determinative intellect -> ego-maker
```

Confusing cosmic and determinative intellect inflates the count and destroys the classical
twenty-five-principle structure. Their identity and ego’s derivation also show the link between
cosmic evolution and false personal agency.

**Why this earns marks:** it preserves the printed split, gives the complete branching sequence and
states the decisive identity/difference without miscounting.

### PYQ 10 — 2025 Q7(a) · 20 marks

> **Why does Śaṅkara consider Sāṃkhya Philosophy as his chief opponent (pradhāna malla)? Examine
> his arguments against Sāṃkhya Philosophy.**

**Directive decoding:** explain *why chief*, then examine arguments rather than merely list them.

#### Model answer

Śaṃkara treats Sāṃkhya as the “chief opponent” because it is the strongest comprehensive rival to
Vedānta, not because it is closest in conclusion. It offers an unmanifest first material ground,
a real causal theory, a full cosmology and psychology, plural conscious witnesses and liberation
without Brahman or creator. Both systems seek a general explanation of world, self and release;
their dispute therefore concerns the identity and nature of the ultimate ground.

Śaṃkara first argues that the Upaniṣadic cause is conscious and knowing, whereas Sāṃkhya’s chief
nature is insentient. Second, unconscious qualities cannot purposively evolve for the experience
and release of witnesses. Third, independent inactive witness and unconscious nature cannot
relate; proximity fails to explain activation, timing or individual association. Fourth, an
independent material ultimate contradicts non-dual Brahman. Fifth, real transformation is inferior
to appearance because ultimacy should not become the changing world.

Sāṃkhya replies that material continuity, determinate power and the three-quality structure support
an unconscious material cause; natural teleology need not be intentional; proximity is
non-physical; and only nature transforms, preserving witness as immutable. These replies protect
realism and avoid making consciousness material.

Śaṃkara remains strongest on unexplained relation and teleology. His scriptural and non-dual
premises are less neutral because Sāṃkhya does not grant them. Sāṃkhya is therefore “chief” as a
systemic rival, yet vulnerable where its two ultimates must cooperate.

**Why this earns marks:** it explains the epithet, evaluates five arguments and weighs Sāṃkhya’s
replies rather than assuming the Vedāntic conclusion.

### PYQ 11 — 2026 Q5(c) · 10 marks

> **Is Purusa one or many? Explain and examine the Sankhya position in this context.**
>
> *Printed wording note in the source ledger: “Sarnkhya” for Sāṃkhya.*

**Directive decoding:** compared with 2018, “examine” explicitly requires testing the inference.

#### Model answer

Sāṃkhya holds that conscious witnesses are numerically many though identical in nature as pure,
inactive awareness. It argues from separate births, deaths and sense-capacities; non-simultaneous
activity; and different quality-configurations. Separate bondage and liberation provide a
supplementary ground: one person’s release does not release all.

These facts make individual experience non-transferable and support many centres of awareness.
However, the inference moves from differences in bodies, intellects and dispositions—all products
of nature—to plurality in quality-transcendent witness. Advaita therefore argues that many
limiting adjuncts can make one consciousness appear many. Further, attributeless and non-spatial
witnesses lack an obvious principle of numerical individuation.

Sāṃkhya replies that one witness would make particular enjoyment, moral continuity and liberation
unintelligible; distinct reflected intellect-streams require distinct experiencers. This protects
the phenomenology of individual ownership but does not completely answer how identical pure
witnesses differ.

Hence the doctrine is explanatorily useful and systemically necessary, but the evidence proves
plural empirical streams more directly than plural transcendental witnesses.

**Why this earns marks:** it retains the classical grounds but visibly upgrades the answer from
explanation to examination.

### PYQ 12 — 2026 Q6(a) · 20 marks

> **“A mango tree is grown out of a mango seed.” How do the Sankhya philosophers explain this
> process through their theory of causation by rejecting their rival perspectives?**

**Directive decoding:** this is a fresh owned question, not permission to omit the rival-rejection
half. Both principal rivals must be answered.

#### Model answer

The mango process exemplifies Sāṃkhya’s pre-existence of the effect and real transformation. A
mango seed rather than sand or another seed is selected because its material constitution contains
the determinate power for a mango tree. Soil, water, heat and time enable that latent effect-form
to become manifest through genuine material change. The doctrine asserts continuity, not a tiny
gross tree hidden in the seed.

Five reasons support it: non-being cannot be produced; appropriate material is deliberately
selected; not everything arises from everything; a cause produces only what lies within its
power; and the effect retains the material being of the cause. The destroyed gross seed and
emergent tree are different states and configurations of a continuous material process.

Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika holds that the tree as a new whole was previously absent. Specific powers and
auxiliary conditions explain why this seed produces this effect; latent identity is unnecessary.
Sāṃkhya responds that effect-directed capacity and continuity are more intelligible if the effect
is grounded in the cause. The residual dispute is whether power entails pre-existence.

Advaita accepts empirical transformation but treats ultimate change as appearance dependent on
changeless Brahman. Sāṃkhya answers that causal specificity and transformed material structure
favour real evolution; Advaita replies that empirical reality need not be ultimate.

Thus the seed–tree case powerfully supports determinate continuity and manifestation. It does not
alone decide between latent identity, new-whole production and empirically valid appearance.

**Why this earns marks:** it is independently complete, accurately rejects two rivals and ends
with a causal, not rhetorical, verdict.

### Original 10-mark Mains practice

> **Distinguish the conscious witness (Puruṣa) from determinative intellect (buddhi). Why is the
> distinction indispensable to Sāṃkhya liberation?**

#### Model solution

Witness is pure, self-luminous, inactive and unchanging consciousness; intellect is the first
evolute of material nature, changing, quality-constituted and insentient. Intellect judges and
discriminates but appears conscious because it reflects witness. Witness illuminates but never
performs the judgement.

The distinction explains error: ego appropriates an illuminated intellectual state as “I know and
act.” Bondage is precisely this non-discrimination. Liberation therefore does not purify witness
or stop all cognition; it establishes that every determination belongs to nature while awareness
is other than it.

The model preserves immutable consciousness, but the reflection relation remains analogical.
Nevertheless, without the distinction, Sāṃkhya could neither explain apparent agency nor define
isolation as discriminative release.

**Why this earns marks:** it uses fixed comparison axes and connects metaphysics directly to
soteriology.

### Original 15-mark Mains practice

> **“Sāṃkhya’s theory of the three qualities is simultaneously cosmological and psychological.”
> Examine.**

#### Model solution

The three qualities constitute primordial material nature and therefore every evolute. Illumination
accounts for disclosure, clarity and pleasure; activity for motion, effort and pain; obstruction
for concealment, inertia and delusion. Their equilibrium is unmanifest nature, while asymmetry
produces the cosmic sequence.

The same structure explains psychological life. Intellect’s relative illumination permits
knowledge and reflection; activity drives desire and effort; obstruction produces confusion and
fixation. Thus Sāṃkhya avoids a hard division between mental and physical substance: both are
different configurations of one nature.

This dual application is economical and supports liberation, because even the subtlest cognition
remains quality-produced and distinguishable from witness. Yet the framework can become
over-elastic if every state is classified post hoc. Its philosophical value lies less in empirical
measurement than in integrating world, mind and release under one transformational grammar.

**Why this earns marks:** it proves both sides of “simultaneously,” links them and supplies a
measured methodological criticism.

### Original 20-mark Mains practice

> **Does Sāṃkhya successfully explain purposive evolution without an intelligent creator? Analyse
> with reference to witness-purpose, causation and liberation.**

#### Model solution

Sāṃkhya rejects a creator because one material nature and plural witnesses suffice for world,
experience and release. Nature acts for witness-purpose: it supplies objects of experience and
eventually enables discrimination. Traditional milk-for-calf and dancer analogies present
end-serving activity without deliberate planning.

Its causal realism strengthens the account. Determinate effects arise from determinate powers;
the three-quality structure unfolds through real transformation rather than arbitrary production.
The process therefore possesses internal order without requiring external design. Since witness is
inactive, assigning creation to it would also compromise the distinction liberation presupposes.

Nyāya and Vedānta object that end-directed order needs intelligence. The problem is sharpened by
many witnesses: why does one nature arrange distinct streams and stop binding one at a particular
time? Mere proximity seems unable to explain activation, timing and association. Sāṃkhya replies
that purposiveness can be structural, not intentional, and that nature transforms through its own
constitution.

The reply plausibly blocks the inference from order to a planner, but does not fully explain
individualised teleology. Sāṃkhya succeeds in showing that intelligent intention is not logically
required for every organised process; it is less successful in explaining why beginningless
unconscious nature serves particular witnesses in a liberation-directed way.

**Why this earns marks:** it integrates three doctrines, answers the strongest theistic objection
and offers a graded conclusion.

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

### Deployment gate

Use optional depth only when:

1. the direct doctrine and directive are already complete;
2. the question is 15 or 20 marks, or explicitly critical;
3. one refinement changes the quality of evaluation;
4. at least three minutes remain for a conclusion.

Stop after one high-value refinement unless the question directly asks the issue.

### A1 — Relation as epistemic conjunction rather than physical contact

An advanced reading treats witness–nature “conjunction” as a condition of manifestation rather than
a causal event between two objects. The object-form occurs in intellect; witness is that to which
the form is manifest. This reduces the demand for mechanical interaction.

**Deployment:** use in a relation or reflection question after stating proximity and analogy limits.

**Counter-pressure:** if conjunction is only epistemic, it may explain awareness of an already
formed cognition but not why material evolution begins. Do not claim that reinterpretation fully
solves activation.

**Stop line:** “The epistemic reading clarifies manifestation, not the origin of the material
sequence.”

### A2 — Structural teleology without intention

Sāṃkhya’s witness-purpose can be read as function rather than foresight. A process may systematically
serve an end because of its organisation, not because it represents that end. This distinguishes
teleology from a divine planner.

**Deployment:** use in non-theism, purpose or Śaṃkara answers.

**Counter-pressure:** individualised liberation remains harder than general function. A single
nature must serve many witnesses without choosing or knowing any.

**Stop line:** “Structural function weakens the design inference but leaves particular association
under-explained.”

### A3 — Plurality as a protective rather than demonstrative doctrine

Plural witnesses preserve personal experience, karma and non-simultaneous liberation. This may
show why the system **needs** plurality more strongly than it shows how attributeless awareness is
numerically divided.

**Deployment:** use only after the classical three grounds.

**Counter-pressure:** calling the doctrine protective does not refute it; inference to the best
explanation may legitimately outrun direct perception.

**Stop line:** “Plurality is systemically indispensable but metaphysically under-individuated.”

## BOUNDED EXPERT / OPTIONAL INDIAN DOSSIER

### E1 — Commentarial source precision

The classical centre is Īśvarakṛṣṇa’s *Sāṃkhyakārikā*, read through traditions such as
Gauḍapāda and Vācaspati Miśra. Later Sūtra and Vijñānabhikṣu materials may be more openly theistic.

**Deploy when:** the question asks classical versus later Sāṃkhya, non-theism or textual identity.

**Do not:** use chronology to evade the doctrine or imply one homogeneous view across all strata.

### E2 — Material causation and configuration

The strongest causal dispute is not “something versus nothing.” It is whether a novel configured
whole is numerically new or a manifest state of a materially continuous cause. This is the exact
hinge between Sāṃkhya and Nyāya.

**Deploy when:** evaluating the fifth causal argument or mango example.

**Do not:** repeat all five arguments after the hinge is clear.

### E3 — Liberation belongs grammatically to nature

Strict Sāṃkhya can say that nature is bound, transmigrates and becomes liberated “for” witness;
witness itself was always isolated in essence. This grammatical inversion is philosophically
important because it preserves immutability.

**Deploy when:** a question presses “who is liberated?”

**Do not:** conclude that suffering is unreal. It remains real within the empirical nature-stream,
though falsely attributed to witness.

### Expert stop rules

- Do not import Yoga’s eight limbs, absorption taxonomy or special God into a Sāṃkhya answer.
- Do not turn controlled Western parallels into evidence.
- Do not use specialist terminology unless it changes an inference, distinction or verdict.
- Do not add optional material before completing the direct PYQ demand.
- Do not present contested historical claims as settled.

## CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

### System identity and method

- **Core formula:** one active unconscious primordial material nature; many inactive conscious
  witnesses; real evolution; bondage through non-discrimination; isolation through discriminative
  knowledge.
- **Classical centre:** Kapila is the founding figure of tradition; Īśvarakṛṣṇa’s
  *Sāṃkhyakārikā* is the earliest extant systematic classical text.
- **Three valid means:** perception, inference and reliable testimony.
- **Inferential hinge:** general-correlation inference reaches imperceptible root nature and
  witness from ordered, dependent and objectifiable experience.
- **Imperceptible ≠ non-existent:** subtlety, obstruction, organ-defect, distance, inattention and
  related conditions can block perception.

### Primordial material nature: definition and proofs

- **Synonyms with emphasis:** root nature = ultimate source; chief = primary material principle;
  unmanifest = undifferentiated state.
- Material nature includes the roots of mind as well as gross matter.
- Five-proof recall: **Limit–Link–Power–Levels–Unity**.
- The fifth, undivided-source proof, supports one root when joined to the premise of one
  quality-structured material order.
- **Trap:** one material root does not imply one conscious witness.

### Three qualities and transformation

| Quality | Recall | Function |
|---|---|---|
| illumination (sattva) | light / pleasure | disclosure and cognition |
| activity (rajas) | movement / pain | energy and mediation |
| obstruction (tamas) | heaviness / delusion | concealment and resistance |

- Equilibrium is the unmanifest state.
- Same-form transformation changes qualities without differentiated products.
- Different-form transformation creates the manifest sequence.
- **Trap:** activity energises both branches; it produces no separate list.

### Conscious witness and proofs

- Witness is self-luminous, changeless, quality-transcendent and non-productive.
- Consciousness is its essence, not a property that comes and goes.
- Five-proof recall: **Instrument–Other–Preside–Experience–Release**.
- Evaluate cumulatively: the proofs support awareness irreducible to mental function more strongly
  than they prove every attribute of an eternal plural witness.
- **Buddhist pressure:** inactivity looks inefficacious.
- **Nyāya pressure:** moral agency appears to require an agent-self.

### Plurality, empirical self and cognition

- Classical plurality grounds: distinct births/deaths/faculties; non-simultaneous activity;
  different quality-configurations.
- Separate liberation is supplementary.
- **Advaita objection:** many adjuncts do not prove many pure consciousnesses.
- **Residual:** numerically distinct but qualitatively identical witnesses lack a clear principle
  of individuation.
- Empirical self is conditioned witness-appearance, not an additional principle.
- Cognition recall: **mind coordinates → ego appropriates → intellect determines → witness
  illuminates**.
- Thirteen instruments = three inner + five cognitive + five action capacities.

### Twenty-five principles

```text
primordial nature
   -> cosmic/determinative intellect
      -> ego-maker
         -> illumination branch: mind + 5 cognitive + 5 action capacities
         -> obstruction branch: 5 subtle potentials -> 5 gross elements
         -> activity energises both

witness is counted as 25th but never evolved.
```

- Causal classes: **1–7–16–1**.
- Cosmic intellect and determinative intellect are one principle; ego-maker is next.
- Subtle body commonly has eighteen components: intellect + ego + eleven capacities + five subtle
  potentials.
- Gross body dies; subtle stream transmigrates; witness does not travel.

### Causation

- Pre-existence of the effect: effect is latent in its material cause.
- Real transformation: the material cause genuinely becomes the manifest effect.
- Five-reason recall: **Cannot–Select–Not-anything–Power–Continuity**.
- Production is manifestation under conditions, not gross spatial concealment.
- Cause and effect differ by state/configuration while retaining material continuity.
- Nyāya: effect as new whole was previously absent; real causes and powers remain.
- Advaita: empirical change is valid but ultimate change is appearance dependent on Brahman.
- Mango route: seed-specific power + conditions + continuity + real change → reconstruct both
  rivals → reply → residual.

### Contact problem and Śaṃkara

- Sāṃkhya uses non-physical proximity and reflection.
- Magnet illustrates activation but itself exerts force.
- Crystal illustrates apparent attribution but not initiation.
- Four problems: **activation, timing, association, termination**.
- “Chief opponent” means comprehensive rival explanatory system, not merely a compliment.
- Śaṃkara’s pressures: conscious-source scripture, purposiveness, relation/timing, independent
  second ultimate and real transformation.
- Graded verdict: Sāṃkhya preserves realism and changeless awareness; its bridge between the two
  ultimates remains under-explained.

### Suffering, bondage and release

- Three sufferings: internal; other-being/material; cosmic/unseen.
- Bondage = non-discrimination and false appropriation.
- Liberation = discriminative knowledge culminating in isolation.
- Isolation is neither merger, God-union nor physical death.
- Dancer = nature withdraws after disclosure.
- Potter’s wheel = body may continue by prior momentum.
- Strictly, witness was never modified; liberation is the cessation of false attribution in nature.
- Classical non-theism: no required creator or grace; later theistic readings must be qualified.
