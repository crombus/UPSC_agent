### Simple start and core doctrine

Jainism is non-theistic about cosmic creation, not spiritually empty. ✅ The *Tattvārthasūtra* or *Tattvārthādhigama-sūtra*, associated with Umāsvāti in Śvetāmbara usage and Umāsvāmī in Digambara usage, supplies a systematic Sanskrit grammar shared across the major traditions. Shared authority does not erase differences of canon, monastic practice or interpretation.

✅ A conqueror (Jina) has overcome passion and karma. A ford-maker (Tīrthaṅkara) re-establishes the path across saṃsāra; neither creates the universe. Jainism is unorthodox (nāstika) because it rejects Vedic authority, not because it rejects soul, karma, rebirth or liberation. Mahāvīra renewed an existing tradition; do not say he created Jainism from nothing.

✅ **Twenty-four ford-makers in the present cycle.** Jaina tradition recognises twenty-four Tīrthaṅkaras for this world-cycle. Ṛṣabha is named as the first, Pārśva as the twenty-third and Vardhamāna Mahāvīra as the twenty-fourth. This is why Mahāvīra is a renewer rather than a founder: he systematised a line that the tradition already carried. ⚠️ Use the framework, not legendary chronology. Mahāvīra and Pārśva are the historical anchors; precise dates and claims about the earliest ford-makers need source-specific qualification, so name the series and stop there. The two great traditions, Śvetāmbara and Digambara, differ on canon, monastic practice and some doctrinal detail while sharing the central grammar of soul, karma, many-sided reality, vows and liberation.

### Argument, objection and use

```text
beginningless substances + modal change + karmic causation
                       -> creator adds no required explanatory work
                       -> perfected teachers guide self-purification
```

**Presupposition:** ⚠️ moral order need not be legislated by a world-maker; karma can carry consequence on its own.
**Objection:** religion without a creator lacks authority.
**Reply:** authority rests on perfected cognition and exemplary liberation.
**Residual:** this depends on the disputed credibility of omniscience.

**Retrieval cue:** **Text–Twenty-four teachers–No creator–Self-effort.**

### Revision notes — teacher, text and cosmos

1. The Tattvārthasūtra supplies a systematic shared doctrinal grammar.
2. Umāsvāti/Umāsvāmī are the two traditional attribution usages.
3. Shared authority does not erase canon or monastic differences.
4. Jina means conqueror of passion and karmic bondage.
5. A Tīrthaṅkara re-establishes a path rather than creates a universe.
6. Twenty-four teachers are recognised; Mahāvīra renews an existing tradition.
7. Nāstika denotes rejection of Vedic authority, not denial of souls.
8. Perfected cognition grounds authority internally; its credibility remains disputed.

### Lesson-local Mains practice

**Question:** Explain how Jainism combines religious authority with rejection of a creator. (10 marks; ceiling 150 words.)

**Model answer:**

Jainism separates the perfected teacher from the world-maker. A Jina conquers passion and karma; a Tīrthaṅkara discloses the path across saṃsāra without creating the cosmos. The Tattvārthasūtra of Umāsvāti/Umāsvāmī systematises this grammar across the major traditions, although their canons and practices differ.

Beginningless substances and karmic causation explain cosmic and moral continuity without divine allocation. Mahāvīra consequently renews a tradition rather than creates it. Rejecting Vedic authority makes Jainism nāstika, not materialistic: it retains souls, rebirth and release through self-purification.

The objection is that creator-free religion lacks warrant. Jainism appeals to perfected cognition and exemplary liberation. That is an internally coherent source of authority, but it does not independently establish omniscience for a critic who rejects its soul–karma framework.

**Scoring guidance:** Separate teacher, text and causal order; qualify the warrant instead of calling Jainism irreligious.

### Concept-check MCQ 1

Which statement best captures Jain religious authority?

A. Perfected teachers disclose an already-existing path in an uncreated cosmos.
B. Tīrthaṅkaras are liberated teachers who additionally allot each soul's karmic fruit.
C. Authoritative teachers discover the path through Vedic testimony confirmed by their perfected cognition.
D. Acceptance of a shared systematic text makes the two traditions' monastic canons identical.

#### Answer and option-specific explanation

**Correct answer: A**

- **A — Correct:** Jina and Tīrthaṅkara name conquerors of passion who re-establish a beginningless path; nothing is created.
- **B — Incorrect:** Allotting karmic fruit is a creator-function; Jaina karma operates with no allocating agent.
- **C — Incorrect:** Jain authority does not depend on Vedic testimony; nāstika marks rejection of that authority.
- **D — Incorrect:** The text supplies a shared doctrinal grammar inside which canon and practice still differ.

## Lesson 2 — Many-sided reality and permanence-in-change

Progress: 2/12 · Stage: Core · Subtopic: permanence-in-change and pluralist realism

### Visual first

```text
REAL (sat) = ORIGINATION + DECAY + PERSISTENCE
             utpāda       vyaya    dhrauvya
                           |
                     SUBSTANCE (dravya)
                     /              \
             QUALITY (guṇa)      MODE (paryāya)
                           |
       jīva | pudgala | motion medium | rest medium | space | time
```

### Core doctrine

✅ Many-sidedness (anekāntavāda) is ontological: a real object has multiple genuine aspects, so no finite unqualified proposition exhausts it. It is neither subjective whim nor merely tolerance.

✅ Reality possesses origination, decay and persistence together. A gold bracelet becomes a ring: an earlier mode ceases, a new mode arises, and gold persists. There is no same-respect contradiction because continuity belongs to substance and change to mode.

| Substance | Role | Astikāya? | Trap |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Soul (jīva) | conscious knower, agent, bearer of release | Yes | many souls, not one universal self |
| Matter (pudgala) | bodies, atoms, aggregates, karmic matter | Yes | karma is subtle matter |
| Motion medium (dharma-dravya) | standing condition that makes motion possible | Yes | not moral merit; not a push or force |
| Rest medium (adharma-dravya) | standing condition that makes rest possible | Yes | not demerit; not inertia or resistance |
| Space (ākāśa) | gives room to every other substance; itself divided into inhabited space (lokākāśa) and the empty space beyond (alokākāśa) | Yes | accommodation is its defining work; it is not Nyāya’s inferred substratum of sound |
| Time (kāla) | succession and before/after | **No** | sixth dravya but not astikāya |

The five astikāyas possess many space-points (pradeśas); time remains a real sixth substance but lacks extension in that sense. Sectarian presentations of time vary, but **six dravyas/five astikāyas** is the safe core.

### Substance, quality and mode

```text
DRAVYA   — what the thing is            (gold / jīva)          PERSISTS
   |
 GUṆA    — abiding defining quality     (yellowness /          ABIDES
   |                                     consciousness)
PARYĀYA  — passing state or mode        (bracelet-form /       ARISES
                                         this pleasure)        AND CEASES
```

✅ Every substance carries **both** abiding qualities (guṇa) and changing modes (paryāya); the two are not synonyms. A quality is a standing defining characteristic without which the substance would not be that substance. A mode is a state or manifestation in which the substance appears at a time and which it can lose while remaining itself.

**The argument in three steps.** ✅ (1) A substance must have stable features, or it would not be the particular kind of thing it is. (2) It must also undergo modal change, or it could never enter experience as something that happens to us or alters before us. (3) Therefore the only intelligible real is substance-with-qualities-and-modes — which is exactly what origination, decay and persistence describe from the inside.

**Example to write, not just remember.** ✅ Consciousness (cetanā) is a *guṇa* of the soul: a soul without it is not a soul. Pleasure, pain, delusion and liberated omniscience are *paryāyas*: conditions in which that same soul appears and which it can shed. In matter the parallel is exact — colour is a quality of a material substance, while bracelet-form and ring-form are its modes.

| Axis | Guṇa (quality) | Paryāya (mode) |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | co-lasting with the substance | arises and ceases |
| Loss | cannot be lost without losing the substance | lost without loss of substance |
| Jīva instance | consciousness, perception, energy | pleasure, pain, delusion, omniscience |
| Pudgala instance | colour, taste, smell, touch | bracelet-form, ring-form, this warmth |

**Objection (Buddhist nominalist):** why introduce a substance at all? Speak only of changing qualities and the explanatory work is done without an extra enduring item.
**Reply:** ✅ changing qualities still presuppose something whose changes they are. "A change" with no subject of change is not a simpler theory but an incomplete one; the Jaina answer is that the triad of origination, decay and persistence is read off experience itself.
**Residual:** ⚠️ this secures the demand for a subject of change only if one already grants that qualities cannot be free-floating; a thorough-going nominalist denies exactly that premise.

### Many souls, and why they are conscious in their own right

✅ The deepest Jaina division is between the conscious (jīva) and the non-conscious (ajīva). Souls are **many, distinct and beginningless**, each intrinsically endowed with infinite knowledge, perception, bliss and power, all four obscured by karma in the bound condition.

**The positive argument for plurality, not merely a denial of monism.** ✅ (1) Liberation is a meaningful goal only if the soul has a native capacity for perfection to recover. (2) Bondage must therefore be obscuration of that nature, not its creation or destruction. (3) But obscuration is distributed unequally and biographically — this soul is bound in this body with this karmic load, that soul differently. (4) Since each career of bondage and release is traced to its own bearer, and since liberation is attained one soul at a time, the bearers are numerically many. ⚠️ Plurality is thus demanded by the soteriology, not merely asserted against Advaita.

**The Nyāya contrast that earns the mark.** ✅ Nyāya also accepts many eternal selves, so "many souls" alone does not distinguish Jainism from it. The real difference is *how* a self is conscious. For Nyāya the self is not intrinsically conscious: cognition arises in it only through conjunction with the internal organ (manas), so consciousness is an adventitious quality of the self rather than its nature. For Jainism consciousness belongs to the jīva intrinsically; karma obscures it but never confers it. ⚠️ Hence a Jaina liberated soul is omniscient, while a Nyāya liberated self — cut off from mind-contact — is free from suffering without that cognitive fullness.

| Axis | Jaina jīva | Advaita ātman | Nyāya self | Cārvāka |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Number | many, really distinct | ultimately one | many | as many as bodies |
| Consciousness | intrinsic (guṇa) | identical with it | adventitious, via manas-contact | emergent from body |
| Liberated state | omniscient individual siddha | non-dual realisation | freedom from suffering | no such state |

✅ **How living beings are classified.** The ontology is graded, and the grading is what makes Jaina ethics so demanding.

| Class | Term | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| Liberated soul | mukta-jīva | free of every karmic obstruction |
| Bound soul | baddha-jīva | embodied and karmically conditioned |
| Immobile bound being | sthāvara | one-sense life possessing touch only — classically earth-, water-, fire-, air- and plant-bodied beings |
| Mobile bound being | trasa | two-, three-, four- and five-sensed beings |

⚠️ Note the payoff for Lesson 9: once earth-, water- and plant-bodied beings are genuine jīvas, non-violence stops being sentiment and becomes a metaphysical obligation.

### The non-conscious side: ajīva and pudgala

```text
AJĪVA  =  everything without consciousness
   |
   +-- pudgala   — the ONLY dravya with form (rūpa)
   +-- dharma-dravya, adharma-dravya, ākāśa, kāla  — formless
```

✅ **Ajīva** is not a thing but a domain: the whole non-conscious side of reality, covering matter together with the media of motion and rest, space and time. It is not a region inside one Brahman, as in Advaita, and consciousness is not an organisation of it, as in Cārvāka.

✅ **Pudgala** is the material member of that domain and the only substance possessing form (rūpa). Three features define it against jīva, and these are what to write rather than a longer list of examples:

| Feature | Pudgala | Jīva |
|---|---|---|
| Form (rūpa) | has it; bears colour, taste, smell and touch | formless |
| Divisibility | divisible down to the indivisible atom | not divisible into parts of that kind |
| Aggregation | combines into aggregates and separates again | never aggregates with another jīva |

✅ **Material configurations.** Bodies, sense-organs, speech, breath, the mind as an embodied instrument, and karmic matter itself are all configurations of pudgala. This single list does heavy work later: it is why karmic bondage can be a real quasi-physical entanglement rather than a metaphor, and why the Jaina mind-as-instrument is on the non-conscious side while the knower is not.

✅ **Atomic structure.** Jaina atoms (paramāṇus) are the indivisible units of matter and combine into aggregates (skandhas). The positive doctrine is **modal**: matter has one atomic kind, and atoms are **not permanently partitioned into elemental sorts**. There is no earth-atom that must stay an earth-atom, no fixed water-, fire- or air-atom. One and the same atom can take different modes, so elemental differentiation — earthiness, wateriness, fieriness, airiness — belongs to **developed modes and combinations**, not to an unalterable atomic essence. Sound too is a material modification rather than a separate immaterial quality. ⚠️ This is exactly why Jaina matter is transformable in a way Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika's four permanently distinct atom-kinds are not: do not import those fixed kinds into a Jaina answer, and do not state the point only negatively when the positive modal thesis is what earns the mark.

**Objection (Nyāya/Mīmāṃsā-style):** calling karma "matter" is strange; moral consequence is not the sort of thing that has colour or weight.
**Reply:** ✅ the strangeness is the originality. Because pudgala is form-bearing, divisible and aggregative, subtle configurations of it can really adhere to an embodied soul — so bondage is an entanglement with consequences, not a bookkeeping metaphor.
**Residual:** ⚠️ the reply explains what Jainism gains; it does not show a rival that subtle karmic matter exists.

### Motion and rest media under the parsimony objection

```text
FISH IN WATER                          MOVER IN DHARMA-DRAVYA
the fish supplies the swimming         the mover supplies the movement
water neither pushes nor steers it     the medium neither pushes nor steers
remove the water -> no swimming        remove the medium -> no motion at all
```

✅ The medium of motion (dharma-dravya) and the medium of rest (adharma-dravya) are **enabling conditions**, not agents. A fish swims by its own effort; water neither starts, directs nor sustains that effort, yet without water swimming is impossible. Dharma-dravya is the cosmic analogue of the water, never of the fish’s muscles. Adharma-dravya correspondingly makes resting possible without itself stopping anything.

| Mistaken reading | Why it fails | Correct reading |
|---|---|---|
| A push, pull, force or impulse | The media never act, supply no energy, and fix no direction, speed or duration | A standing condition under which motion or rest is possible at all |
| Moral merit (dharma) and demerit (adharma) | The ethical senses of these two words belong to karma theory and the nine categories, not to the substance list | Two of the six *dravyas*, carrying no moral content whatever |

**Objection (Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika parsimony):** the two media are explanatorily idle. A particular motion is already fully explained by a mover, its effort or impulse, and contact with other bodies; rest is explained by the cessation or absence of such effort. To posit two further eternal, imperceptible, all-pervading substances whose only office is to “permit” what is already accounted for multiplies entities without explanatory gain — and because they never act, no observation could distinguish a cosmos with them from one without them.

**Reply:** ⚠️ the objection assumes that only efficient causes earn ontological keep. Jainism separates the **agent-cause** of this particular motion from the **general condition** of there being motion or rest anywhere. Dharma and adharma discharge only the second office, and that office is not redundant: both media are exactly co-extensive with inhabited space, so motion and rest occur only within it. That is what gives the Jaina cosmos a determinate boundary instead of an arbitrary one, and why the liberated soul rises to the summit of the inhabited universe and stops there rather than travelling endlessly outward.

**Residual:** ⚠️ the reply shows the media are not idle *inside* Jaina cosmology; it does not independently establish them against a rival who denies a bounded cosmos and holds that space alone, plus movers, is sufficient.

### Inhabited space and the space beyond

```text
            A L O K Ā K Ā Ś A   —  empty space beyond
            ākāśa and nothing else: no jīva, no pudgala,
            no dharma-dravya, no adharma-dravya, no kāla
   +-------------------------------------------------------+
   |  L O K Ā K Ā Ś A  —  inhabited / cosmic space          |
   |  the other five substances are found only here         |
   |  motion and rest media exactly co-extensive with it    |
   |  therefore the outer limit of all movement and rest    |
   +-------------------------------------------------------+
```

✅ Space (ākāśa) is one substance with two divisions. **Lokākāśa** is inhabited cosmic space: souls, matter, the medium of motion, the medium of rest and time exist only here, and the two media are co-extensive with it. **Alokākāśa** is the limitless space beyond, which contains nothing but space itself. ⚠️ So loka and aloka are not two further things *accommodated by* space; they are the occupied and unoccupied regions *of* space. Write “space divided into lokākāśa and alokākāśa,” never “space accommodates loka and aloka.”

**Why Jaina accommodating space is not Nyāya’s sound-substratum.** ✅ For Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika, ākāśa is reached by inference: sound is a quality, a quality must inhere in a substance, and ākāśa is posited as the one eternal all-pervading substratum in which sound inheres. Spatial room and direction are handled by a *different* Nyāya substance, dik. For Jainism, ākāśa’s defining work is **accommodation** — giving room to substances — and sound needs no immaterial substratum at all, because sound is a modification of matter (pudgala). The two schools reach similarly named substances by different arguments and assign them different jobs.

| Axis | Jaina ākāśa | Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika ākāśa |
|---|---|---|
| Defining function | accommodating the other substances | bearing sound as its quality |
| Route of establishment | required by an occupied, bounded cosmos | inferred from sound needing a substance |
| Status of sound | material modification of pudgala | non-material quality inhering in ākāśa |
| Spatial room and direction | the same ākāśa, as lokākāśa | assigned to a separate substance, dik |
| Internal divisions | inhabited lokākāśa and empty alokākāśa | one partless all-pervading substance; no such division |

⚠️ This matters directly for the Cārvāka comparison: a Cārvāka who defeats the sound-to-substratum inference has not thereby touched the Jaina argument, which never ran through sound. Do not recycle one critique against both schools.

### Causation: the same stuff in a new shape

```text
WHOLLY IDENTICAL?              WHOLLY NEW?
production adds nothing        anything could arise from anything
        \                             /
         \___ JAINA ANSWER __________/
    effect = CONTINUOUS as dravya + NOVEL as paryāya
              (qualified sadasatkāryavāda)
```

✅ Jaina causation says the effect both exists and does not exist in its cause: it exists in respect of the enduring substance and does not yet exist in respect of its new mode. The qualification is not decoration — it is the whole doctrine.

**The argument, in the order an examiner wants it.** ✅ (1) If the effect were wholly identical with its cause, production would add nothing and the word "effect" would be empty. (2) If it were wholly non-existent and unrelated to its cause, then anything could arise from anything, and a jar would come as readily from milk as from clay. (3) But in every observed transformation the underlying substance persists while an earlier mode ceases and a new mode originates. (4) Therefore the effect is continuous with the cause as substance and genuinely novel as mode.

**Example.** ✅ A clay jar is not produced from sheer nothing: clay persists through the potting. Yet the jar-form is genuinely new and was not previously present as that completed mode. Say both halves; writing only "clay persists" collapses Jainism into Sāṃkhya, and writing only "the jar is new" collapses it into Nyāya.

| School | Thesis | Jaina verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Sāṃkhya (satkāryavāda) | the effect pre-exists in the cause | right about continuity, wrong to deny real novelty |
| Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika (asatkāryavāda) | the effect is newly produced | right about novelty, wrong to deny substantial continuity |
| Jainism (sadasatkāryavāda) | continuous as dravya, novel as paryāya | each rival absolutises one true aspect |

⚠️ This is many-sidedness doing causal work: each rival school has hold of a real aspect and errs only by treating it as the whole.

**Objection:** saying the effect both exists and does not exist is a plain contradiction.
**Reply:** ✅ existence and non-existence are indexed to *different respects* — substance and mode — so no unqualified contradiction is asserted. The indexing is the same device that licenses "it is and is not" in Lesson 5.
**Residual:** ⚠️ the defence stands or falls with the reality of the substance–mode distinction; a critic who treats dravya and paryāya as two descriptions of one undivided fact will read the formula as evasion rather than precision.

### The body-sized enduring soul under fire

```text
JAINA CLAIM: jīva is permanent AND has pradeśas co-extensive with its body
      |
      +-- Buddhist (Dharmakīrti-style): if it changes it is not permanent;
      |   if it never changes it cannot act          -> arthakriyā dilemma
      |
      +-- Nyāya: an eternal self cannot literally expand and contract,
          and parts threaten eternality               -> partless-self demand
```

✅ The lamp image states the claim: an embodied soul has space-points (pradeśas) and is co-extensive with its body, as a lamp's light fills exactly the room it occupies. The ant's soul fills an ant-body and the elephant's soul an elephant-body without either becoming matter. This explains why awareness is felt throughout the body rather than at one point — but an unchallenged analogy earns almost nothing, so learn it together with the two classical attacks it invites.

**Attack 1 — the Buddhist causal-efficacy dilemma (Dharmakīrti-style).** ✅ (1) If the soul expands and contracts with the body, it undergoes real change. (2) What really changes is not absolutely permanent. (3) Press it as a dilemma about causal efficacy (arthakriyā): a thing is real only if it can do something; but if the soul acts, it is altered by acting, and if it is incapable of alteration, it cannot act at all. Either way the permanent-yet-active jīva is incoherent.

**Jaina reply.** ✅ The dilemma assumes that one and the same real must be permanent or changing in the *same* respect. Jainism denies the assumption with the triad already taught: origination, cessation and persistence are simultaneously real features of every existent. The jīva is permanent **as dravya** and changing **as paryāya**; acting is a modal event in a persisting substance, so efficacy costs it nothing.
**Residual.** ⚠️ The Buddhist objection keeps its full force against anyone who rejects a real substance–mode distinction. Jainism does not refute momentariness here; it shows that momentariness is not forced on us once that distinction is granted.

**Attack 2 — the Nyāya objection to a self that changes size.** ✅ (1) An eternal self should not literally expand and contract, since variable magnitude is the mark of composite bodies. (2) If the self has parts or space-points at all, its eternality is threatened, because what has parts can in principle come apart. (3) Nyāya adds a parsimony charge: an unseen moral potency (adṛṣṭa) explains moral consequence more cheaply than material karma does.

**Jaina reply.** ✅ Extension is not the same as gross material magnitude. A conscious substance can possess pradeśas and pervade its body without becoming pudgala, exactly as lamp-light fills a room without being a wall of the room. The pradeśas are not detachable fragments, so no dismemberment follows; and karmic pudgala explains concrete embodiment — this body, these senses, this lifespan — which an invisible potency only labels.
**Residual.** ⚠️ The reply is internally coherent within Jaina pluralism, but it names a special category rather than proving it. How a pradeśa-bearing conscious substance and matter interact remains the standing external burden, and it reappears in Lessons 7 and 10.

⚠️ Write both attacks when a stem touches the Jaina soul: they come from opposite directions — Buddhism says the jīva changes too much to be permanent, Nyāya says it cannot change at all — and the single substance/mode answer is what lets Jainism face both at once.

### Why pluralistic and realistic?

```text
PLURALISTIC = many irreducible substances and many jīvas
REALISTIC   = substances, qualities and modes exist independently of viewpoints
QUALIFIED   = a standpoint selects a real aspect; it does not manufacture it
```

**Presupposition:** ⚠️ identity and transformation are equally real; neither is a convenient fiction laid over the other.
**Objection:** permanence and change cannot belong to one thing.
**Reply:** they belong in different respects—dravya and paryāya.
**Residual:** the reply depends on accepting a real substance–mode distinction.

**Retrieval cue:** **Many–Six–Five–Guṇa/paryāya–Many conscious jīvas–Form-bearing pudgala–Media-not-forces–Loka/aloka–Continuous-yet-novel effect–Lamp under two attacks–Objective.**

### Revision notes — what persists and what changes

1. Sat combines origination, decay and persistence.
2. Substance persists; abiding qualities belong to it; modes arise and cease.
3. Many-sidedness concerns objective aspects, not subjective opinions.
4. Six dravyas explain consciousness, matter, motion, rest, accommodation and succession.
5. Five astikāyas exclude time: substantiality is not spatial extension.
6. Motion/rest media enable rather than push or halt.
7. Lokākāśa is occupied; alokākāśa is space beyond, not another accommodating substance.
8. Consciousness is intrinsic to each Jaina soul, unlike Nyāya's adventitious cognition.
9. Pudgala has form and aggregation; atoms have one kind with differentiated modes.
10. The effect is continuous as substance and novel as mode.
11. The lamp illustrates body-wide soul-presence, not material composition.
12. Substance–mode and pradeśa replies leave causal-efficacy and spatiality objections open.

### Lesson-local Mains practice

**Question:** Explain permanence-in-change through Jaina causation. (10 marks; ceiling 150 words.)

**Model answer:**

Jainism defines reality through origination, decay and persistence together. A substance endures through qualities while modes change. When clay becomes a jar, clay persists, the earlier configuration ceases and jar-form originates.

This makes the effect continuous with its cause as substance but novel as mode. Complete identity would make production empty; complete novelty would leave no reason why a particular effect requires a particular cause. The qualified account mediates Sāṃkhya's emphasis on pre-existence and Nyāya's emphasis on new production.

The objection is that existence and non-existence are contradictory. Jainism indexes them to substance and mode respectively, preserving both continuity and transformation. Its strength is explanatory balance; its residual burden is establishing that the substance–mode distinction belongs to reality rather than merely redescribing change.

**Scoring guidance:** Make clay and jar carry both causal arguments before naming either rival.


### Visual first

```text
MEDIATED (parokṣa)                    STRICTLY DIRECT (pratyakṣa)
mati: sense/mind                      avadhi: limited material objects
śruta: verbal/sign                    manaḥparyāya: another mind
inference/testimony/memory/            kevala: unlimited omniscience
recognition/tarka
                 \___________________/
finite conditioned cognition -> partial truth -> standpoint (naya)
```

### Core doctrine

✅ Strict direct knowledge is soul-unmediated. Ordinary sense perception is only conventionally direct — the tradition's own term is **sāṃvyavahārika pratyakṣa**, empirical or conventional direct knowledge — and is strictly mediated by senses, mind and karma. ⚠️ The presupposition to state once and then rely on: **directness is measured by freedom from mediation and karmic obstruction, not by sensory vividness.** Without it the whole fivefold scheme looks arbitrary. ⚠️ Never render the term as "ordinary perception is invalid": it is valid, and merely conventionally rather than strictly direct.

| Knowledge | Status | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Mati | mediated | sense-and-mind cognition |
| Śruta | mediated | verbal/sign cognition; not blind faith |
| Avadhi | direct, limited | extraordinary cognition of material objects |
| Manaḥparyāya | direct, limited | cognition of another’s mental modes |
| Kevala | direct, unlimited | omniscience after obstruction removal |

### Developed Jaina logic: memory and recognition as valid cognition

✅ Later Jaina logicians — Akalaṅka, Māṇikyanandi, Hemacandra and Yaśovijaya are the citable names — enlarge mediated (parokṣa) knowledge beyond mati and śruta. Inference (anumāna), testimony (śabda), recollection (smṛti), recognition (pratyabhijñā) and the reasoning that grasps pervasion (tarka/ūha) are all admitted as valid indirect cognition in their proper contexts. Testimony here is reliable verbal authority, not a creator-God’s revelation.

✅ The contrast that must be stated explicitly: **Nyāya does not treat memory (smṛti) as an independent pramāṇa.** Its list is perception, inference, comparison and testimony; recollection is deliberately excluded from it.

**Nyāya’s reproduction objection:** a means of knowledge must yield a *novel* true cognition. Memory only reproduces what an earlier cognition already delivered — it makes no fresh contact with the object, adds no new content, and inherits whatever warrant the original cognition had. Counting it separately would double-count the original pramāṇa, so memory is at best psychologically useful and not epistemically a pramāṇa.

**Jaina reply:** ✅ recollection and recognition are valid cognition when they successfully connect present awareness with a real object or with a past experience. ⚠️ Recognition in particular is not bare replay: “this is the same teacher I met earlier” fuses a present perception with a retained one and delivers a judgement of identity through time that neither cognition yields alone. Because Jaina reality is substance persisting through changing modes, a cognitive source that tracks that persistence is doing genuine work rather than repeating work already done. Validity, on this view, is measured by successful reach to a real object, not by novelty of content.

**Residual:** ⚠️ Nyāya can still answer that the identity-judgement is an inference or a qualified perception rather than a distinct means; the dispute finally turns on how finely pramāṇas should be individuated.

| Axis | Jaina position | Nyāya position |
|---|---|---|
| Memory (smṛti) | valid indirect cognition when it reaches a real past object | not an independent pramāṇa; reproductive only |
| Recognition (pratyabhijñā) | valid; links present and retained cognition | absorbed into perception or inference |
| Test of validity | successful connection with a real object or past experience | production of a novel true cognition |

⚠️ In a 2023-style stem this block does double duty: it shows that Jaina “indirect” knowledge is a graded, disciplined category rather than a residue, and it supplies a precise named contrast instead of a vague claim that Jainism “accepts more pramāṇas.”

### Wrong cognition: when content is right but direction is wrong

```text
SAME CONTENT, TWO ORIENTATIONS
mati    --(under right vision)-->  mati-jñāna      (knowledge)
mati    --(under mithyā-darśana)-> kumati          (wrong cognition)
śruta   --(under mithyā-darśana)-> kuśruta
avadhi  --(under mithyā-darśana)-> vibhaṅga        (perverted avadhi)
```

✅ Jainism recognises three wrong cognitions: **kumati** (mis-oriented sense-and-mind cognition), **kuśruta** (mis-oriented verbal or scriptural cognition) and **vibhaṅga-jñāna**, which is specifically **avadhi gone perverse** — extraordinary direct cognition of material objects held under a deluded orientation. Note the asymmetry worth writing: the list perverts the first three knowledges only. There is no perverted manaḥparyāya and no perverted kevala, because those arise at purities incompatible with wrong vision.

✅ The governing condition is **mithyā-darśana**, false vision or wrong orientation. Wrongness here is not simply absence or mismatch of content; a cognition becomes spiritually wrong when it is held under delusion. ⚠️ Say this explicitly: a cognition may be materially accurate and still count as wrong cognition, because correctness of content and liberating orientation are two different axes. These are therefore not ordinary falsehoods, and translating them as "false knowledge" alone loses the point.

**Example.** ✅ A learned person quotes doctrine accurately but deploys it for pride, sectarian contempt or violence. Nothing in the propositional content need be mistaken; the verbal cognition nevertheless functions as kuśruta, because its governing orientation is deluded.

**Objection (Nyāya):** calling a cognition wrong because of its spiritual orientation confuses truth with moral psychology. Truth is a relation between cognition and object; the knower's character is a separate question.
**Reply:** ✅ Jainism answers that bondage conditions the knower as a whole, so cognition is never a free-standing relation detachable from the karmic state of the one who knows. For a system whose entire aim is release, epistemic correctness without right vision is an incomplete achievement, and the vocabulary should record that.
**Residual:** ⚠️ the reply defends the Jaina use of the word within a soteriological framework; it does not show that a purely alethic notion of truth is defective, so Nyāya may simply keep two vocabularies where Jainism keeps one.

### The bridge and the 2023 trap

```text
many real aspects in the object
        +
karmically limited finite knower
        -> an ordinary judgement grasps one aspect
        -> partial truth under a standpoint
        -> error only when partial truth is absolutised
```

“All human knowledge is empirical” is too strong: avadhi, manaḥparyāya and an embodied kevalin are counterexamples. “Therefore relative” is also incomplete: partiality follows from object-many-sidedness plus cognitive limitation, not empiricism alone. Avadhi is direct yet limited, proving that directness and completeness differ.

✅ Both halves of the bridge have a technical name worth one clause each. The object side is **ananta-dharmātmakaṃ vastu** — the real is constituted by endlessly many aspects, properties and modes. The knower side is **kṣāyopaśamika** cognition: ordinary knowing arises from a partial destruction-cum-subsidence of knowledge-obscuring karma, and is therefore *necessarily* partial rather than accidentally so. ⚠️ That second term is the one that does the work. It explains why Jaina partiality is a structural consequence of bondage and not a complaint about human carelessness, which is exactly why removing the obstruction yields kevala rather than merely better evidence.

**Objection:** extraordinary direct knowledge and omniscience are unverifiable.
**Reply:** internally they follow from graded removal of karmic obstruction.
**Residual:** a critic who rejects soul and material karma remains unconvinced.

### Partial truth, and the exact point where it turns into error

```text
PARTIAL TRUTH                               FALSEHOOD
"the jar exists"                            "the jar exists — full stop,
 scoped to this substance,          -->      everywhere, always, in every mode"
 place, time and mode                        the scope is dropped and the
 TRUE as far as it reaches                   part is passed off as the whole
                                             = EKĀNTA (one-sidedness)
```

✅ This is the hinge of the whole system, so state it as a claim rather than leaving it implied. A standpoint (naya) is aspect-limited cognition, and **a partial judgement is not thereby a false one**. It becomes false at one identifiable moment: when the partial is absolutised — when a claim true under a specified respect is advanced as the unconditioned and exhaustive truth about the object. Jainism names that move **ekānta** (one-sidedness, ekāntavāda), and on the Jaina diagnosis it is the single recurring error of the rival schools: each has hold of something real and converts it into the whole.

✅ "The jar exists" is true with respect to its present substance, place, time and mode; the same sentence is false with respect to another place, another time or an incompatible mode. Nothing changed in the object or in the evidence — only the scope of the assertion did. ⚠️ So the opposite of partial truth is not total truth but **unscoped** truth, and that is why the remedy in Lesson 5 is a scope-marker rather than more information.

**The blind men and the elephant.** ✅ One man touches the trunk and says "the elephant is like a serpent"; another touches the leg and says "like a pillar"; another the ear and says "like a winnowing fan". Each report answers to something really there, so each is **conditionally right and absolutely wrong**. The parable dramatises the difference between partial correctness and exclusive completeness. ⚠️ Two guards must travel with it. First, it is pedagogical, not probative: it illustrates the structure of partial truth and never establishes the formal doctrine, so it may open an answer but can never carry it. Second, the men are blind and the elephant is one — the Jaina point is not that reality is unknowable but that finite access is sectional while the object is whole.

**Objection:** if partial judgements are true, Jainism cannot call any rival wrong.
**Reply:** ✅ it calls the *absolutising* wrong, not the content; the rival's positive insight is preserved and only its claim to exhaustiveness is refused.
**Residual:** ⚠️ this requires that every rival thesis really is a scoped truth. A thesis that is simply false — not partial — has no standpoint to be rescued into, and Jainism owes an account of that case.

**Retrieval cue:** **Inversion–Five knowledges–Memory split–Wrong-orientation trio–Two causes of partiality–Partial-not-false–Ekānta–Naya.**

### Revision notes — direct does not mean complete

1. Strict directness means freedom from mediation, not sensory vividness.
2. Mati and śruta are mediated; ordinary perception is conventionally direct.
3. Avadhi reaches material objects; manaḥparyāya reaches another's mental modes.
4. Kevala is unmediated omniscience following obstruction removal.
5. Developed Jaina logic admits recollection and recognition; Nyāya excludes memory as reproductive.
6. Kumati, kuśruta and vibhaṅga are wrong-oriented mati, śruta and avadhi.
7. Object-many-sidedness and karmic limitation jointly explain finite partiality.
8. A partial claim can be true; absolutisation into ekānta is the error.

### Lesson-local Mains practice

**Question:** Why does Jaina direct knowledge not always constitute omniscience? (10 marks; ceiling 150 words.)

**Model answer:**

Jainism separates directness from completeness. Strict direct knowledge is soul-unmediated, whereas sense-and-mind cognition and verbal cognition are mediated. Ordinary perception remains valid but only conventionally direct.

Limited direct cognition of material objects, avadhi, and cognition of another's mental modes, manaḥparyāya, show that freedom from sensory mediation does not mean unlimited scope. Only kevala manifests comprehensive cognition after obscuring karma is destroyed.

Finite partiality follows from both a many-sided object and karmic limitation in the knower. A standpoint can therefore disclose a real aspect without exhausting its object. The objection that higher knowledge lacks independent verification remains: graded karmic purification explains the hierarchy internally, but does not compel a critic who rejects soul and material karma.

**Scoring guidance:** Use avadhi as the decisive counterexample and distinguish validity, directness and completeness.

|---|---|---|
| Naigama | common/purposive, unspecialised view | “I am cooking” while collecting ingredients |

| Śabda | linguistic usage | not verbal knowledge (śruta-jñāna) |
| Samabhirūḍha | etymologically differentiated word-sense | distinguishes near-synonyms |

### Argument and distinction

Reality has many aspects; finite judgement selects a practical, generic, temporal or linguistic axis. Classification makes the selection visible and preserves partial truth. Dogmatism begins when scope is forgotten.

### The three verbal standpoints, and the charge of scholasticism

✅ The last three nayas — śabda, samabhirūḍha, evambhūta — form a graded series of increasing strictness about *words*, and they are where the taxonomy earns its sharpest objection.

```text
LOOSER  ------------------------------------------------>  STRICTER
śabda                 samabhirūḍha               evambhūta
attends to usage      splits near-synonyms       name applies only
TOLERATES ordinary    that looser usage          while the relevant
speech as it stands   merges, by their roots     function is performed
```

✅ Mark the gradation, because the contrast is examinable. **Śabda-naya tolerates ordinary usage**: it notices that expression conditions what is asserted, but it does not refuse a word that common speech accepts. **Samabhirūḍha** withdraws that tolerance one step, driving a wedge between near-synonyms that looser usage merges, on the ground that distinct roots mark distinct senses. **Evambhūta** withdraws it entirely: the term applies only in the actuality of the relevant function.

**Example.** ✅ Calling a person "a singer" is unobjectionable for śabda-naya and may be true generically or dispositionally; evambhūta naya is stricter and applies the name only while the singing is going on.

**Worked etymological pair.** ✅ “Powerful one” (Śakra, from *śak*, to be able) and “destroyer of fortresses” (Purandara, *pura*, fortress, plus the breaking/destroying sense) are traditional names of Indra. Ordinary verbal usage can use either to identify the same bearer. The etymological standpoint (samabhirūḍha) separates their senses: being powerful is not the same attribute as destroying fortresses, even when one individual bears both names. The actuality-conditioned standpoint (evambhūta) tightens the second name further: “Purandara” applies while that function is performed. ⚠️ This is an illustration of a traditional word analysis, not proof of the mythology or a claim that synonyms must refer to different beings.

**Presupposition:** ⚠️ language itself contributes to one-sidedness if its scope is not critically examined. Without that premise the verbal nayas would be mere lexicography rather than standpoint-theory.

**Objection:** ✅ the linguistic nayas seem overly scholastic — splitting synonyms and restricting names to moments of performance looks like grammar inflated into metaphysics.
**Reply:** ⚠️ they matter because **many philosophical disputes are verbal absolutisations of context-bound usage**: a term is fixed by one context, carried unmarked into another, and the resulting clash is then treated as a substantive disagreement about the world. The verbal nayas are the diagnostic tool for exactly that failure, which is why the taxonomy ends on the word-side rather than beginning there.
**Residual:** ⚠️ the defence shows the distinctions are useful, not that precisely three verbal standpoints are required; the tradition's number is not independently derived.

```text
NAYA = viewpoint / route of access
BHAṄGA = qualified propositional form
```

The distinction controls the compressed 2021 wording. Explain the naya–judgement connection, but never answer a sevenfold-judgement question with only the seven named nayas.

**Presupposition:** ⚠️ partial truth need not be falsehood, which is what makes a taxonomy of standpoints worth having at all.
**Objection:** standpoints proliferate and weaken truth.
**Reply:** classification disciplines recurring sources of selectivity.
**Residual:** it does not prove that every rival thesis can be harmonised once scope is fixed.

**Retrieval cue:** **Purpose–Class–Particular–Present–Usage (tolerant)–Etymology–Function.**

### Revision notes — seven routes of access

1. Naigama attends to purpose, as cooking while collecting ingredients.
2. Saṃgraha stresses class-character; vyavahāra distinguishes particulars.
3. Ṛjusūtra attends to the present mode without abolishing substance.
4. Śabda attends to linguistic usage, not scripture as śruta-jñāna.
5. Samabhirūḍha separates near-synonyms by their distinct etymological senses.
6. Śakra denotes power; Purandara denotes fortress-destruction, though both name Indra.
7. Evambhūta restricts a name to the actuality of its function.
8. A naya selects an aspect; a bhaṅga articulates qualified judgement.

### Lesson-local Mains practice

**Question:** Distinguish the three verbal standpoints with a worked example. (10 marks; ceiling 150 words.)

**Model answer:**

The verbal standpoints progressively tighten linguistic scope. Śabda attends to accepted expression and can treat synonyms as identifying one bearer. Samabhirūḍha distinguishes their etymological senses. Evambhūta requires the relevant function actually to occur.

Śakra, “powerful one”, and Purandara, “destroyer of fortresses”, traditionally name Indra. Ordinary usage can identify him through either name. Etymological precision separates power from fortress-destruction without multiplying the bearer. Actuality-conditioned usage applies Purandara when that function is performed.

The objection is scholastic word-splitting. Jainism replies that moving terms between unmarked contexts can manufacture philosophical disagreement. These distinctions discipline such shifts, though their usefulness does not independently establish why exactly three verbal standpoints are necessary. A standpoint remains a route of cognition, not one of the seven propositional forms.

**Scoring guidance:** Show the same bearer, two senses and a function-now restriction; do not merely list labels.

## Lesson 5 — Conditional assertion and all seven judgements

Progress: 5/12 · Stage: Core · Subtopic: seven qualified judgements on one jar

```text
━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: not available in this checkout; canonical Markdown queried.
CA search: not run — the jar tests predication without importing current facts.
CA found: not assessed; no recent-news assertion is made.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

### Visual first

```text
many-sided object -> finite standpoint -> syāt (“in some respect”)
                  -> seven judgement-forms -> non-absolutism
```

✅ Syāt is a definite scope-marker, not “perhaps,” “probably,” or uncertainty. It indexes substance, place, time and mode (dravya, kṣetra, kāla, bhāva).

### Why exactly seven, and not six or eight

✅ The number is not a tradition-bound list to be memorised. It is **derived**, and the derivation is worth one line in any answer on the topic. There are exactly **three primitive predications**, each taken under *syāt*:

> 🔑 **asti** (it is) · **nāsti** (it is not) · **avaktavya** (it is not expressible in one unqualified utterance)

```text
THREE PRIMITIVES -> SEVEN FORMS

taken SINGLY   (3 of them)        -> 1. asti        2. nāsti       4. avaktavya
taken in PAIRS (3 pairs)          -> 3. asti+nāsti  5. asti+avak.  6. nāsti+avak.
taken ALL TOGETHER (1 triple)     -> 7. asti+nāsti+avaktavya
                                     ---------------------------
                                     3 + 3 + 1 = 7, and the list closes
```

⚠️ **Do not count four elements.** The third form, "it is and it is not," is tempting to treat as a fourth primitive alongside affirmation, negation and inexpressibility — but it is already the *first pair*. Counting it twice makes the arithmetic fail: four primitives would give four singles, six pairs, four triples and one quadruple, which is not seven and would need an arbitrary pruning rule to become seven. With three primitives the schema is exhaustive and closed on its own, which is exactly why the tradition stops at seven.

### One clay jar through all seven steps

| No. | Judgement | Application | Not this |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | syād asti | it exists here/now/as this jar | existence everywhere/always |
| 2 | syād nāsti | it is not elsewhere or before formation | nihilism |
| 3 | syād asti ca nāsti ca | affirmation and negation successively across distinct respects | same-respect contradiction |
| 4 | syād avaktavyam | opposed qualified aspects cannot be stated by one simultaneous unqualified predicate | uncertainty or mystical silence |
| 5 | syād asti ca avaktavyam | this clay jar exists here now in jar-mode; its here-now existence and pre-production absence resist one simultaneous unqualified predicate | a third object |
| 6 | syād nāsti ca avaktavyam | this same jar did not exist in jar-mode before production; that absence and its here-now existence resist the same simultaneous unqualified predicate | absolute non-being |
| 7 | syād asti ca nāsti ca avaktavyam | this jar exists here now and did not exist in jar-mode before production, stated successively; both opposed aspects resist one simultaneous unqualified predicate | seven independent truth-values |

Inexpressibility (avaktavya) is not a free third truth-value. It marks the limit of one **simultaneous, unqualified** utterance. Mere transition or observer uncertainty is insufficient.

✅ Keep the respects visible in the last three forms. Form five takes the jar's **present affirmation** together with the expressive limit; form six takes its **pre-production negation** together with that limit; form seven takes **both, successively indexed**, together with the limit. The same jar is never replaced, and none of these combines existence and non-existence under an identical index. A qualified compound sentence can explain the aspects; what fails is their compression into one simultaneous unqualified assertion.

### Reality reflected in judgement

```text
substance persists while modes differ
 -> one object supports distinct true predications
 -> finite judgement selects a respect
 -> syāt records the selection
 -> seven forms organise affirmation, negation and expressive limit
```

This is the complete 2019 route — many-sidedness (anekāntavāda) → standpoint (naya) → scope-marker (syāt) → the seven forms — so do not present unrelated inventories of ontology and logic. For 2021, give the seven bhaṅgas and their naya basis. For 2026, keep one jar and apply every step.

**Presupposition:** ⚠️ predicates can be indexed to a respect; if they cannot, the whole scheme reduces to contradiction or evasion.
**Objection:** “it is and is not” violates non-contradiction.
**Reply:** contradiction occurs only in the same respect, time and sense; syāt blocks that reading.
**Residual:** the universal demand for qualification may itself face a reflexivity challenge.

**Retrieval cue:** **Three primitives–three singles–three pairs–one triple–seven, closed.**

### Revision notes — one jar, visible respects

1. Syāt means “in some respect”, not “perhaps”.
2. Substance, place, time and mode supply the indices.
3. The jar exists here now and did not exist in jar-mode before production.
4. Form three states these two indexed aspects successively.
5. Form four marks their resistance to one simultaneous unqualified predicate.
6. Forms five and six combine present affirmation or prior negation with that limit.
7. Form seven combines both indexed aspects with the same expressive limit.
8. Three primitives yield three singles, three pairs and one triple, not seven truth-values.

### Lesson-local Mains practice

**Question:** Apply forms five, six and seven to the same clay jar. (10 marks; ceiling 150 words.)

**Model answer:**

Fix one clay jar: it exists here now but did not exist in jar-mode before production. These are different time-respects, not contradictory claims under one index.

Form five, syād asti ca avaktavyam, combines its here-now existence with the inability to express the opposed aspects by one simultaneous unqualified predicate. Form six, syād nāsti ca avaktavyam, combines its pre-production absence with that same expressive limit. Form seven, syād asti ca nāsti ca avaktavyam, affirms here-now existence and negates pre-production jar-existence successively, adding the limit on expressing both unqualified at once.

Syāt records definite scope. Avaktavya does not mean uncertainty or that qualified explanation is impossible. The forms thus discipline finite assertion without permitting F and not-F in the same respect; they do not themselves prove the underlying aspect ontology.

### Visual first

```text
   asserted absolutely? -> it exempts itself   "things can be seen
```

### The dilemma and reply

The 2022 challenge is reflexive. If the rule is unconditionally true, it exempts itself; if only conditional, a rival may deny its authority. Further, every qualification presupposes stable facts about object, place, time and mode.

✅ Śaṃkara's critique has a **third limb** that answers are apt to drop, and it is practical rather than logical. If everything is "somehow yes and somehow no," then **practical action loses clear guidance**: deliberation requires determinate judgements about what is the case, and a discourse in which every assertion arrives pre-qualified seems unable to deliver them. ⚠️ Write this limb separately from the contradiction charge, because it survives even if the Jaina indexing reply succeeds: one can grant that no contradiction occurs in the same respect and still ask what the agent is supposed to *do*.
**Reply:** ✅ the objection mistakes qualification for indeterminacy. *Syāt* names the respect rather than withholding the verdict, so "the jar exists here and now as this jar" is fully determinate for any agent acting here and now; what it refuses is the further unscoped claim that the jar exists everywhere and always. Action needs a determinate judgement under the respect in which one acts, which is exactly what the scheme supplies.
**Residual:** ⚠️ this works where the relevant respect is obvious. Where rival respects are both in play and the agent must choose between them, the doctrine disciplines the description without settling the choice.

Jainism need not deny these stable facts. ✅ Relativity concerns finite access, not the disappearance of truth. ✅ Syādvāda can apply to itself. ⚠️ Its strongest interpretation is regulative: no finite claim should masquerade as an exhaustive whole. Omniscience (kevala) makes complete cognition possible without making ordinary conditional claims false, but it does not by itself prove a finite speaker’s universal rule.

### The second horn: is it instead merely trivial?

✅ The classical objection comes with **two blades**. The first is self-refutation, just given. The second runs in the opposite direction: suppose the doctrine survives reflexive qualification; then it seems to say no more than that things can be looked at in different ways — a platitude every school already concedes. Either the doctrine is strong enough to refute itself or weak enough to be banal.

```text
IF UNQUALIFIED -> violates its own rule        } self-refuting
IF QUALIFIED   -> may lose universal force     }
IF READ MILDLY -> "look at things differently" } trivial
```

**Reply to the triviality blade.** ✅ Three moves, in order.

1. The thesis is **not** that all statements are equally true. It is that truth is **aspect-conditioned**: a claim is true under a determinate respect and false outside it, which is a substantive and falsifiable discipline, not a licence.
2. Its bite lies in **reconciling plurality with realism**. Ordinary tolerance says we see differently; anekāntavāda says the object really has the aspects we severally reach. That is a metaphysical commitment with costs, and a tolerance-platitude carries none.
3. It yields **determinate machinery** — the seven standpoints, the scope-marker, the seven judgement-forms and the ban on same-respect contradiction. A banality does not generate a predicational calculus or rule anything out, and this does both.

⚠️ Note that the two blades cannot both land. Pressing self-refutation concedes that the doctrine makes a strong universal claim; pressing triviality concedes that it makes a weak one. The Jaina strategy is to accept reflexive qualification against the first, which exhibits the very discipline the doctrine teaches, and to point to the ontology and the calculus against the second.

| Critic | Objection | Jaina reply | Residual |
|---|---|---|---|
| Śaṃkara | contradiction, reflexive instability, and loss of clear guidance for practical action | index predicates to substance/place/time/mode; qualify syādvāda itself; show that naming the respect leaves the judgement determinate for the agent acting under it | qualified rule may seem to lose force, and the choice between competing respects is not thereby settled |
| Rāmānuja | if opposed attributes are real, the object is incoherent; if unreal, Jaina realism is surrendered | distinguish real aspects: substance persists, modes change; neither attribute is cancelled | the reality and consistency of the aspect distinction still need defence |
| Triviality critic | the doctrine reduces to banal perspective-talk | truth is aspect-conditioned, aspects are in the object, and the sevenfold calculus rules things out | the reply raises the ontological price it must then defend |

**Verdict:** Jainism is strongest as **qualified realism**—objective determinate reality with non-exhaustive finite assertions.

### Rāmānuja's real-or-unreal attributes dilemma

```text
Opposed attributes claimed of one object
          /                         \
REAL?                               UNREAL?
incompatible attributes             apparent attributes cannot ground
make their bearer incoherent        the promised pluralist realism
          \                         /
Jaina reply: real, DIFFERENT respects — not real contradictories
```

✅ **First horn:** if permanence and change, or existence and non-existence, are genuinely opposed real attributes of the same thing in the same sense, their bearer cannot be intelligible. Calling both real seems to purchase realism at the cost of contradiction. **Second horn:** if the attributes are merely apparent or unreal, the contradiction is avoided only by abandoning the Jaina claim that the object's multiple aspects are genuinely real.

✅ **Jaina reply:** the dilemma assumes that different aspects are identical respects. Gold persists as substance while bracelet-mode ceases and ring-mode begins; the same jar exists here now but not in jar-mode before production. Persistence and modal change are both real without being F and not-F under one index. The reply rejects the first horn's same-respect premise and refuses the second horn's retreat to unreality.

⚠️ **Residual:** indexing proves formal consistency only if the substance–mode and other aspect distinctions correspond to reality. A critic may still challenge that ontology or say the reply changes the sense of the predicates. Therefore do not present grammatical qualification alone as a proof of aspect-realism.

**Presupposition:** ⚠️ truth is stable even when access to it is partial. ✅ A map illustrates the point: it can be accurate without exhausting the terrain, and the territory does not become subjective because every map leaves something out.

**Retrieval cue:** **Self-exemption–Triviality blade–Reflexive qualification–Objective conditions–Residual.**

### Revision notes — critic, reply, remaining burden

1. Absolute anti-absolutism seems self-exempting.
2. Conditional anti-absolutism may lose universally binding force.
3. The triviality objection attacks a weakened, merely tolerant interpretation.
4. Objective aspects and determinate indices distinguish qualification from scepticism.
5. Śaṃkara also asks how qualified discourse guides practical action.
6. Rāmānuja's real-attribute horn threatens contradiction; his unreal-attribute horn threatens realism.
7. Jaina distinct-respect predication rejects both same-respect opposition and retreat to unreal modes.
8. Reflexive qualification and aspect-realism remain claims requiring defence.

### Lesson-local Mains practice

**Question:** Reconstruct Rāmānuja's dilemma and assess the Jaina reply. (10 marks; ceiling 150 words.)

**Model answer:**

Rāmānuja asks whether the opposed attributes of the Jaina real are themselves real or unreal. If real and genuinely opposed in the same sense, they make their bearer incoherent. If unreal, they cannot ground the pluralist realism Jainism promises.

Jainism rejects the assumed identity of respects. Gold persists as substance while bracelet-mode ceases and ring-mode originates. Permanence and modal change are both real, but they are not F and not-F under one index. The reply therefore denies the contradiction premise without declaring changing attributes illusory.

Its strength is a precise account of how plurality and identity coexist. Its residual burden is ontological: grammatical indexing alone does not prove that substance and modes form real, consistent aspects. The reply succeeds only insofar as that aspect-realism can be defended.

**Scoring guidance:** State both real/unreal horns; do not substitute Śaṃkara's reflexivity dilemma.

### Visual first

```text
jīva + ajīva -> āsrava -> bandha -> saṃvara -> nirjarā -> mokṣa
                         |
              puṇya + pāpa make nine padārthas
```

The seven truths (tattvas) are soul, non-soul, influx, bondage, stoppage, shedding and liberation. The nine categories add merit and demerit. Merit is ethically preferable but remains karmic involvement; pleasant rebirth is not release.

### Five causes and the bondage sequence

| Cause | Meaning | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Wrong vision (mithyātva) | misapprehends self, reality and path | misorients cognition |
| Non-restraint (avirati) | leaves desire/action uncontrolled | keeps channels open |
| Carelessness (pramāda) | absence of vigilance | defeats known discipline |
| Passion (kaṣāya) | anger, pride, deceit, greed | supplies “stickiness” |
| Activity (yoga) | body, speech and mind | opens the influx channel |

```text
activity opens influx (āsrava)
passion fixes lasting adhesion (bandha)
```

Influx is not yet lasting bondage. Karma is subtle material pudgala, not Nyāya adṛṣṭa or a generic moral score.

### Psychic and material bondage

| Axis | Psychic bondage (bhāvabandha) | Material bondage (dravyabandha) |
|---|---|---|
| Locus | deluded/passion-coloured soul-state | karmic pudgala adhering to soul |
| Form | intention, attachment, aversion | objective material accretion |
| Causation | conditions new adhesion | inherited karma can condition present passion |
| Removal | right orientation and passion-destruction | stoppage plus shedding |

The relation is reciprocal across beginningless saṃsāra; do not posit one historically first passion before all karma.

### Four parameters

| Parameter | Meaning | Main determinant |
|---|---|---|
| Prakṛti | type of karma | activity |
| Sthiti | duration | passion |
| Anubhāga/rasa | intensity | passion |
| Pradeśa | quantity | activity |

Activity without passion yields momentary bondage; passion-coloured activity yields durable bondage. ✅ The tradition names the pair: **īryāpathika bandha** is momentary bondage from passion-free activity, and **sāmparāyika bandha** is durable bondage from passion-coloured activity. ✅ The standard image is oily dust: dust contacts a surface, but oil makes it adhere durably. Activity provides contact; passion supplies the oil. A genuinely passion-free monk's walking illustrates momentary contact, not the absence of any bondage; ordinary carefulness alone does not prove passionlessness. An angry act illustrates durable adhesion. Keep the determinants separate: **activity → type and quantity; passion → duration and intensity**. ⚠️ The image explains the distinction but does not independently verify karmic particles.

**Presupposition:** ⚠️ psychical states can attract subtle matter — without that, the whole mechanism is metaphor.
**Objection:** matter binding consciousness is a category mistake.
**Reply:** the embodied soul has pradeśas; activity opens contact and passion determines adhesion.
**Residual:** the interaction remains difficult for rival metaphysics.

**Retrieval cue:** **Seven–Nine–Five causes–Influx/adhesion–Two bondages (īryāpathika/sāmparāyika)–Four parameters.**

### Revision notes — contact and durable adhesion

1. Seven tattvas run from soul/non-soul through influx and bondage to release.
2. Merit and demerit produce the nine-category scheme; merit is not liberation.
3. Wrong vision, non-restraint, negligence, passion and activity are five causes.
4. Āsrava is inflow; bandha is karmic association, not identical to inflow.
5. Bhāvabandha concerns soul-state; dravyabandha concerns material adhesion.
6. Activity determines prakṛti/type and pradeśa/quantity.
7. Passion determines sthiti/duration and anubhāga/intensity.
8. Īryāpathika is momentary passion-free bondage; sāmparāyika is durable passion-fixed bondage.

### Lesson-local Mains practice

**Question:** Explain why passionlessness does not mean absence of every kind of bondage. (10 marks; ceiling 150 words.)

**Model answer:**

Jainism separates karmic contact through activity from durable adhesion through passion. Body, speech and mind activity opens influx and determines karma's type and quantity. Passion determines duration and intensity.

Accordingly, passion-free activity yields momentary īryāpathika bondage, whereas passion-coloured activity yields durable sāmparāyika bondage. The oily-dust image clarifies the difference: activity supplies contact and passion makes the deposit cling. A genuinely passion-free walk illustrates the first; an angry act illustrates the second. Careful movement alone does not establish inward passionlessness.

This protects the distinction between influx and durable adhesion without saying that nothing binds when passion is absent. The theory connects moral psychology with material consequence, but its explanatory precision presupposes literal interaction between conscious soul and subtle karmic matter.

Mohanīya is especially dangerous because it misorients both vision and conduct. ✅ That double damage is why the tradition commonly divides it into two subtypes: **darśana-mohanīya**, which corrupts right vision, and **cāritra-mohanīya**, which corrupts right conduct. ⚠️ The split is worth one clause in any karma answer, because it explains why delusion is treated as the pivot of the whole scheme — it is the only karma that attacks both the orientation that starts the path and the discipline that walks it. Ghātiyā karmas obscure essential excellences; aghātiyā karmas sustain embodied conditions without preventing omniscience once the first four are gone.

### Prevention and elimination

```text
NEW KARMA? close the inlet -> stoppage (saṃvara)
OLD KARMA? clean the deposit -> shedding (nirjarā)
```

Stoppage uses restraint, carefulness and non-violence to prevent fresh influx. Shedding removes previously bound karma by natural fruition or accelerated austerity/purification. Neither can replace the other. ✅ Think of a tank fed by a muddy inlet: closing the inlet stops new silt arriving, but the sediment already on the floor has still to be cleaned out, and cleaning while the inlet runs is wasted work.

**Presupposition:** ⚠️ moral causation has ontological effects; a karma-type is a real differentiated veil, not a label for a tendency.
**Objection:** the taxonomy is excessively metaphysical.
**Reply:** it unifies cognition, delusion, agency, feeling, body, status and lifespan in one causal moral science.
**Residual:** explanatory scope does not establish independent empirical verification.

**Retrieval cue:** **Four obscure–Four embody–Close–Clean.**

### Revision notes — four veils, four conditions

1. Knowledge-obscuring karma blocks knowing; perception-obscuring karma blocks apprehension.
2. Deluding karma corrupts vision/conduct; obstructive karma blocks power.
3. Those four ghātiyā prevent the manifestation of essential excellences.
4. Feeling, body, status and lifespan belong to the aghātiyā class.
5. Mohanīya divides into darśana-mohanīya and cāritra-mohanīya.
6. Ghātiyā destruction permits embodied omniscience, not immediate disembodiment.
7. Saṃvara closes fresh influx; nirjarā removes the prior deposit.
8. Fruition and purification shed karma; stoppage cannot replace shedding.

### Lesson-local Mains practice

**Question:** Explain why both stoppage and shedding are necessary for liberation. (10 marks; ceiling 150 words.)

**Model answer:**

Material karma makes liberation a double operation. Stoppage, saṃvara, prevents fresh influx through restraint, carefulness and non-violence. Shedding, nirjarā, removes the karma already attached through fruition and disciplined purification.

The muddy-tank analogy shows joint necessity: closing the inlet leaves existing sediment, while cleaning without closing the inlet allows replenishment. The analogy explains the causal division without proving that karma is material.

The eightfold taxonomy identifies what must be removed. Four ghātiyā veil knowledge, perception, orientation and power; four aghātiyā sustain feeling, body, status and lifespan. Destroying the first permits embodied omniscience; exhausting the second ends embodiment. Jainism thereby integrates cognitive and ethical purification, though the explanatory breadth of the taxonomy does not establish independent empirical verification of its particles.

**Scoring guidance:** Explain the failure of each operation alone before applying the two karma-classes.

Endurance also overlaps: *Tattvārthasūtra* 9.8 says afflictions are endured to remain on the path **and for nirjarā**. Equanimity prevents renewed passion while maturing karma is borne and shed. Natural fruition names the exhaustion of an existing deposit, not the prevention of fresh influx.

The three jewels are mutually reinforcing. Right vision is trustful orientation to truth, not blind fideism. Knowledge without conduct cannot stop influx; conduct without right vision lacks direction. ⚠️ State the failure mode of each jewel taken alone rather than listing the three: knowledge of non-violence without the conduct of non-violence is incomplete, conduct without orientation is mere habit, and orientation without knowledge cannot tell what to do.

**Presupposition:** ⚠️ liberation requires cognitive *and* practical transformation together; neither insight nor discipline alone is sufficient.

### Five vows and graded practice

| Vow | Meaning | Karmic role |
|---|---|---|
| Ahiṃsā | non-violence | curbs injury and deep bondage |
| Satya | truthfulness | purifies speech/intention |
| Asteya | non-stealing | restrains acquisitive harm |
| Brahmacarya | chastity/sensual control | weakens desire |
| Aparigraha | non-possession/non-attachment | weakens grasping |

Ascetics undertake great vows; householders practise graded limited vows. Radical care for one-sense beings follows from the ontology of many jīvas taught in Lesson 2: earth-, water-, fire-, air- and plant-bodied beings are genuine souls, so non-violence is an ontological obligation rather than a sentiment.

### Sorting the supporting disciplines correctly

✅ The five vows do not exhaust the discipline. The following supports show how restraint and purification cooperate. Their principal placement in an account of stoppage does not mean that they can have no role in shedding.

| Discipline | Term | Function | Side |
|---|---|---|---|
| Restraint of body, speech and mind | gupti | controls the three channels of activity | preventive |
| Carefulness in movement, speech, alms-seeking, handling objects and bodily functions | samiti | prevents negligent injury and fresh influx | preventive |
| Ten virtues, enumerated below | dharma | replaces passion with disciplined character | chiefly supports stoppage; includes austerity |
| Contemplation on self, impermanence, solitude, influx and stoppage | anuprekṣā / bhāvanā | reorients cognition and weakens attachment | preventive |
| Patient conquest of afflictions — hunger, thirst, heat, cold, insult | pariṣaha-jaya | maintains equanimity on the path and bears maturing karma without renewed passion | stoppage **and** contribution to shedding (9.8) |
| Right conduct | cāritra | integrates vision, knowledge, vows and equanimity | preventive **and** eliminative |
| Austerity | tapas | stops passion and accelerates the wearing away of stored karma | preventive **and** eliminative |

✅ The ten virtues are **forgiveness/forbearance (kṣamā), humility (mārdava), straightforwardness (ārjava), purity/non-greed (śauca), truthfulness (satya), self-restraint (saṃyama), austerity (tapas), renunciation (tyāga), non-attachment/non-possession (ākiñcanya), and celibacy (brahmacarya)**. “Supreme” (uttama) qualifies each: their aim is liberation rather than worldly reward. Forgiveness counters anger, humility pride, straightforwardness deceit and purity greed. Renunciation relinquishes; non-attachment relinquishes the possessive “mine”, so those two are not duplicates.

⚠️ Avoid two sorting errors. Contemplation chiefly reorients cognition and supports stoppage; it is not by itself a synonym for shedding. But **endurance must not be called preventive only**: 9.8 explicitly connects it to both remaining on the path and nirjarā. Conversely, shedding is not fasting alone: austerity includes internal purification, purified conduct supports karmic exhaustion, and natural fruition removes maturing karma.

```text
SHEDDING HAS TWO FORMS
natural fruition  — karma wears out when it matures, unaided
deliberate        — austerity and purified conduct promote karmic exhaustion

OVERLAPPING ROLES (not an exhaustive exclusive partition)
tapas    -> preventive: stops passion | eliminative: wears stored karma away
cāritra  -> preventive: integrates the path | eliminative: exhausts bound karma
endurance -> maintains the path | contributes to nirjarā (9.8)
```

### External discipline and internal austerity

✅ External austerities (bāhya-tapas) chiefly regulate bodily conditions; internal austerities (ābhyantara-tapas) purify orientation and attention. The traditional six of each are not a prescription for self-harm: the philosophical point is passion-removal, not bodily suffering as an end.

| External austerity | Distinct function | Internal austerity | Distinct function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fasting (anaśana) | temporary abstention to discipline attachment | Expiation (prāyaścitta) | acknowledge and correct faults of negligence |
| Reduced intake (avamaudarya) | limit quantity rather than abstain | Reverence (vinaya) | overcome pride through respect for spiritual excellence |
| Restrictions on obtaining food (vṛtti-parisaṃkhyāna) | limit acquisition, not merely quantity | Service (vaiyāvṛttya) | assist practitioners in difficulty rather than seek prestige |
| Renouncing stimulating tastes (rasa-parityāga) | relinquish attachment to relish | Study (svādhyāya) | attentive doctrinal reflection, not idle recitation |
| Solitary lodging (vivikta-śayyāsana) | reduce disturbance to contemplation | Relinquishment (vyutsarga) | loosen “I” and “mine”, including bodily possessiveness |
| Bodily endurance (kāya-kleśa) | voluntarily bear hardship without attachment | Meditation (dhyāna) | discipline wandering attention toward purification |

✅ Distinguish voluntarily undertaken bodily discipline from endurance of **encountered** afflictions (pariṣaha-jaya). Both can contribute to purification, but their occasions differ. ⚠️ Fasting with pride can renew passion; confession without correction can be empty. Internal austerity explains why tapas is neither mere starvation nor an automatic physical technique: its liberating function depends on right vision and conduct.

### Fourteen stages (guṇasthānas)

| No. | Stage: English and canonical name | Individual distinction |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | False vision (mithyā-dṛṣṭi) | wrong orientation governs cognition and conduct |
| 2 | Lingering taste/fall (sāsvādana / sāsādana) | falling from right vision, retaining its taste; not stable right vision |
| 3 | Mixed vision (miśra / samyag-mithyā-dṛṣṭi) | mixed right and wrong orientation, not the downward taste of stage 2 |
| 4 | Unrestrained right vision (avirata-samyag-dṛṣṭi) | right orientation without vowed restraint |
| 5 | Partial restraint (deśavirata) | limited vows, characteristically householder discipline |
| 6 | Full restraint with negligence (pramatta-saṃyata) | ascetic restraint attained, vigilance still imperfect |
| 7 | Full restraint without negligence (apramatta-saṃyata) | vigilance removes stage 6's carelessness |
| 8 | Unprecedented transformation (apūrva-karaṇa / nivṛtti-bādara) | new purification of gross passions beyond earlier states |
| 9 | Further gross-passion purification (anivṛtti-bādara) | refinement beyond the unprecedented operation of stage 8 |
| 10 | Subtle passion (sūkṣma-samparāya) | only subtle greed remains; not complete passionlessness |
| 11 | Suppressed delusion (upaśānta-moha) | latent delusion covered, not destroyed; relapse follows |
| 12 | Destroyed delusion (kṣīṇa-moha) | annihilation rather than suppression; irreversible purification |
| 13 | Active omniscient (sayoga-kevalī) | four ghātiyā gone; embodied activity and aghātiyā remain |
| 14 | Inactive omniscient (ayoga-kevalī) | activity ceases immediately before karmaless siddha-state |

✅ The numbering orders distinctions, not a compulsory journey through every rung. In particular, the annihilation route bypasses stage 11; the two ladders below explain why ascent is not a single irreversible staircase.

### Two ladders: suppression and annihilation

```text
                stage 10 — only subtle greed remains
                        /                \
      UPAŚAMA-ŚREṆI    /                  \    KṢAPAKA-ŚREṆI
      ladder of       /                    \   ladder of
      SUPPRESSION    /                      \  ANNIHILATION
                    v                        v
        stage 11 — delusion only          stage 12 — delusion
        subsided (upaśānta-moha)          destroyed (kṣīṇa-moha)
                    |                        |
          MUST FALL BACK: moha was      IRREVERSIBLE: nothing is left
          covered, never destroyed      to resurface
                    |                        v
          re-descent into lower        stage 13 sayoga-kevalī -> 14 -> siddha
          stages, rebound of passion
```

✅ Jaina doctrine distinguishes two ascents: suppression can relapse, whereas annihilation removes delusion irreversibly.

- **Upaśama-śreṇi — the ladder of suppression.** Delusion (moha) is progressively *subsided*: held down, covered, rendered temporarily inoperative. This ladder can reach stage eleven, where delusion is entirely suppressed (upaśānta-moha). ✅ Because what is suppressed is not destroyed, stage eleven **necessarily falls back**: the latent deluding karma resurfaces and the climber descends. The gain is real but borrowed.
- **Kṣapaka-śreṇi — the ladder of annihilation.** Delusion is *destroyed* rather than held down. This ladder does not pass through stage eleven at all; it reaches stage twelve, where delusion is destroyed (kṣīṇa-moha), and from there the ascent is **irreversible** — the remaining obscuring karmas fall away, omniscience dawns at stage thirteen, activity ceases at stage fourteen, and release follows.

| Axis | Upaśama-śreṇi | Kṣapaka-śreṇi |
|---|---|---|
| Operation on delusion | subsidence | destruction |
| Highest stage reached | 11 (upaśānta-moha) | 12 (kṣīṇa-moha) and beyond |
| Stability | unstable; relapse is doctrinally necessary | stable; no latent residue remains |
| Outcome | re-descent, renewed bondage | omniscience, then final release |

**Example.** ✅ Suppression is ash over a live coal: the fire is invisible and the hand is safe, until a breath of wind restores it. Destruction is the fire burnt out — there is nothing to revive. ⚠️ The analogy also explains why the eleventh stage is reported as spiritually dangerous rather than merely incomplete: a climber may believe himself free precisely while his bondage is intact.

**Objection:** fourteen numbered rungs and two ladders mechanise liberation into bookkeeping.
**Reply:** ✅ the scheme integrates insight, moral psychology, conduct and karmic purification into one ordered account, and the two-ladder fork is exactly what prevents mechanical reading: the same numerical height can be reached by a route that holds and by a route that fails.
**Residual:** ⚠️ the precision presupposes the disputed ontology of deluding karma; a critic who rejects material karma rejects the measurement along with it.

**Retrieval cue:** **Three jewels–Five vows–Seven disciplines sorted–Fourteen stages–Suppression falls, destruction holds.**

### Revision notes — practices and stages

1. Right vision, knowledge and conduct cooperate; none suffices alone.
2. Five vows are non-violence, truth, non-stealing, chastity and non-possession.
3. Gupti controls activity; samiti regulates its careful performance.
4. The ten virtues include straightforwardness, purity and austerity, not an unnamed remainder.
5. Endurance maintains the path and contributes to nirjarā; its role is not preventive only.
6. Six external austerities regulate conditions; six internal austerities purify faults and attention.
7. The fourteen individually named stages distinguish vision, restraint, passion and omniscience.
8. Stage 11 suppresses and relapses; stage 12 destroys and proceeds irreversibly.

### Lesson-local Mains practice

**Question:** Why is Jaina austerity neither mere fasting nor the whole path? (10 marks; ceiling 150 words.)

**Model answer:**

Jaina austerity integrates bodily discipline with inner purification. Six external austerities include fasting, reduced intake, restrictions on acquisition, taste-renunciation, solitary lodging and bodily endurance. Six internal austerities are expiation, reverence, service, study, relinquishment and meditation. Expiation corrects negligence; relinquishment loosens “I” and “mine”; meditation disciplines attention.

Their purpose is passion-removal and karmic exhaustion, not suffering for prestige. Right vision and knowledge must guide conduct; bodily hardship accompanied by pride can renew attachment.

Austerity is therefore neither fasting alone nor a replacement for the three jewels and vows. Stoppage and shedding must cooperate. Endurance of encountered afflictions also maintains the path and contributes to nirjarā, as Tattvārthasūtra 9.8 states. The scheme's coherence depends on its disputed material-karmic ontology.

**Scoring guidance:** Name internal practices with distinct functions and avoid an exclusive two-column sorting.

### Visual first

```text
BOUND SOUL
limited knowledge + passions + body + rebirth
  -> four ghātiyā destroyed
SAYOGA-KEVALĪ: embodied omniscient arhat; aghātiyā remain
  -> activity ceases
```

| Axis | Bound soul | Liberated soul |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | distinct jīva | same numerically distinct jīva perfected |
| Cognition | obscured and limited | infinite knowledge/perception |
| Passion/activity | karmically productive | passionless; no fresh bondage |
| Body/rebirth | embodied and reborn | no body, death or rebirth |
| Status | saṃsārin | siddha at siddha-śilā |

Liberation is neither merger into Brahman nor annihilation. It is liberation by subtraction: remove karmic obstruction and the infinite fourfold excellence—knowledge, perception, bliss and power—manifests.

### The embodied omniscient as the Jaina liberated-in-life

✅ When Indian philosophy asks whether liberation can be attained **while still embodied** — the jīvanmukti question — the Jaina answer is the **sayoga-kevalī**. At stage thirteen the four obscuring karmas are gone and cognition is unobstructed, but embodied activity and aghātiyā remain. Passion-free activity produces only momentary **īryāpathika**, not durable **sāmparāyika**, bondage. Do not turn “no new passion-fixed bondage” into “no bondage of any kind.” Stage fourteen ends activity; final siddha-state follows when all remaining karma is exhausted.

**Example.** ✅ A lamp whose cover has been lifted shines fully even before it is carried to another room. The light is already complete; only its location has not yet changed. That is the arhat: omniscient now, disembodied later.

⚠️ Keep the comparison to the one the Jaina sources themselves draw. The standing contrast is with Advaita, and it is a contrast about what liberation *is*, not a survey of who permits liberation-in-life.

| Axis | Advaita | Jainism |
|---|---|---|
| Ultimate reality | non-dual Brahman | a plurality of real substances |
| What bondage consists in | ignorance and superimposition (avidyā, adhyāsa) | obscuration by material karma |
| What liberation amounts to | realisation of non-duality | this numerically distinct jīva, perfected, as a siddha |

⚠️ Do **not** extend the row to Sāṃkhya or Nyāya. How those schools treat liberation-in-life is a separate question that the Jaina material does not settle, and an unsupported comparison is a worse answer than none.

**Objection (Rāmānuja-style):** omniscience while embodied is unintelligible, because embodiment is itself a form of limitation; a knower housed in a particular body at a particular place cannot know everything.
**Reply:** ✅ the objection collapses two kinds of karma that Jainism separates. Obscuring karmas block essential cognition; embodiment-determining karmas merely sustain a body, a feeling-tone, a social status and a lifespan. Nothing in the second class touches knowing, so a body can persist around a cognition that is already complete.
**Residual:** ⚠️ the reply works only if the two-class division is itself accepted; a critic who holds that any embodiment conditions cognition will treat the division as a stipulation designed to save the doctrine.

⚠️ Examiner use: this is the cleanest way to show that the arhat is not "almost liberated." He is liberated in the Jaina sense already; what remains is not ignorance but a body.

**Presupposition:** ⚠️ beginningless conditioning can nevertheless be terminable; otherwise the path is pointless.
The soul–karma relation is beginningless (anādi), not endless. A seed/tree analogy blocks a first temporal term; stoppage and shedding explain termination. ✅ The complementary image for release is a soot-blackened lamp: cleaning the soot does not manufacture light, it reveals the light that was always burning. Liberation adds nothing to the soul; it removes what covered it.

### Sallekhanā: the passionless end, and why it is not suicide

✅ **Sallekhanā** (also **santhārā**) is the voluntary, gradual and ritually regulated relinquishment of food *and of passions*, undertaken when death is already unavoidable or bodily decline is incurable. It belongs here because it applies the passion-based account of bondage to the end of life.

**The argument, in four steps.** ✅

1. Bondage tracks **kaṣāya**: passion fixes karmic matter far more deeply than bare activity does (Lesson 7's four parameters).
2. Panic, craving or aversion can reinforce passion-fixed bondage; the doctrine establishes no comparative maximum.
3. Equanimity curbs passion and durable adhesion; passion-free activity may still involve momentary īryāpathika bondage.
4. The aim is accordingly **not to end life but to end passion**, at a time when life is already closing of itself.

```text
NOT:  "I want to die"            -> aversion, despair -> kaṣāya -> deep bondage
NOT:  "I want to stay alive"     -> craving           -> kaṣāya -> deep bondage
YES:  approaching death -> equanimity -> no new kaṣāya -> no durable passion-fixed adhesion
```

> 🔑 **The five forbidden motives.** ✅ The vow is vitiated by (i) desire for death, (ii) desire to continue living, (iii) attachment to friends, (iv) recollection of past pleasures, and (v) hope of reward. If any of these enters, the act becomes an **aticāra** — a transgression of the vow's own discipline — and the practice has failed on its own terms, whatever its outward form.

**The contrast with suicide, drawn fully.** ✅ Jainism distinguishes sallekhanā from **ātma-ghāta** (suicide) on five axes at once, and an answer that names only one of them has not made the distinction.

| Axis | Suicide (ātma-ghāta) | Sallekhanā |
|---|---|---|
| Inner state | passion-driven: despair, aversion, rage | passion-free: equanimity, forgiveness, detachment |
| Timing | sudden; life is cut off while it still runs | gradual; undertaken only when death is already unavoidable |
| Means | violent | non-violent reduction of intake |
| Visibility | typically concealed | public and supervised within the community |
| Object of the act | ending life | ending passion, while life ends of itself |

**Example.** ✅ An elderly ascetic or lay practitioner facing irreversible decline progressively reduces intake under religious supervision, while cultivating forgiveness, detachment and equanimity toward the approaching end.

**Presupposition:** ⚠️ the karmic quality of dying depends on the inner state with which death is faced — not merely on when or how the body stops.

**Objection (modern legal-ethical):** ❓ critics in India have disputed whether the practice is protected religious observance or an impermissible form of self-destruction, on the ground that the distinction between ending passion and ending life cannot be policed from outside.
**Reply:** ⚠️ distinguish **public evidence** from **inner motivation**. Gradual procedure, publicity and supervision can be observed; equanimity, freedom from coercion and absence of death-desire must be assessed from testimony and conduct and cannot be read directly from the outward procedure. The doctrine supplies criteria for a distinction, not an infallible test of compliance.
**Residual:** ⚠️ matching the procedure does not prove passionlessness or settle its ethical or legal permissibility. The suicide column above records the tradition's contrast, not a universal clinical description of every suicide. Write the philosophy and stop: name no case, holding, date or statute, and give no practical advice.

### Objections and bounded extension

**Soul–matter objection:** a conscious soul should not bind material particles. Jainism invokes the embodied soul’s pradeśas, body-wide presence and passion-conditioned attraction. This gives an internal mechanism, not neutral proof.

**Intrinsic-perfection objection:** why can an omniscient-in-nature soul be obscured? Beginningless conditioning can be destroyed, but the reply does not fully explain obscurability.

**Upward-ascent objection:** ✅ the doctrine holds that the liberated soul, no longer weighed down by karmic matter, rises to the topmost region of the cosmos and abides at **siddha-śilā**. ❓ A modern critic replies that the spatial ascent of a disembodied, non-material soul looks mythic rather than philosophical: a soul with no mass cannot be "weighed down," and a soul with no body has no obvious business occupying a location at all.
**Reply:** ⚠️ the correct discipline here is reportorial. The cosmological picture is part of the canonical doctrine and must be stated faithfully — liberation in Jainism is individual *and* cosmologically locatable — while being kept separate from modern philosophical evaluation of it. The two moves are not in competition: one says what the tradition holds, the other assesses it.
**Residual:** ⚠️ the separation protects accuracy but does not defend the claim. A critic can accept the faithful report and still hold that the Jaina soul's extension and location are the same commitment that makes the body-sized soul of Lesson 2 vulnerable, now carried past death.

**Retrieval cue:** **Bound–Four gone–Embodied kevalin as living-liberated–All eight gone–Siddha–Passionless end, five forbidden motives.**

### Revision notes — three conditions and a limit

1. The bound soul has obscured cognition, passions, embodiment and rebirth.
2. Sayoga-kevalī is omniscient with embodied activity and aghātiyā remaining.
3. Passion-free activity can involve momentary bondage, not durable passion-fixed adhesion.
4. Ayoga-kevalī ends activity before final release.
5. Siddha is the individual soul without karma, body or rebirth.
6. Knowledge, perception, bliss and power manifest by removal of obstruction.
7. Sallekhanā's doctrinal aim is passionlessness, not desire for death.
8. Public procedure is observable; equanimity and absence of death-desire require inference.

### Lesson-local Mains practice

**Question:** Distinguish the living omniscient arhat from the siddha. (10 marks; ceiling 150 words.)

**Model answer:**

The embodied omniscient arhat, sayoga-kevalī, has destroyed the four ghātiyā karmas that obscure essential cognition and power. Four aghātiyā still sustain embodied conditions, so omniscience is not yet final disembodiment. Passion-free activity can produce momentary īryāpathika association, not durable passion-fixed bondage.

The uncovered-lamp analogy explains perfected cognition before disembodiment. The objection is that any embodied knower is limited. Jainism replies by distinguishing cognitive obstruction from embodiment, although a critic may dispute that division and the spatial description of a liberated immaterial soul.

### Visual first

```text
present passion/wrong orientation (bhāva)
        -> attracts and fixes new material karma (dravya)
```

The 2024 distinction must work in both directions. Present psychic bondage conditions new material adhesion; beginningless inherited material karma also conditions present passions. | Axis | Jainism | Buddhism | Cārvāka |

| Self | many enduring jīvas | no permanent self-substance | body-based consciousness |
| Karma | subtle material accretion | volitional/causal continuity | transcendent karma rejected |

**Reply:** it is a beginningless feedback relation, not a same-moment explanatory circle. In each new act **activity determines type and quantity; passion determines duration and intensity**. Passion-free activity can bind momentarily (īryāpathika); passion fixes durable bondage (sāmparāyika).

4. Previously bound karma conditions present passions and embodiment.
5. Beginninglessness blocks an invented first passion, not every interaction objection.
6. Activity sets type/quantity; passion sets duration/intensity.

**Question:** Does reciprocal psychic–material conditioning make Jaina bondage circular? (10 marks; ceiling 150 words.)

**Model answer:**

Jaina bondage distinguishes a psychic state from material consequence. Bhāvabandha is the soul's deluded and passionate modification; dravyabandha is the adhesion of karmic pudgala.

Present passion fixes durable karma, while previously bound karma conditions later embodiment and susceptibility to passion. This is a temporally extended, beginningless feedback relation, not a claim that one event explains itself in the same instant. Activity determines type and quantity; passion determines duration and intensity. Passion-free activity can associate momentarily without durable adhesion.

The oily-dust image explains why dispositions matter but cannot verify subtle matter. Beginninglessness answers a demand for a first lapse; it does not establish that consciousness literally interacts with particles. The theory preserves both agency and objective consequence while retaining that metaphysical burden.

**Scoring guidance:** Give the return arrow and the temporal qualification; preserve the determinant split.

## PYQ 1 — 2018 Q6(c), 15 marks

**Exact question:** How do the Jaina philosophers explain 'bondage'? What, according to them, is the distinction between 'liberated soul' and 'bound soul'? What do the Jainas think about the condition of the 'liberated soul'? Discuss.

**Printed directive:** three separate interrogatives — "How do … explain?", "What … is the distinction?", "What … the condition?" — closed by a single "Discuss."
**Examiner move:** the one "Discuss" governs all three questions, and the third is not a restatement of the second. A contrast table answers demand two; demand three asks for a positive description of a state.
**Answer spine:** mechanism of bondage → symmetric bound/liberated contrast → positive description of the siddha condition, with the embodied kevalin inserted between → one critical qualification.
**Core remediation:** Lesson 7 for the five causes and the two bondages, Lesson 8 for the eight karmas in two classes, Lesson 10 for the three conditions and the siddha state. If the script stops after the contrast, return to Lesson 10's condition table and the liberation-by-subtraction paragraph.

### Model answer

Jaina bondage is the association of the conscious soul, jīva, with subtle karmic matter, pudgala. Five causes are wrong vision, non-restraint, carelessness, passion and activity. Anger, pride, deceit and greed supply passion. Bodily, verbal and mental activity opens influx and determines type and quantity; passion determines duration and intensity. Passion-free activity produces momentary īryāpathika bondage; passion-coloured activity produces durable sāmparāyika bondage. Psychic bondage, bhāvabandha, is the deluded soul-state; material bondage, dravyabandha, is actual karmic adhesion. Previously bound karma also conditions present passion, so beginninglessness blocks an invented first lapse.

A bound soul has limited knowledge and perception, passions, embodiment, pleasure/pain, status, lifespan and rebirth. The liberated soul is the same individual after complete karmic exhaustion, not another entity.

Its condition must be described positively: infinite knowledge, perception, bliss and power manifest without body, passion or rebirth. The siddha abides at siddha-śilā, the summit of the inhabited cosmos. Release is neither merger into Brahman nor annihilation.

An intermediate condition matters. Destruction of four ghātiyā yields embodied omniscience, sayoga-kevalī, while aghātiyā remain. Passionless activity here permits momentary, not durable, bondage. Ayoga-kevalī ends activity; exhaustion of the remaining karma yields siddhahood.

The oily-dust image explains passion's adhesive role, but cannot prove literal soul–matter interaction. Space-points make the account internally intelligible without settling that cross-school objection.

**Why this earns marks:** it treats three printed questions as three demands, supplies the mechanism before the contrast, describes the liberated condition positively instead of by negation alone, inserts the kevalin stage that most scripts omit, and closes with a qualified judgement rather than praise.

## PYQ 2 — 2019 Q6(a), 20 marks

**Exact question:** How is reality defined by the Jainas? How is this theory of reality reflected in their view on judgements? Discuss.

**Printed directive:** two "How" questions linked by "reflected in", closed by "Discuss."
**Examiner move:** "reflected in" is a derivation word. The marks sit on the connecting arrows, not on the two inventories; a correct account of reality followed by a correct account of judgement, with nothing joining them, under-answers a 20-marker.
**Answer spine:** definition of the real → substance, quality and mode → why a finite knower reaches one aspect → standpoint → scope-marker → the seven forms on one object → named objection, reply, residual → verdict naming the derivation itself as the achievement.
**Core remediation:** Lesson 2 for the ontology, Lesson 3 for the bridge, Lesson 5 for the judgement-forms. If the two halves read as separate essays, rewrite the joins as explicit arrows before adding any further content.

### Model answer

Jainism defines the real, sat, through origination, decay and persistence together. Substance, dravya, endures with abiding qualities, guṇas, while modes, paryāyas, arise and cease: gold persists through bracelet-to-ring transformation. Six substances and many souls establish plurality; cognition-independent aspects establish realism. Many-sidedness, anekāntavāda, concerns the object, not mere tolerance.

Judgement follows through a necessary bridge. The many-sided object exceeds a karmically limited knower's grasp. A standpoint, naya, reaches one aspect; its partial truth becomes one-sided error only when absolutised. Speech therefore uses syāt, “in some respect”, indexed to substance, place, time and mode.

Apply the seven forms to one clay jar:

1. Syād asti: it exists here now in jar-mode.
2. Syād nāsti: it did not exist in jar-mode before production.
3. Syād asti ca nāsti ca: present existence and pre-production absence are stated successively.
4. Syād avaktavyam: those opposed aspects resist one simultaneous unqualified predicate.
5. Syād asti ca avaktavyam: here-now existence is affirmed together with that expressive limit.
6. Syād nāsti ca avaktavyam: pre-production absence is affirmed together with that same limit.
7. Syād asti ca nāsti ca avaktavyam: both indexed aspects are stated successively, together with the limit on their simultaneous unqualified expression.

Three primitives, taken singly, in pairs and together, yield seven forms; they are not seven truth-values. Syāt expresses definite scope, never probability.

Śaṃkara's contradiction charge meets the distinct-respect reply; his reflexivity charge requires syādvāda itself to be qualified. Whether that qualified rule retains universal force remains unresolved. The achievement is precisely the reflection relation: many-sided reality demands aspectual cognition and consequently qualified judgement, without surrendering objective truth.

**Why this earns marks:** it supplies both halves at 20-mark depth, makes the derivation itself the thesis, runs the forms on a single object instead of listing them, names a classical critic rather than "some critics", and ends on a residual that shows critical control.

## PYQ 6 — 2022 Q6(b), 15 marks

**Exact question:** ‘The doctrine of Relativism of Jain Philosophy cannot be logically sustained without postulating ‘Absolutism’.’ Critically examine this view and give reasons in the favour of your answer.

**Printed directive:** two instructions — "Critically examine this view" and "give reasons in the favour of your answer."
**Examiner move:** the second instruction is not decorative. A balanced survey that never declares a position answers only the first half. The script must evaluate the quoted claim and then state and defend its own verdict.
**Answer spine:** reconstruct the charge at its strongest, including both the self-refutation and the triviality blades → concede precisely what is true → deny precisely what is not → show reflexive qualification working → declared verdict plus the reasons for it.
**Core remediation:** Lesson 6 for both horns and the two named critics, Lesson 5 for the indexing that does the conceding. If the draft praises Jaina tolerance, delete that sentence: it concedes the triviality charge.

### Model answer

The charge has force if absolutism means stable truth-conditions, but overreaches if it means exhaustive one-sided assertion.

Śaṃkara's reflexivity challenge asks whether “all finite claims are conditioned” is absolute. If so, it self-exempts; if conditional, a rival may deny its universal force. A weakened reading also risks triviality: merely saying that viewpoints differ adds little. Rāmānuja's separate dilemma asks whether opposed attributes are real, threatening contradiction, or unreal, threatening realism.

Jainism replies through definite indexing. The jar exists here now but not in jar-mode before production. Objective aspects and conditions remain; what is denied is finite exhaustiveness. Substance persists while modes change, so genuinely different respects do not form same-respect contradictories.

Reflexive qualification applies to syādvāda itself. Its realist ontology and sevenfold discipline answer the triviality charge without pretending that all opinions are equally true. Kevala permits comprehensive cognition in principle, but cannot simply warrant a finite speaker's general rule.

My verdict is qualified rejection of the quotation: objectivity and logical determinacy are necessary, but neither entails the exhaustive absolutism Jainism opposes. The reason is that a true aspectual claim can remain non-exhaustive, like an accurate map that leaves terrain unrepresented. The residual is that real aspects and the regulative force of qualification still require defence. This is qualified realism, not a conclusive defeat of every absolutist objection.

**Why this earns marks:** it builds the objection before answering it, separates what is conceded from what is refused, uses the second instruction to deliver an explicit reasoned verdict, and leaves a residual instead of claiming total victory.

## PYQ 7 — 2023 Q5(a), 10 marks

**Exact question:** “All human knowledge is empirical and therefore relative.” Critically examine Jaina theory of sevenfold judgement (saptabhaṅgīnaya) in the light of above statement.

**Printed directive:** "Critically examine … in the light of above statement."
**Examiner move:** the quotation is a lens, not a thesis to endorse. "In the light of" means the Jaina doctrine must be tested against the statement, and "critically" means the statement itself must be tested. It contains two separable inferences — "all … empirical" and "therefore relative" — and each has to be assessed.
**Answer spine:** split the quotation → refute "all" with named counterexamples → correct the ground of relativity → show what sevenfold judgement actually asserts → deny the sceptical reading → corrected verdict.
**Core remediation:** Lesson 3 for the counterexamples and the two causes of partiality, Lesson 5 for the forms. If the script accepts the quotation and merely describes saptabhaṅgī, the "critically" has gone unanswered.

### Model answer

“All” fails: mati and śruta are mediated, but material-object cognition (avadhi), mind-reading (manaḥparyāya) and embodied omniscience (kevala) are non-sensory direct cognition. Directness means freedom from mediation, not sensory vividness.

“Therefore” is incomplete: partiality follows from object-many-sidedness plus karmic limitation, not empiricism alone. Avadhi is direct yet limited.

Sevenfold judgement expresses qualified truth: is; is-not; both successively; inexpressible; is-and-inexpressible; is-not-and-inexpressible; both-and-inexpressible. Syāt indexes substance, place, time and mode. One jar exists here now but not before production; avaktavya marks opposed aspects' resistance to one simultaneous unqualified predicate.

Thus relativity is not scepticism or subjectivity: aspects are real and no same-respect contradiction follows. The quotation needs correction, not endorsement. Jaina higher knowledge remains externally disputable, but its own theory grounds finite partiality in ontology and karmic limitation rather than declaring every human cognition empirical.

**Why this earns marks:** it treats the quotation as two testable inferences, refutes the first with named counterexamples rather than general denial, repairs the second by naming both causes of partiality, and reaches a corrected verdict instead of endorsing or dismissing the statement wholesale.

## PYQ 8 — 2024 Q6(c), 15 marks

**Exact question:** What is the distinction between Bhāvabandha and Dravyabandha, according to the Jainas? Discuss.

**Printed directive:** "What is the distinction …?" closed by "Discuss."
**Examiner move:** a distinction demands symmetric axes — the same questions asked of both terms — while "Discuss" demands that the relation between them be developed beyond a table. A one-way story in which passion simply produces karma under-answers the stem.
**Answer spine:** define each term → separate them on locus, form, causal role and remedy → develop the relation in both directions → block the first-passion error with beginninglessness → say why they cannot be collapsed → residual.
**Core remediation:** Lesson 7 for the pair and the four parameters, Lesson 11 for the two-way conditioning. If only one direction appears, add the return arrow before anything else.

### Model answer

Psychic bondage, bhāvabandha, is the soul's deluded, passion-coloured modification. Material bondage, dravyabandha, is its association with karmic pudgala. They differ without being causally independent.

By locus, one concerns conscious affect and volition; the other concerns material adhesion to that soul. By form, one is psychic susceptibility, the other an accretion classified through four parameters. By causal role, activity supplies entry and determines type, prakṛti, and quantity, pradeśa; passion fixes duration, sthiti, and intensity, anubhāga. Passion therefore does not determine all four, nor make every association possible: passion-free activity yields momentary īryāpathika, while passion fixes durable sāmparāyika bondage. By remedy, right vision and passion-destruction transform psychic orientation; stoppage and shedding prevent influx and remove the material deposit.

The relation works in both directions. Present anger fixes durable karma, while inherited karma conditions subsequent feeling, embodiment and susceptibility to anger. The relation is beginningless, not initiated by a first historical passion. Temporally successive conditioning is not a claim that one event causes itself simultaneously.

Neither term can absorb the other. Psychic reduction makes material karma metaphorical; material reduction loses the soul's responsible agency. The oily-dust image illustrates their cooperation but does not prove it. Pradeśas explain association internally; literal soul–matter interaction remains the external philosophical burden.

**Why this earns marks:** it keeps the axes symmetric, develops the relation in both directions, names and blocks the first-passion error, explains why collapsing either way destroys something Jainism needs, and closes on the live residual.

## PYQ 9 — 2025 Q7(c), 15 marks

**Exact question:** Is Jaina philosophy pluralistic and realistic? Critically discuss.

**Printed directive:** a stance question, "Is …?", carrying two distinct predicates, closed by "Critically discuss."
**Examiner move:** both adjectives must be separately defined and separately tested — an answer that proves plurality and assumes realism has answered half. "Critically discuss" then requires a declared position that survives at least one serious objection.
**Answer spine:** define and establish pluralistic → define and establish realistic → show the realism is dynamic rather than static → take two objections → qualified verdict naming the costs.
**Core remediation:** Lesson 2 for the substances, the many souls and permanence-in-change, Lesson 6 for the relativity-versus-realism objection. If either adjective was used without definition, define it and attach one test from Lesson 2.

### Model answer

Jainism is pluralistic because it affirms many irreducible reals, and realistic because substances, qualities and modes exist independently of finite viewpoints.

Plurality includes six dravyas: soul, matter, motion-medium, rest-medium, space and time. Souls are individually numerous, intrinsically conscious and responsible for distinct karmic careers. Liberation perfects each individual rather than merging plurality into one Brahman. Non-conscious reality is likewise irreducible to cognition.

The realism is dynamic. Origination, decay and persistence coexist because substance endures while modes change. Clay persists through genuinely new jar-form. A standpoint selects an objectively real aspect rather than creates it; conditional assertion records finite access, not subjective manufacture.

Two objections matter. Rāmānuja asks whether opposed attributes are real, making their bearer incoherent, or unreal, undermining realism. Jainism replies that persistence and change belong to different real substance–mode respects. A second critic argues that relative knowledge destroys objectivity. Jainism locates relativity in limited cognition while retaining determinate truth-conditions.

Both replies leave a burden: real aspect-distinctions need defence, and grammatical qualification alone cannot prove them. Body-sized souls, material karma and omniscience further raise the ontological cost.

My verdict is therefore affirmative but qualified. Jainism is an ethically motivated, dynamic pluralist realism; its integration of identity, change and individual release is philosophically powerful only insofar as its aspect ontology and soul–matter account survive criticism.

**Why this earns marks:** it defines both predicates before testing them, grounds plurality in the soteriology rather than in anti-monism alone, marks the realism as dynamic, meets two distinct objections, and delivers a stance that names its own price.

## PYQ 10 — 2026 Q7(b), 15 marks

**Exact question:** Explain the meaning of seven steps of judgement (Saptabhanginaya) and its application in Jaina's Syadvada for advocating a philosophy of non-absolutism (Anekantavada).

**Printed directive:** "Explain the meaning … and its application … for advocating …" — a two-part explain with an application clause and a stated purpose.
**Examiner move:** three things are being asked, and reciting the seven forms discharges only the first. The script must show the forms being *used* within conditional assertion, and then show how that use advocates non-absolutism. The purpose clause is the examiner's hint about where the final third of the marks sit.
**Answer spine:** meaning of each step on one object → how conditional assertion deploys them → how that deployment advocates non-absolutism → safeguards against the contradiction and truth-value misreadings → residual.
**Core remediation:** Lesson 5 for the forms, Lesson 3 for ekānta and partial truth, Lesson 6 for the reflexivity residual. If the answer stops after the seven forms, it has answered one-third of the stem.

### Model answer

Many-sidedness, anekāntavāda, concerns real aspects. A finite standpoint, naya, selects one; syādvāda marks speech with syāt, “in some respect”, indexed to substance, place, time and mode.

Keep one clay jar throughout:

1. Syād asti: it exists here now in jar-mode.
2. Syād nāsti: it did not exist in jar-mode before production.
3. Syād asti ca nāsti ca: present existence and prior absence are asserted successively.
4. Syād avaktavyam: these opposed aspects resist one simultaneous unqualified predicate.
5. Syād asti ca avaktavyam: present jar-existence is affirmed together with that expressive limit.
6. Syād nāsti ca avaktavyam: pre-production jar-absence is affirmed together with the same limit.
7. Syād asti ca nāsti ca avaktavyam: present existence and prior absence are stated successively, together with the limit on simultaneous unqualified expression.

Affirmation, negation and inexpressibility generate three singles, three pairs and one triple: seven, not four primitives. “Is-and-is-not” is already a pair.

Application supplies definite scope, not doubt. The jar's present presence and prior absence are both true without becoming same-respect contradictories. Forms five to seven retain those respects rather than merely append an unexplained “inexpressible”.

This advocates non-absolutism because partial truth becomes ekānta only when presented as exhaustive. The scheme makes that illicit enlargement visible while preserving realism. Syāt is not probability, and the forms are not independent truth-values. The residual is whether the qualification-rule can defend its own force without self-exemption.

**Why this earns marks:** it answers all three parts of the stem, explains why the list closes at seven rather than merely reciting it, shows the forms doing work inside conditional assertion, connects that use directly to the advocacy of non-absolutism, and ends with both safeguards and a residual.

# ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS MODEL PRACTICE

## Original 10-marker

**Question:** Distinguish many-sidedness (anekāntavāda), standpoint theory (nayavāda) and conditional assertion (syādvāda). Answer in about 150 words.

**Printed directive:** "Distinguish" — three terms, so three separations plus the relation that survives them.

### Model answer

Many-sidedness, anekāntavāda, concerns reality's multiple genuine aspects. Standpoint theory, nayavāda, concerns finite cognition's selective grasp. Conditional assertion, syādvāda, concerns speech: syāt marks “in some respect”, indexed to substance, place, time and mode.

They differ by subject-matter: object, cognition and assertion. Their relation is nevertheless necessary. A many-sided jar exceeds any finite standpoint; an aspectual claim remains true within its scope; qualification prevents its conversion into exhaustive one-sidedness, ekānta. “The jar exists here now” need not deny its pre-production absence.

Sevenfold judgement expands qualified assertion into seven forms; it is not another name for the seven standpoints. Critics ask whether qualification weakens truth. Jainism retains objective aspects and determinate indices while denying finite exhaustiveness. The residual is whether this anti-absolutist rule can itself be qualified without losing force. The chain preserves distinction without turning the doctrines into unrelated lists.

**Why this earns marks:** it separates the three by their distinct subject-matter before relating them, blocks the commonest conflation by name, and still finds room for an objection and a qualified close inside a 10-mark budget.

## Original 15-marker

**Question:** “Jaina karma converts moral psychology into material metaphysics.” Critically discuss. Answer in about 200 words.

**Printed directive:** a quotation plus "Critically discuss" — the quotation must be assessed as a claim, not merely illustrated.

### Model answer

The quotation correctly identifies an ontological commitment, but does not by itself establish a category mistake. Jaina karma is subtle matter, pudgala. Activity opens influx and determines type/quantity; passion determines duration/intensity. Psychic bondage is the deluded soul-state, material bondage actual adhesion. Passion-free activity yields momentary bondage; passion fixes durable association.

The gain is integration: ethics changes what binds, not merely how conduct is judged. Eight karmas connect cognition and orientation with feeling, body, status and lifespan. Stoppage prevents influx; shedding clears the deposit through fruition and purification. Endurance also contributes to nirjarā.

The oily-dust analogy shows activity providing contact and passion supplying adhesion. Yet an analogy cannot prove that conscious states literally attract particles. Jainism invokes the embodied soul's space-points and body-wide presence; a critic can dispute that ontology and the obscurability of intrinsic perfection.

My verdict is therefore conditional: Jainism converts moral psychology into material metaphysics with a precise causal structure. That yields a coherent path of purification, but its coherence is not independent verification of soul–matter interaction. Calling the conversion a category error requires further argument against those commitments.

**Why this earns marks:** it grades the quotation instead of endorsing or rejecting it, supplies the parameter structure that proves the "conversion" claim, states the gain and the price separately, and closes with a verdict on the quoted words themselves.

## Original 20-marker

**Question:** Evaluate Jainism as a philosophy of permanence-in-change, qualified knowledge and liberation by purification. Answer in about 300 words.

**Printed directive:** "Evaluate", across three named axes — so each axis needs a strength, a strain and a judgement, unified by one thesis.

### Model answer

Jainism integrates three domains through one structure: an enduring real acquires changing conditions. Its strength is that identity survives transformation; its cost is the ontology required to make this precise.

Metaphysically, reality combines origination, decay and persistence. Substance endures while modes change. Gold remains through bracelet-to-ring transformation; clay persists while jar-form is genuinely new. This mediates Sāṃkhya's pre-existence and Nyāya's new-production emphases. Six substances and many individual souls make Jainism pluralistic; objective qualities and modes make it realistic. The objection is that opposed attributes make their bearer incoherent. Distinct-respect predication answers formally, but the reality of those distinctions still needs defence.

Epistemologically, a many-sided object exceeds finite cognition. Karmic limitation reinforces that partiality. A standpoint can therefore reach truth without exhausting its object; syāt marks definite scope, and sevenfold judgement organises affirmation, negation and expressive limit. This preserves partial truth without scepticism. Yet the anti-absolutist rule risks self-exemption if unqualified and loss of universal force if qualified. Reflexive qualification meets the first concern without conclusively eliminating the second.

Soteriologically, karma is subtle material association. Activity determines type/quantity; passion determines duration/intensity. Passionless activity binds momentarily, while passion fixes durable adhesion. Stoppage and shedding prevent new influx and remove the deposit. Three jewels, vows, austerities and fourteen stages integrate insight with purification. Ghātiyā destruction yields embodied omniscience; activity-cessation and aghātiyā exhaustion yield individual siddhahood. The strongest burden is literal soul–matter interaction, for which pradeśas provide an internal rather than neutral explanation.

The verdict is qualified realism: an integrated account of continuity, limited knowing and ethical purification, compelling in structure but conditional on aspect-realism and material karma.

**Why this earns marks:** it supplies a unifying thesis instead of three unconnected sections, gives each named axis a strength and a specific strain, carries exact doctrinal vocabulary throughout, and ends with a graded verdict that states its own condition.

## A1 — Is sevenfold judgement a many-valued logic?

| Reading | Gain | Risk | Safe use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proto-many-valued | highlights more than simple yes/no forms | turns avaktavya into a third truth-value | modern analogy only |
| Paraconsistent analogy | prompts a comparison of ways to avoid triviality | mistakes different-respect predication for a non-explosive consequence relation | analogy only; state the different mechanisms |
| Modal | highlights condition and respect | imports possible-world machinery | clarify qualification only |
| Context-indexed predication | fits standpoint, time, place and mode | less formally spectacular | safest primary reading |

**The paraconsistency comparison, carefully qualified.** ⚠️ Paraconsistency is a property of a **consequence relation**: from A and not-A an arbitrary B does not follow merely because of the contradiction. That is non-explosion. **Dialetheism** is the separate thesis that some contradictions are true; adopting a paraconsistent consequence relation does not entail it.

✅ Jaina indexing takes a different route. “The jar exists here now” and “the jar did not exist before production” are F at one index and not-F at another, not A and not-A under an identical index. Clarifying the respects **dissolves the apparent contradiction**; it neither validates true contradictions nor establishes that Jainism rejects explosion. Syāt qualifies assertions, and avaktavya marks an expressive limit rather than a truth-value gap or glut.

⚠️ The analogy is limited to a shared concern with avoiding trivialisation of discourse, **not a shared formal solution**. Paraconsistency handles contradictory premises non-explosively; Jaina qualification denies that these indexed premises form a contradiction in the first place. Use one comparison only when invited, with this disanalogy attached.

**Objection:** seven forms collapse once qualifications are explicit.
**Reply:** their value is disciplinary, organising affirmation, negation and expressive limit.
**Residual:** formalisation can illuminate structure while distorting metaphysical and soteriological context.

**Deployment line:** “Saptabhaṅgī is safest read as context-indexed disciplined predication; syāt is an operator and avaktavya is not simply a third truth-value.”

## A3 — Anekāntavāda and Madhyamaka

| Axis | Jainism | Madhyamaka |
|---|---|---|
| Object | real substances with aspects/modes | dharmas empty of intrinsic nature |
| Conditionality | standpoint-conditioned predicates | dependent origination and emptiness |

**Stop rule:** a Western parallel never substitutes for the Jaina argument, never opens an answer, and never appears without its disanalogy. If the disanalogy will not fit, the comparison does not belong in the script.

**Disciplined model:** "The doctrine invites comparison with perspectivism, since both refuse a view from nowhere; but the parallel breaks exactly where it matters, because Jaina aspects belong to the object rather than to the viewpoint. That is why the position is a qualified realism and not a relativism." Stop.

## Remedial MCQs — answers and all-option explanations

### Remedial MCQ 17 — answer

**Correct answer: A**

- **A — Correct:** Anekāntavāda is a realist thesis: many-sidedness belongs to the object itself.
- **B — Incorrect:** Both modes and substance are real independently of finite viewpoints.
- **C — Incorrect:** Properly scoped claims can each be true; conflict between unqualified reports does not cancel their objective aspectual content.
- **D — Incorrect:** Omniscience perfects cognition; it does not create a previously unreal object.

### Remedial MCQ 18 — answer

**Correct answer: B**

- **A — Incorrect:** Favourable embodiment remains saṃsāric; pain-reduction is not complete release.
- **B — Correct:** It keeps the ethical ranking while denying that any karma is itself liberating.
- **C — Incorrect:** The siddha retains neither favourable nor unfavourable karma.
- **D — Incorrect:** Good activity can produce merit; ethical preference does not identify it with stoppage.

### Remedial MCQ 19 — answer

**Correct answer: C**

- **A — Incorrect:** The indices give scope to assertions, but adding them to the standpoint list does not supply the seven demanded bhaṅgas.
- **B — Incorrect:** The parable illustrates partial access; it cannot replace the formal sevenfold predication scheme.
- **C — Correct:** Give the bhaṅgas in order, then show each is uttered from an appropriate naya.
- **D — Incorrect:** Illustrating each naya enriches the standpoint list; it still does not supply the seven qualified forms requested by the printed saptabhaṅgī question.

### Remedial MCQ 20 — answer

**Correct answer: D**

- **A — Incorrect:** Closing the inlet does not erase the deposit; old karma needs fruition or purification.
- **B — Incorrect:** Stoppage and shedding distinguish operations, not karma-classes.
- **C — Incorrect:** The two functions are exactly reversed here.
- **D — Correct:** Prevention and elimination are jointly necessary; neither alone empties the store.

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

## 1. Authority and boundary

- Syllabus: theory of reality; saptabhaṅgīnaya; bondage and liberation.
- *Tattvārthasūtra*: Umāsvāti/Umāsvāmī; cross-sectarian systematic grammar.
- Jina = conqueror; Tīrthaṅkara = liberated ford-maker/teacher, not creator.
- Twenty-four Tīrthaṅkaras in the present cycle: Ṛṣabha first, Pārśva twenty-third, Mahāvīra twenty-fourth — so Mahāvīra renews, he does not found. Framework only; no legendary chronology.
- Śvetāmbara and Digambara: shared doctrinal grammar, divergent canon and monastic practice.
- Nāstika = rejects Vedic authority, not soul or spiritual perfection.
- Beginningless cosmos; production/destruction concern modes.

## 2. Reality: permanent-changing real

```text
sat = utpāda + vyaya + dhrauvya
modes originate/cease + substance persists
```

- Anekāntavāda: objective many-sidedness, not tolerance or subjectivism.
- Dravya–guṇa–paryāya: substance, abiding quality, changing mode. Guṇa cannot be lost without losing the substance; paryāya arises and ceases. Consciousness = guṇa of jīva; pleasure, delusion, omniscience = paryāyas. Nominalist objection: why a substance at all? Reply: changes presuppose a subject of change.
- Six dravyas: jīva, pudgala, dharma, adharma, ākāśa, kāla.
- Five astikāyas: all except time.
- Dharma/adharma: media of motion/rest, not merit/demerit and not pushes or forces; they are enabling conditions co-extensive with lokākāśa.
- Parsimony objection (Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika): idle extra substances; reply: they supply the general condition of motion/rest and fix the cosmic boundary; residual: no independent proof against an unbounded-cosmos rival.
- Ākāśa: one substance divided into inhabited lokākāśa (holding the other five substances) and empty alokākāśa beyond; its work is accommodation, unlike Nyāya’s sound-substratum reached by inference, with direction assigned there to dik.
- Jīva: many, beginningless, intrinsically conscious bearer of infinite knowledge, perception, bliss and power. Plurality argued from soteriology — each career of bondage and release has its own bearer. Against Nyāya: consciousness is the soul's nature, not a quality arising through mind-contact.
- Living beings: mukta-jīva (liberated), baddha-jīva (bound); among the bound, sthāvara (one-sense, touch only — earth-, water-, fire-, air-, plant-bodied) and trasa (two- to five-sensed). This grounds radical ahiṃsā.
- Ajīva = the whole non-conscious domain. Pudgala is its only form-bearing (rūpa) member: divisible, aggregative, bearing colour, taste, smell and touch; bodies, sense-organs, speech, breath, embodied mind and karmic matter are its configurations.
- Pudgala atoms: one atomic kind only — elemental differentiation belongs to developed modes and combinations, not to fixed atom-sorts; sound is a material modification.
- Embodied jīva has pradeśas, co-extensive with its body (lamp filling a room). Buddhist (Dharmakīrti-style) attack: change versus causal efficacy — if it acts it changes, if it cannot change it cannot act; reply: permanent as dravya, changing as paryāya; residual: strong against anyone denying the substance–mode division. Nyāya attack: an eternal self cannot expand, contract or have parts; reply: pradeśas are not gross magnitude and are not detachable fragments, and material karma explains concrete embodiment better than an unseen potency; residual: the interaction remains contested.
- Causation (qualified sadasatkāryavāda): wholly identical makes production empty, wholly new lets anything arise from anything; so the effect is continuous as substance and novel as mode. Clay persists, jar-form is new. Mediates Sāṃkhya satkāryavāda and Nyāya asatkāryavāda. Objection: a contradiction; reply: indexed to substance and mode; residual: needs a real substance–mode division.
- Pluralistic = many irreducibles; realistic = viewpoint-independent objects/aspects.

## 3. Knowledge and partiality

| Knowledge | Status | Recall |
|---|---|---|
| Mati | mediated | sense/mind |
| Śruta | mediated | verbal/sign; not blind faith |
| Avadhi | direct, limited | material objects |
| Manaḥparyāya | direct, limited | another mind |
| Kevala | direct, unlimited | omniscience |

- Strict pratyakṣa is soul-unmediated; ordinary perception is only conventionally direct — **sāṃvyavahārika pratyakṣa**, which means conventionally direct, never "invalid".
- **Both halves of the bridge, named:** the object is **ananta-dharmātmakaṃ vastu**, constituted by endlessly many aspects; the knower's ordinary cognition is **kṣāyopaśamika**, arising from partial destruction-cum-subsidence of knowledge-obscuring karma and therefore necessarily partial.
- Developed indirect cognition includes inference, testimony, memory, recognition and tarka (Akalaṅka, Māṇikyanandi, Hemacandra, Yaśovijaya).
- Nyāya does **not** treat memory as an independent pramāṇa; its objection is that memory merely reproduces an earlier cognition and yields nothing novel.
- Jaina reply: recollection and recognition are valid when they connect present awareness with a real object or past experience; validity is successful reach, not novelty. Residual: Nyāya may re-describe recognition as inference.
- Wrong cognition under mithyā-darśana: kumati (mis-oriented mati), kuśruta (mis-oriented śruta), vibhaṅga (**perverted avadhi**). No perverted manaḥparyāya or kevala. Content can be accurate while orientation is deluded — a learned quotation used for pride is kuśruta. Nyāya objection: this confuses truth with moral psychology; reply: bondage conditions the knower as a whole; residual: a purely alethic notion of truth is not thereby shown defective.
- Partiality = many-sided object + karmically limited knower.
- Partial truth is **not** falsehood. It becomes falsehood at one point: when absolutised unscoped into **ekānta** (one-sidedness) — Jainism's diagnosis of the rival schools. Blind men and the elephant: conditionally right, absolutely wrong; pedagogical, never probative.
- Direct can be limited; “all empirical, therefore relative” fails.

## 4. Seven standpoints

> **Mnemonic:** Purpose–Class–Particular–Present–Usage–Etymology–Function.

1. Naigama—common/purposive.
2. Saṃgraha—generic.
3. Vyavahāra—practical particular.
4. Ṛjusūtra—present mode.
5. Śabda—linguistic usage.
6. Samabhirūḍha—etymological precision.
7. Evambhūta—name while function is performed.

Naya is a viewpoint; bhaṅga is a qualified proposition.

- **Graded strictness on the word-side:** śabda **tolerates ordinary usage** while noting that expression conditions assertion; samabhirūḍha splits near-synonyms by their roots; evambhūta applies a name only during the relevant function. Śabda-naya ≠ śruta-jñāna.
- **Worked pair:** Śakra = powerful one (*śak*, to be able); Purandara = fortress-destroyer. Ordinary usage identifies the same Indra, samabhirūḍha separates power from destruction, and evambhūta restricts the latter name to performance of that function.
- **Presupposition:** language itself contributes to one-sidedness if its scope is not critically examined.
- **Objection:** the linguistic nayas are overly scholastic. **Reply:** many philosophical disputes are verbal absolutisations of context-bound usage, and these nayas are the diagnostic for that failure. **Residual:** this shows the distinctions are useful, not that exactly three verbal standpoints are required.

## 5. Syādvāda and sevenfold judgement

- Syāt = “in some respect,” indexed to substance, place, time and mode.
- **Three primitives, not four:** asti, nāsti, avaktavya. Singly → 3 forms; in pairs → 3; all together → 1. Three plus three plus one closes the list at seven. "Is-and-is-not" is the first pair, never a fourth primitive.
- Seven: is; is not; is-and-is-not in distinct respects; indescribable; is-and-indescribable; is-not-and-indescribable; is/is-not/indescribable.
- Avaktavya = limit of one simultaneous unqualified predicate.
- Not probability, same-respect contradiction, seven truth-values or mystical silence.
- Chain: anekānta -> naya -> syāt -> saptabhaṅgī.

| Form | The same clay jar, with respects visible |
|---|---|
| 1. syād asti | exists here now in jar-mode |
| 2. syād nāsti | did not exist in jar-mode before production |
| 3. syād asti ca nāsti ca | present existence and prior absence, successively indexed |
| 4. syād avaktavyam | those opposed aspects resist one simultaneous unqualified predicate |
| 5. syād asti ca avaktavyam | here-now jar-existence plus the expressive limit involving its pre-production absence |
| 6. syād nāsti ca avaktavyam | pre-production jar-absence plus the same expressive limit involving its present existence |
| 7. syād asti ca nāsti ca avaktavyam | both indexed aspects, successively stated, plus the limit on simultaneous unqualified expression |

## 6. Relativism and critics

- Dilemma, blade one: absolute anti-absolutism self-exempts; conditional rule may lack force.
- Dilemma, blade two (triviality): if it survives by weakening, it says only that things can be viewed differently. Reply: truth is aspect-conditioned, not opinion-equal; the aspects are in the object; and it generates a determinate calculus that rules things out. The two blades cannot both land.
- Reply: objective truth-conditions remain; finite claims are non-exhaustive; qualification is reflexive.
- Kevala permits completeness but does not settle every finite-warrant objection.
- Śaṃkara: contradiction/reflexivity, **and the practical limb** — if all is "somehow yes and somehow no," action loses clear guidance. Reply: indexed predicates, and *syāt* names the respect rather than withholding the verdict, so the judgement stays determinate for the agent acting under that respect. Residual: it does not settle a choice between competing respects.
- Rāmānuja: **real** opposed attributes make the bearer incoherent; **unreal** attributes surrender Jaina realism. Reply: substance persists while modes change; both aspects are real but not same-respect contradictories. Residual: indexing alone cannot establish that the aspect distinction is genuinely real.
- Verdict: qualified realism, conditional on aspect ontology.

## 7. Bondage mechanics

```text
wrong vision + non-restraint + carelessness + passion + activity
activity opens āsrava and sets type/quantity; passion fixes durable bandha
bhāvabandha <-> dravyabandha
```

- Seven tattvas: jīva, ajīva, āsrava, bandha, saṃvara, nirjarā, mokṣa.
- Nine padārthas add puṇya/pāpa; merit remains karmic.
- Bhāvabandha: passion-coloured soul-state.
- Dravyabandha: material karmic adhesion.
- Present bhāva attracts dravya; inherited dravya conditions bhāva.
- Four parameters: activity -> type/quantity; passion -> duration/intensity.
- **Two bondages by name:** **īryāpathika** = momentary, from genuinely passion-free activity; **sāmparāyika** = durable, from passion-coloured activity. Careful walking alone does not prove inward passionlessness. Oily dust explains contact versus durable adhesion, not empirical proof of particles.

## 8. Eight karmas

| Ghātiyā | Aghātiyā |
|---|---|
| knowledge-obscuring | feeling-producing |
| perception-obscuring | body-making |
| deluding | status-determining |
| obstructive | lifespan-determining |

Mohanīya is most dangerous; ghātiyā destruction permits omniscience; aghātiyā exhaustion permits final disembodiment.

- **Two subtypes of mohanīya:** **darśana-mohanīya** corrupts right vision; **cāritra-mohanīya** corrupts right conduct. Delusion is the pivot because it is the only karma attacking both the orientation that starts the path and the discipline that walks it.

## 9. Liberation technology

```text
saṃvara = stop new influx     <- vows, gupti, samiti, virtues,
                                 anuprekṣā, pariṣaha-jaya,
                                 tapas, cāritra
nirjarā = shed old karma      <- fruition, tapas, purified conduct,
                                 endurance also contributes (9.8)
both required · main roles are not exclusive compartments
```

- Three jewels: right vision, knowledge and conduct.
- Five vows: non-violence, truth, non-stealing, chastity, non-possession.
- Ascetic great vows; graded householder observance; care extends to one-sense jīvas.
- **Stoppage supports:** gupti, samiti, virtues, contemplation, endurance and conduct. Their main placement is not an exclusivity claim.
- **Endurance:** *Tattvārthasūtra* 9.8 links bearing afflictions to remaining on the path **and nirjarā**. Equanimity avoids renewed passion while maturing karma is borne and shed.
- **Ten virtues:** forgiveness (kṣamā), humility (mārdava), straightforwardness (ārjava), purity/non-greed (śauca), truthfulness (satya), restraint (saṃyama), austerity (tapas), renunciation (tyāga), non-attachment (ākiñcanya), celibacy (brahmacarya). Each is uttama, directed beyond worldly reward.
- **External six:** fasting (anaśana); reduced intake (avamaudarya); limits on acquisition (vṛtti-parisaṃkhyāna); taste-renunciation (rasa-parityāga); solitary lodging (vivikta-śayyāsana); voluntary bodily endurance (kāya-kleśa).
- **Internal six:** expiation (prāyaścitta) corrects faults; reverence (vinaya) counters pride; service (vaiyāvṛttya) helps practitioners; study (svādhyāya) sustains reflection; relinquishment (vyutsarga) loosens “I/mine”; meditation (dhyāna) disciplines attention.
- **Shedding:** natural fruition, austerity and purified conduct remove existing karma; endurance also contributes. Tapas is not fasting alone, and externally imposed bodily suffering is not automatically purification.
- **Encountered/undertaken:** pariṣaha-jaya bears encountered afflictions; kāya-kleśa names voluntarily undertaken bodily discipline. Both must be understood through passion-removal, not as personal prescriptions.

| Stage | Canonical name | Distinct recall |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | mithyā-dṛṣṭi | false orientation |
| 2 | sāsvādana / sāsādana | falling with lingering taste of right vision |
| 3 | miśra / samyag-mithyā-dṛṣṭi | mixed right/wrong orientation |
| 4 | avirata-samyag-dṛṣṭi | right vision without restraint |
| 5 | deśavirata | partial restraint/lay vows |

- Two ladders: **upaśama-śreṇi** only subsides delusion, reaches stage 11 (upaśānta-moha) and necessarily falls back; **kṣapaka-śreṇi** destroys delusion, reaches stage 12 (kṣīṇa-moha) and proceeds irreversibly. Ash over a coal versus a fire burnt out.

## 10. Bound soul, kevalin and siddha

| Condition | Karma/body | Cognition | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bound | eight karmas, embodiment/rebirth | limited | saṃsāra |
| Sayoga-kevalī | ghātiyā gone; aghātiyā/body remain | omniscient | embodied arhat |
| Ayoga-kevalī | activity ceases | omniscient | pre-siddha |
| Siddha | all eight gone; no body/rebirth | infinite | siddha-śilā |

- Individuality persists; no merger or annihilation.
- Sayoga-kevalī is the qualified Jaina liberated-in-life answer: ghātiyā are gone, but aghātiyā and activity remain. Passionless activity permits momentary īryāpathika, not durable sāmparāyika. The uncovered lamp illustrates perfected cognition before disembodiment. Objection: embodiment implies cognitive limitation; reply: the two classes do different work; residual: that distinction itself remains disputable.
- Beginningless karma can terminate, but obscurability remains a puzzle.
- **Upward ascent:** the liberated soul, no longer weighed down by karmic matter, rises to siddha-śilā at the top of the cosmos. Objection: spatial ascent of a non-material soul looks mythic. Reply: report the cosmological doctrine faithfully and keep it separate from modern evaluation. Residual: the faithful report does not defend the extension-and-location commitment.
- Soul–matter interaction is the central external burden.
- **Sallekhanā/santhārā:** doctrinal relinquishment of food **and passion** at unavoidable death/incurable decline. Passion can reinforce durable adhesion; no maximum-influx inference follows. Equanimity curbs passion without making every active association impossible. Five forbidden motives: death-desire, desire to live on, attachment to friends, recollection of pleasures, hope of reward. Five contrast axes: inner state, timing, means, visibility and aim. Observable procedure/publicity/supervision are evidence, not proof of equanimity or absent death-desire; inner motives remain inferred. The tradition's suicide contrast is not a universal clinical claim. Ethical/legal permissibility is not settled; give no case, statute or personal advice.

- Reality -> judgement: sat -> dravya/guṇa/paryāya -> anekānta -> naya -> syāt -> seven.
- Karma -> liberation: five causes -> influx -> two bondages -> eight karmas -> stoppage/shedding -> kevalin -> siddha.

## 13. Examiner traps

1. Anekāntavāda, nayavāda, syādvāda and saptabhaṅgī are connected, not synonymous.
2. Six dravyas, five astikāyas; time is the exception.
3. Dharma/adharma as substances are motion/rest media — enabling conditions, never pushes, forces or moral merit/demerit.
4. Ordinary perception is not strict Jaina pratyakṣa.
5. Seven nayas are not seven bhaṅgas.
6. Syāt is definite qualification, not doubt.
7. Avaktavya is expressive limit, not contradiction.
8. Karma is material pudgala.
9. Influx differs from bondage; stoppage differs from shedding.
10. Merit is not liberation.
11. Embodied omniscient arhat differs from siddha.
12. Epistemic relativity is compatible with realism.
13. Lokākāśa and alokākāśa are divisions of space, not things space accommodates; Jaina ākāśa accommodates, Nyāya’s bears sound.
14. Jaina atoms are of one kind; elemental sorts are modes, not fixed essences.
15. Nyāya rejects memory as an independent pramāṇa; developed Jaina logic admits recollection and recognition.
16. Guṇa is not paryāya: a quality cannot be lost while the substance remains, a mode can.
17. Vibhaṅga is perverted avadhi specifically, not a general word for error; and there is no perverted manaḥparyāya or kevala.
18. Endurance is not preventive only: 9.8 connects it to maintaining the path and nirjarā; shedding is not fasting alone.
19. The suppression ladder stops at stage 11 and must fall; only the annihilation ladder passes stage 12.
20. Jaina consciousness is intrinsic to the soul; the Nyāya self is conscious only through contact with the internal organ.

## 14. Optional advanced refinements and bounded expert flags

> Enrichment only. Nothing here is required for any demand or PYQ answered above; the Core lessons already
> carry each underlying objection, reply and residual.

**Optional Advanced (A1–A3).**

- **A1 — qualified logic analogy.** Paraconsistency means non-explosive consequence, not dialetheism's claim of true contradictions. Jaina indexing dissolves the apparent contradiction; it does not validate contradictions or establish rejection of explosion. The analogy concerns avoiding trivialisation, through different mechanisms. Context-indexed predication remains the safest reading; syāt qualifies assertion and avaktavya marks expressive limit.
- **A2 — two readings of the body-sized soul.** Literal ontological (the jīva really resizes; maximum strain on immateriality) versus restrained capacity (pradeśa marks variable manifestation; risks softening the classical claim). The more literal the reading, the sharper the Buddhist change-objection; the more restrained, the stronger the Nyāya charge of retreat.
- **A3 — many-sidedness and Madhyamaka.** Both attack one-sided assertion, but Jainism preserves real substance while Madhyamaka problematises intrinsic nature; the Jaina danger is reflexive relativism, the Madhyamaka danger a nihilistic misreading. Objection: the difference is then merely rhetorical. Reply: refusing absolutism is a shared method, not a shared ontology — the difference is located in the object. Residual: the reply holds only if aspect-realism itself survives the Rāmānuja charge. One contrast sentence unless the stem asks for Buddhist comparison.

**Bounded Expert (E1–E3).**

- **E1 — specialist attribution:** Kundakunda's *Samayasāra* supplies the separate niścaya/vyavahāra two-level distinction; do not conflate its conventional level with the practical-particular naya. Siddhasena Divākara and Samantabhadra identify early logic without invented dates; pūraṇa–galana is only an etymological aid. Core's conventional-perception and partiality vocabulary is not repeated as Expert doctrine. Two items maximum.
- **E2 — citation risk:** qualify sectarian time-atoms; verify any sūtra number against its edition; do not reconstruct quotations or logician dates. A verified 9.8 citation is legitimate. On sallekhanā, retain only the legal-citation boundary, not a second doctrinal exposition. Hedge the detail, never the demand.
- **E3 — controlled external comparison:** perspectivism, Hegelian dialectic, Leibnizian monads, Schweitzer's reverence for life, mechanistic psychology — each usable for one line of contact followed compulsorily by its disanalogy. The logic analogy belongs to A1, not here.

## 15. Final qualified verdict

Jainism is a rigorous **qualified pluralist realism**. It explains permanence through substance, change through modes, partial knowledge through standpoints, responsible speech through conditional assertion, and liberation through prevention and elimination of material karmic bondage. Its architecture is unusually integrated and ethically powerful. Its residual costs are equally clear: body-sized enduring souls, literal soul–matter interaction, reflexive pressure on non-absolutism and external unverifiability of omniscience. A high-scoring answer preserves both coherence and burden.
