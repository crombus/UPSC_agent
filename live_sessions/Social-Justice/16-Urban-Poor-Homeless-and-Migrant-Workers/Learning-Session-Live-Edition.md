# Social Justice 16 — Urban Poor, Homeless and Migrant Workers

A city offers work but does not necessarily offer a roof, a secure place to trade or access to the ration card issued in a different State. This session asks what the city owes people whose work keeps it running. **Follow the person, not just the scheme:** need → eligibility → actual access → remedy → outcome. Learn the Basic mechanisms first; Advanced lessons then test their distributional limits.

*Different losses require different interventions; one person may face several simultaneously.*

Think of a mason arriving in a metro. A wage does not make her adequately housed; a roof does not ensure safe water; a ration card at home does not automatically produce dinner at destination. **Urban poverty is multidimensional**, not a synonym for a particular address. A homeless person has no shelter; a slum resident may have a dwelling but insecure tenure and services; an informal renter may be neither homeless nor a recorded slum resident. Migration is movement, not proof of destitution. Domicile is not citizenship. A city-wide average can rise while its least-served ward deteriorates.

**How deprivation compounds:** irregular earnings raise rent-arrears risk; insecure housing can push workers farther from job centres; relocation raises commuting cost and risks losing daily work; missing proof of residence obstructs registration or care. ⚠️ This is a causal explanation, not a claim that every migrant experiences the same chain. Gender, disability, age, children and occupational exposure modify the need: a woman arriving alone may require safe transport and a shelter with privacy; a family needs childcare and schooling.

✅ Census 2011's slum enumeration reported approximately 65.5 million people in slums, about 17.4% of the then urban population. It is an old slum-specific denominator, **not** a current count of all poor people, migrants or homeless persons. Ask separately about slum population, rooflessness, inadequate rentals and precarious earnings; no single series captures all.

Caste can shape access to occupations, neighbourhoods and rental markets; gender can affect wages, care burdens, safety and documentation; children may lose schooling, nutrition services or protection when a family moves. Heat, flood, fire or disease can destroy both a dwelling and the informal assets used to earn. ⚠️ These are intersecting risks, not labels that predetermine every person's experience. Record them separately so the most invisible member of a household does not disappear behind the household head.

**Objection:** one integrated urban-poverty scheme might simplify delivery. **Reply:** a shared intake and grievance desk can reduce duplication, but a shelter bed, tenancy, legal vending certificate and NFSA ration draw have different eligibility and remedies. ⚠️ Integration should link the person's needs without flattening the entitlements. **UPSC use:** GS-II answers open with the housing–livelihood–portability nexus; do not convert settlement statistics into a total migrant count. **Mini recap:** classify the missing capability before selecting the instrument.

### Revision notes

1. Urban poverty may combine insecure housing, work, services and entitlements.
2. A houseless person has no usable roof; a slum resident may have an inadequate or insecure dwelling.
3. An informal renter can be poor without appearing in either homelessness or notified-slum data.
4. Migration is movement, not proof of poverty, rooflessness or permanent settlement.
5. Domicile, place of work and citizenship are different legal or administrative ideas.
6. Slum population, homelessness, low income and migrant-worker counts require separate denominators.
7. Gender, caste, disability and age can alter safety, work, rent and service access.
8. Migrant children may face interrupted schooling, nutrition and protection even when adults find work.
9. Heat, flood, fire and health shocks can damage both shelter and livelihood assets.
10. Classify the missing capability before choosing a shelter, housing, livelihood or portability remedy.

### Concept check

**Question:** A worker rents a makeshift room near a market on weekdays but sleeps on the pavement between shifts on two nights. How would you record her housing situation without counting her twice or treating her weekday address as adequate housing?

<details>
<summary>Reveal concept-check answer after attempting</summary>

**Model answer:** Record one person and the timing of each sleeping arrangement: she has an inadequate rented roof on some nights and is roofless on the others. Link need to both immediate safe shelter and longer-term housing affordability and quality rather than coding her only by the market area's slum status.

**Misconception to avoid:** A single address or a single night's shelter status cannot describe changing exposure or establish adequate housing.

</details>

**Original Mains practice — 10 marks; answer in 150 words.** *Explain why identifying urban poverty solely by slum residence can misdirect welfare delivery.*

**Mains model answer:** ⚠️ Slum residence identifies one form of deprivation, not all urban poverty. Census 2011 slum enumeration describes a settlement-based population; a pavement sleeper may have no settlement address, while a migrant renting an unsafe room may live outside a notified slum. Both need support even though neither is captured by that classification. Conversely, a slum household's immediate deficit may be tenure or sanitation rather than a night shelter. DAY-NULM's Shelter for Urban Homeless addresses rooflessness, PMAY-U housing routes address eligible households' longer-term needs, and ONORC can assist NFSA beneficiaries who move. A city should map shelter need, rental conditions, work and service access separately, with assisted enrolment and complaints routes. Slum mapping remains useful for area-based investment, but it cannot measure every person's eligibility or prove that services reached those outside mapped settlements.

**Scoring guide (10):** classification and exclusions 3; contrasting named instruments 3; measurement and delivery consequence 2; qualified conclusion 2.

```text
Night without safe shelter
  ↓ outreach finds the person near work/transit site
  ↓ reachable 24-hour, all-weather SUH place
  ↓ bed + water + sanitation + safety
  ↓ referral to health, identity and livelihood services
  ↓ housing or family-reconnection option, where suitable
Outcome test: can the intended person actually enter and safely use it?
```

*A building is only the second arrow in a longer route from street exposure to safety.*

✅ The Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana–National Urban Livelihoods Mission (**DAY-NULM**) was MoHUA's urban livelihoods mission; it **ended on 30 September 2024**, according to the Ministry's 30 March 2026 parliamentary reply. Its **Shelter for Urban Homeless** (**SUH**) component provided for permanent all-weather shelters with round-the-clock access, bedding, water and sanitation, and referral connections to services. States/urban local bodies organise local delivery. The guideline benchmark of **one shelter per 100,000 urban population** is a planning norm, not proof of capacity or universal coverage. End of the central mission does not itself show every previously built shelter shut. Do not substitute DAY-NRLM, the *rural* livelihoods mission.

Suppose a city opens a distant building at the edge of town. A daily-wage worker must be at a labour chowk before dawn; an expensive trip from the shelter defeats its apparent availability. Count **accessible beds near need**, not only sanctioned sites. Examine hours, safety for women and children, disability access, privacy, food/health referrals, capacity on peak nights and treatment of people lacking documents. ⚠️ These are inspection criteria, not claims that every shelter fails.

**Strong objection:** permanent centrally located shelters face costly land and operating demands. **Reply:** local mapping and use of suitable existing premises can help, but a cheap remote bed should not be counted as an equivalent outcome. Sustained municipal staffing and partnerships must remain accountable to shelter users. Shelter is an immediate protective service; it is not title, a permanent dwelling or automatically a route to PMAY-U eligibility.

**UPSC use:** distinguish policy norm, constructed shelter, operational bed, actual night usage and improved safety. A credible GS-II evaluation follows each step and records exclusions and grievances. **Mini recap:** the destination is usable protection, not infrastructure alone.

### Revision notes

1. SUH was the urban-homeless shelter component of DAY-NULM under MoHUA.
2. The DAY-NULM mission period ended on 30 September 2024.
3. Mission closure does not by itself prove that every shelter closed or remained operational.
4. Permanent, all-weather and round-the-clock access describes the shelter design.
5. One shelter per 100,000 urban population is a planning benchmark, not a coverage finding.
6. Constructed site, operational bed, admission and safe use are different stages.
7. Location near work and transit can determine whether a nominally available bed is usable.
8. Privacy, women-and-child safety and disability access must be examined separately.
9. Water, sanitation, health referral and livelihood links determine shelter quality.
10. A shelter is immediate protection, not a tenancy, title or completed rehabilitation.

### Concept check

**Question:** Two shelters are equally close to the same labour market. One has many empty beds but women avoid it at night; the other is full and users report safe access. Which observations would reveal whether moving beds alone could solve the shortfall?

<details>
<summary>Reveal concept-check answer after attempting</summary>

**Model answer:** Compare operating hours, perceived and observed safety, privacy, entry/refusal records and use by women on different nights—not just bed capacity or distance. Moving beds without correcting an unsafe entrance or admission practice could leave nominal capacity unused.

**Misconception to avoid:** Physical proximity and an empty bed do not show the intended users can enter or stay safely.

</details>

**Original Mains practice — 10 marks; answer in 150 words.** *Examine why counting constructed shelters is inadequate for evaluating urban-homeless protection.*

**Mains model answer:** ✅ DAY-NULM's SUH component provided for all-weather urban shelters and links to basic services; the central mission ended in September 2024. ⚠️ A sanctioned or built facility is an input: it may be distant from a labour market, unsafe for women, inaccessible to disabled persons or unable to offer water, sanitation and health referral. A pavement-dweller working near a station may therefore remain unsheltered despite an available bed across the city. Urban local bodies should record operational capacity, geographical access, outreach, use across seasons, refusals, service referrals and user grievances, disaggregated for vulnerable groups. Proximity and quality are not arguments against building shelters; the design norm remains useful for planning. But sustained operating support and routes to suitable longer-term housing are needed if an overnight service is to reduce vulnerability rather than merely improve the construction count.

**Scoring guide (10):** SUH design 2; input–access–outcome chain 3; grounded user example 2; feasible qualified evaluation 3.

| Need | Instrument | What its existence does **not** prove |
|---|---|---|
| Immediate overnight safety | SUH shelter | Secure tenancy or ownership |
| Home construction on available land | PMAY-U 2.0 Beneficiary-Led Construction | Land available to every landless renter |
| Project-based affordable unit | PMAY-U 2.0 Affordable Housing in Partnership | Good location or actual occupancy |

| Eligible financed purchase/construction | PMAY-U 2.0 Interest Subsidy Scheme | Ability to obtain and service a loan |

*Match the tenure instrument to the person's resources, time horizon and location.*

✅ **PMAY-U 2.0**, the restructured urban housing mission launched in **2024**, has four verticals: **BLC, AHP, ARH and ISS** as shown above. Its **ARH** vertical includes eligible EWS/LIG migrants and people unable to purchase; rental housing is not a contradiction of housing policy. Under **ISS**, eligible EWS/LIG/MIG households can access an interest subsidy subject to loan and programme conditions; the dated official September 2026 housing brief describes a maximum subsidy of **₹1.80 lakh**. Avoid importing the *older* PMAY-U CLSS interest bands into the 2.0 ISS. The earlier PMAY-U mission's completion-period extension to **30 September 2026** is not the expiry of PMAY-U 2.0.

A household living in an informal settlement may need drainage, water, tenure assurance and nearby work more urgently than a distant ownership unit. **Slum upgrading** improves the existing area; it is not the same as giving an individual formal title. A worker without land cannot assume access to BLC merely because that vertical exists. **Tenure security** is protection against arbitrary displacement; full legal ownership is one possible, not the only, route to greater security.

✅ **Article 21 supplies a constitutional shelter-and-procedure floor, not an unconditional title.** In *Chameli Singh v. State of U.P.* (1995), the Supreme Court treated shelter as part of a meaningful right to life and explained that it is more than a bare roof: adequate living space, a safe and decent structure, water, sanitation and access to daily avocation matter. The judgment also qualified the State's positive obligation by its economic budgeting; it did not allot every claimant a particular plot or ownership title. In *Olga Tellis v. Bombay Municipal Corporation* (1985), the Constitution Bench held that livelihood is integral to Article 21 and recognised that eviction from a place near work may destroy livelihood. Yet it did not create a fundamental licence to occupy a pavement or public road: deprivation must rest on law and fair, just and reasonable procedure. Eviction and relocation must therefore be analysed through lawful authority, notice and hearing, recognition of tenants as well as owners, rehabilitation terms and continued access to work and school; neither Article 21 nor a project announcement alone settles title to a specific site.

**Objection:** an ownership-focused project provides a more durable asset than rental. **Reply:** assets matter where households can contribute and live near work; circular migrants and low-income renters need a credible rental option. Both approaches can fail if finance, land or services do not match household needs. ⚠️ Test **sanction → completion → allotment → occupancy → safe services and sustained affordability**, with separate counts and denominators. The September brief reports housing administrative stocks; it does not establish that every eligible homeless person or renter is housed.

**UPSC use:** GS-II distinguish mission verticals and household fit; never infer occupied housing from a sanction or old slum census figures. **Mini recap:** shelter, serviced settlement, rental and ownership answer different time horizons.

### Revision notes

1. PMAY-U 2.0 has BLC, AHP, ARH and ISS verticals.
2. BLC requires a feasible beneficiary-led land and construction route.
3. AHP supplies project-based affordable housing rather than an automatic allotment.
4. ARH addresses eligible rental need, including that of mobile low-income workers.
5. ISS is conditional interest support; it is not the earlier CLSS design.
6. The earlier PMAY-U completion extension is not PMAY-U 2.0's expiry date.
7. In-situ upgrading can improve services while preserving work and social networks.
8. Tenure security reduces arbitrary-displacement risk but is not identical to title.
9. Eviction analysis must test procedure, affected tenants, rehabilitation and livelihood continuity.
10. Sanction, completion, allotment, occupancy, affordability and adequate services are separate outcomes.
11. *Chameli Singh* links dignified shelter and civic amenities to Article 21, while *Olga Tellis* protects livelihood and fair procedure without creating an unconditional title or licence to encroach.

**Original Mains practice — 15 marks; answer in 250 words.** *Discuss why affordable rental housing must complement ownership and slum upgrading in a city with circular migration.*

**Mains model answer:** ⚠️ Repeated moves between origin and city make permanent ownership in the destination an unsuitable universal solution. PMAY-U 2.0 offers Beneficiary-Led Construction and Affordable Housing in Partnership, but BLC depends on a workable land/construction route and a project unit must be reachable from jobs. Its Affordable Rental Housing vertical recognises the need of eligible urban migrants and other low-income households who cannot or do not wish to purchase. Upgrading water and sanitation in an existing settlement can also preserve employment networks; it neither gives every tenant secure tenure nor substitutes for a rental unit. A construction worker arriving for a season may require affordable accommodation near a worksite while her family stays at origin. Yet rental projects can themselves fail if supply is remote, rent unaffordable or eligibility documents inaccessible. Municipalities should test completed, occupied and serviced units against actual rents, journey time and user grievances; shelter beds remain necessary for those without any roof. A balanced city strategy treats rental, upgrading and ownership as complementary responses to different durations and vulnerabilities, rather than measuring success by sanctions alone.

*Training, credit and collective organisation are means; durable income is the outcome to test.*

✅ During its mission period ending **30 September 2024**, DAY-NULM combined a **Self-Employment Programme** with interest support for eligible micro-enterprise borrowing, employment-linked skill training, urban self-help groups (**SHGs**) and the SUH shelter route. State Urban Livelihoods Missions and urban local bodies implemented local elements under MoHUA's mission architecture. The *2025–26 MoHUA Annual Report* records a subsequent D JAY-S livelihoods pilot through December 2025; its existence does not show an identical nationwide successor or continuing DAY-NULM allocations. DAY-NRLM, run for rural livelihoods by a different ministry, is not a second name for DAY-NULM.

An urban women's group may pool savings and help a member apply for enterprise finance; the loan is still a liability and demand for her product must exist. Training a newly arrived worker cannot create a job where employers are absent, nor does a completed course demonstrate a stable wage. **Mechanism:** recognition and mobilisation → access to skill/finance → actual job or enterprise revenue → reduced vulnerability. ⚠️ Gender, childcare, language and transport can interrupt each link. Caste-linked occupational segmentation or discrimination in hiring and rental markets can also narrow the real set of choices even when training is formally open. The term **social capital** denotes relationships and trust that can ease coordination; it is not a substitute for bank terms or public services.

**Objection:** subsidised loans may reward borrowing without sustainable businesses. **Reply:** careful market linkage, repayment monitoring and collective support can improve prospects, but evaluate earnings and debt stress, not only loan count. Complementary street-space protections come next: credit does not keep a vendor at her pitch. **UPSC use:** distinguish urban from rural mission and service from livelihood outcome. **Mini recap:** count not only people trained or financed but decent, durable earning opportunities.

1. DAY-NULM was the urban livelihoods mission under MoHUA, distinct from rural DAY-NRLM.

3. The Self-Employment Programme supported eligible micro-enterprise finance.
4. Skills, urban SHGs and social mobilisation addressed different livelihood constraints.

7. Training completion is an input; placement retention and net earnings are outcomes.
8. A loan is repayable capital, not a grant or proof of viable demand.
9. Childcare, safety, language, caste and transport can interrupt an apparently open livelihood route.
10. Evaluate earnings after travel, care and debt costs rather than counting enrolments alone.

A newly arrived woman may have learned a trade but still lack childcare, safe travel or a permitted trading space; a bank loan creates repayment obligations before sales stabilise. Urban SHGs can improve information and mutual support, yet cannot replace functioning local markets or public services. Urban local bodies and State missions should track placement retention, enterprise earnings and debt alongside training and disbursement, with language support and childcare referrals where needed. The answer is not to abandon training or microfinance, but to combine them with local job demand, safe access and responsive grievances, particularly for mobile workers.

*The legal place to work and the money to stock it are complementary, not interchangeable.*

✅ The **Street Vendors (Protection of Livelihood and Regulation of Street Vending) Act, 2014** provides for **Town Vending Committees (TVCs)**, surveys and certificates, vending plans and procedural safeguards relating to eviction and relocation. Regulation can balance passage and public space with livelihood; it is not an unconditional right to block every street. A certificate and scheme eligibility are separate inquiries.

✅ PM SVANidhi, launched in **2020** and administered under MoHUA, provides collateral-free working-capital loans, interest subsidy and digital-transaction incentives. **Beware a changed fact:** the earlier tranche sequence was **₹10,000 → ₹20,000 → ₹50,000**. The official **December 2025** briefing describes the restructured upper limits **₹15,000 → ₹25,000 → ₹50,000** and lending extended to **31 March 2030**. “Up to” is a ceiling, not an automatic award. Later tranches depend on repayment and eligibility; a loan is not a grant. The *Economic Survey 2025–26* reports a follow-up survey of beneficiaries, but a borrower-only association cannot establish that every street vendor received credit or that credit alone caused a city-wide income rise.

Imagine a fruit seller with a loan but no secure vending pitch. *The same concentration that attracts a worker also concentrates land pressure and service demand.*

**Agglomeration economies** are gains from concentrating firms, workers and suppliers: a busy construction market offers many employers, while nearby contractors and migrant acquaintances lower job-search costs. Larger cities can offer diverse formal and informal work and specialist services that small towns often cannot. Origin-side underemployment or a crop shock may simultaneously push a household member to leave. ⚠️ This is a mechanism, not a claim that every migrant is distressed or that large cities offer better net earnings after rent.

The institutional puzzle is collective action. MoHUA designs shelter, housing and vendor-credit support; an urban local body identifies sites and vendors; States implement housing and food entitlements; lenders disburse credit; the Food and Public Distribution system reconciles NFSA portable draws; labour authorities and employers implement the OSHWC framework. A worker might contact several offices, none of which owns the whole problem. **e-Shram** can help identify unorganised workers but does not settle a ration, shelter, housing, vendor or labour claim simply because the profile exists. The DAY-NULM financing design uses **60:40** Centre–State and **90:10** for applicable north-eastern/special-category jurisdictions; these are scheme-specific historical design ratios, not financing rules for PMAY-U 2.0 or proof that any State released its share. Check the applicable year's sanction before applying either ratio to an individual project. Urban capacity also depends on predictable intergovernmental transfers, realistic own-source revenue, available land and recurring operation-and-maintenance budgets; a capital grant can build an asset that a municipality cannot keep staffed or repaired.

Three recurring failure chains demand different repairs. **Shelter:** no centrally located land, funding delay or weak service links → distant or underused facility → maintain local outreach, suitable space and safe services. **Portability:** mismatched records, low awareness or ePoS outage → failed food draw → assisted correction, alternative channel where allowed and tracked complaints. A destination State's fiscal or stock-accounting concerns require transparent inter-State reconciliation, not exclusion of an eligible migrant. **Urban project:** selected investment zone → displacement or unserved peripheral ward → participation and distributional appraisal. ⚠️ These are diagnostic possibilities, not documented outcomes for every city. An ULB's target linked to a dated homeless count can prompt planning, but a count must be updated and access verified before treating a target as universal capacity.

There is also a policy-transition risk. MoHUA's *Annual Report 2025–26*, posted June 2026, says the **Deen Dayal Jan Ajeevika Yojana–Shehari (D JAY-S)** pilot operated in selected cities from **October 2024 to December 2025** following the former DAY-NULM; it focused on construction, transport, gig, domestic, waste and care workers. It records profiling and welfare linkages during the pilot, not confirmed nationwide rollout or individual long-term welfare outcomes. DAY-NULM ended in September 2024; previously built SUH shelters need separate present-day operating evidence. ⚠️ An occupational focus helps mobile workers only when its financing, reach and results are demonstrated.

**Fiscal and governance objection:** fine-grained monitoring burdens constrained city administrations and privacy may be endangered by joining registers. **Reply:** collect the minimum lawful data needed to follow outcomes; publish aggregate disaggregated results and refusals, protect personal data, allow assisted/offline redress and audit a manageable set of indicators rather than equating all portals. ⚠️ State-rule variation and land constraints mean there is no frictionless one-size national implementation. **UPSC use:** write claim → named institution → mechanism → foreseeable failure → measurable remedy, and compare schemes only on their actual scope. **Mini recap:** legal promise, reach and receipt are three different propositions.

# ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS MODEL PRACTICE

**10 marks; answer in 150 words. Explain why a portable identity and portable food are not the same as portable social security.**

**Model answer:** ✅ e-Shram can identify an unorganised migrant worker through a UAN; ONORC permits an NFSA-listed household to draw its eligible foodgrain share at an interoperable destination Fair Price Shop. ⚠️ The first is an identity and linking mechanism; the second is a particular food transaction built on an existing eligibility record. Neither proves that a State construction board has accepted a registration, that an employer has paid a journey allowance under applicable law, or that a health claim has been settled. A mason moving States may hold both identifiers but still require separate scheme eligibility, working authentication, payment and a remedy for refusal. Governments should measure successful ration draws and each social-security claim and appeal separately. Interoperability is useful infrastructure, but portable protection requires enforceable obligations and access wherever the worker lives and works.

**Scoring guide (10):** accurate two mechanisms 3; two omitted benefit routes 3; worker example 2; outcome test 2.

**15 marks; answer in 250 words. Discuss the complementary roles of shelters, rental housing and livelihood rights in addressing urban homelessness.**

**Model answer:** ⚠️ Homelessness is an immediate safety problem embedded in longer housing and earnings insecurity. SUH shelters established during DAY-NULM can provide an accessible overnight place with water, sanitation and referrals **if still operating effectively**; the central mission ended in 2024. A bed cannot confer a stable tenancy, and a shelter far from daily work may be unusable. PMAY-U 2.0's affordable rental vertical recognises that eligible workers who cannot buy, especially migrants, may need accommodation close to employment; completion, reasonable rent and occupancy are separate tests from project sanction. For someone trading on the street, the Street Vendors Act, 2014 provides a regulated vending framework through survey, certificates, Town Vending Committees and fair procedure, while PM SVANidhi may finance stock. Loan receipt neither gives a legal pitch nor pays housing costs automatically. An urban local body should connect outreach and shelter referrals to suitable rentals, health services and workable livelihoods, recording non-users' reasons and complaints. Land scarcity and legitimate uses of public space complicate siting and vending: planning must compare these with displacement costs. A roof, an affordable route to remain housed and the opportunity to earn are complementary conditions, not interchangeable programme counts.

**Scoring guide (15):** shelter 3; rental and tenure 3; legal versus credit distinction 4; institutional integration and qualified constraints 5.

**20 marks; answer in 250 words. Critically analyse whether urban infrastructure-led development can deliver distributive justice for circular migrant workers.**

**Model answer:** ⚠️ Better transport, water and digital services can support migrant workers, but their distribution matters as much as their aggregate supply. Smart Cities Mission area-based projects may concentrate investment in selected zones; pan-city transport can spread gains more widely. A circular worker whose family remains at origin needs affordable rental housing near work, a functioning destination NFSA draw through ONORC if eligible, and enforceable workplace conditions—not merely a smart-road project. PMAY-U 2.0's rental route and DAY-NULM's shelter component address different housing durations. The commenced OSHWC Code supplies an applicable legal migrant-work framework, yet State rules and employer compliance must be tested at the site. Concentrated investment can yield efficiencies, and hazardous sites may justify relocation, so in-situ provision cannot be absolute. But a peripheral resettlement unit may increase travel costs and destroy a vendor's customer base. Compare ward-level spending with access, occupancy, wage and travel outcomes, record failures and displacement, and let renters and migrant workers contest plans. Infrastructure is therefore potentially enabling, but distributive justice requires person-specific eligibility, neighbourhood access, fair procedures and measurable protection.

**Scoring guide (20):** reasoned infrastructure thesis 4; named housing/food/labour links 6; trade-offs and countercase 4; worker-level equity measures and conclusion 6.

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

## The urban-vulnerability test

- **Classify before prescribing:** houseless person; insecure slum resident; informal tenant; vendor; circular, seasonal or permanently settled migrant; intra-State or inter-State mover; rural-to-urban or urban-to-urban stream. Duration/recurrence, State-boundary crossing and origin–destination settlement are separate axes, so categories can overlap without entailing homelessness or a change of citizenship.
- **Measurement:** Census 2011's approximately 65.5 million slum residents and 17.4% urban share describe the historical enumerated slum population, not all poverty or present homelessness. Use separate denominators and ward-level distributions.
- **Causal spine:** uncertain work → housing-cost pressure → distance from work/eviction risk → lost income and failed service access; qualifications: paths vary by gender, disability, status and local market.
- **Intersectional risk:** caste-linked work or rental exclusion, women's safety and unpaid care, children's schooling and nutrition, disability access, and old age can alter each link. Heat, flood, fire and disease can simultaneously remove shelter, documents and livelihood assets.

## Safe shelter and viable dwelling

- **SUH under former DAY-NULM/MoHUA:** the central mission ended on 30 September 2024. Its all-weather, 24x7 shelter design, basic facilities and referrals remain a useful benchmark; planning norm one shelter per 100,000 urban population. Actual current operation, land, maintenance, safety, gender/disability access and user voice must be independently checked.
- **PMAY-U 2.0:** BLC for beneficiary-led construction; AHP for partnership projects; ARH for eligible affordable rentals, including mobile low-income workers; ISS for qualifying interest subsidy. Do not transfer *older* CLSS terms to ISS.
- **Stages that must not collapse:** proposal → sanction → construction → allotment → occupancy → secure affordable serviced living. A shelter bed is not a tenancy. Older PMAY-U project-completion extension is not PMAY-U 2.0's end date.
- **Slum strategy:** in-situ services may preserve work ties; off-site resettlement can disrupt commute and income. Tenure assurance can exist without title; land hazard and density can make simple in-situ solutions inadequate.
- **Article 21 and eviction:** *Chameli Singh* treats dignified shelter as more than a roof and includes basic civic amenities; *Olga Tellis* links livelihood to Article 21 and requires fair, just and reasonable procedure. Neither judgment creates an unconditional title to a particular site or a fundamental licence to encroach.
- **Eviction and resilience:** test lawful authority, notice, hearing, tenant inclusion, rehabilitation and continued work/school access. Compare flood, heat, fire and slope exposure before and after intervention; neither staying nor moving is automatically resilient.

## Ability to earn and space to trade

- **DAY-NULM (former central mission):** urban SHGs, self-employment interest support, skills and SUH; ended September 2024. DAY-NRLM is a different rural mission. Training/certification and disbursement are inputs; sustained income, safe access and reasonable debt are outcomes.
- **Funding and transition:** the advanced DAY-NULM description uses 60:40 Centre–State and 90:10 for applicable north-eastern/special-category jurisdictions; this is not a universal rule for other urban schemes. MoHUA's 2025–26 Annual Report says the D JAY-S pilot ran October 2024–December 2025 in selected cities; pilot outputs do not establish a nationwide replacement or enduring outcomes.
- **Street Vendors Act, 2014:** TVCs, survey/certificate, vending plans and fair eviction/relocation procedure. Public passage is a legitimate competing concern to resolve by regulated planning.
- **PM SVANidhi:** launched 2020; collateral-free repayable working-capital loans, interest subsidy and digital incentives. Earlier caps ₹10,000/₹20,000/₹50,000; revised official December 2025 caps **up to ₹15,000/₹25,000/₹50,000**, with lending period extended to 31 March 2030. Credit neither guarantees sales nor legalises a pitch. Surveyed borrower outcomes are not whole-city causal effects.

## A mobile person's entitlements

- **Migration taxonomy and large-city pull:** agglomeration of jobs, suppliers and services plus migrant networks may attract workers while origin-side distress coexists. Circular movers repeatedly retain origin ties; seasonal movers follow recurring work seasons; permanent migrants shift usual residence. Intra-State and inter-State identify whether a State boundary is crossed. Rural-to-urban means movement from a rural origin to an urban destination; urban-to-urban means movement between urban settlements. Settlement stream, boundary and duration are independent dimensions and can combine in one person's movement.
- **ONORC:** portability of *existing NFSA eligibility* through interoperable ration records and ePoS shops, permitting appropriate family share at origin and migrant share at destination. Missing beneficiary record, mismatched identity, connectivity and shop failure need distinct remedies.
- **Labour Code status:** OSHWC Code enacted 2020, commenced 21 November 2025; 1979 Inter-State Migrant Workmen Act repealed. Central Rules notified 8 May 2026; applicable State rules, covered employer/worksite, enforcement and actual journey-allowance or other claim determine reach. e-Shram UAN is identification, not proof of payment.
- **Social-protection basket:** BOCW welfare, health, maternity, childcare, pension and other benefits retain their own eligibility and claim routes. A ration transaction, UAN or labour-law record cannot stand in for the rest.

## Exam spines and close-option warnings

- **GS-I migration:** explain large *rather than smaller* city via job density, services and networks; add origin factors and housing costs, not an idealised metro.
- **GS-I distributive justice:** area-based/SPV investment may concentrate benefits; pan-city schemes can spread them. Assess ward distribution, displaced livelihoods and participation rather than declaring the whole Mission inclusive or exclusionary.
- **GS-II 10/15/20-mark ladder:** define the intersecting deficit → name appropriate law or mission → trace authority, eligibility, delivery and grievance → illustrate a worker → test indicators and counterarguments → close with place-based services *and* portable person-based entitlements.
- **Core warnings:** no roof ≠ insecure roof; built ≠ usable; loan ≠ grant/right; interoperable ≠ eligible; registration ≠ benefit; Central Rules ≠ universal compliance; dashboard stock ≠ lived outcome.
- **Integrated city reform:** use disaggregated need maps, affordable shelter/rental options, protected livelihood space, portable entitlements, workplace enforcement, climate-resilient services, adequate municipal finance and grievance outcomes.
