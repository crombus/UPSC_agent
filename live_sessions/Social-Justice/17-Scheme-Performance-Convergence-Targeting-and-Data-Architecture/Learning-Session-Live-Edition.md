# Social Justice 17 — Scheme Performance, Convergence, Targeting and Data Architecture

A benefit exists for the citizen only when the intended person is identified, can claim it, receives it without avoidable friction, can carry it through relevant life changes, and can obtain correction when the system fails. This capstone session therefore follows one continuous chain:

```text
NEED → ENTITLEMENT → IDENTIFICATION → ENROLMENT → DELIVERY
     → CONVERGENCE → OUTCOME → REMEDY → REDESIGN
```

Basic and Core lessons build this chain first. Advanced lessons then test data architecture, privacy, federalism, fiscal capacity, evaluation and reform.

## Lesson 1 — The performance chain: from announcement to capability

Progress: 1 / 12 | Stage: Foundation | Subtopic: Scheme-performance stages and evaluation criteria

📰 CA Found: None used here. Standing official material verifies the performance framework without creating an additional current-affairs anchor.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

```text
PUBLIC PURPOSE
     ↓
INPUT        money · staff · technology · legal authority
     ↓
ACTIVITY     enrol · transfer · inspect · counsel · provide service
     ↓
OUTPUT       pension paid · meal served · card issued · visit completed
     ↓
OUTCOME      lower hunger · safer old age · better learning · timely care
     ↓
IMPACT       durable capability, mobility and reduced vulnerability
     ↓
EQUITY TEST  who improved, who remained outside, and who bore the burden?
```

*The chain prevents expenditure, registration or coverage from being mistaken for social change.*

Suppose an elderly widow is entered on a pension list. Registration is not payment; payment is not adequacy; adequacy is not improved security; an average improvement is not proof that the most vulnerable improved. **Scheme performance is the verified movement from public purpose to equitable capability gain.**

### Five stages that must remain separate

1. **Allocation:** money is budgeted.
2. **Release and expenditure:** funds reach an agency and are spent.
3. **Output:** the immediate good, transaction or service is produced.
4. **Outcome:** the target population's condition changes.
5. **Impact:** a longer-term change can plausibly be attributed to the intervention.

Attribution is hardest at the last stage. A nutrition indicator may improve while incomes, sanitation and maternal education also change. Evaluation must ask what the scheme contributed, not merely whether two events occurred together.

### Four performance criteria

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| **Effectiveness** | Did the intended condition improve? |
| **Efficiency** | Was the result achieved at reasonable administrative and fiscal cost? |
| **Equity** | Were gains and burdens fairly distributed? |
| **Accountability** | Could errors be explained, appealed and corrected? |

An output can be necessary without being sufficient. Meals must be served before nutrition can improve, but counting meals alone cannot reveal dietary quality, illness or intra-household distribution.

**Strong objection:** outcome evaluation is slow, expensive and affected by outside variables; administrators need rapid operational numbers. **Reply:** use both. Routine monitoring should detect delays and drop-offs quickly; periodic evaluation should test quality, distribution and attributable change. The mistake is not using outputs—it is using them as the final verdict.

**UPSC use:** begin any “evaluate a scheme” answer by locating the available evidence on this chain. **Mini recap:** performance is not money spent or people registered; it is equitable change plus a correction mechanism.

### Revision notes

1. Allocation, release, expenditure, output, outcome and impact are distinct stages.
2. Output is the immediate product; outcome is change in the target population.
3. Impact adds a causal-attribution claim to longer-term change.
4. Coverage is an output and does not establish quality or adequacy.
5. Effectiveness asks whether the intervention worked.
6. Efficiency asks about result relative to fiscal and administrative cost.
7. Equity requires distributional and exclusion analysis, not only an average.
8. Accountability requires explanation, appeal and correction.
9. Monitoring is continuous and operational; evaluation is periodic and judgment-oriented.
10. Administrative data are useful for operations but usually omit eligible non-enrollees.
11. Survey evidence can reveal outcomes but has sampling and reporting limits.
12. A credible verdict combines operational speed with independent outcome evidence.

### Concept check

**Question:** A nutrition programme reports full fund utilisation, rising enrolment and unchanged child stunting. What has each indicator established, and what remains unknown?

**Model answer:** Fund utilisation establishes expenditure, enrolment establishes a coverage output, and unchanged stunting signals no observed improvement in one outcome. Service quality, other determinants, subgroup distribution and causal attribution remain unknown.

**Misconception to avoid:** Full spending and high enrolment do not establish effectiveness or impact.

**Original Mains practice — 10 marks; answer in 150 words.** *Explain why a welfare dashboard should not treat expenditure, coverage and outcome as interchangeable indicators.*

**Mains model answer:** A dashboard becomes misleading when it collapses different stages of the results chain. Expenditure shows that money was used; coverage shows that an immediate service or transfer reached recorded beneficiaries; outcome shows whether their condition improved. For example, anganwadi enrolment is an output, while child nutritional status is an outcome. High enrolment can coexist with weak food quality, illness or irregular attendance. Administrative records are valuable for rapid management, but they usually omit eligible non-enrollees and cannot by themselves attribute change to the scheme. A sound dashboard should therefore display allocation, release, delivery, quality, exclusion and grievance indicators alongside independently measured outcomes, disaggregated by gender, social group, disability and geography. The purpose is not to discard output data but to prevent an input or transaction count from becoming an unsupported claim of capability improvement.

**Scoring guide (10):** stage distinctions 3; named example 2; data limitation 2; integrated corrective design 3.

## Lesson 2 — Targeting: the two errors and their unequal harms

Progress: 2 / 12 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Inclusion error, exclusion error and error asymmetry

📚 Book context: Queried — canonical welfare and public-finance material distinguishes leakage from exclusion and treats the two errors as different harms.
🔍 CA Search: "site:dbtbharat.gov.in OR site:uidai.gov.in April–October 2026 welfare targeting inclusion exclusion authentication India"
📰 CA Found: None directly linked; no current event is added.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

| Reality / decision | Included | Excluded |
|---|---:|---:|
| Eligible person | Correct inclusion | **Exclusion error** |
| Ineligible person | **Inclusion error** | Correct exclusion |

*The two mistakes occur at the boundary of eligibility, but their costs are not morally or administratively identical.*

**Inclusion error** means an ineligible person receives the benefit. It imposes a fiscal cost, dilutes resources and may undermine trust. **Exclusion error** means an eligible person does not receive the benefit. It can impose hunger, untreated illness or loss of subsistence on the person the scheme exists to protect.

### How the errors arise

```text
OLD OR IMPERFECT PROXY
   ├─ household appears poor but is not now → inclusion
   └─ household became poor after the survey → exclusion

STRICT DOCUMENT RULE
   ├─ false document succeeds → inclusion
   └─ genuine claimant lacks document → exclusion

DIGITAL VERIFICATION
   ├─ duplicate removed correctly
   └─ mismatch/authentication failure blocks genuine claimant → exclusion
```

SECC 2011 used deprivation and occupation criteria for several targeting routes. It is distinct from the decennial Census. Its non-caste socio-economic data were published, but the caste data collected in SECC 2011 were not officially released or validated for general policy use. Its age and static nature mean it cannot be treated as a continuously updated measure of present need. Aadhaar can establish or authenticate identity and support de-duplication; it does not independently establish poverty, disability, widowhood or every scheme condition.

The decision to include caste enumeration in **Census 2027** does not update SECC or create a present beneficiary list. The Cabinet Committee on Political Affairs decided on **30 April 2025** to include caste enumeration. Houselisting and Housing Census operations were scheduled as State/UT-specific 30-day exercises within **1 April–30 September 2026**; the main Population Enumeration is scheduled principally for **February 2027**, with a separate September 2026 schedule for specified snow-bound areas. As of **3 October 2026**, no national Census 2027 caste results exist for targeting. A notified or conducted enumeration stage must not be described as a published database.

### Error asymmetry

For a discretionary incentive, administrators may prioritise tight verification. For food, pension or essential health access, the state should usually place greater weight on avoiding wrongful denial because the human cost of exclusion is immediate and concentrated. That does not require ignoring inclusion error. It requires **risk-sensitive design**: presumptive or broad access where stakes are high, followed by audit, updating and recovery where legally appropriate.

**Objection:** tolerating inclusion wastes scarce public money. **Reply:** zero-error targeting is impossible. Extra verification itself costs money and excludes people. The correct question is which error the programme is designed to minimise, who bears each error, and whether correction is quick.

**UPSC use:** distinguish leakage, inclusion error and exclusion error. A payment can reach the listed person without leakage while the eligible unlisted person remains excluded. **Mini recap:** better authentication can improve identity accuracy and still worsen need-based access.

### Revision notes

1. Inclusion error means an ineligible person is included.
2. Exclusion error means an eligible person is excluded.
3. Leakage is diversion or loss; it is not identical to either targeting error.
4. SECC 2011 is distinct from the decennial Census.
5. A deprivation proxy is not a continuously updated income measure.
6. Aadhaar verifies identity; it does not prove every eligibility condition.
7. Strict documentation can reduce inclusion while increasing exclusion.
8. Authentication success does not establish current need.
9. Exclusion in subsistence schemes can impose immediate human harm.
10. Inclusion mainly imposes fiscal and distributional costs.
11. Error choice should depend on the benefit's stakes and reversibility.
12. Updating, assisted access and time-bound correction reduce exclusion.
13. Audit and lawful recovery can address inclusion without prior over-exclusion.
14. SECC 2011 caste data were not officially released or validated for general policy use.
15. Census 2027 caste enumeration is a separate ongoing census process; its operational schedule is not a published targeting database.

### Concept check

**Question:** A pension payment system accurately transfers money to everyone on an old list, but newly eligible widows cannot enter the list. Is this primarily leakage, inclusion error or exclusion error?

**Model answer:** It is primarily exclusion error caused by an outdated identification system. Accurate payment to the recorded list shows transaction integrity, not targeting completeness.

**Misconception to avoid:** A leak-free payment rail does not prove that the beneficiary list is accurate or complete.

**Original Mains practice — 10 marks; answer in 150 words.** *Distinguish inclusion error from exclusion error and explain why the distinction matters for subsistence welfare.*

**Mains model answer:** Inclusion error occurs when an ineligible person receives a benefit; exclusion error occurs when an eligible person is denied it. The distinction matters because the costs fall differently. Inclusion wastes public resources and may dilute transfers, while exclusion can remove food, pension or treatment from a person facing immediate need. A static deprivation list may include households whose position improved and exclude those impoverished after enumeration. Aadhaar-based de-duplication can remove duplicate identities, but it cannot update poverty or replace a disability or widowhood determination. For subsistence entitlements, design should therefore give greater weight to avoiding wrongful denial through assisted enrolment, alternative authentication, periodic list revision and time-bound appeals, while controlling inclusion through audit and lawful review. The goal is not an impossible zero-error system but a transparent choice of error tolerance matched to the seriousness and reversibility of harm.

**Scoring guide (10):** definitions 2; unequal-harm analysis 3; mechanism/example 3; balanced safeguards 2.

## Lesson 3 — Universalism, targeting and the hybrid welfare state

Progress: 3 / 12 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Coverage design and the 2023 discrimination question

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
📚 Book context: Queried — canonical Social Justice framework on universal basic services, targeted transfers and vertical equity.
🔍 CA Search: "site:indiabudget.gov.in OR site:niti.gov.in April–October 2026 universal welfare targeted benefits India"
📰 CA Found: None used; this lesson is design-based rather than event-led.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

| Design | Who receives? | Strength | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Universal service | Everyone within the relevant population | Low stigma and low exclusion | Fiscal cost; unequal use may persist |
| Categorical universalism | Everyone meeting a simple status, such as age | Administratively clear | Category may not map perfectly to need |
| Means/proxy targeting | Persons below a threshold or deprivation score | Concentrates resources | Documentation and exclusion error |
| Contributory protection | Contributors meeting rules | Links benefit and financing | Irregular workers may remain outside |
| Hybrid | Universal floor plus targeted top-up | Balances floor and depth | Coordination and complexity |

*India's welfare state is not purely universal or purely targeted; it combines instruments for different risks.*

The word **discriminatory** has two meanings. It may mean unjust prejudice, which welfare must oppose. It may also mean deliberate differentiation based on relevant disadvantage. A disability allowance, widow pension or Scheduled Tribe hostel treats people differently because equal treatment of unequal conditions may reproduce injustice. This is **vertical equity**, not automatically wrongful discrimination.

### A design rule

```text
UNIVERSAL FLOOR
essential health · schooling · basic nutrition · minimum income security
          +
TARGETED OR CATEGORICAL TOP-UP
disability cost · remoteness · caste/tribe disadvantage · widowhood · care burden
          +
CONTRIBUTORY LAYER
pension/insurance for those able to contribute
```

Universalism reduces list-based exclusion and stigma, and can build political support for public services. Targeting directs scarce funds toward people with greater need. Yet means tests can be volatile, intrusive and documentation-heavy. A hybrid separates the **floor** from the **top-up**: do not make essential access depend on a perfectly accurate poverty list, but deepen support where disadvantage is greater.

PM-JAY illustrates why one scheme may contain distinct coverage routes. Its original poor-and-vulnerable family architecture uses **SECC 2011 rural deprivation and urban occupational criteria**, alongside the specified legacy RSBY coverage. The later expansion for **all persons aged 70 years and above** is a separate age-based route irrespective of socio-economic status. Therefore, not every current PM-JAY beneficiary is SECC-derived, and the 70+ route should not be described as an update of the SECC list.

NSAP illustrates non-contributory social assistance through old-age, widow and disability pensions, family benefit and Annapurna. It should not be described as one pension. States may add to central assistance and administer eligibility, producing variation that must be analysed rather than assumed away.

**Strong objection:** universal benefits subsidise those who can pay and reduce funds for the poorest. **Reply:** universal basic services can be progressive in use and reduce exclusion; taxes can recover resources from the better-off. For cash top-ups, targeting may remain justified. **Balanced verdict:** choose by the nature of the good, administrative capacity, fiscal space and error cost.

**UPSC use — 2023 link:** agree that vulnerable-group schemes differentiate, but reject the proposition if “discriminatory” implies arbitrary or stigmatising exclusion. Test intelligible need, proportionality, dignity and remedy. **Mini recap:** relevant differentiation can advance equality; arbitrary differentiation defeats it.

### Revision notes

1. Universalism supplies a benefit to the relevant population without a means test.
2. Categorical universalism uses a simple status such as age.
3. Targeting concentrates support but depends on identification.
4. Contributory protection can exclude low or irregular earners.
5. Hybrid design combines a universal floor with targeted top-ups.
6. Universalism lowers stigma and list-based exclusion.
7. Targeting can conserve resources but raises documentation burdens.
8. Equal treatment of unequal conditions can reproduce disadvantage.
9. Relevant differentiation is vertical equity, not necessarily prejudice.
10. Vulnerability categories must remain reviewable and dignity-respecting.
11. NSAP is an umbrella, not one pension.
12. Scheme choice should reflect the good, error cost, capacity and fiscal space.
13. Universal access does not guarantee equal quality or utilisation.
14. Targeted top-ups still require transparent criteria and appeal.
15. PM-JAY's SECC-derived family route and its separate universal 70+ age route must not be conflated.

### Concept check

**Question:** Is an age-based pension for every person above a specified age universal or targeted?

**Model answer:** It is categorical universalism within the age category: no poverty test applies inside that group, but age defines the covered population.

**Misconception to avoid:** Universalism need not mean that every resident receives every benefit regardless of the risk being addressed.

**Original Mains practice — 15 marks; answer in 250 words.** *“Relevant differentiation can promote substantive equality, but targeting can also stigmatise and exclude.” Discuss.*

**Mains model answer:** Welfare differentiates because people face unequal risks. A disability allowance, widow pension or targeted scholarship can advance substantive equality by compensating for a specific disadvantage; treating everyone identically would ignore unequal conversion costs and barriers. This is vertical equity rather than prejudice.

The difficulty lies in identification. Means tests and proxy deprivation scores concentrate scarce resources, but static lists, documentation rules and intrusive verification can exclude newly poor or mobile households. Narrow categories may also stigmatise recipients or divide similarly placed citizens. Universal basic services reduce these risks by making essential health, education or nutrition available without repeated proof of poverty and by building wider political support. Yet universal cash transfers may be fiscally costly and provide unnecessary support to the better-off.

India therefore needs a layered design: a universal or broadly categorical floor for essential services and life-cycle security; targeted top-ups for disability, remoteness or entrenched social disadvantage; and contributory protection where earnings permit. Criteria should be intelligible, periodically updated and appealable. Differentiation is legitimate when connected to need, proportionate and dignity-respecting; it becomes discriminatory when arbitrary categories, opaque data or inaccessible procedures deny equal citizenship.

**Scoring guide (15):** substantive-equality thesis 3; universalism case 3; targeting gains and harms 4; hybrid architecture 3; qualified verdict 2.

## Lesson 4 — Convergence: making many schemes serve one household

📚 Book context: Queried — *Economic Survey 2025–26*, PDF p. 116, notes that income support does not consistently produce nutrition, education or durable poverty exits without complementary services and employment.
🔍 CA Search: "site:niti.gov.in OR site:pib.gov.in April–October 2026 scheme convergence district household India"
📰 CA Found: None used; the Economic Survey passage is book evidence, not a separate current anchor.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

```text
ONE PVTG HOUSEHOLD
   ├─ forest right / secure habitat
   ├─ housing, road and drinking water
   ├─ anganwadi nutrition and maternal care
   ├─ school meal and continued education
   ├─ health protection
   └─ old-age or disability assistance

Different databases + ministries + cadres + payment cycles
                         ↓
       without coordination: fragmented receipt
       with coordination: household capability plan
```

*Convergence is not the existence of several schemes; it is coordinated access to the right combination at the right time.*

**Horizontal convergence** links ministries or departments at the same tier. **Vertical convergence** links Union, State, district, block and local bodies. **Household-level convergence** begins with the person's combined needs rather than a ministry's scheme boundary.

### Why silos persist

1. Different schemes define the household or beneficiary differently.
2. Eligibility lists use different dates, documents and update cycles.
3. Frontline workers report upward to separate departments.
4. Funds arrive on different calendars.
5. No officer may own the whole household outcome.
6. Each dashboard rewards its own output, not the combined capability result.

A cash transfer can stabilise consumption while a child remains malnourished if water, health care, diet diversity and maternal support are weak. This does not make cash useless. It shows **complementarity**: one instrument cannot substitute for all the others.

### Convergence without central overreach

A common intake, referral and grievance layer can reduce repeated applications. Local officials can assemble a household service plan. Departments should share the minimum status information necessary for coordination, not pool every record into an unrestricted database. The district can coordinate, but accountability must remain traceable to the department legally responsible for each benefit.

**Objection:** convergence produces committee overload and blurred responsibility. **Reply:** design a named case coordinator and a responsibility matrix. Integration should reduce citizen burden without allowing every department to blame the collective.

**UPSC use:** replace the vague phrase “better coordination” with actor, data, timing and remedy. **Mini recap:** coordination succeeds when a household experiences continuity while each authority remains answerable.

### Revision notes

1. Multiple schemes do not automatically create convergence.
2. Horizontal convergence operates across departments at one tier.
3. Vertical convergence operates across governmental levels.
4. Household convergence begins from combined needs.
5. Different beneficiary definitions can fragment delivery.
6. Separate databases and update cycles create repeated proof burdens.
7. Frontline cadres often have programme-specific reporting lines.
8. Timing mismatch can make complementary benefits ineffective.
9. Cash support and public services solve different problems.
10. Common intake can reduce applications without erasing scheme responsibility.
11. A named coordinator should not replace the legal duty-holder.
12. Data sharing should be purpose-limited and minimal.
13. District coordination needs authority, information and staff.
14. Combined outcomes matter more than the sum of departmental outputs.

### Concept check

**Question:** A household receives a housing sanction and a pension but lacks drinking water and maternal care. Has convergence occurred?

**Model answer:** No. Separate benefits have been delivered, but the complementary needs and sequencing required for a viable household outcome remain uncoordinated.

**Misconception to avoid:** Benefit multiplicity is not convergence.

**Original Mains practice — 15 marks; answer in 250 words.** *Analyse why scheme convergence frequently fails at the household level and suggest an accountable institutional design.*

**Mains model answer:** Household deprivation crosses departmental boundaries, while schemes are designed and measured in silos. A remote tribal household may simultaneously require secure habitat, nutrition, schooling, health protection, infrastructure and social assistance. Each benefit can use a different list, document, frontline cadre and funding cycle. The citizen repeats information, one benefit arrives too late to complement another, and no official owns the combined outcome.

Convergence should therefore begin with a common local intake and needs assessment, followed by a time-bound household service plan. Panchayat or urban ward staff can coordinate referrals; a district convergence cell can resolve interdepartmental blocks and view purpose-limited status data. A responsibility matrix must identify the department and officer answerable for each action, while one case coordinator tracks the whole sequence. Shared indicators should include successful combinations and unresolved drop-offs, not only departmental coverage.

However, one unrestricted central database would create privacy, profiling and federal-control risks. Coordination should exchange only necessary status fields, preserve departmental legal responsibility and give citizens access, correction and grievance rights. Effective convergence is therefore coordinated continuity for the household with unblurred accountability for the state.

**Scoring guide (15):** silo mechanisms 4; household example 2; local/district architecture 4; privacy and accountability limits 3; conclusion 2.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

```text
BIRTH / CHILDHOOD → SCHOOL AGE → WORK → MIGRATION / SHOCK
      → DISABILITY / CARE BURDEN → OLD AGE → BEREAVEMENT

At every transition ask:
record updated? eligibility reassessed? contribution carried?
service available at destination? claim paid? grievance resolved?
```

*Life-cycle delivery treats welfare as continuity through changing risks, not as one-time registration.*

Portability has several layers:

| Layer | What moves? | What does not automatically move? |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | A recognised identifier or record | Scheme eligibility |
| Transaction | Ability to draw an existing benefit elsewhere | Every other benefit |
| Contribution | Credited history across jobs/platforms | Claim acceptance |
| Entitlement | Legal access across jurisdictions | Service capacity at destination |
| Remedy | Ability to complain and appeal after moving | Automatic resolution |

ONORC can make an existing NFSA food entitlement usable at a destination transaction point; it does not create NFSA eligibility for every migrant. e-Shram can register an unorganised worker and issue a UAN; registration does not itself pay pension, insurance or a welfare-board claim. A database is a gateway only when the downstream scheme, financing, claim and grievance systems work.

### The life-cycle problem

A household can change after marriage, birth, death, migration, disability onset or livelihood loss. A static household record can preserve a deceased member, miss a newborn, bind an adult woman to an old household or fail to recognise newly acquired disability. Updating must therefore be event-responsive and citizen-accessible.

**Objection:** frequent reassessment creates fraud risk and administrative cost. **Reply:** freeze-and-purge systems also create cost through wrongful denial. Use event verification, audit trails and risk-based review rather than making the genuine claimant prove the entire life history repeatedly.

**UPSC use:** separate identification, portability, eligibility, claim and receipt. **Mini recap:** what must follow the person is not merely a number but the enforceable delivery chain.

### Revision notes

1. Life-cycle welfare addresses changing risks from childhood to old age.
2. Household composition changes through birth, death, marriage and separation.
3. Mobility creates origin–destination coordination needs.
4. Identity portability is not benefit portability.
5. Transaction portability is scheme-specific.
6. ONORC uses existing NFSA eligibility; it does not create universal eligibility.
7. e-Shram registration is not automatic social security.
8. Contribution portability requires interoperable records.
9. Claim settlement and grievance must work across jurisdictions.
10. Static records can exclude newly vulnerable persons.
11. Citizens need accessible event-based updating and correction.
12. Audit trails can control manipulation without freezing errors.
13. Destination service capacity remains necessary even with portable records.
14. Portability should reduce mobility penalties, not centralise every benefit.

### Concept check

**Question:** A worker has an e-Shram UAN and successfully draws NFSA grain at destination. What has been proved, and what has not?

**Model answer:** Her worker identity is recorded and her existing food entitlement is transactionally portable. This does not prove eligibility for pension, insurance, welfare-board benefits or successful claims under them.

**Misconception to avoid:** One portable identifier or transaction does not create a portable welfare basket.

**Original Mains practice — 10 marks; answer in 150 words.** *Why must portability be designed beyond identity portability?*

**Mains model answer:** Identity portability means a person can be recognised after moving; social-protection portability requires much more. The destination system must recognise the relevant eligibility, access contribution or household records, provide the service, settle the claim and hear a grievance. ONORC can enable a household already covered by NFSA to draw its food entitlement at destination, but it does not create eligibility for every migrant. Likewise, an e-Shram UAN records an unorganised worker; it does not automatically provide pension, accident cover or a welfare-board payment. Life events—birth, death, disability, marriage or livelihood loss—also require prompt record updating. A sound design therefore combines interoperable scheme-specific records, clear origin–destination responsibility, assisted updating, portable grievance tracking and sufficient destination capacity. A number can travel instantly; an enforceable entitlement travels only when institutions, finance and remedy travel with it.

## Lesson 6 — Social registries and federated data architecture

```text
                     ↓             ↑

```

*Interoperability means systems can exchange necessary, accurate information; it does not require an all-purpose central dossier.*

A **social registry** is a system used to identify or assess persons and households for one or more social programmes. It can reduce repeated documentation and support proactive outreach. But it can also reproduce stale errors across many schemes if a wrong field becomes a common source.

### The data-quality triangle

1. **Coverage:** are eligible persons visible?
2. **Accuracy:** is the record correct now?
3. **Timeliness:** can it respond to shocks and life events?

Improving one does not guarantee the others. A large registry may have broad coverage and stale needs data. A real-time transaction system may be timely but see only enrolled users. A survey may estimate outcomes well but cannot adjudicate an individual's entitlement.

### Core evidence systems: do not confuse their units

| Evidence system | Producer and unit | Primary use | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PLFS** | National Statistics Office, MoSPI; sampled persons and households, reported through labour-status and rural/urban estimates | Employment, unemployment, labour-force participation and worker-population outcomes | A sample survey with status definitions and period/method limits; not an individual eligibility register |
| **Crime in India / NCRB** | NCRB under the Ministry of Home Affairs; police-recorded cases by offence and geography | Recorded crimes, including relevant crimes against women, children and SC/ST communities | Measures recorded cases and police classification, not the full prevalence of unreported harm |
| **SDG India Index** | NITI Aayog; composite State/UT goal and overall scores | Broad inter-State/UT comparison and SDG monitoring | Aggregation can hide district/group gaps; a score or rank is not causal evaluation |
| **National MPI** | NITI Aayog using NFHS household data; national, State/UT and district deprivation estimates | Joint deprivations across health, education and standard of living | Survey-round, indicator, cutoff and weight dependent; not a current beneficiary list or scheme-impact estimate |

These sources answer different questions. PLFS estimates labour conditions; NCRB describes police-recorded crime; the SDG India Index aggregates broad State/UT progress; National MPI estimates overlapping household deprivations. None can replace a beneficiary register, and no composite score or trend proves that one scheme caused the observed condition.

### Core results institutions: OOMF and DMEO

The **Output-Outcome Monitoring Framework (OOMF)** is an annual Union Budget document for covered major Central Sector and Centrally Sponsored Schemes. It places financial outlay beside specified outputs, outcomes, indicators and targets. It makes the expected results chain visible, but it does not cover every scheme in every year and does not by itself establish causal impact.

The **Development Monitoring and Evaluation Office (DMEO)** is an attached office of NITI Aayog, constituted in **September 2015** by merging the Programme Evaluation Organisation and Independent Evaluation Office. It monitors and evaluates government programmes, supports or commissions deeper and third-party assessments, diagnoses why performance is weak and recommends course correction. OOMF states the commitment; routine monitoring tracks delivery; DMEO-type evaluation judges performance. A line ministry, the Ministry of Finance or a legislative forum must still act on the findings.

### Federated rather than indiscriminate

In a federated design, source departments retain authoritative records and share defined status fields under law and purpose. A scheme might query whether a disability certificate is valid without copying the entire medical record. Decision logs should record which data and rule produced the result. Citizens need notice, access, correction and a non-digital route.

**Strong objection:** decentralised data create duplication and inconsistent decisions. **Reply:** common standards, unique transaction references and reconciliation can support interoperability without unrestricted central possession. Centralisation can remove duplication but magnifies breach, profiling and single-point-error risks.

**UPSC use:** distinguish database, registry, identity system, survey and census. **Mini recap:** a good architecture links services while keeping purpose, authority and correction visible.

### Revision notes

1. A social registry supports identification or assessment across programmes.
2. A registry is not the same as an identity system.
3. PLFS estimates sampled labour outcomes; it is not an individual welfare register.
4. NCRB records police-reported crime, while the SDG India Index and National MPI are composite diagnostics with different units and limits.
5. OOMF states outlay–output–outcome commitments; DMEO evaluates performance and recommends, but cannot itself guarantee, course correction.
6. Static data can spread stale exclusion across linked schemes.
7. Interoperability does not require one unrestricted central database.
8. Federated systems query authoritative sources for necessary fields.
9. Purpose limitation restricts reuse beyond the stated welfare function.
10. Data minimisation reduces collection and breach risk.
11. Decision logs support explanation and audit.
12. Citizens need notice, access and correction.
13. Human review must exist for adverse automated decisions.
14. Offline and assisted routes protect people unable to self-serve digitally.
15. Common standards and reconciliation can reduce duplication.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why can linking every scheme to one static household registry worsen rather than solve exclusion?

**Model answer:** A stale or incorrect record can become a shared error source, causing the same person to be rejected across multiple schemes. Interoperability must therefore include updating, provenance and correction.

**Misconception to avoid:** Reuse of one record guarantees consistency, not correctness.

**Original Mains practice — 15 marks; answer in 250 words.** *Examine the case for a federated social-registry architecture rather than an unrestricted central welfare database.*

**Mains model answer:** A social registry can reduce repeated applications, identify missing benefits and support household-level convergence. Yet an unrestricted central database creates a dangerous false equivalence between interoperability and total data pooling. Welfare records contain identity, health, disability, occupation and household information collected for different legal purposes. Central copying magnifies breach, profiling and function-creep risks, while one stale error can exclude a citizen from several schemes.

A federated architecture offers a better balance. Authoritative departments retain their records; schemes request only the status fields necessary for a defined purpose. Common standards and transaction references permit matching and reconciliation. Every adverse decision should record the source data and eligibility rule, provide notice and permit accessible correction and human review. Event-based updates should capture birth, death, disability, migration and livelihood shocks. Assisted and non-digital routes must remain available.

Federation is not fragmentation. It requires clear data stewardship, quality standards, lawful sharing agreements, audit trails and time-bound resolution of interdepartmental conflicts. Nor should privacy prevent useful convergence: proportionate, purpose-limited exchange can reduce citizen burden. The objective is a linked service architecture in which data follow legitimate need without turning the beneficiary into a permanently profiled object.

**Scoring guide (15):** registry gains 3; centralisation risks 4; federated mechanism 4; citizen safeguards 2; balanced conclusion 2.

*“Credited” is one stage before “usable benefit,” and both are after correct eligibility.*

DBT changes the route of a benefit and can reduce duplicate or ghost entries, intermediation and payment delay. PFMS tracks and reports government fund flow and acts as a DBT payment channel. Neither tool decides whether the underlying list is fair, the benefit is adequate or a credited amount is accessible.

### PFMS, SNA and SNA-SPARSH solve different fund-flow problems

| Layer | Mechanism | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| **PFMS** | Tracks sanction, release, payment, accounting and reported expenditure; also channels DBT payments | That the beneficiary list is fair or an outcome occurred |
| **SNA model** | Uses a designated State Single Nodal Agency/account structure for each Centrally Sponsored Scheme, with linked implementing-agency arrangements | That money reached the final service point without delay |
| **SNA-SPARSH** | Integrates PFMS, the State IFMIS and RBI e-Kuber for just-in-time release of Union and State shares against actual payment requirements | That every scheme and State has completed onboarding, reconciliation or error-free operation |

The Department of Expenditure introduced the SNA-SPARSH architecture through its **13 July 2023** Office Memorandum. A **10 June 2025** order extended 37 additional Centrally Sponsored Schemes from 1 July 2025 and required all CSSs in States and Union Territories with legislatures to use the model from **1 November 2025**. This is an applicability stage, not evidence that every operational integration is complete. The reform reduces advance parking and bank float, but depends on timely State matching shares, accurate expenditure claims and reconciliation across financial systems.

### Where digital delivery can fail

| Stage | Failure | Appropriate response |
|---|---|---|
| Identification | Wrong or stale record | Update and eligibility appeal |
| Authentication | Biometric/connectivity mismatch | Alternative authentication or assisted exception |
| Bank mapping | Name, seeding or account error | Account correction and traceable reprocessing |
| Credit | Rejection or delay | Failure code, named officer and retry |
| Cash-out/use | Distance, dormant account, agent shortage | Last-mile access and choice of channel |
| Grievance | Portal closes without remedy | Escalation, hearing and compensation where provided |

Digital systems also shift burden. The state may save administrative time while a claimant makes repeated trips, pays an intermediary or loses wages. Efficiency must count **citizen compliance cost**, not only government processing cost.

**Objection:** manual fallback reintroduces discretion and fraud. **Reply:** design logged exceptions, supervisor review and post-audit. A fallback that is auditable is safer than an inflexible system that converts technical failure into entitlement denial.

**UPSC use:** distinguish payment reform from targeting and provision reform. **Mini recap:** DBT can strengthen transaction integrity while leaving list accuracy, adequacy and digital exclusion unresolved.

2. PFMS is a government financial-management and payment system.
3. De-duplication addresses identity-based leakage.
4. Accurate transfer can still reach an outdated beneficiary list.
5. Authentication failure is distinct from ineligibility.
6. Account credit is distinct from cash or service usability.
7. Bank mapping and name mismatches require traceable correction.
8. Last-mile access includes distance, connectivity and intermediaries.
9. Citizen compliance cost belongs in efficiency analysis.
10. A failure code should lead to action, not merely closure.
11. Logged assisted exceptions can balance access and integrity.

13. Grievance outcomes should be monitored, not just complaint disposal.

### Concept check

**Question:** A payment dashboard shows a successful bank credit, but the beneficiary cannot withdraw the amount for two months. Was delivery successful?

**Model answer:** The electronic credit stage succeeded, but effective delivery did not. Last-mile usability and timely access remain unfulfilled outcomes.

**Misconception to avoid:** Transaction success is not identical to realised benefit.

**Original Mains practice — 15 marks; answer in 250 words.** *“Direct Benefit Transfer is a successful payment reform being asked to solve targeting and service-quality problems it was not designed to solve.” Critically examine.*

**Mains model answer:** DBT has improved the route of welfare delivery. Authenticated electronic transfer can reduce duplicate identities, intermediary diversion and delay, while PFMS creates a traceable payment record. These are substantial gains in transaction integrity.

However, DBT does not determine who ought to be on the beneficiary list. An outdated list can transfer money accurately to the wrong set of people. Nor does a successful bank credit prove that the claimant can withdraw cash, buy the required good or obtain a quality public service. Authentication failure, name mismatch, dormant accounts, weak connectivity and distant banking correspondents can convert digital efficiency into exclusion. Cash can also be an inadequate substitute where markets are thin or prices volatile.

The correction is not to abandon digital transfer but to define its scope. Beneficiary lists need periodic revision; essential schemes need alternative authentication and assisted access; payment failures require reason codes, named responsibility, reprocessing and appeal; and monitoring should count exclusion and citizen compliance costs alongside savings. DBT should be judged as one delivery layer inside a wider targeting, provision and accountability architecture.

*No single data source can show enrolment, population outcome and attributable effect equally well.*

### The one current-affairs anchor

✅ On **29 May 2026**, the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare released **NFHS-6**, conducted during **2023–24**. The official release reported under-five stunting declining from **35.5% in NFHS-5 (2019–21) to 29.3% in NFHS-6**. This is national survey evidence of change between labelled rounds. It does **not** by itself establish which scheme caused the change.

### What each evidence source can see

| Source | Strength | Blind spot |
|---|---|---|
| Administrative register | Timely transactions and implementation | Usually omits eligible non-enrollees |
| Sample survey | Population outcomes and subgroup comparison | Sampling, periodicity and reporting lag |
| Census | Broad enumeration | Infrequent and slow for programme correction |
| Social audit / qualitative study | Mechanism and lived failure | Limited comparability and scale |
| Impact evaluation | Causal contribution under assumptions | Cost, ethics, external validity and implementation variation |

### Advanced synthesis: triangulation and institutional follow-through

The Core taxonomy in Lesson 6 prevents false comparison; advanced evaluation asks how to combine the sources. An administrative register can show delivery among recorded beneficiaries, PLFS or NFHS can reveal population outcomes, NCRB can expose recorded protection failures, and SDG/MPI composites can locate broad geographic disadvantage. Divergence is not a nuisance to average away: it may reveal a denominator mismatch, non-enrolment, reporting incentives, quality failure or a time lag.

The institutional sequence also matters. OOMF targets state what a covered scheme promised; monitoring shows whether activities and outputs occurred; DMEO or another evaluation can test effectiveness, process and contribution. A finding changes policy only if the responsible ministry, finance authority or legislature uses it. The advanced task is therefore to connect evidence to a decision and a duty-holder, not merely to accumulate indicators.

### Attribution ladder

Before–after change establishes movement, not cause. A comparison group provides a counterfactual but may differ systematically. Randomisation can strengthen causal inference for the tested design but may be impractical or unethical for entitlements, and the result may not generalise. Process evaluation explains why a chain failed even when it cannot estimate effect size.

Indicators also change behaviour. If officials are ranked on enrolment, they may prioritise easy registrations and neglect service quality. A robust system uses a basket of output, quality, outcome, equity and grievance indicators, independently checks samples and reports drop-offs.

**Objection:** perfect attribution is impossible in complex welfare. **Reply:** do not wait for perfection. State the level of confidence, triangulate methods and distinguish contribution from exclusive causation.

**UPSC use:** name the evidence rung before citing a statistic. **Mini recap:** numbers become evidence only when their source, denominator, period, distribution and causal limit are clear.

5. An OOMF target is a stated commitment, not proof that the output or outcome was achieved.

7. Baseline is required for credible change analysis.

### Concept check

**Question:** What can the NFHS-5 to NFHS-6 stunting decline establish, and what can it not establish?

**Model answer:** It establishes a measured national change between specified survey rounds. By itself it cannot attribute the decline to one scheme or reveal every local subgroup's experience.

**Misconception to avoid:** A favourable outcome trend is not a single-programme impact estimate.

**Original Mains practice — 15 marks; answer in 250 words.** *“Administrative coverage can coexist with weak or unequal outcomes.” Discuss and propose an evaluation design.*

**Mains model answer:** Administrative coverage records are necessary for managing welfare but are not a complete performance verdict. They show enrolled beneficiaries, transactions or services recorded by the programme; they normally exclude eligible non-enrollees and may reward the implementing officer for reporting a high number. Thus anganwadi enrolment can rise while service quality varies, or a payment can be credited while the claimant cannot access it.

An evaluation should begin with a theory of change and classify indicators as input, activity, output, quality, outcome and impact. It should establish a baseline, define the denominator and disaggregate by gender, social group, disability and geography. Administrative data should be matched with independent sample surveys, grievance and drop-off records, social-audit evidence and process studies. Where causal attribution is required, a defensible comparison or quasi-experimental method may be used, while stating its assumptions and external-validity limits.

The NFHS-5 to NFHS-6 decline in stunting is valuable national outcome evidence, but it does not identify one causal programme. Triangulation should therefore diagnose contribution and implementation rather than manufacture certainty. Evaluation succeeds only when findings trigger correction, publication and resource decisions.

*Administrative capacity is the ability to turn authority and money into a reliable, reviewable service.*

### Capacity has several components

1. **Fiscal:** adequate and predictable finance, including State contributions and local operating expenditure.
2. **Human:** enough trained staff with manageable caseloads.
3. **Technical:** usable systems, connectivity, maintenance and data quality.
4. **Legal/functional:** authority to decide, coordinate and correct.
5. **Organisational:** clear responsibility, procurement and interdepartmental routines.
6. **Democratic:** voice, disclosure, appeal and local oversight.

PFMS can make releases and expenditure visible. Under the earlier **SNA model**, each CSS used a designated State nodal-account structure to make balances and downstream use more visible. **SNA-SPARSH** changes the release mechanism further: PFMS, State IFMIS and RBI e-Kuber support just-in-time release of Union and State shares against actual payment requirements rather than advance parking in bank accounts. Applicability from 1 November 2025 does not prove universal operational onboarding. Union visibility is not a substitute for State and local flexibility. Centrally designed schemes operate through varied State rules, staffing and institutions; federalism is therefore a delivery fact, not merely a constitutional chapter.

### Local institutions

Panchayats and municipalities can identify missing households, integrate services and monitor local quality. Yet responsibility without the 3Fs—funds, functions and functionaries—creates blame without capacity. Parallel scheme committees can further fragment authority.

### Fiscal choice

The *Economic Survey 2025–26* warns that rapidly expanding unconditional transfers can constrain fiscal space for employment, skills, health, education, nutrition and capital investment. The transferable lesson is not “cash is bad.” Immediate support and complementary public services must be budgeted together, with periodic review and outcome evidence.

**Strong objection:** outcome-linked funding rewards efficient States. **Reply:** crude linkage can punish harder geographies and historically weak capacity. Use need-adjusted baselines, improvement measures and capacity grants rather than withdrawing essential support.

**UPSC use:** diagnose whether failure lies in design, release, staffing, authority or accountability. **Mini recap:** the same scheme text produces different outcomes when capacity and federal arrangements differ.

**Model answer:** Not primarily. The allocation exists; human and organisational capacity are binding. Finance must still cover recruitment and operations, but the diagnosis cannot stop at outlay.

**Misconception to avoid:** Every delivery failure is a shortage of budget.

**Original Mains practice — 20 marks; answer in 250 words.** *Critically analyse how fiscal federalism and administrative capacity shape the performance of centrally sponsored welfare schemes.*

**Mains model answer:** Centrally sponsored schemes combine national priorities with State and local implementation. Their performance therefore depends on more than the Union outlay. Funds must be released predictably, State shares reconciled, procurement completed, staff posted and operating costs sustained. PFMS and just-in-time release can reduce idle balances and improve visibility, but delayed integration or contribution can interrupt delivery and reduce State cash-planning flexibility.

Capacity also varies. A district may have the same formal scheme but fewer frontline workers, weak connectivity, difficult terrain or fragmented authority across departments and parastatals. Panchayats and municipalities can identify exclusions and coordinate services only when funds, functions and functionaries accompany responsibility. Outcome-linked funding can reward improvement, yet crude formulas may penalise States serving remote or historically deprived populations.

Reform should combine clear national minimums with federal flexibility: predictable multi-year finance; need-adjusted and improvement-sensitive grants; transparent release and exception dashboards; capacity funding for staff, local institutions and maintenance; common data standards with State stewardship; and grievance outcomes that travel across tiers. Fiscal discipline remains necessary, including periodic review of transfers and expenditure mix. The correct test is whether intergovernmental design equips the responsible tier to deliver and correct the entitlement, not whether the central allocation was announced.

**Scoring guide (20):** federal fund-flow chain 5; administrative-capacity analysis 5; local-institution role 3; trade-offs and countercase 3; reform architecture and verdict 4.

*Privacy is not secrecy from welfare; it is disciplined power over collection, linkage, decision and correction.*

Consent is important but cannot carry the whole burden. A person seeking food or pension may have no realistic bargaining choice, and dense consent screens do not create informed control. Welfare processing therefore needs clear legal authority, necessity, proportionality, purpose limitation and remedy, whether or not consent is the applicable basis.

### Four tensions

| Goal | Counter-risk | Design response |
|---|---|---|
| Interoperability | Function creep | Purpose tagging and access controls |
| De-duplication | Authentication exclusion | Alternative means and human review |
| Proactive inclusion | Profiling | Minimum necessary data and transparent criteria |
| Central standards | Federal overreach | State stewardship, agreed standards and role-based access |

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and Rules, 2025 use phased commencement. At this session's date, the one-year tranche scheduled for **13 November 2026** has not yet arrived, while most substantive processing duties and Data Principal rights remain scheduled for **13 May 2027**. Do not describe an enacted framework as fully operative.

### Accountability for automated or data-driven decisions

The citizen should know that data affected the decision, receive a meaningful reason, identify the responsible public authority and obtain human review. Audit must test error rates across groups. Vendor confidentiality cannot erase public responsibility.

**Strong objection:** stronger privacy rules slow convergence and fraud control. **Reply:** proportionate safeguards improve legitimacy and data quality. Uncorrectable or excessive systems generate avoidance, mistrust and litigation. Privacy and inclusion can conflict, but neither should be treated as expendable.

**UPSC use:** apply legality, necessity, proportionality, minimum data, correction and remedy. **Mini recap:** link services, not unrestricted surveillance.

### Revision notes

1. Welfare data processing needs a lawful and defined purpose.
2. Consent is not always freely given in essential-service contexts.
3. Necessity asks whether the data are needed for the purpose.
4. Proportionality asks whether less intrusive means exist.
5. Data minimisation limits collection to what is necessary.
6. Purpose limitation restricts secondary use.
7. Interoperability can create function-creep risk.
8. Authentication failure requires alternative means.
9. Proactive inclusion must not become permanent profiling.
10. Federal data architecture needs State stewardship and common standards.
11. Accuracy and correction are inclusion safeguards.
12. Audit logs identify who used data and why.
13. Automated adverse decisions need reasons and human review.
14. Enacted, commenced and operational are different legal stages.
15. Retention and deletion rules belong to the welfare-data lifecycle.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why is a clicked consent box inadequate protection when access to an essential welfare benefit depends on accepting data linkage?

**Model answer:** The claimant lacks meaningful bargaining choice. Legality, necessity, purpose limitation, minimisation, security and remedy must independently constrain the state.

**Misconception to avoid:** Formal consent automatically makes welfare-data processing fair or proportionate.

**Original Mains practice — 20 marks; answer in 250 words.** *Design a privacy-respecting and federally workable data architecture for convergent welfare delivery.*

**Mains model answer:** Convergent welfare needs interoperable records, but interoperability should not mean an unrestricted central dossier. A federated design should keep authoritative records with the competent Union, State or local institution and permit schemes to query only necessary status fields for a lawful, declared purpose. Common standards and transaction references can support matching while preserving State stewardship.

Each access should be purpose-tagged and logged. Eligibility decisions should disclose the relevant rule and data source, with accessible correction, alternative authentication and human review. Birth, death, disability and migration updates require defined responsibility and time limits. Sensitive health or disability data should not be copied merely because another scheme needs a yes/no status. Retention and deletion rules should prevent indefinite profiling.

Consent can support optional sharing, but essential benefits cannot rely on take-it-or-leave-it clicks. The controlling safeguards are legality, necessity, proportionality, minimisation, security and remedy. Independent audits should test exclusion and error by group, while legislatures and social-accountability institutions review systemic use. Federal coordination requires agreed minimum standards, reciprocal data-quality duties and dispute resolution—not unilateral central extraction. The architecture should make the state easier to coordinate and easier for the citizen to contest.

*A reform is complete only when failure can be detected, remedied and used to change the design.*

### A nine-question reform test

1. **Purpose:** what capability or risk is being addressed?
2. **Coverage:** universal, categorical, targeted, contributory or hybrid—and why?
3. **Error:** which inclusion/exclusion trade-off is acceptable?
4. **Access:** what documents, channels and fallbacks exist?
5. **Continuity:** do mobility and life events break delivery?
6. **Convergence:** which complementary services and authorities are required?
7. **Capacity:** are finance, staff, authority and local institutions adequate?
8. **Accountability:** can a citizen obtain reasons, correction and remedy?
9. **Evidence:** what would justify expansion, redesign or closure?

### Reform principles

- **Presume access where wrongful denial threatens subsistence.**
- **Make the citizen prove a fact once where lawful data reuse can verify it.**
- **Never let one wrong master record multiply denial across schemes.**
- **Keep non-digital assisted access alive.**
- **Separate the coordinating view from each department's legal responsibility.**
- **Measure unresolved exclusion and grievance outcomes.**
- **Use independent evaluation and beneficiary evidence.**
- **Adjust funding for need and starting disadvantage.**
- **Publish revision triggers, not only targets.**

**Counterargument:** a system with fallbacks, review and participation becomes expensive and slow. **Reply:** apparent simplicity often transfers hidden costs to beneficiaries and produces litigation, duplication and failed outcomes. Proportionality still matters: simple, low-risk schemes need lighter controls; high-stakes or data-intensive schemes need stronger safeguards.

**UPSC use:** this lesson supplies the 10/15/20-mark ladder: diagnose one broken link at 10 marks; compare design choices and institutions at 15; trace rights, data, federalism, finance, evaluation and trade-offs at 20. **Mini recap:** welfare reform is not digitisation or convergence alone; it is equitable capability gain through a correctable chain.

### Revision notes

1. Begin reform with the capability or risk, not the platform.
2. Coverage design must be justified by the benefit and error cost.
3. Subsistence schemes should be especially sensitive to exclusion.
4. Identification must include updating and appeal.
5. Delivery requires assisted and non-digital alternatives.
6. Portability must include eligibility, claim and remedy.
7. Convergence links complementary services without blurring responsibility.
8. Data sharing should be federated, minimal and purpose-limited.
9. Local institutions need the 3Fs and safeguards against capture.
10. Finance must include staffing, operations and maintenance.
11. Grievance and social audit provide different correction routes.
12. Monitoring should include quality, exclusion and grievance outcomes.
13. Evaluation requires baseline, denominator, disaggregation and causal humility.
14. Federal design should combine standards with State/local flexibility.
15. Every scheme needs published redesign, review or exit triggers.

### Concept check

**Question:** Which single reform would solve every scheme-performance problem: a universal registry, DBT, district convergence or outcome budgeting?

**Model answer:** None. Each solves a different link. Performance requires a coherent chain from coverage design and identification through delivery, convergence, capacity, remedy and learning.

**Misconception to avoid:** A powerful tool is not a complete welfare architecture.

**Original Mains practice — 20 marks; answer in 250 words.** *Propose an end-to-end reform framework for equitable and accountable welfare delivery in India.*

**Mains model answer:** Welfare reform should begin with the capability to be secured, not with a database or payment platform. Essential services and life-cycle security need a broad floor, supplemented by targeted support for disability, remoteness or entrenched disadvantage. Eligibility rules should explicitly state the inclusion–exclusion trade-off and provide event-based updating.

Access should be assisted and multi-channel. Identity, transaction and benefit portability must be separated; a registration should never be presented as realised protection. District and local institutions can coordinate complementary services through a household plan, while each department remains legally answerable. A federated data layer should exchange only necessary status fields, log decisions and permit correction, alternative authentication and human review.

Performance also requires predictable finance, trained staff and operating capacity. Grievance redress should correct individual denial; social audit and independent evaluation should detect patterns. Dashboards must distinguish allocation, output, quality, outcome and impact, with subgroup disaggregation and a stated counterfactual limit.

Finally, scheme rules should contain periodic review and redesign triggers. Universal access, targeting efficiency, privacy, federal flexibility and fiscal sustainability are genuine trade-offs; the defensible architecture makes those choices visible, contestable and reversible. Success is equitable capability gain plus timely correction when the system is wrong.

# ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS MODEL PRACTICE

These questions are new and distinct from the official PYQs.

**10 marks; answer in 150 words. Distinguish a social registry from a beneficiary list and explain the governance significance.**

**Mains model answer:** A beneficiary list is the scheme-specific set of persons approved to receive a benefit at a given time. A social registry is a wider system used to identify or assess people and households for one or more programmes. The distinction matters because a registry can reduce repeated applications and support proactive convergence, but it does not itself confer every entitlement. Each scheme still requires lawful eligibility rules and a reasoned decision. If a static registry is treated as an unquestionable master list, one error can exclude the same person across several schemes. A sound architecture therefore uses federated source records, minimum necessary sharing, event-based updating, decision logs and accessible correction. The registry should make it easier to discover and verify need; it must not convert an administrative profile into an irreversible judgment on citizenship or vulnerability.

**Scoring guide (10):** precise distinction 3; benefit-conferral limit 2; systemic risk 2; governance safeguards 3.

**15 marks; answer in 250 words. Examine the role of local institutions in convergent welfare delivery.**

**Mains model answer:** Local institutions see the household rather than the departmental file. Panchayats, municipalities, ward offices and frontline workers can identify unrecorded vulnerability, assist applications, coordinate referrals and verify whether services are usable. Their local knowledge is especially valuable for migrants, disabled persons, elderly people living alone and households facing sudden shocks.

Yet proximity is not capacity. Local bodies often lack predictable funds, control over staff and authority over departmental schemes. Parallel committees and upward reporting can fragment responsibility. Local discretion may also reproduce caste, gender or political exclusion.

Convergent delivery therefore needs a common local intake, a household service plan, a named coordinator and a district mechanism to resolve interdepartmental blocks. Each benefit must retain a named legal duty-holder. Published criteria, record access, representative participation, social audit, reasoned decisions and appeal outside the locality can control capture. Finance must cover staff, outreach and operating costs, not merely infrastructure. Local institutions should be the coordinating and feedback layer of welfare, but only with the 3Fs and safeguards that make proximity accountable.

**Scoring guide (15):** local comparative advantage 3; capacity constraints 3; accountable convergence design 5; capture safeguards 2; verdict 2.

**20 marks; answer in 250 words. “The central challenge of welfare data is not scarcity but governance.” Critically analyse.**

**Mains model answer:** India possesses extensive identity, scheme, payment, labour and survey data, yet more data do not automatically produce fair welfare. Administrative systems see enrolled persons and can miss the eligible outsider. Static deprivation records become stale; incompatible definitions obstruct convergence; and an incorrect common record can multiply exclusion.

Governance determines purpose, authority and correction. A federated architecture should query necessary status fields from authoritative sources rather than pool unrestricted personal dossiers. It should record provenance and decision rules, permit event-based updating and give notice, correction, alternative authentication and human review. Interoperability must be reconciled with purpose limitation, minimisation and security.

Federalism and accountability are equally important. Union standards can enable portability, while States and local bodies retain implementation knowledge and legal responsibilities. Automated or vendor-built systems cannot erase the public duty-holder. Independent audit should test group-wise errors, while grievance and social audit expose failures invisible to dashboards.

Data scarcity still matters in remote or under-recorded populations, and surveys have lags. But the binding challenge is often that systems collect what administration can count rather than what citizens need, and cannot correct what they get wrong. Welfare data become an asset only when governed as contestable public power.

**Scoring guide (20):** data-abundance thesis 4; architecture and quality analysis 5; rights safeguards 4; federal/accountability dimension 4; counterpoint and verdict 3.

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

## The scheme-performance spine

- Start with **need and entitlement**, then trace identification → enrolment → release/service → usable receipt → outcome → remedy → redesign.
- Keep stages separate: allocation ≠ release ≠ expenditure ≠ output ≠ quality ≠ outcome ≠ attributable impact.
- Evaluate through **effectiveness, efficiency, equity and accountability**. Add sustainability where the stem requires.
- Administrative data manage operations; surveys estimate population outcomes; social audit reveals lived discrepancy; evaluation tests contribution or impact.
- A statistic must carry its source, denominator, reference period, distribution and causal limit.

## Targeting and coverage design

- **Inclusion error:** ineligible included. **Exclusion error:** eligible excluded. **Leakage:** diversion/loss. Do not merge them.
- SECC 2011 is a deprivation/occupation data exercise distinct from the decennial Census; its caste data were not officially released or validated for general policy use, and static proxies cannot be treated as present need.
- Census 2027 includes a caste-enumeration decision and scheduled census operations, but as of 3 October 2026 it has produced no national caste results or ready-made targeting list.
- Aadhaar can verify identity and support de-duplication; it does not prove poverty, disability, widowhood or every eligibility condition.
- Error harms are asymmetric: inclusion imposes fiscal/distributional cost; exclusion from subsistence welfare imposes direct human harm.
- Universalism reduces stigma and list-based exclusion; targeting concentrates resources but raises identification and documentation risk.
- Preferred Indian architecture: **universal or broad life-cycle floor + targeted/categorical top-up + contributory layer where feasible**.
- PM-JAY's original poor-and-vulnerable family route uses SECC 2011 deprivation/occupation criteria, while its separate 70+ expansion is age-based irrespective of socio-economic status.
- The 2023 PYQ requires distinguishing unjust discrimination from relevant differentiation that advances vertical equity.
- NSAP is an umbrella comprising old-age, widow and disability pensions, family benefit and Annapurna; it is not one pension.

## Convergence, portability and lifecycle

- Horizontal convergence links departments; vertical convergence links tiers; household convergence coordinates complementary benefits around one family's needs.
- Multiplicity is not convergence. Diagnose databases, eligibility rules, cadres, timing, authority and combined outcomes.
- Common intake and a household service plan may reduce citizen burden; each department must retain a named duty-holder.
- Identity portability ≠ transaction portability ≠ contribution portability ≠ entitlement portability ≠ remedy portability.
- ONORC makes an existing NFSA entitlement transactionally portable; it does not create universal eligibility.
- e-Shram/UAN records a worker and can support convergence; registration is not pension, insurance or paid claim.
- Life events—birth, death, marriage, disability, migration and livelihood shock—need accessible record updating.

## Registry and data architecture

- A social registry identifies or assesses persons/households across programmes; a beneficiary list records approved recipients for one scheme.
- Test **coverage, accuracy and timeliness** separately.
- Prefer a **federated** architecture: authoritative source records + common standards + minimum necessary status exchange.
- One wrong master record can multiply exclusion across schemes; every common field needs provenance and correction.
- Essential safeguards: lawful purpose, necessity, proportionality, minimisation, purpose limitation, security, audit logs, access, correction, human review, retention limits and non-digital assistance.
- Consent alone is inadequate where an essential benefit creates take-it-or-leave-it dependence.
- Enacted, commenced and operational are separate stages. The DPDP framework uses phased commencement; do not claim the entire regime is currently operative.

## Delivery, grievance and accountability

- DBT can reduce duplicate/ghost entries, intermediation and payment delay; it is a payment/delivery reform, not a substitute for fair targeting or quality provision.
- PFMS provides government fund-flow, payment, accounting and reporting infrastructure.
- SNA provides the designated State CSS account structure; SNA-SPARSH is the later PFMS–State IFMIS–RBI e-Kuber just-in-time release architecture. Applicability from 1 November 2025 must not be equated with completed onboarding everywhere.
- Account credit is not necessarily usable benefit; test authentication, bank mapping, cash-out, service access and citizen cost.
- Assisted exceptions should be logged and audited rather than prohibited.
- Grievance redress corrects an individual's denial; social audit checks community-level records and patterns.
- Accountability chain: information → comprehension → verification → answerability → action taken → remedy/consequence → system correction.
- Participation must have information, representation and influence; otherwise awareness campaigns and consultations remain tokenistic.

## Monitoring, capacity and federalism

- Results chain: input → activity → output → outcome → impact. Attribution requires a counterfactual or explicit causal humility.
- NFHS-6, conducted in 2023–24 and released 29 May 2026, reported under-five stunting at 29.3%, compared with 35.5% in NFHS-5; this is survey change, not single-scheme attribution.
- **Core evidence taxonomy:** PLFS/NSO-MoSPI measures sampled labour outcomes; NCRB reports police-recorded crime; the SDG India Index compares composite State/UT progress; National MPI uses NFHS-based multidimensional deprivation estimates. Their units and limits are not interchangeable.
- **Core institutional chain:** OOMF states annual outlay–output–outcome commitments for covered major schemes; DMEO monitors/evaluates, diagnoses weak performance and recommends course correction without automatically redesigning the scheme.
- Indicators can induce gaming; use a balanced basket, independent validation, subgroup disaggregation and drop-off measurement.
- Capacity comprises finance, staff, technology, authority, organisation and democratic accountability.
- Local bodies require funds, functions and functionaries; proximity without capacity creates responsibility without delivery.
- Federal welfare must combine national minimums and interoperability with State/local stewardship and operational flexibility.
- Just-in-time fund flow improves visibility and reduces idle balances but can create reconciliation and State-flexibility costs.
- Outcome-linked finance should adjust for starting disadvantage and fund capacity; it must not punish remote or historically deprived populations.

- **2019, Discuss:** performance thesis → awareness as usable information → involvement at design, outreach, delivery, monitoring, grievance and redesign → participation limits → structured participation plus state capacity.

- Final verdict: **scheme success is equitable capability gain plus timely correction when data, design or delivery is wrong.**
