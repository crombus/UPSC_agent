# Persons with Disabilities — Live Session Edition

## Roadmap

The central question: **what changes when a person has a legal right to participate, but the classroom, workplace, bus or website still excludes them?** Learn the whole answer-ready statutory and delivery chain before the optional deeper evaluation.

|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | Core | Which disabilities are recognised, and who qualifies for a benchmark entitlement? | 30 min |
| 3 | Core | How do legal capacity, community life, education, work, health and social security fit together? | 40 min |
| 4 | Core | What makes accommodation different from general accessibility? | 35 min |
| 5 | Core | How do standards, transport and digital design turn access into a duty? | 30 min |
| 6 | Core | How do certificates, devices, support bodies and complaints reach a person? | 40 min |
| 7 | Core | How do we answer an inclusion question without mistaking a legal target for an outcome? | 30 min |
| 8 | Advanced | Where do thresholds, legacy buildings, data and institutional scope strain the rights model? | 30 min |

**Route:** person and barrier → legal recognition → entitlements → individual adjustment → universal access → delivery and remedy → answer writing → optional stress test. Each lesson contains one concept check and a separate original Mains question with a scored model. The last teaching content is the consolidated register notes; coverage and sources follow as ledgers.

## Lesson 1 — A barrier can disable

Progress: 1/8 | Stage: Foundation | Subtopic: Disability, dignity and the medical–social–rights shift

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

| Lens | Where is the problem? | Typical response | What it misses |
|---|---|---|---|
 Medical | In an individual's impairment | Diagnosis, treatment, rehabilitation | Steps, prejudice and rules that exclude |
 Social | In impairment interacting with barriers | Remove built, informational and attitudinal barriers | Some people still need individually tailored support |
 Rights | In unequal enjoyment of rights | Enforce equal treatment, accessibility and accommodation | An Act alone cannot prove compliance |

*The lenses answer different questions; medical care remains useful without making participation conditional on a cure.*

```text
Impairment + inaccessible setting → unequal participation
                   ↓
        remove the barrier and offer support
                   ↓
              actual opportunity
```

*Neither treatment alone nor removal of just one barrier guarantees equal participation.*

**2026 Civil Services (Preliminary) Examination · General Studies Paper I · Set A · Q56 · objective · 2 marks · two-hour paper; 200 marks; 100 equal-mark questions; one-third penalty for a wrong response:**

> Which of the following statements with regard to the persons with disabilities in India is/are correct?
>
> 1. The Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, an Act passed by the Parliament of India in 2018, mandates reservation in education and employment, places a legal duty on Governments to ensure accessibility and non-discrimination.
> 2. The Sugamya Bharat Abhiyan focuses on achieving universal accessibility for Persons with Disabilities across three key domains - built infrastructure, transport systems and information and communication technology.
> 3. The National Divyangjan Finance and Development Corporation (NDFDC) is a public sector organisation set up by the Ministry of Corporate Affairs as a not-for-profit company to promote entrepreneurship among Persons with Disabilities (PwDs).
>
> Select the answer using the code given below:
>
> (a) 1 and 2
> (b) 2 only
> (c) 1 and 3
> (d) 1 only

The question is presented here before the lessons supply the statutory, accessibility and institutional distinctions needed to analyse it.

Imagine a hearing-impaired student at a lecture without captions. The impairment is real; the **exclusion** depends partly on the teaching format. Add live captioning and the classroom becomes usable without changing the student's hearing. This is why the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (RPwD) Act, 2016 describes a person with disability through long-term physical, mental, intellectual or sensory impairment **in interaction with barriers** hindering equal participation. A barrier can be a staircase, an unreadable form, an inflexible examination rule or an assumption of incapacity. The example does not imply every impairment can be neutralised by a single technology.

India ratified the **UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (UNCRPD)** in 2007. The **2016 Act came into force on 19 April 2017**; its **2017 Rules were notified on 15 June 2017**. It replaced the 1995 disability law and operationalises a rights-oriented approach. **✅ Fact:** Section 3 protects equality and dignity and prohibits disability discrimination; the Act's definition of discrimination includes denial of reasonable accommodation. **⚠️ Inference:** A right is substantive only when a person can use the service, seek an adjustment and obtain a remedy; an announcement of inclusion is not its achievement.

The objection is that “barriers” language ignores chronic illness, care and rehabilitation. The reply is not to deny impairment: combine voluntary medical and assistive support with enforceable changes in institutions. Conversely, kindness or charitable devices alone cannot replace a rights claim. The disability development question therefore begins with **agency**: consult the person about the barrier rather than presuming their needs.

**UPSC use:** GS-II's vulnerable-sections law and welfare clause asks about both protection and performance. Open a “paradigm shift” answer with impairment *and* barriers, then show enforceable duties and a route from law to lived access. Do not call all PwDs “benchmark PwDs.” Next: which forms of disability the Act names, and which entitlements require assessment.

### Revision notes

1. Disability is produced by the interaction of long-term impairment with barriers to equal participation.
2. The medical lens addresses diagnosis, treatment and rehabilitation; it does not explain institutional exclusion by itself.
3. The social lens identifies built, informational, procedural and attitudinal barriers.
4. The rights lens adds equality, dignity, autonomy, accessibility, accommodation and remedies.
5. Medical or rehabilitation support can remain valuable when chosen; participation cannot be made conditional on cure.
6. India ratified the UNCRPD in 2007, and the RPwD Act, 2016 replaced the 1995 law.
7. The Act commenced on 19 April 2017, and the RPwD Rules were notified on 15 June 2017.
8. Section 3 prohibits disability discrimination, including denial of reasonable accommodation.
9. A legal announcement is an input; usable participation and correction of denial are outcome tests.
10. Agency requires consultation with the person rather than assumptions about incapacity or need.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why would adding captioning to the lecture be a rights issue rather than proof that hearing impairment has been cured?

**Model answer:** The impairment persists, but an institutionally produced barrier to equal participation can be reduced. The rights approach requires usable access rather than making a person's participation depend on medical treatment.

**Misconception to avoid:** The social model does not assert that impairments or healthcare needs are imaginary.

### Original Mains practice — 10 marks, 150 words

**Mains prompt:** Explain the difference between a medical and a rights-based understanding of disability with an Indian education example.

**Model (about 98 words):** A medical lens identifies impairment and possible treatment; a rights-based lens also asks why institutions exclude people with that impairment. Under the RPwD Act, 2016, disability involves long-term impairment interacting with barriers to equal participation. A student who cannot follow an uncaptioned lecture may benefit from healthcare, but the college must also examine accessible teaching and individual adjustments. The UNCRPD-informed law connects equal opportunity with dignity, accessibility and reasonable accommodation. This does not make clinical rehabilitation irrelevant: it makes care a support chosen alongside, not a precondition for, education. Success is participation, not merely diagnosis or enrolment.

**Scoring guide (10):** distinct models 3; statutory/UNCRPD link 2; causal Indian example 3; qualified conclusion 2.

| Statutory cluster | Named Schedule entries |
|---|---|
 Physical and sensory | Blindness; low vision; leprosy-cured person; hearing impairment (deaf and hard of hearing); locomotor disability; dwarfism; acid-attack victims; speech and language disability |
 Intellectual/developmental and psychosocial | Intellectual disability; specific learning disabilities; autism spectrum disorder; mental illness |
 Neurological and muscular | Cerebral palsy; muscular dystrophy; chronic neurological conditions; multiple sclerosis; Parkinson's disease |
 Blood and combined | Thalassaemia; haemophilia; sickle-cell disease; multiple disabilities including deafblindness |

*The Act's Schedule specifies **21 categories** (some entries have subtypes); this grouping is a learning aid, not a replacement statutory Schedule or a separate eligibility test.*

The earlier 1995 law recognised **seven** categories; the 2016 Act's Schedule recognises **21**, with statutory scope for additional notified categories. Disability is not a single medical condition. Hearing impairment has deaf and hard-of-hearing subtypes; multiple disabilities can include deafblindness. “Mental illness” is not a synonym for intellectual disability; a specific learning disability is not evidence that a student cannot learn. The National Trust law later discussed is *not* a list of all these categories.

**Static legal update:** Exercising section 99(1), official **S.O. 2627(E), dated 22 May 2026**, substituted Schedule entry 1-A(e) under physical disability → locomotor disability. It now defines an “acid attack victim” as a person disfigured **externally or internally**, due to **violent assault or self-infliction or accident**, by throwing, administering or spilling acid or a similar corrosive substance. The notification operates from its publication in the Official Gazette. It revises the content of an existing Schedule entry; it does **not** create a twenty-second specified-disability category.

Now separate two legal questions:

```text
Long-term impairment + interacting barriers
                 ↓
Person with disability: general equality/access claims
                 │
      separately assess specified disability
                 ↓
Person with benchmark disability: ordinarily 40% or more
of a specified disability as certified by competent authority
                 ↓
Eligible to seek threshold-linked statutory benefits
— subject also to each benefit's applicable conditions
```

*The benchmark is a gate for particular entitlements, not a gate to being treated with dignity.*

Under section 2(r), “benchmark disability” also accommodates the Act's assessment route where a specified disability is not expressed in measurable percentage terms; avoid mechanically applying a number to every condition. Sections 56–58 concern assessment, certifying authorities and certificates. A certificate records assessed status; **UDID** is an administrative identity and portability route, not the legal source of every right. Consider two candidates with similar access barriers but differently assessed percentages: both may need a usable examination, while a threshold-linked reservation requires its own statutory eligibility. This illustration cannot predetermine either person's medical assessment.

**Objection:** A measurable threshold makes limited reserved opportunities administrable. **Reply:** Keep the threshold for its specified statutory purpose but never use it to deny the wider non-discrimination and access duties. The residual difficulty is fair assessment of fluctuating or less readily quantified conditions.

**UPSC use:** Explain **PwD ≠ person with benchmark disability ≠ UDID holder ≠ admitted student**. For Prelims, separate the 21-category Schedule from the 40% benchmark and distinguish certificate from actual access. Next: what the Act actually promises across legal capacity, community life, schools, workplaces, healthcare and social protection.

### Revision notes

1. The 1995 law recognised seven disability categories; the RPwD Act Schedule specifies 21.
2. The Central Government may notify additional specified disabilities; the present Schedule should not be casually expanded by inference.
3. A person with disability is defined through long-term impairment interacting with barriers.
4. A person with benchmark disability ordinarily has at least 40% of a specified disability, duly certified.
5. The Act also provides an assessment route for specified disabilities not expressed in measurable percentages.
6. Benchmark status triggers particular threshold-linked entitlements; it is not the gateway to dignity or every access claim.
7. Sections 56–58 govern assessment, certifying authorities and disability certificates.
8. UDID standardises identification and portability; it does not itself create every benefit.
9. Mental illness, intellectual disability and specific learning disability are distinct statutory concepts.
10. PwD, benchmark PwD, certificate holder, UDID holder and actual beneficiary are not interchangeable statuses.
11. S.O. 2627(E), dated 22 May 2026, broadened the existing acid-attack-victim entry to cover external or internal disfigurement from violent assault, self-infliction or accident involving acid or a similar corrosive substance; it did not add a twenty-second category.

### Concept check

**Question:** A person has an assessed specified disability below the benchmark. May a college treat that finding as automatic permission to refuse a needed access adjustment?

**Model answer:** No. A threshold governs identified benchmark-dependent entitlements; the broader non-discrimination and reasonable-accommodation obligations must be assessed independently on their own terms.

**Misconception to avoid:** A 40% benchmark is not the definition of every person with disability.

### Original Mains practice — 10 marks, 150 words

**Mains prompt:** Distinguish specified disability, benchmark disability and UDID in the operation of the RPwD Act.

**Model (about 99 words):** The Act's Schedule specifies disability categories, expanded from the seven in the 1995 regime to 21 in the 2016 Act. A person with benchmark disability is a separately assessed and certified person ordinarily meeting the 40% criterion for a specified disability, subject to the statutory route for conditions not measured in percentage terms. Benchmark status matters for particular reservations; it is not a condition for everyone's equal dignity. UDID standardises certificate and identity processes and can ease portability. A valid card does not establish that a classroom is accessible or that a reserved seat has actually been occupied; authorities must test delivery separately.

**Scoring guide (10):** three clear definitions 4; accurate threshold qualification 2; right-versus-benefit distinction 2; delivery qualification 2.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

| Setting | General duty | Benchmark-specific entitlement | Common failure |
|---|---|---|---|
 Neighbourhood schooling | Inclusive admission, accessible campus/learning, suitable communication and support (ss. 16–17) | Free education, ages **6–18**, in neighbourhood school or special school **of choice** (s. 31) | A school admits a pupil but excludes them from teaching |
 Higher education | Equal access and appropriate accommodation | At least **5% seats** in government and government-aided higher educational institutions (s. 32); age relaxation applies as prescribed | A formal reserved seat with inaccessible assessment |
 Employment | No disability discrimination; reasonable accommodation and barrier-free workplace in government establishments (s. 20); equal-opportunity policy applies to every establishment (s. 21) | At least **4% of vacancies** in government establishments (s. 34), allocated among prescribed disability groupings and identified posts | Posts remain unidentified or inaccessible |

*A reservation is a legal minimum in a defined sector, not a measured employment or enrolment rate.*

```text
Eligible entry (reserved seat / vacancy)
                 ↓
Usable teaching or workplace
                 ↓
Participation and retention → complaint and correction if blocked
```

*The legal entitlement to enter is distinct from the continuing opportunity to succeed.*

Start with the student: letting them through the gate does not make a lecture readable or assessment usable. Section 16 tasks **appropriate government and local authorities** with ensuring recognised/funded educational institutions provide inclusive education: admit without discrimination, make facilities accessible, provide reasonable accommodation and necessary support, and detect learning needs. Section 17 addresses measures such as teacher training, communication support and teaching material. Section 31's free-education entitlement applies to **children with benchmark disabilities** in its specified age band and respects choice; it is not the only right in school. Section 32's higher-education quota covers **government and government-aided** institutions, not every private college.

Turn to employment: section 20 bars disability discrimination in **government employment** and calls for accommodation and barrier-free settings; a government employee acquiring a disability is not simply dismissed or reduced in rank because of it—the statute provides adjustment to a suitable post and supernumerary arrangement when required. Section 21 calls for an equal-opportunity policy from **every establishment** and section 23 creates grievance-redress arrangements in government establishments. Section 34 reserves government vacancies for **persons with benchmark disabilities**, subject to identification and the statute's procedures/exemptions. Unlike vertical SC/ST/OBC reservations under Article 16(4), disability reservation is a **statutory horizontal** entitlement: eligible people may also belong to other social categories. Do not apply a generic vertical-reservation ceiling as if it itself nullified this statutory design.

These are not the Act's only protections. The UNCRPD and the RPwD Act treat the person as a legal actor and community member, not merely a beneficiary:

| Life domain | Rights content | Necessary qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Legal capacity | UNCRPD Article 12 and RPwD section 13 recognise equal legal capacity, including property, financial affairs and access to credit; support must respect autonomy, dignity and privacy | Support is not permission for another person to substitute their preferences |
| Community living | UNCRPD Article 19 and RPwD section 5 protect living in the community, choice of living arrangement and access to in-house, residential and community support, including personal assistance | Community living does not mean withdrawal of needed care or forcing one model of residence |
| Social security | UNCRPD Article 28 protects an adequate standard of living and equal access to social protection; RPwD section 24 requires schemes, within economic capacity and development, for adequate living and independent/community life, considering disability diversity, gender, age and socio-economic status | Listed measures do not create one automatic identical pension for every person; applicable scheme and income conditions still matter |
| Health | UNCRPD Article 25 requires non-discriminatory healthcare, disability-related services, proximity including rural access and free and informed consent; RPwD section 25 adds barrier-free public and private hospitals, priority and specified government measures | Prevention and rehabilitation cannot override consent, reproductive autonomy or equal treatment |

Section 24's listed measures include community facilities, disaster support, livelihood support for women with disabilities, accessible water and sanitation, aids and diagnostic services subject to notified conditions, pensions, unemployment and caregiver allowances, and insurance routes. Section 25 includes healthcare near the person, barrier-free facilities, priority in attendance and treatment, emergency care and sexual and reproductive healthcare, especially for women with disabilities. These are differentiated duties and scheme commitments, not proof that each listed service has reached every person.

### Intersectionality changes the barrier chain

Intersectionality asks how disability combines with gender, age, poverty, caste, tribal or rural location and type of impairment to change both exposure to exclusion and the route to remedy. It is not a decorative list of identities.

```text
Disability-related barrier
        + gendered control or violence
        + poverty / remote location / social discrimination
        ↓
Greater cost of travel, information, documentation and complaint
        ↓
Right exists, but reaching the institution and remedy becomes harder
        ↓
Accessible local service + income support + safe complaint route
        + communication support + participation in the decision
```

A poor rural woman with locomotor disability may confront an inaccessible primary health centre, unaffordable transport, dependence on a family member for documents and a gendered reluctance to discuss reproductive care. A child with an intellectual disability may need age-appropriate communication and protection without losing the right to express a view. A Dalit or tribal PwD may face social discrimination alongside inaccessible public services. **⚠️ Analytical inference:** the remedy must therefore identify which institution controls each barrier; “double disadvantage” alone is not an implementation plan. Section 4's equal-rights protection for women and children and section 24(2)'s disability, gender, age and socio-economic diversity requirement support differentiated design.

**Strong objection:** A quota may fill posts but leave applicants unable to enter the building, use software or obtain promotion. **Reply:** Combine post identification, usable recruitment, adjustment after appointment and review of actual retention and career progression. The residual difficulty is ensuring the duty is fulfilled by each institution, not simply reporting centrally aggregated vacancies.

**UPSC use:** In a welfare-laws answer, give the exact scope: **4% government vacancies; 5% government/government-aided HE seats; free education 6–18 for benchmark children**. State who owes the duty before proposing a remedy. For health and social security, distinguish a statutory obligation to formulate and deliver measures from proof of an unconditional universal payment. Next: the particular adjustment still needed in an otherwise accessible institution.

### Revision notes

1. UNCRPD Article 12 and RPwD section 13 protect equal legal capacity and supported exercise of choice.
2. Legal capacity includes property, financial affairs and access to credit; support must avoid undue influence and respect privacy.
3. UNCRPD Article 19 and RPwD section 5 protect community living, choice of arrangement and access to personal/community support.
4. Sections 16–17 require inclusive education, accessible facilities, accommodation, support, appropriate communication and monitoring.
5. Section 31 provides free education for benchmark children aged 6–18 in a neighbourhood or special school of choice.
6. Section 32 reserves at least 5% of seats in government and government-aided higher educational institutions.
7. Section 34 reserves at least 4% of government vacancies for benchmark candidates; it is a statutory horizontal entitlement.
8. Sections 20–21 distinguish government-employment accommodation duties from every establishment's equal-opportunity policy.
9. Section 24 social-security schemes must consider disability diversity, gender, age and socio-economic status.
10. Section 25 combines accessible healthcare, priority, rural proximity, emergency care and sexual/reproductive healthcare measures.
11. Intersectionality changes the cost and route of exercising a right; it requires barrier-specific institutional responses.
12. Reservation, community support, healthcare and social protection address different parts of a life-course inclusion chain.

### Concept check

**Question:** A government college fills its benchmark reserved seats but refuses accessible course material. Has it satisfied the whole statutory inclusion obligation?

**Model answer:** No. Reservation secures an entry opportunity; inclusive education and reasonable accommodation concern the student's actual ability to study and participate on an equal basis.

**Misconception to avoid:** A legislated seat percentage is not evidence of accessible teaching or successful completion.

### Original Mains practice — 15 marks, 250 words

**Mains prompt:** Examine how the RPwD Act connects inclusive education and employment reservation with substantive participation.

**Model (about 165 words):** The RPwD Act, 2016 does more than distribute seats: it links entry to the conditions for learning and work. Sections 16–17 require inclusive education measures, such as accessible teaching, suitable support and staff preparation. Section 31 secures a benchmark child's free education between ages six and eighteen through the available school choice, while section 32 reserves at least five per cent of seats in government and government-aided higher educational institutions for benchmark candidates. Admission without usable study material cannot realise the purpose of these provisions. In work, section 34 sets a minimum four-per-cent government-vacancy reservation; sections 20–21 address non-discrimination, accommodation in government employment and equal-opportunity policies more broadly. A candidate facing inaccessible recruitment software cannot convert a formal vacancy into opportunity. Horizontal statutory disability reservation must not be confused with Article 16(4) vertical reservation. Authorities should assess post identification, recruitment access, retention and grievance correction, not only quota announcements. These obligations carry qualification: eligibility and institutional coverage differ across provisions, and measured outcomes require evidence beyond the statute.

**Scoring guide (15):** correct education scope and duties 4; work duties and quota 4; causal example 2; distinction and limits 3; outcome-based conclusion 2.

## Lesson 4 — Equal treatment sometimes needs a different route

Progress: 4/8 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Reasonable accommodation, discrimination and examination access

Book context: queried — substantive equality and examination-access context available.
CA search: "site:depwd.gov.in \"1.7.2026\" \"abeyance\" examination disabilities guidelines"
CA found: DEPwD's 1 July 2026 memorandum (uploaded 2 July) partially modified its 25 June office memorandum on abeyance of revised competitive-examination guidelines dated 1 August 2025; a procedural status change, not a finding that examinations are now accessible.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

```text
Uniform rule: everyone completes a printed paper in identical time
                          ↓
Candidate cannot use print format because of an impairment
                          ↓
Ask: what change is necessary for equal exercise of the right?
                          ↓
Accessible format / eligible scribe / appropriate time or software
                          ↓
Check necessity, suitability and disproportionate or undue burden
                          ↓
Provide and implement the justified individual adjustment
```

*An apparently identical rule can produce unequal access; the adjustment must fit the actual barrier and governing examination rules.*

**Reasonable accommodation**, section 2(y), means necessary and appropriate modification and adjustment, without a disproportionate or undue burden, to ensure equal enjoyment or exercise of rights. The prohibition of discrimination expressly includes denial of reasonable accommodation. Do not treat “reasonable” as a blanket permission to refuse: identify the request, workable alternatives, burden and effect on equal access. In *Vikash Kumar v. UPSC* (2021), the Supreme Court's treatment of scribe access illustrates the danger of equating all examination assistance with a benchmark quota; identify the applicable rule and individual need rather than assume a threshold disposes of every request.

Contrast **universal design**, which makes a service usable by the widest range of people from its outset, with **individual accommodation**, which responds to a remaining person's specific barrier. A screen-reader-compatible examination portal helps many candidates; an appropriately supported candidate may still need a particular arrangement. Not every candidate needs the same adjustment; nor does one accommodation erase all structural barriers.

The **1 July 2026 DEPwD memorandum (uploaded 2 July)** is a useful caution: it addresses the **status of revised examination guidelines**, not repeal of the Act's accommodation principle. Verify the currently applicable examination notification before making an operational claim about scribe entitlement or time concessions. **Objection:** Individual adjustments might threaten examination comparability. **Reply:** Equal assessment should compare the ability being tested rather than reward access to an unusable format; assess concrete alternatives and genuine burdens. The unresolved question is how examination bodies document requests consistently while respecting privacy.

**UPSC use:** For “discuss”, distinguish outcome-equity from identical treatment: definition → actual barrier → appropriate adjustment → proportionate burden → remedy. Do not confuse an examination office memorandum with amendment of the parent statute. Next: the design and standards needed so fewer people must request exceptions.

### Revision notes

1. Section 2(y) defines reasonable accommodation as a necessary and appropriate modification or adjustment.
2. The adjustment must enable equal enjoyment or exercise of rights.
3. A disproportionate or undue burden is a case-specific limit, not a formula for automatic refusal.
4. Denial of reasonable accommodation can amount to disability discrimination.
5. Accessibility is systemic design; accommodation responds to a remaining individual's barrier.
6. Universal design reduces foreseeable barriers but does not eliminate every need for individual support.
7. *Vikash Kumar v. UPSC* warns against mechanically equating examination accommodation with benchmark-quota eligibility.
8. Examination comparability should preserve the ability being tested, not an inaccessible format.
9. The 1 July 2026 DEPwD memorandum concerns the operative status of revised examination guidelines, not the parent Act.
10. A reasoned decision should identify the request, alternatives, burden, privacy concern and review route.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why does a wheelchair ramp at an examination centre not by itself resolve a screen-reader user's accommodation request?

**Model answer:** The ramp removes a built-environment barrier, not a digital or examination-format barrier. Reasonable accommodation tests the particular candidate's access need separately and considers a suitable adjustment and its burden.

**Misconception to avoid:** One visible accessibility feature does not prove equal access to every service.

### Original Mains practice — 15 marks, 250 words

**Mains prompt:** Discuss reasonable accommodation as an instrument of equality in competitive examinations, distinguishing it from universal accessibility.

**Model (about 148 words):** Reasonable accommodation under section 2(y) of the RPwD Act is a necessary, appropriate adjustment, short of disproportionate or undue burden, enabling equal exercise of rights. An accessible examination website is a systemic design measure, but a candidate may also need an accessible question format or scribe arrangement under the applicable rules. Identical print papers would then assess access to print as well as the ability the examination intends to test. The Supreme Court's *Vikash Kumar v. UPSC* illustrates why an individual claim cannot automatically be reduced to benchmark-reservation eligibility. DEPwD's July 2026 modification regarding abeyance of revised examination guidelines concerns the applicable procedural framework, not the survival of statutory equality duties. Examination bodies should give reasoned, individual decisions, train staff and offer review while protecting comparability and privacy. Neither a ramp nor a legal declaration alone establishes usable examination access.

**Scoring guide (15):** correct statutory test 4; accessibility/accommodation contrast 3; Indian example and case 3; procedural-status caution 2; proportionate resolution 3.

*An accessible destination is not accessible to a person who cannot reach it or complete its digital transaction.*

The Accessible India Campaign (Sugamya Bharat) framed the three pillars as **built environment, transportation and information/communication technology (ICT)**. The relevant statutory framework is broader than a campaign name. RPwD sections 40–46 address standards, access to transport and services and accessibility obligations for buildings and service providers; distinguish rule-making, certification and deadlines from an assertion that every site complied. After March 2024, DEPwD's accessibility work is supported through the **Scheme for Implementation of the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act (SIPDA)**. A programme or funding vehicle assists implementation; it does not itself certify each station.

### Statute, person and legal threshold

- UNCRPD ratified **2007**; RPwD Act **2016**, commenced **19 April 2017**, with **2017 Rules notified 15 June 2017**, replaces the **1995** law. Old specified categories **seven**, new Schedule **21**, with scope for notification of additions.
- **Static legal update, not the CA anchor:** S.O. 2627(E), dated **22 May 2026**, substituted the existing acid-attack-victim entry to cover external or internal disfigurement caused by violent assault, self-infliction or accident through acid or a similar corrosive substance. It did not add a twenty-second category.
- Disability involves long-term impairment **interacting with barriers**. Medical support can coexist with social barrier removal and enforceable rights.
- Schedule clusters: vision, hearing, locomotor/physical, developmental/intellectual, psychosocial, neurological, blood, speech/language, acid attack and multiple disabilities. **Mental illness ≠ intellectual disability; learning disability ≠ inability to learn.**
- Benchmark ordinarily means **at least 40% of a specified disability**, appropriately certified; the Act also provides for specified disabilities not expressed in measurable percentages. Benchmark controls particular entitlements, not all protection.
- Section **2(y)** accommodation = necessary appropriate adjustment without disproportionate/undue burden; its denial can be discrimination. System-wide accessibility and person-specific accommodation are complementary.

### Education and working life

- Sections **16–17**: inclusive educational environments, accessible facilities, support and teacher preparation. Section **31**: free education for benchmark children **6–18**, with neighbourhood/special-school choice. Section **32**: minimum **5% seats** in government and government-aided higher education for benchmark candidates.
- Sections **20–21**: government-employment non-discrimination and accommodation; all establishments' equal-opportunity policies. Section **23** government-establishment grievance route; section **34** minimum **4% government vacancies** for benchmark candidates, subject to statutory implementation rules.
- UNCRPD Article **12** and RPwD section **13** protect equal legal capacity, including property and financial affairs; support must respect autonomy, dignity and privacy. Section **14** limited guardianship is joint, decision-specific, time-limited and governed by the person's will, with an appeal route.
- UNCRPD Article **19** and RPwD section **5** protect community living, choice of arrangement and access to personal/community support. Sections **24–25** address differentiated social-security schemes and accessible healthcare, including rural proximity, priority, emergency and sexual/reproductive care; no universal automatic pension follows merely from listing a statutory duty.
- Intersectionality changes the route to a right: disability may combine with gender, age, poverty, caste, tribal or rural location and impairment type. Map each extra barrier to a responsible institution, accessible service, income or safety support and remedy.
- Quota is **statutory/horizontal**; do not identify it with Article 16(4) vertical caste reservation or claim the same quota in all private firms.
- *Vikash Kumar v. UPSC* (2021): exam accommodation must be considered on its proper rights basis, not mechanically equated with benchmark quota; check the governing rules. *Jeeja Ghosh* (2016): dignified treatment in travel.

### Usable access and delivery chain

- Sugamya Bharat pillars: **built environment, transport, ICT**. SIPDA supports current accessibility implementation; neither a target nor allocation proves that an entire trip or transaction works.
- Universal design plans for diverse users from inception; retrofit repairs an old site; individual accommodation addresses a remaining specific barrier.
- Assessment/certification → portable UDID → separately eligible benefit or device → fitted support + usable institution → complaint/remedy → independently checked participation.
- The latest published ADIP approval covered the revised scheme only through **31 March 2026**; no authoritative later continuation order was located as of **3 October 2026**, so neither continuation nor termination after that date is asserted. The **National Trust Act 1999** names autism, cerebral palsy, its historic term “mental retardation”, and multiple disabilities; this is narrower than the RPwD Schedule. Rehabilitation Council of India regulates professional training.
- Chief and State Commissioners inquire into deprivation of safeguards and recommend correction. **National Divyangjan Finance and Development Corporation (NDFDC)**, a central public-sector not-for-profit company under **DEPwD, Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment**, finances livelihoods and entrepreneurship, **not** rights oversight.
- Central and State Advisory Boards advise, coordinate and monitor policy; National and State Funds finance statutory purposes under prescribed management and audit. Special Courts try offences; section **89** fines first and subsequent contraventions, section **91** covers fraudulent benefit claims, and section **92** punishes listed atrocities.

### Current linkage, PYQs and evaluative conclusion

- DEPwD's **1 July 2026 memorandum (uploaded 2 July)** partially modified the abeyance arrangements for revised competitive-examination guidelines: check a current examination's operative rules rather than treating a revised guideline as automatically in force or claiming the Act itself is suspended.
- **2022 GS-II Q7, Comment (10/150):** the printed question is in Lesson 7; sensitisation of government functionaries and citizens → legal rights, enforceable correction, balanced verdict.
- **2026 Prelims GS-I Set A Q56 (2 marks):** the exact stem, statements, choices and paper metadata appear in Lesson 1 before decisive clues; Lessons 1, 5 and 6 then teach the distinctions, without supplying an answer choice.
- Answer spine: **right → eligibility → barrier → responsible institution → support/accommodation → grievance → actual outcome**. Census **2011** prevalence estimates are dated; never silently promote them to 2026 evidence.
- Advanced caution: a legitimate threshold, legacy-building constraint or specialist remit cannot be turned into a universal exception to dignity and accessible public life.
