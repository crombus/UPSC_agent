## Lesson 1 — Motion, forces and the three laws

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

```text
position changes with time → velocity changes? → acceleration
                                      ↑
                       unbalanced external force
                       F = Δp/Δt; for fixed mass F = ma
```
*The diagram distinguishes moving from changing motion; force is needed for the latter, not for perpetual uniform motion.*

Imagine a bus braking: the passenger tends to continue forward. **Inertia** means resistance to a change of velocity; greater mass generally means more inertia. Speed has magnitude only; velocity includes direction. Acceleration measures the rate of change of velocity, so a turning vehicle can accelerate at constant speed. Distance is path length; displacement joins starting and final positions with direction. Use SI base units **metre (m)** for length, **second (s)** for time and **kilogram (kg)** for mass. Thus position/displacement is in m, speed or velocity `u, v` in m/s, acceleration `a` in m/s² and time `t` in s. For constant acceleration, `v = u + at` and `s = ut + ½at²`; neither applies without its constant-acceleration assumption.

### Units, measurement and honest reporting

| Term | Operational meaning | Exam-safe treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Accuracy | Closeness of a measured result to the accepted/reference value | A result may be precise yet inaccurate because of systematic bias |
| Precision | Closeness of repeated readings to one another | High repeatability does not by itself prove accuracy |
| Least count / resolution | Smallest scale division or smallest displayed increment the instrument can resolve | Record no unsupported extra digit; least count is not automatically the full uncertainty |
| Absolute, relative and percentage error | `|measured − reference|`; absolute error divided by reference; then `×100` for percentage | State the reference and units; percentage error is dimensionless |
| Significant figures | Digits justified by the measurement, including the last estimated digit | Leading zeros are not significant; captive zeros are; trailing decimal zeros can be significant |

Repeated readings help reveal **random error** through their spread; averaging can reduce random scatter but cannot remove a shared **systematic error** such as a zero offset or faulty calibration. Correct an identified zero error with its sign, avoid parallax by reading a scale at eye level, and report the result with an uncertainty rather than claiming perfect accuracy. For multiplication/division, round the final result to the least justified number of significant figures; for addition/subtraction, round to the least justified decimal place. In a worst-case introductory treatment, absolute uncertainties add for sums/differences and fractional uncertainties approximately add for products/quotients. Keep guard digits during calculation and round only the final result; otherwise false precision can conceal measurement limits.

1. ✅ **First law:** in an inertial frame, a body remains at rest or in straight-line uniform motion unless a net external force acts. Friction and drag, not an intrinsic need for a forward force, explain why a rolling ball stops.
2. ✅ **Second law:** net force equals rate of change of momentum, `F = dp/dt`; at constant mass, `F = ma`. Force uses the derived SI unit **newton (N) = kg·m/s²**. A loaded trolley accelerates less under the same push. Separate *net force* from one constituent force and from weight `mg`, also measured in N.
3. ✅ **Third law:** interacting bodies exert equal and opposite forces **on different bodies** simultaneously. When a person walks, the foot pushes the ground backward; ground friction pushes the person forward. Those forces cannot cancel on the person because they act on different bodies.

**Where the intuition fails:** "The bus is moving, so a force must keep it moving" ignores that balanced forces give zero acceleration, not necessarily zero velocity. Friction in a real bus demands engine force to maintain speed, but the ideal no-friction law remains true. ⚠️ In a transport answer, distinguish safety devices that reduce collision force from devices that merely announce a speed.

**UPSC use:** a conceptual stem can change "straight line" to "circular path", or replace net force with an individual force. Use the law's conditions before substituting numbers. **Rapid bridge:** displacement ≠ distance; acceleration ≠ speed; zero net force ≠ rest; action–reaction ≠ cancellation on one object. Next we need momentum to explain *how* a brief force harms or protects passengers.

### Revision notes

1. Distance is path length; displacement is the directed change from initial to final position.
2. Speed is scalar; velocity includes direction, so uniform circular motion has acceleration.
3. Inertia resists a change of velocity and increases with mass.
4. Newton's first law applies in an inertial frame and concerns net external force.
5. Newton's second law is `F = dp/dt`; `F = ma` requires constant mass.
6. Action and reaction are simultaneous, equal and opposite, but act on different bodies.
7. Accuracy concerns closeness to a reference; precision concerns repeatability.
8. Least count limits readable resolution; significant figures and uncertainty prevent false precision.
9. Random error produces scatter; calibration/zero error can produce systematic bias.
10. Constant-acceleration equations must not be used when acceleration varies.

### Concept check

**Question:** A bus moves at constant speed around a bend. Is its acceleration zero, and what must be true of its net force?

**Model answer:** No. Its velocity changes direction, so acceleration and a net inward force exist despite constant speed.

**Misconception to avoid:** Constant speed implies zero acceleration only when direction also remains constant.

**Original Mains practice — 10 marks, answer in 150 words:** Explain how Newton's laws clarify passenger safety during sudden braking.

**Model (103 words):** ✅ A passenger resists a sudden velocity change by inertia, so continues forward relative to a braking bus. The bus decelerates when road friction acts on its tyres; the passenger needs a separate force to decelerate. A seat belt supplies that force over a safer time interval, while an airbag distributes it and lengthens stopping time. The bus and road form a third-law action–reaction pair on different bodies; that pair does not cancel the force on a passenger. ⚠️ The laws explain the mechanism, but protection also depends on belt fit, seating and crash severity; no single equation certifies a vehicle as safe.

**Score (10):** inertia and relative motion 2; net force and second law 3; belt/airbag mechanism 3; different-body qualification and limit 2.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

```text
before impact                  during impact                     after impact
mass × velocity = p   →   force × contact time = Δp   →   changed p
                                   ↑
                    airbag: longer time, smaller average force
```
*Impulse connects a collision's momentum change to the time over which forces act.*

Two similarly fast objects can be differently difficult to stop because momentum `p = mv` includes mass and direction; its SI unit is **kg·m/s**. ✅ Impulse `J = ∫F dt = Δp`; for approximately constant force, `J = FΔt`, with `Δt` in s and impulse in **N·s = kg·m/s**. A helmet does not erase momentum change; padding prolongs deceleration and spreads contact stress. This conclusion presumes similar initial/final momentum and proper use.

For an **isolated system**, total vector momentum before interaction equals total vector momentum afterward: internal forces cancel in pairs across the system. A rocket expels gas backward and gains forward momentum; a gun recoils; two carts collide. The surrounding Earth can absorb momentum, so "each individual object conserves its own momentum" is false. Collisions may dissipate kinetic energy as heat, deformation and sound even when system momentum remains conserved. A large crumple zone aims to absorb energy and lengthen stopping time, not to cancel third-law forces.

An **accelerometer** detects acceleration (often including acceleration due to gravity in a sensor's measured frame); a phone can infer tilt from its orientation relative to gravity, detect free fall or flag a crash through a pattern of measured changes. It does not by itself establish location, ultimate cause or injury. **PYQ linkage:** 2023 Prelims GS-I Q54 is reproduced exactly and answer-neutrally in the final verified-PYQ section.

**UPSC use:** distinguish momentum from kinetic energy (`½mv²`), impulse from a bare force, and accelerometer from a gyroscope (rotation) or GPS (position). **Rapid bridge:** `p = mv`, `J = Δp`, isolated-system vector conservation, longer stop → smaller average force *for the same change*. Next: acceleration can exist without speeding up, as an orbit shows.

### Revision notes

1. Momentum `p=mv` is a vector and has unit kg·m/s.
2. Impulse equals momentum change: `J=Δp`; for nearly constant force, `J=FΔt`.
3. For the same `Δp`, increasing stopping time lowers average force.
4. Total momentum is conserved only for the chosen approximately isolated system.
5. An individual body's momentum can change while system momentum remains conserved.
6. Kinetic energy may become heat, sound and deformation in an inelastic collision.
7. Rocket recoil and gun recoil follow momentum exchange, not creation of forward momentum.
8. An accelerometer senses acceleration-related signals, not position or accident cause by itself.
9. A gyroscope primarily senses rotation; GPS estimates position using external signals.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why can two colliding vehicles conserve total momentum while losing kinetic energy?

**Model answer:** Their internal forces transfer equal-and-opposite momenta within an approximately isolated system; deformation, heat and sound convert some kinetic energy into other forms.

**Misconception to avoid:** Conservation of momentum does not imply conservation of kinetic energy in an inelastic crash.

**Original Mains practice — 10 marks, answer in 150 words:** Discuss why accelerometers aid safety systems but cannot alone reconstruct an accident.

**Model (98 words):** ✅ Acceleration is the change of velocity per unit time; an accelerometer responds to inertial changes and orientation-dependent gravitational readings. A device can thus identify a sharp deceleration or plausible free-fall interval and trigger an alert or restraint system. Crash-survivable recording additionally preserves flight parameters and voice; radar or acoustic beacons help locate objects by different principles. ⚠️ Sensor calibration, placement and context matter: abrupt movement need not be an accident, and one time series cannot establish every causal step. Combine sensor data with location, recordings and physical investigation rather than calling an accelerometer a complete accident recorder.

**Score (10):** measured quantity 3; safety mechanism 3; distinction among instruments 2; evidentiary limitation 2.

Book context: No local searchable Physics textbook; gravity and orbit context checked.
CA search: Not applicable to orbit mechanics; no dated event claimed.
CA found: None included.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

```text
             velocity → tangent
                    ● satellite
                    ↓ gravity = inward force
                 (Earth)
orbit: falling toward Earth while moving sideways fast enough to keep missing it
```
*The orbit diagram shows why an inward gravitational pull and tangential motion coexist.*

If you swing a stone on a string, it changes direction each moment; that requires inward **centripetal acceleration** `v²/r` in m/s² and inward net force `mv²/r` in N for a uniform circle (`v` in m/s; orbital radius `r` in m). "Centripetal" names a force's **role**, not a new source of force: tension, friction or gravity can fill it. The outward "centrifugal" effect is a frame-dependent pseudo-force in a rotating reference frame, not a second outward interaction on an orbiting satellite in an inertial frame.

✅ Universal gravitation gives `F = GMm/r²` between point masses (or suitable spherically symmetric bodies): `M, m` are masses in kg, `r` is centre-to-centre distance in m, and the gravitational constant `G` has SI unit **N·m²/kg²**. Near Earth's surface, weight `W = mg` is in N (`g` in m/s²); here `W` denotes *weight*, not work or the unit watt. Mass is invariant quantity of matter while weight varies with local gravitational field. An astronaut and craft orbit together in continuous free fall, so the astronaut feels weightless locally although Earth's gravity remains strong. The **escape speed** from a non-rotating spherical body's surface of radius `R` in m, neglecting air drag, is `√(2GM/R)` in m/s (Earth about **11.2 km/s**, equivalently 11,200 m/s), independent of the launched body's mass: set initial kinetic energy `½mv²` equal to the magnitude of `−GMm/R`; cancel `m`. Real rockets need sustained thrust and suffer drag/gravity losses, so launch speed is not simply escape speed.

| Orbit | Geometry/period | Why use it | Important limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Geostationary | Equatorial, circular, prograde; about 35,786 km altitude; one **sidereal** day period | Appears fixed over Earth; communications | Poor high-latitude geometry; signal delay |
| Near-polar / sun-synchronous | Usually low-Earth, near-polar; sun-synchronous orbit maintains near-constant local solar time | Repeated Earth observation under comparable lighting | Not fixed over one ground point |

**UPSC use:** gravity both supplies centripetal force and underlies orbital dynamics; distinguish apparent stillness from actual rest. **Rapid bridge:** free fall ≠ zero gravity; escape depends on central body's `M,R`; geostationary ≠ any polar orbit. Next: the same forces acting on a contact area create pressure.

### Revision notes

1. Centripetal acceleration points inward and equals `v²/r` for uniform circular motion.
2. Centripetal force is a role supplied by gravity, tension, friction or another real force.
3. Centrifugal force is a rotating-frame pseudo-force, not an outward interaction in an inertial frame.
4. Gravitational force follows `GMm/r²`; weight near Earth is `mg`.
5. Mass is not weight: mass remains, while weight changes with the gravitational field.
6. Orbital weightlessness is common free fall, not absence of gravity.
7. Escape speed depends on the central body's mass and radius, not projectile mass under ideal assumptions.
8. A geostationary orbit is circular, equatorial, prograde and has a sidereal-day period.
9. A near-polar or sun-synchronous satellite is not fixed over one ground point.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why does an astronaut float inside a station despite gravitational attraction from Earth?

**Model answer:** Station and astronaut accelerate together in orbit under gravity; with no substantial support force between them, the astronaut feels weightless.

**Misconception to avoid:** Floating does not mean Earth's gravitational force has vanished.

**Original Mains practice — 10 marks, answer in 150 words:** Explain the difference between an apparently stationary geostationary satellite and a polar observation satellite.

**Model (101 words):** ✅ A geostationary craft must orbit eastward in a circular equatorial path with a period of one sidereal day; therefore its direction from a fixed point on Earth remains nearly unchanged. A near-polar observation satellite passes above successive latitudes as Earth rotates below it; a sun-synchronous design revisits at roughly the same local solar time. India's communication use benefits from the former's persistent coverage, while imaging benefits from the latter's varying ground tracks and comparable lighting. ⚠️ Neither orbit guarantees universal coverage: terrain, latitude, sensor geometry and orbital altitude constrain service. Weightlessness in both cases is free fall, not gravity's absence.

**Score (10):** geostationary conditions 3; polar/sun-synchronous distinction 3; uses 2; coverage/weightlessness qualification 2.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

| Physical question | Rule with operating condition | Everyday use |
|---|---|---|
| Force concentrated where? | Pressure `P = F/A`; static-fluid pressure rises with depth (`ρgh`) | Wide foundations, dams |
| How is imposed pressure transmitted? | Pascal: confined fluid transmits an applied pressure change | Hydraulic jack and brakes |
| Why can an object float? | Archimedes: upthrust equals weight of fluid displaced | Boats, hydrometers |
| How does steady flow exchange pressure and speed? | Bernoulli along a streamline for ideal flow | Atomiser, airflow analysis |

*Each row solves a different fluid problem; no one law substitutes for the others.*

```text
input force / small piston area → pressure increment → output force / large piston area
floating hull → displacement of water → upward pressure difference → upthrust
```
*The two paths distinguish pressure transmission from a vertical pressure imbalance.*

Press a nail against wood: a small tip area raises pressure although force is unchanged. In `P = F/A`, area `A` is in m² and pressure uses the SI unit **pascal (Pa) = N/m²**; `P` here means pressure, not power or lens power. Hydrostatic pressure change `ρgh` is in Pa when density `ρ` is in kg/m³, `g` in m/s² and depth `h` in m. In a hydraulic press a confined liquid transmits the pressure increment; a larger output piston experiences greater force because `F = PA`, but it moves a proportionately shorter distance. **Reply to the "free energy" objection:** greater force does not mean greater output work; friction makes actual output lower.

Immersion produces a pressure difference between an object's bottom and top. This creates **buoyancy**; the displaced liquid's weight equals upthrust. A vessel floats when equilibrium is reached with enough fluid displaced for upthrust to match its weight; steel ships are possible because the whole hull encloses air, reducing average density. A hydrometer exploits different immersion depths in liquids of different density. Surface tension is the energy cost of increasing a liquid surface, encouraging droplets to minimise area; **capillarity** involves adhesion, cohesion and narrow pores/tubes, contributing to water movement in soil and plants, but tall-tree water transport also depends on transpiration pull.

In an approximately steady, incompressible, non-viscous flow at similar height, Bernoulli relates higher speed to lower *static* pressure along a streamline. A real wing also turns air downwards and its lift depends on flow geometry and angle of attack: equal transit-time stories are not a valid general proof. **UPSC use:** avoid applying ideal-flow equations to arbitrary turbulent situations. **Rapid bridge:** same added pressure ≠ same piston force; displaced-fluid weight, not submerged object's weight, determines buoyancy; capillarity alone ≠ full tree water transport. Hot and cold fluids now lead us to energy transfer.

### Revision notes

1. Pressure is force per area, `P=F/A`, measured in pascals.
2. Hydrostatic pressure difference grows as `ρgh` under the stated approximation.
3. Pascal's principle transmits an imposed pressure change through a confined fluid.
4. A larger hydraulic output force is balanced by shorter displacement and real losses.
5. Archimedean upthrust equals the weight of displaced fluid.
6. A steel ship floats because its overall hull volume lowers average density and displaces enough water.
7. Bernoulli's relation needs steady, incompressible, low-viscosity flow along a streamline.
8. Surface tension minimises interfacial area; capillarity depends on adhesion, cohesion and geometry.
9. Capillarity alone does not explain water rise in tall trees; transpiration also matters.

### Concept check

**Question:** Can a hydraulic press multiply force without multiplying energy? Explain.

**Model answer:** Yes. Nearly equal pressure increments act on pistons of different area, giving greater force on the larger piston; it moves a shorter distance, and friction dissipates some input work.

**Misconception to avoid:** Force multiplication is not creation of work or energy.

**Original Mains practice — 10 marks, answer in 150 words:** Examine why different principles are needed to explain a ship floating, a hydraulic brake and an atomiser.

**Model (94 words):** ✅ A ship floats when water displaced by its hull supplies an upthrust balancing total weight; hull volume, not steel's intrinsic low density, is decisive. A hydraulic brake transmits an applied pressure increment through confined fluid; actuator area translates it into force at the brakes. An atomiser uses fast-moving air to produce a pressure difference that helps draw and disperse liquid, under appropriate flow conditions. ⚠️ Archimedes describes static buoyancy, Pascal describes pressure transmission, and Bernoulli describes a qualified moving-flow energy relation. Confusing them obscures failure risks such as leaks, overloading or flow turbulence.

**Score (10):** buoyancy 3; hydraulic transmission 2; atomiser and assumptions 3; distinction and limit 2.

Book context: No local searchable Physics textbook; work and energy context checked.

CA found: None included.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

```text
fuel/electrical input  →  useful shaft work / lifted load
        │                            │
        └────────→ heat + sound + frictional losses
time to transfer energy → power; useful output / input → efficiency
```
*Conservation counts all energy, whereas efficiency counts only the desired output.*

Push a stationary wall: muscular effort may tire you, but **mechanical work on the wall** is zero because its displacement is zero. Here `W` denotes *work* (unlike weight in Lesson 3); in `W = F s cos θ`, `F` is in N, displacement `s` in m and `cos θ` is dimensionless. The **net work-energy theorem** says `W_net = Δ(½mv²)`. Work and energy use the SI unit **joule (J) = N·m = kg·m²/s²**. For a raised mass, gravitational potential-energy gain near Earth is `mgh` in J. In a controlled fall, potential energy becomes kinetic energy; with air resistance, some becomes thermal energy and sound. Energy is not destroyed because a brake becomes warm.

**Power** measures rate of energy transfer, `P = W/t`, in **watts (W) = J/s**; here italic `W` in the formula means work, whereas upright W after a numerical power is its SI unit. A faster hoist can do the same work in less time with greater power. For a device, `efficiency = useful energy output / total energy input` over matching intervals is a dimensionless ratio and must not exceed unity. Example: compare two pumps lifting the same water mass to the same height; the one consuming less electrical energy is more efficient, but the pump finishing sooner delivers higher useful power. Efficiency and power answer different questions.

⚠️ Transport or manufacturing productivity cannot be inferred from a motor's nominal wattage alone; duty cycle, friction, maintenance and electrical supply matter. **UPSC use:** if force is perpendicular to displacement it can change direction without doing work on an ideal point mass; don't conflate a centripetal force with power delivered to a moving load. **Rapid bridge:** work is a transfer, energy is a state capacity, power is a rate, losses are converted energy. Energy degraded to heat motivates the next lesson.

### Revision notes

1. Mechanical work requires force with a displacement component: `W=Fs cosθ`.
2. Net work changes kinetic energy: `W_net=ΔK`.
3. Near Earth's surface, gravitational potential-energy change is `mgh`.
4. Energy conservation includes useful output, heat, sound and deformation.
5. Power is the rate of energy transfer, `P=W/t`, not total energy.
6. Efficiency compares useful output with total input over matching boundaries.
7. High power does not necessarily mean high efficiency.
8. A perpendicular centripetal force can change direction without changing speed or doing work on an ideal point mass.
9. Machine performance also depends on friction, duty cycle, maintenance and supply quality.

### Concept check

**Question:** Two motors lift the same load through the same height, one twice as fast. Must the faster motor be more efficient?

**Model answer:** No. Both deliver the same ideal useful work `mgh`; the faster motor has higher output power. Efficiency depends on each motor's total input energy, which is not given.

**Misconception to avoid:** Higher power is not synonymous with higher efficiency.

**Original Mains practice — 10 marks, answer in 150 words:** Explain why energy conservation alone does not imply an efficient public transport system.

**Model (93 words):** ✅ The work-energy relation tracks transfers from fuel or electricity to vehicle motion, friction, heat and sound. Conservation includes both useful motion and dissipated energy; it cannot guarantee a high useful-output fraction. India's electric transit systems can improve service only if motor and transmission efficiency, occupancy, braking recovery and supply reliability are considered together. ⚠️ A high motor rating indicates capacity to transfer energy per unit time, not the fraction of input reaching passengers as useful transport. Examine the whole system and operating conditions rather than claiming the energy equation itself measures efficiency.

**Score (10):** conservation and pathways 3; efficiency versus power 3; transport example 2; system qualification 2.

## Lesson 6 — Heat, phases and the direction of change

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: No local searchable Physics textbook; heat and thermodynamics context checked.
CA search: Not applicable to these thermal laws; no dated event claimed.
CA found: None essential; pressure-cooker PYQ is the application anchor.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

```text
HOT reservoir → [engine] → useful work
                  │
                  └→ waste heat → COLD reservoir
conduction: contact | convection: moving fluid | radiation: electromagnetic
```
*The engine must reject some heat; the arrows also separate three transfer pathways.*

**Temperature** measures thermal state and, in an idealised particle picture, relates to average particle kinetic energy; its SI base unit is **kelvin (K)**. **Heat** `Q` is energy transferred because temperatures differ, measured in J; an object does not contain "heat" as a conserved substance. ✅ The **first law** expresses energy accounting, conventionally `ΔU = Q − W` when `W` is work done by the system: internal-energy change `ΔU`, `Q` and work `W` are each in J. The **second law** says spontaneous net heat flow is from hotter to colder bodies and no cyclic heat engine can convert all supplied heat into work; a refrigerator transfers heat cold → hot only with external work. Energy may be conserved while its ability to do useful work diminishes.

For heating without a phase transition, `Q = mcΔT` defines **specific heat capacity** `c` in **J/(kg·K)**, with mass `m` in kg and temperature difference `ΔT` in K (a 1 K interval also equals a 1 °C interval); water's high value moderates coastal temperatures and makes it useful as coolant. During melting/boiling at a fixed pressure, `Q = mL` defines **specific latent heat** `L` in **J/kg**; energy changes molecular arrangement while temperature stays approximately constant. Evaporating sweat removes heat; steam condensing on skin releases latent heat as well as cooling energy. These are mechanisms, not universal medical severity predictions.

| Transfer | Mechanism | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | Neighbour-to-neighbour energy transfer; important in solids | A medium is required |

*A boundary can reflect, transmit or disperse light; the material and incidence angle decide.*

See your face in a mirror: **reflection** redirects light back into the original medium; a plane mirror forms a virtual image. A convex mirror offers a wider field of view than a plane mirror; a concave mirror can focus rays. A lens primarily **refracts** rays passing through it. For oblique entry into a medium where light slows, rays bend toward the normal; the wave's **frequency stays constant** at a stationary boundary while wavelength changes because `v=fλ`. Snell's law `n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r` describes ideal interfaces; refractive index `n` is a dimensionless ratio of speeds. **Lens power** `P_lens=1/f_lens` with focal length `f_lens` in metres is measured in **dioptres (D) = m⁻¹**: positive converging convex, negative diverging concave. | Eye condition | Imaging error | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Myopia | Distant image tends to form before retina | Concave/diverging lens |
| Hypermetropia | Near image tends to form behind retina | Convex/converging lens |
| Presbyopia | Age-related loss of accommodation | Suitable reading/bifocal or progressive correction |
| Astigmatism | Unequal curvature across meridians | Cylindrical correction |

**Total internal reflection (TIR)** requires propagation from higher to lower refractive index and incidence beyond the critical angle. A fibre's core/cladding arrangement guides light; bending losses, material absorption and coupling prevent "perfect" loss-free transmission. A mirage is explained by gradients in refractive index and can involve TIR-like ray bending, not a literal pool of water. Prism dispersion spreads wavelengths because refractive index varies with wavelength; a rainbow also involves refraction and internal reflection within drops. **Scattering** instead redirects light on particles: molecular Rayleigh scattering is approximately proportional to `1/λ⁴` (blue sky, reddened long atmospheric path); larger-droplet/cloud **Mie** behaviour differs and often looks whitish; a visible **Tyndall** beam evidences scattering by colloidal particles. These mechanisms are not interchangeable.

**Why a laser is not just a bright lamp:** A photon (one packet of light) arriving at a suitably excited atom or gain material can stimulate emission of a second matched photon. Pumping replenishes excited states, an inversion makes gain exceed absorption, and optical feedback can build a directional, highly coherent and relatively narrow-band beam. **LASER** expands to *light amplification by stimulated emission of radiation*. By contrast, a normal lamp's light and an ordinary LED's junction emission are largely spontaneous and much less coherent. India-relevant examples include fibre-optic transmitters, surveying, precision cutting and selected surgery: each needs suitable wavelength, intensity, alignment and eye/skin safety measures. Coherence, not brightness alone, is diagnostic; not every laser has identical wavelength or penetration.

**UPSC use:** myopia/concave; mirror/reflection versus lens/refraction; TIR/conditions; dispersion versus scattering; stimulated versus spontaneous emission. **Rapid bridge:** a convex lens need not always produce a real image—object placement matters; fibre is guided light, not electricity moving through glass; laser directionality and coherence require gain and control. Which wavelengths actually carry communication comes next.

## Lesson 11 — Magnetism, induction and the grid

Progress: 11 / 16 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Motors, generators, flux, transformers and superconductivity

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: No local searchable Physics textbook; induction and grid context checked.

CA found: None necessary.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

```text
motor: electrical input → magnetic force/torque → rotation
generator: rotation → changing magnetic flux → induced emf
transformer: AC in coil → varying flux in core → AC emf in second coil
transmit: step voltage up → current down → I²R loss down → step voltage down
```
*Force on current and induction by changing flux are related but not identical directions of conversion.*

Electric current makes a magnetic field; a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field can feel a force. **Motor:** interaction generates torque and mechanical motion. **Generator:** relative motion between field and conductor changes **magnetic flux** (`Φ = BA cosθ` for a uniform field across a flat loop). Here magnetic flux density `B` uses **tesla (T) = Wb/m²**, loop area `A` m² and flux `Φ` **weber (Wb) = V·s**; the angle factor is dimensionless. For one loop, Faraday's law expresses induced emf `ε = −dΦ/dt`, in **volts (V)** for `t` in s, with the minus sign indicating opposition to the change (Lenz's law); for `N` turns, `ε = −N dΦ/dt`, with turn count dimensionless. A closed path is needed for a sustained induced current; a changing flux can create an emf even when the circuit is open.

Transformers couple alternating flux from one winding to another. In an ideal transformer, `V_s/V_p = N_s/N_p`; approximately conserved input/output power means raising voltage lowers current. A **steady DC** primary produces no continuously changing flux after the switching transient, so an ordinary passive transformer does **not** step steady DC voltage up or down. Modern power electronics can switch DC into a varying waveform and transform it, which is a distinct system, not a counterexample. A bicycle dynamo and large generator share the induction principle but differ vastly in architecture.

**Superconductors** have vanishing DC electrical resistance below their material-specific critical temperature and exhibit magnetic-field expulsion (Meissner effect); this is not merely an "extra good" room-temperature wire. Superconducting magnets can be used in MRI and research systems; cooling cost and critical current/field constrain scaling. ⚠️ A reliable grid additionally needs frequency management, insulation, metering and institutions: a physics law does not guarantee service delivery.

**UPSC use:** no changing flux, no continuing induction; distinguish motor from generator and step-up from energy multiplication. **Rapid bridge:** flux change, emf versus current, turns ratio/idealisation, `I²R`, critical temperature/Meissner. Next, semiconductors control current without the mechanical rotation of a generator.

### Revision notes

1. Current creates magnetic fields, and magnetic fields can exert forces on currents.
2. A motor converts electrical input into torque and motion.
3. A generator converts changing magnetic flux into induced emf.
4. Magnetic flux is `BA cosθ` for the stated uniform-field geometry.
5. Faraday's law depends on flux change; motion is only one way to create it.
6. Lenz's minus sign expresses opposition to the change producing the emf.
7. An open circuit can have induced emf, but sustained current needs a closed path.
8. A transformer needs varying flux and ideally follows the turns ratio.
9. Step-up transformation does not multiply energy; real windings and cores have losses.
10. Superconductivity requires conditions below critical temperature and within critical current/field limits.

*The comparison separates nuclear particles, high-energy photons and a light–matter interaction.*

```text
decaying nucleus ──→ alpha / beta / gamma
incoming photon  ──→ electron emission ONLY if photon energy ≥ work function
```
*Decay emerges from the nucleus; photoelectric emission instead responds to incident photons at a surface.*

Shine light on a suitable metal: one photon supplies discrete energy `hf`. Emission occurs when `hf ≥ φ`, the work function; maximum electron kinetic energy `K_max = hf − φ`. Raising light intensity **below** threshold does not cause emission; above threshold it can increase the number of emitted electrons without changing the maximum energy at fixed frequency. This is the **photoelectric effect**, distinct from a solar cell's junction-based photovoltaic conversion even though photons participate in both.

Unstable nuclei decay probabilistically. A sample's **half-life** is the interval for expected undecayed nuclei to halve; `N(t)=N₀(½)^(t/T½)` for simple radioactive decay. Ordinary temperature and pressure do not change typical nuclear half-lives; a half-life does not mean all nuclei disappear after two intervals. Carbon-14 dating relies on decay and suitable sample assumptions. **Fission** splits a heavy nucleus and can release neutrons sustaining a controlled chain reaction in a reactor; **fusion** combines light nuclei under extreme conditions, powering stars. Both involve changes in nuclear binding energy; mass–energy equivalence `E=mc²` tracks energy and mass, not limitless free fuel. Shielding and dose control depend on radiation type and exposure route. Ionising radiation can aid imaging, radiotherapy or sterilisation when regulated; medical benefit does not nullify risk.

✅ The 2023 Nobel-recognised attosecond methods are retained as historical evidence that ultrashort light pulses can study electron dynamics. ⚠️ This scientific recognition does not claim a commercial device is deployed everywhere. **UPSC use:** distinguish alpha/beta/gamma; frequency threshold versus brightness; nuclear reactions versus electron-level effects. **Rapid bridge:** photon energy, work function, half-life, fission/fusion and penetration-versus-ionisation; now ask how other invisible phenomena are inferred from evidence.

# CUMULATIVE CONCEPT CHECKS

**Mechanics → thermal:** If a braking bus stops, where did its kinetic energy go, and what conserved quantity should be tracked in a near-isolated collision? **Model:** friction/deformation convert kinetic energy to heat and sound; total momentum is approximately conserved for a sufficiently isolated colliding system. **Remediation:** kinetic energy alone need not be conserved in an inelastic impact.

**Waves → electricity:** Can a radio wave carry information through vacuum, and can a passive transformer operate on steady DC? **Model:** yes, EM waves propagate through vacuum; no, steady DC does not sustain varying core flux. **Remediation:** information propagation and induction concern different operating conditions.

**Modern physics → policy:** Does a blue LED device establish chip supply-chain resilience? **Model:** no; controlled junction emission demonstrates a device, whereas design, manufacturing, testing, heat removal and standards determine scale. **Remediation:** do not turn a physical proof into a claim of system capacity.

**Radiation → astronomy:** Why can a photon-based observation infer a distant object's distance but not directly certify every property? **Model:** travel, spectrum or periodicity yield model-dependent measurements; distance inference needs calibration and stated assumptions. **Remediation:** observable signal ≠ exhaustive knowledge of its source.

**Original 10 marks, answer in 150 words — Examine why a pressure cooker and a hydraulic brake cannot be explained by the same fluid law alone.**

**Model (101 words):** ✅ A pressure cooker traps steam, raises internal pressure and thus raises water's boiling temperature, making higher-temperature cooking possible. The boiling-point change depends on phase equilibrium and pressure; Pascal's law alone does not predict it. A hydraulic brake instead transmits an applied pressure change through a confined fluid so pistons convert input force into force at braking components; its effectiveness also depends on fluid integrity and friction at the wheel. ⚠️ Both use pressure, but one exploits pressure-dependent boiling and the other pressure transmission. Describe the causal step required by each appliance rather than merely listing the name of a law.

**Score (10):** cooker causal chain 3; brake chain 3; distinction 2; limits 2.

**Original 15 marks, answer in 250 words — Analyse how the choice of wave changes the design and risks of medical diagnosis, fibre communication and radar monitoring.**

**Model (118 words):** ✅ Ultrasound needs a material medium and uses reflected acoustic pulses to build an image of tissue; resolution and attenuation depend on frequency and tissue. Fibre communication modulates light guided by total internal reflection in suitable core/cladding, with loss from bends and connections. Radar transmits and receives radio waves to infer target range from timing and sometimes speed from Doppler shift; line of sight and clutter constrain results. ⚠️ Calling all three "wireless" erases different physical carriers and failure modes. **Score (15):** three wave mechanisms 6; named design limits 4; India-specific system application 2; distinction and verdict 3.

**Original 20 marks, answer in 250 words — Evaluate the proposition that greater efficiency in electricity, lighting and computing follows automatically from better physics discoveries.**

**Model (156 words):** ✅ Faraday's induction makes electricity generation and AC transformation possible, while higher transmission voltage can reduce `I²R` losses for a given power. Blue semiconductor LEDs can provide efficient solid-state lighting; MOSFET-based CMOS logic reduces static circuit power relative to many alternatives. Thermodynamics nevertheless prevents perfect heat-to-work conversion and obliges cooling and heat management. ⚠️ These principles create opportunities, not guaranteed results. A transformer has resistive and magnetic-core losses; an LED's driver, optical design and junction temperature affect energy use and lifetime; a chip's switching activity and cooling can dominate its static power. Grid balancing and equitable connections determine who benefits. India's innovation strategy should combine device research with calibrated testing, standards, material/process quality and operating data. Efficiency must be assessed at device and system boundaries, under stated loads, rather than inferred from a discovery or a product label.

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

## Motion and forces

- **Motion:** displacement is vector endpoint change; velocity has direction; acceleration measures velocity change. For constant acceleration only, `v=u+at`, `s=ut+½at²`.
- **Three laws:** inertial straight-line uniform motion if zero net force; `F=dp/dt` (`ma` for fixed mass); equal opposite interaction forces act on different bodies.
- **Measurement discipline:** accuracy = closeness to a reference; precision = repeatability; least count/resolution bounds readable detail. Distinguish random scatter from systematic/zero/parallax error, report uncertainty, apply significant-figure rules and round only the final result.
- **Mechanics SI recall:** length m, time s, mass kg (base units); velocity m/s, acceleration m/s², force N = kg·m/s², momentum kg·m/s, impulse N·s, work/energy J = N·m, power W = J/s. The letter `W` also denotes work or weight in some equations; read the context.
- **Collision:** `p=mv`, impulse `J=Δp≈FΔt`; longer stop lowers average force for same `Δp`; isolated-system total vector momentum persists though kinetic energy can become heat and deformation.
- **Accelerometer:** measures acceleration-related signals; orientation/free-fall/crash interpretations require context. **2023 Q54** route: sensed quantity → possible functions → qualifiers.
- **Circular path:** inward `mv²/r` supplied by gravity, friction or tension; outward centrifugal effect is rotating-frame pseudo-force. `F=GMm/r²`, weight `mg`; orbiting astronauts free-fall.
- **Escape:** `√(2GM/R)` (Earth ~11.2 km/s) independent of projectile mass under ideal assumptions. Equatorial circular geostationary period = sidereal day at ~35,786 km altitude; polar/sun-synchronous imaging ≠ stationary.

## Fluids, energy and heat

- **Fluid statics:** `P=F/A`, depth component `ρgh`; Pascal transmits imposed pressure, not energy creation; Archimedes gives displaced-fluid weight as upthrust.
- **Thermal and fluid SI recall:** area m², pressure Pa = N/m², density kg/m³, heat and internal energy J, temperature K (kelvin), specific heat capacity J/(kg·K), specific latent heat J/kg.
- **Flow and surfaces:** ideal Bernoulli links static pressure and speed *along a streamline*; wing lift additionally involves air deflection. Surface tension shrinks interfaces; capillarity plus transpiration contributes to water transport.
- **Work:** `W=Fs cosθ`, `W_net=Δ(½mv²)`, near-surface potential energy `mgh`; zero displacement along force gives zero mechanical work on that object.
- **Power/efficiency:** `P=W/t`; efficiency useful energy/total supplied. Heat and sound are transformed energy, not energy disappearing.
- **Heat:** energy moving due to thermal difference; temperature measures thermal state. `Q=mcΔT` when no phase change; `Q=mL` for phase transition. Sweat evaporates/steam condenses with latent heat.
- **Transfer:** conduction via material, convection by moving fluid, radiation by EM waves including across vacuum. First law `ΔU=Q−W` for system work-out convention; second law limits heat-engine conversion, refrigerator needs work.
- **Pressure cooker, 2021 Q68:** greater pressure → higher water boiling point; altitude reverses pressure tendency. Black-body peak moves shorter with increased temperature; real greenhouse absorption is wavelength-selective.

## Waves, optics and detection

- **Wave:** disturbance transports energy without bulk net transport of matter; `v=fλ`. Sound needs medium; its amplitude tracks loudness more closely, frequency pitch. Infrasound <20 Hz; ultrasound >20 kHz; supersonic describes speed of an object.
- **Wave/optical SI recall:** frequency Hz = s⁻¹, wavelength m, period s, wave speed m/s, photon energy J and Planck constant J·s; lens power D = m⁻¹ (not watts).
- **Sound devices:** sonar/sonography use acoustic reflection; echo distinct return, reverberation unresolved persistence; resonance near natural frequency, damping limits; Doppler shift needs relative motion.
- **Optical boundary:** reflection for mirror, refraction for lens (frequency unchanged at stationary interface). `n₁sin i=n₂sin r`; `P_lens=1/f(m)`. Myopia–concave; hypermetropia–convex; presbyopia–reading/bifocal; astigmatism–cylindrical.
- **Guiding/colour:** TIR from higher to lower index beyond critical angle; fibre has loss. Dispersion splits wavelength-dependent rays; rainbow combines processes; Rayleigh `~1/λ⁴` for blue sky/red sunrise; larger-particle Mie differs; Tyndall identifies colloidal scattering.
- **Optical sources:** pumped, inverted gain medium + stimulated emission and optical feedback → coherent, directional, relatively narrow-band laser; ordinary lamp/LED emission is largely spontaneous. Fibre transmitters, surveying, selected surgery and cutting require wavelength/power/safety controls. Advanced treatment adds gain-loss balance and finite linewidth.
- **Spectrum:** radio → microwave → infrared → visible red→violet → UV → X-ray → gamma, increasing frequency/energy, decreasing wavelength. EM waves cross vacuum. Infrared emission can be detected for thermal imaging of hot equipment, subject to emissivity and reflection; ultraviolet can cause photochemical skin/eye effects, while ozone absorbs much incident UV and carefully controlled UV can disinfect. X/gamma ionise; UV needs wavelength qualification.
- **Communication:** 2020 Q39 VLC requires modulated light/receiver/path; 2022 Q36 wireless classifications need protocol and configuration; 2024 Q34 radar uses reflected radio, time-of-flight and possibly Doppler; 2026 Q44 recorder retains data, acoustic beacon helps underwater location, radio radar cannot serve as generic deep-water locator. **2026 key provisional; no option selected.**

## Electrical and electronic systems

- **Circuit:** `I=ΔQ/Δt`; `V=W/Q`; for ohmic fixed-temperature element `V=IR`; `P=VI`, resistive heating `I²R`. Current is not used up; power and voltage are not current.
- **Electrical/magnetic SI recall:** charge C, current A = C/s, voltage V = J/C, resistance Ω = V/A, power W = V·A, magnetic flux Wb (weber) = V·s, flux density T = Wb/m², induced emf V; turns ratio is dimensionless.
- **Topology/safety:** series → same current; parallel → same voltage; fuse/MCB interrupt excessive current, earthing supplies fault path. AC aids passive transformation; DC voltage conversion needs active electronics if starting from steady DC.
- **Induction:** changing magnetic flux → emf `ε=−dΦ/dt`; closed path → current. Motor converts electrical → motion; generator reverses; passive transformer needs varying flux, ideal `V_s/V_p=N_s/N_p`. Higher transmission voltage at same power lowers current and `I²R` loss.
- **Superconductivity:** vanishing DC resistance and Meissner expulsion below critical temperature; cryogenics/field/current constraints persist.
- **Bands/carriers:** donor pentavalent → n electrons; acceptor trivalent → p holes. Junction forward/reverse bias supports rectification; LED recombination produces light; solar cell separates photo-carriers; photoelectric metal emission instead needs threshold `hf≥φ`.
- **Transistors:** BJT base current controls collector current; insulated MOSFET gate field controls channel. CMOS has low static but nonzero dynamic/leakage power; chips require design, fab, packaging, testing and metrology.
- **Lighting routes:** sodium discharge ≠ LED junction; 2021 Prelims Q72 compare mechanisms/qualified performance. 2021 GS-III Q16 blue emission + phosphor/white → practical benefits and thermal/driver/lifecycle qualification. An ecosystem slogan or support measure does not demonstrate production yield.

## Nuclear, quantum and cosmic measurement

- **Ionisation/penetration:** alpha helium nucleus strongly ionising but easily stopped; beta electron/positron; gamma photon highly penetrating. Shielding depends on route and dose, not label alone.
- **Decay:** `N=N₀(½)^(t/T½)`; ordinary heat/pressure do not reset nuclear half-life. Fission splits heavy nuclei; fusion joins light; `E=mc²` connects mass-energy.
- **Bosons (Core Lesson 14):** identical integer-spin particles may share a quantum state; fermions half-integer obey Pauli exclusion. **2018 GS-III Q5:** discuss Bose/Einstein's quantum-statistical contribution and limits, not consumer-product mythology. Advanced Lesson 15 adds cold-gas conditions and why photon lasers are not condensate experiments.
- **Laser refinement (Advanced Lesson 15):** gain must exceed cavity loss, beam has finite linewidth; attosecond light pulses enable study of electron motion; this is static scientific evidence, not a current-affairs claim.
- **Cosmic measures:** light-year = distance; special-relativity signal limit `c`; general relativity predicts light bending/gravitational waves. 2015 first gravitational-wave detection (announcement 2016); model inference ≠ optical image.
- **2018 Q62 / 2019 Q97 / 2021 Q76 / 2023 Q56:** predictions / inferred merger signal / distance unit / distinguish Cepheid variable star, nebula gas-dust cloud, pulsar neutron star. Antimatter opposite counterpart charges; weakly interacting neutrino neutral; Higgs boson discovered 2012; Higgs mechanism does not account for every component of ordinary mass.
- **GS-III answer spine:** law → conditional device → system → India-centred use → material, thermal, standards and access limits → qualified verdict. Reactor strategy, mission targets and planetary defence require additional domain-specific evidence.
