# Defence Indigenization, Atmanirbhar and Procurement — Live Session Edition

What must happen between a successful Indian prototype and a reliable system in service? First, a service specifies its need. Then a buyer selects a permissible acquisition route, a manufacturer builds at scale, suppliers provide dependable components, and the services sustain the equipment over years. **Self-reliance is a property of this whole chain**, not merely of its final assembly line.

     ├─ Who owns its design and upgrade rights?        DESIGN
     ├─ Where are its parts and final system made?     MANUFACTURE
     ├─ How much contract value originates in India?   VALUE ADDITION
     └─ Who repairs and improves it during service?    SUSTAINMENT
```

*The four questions can produce four different answers for the same platform.*

Suppose an aircraft is assembled in India using foreign drawings and imported critical equipment. Assembly generates skills and jobs. Yet a foreign supplier may still control a crucial upgrade, a replacement part or the underlying design. The lesson is not that assembly is worthless; it is that **assembly and design control answer different questions**. The analogy stops where a complex defence platform contains many independently designed subsystems: no one label describes them all.

1. **Atmanirbhar defence** means strengthening Indian design, development, production, sourcing and lifetime support; it does not mean that every import ends immediately. **Indigenisation of technology**, within the GS-III syllabus, is wider than national branding: it asks what technical and industrial control actually grows.
2. **Indigenous design** means control over architecture and the ability to redesign and upgrade. **Domestic manufacture** means physical production in India, whether independently developed or under licence. **Indigenous content (IC)** is the specified Indian **value share** used by procurement rules. A high value share need not prove domestic control over a critical foreign engine, electronic component or software dependency.
3. **Lifecycle support** includes spares, maintenance, repair, overhaul and upgrades. A platform whose support chain fails during service is not fully self-reliant simply because its initial delivery was domestic. Design → certified production → repeat orders → maintainability is a causal sequence, not a single event.
4. The Ministry of Defence's research arm, the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO), can develop a system, while a different institution buys it and a public or private manufacturer builds it. Keep development separate from this lesson's acquisition-and-scale problem.

**India case.** Indigenous aircraft design, licensed production of an aircraft, and domestic manufacture of a subsystem are three different accomplishments. For a named aircraft, ask who designed it, who produced this variant, and what was delivered to the user; do not infer one from another.

**Objection and reply.** Does this distinction unfairly dismiss domestic production? No: local assembly can teach industrial processes and protect jobs. The reply to overclaiming is to ask whether component sourcing, design rights, quality control and future upgrades also become domestic. Neither an import ban nor one domestic factory guarantees that outcome.

**UPSC use.** In a 10-marker, state a spectrum rather than a slogan. The trap is to equate the phrases *made in India*, *indigenously designed* and *minimum IC*. A fact is the policy's separate categories; the claim that deeper design control improves autonomy is a **reasoned inference**, conditional on viable capacity.

### Concept check

**Question:** An Indian factory assembles a platform with a high domestic-value share but depends on imported design rights and essential spares. What can and cannot be concluded about self-reliance?

**Model answer:** Domestic production and value addition are real achievements, but the foreign design and spares dependencies limit independent upgrades and sustained use. Evaluate those dimensions separately.

**Misconception to avoid:** Treating the content percentage as proof of indigenous design hides dependence in small but critical subsystems.

### Original Mains practice — design is not a percentage

**Question (Examine; 10 marks; answer in 150 words):** Examine whether high indigenous content in an Indian-assembled defence platform is sufficient evidence of technological self-reliance.

**Mains model answer:** High indigenous content (IC) records Indian value added under the applicable procurement test; it is useful evidence of domestic manufacturing activity, not a complete audit of technology. An aircraft assembled in India under foreign drawings may contain many locally produced parts while an imported engine or restricted software still controls its future operation. Design rights determine whether Indian engineers can modify the architecture; manufacture shows where production occurs; spares and overhaul determine whether a fleet remains usable. These dimensions can advance at different speeds. For example, Buy (Indian-IDDM) adds an indigenous design-and-development requirement beyond a mere Indian production address. A higher IC share may strengthen suppliers and jobs, but it cannot alone establish independent upgrades or resilience to interruption. Judge progress by verified design authority, critical-input sourcing, repeatable production and lifetime support, without dismissing useful licensed manufacture during transition.

**Scoring guide (10 marks):** 2 for defining IC as value share; 3 for separating design, manufacture and sustainment; 2 for the licensed-assembly/critical-input example; 2 for explaining why IC alone fails; 1 for a qualified, evidence-based verdict.

Imagine a school ordering computers: the teacher describes what is needed, the administration authorises a purchase, a supplier manufactures machines, and the school verifies delivery. No one should report a usable classroom merely because a need was approved. Defence procurement adds security, technical trials, contracting and service-life obligations; the classroom analogy only illustrates **different institutional decisions**.

The **DAC's Acceptance of Necessity (AoN)** is an in-principle decision on a proposal and acquisition categorisation. DDP shapes industrial-production capacity and oversees DPSU-related matters. DRDO designs and tests technologies and can facilitate production but is neither the DAC nor the services' bulk purchasing office. The services specify needs and participate in user trials and induction. Joint ventures and licensed production can be useful, but a foreign-design partner's role must remain visible.

**Why the division matters.** A successful DRDO test demonstrates a developmental milestone. A DAC announcement concerns a procurement proposal. A signed contract creates binding orders. A factory shipment is a delivery. Service acceptance brings a system into use. One stage does not follow automatically from the previous one. **India case:** the [MoD/PIB release of 16 January 2025](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2093286) reports a BDL MRSAM contract: an actual contract, unlike a later DAC AoN. The release does not establish how many systems have since been inducted.

**Challenge and reply.** Separate actors can slow decisions and dilute responsibility. But treating technical evaluation or public scrutiny as expendable can buy unreliable equipment. A better reply is predictable requirements, timely testing and measurable conversion from approved need to contract, not abolishing scrutiny. The trade-off is between **speed and lifecycle reliability**.

**UPSC use:** GS-II administrative questions and GS-III technology questions may meet here, but do not assign military command to a production department.

**Revision notes:**

1. The Ministry of Defence provides the overall policy setting.
2. The armed services state operational requirements and participate in trials.
3. The DAC, chaired by the Raksha Mantri, grants AoN and categorisation in principle.
4. An AoN does not establish a signed purchase, delivery or induction.
5. DRDO and other developers research, design and test technology.
6. DDP supports the defence-production ecosystem and DPSU-related matters.
7. DPSUs, private primes, joint ventures and suppliers manufacture and support equipment.
8. User services accept, induct and operate equipment after the applicable process.
9. The CDS heads DMA and is Permanent Chairman of the Chiefs of Staff Committee.
10. Departmental coordination and military advice do not amount to command over all three Service Chiefs.

### Concept check

**Question:** A new system passes a developmental test and receives a DAC AoN. What further milestones must occur before it can be described as in service?

**Model answer:** It still needs the applicable solicitation and evaluation, negotiation, a signed contract, production, delivery, user acceptance and induction; a test and an AoN are separate early milestones.

**Misconception to avoid:** An AoN is neither a financial purchase order nor evidence of delivery.

### Original Mains practice — institutional accountability

**Question (Analyse; 15 marks; answer in 250 words):** Analyse how distinct institutional responsibilities affect the conversion of an Indian defence design into an operational capability.

**Mains model answer:** A domestic prototype is a technological milestone, not an acquisition. DRDO or another developer designs and tests; the services define the operational requirement and take part in trials. The Defence Acquisition Council, chaired by the Raksha Mantri, grants Acceptance of Necessity (AoN) and categorisation in principle. Subsequent evaluation, a signed contract, manufacturer output, delivery and service acceptance are separate tests. The Department of Defence Production supports the production ecosystem; DPSUs such as BDL, private primes and component suppliers must supply repeatable, supportable equipment. The 16 January 2025 MoD/PIB BDL MRSAM contract notice illustrates a contractual stage, whereas the 3 July 2026 DAC notice concerns in-principle approvals; neither alone establishes induction. Dividing functions makes requirements, technical scrutiny, industrial execution and user acceptance individually accountable. It can also create delay or fragmented responsibility if requirements change or trials cannot be completed. Predictable specifications, shared testing access and tracking AoN-to-contract-to-acceptance milestones address that problem without treating scrutiny as dispensable. The CDS/DMA's coordination and advisory duties should not be mistaken for procurement approval or command over all Service Chiefs. Thus institutional coordination matters only if validated technology becomes a contracted, delivered and maintainable service capability.

**Scoring guide (15 marks):** 3 for accurately assigning services/DAC/DRDO/DDP roles; 3 for distinguishing AoN, contract and induction; 3 for the dated BDL-versus-DAC evidence; 3 for causal bottlenecks and a workable remedy; 3 for qualified institutional accountability without conflating CDS command.

*Once the actors are separated, ask which purchasing route changes the industrial outcome.*

     ├── Buy / Buy and Make: buy finished capability, perhaps with local production

```

*The umbrella schemes and their named procurement categories are related, but they are not a single unqualified list of identical routes.*

| Spending need | Applicable procedure | What not to assume |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase of a new equipment/platform as **capital** acquisition | **DAP 2020**, the acquisition procedure currently in force in the cited MoD announcement | A draft revision already governs the purchase |
| Maintenance and sustenance under the **revenue** head | **Defence Procurement Manual (DPM) 2025** | A revenue-head support purchase follows the capital-acquisition DAP just because it concerns defence equipment |

*The budget head and purpose determine which procedural rulebook is relevant; servicing a platform is not automatically a new capital acquisition.*

**First the intuition.** One need can be met by buying an existing Indian design; another may require a foreign-origin system with Indian manufacture; a third may need time and investment to develop in India. Choosing a procurement route therefore chooses **which capability arrives, which suppliers qualify and what domestic value is expected**. This cannot be decided merely by asking whether a factory stands on Indian soil.

| Preference among named Buy-type categories | Practical distinction |
|---|---|
| **Buy (Indian-IDDM)** | Buy from an Indian vendor an **indigenously designed, developed and manufactured** product with required indigenous content; preferential domestic-design route. |
| **Buy (Indian)** | Buy from an Indian vendor meeting applicable IC requirements; domestic design is **not** the defining condition. |
| **Buy & Make (Indian)** | Indian vendor supplies an initial purchase and then undertakes Indian manufacture linked to technology transfer from a foreign original-equipment manufacturer. |
| **Buy (Global – Manufacture in India)** | Global vendor participation coupled to prescribed manufacture in India through the approved industrial arrangement; not the same as an Indian-designed product. |
| **Buy (Global)** | Global sourcing where the applicable domestic routes do not satisfy the requirement; it is not an indigenisation badge. |

This is a **preference ordering**, not a claim that every purchase begins with and automatically passes through all five categories. Category eligibility and prescribed minimum IC depend on the actual DAP provisions; do not invent a universal percentage for every route. DAP 2020 also treats Make, Design and Development, Leasing and the Strategic Partnership Model as distinct schemes. Buy & Make (Indian) must not disappear simply because it combines buying and domestic production.

**A conditional foreign-procurement obligation: offsets.** An offset requires an eligible foreign vendor, under applicable contract rules, to generate specified industrial benefit in India—for example through permitted Indian purchases, investment or technology-transfer activity. The intended bridge is **foreign purchase → Indian supplier opportunity → industrial learning**, not “foreign purchase → automatically indigenous design.” This is distinct from a Buy category, the indigenous-content (IC) value-share test on the purchased item, and a positive-list domestic-sourcing restriction. A hypothetical foreign supplier's purchase from an Indian components firm could expand orders, but neither that transaction nor formal discharge of the obligation proves that Indian engineers acquired independent design or upgrade rights. DAP 2020 narrowed offset application, removing it for government-to-government, inter-governmental and single-vendor procurements; therefore do not append an offset to every import. Inspect the applicable contract terms and what Indian capability was actually absorbed. Lesson 11 evaluates this instrument's costs and limits after the full production chain is clear.

**What about “DAP 2026”?** In its [10 February 2026 announcement](https://archive.pib.gov.in/newsite/erelcontent.aspx?relid=283920), the Ministry of Defence described a **draft** DAP-2026, invited comments by **3 March 2026**, and explicitly said it would replace DAP-2020 **once approved**. Among the *proposals*, not rules to apply prematurely, are choosing categorisation by technology availability and manufacturing readiness, a faster path for short-cycle equipment, spiral design/procurement, pragmatic indigenous-content and indigenous-design assessment, revised trials/quality assurance, innovation incentives and indigenous design/IPR retention. A proposal may address slow acquisition and shallow assembly, but tests, affordability and actual design-rights agreements would still decide outcomes. **DAP 2020 remained the operative capital framework in that announcement**; check an official final notification before asserting that any draft provision entered into force. This February baseline is relevant to the framework even though it lies outside the six-month news window.

**Example.** A domestically designed electronic system offered by an Indian vendor differs from an Indian firm's licensed manufacture of a foreign-designed system. Either may satisfy a particular need; the former generally strengthens design autonomy, while the latter may deliver capability and factory learning sooner. Licence terms and actual absorption decide the longer-run result. A manufacturer's label does not reveal the contract's indigenous-content calculation.

**Objection and reply.** Does giving preference to Indian routes always provide the quickest or cheapest capability? Not necessarily: production readiness, verification and urgency matter. Conversely, unrestricted imports can lock in foreign spares and suppress domestic learning. The balanced response is capability-specific category choice with transparent evaluation, realistic domestic-content requirements and verified delivery.

**UPSC trap:** DAP is the **capital-acquisition procedure**, not an innovation grant or the DPM 2025 revenue-maintenance manual. “Draft DAP-2026” does **not** mean it replaced DAP 2020. Make is a **development/acquisition pathway**, not the whole "Make in India" campaign; iDEX is an innovation channel, not a Buy category. An offset is a conditional industrial obligation on an eligible foreign purchase, not a sixth Buy category or a guarantee of technology transfer. GS-III answers can show how category selection connects technology control to market demand.

**Revision notes:** capital DAP 2020 ≠ revenue DPM 2025; draft DAP-2026 replaces DAP 2020 only **once approved**; route before vendor; preference ≠ guaranteed award; IDDM adds design and development; Indian vendor ≠ Indian design; two middle routes address locally manufactured output with foreign linkage; IC ≠ intellectual-property control; leasing ≠ ownership purchase; applicable foreign-vendor offsets may generate Indian purchases, investment or technology activity, but not guaranteed absorption; DAP 2020 excludes government-to-government, inter-governmental and single-vendor routes from offsets.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why might an Indian-assembled foreign design qualify under a domestic-manufacturing arrangement yet not satisfy Buy (Indian-IDDM), even if the purchase also generates an offset?

**Model answer:** Manufacturing location and indigenous-value conditions can be met without indigenous design and development; Indian-IDDM requires those additional origins, not just local assembly. An applicable foreign-vendor offset might benefit Indian firms but does not itself transfer design ownership.

**Misconception to avoid:** Reading the word “Indian” in a category as a universal guarantee that every part and design belongs to India.

### Original Mains practice — choosing a route

**Question (Distinguish; 15 marks; answer in 250 words):** Distinguish the five named DAP 2020 procurement categories by the domestic capability they seek. When can an offset arise, and why does neither a category nor an offset prove technological autonomy?

**Mains model answer:** DAP 2020 structures capital acquisition, but a preference order does not mean five compulsory stages in each purchase. Buy (Indian-IDDM) prioritises an Indian vendor's indigenous design, development and manufacture with applicable IC. Buy (Indian) requires an Indian vendor and prescribed domestic value without making indigenous design its defining test. Buy & Make (Indian) links an initial Indian-vendor purchase to subsequent Indian manufacture through foreign technology transfer. Buy (Global – Manufacture in India) allows global participation with prescribed local production; Buy (Global) permits global sourcing where applicable domestic routes cannot meet the need. These routes answer different urgency and industrial-readiness problems: a domestic design may deepen upgrade control, while foreign-linked manufacture may supply capability and factory learning sooner. Yet indigenous content measures value, not ownership of a critical component's design, and a transfer agreement need not produce actual absorption. An applicable foreign-vendor offset can direct purchases, investment or technology activity to Indian industry, but DAP 2020 excludes government-to-government, inter-governmental and single-vendor routes; discharge alone cannot prove learning. Verify the category's conditions, contract, component sourcing, quality and upgrade rights before claiming autonomy. Make, leasing and strategic partnership are other DAP pathways, not missing rungs of this preference list. The February 2026 DAP-2026 announcement described a **draft** replacement only once approved; revenue-head maintenance follows DPM 2025 instead. Choose a route for the need, then measure delivered domestic depth rather than the category's name.

**Scoring guide (15 marks):** 4 for five accurate category distinctions; 2 for offset mechanism, eligibility and exclusions; 2 for separating other schemes and capital/revenue rules; 3 for the design-versus-value/transfer reasoning; 2 for draft-status accuracy; 2 for an applied, qualified route-choice conclusion.

*A suitable route is still not a signed order. Follow the decisions chronologically.*

*The arrow chain marks different decision and evidence thresholds, not an automatic timetable.*

In ordinary language, **AoN says a proposed purchase is needed in principle**. A request for proposal invites eligible offers. Trials check whether an offer works under the specified conditions; financial evaluation considers contractual value and other procurement criteria. A signed contract is the purchase commitment. Delivery and service acceptance come later. Some routes have distinct procedural details: the diagram is a learning map, not a substitute for the current DAP clauses.

**Concrete case.** In a July 2026 AoN news report, the correct status verb is “approved the need/proposal in principle.” Contrast the [16 January 2025 MoD/PIB BDL MRSAM contract notice](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2093286): “contract signed” describes a later stage. Neither phrase alone entitles one to claim that a fielded fleet exists. For a truly useful capability, spares, trained maintainers and user acceptance must follow deliveries.

Why insist on these stages? A prototype may pass one demonstration but fail repeatability or support requirements. Conversely, excessive paperwork or repeated trials can make a needed capability obsolete before induction. **Reply:** specify the user requirement clearly at the start, share transparent test infrastructure, track AoN-to-contract conversion and monitor delivery and serviceability. Do not suggest bypassing legitimate scrutiny.

**UPSC use:** assign a status verb to every report—*proposed, AoN, RFP, trials, contracted, delivered, inducted*. The distinction is especially valuable in current-affairs questions where large announced values may be mistaken for expenditure or operational capability. Report any rupee figure only with its correct stage and dated source.

**Revision notes:**

1. A user requirement begins the acquisition chain.
2. DAC AoN approves the need and categorisation in principle.
3. An RFP or solicitation invites eligible offers; it is not an award.
4. Technical evaluation and user trials test compliance with the stated requirement.
5. Commercial assessment and negotiation precede the binding purchase commitment.
6. Only a signed contract establishes a contracted order.
7. Production and delivery remain separate from contracting.
8. User acceptance and induction remain separate from delivery.
9. Quality assurance and service support connect factory output to usable capability.
10. Classify every report as proposed, AoN, tendered, trialled, contracted, delivered or inducted.

### Concept check

**Question:** A DAC notice values a set of proposals and a supplier separately reports completion of user trials. Can either event be counted as contracted indigenous production?

**Model answer:** No. The DAC notice authorises a need in principle, while a trial concerns performance; only a signed contract establishes a contracted order, and actual production must then be verified separately.

**Misconception to avoid:** Adding the monetary value of AoNs to the value of signed contracts treats different pipeline stages as the same output.

### Original Mains practice — the approval-to-induction gap

**Question (Analyse; 10 marks; answer in 150 words):** Analyse why announced defence acquisition approvals should not be counted as inducted indigenous capability.

**Mains model answer:** A DAC Acceptance of Necessity (AoN) approves a proposed need and category in principle; it neither selects a final supplier nor obliges delivery. The 3 July 2026 DAC notice illustrates this approval stage. Under the applicable capital procedure, a request for proposal, technical evaluation and trials, commercial assessment and negotiation must precede a signed contract. The 16 January 2025 BDL MRSAM contract notice illustrates a different, later stage, not proof of induction. The producer must then deliver repeatable quality, and the user must accept and support the equipment. Treating an AoN's announced value as factory output overstates both expenditure and domestic capability. Delays in specifications, trials or suppliers can prevent conversion; predictable requirements and monitoring contract, delivery and serviceability milestones improve accountability. Different DAP routes vary in detail, so this sequence is a conceptual map, not an invariant timetable.

**Scoring guide (10 marks):** 2 for AoN's exact status; 2 for procurement milestones; 2 for contrasting the dated AoN and contract notices; 2 for delivery/induction bottlenecks; 2 for a measured conversion remedy and procedural qualification.

**Question (Critically examine; 10 marks; answer in 150 words):** Critically examine the claim that rising defence exports alone establish a self-reliant Indian defence industry.

**Mains model answer:** Exports can enlarge production runs, support supplier learning and test commercial credibility, but the value of sales is not a measure of Indian design rights. A DPSU or private exporter may sell equipment assembled in India while depending on imported critical components and foreign upgrade permissions. Buyers also need training, spare parts and reliable maintenance after delivery; domestic services need the same lifecycle capacity. An export order is only one stage: verify repeatable quality and support for Indian and overseas users. Export demand might compete with domestic orders if capacity is limited; it might instead spread fixed costs and strengthen skills when output expands. Assess the actual contract, critical-input sourcing, design and repair authority, spares availability and service reliability alongside exports. Higher sales can strengthen self-reliance if domestic production and support scale together; they do not prove it alone.

*No single instrument maximises procurement speed, domestic depth and low cost at once.*

**Offset effectiveness, beyond the Core mechanism.** Lesson 3 explained when a foreign-vendor offset can arise and what it seeks to bring into Indian industry. The harder evaluation asks whether a formally discharged obligation produced difficult-to-transfer design knowledge. DAP 2020's narrower coverage may prevent weak paper-only obligations, but can also remove a bargaining tool in excluded routes. Where contractual conditions permit, negotiate concrete, verifiable development, supplier capability and support outcomes instead of treating nominal discharge as an automatic technology gain. **Status and accounting.** An AoN measures planned demand, not industrial output. A notified list measures stated localisation targets, not capability. Contract values measure order commitment, not delivery. Domestic production and export totals measure outputs, not control over critical intellectual property. One should follow a proposal's conversion to award, on-time production, certified serviceability and upgrade rights. **Hard industrial bottlenecks:** supplier finance and order certainty; certification and testing; specialised electronics, engines, seekers and materials; absorption of technology in a joint venture; after-sales support. Here the positive lists, DAP preference, Make/iDEX/TDF, public-private primes, FDI and corridors must **meet at the weakest link**, not substitute for one another.

**Balanced answer.** Imports can bridge urgent needs while domestic capability matures; a procurement preference alone cannot conjure a mature supplier. Conversely, leaving all critical systems to external supply invites disruption and limits upgrades. A phased policy with credible tests, stable orders, competitive vendors, verified Indian design depth and maintenance capability addresses both risks. Whether that balance has been attained for a particular platform remains an empirical question, not a doctrine to assume.

**UPSC use:** argue **policy instrument → intended causal mechanism → example → implementation objection → conditional remedy**. Keep claims of self-reliance proportional to evidence. This evaluation is advanced depth, not a replacement for the full core chain already taught.

# ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS MODEL PRACTICE

## 10 marks — distinction before prescription

**Question (Analyse; 150 words):** Why is indigenous defence development alone insufficient for self-reliant capability in service?

**Model answer (130 words):** An Indian design creates a possibility; a dependable capability requires purchase, industrial scale and lifetime support. DRDO can develop and trial a system, but a service's requirement must proceed through the applicable DAP category, evaluation, contract and user acceptance. A DAC Acceptance of Necessity approves a need in principle, not equipment in service. DPSUs such as BDL and private suppliers then have to produce certified systems repeatedly. Positive Indigenisation Lists can signal demand, while iDEX or TDF can support suppliers; neither guarantees an order. Imported critical electronics or spares may still constrain upgrades even after domestic assembly. Therefore assess design control, indigenous-value depth, AoN-to-contract conversion, deliveries and maintainability separately. A phased mix of local development and necessary strategic sourcing is more credible than counting prototypes or announcements as operational self-reliance.

**Scoring guide (10 marks):** 2 for the design-versus-capability thesis; 2 for the DRDO/service/DAC/DAP acquisition chain; 2 for production and demand-side instruments with named evidence; 2 for lifecycle and critical-import limits; 1 for evidence-stage discipline; 1 for the qualified conclusion. **Total: 10.**

## 15 marks — join demand to industrial capacity

**Question (Examine; 250 words):** How do DAP 2020, positive indigenisation lists and supplier-development instruments jointly shape defence manufacturing in India? Identify implementation risks.

**Model answer (222 words):** Domestic capability depends on matching a credible buyer to a producer who can meet service standards. DAP 2020 structures capital-acquisition routes and preference: Buy (Indian-IDDM) emphasises indigenous design and development, while Buy & Make (Indian) and Buy (Global – Manufacture in India) allow differing paths to Indian production. Category choice therefore affects who may bid and which industrial capability is rewarded; it is not itself a factory order.

Services' positive lists shape platform-level sourcing; DPSU lists and the SRIJAN dashboard identify components for Indian development. These are demand signals with item-specific timelines, not proof that listed components are already available. A domestic capital-procurement earmark reserves planned buying space for Indian industry, but is not a signed order. On the supply side, the DAP Make route, DIO's iDEX/ADITI and DRDO's Technology Development Fund help projects or entrants develop technologies. DPSUs, private integrators, smaller suppliers and the two defence industrial corridors can then support scale, testing and skills.

The chain may fail at unclear requirements, slow AoN-to-contract conversion, unavailable trials, unstable orders or imported critical inputs. An innovation award cannot replace certification, and a domestic-content percentage cannot establish ownership of design. India should track contracts, qualified suppliers, repeatable deliveries and spares availability, while retaining justified external sourcing during transition. Thus procurement preference is useful only when industrial learning and operational support actually follow it.

**Scoring guide (15 marks):** 3 for accurate DAP 2020 category mechanisms; 3 for distinguishing both list families, SRIJAN and the domestic capital earmark; 3 for Make/iDEX/ADITI/TDF and producer-ecosystem linkage; 3 for implementation risks across requirements, trials, orders and imported inputs; 2 for measurable remedies and indicators; 1 for a qualified conclusion. **Total: 15.**

## 20 marks — evaluate the strategy

**Question (Critically evaluate; 250 words):** Can India's Atmanirbhar defence strategy reconcile timely acquisition with deep technological autonomy? Consider public/private industry, foreign participation, lifecycle support and exports.

**Model answer (228 words):** Self-reliance is not the absence of every import; it is growing control over design, production and service when capability is needed. DAP 2020's Indian-IDDM preference rewards domestic design, while its Indian-manufacture and global routes permit alternative responses to urgency. The DAC's AoN, however, is not a contract; an effective policy must shorten the transition from validated requirements to quality deliveries without abandoning scrutiny.

Public DPSUs such as HAL, BEL and BDL offer industrial anchors; private firms and MSMEs add specialised suppliers and innovation. The 2021 creation of seven OFB-successor DPSUs changed governance, but productivity and orders, not corporatisation alone, test its value. Services' and DPSUs' positive lists can create demand, while iDEX, TDF and the Uttar Pradesh and Tamil Nadu corridors can support entry and scale. None guarantees indigenous intellectual property or certified output.

Foreign investment, under different automatic and government routes, and strategic partnerships can bring capital and learning. They may also produce assembly without upgrade rights. Offsets illustrate the same risk: nominal industrial obligations need not transfer critical technology. Locally available spares, overhaul, testing and upgrades ultimately decide service resilience. Exports may lengthen production runs but require support and can retain imported inputs.

The reconciliation is therefore **conditional**: procure urgent gaps responsibly, phase localisation, verify design and supplier absorption, maintain competition, and judge contracts by delivered, maintainable capability rather than approvals, list lengths or export totals.

**Scoring guide (20 marks):** 3 for a conditional thesis on timely acquisition and autonomy; 4 for public/private industry, OFB-successor and demand-support evidence; 3 for FDI, strategic partnership and technology-absorption analysis; 4 for lifecycle, critical-input and export evaluation; 4 for balanced counterarguments, phased localisation and measurable remedies; 2 for a platform-specific qualified verdict. **Total: 20.**

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

## The four tests of Atmanirbhar defence

- **Design**: architecture, IP and independent upgrade rights. **Manufacture**: where production happens. **IC**: Indian share of procurement value, not design ownership. **Sustainment**: spares, MRO and upgrades. Never turn one test into another.
- Development/test (DRDO and other developers) precedes but does not constitute DAC approval, a purchase, manufacturing scale or service induction.

## Capital-acquisition route and status verbs

- DAP 2020 schemes include Buy, Buy and Make, Leasing, Make, Design and Development and the Strategic Partnership Model. Preference sequence: **Indian-IDDM → Indian → Buy & Make (Indian) → Global – Manufacture in India → Global**, subject to category-specific conditions. Do not assign an invented universal IC percentage.
- MoD announced **draft DAP-2026 on 10 February 2026** and requested comments by **3 March**; it replaces DAP 2020 **only once approved**. Its readiness-based categories, faster short-cycle acquisition, spiral procurement, pragmatic IC/design evaluation, trial/QA changes, innovation incentives and indigenous IPR retention are **proposals**, not operative DAP-2020 clauses. **DAP: capital equipment procurement; DPM 2025: revenue-head maintenance/sustenance.** Check official final approval before describing a new procedure as in force.
- **Requirement → AoN → RFP/evaluation/trials → contract → delivery → induction → support.** These are not synonyms or a promise of automatic conversion.
- DAC is chaired by the Raksha Mantri; DDP oversees production; DRDO develops; DPSUs/private firms manufacture; services specify requirements and use equipment.

## Domestic demand and makers

- Services' lists address platform/item import substitution; DPSU lists address components and materials; SRIJAN exposes DPSU item progress. The **18 August 2026 sixth list** has **405 announced items**, indexed as **389 DPSU and 16 Indian Coast Guard items**, not 405 DPSU-only completed indigenous products. Direct release fetch was unavailable; check specific item timelines.
- A **domestic capital-procurement budget earmark** creates planned demand for Indian vendors, distinct from positive-list restrictions, category preference and IC rules. No percentage is established here; a budget allocation is not a contract, expenditure or inducted capability.
- HAL, BEL, BDL, MDL, GRSE and GSL illustrate distinct DPSU roles. **OFB corporatised in 2021** into Munitions India, Armoured Vehicles Nigam, Advanced Weapons and Equipment India, Troop Comforts, Yantra India, India Optel and Gliders India. Incorporation ≠ productivity.
- DAP Make is a development route; **DIO/iDEX/ADITI** foster innovators; **DRDO/TDF** supports industry development. Grants and prototypes require separate qualification and procurement.
- Strategic partners are selected private capacity builders. **UP and Tamil Nadu** host the two defence industrial corridors; corridors are not buying categories. Defence FDI: up to **74% automatic**, above 74%–100% **government route** under specified grounds; equity is not indigenous content.

## Evidence of durable capability

- A dependable supply chain needs recurring orders, certification, component depth, repair and upgrade rights. Offsets were narrowed under DAP 2020 in specified foreign-procurement routes; offset discharge ≠ technology absorption.
- Defence Exim facilitates authorisations, not sales; exports can help scale but do not prove domestic origin of every subsystem. Distinguish source year, status and outcome.
- PYQ routes: **2024 Q35** fighter generation; **Q69** cross-service rank grades; **Q77** CDS/DMA duties; **2025 Q84** aircraft functions; **2026 Q68** manufacture of precise named hardware, with **provisional 2026 key withheld**. Answer each by the attribute actually asked, not by mere service use.

## Reusable GS-III answer spine
