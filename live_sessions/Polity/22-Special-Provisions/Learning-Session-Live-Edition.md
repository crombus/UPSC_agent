# Special Provisions - Live Session Edition

India's Constitution does not protect unity by insisting that every territorial unit be governed identically. Part XXI uses carefully bounded differences to address distinct histories, identities, customary systems, regional inequalities and integration settlements. The constitutional skill is therefore not memorising a State-Article list. It is learning **who acts, through which legal mechanism, for what protected purpose, and with what limit**.

The visual shows the core idea: constitutional difference is attached to a defined problem. It is not a free-standing privilege granted without purpose.

### 1. Plain intuition: equality does not always mean identical treatment

Suppose two regions enter or remain within a federation under very different conditions. One needs protection for customary land relations; another needs correction of regional underdevelopment; a third needs continuity while an earlier political order is integrated. Giving all three the same institutional answer may be formally uniform but practically unequal.

**Asymmetric federalism** therefore means that territorial units possess different constitutional arrangements within the same Union. The units do not become externally sovereign or acquire a right to secede. They remain governed by the Constitution, but the Constitution calibrates powers, safeguards or institutions differently.

> **Answer line:** Indian asymmetry is differentiated integration within one sovereign constitutional order, not a treaty-based confederation of sovereign States.

### 2. Why Part XXI contains several kinds of provision

Part XXI is titled **Temporary, Transitional and Special Provisions**. Those three words must not be treated as synonyms.

| Type | Function | Illustration |
|---|---|---|
| Temporary | limited by time, condition or an intended transition | Article 369's five-year power; the pre-2019 design of Article 370 |
| Transitional | carries old law, institutions or office-holders into the new Constitution | Articles 372-378 and related provisions |
| Special | creates a tailored arrangement for a State or region | Articles 371-371J |

Article 370 was textually described as temporary. Articles 371-371J are special State-specific provisions, although some individual clauses within them were transitional. Article 371F, for example, contains both enduring safeguards and one-time integration machinery.

### 3. The purpose test

A useful way to analyse any special provision is:

```text
PROTECTED PURPOSE
      |
      +--> protected subject or region
      +--> constitutional actor
      +--> legal mechanism
      +--> democratic or supervisory check
      +--> express limit / condition / termination route
```

Apply it to Article 371A:

- purpose: protect Naga practices, custom, customary justice and land/resources;
- actor: Nagaland Legislative Assembly;
- mechanism: a specified parliamentary law does not apply unless the Assembly resolves;
- limit: only four listed subject fields receive that shield;
- additional architecture: Governor and Tuensang clauses have their own conditions.

This prevents the common error of saying "Article 371 gives special status" without identifying what is actually special.

### 4. Ownership boundaries: what this topic teaches and what it only links

| Included here | Bounded link only | Excluded detailed doctrine |
|---|---|---|
| Article 370/35A history and present constitutional result | J&K and Ladakh's present UT classification | detailed J&K UT legislature/LG machinery - Topic 25 |
| Articles 371-371J clause architecture | Fifth/Sixth Schedules as comparison | councils, TACs and full Article 244 machinery - Topic 26 |
| asymmetric-federalism logic | PESA as a contrast in institutional source | PESA detail - Topic 23 |
| Part XXI boundary map | Articles 330-342A acknowledged as another field | Part XVI provisions for certain classes - Topic 53 |

The distinction matters because Article 371B's Assam committee is not a Sixth Schedule council, Article 371C's Hill Areas Committee is not a Fifth Schedule Tribes Advisory Council, and Articles 371A/371G are not PESA.

### 5. Part XXI boundary beyond Articles 370-371J

Article **369** temporarily enabled Parliament, for five years from commencement, to legislate on specified State List matters as if they were Concurrent List matters. Its competence was time-bound; laws already made retained effect for past acts and omissions.

Articles **372-378A** form a compact transition sequence:

| Article | Exact transitional job |
|---|---|
| 372 | continued existing laws and temporarily empowered presidential adaptation |
| 372A | enabled adaptation of laws to the Seventh Amendment reorganisation |
| 373 | temporarily substituted the President for Parliament in specified preventive-detention matters under Article 22 |
| 374 | transferred Federal Court proceedings and judges to the Supreme Court framework and dealt with Privy Council transitions |
| 375 | continued courts, authorities and officers subject to the Constitution |
| 376 | continued and adjusted High Court judges |
| 377 | continued the Auditor-General as Comptroller and Auditor-General |
| 378 | continued Union, State and Joint Public Service Commission members |
| 378A | fixed the duration of the Andhra Pradesh Assembly constituted under the States Reorganisation Act, 1956 |

Articles **379-391 are omitted**. Article **392** empowered the President to remove initial implementation difficulties only within its commencement-era limit. These provisions explain constitutional continuity; they are not additional State-specific autonomy clauses.

### 6. Strongest objection, reply and residual

**Objection:** Differentiated rights for territories offend equal citizenship and encourage permanent separatism.

**Reply:** The protected interest is usually institutional, territorial or cultural, not a hierarchy of citizenship. A consent shield may preserve customary law; a development board may correct internal inequality; continuity clauses may prevent legal chaos. Each arrangement remains constitutionally bounded and judicially reviewable.

**Qualified residual:** Asymmetry can become politically corrosive if its purpose is vague, implementation opaque or benefits captured by elites. Constitutional legitimacy therefore depends on a close fit between the protected problem and the chosen mechanism.

### UPSC application

- Define asymmetry before listing Articles.
- Separate **purpose**, **actor** and **mechanism**.
- Use Article 371A/371G for consent, 371D for opportunity, 371F for integration and 371J for regional development.
- Do not call special category status a constitutional Part XXI category.
- In evaluation, pair accommodation gains with accountability and inter-regional fairness concerns.

### Revision notes

1. Part XXI contains temporary, transitional and special provisions.
2. Asymmetry means differentiated constitutional arrangements within one Union.
3. It does not create a right to secede or continuing State sovereignty.
4. Every special provision should be analysed by purpose, actor, mechanism and limit.
5. Articles 371A and 371G use subject-specific Assembly-consent shields.
6. Articles 371 and 371J address regional development and opportunity.
7. Article 371F is an integration settlement.
8. Articles 371B and 371C create legislative-committee models, not autonomous councils.
9. Article 369 was a spent five-year legislative bridge.
10. Articles 372-392 chiefly concern constitutional continuity and transition.
11. UT machinery, tribal-area institutions, PESA and Part XVI remain separately owned topics.
12. Asymmetry is defensible when difference is purpose-specific and constitutionally bounded.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why is asymmetric federalism compatible with equal membership of States in the Union?

**Model answer:** Equal membership concerns subjection to one Constitution and Union, while asymmetry concerns different instruments for different constitutional problems. A consent shield, development board or integration clause does not confer external sovereignty; it tailors governance to identity, inequality or history within constitutional limits.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not equate equality with identical institutions. Uniform treatment can ignore materially different constitutional needs.

### Original Mains practice - 10 marks, 150 words

**Mains question:** Explain the constitutional logic of asymmetric federalism in India.

**Mains model answer:** Asymmetric federalism permits different territorial units to possess different constitutional arrangements while remaining equal members of one Union. Its logic is accommodation rather than hierarchy.

Part XXI illustrates several purposes. Article 371A protects specified Naga practices, customary justice and land/resources through an Assembly-consent shield. Article 371D permits local cadres and educational preferences to distribute opportunity within Andhra Pradesh and Telangana. Article 371F preserved representation, laws, courts and administration during Sikkim's integration. Article 371J links a development board, equitable funds and local opportunity to regional imbalance in Kalyana Karnataka.

These provisions remain bounded: they identify subjects, actors and procedures; they do not confer a right to secede. Their strength is that differentiated treatment can deepen voluntary integration. Their risk is opaque implementation or indefinite privilege. Thus Indian asymmetry is legitimate when the constitutional difference remains purpose-specific, accountable and consistent with common citizenship.

## Lesson 2 - Article 370 Before 2019: Temporary Compact, Constitutional Application and Article 35A

Progress: 2/11 | Stage: Foundation | Subtopic: Pre-2019 Article 370, Presidential Orders and Article 35A

### PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST

### Visual first: Article 370 was an application gateway

```text
INSTRUMENT OF ACCESSION
 defence + external affairs + communications
                 |
                 v
 ARTICLE 370(1): HOW INDIA'S CONSTITUTION APPLIES
   +-------------+-----------------------------+
   |                                           |
consultation for accession fields     concurrence for additional fields/
                                      constitutional modifications
   |                                           |
   +----------------- PRESIDENTIAL ORDER ------+
                         |
                         v
             gradual constitutional application
                         |
                         v
      1954 Order + Article 35A + many later extensions
```

Article 370 did not operate merely as a list of exemptions. It was the constitutional gateway through which Union legislative competence and provisions of the Indian Constitution were progressively applied to Jammu and Kashmir.

### 1. The pre-2019 constitutional compact

Article 370(1) began with a non obstante clause. Its original architecture did four things:

1. former Article 238 did not apply to Jammu and Kashmir;
2. Parliament's legislative power initially followed the Instrument of Accession and further matters specified through the constitutional process;
3. Articles 1 and 370 applied directly;
4. other constitutional provisions could be applied by Presidential Order with exceptions and modifications.

The critical distinction was between:

- **consultation** for matters corresponding to the Instrument of Accession; and
- **concurrence** for other matters.

If concurrence had been given before the J&K Constituent Assembly was convened, Article 370(2) required it to be placed before that Assembly.

### 2. Article 370(3): the termination route

The President could notify that Article 370 would cease to operate or operate with exceptions and modifications. The proviso originally required the recommendation of the J&K Constituent Assembly.

That produced the later constitutional controversy:

```text
Constituent Assembly dissolved in 1957
                 |
                 +--> permanence argument:
                 |    termination route disappeared
                 |
                 +--> temporary/integration argument:
                      Article 370(3) power survived
```

The 2023 Supreme Court accepted the second broad conclusion: Article 370 remained temporary and the President's power under clause (3) did not vanish with the Constituent Assembly.

### 3. Why "temporary" did not mean "automatically expired"

Article 370 had no calendar expiry date. It remained operative for decades because the Constitution itself supplied a special process for application and cessation. "Temporary" described its integration-oriented constitutional function; it did not mean that courts or governments could ignore its text while it operated.

This is an important analytical distinction:

| Proposition | Correct position |
|---|---|
| Temporary means legally irrelevant | Incorrect |
| Temporary means automatic expiry in 1957 | Incorrect |
| Temporary means an intended transitional arrangement governed by its own clauses | Correct |

### 4. The 1954 Presidential Order

The Constitution (Application to Jammu and Kashmir) Order, 1954 was issued under Article 370(1). It became the principal instrument applying the Indian Constitution to J&K with numerous exceptions, additions and modifications. Repeated later orders expanded constitutional application.

The result was neither complete constitutional separation nor ordinary uniform application. It was **progressive application through a special constitutional gateway**.

### 5. Article 35A

Article 35A was inserted into the Constitution's application to J&K through the 1954 Presidential Order, rather than by a constitutional amendment under Article 368. It protected J&K laws defining **permanent residents** and conferring special rights or restrictions concerning:

- State-government employment;
- acquisition of immovable property;
- settlement in the State; and
- scholarships and other forms of State aid.

It also insulated such laws from invalidity merely because they were inconsistent with specified Fundamental Rights.

Two traps must be avoided:

1. Article 35A did not itself define permanent resident in full; it constitutionally protected the State-law field.
2. It was not a free-standing Article 35 clause enacted by Parliament; it came through the 1954 application order.

### 6. Institutional consequence before 2019

Before 2019, J&K had its own Constitution and flag, while remaining part of India under Article 1. Union constitutional and legislative application had developed substantially, but the Article 370 route retained formal significance.

> **Safe formulation:** Pre-2019 J&K possessed constitutionally differentiated autonomy within India, not international sovereignty outside India.

### 7. Strongest objection and reply

**Objection:** Because extensive Union provisions already applied before 2019, Article 370 had become an empty shell.

**Reply:** Substantive convergence does not erase the legal gateway. Article 370 continued to structure how constitutional provisions were applied and retained legal consequences, including the 1954 Order and Article 35A.

**Qualified residual:** Political practice greatly reduced the autonomy originally associated with the provision, but a change in practical intensity is not the same as legal extinction.

### UPSC application

- Write "consultation" and "concurrence" accurately.
- Distinguish Article 370(1), (2) and (3).
- Describe Article 35A as inserted through the 1954 Presidential Order.
- Avoid saying J&K was sovereign or merely another ordinary State before 2019.
- Use "constitutional application gateway" as the organising idea.

### Revision notes

1. Article 370 was located in Part XXI and textually temporary.
2. Articles 1 and 370 applied directly to J&K.
3. Union competence initially tracked the Instrument of Accession and later extensions.
4. Consultation and concurrence were legally distinct.
5. Presidential Orders progressively applied constitutional provisions.
6. Article 370(2) linked pre-Constituent-Assembly concurrence to that Assembly.
7. Article 370(3) supplied the cessation/modification route.
8. The provision had no automatic calendar expiry.
9. The 1954 Order became the central constitutional-application instrument.
10. Article 35A protected permanent-resident laws in four specified fields.
11. Article 35A did not itself fully define permanent residents.
12. Pre-2019 autonomy existed within India, not outside Indian sovereignty.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why is it more accurate to call pre-2019 Article 370 an application gateway than a simple exemption?

**Model answer:** Article 370 determined how Parliament's competence and other provisions of the Indian Constitution extended to J&K through consultation, concurrence and Presidential Orders. It therefore enabled progressive constitutional application while preserving differentiated procedure, rather than merely subtracting J&K from the Constitution.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not describe the provision only through what did not apply. Its positive function was to authorise and structure application.

### Original Mains practice - 15 marks, 250 words

**Mains question:** Examine the constitutional operation of Article 370 before 2019, with special reference to Article 35A.

**Mains model answer:** Before 2019, Article 370 operated as the constitutional gateway linking Jammu and Kashmir to the wider Indian constitutional order. Articles 1 and 370 applied directly, while Parliament's initial competence reflected the Instrument of Accession. Presidential Orders could extend other constitutional provisions with exceptions and modifications.

The mechanism distinguished consultation from concurrence. Accession-related matters required consultation with the State Government; additional matters and wider constitutional application generally required concurrence. Where concurrence preceded the J&K Constituent Assembly, Article 370(2) required placement before it. Article 370(3) authorised cessation or modification by Presidential notification, originally on that Assembly's recommendation.

The 1954 Presidential Order became the principal application instrument. It also inserted Article 35A, which protected State laws defining permanent residents and granting them special treatment in public employment, immovable property, settlement, scholarships and State aid. Article 35A did not itself create the complete permanent-resident code; it shielded the State's legislative field from specified Fundamental Rights challenges.

Although repeated orders produced substantial constitutional convergence, Article 370 was not legally empty. Its procedure continued to organise application. Thus the pre-2019 arrangement was differentiated autonomy within Indian sovereignty: progressively integrated, yet constitutionally distinct in method.

The steps must be written separately because they relied on different legal instruments and produced different effects.

### 1. C.O. 272: Article 370(1)(d), constitutional application and concurrence

The Constitution (Application to Jammu and Kashmir) Order, 2019, **C.O. 272**, was issued on 5 August specifically under **Article 370(1)(d)**. It superseded the 1954 Order and applied all provisions of the Indian Constitution to J&K with the stated modification.

The Order recited that it was made with the concurrence of the Government of the State of Jammu and Kashmir. In the President's Rule setting, the President had assumed the State Government's functions. This claimed concurrence setting belongs to the **Article 370(1)(d) application step**; it must not be merged with Parliament's different institutional capacity in the later purported clause (3) recommendation step.

C.O. 272 also inserted Article 367(4), an interpretation clause for J&K. Most controversially, it read the "Constituent Assembly" in Article 370(3) as the "Legislative Assembly."

### 2. Recommendation and C.O. 273

Parliament separately adopted the recommendation presented as the State Legislative Assembly's recommendation for action under Article 370(3). It did so because, under the President's Rule proclamation, Parliament was exercising the powers of the State legislature. This was not Parliament acting as the "State Government" for concurrence under Article 370(1)(d).

On 6 August, **C.O. 273** declared that all clauses of Article 370 would cease to operate except a substituted formulation stating that all provisions of the Constitution, as amended, apply to J&K notwithstanding contrary law or instrument.

Article 370 therefore remains printed in the Constitution, but its earlier clauses are **inoperative**. "Deleted" is not the precise formulation.

### 3. Article 35A after the 2019 orders

Because C.O. 272 superseded the 1954 Order and the Constitution became applicable without the former exceptions, Article 35A ceased to operate. The correct sequence is:

```text
1954 Order supplied Article 35A
          |
          v
C.O. 272 superseded 1954 Order
          |
          v
Article 35A no longer operative
```

### 4. Reorganisation

The Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019 converted the former State, with effect from **31 October 2019**, into:

- the Union Territory of **Jammu and Kashmir**, with a legislature; and
- the Union Territory of **Ladakh**, without a legislature.

Detailed LG, legislative and reserved-subject machinery belongs to the Union Territories topic. Here the relevant point is federal: a former State was converted into two Union Territories, creating a major Article 3 and federalism controversy.

### 5. What the Supreme Court upheld in 2023

In *In Re: Article 370 of the Constitution*, 2023 INSC 1058, the Court's operative result included:

1. Article 370 was temporary.
2. J&K did not retain continuing internal sovereignty after constitutional integration.
3. The President's Article 370(3) power survived dissolution of the J&K Constituent Assembly.
4. C.O. 273 was valid.
5. Applying the whole Constitution through the President's Article 370(1)(d) power was valid.
6. State-Government concurrence was unnecessary for applying the whole Constitution in this setting because that exercise had the same effect as the President's unilateral clause (3) power, for which State concurrence or collaboration was not required.
7. The creation of Ladakh as a Union Territory was upheld.
8. Steps were directed for Assembly elections by 30 September 2024.
9. Statehood for J&K was to be restored at the earliest, reflecting the Union's assurance.

### 6. The crucial invalidity inside C.O. 272

The Court held that Article 367, an interpretation provision, could not be used to alter the character of Article 370. The substitution that effectively changed "Constituent Assembly" to "Legislative Assembly" was **ultra vires**.

Yet this did not invalidate the final constitutional result. The Court held that the President could apply the whole Constitution under Article 370(1)(d), without State-Government concurrence in this setting, and could exercise the surviving clause (3) power without the now-dissolved Constituent Assembly's recommendation. Thus:

| Question | Holding |
|---|---|
| Was the Article 367 substitution valid? | No, to the stated extent |
| Was State-Government concurrence necessary to apply the whole Constitution? | No, for the reason stated by the Court |
| Did that defect invalidate the entire 2019 result? | No |
| Was C.O. 273 valid? | Yes |

This is the highest-value close distinction in the judgment.

### 7. What remained undecided or bounded

The Court did **not finally adjudicate** the validity of converting J&K itself from a State into a Union Territory. It recorded the Union's assurance that Statehood would be restored and treated a final ruling on that issue as unnecessary. Therefore, the judgment cannot be cited as a full merits holding that Parliament may always extinguish Statehood under Article 3.

The Court upheld Ladakh's separation as a Union Territory. Justice Khanna separately stressed that converting a State into a UT has grave federal consequences and requires strong justification and strict Article 3 compliance.

Challenges concerning the President's Rule setting were addressed within the case's pleaded limits; the judgment is not a general licence for every irreversible federal change during Article 356.

### 8. Date-controlled status through 1 October 2026

The judgment's **30 September 2024** date was a judicial deadline for the Election Commission to conduct the election process; it is not the actual polling chronology. Official ECI material records three polling phases on **18 September, 25 September and 1 October 2024**, followed by counting on **8 October 2024**, for the 90 elected Assembly constituencies. An elected Assembly and Council of Ministers now function in the J&K Union Territory.

As of **1 October 2026**:

- J&K remains a **Union Territory with legislature**;
- Ladakh remains a **Union Territory without legislature**;
- no enactment restoring J&K Statehood was located;
- no Sixth Schedule grant to Ladakh was located.

These are status controls, not permission to reproduce detailed Topic 25 or Topic 26 machinery.

### 9. Strongest objection, reply and residual

**Objection:** Once the Article 367 substitution was invalid, the entire abrogation sequence should have failed.

**Reply:** The Court separated the defective interpretive route from the President's substantive powers under Article 370(1)(d) and (3). It upheld whole-Constitution application without requiring State concurrence in this setting and considered the invalid substitution unnecessary to sustain the outcome.

**Qualified residual:** The judgment settled the Article 370 result but left a major federal question open: whether and on what conditions Article 3 permits reduction of a State into a Union Territory.

### UPSC application

- Present the sequence by date and instrument.
- Write "inoperative," not simply "repealed" or "deleted."
- State that the Article 367 substitution was invalid but severable from the result.
- Separate Ladakh's upheld UT creation from J&K's undecided State-to-UT conversion.
- Date-control the present status.

### Concept check

**Question:** How could the Supreme Court invalidate the Article 367 substitution yet uphold the 2019 constitutional result?

**Model answer:** The Court treated Article 367 as an impermissible indirect route for altering Article 370. It nevertheless held that applying the whole Constitution under Article 370(1)(d) was valid and did not require State-Government concurrence in this setting, while the President's clause (3) power survived without the dissolved Constituent Assembly's recommendation. The defective interpretive device was therefore unnecessary to sustain C.O. 273 and the broader constitutional result.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not infer that every component of C.O. 272 was upheld, or that one invalid component automatically nullified the whole sequence.

### Original Mains practice - 20 marks, 250 words

**Mains question:** Critically examine the 2019 Article 370 sequence in light of the Supreme Court's 2023 judgment.

**Mains model answer:** The 2019 change involved distinct capacities and instruments. C.O. 272, issued on 5 August under Article 370(1)(d), recited State-Government concurrence in the President's Rule setting, superseded the 1954 application order, extended the Constitution and inserted Article 367(4). Separately, Parliament, exercising State-legislature powers under the proclamation, supplied the purported recommendation for Article 370(3). C.O. 273 then made Article 370's former clauses inoperative. The Reorganisation Act created J&K, a Union Territory with legislature, and Ladakh, a Union Territory without legislature, from 31 October 2019.

In *In Re Article 370* (2023), the Supreme Court held Article 370 temporary, rejected continuing J&K sovereignty, upheld the President's surviving clause (3) power and sustained C.O. 273. It held that the entire Constitution could be applied under Article 370(1)(d) and that State concurrence was unnecessary in this setting.

However, the Court invalidated C.O. 272's use of Article 367 to substitute "Legislative Assembly" for "Constituent Assembly": interpretation could not indirectly amend Article 370. The defect was treated as unnecessary to the outcome because corresponding substantive power existed.

The federal residual is significant. Ladakh's creation was upheld, but J&K's reduction from State to UT was not finally decided after the Union assured restoration. The Court fixed 30 September 2024 as the election deadline; ECI polling actually ran through 1 October, with counting on 8 October. J&K remained a UT on 1 October 2026. The judgment settles Article 370's inoperability, not the full limits of extinguishing Statehood.

## Lesson 4 - Articles 371–371J as a Family: Actors, Mechanisms and Article 371 for Maharashtra–Gujarat

Several Articles belong to more than one branch. That is why a one-row-per-State memory list is necessary but insufficient.

### 1. The actor-mechanism matrix

| Article | Principal actor | Main mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| 371 | President assigns Governor responsibility | boards, reports, equitable funds and opportunity |
| 371A | State Assembly; Governor in separate clauses | non-application unless Assembly resolves; law and order; finance; Tuensang |
| 371B | President | Assam Assembly committee |
| 371C | President, Governor and Union | Hill Areas Committee, reporting and directions |
| 371D | President | local cadres, local areas, preferences/reservations and possible tribunal |
| 371E | Parliament | enabling power for a Central University |
| 371F | Parliament, President and Governor by clause | complete Sikkim integration settlement |
| 371G | Mizoram Assembly | non-application unless Assembly resolves |
| 371H | Governor, subject to President's termination power | special law-and-order responsibility |
| 371I | Constitution directly | minimum Goa Assembly strength |
| 371J | President assigns Governor responsibility | board, funds, opportunity, local seats and posts |

The constitutional actor is often the examiner's trap. "The Governor has power under Article 371" is incomplete: the President may, by order, provide for a special responsibility of the Governor.

### 2. Article 371: current clause structure

The present Maharashtra-Gujarat provision is in Article 371(2). The earlier clause (1), associated with Andhra Pradesh, was omitted after Articles 371D and 371E were inserted.

The President may by order provide for a special responsibility of the Governor concerning:

1. separate development boards for **Vidarbha, Marathwada and the rest of Maharashtra**, or **Saurashtra, Kutch and the rest of Gujarat**;
2. an annual report on each board's working to be placed before the State Legislative Assembly;
3. equitable allocation of developmental expenditure, subject to the State's requirements as a whole; and
4. equitable arrangements for technical education, vocational training and State-controlled service employment.

### 3. Why the "rest of the State" matters

The provision is not framed as benefits for only a disadvantaged sub-region. It requires an arrangement across all named areas and repeatedly preserves the requirements of the State as a whole.

```text
REGIONAL CORRECTION
        +
STATE-WIDE REQUIREMENTS
        =
EQUITY, NOT REGIONAL EXCLUSIVITY
```

This language protects both sides: neglected regions receive constitutional attention, while the State remains an integrated fiscal and administrative unit.

### 4. Board, report, funds and opportunity are separate obligations

A weak answer says "Article 371 creates development boards." A complete answer identifies four linked controls:

| Control | Democratic or distributive function |
|---|---|
| separate boards | institutional focus |
| annual Assembly report | visibility and legislative accountability |
| equitable development expenditure | territorial fiscal fairness |
| education/training/service opportunity | human-capital and employment fairness |

The board does not itself replace the elected State government or create an autonomous legislature.

### 5. Comparison with Article 371J

Both provisions use a Presidential order assigning special responsibility to the Governor, a development board, an annual Assembly report and equitable funds/opportunity. Article 371J goes further by expressly permitting proportionate reservation of seats and identification/reservation of posts for persons belonging to the region by birth or domicile.

### 6. Strongest objection and reply

**Objection:** Development boards duplicate ordinary planning departments and weaken Cabinet responsibility.

**Reply:** Their constitutional purpose is not routine administration but sustained territorial visibility, equitable allocation and Assembly-facing reporting where normal politics may underrepresent a region.

**Qualified residual:** A board can become symbolic if reports, outcome measures and fund-allocation reasons are inaccessible. Constitutional creation does not guarantee administrative effectiveness.

### UPSC application

- Group the family by mechanism before giving the State list.
- For Article 371, write all four components.
- Mention that the current clause is clause (2).
- Do not describe the boards as autonomous councils.
- Compare 371 with 371J to show graduated development asymmetry.

### Revision notes

1. Articles 371-371J are a family of different mechanisms.
2. The same Article may combine more than one mechanism.
3. The President often activates the Governor's special responsibility by order.
4. Article 371 currently concerns Maharashtra and Gujarat.
5. Its earlier Andhra-related clause was omitted.
6. Named Maharashtra regions are Vidarbha, Marathwada and the rest.
7. Named Gujarat regions are Saurashtra, Kutch and the rest.
8. Each board's report goes annually to the Assembly.
9. Development expenditure must be equitably allocated.
10. Technical education, vocational training and State-service opportunity are included.
11. State-wide requirements remain an express qualification.
12. Article 371J adds explicit local seats/posts machinery.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why is Article 371 more than a development-board provision?

**Model answer:** It joins the board to annual legislative reporting, equitable developmental expenditure and equitable technical-education, vocational-training and State-service opportunities. The mechanism therefore combines institution, transparency, finance and human opportunity, subject to State-wide needs.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not treat the board as an autonomous government or omit the annual report and opportunity clauses.

### Original Mains practice - 10 marks, 150 words

**Mains question:** Explain how Article 371 addresses intra-State regional inequality in Maharashtra and Gujarat.

**Mains model answer:** Article 371 permits the President, by order, to assign the Governor a special responsibility for specified regional-equity measures in Maharashtra and Gujarat.

Its design has four elements. First, separate development boards may be established for Vidarbha, Marathwada and the rest of Maharashtra, and for Saurashtra, Kutch and the rest of Gujarat. Second, a report on each board's working must be placed annually before the State Assembly, creating political visibility. Third, developmental expenditure must be allocated equitably across the named areas. Fourth, equitable arrangements must provide technical education, vocational training and opportunities in State-controlled services.

The repeated qualification "subject to the requirements of the State as a whole" prevents regional correction from becoming fiscal separation. Article 371 therefore constitutionalises sustained attention to internal imbalance without creating autonomous territorial governments. Its effectiveness, however, depends on transparent allocations, measurable outcomes and meaningful Assembly scrutiny rather than the board's formal existence alone.

## Lesson 5 - Article 371A—Nagaland: Consent Shield, Land, Custom, Governor and Tuensang Provisions

Progress: 5/11 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Article 371A clauses (1)-(3) and operability caution

### PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST

Book context: Article 371A's consent, Governor, fiscal and Tuensang clauses are available.
CA Search: "official Nagaland Article 371A Tuensang Regional Council notification current"
CA Found: The constitutional text remains; no accessible official notification conclusively establishing the present operation or extension of the original Tuensang transitional arrangement was located.

### Visual first: Article 371A contains four distinct constitutional layers

```text
ARTICLE 371A
|
+-- 1(a) ASSEMBLY-CONSENT SHIELD
|     practices | custom | customary justice | land/resources
|
+-- 1(b) GOVERNOR: LAW AND ORDER
|     consultation + individual judgment + Presidential termination
|
+-- 1(c) EARMARKED UNION MONEY
|     specific service/purpose -> matching demand for grant
|
+-- 1(d), (2), (3) TUENSANG ARCHITECTURE
      Regional Council + administration + law adaptation
      Minister chain + Governor's final decision
      Articles 54/55/80(4)/170 adaptations + difficulty power
```

The Article should never be reduced to land protection alone.

### 1. Clause 1(a): the four-field consent shield

No Act of Parliament concerning the following applies to Nagaland unless the Nagaland Legislative Assembly resolves that it should:

1. religious or social practices of the Nagas;
2. Naga customary law and procedure;
3. administration of civil and criminal justice involving decisions according to Naga customary law; and
4. ownership and transfer of land **and its resources**.

The last words distinguish Article 371A from Article 371G, whose land clause does not reproduce "and its resources."

### 2. What the shield does - and does not do

The shield is a rule of **non-application**, not a general declaration that Parliament lacks legislative competence over Nagaland. Parliament may enact a law nationally; its application to Nagaland in a protected field depends on an Assembly resolution.

```text
PARLIAMENT ENACTS LAW
        |
        +--> outside four protected fields: ordinary application rules
        |
        +--> within four protected fields:
              does not apply unless Nagaland Assembly resolves
```

It is subject-specific, not a blanket veto over every Union law. It also does not remove constitutional judicial review or convert customary practice into an unlimited power against every constitutional norm.

### 3. Clause 1(b): Governor's law-and-order responsibility

The Governor has a special responsibility for law and order for so long as, in the Governor's opinion, the relevant internal disturbance connected with the Naga Hills-Tuensang Area continues. The Governor:

- consults the Council of Ministers;
- then exercises individual judgment;
- has a final decision on whether a matter falls within that responsibility; and
- may cease to possess the responsibility if the President so directs after a report or otherwise.

The first proviso supplies a **narrow protection**, not blanket immunity. If a challenge alleges that a matter did or did not require the Governor's individual judgment, the Governor's discretionary classification is final and the validity of the resulting act cannot be challenged merely on the ground that individual judgment ought, or ought not, to have been exercised. Other constitutional grounds are not textually erased.

This is not ordinary gubernatorial discretion and should not be generalised to all Nagaland administration.

### 4. Clause 1(c): fiscal earmarking

When recommending a demand for grant, the Governor must ensure that Union money provided from the Consolidated Fund of India for a specified service or purpose appears in the demand relating to that service or purpose, not another demand.

This is a traceability rule. It prevents special-purpose support from disappearing into an unrelated budget head.

### 5. Clause 1(d): Tuensang Regional Council

The constitutional text provides for a **35-member Regional Council** for Tuensang from a date specified by gubernatorial public notification. The Deputy Commissioner is ex officio Chairperson; members elect the Vice-Chairperson. The Governor makes rules in individual discretion concerning composition, selection, qualifications, tenure, allowances, procedure, staff and proper functioning.

This is a constitutionally described Regional Council, but it must not be confused with a Sixth Schedule Regional Council.

### 6. Clause (2): the transitional Tuensang settlement

For ten years from Nagaland's formation, or a further period specified by the Governor on the Regional Council's recommendation, clause (2) provided:

- **(a)** administration of Tuensang by the Governor;
- **(b)** discretionary equitable allocation of Union money between Tuensang and the rest of Nagaland;
- **(c)** State laws applying only after gubernatorial notification on the Council's recommendation, with possible exceptions, modifications and retrospective effect;
- **(d)** gubernatorial regulations for peace, progress and good government, capable of retrospectively repealing or amending a parliamentary Act or other applicable law;
- **(e)(i)** appointment of a Minister for Tuensang Affairs from among the Assembly members representing Tuensang: the Governor appoints on the Chief Minister's advice, and the Chief Minister must base that advice on the recommendation of the majority of those Tuensang members;
- **(e)(ii)** that Minister deals with all Tuensang matters, has direct access to the Governor and must keep the Chief Minister informed;
- **(f)** the Governor makes the final decision on all Tuensang matters in individual discretion;
- **(g)** for Articles 54, 55 and 80(4), references to elected State-Assembly members include Nagaland Assembly members elected by the Regional Council;
- **(h)(i)** Article 170(1)'s Assembly minimum was adapted from 60 to 46 for Nagaland;
- **(h)(ii)** Article 170(1)'s direct election from territorial constituencies included election by Regional Council members; and
- **(h)(iii)** Article 170(2)-(3) references to territorial constituencies meant constituencies in Kohima and Mokokchung districts.

A contemporaneous Removal of Difficulties Order also allowed an interim Tuensang Affairs Minister to be appointed on the Chief Minister's advice until Tuensang's allocated Assembly seats were filled according to law.

The clause was explicitly transitional, but any extension depended on a public notification.

### 7. Operability uncertainty: text is not proof of present activation

The constitutional clause remains printed. However, the initial ten-year period expired long ago, and present continuation would require the specified extension route. No accessible official Gazette notification conclusively proving a current extension or an operating original Regional Council was located in this verification.

Therefore use this disciplined formulation:

> **The Tuensang clauses remain part of the constitutional text; their present operational status should not be asserted without the relevant public notification.**

Do not claim either "fully operational" or "constitutionally repealed." Current eastern-Nagaland political proposals are not automatically the same institution.

### 8. Clause (3): spent difficulty power

The President could remove implementation difficulties, including adapting another Article, but no such order could be made after three years from Nagaland's formation. The power is spent. The Article's Explanation ties Kohima, Mokokchung and Tuensang districts to their meanings in the State of Nagaland Act, 1962.

### 9. Strongest objection and reply

**Objection:** The Assembly-consent shield creates a State veto over Parliament and fragments national law.

**Reply:** It covers four historically central identity fields and was part of Nagaland's constitutional settlement. Parliament remains competent elsewhere, and the Assembly may resolve to accept a law.

**Qualified residual:** Hard cases arise where land/resources, environmental regulation, taxation and national policy overlap. The protected-field inquiry must be precise rather than politically assumed.

### UPSC application

- Write "land and its resources."
- Separate the shield, law-and-order, earmarking and Tuensang layers.
- State consultation before individual judgment.
- Preserve uncertainty about current Tuensang operation.

### Revision notes

1. Article 371A came through the Thirteenth Amendment.
2. Clause 1(a) contains four protected fields.
3. Parliamentary law does not apply unless the Assembly resolves.
4. The shield is not a veto over every Union law.
5. The land clause includes resources.

7. The Governor's law-and-order role is condition-bound.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why should Article 371A not be described simply as a customary-law veto?

**Model answer:** Its Assembly-consent shield covers four specified fields, but the Article also creates a conditional gubernatorial law-and-order responsibility with a narrowly worded individual-judgment protection, protects earmarked Union grants and contains detailed Tuensang machinery. That machinery includes a Regional Council, a structured Tuensang Affairs Minister appointment, gubernatorial administration and final decision, and adaptations of Articles 54, 55, 80(4) and 170.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not infer a blanket Nagaland veto over all parliamentary legislation or assert present Tuensang operation from constitutional text alone.

### Original Mains practice - 15 marks, 250 words

**Mains question:** Analyse Article 371A as a constitutional accommodation of identity, resources and transitional governance.

**Mains model answer:** Article 371A accommodates Nagaland through four linked but distinct mechanisms.

First, a parliamentary Act concerning Naga religious or social practices, customary law and procedure, customary civil or criminal justice, or ownership and transfer of land and its resources does not apply unless the Nagaland Assembly resolves otherwise. This is a subject-specific consent shield, not a general denial of parliamentary competence.

Second, the Governor holds a conditional law-and-order responsibility. After ministerial consultation, the Governor may use individual judgment; the proviso protects an act only against challenge that such judgment ought or ought not to have been used, while the President may terminate the responsibility. Third, purpose-specific Union grants must enter the corresponding demand.

Fourth, the Article created detailed Tuensang machinery: a 35-member Regional Council; gubernatorial administration, finance, adapted laws and regulations; a Minister appointed by the Governor on Chief Ministerial advice founded on the majority recommendation of Tuensang MLAs; final gubernatorial decision; and adaptations of Articles 54, 55, 80(4) and 170. The initial ten-year period was extendable by the specified notification route.

The settlement's strength is that it protects identity and land while retaining democratic consent through the Assembly. Its limit is interpretive overlap between protected resources and national regulation. Further, the Tuensang clauses remain in text, but present operation should not be claimed without an official extension notification. Article 371A is thus differentiated integration, not unbounded autonomy.

### Visual first: two committees, different supervisory intensity

```text
ASSAM - ARTICLE 371B
President's order
      |
Assembly committee
tribal-area MLAs + specified other MLAs
      |
functions + procedural modifications

MANIPUR - ARTICLE 371C
President's order
      |
Hill Areas Committee
      |
rules of government business + Assembly procedure
      |
Governor special responsibility
      |
annual/on-demand report to President
      |
Union directions to State
```

Manipur adds an executive-supervisory chain that Article 371B does not reproduce.

### 1. Article 371B: Assam's committee model

The President may by order provide for:

- constitution and functions of a committee of the Assam Legislative Assembly;
- members elected from the tribal areas specified in Part I of the table appended to paragraph 20 of the Sixth Schedule;
- such number of other Assembly members as the order specifies; and
- modifications to Assembly procedure necessary for the committee's constitution and proper functioning.

The committee remains part of the Assam Assembly. Article 371B does not itself create legislative, judicial or taxing powers comparable to a Sixth Schedule Autonomous District Council.

### 2. Why the Sixth Schedule reference does not make it a Sixth Schedule body

The Constitution uses the Sixth Schedule table to identify territorial representation. That cross-reference does not import the entire council architecture.

| Article 371B committee | Sixth Schedule council |
|---|---|
| committee of State Assembly | territorial self-government body |
| composed of MLAs | ordinarily elected/nominated council members under Schedule design |
| functions set by Presidential order | constitutionally enumerated legislative, judicial and fiscal powers |
| works within Assembly procedure | operates as district/regional institution |

### 3. Article 371C: Manipur's Hill Areas Committee

The President may by order provide for:

1. constitution and functions of a committee of the Manipur Assembly;
2. membership consisting of MLAs elected from the Hill Areas;
3. modifications to State-government business rules;
4. modifications to Assembly procedure; and
5. a special responsibility of the Governor to secure proper committee functioning.

"Hill Areas" means areas declared as such by Presidential order. It is a legal designation, not any area colloquially described as hilly.

### 4. Reporting and Union directions

Article 371C(2) requires the Governor:

- to report annually to the President on administration of the Hill Areas; and
- to report whenever the President requires.

The Union's executive power extends to giving directions to the State regarding administration of those areas.

This creates a three-level accountability chain:

```text
HILL-AREA REPRESENTATIVES
        |
Hill Areas Committee
        |
Governor report / special responsibility
        |
President and Union directions
```

### 5. Comparison with Fifth Schedule reporting

Both Article 371C and the Fifth Schedule use gubernatorial reporting and Union supervision, but the legal sources and institutions differ. Article 371C centres an Assembly committee for President-declared Hill Areas in Manipur. The Fifth Schedule centres Scheduled Areas, a Tribes Advisory Council and separate gubernatorial powers. Detailed Fifth Schedule doctrine belongs elsewhere.

### 6. Strongest objection and reply

**Objection:** A committee cannot protect hill interests if the majority Assembly and executive retain final authority.

**Reply:** Committee representation, modified procedure, gubernatorial responsibility, reporting and Union directions collectively raise the political and constitutional cost of ignoring hill-area concerns.

**Qualified residual:** Effectiveness depends on which matters reach the committee, procedural compliance, information access and whether reports produce corrective action.

**Model answer:** Both place territorial representatives in Assembly committees, but Article 371C adds modification of government-business rules, a gubernatorial special responsibility, mandatory reporting to the President and Union directions concerning Hill Areas administration. Article 371B is the narrower legislative-committee model.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not infer autonomous-council powers merely because Article 371B identifies areas through a Sixth Schedule table.

**Mains question:** Distinguish the special committee arrangements under Articles 371B and 371C.

**Mains model answer:** Articles 371B and 371C protect territorial interests through committees inside State legislatures, but their supervisory intensity differs.

The Governor must report annually, or when required, to the President; Union executive power extends to directions on Hill Areas administration.

Thus Assam receives representational voice within the Assembly, while Manipur receives representation plus gubernatorial and Union oversight. Neither arrangement should be confused with Fifth or Sixth Schedule institutions.

## Lesson 7 - Articles 371D–371E—Andhra Pradesh and Telangana: Local Cadres, Education and Tribunal Status

Progress: 7/11 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Equitable opportunity, local areas and tribunal clauses

### PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST

Book context: Article 371D/E, the 2014 adaptation and the distinction between enabling tribunal text and current institutional status are available.
CA Search: "official Article 371D Andhra Pradesh Telangana local cadres tribunal abolition G.S.R. 30(E)"

### Visual first: from State-wide inequality to local opportunity

```text
DIFFERENT PARTS OF A STATE
unequal access to jobs and education
              |
              v
PRESIDENTIAL ORDER UNDER 371D(1)
              |
   +----------+-----------+
   |                      |
local cadres          local areas
   |                      |
allocation of staff   recruitment + admission
   +----------+-----------+
              |
preference/reservation for candidates
with specified residence or study link
```

The objective is equitable opportunity within each State, not a general constitutional power for every State to impose domicile reservations.

### 1. Clause (1): the purpose and post-2014 scope

The President may make separate orders for Andhra Pradesh or Telangana, having regard to each State's requirements, to provide equitable opportunities and facilities for people belonging to different parts of that State in:

- public employment; and
- education.

Different provisions may be made for different parts. Section 97 of the Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2014 adapted Article 371D to cover Telangana as well as Andhra Pradesh.

### 2. Clause (2): the three operating tools

An order may:

1. require organisation of classes of State civil-service posts or civil posts into different **local cadres** and allocate existing personnel;
2. specify **local areas** for direct recruitment to State or local-authority cadres and for admission to State-controlled universities or educational institutions; and
3. specify the extent, manner and conditions of preference or reservation for candidates who have resided or studied for the specified period in the local area.

Residence or study conditions come from the Presidential order. They should not be improvised from memory.

### 3. Why this is constitutionally exceptional

Article 16 ordinarily restricts residence-based discrimination in public employment, while Parliament has a role under Article 16(3). Article 371D is a specific constitutional arrangement with a non obstante effect through clause (10). It is not a model that State governments can copy without constitutional or statutory authority.

### 4. Clauses (3)-(4): possible Administrative Tribunals

The President may constitute Administrative Tribunals for Andhra Pradesh and Telangana concerning specified appointment, allotment, promotion, seniority and service-condition matters. An order may define:

- representations and remedial orders;
- powers, procedure and contempt;
- transfer of pending proceedings other than Supreme Court proceedings; and
- supplemental matters such as limitation and evidence.

This is an enabling constitutional power. It does not prove that a tribunal presently exists.

### 5. Clause (5), *P. Sambamurthy* and rule of law

Clause (5) made tribunal orders effective after State confirmation or three months and allowed the State Government to modify or annul them. In *P. Sambamurthy v. State of Andhra Pradesh* (1987), the Supreme Court declared clause (5), including its proviso, unconstitutional because executive power to override judicial determinations damaged rule of law and the basic structure.

Clause (6), which required special executive orders under the proviso to be laid before the legislature, cannot rescue the invalid override.

### 6. Clauses (7)-(8): jurisdiction text and abolition

Clause (7) text excludes High Court superintendence and other jurisdiction over tribunal matters except the Supreme Court. Later constitutional administrative-tribunal jurisprudence, especially judicial-review doctrine, must be read alongside such exclusion language.

Clause (8) allows the President to abolish a tribunal, provide for transfer and disposal of pending cases, and make consequential or incidental provisions. The printed enabling clauses do not prove that a tribunal currently operates. The current status of the former Andhra Pradesh Administrative Tribunal is left unresolved rather than inferred from an unverified notification citation.

### 7. Clauses (9)-(10): saving and overriding effect

Clause (9) protects specified historic appointments, postings, promotions, transfers and acts from invalidity merely because older residence requirements were not followed.

Clause (10) gives Article 371D and Presidential orders under it effect notwithstanding anything in the Constitution or other law. This explains the provision's exceptional force.

### 8. Article 371E

Article 371E says Parliament **may by law** establish a University in Andhra Pradesh. It is an enabling provision, not self-executing proof that a particular university was created directly by the Constitution. It now textually concerns Andhra Pradesh, not Telangana.

### 9. Strongest objection and reply

**Objection:** Local cadres and preferences entrench regionalism and compromise merit.

**Reply:** The provision responds to unequal access within a linguistically organised State and uses defined local areas, specified periods and Presidential orders rather than an unrestricted local preference.

**Qualified residual:** Excessive localisation can reduce administrative mobility and generate boundary disputes. Orders must be transparent, updated lawfully and tested against the Article's equity purpose.

- Mention the 2014 Telangana adaptation.
- Do not list the former tribunal as current.
- Use *P. Sambamurthy* for basic-structure control.
- Describe Article 371E as enabling.

### Concept check

The arrangement can democratise access for neglected regions. Its costs are fragmented cadres, reduced mobility and disputes over local status. Legitimacy therefore depends on transparent local-area definitions, proportionate preferences and periodic review against the constitutional objective of equitable—not exclusive—opportunity.

## Lesson 8 - Article 371F—Sikkim’s Complete Constitutional Integration Settlement

Progress: 8/11 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Article 371F clauses (a)-(p)

### PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST

CA Search: "official Article 371F Sikkim current constitutional provision 2026"
CA Found: No amendment displacing Article 371F's integration and representation framework was verified.

### Visual first: one Article, a complete constitutional transfer

```text
PRE-INTEGRATION SIKKIM
         |
36th Amendment, 1975
         |
         v
ARTICLE 371F SETTLEMENT
|
+-- Assembly continuity and strength          (a)-(c)
+-- Lok Sabha representation                  (d)-(e)
+-- protected-section representation          (f)
+-- Governor: peace/equitable advancement     (g)
+-- property, High Court, courts, officers    (h)-(j)
+-- existing laws + adaptation                (k)-(l)
+-- treaty-dispute jurisdiction limit         (m)
+-- extension of enactments                   (n)
+-- difficulty removal + validation           (o)-(p)
```

Article 371F does not protect one isolated subject. It translates a prior political and legal order into the Indian constitutional system.

### 1. Clauses (a)-(c): Assembly formation and continuity

Clause (a) fixes a minimum Sikkim Assembly strength of 30. Clause (b) deemed the 32-member Assembly elected in April 1974 to be Sikkim's duly constituted State Assembly, treated its sitting members as constitutionally elected members and vested it with ordinary State-Assembly powers.

Clause (c) adapted the term of that deemed Assembly. This was a one-time bridge, not the current general duration rule.

### 2. Clauses (d)-(e): parliamentary representation

Until Parliament otherwise provided:

- Sikkim received one Lok Sabha seat and formed one parliamentary constituency; and
- the existing representative was to be elected by the Sikkim Assembly.

These clauses prevented a representational vacuum during integration. Later ordinary electoral law replaced the transitional method.

### 3. Clause (f): protection of different sections

Parliament may protect the rights and interests of different sections of Sikkim's population by providing:

- the number of Assembly seats fillable by candidates belonging to those sections; and
- constituencies from which only such candidates may stand.

This is a specific constitutional foundation for Sikkim's differentiated representational design. Detailed litigation or Part XVI doctrine is not imported here.

### 4. Clause (g): Governor's special responsibility

The Governor has a special responsibility:

- for peace; and
- for an equitable arrangement ensuring social and economic advancement of different sections.

The Governor acts in discretion, subject to directions the President may issue. This is wider than a ceremonial role but tied to identified integration purposes.

### 5. Clauses (h)-(j): institutional succession

The Article transferred or continued:

- pre-existing Sikkim government property and assets to the State Government;
- the existing High Court as the High Court for the State; and
- civil, criminal and revenue courts, authorities and judicial, executive and ministerial officers, subject to the Constitution.

Without these clauses, integration could have produced a break in title, adjudication and administration.

### 6. Clauses (k)-(l): legal continuity and adaptation

Existing Sikkim laws continued until amended or repealed by a competent authority. For two years, the President could adapt or modify those laws to facilitate constitutional application and conformity. Such adaptations were protected from court challenge by the clause.

This illustrates a general transition principle:

```text
CONTINUE FIRST
      |
ADAPT TO NEW CONSTITUTIONAL ORDER
      |
ORDINARY LEGISLATIVE CHANGE LATER
```

### 7. Clauses (m)-(n): old instruments and legal extension

Clause (m) excludes court jurisdiction over disputes arising from pre-appointed-day treaties, agreements, engagements or similar instruments involving India or predecessor governments, while preserving Article 143.

Clause (n) allows the President by notification to extend to Sikkim, with restrictions or modifications, an enactment then in force in another Indian State.

### 8. Clauses (o)-(p): difficulty removal and validation

For two years, the President could remove difficulties, even adapting another Article. Clause (p) validated actions taken during the short interval between the appointed day and presidential assent to the Thirty-sixth Amendment, insofar as they conformed to the amended Constitution.

Both are classic transition-clearing clauses; neither is a continuing general power to rewrite Sikkim's constitutional position.

### 9. Strongest objection and reply

**Objection:** Broad gubernatorial discretion and section-specific seats conflict with ordinary democratic equality.

**Reply:** Article 371F was an integration settlement designed to protect peace and different sections during a fundamental constitutional transition. The differentiation rests on express constitutional text, not executive grace.

**Qualified residual:** Integration-era safeguards must still be interpreted consistently with constitutional democracy and their protected purpose; history does not eliminate the need for reasoned contemporary application.

- Use the clause clusters rather than attempting an unstructured list.

- Mention law, court, property and officer continuity.
- Do not reduce the Article to "Assembly not less than 30."

## Lesson 9 - Articles 371G–371I—Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh and Goa: Consent, Law and Order, Assembly Floors

Progress: 9/11 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Three sharply different special provisions

### PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST

Book context: Articles 371G, 371H and 371-I are available for comparison.
CA Search: "official Articles 371G 371H 371I current status 2026"

### Visual first: same Part, different constitutional intensity

```text
ARTICLE 371G - MIZORAM
Assembly-consent shield + Assembly minimum 40

ARTICLE 371H - ARUNACHAL PRADESH
Governor law-and-order responsibility + Assembly minimum 30

ARTICLE 371-I - GOA
Assembly minimum 30 only

INTENSITY:
consent over law  >  bounded executive responsibility  >  structural floor
```

Alphabetical proximity does not imply similar powers.

### 1. Article 371G: four fields and one proviso

No Act of Parliament concerning the following applies to Mizoram unless its Legislative Assembly resolves:

1. religious or social practices of the Mizos;
2. Mizo customary law and procedure;
3. administration of civil and criminal justice involving decisions according to Mizo customary law; and
4. ownership and transfer of land.

However, the shield does not apply to a Central Act already in force in the Union Territory of Mizoram immediately before commencement of the Fifty-third Amendment. The Article also requires a Legislative Assembly of not less than 40 members.

### 2. Article 371A versus 371G

| Dimension | Nagaland - 371A | Mizoram - 371G |
|---|---|---|
| practices | Naga religious/social | Mizo religious/social |
| custom/procedure | protected | protected |
| customary justice | protected | protected |
| land wording | land **and its resources** | land |
| pre-existing Central Acts proviso | no parallel text in clause 1(a) | express saving |
| additional machinery | Governor, grants, Tuensang | Assembly minimum 40 |

The Articles are parallel, not identical.

### 3. Article 371H: bounded individual judgment

The Governor of Arunachal Pradesh has a special responsibility for law and order. In discharging it, the Governor:

- consults the Council of Ministers;
- exercises individual judgment on action;
- decides finally, in discretion, whether a matter is one requiring individual judgment; and
- ceases to have the responsibility if the President, on a report or otherwise, directs that it is no longer necessary.

The first proviso is a narrow judicial-protection rule: an act cannot be challenged **on the ground that the Governor ought or ought not to have exercised individual judgment**. It does not textually immunise the act from every other constitutional challenge.

The Assembly must have not fewer than 30 members.

Unlike Article 371A, Article 371H does not contain an Assembly-consent shield over custom or land.

### 4. Article 371-I: a minimum and nothing more

Article 371-I provides only that Goa's Legislative Assembly shall consist of not fewer than 30 members. It creates no consent shield, development board, Governor's individual-judgment field or special local reservation.

This modest clause is a classic Prelims trap because learners often assume every Article 371 provision grants extensive autonomy.

### 5. Strongest objection and reply

**Objection:** Governor's individual judgment under Article 371H weakens responsible government.

**Reply:** The field is confined to law and order, consultation is mandatory, the judicial-protection proviso is ground-specific, and the President may terminate the responsibility. It is a bounded exception tied to the State's constitutional transition.

**Qualified residual:** Even bounded discretion can generate accountability concerns. Its use must remain connected to the precise constitutional purpose rather than ordinary political disagreement.

### UPSC application

- Include the 371G pre-existing-Central-Act proviso.
- Distinguish land from land and resources.
- State consultation under 371H.
- Mention Presidential termination.
- Treat 371-I as only the Goa Assembly minimum.

### Revision notes

1. Article 371G came through the Fifty-third Amendment.
2. It protects four specified Mizo fields.
3. Assembly resolution activates a protected parliamentary law.
4. The land clause omits "resources."
5. Pre-existing Central Acts in the former UT receive an express saving.
6. Mizoram's Assembly minimum is 40.
7. Article 371H came through the Fifty-fifth Amendment.
8. Arunachal's Governor has special law-and-order responsibility.

14. Article 371-I came through the Fifty-sixth Amendment.

16. Goa receives no additional mechanism under 371-I.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why is it inaccurate to describe Articles 371G, 371H and 371-I as three versions of special autonomy?

**Model answer:** Article 371G conditions application of laws in four cultural/customary fields; Article 371H creates a terminable gubernatorial law-and-order responsibility; Article 371-I only fixes Goa's Assembly minimum. They differ in subject, actor, mechanism and constitutional intensity.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not infer a consent shield or gubernatorial special power for Goa from its placement in the Article 371 sequence.

### Original Mains practice - 10 marks, 150 words

**Mains question:** Compare the constitutional techniques used in Articles 371G, 371H and 371-I.

**Mains model answer:** Articles 371G, 371H and 371-I use three different techniques.

Article 371G protects four fields—Mizo practices, customary law, customary justice and land—by requiring an Assembly resolution before a parliamentary law applies. It saves Central Acts already operating before the Fifty-third Amendment and fixes a 40-member Assembly minimum.

Thus the provisions respectively use legislative consent, bounded executive responsibility and a representation floor. Their common location does not create a common degree of autonomy.

The President may by order provide for the Governor's special responsibility concerning:

1. a separate development board, with an annual report placed before the Karnataka Legislative Assembly;
2. equitable allocation of developmental funds over the region, subject to the State's requirements as a whole; and
3. equitable opportunities and facilities in public employment, education and vocational training, also subject to State-wide requirements.

Like Article 371, it embeds legislative reporting and a State-wide qualification.

### 3. Clause (2)(a): seats

An order may reserve a proportion of seats in educational and vocational-training institutions **in the region** for students belonging to the region by birth or domicile.

The Constitution does not itself print a universal percentage. Percentages, eligibility periods and certificate rules come from the operative order and State implementation and must be verified before quotation.

### 4. Clause (2)(b): posts

An order may:

- identify posts or classes of posts under the State Government and State-controlled bodies or organisations **in the region**; and
- reserve a proportion for persons belonging to the region by birth or domicile;
- for direct recruitment, promotion or another specified appointment route.

The promotion reference is particularly examinable; the clause is not limited to entry-level recruitment.

### 5. Why Article 371J is stronger than a planning promise

Ordinary regional policy can be changed administratively. Article 371J places the framework in the Constitution and requires action through a Presidential order. Its components support one another:

| Problem | Constitutional response |
|---|---|
| weak project focus | development board |
| opaque implementation | annual Assembly report |
| territorial under-spending | equitable allocation |
| education and skill gap | local seats/facilities |
| employment exclusion | identified and reserved posts |

### 6. Limits and implementation discipline

Article 371J does not:

- create a separate legislature;
- give the region law-making autonomy;
- provide an Assembly-consent shield over parliamentary law; or
- remove the repeated State-wide-needs qualification.

Official-board existence proves institutional implementation, but not that every development outcome or benefit-distribution claim is successful. Avoid unverified expenditure totals or reservation percentages.

### 7. Strongest objection and reply

**Objection:** Birth/domicile reservations fragment State citizenship and reduce merit.

**Reply:** The Constitution authorises proportionate, region-linked correction within a historically disadvantaged area and joins it to development expenditure and education rather than using reservation alone.

**Qualified residual:** Local preference must remain accurately targeted. Weak certificate administration, exclusion within the region or poor outcome measurement can convert corrective design into rent-seeking.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not organise the topic only as Article-State pairs; that memorises labels while hiding legal consequences.

### Original Mains practice - 20 marks, 250 words

**Mains question:** "India's special provisions are instruments of differentiated integration, but their legitimacy depends on constitutional accountability." Critically examine.

**Mains model answer:** India's State-specific provisions accommodate diversity through differentiated integration rather than external sovereignty. Their legitimacy rests on matching a constitutional mechanism to a defined problem.

Articles 371A and 371G protect custom, customary justice and land through State-Assembly consent. Articles 371B and 371C create territorial voice within the Assam and Manipur Assemblies, with Manipur adding gubernatorial reporting and Union directions. Article 371D uses local cadres, areas and preferences to distribute employment and educational opportunity. Article 371F preserved representation, laws, courts and administration during Sikkim's integration. Articles 371 and 371J connect boards, legislative reporting and equitable funds to regional development, with 371J adding local seats and posts.

These devices can strengthen unity by making national membership compatible with identity and regional fairness. Yet they create tensions: gubernatorial discretion may weaken responsible government; local preferences may generate insider-outsider conflict; consent shields may clash with regulatory uniformity; and transition clauses may outlive clear evidence of operation.

Accountability therefore requires precise subject boundaries, transparent Presidential orders, Assembly scrutiny, accessible reports, judicial review and evidence before claiming that an enabled institution currently functions. The Constitution's achievement is not asymmetry alone, but bounded asymmetry: difference justified by purpose, exercised by named actors and limited by common constitutional supremacy.

## Final 10-marker

**Question:** Distinguish a constitutional consent shield from a development-board mechanism in Part XXI. Answer in 150 words.

**Model answer:** A constitutional consent shield controls whether a parliamentary law applies in specified subject fields. Under Article 371A, laws concerning Naga practices, custom, customary justice and land/resources do not apply unless the Nagaland Assembly resolves. Article 371G creates a parallel but textually distinct shield for Mizoram.

A development-board mechanism does not condition parliamentary law. Article 371 permits a Presidential order assigning the Governor responsibility for regional boards, annual Assembly reports, equitable expenditure and opportunities in Maharashtra and Gujarat. Article 371J applies a stronger version to Kalyana Karnataka, adding possible reservation of regional educational seats and identified posts.

The first model protects identity through democratic consent; the second corrects territorial inequality through institutions, finance and opportunity. Neither creates sovereignty or an autonomous legislature. The distinction demonstrates why Articles 371-371J must be classified by legal mechanism rather than loosely described as "special status."

## Final 15-marker

**Question:** Compare Articles 371A and 371F as two different constitutional strategies of integration. Answer in 250 words.

**Model answer:** Articles 371A and 371F both deepen integration through differentiation, but they respond to different constitutional problems.

Article 371A protects an existing social and legal order. Parliamentary Acts concerning Naga practices, custom, customary justice, and land/resources do not apply unless the Assembly resolves. Additional clauses create a conditional law-and-order responsibility with narrow individual-judgment protection, earmarked grants and detailed Tuensang machinery. The Tuensang Affairs Minister followed a majority recommendation, Chief Ministerial advice and gubernatorial appointment; electoral adaptations reached Articles 54, 55, 80(4) and 170. Its central technique is preservation through consent and structured transition.

Article 371F, by contrast, translated Sikkim's prior political order into Indian Statehood. It continued the 1974 Assembly, created parliamentary representation, authorised protection of population sections, assigned the Governor responsibility for peace and equitable advancement, transferred assets, continued courts and officers, preserved and adapted laws, addressed prior treaty disputes and validated transitional action. Its central technique is institutional and legal succession.

Both provisions reject forced uniformity. Yet their limits differ. Article 371A is subject-specific and does not veto all Union law; current Tuensang operation requires evidence. Article 371F's time-bound adaptation and difficulty powers cannot be treated as permanent executive authority.

Thus 371A integrates by protecting identity and local consent, while 371F integrates by ensuring continuity during constitutional incorporation.

## Final 20-marker

**Question:** Evaluate whether India's State-specific special provisions strengthen federal unity or institutionalise unequal citizenship. Answer in 250 words.

**Model answer:** India's State-specific provisions differentiate territorial governance, but they do not ordinarily create unequal national citizenship. Their constitutional justification is that materially different histories and inequalities sometimes require different institutions.

The evidence is mechanism-specific. Articles 371A and 371G preserve customary practices, justice and land through Assembly consent. Articles 371B and 371C give tribal or hill representatives an institutional voice, with Manipur adding reporting and Union directions. Article 371D corrects territorial inequality in jobs and education through local cadres and areas. Article 371F ensured Sikkim's representative, legal and administrative continuity. Articles 371 and 371J constitutionalise regional development, legislative reporting, equitable funds and opportunity.

These arrangements can strengthen unity by reducing the perceived conflict between identity and national membership. However, risks are real. Governor discretion may strain responsible government; local preferences may create insider-outsider divisions; development institutions may suffer elite capture; and unclear continuation of transitional machinery undermines legal certainty.

The answer is therefore conditional. Asymmetry does not itself violate equality; arbitrary or unaccountable asymmetry does. Legitimacy requires a defined protected purpose, named actor, transparent order, Assembly scrutiny, accessible outcome data and judicial review. India's best constitutional principle is neither mechanical uniformity nor permanent exceptionalism, but accountable, purpose-specific difference under one sovereign Constitution.

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

## Core federal idea

- Asymmetric federalism means different constitutional arrangements for different territorial units within one Union.
- Its justification is problem-specific accommodation: identity, customary law, land, representation, development, opportunity or integration.
- It does not create external sovereignty, secession, immunity from judicial review or a blanket exemption from common citizenship.
- Use the test: **purpose -> actor -> mechanism -> check -> limit -> current status**.

## Part XXI functional map

- **Article 369:** five-year temporary parliamentary competence over specified State List matters; spent.
- **Article 370:** temporary constitutional-application gateway for J&K; former clauses rendered inoperative in 2019.
- **Articles 371-371J:** State-specific special provisions.
- **Articles 372/372A:** existing-law continuity and presidential adaptation, including Seventh Amendment adjustment.
- **Article 373:** temporary preventive-detention substitution for specified Article 22 references.
- **Article 374:** Federal Court judges/proceedings and Privy Council transitions into the Supreme Court framework.
- **Articles 375-378A:** courts/authorities/officers; High Court judges; CAG; Public Service Commissions; Andhra Pradesh Assembly duration.
- **Articles 379-391:** omitted.
- **Article 392:** initial difficulty-removal power, historically bounded.

## Article 370 before 2019

- Article 370(1) structured Union competence and constitutional application.
- Consultation applied to accession-corresponding fields; concurrence applied to wider extensions.
- Article 370(2) required relevant pre-Constituent-Assembly concurrence to be placed before it.
- Article 370(3) supplied the cessation/modification route.
- The 1954 Order became the main application instrument.
- Article 35A protected permanent-resident laws concerning employment, property, settlement, scholarships and State aid.
- Article 35A did not itself fully define permanent residents.

## 2019-2026 control

- **5 August 2019:** C.O. 272, under Article 370(1)(d), recited State-Government concurrence, superseded the 1954 Order, broadly applied the Constitution and inserted Article 367(4).
- Parliament separately acted in its State-legislature capacity for the purported clause (3) recommendation.
- **6 August 2019:** C.O. 273 rendered former Article 370 clauses inoperative.
- Article 35A ceased with supersession of its application-order foundation.
- **31 October 2019:** J&K became a UT with legislature; Ladakh a UT without legislature.
- **2023 judgment:** Article 370 temporary; continuing internal sovereignty rejected; applying the whole Constitution under Article 370(1)(d) valid without State concurrence in this setting; C.O. 273 valid; Article 367 substitution invalid but unnecessary to result.
- Ladakh's creation upheld; J&K's State-to-UT conversion not finally adjudicated.
- The Court directed election steps by 30 September 2024; ECI polling occurred on 18 and 25 September and 1 October, with counting on 8 October.
- On **1 October 2026**, J&K remained a UT; no Statehood-restoration enactment was located.

## Article 371 - Maharashtra and Gujarat

- President may assign Governor special responsibility.
- Regions: Vidarbha, Marathwada, rest of Maharashtra; Saurashtra, Kutch, rest of Gujarat.
- Four controls: boards, annual Assembly reports, equitable expenditure, technical/vocational/service opportunity.
- State-wide requirements qualify regional correction.

## Article 371A - Nagaland

- Four protected fields: Naga religious/social practices; custom/procedure; customary civil/criminal justice; land and resources.
- Parliamentary law needs Nagaland Assembly resolution to apply in these fields.
- Governor: conditional law-and-order responsibility after consultation, using individual judgment; the first proviso only bars challenge on the ground that such judgment ought or ought not to have been used; President may terminate.
- Specific Union money must enter the matching grant demand.
- Tuensang: 35-member Regional Council; gubernatorial administration, finance, adapted laws and regulations.
- Minister chain: majority recommendation of Tuensang MLAs -> Chief Minister's advice -> Governor's appointment; direct access to Governor while keeping Chief Minister informed.
- Governor's final Tuensang decision; Article 54, 55, 80(4) and 170 electoral/representation adaptations.
- Initial clause (2) period was ten years, extendable through the specified notification route.
- Present operation of the original Tuensang arrangement remains unproven without an accessible official extension notification.

## Articles 371B and 371C

- **371B Assam:** Presidential-order Assembly committee of specified tribal-area MLAs plus other specified MLAs; procedural modifications.
- **371C Manipur:** Hill Areas Committee; government-business and Assembly-procedure modifications; Governor special responsibility; annual/on-demand presidential report; Union directions.
- Neither committee is a Sixth Schedule autonomous council.

## Articles 371D and 371E

- 371D covers Andhra Pradesh and Telangana after the 2014 adaptation.
- Purpose: equitable public-employment and educational opportunity among different State parts.
- Tools: local cadres, local areas, residence/study-linked preferences or reservations.
- Tribunal clauses are enabling; clause (8) separately permits abolition and case transfer.
- *P. Sambamurthy* invalidated clause (5) and its executive override.
- The former AP Administrative Tribunal's current status is unresolved here because the reported G.S.R. 30(E) claim was not independently authenticated from an authoritative official text.
- Clauses (9)-(10): historic saving and overriding effect.
- 371E enables Parliament to establish a Central University in Andhra Pradesh.

## Article 371F - Sikkim

- Minimum Assembly 30; 1974 Assembly and members constitutionally continued.
- Transitional Lok Sabha seat/representation.
- Parliament may protect different population sections through seat design.
- Governor responsibility for peace and equitable advancement, subject to presidential directions.
- Assets, High Court, courts, authorities and officers continued.
- Existing laws continued; two-year adaptation power.
- Prior-instrument jurisdiction rule, extension of enactments, difficulty removal and validation.
- Best recall label: **complete integration settlement**.

## Articles 371G-371I

- **371G Mizoram:** four-field Assembly-consent shield; land but not express resources; pre-existing Central Acts saving; Assembly at least 40.
- **371H Arunachal Pradesh:** Governor law-and-order responsibility after consultation and individual judgment; ground-specific protection against challenge that individual judgment ought or ought not to have been used; presidential termination; Assembly at least 30.
- **371-I Goa:** Assembly at least 30 only.

## Article 371J - Karnataka

- Constitution says Hyderabad-Karnataka; current official name Kalyana Karnataka.
- S.O. 2413(E), dated 30 September 2013, commenced Article 371J on 1 October 2013.
- President may assign Governor responsibility for board, annual report, equitable funds and equitable employment/education/training.
- Order may reserve regional educational/training seats for persons belonging by birth or domicile.
- Order may identify and reserve regional State/state-controlled posts through direct recruitment, promotion or another specified method.
- Constitution prints no universal reservation percentage.
- No autonomous legislature or consent shield.

## High-yield comparison lines

- **371A vs 371G:** both consent shields; resources wording and saved-Central-Act proviso differ.
- **371B vs 371C:** Assam committee; Manipur committee plus Governor report and Union directions.
- **371 vs 371J:** both development architectures; 371J expressly adds local seats and posts.
- **371D vs 371J:** local cadres/areas across a State versus a named-region board plus seats/posts.
- **371F vs all others:** full integration continuity rather than one protected field.
- **371H vs 371-I:** bounded gubernatorial power versus Assembly floor only.

## Federal evaluation

- Gain: accommodation can convert diversity from a centrifugal pressure into constitutional participation.
- Gain: special mechanisms can correct internal territorial inequality ignored by ordinary majoritarian politics.
- Risk: Governor discretion may weaken responsible government.
- Risk: local preference can produce insider-outsider conflict.
- Risk: boards and committees may become symbolic without transparent reports and outcomes.
- Rule: never infer present operation merely from surviving enabling text.
- Verdict: support **accountable, purpose-specific asymmetry**, not mechanical uniformity or unreviewable exceptionalism.
