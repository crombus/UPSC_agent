# Rights and Liabilities of the Government — Complete Live Session

Articles 294-300 answer a deceptively simple question: when government owns property, makes a promise, causes harm or enters court, which legal rules apply? The constitutional answer rejects both extremes. The State is not an ordinary private trader, because public power and public money require special controls. Yet it is not a legally untouchable sovereign either.

The visual separates an office-holder from the juristic State. A change of government does not extinguish public property, debt or litigation.

### 1. The intuition: government must outlive governments

Suppose a ministry buys land, borrows money and enters a ten-year supply arrangement. An election then replaces the ministry. If legal obligations belonged personally to outgoing ministers, administration would collapse after every political transition. The Constitution therefore treats the Union and each State as continuing legal actors.

✅ **Fact:** The Union and the States can hold property, enter authorised contracts, incur obligations, sue and be sued. Their legal continuity is distinct from the tenure of a President, Governor, minister or civil servant.

⚠️ **Inference:** Juristic personality is an accountability device as much as an administrative convenience. It gives a claimant a continuing public defendant while preventing public assets from being confused with an office-holder's private estate.

### 2. Article 294: succession at constitutional commencement

Article 294 transferred to the Union or the corresponding State the property, assets, rights, liabilities and obligations that immediately before the Constitution belonged to the Dominion of India or a Governor's Province, subject to constitutional allocation.

```text
PRE-CONSTITUTION HOLDER
Dominion / Governor's Province
             |
             v
CONSTITUTIONAL SUCCESSOR
Union / corresponding State
             |
             v
property + assets + rights + liabilities + obligations
```

✅ **Fact:** Article 294 is a continuity provision. It prevents a legal vacuum at commencement.

### 3. Article 295: former Indian States

Article 295 performs a related transition for property, assets, rights, liabilities and obligations connected with former Indian States. Allocation turns on the governmental purpose involved and the constitutional distribution of functions, subject to agreement.

The important distinction is not merely historical:

| Article | Transitional source | Allocation idea |
|---:|---|---|
| 294 | Dominion and Governor's Provinces | Union or corresponding State continuity |
| 295 | Former Indian States | Purpose and Union-State constitutional field, subject to agreement |

### 4. What legal personality does not mean

Legal capacity is bounded:

- public money still requires legislative appropriation;
- executive action must remain within constitutional and statutory authority;
- property disposal and procurement remain subject to equality and non-arbitrariness;
- audit may examine legality, regularity and performance within its mandate;
- courts may review public-law illegality even when the State acts through a contract.

**Example:** A State owns a warehouse. That ownership does not permit a department to sell it through favouritism. The transaction remains public action, even though sale of property resembles a private transaction.

**Limit of the example:** Not every defect in disposal automatically produces contractual damages. The claimant must identify the applicable public-law, contractual, restitutionary or statutory route.

### 5. Objection, reply and residual

**Objection:** Calling government a legal person may conceal the real official who acted unlawfully.

**Reply:** Institutional liability and personal responsibility can coexist. The State may answer for an official act while the officer separately faces disciplinary, criminal or personal civil consequences where the governing law and facts justify them.

**Residual:** Neither State liability nor personal liability follows automatically. Function, authority, course of employment, fault, causation, statutory protection and remedy must be tested separately.

### UPSC application

- **Prelims:** distinguish Union/State juristic identity from President/Governor personally.
- **Mains:** use continuity to introduce Articles 294-300 as a rule-of-law sequence.
- **Trap:** legal personality does not mean unlimited executive power.
- **Answer line:** "Articles 294-300 preserve an enduring public legal person while subjecting its property, promises and wrongs to constitutional form and judicial remedy."

### Revision notes

1. The Union and every State possess continuing legal personality.
2. Political change does not extinguish public assets or obligations.
3. Article 294 concerns succession from the Dominion and Governor's Provinces.
4. Article 295 concerns succession connected with former Indian States.
5. Both provisions primarily secure constitutional continuity at commencement.
6. Public property belongs to the juristic State, not personally to constitutional heads.
7. Legal capacity remains limited by legislation, appropriation, equality and review.
8. Institutional liability does not automatically exclude an officer's personal responsibility.
9. The correct sequence is capacity, valid commitment, wrong, remedy and forum.
10. Juristic personality makes government governable and legally answerable.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why does a change of ministry not terminate the State's contractual obligations?

**Model answer:** The obligation belongs to the continuing Union or State as a juristic entity, not to the ministers personally. Articles 294-300 reflect this institutional continuity while requiring constitutional authority and form for governmental commitments.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not say the President or Governor personally owns public assets or personally becomes the contracting party.

### Lesson-local Mains practice

**Question (10 marks, 150 words):** Explain how governmental legal personality combines administrative continuity with constitutional accountability.

**Model answer (124 words):** Governmental legal personality means that the Union and each State continue as juristic entities despite changes in office-holders. Articles 294 and 295 secured succession to public property, rights, liabilities and obligations at constitutional commencement. This continuity allows government to own assets, make authorised contracts and participate in litigation without treating the President, Governor or minister as the personal owner or debtor. However, legal personality does not create unrestricted executive power. Public transactions remain controlled by legislative competence, appropriation, equality, statutory authority, audit and judicial review. Institutional liability may also coexist with personal disciplinary, criminal or civil responsibility where an official acts unlawfully. Thus, the doctrine protects stable administration while ensuring that public power has a continuing legal bearer against whom rights may be enforced.

**Unique quantified rubric (10):** continuity concept 2; Articles 294-295 2; juristic-person distinction 2; constitutional limits 2; qualified conclusion 2.

### Visual: three different property questions

| Question | Article | Decisive test |
|---|---:|---|
| Who receives ownerless property? | 296 | location, then existing governmental purpose/control |
| Who holds specified offshore value and resources? | 297 | constitutional vesting in the Union |
| May government trade, own, dispose and contract? | 298 | executive capacity subject to legislative controls |

These Articles should not be memorised as one generic "government property" rule. Each answers a different legal question.

### 1. Article 296: escheat, lapse and bona vacantia

Three expressions often appear together but are not exact synonyms:

| Term | Exam-safe meaning |
|---|---|
| **Escheat** | property returning to the State for want of a lawful heir |
| **Lapse** | accrual to the State when a legally recognised title or right fails or terminates |
| **Bona vacantia** | ownerless property |

✅ **Fact:** As a general rule, property situated in a State vests in that State; property in other cases vests in the Union. The proviso recognises the governmental purpose for which property was already possessed or controlled.

**Example:** If a person dies intestate without a lawful heir and the immovable property is situated within a State, Article 296's location rule points generally to that State.

**Limit:** Article 296 does not abolish succession law or permit government to label disputed private property "ownerless" without lawful determination.

### 2. Article 297: maritime value and resources

✅ **Fact:** Lands, minerals and things of value underlying the ocean within India's territorial waters, continental shelf and exclusive economic zone, along with other resources of the exclusive economic zone, vest in the Union.

```text
CONSTITUTIONAL VESTING: Article 297
             |
             +-> specified offshore lands
             +-> minerals and things of value
             +-> continental-shelf resources
             +-> EEZ resources

STATUTORY DETAIL
             |
             +-> maritime-zone limits and regulatory machinery
```

> **Trap:** A distance fixed by maritime legislation should not be presented as though the number appears in Article 297 itself.

### 3. Article 298: capacity to act economically

Article 298 extends Union and State executive power to:

- carrying on trade or business;
- acquiring property;
- holding property;
- disposing of property; and
- making contracts.

The provisos preserve legislative-competence controls where the activity lies outside the government's normal legislative field.

✅ **Fact:** Article 298 gives capacity; Article 299 controls the form of executive contracts.

⚠️ **Inference:** The sequence prevents two opposite mistakes. Government is not barred from economic action merely because it is government; nor may it invoke Article 298 as a charter for arbitrary commercial conduct.

### 4. Public power follows the transaction

When a private buyer chooses a supplier, ordinary private law predominates. When government chooses one, it spends public money and distributes public opportunity. Equality and relevance of criteria therefore accompany the transaction.

```text
COMMERCIAL FORM
purchase / sale / lease / tender
                 +
PUBLIC CHARACTER
public money / equality / recorded authority
                 =
CONSTITUTIONAL ADMINISTRATION
```

**Objection:** Subjecting every commercial choice to public-law review will make government unable to bargain efficiently.

**Reply:** Judicial review does not ordinarily ask courts to become tender committees. It tests legality, authority, fairness, relevance of criteria and absence of arbitrariness.

**Residual:** Courts generally preserve administrative and commercial latitude. Equality does not mean the lowest bid must always win; quality, lifecycle cost and risk may be relevant if criteria are disclosed and applied fairly.

### UPSC application

- Link Article 296 to ownerless property, not compulsory acquisition.
- Link Article 297 to constitutional vesting, while keeping statutory maritime distances separate.
- Link Article 298 to capacity, not contract validity.
- Use the phrase **capacity before commitment**: Article 298 answers whether government may act; Article 299 answers how it binds itself.

### Revision notes

1. Article 296 covers escheat, lapse and bona vacantia.
2. Escheat, lapse and ownerlessness are related but distinct ideas.
3. Location is Article 296's general vesting rule.
4. Existing governmental possession or control for a purpose may affect vesting.
5. Article 297 vests specified maritime value and resources in the Union.
6. Maritime-zone distances arise from legislation, not the bare wording of Article 297.
7. Article 298 authorises governmental trade, business, property dealings and contracts.
8. Article 298 capacity remains subject to legislative-competence limits.
9. Article 298 does not validate an Article 299-defective contract.
10. Public economic action remains subject to equality, authority, appropriation and review.
11. Lowest price and constitutional fairness are not identical tests.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why can Article 298 not by itself prove that a supplier has an enforceable contract against government?

**Model answer:** Article 298 establishes executive capacity to trade, hold or dispose of property and make contracts. Enforceability of an executive government contract must still satisfy Article 299's expression, execution and authorisation requirements and any applicable ordinary law.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not merge the power to contract with the constitutional form required to create the contract.

### Lesson-local Mains practice

**Question (10 marks, 150 words):** Distinguish the constitutional functions performed by Articles 296, 297 and 298.

**Model answer (125 words):** Articles 296-298 address separate aspects of governmental property and economic power. Article 296 governs escheat, lapse and bona vacantia: ownerless property generally vests according to location, subject to the proviso concerning existing governmental possession or control for a purpose. Article 297 constitutionally vests specified offshore lands, minerals, things of value and exclusive-economic-zone resources in the Union; statutory law supplies maritime limits and regulation. Article 298, by contrast, grants executive capacity to the Union and States to carry on trade or business, acquire, hold and dispose of property, and make contracts, subject to legislative-competence controls. It does not dispense with equality, statutory authority or Article 299 formalities. Thus, Article 296 allocates ownerless property, Article 297 vests strategic maritime resources, and Article 298 enables regulated economic action.

**Unique quantified rubric (10):** Article 296 terms and rule 3; Article 297 distinction 2; Article 298 scope 3; capacity/form qualification 1; synthesis 1.

The three constitutional locks are necessary but not sufficient. They protect the public exchequer by establishing public authority and form; ordinary contract-law requirements must also be satisfied.

### 1. Why special form is necessary

Private parties can often bind themselves informally because they bear the financial consequence. A government officer spends public money. Article 299 therefore demands an identifiable authorised commitment.

✅ **Fact:** A contract made in exercise of the executive power of the Union or a State must:

1. be expressed to be made by the President or Governor;
2. be executed on behalf of the President or Governor; and
3. be executed by a person, and in a manner, directed or authorised for that purpose.

These requirements create:

```text
authority -> authentication -> audit trail -> protection of public funds
```

### 2. The constitutional head is not the bargaining individual

The President or Governor is the constitutional name in which executive action is expressed. The actual execution is performed by an authorised officer on behalf of that head.

✅ **Fact:** Article 299(2) protects the President or Governor from personal contractual liability. It also protects the person making or executing the contract on their behalf from personal liability in respect of that official execution.

> **Central distinction:** personal immunity of the constitutional head does not immunise the Union or State from a valid contractual obligation.

### 3. Mandatory safeguards and their consequence

The constitutional formalities are mandatory safeguards, but satisfying them does not by itself prove a valid contract. Ordinary requirements governing agreement, competence, lawful consideration and object, free consent and absence of vitiating illegality continue to apply. Their purpose would be defeated if informal assurance, acquiescence or later administrative convenience routinely cured non-compliance.

**Example:** A departmental engineer orally asks a contractor to build an extra structure and promises payment. The contractor must not jump directly from performance to "valid Article 299 contract." The first inquiry is whether the commitment was expressed, executed and authorised as constitutionally required.

**Limit:** Failure of the contractual route does not always end the legal inquiry. If government lawfully accepted and enjoyed a non-gratuitous benefit, a distinct restitutionary route may arise. That is Lesson 4.

### 4. Contract validity versus public-law fairness

Two questions can coexist:

| Question | Legal frame |
|---|---|
| Was a binding government contract created? | Article 299 and contract law |
| Was tender cancellation, blacklisting or exclusion arbitrary? | equality, natural justice and judicial review |

A claimant cannot use public-law fairness automatically to manufacture a contract that fails Article 299. Conversely, government cannot rely on contractual language to place arbitrary public action beyond review.

### 5. Strongest objection and reply

**Objection:** Strict form lets government escape fair payment after encouraging performance.

**Reply:** Article 299 protects public funds from unauthorised commitments. Fairness is addressed through independent restitution where its ingredients are proved, not by pretending that the defective bargain was constitutionally valid.

**Residual:** Strict form can still generate hardship, especially where internal authority is opaque. Better digital delegation records and pre-contract verification reduce that risk without weakening Article 299.

### UPSC application

- Start with Article 298 capacity, then Article 299 form.
- List all three formalities; two out of three is not a safe answer.
- After constitutional form, test ordinary contract-law validity; Article 299 compliance is necessary, not sufficient.
- Separate Union/State liability from signatory or constitutional-head personal liability.
- Separate contract enforcement from restitution and judicial review.
- **Trap:** "Work was completed" does not itself prove an enforceable government contract.

### Revision notes

1. Article 299 governs executive government contracts.
2. The contract must be expressed in the name of the President or Governor.
3. It must be executed on behalf of that constitutional head.
4. The executing person and manner must be duly directed or authorised.
5. The requirements protect public funds through authority and auditability.
6. The President or Governor does not personally negotiate or become personally liable.
7. An authorised signatory is protected from personal liability for valid official execution.
8. Governmental contractual liability may still attach to the Union or State.
9. Informal assurance cannot safely substitute for constitutional form.
10. Public-law review and private contractual enforceability ask different questions.
11. Article 299 compliance is necessary but ordinary contract-law validity must still be established.
12. A defective contract may invite a separate section 70 inquiry, not automatic validation.

### Concept check

**Question:** A department receives the goods it informally ordered. Does receipt alone establish a valid Article 299 contract?

**Model answer:** No. Receipt may be relevant to benefit and restitution. A valid government contract requires Article 299 form and must also satisfy ordinary contract-law requirements such as agreement, competence, lawful object and absence of vitiating illegality.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not treat subsequent benefit, ratification-like conduct or an officer's designation as an automatic cure for missing constitutional form.

### Lesson-local Mains practice

**Question (10 marks, 150 words):** Article 299 protects the exchequer without making government contractually immune. Explain.

**Model answer (131 words):** Article 299 constitutionalises the form of executive contracts. A Union or State contract must be expressed in the name of the President or Governor, executed on that head's behalf, and executed by an authorised person in the authorised manner. These safeguards prevent informal officers from binding public funds and create an auditable chain of authority. Compliance is necessary but not sufficient: ordinary contract-law requirements concerning agreement, competence, lawful object and consideration, free consent and vitiating illegality also apply. Article 299(2) removes personal liability from the constitutional head and authorised executor; it does not remove institutional liability under a valid contract. If constitutional form is absent, enforcement may fail, although section 70 restitution can arise independently for a lawful non-gratuitous benefit enjoyed. Article 299 therefore disciplines public commitment without creating contractual immunity.

**Unique quantified rubric (10):** three formalities 3; purpose 2; personal/institutional distinction 2; defective-contract qualification 2; verdict 1.

Restitution prevents unjust retention. It does not rewrite the defective transaction into a valid constitutional contract.

### 1. Section 70's three ingredients

📜 **Statutory rule:** Under section 70 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, compensation may arise when:

1. a person lawfully does something for another or delivers something to another;
2. the act or delivery was not intended to be gratuitous; and
3. the other person enjoys the benefit.

⚖️ **Judicial anchor:** *State of West Bengal v. B.K. Mondal & Sons* illustrates the independent restitutionary basis. *Mulamchand v. State of Madhya Pradesh* reinforces that restitution does not validate a constitutionally defective government contract.

### 2. Contract price and restitutionary value are not identical

An enforceable contract protects the agreed allocation of price, performance and risk. Restitution asks what benefit was lawfully conferred and enjoyed.

| Contract | Restitution |
|---|---|
| founded on enforceable promise | founded on benefit retained |
| expectation interest may matter | value of proved benefit matters |
| agreed price and clauses guide relief | promised price is not automatically controlling |
| Article 299 validity is foundational | Article 299 defect is not "cured" |

**Example:** A contractor builds flood-protection work at an authorised department's request, but the constitutional contract form is defective. If the State accepts and uses the structure, section 70 may support compensation for the benefit proved.

**Limit:** A person cannot impose an unwanted service on government and demand payment. Lawfulness, non-gratuitous intention and actual enjoyment must all be established.

### 3. Objection, reply and residual

**Objection:** Section 70 undermines Article 299 by rewarding parties who bypass formalities.

**Reply:** The doctrines protect different interests. Article 299 blocks unauthorised contractual commitment; section 70 prevents proven unjust enrichment after lawful, non-gratuitous benefit is actually enjoyed.

**Residual:** Courts must avoid converting restitution into expectation damages. Otherwise, the exception would swallow the constitutional form.

### UPSC application

- Use a two-column contract-versus-restitution table.
- State the three section 70 conditions exactly.
- Cite *B.K. Mondal* for restitution and *Mulamchand* for the non-validation boundary.
- **Trap:** "Quantum meruit" should not be used loosely as a device to enforce every defective bargain.

### Revision notes

1. Section 70 is an independent restitutionary obligation.
2. The act or delivery must be lawful.
3. It must not be intended to be gratuitous.
4. Government must actually enjoy the benefit.
5. Article 299-defective contracts are not silently validated.
6. Restitution measures the benefit proved, not automatically the promised price.
7. *B.K. Mondal* is the leading government-benefit anchor.
8. *Mulamchand* preserves the contract-restitution firewall.
9. Contract, restitution and judicial review may arise from one episode but retain separate ingredients.

### Concept check

**Question:** What prevents section 70 from becoming an indirect method of enforcing every invalid government contract?

**Model answer:** Section 70 requires independent proof of a lawful, non-gratuitous act or delivery and actual enjoyment of its benefit. Relief is restitution for that benefit, not automatic enforcement of the defective bargain or its promised price.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not describe restitution as retrospective ratification of an Article 299-defective contract.

### Lesson-local Mains practice

**Question (10 marks, 150 words):** Distinguish contractual liability from restitutionary liability of government.

**Model answer (125 words):** Contractual liability rests on an enforceable promise. For executive government contracts, Article 299 first requires expression in the President's or Governor's name, execution on that head's behalf and execution by an authorised person in the authorised manner. Contract terms then allocate price, performance and risk. Restitution under section 70 of the Contract Act is different. It may arise where a person lawfully performs or delivers something without intending it gratuitously and government enjoys the benefit. *B.K. Mondal* supports compensation on this independent basis, while *Mulamchand* cautions that restitution does not validate the defective contract. Its measure is the benefit proved, not automatically the promised contractual price. Thus, Article 299 protects public authority and form, while section 70 prevents unjust enrichment without dissolving that constitutional safeguard.

**Unique quantified rubric (10):** contract basis 2; section 70 ingredients 3; two cases 2; remedial measure 2; distinction-based conclusion 1.

Procedure organises the suit. It does not create a general substantive immunity.

### 1. Article 300: the named governmental party

✅ **Fact:** The Government of India may sue or be sued in the name of the **Union of India**. A State Government may sue or be sued in the **name of the State**.

Article 300 also carries forward the historical liability baseline, subject to legislation by the competent legislature.

This creates a stable defendant:

```text
wrong by department or authorised public operation
                  |
                  v
       not "President personally"
       not "minister personally" by default
                  |
                  v
         Union of India / State
```

### 2. CPC section 79: party description

📜 **Statutory rule:** Section 79 of the Code of Civil Procedure identifies the Union of India or the State as the party in covered suits by or against government.

Article 300 supplies constitutional suability; section 79 supplies procedural naming. They are connected but not interchangeable.

### 3. CPC section 80: prior notice

📜 **Statutory rule:** Section 80(1) ordinarily requires two months' prior notice before specified civil suits against government or a public officer for an act purporting to be done in official capacity.

The notice serves practical purposes:

- identifies the claimant and cause;
- gives government an opportunity to investigate;
- permits settlement or correction;
- reduces avoidable litigation;
- helps preserve records.

📜 **Urgent route:** Section 80(2) permits a court-controlled suit seeking urgent or immediate relief without prior notice, subject to the statutory procedure.

> **Trap:** Notice is a procedural condition. It is not sovereign immunity and does not decide whether the claimant has a substantive right.

### 4. Cause of action, forum and remedy remain separate

Even after naming the proper party and addressing notice, the claimant must prove:

- jurisdiction;
- limitation;
- legal duty or enforceable obligation;
- breach or wrongful act;
- causation where required;
- damage or entitlement;
- absence of a governing defence; and
- the requested relief.

**Example:** A supplier with a valid government contract may sue the Union of India after satisfying the contractual dispute clause and applicable procedure.

**Limit:** Article 300 does not make every departmental disagreement a civil cause of action. The substantive right must arise from contract, statute, tort or another recognised source.

### 5. Objection, reply and residual

**Objection:** Mandatory notice unfairly delays citizens seeking relief against a powerful defendant.

**Reply:** The ordinary notice period promotes settlement and administrative investigation; section 80(2) recognises that urgent relief cannot always wait.

**Residual:** Procedural safeguards become unjust when departments use them only to postpone meritorious claims. Effective government litigation policy should evaluate claims early rather than appeal routinely.

### UPSC application

- Correct party: Union of India or the State.
- Article 300 is not the same as Article 300A.
- CPC section 79 concerns party description.
- CPC section 80 concerns notice and includes an urgent-relief route.
- Procedure must not be confused with Article 361 personal immunity, Article 299 signatory protection or sovereign tort immunity.

### Revision notes

1. Article 300 makes the Union and States suable juristic entities.
2. The Union is sued as the Union of India.
3. A State is sued in the name of the State.
4. Article 300 preserves a historical liability baseline subject to legislation.
5. CPC section 79 identifies the proper governmental party.
6. CPC section 80(1) ordinarily requires two months' prior notice for covered suits.
7. Section 80(2) provides a court-controlled urgent-relief route.
8. Notice is procedural, not substantive immunity.
9. Cause of action, limitation, jurisdiction and proof remain independently necessary.
10. Article 300 and Article 300A answer different questions.
11. Litigation policy should encourage early evaluation and settlement of justified claims.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why is CPC section 80 notice not equivalent to sovereign immunity?

**Model answer:** Notice regulates when and how a covered civil suit may begin; it gives government an opportunity to investigate or settle. Sovereign immunity concerns whether liability exists for a category of governmental conduct. A procedural gateway does not extinguish the substantive cause.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not say that failure to satisfy notice proves government committed no wrong; it concerns maintainability and procedure.

### Lesson-local Mains practice

**Question (10 marks, 150 words):** Explain the relationship among Article 300 and sections 79-80 of the Code of Civil Procedure.

**Model answer (131 words):** Article 300 constitutionally recognises that the Government of India and State Governments may sue and be sued, using the juristic names Union of India and the name of the State. It also preserves the historical liability baseline subject to competent legislation. The Code of Civil Procedure operationalises this capacity. Section 79 identifies the proper governmental party in covered civil litigation, while section 80(1) ordinarily requires two months' prior notice before specified suits against government or public officers for official acts. Section 80(2) provides a court-controlled route where urgent or immediate relief is sought. These provisions do not create blanket substantive immunity. The claimant must still establish jurisdiction, limitation, a recognised cause of action, breach, causation where relevant and entitlement to relief. Article 300 provides suability; sections 79-80 structure its civil-procedural exercise.

The timeline shows evolution, not a neat statutory repeal. India still relies heavily on cases rather than a comprehensive State-tort code.

### 1. Ordinary tort ingredients

Private-law tort liability generally asks:

```text
duty -> breach -> causation -> legally recognised damage -> defences -> damages
```

Government may be vicariously liable where an employee commits a tort in the course of employment. But State liability, the officer's personal liability and disciplinary or criminal consequences remain distinct questions.

### 2. The colonial distinction

⚖️ ***P & O Steam Navigation Co. v. Secretary of State for India* (1861):** associated liability with non-sovereign or commercial functions and immunity with sovereign functions.

The problem is classification. As the welfare State regulates, transports, stores, polices, inspects, contracts and supplies services, almost any operation can be described as "governmental" at a high level.

### 3. The two early post-Constitution poles

⚖️ ***State of Rajasthan v. Vidyawati* (1962):** State liability was recognised for negligence involving a government vehicle used in an operational setting.

⚖️ ***Kasturi Lal Ralia Ram Jain v. State of Uttar Pradesh* (1964):** the older immunity was applied to loss of property taken into police custody in the exercise of sovereign powers.

| Case | Function framing | Exam use |
|---|---|---|
| *Vidyawati* | ordinary operational negligence | liability-compatible welfare-State approach |
| *Kasturi Lal* | police sovereign function | restrictive older rule and principal criticism target |

### 4. Strong narrowing

⚖️ ***N. Nagendra Rao & Co. v. State of Andhra Pradesh* (1994):** broad immunity was treated as inconsistent with the expanding welfare State; any surviving immunity was confined, if at all, to narrow inalienable governmental functions.

⚖️ ***Common Cause* (1999)** and ***State of A.P. v. Challa Ramkrishna Reddy* (2000):** reinforce rule-of-law resistance to broad immunity.

⚠️ **Inference:** A functional approach should ask whether liability would improperly intrude into genuinely inalienable high-policy fields or merely hold government accountable for operational negligence.

### 5. Objection, reply and residual

**Objection:** Every rupee paid in damages reduces money available for public services.

**Reply:** Immunity also socialises loss—onto the injured individual. Liability prices preventable harm, improves institutional incentives and vindicates equality before law.

**Residual:** Courts must distinguish policy choice from negligent implementation and avoid converting tort adjudication into general supervision of administration.

### UPSC application

- Present the cases chronologically.
- Do not stop at *Kasturi Lal*.
- Do not say sovereign immunity is wholly abolished.
- Distinguish private tort from constitutional compensation.
- Use the welfare-State argument: expansion of governmental functions makes a broad sovereign/non-sovereign divide unstable.

### Revision notes

1. Private tort requires a recognised duty, breach, causation and damage.
2. Vicarious liability turns on course of employment and the governing legal rules.
3. *P & O Steam Navigation* supplied the colonial functional distinction.
4. *Vidyawati* supports liability for operational negligence.
5. *Kasturi Lal* applied the older sovereign-function immunity.
6. *N. Nagendra Rao* strongly narrowed broad immunity in the welfare State.
7. *Common Cause* and *Challa Ramkrishna Reddy* reinforce accountability.
8. The safest conclusion is narrow, reasoned immunity rather than either absolute extreme.
9. India lacks a single comprehensive State-tort code.
10. Private tort and constitutional tort must be analysed separately.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why is "policing is sovereign, therefore no liability" an unsafe modern conclusion?

**Model answer:** *Kasturi Lal* reflects the older approach, but later welfare-State and rule-of-law cases strongly narrow broad immunity. The analysis must examine the precise function, operational negligence, rights implications and remedy rather than attach automatic immunity to a departmental label.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not infer that the narrowing cases have enacted a complete statutory abolition of every immunity.

### Lesson-local Mains practice

**Question (10 marks, 150 words):** Trace the evolution of sovereign immunity in Indian State-tort law.

**Model answer (130 words):** Indian State-tort law began with the colonial distinction in *P & O Steam Navigation* between sovereign and non-sovereign functions. After the Constitution, *Vidyawati* recognised State liability for operational negligence involving a government vehicle, while *Kasturi Lal* applied immunity to property lost from police custody, preserving the older sovereign-function approach. The expansion of the welfare State made that broad distinction increasingly unworkable. *N. Nagendra Rao* strongly narrowed immunity, indicating that it could survive, if at all, only in restricted inalienable fields. *Common Cause* and *Challa Ramkrishna Reddy* further aligned public accountability with rule of law. Yet it is unsafe to claim complete abolition because India lacks a comprehensive State-tort statute and liability remains fact-sensitive. The defensible position is narrow immunity for exceptional high sovereign functions and ordinary accountability for operational negligence.

## Lesson 7 — Constitutional Tort and Public-Law Compensation

### Visual: one event, several non-identical remedies

```text
STATE CUSTODY CAUSES AN ESTABLISHED RIGHTS VIOLATION
                       |
        +--------------+--------------+
        |              |              |
        v              v              v
PUBLIC-LAW         PRIVATE TORT     CRIMINAL /
```

The visual prevents the common error of treating "constitutional tort" as a synonym for every civil wrong committed by government.

### 1. Why ordinary damages may be insufficient

If unlawful State detention or custodial violence violates Article 21, merely telling the victim to begin a long private suit may make the guaranteed right ineffective. Constitutional courts developed public-law compensation to vindicate an established Fundamental-Right violation.

⚖️ ***Rudul Sah v. State of Bihar* (1983):** compensation was granted after continued illegal detention despite acquittal.

⚖️ ***Nilabati Behera v. State of Orissa* (1993):** public-law compensation for a Fundamental-Right violation was distinguished from a private tort action.

⚖️ ***D.K. Basu v. State of West Bengal* (1997):** custodial safeguards and compensation principles strengthened rights-based State accountability.

### 2. The remedial logic

```text
Fundamental Right
       +
established State violation
       +
constitutional court's remedial power
       =
public-law compensation to vindicate the right
```

✅ **Fact:** Article 32 is directed to enforcement of Fundamental Rights. Article 226 reaches Fundamental Rights and other legal rights.

⚠️ **Inference:** Public-law compensation has an expressive function: it declares that State power itself violated the constitutional relationship, rather than merely causing an ordinary private loss.

### 3. Public law versus private law

| Dimension | Constitutional compensation | Private tort damages |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Constitution and rights enforcement | ordinary civil law |
| Trigger | established State violation of Fundamental Rights | duty, breach, causation and damage |
| Forum | Supreme Court or High Court | civil court |
| Primary object | vindication and immediate constitutional remedy | compensation for civil wrong |
| Relationship | may coexist with other action | not displaced in every case |

Compensation under writ jurisdiction is not automatic whenever an allegation is made. The court must be satisfied that the case is appropriate for public-law relief and that the violation is established.

### 4. Sovereign immunity cannot defeat the constitutional remedy

The State cannot answer an established Fundamental-Right violation merely by invoking the older private-law sovereign immunity doctrine. Constitutional supremacy would be hollow if an ordinary immunity defeated the remedy for unconstitutional State action.

**Example:** A person remains imprisoned after a lawful release order. The wrong is not merely negligent record handling; it is continued deprivation of personal liberty by State authority.

**Limit:** The public-law route does not determine every disputed factual damages claim. Complex questions of negligence, contributory fault or quantum may still require ordinary proceedings.

### 5. Objection, reply and residual

**Objection:** Writ compensation bypasses trial safeguards and may award money on an incomplete factual record.

**Reply:** Constitutional courts reserve the remedy for clear or established violations and can leave wider private-law claims open.

**Residual:** The doctrine must remain principled. It should not become a universal substitute for civil suits, statutory compensation or criminal prosecution.

### UPSC application

- Define constitutional tort through rights violation and public-law remedy.
- Cite *Rudul Sah*, *Nilabati Behera* and *D.K. Basu*.
- Explain why sovereign immunity cannot bar the constitutional remedy.
- Qualify: writ compensation does not replace every civil, criminal or disciplinary route.
- Link to Article 21's transformation from a promise into an enforceable remedial guarantee.

### Revision notes

1. Constitutional tort concerns State violation of Fundamental Rights.
2. Public-law compensation is available through constitutional jurisdiction.
3. *Rudul Sah* concerns compensation for illegal continued detention.
4. *Nilabati Behera* distinguishes public-law compensation from private tort.
5. *D.K. Basu* strengthens custodial safeguards and compensation logic.
6. Article 32 enforces Fundamental Rights.
7. Article 226 also reaches other legal rights.
8. Sovereign immunity cannot nullify an established constitutional remedy.
9. Public-law compensation has vindicatory as well as compensatory significance.
10. Private damages, criminal action and disciplinary proceedings remain distinct.
11. Writ compensation is not automatic for every disputed civil wrong.
12. Remedy selection depends on wrong, ingredients, forum and relief.

### Concept check

**Question:** What makes constitutional compensation "public law" rather than merely an accelerated tort award?

**Model answer:** Its foundation is an established violation of a Fundamental Right by State action and the constitutional court's duty to vindicate that right. It does not merely apply ordinary duty-breach-damage rules, though private-law claims may coexist.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not equate every negligent act of a public employee with a constitutional tort.

### Lesson-local Mains practice

**Question (10 marks, 150 words):** Why is constitutional tort distinct from private tort liability?

**Model answer (129 words):** Constitutional tort is a public-law response to an established violation of Fundamental Rights by State action. In *Rudul Sah*, compensation addressed continued illegal detention; *Nilabati Behera* expressly distinguished public-law compensation from an ordinary private tort suit; and *D.K. Basu* connected custodial safeguards with effective rights enforcement. The remedy operates through Articles 32 or 226 and vindicates the constitutional guarantee itself. Private tort liability, by contrast, ordinarily requires proof of duty, breach, causation, damage and applicable defences in civil proceedings. Sovereign immunity cannot defeat a constitutional remedy for established rights violations, but writ compensation is not automatic for every allegation or operational mistake. It may coexist with private damages, criminal prosecution and disciplinary action. Its distinctiveness therefore lies in source, trigger, forum and vindicatory purpose, not merely speed of payment.

### Visual: adjacent numbers, different doctrines

| Provision | Subject | What it does not do |
|---|---|---|
| Article 299 | government contracts | create tort immunity |
| Article 300 | suits and proceedings | state the right to property |
| Article 300A | deprivation of property | govern contract execution |
| Articles 74(2), 163(3) | ministerial advice to President/Governor | immunise the resulting action or every underlying record |
| Article 361 | personal protection of President/Governor | immunise governmental action from review |
| CPC 80 | prior civil-suit notice | erase substantive liability |
| BNSS 218 | cognizance sanction in specified official-act cases | create general civil immunity |

The most frequent error is not ignorance of each rule but collapse of distinct rules into one vague "government immunity."

### 1. Article 300A: authority of law

✅ **Fact:** No person shall be deprived of property save by authority of law. The protection applies to every person, not only citizens. It is a constitutional right outside Part III, not a Fundamental Right and not merely an ordinary statutory right.

⚖️ ***K.T. Plantation v. State of Karnataka* (2011):** supports public purpose, non-arbitrariness and the rejection of illusory compensation.

⚖️ ***Vidya Devi v. State of Himachal Pradesh* (2020):** executive occupation without lawful acquisition and compensation violates Article 300A.

⚖️ ***Kolkata Municipal Corporation v. Bimal Kumar Shah* (2024 INSC 435):** identified seven acquisition-related sub-rights:

```text
notice -> hearing -> reasoned decision -> public purpose
       -> restitution/fair compensation
       -> efficient and expeditious process
       -> conclusion of proceedings
```

⚖️ ***Bernard Francis Joseph Vaz v. Government of Karnataka* (2025 INSC 3):** reinforced timely adequate compensation in an exceptional long-delayed acquisition setting, with Article 142 relief tailored to the case.

### 2. Article 361: the person and the action

✅ **Fact:** The President or Governor is not answerable to a court for exercise and performance of official powers and duties. No criminal proceeding may be instituted or continued, and no arrest or imprisonment may issue, during the term. A personal civil proceeding for conduct outside official capacity requires the constitutionally specified prior notice.

The decisive distinction:

```text
PERSON OF PRESIDENT / GOVERNOR protected
                    ≠
GOVERNMENTAL ACTION beyond review
```

Appropriate proceedings may test the validity of governmental action against the Union or State.

### 3. Other protections come from other texts

- Article 299(2): personal contract protection for constitutional heads and official executors.
- Articles 74(2) and 163(3): courts shall not inquire into whether any, and if so what, advice was tendered by ministers to the President or Governor.
- CPC sections 79-80: party naming and civil notice.
- BNSS section 218: prior sanction before cognizance of specified offences alleged against protected public servants acting or purporting to act in official duty, subject to the section's text and exceptions.
- Judicial Officers' Protection Act, 1850 and Judges (Protection) Act, 1985: statutory protection connected with judicial acts and duties.

No single provision creates a universal shield for all official conduct.

### 4. Ministerial advice: Articles 74(2) and 163(3)

✅ **Fact:** Article 74(2) bars judicial inquiry into whether any, and if so what, advice was tendered by ministers to the President. Article 163(3) supplies the corresponding protection for advice tendered by ministers to the Governor.

```text
PROTECTED
existence and content of ministerial advice
                    |
                    ≠
                    |
NOT AUTOMATICALLY PROTECTED
validity of resulting governmental action
independently existing records or objective material
constitutional competence, mala fides or procedural legality
```

The protection preserves confidential collective advice and prevents a court from compelling disclosure of the advice itself. It does not make the resulting order immune from review. Nor does a document become privileged merely because ministers considered it; independently existing material must be tested under the law governing that material.

> **Trap:** Articles 74(2) and 163(3) protect advice from judicial inquiry. They do not create personal immunity for ministers, replace Article 361 or establish a general State-secrets privilege.

### 5. State-affairs privilege and RTI

📜 Under the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023, section 129 addresses unpublished official records relating to affairs of State; section 130 concerns official communications whose disclosure would injure public interest.

📜 Under the Right to Information Act, 2005:

- section 8(1)(a) protects specified national interests;
- section 8(2) permits disclosure where public interest outweighs protected harm;
- section 22 gives overriding effect over inconsistent law, including the Official Secrets Act, 1923.

⚖️ ***State of U.P. v. Raj Narain* (1975)** and ***S.P. Gupta v. Union of India* (1981)** reject absolute executive secrecy and require a public-interest approach.

**Objection:** Disclosure may harm security, diplomacy or confidential decision-making.

**Reply:** The law recognises protected interests but requires the exact provision, claimed harm, competent decision-maker and reviewable balance.

**Residual:** Openness is not absolute; neither is secrecy.

### UPSC application

- Article 300A demands authority of law, not unsupported executive occupation.
- Article 361 protects the office-holder personally, not the legality of State action.
- Articles 74(2) and 163(3) protect ministerial advice, not the validity of the resulting action or every underlying record.
- Keep civil notice, criminal sanction, evidentiary privilege and RTI exemption separate.
- State the seven 2024 property safeguards in sequence.
- **Trap:** Do not call Article 300A a Fundamental Right.

### Revision notes

1. Article 300A protects every person.
2. Property may be deprived only by authority of law.
3. Article 300A is a constitutional, non-Fundamental Right.
4. *K.T. Plantation* supplies public-purpose and non-illusory-compensation qualifications.
5. *Vidya Devi* rejects unsupported executive occupation.
6. *Bimal Kumar Shah* identifies seven procedural sub-rights.
7. *Bernard Francis Joseph Vaz* highlights timely adequate compensation in exceptional delay.
8. Article 361 protects the President and Governor personally during the term.
9. Governmental action remains judicially reviewable.
10. CPC notice and BNSS sanction are different procedural devices.
11. BSA privilege and RTI exemptions answer different disclosure questions.
12. *Raj Narain* and *S.P. Gupta* reject absolute secrecy.
13. Articles 74(2) and 163(3) bar inquiry into the existence and content of ministerial advice.
14. The resulting governmental action and independently existing material remain reviewable under their governing law.
15. Every claimed protection requires its own source, scope, conditions and remedy.

### Concept check

**Question:** How can Articles 361 and 163(3) protect the Governor and ministerial advice while a court still reviews the impugned governmental action?

**Model answer:** Article 361 protects the Governor's person and answerability in specified ways, while Article 163(3) bars inquiry into whether any, and if so what, ministerial advice was tendered. Neither provision validates unlawful State action; a court may review the resulting action and independently existing material through appropriate proceedings.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not convert personal constitutional protection into substantive immunity for every decision taken in the Governor's name.

### Lesson-local Mains practice

**Question (10 marks, 150 words):** "Official protection is not governmental impunity." Explain with constitutional and statutory distinctions.

**Model answer (124 words):** Indian law protects particular offices and processes without placing government beyond law. Article 361 shields the President and Governor personally in specified ways, while Articles 74(2) and 163(3) bar judicial inquiry into whether any, and if so what, ministerial advice was tendered. The resulting governmental action and independently existing material remain reviewable under their governing law. Article 299(2) separately removes personal contractual liability from constitutional heads and authorised executors without extinguishing institutional liability. CPC section 80 ordinarily requires civil-suit notice; BNSS section 218 concerns cognizance of specified official-act offences. BSA sections 129-130 and RTI section 8 address different disclosure questions. Each protection therefore has a distinct actor, subject and limit. Rule of law requires functional confidentiality and independence without converting them into governmental impunity.

### 1. Genuine current anchor: GeM

✅ **Fact:** An official Press Information Bureau backgrounder titled **"Where Every Purchase Finds a GeM"**, published on 8 August 2026, described the Government e-Marketplace as India's national digital public-procurement platform and highlighted its role in transparent, efficient and inclusive government purchasing.

**Boundary:** A digital platform does not amend Articles 298-300. Nor does platform compliance by itself cure want of constitutional or statutory authority.

### 2. Equality in procurement

Government need not accept the cheapest bid regardless of quality, risk or lifecycle cost. It must use relevant criteria, offer fair opportunity, record reasons and avoid favouritism.

| Legitimate differentiation | Constitutionally suspect differentiation |
|---|---|
| technical capacity | undisclosed preference |
| past performance | criteria changed after bids |
| lifecycle cost | favouritism |
| security or interoperability | irrelevant local connection |
| delivery risk | selective relaxation |

### 3. Arbitration and public accountability

A valid authorised government contract may include arbitration. Arbitration supplies a chosen adjudicatory route; it does not:

- authorise an officer who lacked Article 299 power;
- waive every statutory or constitutional defence;
- prevent review of a separate public-law wrong;
- remove appropriation and audit obligations; or
- turn a legislative proposal into enacted law.

⚠️ **Inference:** Government litigation strategy should assess claims early, settle justified disputes, preserve evidence and comply with awards rather than treat appeal as the default administrative response.

### 4. Relational contracting and blacklisting

Long-duration government contracts may require variation, extension, force-majeure allocation, change-in-law treatment, price adjustment, performance security, termination for public interest, payment control, audit and disclosure. Article 299 establishes the authorised public commitment; it does not decide every later allocation of contractual risk.

Blacklisting has a wider public-law effect because it restricts access to future public opportunities. Even where contractual power exists, the decision should rest on relevant grounds, notice, opportunity to respond, recorded reasons and proportionality.

⚠️ **Inference:** The public-law overlay is strongest when government uses institutional power to exclude a person from future public dealing, rather than merely calculating relief under an existing bargain.

### 5. Vicarious liability and personal fault

Government may be vicariously liable for an employee's tort in the course of employment. The official may separately face consequences for mala fides, criminal conduct or personal fault.

Apply this sequence:

```text
function -> authority -> course of employment -> fault
        -> causation -> institutional liability
        -> personal/statutory protection -> remedy
```

Do not assume every official wrong creates State liability, or that State liability excludes personal responsibility.

### 6. Institutional incentives

| Liability too broad | Immunity too broad |
|---|---|
| defensive administration | weak safety incentives |
| diversion of public funds | under-investment in training |
| hindsight review of policy | poor record preservation |
| excessive personal risk-aversion | victims bear public-operation losses |

A principled approach protects narrow high-policy space while imposing ordinary accountability for avoidable operational harm.

### 7. Reorganisation and later succession

Articles 294-295 secured commencement-era continuity. Later territorial or governmental reorganisation depends on the governing reorganisation statute, agreements and transfer instruments.

These may allocate:

- land, buildings, records and undertakings;
- debts, guarantees and contracts;
- employees and pensions;
- pending litigation;
- tax and public-account balances; and
- licences and statutory orders.

> **Trap:** There is no universal constitutional arithmetic for apportioning every later liability.

### 8. Reform framework

1. enact clearer State-tort principles without restoring broad immunity;
2. preserve evidence rapidly after custodial or operational harm;
3. publish and maintain digital delegations for Article 299 authority;
4. train procurement and contract-management cadres;
5. use notice, reasons and proportionality in blacklisting;
6. screen government claims and appeals for public value;
7. align arbitration with settlement and award compliance;
8. disclose performance and dispute data subject to legitimate confidentiality.

### 9. Objection, reply and residual

**Objection:** Stronger liability and disclosure will paralyse procurement officials.

**Reply:** Clear authority, standard clauses, recorded reasons, professional contract management and narrow good-faith protections reduce uncertainty better than broad immunity.

**Residual:** Accountability design must distinguish corruption or arbitrariness from reasonable decisions that later perform poorly. Constitutional review controls legality, not commercial hindsight.

- Connect equality, Article 299, audit and dispute resolution in one chain.
- Treat arbitration as contractual procedure within a larger public-law framework.
- Distinguish commencement succession from later statutory reorganisation.
- Conclude with calibrated accountability: narrow immunity, clear authority, timely compensation and professional contract management.

7. Arbitration requires a valid authorised contractual foundation.
8. Arbitration does not oust every public-law remedy or audit duty.
9. Vicarious liability and personal fault may coexist.
10. Long-duration contracts may raise variation, force-majeure, price-adjustment and termination questions.
11. Blacklisting requires fair procedure and proportionate reasoning.
12. Liability design should preserve safety incentives without inducing hindsight paralysis.
13. Later reorganisation follows the governing statute and transfer instruments.
14. No universal liability-apportionment formula should be invented.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why is a government tender simultaneously commercial and constitutional?

**Model answer:** It is commercial because government buys goods or services and allocates contractual risk. It is constitutional because public money and public opportunity require lawful authority, equality, relevant criteria, fair procedure, Article 299-compliant commitment, audit and review.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not say constitutional fairness requires acceptance of the numerically lowest bid in every case.

### Lesson-local Mains practice

**Question (10 marks, 150 words):** Public procurement is constitutional administration conducted through contract. Discuss.

**Model answer (133 words):** Public procurement uses contractual instruments, but government acts with public money and distributes public opportunity. Article 298 supplies economic capacity, while Article 299 requires an authorised and auditable commitment. Equality demands relevant disclosed criteria, fair competition and freedom from favouritism; it does not mechanically require selection of the lowest price where quality, lifecycle cost or risk are material. The August 2026 PIB backgrounder on Government e-Marketplace illustrates how a digital platform can strengthen transparency, access and records, though platform use cannot cure want of legal authority. After award, performance monitoring, payment, audit, blacklisting safeguards and dispute resolution remain necessary. Arbitration may resolve contractual disputes but does not erase statutory duties or separate public-law review. Procurement is therefore private-law exchange embedded in constitutional controls, and professional contract management is as important as tender-stage fairness.

**Unique quantified rubric (10):** constitutional-contract thesis 2; Articles 298-299 2; equality criteria 2; current anchor with boundary 2; post-award accountability 1; conclusion 1.

## Check 1 — Constitutional sequence

**Question:** Arrange the following in a legal reasoning sequence: Article 299 validity, Article 298 capacity, remedy, Article 300 proper party.

**Model answer:** Begin with Article 298 capacity; test Article 299 validity; identify the substantive wrong and remedy; then use Article 300 and procedural law to identify the governmental party and litigation route.

## Check 2 — Defective bargain

**Question:** A State retains goods supplied under an Article 299-defective arrangement. What must a claimant prove?

**Model answer:** The claimant cannot enforce the defective bargain merely as a contract. For section 70 restitution, the claimant must independently prove lawful supply, non-gratuitous intention and actual enjoyment of the benefit by the State.

## Check 3 — Immunity evolution

**Question:** Give the safest one-sentence conclusion on sovereign immunity in India.

**Model answer:** The colonial sovereign-function immunity has been strongly narrowed by welfare-State and rule-of-law jurisprudence, but fact-sensitive private tort law and the absence of a comprehensive statute make a claim of total abolition unsafe.

## Check 4 — Remedy selection

**Question:** Distinguish the remedy for operational negligence from the remedy for an established custodial Article 21 violation.

**Model answer:** Operational negligence ordinarily follows private tort requirements in civil proceedings; an established custodial Article 21 violation may additionally justify public-law compensation under Articles 32 or 226, without necessarily excluding other proceedings.

## Check 5 — Protection firewall

**Question:** Why must Articles 74(2)/163(3), Article 361, CPC section 80 and BNSS section 218 be written separately?

# ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS MODEL PRACTICE

## Original 10-mark question

**Question (10 marks, 150 words):** How do Articles 298 and 299 balance governmental commercial capacity with protection of the public exchequer?

**Model answer (135 words):** Article 298 enables the Union and States to trade, acquire, hold and dispose of property, and make contracts, subject to legislative-competence controls. Article 299 then controls how that capacity becomes a public commitment. The contract must be expressed in the President's or Governor's name, executed on that head's behalf, and executed by an authorised person in the authorised manner. These safeguards establish authority and an audit trail. They are necessary but not sufficient: ordinary contract-law requirements concerning agreement, competence, lawful object and consideration, free consent and vitiating illegality also apply. Article 299(2) protects constitutional heads and authorised executors personally without immunising the Union or State under a valid contract. Where constitutional form fails, section 70 restitution may independently prevent unjust enrichment without validating the bargain. Capacity is broad; enforceable commitment is constitutionally and contractually disciplined.

**Quantified rubric (10):** Article 298 scope 2; three Article 299 formalities 3; public-fund purpose 2; personal/institutional distinction 1; restitution qualification 1; conclusion 1.

## Original 15-mark question

**Question (15 marks, 250 words):** Examine the transformation of State liability from sovereign immunity to constitutional accountability in India.

**Model answer (207 words):** Article 300 makes the Union and States suable legal persons but preserves a historical liability baseline, leaving State-tort law substantially case driven. The colonial decision in *P & O Steam Navigation* distinguished sovereign from non-sovereign functions. After the Constitution, *Vidyawati* recognised liability for operational negligence involving a government vehicle, whereas *Kasturi Lal* applied immunity to property lost in police custody.

The welfare State weakened this broad functional divide. *N. Nagendra Rao* reasoned that extensive governmental activity made blanket immunity untenable and confined any surviving protection to narrow inalienable fields. *Common Cause* and *Challa Ramkrishna Reddy* further aligned State liability with rule of law.

A second transformation occurred through constitutional tort. *Rudul Sah*, *Nilabati Behera* and *D.K. Basu* support public-law compensation for established Fundamental-Right violations. This remedy vindicates the Constitution through Articles 32 or 226 and is distinct from private tort damages, criminal responsibility and disciplinary action. Older sovereign immunity cannot defeat an established constitutional remedy.

Yet complete abolition should not be claimed. India lacks a comprehensive State-tort code; policy choices, operational negligence, course of employment, causation and statutory protection remain fact-sensitive. The modern position is calibrated accountability: narrow protection for exceptional high sovereign functions, ordinary tort responsibility for operational harm and effective public-law compensation for rights violations.

**Quantified rubric (15):** Article 300 frame 2; colonial and early cases 3; narrowing jurisprudence 3; constitutional-tort cases 3; route distinction 2; qualified reform verdict 2.

## Original 20-mark question

**Question (20 marks, 250 words):** "The constitutional law of government liability is best understood as a system of separate gateways rather than a single doctrine of immunity." Analyse.

**Model answer (234 words):** Government liability requires a sequence of separate gateways. First, Articles 294-295 establish continuity of public assets, rights and obligations. Articles 296-297 allocate ownerless property and specified maritime resources, while Article 298 grants executive capacity to trade, hold property and contract.

Second, commitment has its own gate. Article 299 requires expression in the President's or Governor's name, execution on that head's behalf and an authorised executor and manner; ordinary contract-law validity must also exist. Personal protection of constitutional heads and signatories does not remove institutional liability. If form fails, Contract Act section 70 may independently compensate a lawful, non-gratuitous benefit actually enjoyed; it does not validate the bargain.

Third, litigation and wrongs divide further. Article 300 and CPC section 79 identify the Union of India or State as party; CPC section 80 ordinarily supplies notice, not immunity. Private tort applies duty, breach, causation, damage and defences. The *Kasturi Lal* sovereign-function rule has been strongly narrowed by *N. Nagendra Rao*, *Common Cause* and *Challa Ramkrishna Reddy*. Public-law compensation under *Rudul Sah*, *Nilabati Behera* and *D.K. Basu* separately vindicates Fundamental Rights.

Finally, Article 300A property protection, Article 361 personal immunity, Articles 74(2)/163(3) advice protection, BNSS sanction, evidentiary privilege, RTI exemptions, arbitration and procurement review each possess distinct sources and limits. A sound answer therefore identifies actor, authority, wrong, forum and remedy. India's weakness is not absence of accountability, but fragmentation and lack of a comprehensive State-tort law.

**Quantified rubric (20):** continuity/property/capacity 3; Article 299 4; restitution 2; Article 300/CPC 2; private-tort evolution 3; constitutional tort 3; protection firewalls 2; synthesis/reform 1.

# REMEDIATION

## Error 1: "Article 300A governs suits against government"

**Repair:** Article 300 governs suits and proceedings; Article 300A protects property from deprivation except by authority of law.

## Error 2: "The President signs every government contract"

**Repair:** The contract is expressed in the President's or Governor's name and executed on that head's behalf by an authorised person in an authorised manner. Those formalities are necessary, but ordinary contract-law validity must also exist.

## Error 3: "An invalid contract means government may keep the benefit free"

**Repair:** Contract enforcement may fail, but section 70 restitution can arise independently if all three statutory ingredients are proved.

## Error 4: "CPC notice is sovereign immunity"

**Repair:** Section 80 is a procedural gateway with an urgent-relief route. Substantive liability must be tested separately.

## Error 5: "Kasturi Lal remains the complete modern rule"

**Repair:** Write the whole trajectory through *N. Nagendra Rao*, *Common Cause* and *Challa Ramkrishna Reddy* and reach a qualified conclusion.

## Error 6: "Constitutional tort is any tort committed by an officer"

**Repair:** It is a public-law remedy for an established Fundamental-Right violation by State action; private tort has different ingredients and forum.

## Error 7: "Article 361 makes a Governor's action unreviewable"

**Repair:** It protects the person in specified ways. Appropriate proceedings may still challenge governmental action.

## Error 8: "Article 300A guarantees an absolute market-price formula"

**Repair:** State authority must rest on law, public purpose, fair process and non-illusory compensation; the exact statutory measure remains context dependent.

## Error 9: "The lowest bidder must constitutionally win"

**Repair:** Government may use relevant disclosed criteria such as quality, lifecycle cost and risk, applied equally and without favouritism.

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

## CORE REGISTER NOTES

## Constitutional spine

- **Part XII, Chapter III:** Articles 294-300 deal with property, contracts, rights, liabilities, obligations and suits involving government.
- **Central thesis:** government is a continuing legal person with broad capacity but constitutionally controlled commitment and reviewable liability.
- **Sequence:** succession and vesting -> capacity -> contract form -> suits and liability -> adjacent property right.

## Succession, property and capacity

- **Article 294:** Dominion/Governor's Province succession at commencement.
- **Article 295:** succession connected with former Indian States, guided by purpose and constitutional field, subject to agreement.
- **Article 296:** escheat, lapse and bona vacantia; general location rule with purpose/control proviso.
- **Article 297:** specified offshore lands, minerals, things of value and EEZ resources vest in Union.
- **Article 298:** Union/State may trade, conduct business, acquire, hold, dispose of property and contract, subject to legislative limits.
- **Trap:** capacity under Article 298 is not validity under Article 299.

## Article 299 contract checklist

1. expressed to be made by President/Governor;
2. executed on behalf of President/Governor;
3. executed by authorised person in authorised manner.

- These formalities are necessary but not sufficient; ordinary contract-law requirements also apply.
- Constitutional head and official executor are not personally liable for valid official execution.
- Union or State can remain institutionally liable.
- Form protects the public exchequer through authority, authentication and audit.

## Restitution

- **Contract Act section 70:** lawful act/delivery + non-gratuitous intention + benefit enjoyed.
- ***B.K. Mondal*:** independent restitution against government can arise.
- ***Mulamchand*:** restitution does not validate an Article 299-defective contract.
- Relief follows benefit proved, not automatically the invalid bargain's promised price.

## Suits and procedure

- **Article 300:** Union sues/is sued as Union of India; State in the State's name.
- **CPC 79:** proper party.
- **CPC 80(1):** ordinarily two months' notice for covered suits.
- **CPC 80(2):** court-controlled urgent/immediate-relief route.
- Procedure is not substantive immunity.

## Private tort trajectory

| Case | Recall line |
|---|---|
| *P & O Steam Navigation* (1861) | colonial sovereign/non-sovereign distinction |
| *Vidyawati* (1962) | operational vehicle negligence; liability |
| *Kasturi Lal* (1964) | older immunity in police-custody property loss |
| *N. Nagendra Rao* (1994) | broad immunity untenable; narrow residual field |
| *Common Cause* (1999) | rule-of-law accountability |
| *Challa Ramkrishna Reddy* (2000) | further erosion of broad immunity |

- **Safe verdict:** strongly narrowed, not safely described as either intact or completely abolished.
- India lacks a comprehensive State-tort code.

## Constitutional tort

- Public-law compensation vindicates an established Fundamental-Right violation.
- ***Rudul Sah*:** illegal continued detention.
- ***Nilabati Behera*:** constitutional compensation distinct from private tort.
- ***D.K. Basu*:** custodial safeguards and rights-effective remedy.
- Private damages, criminal responsibility and discipline remain separate routes.

## Article 300A

- Every person; constitutional non-Fundamental Right.
- Deprivation only by authority of law.
- ***K.T. Plantation*:** public purpose, non-arbitrariness, no illusory compensation.
- ***Vidya Devi*:** no unsupported executive occupation.
- ***Bimal Kumar Shah* (2024):** notice, hearing, reasons, public purpose, restitution/fair compensation, efficient process, conclusion.
- ***Bernard Francis Joseph Vaz* (2025):** timely adequate compensation in exceptional delayed acquisition.

## Protection firewalls

- **Article 361:** personal protection of President/Governor; action remains reviewable.
- **Articles 74(2)/163(3):** courts cannot inquire into whether any, and if so what, ministerial advice was tendered; resulting action and independently existing material remain reviewable under their governing law.
- **Article 299(2):** personal contract protection.
- **CPC 80:** civil notice.
- **BNSS 218:** criminal cognizance sanction in specified cases.
- **BSA 129-130:** State-affairs records and official communications.
- **RTI 8/22:** exemptions, public-interest balance and overriding effect.
- Never combine them into a universal immunity.

## Procurement and arbitration

- Procurement chain: need -> budget -> specification -> competition -> award -> Article 299 -> monitoring -> payment/audit -> dispute resolution.
- Equality requires relevant criteria and fair opportunity, not automatic lowest-price selection.
- GeM is a 2026 current illustration of digital public procurement; it does not alter constitutional validity rules.
- Arbitration depends on valid authority and does not remove public-law review, statutory duties or audit.
- Vicarious institutional liability and an official's personal fault can coexist.
- Later governmental reorganisation follows its governing statute and transfer instruments; no universal apportionment formula exists.
- Reform priorities are professional contract management, transparent authority records, proportionate blacklisting, early settlement and a clearer State-tort framework.
