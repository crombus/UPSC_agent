# Governor, Chief Minister and State Council of Ministers - Live Session Edition

The State executive has two centres that must not be confused. The Governor gives constitutional form and continuity to State action; the Chief Minister and Council possess democratic authority and political responsibility. Most controversies arise when a power formally vested in the Governor is mistaken either for personal political power or for an entirely mechanical signature.

```text
PEOPLE elect Legislative Assembly
              │ confidence
              ▼
CHIEF MINISTER + COUNCIL ── aid/advice ──► GOVERNOR
 real political executive                    constitutional head
              │                                      │
              └──── collective responsibility ───────┘
```

Formal vesting and political responsibility are deliberately separated.

### 1. Begin with the apparent contradiction

A State order is expressed in the Governor's name, but voters reward or punish the Chief Minister. This is the logic of a parliamentary **dual executive**, not a contradiction. Article 154 formally vests State executive power in the Governor. Articles 163-164 create a Council of Ministers headed by the Chief Minister, require it ordinarily to aid and advise the Governor, and make it collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly.

The Governor is therefore the **de jure**, or formal, executive. The CM-led Council is the **de facto**, or working, executive. “Nominal” does not mean useless: the office supplies continuity, constitutional form and a neutral point for exceptional transitions. It does mean that ordinary policy belongs to ministers who can be removed by the elected House.

### 2. The federal link and its limit

The President appoints the Governor. This creates a federal link, but does not convert Raj Bhavan into a branch office of the Union Cabinet. The Supreme Court has treated the governorship as an independent constitutional office rather than employment under the Union. “Representative of the Centre” is therefore descriptive shorthand, not authority to obey partisan instructions.

The strongest objection is that an appointed head can obstruct an elected government. The constitutional reply is threefold: advice is the rule, discretion is narrow, and disputed majority must return to the Assembly floor. The reply is only as strong as appointments, tenure conventions and judicial control make it in practice.

### 3. Office map

Articles 153-162 concern the Governor and State executive power; Articles 163-164 concern advice, discretion and ministers; Article 165 creates the Advocate General; Article 166 regulates State business; Article 167 makes the CM the information bridge. Later Articles distribute legislative, financial, judicial and immunity functions.

**Example with limit:** The Governor formally appoints ministers, but ordinarily appoints only persons recommended by the CM. The signature gives legal form; it does not transfer political authorship. The analogy stops where the Constitution creates a genuine discretionary field.

### UPSC application

Reject both extremes: “the Governor governs” and “the Governor is irrelevant.” The defensible thesis is **advice-bound in ordinary administration and independently consequential only at tightly controlled constitutional margins**.

### Revision notes


1. Article 154 formally vests State executive power in the Governor.
2. Articles 163-164 make the CM-led Council the working executive.
3. The Council is collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly.
4. The Governor is constitutional head; the CM is head of government.
5. Union appointment does not make the Governor a Union employee.
6. Discretion is an exception, not a parallel executive.
7. Assembly confidence is the democratic control point.
8. Formal action and political authorship are different.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why does formal vesting of State executive power in the Governor not make the Governor the day-to-day ruler?

**Model answer:** Article 154 supplies the constitutional form, while Articles 163-164 place ordinary executive direction with the CM-led Council responsible to the Assembly. The Governor is neither merely a Union employee nor a parallel elected executive.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not equate formal vesting or Union appointment with a personal policy mandate.

### Original Mains practice - 10 marks, 150 words

**Mains question:** Explain the constitutional logic of the dual executive at the State level.

**Directive decoding:** Explain requires design, mechanism and purpose, not a bare Article list.

**Model answer (within stated limit):**

Article 154 gives the State executive a continuous constitutional identity by formally vesting power in the Governor. Articles 163-164, however, place ordinary exercise within a CM-led Council collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly.

The Governor supplies constitutional form, continuity and a head for exceptional transitions. The Chief Minister supplies policy command, coordinates ministers and survives only while Assembly confidence continues. *Shamsher Singh* confirms that gubernatorial satisfaction ordinarily means advised governmental satisfaction.

This design combines stability with democratic responsibility. A separate head can facilitate a hung-House transition, but cannot become a rival executive. Its limited discretionary field is controlled by constitutional text, objective material, floor tests and judicial review.

Thus the Governor gives legal form and continuity; the CM-led Council gives executive direction and electoral accountability.

**Why this earns marks:** It answers the directive with constitutional text, named evidence, analysis and qualification.

**Scoring rubric (10 marks):** constitutional design 3; nominal-real distinction 2; purpose 2; case and qualification 2; conclusion 1

Once constitutional form and democratic command are separated, the next question is how the formal head is chosen without acquiring a rival popular mandate.

Legal qualifications are sparse; neutrality depends substantially on conventions.

### 1. Why appointment, not election?

Article 155 provides appointment by the President through warrant. The Constituent Assembly rejected direct election because it could produce a second popular mandate competing with the Assembly-supported CM, increase partisanship and cost, and sit uneasily with responsible government. The Canadian appointment model was preferred to the American elected-Governor model.

The objection is federal dependence. Appointment by the Union can weaken perceived neutrality. The reply is that appointment avoids a rival mandate and should produce an impartial head. Whether that promise works depends on consultative selection, secure tenure and conduct.

### 2. Qualifications and conditions

Article 157 requires Indian citizenship and completion of 35 years. Article 158 prevents simultaneous membership of Parliament or a State Legislature and another office of profit. A legislator's seat is deemed vacated on entering office. The Governor receives an official residence and statutory emoluments; emoluments cannot be diminished during the term.

Two important conventions are often confused with qualifications: selecting an eminent outsider and consulting the State CM. They are neutrality-oriented conventions and reform recommendations, not enforceable Article 157 conditions.

### 3. Oath, multiple States and contingencies

Under Article 159, the State High Court Chief Justice, or senior-most available judge, administers the oath to execute the office faithfully, preserve, protect and defend the Constitution and law, and serve the people's well-being. The Article 153 proviso allows one person to govern two or more States. Article 160 lets the President arrange discharge of functions in a contingency not otherwise provided for.

The 5 March 2026 official communique illustrates fresh appointment, transfer, accepted resignation and additional charge. It proves institutional flexibility, not the political merit of any individual choice.

### UPSC trap

Citizenship and age 35 are constitutional qualifications. “Outsider,” political detachment and CM consultation belong to the convention-and-reform column.

### Revision notes


1. Article 155 provides presidential appointment by warrant.
2. Article 157 requires citizenship and age 35.
3. Article 158 bars simultaneous legislative membership and office of profit.
4. Emoluments cannot be diminished during the term.
5. Article 159 oath is administered by the High Court Chief Justice or available senior-most judge.
6. Article 160 covers unprovided contingencies.
7. One person may govern multiple States.
8. Outsider status and CM consultation are conventions.

### Concept check

**Question:** Separate the constitutional qualifications for Governor from conventions intended to promote neutrality.

**Model answer:** Article 157 requires Indian citizenship and completion of thirty-five years. Outsider status, political detachment and consultation with the Chief Minister are reform criteria or conventions, not constitutional qualifications; legislative membership must cease on entering office.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not turn reform-oriented appointment preferences such as outsider status, political detachment and CM consultation into Article 157 eligibility conditions.

### Original Mains practice - 10 marks, 150 words

**Mains question:** Why did the Constitution prefer an appointed Governor to a directly elected Governor?

**Directive decoding:** Why requires institutional reasons plus a federal qualification.

**Model answer (within stated limit):**

A directly elected Governor would possess a popular mandate alongside the Assembly-supported Chief Minister. That could create rival claims to policy authority, weaken collective responsibility and turn a constitutional-head office into a partisan executive contest.

Article 155 instead adopts presidential appointment. It avoids a competing mandate, reduces electoral cost and creates a common constitutional link between Union and State. The office remains independent rather than Union employment.

The arrangement has a federal cost because appointment and removal lie effectively with the Union executive. Sarkaria therefore favoured an eminent non-partisan outsider and consultation with the CM; later bodies sought stronger consultation and tenure safeguards.

Appointment is coherent with parliamentary government, but legitimate only when selection is transparent, federal consultation is meaningful and removal is not partisan.

**Why this earns marks:** It answers the directive with constitutional text, named evidence, analysis and qualification.

**Scoring rubric (10 marks):** parliamentary rationale 3; advantages 2; federal concern 2; reform 2; conclusion 1

Appointment explains entry into office; tenure and immunity explain whether the incumbent can act independently without becoming legally unaccountable.

### Visual: tenure, pleasure and immunity map

```text
FIVE-YEAR TERM
      │ subject to
PRESIDENT'S PLEASURE
      ├── resignation to President
      ├── transfer / reappointment possible
      └── continues until successor

ARTICLE 361 PERSONAL IMMUNITY
      ├── no criminal proceeding during term
      ├── no court process for arrest or imprisonment during term
      └── personal civil action after 2 months' notice
```

Tenure dependence and personal immunity solve different constitutional problems.

### 1. Five years is not guaranteed tenure

Article 156 states a five-year term but subjects it to presidential pleasure. The Governor may resign to the President, may be transferred or reappointed, and continues until a successor assumes charge. The term prevents no interregnum but creates no enforceable five-year right.

Because the President acts on Union ministerial advice, pleasure creates a structural incentive to satisfy the Union. *B.P. Singhal v Union of India* (2010) provides a limited check: removal cannot be arbitrary, capricious, unreasonable or mala fide. The Union need not routinely publish reasons, but must justify action if a prima facie arbitrariness case arises. The judgment did not create impeachment or a fixed term.

### 2. Article 361 protects the person, not illegality

The Governor is not answerable to a court for official powers and duties. No criminal proceeding may be instituted or continued, and **no process for the arrest or imprisonment of the Governor shall issue from any court during the term**. A civil proceeding about personal acts requires two months' notice.

Governmental action remains reviewable through proper parties. Article 361 cannot validate an unconstitutional proclamation, floor-test direction or assent process. Nor does being “part of the Legislature” give the Governor Article 194 member privilege; the Governor is not an MLA or MLC.

### 3. Institutional purpose and criticism

Protected emoluments guard against financial retaliation; continuity until a successor prevents vacancy. Yet immunity plus insecure tenure creates an odd combination: protected against courts personally, but dependent on Union pleasure institutionally.

The answer is not to weaken lawful personal immunity. It is to strengthen appointment and removal safeguards while preserving judicial review of the resulting State action.

### Revision notes


1. The five-year term is subject to presidential pleasure.
2. The Governor resigns to the President.
3. Continuation until a successor prevents interregnum.
4. Transfer and reappointment are possible.
5. B.P. Singhal rejects arbitrary or mala fide removal.
6. Article 361 bars criminal proceedings and any court process for arrest or imprisonment during the term.
7. Personal civil action requires two months' notice.
8. Governmental action remains reviewable.
9. Article 194 member privilege does not belong to the Governor.

### Concept check

**Question:** How can a Governor have a five-year term and still serve during presidential pleasure?

**Model answer:** Article 156 states five years but expressly subjects the term to presidential pleasure and permits continuation until a successor enters office. B.P. Singhal restrains arbitrary or mala fide removal without creating guaranteed tenure; Article 361 protects the incumbent personally, not unconstitutional governmental action.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not treat either the five-year period or Article 361 as complete immunity from constitutional control.

### Original Mains practice - 15 marks, 250 words

**Mains question:** Critically examine whether the pleasure doctrine makes the Governor an agent of the Union.

**Directive decoding:** Critically examine needs the dependence case, legal counter-position, case limit and verdict.

**Model answer (within stated limit):**

Article 156 makes the stated five-year term subject to presidential pleasure. Since the President acts on Union advice, appointment, transfer and removal create incentives for responsiveness to the Centre. Regime-change removals and controversies in opposition-ruled States support the “agent” critique.

The label remains legally incomplete. The Governor occupies an independent constitutional office, takes an oath to the Constitution and is ordinarily governed by the State Council's advice. *Shamsher Singh* rejects a general personal executive; *Bommai* and *Rameshwar Prasad* make major political action reviewable and House-centred.

*B.P. Singhal* bars arbitrary, capricious, unreasonable or mala fide removal, though it neither guarantees tenure nor requires routine reasons. The check is therefore weaker than institutional security.

Sarkaria, Punchhi and NCRWC sought consultative appointment and stronger tenure safeguards. Thus pleasure creates real federal dependence but not legal agency. Neutrality requires balanced appointment, protected tenure and reviewable reasoned conduct.

**Why this earns marks:** It answers the directive with constitutional text, named evidence, analysis and qualification.

**Scoring rubric (15 marks):** dependence 4; constitutional counter 3; cases 3; reforms 3; verdict 2

The office may be personally protected yet still formally exercise a wide field of appointments and administration; the next task is to separate formal attribution from real decision-making.

The Constitution separates signature, recommendation, responsibility and removal.

### 1. Formal executive field

All State executive action is expressed in the Governor's name. Under Article 166, the Governor makes authentication and business-allocation rules. State executive power generally follows State legislative competence, subject to constitutional limits.

The Governor appoints the CM; appoints other ministers on CM advice; appoints the Advocate General; and formally appoints authorities such as the State Election Commissioner and SPSC members under their provisions. Appointment does not imply identical removal power: the SEC has High Court judge-like protection, while SPSC members are removable through the prescribed presidential process.

### 2. Information, Cabinet consideration and Article 356

Article 167 requires the CM to communicate Council decisions and legislative proposals, furnish requested information and place a minister-only decision before the full Council if required. These powers reinforce collective government; they do not let the Governor substitute policy.

A Governor may report possible failure of constitutional machinery. The report is important but not conclusive: Union action, parliamentary approval and *Bommai* review remain. Political disagreement does not equal constitutional breakdown.

### 3. University offices

Many Governors act as Chancellors under State university statutes. The legal source is the applicable statute, not the Constitution merely by virtue of governorship. Models differ and may be amended. A statutory Chancellor dispute must therefore be analysed under State law.

### Objection and reply

Formal appointment language can appear to confer control. The functional reply is to ask who recommends, who is accountable and who may remove. Constitutional signatures cannot silently become a personal administration.

### Revision notes


1. State executive action is expressed in the Governor's name.
2. Article 166 governs authentication and business allocation.
3. Other ministers are appointed only on CM advice.
4. Formal appointment and removal may belong to different authorities.
5. Article 167 preserves information flow and collective consideration.
6. University Chancellor powers arise under State statutes.
7. An Article 356 report is reviewable input, not a final verdict.
8. Political disagreement is not constitutional breakdown.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why does a formal gubernatorial appointment power not necessarily imply personal choice or unrestricted removal?

**Model answer:** Responsible government and the governing provision determine the real decision process. Other ministers are appointed on CM advice; some offices require specified consultation; appointment and removal safeguards may differ. Article 167 enables information and collective reconsideration, not gubernatorial policy substitution.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not infer personal discretion merely from the words 'appointed by the Governor'.

### Original Mains practice - 10 marks, 150 words

**Mains question:** Distinguish formal executive powers from personal executive discretion of the Governor.

**Directive decoding:** Distinguish requires a principle and examples on each side.

**Model answer (within stated limit):**

Article 154 formally vests executive power in the Governor, while Article 166 expresses State action in the Governor's name. The office formally appoints ministers and constitutional authorities and authenticates government orders.

These acts are ordinarily advice-based. Other ministers are appointed on CM advice; business decisions belong to the responsible ministry; appointment and removal may be constitutionally separated. Formal vesting identifies the legal channel, not a personal policy sphere.

Personal judgment exists only where text or necessary implication permits it: selecting a claimant in a hung House, responding to objective majority doubt, choosing among Article 200 courses under the current doctrine, or reporting under Article 356. Even then, reasons, floor tests and review control action.

Thus formal power is the normal constitutional form of responsible government; discretion is a narrow, reviewable exception.

The Governor is part of the Legislature without being a member of either House.

### 1. Legislative position

Article 168 includes the Governor within the State Legislature. The office summons and prorogues the House or Houses, dissolves the Assembly, gives the post-election and annual special address, and sends messages. *Nabam Rebia* makes these powers ordinarily advice-bound; Article 163 cannot become a power to set a personal legislative agenda.

The Governor nominates one-sixth of a Legislative Council from persons with special knowledge or practical experience in **literature, science, art, the cooperative movement and social service**. The former Anglo-Indian Assembly nomination is no longer current. The Governor is part of the Legislature but neither an MLA nor MLC and has no legislative vote.

When the offices of Speaker and Deputy Speaker of an Assembly are both vacant, Article 180(1) allows the Governor to appoint an Assembly member to perform the Speaker's duties; Article 184 supplies the parallel Council rule. Under Article 192, a question whether a State legislator has incurred an Article 191(1) disqualification is decided by the Governor, who must obtain and act according to the Election Commission's opinion. This is a structured constitutional role, not a free personal verdict.

### 2. Financial functions

The State budget is caused to be laid before the Legislature. A Money Bill needs prior recommendation, and no demand for a grant may be made without recommendation. The Governor may authorise Contingency Fund advances according to law and constitutes the State Finance Commission for panchayat and municipal finances under Article 243-I.

These functions do not create a personal gubernatorial budget. Ministers prepare and defend fiscal policy and remain responsible to the Assembly.

### 3. Reports and accountability

Reports of the SPSC, State Finance Commission and CAG material concerning State accounts reach legislative scrutiny through prescribed processes. The Governor is the constitutional channel, not the final auditor or policy maker.

**Trap:** recommendation is a procedural precondition, not personal fiscal authorship. Nomination goes to the Legislative Council, never the Assembly.

**Question:** How can the Governor be part of the State Legislature without being a member of either House?

**Model answer:** Article 168 makes the Governor a component of the legislative process through summoning, addresses, messages, nominations, financial recommendations and assent functions, but confers no House membership or vote. Articles 180/184 and 192 assign additional structured roles subject to their stated procedures.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not confuse participation in constituting the Legislature with MLA or MLC status.

**Mains question:** How can the Governor be part of the State Legislature without being a legislator?

**Directive decoding:** How requires institutional functions and the membership distinction.

**Model answer (within stated limit):**

Article 168 constitutes the Legislature from the Governor and the House or Houses, making the office a component of the legislative process rather than a chamber member.

The Governor summons, prorogues and may dissolve the Assembly; addresses and sends messages; nominates one-sixth of a Legislative Council; participates in financial procedure; and acts on Bills under Article 200. These functions connect executive responsibility and law-making.

The Governor does not sit, deliberate or vote as an MLA or MLC and therefore does not receive Article 194 member privilege. Article 361 instead provides office-specific immunity.

Most legislative functions are also advice-bound. *Nabam Rebia* prevents summoning and messaging powers from becoming a personal political agenda. Thus the Governor is an institutional limb of legislation, while elected members remain the deliberative and voting body.

### Visual: advice-default decision tree

```text
GOVERNORAL FUNCTION
       │
       ├── express / necessarily implied discretion? → bounded judgment
       └── otherwise → CM-LED COUNCIL ADVICE

“Governor's satisfaction” ordinarily =
responsible government's satisfaction

INFORMATION / WARNING / COUNCIL CONSIDERATION
≠ general veto over advice
```

Article 163 is an exception clause within parliamentary government, not a general autonomy clause.

### 1. Advice is the rule

Article 163 creates a Council to aid and advise except where discretion is constitutionally required. *Shamsher Singh v State of Punjab* (1974), seven judges, explains that the Governor is a constitutional head and ordinarily acts on advice. References to gubernatorial “satisfaction” normally mean advised governmental satisfaction.

Article 163 lacks Article 74's identical post-42nd-Amendment wording. That textual difference matters but cannot erase responsible government. An exception must be found in text or necessary implication, not in policy disagreement.

### 2. Reconsideration and information

The Governor has no general equivalent of the President's express Article 74 one-time reconsideration rule. Article 167 permits information requests, counselling and submission of a minister-only decision to the whole Council. A specific return power, especially Article 200 for a non-Money Bill, follows its own procedure. These do not create indefinite resistance to ordinary advice.

### 3. Confidentiality and review

Article 163(3) bars judicial inquiry into the advice tendered. It protects deliberative confidentiality, not resulting illegality. Courts may review whether a discretionary field exists, whether relevant material supported action and whether mala fides or constitutional excess occurred.

**Objection:** without personal assessment, the office adds no safeguard. **Reply:** safeguards include information, warning, specified discretion, Assembly confidence and review; they need not become unelected policy rule.

For the 2026 question, answer with a qualified no: advice controls ordinary functions; narrow text-based or necessarily implied exceptions remain.

### Revision notes


1. Shamsher Singh makes advice the default.
2. Article 163 differs textually from Article 74 but creates no general discretion.
3. Exceptions require text or necessary implication.
4. Article 167 information powers are not a policy veto.
5. No general Article 74-style reconsideration belongs to the Governor.
6. Specific return procedures govern specific powers.
7. Article 163(3) protects advice confidentiality.
8. Resulting action and the boundary of discretion remain reviewable.

### Concept check

**Question:** What is the relationship between the aid-and-advice rule and Article 167?

**Model answer:** Shamsher Singh makes ministerial advice the ordinary rule. Article 167 lets the Governor receive decisions and information and require a minister-only decision to be placed before the Council; it does not create a general power to reject or replace Council policy.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not import the President's express Article 74 reconsideration mechanism as a general gubernatorial power.

### Original Mains practice - 15 marks, 250 words

**Mains question:** Is the Governor bound by ministerial advice in all functions? Discuss.

**Directive decoding:** Discuss needs default, exceptions, textual nuance and controls.

Codification promises predictability but an exhaustive list may fail in novel coalition situations. A better middle path codifies procedure: objective material, recorded reasons, fair claimant treatment, prompt floor tests and no party-management role.

**Answer line:** discretion is remedial, not governmental. It exists to restore accountable government, not conduct it.

### 1. The date-controlled answer

Can the Governor simply do nothing? The controlling answer dated **20 November 2025** is no. But courts also cannot rewrite Article 200 through universal rigid deadlines or automatic assent.

The five-judge Article 143 opinion, *In re: Assent, Withholding or Reservation of Bills by the Governor and the President of India*, identifies three operative choices on first presentation: assent; reserve for the President; or withhold and, for a non-Money Bill, return with a reconsideration message. A Money Bill cannot be returned. Reservation is mandatory where derogation from High Court powers would endanger its constitutional position.

The repassage branch must be learned separately. If the Legislature reconsiders a returned non-Money Bill and passes it again, with or without amendment, **withholding is no longer available**. The Governor must then either assent or reserve the Bill for the President. Reservation activates Article 201; it does not mean the Governor has withheld the repassed Bill. Nor should this branch be confused with Article 201: after presidential return and State repassage, the President may still assent or withhold.

The opinion treats selection among Article 200 choices as a specific discretionary function rather than one controlled by State-Cabinet advice. This exception cannot become general Article 163 autonomy.

### 2. Earlier and later 2025 positions

The 8 April 2025 Tamil Nadu judgment prescribed timelines and used Article 142 deemed assent on its facts. The later November Presidential Reference rejected court-created rigid timelines and deemed assent as a general constitutional remedy. Current answers must lead with the later position and identify the earlier ruling as qualified history.

### 3. Review

Courts ordinarily cannot review the merits of a pre-enactment choice as though the Bill were already law. Prolonged, unexplained and indefinite inaction, however, permits limited mandamus requiring discharge of the function within a reasonable time without dictating which option to choose.

The strongest federal objection is that discretion lets an appointee defeat elected legislation. The reply is that reservation and reconsideration are textual checks, but silent burial is not. Prompt reasoned action is the legitimacy condition.

Article 161 permits pardons, reprieves, respites, remissions, suspension and commutation for an offence against a law within State executive power. Older textbook tables often say a Governor cannot pardon a death sentence. *State of Haryana v Raj Kumar @ Bittu* (2021) makes that categorical statement unsafe: State-field constitutional clemency can include pardon in a death-sentence case.

Article 72 is broader in two directions: court-martial sentences and every death sentence irrespective of the Union-field limb. Article 161 has no court-martial limb and remains tied to State executive competence.

### 2. Advice, delay and review

Clemency follows State Council advice, not private gubernatorial compassion. *Maru Ram* supports advice-bound exercise. In *A.G. Perarivalan v State of Tamil Nadu* (2022), the Supreme Court held that the State Cabinet's Article 161 advice binds the Governor and that the unexplained delay in acting on it was constitutionally reviewable. The Court ultimately ordered release through **Article 142**; that remedial order must not be relabelled as an Article 161 pardon by the Governor.

The cases answer different questions. *Raj Kumar @ Bittu* concerns the substantive reach of Article 161 in a State-field death-sentence case. *Perarivalan* concerns binding Cabinet advice, prolonged non-exercise and judicial relief. Limited review may also examine mala fides, arbitrariness, irrelevant grounds or non-application of mind.

A pardon removes sentence and penal consequences; commutation substitutes a lighter punishment; remission reduces duration; respite substitutes a lesser sentence for special circumstances; reprieve temporarily stays execution.

### 3. Other judicial functions

The Governor is consulted in High Court judge appointments, appoints district judges in consultation with the High Court under Article 233, and under Article 234 makes appointments of persons other than district judges to the State judicial service after consultation with **both the State Public Service Commission and the High Court**. These are structured appointments, not adjudication.

Executive mercy does not declare the court legally wrong. It supplies a constitutional safety valve for exceptional justice and policy under advice and review.

The CM converts an Assembly majority into coordinated executive government.

### 1. Appointment, membership and tenure

The Governor appoints the CM under Article 164. A clear-majority leader must be appointed; uncertainty permits bounded judgment followed by floor test. A non-legislator may become CM but must be qualified for membership and enter either House within six consecutive months. The House distinction matters: the minimum age is **25 for the Legislative Assembly and 30 for the Legislative Council**, alongside the other applicable qualifications. Mere eligibility to vote is not enough. In bicameral States, a CM may belong to the Legislative Council.

Pleasure is formal language. While Assembly confidence continues, the Governor cannot dismiss the CM. Majority is the democratic source of tenure.

The Governor administers the CM's oaths of office and secrecy. The office oath commits the minister to the Constitution, India's sovereignty and integrity, faithful and conscientious discharge, and justice without fear, favour, affection or ill-will. The secrecy oath prevents direct or indirect disclosure except as required for due discharge. Salary and allowances are determined by the State Legislature; the CM also receives the legislative entitlement attached to House membership and prescribed official facilities.

### 2. Council and administrative leadership

The CM recommends ministers, allocates and reshuffles portfolios, presides over meetings, coordinates departments, asks a minister to resign or recommends dismissal, and directs policy. The CM's resignation or death ends the entire Council; another minister's exit creates only a vacancy.

Beyond chairing meetings, the CM is the ministry's chief public spokesman, the principal crisis coordinator and the political head who supplies direction across the State services. These descriptions do not make the CM the appointing or disciplinary authority for every civil servant; service powers remain distributed by the Constitution, statutes and business rules. The CM instead aligns departments, resolves inter-ministerial conflict and converts Cabinet policy into coordinated administration.

### 3. Governor, Legislature and caretaker continuity

Article 167 makes the CM the principal communication channel. The CM communicates decisions and legislative proposals, provides requested information and places a minister-only decision before the Council if required. This allows counsel and warning without two governments.

The CM advises on sessions and dissolution, announces policy and leads the legislative programme. The CM also advises the Governor on important appointments—most visibly the other ministers and, where the governing provision so requires, offices within the State constitutional-administrative structure. This must not be converted into a claim that every formally gubernatorial appointment follows identical CM advice; the Constitution or statute may prescribe another consultation process. Membership of the Inter-State Council and NITI Aayog Governing Council and rotating vice-chairmanship of a Zonal Council are coordination roles. State planning-board arrangements vary.

Responsible government cannot disappear between ministries. After resignation or dissolution, the existing CM-led Council ordinarily continues as a caretaker until the successor assumes charge, because the Governor must have a Council available to advise. A caretaker avoids major policy commitments and appointments that can await the incoming government, but performs routine administration and responds to genuine emergencies. Continuity does not enlarge the Governor's personal executive field; gubernatorial “satisfaction” ordinarily remains advised satisfaction.

CM dominance can weaken Cabinet deliberation, but the remedy is collective responsibility, Assembly accountability, courts and audit—not enlarged gubernatorial policy control.

The Council is the constitutional body; the Cabinet is its smaller political core.

### 1. Appointment, size and membership

Other ministers are appointed on CM advice. Article 164(1A), inserted by the 91st Amendment, caps the total including CM at 15 per cent of Assembly strength, with minimum 12. The denominator is the Assembly, not both Houses. Article 164(1B) bars specified defectors from ministership.

A non-member may serve for six consecutive months and must then enter either House. The temporary exception does not waive eligibility: Assembly membership requires the applicable qualifications including age 25, Council membership age 30, and being merely an eligible voter is insufficient. Article 177 lets ministers speak and participate in either House and committees, but vote only in the House of membership. A Tribal Welfare Minister is required in Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh and Odisha; the 94th Amendment removed Bihar.

The Governor administers each minister's oaths of office and secrecy. The office oath concerns constitutional fidelity, sovereignty and integrity, faithful discharge and impartial justice; the secrecy oath protects official information except where disclosure is required for due discharge. Ministerial salaries and allowances are determined by the State Legislature.

### 2. Responsibility and composition

Collective responsibility means the Council stands or falls together before the Assembly. A no-confidence defeat requires all ministers, including MLC ministers, to leave. Cabinet decisions bind ministers publicly; a dissenting minister must defend or resign.

Individual responsibility means a minister formally serves during gubernatorial pleasure, but removal while the ministry survives operates on CM advice. There is no countersignature-based legal-responsibility system.

Rank indicates function, not separate constitutional responsibility:

| Rank | Functional position |
|---|---|
| Cabinet Minister | Heads an important department, belongs to the Cabinet, attends its meetings and participates in major policy decisions. |
| Minister of State with independent charge | Heads a department without a Cabinet Minister above it, but is not ordinarily a Cabinet member; attends when specially invited for relevant business. |
| Minister of State attached | Assists a Cabinet Minister and handles allocated work; attends Cabinet only by special invitation. |
| Deputy Minister | Has no independent departmental charge, assists the senior minister and is not a Cabinet member. |

The Deputy CM label is a political designation, not a separate constitutional office.

### 3. Cabinet and its committees

The Cabinet is the Council's smaller substantive decision nucleus. It formulates major policy, coordinates departments, advises the Governor through responsible government, manages crises, settles important legislative and financial proposals and considers higher appointments where the applicable law and business rules place them before government.

Cabinet committees reduce the load and permit focused coordination. **Standing committees** continue across a class of recurring business; **ad hoc committees** are temporary and problem-specific. They are constituted and arranged under the CM's leadership as governmental needs require. A committee may formulate proposals and, within delegated authority, take decisions, but the Cabinet retains power to review them. These are instruments of collective decision, not rival constitutional executives.

### 4. Advocate General

Article 165 requires a person qualified to be a High Court judge. The Advocate General advises the State Government on legal matters, performs legal duties referred or assigned by the Governor, and discharges functions conferred by the Constitution or law. The office continues during gubernatorial pleasure, and remuneration is determined by the Governor.

The Advocate General has a right of audience in all courts within the State's territory. That professional court right is distinct from Article 177, which permits speaking and participation in either House and legislative committees without a vote. The Advocate General need not be a sitting High Court judge.

**Traps:** responsibility is to the Assembly; cap uses Assembly strength; qualification is not current judgeship.

### 2. Judicial controls

A practical package needs transparent criteria, formal State consultation, balanced appointment, reason standards for removal, prompt reasoned Article 200 action, published formation guidance, early floor tests and statutory clarity on Chancellor roles.

Both select and coordinate ministers, lead legislative government and depend on lower-House confidence. The CM operates in a field more directly affected by reservation, Article 356 reporting and a Union-appointed constitutional head.

For a Governor controversy: identify Article, state advice default, isolate exact exception, name procedure/case control, give federal verdict. For CM/Council: begin with Assembly confidence, explain CM coordination, collective responsibility and 91st Amendment limits.

# CUMULATIVE CONCEPT CHECKS

## Check 1 - Majority and responsible government

**Question:** When objective material creates genuine doubt about a ministry's majority, what constitutional sequence should follow?

**Model answer:** The Governor should avoid factional adjudication, obtain House-based verification through a prompt Assembly floor test and act on the demonstrated result. Mere intra-party dissent or private assurances cannot replace the confidence chamber.

**Misconception to avoid:** A floor test is not a device for resolving every party quarrel; it tests governmental confidence on objective grounds.

## Check 2 - Articles 200 and 201

**Question:** Trace the two different repassage consequences under Articles 200 and 201.

**Model answer:** Under Article 200, once a returned non-Money Bill is repassed, the Governor cannot withhold it and must assent or reserve. If reservation activates Article 201 and the President directs return, later State repassage does not compel presidential assent; the President may still assent or withhold.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not transfer Article 200's bar on renewed gubernatorial withholding to the President's distinct Article 201 choice.

## Check 3 - Bounded executive powers

**Question:** What common principle limits ordinance and clemency powers?

**Model answer:** Both are constitutional executive powers operating within defined jurisdiction, responsible-government advice and judicially reviewable legality. Ordinances remain temporary and Legislature-facing; clemency remains tied to the relevant offence-law field and is not a merits appeal.

**Misconception to avoid:** Formal exercise by the Governor does not make either power a personal prerogative.

## Check 4 - Chief Minister and Council

**Question:** How do eligibility, ministry size and collective responsibility combine to structure the real State executive?

**Model answer:** A qualified non-member may serve only for six months before entering either House; voter status alone is insufficient. The ministry is capped at fifteen per cent of Assembly strength with minimum twelve, while the whole Council remains collectively responsible to the Assembly under CM leadership.

**Misconception to avoid:** Bicameral membership does not divide confidence responsibility between the two Houses.

# ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS MODEL PRACTICE

## Final 10-mark question - 150 words

**Question:** Distinguish constitutional discretion from situational discretion of the Governor.

**Directive decoding:** Distinguish requires a shared frame, separate sources, examples and a caution against overlap.

**Model answer (140 words):**

Constitutional discretion arises from express text or a function necessarily assigned outside ordinary State-Cabinet advice. Examples include choosing among Article 200 courses under the 20 November 2025 opinion, reporting possible constitutional breakdown under Article 356, and specially worded responsibilities under Article 371 or the Sixth Schedule.

Situational discretion arises from a temporary political condition in which no settled adviser exists or confidence is disputed. It includes selecting a CM in a hung Assembly, requiring a prompt floor test on objective material, and considering dissolution advice from a ministry that has lost majority.

Both are exceptional. *Shamsher Singh* makes advice the rule; *Nabam Rebia* prevents Article 163 from creating general autonomy. *Bommai*, *Rameshwar Prasad* and *Subhash Desai* require House-based verification and reject speculation or party-dispute management.

Thus constitutional discretion is text/function-based, while situational discretion is transition-based. Neither authorises a parallel government.

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes source, examples, purpose and controls.

**Scoring rubric (10 marks):** common frame 1; constitutional category 3; situational category 3; cases 2; conclusion 1.

## Final 15-mark question - 250 words

**Question:** Examine whether the constitutional design of the Governor adequately protects Indian federalism.

**Directive decoding:** Examine whether requires design strengths, structural weaknesses, safeguards and a graded answer.

**Model answer (180 words):**

The Governor's design seeks to reconcile State parliamentary government with national constitutional unity. The office supplies continuity, authenticates State action and facilitates exceptional transitions. Presidential appointment creates a federal link; reservation and Article 356 reporting provide constitutional checks.

The same features produce asymmetry. Articles 155-156 place appointment and pleasure tenure effectively with the Union executive. In opposition-ruled States, invitations, floor tests, delayed Bills and university disputes can create a partisan counter-government. Personal neutrality is an inadequate safeguard against institutional incentives.

Doctrine partly protects federalism. *Shamsher Singh* makes State advice the norm. *Bommai* renders Article 356 reviewable and locates majority on the floor. *Rameshwar Prasad* rejects speculative dissolution; *Nabam Rebia* narrows session discretion; *Subhash Desai* requires objective material. The November 2025 opinion bars indefinite Article 200 inaction but rejects rigid deadlines and deemed assent. *B.P. Singhal* bars arbitrary removal without creating secure tenure.

Sarkaria favoured an eminent outsider and CM consultation; Punchhi sought stronger tenure/removal safeguards; NCRWC proposed a consultative committee.

The design contains federal safeguards but does not adequately secure neutrality. It needs joint selection, tenure protection and reviewable procedure.

**Why this earns marks:** It evaluates purpose against incentives, cases and implementable reform.

**Scoring rubric (15 marks):** strengths 3; weakness 4; cases 4; reforms 2; verdict 2.

## Final 20-mark question - 250 words

**Question:** Evaluate the Governor-Chief Minister relationship as the central working relationship of State parliamentary government.

**Directive decoding:** Evaluate requires mechanism, strengths, conflict points, controls and overall judgment.

**Model answer (198 words):**

The Governor-CM relationship joins constitutional form to democratic command. Article 154 formally vests power in the Governor; Articles 163-164 place ordinary exercise with the CM-led Council responsible to the Assembly. Article 167 makes the CM the information channel, while Article 166 organises action in the Governor's name.

Normal operation is cooperative. The CM selects and coordinates ministers, leads policy and advises on sessions. The Governor receives decisions, seeks information, counsels and performs formal acts. *Shamsher Singh* ensures that gubernatorial satisfaction ordinarily means advised governmental satisfaction.

Conflict arises at constitutional margins. In a hung House, appointment must lead promptly to a floor test. *Bommai* and *Rameshwar Prasad* reject subjective majority estimates and speculative dissolution. *Subhash Desai* requires objective material and excludes intra-party dispute management. Article 200 creates a specific discretionary Bill stage; the November 2025 opinion bars indefinite inaction but denies rigid deadlines and deemed assent. Article 356 reports remain reviewable.

Structural tension persists because a Union-appointed, pleasure-tenure Governor deals with an elected CM. Reform should improve appointment consultation and tenure, require reasons and codify formation procedure without eliminating flexibility.

The relationship works when the Governor warns without governing and the CM informs without treating constitutional scrutiny as hostility.

**Why this earns marks:** It treats the relationship as a mechanism and tests conflict points.

**Scoring rubric (20 marks):** mechanism 5; normal functions 4; conflict/cases 6; reforms 3; judgment 2.

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

## State executive spine

- Articles 153-162: Governor; 163-164: advice and ministers; 165: Advocate General; 166: business; 167: CM duties.
- Governor is constitutional head; CM-led Council is real executive.
- Council is collectively responsible to the Assembly.
- Article 154 formal vesting does not create personal policy power.

## Appointment and entry

- Article 155: presidential appointment by warrant.
- Article 157: citizen and age 35.
- Article 158: no legislative seat or other office of profit; emoluments protected.
- Outsider status and CM consultation are conventions, not qualifications.
- Article 159 oath by High Court Chief Justice or senior-most available judge.
- Article 153 proviso allows one person for multiple States; Article 160 covers contingencies.

## Tenure and immunity

- Article 156: five years subject to pleasure; resignation to President; continuation until successor.
- Transfer and reappointment possible.
- *B.P. Singhal*: no arbitrary, capricious, unreasonable or mala fide removal; no fixed tenure created.
- Article 361: no criminal proceeding may be instituted or continued, and no court process for arrest or imprisonment may issue during the term; personal civil action requires two months' notice.
- Governmental action remains reviewable; Article 194 member privilege does not apply.

## Executive, legislative and financial powers

- Article 166: executive action in Governor's name; authentication and business rules.
- CM appointed; other ministers only on CM advice.
- Appointment and removal authorities may differ for SEC/SPSC.
- Article 167: communicate decisions, furnish information, refer minister-only decision to Council.
- Chancellor roles depend on State statutes.
- Article 168: part of Legislature, not a House member.
- Article 174 powers ordinarily follow advice; Articles 175-176 concern messages/address.
- Article 171: nominate one-sixth of Legislative Council, never Assembly.
- Nomination fields: literature, science, art, cooperative movement and social service.
- Articles 180/184: temporary presiding arrangements when the named presiding offices are vacant.
- Article 192: Governor decides Article 191(1) disqualification after obtaining and acting according to Election Commission opinion.
- Budget, Money Bill/grant recommendation, Contingency Fund and State Finance Commission are formal financial functions.

## Advice and discretion

- *Shamsher Singh*: advice default; satisfaction ordinarily advised.
- Article 163 differs from Article 74 but creates no general autonomy.
- Constitutional discretion: Article 200, Article 356 report and specified texts.
- Situational discretion: hung House, leadership vacancy, proved loss and dissolution after loss.
- Special responsibilities are State- and subject-specific: regional equity in Maharashtra/Gujarat and Karnataka; specially worded law-and-order roles in Nagaland and Arunachal Pradesh; Hill Areas committee responsibility in Manipur; and peace/equitable advancement in Sikkim.
- Article 371B provides an Assam tribal-areas Assembly committee mechanism, not the same individual-judgment formula as Articles 371A and 371H.
- Sixth Schedule paragraph 9 sends disputed autonomous-district mineral-royalty shares in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram to the Governor for discretionary determination.
- Article 371J's constitutional text says “Hyderabad-Karnataka region”; Kalyana Karnataka is updated public usage.
- *Nabam Rebia*: discretion narrow; sessions ordinarily advice-bound.
- Article 163(3) protects advice itself, not resulting action.

## Government formation

- Clear majority: appoint recognised leader.
- Hung House: likely confidence-holder plus prompt floor test.
- No absolute single-largest-party right against majority coalition.
- *Bommai*: floor test and reviewable Article 356.
- *Rameshwar Prasad*: speculative dissolution unconstitutional.
- *Subhash Desai*: objective material; dissent alone insufficient; no restoration after CM resignation.
- CM cannot be dismissed while confidence continues.

## Articles 200-201 - status at 1 October 2026

- Controlling date: 20 November 2025, 2025 INSC 1333.
- Article 200: assent; reserve; withhold-and-return non-Money Bill.
- Money Bill cannot be returned.
- If a returned non-Money Bill is repassed, the Governor cannot withhold again and must assent or reserve.
- Do not carry that rule into Article 201: after presidential return and State repassage, the President may still assent or withhold.
- Mandatory reservation where High Court position is endangered.
- Article 200 selection treated as specific discretion.
- No rigid judicial timeline or automatic deemed assent.
- Limited mandamus may address indefinite unexplained inaction.
- April 2025 Tamil Nadu timeline/deemed-assent approach is qualified by later opinion.
- Article 201: President assents/withholds; may direct return; State reconsiders within six months; re-passage does not compel assent.
- Governor has no renewed role after reservation.
- Article 143 opinion is authoritative but advisory.

## Ordinance and clemency

- Article 213: Legislature not fully sitting plus immediate necessity; advice-bound and competence-limited.
- Must be laid; ordinarily ends six weeks after reassembly.
- Presidential instruction needed in specified equivalent-Bill cases.
- *D.C. Wadhwa*: serial re-promulgation is constitutional fraud.
- *Krishna Kumar Singh*: laying mandatory; satisfaction reviewable; effects not automatically permanent.
- Article 161 follows State offence-law competence and includes all listed clemency forms.
- *Raj Kumar @ Bittu*: do not claim Governor can never pardon death sentence.
- *A.G. Perarivalan*: State-Cabinet advice binds the Governor and unexplained delay is reviewable; the Supreme Court's ultimate release direction used Article 142, not Article 161.
- Article 161 lacks court-martial and universal death-sentence limbs.
- Article 233 district judges: High Court consultation; Article 234 other judicial service: both High Court and SPSC consultation.

## Chief Minister, Council and Advocate General

- CM may come from either House; a qualified non-member has six months.
- Voter status alone is insufficient; minimum age is 25 for Assembly membership and 30 for Council membership.
- Assembly confidence limits pleasure.
- Governor administers the CM's and ministers' oaths of office and secrecy; State Legislature determines salaries and allowances.
- CM chooses, allocates and coordinates ministers, speaks for government, leads crisis response and supplies political direction to administration within legal service structures.
- CM advises on important appointments where the governing law assigns that role; resignation/death ends Council.
- A resigned ministry ordinarily continues as a restrained caretaker until its successor assumes charge, preserving continuous advice to the Governor.
- 91st Amendment: ministry including CM no more than 15% of Assembly, minimum 12; defector bar.
- Collective responsibility to Assembly; Cabinet is smaller than Council.
- Cabinet Ministers head important departments and decide policy; Ministers of State may hold independent charge or assist; Deputy Ministers have no independent charge.
- Cabinet formulates major policy, coordinates departments, handles crises and major legislative/financial business; standing committees are continuing and ad hoc committees temporary, subject to Cabinet review.
- Tribal Welfare Minister required in Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh and Odisha.
- Advocate General: Article 165; HC-judge qualification; legal advice and assigned duties; constitutional/statutory functions; pleasure tenure; remuneration determined by Governor; right of audience in all State courts.
- Article 177 separately permits legislative speaking and participation without vote.

## Reform and answer routes

- Sarkaria: eminent outsider, non-partisan, CM consultation, compelling removal reason.
- Punchhi: fixed tenure and a State-legislature removal/impeachment proposal.
- NCRWC committee: PM, Union Home Minister, Lok Sabha Speaker and concerned State CM.
- Recommendations are not law.
- Governor answer: Article → advice default → exact exception → procedure/case → federal verdict.
- CM/Council answer: Assembly confidence → CM coordination → collective responsibility → 91st Amendment.
- Best verdict: Governor warns without governing; CM informs without treating scrutiny as hostility.
