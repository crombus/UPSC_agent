A fast-preparation edition built for answer-writing. **The one idea that organises everything.** The source chapter is not a survey of feminist schools. It is a single argument with a two-limbed verdict at the end. Nature divides the human race into men and women; **culture** converts that division into a difference of **status and role**; the conversion was once functional, outlived its function, and was then concealed by honour rather than corrected by power; and so the remedy has to reach **power**, not only provision. The chapter closes on exactly that: **"feminist perspective on justice calls for securing the overall development of women, including improvement of their health and education as well as giving them adequate share in economic and political power"** (PDF p.93). Place a stem inside that argument and it becomes answerable. ## Four standing qualifications

Each is stated **once, here**, and assumed thereafter. ❌ They are not repeated lesson by lesson.

- **Q1 — The distinction concedes the difference.** The hinge sentence opens **"Nature has divided human race between men and women"** before it says anything about culture. ❌ An answer that denies biological difference has misread the chapter and has thrown away the hedges that protect the construction claim. The chapter's own position is that the **division** is natural and the **status and role** are cultural.
- **Q2 — Every number in this chapter is 2009 or earlier.** The 1980 United Nations ratio, the legislature percentages, the 1995 index ranks and the 2005–2007 heads of state are Fifth-Edition statements, and every *today*, *now*, *currently*, *recent* and *contemporary* in the chapter is 2009's. They travel **with their dates attached** or not at all. ❌ One sentence is unusable even with a date: the chapter's **"The condition remains more or less unchanged today"** is asserted with no evidence and its *today* is 2009's; it is reproduced in this edition only to be marked unusable.
- **Q3 — The chapter criticises itself, and that is the asset.** Three in-text tensions are live: liberal feminism is **"the most popular stream of feminist movement, but it is not considered to be very influential"**; the preferred second view exempts women from **"hazardous tasks, like underground mining and working in night shifts"** while an earlier page makes **removal of barriers** the nineteenth-century achievement; and the endorsement **"this view seems to be more reasonable and is widely endorsed"** is hedged twice and argued nowhere. ❌ Do not smooth any of them. They let a candidate criticise the source from inside the source, which is the cheapest route to a *critical* mark.

*Three sentences, three grammars: a four-limbed definition, a one-word history, and a sentence split at its comma. Reproducing any one of them takes fifteen seconds in the examination hall and converts an assertion into evidence.*

### 1. The definition, at full length

Feminism **"stands for the concern with the status and role of women in society in relation to men"**. It holds that **"women have suffered and are still suffering injustice because of their sex; hence it seeks effective measures for the redressal of that injustice."** And then the summary clause: **"In short, it implies a voice of protest against the inferior status accorded to women in society, which is the product of the institution of 'patriarchy', and not based on reason."**

⚠️ Both owners carry a one-line gloss of this. The printed sentence has four limbs and the fourth is load-bearing. **"Not based on reason"** is the warrant for every *is this natural?* stem, because it asserts that the status is unjustified independently of whether the biological difference is real. ⭐ It is the opening line for the 2019, 2022 and 2025 construction stems. And **"seeks effective measures for the redressal"** is the clause that makes feminism **remedial** — useful the moment an examiner or a co-candidate frames feminism as protest without programme.

### 2. Patriarchy, and why the arrow matters more than the gloss

The printed box reads: **"It literally means 'rule of the father'. Originally this term was used to describe a social system based on the authority of male head of the household. Now it is applied to denote male domination in general, including its occurrence in labour market as well as domestic division of labour."**

❗ Both owners carry the gloss and drop the **originally → now** movement. That movement *is* the argument. A term that began by describing who ran a household now describes a pattern of domination that shows up **in the labour market**, which is to say in the public economy. The advanced owner's second answer-engine prompt — *why is patriarchy a political category and not merely a family arrangement?* — is answered by reading the box's two sentences in order. So is the 2020 Essay prompt on patriarchy as a structure of social inequality, whose entire difficulty is showing that a private-looking thing is structural.

### 3. The Enlightenment claim: feminism as an immanent extension

**"Early feminism emerged in the wake of Enlightenment, which sought to enlarge the scope of 'rights of man' so as to include equal rights to women therein."** The glossary box dates the Enlightenment to **"an intellectual movement of eighteenth century France, Germany and Great Britain"**, a period when **"people's religious and political life was set free from obscure and orthodox beliefs and new light was shed on the conduct of human affairs"**, producing **"a new outlook, informed by reason and power of scientific research and discovery"** in which **"old superstitions were discarded, old fears were dispelled."**

❗ Absent from both owners, and it supplies feminism's **internal** warrant. The demand is not external to the rights tradition; it is that tradition's own vocabulary applied without an arbitrary exception. That is the historically correct and the strongest available reply to *"feminism is a Western import"* or *"feminism is a sectional creed"* — and note the shape of the reply: it does not deny the European origin, it denies that the claim is foreign to the rights framework the objector is already using. ⚠️ The Enlightenment's own tenets belong to **Topic 03**, `basic\03_Liberalism-and-Neoliberalism.md`; this edition uses only Gauba's one clause about the enlargement of *rights of man*. ➡️ Rights doctrine and generations of rights belong to **Topic 17**, `basic\17_Human-Rights-Civil-Liberties-and-Democratic-Rights.md`.

### 4. Three named thinkers, two titled works, four dates

In Britain, **"free-thinking women like Mary Wollstonecraft (1759-97) and Harriet Taylor (1807-59) made a fervent appeal for equal rights of women."** Later **John Stuart Mill (1806-73)**, in his essay ***The Subjection of Women* (1869)**, argued that **"women were by no means less talented than men and hence deserved equal rights with men"** — restated at p.88 as women being **"in no way inferior to men in their talents"**, with Mill pleading **"to give them full legal and political rights."**

❗ **Neither owner names Harriet Taylor at all, and neither carries a single title or a single date.** Three names, two titled works and four dates is the whole distance between an assertion and evidence. ⚠️ On Taylor's dates: the chapter prints **(1807-59)**; standard reference gives **1807–1858**. The chapter's form is reproduced inside reproduced wording; the standard form is used in a candidate's own voice. Nothing in the argument turns on it, because the chapter attaches no work and no thesis to her — which is itself worth one honest clause in an answer.

⭐ **Mill's two-step is the structure of every liberal-feminist argument and takes one line:** a **capability** claim (*by no means less talented*) grounds a **rights** claim (*full legal and political rights*). Keep the order. An answer that asserts the right without the capability premise has skipped the argument.

### 5. The two-limbed definition of feminist theory, and why it refuses a disjunction

At p.86 the chapter restates its object: **"Feminism or feminist theory implies the advocacy of equal rights for women and men, accompanied by the commitment to improve the position of women in society. It seeks restoration of justice for women who remained deprived of equal status and opportunities vis-a-vis men since earliest times."**

❗ Read **"accompanied by"**. ### 1. The three causes, and why they make Lesson 5 cheap

❗ This single sentence is **the whole of socialist feminism stated in advance**, and it is absent from both owners. ### 2. The enforcement device and its sanction

The chapter does not merely say that masculinity and femininity exist. ❗ That is the **sanction**, and a norm without a sanction is only a description. - **Natural inequality** describes **"the inequality of age, health, beauty, physical and intellectual capacities of different people, which were created by nature"**; these **"are largely unalterable."**
- **Conventional inequalities** represent **"disparities of wealth, prestige and power among different individuals"**; they are **"the product of our social arrangements"**; **"we can undertake a critical examination of these inequalities from the point of view of justice, and can reduce them by altering our social arrangements"**; in other words **"conventional inequalities are alterable."**

❗ Both owners say *"Rousseau's distinction"* with no essay, no date and no contents on either side. The contents are what makes the move work: the instant **power** is listed among conventional inequalities, gender hierarchy — which is a distribution of power — falls inside the alterable column **by definition**, not by assertion. ⚠️ Note also that the alterability claim comes with a method attached: **critical examination from the point of view of justice**. That is the chapter licensing evaluation, not merely description.

### 3. The reversal, which is the chapter's hardest point

**"It has now been proved beyond doubt that biological differences between man and woman do not justify inferior status of women. On the contrary, cultural factors have contributed to accentuate the biological differences between men and women. The rapid improvement in women's athletic records in recent decades is an indication that social norms had shaped biology and restricted women's physical development. Feminist anthropologists have pointed out that in some ethnic groups physical differentiation between men and women is not as prominent as in others."**

❗ Read the direction of travel. The ordinary construction claim runs *nature → culture*: a biological difference acquires cultural meaning. This passage runs the arrow **backwards**: culture has **accentuated the biological difference itself**. If some of the measured physical gap is the residue of restricted development, then the "natural" column of the chart is partly a cultural product, and an opponent who points to a physical difference as the ground of a role has pointed to something partly produced by the role. ⭐ As a finishing move on **2022 Q3(b)** this is worth more than any amount of additional definition, because it does not merely separate nature from culture — it shows the separation is not clean in the direction the opponent needs.

⚠️ **Sourcing limit, stated at the point of use.** **"Proved beyond doubt"** is the chapter's own confidence, not a citation. The athletic illustration names **no sport, no figure, no period and no record**; the anthropological illustration names **no study, no scholar, no group and no society**. ❌ Therefore cite this passage as **the chapter's claim**, never as a demonstrated result, and never write *studies show*. ✅ The correct form in an answer is: *the chapter goes further, asserting that cultural factors have accentuated the biological differences and offering women's athletic improvement and anthropological variation as indications.* The word **indication** is the chapter's own and is appropriately weak; keep it.

### 5. The second internal tension: choice against necessity

❗ The chapter's historical paragraph (taught in full at Lesson 4) says that biological differences **"necessitated the division of labour"**, and that women were **"constrained to undertake child-bearing and rearing"** — and in the same two sentences says that men **"chose to go out for hunting"** and women **"chose to remain at home."** ⚠️ **Necessitated** and **constrained** are not compatible with **chose**, and the chapter never reconciles them.

### Concept check

**Question:** A candidate writes: "Gender inequality is conventional, therefore alterable, therefore unjustified." Identify what is correct, what is missing, and one qualification the chapter itself would insist on.

**Model answer:** **Correct.** The inference from *conventional* to *alterable* is the chapter's own: conventional inequalities concern **"wealth, prestige and power"**, are **"the product of our social arrangements"**, and **"can be reduced by altering our social arrangements."** Since gender hierarchy is a distribution of power, it falls in that column, and the chart's final row states the consequence — **Alterable**. **Missing.** *Alterable* does not by itself entail *unjustified*; the chapter supplies the second step separately, holding that the deprivations **"had no logical connection with her biological status as a woman"** and that the inferior status is **"not based on reason."** Alterability establishes that change is possible; the no-connection clause establishes that change is owed. An answer that collapses the two has skipped the normative premise. **Qualification.** The chapter concedes in its own voice that **"nature has divided human race between men and women"** and files that division under **natural inequality**; and its historical paragraph says biological differences **"necessitated"** the original division of labour and that women were **"constrained"**. ⚠️ So the defensible claim is that the **status and role** are conventional, not that nothing in the arrangement ever had a biological ground. ❗ There is also a tension the chapter leaves open: a division into two kinds is a classification, whereas Rousseau's natural inequality concerns differences of degree.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not treat the counter-arrow — culture accentuating biological difference — as proof that biological differences are unreal. The chapter's claim is that part of the observed gap is a residue of restricted development, offered as an **indication** with no study named. Overstating it converts the chapter's strongest critical move into an unsupported assertion.

**Original Mains practice [10 marks]:** Explain how Rousseau's distinction between natural and conventional inequality is used to show that gender inequality is alterable. *(Explain — answer in about 150 words.)*

**Model (within 150 words):** **J. J. Rousseau (1712-78)**, in ***A Discourse on the Origin of Inequality* (1755)**, separated two kinds. **Natural inequality** covers **"age, health, beauty, physical and intellectual capacities"**, **"created by nature"** and **"largely unalterable."** **Conventional inequality** covers **"disparities of wealth, prestige and power"**, **"the product of our social arrangements"**, open to **"critical examination … from the point of view of justice"**, and therefore **"alterable."** Gauba's move is single: gender hierarchy is a distribution of **status, prestige and power**, so it belongs to the second category, and the chapter's chart states the result — Sex is **Product of Nature** and **Unalterable**, Gender is **Product of Culture**, **Conventional Inequality** and **Alterable**. ⚠️ Two limits: the chapter itself files the two-sex division under natural inequality; and alterability shows change is **possible**, while **"no logical connection"** shows it is **owed**.

*The ladder descends: each rung is the chapter's own explanation of the next, and the floor is the part that converts a historical narrative into an entitlement claim a conditions-stem can be built on.*

### 1. The lag, which is the argument

The chapter's question is historical: **"If injustice against women has existed since earliest times, why has it come to the forefront only in recent times?"** Its answer is a **lag** argument, and lag arguments are rare and valuable because they explain both why an arrangement was tolerated and why it stopped being tolerable.

Move one: a division of labour that was once functional — hunting and hazardous work against child-bearing and rearing — **"based on mutual care and adjustment"**, and which **"did not involve any significant level of resentment."** Move two, the engine: **"with the development of technology, sweeping changes took place in other parts of social organization, but the division of labour between men and women remained more or less unchanged."** ❗ The function that justified the arrangement dissolved; the arrangement did not. Meanwhile **"man as head of the family, of the clan and of the tribe acquired more and more power"** while woman **"largely continued to hold the subordinate position"** — so the gap did not merely persist, it **widened**, because new forms of power accrued on one side only.

⚠️ Reproduce the first move honestly, including **"necessitated"** and **"constrained"**, and including the chapter's unreconciled **"chose"**. A candidate who presents the original division as pure imposition has made the chapter's argument weaker, not stronger: the force of a lag argument comes precisely from conceding that the arrangement once had a reason.

### 2. The pedestal: compensation instead of correction

What filled the gap is the part examiners reward. Woman **"was given some concessions and exemptions from strenuous and hazardous tasks as she was regarded the 'weaker sex'"**; being **"sexually (and also perhaps emotionally) vulnerable, she was not allowed to mix with strangers"**; **"shyness was eulogized as a woman's ornament"**; her beauty was **"admired in poetry, music, paintings and other works of art"**; she was recognised as the **"'fair sex', endowed with special dignity"**; manners like **"'ladies first' were evolved to confirm that dignity"**; and helping **"'a damsel in distress' was admired as an act of chivalry among men."**

❗ This is **honour substituted for power**, and the chapter names the substitution without sentiment: **"However, in spite of so much importance accorded to woman in social life, she was systematically deprived of her share in power."** ⭐ Then the single most quotable formulation available on this topic: **"She was given security but not an opportunity to learn certain things that would make her as competent as man, and thereby vindicate her claim to equality."**

Note what that sentence does. It identifies the deprivation not as a shortage of goods but as a shortage of **the conditions of demonstrating competence** — and competence is what a claim to equality was then required to rest on. The structure is circular and the sentence exposes the circle: denied the training, she cannot demonstrate the capacity; lacking the demonstration, the denial appears justified. ⭐ It is also the pedestal-as-subordination move in full, which is the whole of the **2020 Essay prompt** on patriarchy as the least noticed structure of inequality — a structure stays unnoticed when it presents itself as **respect**.

### 3. The three deprivations and the clause that voids them

She was deprived of **"the right to ownership of property, right to vote and opportunities of education and higher learning even though these deprivations had no logical connection with her biological status as a woman."**

❗ The final clause is the whole entitlement argument in nine words, and it supplies the **necessity** half of a necessary/sufficient answer **in the chapter's own voice**. The reasoning is this: a deprivation is justified only by some connection between the trait and the thing withheld. No such connection exists here. Therefore the deprivations are arbitrary, and their removal is **owed** — which is a stronger claim than that their removal would be useful. ⚠️ And the three run together: property with no vote cannot defend itself politically; a vote with no education cannot be informed; education with no property leaves no independent material base. That is why the chapter lists them as one package, and it is why **2026 Q2(b)** must conclude *necessary but not sufficient* rather than *one of several useful measures*.

### 4. Wollstonecraft dated, and the nineteenth-century arc

**"When Mary Wollstonecraft (1759-97) published her essay *Vindication of the Rights of Woman* (1792), woman was not only restrained from voting, but was deemed unfit for education, was debarred from many occupations, and had no legal right to own property. She had no real right to divorce even if her husband abused her."** She **"forcefully challenged the prevailing belief in female inferiority"**, argued that **"women, like men, are rational individuals and should have equal rights"**, and — the clause that matters most — **"she established the principles on which campaigns for women's right to education, employment, property and the vote were later built up."**

❗ Five dated disabilities and an explicit claim about *why* she matters: she supplied the **principles**, not merely the protest. Both owners say she argued women are rational individuals, and no more.

Then the periodisation, which lets an answer sequence instead of list. **Nineteenth century:** feminist theory **"largely focused on removing educational and professional barriers from woman's life"**, and the reforming spirit **"was often quite militant which culminated in early twentieth century struggles for women's suffrage."** **Later, in the West:** demands moved to **"employment rights, equal pay and equality in social benefits, taxation and so on."** ⭐ Barriers → suffrage → material equality, in three clauses.

⚠️ **The chapter's concession to progress, with its hedge.** In the contemporary world, **"further advancement in technology, diversification of business, industry, administration, arts and professions"** and demand for **"new skills, talents, and professional competence, have given women the opportunity of proving their abilities"**; it is **"now realized that women are fit to perform most of the jobs that men do"**; and **"equal rights for women are no longer questioned in enlightened circles."** ❗ Reproduce **"in enlightened circles"**. The hedge concedes that the change is **uneven**, and a balanced verdict that drops the hedge has overstated the concession. ⚠️ Note also the symmetry with Rung 2: technology appears here as an **enabler**, having appeared earlier as the thing whose advance the division of labour failed to track. That double role is the key to **2021 Q4(b)**, and it is drawn out at Lesson 11.

### Concept check

**Question:** A conditions stem asks whether land and property rights are necessary, sufficient, or both, for gender equality. Using this chapter only, construct the **necessary** half and say precisely where the chapter stops being able to help.

**Model answer:** **The necessary half.** The chapter treats property not as one benefit among many but as one of three linked deprivations: she was deprived of **"the right to ownership of property, right to vote and opportunities of education and higher learning"**. The package also interlocks: property without the vote has no political defence, the vote without education cannot be informed, and education without property leaves no independent material base. Behind the package stands the verdict that she **"was systematically deprived of her share in power"** and **"was given security but not an opportunity"** — which identifies the deprivation as a denial of the means of demonstrating competence, the very thing on which her claim to equality was then made to rest. **Misconception to avoid:** Do not use the pedestal material as evidence that women were valued and therefore not subordinated. ### 1. Liberal feminism, and the verdict both owners drop

It **"aims at the revival of the conventional feminist movement"** and demands **"complete removal of gender-based discrimination in society"**; its programme includes **"equal pay for equal work, abortion laws reform, increasing representation of women in parliaments, bureaucracy and dignified professions, etc."** And then: **"This is the most popular stream of feminist movement, but it is not considered to be very influential."**

❗ **An in-text critical evaluation that both owners drop.** Both present liberal feminism neutrally, which leaves a candidate with no source-grounded criticism of the stream a question is most likely to assume. ⭐ A ready-made criticism **from the textbook itself** reads at a different level than an imported one. ⚠️ And the preceding clause explains the verdict: a programme that **"aims at the revival of the conventional"** is popular precisely because it is **familiar** — it asks for the extension of an agreed vocabulary rather than a change of vocabulary, which is why it attracts assent and why it changes less. ✅ Note also **abortion laws reform** as a printed programme item neither owner carries. ➡️ Equality of opportunity as a doctrine is **Topic 18**'s; this lesson uses it only as liberal feminism's inherited vocabulary.

### 2. Radical feminism: the irreducibility thesis, stated so that it can be argued

Firestone argued that women's subordination **"could not be understood as a symptom or aspect of some deeper or more comprehensive system of domination, such as racism or class-based division of society"**; that **"historically women constituted the first oppressed group"**; and that their subordination **"could not be eliminated by the changes such as the elimination of prejudice or even the abolition of class society."**

❗ The owners say *oppression is primary*. The printed version states an **irreducibility** thesis with **two named rivals** and a **counterfactual test**: abolish class, and the subordination would remain. That is the form in which the claim can be argued rather than asserted, and it is the pivot of **2019 Q1(e)** — *can gender equality be realised within a socialist regime?*

Then the mechanism. The basis **"was ultimately biological"**: **"human reproductive biology was responsible for considering women the weaker sex"**, and survival required **"that infants should depend on lactating women and women in turn, should depend on men."** The escape is named with instruments: the material conditions **"had finally been achieved in the twentieth century with the advent of reliable contraceptives, baby foods and 'test-tube babies'"**, which provided the means of **"freeing themselves from the tyranny of their reproductive biology and diffusing the child-bearing and child-rearing role to society as a whole, men as well as women."**

⭐ Two things to carry. The **dependence chain** — infants on lactating women, women on men — is what makes a biological cause compatible with a **non-biological remedy**, which is the paradox examiners test. And the **diffusion** clause is what makes Firestone's position a programme rather than a hope: the remedy is not that women stop reproducing but that the role is **redistributed to society as a whole, men as well as women**. ❌ Do not generalise Firestone's biological emphasis to feminism as such; the chapter presents it as the position of one named thinker.

### 3. Millett, and the derivation neither owner names

Millett argued that the relation between the sexes **"was based on power and further sustained by an ideology"**, that it was **"similar to the relationship between classes and races"**, and hence that **"it should be treated as political relationship."** Then the sentence that matters: **"Basing her analysis of women's subordination on Max Weber's theory of domination, Millett argued that men have exercised domination over women in two forms: through social authority and economic force."** The time had come **"to smash these implements of man's domination."**

❗ **Neither owner names Weber.** The derivation earns twice over: it tells the examiner the claim has a theoretical pedigree rather than being an assertion of grievance, and it supplies the chapter's own answer to *what makes a private relation political?* — namely **power plus ideology**, exactly as in any other relation of domination. ➡️ Weber's typology of authority, legitimacy and the faces of power are **Topic 15**'s, `basic\15_Power-Authority-and-Legitimacy.md`; this topic cites the derivation and does not expound it. ➡️ The conclusion that the relation **"should be treated as political relationship"** also borders **Topic 07**, `basic\07_Nature-of-Politics-and-Communitarianism.md`; ❌ what politics *is* is not taught here. ❗ Note in passing that Gauba's own example of men's and women's mutual claims becoming a public issue sits at **PDF p.98 — inside Topic 07's span, not this one** — and is therefore not used.

### 4. Socialist feminism, and the nine words that are the whole dual-systems thesis

It **"represents a combination of patriarchal analysis of radical feminism and class analysis of Marxism."** ⭐ Then: **"It implies that capitalists as well as men are the beneficiary of women's subordination."** Socialist feminists **"have particularly developed analysis of labour, both wage-labour and domestic labour"**, and **"have also considered the role of culture and psycho-analytical aspects of sexuality."**

❗ The **dual-beneficiary** sentence is the dual-systems thesis complete, and it is the fastest way to separate socialist from Marxist feminism: if **both** capitalists and men benefit, then neither system reduces to the other. ✅ **wage-labour and domestic labour** is also the chapter's own gesture toward social reproduction, which is the hinge Lesson 10 uses.

**Sheila Rowbotham (1943- )** advocated **"a participatory, decentralized approach to social change that contemplates linking of the struggles of all oppressed groups."** In ***Women, Resistance and Revolution* (1972)** and ***Hidden from History* (1973)** she **"tried to reclaim the past for women as a source of knowledge and strength that could contribute to their present struggle"**, continuing in ***The Past is before Us* (1989)**. **"As a Marxist"** she held that the struggle for women's liberation **"is essentially bound with the struggle against capitalism"**; she showed **"from historical evidence that class exploitation and women's oppression are closely linked phenomena"**; and success **"can be achieved only through combining these struggles."**

**Question:** A stem asks whether legal reform is sufficient for women's empowerment. Using this lesson only, build the strongest case that it is **not**, and state the boundary your answer must observe.

### 1. Why the falsification step is worth more than the conclusion

The conclusion — formal equality is insufficient — is available to anyone. What is rare is a **reason to believe it** that does not rest on asserting bad faith. The chapter supplies one: the preference explanation was a **testable** alibi, and a social change tested it. When **"the size of the family began to shrink and more and more women took up jobs"**, the claim that women were simply less interested in public affairs and **"had no time"** lost its basis; the time constraint eased and the domestic sphere contracted, yet the authority share did not move. ⭐ That is an elimination argument, and elimination arguments are what *critically examine* and *do you agree* directives reward, because they rule something out instead of merely preferring something.

⚠️ **Sourcing limit, stated at the point of use.** The voting-behaviour finding is **"some studies"** with **no author, no country, no date and no figure**. The US comparison is **"not very different"** with **no percentage**. And the Scandinavian exception is reported with **no explanation at all** — the chapter notes that the situation **"was not that bad"** and leaves it there. ❌ Therefore present all of this as **the chapter's report**, never as cited research, and ❌ never invent a cause for the Scandinavian figures. ✅ What the exception does establish, without any explanation, is that the deficit is **contingent**: it is not a universal feature of representative politics, because two named countries in the same period did substantially better. That is enough, and claiming more is a checkable error.

### 2. The Charter of Paris, and what to do with it

**"In November 1990 thirty-four Heads of Government of European countries gathered to sign the historic Charter of Paris for the New Europe. The gathering marked the end of the Cold War. In newspaper headlines it was described as the 'end of an era'. But feminists ask: 'Which era had come to an end?' In any case, it was not an end of the patriarchal era."** In the group photograph of the thirty-four, **"only two women could be spotted after a thorough search"** — **Gro Brundtland**, Prime Minister of **Norway**, and **Margaret Thatcher**, Prime Minister of **Britain**. **"Two days after this Conference, Mrs. Thatcher also resigned, and a man replaced her as Prime Minister."**

❗ This is the chapter's hardest illustration and it does three things at once. It **dates** a world-historical moment. It **reframes periodisation as a gendered question** — *which* era ended? — which is a move transferable to any claim that an era has closed. And its last sentence turns the single remaining counter-example **into further evidence**, which is as economical as argument gets. ⭐ It is the strongest available **Essay** opening for the 2020 patriarchy prompt and for any *end of an era* prompt.

⚠️ **Three precision points.** The chapter prints the title as *Charter of Paris for **the** New Europe*; the official CSCE instrument is *for **a** New Europe*. The chapter's form stands inside reproduced wording; the official form is used in this edition's own voice. ⚠️ The **thirty-four** count and the **group-photograph** claim are carried **as the chapter's**, not as independently verified. ✅ The November 1990 date, Brundtland's and Thatcher's offices at that date and Thatcher's resignation within days are consistent with standard references.

### 3. The chapter balances its own illustration — reproduce both halves

**"Happily, however, by the end of 2005, Germany had elected its first woman Chancellor. By the beginning of 2006, Chile and Liberia had elected their women Presidents. Then in 2007 India and Argentina had the distinction of having women Presidents."**

❗ A chapter that produced its hardest illustration and then **supplied the counter-evidence against itself** is modelling what a graded verdict looks like. Reproducing both halves is how an answer avoids reading as a complaint. ⚠️ It also dates the Fifth Edition's revision window, which is a useful discipline check: nothing after 2007 appears anywhere in this chapter.

### 4. Potential against opportunity — the single sharpest sentence in the chapter

Outside Europe and America, representation is **"very insignificant at the level of political authority although some women have been successful in attaining top positions"**: **Sri Lanka, Israel, India, Pakistan and Bangladesh** have had women Prime Ministers or Heads of Government. Some women have an excellent record in **"legislatures, cabinets, bureaucracy, diplomacy, journalism, legal profession, fine arts, academics and scientific research"**. And then: **"This record is enough proof of the potential of women's power. But it is no proof of the opportunities open to women as their share in these positions continues to be very meagre."**

⭐ This is the pre-built answer to the standard objection — *but India had a woman Prime Minister* — and it is better than a rebuttal because it **concedes the example first and then shows the example proves something else**. Individual attainment is evidence about **capacity**; it is not evidence about **access**, because a small number of exceptional outcomes is compatible with a closed distribution. ❗ The potential/opportunity distinction is portable to every empowerment stem, and it costs one sentence.

### Concept check

**Question:** How does the GDI/GEM decoupling settle whether provision for women is sufficient for their empowerment, and what does it leave unsettled?

**Model answer:** **What it settles.** It establishes the insufficiency as a **measured** result rather than a complaint. The two indices measure different things: GDI gender-adjusts development, while GEM is defined as **"the extent to which women participate in a country's economic and political life"** — a share-in-power measure. The report found **"no essential correlation between GDI and GEM in many cases"**, and the chapter states the meaning plainly: **"they have given adequate attention to health care and education of women, but no adequate share in the exercise of power."** The named case is the **'economic tigers' of East Asia**, where **"in spite of substantial improvement in the level of development of women, they have been denied a tangible share in economic and political power, in an essentially male-dominated society."** Finding 1 reinforces the point from the other direction: there is **"no correlation between gender bias and a country's economic development"**, and **Cuba**, **"a poor economy"**, ranks **72 on HDI, 47 on GDI and 16 on GEM**. So provision — health, education, even general prosperity — is demonstrably compatible with a continuing exclusion from power. **What it leaves unsettled.** It does not show what **would** be sufficient. The chapter names no case in which a share in power was secured and discrimination ended, offers no mechanism linking the two, and its own preferred instruments (Lesson 8) are endorsed without argument. ⚠️ All of this is 1995 data inside a 2009 text, and the indices themselves were retired by UNDP in 2010 in favour of the Gender Inequality Index.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not read Finding 1 as a claim that development is irrelevant to gender outcomes. The finding is that bias does not **correlate** with development, which cuts both ways — it denies that growth delivers equality, and equally denies that poverty explains inequality. The chapter's closing formula keeps **both** limbs: overall development **as well as** an adequate share in economic and political power.

**Original Mains practice [10 marks]:** Explain what the decoupling of GDI and GEM shows about the relation between development and women's empowerment. *(Explain — answer in about 150 words.)*

**Model (within 150 words):** The two indices in the **Human Development Report, 1995** measure different objects: GDI gender-adjusts development, while GEM is **"the extent to which women participate in a country's economic and political life."** The report found **"no essential correlation between GDI and GEM in many cases"**, which the chapter glosses as **"adequate attention to health care and education of women, but no adequate share in the exercise of power"**, illustrated by the **'economic tigers' of East Asia**, where improved development coexisted with denial of **"a tangible share in economic and political power, in an essentially male-dominated society."** The relation is therefore **one-directional and incomplete**: development can raise provision without transferring authority. ⚠️ The report's other finding runs the same way — no correlation between gender bias and economic development, with **Cuba** at **HDI 72, GDI 47, GEM 16**. Hence the chapter's two-limbed conclusion, development **as well as** power.

### 1. Provenance — what clarifies the distinction

🔶 **Simone de Beauvoir**, *The Second Sex* (**1949**), supplies the philosophical root of the distinction Gauba states flatly: womanhood as a **social becoming** rather than a fixed biological given. ⚠️ **Paraphrase, do not quote.** The exact wording is not independently verified in this edition's source base, and the owner's standing instruction is to prefer a named proposition to a risky verbatim line. ✅ The safe form is: *de Beauvoir's proposition that womanhood is socially produced rather than simply born.*

🔶 **Ann Oakley**, *Sex, Gender and Society* (**1972**), is usually credited with popularising the working terminology in sociology — **sex** as biological, **gender** as the culturally learned, historically variable content attached to it. One line, and it explains why the two words can be used as technical terms at all.

🔶 **Gayle Rubin**, "The Traffic in Women" (**1975**), supplies the **sex/gender system**: the social arrangements that transform biological sex into a hierarchy of gendered obligation, kinship exchange and constrained sexuality. ❗ **This is the strongest single enrichment on the topic**, because it *mechanises* what Gauba asserts. Gauba says sex becomes gender; Rubin says **how**. Where a stem asks for the process rather than the distinction, Rubin is the one name worth importing.

⚠️ **Anti-origin-story caveat, mandatory.** ❌ Do not present a single, uncontested origin for the sex/gender distinction. It has **multiple co-formulators** and was consolidated gradually; cite these three as standard provenance and avoid taxonomic overclaim.

### 2. Marxist feminism distinguished from socialist feminism

🔶 Gauba and the basic owner both use **socialist feminism** as the umbrella term. **Marxist feminism** is a related but analytically prior strand that a precise stem may require. **Claim:** women's oppression is generated principally by the capitalist mode of production, especially unpaid **domestic and reproductive labour** that reproduces the workforce free of charge. **Evidence:** the **1970s domestic-labour debate** treated housework as unwaged reproduction of labour power necessary to accumulation. **Significance:** it relocates the "private" household inside political economy, making family labour a class question rather than only a cultural one. **Limit:** a pure class reduction cannot explain patriarchal violence or control of sexuality that predates and outlasts capitalism — which is exactly why Rowbotham's synthesis treats patriarchy and capitalism as **two distinct, mutually reinforcing systems** rather than one reducible to the other.

❗ The in-source hinge is Gauba's own clause that socialist feminists **"have particularly developed analysis of labour, both wage-labour and domestic labour"** — he names the object without naming the debate. ⚠️ **Examination use:** if a stem says *Marxist feminism*, lead with the domestic-labour and social-reproduction claim; if it says *socialist feminism*, lead with the dual-beneficiary synthesis. The labels overlap and are not synonyms. ➡️ Historical materialism, surplus value and class conflict belong to `Political-Theory\basic\04_Marxism-and-Neo-Marxism.md`; ❌ none of it is taught here.

### 3. Two critiques that reach liberal theory

🔶 **Carole Pateman**, *The Sexual Contract* (**1988**). **Claim:** classical social-contract theory silently assumes a prior **sexual contract** securing men's access to women's bodies and labour before the "original" social contract, so the public contract of citizens rests on an unmentioned private contract of subordination. **Evidence:** contract narratives are silent on the marriage contract and the domestic subordination that make male civic equality possible. **Significance:** it reframes liberal equality as resting on an unexamined patriarchal foundation. ⚠️ **Limit, mandatory:** the thesis reconstructs contract theory's **silences**. It is an **interpretive lens, not a documented historical event**, and ❌ must never be used as a factual claim that any contract was signed. ❗ It pairs exactly with Gauba's own clause that **"even the more personal and private domain of sexuality needed to be redefined"** and with the pedestal argument of Lesson 4, which makes it the best available Essay move on the 2020 prompt after the Charter of Paris. ➡️ The sexual-contract critique of citizenship belongs to **Topic 16**, `basic\16_Citizenship-and-Its-Critiques.md`.

🔶 **Susan Moller Okin**, *Justice, Gender, and the Family* (**1989**). **Claim:** theories of justice wrongly treat the family as a pre-political given rather than a site justice must examine. **Evidence:** the unequal domestic burden restricts women's access to the time, income and position that public justice is meant to distribute. **Significance:** it extends justice from state and market into the household. ⚠️ **Limit:** it risks over-extending justice into every private relationship, which is why most syntheses preserve some sphere of family autonomy alongside gender-just background conditions. ❗ It serves **2026 Q2(b)** directly, by explaining why a title held without control over the household's decisions fails to deliver what the title was supposed to deliver. ➡️ Feminist justice theory belongs to **Topic 20**, `basic\20_Diverse-Perspectives-on-Justice.md`.

### 4. Performativity, intersectionality, and the two mandatory chains

🔶 **Judith Butler**, *Gender Trouble* (**1990**): gender is not a cultural coating on a stable biological sex but **performative** — produced through repeated, stylised acts (speech, gesture, dress, behaviour) that create the appearance of a natural gendered self; on this account even *sex* is partly constituted through gendered discourse rather than being a wholly pre-discursive bedrock. ⚠️ **Three caveats, all mandatory:** (a) ❌ this is **not** Gauba's position — it is a later and more radical move than the textbook split; (b) ❌ it is **not** a denial that bodies exist — the claim concerns how the **meaning and categorisation** of bodies is produced; (c) it must be paired **immediately** with the first chain below, so that the answer shows awareness of a debate rather than presenting performativity as settled. ❗ The in-source bridge is the counter-arrow of Lesson 3: Gauba has **already** conceded that **"cultural factors have contributed to accentuate the biological differences"**, which is why Butler belongs in a critical block and not in Lesson 1.

🔶 **Kimberlé Crenshaw**, "Demarginalizing the Intersection of Race and Sex" (**1989**). **Claim:** treating race and sex discrimination as separate, additive categories fails women who face a distinct, compounded disadvantage at their intersection. **Evidence:** Black women's employment-discrimination claims fell through the gap between a race-discrimination template and a sex-discrimination template, each implicitly modelled on a different unmarked group. **Significance:** it forces every school in Lesson 5 to answer *which women?*, correcting an unstated universalism. ⚠️ **Limit:** an intersectional list can become analytically unwieldy without a clear political question in view; it supplements the structural claims of the four schools and ❌ does not replace them. ❗ **Introduce it as a development of Rowbotham's own clause** about **"linking of the struggles of all oppressed groups"**, not as an import — the chapter already contains the proto-intersectional move.

**Objection–reply chain 1.** ⚠️ **Objection:** if gender is entirely performative or socially constructed, feminism loses a stable category of *woman* around which to organise and to claim rights. **Reply:** many feminist theorists respond that political claims can be built on provisionally and strategically stabilised categories even while accepting that the category is historically constructed ("strategic essentialism"), and that legal sex-discrimination categories function pragmatically regardless of deeper philosophical instability. ⚠️ Present as **live and contested**, not as a settled result.

**Objection–reply chain 2.** ⚠️ **Objection:** radical feminism's emphasis on patriarchy as the primary, near-universal structure of oppression risks treating *women* as one undifferentiated class, erasing differences of race, class and caste. **Reply:** Crenshaw's intersectionality and Marxist or socialist feminism's attention to class supply the corrective — oppression is compounded and differently configured across axes, producing a more accurate map of **whose** subordination is being explained. ⚠️ This reply **strengthens rather than replaces** the original radical-feminist insight that sexual and reproductive domination is analytically distinct from class or race domination.

# CUMULATIVE CONCEPT CHECKS

Four checks. Three consolidate a block and the fourth spans the whole arc; each model is held within 250 words.

### Cumulative check A — Lessons 1 to 3

**Question:** Build the chain from the chapter's definition of feminism to its claim that gender hierarchy is alterable, naming the sentence that carries each step.

**Model answer:** One: feminism is **"the concern with the status and role of women in society in relation to men"**, holding that women **"have suffered and are still suffering injustice because of their sex"**, and is therefore remedial — it **"seeks effective measures for the redressal of that injustice."** Two: the status is **"the product of the institution of 'patriarchy', and not based on reason"**, and patriarchy moved from **"the authority of male head of the household"** to **"male domination in general, including its occurrence in labour market"**, a political category. Three: the hinge — **"Nature has divided human race between men and women, but their status and role in society are determined by our culture"** — concedes the division and relocates the ranking. Four: culture is the **"values, beliefs, symbols, modes of thought and behaviour"** a group evolves **"in order to streamline its social life"**, and its gendered content is a pair of trait sets ending in **dominance** and **submissiveness**, **"instruments of social control"** sanctioned by calling deviation **"indecent"**. Five: those patterns are **"the products of the social organization based on patriarchy and its institutions, the division of labour in the family and the competitive and exploitative character of capitalism."** Six: Rousseau's **conventional** inequalities cover **"wealth, prestige and power"**, are **"the product of our social arrangements"** and are **alterable**, so the chart ends **Unalterable / Alterable**. ⚠️ Seven: the chapter then runs the arrow back — **"cultural factors have contributed to accentuate the biological differences"** — as its own claim, not as evidence.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not treat step three as a throwaway. The concession in the hinge sentence is what makes steps four to six defensible under a critical directive; an answer that denies the biological division cannot then rely on the chapter's hedges, because it has already taken a stronger position than the chapter does.

### Cumulative check B — Lessons 4 to 6

**Question:** Set out the chapter's historical argument for why the deprivations are owed, and show exactly where this topic hands over to another owner.

**Model answer:** The argument is a **lag**. At early stages biological differences **"necessitated the division of labour"**, men going out for **"hunting and other hazardous jobs"** and women **"constrained to undertake child-bearing and rearing"**, in a system **"based on mutual care and adjustment"** with **"no significant level of resentment"**. Then: **"with the development of technology, sweeping changes took place in other parts of social organization, but the division of labour between men and women remained more or less unchanged"**, while man **"acquired more and more power."** Honour replaced correction — concessions as the **"weaker sex"**, shyness **"eulogized as a woman's ornament"**, the **"fair sex, endowed with special dignity"**, **"ladies first"**, chivalry — and the verdict is that **"in spite of so much importance accorded to woman in social life, she was systematically deprived of her share in power"**: **"she was given security but not an opportunity."** The entitlement follows from three deprivations — **property, the vote, education and higher learning** — carrying the clause **"even though these deprivations had no logical connection with her biological status as a woman."** The chapter's own corrective instance is the **Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005**, **"which gives equal right to daughters in joint family property"**, and its own conceptual conclusion is that **"until the condition of equal participation of women in public life is fulfilled, the concept of citizenship cannot be brought to its logical conclusion."** ➡️ **Handover:** every section, article, judgment and Indian datum belongs to `Polity\advanced\07_Fundamental-Rights.md`, `Polity\basic\Parliament.md` and `Social-Justice\basic\05_Women-and-Gender-Justice.md`.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not let the seven named Acts become an Indian legal argument. The chapter names them and analyses none; the only parenthetical it supplies is the one about daughters' equal right in joint family property, and everything beyond that naming is another owner's material.

### Cumulative check C — Lessons 7 to 9

**Question:** Show how the chapter establishes that formal equality and provision are both insufficient, and state the limits on each piece of evidence it uses.

**Model answer:** **Formal equality.** The chapter sets out the assumption — **"after establishing legal equality of men and women, women were not left with any issue of complaint"** — then the observation that women's **"participation in voting was lower in comparison to men"**, then the preference explanation (**"less interested in politics and public affairs and probably they had no time"**), then the falsifier: **"when the size of the family began to shrink and more and more women took up jobs, the above explanation … was no longer held to be valid."** The residue: **"more and more women were taking part in voting, but their share at various levels of political authority had remained insignificant."** ⚠️ Limits: **"some studies"**, with no author, country, date or figure; the US comparison **"not very different"**, with no percentage; the Scandinavian exception — **Sweden and Denmark twenty-six per cent**, **Norway thirty-four** — is reported with **no explanation**, and none may be invented. **Provision.** The 1995 report found **"no correlation between gender bias and a country's economic development"**, with **Cuba** at **HDI 72, GDI 47, GEM 16**; and **"no essential correlation between GDI and GEM in many cases"**, meaning **"adequate attention to health care and education of women, but no adequate share in the exercise of power"**, illustrated by the **'economic tigers' of East Asia**. ⚠️ Limits: all 1995 data inside a 2009 book; the socialist-models passage supports an **education-and-health** claim only; and the indices were **retired by UNDP in 2010** in favour of the GII, from which no figure is available here.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not treat the 2005–2007 women heads of state as a refutation of the argument. The chapter records them itself and then supplies the distinction that absorbs them: the record is **"enough proof of the potential of women's power. But it is no proof of the opportunities open to women."**

### Cumulative check D — the whole arc, Lessons 1 to 11

**Question:** In one connected argument, move from the chapter's definition to its closing verdict, marking where each lesson does work and where the topic must route outward.

**Model answer:** Feminism protests a status that is **"the product of the institution of 'patriarchy', and not based on reason"**, patriarchy having grown into **"male domination in general, including its occurrence in labour market"** (L1). Nature divides; **"their status and role in society are determined by our culture"** (L1), and culture does this through trait pairs that **"serve as instruments of social control"**, sanctioned by calling deviation **"indecent"**, caused by **patriarchy, the division of labour in the family and the competitive and exploitative character of capitalism**, and transmitted by two curricula ending in the pipeline sentence (L2). Being conventional, the hierarchy is **alterable** — though the chapter itself concedes that **"cultural factors have contributed to accentuate the biological differences"** (L3). Historically the once-functional division outlived its function, honour replaced power, and she was **"given security but not an opportunity"**, deprived of **property, vote and education** with **"no logical connection"** to biology (L4). The three schools divide over obstacle, beneficiary and success condition, with Millett deriving domination from **Max Weber** (L5). The movement demanded change at the level of **"myths, values and beliefs"**, and the chapter concluded that citizenship **"cannot be brought to its logical conclusion"** without participation (L6). Law did not suffice (L7), provision did not (L9), and the instruments proposed are endorsed without argument (L8). 🔶 Enrichment refines but is not required (L10). ⚠️ Verdict, in the chapter's words: **"overall development … as well as … adequate share in economic and political power"** (L11). ➡️ Foeticide, Indian legal substance and current data route out.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not end on the strong claim that discrimination is purely man-made. The chapter's own origin account says biology **"necessitated"** the division and that women were **"constrained"**, and it never reconciles that with its **"chose"**. The defensible verdict is that the **status and role** are cultural, which is both what the chapter argues and what survives a critical follow-up.

# ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS MODEL PRACTICE

⚠️ Original practice prompts written for this edition. They are **not** previous-year questions and ❌ must not be cited as such. Models at 10 marks are held within 150 words; models at 15 and 20 marks within 250.

**Practice question 1 [10]:** Distinguish sex from gender using the chapter's own chart, and state one limit of the distinction. *(Distinguish — answer in about 150 words.)*

**Model answer:** The chapter's chart divides one issue — **"Division of Society into Men and Women"** — along four rows. **Point of division:** sex against gender. **Basis:** **"Product of Nature"** against **"Product of Culture"**. **Symptom:** **"Natural Inequality"** against **"Conventional Inequality"**. **Character:** **"Unalterable"** against **"Alterable"**. The axis is therefore **origin**, with alterability following from it: conventional inequalities concern **"wealth, prestige and power"**, are **"the product of our social arrangements"**, and can be reduced by altering those arrangements. The hinge sentence states both halves at once — **"Nature has divided human race between men and women, but their status and role in society are determined by our culture."** ⚠️ **One limit:** the chapter itself reverses the arrow, holding that **"cultural factors have contributed to accentuate the biological differences between men and women"**, with women's athletic improvement offered as an **indication** that **"social norms had shaped biology"**. The columns are therefore not sealed.

**Scoring note:** the marks concentrate in reproducing four rows rather than two definitions, in naming **origin** as the axis so that alterability is derived rather than asserted, and in supplying the limit from the chapter itself. ❌ An answer that denies biological difference forfeits the hinge sentence and the hedges that protect the rest.

**Practice question 2 [10]:** Explain why the chapter treats the historic deprivation of property, the vote and education as a single package. *(Explain — answer in about 150 words.)*

**Model answer:** Because one warrant covers all three, and each is weak without the others. The warrant is printed as a single clause: she was deprived of **"the right to ownership of property, right to vote and opportunities of education and higher learning even though these deprivations had no logical connection with her biological status as a woman."** A deprivation with no connection between the trait and the thing withheld is arbitrary, so removal is **owed**, not merely desirable. They also interlock: property without the vote has no political defence, the vote without education cannot be informed, and education without property leaves no independent base. The package is the form of the chapter's verdict — **"she was systematically deprived of her share in power"** and **"was given security but not an opportunity to learn certain things that would make her as competent as man."** ⚠️ The chapter supplies no empirical record of their removal.

**Scoring note:** the answer earns its mark by deriving the package from the shared warrant rather than listing three entitlements, and by closing with an explicit statement of what the chapter cannot supply — which is what prevents a candidate drifting into invented Indian data.

**Practice question 3 [15]:** "The sex/gender distinction establishes that gender hierarchy is alterable, but not that it is unjustified." Critically examine. *(Critically examine — answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer:** The proposition is correct about the distinction and incomplete about the chapter. **Where it holds.** Rousseau's apparatus delivers only a modal conclusion. **Natural** inequality covers **"age, health, beauty, physical and intellectual capacities"**, **"created by nature"** and **"largely unalterable"**; **conventional** inequality covers **"wealth, prestige and power"**, is **"the product of our social arrangements"**, and is **"alterable."** The chart's last row accordingly reads **Unalterable / Alterable**. That classification tells us change is **possible**; it does not by itself tell us change is **owed**, since many alterable arrangements are perfectly justified. **Where it fails.** The chapter supplies the normative premise separately and repeatedly. The definition holds the inferior status to be **"not based on reason"**. The historical account states that the deprivations of **property, vote and education** had **"no logical connection with her biological status as a woman."** And Rousseau's own clause attaches a method to the conventional column: these inequalities may be subjected to **"critical examination … from the point of view of justice."** Together these make the hierarchy unjustified, not merely contingent. ⚠️ **Two qualifications.** First, the chapter's chart files the division of society into two sexes under *natural inequality*, although a division into two kinds is a classification rather than a difference of degree, which is what Rousseau's category actually describes; the chapter does not notice. Second, its own origin account says biology **"necessitated"** the division and that women were **"constrained"**, unreconciled with its **"chose"**. **Verdict:** the distinction establishes alterability; the chapter, not the distinction, establishes injustice.

**Scoring note:** a **critically examine** directive requires the proposition to be tested rather than rejected, so the answer concedes its logical point in full before supplying the missing premise. The two unresolved tensions are what demonstrate that the source has been read against itself.

**Practice question 4 [15]:** Assess the claim that this chapter's account of feminism is a critique of liberalism rather than an extension of it. *(Assess — answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer:** The claim is half right; the chapter supplies both halves. **Extension.** Early feminism **"emerged in the wake of Enlightenment, which sought to enlarge the scope of 'rights of man' so as to include equal rights to women therein"** — an **immanent** move using the rights tradition's own vocabulary without exception. Mill's argument runs in that tradition's exact form: women are **"by no means less talented than men"** and therefore deserve **"full legal and political rights."** Wollstonecraft is credited with establishing **"the principles on which campaigns for women's right to education, employment, property and the vote were later built up."** And liberal feminism, the chapter's first stream, demands **"absolute equality of opportunity for men and women in all walks of life."** **Critique.** The chapter then shows the extension failing on its own terms. Legal equality was reached, and **"more and more women were taking part in voting, but their share at various levels of political authority had remained insignificant."** It diagnoses the residue at a level liberalism does not reach: **"the expectations attached to differential roles of men and women serve as the foundation of gender inequality in society"**, requiring **"cultural transformation of society"**. ❗ And it delivers its own verdict on the liberal stream: **"the most popular stream of feminist movement, but it is not considered to be very influential."** ⚠️ **Verdict:** an extension in **warrant** and a critique in **sufficiency** — feminism borrows liberalism's premise and denies that liberalism's instruments complete the job. ➡️ Liberalism's doctrine itself belongs to `Political-Theory\basic\03_Liberalism-and-Neoliberalism.md`.

**Scoring note:** **Assess** requires a weighed judgment, so the two halves are given separate evidence rather than a concession appended at the end. The in-text verdict on liberal feminism is the highest-value element available, because it is a criticism drawn from the source itself and almost no competing answer will carry it.

**Practice question 5 [20]:** Examine the proposition that securing women's share in power, rather than improving their condition, is the test of gender justice. *(Examine — answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer:** The proposition captures the chapter's sharpest finding but overstates its conclusion. **The case for it.** GEM is defined as **"the extent to which women participate in a country's economic and political life"**, and the 1995 report found **"no essential correlation between GDI and GEM in many cases"**, meaning **"adequate attention to health care and education of women, but no adequate share in the exercise of power"** — illustrated by the **'economic tigers' of East Asia**, where development improved while women were **"denied a tangible share in economic and political power."** Politically, after suffrage, **"more and more women were taking part in voting, but their share at various levels of political authority had remained insignificant"**; and the record of women heads of government is **"enough proof of the potential of women's power. But it is no proof of the opportunities open to women."** **Against it.** The chapter declines the disjunction twice. Feminist theory is **"the advocacy of equal rights for women and men, accompanied by the commitment to improve the position of women in society"**, and its closing formula calls for **"the overall development of women, including improvement of their health and education as well as giving them adequate share in economic and political power."** ⚠️ Condition is also causally prior: the two curricula route women away from the experience political careers draw on. **Verdict:** power is the **test**, condition is the **route**; a measure that reports the second as if it answered the first mistakes provision for justice.

**Scoring note:** at 20 marks the mark separation lies in refusing the disjunction explicitly while still conceding what the proposition gets right. The GEM definition must be given before the findings are used, or the argument reads as two statistics rather than as a claim about power.

**Practice question 6 [20]:** "Removing legal disabilities was necessary for women's equality; it was never going to be sufficient." Discuss, with reference to the arguments of this chapter. *(Discuss — answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer:** Both halves are the chapter's own; the second is argued, not asserted. **Necessity.** In 1792 woman was **"not only restrained from voting, but was deemed unfit for education, was debarred from many occupations, and had no legal right to own property"**, with **"no real right to divorce even if her husband abused her."** The warrant is the clause attached to the three central deprivations — **"no logical connection with her biological status as a woman"** — which makes removal owed, not merely useful. Wollstonecraft is credited with establishing **"the principles on which campaigns for women's right to education, employment, property and the vote were later built up."** **Insufficiency, demonstrated.** The chapter reports the assumption that **"after establishing legal equality of men and women, women were not left with any issue of complaint"**, then the lower turnout, then the preference explanation, then its falsification **"when the size of the family began to shrink and more and more women took up jobs"**, leaving the residue that turnout rose while **"their share at various levels of political authority had remained insignificant."** The reason lies upstream: **"the expectations attached to differential roles of men and women serve as the foundation of gender inequality"**, and **"the experience gained by women in their own professional life does not help them to take up a political career."** ⚠️ **Verdict:** law removes the barrier, not the foundation; hence the chapter's demand for **"cultural transformation of society"** and its two-limbed close — development **as well as** power. ⚠️ All figures here are 2009-edition.

**Scoring note:** **Discuss** requires both halves at strength and then a position. The falsification sequence is the hinge: it is what converts the insufficiency half from an assertion into an argument, because it eliminates the preference explanation instead of merely disagreeing with it.

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

Retrieval only. The teaching exposition above is not repeated here; these are the compressed recall units for every lesson, in the order an answer uses them.

## A. The four-limbed definition, and the two words that carry it

- Feminism = **"the concern with the status and role of women in society in relation to men"**; women **"have suffered and are still suffering injustice because of their sex"**; hence it **"seeks effective measures for the redressal of that injustice"**.
- ⭐ **"In short, it implies a voice of protest against the inferior status accorded to women in society, which is the product of the institution of 'patriarchy', and not based on reason."** Four limbs: relation · injustice-by-sex · remedy · warrant.
- Feminist theory, restated p.86: **"the advocacy of equal rights for women and men, accompanied by the commitment to improve the position of women in society"**; it seeks **"restoration of justice for women who remained deprived of equal status and opportunities vis-a-vis men since earliest times."** ⭐ *Accompanied by* = two limbs, so the 2018 disjunction is refused from inside the source.
- *Gender* in social science and literary criticism marks **"the differences in social status of man and woman, particularly … that women are placed in a lower status in relation to their intrinsic worth"** — a status/worth gap, hence evaluative.
- Remedy level: **"cultural transformation of society"**, i.e. **"the right ordering of status of women in relation to men in social and political life."**

## B. Patriarchy, the Enlightenment warrant, and the three early names

- **Patriarchy** box: literally **"rule of the father"**; **originally** **"a social system based on the authority of male head of the household"**; **now** **"male domination in general, including its occurrence in labour market as well as domestic division of labour."** ⭐ The movement is the argument.
- Early feminism emerged **"in the wake of Enlightenment, which sought to enlarge the scope of 'rights of man' so as to include equal rights to women therein."** ⭐ An **immanent extension**, not an import.
- **Enlightenment** box: **"an intellectual movement of eighteenth century France, Germany and Great Britain"**; religious and political life **"set free from obscure and orthodox beliefs"**; **"a new outlook, informed by reason and power of scientific research and discovery."**
- **Mary Wollstonecraft (1759-97)** · **Harriet Taylor (1807-59** as printed; standard **1807–1858**, with no work attached by the chapter**)** · **John Stuart Mill (1806-73)**, ***The Subjection of Women* (1869)**.
- ⭐ Mill's two-step: **"by no means less talented than men"** → **"full legal and political rights"**; restated at p.88 as **"in no way inferior to men in their talents."**

## C. The hinge, the test, and the content of "culture"

- ⭐ **"Nature has divided human race between men and women, but their status and role in society are determined by our culture."**
- ⭐ The test: **"when we speak of women as 'fair sex' or 'weaker sex' or when we invoke the etiquette of 'ladies first', our attention is not confined to the biological fact, but have already entered the realm of culture."**
- **Culture** box: **"values, beliefs, symbols, modes of thought and behaviour, styles of art and various skills"** evolved **"in order to streamline its social life"**, establishing **"a distinctive identity of the group."**
- **Sex** = **"biological differences such as reproductive function and secondary characteristics such as body hair and breast development"**; **gender** = **"cultural ideas that construct images and expectations of both females and males."**

## D. The mechanism: traits, hedges, causes, sanction, curricula

- Masculinity: **aggressiveness · logical outlook · control of emotional expression · attitude of dominance.** Femininity: **peacefulness · intuitiveness · emotional expressiveness · submissiveness.**
- ⚠️ Five hedges: **"some variations … in different social contexts"** · the wife-who-is-not-submissive-as-a-mother example · **"the degree of submissiveness … may vary from one case to another"** · **"almost universal cultural traits"** · **"not directly based on biological differences."**
- ⭐ Three causes: **"the social organization based on patriarchy and its institutions, the division of labour in the family and the competitive and exploitative character of capitalism."** The schools are these clauses ranked.
- Enforcement: **"instruments of social control that reinforce male dominance"**; sanction — authoritarian behaviour **"particularly towards men"** is **"termed to be indecent"**; and **"the expectations attached to differential roles of men and women serve as the foundation of gender inequality in society."**
- Belief formation: women **"are made to believe that the proper sphere of their activity is within their household and that they need not take interest in public life."**
- Two curricula: **"personal relations, not … personal success"** · **firm, assertive, aggressive** against **obedient, shy, submissive** · **doctors, engineers and lawyers** against **school teachers, nurses or secretaries**.
- ⭐ The pipeline: **"the experience gained by women in their own professional life does not help them to take up a political career."**
- Time use, hedged: status is **"chiefly"** the product of social arrangements; **"most women do not get an opportunity to develop their own personality."**

## E. Alterability: Rousseau, the chart, and the arrow that runs back

- **Rousseau (1712-78)**, ***A Discourse on the Origin of Inequality* (1755)**. **Natural** = **age, health, beauty, physical and intellectual capacities**, **"created by nature"**, **"largely unalterable."** **Conventional** = **wealth, prestige and power**, **"the product of our social arrangements"**, open to **"critical examination … from the point of view of justice"**, **alterable**.
- Chart, under **Division of Society into Men and Women**: Sex / Gender · **Product of Nature** / **Product of Culture** · **Natural Inequality** / **Conventional Inequality** · **Unalterable** / **Alterable**.
- ⭐ Counter-arrow: **"cultural factors have contributed to accentuate the biological differences between men and women"**; athletic improvement as an **indication** that **"social norms had shaped biology and restricted women's physical development"**; **feminist anthropologists** on variable **"physical differentiation"**.
- ⚠️ Open seam 1 — the chart files a **classification** (two kinds) under a category Rousseau defines by **degree**. ⚠️ Open seam 2 — **"necessitated"** / **"constrained"** against **"chose"**.
- **United Nations Report, 1980**: half the population · nearly **two thirds** of work hours · **one tenth** of income · **less than one hundredth** of property. ❌ The follow-up **"the condition remains more or less unchanged today"** is unusable.

## F. The lag, the pedestal, and the three entitlements

- The chapter's own question: **"If injustice against women has existed since earliest times, why has it come to the forefront only in recent times?"**
- ⭐ The lag: **"with the development of technology, sweeping changes took place in other parts of social organization, but the division of labour between men and women remained more or less unchanged"**; man **"acquired more and more power."**
- Pedestal: **"weaker sex"** concessions and exemptions from **"strenuous and hazardous tasks"** · not allowed to mix with strangers · **"shyness was eulogized as a woman's ornament"** · beauty **"admired in poetry, music, paintings"** · **"fair sex, endowed with special dignity"** · **"ladies first"** **"evolved to confirm that dignity"** · **"a damsel in distress"** as **"an act of chivalry."**
- ⭐ **"In spite of so much importance accorded to woman in social life, she was systematically deprived of her share in power. She was given security but not an opportunity to learn certain things that would make her as competent as man, and thereby vindicate her claim to equality."**
- ⭐ **Property · vote · education and higher learning**, **"even though these deprivations had no logical connection with her biological status as a woman."**
- **1792** disability list: no vote · **"deemed unfit for education"** · **"debarred from many occupations"** · **"no legal right to own property"** · **"no real right to divorce even if her husband abused her."** Credit clause: she **"established the principles on which campaigns … were later built up."**
- Arc: nineteenth century **"removing educational and professional barriers"** → militancy → **"early twentieth century struggles for women's suffrage"** → West: **employment rights, equal pay, social benefits, taxation**.
- ⚠️ Concession, hedged: technology and diversification **"have given women the opportunity of proving their abilities"**; **"equal rights for women are no longer questioned in enlightened circles."**

## G. The schools, separated by four questions

- Shared object: **"all streams of feminist thought focus on the causes and remedies of women's inequality, subordination or oppression."**
- **Liberal** — obstacle: formal barriers; **"revival of the conventional feminist movement"**; **"absolute equality of opportunity … complete removal of gender-based discrimination"**; programme: **equal pay for equal work, abortion laws reform, representation in parliaments, bureaucracy and dignified professions**. ⭐ Verdict: **"the most popular stream … but it is not considered to be very influential."**
- **Radical** — obstacle: irreducible subordination, not **"a symptom or aspect of some deeper or more comprehensive system of domination, such as racism or class-based division of society"**; **"historically women constituted the first oppressed group"**; not removable by **"the abolition of class society."**
- **Firestone (1945- )**, ***The Dialectic of Sex* (1970)**: basis **"ultimately biological"**; chain = infants on **lactating women**, women on men; escape = **"reliable contraceptives, baby foods and 'test-tube babies'"**; remedy = **"diffusing the child-bearing and child-rearing role to society as a whole, men as well as women."**
- **Millett (1934- )**, ***Sexual Politics*** (printed **1971**; standard **1970**): power **"further sustained by an ideology"**, **"similar to the relationship between classes and races"**, hence **"should be treated as political relationship"**; ⭐ **"basing her analysis … on Max Weber's theory of domination"** → **social authority** and **economic force**.
- **Two pillars** of radical feminism, with **"enormous influence on developing the Women's Liberation Movement in 1970s."**
- **Socialist** — **"a combination of patriarchal analysis of radical feminism and class analysis of Marxism"**; ⭐ **"capitalists as well as men are the beneficiary of women's subordination"**; **wage-labour and domestic labour**; culture and **"psycho-analytical aspects of sexuality."**
- **Rowbotham (1943- )**: **"participatory, decentralized approach"** linking **"the struggles of all oppressed groups"**; *Women, Resistance and Revolution* **(1972)**, *Hidden from History* **(1973)**, *The Past is before Us* **(1989)**; **"as a Marxist"**; **"class exploitation and women's oppression are closely linked phenomena"**; success **"only through combining these struggles."**

## H. The empowerment agenda and the Indian material, with its boundary

- **Women's Liberation Movement**: **"emerged in the United States since the early 1970s"**, then Europe; six organisations — **NOW**, **Boston's Bread and Roses**, **Berkley Women's Liberation Group**, **Women's Radical Action Project**, **WEAL**, **NWPC**.
- Demand: **"myths, values and beliefs … fundamentally reassessed and changed"** across **"work and family life, social behaviour, decision-making, politics, religion and education"**; ⭐ **"even the more personal and private domain of sexuality needed to be redefined."**
- Concrete demands: **day-care facilities** · **"chairman" → "chairperson"** · **media and advertising representation**.
- ⭐ Result **pair**: **"not only … the enactment of new laws in some countries, but also … the worldwide acceptance of new norms protecting the dignity of women."**
- Seven Acts, **named only**: **1956** Immoral Traffic (Prevention) · **1961** Dowry Prohibition · **1986** Indecent Representation of Women (Prohibition) · **1987** Commission of Sati (Prevention) · **1994** Prenatal Diagnostic Techniques (Regulation and Prevention of Misuse) · **2005** Hindu Succession (Amendment), **"which gives equal right to daughters in joint family property"** · **2005** Protection of Women from Domestic Violence.
- ❌ Boundary: no section, amendment number, article, commencement date, judgment, renaming history or implementation record. ➡️ `Polity\advanced\07_Fundamental-Rights.md`, `Social-Justice\basic\05_Women-and-Gender-Justice.md`.
- ⭐ **"Until the condition of equal participation of women in public life is fulfilled, the concept of citizenship cannot be brought to its logical conclusion."** One-third of **panchayat** seats → **"grass-root level"** → **"by and by … one-half"** → **"legislative assemblies and parliament."** Second route: **higher education** → **"administration and high-profile professions."**

## I. The participation puzzle, quantified and dated

- Assumption → observation → alibi → falsifier → residue: **"after establishing legal equality … women were not left with any issue of complaint"** → turnout **"lower in comparison to men"** → **"private and domestic affairs … probably they had no time"** → **"when the size of the family began to shrink and more and more women took up jobs, the above explanation … was no longer held to be valid"** → ⭐ **"more and more women were taking part in voting, but their share at various levels of political authority had remained insignificant."**
- Figures, **2009 edition**: electorates **"nearly fifty per cent women"** · Western Europe **"less than ten per cent"** · British House of Commons **"less than five per cent"** · US House **"not very different"** · **Sweden and Denmark twenty-six per cent** · **Norway thirty-four per cent**. ⚠️ No explanation of the exception is offered or to be invented.
- **Charter of Paris**, **November 1990**, **thirty-four** Heads of Government; **"marked the end of the Cold War"**; **"end of an era"** → ⭐ **"Which era had come to an end?… it was not an end of the patriarchal era."** Two women in the photograph: **Gro Brundtland** (Norway), **Margaret Thatcher** (Britain); **"two days after this Conference, Mrs. Thatcher also resigned."** ⚠️ Chapter prints *for the New Europe*; official form *for a New Europe*.
- Counter-evidence: **end of 2005** Germany · **beginning of 2006** Chile and Liberia · **2007** India and Argentina. Outside Europe and America: **Sri Lanka, Israel, India, Pakistan, Bangladesh**.
- ⭐ **"This record is enough proof of the potential of women's power. But it is no proof of the opportunities open to women as their share in these positions continues to be very meagre."**

## J. The design fork, its instruments and its contradiction

- **View (a):** **"no difference between men and women as regards their capabilities; hence they should be governed by the same laws."**
- **View (b):** **"essentially different from men—biologically, culturally and socially"**; **"equal opportunities to develop and apply their distinctive capabilities along with equal rights."**
- Seven instruments: exemption from **"hazardous tasks, like underground mining and working in night shifts"** · **maternity leave and related benefits** · **maintenance** · **custody** after divorce · reservation in **"seats for higher learning"** · reservation in **"appointments, seats in legislatures"** · **tax concessions** to **"encourage them to work for additional income."**
- Sort: **protective** (1–4) narrows the option set · **compensatory** (5–6) widens access · **incentive** (7).
- ⚠️ **"This view seems to be more reasonable and is widely endorsed"** — hedged twice, argued nowhere. Any reason supplied is a **reconstruction**.
- ❗ Contradiction: instrument [1] repeats the pedestal's **"concessions and exemptions from strenuous and hazardous tasks"** and cuts against **"removing educational and professional barriers."**

## K. Measurement, and the decoupling of provision from power
