# Liberalism and Neoliberalism — Mains-Focused Live Edition

A fast-preparation edition for GS-II, GS-IV and the Essay. It teaches only what enriches a Mains answer on liberty, the state, welfare, markets and constitutional limits, and stops there. The comprehensive eighteen-lesson session remains the unchanged reference layer; this is not a copy or a condensation of it, and nothing here is a substitute for it when depth is actually wanted.

**The one idea that organises everything.** Liberalism is a **family**, not a doctrine. Its members agree that individual liberty ranks first and disagree about what liberty requires. Classical liberals fear the state. Welfare liberals fear deprivation. Neoliberals fear the redistributive state itself. Place a stem inside that quarrel and it becomes answerable; miss it and the answer collapses into a definition dump.

**Scope.** This is **Topic 03 of the subject's twenty-three topics**, and it remains a standalone session; every other topic keeps its own. Political Theory is supplementary conceptual support. The Commission prints no Political Theory paper, and every General Studies question touching these ideas belongs to another subject — Polity, Governance, Social Justice, Ethics, International Relations or the Essay. This topic owns no General Studies previous-year question and none is manufactured here. Concepts it needs but does not own — liberty and equality, justice, democracy — are named and deferred to their own topics rather than absorbed.

**What is deliberately left out, and why.** Minor-exponent catalogues and full date registers; the OCR-restoration narrative, reduced to one consolidated line; bibliographic accumulation; optional-only depth; repeated provenance litigation; and encyclopaedic register notes. Three source variances survive because each can produce a checkable error: Adam Smith described as English, the compressed market-democracy coda, and the hedged against unhedged sentences in the critique. Definitions, causal logic, objections and replies, deployment, answer frameworks and required practice are kept intact.

### 1. Two clauses carry the definition

Liberalism is **a principle of politics which insists on 'liberty' of individual as the first and foremost goal of public policy**, liberty here implying **'liberation' from restraints — particularly, from the restraints imposed by an authoritarian state** (PDF p.39).

*First and foremost goal of public policy* is a **priority** claim, not an exclusivity claim: liberty ranks first when values compete. *Liberation from restraints* is **relational**: liberty is always liberty *from* something, so each liberal must specify the restraint. ⚠️ That is the hinge of the whole topic. State → classical. Deprivation → welfare. The redistributive state → neoliberal. All three accept the definition word for word.

The **authoritarian state** box: a state where **an individual, a group, an institution, or a set of rules enshrined in a sacred book are regarded as the source of authority, i.e. legitimate power; its orders or directions are required to be obeyed by all without questioning** (PDF p.40). The defect is not that authority exists — liberalism has a state — but that its directions are **beyond questioning**.

⚠️ The dating clause doubles as the first criticism. The principle **was evolved in the West in late seventeenth century in order to liquidate feudal privileges of the land-owning class and to create favourable conditions for the new entrepreneurial class** (PDF p.40). Its origin was useful to a particular class. ⚠️ But the chapter does **not** infer falsity from origin — and resisting that inference is what keeps a critical answer analytical rather than merely hostile.

Finally, the licence for plurality: **in fact liberalism is not a fixed mode of thought, but an intellectual movement which seeks to accommodate new ideas in order to face new situations and new challenges** (PDF p.40). This is the sentence that lets you refuse a stem's premise that liberalism *means* minimum government.

### 2. The eight tenets

| | Tenet (PDF p.40) | What it does in an answer |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | Man is a **rational creature** with **immense potential to contribute to social progress** | Sets the burden of proof: displacing a competent agent's judgment needs justification |
| (b) | **No basic contradiction** between self-interest and common interest; common interest is a **point of reconciliation** | **basic** is the whole clause — conflict is admitted, irreducible antagonism denied |
| (c) | **Natural rights which cannot be transgressed by any authority** | Converts liberty from a preference into a constraint on majorities |
| (d) | Civil society and state are **artificial institutions created by individuals to serve the common interest**, owed obedience **on the condition of fulfilling this function** | **Conditional**, performance-linked legitimacy in one clause |
| (e) | **Primacy of procedure over the end-product**; the liberal view of freedom, equality, justice and democracy is **a search for right procedure** | The most under-used tenet; decides most due-process stems |
| (f) | Civil liberties, including personal freedom **which rules out search or arrest without a warrant**; restriction only **to ensure equal freedom for others** | The proviso makes some restrictions **constitutive** of liberty |
| (g) | **Freedom of contract**, the state as **umpire**; but a contract **concluded under pressure**, or one which **compromises dignity of the individual**, **shall be void** | Liberalism's own resource against unequal bargaining power |
| (h) | Public policy as the product of **free bargaining between groups** | The tenet the second critique attacks at Lesson 8 |

> 🔑 Mnemonic: **R-I-N-A / P-C-C-B** — Rational, Interests reconcilable, Natural rights, Artificial institutions // Procedure, Civil liberties, Contract, Bargaining.

### Deployment in GS-II, GS-IV and the Essay

- **GS-II.** On freedom-against-state-action stems, refuse the anti-state framing with tenet (d): the question is never whether the state may act, but whether this act serves the function that licenses obedience. On process stems — consultation, hearings, regulatory procedure — tenet (e) turns description into argument.
- **GS-IV.** Tenet (a) grounds administrative humility: a rational agent's judgment is displaced only with reasons. Tenet (g)'s dignity clause tests consent extracted under structural pressure. ⚠️ Codes, rules and case material are Ethics content and must come from your own verified preparation.
- **Essay.** On freedom, authority or dissent, define the opposition precisely — not freedom against order, but authority that may be questioned against authority that may not.

**Answer framework — the opening paragraph.** Definition with **both** clauses → name the restraint the stem is really about → name the tenet it engages → body. Roughly forty words, and it replaces the definition dump.

### Concept check

**Question:** A candidate writes: "Since liberalism holds the state to be a necessary evil, any expansion of state activity is a departure from liberalism." Identify the error and give the repaired claim.

**Model answer:** The error treats one strand's inference as the family's definition. Liberalism is defined by the priority of individual liberty as the first and foremost goal of public policy; "necessary evil" is the classical strand's reading of that priority, not an entailment of the tenets — and the chapter expressly calls liberalism **not a fixed mode of thought**. Tenet (d) makes the state an artificial institution owed obedience **on the condition of fulfilling this function**, which is a performance test, not a size limit. Tenet (f) permits restriction that secures **equal freedom for others**, so some state action is liberty-protecting rather than liberty-reducing. Repaired claim: an expansion departs from liberalism only if it fails the function test in (d), transgresses the natural rights in (c), or restricts freedom otherwise than to secure equal freedom for others.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not read the movement claim as saying liberalism has no content. It has eight stated tenets; what it lacks is one fixed institutional programme.

**Original Mains practice [10 marks]:** "Liberalism is best understood as a family of doctrines rather than a single theory of the state." Comment. *(Comment — answer in about 150 words.)*

**Model (within 150 words):** The proposition holds, and the source supplies its own warrant. Liberalism is defined by the priority of individual liberty and by liberty as liberation from restraints, and is expressly **not a fixed mode of thought** but an intellectual movement accommodating new ideas. What unifies the family is the eight tenets — rational agency, no **basic** conflict between self-interest and common interest, untransgressable natural rights, artificial institutions owed only conditional obedience, primacy of procedure, civil liberties under the equal-freedom proviso, contract void under pressure or where dignity is compromised, and policy by free bargaining. What divides it is one downstream question: which restraint most endangers liberty. ⚠️ One qualification is owed — "family" must not become "anything goes": a position denying natural rights, or unconditional in its demand for obedience, has left the family whatever it calls itself.

**Rubric (quantified, 10 marks: 3 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 1):** 3 for the definition with **both** clauses; 2 for the movement sentence used as the warrant for plurality rather than as decoration; 2 for at least four tenets with their operative words; 2 for naming the dividing question as *which restraint*, since an answer listing strands without it cannot exceed 6; 1 for the boundary qualification. Deduct 2 if "necessary evil" is offered as liberalism's definition.

### 1. Four functions and a compound

**Liberalism treats market society as the model of social organization where role of the state should be confined to the protection of individuals' life and property, enforcement of contracts, and maintenance of minimum common services which would not be undertaken by private entrepreneurs. In liberal view, the state is a necessary evil. Liberalism treats the state as the means and individual as the end. It rules out absolute authority of the state** (PDF p.41). ⚠️ The source states three conjoined clauses; this edition counts **four** functions by separating life from property, which is a decomposition, not the source's own enumeration.

Each half of **necessary evil** argues. *Evil*: the state is itself coercion, a standing restriction on the liberty that ranks first, so every substitution of its judgment for a rational agent's needs a reason. *Necessary*: protection and umpiring cannot be dispensed with. Together they set a **presumption** — state action is a restriction whose burden of justification lies on whoever proposes to extend it. ⚠️ Report only "evil" and you have described anarchism; only "necessary" and you have described a state with no limiting principle.

⚠️ The fourth function carries a **test**: the service must be one private entrepreneurs **would not** undertake. That test is itself a usable criterion for public provision, and almost no answer reaches for it.

### 2. The label is institutional

The founders **defended the principle of laissez-faire which implies least interference of the state in the economic activities of individuals**, and classical liberalism **is called negative liberalism because it contemplates negative role of the state in the sphere of mutual interaction of individuals** (PDF p.41).

⚠️ This is the single most profitable correction in the topic. *Negative* and *positive* here describe the **state's role**, not a conception of liberty. The negative/positive **liberty** analysis is separate and separately owned. Conflating them produces an answer that cannot explain how a thinker holds a negative-liberty conception alongside a positive state role. Note too that **laissez-faire** is defined narrowly — least interference in the **economic** sphere. Lesson 5 turns on that word.

**Locke (1632-1704)**, **Smith (1723-90)** and **Bentham (1748-1832)** are named as the founders: father of liberalism, father of economics, founder of utilitarianism. ⚠️ The chapter calls all three **English philosophers**; on Smith that conflicts with the standard identification, **Scottish**. Reproduce the chapter where you report the chapter, and do not build an argument on it.

### Deployment in GS-II, GS-IV and the Essay

- **GS-II.** The four functions give a precise baseline against which any proposed extension is measured, and the fourth function's test supplies a criterion for public provision that most answers never reach for.
- **GS-IV.** The means/end sentence answers any question about an administration treating citizens as instruments of a programme: the state is the **means**, the individual the **end**, so instrumentalising the citizen is a category error rather than merely a harsh policy.
- **Essay.** "Necessary evil" models the habit the examiner rewards — holding two true things in tension instead of choosing the comfortable one.

**Answer framework — baseline and extension.** Four functions as baseline → the extension the stem proposes → apply the private-entrepreneur test and tenet (d)'s function test → state who bears the burden and whether it is discharged.

- **Laissez-faire** = **least interference of the state in the economic activities of individuals** — note **economic**.

- **Locke (1632-1704)** father of liberalism · **Smith (1723-90)** father of economics · **Bentham (1748-1832)** founder of utilitarianism.

### Concept check

**Question:** "Classical liberalism is called negative liberalism because its exponents held a negative conception of liberty." Correct this, and say why the correction matters for a Mains answer.

**Model answer:** In this chapter the label attaches to the **state's role**, not to a conception of liberty: classical liberalism is negative liberalism because it **contemplates negative role of the state in the sphere of mutual interaction of individuals**, just as welfare liberalism is positive liberalism because it **contemplates positive role of the state in securing a dignified life**. The correction matters on three counts. Accuracy: the chapter's labels are institutional, and the negative/positive liberty analysis is a separate apparatus owned by another topic. Explanatory power: only the institutional reading explains why one shared priority of liberty yields two opposite programmes, since the strands differ on what the state must do, not on whether liberty ranks first. Exam safety: an answer that silently swaps in the liberty distinction cannot account for a thinker who treats liberty negatively while supporting positive state action, and the gap is visible to an examiner.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not conclude that the two analyses are unrelated. They are closely related and often discussed together; what is forbidden is treating the chapter's institutional label as though it were the conceptual distinction, which imports claims the source has not made.

**Original Mains practice [10 marks]:** Explain why classical liberalism regards the state as a necessary evil. *(Explain — answer in about 150 words.)*

**Model (within 150 words):** The phrase is a compound, and each half carries an argument. **Evil** registers that the state is itself a system of coercion and so a standing restriction on the liberty liberalism ranks first; since man is a **rational creature** with immense potential to contribute to social progress, every substitution of the state's judgment for his own requires justification. **Necessary** registers that it cannot be dispensed with, because life and property require protection and contracts require an umpire, and private action supplies neither reliably. Combined, the phrase sets a presumption: state action is a restriction whose burden of justification lies on whoever proposes to extend it. Two consequences follow in the source's own order — the state is the **means** and the individual the **end**, and **absolute authority of the state is ruled out**. ⚠️ Reporting only "evil" yields anarchism; only "necessary" yields a state with no limiting principle.

**Rubric (quantified, 10 marks: 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 1):** 2 for an argued reading of **evil** connecting it to coercion and to tenet (a)'s rational agent; 2 for an argued reading of **necessary** naming protection and umpiring; 2 for combining them into a burden-of-justification presumption rather than restating both halves; 2 for the means/end sentence and the ruling out of absolute authority; 1 for the mis-reporting warning; 1 for directive fidelity, since **Explain** forbids a bare list. ## Lesson 3 — The two streams, and Mill's transformation from inside

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The settlement looks monolithic from outside. It is not. **Early liberal theory developed in two main directions: (a) individualism; and (b) utilitarianism** (PDF p.42). They reach the same institutional conclusion by incompatible reasoning, and that incompatibility is what eventually cracks the settlement open. ⚠️ This lesson carries the single most-tested axis in the verified record — the liberty-and-equality relation — because the individualist fiscal rule is what makes liberty *limit* equality, and Mill's breach of it is what makes liberty *require* equality's conditions.

### 1. The two streams, side by side

| Axis | **Individualism** (Locke, Smith) | **Utilitarianism** (Bentham) |
|---|---|---|
| Core claim | **Only an individual can have any rights** | **Greatest happiness of the greatest number** |
| The few | **No individual shall be made to suffer in order to benefit any larger unit of society** | **Interest of the few may be sacrificed in the interest of the collectivity** |
| Interests | **No social unit can have any interests which could be distinguished from the interests of its individual members** | Interests aggregate and may be traded |
| Measure | Rights and **voluntary transactions** | **Balance of pleasure over pain**; **no distinction between qualities**, **maximizing the quantity** |
| Fiscal rule | **Even taxation should be confined to the provision and maintenance of common services** | Whatever maximises the aggregate |
| Route to laissez-faire | **Principled** | **Contingent** |

⚠️ Two precision points. The rights and interests sentences are claims about where rights and interests **ultimately reside**, not denials that groups exist or matter. And the taxation clause restricts the **purpose** of taxation; it does not prohibit taxation. "Individualists opposed taxation" misstates the position *and* destroys the drama of what comes next.

⚠️ The sacrifice clause is the direct contradiction of individualism's rule that **no individual shall be made to suffer in order to benefit any larger unit of society**. Two doctrines inside one settlement disagree on exactly the question distributive politics is about — which is why "classical liberalism" cannot be treated as one argument.

### 2. Mill breaks the settlement from inside

```text

  NO distinction of quality            different types of pleasure
```

Mill **pointed to qualitative differences between different types of pleasure, and thus recognized the variety of tastes of different individuals**; he **projected the liberty of individual as the highest value**; these modifications **tilted it toward individualism and transformed its basic character**; and he **also pleaded for taxation of the rich for the benefit of the poor, and thus paved the way for the welfare state** (PDF p.42).

⚠️ Modifications (1) and (2) make happiness non-fungible: if pleasures differ in kind and tastes differ between persons, aggregate quantity stops being a sufficient measure. (3) inserts a value outranking the aggregate, which Bentham's framework had no room for. (4) is the operative one here — a direct breach of individualism's rule confining **even taxation** to common services.

⚠️ The analytical payoff: the welfare turn is not a socialist import. It is generated **inside** the classical settlement by taking one of its own two streams seriously enough to notice that its support for laissez-faire had always been conditional.

### Concept check

**Question:** Show how a committed Benthamite utilitarian could consistently support an extensive welfare state, and say what this reveals about classical liberalism.

**Model answer:** Bentham's criterion is the **greatest happiness of the greatest number**, happiness being the **balance of pleasure over pain**. Laissez-faire is not part of that criterion; it is an empirical bet that least interference in economic activity maximises the balance. If the evidence runs the other way — if provision of health, education or security raises aggregate happiness more than non-interference — the same criterion mandates provision. The sacrifice clause reinforces this, since **the interest of the few may be sacrificed in the interest of the collectivity** licenses redistributive transfer that individualism expressly forbids by confining **even taxation** to common services. What this reveals is that the classical settlement rested on two incompatible foundations, only one of them principled; its unity was a coincidence of conclusions. That is why Mill could transform the doctrine from within rather than leave it.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not infer that Bentham personally favoured welfare provision. The claim is about the **structure** of his criterion — that it makes institutional conclusions hostage to evidence — not about his own policy preferences, which the chapter records as laissez-faire.

**Original Mains practice [10 marks]:** Distinguish individualism from utilitarianism as streams of early liberal thought. *(Distinguish — answer in about 150 words.)*

**Model (within 150 words):** Both streams end at laissez-faire; they disagree about why. Individualism treats the individual as a rational creature whose dignity, independent existence and judgment must receive full recognition, and holds that **no individual shall be made to suffer in order to benefit any larger unit of society**; only an individual can have rights, no social unit has interests distinguishable from its members', and **even taxation should be confined to the provision and maintenance of common services**. Locke and Smith are its exponents. Utilitarianism stands for the greatest happiness of the greatest number, where **the interest of the few may be sacrificed in the interest of the collectivity**; happiness is the balance of pleasure over pain, and Bentham, admitting no distinction between qualities, insisted on maximising quantity. ⚠️ Hence the individualist route to laissez-faire is principled and the utilitarian route contingent.

### 1. The sequence, with credit correctly divided

**John Stuart Mill (1806-73) ... sought to modify utilitarianism and the principle of laissez-faire on philosophical grounds which paved the way for the theory of welfare state. Then T.H. Green (1836-82) ... sought to add a moral dimension to liberalism and thus advanced a full-fledged theory of welfare state. This tradition was further developed by L.T Hobhouse, Harold J. Laski and R.H. Tawney ... Thus the theory and practice of welfare state flourished in the first half of the twentieth century in England** (PDF p.41).

⚠️ Three checkable precision points: Mill **paved the way**, he did not build the theory; the **full-fledged theory** is Green's; and both the chronology and the place are stated. ⚠️ The canonical owner adds a useful refinement — Green's contribution is moral-philosophical (liberty needs conditions) while Laski's and Tawney's is institutional (the state must provide them). Use the distinction; the "New Liberalism" label for the first pair is the owner's term, not the chapter's.

### 2. The welfare state, as the chapter defines it

A welfare state **provides for various types of social services for its citizens**: **social security** (loss of job or any other source of income, death of the bread-winner, prolonged illness, physical disability or any other calamity), **free education**, **public health**, **poor relief**, and **supply of essential goods and services like foodgrains, milk, fuel and transport to the needy at subsidized rates**. It also undertakes **protection of cultural heritage** including monuments, museums, libraries, art galleries, botanical gardens and zoological parks, and **promotes higher education and scientific research** to **step up intellectual and cultural development of society** (PDF p.41).

⚠️ That is a **schedule of provision**, not a levelling programme — an answer equating the welfare state with equality of outcome concedes the neoliberal objection for free. ⚠️ Equally, the last two items are the best evidence **for** the neoliberal complaint about an expanded sphere, and conceding them from the source's own definition reads as honesty rather than weakness.

The theory **contemplates positive role of the state in securing a dignified life to individuals. It is therefore called positive liberalism** — institutional, exactly as *negative liberalism* was.

### 3. The argument, handled precisely

**On political side, liberalism promotes democracy; on economic side, it promotes capitalism. Democracy is concerned with fulfilling needs and aspirations of ordinary people, but capitalism results in the concentration of economic power in the hands of the few who may use it against the interests of ordinary people. This situation is sought to be rectified by the mechanism of the welfare state** (PDF p.41).

⚠️ The modal is load-bearing. Concentration creates the **possibility** of power being used against ordinary people — **may use it**, not *will*. That is what justifies a **standing** corrective mechanism rather than an episodic rescue: you need not prove harm has occurred to justify an institution designed against a structural possibility. Hardening the modal makes the claim stronger and far easier to refute.

⚠️ The conclusion is also passive — the situation **is sought to be rectified**. The chapter reports the welfare state's claimed function without certifying its success, and at Lesson 8 it will say the welfare state **seems to create an illusion of welfare**. Keeping these apart lets one answer carry both the rationale and the scepticism without contradiction.

### 1. The claim is about the existence of information, not its quantity

Needs **manifest themselves through the myriad unpredictable transactions between individuals living in a free or open society**; **no government can have such knowledge**; regulation **would amount to curtailing their freedom without fulfilling their genuine needs**; so it is advisable to **transfer such decisions to the market which will maximize their choice** (PDF p.43).

⚠️ This is not a complaint about missing statistics. The claim is that the relevant information **does not exist in collectible form** before the transactions occur, because a preference is often discovered in the act of choosing. "The state can conduct surveys" concedes the premise while pretending to rebut it. ⚠️ Note also that the inference is **double** — a liberty cost **and** an effectiveness failure; rebutting one leaves the other standing.

⚠️ The coda needs careful handling. **In the economic sphere, market exemplifies the genuine democracy** is a compressed restatement of the choice claim and is usable as an argument. The next sentence — **in the political sphere, market represents a model of genuine democracy, where votes are traded against welfare benefits, and the cost is borne by the most productive members of society** — compresses a contested empirical claim about electoral behaviour into a definition and repeats the attribution-of-outcome assumption of Lesson 5. Reproduce it if a stem demands the full passage; do not rely on it.

### 2. Where the argument stops working

| It establishes | It does not establish | Why the gap matters |
|---|---|---|
| A real informational problem for **full central planning** | That **every** welfare instrument needs the same centrally processed information | A cash transfer or minimum-income scheme leaves the determination of need with the recipient |
| That regulation can impose a liberty cost | That the liberty cost always exceeds the benefit | The inference needs a comparison the argument never performs |
| That markets coordinate dispersed choices | That market outcomes track **genuine needs** rather than effective demand | A person with needs and no purchasing power registers nothing |
| That intervention involves compulsion | That market-generated deprivation involves none | ⚠️ Calling tax "coercion" while calling deprivation natural is a value-laden framing |

⚠️ The defensible finding: the argument's force **diminishes as the instrument decentralises the judgment of need**. A reply is available — thresholds and eligibility rules are still central judgments — which makes the difference one of degree. Saying so is stronger than claiming refutation, because it is both true and gradable.

### Deployment in GS-II, GS-IV and the Essay

- **GS-II.** This is the strongest objection a candidate can voice against any proposed state programme, and voicing it before answering it is what makes a "critically examine" answer critical. It also yields a design criterion: an instrument is more defensible to the extent that it leaves the judgment of need with the person who has the need.
- **GS-IV.** The epistemic humility at the argument's centre — that no authority can fully know what people need — transfers directly to administrative ethics and cuts against paternalism without requiring the political conclusion.
- **Essay.** On knowledge, planning, expertise or humility, the claim that the relevant information does not exist in collectible form is genuinely interesting material, and far better than a generic market-against-state contrast.

**Answer framework — the strongest-objection paragraph.** State the argument in its own three steps — needs manifest through myriad unpredictable transactions; no government can have such knowledge; regulation curtails freedom without fulfilling genuine needs. Then name the instrument at issue and ask who retains the judgment of need. Then grade. This structure earns marks under every critical directive.

### 1. The consequentialist pair and the deontological outlier

Hayek's and Friedman's arguments are **defeasible by evidence about results**: show that an instrument coordinates better than the market in a given domain and their case weakens. Nozick's is not — a rights claim is not answered by showing that violating the right produces good outcomes. ⚠️ So "the neoliberals say no" is an unsafe answer to any results-based stem. Identify which argument is in play first.

### 2. Nozick's entitlement architecture — a flagged clarification

⚠️ **Standard exposition, not page-anchored to this chapter**, which names Nozick without setting out his apparatus. A distribution is just if and only if it satisfies three principles: **justice in acquisition** (how previously unowned things may first be appropriated), **justice in transfer** (validly acquired holdings may be voluntarily exchanged or gifted), and **rectification** (past injustice in acquisition or transfer must be corrected, approximating the pattern that would have obtained without it). Because the test is **historical**, no particular pattern is required, and a state enforcing a pattern violates entitlements.

❌ **The usual trap:** using Nozick as a blanket defence of existing holdings. His third principle defeats that use — where acquisition or transfer was unjust, **rectification** is required, so an entitlement theorist facing a history of dispossession is committed to corrective transfer. ⚠️ The honest objection is evidentiary: establishing the historical facts that trigger rectification is extremely hard.

### 3. The welfare-liberal and Rawlsian reply — also flagged

⚠️ **Also standard exposition, not page-anchored here.** The reply does not deny that voluntary transfer transmits entitlement. It denies that a clean procedural history is **sufficient**, because persons are born into starting points they neither chose nor consented to, and a procedure beginning from unchosen disadvantage inherits it. The proposed test is substantive as well as procedural: inequalities should be judged partly by whether they work to the benefit of the **least advantaged**.

### 1. The merit is a method, and the method outran its authors

**The greatest merit of liberalism lies in initiating the process of replacing traditionalism by modern rationalism** — relations **hitherto based on 'tradition'** to be based on **'reason'** (PDF p.44). The chapter concedes who benefited — **the new middle class — the merchants and the industrialists** — and what it cost: **the condition of the working classes deteriorated with the success of classical liberalism** (PDF p.45).

Then the argument worth carrying into every answer on this topic: once relations were being redefined by reason, the process **could not be stopped from reaching its logical conclusion: the rise of socialism**, which **sought a better deal for the working class on the same principle of 'reason' which was initially invoked by liberalism**. **Faith in 'reason' is a dynamic force.**

⚠️ The structure is unusual and examiners reward noticing it: the doctrine **generated the standard by which it was later judged**. That is a defence of liberalism's **method** which survives every criticism of its **outcomes**, because it does not depend on the outcomes being good. ⚠️ Its own limit: releasing a standard is not the same as satisfying it.

### 2. The two contexts, which is the verdict

**Liberalism is invoked today in two important contexts: (a) as a theory of capitalism, and (b) as a theory of constitutionalism** (PDF p.45).

- As a theory of capitalism: **so long as liberalism is commended with a view to vindicating the economic relations of capitalist society, it is bound to suffer from its inner contradictions which must be resolved by invoking human values.** ⚠️ A **condition** and a **remedy**, not an exit from economic argument; the contradictions are declared insoluble *within economics alone*.
- As a theory of constitutionalism: **when liberalism is invoked as the foundation of constitutionalism and suitable curbs on political power, it embodies lasting political values. It is, therefore, bound to survive on this front.**

**Constitutionalism** is the chapter's own box: the principle insisting on organisation and working of the state **according to a constitution so that no organ or office-holder of the state is allowed to use arbitrary power**, the constitution prescribing **powers of various organs of government and the limits of those powers**.

⚠️ The continuity is exact, and stating it is the strongest close available in this topic: constitutionalism's target, **arbitrary power**, is Lesson 1's **authoritarian state**, whose defect was that its directions were **obeyed by all without questioning**. The tradition ends where it began, on the one commitment all strands share.

### 3. The objection the close must absorb

⚠️ Two objections stop the verdict being complacent, and including one is what separates a 15-mark close from a 10-mark one. **Constitutionalism is not distributively neutral** — who obtains legal standing, whose claims receive protection and which interests are justiciable are themselves distributive questions. And **Lesson 8's second head bears on constitutional practice**, not only on economics: if groups differ in consciousness, organisation and voice, representative and legal institutions register their claims unequally. ⚠️ So the verdict is graded: what endures is a **framework** for limiting arbitrary power, and survival is not the same as neutrality.

- **GS-IV.** The constitutionalism box — **no organ or office-holder of the state is allowed to use arbitrary power** — is a precise statement of the limit on official discretion, expressed as a principle rather than a rule, which is the form GS-IV asks candidates to apply.
- **Essay.** The rise-of-socialism argument is first-rate material on reform, reason and unintended consequences: a doctrine that releases a method it cannot then confine.

**Answer framework — the graded close, in three sentences.** One: what survives and why — the constitutional core, because its target, arbitrary power, has not changed since the seventeenth century. Two: what stays contested and why — the economic programme, because the strands draw incompatible conclusions from one shared priority. Three: the residual — the surviving framework is not distributively neutral, so survival is not vindication.

Three further eligible years were consulted and **deliberately not used**, so that "contributed no stem" is not read as "contained nothing relevant": **2023 Q1(a)** on justice as fairness and Rawls's theory, **owned by the justice owner**, to which this topic contributes only the shape of the welfare-liberal reply; **2022 Q1(d)** on complete liberty and inequality, which the same liberty-equality apparatus answers and which would duplicate the 2019 and 2024 anchors; and **2020 Q1(a)** on whether liberty is realisable in a modern technological society, which the strand-diagnosis check can route but which invites undated contemporary claims.

# CUMULATIVE CONCEPT CHECKS

Four checks: three consolidate a block, and the fourth spans the whole arc. Each model is held within 250 words.

### Cumulative check A — Lessons 1 to 4

**Question:** Build the chain from liberalism's definition to the welfare state, naming each step and the clause that carries it.

**Model answer:** One: liberalism **insists on 'liberty' of individual as the first and foremost goal of public policy** — a priority claim — where liberty is **'liberation' from restraints**, particularly those of an **authoritarian state** whose orders are **required to be obeyed by all without questioning**. Two: it is **not a fixed mode of thought** but an **intellectual movement**, which licenses several strands. Three: membership is fixed by eight tenets, among them **no basic** contradiction between self-interest and common interest, **natural rights which cannot be transgressed by any authority**, **artificial institutions** owed obedience **on the condition of fulfilling this function**, **primacy of procedure over the end-product**, and contract void where it **compromises dignity of the individual**. Four: the classical settlement reads these as **market society** plus four state functions, the state a **necessary evil**, the **means** to the individual as **end**. Five: that settlement held two incompatible streams — individualism, where **even taxation should be confined to the provision and maintenance of common services**, and utilitarianism, where **the interest of the few may be sacrificed**. Six: Mill's modifications **tilted it toward individualism and transformed its basic character** and **paved the way for the welfare state**, Green supplying the **full-fledged theory**. Seven: the chapter's reason is structural — democracy fulfils ordinary people's needs while capitalism **concentrates economic power in the hands of the few who may use it against** them, and the welfare state is the mechanism by which this **is sought to be rectified**.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not present step four as the definition of liberalism. It is one historically situated reading of the eight tenets, and the movement claim at step two is what makes the later readings possible.

### Cumulative check B — Lessons 5 to 7

**Question:** State what is genuinely new in neoliberalism, then the strongest objection to its central argument and the reply its defenders can make.

**Model answer:** Three things are new. **Scope**: laissez-faire is revived **not only in economic sphere, but also in social and political sphere**, where the classical definition confined it to **economic** activities. **Targets**: liberation is sought from **all institutions which tend to restrict his vision of the world, including the institutions of religion, family and customs of social conformity apart from political institutions**. **Anthropology**: it **repudiates the deterministic outlook of human life**, holding that personality, character, thought and actions **cannot be construed as the outcome of his circumstances**, so that **man is the maker of his destiny** — the premise beneath the charge of **forced transfer of resources from the more competent to the less competent**. The central argument is epistemic: needs **manifest themselves through the myriad unpredictable transactions** of a free or open society, **no government can have such knowledge**, and regulation **would amount to curtailing their freedom without fulfilling their genuine needs**. The strongest objection is one of **scope**: that establishes an informational problem for comprehensive planning, not for every welfare instrument, since a transfer leaving the determination of need with the recipient requires far less centrally processed information. ⚠️ The available reply is that eligibility thresholds remain central judgments of need, which makes the difference one of degree — so the defensible finding is that the argument's force **diminishes as the instrument decentralises the judgment of need**.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not use Nozick interchangeably with Hayek and Friedman. ⚠️ On the standard typing their arguments are consequentialist and defeasible by evidence about results, while his is a rights claim outcome evidence cannot touch — so a results-based stem gets two answers from one umbrella.

### Cumulative check C — Lessons 8 and 9

**Question:** Set out the chapter's two-head critique and its closing verdict, and show how they fit together without contradiction.

**Model answer:** Head one is that liberalism **arose for the protection of the interests of the bourgeoisie when political power was wielded by feudal interests**, and in pleading for **minimum government, minimum regulation** it **insisted so much on freedom of the property-owning class that it set aside all human considerations** — evidenced by Tawney's record of opposition in the name of property to factory legislation, housing reform, interference with adulteration and **even to compulsory sanitation of private houses**. From the **French Revolution (1789)** liberalism widened the state's functions, and **it cannot be denied** that the welfare state was evolved **not out of sympathy** but **to enlist support in order to maintain the status quo**. Head two is the **actual imbalance of group interests**: the assumption that the state represents all groups **could be true in the case of some societies but it cannot be demonstrated as a universal phenomenon**, because groups are not equally **conscious**, **well-organized** or **vocal**. The verdict then credits liberalism with **replacing traditionalism by modern rationalism**, concedes that **the condition of the working classes deteriorated**, and argues that reason **could not be stopped from reaching its logical conclusion: the rise of socialism**. The two fit because the critique targets liberalism's **economic** embodiment while the verdict defends its **method** and its **constitutional** form — different objects, assessed separately.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not report the critique as the chapter's conclusion. Two of its framing sentences are hedged — ventures **appear to be** devices, the welfare state **seems to create** an illusion — and they are followed by a section crediting liberalism with the standard by which it has just been judged.

### Cumulative check D — the whole arc, Lessons 1 to 10

**Question:** In one connected argument, move from liberalism's definition to a graded verdict on its contemporary standing, marking where each lesson does work.

**Model answer:** Liberalism places **liberty of individual as the first and foremost goal of public policy**, liberty being **liberation from restraints**, so the family is defined by a priority and divided by the identity of the restraint (L1). The eight tenets fix membership, and the classical settlement is one reading of them — market society, four state functions, the state a **necessary evil**, the **means** to the individual as **end** (L2). That settlement held two incompatible streams, and because Bentham's route to laissez-faire was **contingent**, Mill could transform it from inside with qualitative pleasures, liberty as the highest value and **taxation of the rich for the benefit of the poor** (L3). Green then supplied the **full-fledged theory of welfare state**, on the structural reason that democracy fulfils ordinary people's needs while capitalism **concentrates economic power in the hands of the few who may use it against** them (L4). Neoliberalism reacted, widening laissez-faire to the **social and political** spheres and resting on an anti-deterministic anthropology (L5), with an epistemic core holding that **no government can have** knowledge of genuine needs (L6), and three distinct arguments beneath one umbrella met by the reply that a clean procedural history still leaves unchosen disadvantage unanswered (L7). The chapter's critique then finds a retained bourgeois character and an **obvious imbalance** of group interests (L8). ⚠️ Graded verdict: the **economic** programme stays contested while the **constitutional** core — no office-holder permitted **arbitrary power** — survives (L9), and L10 writes that finding to the directive.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not let the chain become a tour of the lessons. Each step is load-bearing: remove utilitarianism's contingency and Mill's transformation is inexplicable; remove the democracy-capitalism tension and the welfare state becomes charity; remove the two-contexts distinction and the verdict becomes either apologetics or dismissal.

# ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS MODEL PRACTICE

⚠️ Original practice prompts written for this edition. They are **not** previous-year questions and must not be cited as such.

**Practice question 1 [10]:** Distinguish classical liberalism from welfare liberalism. *(Distinguish — answer in about 150 words.)*

**Model answer:** Both rank individual liberty first; they differ on what the state must do to secure it. Classical liberalism treats **market society as the model of social organization** and confines the state to protecting **life and property**, **enforcement of contracts**, and **minimum common services which would not be undertaken by private entrepreneurs**; the state is a **necessary evil**, the **means** to the individual as **end**. Because its role in **mutual interaction** is negative, it is called **negative liberalism**, and Locke, Smith and Bentham are its founders, all defending **laissez-faire** as least interference in **economic** activity. Welfare liberalism **contemplates positive role of the state in securing a dignified life** and is therefore **positive liberalism**: Mill modified utilitarianism and laissez-faire, Green added a **moral dimension** and a **full-fledged theory**, and Hobhouse, Laski and Tawney developed it in England in the **first half of the twentieth century**. ⚠️ The dispute is about conditions, not rank.

**Scoring note:** marks concentrate in the four permitted functions rather than a vague "minimum state"; in the two labels explained by the **state's role** rather than by a theory of liberty; and in the closing clause, which keeps both inside one family and pre-empts the obvious follow-up.

**Practice question 2 [10]:** Explain what is new in neoliberalism's conception of freedom. *(Explain — answer in about 150 words.)*

**Model answer:** Classical liberalism sought freedom from the state in the **economic** sphere; neoliberalism enlarges the conception in three ways. First, by **sphere**: laissez-faire is revived **not only in economic sphere, but also in social and political sphere**. Second, by **target**: it seeks **liberation from all institutions which tend to restrict his vision of the world, including the institutions of religion, family and customs of social conformity apart from political institutions**, so the restraint is no longer only political. Third, by **philosophical premise**: it **repudiates the deterministic outlook of human life**, holding that personality, character, thought and actions **cannot be construed as the outcome of his circumstances**, and so treats **man as the maker of his destiny**. The result is a doctrine **hostile to all social and legal restrictions on individual's freedom of action**. ⚠️ The third element carries the other two.

**Scoring note:** an **Explain** directive forbids a list, so each element is given its function and the third is identified as load-bearing. Naming religion, family and custom is what distinguishes this from an economics answer, and it is the part most candidates omit.

**Practice question 3 [15]:** "Liberalism's defence of property set aside all human considerations." Critically examine. *(Critically examine — answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer:** The criterion must come first: whether the proposition survives as stated or only in a qualified form. It is the chapter's own and its evidence is strong. Liberalism **arose for the protection of the interests of the bourgeoisie when political power was wielded by feudal interests**, and in pleading for **minimum government, minimum regulation** it **insisted so much on freedom of the property-owning class that it set aside all human considerations**. Tawney's record makes this concrete: opposition in the name of the right of property **to factory legislation, to housing reform, to interference with the adulteration of goods, even to compulsory sanitation of private houses** — an escalation ending where the public-health stake is clearest.

Three qualifications follow. The chapter's own rider limits the charge to a phase: **such arguments are no longer advanced**, though **at times** property is still held **sacrosanct** where it produces wide disparities. The critic is Tawney, one of welfare liberalism's own developers, so this is liberalism correcting itself. And the tenets already contained the correction, since freedom of contract is void where a contract is **concluded under pressure** or **compromises dignity of the individual** — the principle the factory and housing cases turn on.

Attacking the logic: **all human considerations** is an absolute, and a single counter-instance defeats it as stated.

⚠️ Verdict: the proposition holds of classical liberalism's **practice in a phase** and fails of its **principles**, which supplied the grounds on which that practice was reversed.

**Scoring note:** the "critically" element is discharged by attacking an absolute quantifier with a counter-instance from the source's **own** tenets, not by hostile adjectives. The third qualification is the distinctive move: showing the correction came from inside the doctrine is more defensible than agreeing with the charge.

**Practice question 4 [15]:** Examine the claim that neoliberalism is merely classical liberalism revived. *(Examine — answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer:** Restated as a testable proposition, the claim is that neoliberalism adds nothing to the classical position. The support is real and the source supplies it: neo-liberalism, neo-classical liberalism or libertarianism **stands for contemporary version of classical liberalism which seeks to restore laissez-faire individualism**, and its programme — denouncing the welfare state, opposing state intervention, **'rolling back' the state which has immensely expanded its sphere of activities** — reads as classical minimalism reasserted.

Three considerations defeat the claim as stated. The **scope** differs: classical laissez-faire was defined as least interference in the **economic** activities of individuals, whereas neoliberalism revives it **not only in economic sphere, but also in social and political sphere**. The **targets** differ: it seeks liberation from **all institutions which tend to restrict his vision of the world, including the institutions of religion, family and customs of social conformity apart from political institutions**, which no fiscal doctrine requires. The **arguments** differ, because Locke and Smith had no welfare state to answer, whereas the knowledge claim that **no government can have such knowledge** and the entitlement test are both built against one. The **anthropology** is explicit where classical liberalism left it implicit — personality, character, thought and actions **cannot be construed as the outcome of his circumstances**.

⚠️ Finding: the claim holds as a description of the **conclusion** and fails as a description of the **position**.

**Scoring note:** "Examine" requires the claim restated as testable, evidenced both ways, and closed with a finding. The move that earns the marks is conceding the chapter's own umbrella sentence first — an answer attacking the grouping without acknowledging that its source uses it has set itself against its own evidence.

**Practice question 5 [20]:** Critically evaluate the claim that liberty requires a positive role for the state. *(Critically evaluate — answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer:** Evaluation needs a criterion: whether a conception of liberty is realisable through the institutional arrangement it recommends.

The case for the claim is the welfare turn's own. Liberty means **liberation from restraints**, and nothing requires a restraint to come from an authority; a person without education, health or security cannot direct his own life. Green accordingly **added a moral dimension** and advanced a **full-fledged theory of welfare state**, developed by Hobhouse, Laski and Tawney, **contemplating positive role of the state in securing a dignified life**. The chapter supplies the structural reason: liberalism promotes **democracy** politically and **capitalism** economically, and capitalism **results in the concentration of economic power in the hands of the few who may use it against the interests of ordinary people**.

Against the criterion the claim is strong but incomplete. **No government can have** definitive knowledge of needs, which limits what provision can achieve, though the limit weakens where the instrument leaves the judgment of need with the recipient. ⚠️ On the entitlement account a rights-respecting state may not redistribute at all. And the welfare position supplies no stopping rule: if the state's sphere extends to whatever liberty requires, it expands without limit — a point the chapter's own definition, reaching museums and scientific research, concedes.

⚠️ Graded verdict: liberty requires **conditions**, and securing some of them requires state action, so the claim holds; the unbounded version fails for want of any criterion of sufficiency. The rival wins wherever provision substitutes the state's judgment of need for the individual's.

**Scoring note:** "Critically evaluate" fails without a named criterion, so it is declared first and every judgment refers back to it. What lifts this above competence is conceding the opponent's strongest point from the **source's own text**, and closing with a condition under which the rival wins.

**Practice question 6 [20]:** "Liberalism survives as constitutionalism, not as an economic doctrine." Discuss. *(Discuss — answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer:** The proposition is the chapter's own verdict and should be stated at full strength before being tested. Liberalism is invoked in two contexts. As **a theory of capitalism**, **so long as liberalism is commended with a view to vindicating the economic relations of capitalist society, it is bound to suffer from its inner contradictions which must be resolved by invoking human values**. As **a theory of constitutionalism**, invoked as the **foundation of constitutionalism and suitable curbs on political power**, it **embodies lasting political values** and is **bound to survive on this front**.

The evidence for the first half is the topic's third movement. Classical minimalism, welfare provision and neoliberal rollback are three incompatible economic programmes held by people who agree on liberty's priority, and both of the chapter's critiques — retained bourgeois character, and the **actual imbalance of group interests** — bear on the economic dimension. The evidence for the second is continuity: constitutionalism is the principle that **no organ or office-holder of the state is allowed to use arbitrary power**, which is the same target as the **authoritarian state** liberalism was built against in the late seventeenth century.

Two objections must be met. Constitutionalism is not distributively neutral, since legal standing and protection are themselves distributive. And the second critique bears on constitutional practice, not only on economics.

⚠️ Verdict: the proposition holds, with the asymmetry narrower than it appears. What endures is a **framework** for limiting arbitrary power — and survival is not neutrality.

**Scoring note:** a Discuss stem needs both sides reconstructed fairly and a verdict. The distinguishing move is turning the chapter's **own** second critique against its **own** verdict, which shows the candidate testing the source rather than reciting it, while still landing where the source lands.

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

Retrieval only. Teaching exposition is not repeated here.

## A. Definition, target, licence

- **'Liberty' of individual as the first and foremost goal of public policy**; liberty = **'liberation' from restraints**, especially those of an **authoritarian state**.
- **Authoritarian state**: individual, group, institution **or a set of rules enshrined in a sacred book** as **the source of authority**; orders **obeyed by all without questioning**.
- Origin: **West**, **late seventeenth century**, to **liquidate feudal privileges of the land-owning class** and enable the **new entrepreneurial class**.
- **Not a fixed mode of thought, but an intellectual movement** — the licence for several strands.

## B. The eight tenets and their operative words

| | Tenet | Word that carries it |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | **Rational creature** with potential for **social progress** | rational |
| (b) | **No basic contradiction** between self-interest and common interest | **basic** |
| (c) | **Natural rights which cannot be transgressed by any authority** | untransgressable |
| (d) | **Artificial institutions**, obedience **on the condition of fulfilling this function** | **conditional** |
| (e) | **Primacy of procedure over the end-product** | procedure |
| (f) | Civil liberties; restriction only to **ensure equal freedom for others** | **proviso** |
| (g) | Contract **void** if **concluded under pressure** or if it **compromises dignity of the individual** | **void** |
| (h) | Policy as **free bargaining between groups** | bargaining |

> 🔑 **R-I-N-A / P-C-C-B.**

## C. Classical settlement and the two streams

- **Market society as the model of social organization**; four functions: **protect life**, **protect property**, **enforce contracts**, **minimum common services which would not be undertaken by private entrepreneurs**.
- **Necessary evil** · **state = means, individual = end** · **absolute authority ruled out**.
- **Laissez-faire** = **least interference of the state in the economic activities of individuals**. **Negative liberalism** = **negative role of the state in the sphere of mutual interaction of individuals**.
- **Locke (1632-1704)**, **Smith (1723-90)**, **Bentham (1748-1832)**. ⚠️ The chapter calls all three **English**; Smith is standardly **Scottish**.
- Individualism: **only an individual can have any rights**; **no social unit can have any interests which could be distinguished from the interests of its individual members**; **no individual shall be made to suffer in order to benefit any larger unit of society**; **even taxation should be confined to the provision and maintenance of common services**.
- Utilitarianism: **greatest happiness of the greatest number**, **the interest of the few may be sacrificed**, **balance of pleasure over pain**, **no distinction between qualities**, **maximizing the quantity**.
- ⚠️ Individualist route **principled**; Benthamite route **contingent**.
- **Mill (1806-73)**: **qualitative differences** · **variety of tastes** · **liberty of individual as the highest value** · **taxation of the rich for the benefit of the poor** → **tilted it toward individualism and transformed its basic character** → **paved the way** for the welfare state.

## D. The welfare turn and its structural reason

- **Green (1836-82)** added a **moral dimension** and the **full-fledged theory of welfare state**; **Hobhouse**, **Laski**, **Tawney** developed it; it **flourished in the first half of the twentieth century in England**. **Positive liberalism** = **positive role of the state in securing a dignified life to individuals**.
- Schedule: **social security** (loss of job or income, death of the bread-winner, prolonged illness, disability, any other calamity) · **free education** · **public health** · **poor relief** · essentials at **subsidized rates** · **protection of cultural heritage** · promotion of **higher education and scientific research**.
- **On political side, democracy; on economic side, capitalism.** Democracy = **fulfilling needs and aspirations of ordinary people**; capitalism = **concentration of economic power in the hands of the few who may use it against the interests of ordinary people**; the welfare state is the **mechanism** by which this **is sought to be rectified**.
- ⚠️ Keep **may** and **is sought to be**.

## E. Neoliberalism

- **Neo-liberalism, neo-classical liberalism or libertarianism stands for contemporary version of classical liberalism which seeks to restore laissez-faire individualism**; **'rolling back' the state which has immensely expanded its sphere of activities**.
- **Hayek (1899-1992)** · **Friedman (1912-2006)** · **Nozick (1938-2002)**; **second half of the twentieth century**.
- Charge: **inimical to individual liberty**, involving the **forced transfer of resources from the more competent to the less competent**. ⚠️ Second half contested.
- New: **scope** (also **social and political sphere**) · **targets** (**religion, family and customs of social conformity apart from political institutions**) · **anthropology** (**repudiates the deterministic outlook**; **cannot be construed as the outcome of his circumstances**; **man is the maker of his destiny**).
- Epistemic core: **spontaneous order ... as exemplified in free markets** · **no government can have such knowledge** · **myriad unpredictable transactions** · **curtailing their freedom without fulfilling their genuine needs** · **transfer such decisions to the market which will maximize their choice**.
- ⚠️ Coda 1 usable: **in the economic sphere, market exemplifies the genuine democracy**. Coda 2 (**votes traded against welfare benefits ... the most productive members**) — report only.
- ⚠️ Flagged clarifications: the epistemic / consequentialist / rights-based typing; Nozick's **acquisition — transfer — rectification** triad; the welfare-liberal reply from unchosen starting points.

## F. Critique and verdict

- **Clung to capitalism** so firmly that new ventures **appear to be** devices for sustaining it.
- Head 1: arose for the **bourgeoisie** against **feudal interests**; **insisted so much on freedom of the property-owning class that it set aside all human considerations**. **Tawney, *The Acquisitive Society* (1920)** — the only quotation: **factory legislation**, **housing reform**, **adulteration of goods**, **even to compulsory sanitation of private houses**. Rider: **no longer advanced**, but **at times** property is **sacrosanct**.
- **1789** → state functions widen; **it cannot be denied** the welfare state was evolved **not out of sympathy** but to **maintain the status quo**; **incremental change ... cleverly devised to contain unrest**; it **seems to create an illusion of welfare**.
