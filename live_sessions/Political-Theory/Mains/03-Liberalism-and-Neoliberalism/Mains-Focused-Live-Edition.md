# Liberalism and Neoliberalism — Mains-Focused Live Edition

**The one idea that organises everything.** Liberalism is a **family**, not a doctrine. Its members agree that individual liberty ranks first and disagree about what liberty requires. Classical liberals fear the state. Welfare liberals fear deprivation. Neoliberals fear the redistributive state itself. Place a stem inside that quarrel and it becomes answerable; miss it and the answer collapses into a definition dump.

### 1. Two clauses carry the definition

Gauba defines liberalism as a political principle giving individual liberty first priority in public policy, especially by releasing persons from authoritarian restraint (PDF p.39).

*First and foremost goal of public policy* is a **priority** claim, not an exclusivity claim: liberty ranks first when values compete. *Liberation from restraints* is **relational**: liberty is always liberty *from* something, so each liberal must specify the restraint. ⚠️ That is the hinge of the whole topic. State → classical. Deprivation → welfare. The redistributive state → neoliberal. All three accept the definition word for word.

Gauba's authoritarian-state box covers systems in which a person, group, institution or sacred code is treated as the unquestionable source of legitimate power. The liberal objection is not to authority as such, but to commands placed beyond challenge (PDF p.40).

⚠️ Gauba dates the Western emergence of liberalism to the late seventeenth century and links it to dismantling landed-feudal privilege while enabling an entrepreneurial class (PDF p.40). That class setting explains origin but does not by itself decide validity.

Finally, Gauba presents liberalism as an adaptable intellectual movement rather than a fixed doctrine, allowing it to absorb new ideas when circumstances change (PDF p.40). This blocks the equation of liberalism with one permanent state programme.

### 2. The eight tenets

| | Tenet (PDF p.40) | What it does in an answer |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | Persons are rational agents capable of contributing to their own good and social progress | Displacing agency requires justification |
| (b) | **No basic contradiction** between self-interest and common interest; common interest is a **point of reconciliation** | **basic** is the whole clause — conflict is admitted, irreducible antagonism denied |
| (c) | Natural rights place limits on every authority | Converts liberty from preference into constraint |
| (d) | State and civil society are human creations serving common interests; obedience depends on performance of that function | Performance-linked legitimacy |
| (e) | Correct procedure has priority over a preferred result | Governs due-process analysis |
| (f) | Civil liberties, including personal freedom **which rules out search or arrest without a warrant**; restriction only **to ensure equal freedom for others** | The proviso makes some restrictions **constitutive** of liberty |
| (g) | **Freedom of contract**, the state as **umpire**; but a contract **concluded under pressure**, or one which **compromises dignity of the individual**, **shall be void** | Liberalism's own resource against unequal bargaining power |
| (h) | Public policy as the product of **free bargaining between groups** | The tenet the second critique attacks at Lesson 8 |

> 🔑 Mnemonic: **R-I-N-A / P-C-C-B** — Rational, Interests reconcilable, Natural rights, Artificial institutions // Procedure, Civil liberties, Contract, Bargaining.

### Deployment in GS-II, GS-IV and the Essay

- **GS-II.** On freedom-against-state-action stems, refuse the anti-state framing with tenet (d): the question is never whether the state may act, but whether this act serves the function that licenses obedience. On process stems — consultation, hearings, regulatory procedure — tenet (e) turns description into argument.
- **GS-IV.** Tenet (a) grounds administrative humility: a rational agent's judgment is displaced only with reasons. Tenet (g)'s dignity clause tests consent extracted under structural pressure. ⚠️ Codes, rules and case material are Ethics content and must come from your own verified preparation.
- **Essay.** On freedom, authority or dissent, define the opposition precisely — not freedom against order, but authority that may be questioned against authority that may not.

**Answer framework — the opening paragraph.** Definition with **both** clauses → name the restraint the stem is really about → name the tenet it engages → body. Roughly forty words, and it replaces the definition dump.

- Liberalism gives individual liberty first priority in policy and targets authoritarian restraint.
- *First and foremost* = **priority**, not exclusivity. *Liberation from* = **relational**: name the restraint, you have named the strand.
- **Authoritarian state:** legitimate power is vested in a person, group, institution or sacred code whose commands cannot be questioned.
- Origin: late-seventeenth-century West; anti-feudal change created space for an entrepreneurial class.
- Liberalism is an adaptable movement rather than a fixed institutional programme.

### Concept check

**Question:** A candidate writes: "Since liberalism holds the state to be a necessary evil, any expansion of state activity is a departure from liberalism." Identify the error and give the repaired claim.

**Model answer:** The error treats one strand's inference as the family's definition. Liberalism is defined by the priority of individual liberty as the first and foremost goal of public policy; "necessary evil" is the classical strand's reading of that priority, not an entailment of the tenets — and the chapter expressly calls liberalism **not a fixed mode of thought**. Tenet (d) makes the state an artificial institution owed obedience **on the condition of fulfilling this function**, which is a performance test, not a size limit. Tenet (f) permits restriction that secures **equal freedom for others**, so some state action is liberty-protecting rather than liberty-reducing. Repaired claim: an expansion departs from liberalism only if it fails the function test in (d), transgresses the natural rights in (c), or restricts freedom otherwise than to secure equal freedom for others.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not read the movement claim as saying liberalism has no content. It has eight stated tenets; what it lacks is one fixed institutional programme.

**Original Mains practice [10 marks]:** "Liberalism is best understood as a family of doctrines rather than a single theory of the state." Comment. *(Comment — answer in about 150 words.)*

**Model (within 150 words):** The proposition holds. Gauba defines a common priority—individual liberty against restraint—while describing liberalism as an adaptable movement. Family membership is constrained by eight commitments: rational agency, reconcilability of personal and common interests, natural rights, function-dependent obedience, procedural priority, civil liberty with an equal-freedom limit, voluntary contract protected against pressure or indignity, and bargaining among groups. The strands divide over which restraint is most dangerous: state coercion, deprivation or redistributive intervention. ⚠️ "Family" does not mean anything goes. A doctrine denying rights or demanding unconditional obedience falls outside the shared core.

**Rubric (quantified, 10 marks: 3 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 1):** 3 for the definition with **both** clauses; 2 for the movement sentence used as the warrant for plurality rather than as decoration; 2 for at least four tenets with their operative words; 2 for naming the dividing question as *which restraint*, since an answer listing strands without it cannot exceed 6; 1 for the boundary qualification. Deduct 2 if "necessary evil" is offered as liberalism's definition.

### 1. Four functions and a compound

Gauba's classical settlement models society through markets and confines government to protecting life and property, enforcing contracts and supplying minimal common services that private enterprise would not provide. Public authority is necessary but coercive, remains instrumental to individuals and cannot claim absoluteness (PDF p.41). ⚠️ Counting four functions separates life from property; Gauba prints them within three clauses.

Each half of **necessary evil** argues. *Evil*: the state is itself coercion, a standing restriction on the liberty that ranks first, so every substitution of its judgment for a rational agent's needs a reason. *Necessary*: protection and umpiring cannot be dispensed with. Together they set a **presumption** — state action is a restriction whose burden of justification lies on whoever proposes to extend it. ⚠️ Report only "evil" and you have described anarchism; only "necessary" and you have described a state with no limiting principle.

⚠️ The fourth function carries a **test**: the service must be one private entrepreneurs **would not** undertake. That test is itself a usable criterion for public provision, and almost no answer reaches for it.

### 2. The label is institutional

The founders defend laissez-faire as minimal governmental interference in individual economic activity. Gauba calls the strand negative liberalism because the state assumes a restrained role in interactions among persons (PDF p.41).

⚠️ This is the single most profitable correction in the topic. *Negative* and *positive* here describe the **state's role**, not a conception of liberty. The negative/positive **liberty** analysis is separate and separately owned. Conflating them produces an answer that cannot explain how a thinker holds a negative-liberty conception alongside a positive state role. Note too that **laissez-faire** is defined narrowly — least interference in the **economic** sphere. Lesson 5 turns on that word.

**Locke (1632-1704)**, **Smith (1723-90)** and **Bentham (1748-1832)** are named as founders associated respectively with liberalism, economics and utilitarianism. The nationality discrepancy is confined to the source ledger because no argument depends on it.

### Deployment in GS-II, GS-IV and the Essay

- **GS-II.** The four functions give a precise baseline against which any proposed extension is measured, and the fourth function's test supplies a criterion for public provision that most answers never reach for.
- **GS-IV.** The means/end sentence answers any question about an administration treating citizens as instruments of a programme: the state is the **means**, the individual the **end**, so instrumentalising the citizen is a category error rather than merely a harsh policy.
- **Essay.** "Necessary evil" models the habit the examiner rewards — holding two true things in tension instead of choosing the comfortable one.

**Answer framework — baseline and extension.** Four functions as baseline → the extension the stem proposes → apply the private-entrepreneur test and tenet (d)'s function test → state who bears the burden and whether it is discharged.

- Classical baseline: market coordination; protect life and property, enforce contracts, and supply only common services private enterprise would not provide.

- The fourth function carries a **test**, not a loophole.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Gauba divides early liberal thought into individualist and utilitarian streams (PDF p.42). They converge on laissez-faire through incompatible reasons, which later makes internal transformation possible. The full liberty-equality relation belongs to the Philosophy owner; this lesson supplies historical framing.

### 1. The two streams, side by side

| Axis | **Individualism** (Locke, Smith) | **Utilitarianism** (Bentham) |
|---|---|---|
| Core claim | **Only an individual can have any rights** | **Greatest happiness of the greatest number** |
| The few | A person may not be burdened merely to advantage a larger social unit | Collective benefit may justify costs imposed on a minority |
| Interests | Group interests ultimately reduce to those of members | Interests aggregate and may be traded |
| Measure | Rights and **voluntary transactions** | **Balance of pleasure over pain**; **no distinction between qualities**, **maximizing the quantity** |
| Fiscal rule | Taxation is justified only for common services | Whatever maximises the aggregate |
| Route to laissez-faire | **Principled** | **Contingent** |

⚠️ Two precision points. The rights and interests sentences are claims about where rights and interests **ultimately reside**, not denials that groups exist or matter. And the taxation clause restricts the **purpose** of taxation; it does not prohibit taxation. "Individualists opposed taxation" misstates the position *and* destroys the drama of what comes next.

⚠️ The sacrifice clause directly contradicts the individualist prohibition on burdening one person for a larger unit. Classical liberalism therefore contains two arguments, not one.

### 2. Mill breaks the settlement from inside

```text
  BENTHAM                          J.S. MILL (1806-73)
  quantity of pleasure only   -->  (1) pleasures differ in QUALITY
          |                        (2) the VARIETY OF TASTES of
          |                            different individuals
          |                        (3) LIBERTY OF INDIVIDUAL as the
          |                            HIGHEST VALUE
          |                        (4) TAXATION OF THE RICH FOR THE
          v                            BENEFIT OF THE POOR
  an AGGREGATE criterion                      |
  whose support for                           v
  laissez-faire is                 CONTINGENT                      -> individual liberty constrains aggregation
  -> redistribution becomes available
  -> the welfare turn becomes possible
```

Mill differentiates pleasures by quality, recognises diverse tastes, raises individual liberty above simple aggregation and supports taxing the rich for poorer citizens. Gauba treats these moves as changing utilitarianism's character and opening the path toward welfare liberalism (PDF p.42).

⚠️ Modifications (1) and (2) make happiness non-fungible: if pleasures differ in kind and tastes differ between persons, aggregate quantity stops being a sufficient measure. (3) inserts a value outranking the aggregate, which Bentham's framework had no room for. (4) is the operative one here — a direct breach of individualism's rule confining **even taxation** to common services.

⚠️ The analytical payoff: the welfare turn is not a socialist import. It is generated **inside** the classical settlement by taking one of its own two streams seriously enough to notice that its support for laissez-faire had always been conditional.

### Deployment in GS-II, GS-IV and the Essay

- **GS-II.** This supplies the vocabulary for any stem opposing individual entitlement to collective benefit — public-purpose displacement, aggregate-gain justifications for concentrated loss. Individualism's rule against making one suffer for a larger unit, set against utilitarianism's licence to sacrifice the few, states the disagreement in one sentence apiece and beats an unattributed appeal to "balance".
- **GS-IV.** The pair is the cleanest available statement of the deontological-against-consequentialist tension inside a liberal frame, in political rather than technical vocabulary. ⚠️ The moral-philosophy apparatus itself is owned by Ethics; take the frame here and the content there.
- **Essay.** Mill's sequence is ready-made for essays on reform from within, and on the difference between abandoning a principle and discovering what it required.

**Answer framework — the two-streams paragraph.** Name the shared conclusion (laissez-faire) → the two incompatible routes → the clause on which they diverge (sacrifice of the few) → then show which route the stem's objection actually defeats. An objection defeating Bentham's contingent route leaves Locke's rights route standing, and saying so is worth more than a general concession.

Revision notes:

- Two early streams: individualism and utilitarianism.
- Individualism locates rights and interests in persons, rejects burdening one for a larger unit and favours voluntary transactions.
- Fiscal rule: taxation only for common services—a limit on purpose, not a ban. Exponents: Locke and Smith.
- Utilitarianism aggregates happiness and permits minority sacrifice where the collective balance improves.
- ⚠️ The sacrifice clause directly contradicts individualism's rule — the settlement held two incompatible foundations.
- ⚠️ Individualist route to laissez-faire **principled**; Benthamite route **contingent** and empirically defeasible.
- Mill's four modifications: **qualitative differences** · **variety of tastes** · **liberty of individual as the highest value** · **taxation of the rich for the benefit of the poor**.
- Gauba's verdict: Mill shifted utilitarianism toward individualism and opened the route to the welfare state.
- ⚠️ (4) breaches the individualist fiscal rule from inside liberalism — the hinge of the whole topic.
- ⚠️ The welfare turn is generated **inside** liberalism, not imported into it.

### Concept check

**Question:** Show how a committed Benthamite utilitarian could consistently support an extensive welfare state, and say what this reveals about classical liberalism.

**Model answer:** Bentham evaluates institutions through aggregate happiness, so laissez-faire is an empirical conclusion rather than an inviolable right. If health, education or security provision produces a better aggregate balance, the same criterion can support welfare. Utilitarian willingness to impose costs on a minority also permits redistribution that individualism resists. Classical liberalism therefore joined two incompatible foundations whose agreement on laissez-faire was contingent. Mill could redirect one stream from within rather than abandon liberalism.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not infer that Bentham personally favoured welfare provision. The claim is about the **structure** of his criterion — that it makes institutional conclusions hostage to evidence — not about his own policy preferences, which the chapter records as laissez-faire.

**Original Mains practice [10 marks]:** Distinguish individualism from utilitarianism as streams of early liberal thought. *(Distinguish — answer in about 150 words.)*

**Model (within 150 words):** Both streams reach laissez-faire but for different reasons. Individualism locates rights in rational persons, reduces group interests to those of members, rejects burdening one person merely for a larger unit and confines taxation to common services. Locke and Smith represent this rights-based route. Benthamite utilitarianism instead maximises aggregate happiness and may impose costs on a minority where the collective balance improves; pleasure is measured quantitatively. ⚠️ Individualism therefore reaches laissez-faire as a principle, while utilitarianism reaches it contingently and may support intervention when expected results change.

**Rubric (quantified, 10 marks: 2 + 3 + 2 + 2 + 1):** 2 for opening on the shared conclusion, which is what makes this a distinction rather than two descriptions; 3 for the contradictory pair — no individual made to suffer for a larger unit, against the sacrificeable few — since an answer missing it cannot exceed 5; 2 for the rights and interests sentences stated as claims about where rights and interests reside; 2 for the taxation rule reported as a restriction on **purpose**; 1 for the principled-against-contingent close. Deduct 1 for writing "individualists opposed taxation".

### 1. The sequence, with credit correctly divided

### 3. The argument, handled precisely

Gauba identifies an internal tension: liberal democracy registers ordinary people's needs, while capitalism concentrates economic power and creates a risk of its use against them. The welfare state is proposed as the corrective mechanism (PDF p.41).

⚠️ The modal is load-bearing. Concentration creates the **possibility** of power being used against ordinary people — **may use it**, not *will*. That is what justifies a **standing** corrective mechanism rather than an episodic rescue: you need not prove harm has occurred to justify an institution designed against a structural possibility. Hardening the modal makes the claim stronger and far easier to refute.

⚠️ The conclusion is also passive — the situation **is sought to be rectified**. The chapter reports the welfare state's claimed function without certifying its success, and at Lesson 8 it will say the welfare state **seems to create an illusion of welfare**. Keeping these apart lets one answer carry both the rationale and the scepticism without contradiction.

### 1. The claim is about the existence of information, not its quantity

Gauba's shared neoliberal argument says needs emerge through unpredictable exchanges and cannot be definitively known by government. Regulation may then reduce freedom without meeting genuine need, so market choice is preferred (PDF p.43).

⚠️ This is not a complaint about missing statistics. The claim is that the relevant information **does not exist in collectible form** before the transactions occur, because a preference is often discovered in the act of choosing. "The state can conduct surveys" concedes the premise while pretending to rebut it. ⚠️ Note also that the inference is **double** — a liberty cost **and** an effectiveness failure; rebutting one leaves the other standing.

⚠️ The coda needs careful handling. Gauba's economic claim equates market choice with economic democracy. His further political-market analogy adds a contested claim about votes, welfare benefits and productive taxpayers. Treat the second as a historically situated source claim, not as evidence on which the answer depends.

### 2. Where the argument stops working

| It establishes | It does not establish | Why the gap matters |
|---|---|---|
| A real informational problem for **full central planning** | That **every** welfare instrument needs the same centrally processed information | A cash transfer or minimum-income scheme leaves the determination of need with the recipient |
| That regulation can impose a liberty cost | That the liberty cost always exceeds the benefit | The inference needs a comparison the argument never performs |
| That markets coordinate dispersed choices | That market outcomes track **genuine needs** rather than effective demand | A person with needs and no purchasing power registers nothing |
| That intervention involves compulsion | That market-generated deprivation involves none | ⚠️ Calling tax "coercion" while calling deprivation natural is a value-laden framing |

⚠️ The defensible finding: the argument's force **diminishes as the instrument decentralises the judgment of need**. A reply is available — thresholds and eligibility rules are still central judgments — which makes the difference one of degree. Saying so is stronger than claiming refutation, because it is both true and gradable.

### 1. The merit is a method, and the method outran its authors

Gauba credits liberalism with shifting social relations from inherited tradition toward rational justification. Merchants and industrialists benefited first, while working-class conditions deteriorated during classical liberal success (PDF pp.44-45).

Gauba then argues that the rationalist method could not be confined to its first beneficiaries: socialism used the same appeal to reason to demand a better settlement for workers.

⚠️ The structure is unusual and examiners reward noticing it: the doctrine **generated the standard by which it was later judged**. That is a defence of liberalism's **method** which survives every criticism of its **outcomes**, because it does not depend on the outcomes being good. ⚠️ Its own limit: releasing a standard is not the same as satisfying it.

### 2. The two contexts, which is the verdict

Gauba's verdict separates liberalism's capitalist and constitutional contexts (PDF p.45).

- As capitalism's defence, liberalism inherits internal contradictions that require judgment through human values rather than economics alone.
- As constitutionalism, it supplies durable curbs on political power.

Gauba defines constitutionalism as organising government under a constitution that allocates and limits institutional powers so no office may act arbitrarily.

⚠️ The continuity is exact, and stating it is the strongest close available in this topic: constitutionalism's target, **arbitrary power**, is Lesson 1's **authoritarian state**, whose defect was that its directions were **obeyed by all without questioning**. The tradition ends where it began, on the one commitment all strands share.

### 3. The objection the close must absorb

⚠️ Two objections stop the verdict being complacent, and including one is what separates a 15-mark close from a 10-mark one. **Constitutionalism is not distributively neutral** — who obtains legal standing, whose claims receive protection and which interests are justiciable are themselves distributive questions. And **Lesson 8's second head bears on constitutional practice**, not only on economics: if groups differ in consciousness, organisation and voice, representative and legal institutions register their claims unequally. ⚠️ So the verdict is graded: what endures is a **framework** for limiting arbitrary power, and survival is not the same as neutrality.

- **GS-IV.** Constitutionalism makes non-arbitrariness the governing limit on official discretion; exact rules and cases come from Ethics and Polity owners.
- **Essay.** The rise-of-socialism argument is first-rate material on reform, reason and unintended consequences: a doctrine that releases a method it cannot then confine.

**Answer framework — the graded close, in three sentences.** One: what survives and why — the constitutional core, because its target, arbitrary power, has not changed since the seventeenth century. Two: what stays contested and why — the economic programme, because the strands draw incompatible conclusions from one shared priority. # CUMULATIVE CONCEPT CHECKS

Four checks: three consolidate a block, and the fourth spans the whole arc. Each model is held within 250 words.

### Cumulative check A — Lessons 1 to 4

**Question:** Build the chain from liberalism's definition to the welfare state, naming each step and the clause that carries it.

**Model answer:** Liberalism begins with individual liberty as policy's highest priority against unquestionable authority. Because Gauba treats it as adaptable, several strands can share eight commitments while differing institutionally. Classical liberalism joins market society and limited government with two incompatible foundations: individual rights restrict sacrifice and taxation, while utilitarian aggregation may permit both. Mill modifies the second stream through qualitative pleasure, liberty and redistribution; Green develops welfare liberalism. The structural reason is internal: democracy registers ordinary needs while capitalism concentrates power and creates a risk of misuse, so welfare becomes a proposed corrective.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not present step four as the definition of liberalism. It is one historically situated reading of the eight tenets, and the movement claim at step two is what makes the later readings possible.

### Cumulative check B — Lessons 5 to 7

**Question:** State what is genuinely new in neoliberalism, then the strongest objection to its central argument and the reply its defenders can make.

**Model answer:** Neoliberalism extends laissez-faire across economic, social and political life; targets government, religion, family and conformist custom; and explicitly rejects determinism by treating persons as authors of their outcomes. Gauba's shared epistemic case adds that needs emerge through decentralised choices unavailable to definitive state knowledge. The strongest objection concerns scope: comprehensive planning and recipient-directed assistance do not demand the same information. Defenders reply that eligibility rules remain central judgments. ⚠️ The force therefore declines as institutional design leaves more judgment with recipients.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not assign Gauba's collective knowledge argument to one named thinker. Nozick's separate rights-based structure comes from independent grounding and must be identified as such.

### Cumulative check C — Lessons 8 and 9

**Question:** Set out the chapter's two-head critique and its closing verdict, and show how they fit together without contradiction.

**Model answer:** The first critique links early liberal practice to bourgeois interests and property-based resistance to factory, housing, consumer and sanitation reform. Gauba later gives welfare's origin a categorical status-quo motive while expressing its apparent results more tentatively. The second critique says representative bargaining cannot universally reconcile interests because groups differ in awareness, organisation and voice. The verdict remains compatible: liberalism's capitalist embodiment is criticised, while its rationalist method and constitutional limit on arbitrary power are defended. Different objects receive different judgments.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not report the critique as the chapter's conclusion. Two of its framing sentences are hedged — ventures **appear to be** devices, the welfare state **seems to create** an illusion — and they are followed by a section crediting liberalism with the standard by which it has just been judged.

### Cumulative check D — the whole arc, Lessons 1 to 10

**Question:** In one connected argument, move from liberalism's definition to a graded verdict on its contemporary standing, marking where each lesson does work.

**Model answer:** Liberalism prioritises individual liberty but adapts institutionally (L1). Classical liberalism combines markets and limited government through rights-based and utilitarian routes (L2-L3). Mill and Green open the welfare turn because democratic responsiveness conflicts with concentrated capitalist power (L4). Neoliberalism reacts through wider laissez-faire, anti-determinism and a collective knowledge objection (L5-L6). Nozick's independently grounded entitlement account adds a rights-based constraint, answered by concerns about unjust history and unchosen disadvantage (L7). Gauba then criticises bourgeois continuity and unequal group voice (L8). ⚠️ Verdict: the economic programme remains contested while constitutional non-arbitrariness endures, without becoming distributively neutral (L9-L10).

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not let the chain become a tour of the lessons. Each step is load-bearing: remove utilitarianism's contingency and Mill's transformation is inexplicable; remove the democracy-capitalism tension and the welfare state becomes charity; remove the two-contexts distinction and the verdict becomes either apologetics or dismissal.

**Practice question 1 [10]:** Distinguish classical liberalism from welfare liberalism. *(Distinguish — answer in about 150 words.)*

**Model answer:** Both strands prioritise individual liberty but disagree about government's role. Classical liberalism favours market coordination and limits the state to protection, contract enforcement and residual common services; its role in interpersonal relations is negative. Welfare liberalism assigns an enabling role directed toward dignified life. Mill opens the transition, Green supplies the moral and welfare theory, and Hobhouse, Laski and Tawney develop it. ⚠️ The dispute concerns the conditions liberty requires, not liberty's rank.

**Scoring note:** marks concentrate in the four permitted functions rather than a vague "minimum state"; in the two labels explained by the **state's role** rather than by a theory of liberty; and in the closing clause, which keeps both inside one family and pre-empts the obvious follow-up.

**Practice question 2 [10]:** Explain what is new in neoliberalism's conception of freedom. *(Explain — answer in about 150 words.)*

**Model answer:** Classical laissez-faire focuses on economic restraint; neoliberalism widens the demand across social and political life. Its targets include government, religion, family and conformist custom. Its explicit anti-determinist premise treats persons as authors rather than products of circumstance, supporting hostility to broad legal and social restrictions. ⚠️ The anthropology is load-bearing because it underwrites the claim that unequal outcomes reflect competence rather than inherited conditions.

**Scoring note:** an **Explain** directive forbids a list, so each element is given its function and the third is identified as load-bearing. Naming religion, family and custom is what distinguishes this from an economics answer, and it is the part most candidates omit.

**Practice question 3 [15]:** "Liberalism's defence of property set aside all human considerations." Critically examine. *(Critically examine — answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer:** The criterion is whether the proposition holds categorically or only historically. Gauba links early liberal practice to bourgeois interests and property-based opposition to factory rules, housing reform, controls on adulteration and household sanitation. The evidence supports a serious criticism of one phase.

Three qualifications follow. The chapter's own rider limits the charge to a phase: **such arguments are no longer advanced**, though **at times** property is still held **sacrosanct** where it produces wide disparities. The critic is Tawney, one of welfare liberalism's own developers, so this is liberalism correcting itself. And the tenets already contained the correction, since freedom of contract is void where a contract is **concluded under pressure** or **compromises dignity of the individual** — the principle the factory and housing cases turn on.

Attacking the logic: **all human considerations** is an absolute, and a single counter-instance defeats it as stated.

⚠️ Verdict: the proposition holds of classical liberalism's **practice in a phase** and fails of its **principles**, which supplied the grounds on which that practice was reversed.

**Scoring note:** the "critically" element is discharged by attacking an absolute quantifier with a counter-instance from the source's **own** tenets, not by hostile adjectives. The third qualification is the distinctive move: showing the correction came from inside the doctrine is more defensible than agreeing with the charge.

**Practice question 4 [15]:** Examine the claim that neoliberalism is merely classical liberalism revived. *(Examine — answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer:** Restated testably, the claim says neoliberalism adds nothing to classical liberalism. Support exists because Gauba presents it as a contemporary restoration of laissez-faire against welfare-state expansion.

Four differences qualify that support. Neoliberal scope extends into social and political life; its targets include religion, family and conformity; its anti-determinism is explicit; and its shared knowledge argument responds to an existing welfare state. Nozick's independently grounded entitlement account adds a separate rights-based layer.

⚠️ Finding: the claim holds as a description of the **conclusion** and fails as a description of the **position**.

**Scoring note:** "Examine" requires the claim restated as testable, evidenced both ways, and closed with a finding. The move that earns the marks is conceding the chapter's own umbrella sentence first — an answer attacking the grouping without acknowledging that its source uses it has set itself against its own evidence.

**Practice question 5 [20]:** Critically evaluate the claim that liberty requires a positive role for the state. *(Critically evaluate — answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer:** Evaluation needs a criterion: whether a conception of liberty is realisable through the institutional arrangement it recommends.

The welfare case treats deprivation as a restraint on agency. Green adds the moral and welfare-state argument, later developed by Hobhouse, Laski and Tawney. Gauba's structural support is the tension between democratic responsiveness and concentrated capitalist power.

Against the criterion the claim is strong but incomplete. **No government can have** definitive knowledge of needs, which limits what provision can achieve, though the limit weakens where the instrument leaves the judgment of need with the recipient. ⚠️ On the entitlement account a rights-respecting state may not redistribute at all. And the welfare position supplies no stopping rule: if the state's sphere extends to whatever liberty requires, it expands without limit — a point the chapter's own definition, reaching museums and scientific research, concedes.

⚠️ Graded verdict: liberty requires **conditions**, and securing some of them requires state action, so the claim holds; the unbounded version fails for want of any criterion of sufficiency. The rival wins wherever provision substitutes the state's judgment of need for the individual's.

**Scoring note:** "Critically evaluate" fails without a named criterion, so it is declared first and every judgment refers back to it. What lifts this above competence is conceding the opponent's strongest point from the **source's own text**, and closing with a condition under which the rival wins.

**Practice question 6 [20]:** "Liberalism survives as constitutionalism, not as an economic doctrine." Discuss. *(Discuss — answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer:** Gauba's verdict separates liberalism's defence of capitalist relations from its constitutional commitment. The first remains internally contradictory and requires judgment through human values; the second supplies durable curbs on political power.

Classical minimalism, welfare provision and neoliberal rollback are incompatible economic programmes sharing liberty's priority, while Gauba's bourgeois and group-imbalance criticisms bear on their practice. Constitutionalism endures by allocating and limiting public authority so no office can act arbitrarily—the same target as liberalism's original opposition to authoritarian rule.

Two objections must be met. Constitutionalism is not distributively neutral, since legal standing and protection are themselves distributive. And the second critique bears on constitutional practice, not only on economics.

⚠️ Verdict: the proposition holds, with the asymmetry narrower than it appears. What endures is a **framework** for limiting arbitrary power — and survival is not neutrality.

**Scoring note:** a Discuss stem needs both sides reconstructed fairly and a verdict. The distinguishing move is turning the chapter's **own** second critique against its **own** verdict, which shows the candidate testing the source rather than reciting it, while still landing where the source lands.

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

## A. Definition, target, licence

- Individual liberty has first priority in public policy, especially against authoritarian restraint.
- **Authoritarian state**: individual, group, institution **or a set of rules enshrined in a sacred book** as **the source of authority**; orders **obeyed by all without questioning**.
- Origin: **West**, **late seventeenth century**, to **liquidate feudal privileges of the land-owning class** and enable the **new entrepreneurial class**.
- Liberalism is an adaptable movement rather than a fixed programme.

## B. The eight tenets and their operative words

| | Tenet | Word that carries it |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | **Rational creature** with potential for **social progress** | rational |
| (b) | **No basic contradiction** between self-interest and common interest | **basic** |
| (c) | **Natural rights which cannot be transgressed by any authority** | untransgressable |
| (d) | **Artificial institutions**, obedience **on the condition of fulfilling this function** | **conditional** |
| (e) | **Primacy of procedure over the end-product** | procedure |
| (f) | Civil liberties; restriction only to **ensure equal freedom for others** | **proviso** |
| (g) | Contract **void** if **concluded under pressure** or if it **compromises dignity of the individual** | **void** |
| (h) | Policy as **free bargaining between groups** | bargaining |

> 🔑 **R-I-N-A / P-C-C-B.**

## C. Classical settlement and the two streams

- **Market society as the model of social organization**; four functions: **protect life**, **protect property**, **enforce contracts**, **minimum common services which would not be undertaken by private entrepreneurs**.
- **Necessary evil** · **state = means, individual = end** · **absolute authority ruled out**.
- **Laissez-faire:** minimal state interference in economic activity. **Negative liberalism:** restrained public role in interpersonal relations.
- **Locke (1632-1704)**, **Smith (1723-90)**, **Bentham (1748-1832)**; nationality verification belongs to the ledger.
- Individualism locates rights and interests in persons, rejects burdening one merely for a larger unit and limits taxation to common services.
- Utilitarianism: **greatest happiness of the greatest number**, **the interest of the few may be sacrificed**, **balance of pleasure over pain**, **no distinction between qualities**, **maximizing the quantity**.
- ⚠️ Individualist route **principled**; Benthamite route **contingent**.
- **Mill (1806-73)**: **qualitative differences** · **variety of tastes** · **liberty of individual as the highest value** · **taxation of the rich for the benefit of the poor** → **tilted it toward individualism and transformed its basic character** → **paved the way** for the welfare state.

## D. The welfare turn and its structural reason

- **Green (1836-82)** adds the moral and welfare-state argument; **Hobhouse**, **Laski** and **Tawney** develop the tradition. Positive liberalism assigns government an enabling role in dignified life.
- Schedule: **social security** (loss of job or income, death of the bread-winner, prolonged illness, disability, any other calamity) · **free education** · **public health** · **poor relief** · essentials at **subsidized rates** · **protection of cultural heritage** · promotion of **higher education and scientific research**.
- Democracy registers ordinary needs; capitalism concentrates economic power and creates a risk of misuse; welfare is proposed as a corrective.
- ⚠️ Keep **may** and **is sought to be**.

## E. Neoliberalism

- Gauba groups neoliberalism, neo-classical liberalism and libertarianism as contemporary laissez-faire restoration against welfare-state expansion.
- **Hayek (1899-1992)** · **Friedman (1912-2006)** · **Nozick (1938-2002)**; **second half of the twentieth century**.
- Charge: **inimical to individual liberty**, involving the **forced transfer of resources from the more competent to the less competent**. ⚠️ Second half contested.
- New: **scope** (also **social and political sphere**) · **targets** (**religion, family and customs of social conformity apart from political institutions**) · **anthropology** (**repudiates the deterministic outlook**; **cannot be construed as the outcome of his circumstances**; **man is the maker of his destiny**).
- Epistemic core: **spontaneous order ... as exemplified in free markets** · **no government can have such knowledge** · **myriad unpredictable transactions** · **curtailing their freedom without fulfilling their genuine needs** · **transfer such decisions to the market which will maximize their choice**.
- ⚠️ Coda 1 usable: **in the economic sphere, market exemplifies the genuine democracy**. Coda 2 (**votes traded against welfare benefits ... the most productive members**) — report only.
- Source control: Gauba's knowledge case remains collective; Nozick's **acquisition — transfer — rectification** triad is independently grounded; the welfare reply remains bounded and cross-owned.

## F. Critique and verdict

- **Clung to capitalism** so firmly that new ventures **appear to be** devices for sustaining it.
- Head 1: arose for the **bourgeoisie** against **feudal interests**; **insisted so much on freedom of the property-owning class that it set aside all human considerations**. **Tawney, *The Acquisitive Society* (1920)** — the only quotation: **factory legislation**, **housing reform**, **adulteration of goods**, **even to compulsory sanitation of private houses**. Rider: **no longer advanced**, but **at times** property is **sacrosanct**.
- **1789** → state functions widen; **it cannot be denied** the welfare state was evolved **not out of sympathy** but to **maintain the status quo**; **incremental change ... cleverly devised to contain unrest**; it **seems to create an illusion of welfare**.
- ⚠️ Hedge map: **appear to be** and **seems to create** hedged; **it cannot be denied** unhedged.
- Head 2: representative reconciliation is possible but not universal; groups differ in awareness, organisation and voice.
- Merit: rational justification replaces tradition; cost: worker deterioration; twist: the same rational method enables socialism's challenge.
- Verdict: capitalist liberalism remains contradictory; constitutional liberalism supplies durable limits on arbitrary public power. ⚠️ Residual: not distributively neutral.

## H. Six exam-ready formulations

- Individual liberty is public policy's highest priority.
- **Minimum common services which would not be undertaken by private entrepreneurs.**
- Individualism limits taxation to common services.
