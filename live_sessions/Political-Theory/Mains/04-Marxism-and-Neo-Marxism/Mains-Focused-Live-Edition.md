### 1. The opening block, restored

Marxism derives its name from **Karl Marx (1818–83)**, whom Gauba describes as a prominent German economist and social philosopher. The label was unknown during Marx's lifetime. **Friedrich Engels (1820–95)**, Marx's close associate and collaborator, reported Marx's remark: **“All I know is that I am not a Marxist.”** Gauba suggests either that Marx rejected claims to a complete world-view or that modesty shaped the remark. By the late nineteenth century, **G. V. Plekhanov (1856–1918)** described Marxism as a whole world-view. Gauba's conclusion is broader: Marxism is not Marx's exclusive system but a living tradition of social thought with further possibilities (p.45).

⚠️ This rebuts the framing *“Marxism means Marx, and Marx is dated.”* It opens the 2025, 2020 and 2019 relevance questions by shifting the object of assessment from one author to an unfinished intellectual tradition.

### 2. The condition, stated without a single invented statistic

Marxism *in its proper sense* **first emerged in the mid-nineteenth century as a response to the oppressive conditions created by the capitalist system** (p.46). The chapter's account of those conditions is an argument against liberalism conducted in liberalism's own terms. Liberalism arose in the seventeenth century **as a philosophy of human liberty**, but by the middle of the nineteenth it had become clear that its classical form, **with its doctrine of laissez-faire and market economy, had failed to create conditions of human freedom**. It had instead **achieved the objective of establishing capitalism under which a narrow class of capitalists enjoyed distinctive power and privileges at the expense of the substantial majority of the working class**. The sentence that carries the whole indictment: **the equality before law and in political status sought by the exponents of liberalism had been realised with tremendous economic inequalities and consequent injustice** (p.46).

⚠️ That sentence sits **inside Topic 04's own window** and is used freely here even though liberalism is Topic 03's subject. It is the formal-versus-substantive premise that both the democracy stem (2026) and the equity stem (2022) need. ❌ The welfare-state-as-illusion paragraph at p.44 is **Topic 03's** and is not imported — one pointer only, to `basic\03_Liberalism-and-Neoliberalism.md`.

Gauba contrasts concentrated gains among owners, bankers and speculators with degrading living conditions, insecurity, malnutrition, disease and death among industrial workers. His point is that capitalist development frustrated humanitarian expectations of broadly shared progress (p.46).

### 3. The utopians, and the criterion they generate

The **first response** came as an **early socialist movement**, which **rejected the policy of unregulated market competition** and highlighted the deteriorating conditions of the working classes. The chapter names five, and the division among them matters as much as the label they share.

| Thinker (dates, country) | Programme, in the chapter's terms | What the programme concedes |
|---|---|---|
| **Saint-Simon (1760–1825)**, France | **A more or less centralized economy under state control** | Remedy is *statist* |
| **Louis Blanc (1811–82)**, France | Same — **centralized economy under state control** | Remedy is *statist* |
| **Robert Owen (1771–1858)**, England | **Elaborate plans** for **model communities** on **'free cooperation'** instead of **'free competition'** | Remedy is *exemplary*, not systemic |
| **Charles Fourier (1772–1837)**, France | Same — **model communities** | Remedy is *exemplary* |
| **P. J. Proudhon (1809–65)**, France | A nationwide network of decentralised workers' cooperatives exchanging goods and services | Remedy is *anti-statist* |

⚠️ Centralised state control, withdrawn model communities and decentralised bargaining cooperatives are **three incompatible remedies** for one agreed diagnosis. That incompatibility is itself part of the defect the chapter is naming.

Gauba's verdict is that these thinkers diagnosed what was wrong but lacked a practicable response: their visionary remedies remained detached from lived conditions. They therefore offered **an accurate diagnosis of capitalism's ills without a clear conception of the remedy**, which explains the label **utopian socialists** (p.46).

In the **1830s and 1840s**, Marx and Engels criticised these programmes and sought to replace utopian socialism with a scientific account that connected social problems to a mechanism of solution (pp.46–47).

> 🔑 **The criterion, in one line:** *diagnosis without a mechanism is utopian; a remedy tied to a mechanism is what the chapter calls scientific.* Every later lesson either supplies that mechanism or tests it.

### Concept check

**Question:** A candidate opens an evaluation of Marxism with: "Marxism is the thought of Karl Marx, a nineteenth-century thinker whose predictions have not come true." Identify the two errors the chapter itself would charge, and give the repaired opening.

**Model answer:** The first error is identifying Marxism wholly with Marx. The second error is judging outcomes before fixing a criterion. ### 1. The definition, and why its fourth clause matters

Gauba defines Marxism as political-economic principles associated with Marx and Engels that seek a scientific basis for socialism. Its method is historical: conflict arises when one class owns and controls productive assets—land, buildings, mines, forests, machinery and capital—and compels another class to work under conditions it sets. The proposed resolution combines overthrow of capitalism, social ownership and control of productive assets, work required of all and fuller development of productive forces (p.47).

### 1. Hegel at full strength, because the inversion depends on it

Gauba explains Marx's dialectical method through Hegel. In Hegel's idealist account, idea or consciousness is primary; a partial thesis encounters an opposing partial truth and produces a synthesis, which in turn becomes the starting point for further development toward absolute consciousness. Marx retains the dialectical movement but rejects its idealist foundation (p.48).

⚠️ Two details earn marks and are usually dropped. **Thesis and antithesis are both described as partial truths**, so the process is one of successive approximation rather than of error being replaced by truth. And **synthesis becomes the next thesis**, which is what makes the process iterative rather than a single three-step event.

❌ **The standing trap.** Thesis–antithesis–synthesis is reported here as **Hegel's** schema. It must not be presented as **Marx's own settled formula**.

### 2. The inversion, and the terminus it leaves standing

In Gauba's presentation, Marx makes material conditions primary and treats institutions as expressions of changing material circumstances. Contradictions within those conditions drive dialectical change toward what the chapter calls a **rational mode of production** (p.48).

⚠️ Notice what the inversion does **not** change. Hegel's process ends in **absolute idea**; Marx's ends in **perfect material conditions, exemplified by a 'rational mode of production'**. The substance is inverted; the **teleology is retained**. That is the chapter's own wording, and it is the fairest available ground for the determinism objection — which is why it is better to state it here, in the chapter's words, than to let an examiner supply it. Lesson 10 shows how to concede it without conceding the whole doctrine.

### 3. Engels's three laws, named

Gauba attributes three laws to Engels's *Anti-Dühring* (1878): transformation of quantity into quality and the reverse; interpenetration of opposites; and negation of negation. He links them to the resolution of material contradictions and treats conflict between classes as one expression of that dialectical process (p.48).

⚠️ Gauba supplies the **names** of Engels's three laws but not explanatory glosses. A one-line explanation—such as accumulated quantitative change producing a qualitative shift—is analytical synthesis and must not be attributed to Gauba.

The last sentence is the hinge to everything that follows: **class conflict is also a manifestation of this process**. Dialectical materialism is not a separate doctrine standing beside class conflict; class conflict is its instance in social life.

**Question:** "Marx turned Hegel upside down, and that is the whole of dialectical materialism." State what the inversion changes, what it leaves untouched, and why the second half matters more in an answer.

**Model answer:** The inversion changes the **substance**. For Hegel, **'idea' or 'consciousness' was the essence of universe** and **all social institutions were the manifestation of changing forms of idea**; for Marx, **'matter' (and not the idea) was the essence of universe** and social institutions were **the manifestation of changing material conditions**. What it leaves untouched is the **engine and the destination**. In both accounts movement is driven by **inherent tension**; in both, each resolution is itself partial and provokes the next; and in both the process runs to a terminus — **'absolute idea'** for Hegel, **perfect material conditions, exemplified by a 'rational mode of production'** for Marx. ### 1. What is inside the mode of production, and what moves first

**Mode of production has two components: productive forces and relations of production. Forces of production cannot remain static; they have an internal tendency of development in the direction of achieving the perfect society** (pp.48–49).

**Productive forces have two components: productive resources (tools and equipment), and labour power (human knowledge and skills)** (p.49). ⚠️ Both halves matter. Forces are not only machinery; **labour power is human knowledge and skills**, which is what lets the theory treat education and technical capacity as economic rather than superstructural.

Why do productive forces move first? Gauba points to deliberate efforts to improve production, technological change and the corresponding growth of human knowledge and skills—labour power (p.49). The mechanism therefore retains agency rather than treating change as wholly impersonal.

Production relations, by contrast, are fixed by the ownership pattern of productive assets, dividing society into contending owners and non-owners (p.49). Thus productive forces move through cumulative ingenuity while relations remain tied to ownership. That asymmetry drives the mechanism.

### 2. Why revolution is episodic

Gauba says productive development may initially leave the ownership pattern unchanged. When the mode of production changes, the contending classes also change, but conflict between classes continues (p.49).

⚠️ This timing clause makes the fetter model coherent. Productive development is continuous; revolution is episodic. Pressure accumulates while ownership remains unchanged. Rupture occurs when society's material productive forces collide with existing production relations, which then become fetters; a class linked to new productive resources displaces the old ruling class (p.49).

⚠️ Note who acts. The revolutionary agent is **the new social class which comes to own new productive resources** — a class defined by its position in the emerging economy, not by its suffering. That is a precise claim and it is frequently misreported as an uprising of the worst-off.

**As a result of social revolution, an old social order is replaced by a new social order. In this dynamic old contending classes are replaced by new contending classes, but conflict between classes continues on a different plane. This pattern continued until the emergence of capitalism, which will be overthrown by a socialist revolutionary change, leading to the eventual emergence of classless society** (p.49).

### 1. The claim, and the qualification that protects it

The opening sentence of the *Communist Manifesto* **(1848)** reads, as the chapter prints it: **The history of all hitherto society is the historical record of class struggles** (p.50).

⚠️ **Wording note, made once.** The chapter's form is *"all hitherto society"*; the standard English translation reads *"all hitherto **existing** society"*. Reproduce the chapter's form only when quoting the chapter, and use the standard wording when quoting the *Manifesto* in an examination answer.

Gauba immediately narrows the claim to **recorded history**. The *Manifesto* preceded later knowledge of prehistoric communities with collective ownership; these were subsequently described as primitive communism. Its class-struggle thesis therefore begins with societies divided by private property into antagonistic classes (p.50).

❗ This is the chapter **pre-empting the standard objection** that classless societies existed. A candidate who supplies the qualification before the examiner raises it converts a vulnerability into a demonstration of textual control; a candidate who quotes only the slogan hands the examiner a free counter-example.

The **`Primitive Communism`** box describes small communities using rudimentary tools, collective ownership, common labour and shared output. In Gauba's account, the absence of private property also means the absence of class exploitation in this early stage (p.50).

⚠️ The last clause is doing analytical work, not decoration: it states the chapter's criterion of exploitation. **No private property, therefore no exploitation of man by man.** Exploitation is a property relation, not a measure of hardship — which is why Lesson 8's arithmetic is about ownership and not about how poor anyone is.

### 2. The branch the slogan omits

The *Manifesto* then lists successive opposing pairs—freeman and slave, patrician and plebeian, feudal lord and serf, guild-master and journeyman—and allows two outcomes: revolutionary reconstruction or the common ruin of the contending classes (p.50).

⭐ **The “common ruin” branch means revolution is not guaranteed.** The source permits two outcomes, and using both prevents an inevitabilist reading. Conflict may also be hidden rather than open, allowing the theory to explain quiet periods without abandoning the class-conflict claim.

### 3. Social formation, and the three named pairs

The **`Social Formation`** box defines a social formation as an order organised around a particular mode of production. Household production is associated with slave-owning society, large-scale agriculture with feudal society and machine production with capitalist society (p.50).

As productive forces develop, one mode of production replaces another, but class conflict reappears in a new social form: master against slave, lord against serf, and bourgeoisie against proletariat (pp.50–51).

The **`Lord and Serf`** box distinguishes serfdom from slavery. The lord owns the land; the serf cultivates it for a fixed share, retains family and possessions, but cannot leave the duty without permission. The key contrast is standing without exit (p.51).

The **`Bourgeoisie and Proletariat`** box cites Engels's 1888 note to the English edition of the *Communist Manifesto*. Bourgeoisie denotes owners of productive assets who employ wage labour; proletariat denotes wage-labourers who lack productive assets and must sell their labour power to live (p.51).

⚠️ **Wording note.** Engels's note says the proletarian sells **labour power**; the surplus-value paragraph at p.52 says the worker is **forced to sell his labour**. This edition uses **labour power** wherever the mechanism is at issue, because the distinction is doing work: what is sold is the *capacity* to work for a period, while what the purchaser obtains is the *use* of that capacity — which is where a gap between what is paid and what is produced becomes possible at all.

### 4. Why the chapter calls the modern conflict "crystallized"

Gauba presents modern capitalist conflict as more sharply crystallised and reports the *Manifesto*'s two-camp thesis: bourgeoisie and proletariat increasingly confront one another. Yet capitalism did not create class antagonism from nothing; it replaced feudal classes, oppressive conditions and forms of struggle with new ones (p.51).

⚠️ **Simplified** is the chapter's own word, and it is a claim about the **number of camps**, not about the severity of the conflict. It is also the most empirically exposed claim in the topic — and the honest place to note that exposure is here, not in a critical paragraph bolted on later.

### 1. Agency: why the proletariat and not another class

Gauba says Marx and Engels believed the struggle had reached a decisive phase and placed their confidence in the proletariat's revolutionary capacity. In the *Manifesto* account, other classes decline with modern industry while the proletariat, produced by that industry, becomes the principal revolutionary agent (p.51).

⚠️ **Special and essential product** is the claim worth carrying. The proletariat is not an external force opposing capitalism; it is what capitalism **necessarily produces** in the course of operating. That is why the antagonism is described as internal to the system rather than as an attack on it, and it is the cleanest available statement of what "contradiction" means in this tradition.

### 2. Scale: the majoritarian claim

Gauba contrasts earlier minority movements with the proletarian movement's claim to be self-conscious, independent and conducted in the interest of an overwhelming majority (p.52).

⭐ This is the natural bridge to any democracy stem. It supplies a **counter-standard**: the tradition does not reject majority rule, it claims that existing arrangements do not deliver it and that the proletarian movement would. ⚠️ Note the three qualifiers — **self-conscious**, **independent**, **of the immense majority** — because each is a condition that can be assessed, and an answer that treats the claim as an assessable condition rather than as a boast is doing analysis.

### 3. Structure: the finality argument, and why it is not optimism

**According to Marx and Engels, this revolutionary change would bring about the ultimate emancipation of mankind because there is no class beneath the proletariat which could be exposed to exploitation when the proletariat comes to power** (p.52).

❗ Read the reason carefully. Finality is **not** claimed because the proletariat is virtuous or because its leaders would be restrained. It is claimed because, on this analysis, exploitation requires a class **below** the exploiting class, and after the proletariat takes power there is none. That is a **structural** argument, and treating it as an optimistic assertion about human nature misses what makes it interesting — and makes it harder to criticise well, because the real objection has to be addressed to the structural premise rather than to naivety.

⚠️ **The chapter's own counterweight.** Flow Chart 2's **Future-I** row still lists contending classes under socialism: **"Workers in power and the former capitalists"**. So on the chapter's own chart, conflict persists through the socialist stage; classlessness arrives at **Future-II**, after the **liquidation of remnants of Capitalism**. A critical answer that cites Future-I against the finality claim is using the source against itself, which is the most durable form of criticism available.

### 4. The end-state, and the cost the chapter prints

The projected end-state combines collective control of productive assets, compulsory work for all and fuller development of productive forces, leading to a classless order and the claimed end of class conflict (p.52).

⭐ **“Make work compulsory for everyone.”** This establishes that the end-state includes a **universal obligation**. Do not soften it into a voluntary duty or inflate it into a description of any historical regime. Gauba states no exemption, enforcing authority or limit.

That is the genuine tension a freedom stem needs. It is not the crude claim that socialism abolishes liberty; it is the precise observation that this programme's own description includes a universally imposed obligation, so the question *consistent with individual freedom?* has real content and a serious answer must engage it rather than deny it. ⚠️ The reply available from inside the tradition is a **relocation** rather than a denial: the humanist strand (Lesson 9) treats freedom as something capitalism has already removed through alienation, so the comparison is not obligation against freedom but one unfreedom against another — and that reply must be stated as a relocation, honestly, or it looks like evasion.

### 1. What the theory is for

Gauba presents the **theory of surplus value** as the economic foundation of the Marxist critique of capitalism and as an explanation of working-class exploitation within the capitalist mode of production (p.52).

### 2. The premises

**According to Marx, labour is the sole source of value. Of the four components of production, viz. land, labour, capital and organization, three components—land, capital and organization are sterile since they are capable of replicating only what is put in them. Labour is the only component which produces value in society. Put differently, the value of a commodity is the product of labour** (p.52).

The accounting rule includes labour used in raw-material production, processing, energy supply, machinery and buildings, assessed under average social conditions of production and average skill (p.52). This specifies what the value calculation is meant to include.

### 3. The mechanism: two prices, and why wages fall to subsistence

Gauba calls the total labour embodied in a commodity its **natural price** and distinguishes it from the **market price**, which varies with demand and supply. Under capitalism, the worker must sell labour power in that market (p.52).

⚠️ Here is the **labour / labour power** point in its operative form. Engels's 1888 note (Lesson 6) says the proletarian sells **labour power**; this paragraph says **labour**. Use **labour power** when stating the mechanism, because the gap the theory describes opens precisely between the **capacity** that is sold at a market price and the **value** that the exercise of that capacity creates. Say so in a clause; it takes six words and shows control of the distinction.

Gauba links wage decline to an excess supply of job-seekers: the employer obtains the worker's full productive capacity while paying a subsistence-level wage (p.52).

❗ That first sentence is the chapter's printed **reason why** wages settle at subsistence. Without it, "subsistence wages" is an assertion that any examiner can ask you to justify; with it, the claim is a supply-side mechanism stated in one line.

### 4. The conclusion

The value produced is divided between wages and **surplus value**. Gauba describes the latter as unpaid labour appropriated by the capitalist and distributed through profit, rent and interest. This is the mechanism by which the theory identifies exploitation even when the market wage is paid (pp.52–53).

❌ **Trap.** Surplus value is **not** “any profit” and is not a movement in market price. It is the unpaid residue identified by the labour-accounting argument.

⚠️ **The equity point.** Worker and capitalist possess formal legal standing, but the worker is economically compelled to sell labour power at the prevailing market wage. Payment of that wage can coexist with appropriation of a residue because the capacity purchased and the value produced differ. The argument therefore contrasts formal equality with structurally compelled exchange. Full equity doctrine belongs to `upsc-ai-kit\knowledge\Philosophy\paper-2\socio-political\Social-Political-Ideals.md`.

⚠️ **The sterility premise is asserted without argument.** The chapter says land, capital and organization **are sterile since they are capable of reproducing only what is put in them** and offers nothing further. ❗ **Concede this explicitly.** Gauba offers no defence of the sterility premise. The theory's **structure** is clear and its distributive claim intelligible, but its foundation is **stipulated rather than demonstrated**. The wage-decline mechanism is also conditional on an excess supply of job-seekers.

### 1. The doctrinal turn, stated exactly

Gauba says contemporary Marxist debate reassesses the weight of its tenets and identifies newer forms of domination and conflict. The superstructure is no longer treated as wholly dependent on the base; recognising interdependence prompts wider analysis of law, culture and ideology (p.53).

⚠️ Note the logic of that third sentence: the interdependence recognition **causes** the analytical expansion. Neo-Marxism's interest in culture is therefore presented as a **consequence of a theoretical correction**, not as a change of subject. That is the whole reply to the charge that neo-Marxism abandoned Marxism — and it is the chapter's own sequence, not a sympathetic reconstruction.

### 2. The Frankfurt School, and the double distance

Gauba traces major neo-Marxist controversies to the Frankfurt School: the Institute of Social Research was established at Frankfurt in **1923**, exiled after Hitler's rise in **1933**, relocated to the United States and re-established in Frankfurt in the **early 1950s** (p.53).

❗ The institutional position is decisive: the school's scholars opposed capitalism, were also disillusioned with Soviet socialism and sought an alternative path consistent with their understanding of Marxism (p.53).

⭐ **The double distance is the move for the future-of-Marxism stem.** The standard objection runs: socialist regimes collapsed, therefore Marxism is finished. The reply is not a denial but a **chronology** — a major current of the tradition was **already disillusioned with Soviet socialism** and **seeking an alternative path** decades before those regimes fell, so their collapse does not refute the current that had disowned them. ⚠️ Keep the reply bounded: it rescues *that current*, not every Marxist claim, and Lesson 10 concedes what the collapse genuinely does touch.

**The ideas evolved by the Frankfurt School are compendiously characterised as 'critical social theory', although they do not fit into a single framework** (p.53). ⚠️ The qualifying clause is useful: it licenses you to treat critical theory as a family rather than a doctrine, and it protects against a stem that attributes one position to the whole school.

### 3. A ready-made, attributable definition of ideology critique

Gauba quotes **David Held**, via *A Dictionary of Marxist Thought*, edited by Tom Bottomore (1983), to characterise critical theory. In paraphrase, the approach examines distorted representations that conceal unequal power, traces how interests and conflicts appear in thought and seeks greater awareness capable of weakening legitimating ideologies and changing action (p.53).

❗ This is an **attributable, dated** account of ideology critique. Attribute it to **David Held**, as quoted by Gauba from the Bottomore volume of **1983**; do not attach it directly to a Frankfurt School member.

Critical theory extended Marxist interpretation across philosophy, economics, political science, sociology, anthropology, social psychology, psychoanalysis, music and fine arts. It is therefore a multidisciplinary research programme rather than one uniform thesis (p.53).

### 4. The figures, the phrase, and the generation note

Gauba names **Theodor Adorno (1903–69)**; **Max Horkheimer (1895–1973)**, another first-generation figure; **Herbert Marcuse (1898–1979)**; and **Jurgen Habermas (1929– )**. He associates the school with cultural critique and with **technological domination** rather than a solely ownership-based account (p.53).

⭐ **“Technological domination” rather than capitalist domination** is Gauba's phrase for the shift.

⚠️ **Independent generation note.** Gauba lists the four figures and dates but gives no generational classification. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy identifies Adorno, Horkheimer and Marcuse with the first generation and Habermas as the leading second-generation figure: `https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/critical-theory/` §1.1 (accessed 5 Oct 2026). This classification is independent context, not Gauba's wording.

### 5. The two strains

Gauba divides neo-Marxism into humanist and scientific directions. The humanist strain draws on the Young Marx, centres alienation and emancipation, and is described as the mainstream of critical theory (pp.53–54).

⚠️ **Mainstream** is a ranking, and it is the chapter's. The humanist strain is not one option among two equals; it is named as the main current. That matters on a 2024-style alienation stem, because it places alienation at the centre of the later tradition rather than at its margin.

Gauba presents Marcuse's **one-dimensional man** as a condition in which communication channels dull discontent by stimulating easily satisfied material wants. The proposed remedy begins with awareness of unfreedom; Gauba's further suggestion that freedom then follows easily should be treated as optimistic (p.54).

⭐ This is the best printed sentence in the chapter on manufactured consent, and the **only** printed statement of a neo-Marxist remedy. ⚠️ Two flags. The word **easily** in the final clause is the chapter's; ❌ do not soften it, and note it as optimistic — the claim that awareness is the hard part and freedom follows easily is a strong assumption, and an examiner rewards a candidate who notices. And ⚠️ this is also the chapter's one substantive sentence on the **experience** of alienation, which is why a 2024-style answer that has the 1844/1927 chronology from Lesson 2 should land here for content.

**On the other hand, the scientific strain of neo-Marxism is chiefly concerned with its scientific-explanatory character. It is especially interested in configurations as well as relative importance of culture-based, ideological and social factors. Consequently Louis Althusser (1918–90), a French communist philosopher, challenged the humanist orientation of Marxian thought in the opening years of the 1960s, and asserted the importance of analysing the underlying structures of human societies—especially their modes of production** (p.54).

⚠️ **The early 1960s** dates the scientific turn and gives it an agent. ### 1. The conclusion, in full

Gauba treats the collapse of Eastern European socialism by the end of the 1980s and of the Soviet Union by 1991—followed by market reforms and multiparty systems—as evidence requiring reconsideration of classical Marxism. His 2009 discussion also notes liberalising changes in China and contrasts them with the rigidity associated with Stalin and Mao. Most importantly, the predicted withering away of the state did not occur; socialist states collapsed and moved toward liberal arrangements. Yet Gauba does not infer that Marxism should simply be abandoned. He argues that domination and exploitation extend beyond a worker-capitalist conflict: dependency theory shifts attention to developing nations confronting colonial and neo-colonial forces, while other neo-Marxists identify more complex forms of domination. ### 4. The spine: premises → mechanism → conclusion

⚠️ **Analytical synthesis.** The combined spine below assembles claims from pp.47–54; Gauba does not present it as one continuous passage. It is the reusable skeleton for 15- and 20-mark answers.

- **Premises.** Private property in the means of social production divides society into **dominant and dependent classes with irreconcilable class interests** (p.47). The mode of production — forces plus relations — forms the base, and economic relations **play important role in shaping** the superstructure (p.48). Forces develop; relations, fixed by the **pattern of ownership**, do not (p.49).
- **Mechanism.** Development of the forces **fails to make any dent** in the ownership pattern until conflict arrives, at which point **these relations turn into their fetters**; the new owning class overthrows the old; a new social formation replaces the old and **class conflict continues on a new plane** (p.49). The relation is experienced economically as **surplus value** (pp.52–53), politically as a unity **held together only by the ideological power of the dominant class** (p.47), and humanly as **alienation** (pp.47, 54).
- **Conclusion.** The framework survives as a structural account of domination and exploitation, is **not** mechanical on its own statement, and has been extended rather than abandoned; but its state prediction failed, its sterility premise is stipulated, and **the framework of Marxism must be modified suitably** (p.54).

# CUMULATIVE CONCEPT CHECKS

Four checks. Three consolidate a block and the fourth spans the whole arc; each model is held within 250 words.

### Cumulative check A — Lessons 1 to 3

**Question:** Build the chain from the condition that produced Marxism to the method it adopted, naming at each step the sentence that carries it.

**Model answer:** One: liberalism arose as **a philosophy of human freedom** but by the mid-nineteenth century its classical form **had failed to create conditions of human freedom**, having established capitalism in which **legal and political equality … had been achieved with tremendous economic inequalities and consequent injustice**. Two: the first response, the early socialist movement, reached **a correct diagnosis of the ills of the capitalist order, but had no clear conception of the remedy** — five named thinkers with three incompatible programmes — which fixes the criterion: a remedy must be tied to a mechanism. Three: Marx and Engels accordingly **sought to replace Utopian socialism by scientific socialism**. Four: the doctrine is defined as principles founded **in order to lay scientific foundations of socialism**, treating history as **a process of conflict between antagonistic forces and classes** arising from **the faults in the mode of production** where one class gains **ownership and control of the means of social production** and **compels the other class to work on conditions dictated by itself**. Five: the chapter divides the tradition itself — **Classical Marxism** as the **original tenets … as a scientific system of thought**, held together **only by the ideological power of the dominant class**, against **Neo-Marxism**, the **wider implications … including humanist thought of the Young Marx**. Six: the method is Hegel's dialectic inverted — **matter**, not idea, as the essence — with Engels's three laws in *Anti-Dühring* (1878), and **class conflict is also a manifestation of this process**.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not treat step two as background. The diagnosis/remedy criterion is the only standard in the chapter against which the doctrine can be *evaluated* rather than merely described, and dropping it is why most answers to the 2025-style stem become summaries.

### Cumulative check B — Lessons 4 to 6

**Question:** Set out the materialist mechanism at the exact strength the source gives it, and show how Flow Chart 2 and the written-history qualification each protect it from an obvious objection.

**Model answer:** Gauba's calibrated claim is that economic relations **play important role** in shaping social, political, intellectual and ethical relationships. The base is the mode of production; the superstructure includes legal and political institutions, religion, morals, social practices, literature, art and culture. Forces of production—productive resources plus labour power—develop as people devise better techniques, while relations remain tied to ownership. When those relations obstruct further development, they become fetters, conflict produces a new social formation and class struggle continues on a new plane. Flow Chart 2 adds causes to the stages rather than presenting a bare chronology. The written-history qualification limits the class-struggle proposition: Gauba treats the *Manifesto* as addressing recorded history after private property and antagonistic classes emerged, while prehistory is described as primitive communism.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not let the qualification become a retreat. The chapter restricts the proposition's scope to societies with private property and then **explains** the prior stage within the same framework; that is a narrowing with a reason, not an admission that the theory failed.

### Cumulative check C — Lessons 7 to 9

**Question:** State the chapter's case that the proletarian revolution is final, the cost it prints, and how neo-Marxism relocates the argument — with the price of each move.

**Model answer:** **Finality** rests on three grounds: the proletariat is treated as capitalism's revolutionary product; its movement claims majority scale; and Gauba argues that no lower class remains for it to exploit once it takes power. **The cost** is explicit: collective ownership, compulsory work for all and fuller development of productive forces. Flow Chart 2 nevertheless preserves a transitional Future-I conflict between workers in power and former capitalists, so classlessness is an asserted destination rather than an immediate result. The humanist reply relocates freedom by treating alienation under capitalism as prior unfreedom. Neo-Marxism then extends analysis from ownership to culture and technological domination, while Althusser retains modes of production. The costs are therefore threefold: finality requires an unproven transition, compulsory work creates a liberty tension, and the cultural turn becomes less distinctively Marxist as it moves away from ownership.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not present neo-Marxism as a united correction of classical Marxism. Its two strains are in dispute with one another — Althusser **challenged the humanist themes** in the **early 1960s** — so a stem about "the neo-Marxist view" needs two answers, not one.

### Cumulative check D — the whole arc, Lessons 1 to 10

**Question:** In one connected argument, move from the condition that produced Marxism to a graded verdict on it, marking where each lesson does work.

**Model answer:** Liberalism delivered capitalism, in which **legal and political equality … had been achieved with tremendous economic inequalities and consequent injustice**, and the early socialists answered with **a correct diagnosis … but no clear conception of the remedy** — the criterion the whole topic is then scored against (L1). Marxism is defined as principles laying **scientific foundations of socialism**, resting on ownership of the **means of social production** and a unity **held together only by the ideological power of the dominant class**, with the humanist *Manuscripts* recovered only in **1927** (L2). Its method inverts Hegel — **matter**, not idea — retaining the terminus of a **'rational mode of production'** (L3). The social claim is calibrated: economic relations **play important role in shaping** a superstructure that always included **religion, morals, social practices, literature, art and culture** (L4). The mechanism follows: forces develop, relations fixed by the **pattern of ownership** do not, **these relations turn into their fetters**, and Flow Chart 2 supplies the causes and the **"-do-"** point (L5). Class conflict is claimed for **all written history** (L6), the revolution is final because **there is no class below the proletariat** — at the cost of making **work compulsory for everyone** (L7) — and surplus value supplies the economic proof on a premise the chapter never argues (L8). Neo-Marxism then widens the frame from a double distance (L9). ⚠️ Verdict: the state prediction failed, the no-true-socialism defence is **an oversimplification of the issue**, and **the framework of Marxism must be modified suitably** (L10).

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not let the chain become a tour of the lessons. Each step is load-bearing: remove the criterion and the evaluation has no standard; remove **fails to make any dent** and the mechanism cannot explain timing; remove the Future-I cell and the finality claim looks stronger than the source allows; remove the double distance and the verdict collapses into the regime record.

# ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS MODEL PRACTICE

⚠️ Original practice prompts written for this edition. They are **not** previous-year questions and must not be cited as such. Models at 10 marks are held within 150 words; models at 15 and 20 marks within 250.

**Practice question 1 [10]:** Distinguish classical Marxism from neo-Marxism using the chapter's own division. *(Distinguish — answer in about 150 words.)*

**Model answer:** The division is the source's own. **Classical Marxism** is **the original tenets of Marxism—as a scientific system of thought**, whose exponents besides Marx and Engels are **V. I. Lenin (1870–1924)**, **Rosa Luxemburg (1871–1919)** and **Mao Zedong (1893–1976)**; it holds that **private property divides society into dominant and subordinate classes with irreconcilable class interests**, that society **is held together only by the ideological power of the ruling class**, and that history moves to a **classless society**. **Neo-Marxism** is **the wider implications of Marxism, covering humanist thought of the Young Marx**, seeking to analyse **the less visible dimensions of the phenomenon of dominance and dependence, distortions in the modern civilisation and the possible ways to human emancipation**. The doctrinal hinge is that **it is no longer thought that the superstructure is wholly dependent on the base**. ⚠️ Neo-Marxism itself splits into **humanist** and **scientific** strains, which dispute each other.

**Scoring note:** marks concentrate in using the chapter's own two definitions rather than a generic "classical is economic, neo is cultural" contrast; in the ideological-power clause, which most answers never reach; and in the closing qualification, which pre-empts the follow-up question about whether neo-Marxism is one thing.

**Practice question 2 [10]:** Explain why the Marxist account treats exploitation as a property relation rather than as severe hardship. *(Explain — answer in about 150 words.)*

**Model answer:** The criterion is stated where the chapter describes the one society it calls free of exploitation. Under **primitive communism**, tools were **held in common ownership**, labour was **common labour** and its fruits were **shared in common**, so **there was no concept of private ownership, hence no exploitation of man by man** — although conditions were severe: **the cudgel, stone hatchet and flint blade, the stone-tipped spear**, with **muscular strength** the only motive force. Hardship was maximal, exploitation absent. Conversely, exploitation appears wherever one class holds **ownership and control of the means of social production** and **compels the other class to work on conditions dictated by itself**; its measure is **surplus value**, **the value of the labour done by the labourer for which he is not paid at all**. ⚠️ Hence the serf, with family and belongings, is still exploited, while the better-off wage-labourer is too.

**Scoring note:** the answer is built from a single pair of contrasts that the chapter supplies on facing pages, and it protects against the commonest conceptual error on this topic — treating exploitation as a synonym for poverty, which makes both the surplus-value argument and the equity distinction unintelligible.

**Practice question 3 [15]:** "Historical materialism explains change but cannot explain its timing." Critically examine. *(Critically examine — answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer:** The mechanism explains a condition for rupture, not a date. Productive forces develop as technology and labour power advance, while production relations remain tied to ownership. Pressure can therefore accumulate without changing the ownership pattern. Rupture becomes possible when society's material productive forces collide with existing production relations and those relations become fetters. A class associated with new productive resources then displaces the old ruling class. Flow Chart 2 adds a cause to each transition—private property, feudalism, capitalism and socialist revolution—rather than presenting a bare chronology. Two qualifications remain: the model does not predict timing, and the *Manifesto* allows common ruin rather than successful reconstruction. Agency is also specified structurally, leaving consciousness and organisation underexplained.

**Scoring note:** a **critically examine** directive requires the proposition to be tested rather than rejected, so the answer concedes the two genuine gaps after refuting the main charge. The **fails to make any dent** clause is the hinge; without it the answer can describe the fetter but cannot explain why revolutions are episodic.

**Practice question 4 [15]:** Assess the claim that Marxism's turn to culture and ideology strengthened the tradition. *(Assess — answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer:** The turn strengthened explanatory reach but weakened distinctiveness. It began from a revised base-superstructure relation: culture, law and ideology gained relative analytical weight without eliminating modes of production. The Frankfurt School's opposition to capitalism and disillusionment with Soviet socialism widened the space for criticism. Gauba's quoted Held formulation, paraphrased, treats ideology critique as exposing distorted representations that legitimate unequal power; Marcuse adds technologically mediated consent. Yet the further domination is detached from ownership, the less the explanation depends on a specifically Marxist mechanism. The turn therefore preserved relevance at the cost of uniqueness, supporting Gauba's verdict of modification rather than defence or abandonment.

**Scoring note:** **Assess** requires a weighed judgment, so the two halves are given separate evidence rather than a concession tacked on at the end. The Held quotation is the highest-value element available, because it is attributable and dated and almost no competing answer will have it.

**Practice question 5 [20]:** Explain the Marxian theory of surplus value and examine its adequacy as an account of exploitation under capitalism. *(Explain and examine — answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer:** **Theory.** Gauba presents labour as the sole creator of value and treats land, capital and organisation as reproducing prior inputs rather than independently creating value. Labour embodied across materials, processing, energy and equipment supplies the natural-price benchmark. Labour power is sold under market conditions; excess job-seekers can depress wages while employers obtain the worker's full productive capacity. Output then divides into wages and surplus value, from which profit, rent and interest are drawn. **Adequacy.** The account explains how formal legal standing and payment of a market wage can coexist with economic compulsion and appropriation. Its foundational weakness is that the sterility of non-labour inputs is asserted rather than defended, while the wage mechanism depends on excess labour supply. The theory is structurally clear but evidentially vulnerable.

**Scoring note:** at 20 marks both verbs must be visibly answered. The concession on sterility is not a weakness in the answer but the single clearest signal of source control, because the premise is genuinely undefended in the chapter and a candidate who claims otherwise has invented material.

**Practice question 6 [20]:** "Marxism survives as a method of analysis even where it has failed as a programme of government." Discuss. *(Discuss — answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer:** **Failure as programme:** socialist regimes collapsed in Eastern Europe and the Soviet Union, adopted market and multiparty reforms, and did not realise the predicted withering away of the state. Gauba also rejects the claim that they were never socialist as an oversimplification. **Survival as method:** regime failure does not independently disprove analysis of ownership, the wage relation, surplus value, base and superstructure, or ideology. Neo-Marxism extends the method toward colonial dependence and complex cultural forms of domination, while the Frankfurt School had already rejected Soviet socialism. **Verdict:** the proposition broadly holds, but the extension has a cost—the further analysis moves from ownership, the less distinctively Marxist it becomes. Gauba's final prescription is modification rather than vindication or abandonment.

**Scoring note:** the mark separation on this question lies in refusing the no-true-socialism defence explicitly. Answers that rely on it lose the critical half entirely; answers that report and reject it demonstrate that the defence of the method is being made on the source's terms and not on sympathy.

## A. The condition Marxism answered, and the criterion it inherited

- Liberalism = a seventeenth-century **philosophy of human freedom**; by the mid-nineteenth century its classical form, **with its doctrine of laissez-faire and market economy, had failed to create conditions of human freedom**.
- It **achieved the objective of establishing capitalism** under which **a narrow class of capitalists enjoyed distinctive power and privileges at the expense of the substantial majority of the working class**.
- ⭐ **The equality before law and in political status sought by the exponents of liberalism had been realised with tremendous economic inequalities and consequent injustice** (p.46).
- Evidence without statistics: gains concentrated among owners, bankers and speculators while industrial workers faced degrading living conditions, insecurity, malnutrition, disease and death.
- Utopian socialists, with programmes: **Saint-Simon (1760–1825)** and **Louis Blanc (1811–82)**, France — **a more or less centralized economy under state control**; **Robert Owen (1771–1858)**, England, and **Charles Fourier (1772–1837)**, France — **model communities** on **'free cooperation'** against **'free competition'**; **P. J. Proudhon (1809–65)**, France — **a nationwide system of decentralized workers' cooperatives** bargaining for **the mutual exchange of goods and services**.
- ⭐ Verdict and criterion: **a correct diagnosis of the ills of the capitalist order, but had no clear conception of the remedy**; **visionary solutions—far removed from the hard realities of life**; **'utopian socialists'**.
- **1830s–1840s**: **Karl Marx, described as a German scholar**, and **Friedrich Engels, described as a young German businessman residing in England**, **sought to replace Utopian socialism by scientific socialism for the analysis of social problems and finding their solution**.

## B. The name, the tradition, and why the object of assessment matters

- **Karl Marx (1818–83)**, German economist and social philosopher; **Friedrich Engels (1820–95)**, close friend and collaborator.
- The term was **unknown in Marx's own lifetime**; Engels reported Marx's **"All I know is that I am not a Marxist"**; the chapter's two readings are no claim to a comprehensive world-view, or modesty.
- **G. V. Plekhanov (1856–1918)**, *a Russian Marxist*: **'Marxism is a whole world-view.'**
- Marxism is not Marx's exclusive system; Gauba presents it as a living tradition of social thought (p.45).

## C. The definition and its four-part resolution test

- Political-economic principles associated with Marx and Engels claim a scientific basis for socialism; method = **historical analysis**; history = conflict among opposed forces and classes.
- Cause: the **mode of production** allows one class to own and control productive assets while compelling another class to work under conditions it sets.
- Resolution, four conjoined parts: **overthrowing capitalism** · **all socially productive assets under social ownership and control** · **enforcing universal labour** · **ensuring full development of the forces of production**.
- *Manifesto* **(1848)**: the working class's role in **past and future history**; the call to unite for **their own emancipation and, through that emancipation, the liberty of all mankind**.

## E. Classical and Neo — the chapter's own division

- **Classical Marxism** = **the original tenets of Marxism—as a scientific system of thought**. Exponents besides Marx and Engels: **V. I. Lenin (1870–1924)**, *a Russian revolutionary and thinker*; **Rosa Luxemburg (1871–1919)**, *a Polish activist*; **Mao Zedong (1893–1976)**, *a Chinese revolutionary and thinker*. ⚠️ Descriptions asymmetric; report, do not level or explain.
- Compressed doctrine: private property divides society into dominant and subordinate classes; ruling-class ideology helps sustain the order; revolutionary resolution is expected to lead toward classlessness.
- **Neo-Marxism** = **the wider implications of Marxism, covering humanist thought of the Young Marx**, analysing **the less visible dimensions of the phenomenon of dominance and dependence, distortions in the modern civilisation and the possible ways to human emancipation**.
- `Young Marx` box: early work **unpublished during his lifetime**, **discovered from the archives of German Social Democrats only in 1927**, published as the ***Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts of 1844***, **distinguished from Marx's later work which is characterized by scientific rigour**, containing **humanist thought on communism**, focused on **alienation and freedom**, and **exposing the dehumanizing effect of capitalism**. ⚠️ No forms of alienation are enumerated anywhere in the chapter.

## F. Dialectical materialism and Engels's three laws

- Four named tenets: **(a) Dialectical Materialism; (b) Historical Materialism; (c) Doctrine of Class Conflict; (d) Theory of Surplus Value.**
- **G. W. F. Hegel (1770–1831)**: **'idea' or 'consciousness' was the essence of universe**; institutions = **manifestation of changing forms of idea**; **thesis (partial truth) → antithesis (opposite of thesis—again a partial truth) → synthesis (nearer the truth)**, synthesis becoming the next thesis, until **'absolute idea' or 'absolute consciousness'**.
- Marx: **'matter' (and not the idea) was the essence of universe**; institutions = **manifestation of changing material conditions**; ⭐ terminus = **perfect material conditions, exemplified by a 'rational mode of production'**.
- ⭐ **Engels, *Anti-Dühring* (1878)**: **(a) the transformation of quantity into quality, as well as the reverse; (b) the interpenetration of opposites; (c) the negation of negation** — principles that **signify the process of resolving contradictions of material circumstances of human life which paves the way for social progress**.
- ⭐ **Conflict between classes is also a manifestation of this process.**
- ❌ The triad is **Hegel's** as reported here, never Marx's settled formula. ⚠️ Any gloss on a law is analytical, not page-anchored.

## G. Historical materialism, at its printed strength

- **Dialectical materialism represents the philosophical basis of Marxism; the materialist interpretation of history represents its scientific basis.**
- ⭐ Economic relations **play important role** in shaping social, political, intellectual and ethical relationships; do not inflate this into sole determination.
- An ideal society would meet its members' necessities only through an extended process containing imperfect social forms.
- **Base (the foundation) = the mode of production.** ⭐ **Superstructure (the external build-up) = legal-political structure, religion, morals, social practices, literature, art and culture, etc.**
- Forward link: the superstructure is not wholly dependent on the base; a degree of interdependence is recognised (p.53).

## H. The mechanism, and Flow Chart 2's three retained points

- **Mode of production = forces + relations.** **Forces of production cannot remain static; they have an inherent tendency of development.**
- **Forces = means of production (tools and equipment) + labour power (human knowledge and skills).**
- Why forces move: **men and women constantly endeavour to devise better ways of production**; technology improves; **corresponding development of labour power**.
- **Relations … are determined by the arrangement of ownership of the means of social production** → **two contending classes—haves and have-nots**.
- ⭐ **In previous stages of historical development, development of the productive forces fails to make any dent in this pattern** — the timing clause.
- Production relations become fetters; a class linked to new productive resources displaces the old ruling class; the social formation changes and class conflict continues in a new form.
- **Flow Chart 2 — *Marxian View of the Stages of Historical Development*** (p.49), five columns: Historical Epoch · Prevailing Mode of Production · **Caused by** · Social Formation · Contending Classes.
- ⭐ Three retained points: the **"Caused by"** column (*emergence of private property · rise of feudalism · rise of capitalism · socialist revolution · liquidation of remnants of capitalism*); **Future-I's "Workers in power and the former capitalists"**; and the **"-do-"** rows showing the prevailing mode of production unchanged across capitalist → socialist → communist.

## I. Class conflict, the written-history cut, and the named pairs

- **Conflict between classes or class struggle is an integral part of historical materialism.**
- *Manifesto* opening, as the chapter prints it: **The history of all hitherto society is the historical record of class struggles.** ⚠️ Standard translation: **all hitherto *existing* society**.
- ⭐ **Here, history means all written history**; pre-history **was not known** when the *Manifesto* was written and was later called **primitive communism**; the *Manifesto* **addresses the period starting with the division of social order into antagonistic classes since the emergence of private property**.
- `Primitive Communism`: **club, stone axe, flint knife, stone-tipped spear**, later **bow and arrow**; **muscular strength the only motive force**; **common ownership, common labour**, fruits **shared in common**; ⭐ **no concept of private ownership, hence no exploitation of man by man**.
- Second *Manifesto* proposition: opposing class pairs may produce either social reconstruction or the common ruin of the contending classes.
- `Social Formation`: organisation **around a specific mode of production** — household-based small-scale → **slave-owning**; large-scale agriculture-based → **feudal**; large-scale machine-based → **capitalist**.
- Pairs: **master and slave** · **lord and serf** · **bourgeoisie (capitalists) and proletariat (workers)**.
- `Lord and Serf`: the **landlord who was the owner of land** against **the peasant who did not possess land but cultivated it, and received a limited, fixed share of the produce**; ⭐ **Serf was not a slave** — family and belongings permitted, **could not leave his duty without permission of his lord**.
- Engels's 1888 note: bourgeoisie = owners of productive assets who employ wage labour; proletariat = wage-labourers who lack productive assets and must sell labour power.
- Capitalism replaces feudal classes, oppressive conditions and forms of struggle rather than abolishing class antagonism.
- **It has simplified the class antagonisms** → **two great hostile camps**. ⚠️ The most empirically exposed claim in the topic.

## J. The revolution, the finality argument, and the printed cost

- **The proletarian revolutionary change would be different from all previous revolutions of history.**
- Agency: Gauba presents the proletariat as modern industry's principal revolutionary product while other classes decline.
- Scale: earlier movements served minorities; the proletarian movement claims self-conscious, independent majority scale.
- ⭐ Structure: **there is no class beneath the proletariat which could be exposed to exploitation when the proletariat comes to power** — a structural claim, not an optimistic one.
- End-state, three conjoined acts: **all socially productive assets under social ownership** · ⭐ **make work compulsory for everyone** · **develop the forces of production to their full potential** → **classless society which will mark the end of class conflict**.
- ⚠️ No exemption, enforcing authority or limit is stated for the obligation; and Flow Chart 2's **Future-I** still names contending classes. The humanist answer on freedom is a **relocation**, not a refutation.

## K. Surplus value: premises, accounting, mechanism, split, concession

- The **theory of surplus value** supplies the economic basis of the Marxist critique of capitalism.
- ⭐ **Labour is the sole creator of value.** Of **land, labour, capital and organization**, three are **sterile because they are capable of reproducing solely what is put in them**.
- Accounting: count labour from **raw material → processing → sources of energy → machinery and building**, at **average conditions of social production** and **average skill**.
- **Natural price** = embodied labour; **market price** varies with demand and supply. Use **labour power** when stating the mechanism.
- Excess job-seekers can depress wages while the employer acquires the worker's full productive capacity.
- Split: wages | **surplus value**, **pocketed by the capitalist as his profit**; **rent and interest are paid from the surplus value**; surplus value = ⭐ **the value of the labour done by the labourer for which he is not paid at all**, on **the sterile elements of production (organization, land and capital)** — **a glaring example of the worker's exploitation under capitalism**.
- ❗ **Concession:** the sterility premise is **asserted without argument**; no reply is available from these sources and none is constructed. The wage mechanism is conditional on a surplus of job-seekers.

## L. Neo-Marxism: the turn, the institution, the strains

- ⭐ **It is no longer thought that the superstructure is wholly reliant on the base** → **acknowledgment of mutual dependence between base and superstructure** → **extensive analysis of various aspects of superstructure**.
- Frankfurt School: Institute of Social Research established in **1923** → exiled in **1933** after Hitler's rise → relocated to the United States → re-established in Frankfurt in the **early 1950s**.
- ⭐ Double distance: opposed capitalism, disillusioned with Soviet socialism and seeking an alternative path.
- **'Critical theory'** — **although they do not fit into a single framework**.
- ⭐ **David Held**, quoted in ***A Dictionary of Marxist Thought*, ed. Tom Bottomore (1983)**: a perspective **centred on ideology critique—of systematically distorted representations of reality which attempt to conceal and legitimate asymmetrical power relations**, concerned with how **social interests, conflicts and contradictions are expressed in thought**, hoping to **enhance awareness of the roots of control, undermine ideologies and help to compel changes in consciousness and action**.
- Disciplines entered: philosophy, economics, political science, sociology, anthropology, social psychology, psychoanalysis, music and fine arts.
- Figures: **Theodor Adorno (1903–69)**; **Max Horkheimer (1895–1973)**, first-generation; **Herbert Marcuse (1898–1979)**; **Jurgen Habermas (1929– )**, independently classified by SEP as second-generation.
- ⭐ **A cultural critique of bourgeois society—particularly in terms of 'technological domination' rather than capitalist domination.**
- **Humanist strain**: draws on the Young Marx and centres alienation and emancipation. **Marcuse**: *one-dimensional man*; communication channels dull discontent by stimulating easily satisfied material wants. Gauba's claim that awareness of unfreedom leads easily to freedom is optimistic and should be flagged.
- **Scientific strain**: concerned with **scientific and explanatory character**, **structures** and **relative importance of cultural, ideological and social factors**. **Althusser (1918–90)**, *a French communist philosopher*, **challenged the humanist themes … in the early 1960s**, asserting analysis of **the deep structures of human societies—especially their modes of production**.
- ⚠️ Gramsci is **cross-chapter** (pp.306, 309–311; Topic 15), one bounded paragraph only. ⚠️ **'Dependency theory'** only — no theorist, work, year or world-systems vocabulary.

## M. The conclusion and the graded verdict

- Record: collapse of socialism in **Eastern Europe by the end of 1980s**; in the **former Soviet Union by 1991**; **followed by the adoption of market economies and multiparty political systems**; this **necessitated new thinking on the adequacy of orthodox Marxism on which these regimes were based**.
- 📝 Book-period: the **People's Republic of China** liberalisation remark, and **no scope of rigidity … as it was during Stalin's regime (1879–1953) in Russia and Mao Zedong (1893–1976) in China**.
- The predicted withering away of the state did not occur; socialist states collapsed and moved toward liberal arrangements. Concede fully.
- ⭐ **"These countries were not really socialist at all!"** → **This is, yet, an oversimplification of the issue.**
- The struggle against domination and exploitation extends beyond a worker-versus-capitalist conflict.
- **'Dependency theory'**: **the focus of contest now centres on developing nations' struggle against the forces of colonialism and neo-colonialism**; other neo-Marxists show that **domination and exploitation in social life assume many complex patterns**.
- ⭐ **The framework of Marxism must be modified suitably to address all these problems in the contemporary context.**
