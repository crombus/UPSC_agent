# Marxism and Neo-Marxism — Mains-Focused Live Edition

A fast-preparation edition built for answer-writing. It teaches what raises the mark on a question about capitalism, class, historical change, exploitation and the later Marxist turn to ideology and culture, and it stops there. The comprehensive twenty-lesson session for this topic remains the unchanged reference layer; this is neither a copy nor a condensation of it, and it is not a substitute for it when depth is actually wanted.

**The one idea that organises everything.** The source chapter is not an exposition of Marx. It is an argument with a verdict at the end. Marxism earned the word **scientific** by supplying a *mechanism* where the early socialists had supplied only a *diagnosis*; it is then judged by whether that mechanism still works, and the chapter's own answer is a graded one — **"The framework of Marxism must be modified suitably"** (PDF p.54). Place a stem inside that argument and it becomes answerable. Treat the topic as four tenets to be recited and the answer collapses into a summary, which is exactly what the heaviest-weighted demand in the verified record — *evaluate*, *how far*, *what future* — punishes hardest.

### 1. The opening block, restored

Marxism derives its name from **Karl Marx (1818–83)**, described as **a famous German economist and social philosopher of the nineteenth century** and the chief exponent of the theory. ⚠️ The chapter immediately destabilises the name: **this term was unknown in Marx's own lifetime**, and **Friedrich Engels (1820–95)**, *a close friend and collaborator of Marx*, once reported Marx's own comment — **"All I know is that I am not a Marxist."** The chapter offers two readings: Marx *probably said so as he did not claim to offer a comprehensive world-view*, and it is *also possible that he did not advance such a claim out of modesty*. Towards the end of the nineteenth century **G. V. Plekhanov (1856–1918)**, *a Russian Marxist*, announced that **'Marxism is a whole world-view.'** The chapter then settles the matter in its own voice: Marxism **should not be regarded as a system of thought exclusively belonging to Marx**; it **comprises a rich tradition of social thought—a living tradition, with immense possibilities** (all PDF p.45).

⚠️ This is the chapter's own warrant for refusing the framing *"Marxism means Marx, and Marx is dated."* It is the opening move for three of the eight verified stems — the 2025, 2020 and 2019 relevance questions — because it shifts the object of assessment from one nineteenth-century author to a tradition the chapter itself describes as unfinished. ❗ It sits on **p.45**, outside the span both owners declare, which is why most prepared answers never reach it.

### 2. The condition, stated without a single invented statistic

Marxism *in its proper sense* **first appeared in the middle of the nineteenth century in response to the oppressive conditions created by the capitalist system** (p.46). The chapter's account of those conditions is an argument against liberalism conducted in liberalism's own terms. Liberalism arose in the seventeenth century **as a philosophy of human freedom**, but by the middle of the nineteenth it had become clear that its classical form, **with its doctrine of laissez-faire and free market economy, had failed to create conditions of human freedom**. It had instead **achieved the goal of establishing capitalism under which a tiny class of capitalists enjoyed special power and privileges at the expense of the large majority of the working class**. The sentence that carries the whole indictment: **the legal and political equality sought by the exponents of liberalism had been achieved with tremendous economic inequalities and consequent injustice** (p.46).

⚠️ That sentence sits **inside Topic 04's own window** and is used freely here even though liberalism is Topic 03's subject. It is the formal-versus-substantive premise that both the democracy stem (2026) and the equity stem (2022) need. ❌ The welfare-state-as-illusion paragraph at p.44 is **Topic 03's** and is not imported — one pointer only, to `basic\03_Liberalism-and-Neoliberalism.md`.

The evidence the chapter gives is concrete and quotable without inventing data: **the tremendous increase of wealth was cornered by a small section which happened to own the means of production while the large majority of the industrial population was forced to a sub-human living**, and **successful bankers and market speculators increased their wealth by leaps and bounds while the slum-dwelling working classes were living under the constant threat of insecurity, malnutrition, discomfort, disease and death**. These developments **belied all the humanitarian hopes of universal economic progress** (p.46). ⚠️ Use the second sentence as the vivid one; it does the work candidates usually attempt with an undated statistic.

### 3. The utopians, and the criterion they generate

The **first response** came as an **early socialist movement**, which **opposed the policy of free market competition** and drew attention to the deteriorating conditions of the working classes. The chapter names five, and the division among them matters as much as the label they share.

| Thinker (dates, country) | Programme, in the chapter's terms | What the programme concedes |
|---|---|---|
| **Saint-Simon (1760–1825)**, France | **A more or less centralized economy under state control** | Remedy is *statist* |
| **Louis Blanc (1811–82)**, France | Same — **centralized economy under state control** | Remedy is *statist* |
| **Robert Owen (1771–1858)**, England | **Elaborate plans** for **model communities** on **'free cooperation'** instead of **'free competition'** | Remedy is *exemplary*, not systemic |
| **Charles Fourier (1772–1837)**, France | Same — **model communities** | Remedy is *exemplary* |
| **P. J. Proudhon (1809–65)**, France | A **nationwide system of decentralized workers' cooperatives** which would **bargain with one another for the mutual exchange of goods and services** | Remedy is *anti-statist* |

⚠️ Centralised state control, withdrawn model communities and decentralised bargaining cooperatives are **three incompatible remedies** for one agreed diagnosis. That incompatibility is itself part of the defect the chapter is naming.

The verdict is printed twice over. **All these thinkers knew clearly what was wrong with the world, but they were not clear as to what to do about it for they suggested only visionary solutions—far removed from the hard realities of life.** In other words, they had arrived at **a correct diagnosis of the ills of the capitalist system, but had no clear conception of the remedy**, and are **therefore rightly described as 'utopian socialists'** (p.46).

During the decades beginning with the **1830s and the 1840s** their ideas were criticised by **a group of brilliant writers committed to fundamental social change**, notably **Karl Marx, a German scholar**, and **Friedrich Engels, a young German businessman residing in England**, who **sought to replace Utopian socialism by scientific socialism for the analysis of social problems and finding their solution** (pp.46–47).

> 🔑 **The criterion, in one line:** *diagnosis without a mechanism is utopian; a remedy tied to a mechanism is what the chapter calls scientific.* Every later lesson either supplies that mechanism or tests it.

### Concept check

**Question:** A candidate opens an evaluation of Marxism with: "Marxism is the thought of Karl Marx, a nineteenth-century thinker whose predictions have not come true." Identify the two errors the chapter itself would charge, and give the repaired opening.

**Model answer:** The first error is **identifying the doctrine with its author**. The chapter records that the term **was unknown in Marx's own lifetime**, reports Marx's own **"All I know is that I am not a Marxist,"** and states in its own voice that Marxism **should not be regarded as a system of thought exclusively belonging to Marx** but **comprises a rich tradition of social thought—a living tradition, with immense possibilities**. An evaluation addressed only to Marx therefore evaluates something narrower than its object. The second error is **beginning from outcomes before fixing a criterion**. The chapter supplies the criterion before it states the doctrine: the early socialists reached **a correct diagnosis of the ills of the capitalist system, but had no clear conception of the remedy**, and Marx and Engels **sought to replace Utopian socialism by scientific socialism**. The test is therefore whether the remedy is tied to a mechanism, not whether a prediction was fulfilled. Repaired opening: Marxism is assessed as a tradition, against the criterion it set for itself — a diagnosis of capitalism joined to a mechanism of change — after which the record of socialist regimes becomes evidence to be weighed rather than a verdict already reached.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not read the living-tradition sentence as immunising Marxism from refutation. It fixes **what** is being judged, not **how leniently**; the chapter's own conclusion is severe, and it expressly rejects the defence that failed regimes **"were not really socialist at all!"**

**Original Mains practice [10 marks]:** Explain why the early socialists are described as "utopian". *(Explain — answer in about 150 words.)*

**Model (within 150 words):** The label records a specific failure, not mere idealism. The early socialist movement **opposed the policy of free market competition** and had concrete programmes: Saint-Simon (1760–1825) and Louis Blanc (1811–82) urged **a more or less centralized economy under state control**; Owen (1771–1858) and Fourier (1772–1837) produced **elaborate plans** for **model communities** governed by **'free cooperation'** instead of **'free competition'**; Proudhon (1809–65) proposed a nationwide system of **decentralized workers' cooperatives** bargaining for mutual exchange. The chapter's charge is that they **knew clearly what was wrong with the world, but they were not clear as to what to do about it**, offering only **visionary solutions—far removed from the hard realities of life**: **a correct diagnosis of the ills of the capitalist system, but no clear conception of the remedy**. ⚠️ Their three remedies also contradict one another, which is the same defect seen from outside.

### 1. The definition, and why its fourth clause matters

**Marxism may be defined as a set of political and economic principles founded by Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels in order to lay scientific foundations of socialism. It seeks to understand the problems of human society through historical analysis and treats history as a process of conflict between antagonistic forces and classes. This conflict arises from the faults in the mode of production in which one class comes to gain ownership and control of the means of social production (land, buildings, mines, forests, machinery and capital, etc.) and compels the other class to work on terms and conditions dictated by itself. ### 1. Hegel at full strength, because the inversion depends on it

Marx **borrowed his dialectical method from German philosopher, G. W. F. Hegel (1770–1831)** and **sought to combine it with his materialism**. Hegel **had postulated that 'idea' or 'consciousness' was the essence of universe, and that all social institutions were the manifestation of changing forms of idea. Idea evolved into new forms because of its inherent tension, exemplified in the clash between thesis (partial truth) and antithesis (opposite of thesis—again a partial truth) resulting in synthesis (which is nearer the truth). As long as synthesis itself contains partial truth, it takes the role of thesis and undergoes the same process until this process reaches absolute truth, exemplified in 'absolute idea' or 'absolute consciousness'** (p.48).

⚠️ Two details earn marks and are usually dropped. **Thesis and antithesis are both described as partial truths**, so the process is one of successive approximation rather than of error being replaced by truth. And **synthesis becomes the next thesis**, which is what makes the process iterative rather than a single three-step event.

❌ **The standing trap.** Thesis–antithesis–synthesis is reported here as **Hegel's** schema, inside the chapter's exposition of what Marx borrowed. It is pedagogic shorthand and must never be presented as **Marx's own settled formula** (basic owner §9; advanced owner §12). An answer that attributes the triad to Marx as his canonical method has made a factual error in its first paragraph.

### 2. The inversion, and the terminus it leaves standing

**Marx believed that 'matter' (and not the idea) was the essence of universe, and that social institutions were the manifestation of changing material conditions. Matter underwent the dialectical process because of its inherent tension, until perfect material conditions, exemplified by a 'rational mode of production', come into existence** (p.48).

⚠️ Notice what the inversion does **not** change. Hegel's process ends in **absolute idea**; Marx's ends in **perfect material conditions, exemplified by a 'rational mode of production'**. The substance is inverted; the **teleology is retained**. That is the chapter's own wording, and it is the fairest available ground for the determinism objection — which is why it is better to state it here, in the chapter's words, than to let an examiner supply it. Lesson 10 shows how to concede it without conceding the whole doctrine.

### 3. Engels's three laws, named

**Engels, in his *Anti-Dühring* (1878) postulated three laws of material dialectics (or dialectical materialism): (a) the transformation of quantity into quality, and vice versa; (b) the interpenetration of opposites; and (c) the negation of negation. These principles signify the process of resolving contradictions of material conditions of human life which paves the way for social progress. Class conflict is also a manifestation of this process** (p.48).

❗ The advanced owner says Engels "supplies the three laws" and **never names them**. Naming them exactly is the difference between a paragraph that could have been written about any philosopher and one that shows the source has been read. ⚠️ The chapter supplies the **names only**. Any one-line gloss on what a law means is analytical, not page-anchored, and must be marked as such — for instance, that *quantity into quality* describes accumulated incremental change producing a change of kind. Write the gloss if it helps, flag it, and never attribute it to the chapter.

The last sentence is the hinge to everything that follows: **class conflict is also a manifestation of this process**. Dialectical materialism is not a separate doctrine standing beside class conflict; class conflict is its instance in social life.

For Hegel, **'idea' or 'consciousness' was the essence of universe** and **all social institutions were the manifestation of changing forms of idea**; for Marx, **'matter' (and not the idea) was the essence of universe** and social institutions were **the manifestation of changing material conditions**. What it leaves untouched is the **engine and the destination**. In both accounts movement is driven by **inherent tension**; in both, each resolution is itself partial and provokes the next; and in both the process runs to a terminus — **'absolute idea'** for Hegel, **perfect material conditions, exemplified by a 'rational mode of production'** for Marx. The second half matters more because it is where the standard objection enters. A doctrine with a built-in destination invites the charge of teleology, and a candidate who has already stated the terminus in the chapter's own words can meet that charge on prepared ground, whereas one who has advertised only the inversion is answering an objection they did not see coming. ⚠️ Engels's three laws — **transformation of quantity into quality, and vice versa**, **interpenetration of opposites**, **negation of negation** — describe the same engine, and the chapter adds that **class conflict is also a manifestation of this process**.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not present thesis–antithesis–synthesis as Marx's own formula. The chapter sets out the triad as **Hegel's**, in the course of explaining what Marx borrowed; treating it as Marx's settled method is a first-paragraph factual error that an examiner can mark without reading further.

**Original Mains practice [10 marks]:** Explain the relationship between dialectical materialism and historical materialism in Marxist theory. *(Explain — answer in about 150 words.)*

**Model (within 150 words):** The chapter states the relation in its own labels: **dialectical materialism represents the philosophical basis of Marxism** while **historical materialism represents its scientific basis**. Dialectical materialism supplies the general claim — **matter**, not idea, is the essence of the universe; social institutions are **the manifestation of changing material conditions**; matter moves through **inherent tension** until **perfect material conditions, exemplified by a 'rational mode of production', come into existence** — with Engels's three laws in *Anti-Dühring* (1878) naming the forms that movement takes. Historical materialism is that claim applied to society: in any given epoch the **economic relations of society … play important role in shaping their social, political, intellectual and ethical relationships**. The junction is printed: **class conflict is also a manifestation of this process**. ⚠️ So the second is not a separate doctrine but the first specified to human history.

### 1. What is inside the mode of production, and what moves first

**Mode of production has two components: forces of production and relations of production. Forces of production cannot remain static; they have an inherent tendency of development in the direction of achieving the perfect society** (pp.48–49).

**Forces of production have two components: means of production (tools and equipment), and labour power (human knowledge and skills)** (p.49). ⚠️ Both halves matter. Forces are not only machinery; **labour power is human knowledge and skills**, which is what lets the theory treat education and technical capacity as economic rather than superstructural.

The chapter then answers a question examiners reward and most answers never address — *why do the forces move first?* **Men and women constantly endeavour to devise better ways of production. Improvement in the means of production is manifested in the development of technology. This is matched by development of human knowledge and skills as required to operate the new technology. Hence there is the corresponding development of labour power** (p.49). The engine is **deliberate human effort**, not an impersonal law acting on people. ⚠️ That is also the best available answer to the charge that historical materialism removes agency.

**On the other hand, relations of production in any given epoch are determined by the pattern of ownership of the means of social production. This gives rise to two contending classes—haves and have-nots** (p.49). So the two components move on different principles: forces by cumulative human ingenuity, relations by a **pattern of ownership** which has no such tendency to change. That asymmetry is the whole mechanism.

### 2. Why revolution is episodic

**In earlier stages of historical development, development of the forces of production fails to make any dent in this pattern. In other words, changes in the mode of production bring about changes in the nature of contending classes, but they do not bring about an end of the class conflict** (p.49).

⚠️ This sentence is routinely skipped and it is what makes the fetter model coherent. Productive development is continuous; revolution is not. For long stretches the development of the forces **fails to make any dent** in the ownership pattern, and pressure accumulates. **When material productive forces of society come in conflict with the existing relations of production, these relations turn into their fetters. The new social class which comes to own new means of production, feels constrained by these fetters and overthrows the old dominant class in a revolution** (p.49).

⚠️ Note who acts. The revolutionary agent is **the new social class which comes to own new means of production** — a class defined by its position in the emerging economy, not by its suffering. That is a precise claim and it is frequently misreported as an uprising of the worst-off.

**As a result of social revolution, an old social formation is replaced by a new social formation. In this process old contending classes are replaced by new contending classes, but class conflict continues on a new plane. This has been the case till the rise of capitalism, which will be overthrown by a socialist revolution, leading to the eventual emergence of classless society** (p.49).

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not read the "-do-" entries as an omission in the printed table or as a sign that the chart is incomplete. They are ditto marks carrying the modern epoch's mode of production downward, and that carrying-down is the chart's substantive claim about what a socialist transformation does and does not alter.

**Original Mains practice [10 marks]:** Explain the fetter-and-rupture mechanism by which Marxism accounts for social revolution. *(Explain — answer in about 150 words.)*

**Model (within 150 words):** The mechanism turns on an asymmetry between the two components of the mode of production. **Forces of production** — **means of production (tools and equipment)** plus **labour power (human knowledge and skills)** — **cannot remain static**, because **men and women constantly endeavour to devise better ways of production**, so technology improves and skills develop with it. **Relations of production** are instead **determined by the pattern of ownership of the means of social production**, which has no such tendency. For long periods the development of the forces **fails to make any dent in this pattern**, which is why revolution is episodic. When the two finally conflict, **these relations turn into their fetters**; the **new social class which comes to own new means of production** feels constrained and **overthrows the old dominant class**. An **old social formation is replaced by a new social formation**, and **class conflict continues on a new plane**.

**Rubric (quantified, 10 marks: 2 + 2 + 3 + 2 + 1):** 2 for both components of the forces, including **labour power as human knowledge and skills**; 2 for relations defined by the **pattern of ownership**, which is what creates the asymmetry; 3 for the fetter sentence plus the **fails to make any dent** clause, since without the second the model cannot explain timing and the answer cannot exceed 6; 2 for the outcome stated as a replacement of social formations with **class conflict continuing on a new plane**; 1 for identifying the agent as the **new owning class** rather than the poorest class. ❌ Deduct 2 if the socialist stage is described as classless, which contradicts Flow Chart 2's Future-I cell.

### 1. The claim, and the qualification that protects it

The opening sentence of the *Communist Manifesto* **(1848)** reads, as the chapter prints it: **The history of all hitherto society is the history of class struggles** (p.50).

⚠️ **Wording note, made once.** The chapter's form is *"all hitherto society"*; the standard English translation reads *"all hitherto **existing** society"*. Reproduce the chapter's form only when quoting the chapter, and use the standard wording when quoting the *Manifesto* in an examination answer.

The chapter then supplies its own qualification, immediately: **Here, history means all written history. When *Communist Manifesto* was originally written, the pre-history giving account of primitive tribal communities with common ownership of means of production, was not known. When it became known, it was described as 'primitive communism'. *Communist Manifesto* deals with the period beginning with the division of society into antagonistic classes since the emergence of private property** (p.50).

❗ This is the chapter **pre-empting the standard objection** that classless societies existed. A candidate who supplies the qualification before the examiner raises it converts a vulnerability into a demonstration of textual control; a candidate who quotes only the slogan hands the examiner a free counter-example.

The **`Primitive Communism`** box supplies the content: **a form of communism (classless society with common ownership of means of production) found in primitive societies. At this stage, the instruments of labour were of most primitive kind—the club, the stone axe, the flint knife, the stone-tipped spear, followed later by the bow and arrow. Man's muscular strength was the only motive force employed to operate these elementary tools. These tools were held in common ownership by the members of the primitive community which engaged itself in common labour, e.g. common hunting, common fishing, and the fruits of this common labour were also shared in common. There was no concept of private property, hence no exploitation of man by man** (p.50).

⚠️ The last clause is doing analytical work, not decoration: it states the chapter's criterion of exploitation. **No private property, therefore no exploitation of man by man.** Exploitation is a property relation, not a measure of hardship — which is why Lesson 8's arithmetic is about ownership and not about how poor anyone is.

### 2. The branch the slogan omits

**So *Communist Manifesto* proceeds: Freeman and slave, patrician and plebeian, lord and serf, guild-master and journeyman, in a word, oppressor and oppressed, stood in constant opposition to one another, carried on an uninterrupted, now hidden, now open fight, a fight that each time ended, either in a revolutionary re-constitution of society at large, or in the common ruin of the contending classes** (p.50).

⭐ **The "common ruin" branch is the tradition's own admission that revolution is not guaranteed.** Two outcomes are printed, not one. A 15-marker that quotes both branches has shown that the doctrine's own canonical text contains its contingency, and that single move lifts an answer out of the caricature in which Marxism is an inevitabilist creed. ⚠️ Note also **now hidden, now open**: conflict is claimed to persist even when it is not visible, which is how the theory accounts for quiet periods without abandoning the claim.

### 3. Social formation, and the three named pairs

The **`Social Formation`** box compresses Lesson 5 into one definition and three instances: **in Marxist thought, a form of organization of society which comes into existence around a specific mode of production. Changes in mode of production give rise to different social formations which are associated with different historical epochs. Thus household-based small-scale production gives rise to slave-owning society; large-scale agriculture-based production gives rise to feudal society; and large-scale machine-based production gives rise to capitalist society** (p.50). ⚠️ This is the single best compressed revision note in the span.

**With the development of forces of production, one mode of production is replaced by another, but class conflict (between the new social classes) reappears under the new social formation. Thus ancient slave-owning society was characterized by class conflict between master and slave; medieval feudal society, by class conflict between lord and serf; and the modern capitalist society, by class conflict between bourgeoisie (capitalists) and proletariat (workers)** (pp.50–51).

The **`Lord and Serf`** box supplies a precision most answers lack: **Lord meant the landlord who was the owner of land. Serf meant the peasant who did not own land but cultivated it, and received a small, fixed share of the produce as a return for his toil. Serf was not a slave. He could have his family and its belongings, but he could not leave his duty without permission of his lord. Exploitation of serfs was very common in feudal society** (p.51). ⚠️ **"Serf was not a slave"** is worth a mark on any stem that asks how forms of unfreedom differ: the serf has standing — family, belongings, a fixed share — and still lacks exit.

The **`Bourgeoisie and Proletariat`** box gives the topic its one dated, attributable definition. **Engels's note to the English edition of *Communist Manifesto* (1888) reads: "By bourgeoisie is meant the class of modern Capitalists, owners of the means of social production and employers of wage labour. By proletariat, the class of modern wage-labourers who, having no means of production of their own, are reduced to selling their labour power in order to live."** (p.51).

⚠️ **Wording note.** Engels's note says the proletarian sells **labour power**; the surplus-value paragraph at p.52 says the worker is **forced to sell his labour**. This edition uses **labour power** wherever the mechanism is at issue, because the distinction is doing work: what is sold is the *capacity* to work for a period, while what the purchaser obtains is the *use* of that capacity — which is where a gap between what is paid and what is produced becomes possible at all.

### 4. Why the chapter calls the modern conflict "crystallized"

**Class conflict in modern capitalist society has appeared in more crystallized form**, and the chapter cites the *Manifesto* for the reason: **Our epoch, the epoch of the bourgeoisie, possesses, however, this distinctive feature: it has simplified the class antagonisms. Society as a whole is more and more splitting up into two great hostile camps, into two great classes directly facing each other: Bourgeoisie and Proletariat** (p.51). The continuity claim is equally quotable: **The modern bourgeois society that has sprouted from the ruins of feudal society has not done away with class antagonisms. It has but established new classes, new conditions of oppression, new forms of struggle in place of the old ones** (p.51).

⚠️ **Simplified** is the chapter's own word, and it is a claim about the **number of camps**, not about the severity of the conflict. It is also the most empirically exposed claim in the topic — and the honest place to note that exposure is here, not in a critical paragraph bolted on later.

### 1. Agency: why the proletariat and not another class

**Marx and Engels hoped that this conflict had entered a decisive phase. They had full faith in revolutionary potential of the proletariat, i.e. their ability to overthrow capitalism and establish a socialist society with social ownership of means of social production. So they observed: Of all the classes that stand face to face with the bourgeoisie today, the proletariat alone is a really revolutionary class. The other classes decay and finally disappear in the face of modern industry; the proletariat is its special and essential product** (p.51).

⚠️ **Special and essential product** is the claim worth carrying. The proletariat is not an external force opposing capitalism; it is what capitalism **necessarily produces** in the course of operating. That is why the antagonism is described as internal to the system rather than as an attack on it, and it is the cleanest available statement of what "contradiction" means in this tradition.

### 2. Scale: the majoritarian claim

**All previous historical movements were movements of minorities, or in the interest of minorities. The proletarian movement is the self-conscious, independent movement of the immense majority, in the interest of the immense majority** (p.52).

⭐ This is the natural bridge to any democracy stem. It supplies a **counter-standard**: the tradition does not reject majority rule, it claims that existing arrangements do not deliver it and that the proletarian movement would. ⚠️ Note the three qualifiers — **self-conscious**, **independent**, **of the immense majority** — because each is a condition that can be assessed, and an answer that treats the claim as an assessable condition rather than as a boast is doing analysis.

### 3. Structure: the finality argument, and why it is not optimism

**According to Marx and Engels, this revolution would bring about the final emancipation of mankind because there is no class below the proletariat which could be subjected to exploitation when the proletariat comes to power** (p.52).

❗ Read the reason carefully. Finality is **not** claimed because the proletariat is virtuous or because its leaders would be restrained. It is claimed because, on this analysis, exploitation requires a class **below** the exploiting class, and after the proletariat takes power there is none. That is a **structural** argument, and treating it as an optimistic assertion about human nature misses what makes it interesting — and makes it harder to criticise well, because the real objection has to be addressed to the structural premise rather than to naivety.

⚠️ **The chapter's own counterweight.** Flow Chart 2's **Future-I** row still lists contending classes under socialism: **"Workers in power and the former capitalists"**. So on the chapter's own chart, conflict persists through the socialist stage; classlessness arrives at **Future-II**, after the **liquidation of remnants of Capitalism**. A critical answer that cites Future-I against the finality claim is using the source against itself, which is the most durable form of criticism available.

### 4. The end-state, and the cost the chapter prints

**It would place all means of social production under social ownership, make work compulsory for everyone, and develop the forces of production to their full potential. This will pave the way for the emergence of classless society which will mark the end of class conflict** (p.52).

⭐ **"Make work compulsory for everyone."** This is printed, not inferred, and **neither canonical owner carries it**. ⚠️ Teach it exactly as the chapter prints it and resist both temptations: do not soften it into "the duty to contribute," and do not inflate it into a description of any historical regime, which would breach the edition's boundary on regime history. What it establishes is that the end-state involves a **universal obligation**, imposed as part of the transformation — and the chapter states no exemption, names no enforcing authority and sets no limit.

That is the genuine tension a freedom stem needs. It is not the crude claim that socialism abolishes liberty; it is the precise observation that this programme's own description includes a universally imposed obligation, so the question *consistent with individual freedom?* has real content and a serious answer must engage it rather than deny it. ⚠️ The reply available from inside the tradition is a **relocation** rather than a denial: the humanist strand (Lesson 9) treats freedom as something capitalism has already removed through alienation, so the comparison is not obligation against freedom but one unfreedom against another — and that reply must be stated as a relocation, honestly, or it looks like evasion.

- Result: **classless society which will mark the end of class conflict**.
- ⚠️ **Make work compulsory for everyone** is printed and absent from both canonical owners; neither soften nor inflate it.
- ⚠️ No exemption, enforcing authority or limit is stated for that obligation — which is exactly where a freedom stem bites.
- ⚠️ Flow Chart 2's **Future-I** still lists **Workers in power and the former capitalists**; classlessness is **Future-II**.
- The internal reply on freedom is a **relocation** — alienation under capitalism as a prior unfreedom — and must be labelled as such.
- ⚠️ Marx and Engels **hoped** the conflict had **entered a decisive phase**; the chapter reports an expectation, not a demonstration.
- **Move:** structural finality → three acts → the cost → Future-I → relocation reply → graded verdict.

### Concept check

**Question:** State the chapter's reason for holding that the proletarian revolution is final, explain why it matters that the reason is structural, and give the strongest qualification available from the same source.

**Model answer:** The reason is that this revolution **would bring about the final emancipation of mankind because there is no class below the proletariat which could be subjected to exploitation when the proletariat comes to power**. It matters that this is structural because it changes what a critic must attack. If finality rested on the proletariat's good character, the objection would be that power corrupts, and the claim would be naive. As stated, the claim is that exploitation requires a class positioned below the exploiting class, and that after this transfer of power no such class exists — so an objection must either deny that exploitation requires such a position, or deny that the post-revolutionary structure lacks it. ⚠️ The strongest qualification comes from the chapter itself. Flow Chart 2 lists the contending classes of Future-I, the socialist society, as **"Workers in power and the former capitalists"**, so conflict persists after the revolution; classlessness belongs to **Future-II**, caused by the **liquidation of remnants of Capitalism**. It is a claim about the availability of a position to exploit within the class structure the theory describes; forms of domination outside that structure are precisely what the chapter's own conclusion later concedes, when it observes that **domination and exploitation in human society assume many complex forms**.

**Original Mains practice [10 marks]:** "Marxism's classless society is purchased at a price it does not advertise." Comment. *(Comment — answer in about 150 words.)*

**Model (within 150 words):** The proposition is half right, and the chapter itself supplies the correction. *The matrix carries the premise, the bar carries the mechanism, and the split carries the conclusion; the arrow marked "asserted, not argued" is where this lesson ends, deliberately.*

### 1. What the theory is for

**Theory of surplus value represents economic basis of Marxist critique of capitalism. It is meant to demonstrate how the capitalist mode of production involves the exploitation of working class** (p.52). ⚠️ *Economic basis* is a precise claim about the division of labour within the doctrine: Lesson 6 gives the historical claim, Lesson 7 the political one, and this lesson the economic proof on which both are said to rest.

### 2. The premises

**According to Marx, labour is the sole creator of value. Of the four elements of production, viz. land, labour, capital and organization, three elements—land, capital and organization are sterile because they are capable of reproducing only what is put in them. Labour is the only element which produces value in society. In other words, the value of a commodity is the product of labour** (p.52).

Then the accounting rule, which matters because it pre-empts the obvious question *which labour?*: **the quantity of labour employed in it should be calculated right from the production of the raw material, processing the raw material, acquiring the sources of energy for its processing, and constructing the required machinery and building for its production. The quantity of labour required in its production is also determined by the average conditions of social production and the average skill of the labour employed** (p.52). ⚠️ Reproduce this. It shows the theory is **specified** rather than hand-waved, and it answers the standard first objection before it is made.

### 3. The mechanism: two prices, and why wages fall to subsistence

**The actual amount of labour employed in the production of a commodity is called its natural price. It differs from its price in the market, or market price which fluctuates with the changing conditions of demand and supply. In a free market society, fostered by capitalism, the worker is forced to sell his labour at the market price** (p.52).

⚠️ Here is the **labour / labour power** point in its operative form. Engels's 1888 note (Lesson 6) says the proletarian sells **labour power**; this paragraph says **labour**. Use **labour power** when stating the mechanism, because the gap the theory describes opens precisely between the **capacity** that is sold at a market price and the **value** that the exercise of that capacity creates. Say so in a clause; it takes six words and shows control of the distinction.

**When more and more job-seekers come to the market place, the market price of their labour, i.e. their wage-rate declines. Their employer—the capitalist exploits their full potential to work but pays them only subsistence wages for their own and their families' sustenance** (p.52).

❗ That first sentence is the chapter's printed **reason why** wages settle at subsistence. Without it, "subsistence wages" is an assertion that any examiner can ask you to justify; with it, the claim is a supply-side mechanism stated in one line.

### 4. The conclusion

**Thus the value produced by the worker may be split into two parts: one part is paid to the worker toward his wages; the other part is pocketed by the capitalist as his profit. This second part is described by Marx as 'surplus value'. Rent and interest are paid from the surplus value. In other words, surplus value denotes the value of the labour done by the worker for which he is not paid at all; it forms part of the capitalist's profit, rent and interest on the sterile elements of production (organization, land and capital). It is, therefore, a glaring example of the worker's exploitation under capitalism** (pp.52–53).

❌ **Trap.** Surplus value is **not** "any profit" and it is **not** a movement in market price. It is defined as **the value of the labour done by the worker for which he is not paid at all** — a quantity determined by the labour accounting above, not by what a commodity happens to fetch (basic owner §9, fourth trap).

⚠️ **The equity point, which is what 2022 needs.** Put Lessons 6 and 8 together and the argument runs: the worker and the capitalist meet as **legal equals**, the contract is freely entered, the wage is paid in full at the going market price — and a residue is still appropriated, because what the worker sells (labour power, at market price) and what the employer obtains (the worker's **full potential to work**) are different things. Equality of legal standing is therefore compatible with a systematic transfer, which is why this tradition insists on a different standard from formal equality. ⚠️ The conceptual architecture of **equity** as a value belongs to `Philosophy\paper-2\socio-political\Social-Political-Ideals.md`; Topic 04 supplies this mechanism and stops.

### 5. Where it is vulnerable — conceded, not defended

⚠️ **The sterility premise is asserted without argument.** The chapter says land, capital and organization **are sterile because they are capable of reproducing only what is put in them** and offers nothing further. The premise does the heaviest lifting in the proof — if any of the three contributes value, the residue is not wholly unpaid labour — and it is exactly the premise a trained economist would contest.

❗ **Concede this explicitly.** No reply is available from the chapter or from either canonical owner, and ❌ none is manufactured here: inventing a defence, or importing an argument from outside the span, would be a fabrication. ⚠️ The honest position in an answer is that the theory's **structure** is clear and its distributive claim is intelligible, while its foundational premise is **stipulated rather than demonstrated** — and saying so is a sign of control, not of weakness. A second, smaller exposure sits alongside it: the wage-decline mechanism assumes a labour market with **more and more job-seekers**, so the conclusion is conditional on that condition holding.

### Deployment in GS-II, GS-IV and the Essay

### 1. The doctrinal turn, stated exactly

**Contemporary debates on Marxism focus on the relative importance of its basic tenets and identification of some new forms of domination and conflict emerging in the present-day society. It is no longer believed that the superstructure is wholly dependent on the base. Recognition of a sort of interdependence between base and superstructure has led to extensive analysis of various aspects of superstructure** (p.53).

⚠️ Note the logic of that third sentence: the interdependence recognition **causes** the analytical expansion. Neo-Marxism's interest in culture is therefore presented as a **consequence of a theoretical correction**, not as a change of subject. That is the whole reply to the charge that neo-Marxism abandoned Marxism — and it is the chapter's own sequence, not a sympathetic reconstruction.

### 2. The Frankfurt School, and the double distance

**The ongoing controversies in the arena of Marxist thought largely owe their origin to the work of the Frankfurt School. It was originally set up in 1923 as the Institute of Social Research in the University of Frankfurt, exiled from Germany in 1933 consequent upon the rise of Hitler, relocated in the United States shortly thereafter and after the downfall of Hitler it was reestablished in Frankfurt in the early 1950s** (p.53).

❗ Absent from both canonical owners, and decisive. **Scholars of this institute were hostile to capitalism; they were also disillusioned with Soviet socialism. They sought to evolve an alternative path for social development in accordance with the essence of Marxism as understood by them** (p.53).

⭐ **The double distance is the move for the future-of-Marxism stem.** The standard objection runs: socialist regimes collapsed, therefore Marxism is finished. The reply is not a denial but a **chronology** — a major current of the tradition was **already disillusioned with Soviet socialism** and **seeking an alternative path** decades before those regimes fell, so their collapse does not refute the current that had disowned them. ⚠️ Keep the reply bounded: it rescues *that current*, not every Marxist claim, and Lesson 10 concedes what the collapse genuinely does touch.

**The ideas evolved by the Frankfurt School are compendiously described as 'critical theory', although they do not fit into a single framework** (p.53). ⚠️ The qualifying clause is useful: it licenses you to treat critical theory as a family rather than a doctrine, and it protects against a stem that attributes one position to the whole school.

### 3. A ready-made, attributable definition of ideology critique

**The general outlook of the exponents of critical theory has been described by David Held as follows: "They tried to develop a critical perspective in the discussion of all social practices, that is, a perspective which is preoccupied by the critique of ideology—of systematically distorted accounts of reality which attempt to conceal and legitimate asymmetrical power relations. They were concerned with the way in which social interests, conflicts and contradictions are expressed in thought, and how they are produced and reproduced in systems of domination. Through an examination of these systems they hoped to enhance awareness of the roots of domination, undermine ideologies and help to compel changes in consciousness and action." ### 5. The two strains

**Broadly speaking, contemporary Marxist thought—better known as neo-Marxism—has developed in two directions: humanist and scientific. The humanist strain of neo-Marxism draws particularly on the work of the Young Marx and constitutes the mainstream of critical theory. Its dominant themes are the problems of alienation and ways to human emancipation** (pp.53–54).

### 1. The conclusion, in full

### 4. The spine: premises → mechanism → conclusion

⚠️ Re-anchored from basic owner §17 to the page clusters taught above. This is the reusable skeleton for every 15- and 20-marker.

- **Premises.** Private property in the means of social production divides society into **dominant and dependent classes with irreconcilable class interests** (p.47). The mode of production — forces plus relations — forms the base, and economic relations **play important role in shaping** the superstructure (p.48). Forces develop; relations, fixed by the **pattern of ownership**, do not (p.49).
- **Mechanism.** Development of the forces **fails to make any dent** in the ownership pattern until conflict arrives, at which point **these relations turn into their fetters**; the new owning class overthrows the old; a new social formation replaces the old and **class conflict continues on a new plane** (p.49). The relation is experienced economically as **surplus value** (pp.52–53), politically as a unity **held together only by the ideological power of the dominant class** (p.47), and humanly as **alienation** (pp.47, 54).
- **Conclusion.** The framework survives as a structural account of domination and exploitation, is **not** mechanical on its own statement, and has been extended rather than abandoned; but its state prediction failed, its sterility premise is stipulated, and **the framework of Marxism must be modified suitably** (p.54).

# CUMULATIVE CONCEPT CHECKS

Four checks. Three consolidate a block and the fourth spans the whole arc; each model is held within 250 words.

### Cumulative check A — Lessons 1 to 3

**Question:** Build the chain from the condition that produced Marxism to the method it adopted, naming at each step the sentence that carries it.

**Model answer:** One: liberalism arose as **a philosophy of human freedom** but by the mid-nineteenth century its classical form **had failed to create conditions of human freedom**, having established capitalism in which **legal and political equality … had been achieved with tremendous economic inequalities and consequent injustice**. Two: the first response, the early socialist movement, reached **a correct diagnosis of the ills of the capitalist system, but had no clear conception of the remedy** — five named thinkers with three incompatible programmes — which fixes the criterion: a remedy must be tied to a mechanism. Three: Marx and Engels accordingly **sought to replace Utopian socialism by scientific socialism**. Four: the doctrine is defined as principles founded **in order to lay scientific foundations of socialism**, treating history as **a process of conflict between antagonistic forces and classes** arising from **the faults in the mode of production** where one class gains **ownership and control of the means of social production** and **compels the other class to work on terms and conditions dictated by itself**. Five: the chapter divides the tradition itself — **Classical Marxism** as the **original tenets … as a scientific system of thought**, held together **only by the ideological power of the dominant class**, against **Neo-Marxism**, the **wider implications … including humanist thought of the Young Marx**. Six: the method is Hegel's dialectic inverted — **matter**, not idea, as the essence — with Engels's three laws in *Anti-Dühring* (1878), and **class conflict is also a manifestation of this process**.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not treat step two as background. The diagnosis/remedy criterion is the only standard in the chapter against which the doctrine can be *evaluated* rather than merely described, and dropping it is why most answers to the 2025-style stem become summaries.

### Cumulative check B — Lessons 4 to 6

**Question:** Set out the materialist mechanism at the exact strength the source gives it, and show how Flow Chart 2 and the written-history qualification each protect it from an obvious objection.

**Model answer:** The claim is that in any given epoch economic relations **play important role in shaping** social, political, intellectual and ethical relationships — not "the decisive role", which is the canonical owners' wording. The **base (the foundation)** is the **mode of production**; the **superstructure (the external build-up)** is **legal and political structure, religion, morals, social practices, literature, art and culture**. The mechanism: forces of production — **means of production (tools and equipment)** plus **labour power (human knowledge and skills)** — develop because **men and women constantly endeavour to devise better ways of production**, while relations are fixed by **the pattern of ownership**; development **fails to make any dent in this pattern** until conflict, when **these relations turn into their fetters**, the **new social class which comes to own new means of production** overthrows the old, and **class conflict continues on a new plane**. Flow Chart 2 protects the mechanism against the charge that the stages are a mere chronology, because its **"Caused by"** column names a cause for each transition, and its **"-do-"** rows show the transformation occurring in relations rather than forces. The written-history qualification protects the class-struggle proposition against the counter-example of classless early societies: **here, history means all written history**, pre-history being **not known** when the *Manifesto* was written and later called **primitive communism**, where **there was no concept of private property, hence no exploitation of man by man**.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not let the qualification become a retreat. The chapter restricts the proposition's scope to societies with private property and then **explains** the prior stage within the same framework; that is a narrowing with a reason, not an admission that the theory failed.

### Cumulative check C — Lessons 7 to 9

**Question:** State the chapter's case that the proletarian revolution is final, the cost it prints, and how neo-Marxism relocates the argument — with the price of each move.

**Model answer:** **Finality** rests on three grounds. Agency: **the proletariat alone is a really revolutionary class**, capitalism's **special and essential product**. Scale: the movement is that **of the immense majority, in the interest of the immense majority**, where **all previous historical movements were movements of minorities**. Structure, the load-bearing one: **there is no class below the proletariat which could be subjected to exploitation when the proletariat comes to power**. **The cost** is printed in the same sentence as the end-state: the revolution would place all means of social production under **social ownership**, **make work compulsory for everyone**, and develop the forces **to their full potential**. ⚠️ Flow Chart 2 adds a second cost, since Future-I still names **"Workers in power and the former capitalists"**. **The relocation**: once **it is no longer believed that the superstructure is wholly dependent on the base**, analysis expands across the superstructure; the Frankfurt School, **hostile to capitalism** and **also disillusioned with Soviet socialism**, develops a cultural critique in terms of **'technological domination' rather than capitalist domination**, with Marcuse's **'one-dimensional man'** and desires **stimulated** so as to be **easily satisfied**, while Althusser's scientific strain returns to **the deep structures … especially their modes of production**. ⚠️ Prices: the finality argument is contradicted by the chapter's own Future-I cell; the humanist reply on freedom is a relocation, not a refutation; and the cultural turn weakens the distinctively Marxist mechanism as it moves away from ownership.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not present neo-Marxism as a united correction of classical Marxism. Its two strains are in dispute with one another — Althusser **challenged the humanist themes** in the **early 1960s** — so a stem about "the neo-Marxist view" needs two answers, not one.

### Cumulative check D — the whole arc, Lessons 1 to 10

**Question:** In one connected argument, move from the condition that produced Marxism to a graded verdict on it, marking where each lesson does work.

**Model answer:** Liberalism delivered capitalism, in which **legal and political equality … had been achieved with tremendous economic inequalities and consequent injustice**, and the early socialists answered with **a correct diagnosis … but no clear conception of the remedy** — the criterion the whole topic is then scored against (L1). Marxism is defined as principles laying **scientific foundations of socialism**, resting on ownership of the **means of social production** and a unity **held together only by the ideological power of the dominant class**, with the humanist *Manuscripts* recovered only in **1927** (L2). Its method inverts Hegel — **matter**, not idea — retaining the terminus of a **'rational mode of production'** (L3). The social claim is calibrated: economic relations **play important role in shaping** a superstructure that always included **religion, morals, social practices, literature, art and culture** (L4). The mechanism follows: forces develop, relations fixed by the **pattern of ownership** do not, **these relations turn into their fetters**, and Flow Chart 2 supplies the causes and the **"-do-"** point (L5). # ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS MODEL PRACTICE

**Practice question 1 [10]:** Distinguish classical Marxism from neo-Marxism using the chapter's own division. *(Distinguish — answer in about 150 words.)*

**Model answer:** The division is the source's own. **Classical Marxism** is **the original tenets of Marxism—as a scientific system of thought**, whose exponents besides Marx and Engels are **V. I. Lenin (1870–1924)**, **Rosa Luxemburg (1871–1919)** and **Mao Zedong (1893–1976)**; it holds that **private property divides society into dominant and dependent classes with irreconcilable class interests**, that society **is held together only by the ideological power of the dominant class**, and that history moves to a **classless society**. **Neo-Marxism** is **the wider implications of Marxism, including humanist thought of the Young Marx**, seeking to analyse **the subtle aspects of the phenomenon of dominance and dependence, distortions in the contemporary civilization and the possible ways to human emancipation**. The doctrinal hinge is that **it is no longer believed that the superstructure is wholly dependent on the base**. ⚠️ Neo-Marxism itself splits into **humanist** and **scientific** strains, which dispute each other.

**Scoring note:** marks concentrate in using the chapter's own two definitions rather than a generic "classical is economic, neo is cultural" contrast; in the ideological-power clause, which most answers never reach; and in the closing qualification, which pre-empts the follow-up question about whether neo-Marxism is one thing.

**Practice question 2 [10]:** Explain why the Marxist account treats exploitation as a property relation rather than as severe hardship. *(Explain — answer in about 150 words.)*

**Model answer:** The criterion is stated where the chapter describes the one society it calls free of exploitation. Under **primitive communism**, tools were **held in common ownership**, labour was **common labour** and its fruits were **shared in common**, so **there was no concept of private property, hence no exploitation of man by man** — although conditions were severe: **the club, the stone axe, the flint knife, the stone-tipped spear**, with **muscular strength** the only motive force. Hardship was maximal, exploitation absent. Conversely, exploitation appears wherever one class holds **ownership and control of the means of social production** and **compels the other class to work on terms and conditions dictated by itself**; its measure is **surplus value**, **the value of the labour done by the worker for which he is not paid at all**. ⚠️ Hence the serf, with family and belongings, is still exploited, while the better-off wage-labourer is too.

**Scoring note:** the answer is built from a single pair of contrasts that the chapter supplies on facing pages, and it protects against the commonest conceptual error on this topic — treating exploitation as a synonym for poverty, which makes both the surplus-value argument and the equity distinction unintelligible.

**Practice question 3 [15]:** "Historical materialism explains change but cannot explain its timing." Critically examine. *(Critically examine — answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer:** The criterion: does the mechanism specify **when** a rupture occurs, or only **that** ruptures occur? On the chapter's own text the proposition fails: timing is what the fetter model supplies. The mode of production has two components moving on different principles. **Forces of production cannot remain static; they have an inherent tendency of development**, because **men and women constantly endeavour to devise better ways of production**, so technology advances and **labour power (human knowledge and skills)** advances with it. **Relations of production** are instead **determined by the pattern of ownership of the means of social production** and have no such tendency. The timing claim is then explicit: **in earlier stages of historical development, development of the forces of production fails to make any dent in this pattern**, so pressure accumulates without effect until **material productive forces of society come in conflict with the existing relations of production**, at which point **these relations turn into their fetters** and the **new social class which comes to own new means of production** overthrows the old. Flow Chart 2 names a cause for each such moment — **emergence of private property**, **rise of feudalism**, **rise of capitalism**, **socialist revolution**. ⚠️ Two qualifications are owed. The model gives a **condition**, not a date: it says when revolution becomes possible, not when it occurs, and the *Manifesto*'s second sentence allows the alternative of **the common ruin of the contending classes**. And the agent is identified structurally, as the new owning class, leaving consciousness and organisation underspecified.

**Scoring note:** a **critically examine** directive requires the proposition to be tested rather than rejected, so the answer concedes the two genuine gaps after refuting the main charge. The **fails to make any dent** clause is the hinge; without it the answer can describe the fetter but cannot explain why revolutions are episodic.

**Practice question 4 [15]:** Assess the claim that Marxism's turn to culture and ideology strengthened the tradition. *(Assess — answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer:** The turn strengthened the tradition's explanatory reach and weakened its distinctiveness, both visible in the source. **Strength.** The move began as a correction rather than a change of subject: **it is no longer believed that the superstructure is wholly dependent on the base**, and **recognition of a sort of interdependence between base and superstructure has led to extensive analysis of various aspects of superstructure** — a superstructure always including **religion, morals, social practices, literature, art and culture**. The Frankfurt School, **originally set up in 1923 as the Institute of Social Research**, **exiled from Germany in 1933**, relocated to the **United States** and **reestablished in Frankfurt in the early 1950s**, was **hostile to capitalism** and **also disillusioned with Soviet socialism**, and produced a critique of ideology defined, in David Held's words as quoted in the chapter, as a preoccupation with **systematically distorted accounts of reality which attempt to conceal and legitimate asymmetrical power relations**. Marcuse's account of desires **stimulated** so as to be **easily satisfied** explains quiescence that a purely economic model cannot. **Weakness.** The chapter itself names the shift as a critique conducted **particularly in terms of 'technological domination' rather than capitalist domination**, and the further domination is detached from ownership, the less the account depends on anything specifically Marxist. ⚠️ Balanced assessment: the turn preserved relevance at the cost of uniqueness, which is why the chapter prescribes modification — **the framework of Marxism must be modified suitably** — rather than defence or abandonment.

**Scoring note:** **Assess** requires a weighed judgment, so the two halves are given separate evidence rather than a concession tacked on at the end. The Held quotation is the highest-value element available, because it is attributable and dated and almost no competing answer will have it.

**Practice question 5 [20]:** Explain the Marxian theory of surplus value and examine its adequacy as an account of exploitation under capitalism. *(Explain and examine — answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer:** **The theory.** **Theory of surplus value represents economic basis of Marxist critique of capitalism.** Premise: **labour is the sole creator of value**; of the four elements of production — **land, labour, capital and organization** — three are **sterile because they are capable of reproducing only what is put in them**. Value is the labour embodied, counted from **raw material** through **processing**, **sources of energy** and **machinery and building**, at **average conditions of social production** and **average skill**; that quantity is the **natural price**, distinct from the **market price** which **fluctuates with the changing conditions of demand and supply**. The worker must sell **labour power** at the market price, and **when more and more job-seekers come to the market place, the market price of their labour, i.e. their wage-rate declines**, so the employer **exploits their full potential to work but pays them only subsistence wages**. The value produced splits into wages and **surplus value**, **the value of the labour done by the worker for which he is not paid at all**, from which **profit, rent and interest** are drawn. **Adequacy.** Its strength is that it locates a wrong where no rule is broken: legal equals contract, the agreed price is paid, and a residue is still appropriated, so formal equality does not settle the question. ⚠️ Its weakness is foundational: the **sterility** premise is asserted without argument, and no defence is available here. The wage mechanism also presupposes a surplus of job-seekers. The theory is structurally clear, evidentially under-grounded.

**Scoring note:** at 20 marks both verbs must be visibly answered. The concession on sterility is not a weakness in the answer but the single clearest signal of source control, because the premise is genuinely undefended in the chapter and a candidate who claims otherwise has invented material.

**Practice question 6 [20]:** "Marxism survives as a method of analysis even where it has failed as a programme of government." Discuss. *(Discuss — answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer:** The proposition tracks the chapter's own conclusion; each half must be stated at full strength. **Failure as a programme.** The record is conceded: socialism collapsed in **Eastern Europe by the end of 1980s** and in the **former Soviet Union by 1991**, **followed by the introduction of market economies and multiparty political systems**, which **necessitated new thinking on the adequacy of classical Marxism on which these regimes were based**; and **instead of 'withering away of the state' … there has been a collapse of socialist state and consequent return toward liberalism**. The defence that **these countries were not really socialist at all!** must be refused, as the chapter does: **this is, however, an oversimplification of the issue**. **Survival as a method.** The record does not touch the analysis: ownership, the wage relation and **surplus value**; the base and superstructure architecture at its printed strength, where economic relations **play important role in shaping** the rest; and ideology critique, whose practitioners were **hostile to capitalism** while **also disillusioned with Soviet socialism**. The chapter states the extension — **the problem of fighting out the forces of domination and exploitation is no longer confined to the struggle of working class against capitalist class** — through **dependency theory** and the demonstration that **domination and exploitation … assume many complex forms**. ⚠️ Verdict: the proposition holds, with one residual — the further the method travels from ownership, the less distinctively Marxist it is. The chapter's close is exact: **the framework of Marxism must be modified suitably**.

**Scoring note:** the mark separation on this question lies in refusing the no-true-socialism defence explicitly. Answers that rely on it lose the critical half entirely; answers that report and reject it demonstrate that the defence of the method is being made on the source's terms and not on sympathy.

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

## A. The condition Marxism answered, and the criterion it inherited

- Liberalism = a seventeenth-century **philosophy of human freedom**; by the mid-nineteenth century its classical form, **with its doctrine of laissez-faire and free market economy, had failed to create conditions of human freedom**.
- It **achieved the goal of establishing capitalism** under which **a tiny class of capitalists enjoyed special power and privileges at the expense of the large majority of the working class**.
- ⭐ **The legal and political equality sought by the exponents of liberalism had been achieved with tremendous economic inequalities and consequent injustice** (p.46).
- Evidence without statistics: wealth **cornered by a small section which happened to own the means of production**; the majority **forced to a sub-human living**; **successful bankers and market speculators increased their wealth by leaps and bounds while the slum-dwelling working classes were living under the constant threat of insecurity, malnutrition, discomfort, disease and death**; these developments **belied all the humanitarian hopes of universal economic progress**.
- Utopian socialists, with programmes: **Saint-Simon (1760–1825)** and **Louis Blanc (1811–82)**, France — **a more or less centralized economy under state control**; **Robert Owen (1771–1858)**, England, and **Charles Fourier (1772–1837)**, France — **model communities** on **'free cooperation'** against **'free competition'**; **P. J. Proudhon (1809–65)**, France — **a nationwide system of decentralized workers' cooperatives** bargaining for **the mutual exchange of goods and services**.
- ⭐ Verdict and criterion: **a correct diagnosis of the ills of the capitalist system, but had no clear conception of the remedy**; **visionary solutions—far removed from the hard realities of life**; **'utopian socialists'**.
- **1830s–1840s**: **Karl Marx, a German scholar**, and **Friedrich Engels, a young German businessman residing in England**, **sought to replace Utopian socialism by scientific socialism for the analysis of social problems and finding their solution**.

## B. The name, the tradition, and why the object of assessment matters

- **Karl Marx (1818–83)**, *a famous German economist and social philosopher of the nineteenth century*; **Friedrich Engels (1820–95)**, *a close friend and collaborator*.
- The term was **unknown in Marx's own lifetime**; Engels reported Marx's **"All I know is that I am not a Marxist"**; the chapter's two readings are no claim to a comprehensive world-view, or modesty.
- **G. V. Plekhanov (1856–1918)**, *a Russian Marxist*: **'Marxism is a whole world-view.'**
- ⭐ **Marxism should not be regarded as a system of thought exclusively belonging to Marx** … **a rich tradition of social thought—a living tradition, with immense possibilities** (p.45).

## C. The definition and its four-part resolution test

- **A set of political and economic principles founded by Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels in order to lay scientific foundations of socialism**; method = **historical analysis**; history = **a process of conflict between antagonistic forces and classes**.
- Cause: **the faults in the mode of production** where one class gains **ownership and control of the means of social production (land, buildings, mines, forests, machinery and capital, etc.)** and **compels the other class to work on terms and conditions dictated by itself**.
- Resolution, four conjoined parts: **overthrowing capitalism** · **all means of social production under social ownership and control** · **enforcing universal labour** · **ensuring full development of the forces of production**.
- *Manifesto* **(1848)**: the working class's role in **past and future history**; the call to unite for **their own emancipation and, through that emancipation, the freedom of all mankind**.

## E. Classical and Neo — the chapter's own division

- **Classical Marxism** = **the original tenets of Marxism—as a scientific system of thought**. Exponents besides Marx and Engels: **V. I. Lenin (1870–1924)**, *a Russian revolutionary and thinker*; **Rosa Luxemburg (1871–1919)**, *a Polish activist*; **Mao Zedong (1893–1976)**, *a Chinese revolutionary and thinker*. ⚠️ Descriptions asymmetric; report, do not level or explain.
- Compressed doctrine: **private property divides society into dominant and dependent classes with irreconcilable class interests**; ⭐ **It is held together only by the ideological power of the dominant class**; history moves to **human freedom through the revolutionary destruction of inherent contradictions … culminating in the emergence of a classless society**.
- **Neo-Marxism** = **the wider implications of Marxism, including humanist thought of the Young Marx**, analysing **the subtle aspects of the phenomenon of dominance and dependence, distortions in the contemporary civilization and the possible ways to human emancipation**.
- `Young Marx` box: early work **unpublished during his lifetime**, **discovered from the archives of German Social Democrats as late as 1927**, published as the ***Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts of 1844***, **distinguished from Marx's later work which is characterized by scientific rigour**, containing **humanist thought on communism**, focused on **alienation and freedom**, and **exposing the dehumanizing effect of capitalism**. ⚠️ No forms of alienation are enumerated anywhere in the chapter.

## F. Dialectical materialism and Engels's three laws

- Four named tenets: **(a) Dialectical Materialism; (b) Historical Materialism; (c) Doctrine of Class Conflict; (d) Theory of Surplus Value.**
- **G. W. F. Hegel (1770–1831)**: **'idea' or 'consciousness' was the essence of universe**; institutions = **manifestation of changing forms of idea**; **thesis (partial truth) → antithesis (opposite of thesis—again a partial truth) → synthesis (nearer the truth)**, synthesis becoming the next thesis, until **'absolute idea' or 'absolute consciousness'**.
- Marx: **'matter' (and not the idea) was the essence of universe**; institutions = **manifestation of changing material conditions**; ⭐ terminus = **perfect material conditions, exemplified by a 'rational mode of production'**.
- ⭐ **Engels, *Anti-Dühring* (1878)**: **(a) the transformation of quantity into quality, and vice versa; (b) the interpenetration of opposites; (c) the negation of negation** — principles that **signify the process of resolving contradictions of material conditions of human life which paves the way for social progress**.
- ⭐ **Class conflict is also a manifestation of this process.**
- ❌ The triad is **Hegel's** as reported here, never Marx's settled formula. ⚠️ Any gloss on a law is analytical, not page-anchored.

## G. Historical materialism, at its printed strength

- **Dialectical materialism represents the philosophical basis of Marxism; historical materialism represents its scientific basis.**
- ⭐ Economic relations — **the means whereby men and women undertake production, distribution and exchange of material goods for the satisfaction of their needs** — **play important role in shaping their social, political, intellectual and ethical relationships**. ❗ Both owners print "the decisive role"; the source does not.
- **A perfect society will secure all the necessities of life to the satisfaction of all its members**, achieved **through a long-drawn process**; initially **many imperfect forms of society**.
- **Base (the foundation) = the mode of production.** ⭐ **Superstructure (the external build-up) = legal and political structure, religion, morals, social practices, literature, art and culture, etc.**
- Forward link: **it is no longer believed that the superstructure is wholly dependent on the base**; **recognition of a sort of interdependence** (p.53).

## H. The mechanism, and Flow Chart 2's three retained points

- **Mode of production = forces + relations.** **Forces of production cannot remain static; they have an inherent tendency of development.**
- **Forces = means of production (tools and equipment) + labour power (human knowledge and skills).**
- Why forces move: **men and women constantly endeavour to devise better ways of production**; technology improves; **corresponding development of labour power**.
- **Relations … are determined by the pattern of ownership of the means of social production** → **two contending classes—haves and have-nots**.
- ⭐ **In earlier stages of historical development, development of the forces of production fails to make any dent in this pattern** — the timing clause.
- ⭐ **These relations turn into their fetters**; the **new social class which comes to own new means of production** **feels constrained by these fetters and overthrows the old dominant class in a revolution**; **an old social formation is replaced by a new social formation**; **class conflict continues on a new plane**.
- **Flow Chart 2 — *Marxian View of the Stages of Historical Development*** (p.49), five columns: Historical Epoch · Prevailing Mode of Production · **Caused by** · Social Formation · Contending Classes.
- ⭐ Three retained points: the **"Caused by"** column (*emergence of private property · rise of feudalism · rise of capitalism · socialist revolution · liquidation of remnants of capitalism*); **Future-I's "Workers in power and the former capitalists"**; and the **"-do-"** rows showing the prevailing mode of production unchanged across capitalist → socialist → communist.

## I. Class conflict, the written-history cut, and the named pairs

- **Class conflict or class struggle is an integral part of historical materialism.**
- *Manifesto* opening, as the chapter prints it: **The history of all hitherto society is the history of class struggles.** ⚠️ Standard translation: **all hitherto *existing* society**.
- ⭐ **Here, history means all written history**; pre-history **was not known** when the *Manifesto* was written and was later called **primitive communism**; the *Manifesto* **deals with the period beginning with the division of society into antagonistic classes since the emergence of private property**.
- `Primitive Communism`: **club, stone axe, flint knife, stone-tipped spear**, later **bow and arrow**; **muscular strength the only motive force**; **common ownership, common labour**, fruits **shared in common**; ⭐ **no concept of private property, hence no exploitation of man by man**.
- ⭐ Second *Manifesto* sentence: **Freeman and slave, patrician and plebeian, lord and serf, guild-master and journeyman, in a word, oppressor and oppressed** … **an uninterrupted, now hidden, now open fight, a fight that each time ended, either in a revolutionary re-constitution of society at large, or in the common ruin of the contending classes.**
- `Social Formation`: organisation **around a specific mode of production** — household-based small-scale → **slave-owning**; large-scale agriculture-based → **feudal**; large-scale machine-based → **capitalist**.
- Pairs: **master and slave** · **lord and serf** · **bourgeoisie (capitalists) and proletariat (workers)**.
- `Lord and Serf`: the **landlord who was the owner of land** against **the peasant who did not own land but cultivated it, and received a small, fixed share of the produce**; ⭐ **Serf was not a slave** — family and belongings permitted, **could not leave his duty without permission of his lord**.
- ⭐ Engels, note to the English edition **(1888)**: bourgeoisie = **the class of modern Capitalists, owners of the means of social production and employers of wage labour**; proletariat = **the class of modern wage-labourers who, having no means of production of their own, are reduced to selling their labour power in order to live**.
- Continuity: modern bourgeois society **has not done away with class antagonisms … new classes, new conditions of oppression, new forms of struggle in place of the old ones**.
- **It has simplified the class antagonisms** → **two great hostile camps**. ⚠️ The most empirically exposed claim in the topic.

## J. The revolution, the finality argument, and the printed cost

- **The proletarian revolution would be different from all previous revolutions of history.**
- Agency: **the proletariat alone is a really revolutionary class**; other classes **decay and finally disappear in the face of modern industry**; the proletariat is **its special and essential product**.
- Scale: **all previous historical movements were movements of minorities, or in the interest of minorities. The proletarian movement is the self-conscious, independent movement of the immense majority, in the interest of the immense majority.**
- ⭐ Structure: **there is no class below the proletariat which could be subjected to exploitation when the proletariat comes to power** — a structural claim, not an optimistic one.
- End-state, three conjoined acts: **all means of social production under social ownership** · ⭐ **make work compulsory for everyone** · **develop the forces of production to their full potential** → **classless society which will mark the end of class conflict**.
- ⚠️ No exemption, enforcing authority or limit is stated for the obligation; and Flow Chart 2's **Future-I** still names contending classes. The humanist answer on freedom is a **relocation**, not a refutation.

## K. Surplus value: premises, accounting, mechanism, split, concession

- **Theory of surplus value represents economic basis of Marxist critique of capitalism.**
- ⭐ **Labour is the sole creator of value.** Of **land, labour, capital and organization**, three are **sterile because they are capable of reproducing only what is put in them**.
- Accounting: count labour from **raw material → processing → sources of energy → machinery and building**, at **average conditions of social production** and **average skill**.
- ⭐ **Natural price** = **the actual amount of labour employed**; **market price** **fluctuates with the changing conditions of demand and supply**; the worker is **forced to sell his labour at the market price**. ⚠️ Use **labour power** where the mechanism is at issue.
- ⭐ **When more and more job-seekers come to the market place, the market price of their labour, i.e. their wage-rate declines**; the employer **exploits their full potential to work but pays them only subsistence wages**.
- Split: wages | **surplus value**, **pocketed by the capitalist as his profit**; **rent and interest are paid from the surplus value**; surplus value = ⭐ **the value of the labour done by the worker for which he is not paid at all**, on **the sterile elements of production (organization, land and capital)** — **a glaring example of the worker's exploitation under capitalism**.
- ❗ **Concession:** the sterility premise is **asserted without argument**; no reply is available from these sources and none is constructed. The wage mechanism is conditional on a surplus of job-seekers.

## L. Neo-Marxism: the turn, the institution, the strains

- ⭐ **It is no longer believed that the superstructure is wholly dependent on the base** → **recognition of a sort of interdependence between base and superstructure** → **extensive analysis of various aspects of superstructure**.
- Frankfurt School: **set up in 1923 as the Institute of Social Research in the University of Frankfurt** → **exiled from Germany in 1933 consequent upon the rise of Hitler** → **relocated in the United States** → **reestablished in Frankfurt in the early 1950s**.
- ⭐ Double distance: **hostile to capitalism**; **also disillusioned with Soviet socialism**; sought **an alternative path for social development in accordance with the essence of Marxism as understood by them**.
- **'Critical theory'** — **although they do not fit into a single framework**.
- ⭐ **David Held**, quoted in ***A Dictionary of Marxist Thought*, ed. Tom Bottomore (1983)**: a perspective **preoccupied by the critique of ideology—of systematically distorted accounts of reality which attempt to conceal and legitimate asymmetrical power relations**, concerned with how **social interests, conflicts and contradictions are expressed in thought**, hoping to **enhance awareness of the roots of domination, undermine ideologies and help to compel changes in consciousness and action**.
- Disciplines entered: **philosophy, economics, political science, sociology, anthropology, social psychology, psycho-analysis, music and fine arts**.
- Figures: **Theodor Adorno (1903–69)**, **Max Horkheimer (1895–1973)**, **Herbert Marcuse (1898–1979)**, **Jurgen Habermas (1929– )**. ✅ dates; ⚠️ "second generation" is not the chapter's label; ❌ never attach a work or year to Habermas.
- ⭐ **A cultural critique of bourgeois society—particularly in terms of 'technological domination' rather than capitalist domination.**
- **Humanist strain**: draws on **the work of the Young Marx**, **constitutes the mainstream of critical theory**; themes **alienation and ways to human emancipation**. **Marcuse**: **'one-dimensional man'**; capitalism **cunningly anaesthetized the discontent of the oppressed by manipulating the means of communication so as to stimulate trivial, material desires which are easily satisfied**; remedy — awareness of **unfreedom** first, **whereafter they will easily find their way to freedom** ⚠️.
- **Scientific strain**: concerned with **scientific and explanatory character**, **structures** and **relative importance of cultural, ideological and social factors**. **Althusser (1918–90)**, *a French communist and philosopher*, **challenged the humanist themes … in the early 1960s**, asserting analysis of **the deep structures of human societies—especially their modes of production**.
- ⚠️ Gramsci is **cross-chapter** (pp.306, 309–311; Topic 15), one bounded paragraph only. ⚠️ **'Dependency theory'** only — no theorist, work, year or world-systems vocabulary.

## M. The conclusion and the graded verdict

- Record: collapse of socialism in **Eastern Europe by the end of 1980s**; in the **former Soviet Union by 1991**; **followed by the introduction of market economies and multiparty political systems**; this **necessitated new thinking on the adequacy of classical Marxism on which these regimes were based**.
- 📝 Book-period: the **People's Republic of China** liberalisation remark, and **no scope of rigidity … as it was during the regimes of Stalin (1879–1953) in Russia and Mao Zedong (1893–1976) in China**.
- ⭐ **Instead of 'withering away of the state' in socialist countries, there has been a collapse of socialist state and consequent return toward liberalism.** Concede fully; no reply available.
- ⭐ **"These countries were not really socialist at all!"** → **This is, however, an oversimplification of the issue.**
