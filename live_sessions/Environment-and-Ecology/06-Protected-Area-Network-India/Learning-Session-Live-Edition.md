Imagine a tiger crossing the edge of a National Park into a village forest. The park's boundary has not made the tiger safe, and the village has not ceased to exist. ### Visual — the same landscape carries different questions

```text

                             ↓
DOMESTIC LEGAL SITE:       sanctuary / National Park / other statutory area
```

*Caption: A notification draws a legal boundary; movement and management determine whether habitat functions as a connected network.*

**Start with the animal, not the label.** Protecting organisms *in situ* means maintaining them in their natural habitat and ecological relationships; it is unlike ex-situ conservation in a zoo or seed bank. An isolated pocket can protect breeding ground yet fail when animals need mates, seasonal water or dispersal routes outside it. ⚠️ Therefore count, area, placement and ecological performance answer different questions. India's Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 (WLPA) creates several domestic area designations, while an international label or an outside buffer can occupy the same landscape without replacing those domestic notifications.

✅ The four often grouped as statutory protected areas are **Wildlife Sanctuary**, **National Park**, **Conservation Reserve** and **Community Reserve**. It is useful to picture a gradient of governance, **not** a legally exact single rank by "strictness": permitted activities depend on the particular provision, declared rights and management decision. A Tiger Reserve is a **statutory species-management designation under WLPA section 38V**, not a fifth member of those four generic land-category labels; its core can comprise NP/WLS lands and its buffer need not all be an NP. Elephant Reserves are programme/landscape designations, not equivalent section-38V units. An **eco-sensitive zone (ESZ)** is an adjoining regulatory area notified under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, not automatically a fifth WLPA site. Other effective area-based conservation measures (OECMs) recognise effective conservation **outside** formal protected-area designation; an unassessed grove is not automatically a certified OECM.

### Biosphere Reserve zoning is a different architecture

| Biosphere Reserve zone | Main role | Relationship with domestic PA categories |
|---|---|---|
| **Core** | Strict protection and minimal disturbance | Often overlaps a National Park or Wildlife Sanctuary, but the overlap does not convert one designation into the other |
| **Buffer** | Research, monitoring, education and compatible limited use | May surround or adjoin domestic PAs; its rules derive from the Biosphere Reserve arrangement and applicable underlying laws |
| **Transition** | Settlements, agriculture and sustainable livelihoods | Usually extends beyond strict PA land and demonstrates conservation with development |

A **Biosphere Reserve** is a landscape-scale Indian conservation arrangement following the UNESCO Man and Biosphere model; some Indian Biosphere Reserves also receive separate recognition in UNESCO's World Network. It is **not** a fifth WLPA protected-area category. One Biosphere Reserve may contain an NP, sanctuary, reserve forests, villages and working landscapes across its core–buffer–transition pattern. Map the physical overlap once, then state the legal or international function of each designation separately.

### Wildlife Institute of India: science, not notification

The **Wildlife Institute of India (WII), Dehradun**, is a wildlife research and training institution under MoEFCC. Its population studies, habitat assessments, connectivity analysis, training and management-planning inputs can inform States, NTCA and protected-area managers. WII does **not** notify a National Park, settle rights, grant a statutory clearance or replace the State wildlife department. This distinction matters whenever a scientific report is mistaken for a legal declaration.

**Map discipline and dated anchor:** A historical map can place Madhav, Kanha and other sites but cannot establish today's notification date, boundary or breeding success. ✅ The June 2026 PIB biodiversity note describes over **1,134 protected areas** and prints **187,592 km²**; its neighbouring phrase “more than 1.88 lakh” is rounded differently, so retain the exact printed figure with its source date rather than treating both as identical arithmetic. ⚠️ More hectares notified do not establish that corridors are passable, management is adequate or residents were heard.

### The ecology behind the site names

| Physical driver → vegetation | Indian landscape | What a PA can protect, and what a map alone cannot |
|---|---|---|
| Warmth + sustained high rain → tropical evergreen/semi-evergreen canopy | Windward Western Ghats, north-east hills | Rainforest species, water-regulating catchments and endemics; a boundary alone cannot secure an upstream catchment. |
| Seasonal rain → moist/dry deciduous; prolonged aridity → thorn and grassland | Central Indian forest; Rajasthan's Thar | A protected grassland can be as important as forest; calling a desert “empty” ignores both birds and villages. |
| Altitude, temperature, slope aspect → temperate then subalpine/alpine belts | Himalayan slopes | Check the *entire* candidate park's elevation and habitats, not merely whether one alpine meadow exists. |
| Saline tidal water + sediment → mangrove | Sundarbans delta | A tidal forest is neither an upland rainforest nor an alpine grassland. |

*Caption: rainfall, dry-season length, temperature/altitude, soil and drainage make India's vegetation varied; a site may straddle several belts.*

To discuss **2023 GS-I Q15** rather than just list names, derive the chain: monsoon rainfall and its seasonality govern moisture; altitude changes temperature; relief creates wet windward and drier leeward slopes; drainage, salinity and soils determine whether trees or mangroves can establish; human clearing fragments the resulting mosaic. The Western Ghats and north-east hold wet evergreen forest, central India has seasonal deciduous formations, drier Rajasthan supports thorn/grassland, higher Himalaya grades towards alpine vegetation, and tidal deltas support mangroves. These are *typical formations*, not impermeable borders. **Periyar Wildlife Sanctuary**, **Periyar National Park** and the Tiger Reserve are distinct overlapping labels in a Western Ghats evergreen-forest and river-catchment landscape. A sanctuary can protect endemic species and source waters, while connectivity, tourism pressure, hydropower or land uses outside it still require wider management. **Assess** rainforest sanctuaries rather than merely praise them: habitat security and endemism are gains; fragmentation and catchment-edge disturbance are limitations.

**Site-reading practice after the first PYQ attempt:** Pakhui/Pakke Wildlife Sanctuary is in **Arunachal Pradesh**. In **Uttarakhand**, the Valley of Flowers represents high-elevation alpine meadow habitat; contrast **Namdapha, Arunachal Pradesh**, which spans lowland to high montane habitats, so the presence of alpine plants alone cannot characterise a *whole* park. **Kanha, Madhya Pradesh**, is associated with conservation of the hard-ground barasingha; do not confuse this deer with other swamp-deer populations. Rajasthan's officially styled **Desert National Park Wildlife Sanctuary** extends across **Jaisalmer and Barmer**, contains villages and includes Great Indian Bustard habitat. Its place name does not by itself establish a National Park legal category. None of these facts amounts to a new notification or current animal census.

**Challenge and response:** Why not simply fence every site? Fencing may prevent a local intrusion but also fragment animal movement and human access. The reply is not to abandon well-protected breeding cores; it is to pair them with ecologically appropriate corridors and rights-aware governance. **UPSC use:** start a GS-III answer with *site → connectivity → measurable outcome*. **Mini recap:** in situ preserves relationships; notification ≠ functioning network; statutory category ≠ species overlay ≠ international recognition.

**Revision notes:** In situ = native habitat, ex situ = outside habitat; four WLPA site categories while Biosphere Reserve core–buffer–transition can overlap but does not replace them; tiger = separate statutory overlay, elephant = programme landscape; ESZ = different parent statute; OECM = effective area outside formal PA; WII supplies research/training/management inputs but does not notify sites; rainfall/dry season + altitude + soils/drainage → vegetation; rainforest WLS protects endemics and catchments but cannot secure exterior land alone; Pakhui/Pakke—Arunachal; Valley of Flowers—alpine, Namdapha—multi-belt; Kanha—hard-ground barasingha; Desert NP Wildlife Sanctuary—two Rajasthan districts + inhabited bustard habitat; connectivity enables gene flow; counts need dates.

### Visual — protection is a sequence, not an instant eviction

| Question | Wildlife Sanctuary | National Park |
|---|---|---|
| Who initiates? | Ordinarily the State: intention s. 18, final notification s. 26A; Central Government may act under conditional s. 38(1) | Ordinarily the State: intention/final steps s. 35; Central Government can notify under s. 38(2) on qualifying transferred land |
| What happens to existing rights? | Collector investigates and settles claims under ss. 19–25; admitted continuing rights may be allowed subject to controls | Rights are investigated and acquired/extinguished before final notification, subject to statutory exceptions |
| Ordinary entry and use? | Regulated; s. 27 recognises specified entrants, s. 28 permits; s. 29 restricts habitat disturbance | More restrictive: s. 35 applies sanctuary provisions with park-specific prohibitions; s. 35(7) bars grazing, except permitted livestock use by authorised persons |
| Boundary safeguard | Under current s. 26A(3), State alteration requires NBWL recommendation | Under current s. 35(5), State alteration requires NBWL recommendation |

*Caption: Distinguish proposed designation, adjudicated rights and final site notification before comparing activities.*

Suppose a family grazes animals on land later proposed for conservation. A red line on a map is not proof their claim was heard or automatically abolished. ✅ The **State Government ordinarily declares** a sanctuary or National Park; the **Collector** investigates claims, while the **Chief Wild Life Warden** administers wildlife protection and permits. Calling the Warden the declaring authority merges three jobs. For a State sanctuary, the government first notifies *intention* under section 18, claims are investigated under sections 19–25, then the section-26A declaration establishes the sanctuary. A park under section 35 likewise requires the applicable process; its final regime generally admits less continuing use.

**Who else may declare?** Section **38(1)** applies where a State leases or otherwise transfers an area under its control **not already within a sanctuary** to the **Central Government**. If the section-18 conditions are met, the Centre can notify a **sanctuary** on that transferred land. Under **s. 38(2)** it can notify a **National Park** if section-35 conditions are met, whether or not the transferred area was earlier a sanctuary. The **2022 amendment, effective 2023**, added **s. 38(2A)** for a Central **Conservation Reserve** when s. 36A(1) conditions are met on such land. Section 38 does **not** provide a corresponding Central Community Reserve declaration. For these Central notifications, **s. 38(3)** substitutes the Director or authorised officer for the State Chief Wild Life Warden in the specified functions, and reads references to State Government as Central Government. This is a narrow transfer-and-conditions route, **not** a general power to declare any land at will.

✅ Sanctuaries do **not** imply hunting is allowed: protective prohibitions apply there too. But some authorised entry, subsisting rights and controlled activity are possible; neither "all residents expelled" nor "all traditional activity free of restriction" follows. In a National Park grazing is generally barred by section 35(7), with statutory exceptions, and habitat disturbance needs the exact Act's permits and findings; avoid absolute statements such as "no human ever enters." **Boundaries:** current India Code text as on 5 June 2025 states **NBWL recommendation before a State alters sanctuary boundaries under s. 26A(3) or NP boundaries under s. 35(5)**. The older categorical assertion that a State Legislature resolution is needed to alter both does not describe this current text. Tiger Reserve alteration has its *own* additional NTCA/approval rule under s. 38W. Do not casually say the Chief Wildlife Warden can shrink a park. ✅ The National Board for Wild Life (NBWL) is chaired by the Prime Minister; its Standing Committee has a different chair and handles much routine consideration. Neither body is the owner of a State notification.

**Indian use and limit:** Kaziranga **National Park** and the Manas tiger landscape illustrate why park, sanctuary and species-management labels must be checked against current legal boundaries rather than inferred from names. ⚠️ If a park's habitat benefits neighbouring communities, the law must still settle and respect legal claims; conversely a claim of custom does not make hunting or habitat destruction lawful. **UPSC trap:** compare jurisdiction, stages and permitted use, not only the two names. **Mini recap:** ordinarily State notification, conditional Central s. 38 route; Collector settles claims; Warden manages (Central route: Director/authorised officer); park restrictions differ from sanctuary restrictions.

**Revision notes:** WLPA ss. 18, 19–25, 26A = ordinary State sanctuary pathway; s. 35 = ordinary State NP pathway; s. 38(1)/(2)/(2A) = transferred-land Central sanctuary/NP/conservation-reserve exception, not Community Reserve; s. 38(3) = Director/authorised officer substitutes for Warden; intention ≠ final declaration; admitted rights ≠ universal exemption; hunting not normal permitted livelihood; s. 27 entrants and s. 28 permits; s. 29 habitat protection; s. 35(7) park grazing prohibition with exception; ss. 26A(3), 35(5) require NBWL recommendation for State boundary alteration.

### Visual — two different starting conditions

| Starting land and actor | Declaration pathway | Management / control |
|---|---|---|
| **Government-owned land** useful as link or neighbour to PA | State consults local communities; central consent if central land is included; conservation reserve, **s. 36A** | Conservation reserve management committee, **s. 36B**, advises Chief Wild Life Warden |
| **Private/community land** whose individual/community volunteers to conserve | State declares community reserve on qualifying land, **s. 36C** | Committee **s. 36D** is the management authority; land-use change needs committee resolution **and** State approval, **s. 36C(3)** |

*Caption: The decisive first question is whose land and whose initiative; neither category is a miniature National Park.*

Why extend conservation without making each connector a park? A strip of government land can link two larger habitats; section 36A makes consultation central to its notification. For a meadow conserved by its own landholders, acquiring the land as a park would undermine stewardship; section 36C instead starts from **voluntary conservation of qualifying private or community land**. Both provisions followed the **2002 WLPA amendment**; they do not make rights, management institutions and restrictions identical.

✅ A conservation reserve may protect **landscapes and seascapes** as well as flora, fauna and habitat; it need not physically join two parks. A community reserve lies outside NP, sanctuary and conservation reserve. **Who governs after notification?** Section **36D(1),(3)** makes the **community reserve management committee** responsible for conservation, maintenance, management, preparation and implementation of its management plan. On community land, s. 36D(2) provides **at least five** representatives nominated by the Village Panchayat (or Gram Sabha where there is no Panchayat) and a State wildlife/forest representative; on private land s. 36D(2A) instead specifies the owner, a State representative and the Panchayat/tribal representative as applicable. Under **s. 36C(3)** a change of land-use pattern after notification needs **both a management-committee resolution and State Government approval**. The committee's management role is neither unilateral land-use power nor ownership transfer. Do not equate participation with unregulated hunting, unconditional produce extraction or automatically permitted cultivation: examine the Act, notifications and applicable rights. The Warden is not the management committee. ⚠️ A legally protected but road-blocked connector may not restore animal movement.

**Named Indian examples, with a limit:** The MoEFCC's **21 April 2026 Ramsar-sites register** lists **Asan Conservation Reserve (Uttarakhand)** and **Keshopur–Miani Community Reserve (Punjab)** by those names. Their simultaneous wetland recognition illustrates overlap, **not** an additional WLPA land category or proof of wildlife movement and local participation. A state-owned strip between two large forest habitats is an appropriate *candidate* for conservation-reserve evaluation, while voluntarily conserved village-held land is a *candidate* for community-reserve evaluation; these unnamed candidates are **not** asserted to be notified. **Objection:** Does weaker exclusion inevitably weaken conservation? **Reply:** Protection matched to ownership and sustained local participation can defend habitat otherwise left outside a park. **Residual:** measure whether communities have meaningful power and whether the intended species really uses the link. **UPSC use:** first identify ownership → declaration → management → activity rules; do not turn a four-statement PYQ into a memorised slogan.

**Mini recap / revision notes:** 2002 amendment; ss. 36A–36B = government land + consultation + advisory committee; ss. 36C–36D = volunteered community/private land + statutory management authority; **s. 36C(3): resolution AND State approval for changed land use**; s. 38(2A) conditional Central conservation reserve, **not** Central community reserve; seascapes can qualify; land tenure is not automatically acquired; notified rights are site-specific; a corridor label is not proof of movement.

**Model (about 172 words):** A park can shelter breeding habitat but cannot by itself ensure dispersal across roads, farmland and shared forests. The 2002 WLPA additions give governments more than one instrument. Under section 36A, a State may consult local communities and notify suitable government land, including a connector between protected areas, as a Conservation Reserve. Under section 36C, voluntarily conserved private or community land outside the existing protected categories may become a Community Reserve, with its own representative management committee under section 36D. The latter starts with the landholder's initiative rather than compulsory park-style acquisition. A state-owned link and a community-held grove thus require different legal processes even when both serve a landscape. *Caption: A bounded breeding core can support tigers; wide-ranging elephants require functioning passages and negotiated coexistence across boundaries.*

✅ The **National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA)** is a statutory body under WLPA section 38L. The **State Government notifies a Tiger Reserve on NTCA recommendation** under section 38V; the plan provides for core/critical tiger habitat and a buffer/peripheral area. Critical habitat is identified on scientific and objective criteria, with safeguards for Scheduled Tribes and other forest dwellers; the buffer recognises coexistence rather than automatically imposing the same exclusion as the core. Section 38W governs alteration/de-notification safeguards involving NTCA and NBWL; always identify the exact legal object before comparing boundary rules. Project Tiger's funding and monitoring do not substitute for the section-38V notification.

### Adequate management requires a Tiger Conservation Plan

| Tiger Conservation Plan component | Management purpose |
|---|---|
| Staff development and deployment | Match protection, monitoring and field capacity to the reserve |
| Protection and site-specific habitat inputs | Maintain viable tiger, co-predator and prey populations without distorting the natural prey–predator cycle |

The strongest objection to graded regulation is that variable rules may weaken cores; the reply is to enforce core-specific protections while tailoring adjacent land use. The strongest objection to rigid exclusion is that residents bear uncompensated costs; the reply is rights recognition and demonstrably effective conservation, not abandonment of habitat. Remaining test: publish ecological and livelihood outcomes **by site**.

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

## Statutory geography and in-situ logic

- In situ preserves species and ecological relationships in natural habitat; ex situ protects outside it. Habitat patch → passage → viable population; a park's legal perimeter alone does not secure passage.
- Biosphere Reserve = landscape-scale core–buffer–transition arrangement following the UNESCO MAB model, not a fifth WLPA category. Core may overlap NP/WLS; buffer supports research/compatible use; transition supports settlements and sustainable livelihoods. An Indian declaration and UNESCO World Network recognition are separate steps.
- WLPA four commonly grouped statutory PA land categories: WLS, NP, Conservation Reserve, Community Reserve. Their regimes cannot be replaced by a single mathematically ranked "strictness" scale. **Ordinarily the State Government declares**; Collector investigates applicable rights; Chief Wild Life Warden manages State-protected areas.
- WII, Dehradun = wildlife research and training institution under MoEFCC; it supplies population, habitat, connectivity, monitoring and management-planning inputs but does not notify a PA, settle rights or replace NTCA/State implementation.
- WLS: State intention s. 18 → claims ss. 19–25 → final s. 26A; controlled entry ss. 27–28 and habitat-disturbance restriction s. 29. NP: s. 35 and rights process, stricter use; s. 35(7) generally bars grazing with statutory exception. Neither category legalises hunting. **Exception: s. 38(1),(2)** allows Central sanctuary/NP declaration on qualifying State-leased/transferred land outside a sanctuary when corresponding conditions hold; **s. 38(2A)** also allows a Central Conservation Reserve, **not** Community Reserve; **s. 38(3)** substitutes Director/authorised officer for Warden in specified provisions.
- 2002 additions: s. 36A government-owned Conservation Reserve (consult communities; can include seascapes or connectors; central land needs central concurrence on ordinary State route), advisory committee s. 36B; s. 36C volunteered qualifying private/community Community Reserve, **s. 36D committee itself the management authority**. On community land committee includes Panchayat/Gram Sabha nominees and forest representative; private land has owner/forest/Panchayat-or-tribal representation. After notification, **s. 36C(3)** requires **committee resolution + State approval** for any change of land-use pattern; participation is not permission for unrestricted harvesting, cultivation or hunting.
- Vegetation drivers: rain and dry season → evergreen/deciduous/grassland; elevation/aspect → temperate and alpine; soil/drainage and salinity → mangroves. **Periyar WLS**, Kerala Western Ghats, conserves evergreen forest, endemics and headwaters; a sanctuary still depends on surrounding catchment and corridors. This supplies both the explanation and qualification for 2023 GS-I Q15.
- Site-specific PYQ memory *after first attempts*: Pakhui/Pakke—Arunachal; Valley of Flowers—Uttarakhand alpine, unlike broad altitudinal gradient of Namdapha; Kanha/MP—hard-ground barasingha; Rajasthan's officially named Desert NP Wildlife Sanctuary—**Jaisalmer + Barmer, inhabited and bustard habitat**. Treat designation and name separately.
- Named land-category examples: **Asan Conservation Reserve — Uttarakhand; Keshopur–Miani Community Reserve — Punjab** (MoEFCC's 21 April 2026 Ramsar register); an overlapping Ramsar badge does not itself establish PA governance or ecological outcomes.
- Boundary alteration is never a unilateral Warden map change: current India Code **s. 26A(3)** for sanctuary and **s. 35(5)** for NP each require NBWL recommendation for State alteration; **s. 38W** imposes separate NTCA recommendation and NBWL approval for tiger-reserve alteration, with its own de-notification test. Do not import an outdated State Legislature formula. NBWL chair = Prime Minister; standing committee ≠ whole board.

## Overlays, edges and dated evidence

- NTCA statutory s. 38L; State notifies Tiger Reserve on NTCA recommendation s. 38V; core/critical tiger habitat and buffer have different purposes, and s. 38W governs alteration/de-notification. The Tiger Conservation Plan covers staff development/deployment, protection and site-specific habitat inputs, ecologically compatible reserve/corridor land use, compatible forestry operations and local agricultural/livelihood/development interests. WII may supply science; NTCA approves and the State implements. Core may overlap NP/WLS; do not sum overlapping footprints. Compare like with like.
- Project Elephant / Elephant Reserves are not an NTCA-style statutory core; forest, corridor, rail and people must be managed together. An identified corridor is a planning input, not proof of unobstructed passage.
- ESZ: Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, individually notified boundaries and activity schedules. Tiger buffer ≠ ESZ ≠ ecological corridor. A 10 km appraisal screening convention is not an automatic ESZ width; 2022 Court interim 1 km approach modified in 2023.

## Governance debates, marine scale and answer methods

- Critical tiger habitat: objective scientific case → recognise lawful forest rights → consider coexistence → follow applicable informed-consent and rehabilitation safeguards before any relocation → independently check outcomes. Forest Rights Act, 2006 and WLPA must operate together.
- Marine NP/WLS and s. 36A seascape are legally possible; fishing and coastal pollution require fisheries and Environment (Protection) Act/CRZ coordination. Bounded site remains useful for nursery habitat but mobile species need wider governance.
- CBD Kunming–Montreal Target 3 = effective and equitable **terrestrial/inland + coastal/marine** conservation ambition by 2030, not an achieved India count. OECMs are verified lasting outside-PA outcomes, not every locally valued grove.
- Cauvery landscape: Kabini forms Nagarhole NP's edge; Wayanad WLS includes Kabani and other streams in its drainage; the Sathyamangalam reserve buffer contains local Palar → Cauvery and Moyar → Bhavani → Cauvery links. **Papikonda's hills belong to the Godavari setting**. Do not turn a site-level drainage link into a claim that all hectares lie in one basin or mistake a nearby corridor for the protected-area boundary.
- Prelims method: type of land → applicable statute/section including conditional Centre → declaring versus managing actor → rights and activity rule → site-specific habitat and dated core/area. Mains method: describe vegetation drivers and why rainforest sanctuaries matter; protect breeding core → enable landscape/sea connectivity → reconcile local rights → evaluate ecological and livelihood outcomes.
