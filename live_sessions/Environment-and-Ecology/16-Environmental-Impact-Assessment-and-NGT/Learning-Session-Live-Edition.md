# Environmental Impact Assessment and National Green Tribunal — Live Session Edition

Environmental governance needs two different kinds of protection. **Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA)** asks whether a proposed activity should proceed, where it should be located and under what safeguards. The **National Green Tribunal (NGT)** adjudicates specified environmental disputes, appeals and claims for restoration or compensation. One is mainly preventive; the other is adjudicatory and corrective.

## Lesson 1 — EIA as a decision rule before ecological commitment

Progress: 1/12 | Stage: Foundation | Subtopic: Rationale, preventive logic and the decision window

### Visual gateway: the shrinking choice set

```text
BEFORE SITE COMMITMENT
many sites + technologies + scales + designs remain open
                         ↓
                   PRIOR EIA
                         ↓
avoid site? reduce scale? change technology? add safeguards? reject?

AFTER CONSTRUCTION
capital sunk + jobs tied + contracts signed + habitat altered
                         ↓
choices shrink toward compensation, retrofit, closure or restoration
```

The simplest reason for EIA is that **prevention is possible only while genuine choices remain open**. Once a mine, port, highway or factory is substantially built, the decision-maker faces sunk costs and irreversible ecological change. A later study may measure damage, but it cannot recreate the original opportunity to choose another site or design.

### 1. Definition and purpose

EIA is a structured, forward-looking process for identifying, predicting and evaluating the likely environmental consequences of a proposed project before approval. It should help the authority decide:

1. whether the project is environmentally acceptable;
2. whether another site, scale, technology or design is preferable;
3. which impacts must be avoided, minimised, restored or compensated;
4. which measurable conditions must bind construction and operation; and
5. whether uncertainty is too serious to permit the proposal as designed.

EIA is therefore not merely a report. The report is evidence within a **decision process**.

### 2. The causal logic

```text
Project action
   → pressure: clearing / extraction / emission / abstraction / traffic
   → pathway: air / water / soil / noise / habitat fragmentation
   → receptor: people / species / aquifer / river / livelihood
   → impact: magnitude × extent × duration × probability × sensitivity
   → decision: avoid / redesign / condition / reject
```

A road through intact habitat illustrates the chain. Tree loss is not the only impact. The road may fragment habitat, increase edge effects, accelerate access and induce later settlement. A narrow project boundary can therefore hide a wider causal footprint.

### 3. EIA and the precautionary principle

The precautionary principle does not mean “ban every uncertain activity.” It means that credible threats of serious or irreversible harm cannot be ignored merely because every causal detail is not scientifically certain. The proponent and authority must investigate uncertainty before taking a decision that may foreclose ecological options.

### 4. EIA is not permission by paperwork

Three weak substitutes must be rejected:

| Weak substitute | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| A long consultant report | Length does not prove representative data or sound prediction |
| A list of generic safeguards | Conditions must answer the identified site-specific impact |
| Compensation after damage | Money cannot always restore an extinct population, aquifer or sacred landscape |

**Objection:** EIA delays urgently needed development.

**Reply:** redundant procedure should be removed, but the answer is early screening, clear terms of reference, reliable baseline data and time-bound reasoned decisions—not assessment after irreversible commitment.

**Residual:** even a well-designed EIA must operate under uncertainty. The correct response is adaptive monitoring and enforceable stop-correct triggers, not a claim of perfect prediction.

### UPSC application

Open a Mains answer with the phrase **“EIA is a decision-forcing, preventive instrument, not a post-construction audit.”** Then show the shrinking-choice logic and qualify it with uncertainty and monitoring.

### Revision notes

1. EIA is a process, not merely a document.
2. Its central purpose is informed choice before project commitment.
3. Prior assessment preserves site, scale, technology and design alternatives.
4. Sunk costs create pressure to regularise rather than prevent.
5. The source–pathway–receptor chain explains how an activity becomes an impact.
6. Significance depends on magnitude, extent, duration, probability and receptor sensitivity.
7. Induced and indirect impacts may exceed the project footprint.
8. Precaution acts despite incomplete certainty where harm may be serious or irreversible.
9. Compensation is not always ecological restoration.
10. Efficient EIA removes redundant delay without removing substantive scrutiny.
11. Monitoring is necessary because prediction remains uncertain.
12. A clearance condition must be measurable and linked to a predicted impact.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why can a technically detailed study completed after construction not perform the same function as prior EIA?

**Model answer:** Prior EIA informs a live choice among rejection, alternative sites, technologies, scales and safeguards. After construction, sunk costs and completed ecological change narrow the decision to retrofit, compensation, closure or restoration. A later study may quantify harm, but it cannot restore the lost decision window.

**Misconception to avoid:** Better data collected later do not cure the loss of preventive choice.

### Original Mains practice — 10 marks, 150 words

**Mains prompt:** Explain why the timing of environmental assessment is as important as its technical quality.

**Model:** Environmental assessment has value only while it can influence the proposal. Before construction, the authority may compare sites, technologies and scales, require redesign or reject a project whose residual harm is unacceptable. This implements precaution by examining credible risks before capital commitment and ecological alteration. After construction, sunk costs, employment dependence and public expenditure create pressure to preserve the project, while destroyed habitat or displaced livelihoods may be irrecoverable. A technically sophisticated post-construction report can measure damage and design remediation, but it cannot recreate the original option to avoid that damage. Timing and quality are therefore complementary: an early but superficial report is weak, while a rigorous but late report is no longer genuinely preventive. India needs early scoping, representative baseline data, reasoned appraisal and enforceable monitoring rather than retrospective paperwork.

**Unique quantified rubric (10):** preventive timing 2; shrinking-choice logic 2; precaution 2; sunk-cost consequence 2; balanced qualification and reform 2.

## Lesson 2 — The EIA Notification architecture, categories and authorities

### Legal ladder

```text
ENVIRONMENT (PROTECTION) ACT, 1986
              ↓ Section 3 + Environment (Protection) Rules
S.O. 1533(E), 14 September 2006
EIA Notification, 2006, as amended
              ↓
Schedule identifies covered projects and thresholds
              ↓
Category A                     Category B
MoEFCC + EAC                   SEIAA + SEAC
                               ├─ B1: EIA report
                               └─ B2: no EIA report under screening classification
```

### 1. Current status

As at **3 October 2026**, the operative general framework remains the **EIA Notification, 2006, as amended**. The Draft EIA Notification, 2020 did not replace it. The Supreme Court’s 29 July 2026 decision in *Vanashakti v. Union of India* also treated the 2006 Notification’s prior-clearance regime as governing law.

Prior environmental clearance applies to:

- new projects or activities listed in the Schedule;
- covered expansions or modernisations crossing the relevant threshold; and
- specified changes in product mix.

Projects outside the Schedule are not brought into the notification merely because they may have some environmental effect; other laws may still apply.

### 2. July 2026 continuity amendment

Two Gazette notifications must be read together. **S.O. 3862(E), dated 13 July 2026**, amended the EIA Notification, 2006. **S.O. 3863(E), issued on the same date**, constituted the standing bodies and prescribed their composition.

| Question | Position as at 3 October 2026 |
|---|---|
| Regular tenure | The text substituted **four years** for three years for EACs, SEIAAs and SEACs and removed the earlier power to extend the term by up to twelve months |
| Prospective operation | MoEFCC’s Office Memorandum of 29 July 2026 clarifies that existing bodies continue on their original three-year term; four years applies to EACs, SEIAAs and SEACs constituted after the amendment came into force |
| Reconstitution duty | The State Government must initiate reconstitution of SEIAA and SEAC six months before expiry |
| If SEIAA is unavailable | The **Standing Authority on Environment Impact Assessment (SAEIA)** discharges SEIAA functions |
| If SEAC is unavailable | The **Standing Committee on Environment Impact Appraisal (SCEIA)** discharges SEAC functions |
| Duration of fallback | Not more than **six months in the first instance**, subject to one further extension of up to six months |
| Earlier default route | The sentence sending Category B proposals to the Central level when SEIAA or SEAC was absent was omitted |

The activation conditions are precise. SAEIA acts where SEIAA is non-functional, has not been constituted or receives another responsibility from the Central Government. S.O. 3863(E) defines a non-functional SEIAA as one disabled by expiry of tenure, absence of its Chairperson or Member for more than one month, or a judicial stay. SCEIA acts where SEAC is non-functional, has not been constituted or receives another Central assignment; its express non-functionality definition covers tenure expiry or a judicial stay.

| Standing body | Gazette composition under S.O. 3863(E) | Function |
|---|---|---|
| SAEIA | **Chairperson:** Chief Secretary or Advisor to the Administrator or Lt. Governor. **Member:** Principal Chief Conservator of Forest (Head of Forest Force). **Member Secretary:** an officer of the State or Union territory Government working as Special Secretary or Joint Secretary or Deputy Secretary in the Environment Department. | Exercises the Category B decision powers and procedures of SEIAA during valid activation |
| SCEIA | **Chairperson:** Administrative Secretary, Environment Department of the State or Union territory. **Members:** Chief Wildlife Warden; Director General or Director, Health Services of the State; Member Secretary of the State Pollution Control Board or Union territory Pollution Control Committee. **Domain Expert:** Director or Principal of an Indian Institute of Technology or National Institute of Technology located in the State or Union territory, or their nominees as per requirement; if neither institution exists there, a domain expert nominated by the Director, National Environmental Engineering Research Institute. **Member Secretary:** an officer of the State or Union territory Government working as Special Secretary or Joint Secretary or Deputy Secretary in the Environment Department. | Exercises the Category B appraisal powers and procedures of SEAC during valid activation |

The SCEIA Chairperson may also **co-opt expert(s) or institutions of repute to assist the SCEIA in particular matter(s)**. This is a matter-specific assistance power, not a rewriting of the six notified offices and nomination routes above.

The SPCB or UTPCC serves as secretariat, with the State or Union Territory administration supplying manpower and logistics. These are **time-limited continuity mechanisms**, not substitutes for screening, EIA where required, consultation, appraisal or a reasoned clearance decision. Continuity therefore has to be balanced against the scientific independence expected from expert appraisal.

### 3. Category A and Category B

| Feature | Category A | Category B |
|---|---|---|
| Regulatory authority | Central Government/MoEFCC | State/UT Environment Impact Assessment Authority |
| Expert body | Expert Appraisal Committee (EAC) | State/UT Expert Appraisal Committee (SEAC) |
| Screening | Not the B1/B2 screening stage | Determines B1 or B2 where applicable |
| Assessment | Normally full appraisal under project-specific terms | B1 requires EIA; B2 follows the prescribed reduced route |
| Federal logic | Wider or more significant potential impact | State-level decision within the notified schedule |

**EAC and SEAC recommend; MoEFCC and SEIAA decide.** A recommendation is not itself the clearance order.

### 4. General Condition

The notification’s General Condition can elevate specified Category B projects to Category A when they are located within the notified distance of sensitive boundaries such as protected areas, critically polluted areas, notified eco-sensitive areas or inter-State/international boundaries. Apply it only where the Schedule makes it applicable.

The Supreme Court held on 5 August 2025 that the General Condition was not applicable to Schedule entries **8(a)** and **8(b)**. This is a specific legal distinction, not a general exemption from prior clearance.

### 5. Building and area-development position after the 2025 judgment

S.O. 523(E) of 29 January 2025 substituted Entry 8:

- **8(a):** building and construction projects with built-up area at least 20,000 square metres and below 150,000 square metres;
- **8(b):** townships and area-development projects covering at least 50 hectares and/or built-up area at least 150,000 square metres; these require an EIA report and are appraised as Category B1;
- General Conditions do not apply to either entry.

The notification attempted to exclude industrial sheds, schools, colleges and hostels for educational institutions through Note 1 to Entry 8(a). The Supreme Court in *Vanashakti v. Union of India* (5 August 2025) quashed that Note as arbitrary, while upholding the remainder of the notification and the connected 30 January 2025 Office Memorandum.

### 6. Draft EIA Notification, 2020: proposal versus operative law

The 2020 draft was a proposed replacement, not an amendment that silently became law. Its important differences from the operative 2006 design as understood in the 2020 debate were:

| Dimension | EIA Notification, 2006 as it stood when the 2020 PYQ was asked | Draft EIA 2020 proposal |
|---|---|---|
| Legal status | Binding notification, as amended | Draft S.O. 1199(E); never replaced the 2006 Notification |
| B2 route | B2 projects did not require an EIA report; the framework used prior environmental clearance rather than a separate prior-EP instrument | Split B2 into two routes: specified B2 projects placed before an appraisal committee could receive prior EC on an EMP-based route; other B2 projects could receive **prior Environment Permission (prior-EP)** after completeness verification, without committee appraisal |
| Public-hearing notice | Minimum **30 days** | Proposed minimum **20 days** |
| Consultation exclusions | Existing notified exemptions, including the B2 route and specified classes | Excluded all B2 projects; specified linear projects under items 31 and 38 in a “Border Area”, defined as within **100 km aerial distance from the Line of Actual Control**; offshore projects beyond 12 nautical miles; and other listed classes |
| Compliance reporting | Half-yearly compliance reports | Proposed one online report each year, by 30 June, while allowing the authority to demand more frequent reporting |
| Violation treatment | Prior EC remained the norm; S.O. 804(E) of 2017 had created a time-bound violation window | Clause 22 proposed a continuing process triggered by the proponent, a government authority, the appraisal committee or the regulatory authority. An impermissible or unsustainable site was to face closure recommendation; a positive appraisal required damage assessment, remediation and natural/community-resource augmentation, with higher multipliers for violations detected rather than self-reported |
| Validity design | In 2020 the broad comparison was 30 years for mining, 10 years for river-valley projects and 7 years for other projects | Proposed construction/installation windows of project life up to 50 years or the mining-lease period, whichever was earlier, with mining to commence within 10 years; 15 years for river-valley, irrigation and nuclear projects; and 10 years for other projects. For non-mining projects completed within that window, the operational phase was proposed to continue for the remaining project life |

The exam-safe conclusion is not that every proposal was “dilution” or “reform.” It is that the draft proposed a differentiated EC/EP architecture, an EMP for every B2 project, shorter hearing notice, annual default reporting, broader specified exemptions, longer construction/installation windows and a continuing violation-processing clause. None of those draft provisions became current law merely because it appeared in S.O. 1199(E).

### 7. Instrument hierarchy

```text
STATUTE
   ↓
VALID DELEGATED LEGISLATION: rules / gazette notification
   ↓
ADMINISTRATIVE INSTRUCTION: office memorandum / circular
   ↓
PROJECT-SPECIFIC CLEARANCE AND CONDITIONS

Lower instrument cannot contradict the higher one.
Binding constitutional-court judgment controls their interpretation.
```

**Objection:** categorisation by thresholds is administratively simple but ecologically crude.

**Reply:** thresholds create predictable jurisdiction and proportional procedure.

**Residual:** a smaller project in a fragile or already saturated landscape may be more harmful than a larger project in a resilient location. General Conditions, cumulative assessment and carrying-capacity studies partly address this, but not comprehensively.

### UPSC application

Never write “Category A means environmentally harmful and Category B means harmless.” The categories allocate appraisal level; they do not certify ecological acceptability.

### Concept check

**Question:** A project is placed in Category B. Does that alone establish that it will be appraised by the State and will not require an EIA report?

**Model answer:** No. A notified General Condition may shift an applicable Category B project to Category A, and the State-level screening process may classify it as B1, which requires an EIA report. The schedule entry and current amendments must be read before deciding the authority and procedure.

**Misconception to avoid:** “B” does not automatically mean B2, minor impact or exemption from prior clearance.

### Original Mains practice — 15 marks, 250 words

**Mains prompt:** Explain the federal and legal architecture of the EIA Notification, 2006, while identifying its principal classification limits.

**Model:** The EIA Notification, 2006, issued under the Environment (Protection) Act, creates a schedule-based prior-clearance regime. Category A proposals are decided by MoEFCC on EAC recommendations; Category B proposals ordinarily go to SEIAA on SEAC recommendations, with B1 requiring an EIA report and B2 following its prescribed reduced route. The General Condition can elevate only specified B entries. The Supreme Court clarified in 2025 that it does not apply to entries 8(a) and 8(b), and quashed the attempted exemption for large industrial sheds and educational buildings. S.O. 3862(E) gives bodies constituted after 13 July 2026 four-year tenure, removes the former extra-year extension and requires reconstitution to begin six months before expiry; earlier bodies retain three years under the 29 July clarification. SAEIA and SCEIA provide a six-month, once-extendable fallback when regular State bodies are unavailable, while S.O. 3863(E) supplies their ex-officio composition. Draft EIA 2020 proposals such as prior-EP, shorter hearing notice, annual reporting and a continuing violation route never replaced current law. The architecture combines national minimum rules with State appraisal, but thresholds, variable capacity and temporary official-led fallback can weaken location-sensitive expertise. Predictable categories need transparent reasons, stable expertise and cumulative evidence.

## Lesson 3 — Screening, scoping and baseline: asking the right question

Progress: 3/12 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Entry gate, terms of reference and evidence before prediction

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Book context: Scientific validity begins with a correctly bounded question and a representative description of the receiving environment.

CA search: "official EIA screening scoping baseline data India 2026"

CA found: No material legal change alters the foundational sequence.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Funnel from proposal to study design

```text
SCREENING
Does this Category B proposal need a full EIA?
          ↓
SCOPING
Which impacts, seasons, receptors, alternatives and methods must be studied?
          ↓
BASELINE
What is the environmental condition before the project?
          ↓
PREDICTION
What changes because of the project?
```

### 1. Screening

Screening is the Category B entry decision. The SEAC scrutinises the application to determine whether further environmental studies and an EIA report are required. Projects requiring the report are B1; the remaining screened projects are B2.

Good screening considers more than project size:

- location sensitivity;
- pollution intensity and accident potential;
- water and land demand;
- proximity to communities and critical habitats;
- linked and induced activities; and
- existing environmental stress.

### 2. Scoping

Scoping produces the **Terms of Reference (ToR)**. For Category A the EAC performs it; for B1 the SEAC performs it. A useful ToR identifies:

1. spatial and temporal study boundaries;
2. valued environmental and social receptors;
3. baseline parameters and seasons;
4. impact-prediction methods;
5. alternatives to compare;
6. accident and disaster scenarios;
7. cumulative pressures requiring study; and
8. consultation and disclosure needs.

Item 8 construction and township proposals follow their special notified route and do not use the ordinary scoping stage in the same manner.

### 3. Baseline is the counterfactual anchor

Baseline is the measured condition against which project-induced change is judged. It includes:

| Domain | Illustrative evidence |
|---|---|
| Air and climate | pollutants, wind, temperature, inversion context |
| Water | flows, levels, quality, seasonal dependence and competing use |
| Land and soil | erosion, contamination, slope, land capability |
| Biodiversity | habitat, species, movement routes, breeding seasons |
| Human environment | settlement, health, livelihoods, cultural and sacred use |

Baseline is not a decorative inventory. It must answer: **what condition would likely exist without the project?**

### 4. Data-quality tests

```text
REPRESENTATIVE?
season + location + duration + frequency

TRACEABLE?
method + laboratory + detection limit + raw record

COMPARABLE?
same parameter, unit, protocol and reference period

COMPLETE?
includes vulnerable receptors and inconvenient results
```

A single dry-season biodiversity visit cannot safely represent monsoon breeding. A downstream water sample cannot establish an upstream baseline. Secondary data may provide context, but stale or coarse data cannot replace site-specific evidence where local variation controls risk.

**Objection:** wider scoping makes every EIA unmanageably large.

**Reply:** scoping should be selective, not indiscriminate. It removes immaterial issues while protecting material ones.

**Residual:** the committee often depends on information initially supplied by the proponent. Disclosure, independent datasets and field verification are therefore essential.

### UPSC application

Use the line: **“A weak ToR produces a precise answer to the wrong question; a weak baseline makes every later prediction unverifiable.”**

### Revision notes

1. Screening applies to Category B classification.
2. B1 requires an EIA report; B2 does not under the screening route.
3. Scoping defines the Terms of Reference.
4. EAC scopes Category A; SEAC scopes B1.
5. ToR must identify receptors, boundaries, seasons, methods and alternatives.
6. Item 8 follows a special route.
7. Baseline describes the pre-project receiving environment.
8. Baseline supplies the counterfactual for measuring change.
9. Seasonal and spatial representativeness are essential.
10. Method, laboratory, unit and detection limit make data traceable.
11. Secondary data cannot automatically substitute for site evidence.
12. Linked and induced impacts should enter the study boundary.
13. Good scoping is selective but not convenient.
14. Independent evidence reduces proponent-information dependence.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why is an accurate air-quality dataset still inadequate if monitoring stations are placed away from the settlement most exposed to the project plume?

**Model answer:** Accuracy at the sampled points does not establish representativeness. If the study design omits the sensitive receptor predicted to receive the plume, the baseline and later impact comparison answer the wrong spatial question even though each laboratory result is technically correct.

**Misconception to avoid:** Data quality includes study design and representativeness, not only laboratory accuracy.

### Original Mains practice — 20 marks, 250 words

**Mains prompt:** “The credibility of EIA is largely determined before impact prediction begins.” Analyse with reference to screening, scoping and baseline studies.

**Model:** Screening and scoping determine what the assessment will see. Screening separates B1 proposals requiring full EIA from B2 proposals, but a size-only approach can miss ecological sensitivity, cumulative pollution or accident potential. Scoping then converts a broad proposal into enforceable Terms of Reference covering study area, seasons, receptors, alternatives, methods and linked activities. If groundwater drawdown, wildlife movement or downstream livelihoods are excluded here, later modelling cannot repair the omission. Baseline data provide the counterfactual against which change is measured. They must be spatially and seasonally representative, methodologically traceable and focused on vulnerable receptors. A technically accurate sample from the wrong place or season remains decision-poor evidence. The proponent’s information advantage also creates a risk of narrow boundaries and selective data. Committees should therefore use public datasets, disclose raw information, permit expert challenge and verify critical locations. Sound prediction begins with the right question and a defensible account of conditions before the project.

**Unique quantified rubric (20):** screening logic 3; scoping/ToR 4; baseline counterfactual 4; data-quality dimensions 4; proponent-information risk 2; reforms 2; qualified conclusion 1.

## Lesson 4 — Prediction, alternatives, mitigation, EMP and risk assessment

Progress: 4/12 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Turning evidence into a project decision and operating obligations

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Book context: The official generic EIA structure links anticipated impacts, alternatives, monitoring, risk assessment and the Environment Management Plan.

CA search: "official India EIA alternatives mitigation EMP risk assessment 2026"

CA found: No material recent change alters these analytical functions.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### The decision spine

```text
BASELINE
   ↓
PREDICT each phase: construction → operation → accident → closure
   ↓
COMPARE alternatives: no-project / site / technology / scale / layout
   ↓
MITIGATION HIERARCHY
AVOID → MINIMISE → RESTORE → OFFSET residual harm
   ↓
EMP: who does what, by when, at what cost, measured how
   ↓
MONITOR → trigger correction, suspension or redesign
```

### 1. Impact prediction

Prediction should distinguish:

- direct, indirect and induced impacts;
- construction, operation, accident and decommissioning phases;
- short-, medium- and long-term effects;
- reversible and irreversible effects;
- routine loads and low-probability/high-consequence events; and
- project-only and cumulative effects.

Significance combines the pressure with receptor sensitivity. The same noise level may be tolerable beside an industrial estate but unacceptable near a nesting colony or hospital.

### 2. Alternatives analysis

The notification’s generic structure requires analysis of technology and site alternatives where scoping calls for it. A robust assessment also asks whether the project, scale or timing should change.

| Alternative | Question |
|---|---|
| No-project | What benefits and harms occur if the project is not built? |
| Site | Can the same objective avoid a forest, wetland, floodplain or dense settlement? |
| Technology | Can a cleaner process reduce resource use, waste or accident risk? |
| Scale/layout | Can a smaller footprint or corridor design preserve key functions? |
| Timing | Can work avoid breeding, migration or high-flow periods? |

The no-project option is not automatic rejection; it is the counterfactual needed to expose whether the chosen proposal is truly preferable.

### 3. Mitigation hierarchy

Avoidance has priority because restoration and offsets carry ecological uncertainty. Moving a road away from a wetland is stronger than promising to recreate the wetland later. Minimisation reduces unavoidable harm; restoration repairs disturbed areas; offsets address residual impact only after the earlier steps are exhausted.

### 4. Environment Management Plan

An EMP must convert promises into administration:

```text
impact → measure → responsible officer → budget → indicator
      → monitoring frequency → disclosure → corrective trigger
```

“Dust will be controlled” is weak. “Cover haul trucks, pave the internal route, monitor PM10 at named receptors weekly during construction and stop hauling when the action threshold is exceeded” is auditable.

### 5. Risk assessment

Risk assessment asks a narrower but deeper question than ordinary impact prediction:

```text
hazard × likelihood × exposure × consequence
```

It considers credible accident scenarios, domino effects, emergency response, off-site consequences and vulnerable populations. It does not replace routine pollution assessment.

**Objection:** offsets permit developers to buy permission to destroy.

**Reply:** an offset can address genuinely residual, measurable impact after avoidance and minimisation.

**Residual:** ecological equivalence, additionality, time lag and long-term stewardship are difficult to prove. Some habitats and cultural values are effectively non-substitutable.

### UPSC application

In answers, connect **prediction → alternative → mitigation → EMP indicator**. Lists of safeguards score poorly when no mechanism links them to the identified impact.

### Revision notes

1. Prediction covers construction, operation, accidents and closure.
2. Direct, indirect, induced and cumulative impacts are distinct.
3. Significance depends on receptor sensitivity as well as magnitude.
4. Irreversible commitments require special caution.
5. Alternatives include site, technology, scale, layout, timing and no-project.
6. No-project is a counterfactual, not a predetermined veto.
7. Avoidance precedes minimisation, restoration and offset.
8. Offsets apply only to residual harm.
9. EMP converts mitigation into roles, budgets, indicators and triggers.
10. A vague condition is difficult to enforce.
11. Risk assessment focuses on accident likelihood and consequences.
12. Routine pollution and catastrophic risk require different tools.
13. Ecological restoration may face time lag and uncertainty.
14. Some values are non-substitutable.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why is a promise to create compensatory habitat not equivalent to avoiding an old-growth habitat in the first place?

**Model answer:** Avoidance preserves an existing, functioning and connected ecological system. Compensatory habitat may differ in species, soil, hydrology and location, may take decades to mature and may fail. It can address residual harm only after feasible avoidance and minimisation have been exhausted.

**Misconception to avoid:** Equal area does not establish equal ecological function.

### Original Mains practice — 10 marks, 150 words

**Mains prompt:** Distinguish an Environment Management Plan from a generic list of environmental safeguards.

**Model:** A generic safeguard states an intention; an Environment Management Plan makes it enforceable. The EMP links each predicted impact to a specific measure, responsible unit, budget, timetable, monitoring location, indicator, reporting frequency and corrective trigger. For a mine-haul road, “control dust” is vague. An auditable EMP specifies covered trucks, paved or treated routes, speed limits, receptor-based particulate monitoring and action when thresholds are exceeded. It must also cover accident response, contractor obligations, closure and public disclosure. The plan should follow the mitigation hierarchy: avoid harm first, then minimise, restore and only finally offset residual damage. Yet an elaborate EMP cannot rescue a fundamentally unsuitable site or unreliable baseline. It operationalises an acceptable decision; it is not a substitute for alternatives analysis or reasoned appraisal.

**Unique quantified rubric (10):** enforceability definition 2; six EMP elements 3; concrete example 2; mitigation hierarchy 1; boundary/qualification 2.

### Participation is a ladder, not one word

```text
      ↓
```

### 1. Statutory EIA consultation

For applicable Category A and B1 projects, public consultation ordinarily contains:

1. a district-wise public hearing at or near the site, conducted by the SPCB/UTPCC to ascertain concerns of locally affected persons; and
2. written responses from other persons with a plausible stake in environmental aspects.

The original notification requires a minimum 30-day public notice and expects the hearing process to be completed and forwarded within the prescribed period. The proponent must address material concerns and revise the draft EIA/EMP or submit a supplementary response before appraisal.

### 2. Consultation is not universal

The notification contains exemptions, including B2 projects, national-defence/security or strategic projects as determined by government, and specified project classes. Building and area-development Entry 8 projects follow their special route and are not subject to ordinary public consultation merely because Entry 8(b) is B1.

### 3. Quality tests

| Test | Good participation requires |
|---|---|
| Notice | sufficient time, correct villages and accessible venue |
| Information | full draft availability and intelligible local-language summary |
| Inclusion | women, workers, fishers, forest users, pastoralists and remote hamlets |
| Freedom | no intimidation; speakers can state objections |
| Responsiveness | issue-wise reply and visible design/condition change |
| Record | accurate proceedings, written submissions and reasoned treatment |

Technical disclosure without intelligibility is formal access, not meaningful participation.

### 4. Federal, local and tribal dimensions

EIA consultation does not absorb every other legal process:

- the SPCB conducts the hearing, but MoEFCC/SEIAA decides EC;
- Panchayats and urban local bodies possess planning and service information that may reveal water, waste and land-use constraints;
- forest diversion, wildlife approval, land acquisition and pollution-control consent remain distinct;
- Scheduled Area governance and forest-rights processes may give the Gram Sabha a role different from an EIA hearing;
- affected Scheduled Tribes and other forest dwellers may raise livelihood, community forest resource, cultural and religious claims under the Forest Rights Act framework.

|---|---|---|---|

**Residual:** participation can also be unequal; organised actors may dominate, and local support may reflect benefit promises rather than ecological acceptability. The authority must evaluate evidence rather than count voices.

### The accountability loop

```text
PROPONENT supplies proposal and bears compliance duty
      ↓
ACCREDITED CONSULTANT prepares sector-scoped EIA/EMP
      ↓
EAC/SEAC interrogates evidence and recommends
      ↓
MoEFCC/SEIAA gives reasoned decision and conditions
      ↓
PROPONENT implements + reports half-yearly
      ↓
REGULATOR verifies → corrects / suspends / cancels / prosecutes as law permits
      ↺ feedback to future appraisal
```

### 1. Appraisal

Appraisal is detailed scrutiny of the application, final EIA, consultation outcome and supporting material. The EAC/SEAC may seek clarification and must recommend grant with conditions or rejection with reasons. The regulatory authority normally acts on that recommendation but remains the legal decision-maker.

A defensible appraisal asks:

- Were all ToR items answered?
- Are baseline methods and raw data credible?
- Were alternatives genuinely compared?
- Did the project respond to consultation?
- Are residual impacts acceptable?
- Are conditions measurable and institutionally feasible?
- What happens if predictions prove wrong?

### 2. Consultant and proponent accountability

MoEFCC’s accreditation framework requires EIA/EMP reports to be prepared by appropriately accredited consultant organisations within their approved sectoral scope. Accreditation is a competence screen, not a guarantee of truth.

The notification’s generic structure requires disclosure of consultants and their role. It also provides that deliberate concealment or materially false or misleading information can lead to rejection of the application or cancellation of clearance after hearing the applicant.

The proponent cannot escape responsibility by blaming the consultant. The proponent controls project information, submits the application and must comply with the clearance.

### 3. Detecting weak evidence

```text
RED FLAGS
copied species lists
identical data across distant stations
missing raw laboratory records
baseline season chosen to hide peak impact
impact model without assumptions or calibration
consultation concern answered by generic assurance
EMP budget unrelated to stated measures
```

Independent datasets, geotagged sampling, laboratory audit trails, public raw-data access and conflict-of-interest disclosure improve reliability.

### 4. Post-clearance monitoring

The 2006 Notification requires half-yearly compliance reports, originally due on 1 June and 1 December, and treats them as public documents. Monitoring should combine:

- self-reports;
- regulator and field inspections;
- continuous or periodic instrumental data;
- community complaints and health/ecological signals;
- remote sensing where suitable; and
- condition-specific corrective action.

### 5. PARIVESH 3.0: digital workflow, not digital clearance by default

**PARIVESH** expands to **Pro-Active and Responsive facilitation by Interactive, Virtuous and Environmental Single-window Hub**. It is the Union’s single-window online platform for processing and monitoring environmental, forest, wildlife and Coastal Regulation Zone proposals. PARIVESH 3.0 links the application and compliance workflow through:

| Module | Governance function |
|---|---|
| Know Your Approval | Gives a preliminary indication of approvals that a proposal may require |
| Common Application Form | Reuses common project information across applicable green approvals |
| GIS-based Decision Support System | Places project location against mapped environmental features for appraisal support |
| Online workflow and dashboards | Tracks submission, scrutiny, committee consideration and authority decision |
| Compliance management | Receives and organises post-approval reports and regulatory follow-up |

The platform can improve traceability and reduce repetitive filing. It does not alter a statutory threshold, waive public consultation or convert an incomplete appraisal into a lawful clearance. Digital availability is valuable only when underlying documents, reasons and compliance data are intelligible and complete.

### 6. Environment Audit Rules, 2025

The **Environment Audit Rules, 2025**, notified through **S.O. 3973(E) on 29 August 2025**, commenced on publication. The Central Government designates an **Environment Audit Designated Agency (EADA)**, while persons or organisations qualify and register as **Registered Environment Auditors (REAs)** to perform assigned audit functions. The framework is:

```text
Central Government
      ↓ designates and oversees
Environment Audit Designated Agency (EADA)
      ↓ certifies, registers, trains, monitors
Registered Environment Auditors (REAs)
      ↓ audit / sample / verify / report
Projects, processes and environmental obligations
      ↓
Regulatory authority retains inspection, decision and enforcement power
```

REAs may audit projects, sample emissions, effluents and waste, report violations, verify self-compliance, calculate environmental compensation when assigned, and perform specified EIA, CRZ, EPR, Ecomark or Green Credit verification functions. Certification can occur through the notified examination or a limited recognition-of-prior-learning route; registration is valid for five years and renewable subject to guidelines. Rule 13 requires random assignment for audits allocated under the Rules, while duties, a code of conduct, performance review and sanctions seek to protect integrity.

The Rules expressly state that this audit system **supplements rather than replaces** government inspection and verification. That boundary is crucial: specialised auditors can expand measurement capacity, but the State cannot outsource legal judgment, coercive power or responsibility for checking conflicts and false assurance.

### 7. Core notification clocks

| Step | Notification clock |
|---|---|
| ToR communication at scoping | within 60 days of Form 1 receipt under the original framework |
| Public hearing by SPCB/UTPCC | proceedings forwarded within 45 days of request |
| Substitute public agency if the Board/Committee fails | a further period of 45 days |
| Public notice before hearing | minimum 30 days |
| EAC/SEAC appraisal | within 60 days of final EIA or complete prescribed forms |
| Regulatory decision | notification frames communication within 45 days of recommendation and an overall 105-day route, subject to reconsideration procedure |

These clocks organise responsibility; they do not excuse an incomplete EIA or denial of natural justice.

### 8. Adaptive conditions

Monitoring is useful only if exceedance changes behaviour:

```text
indicator crosses trigger
      → verify
      → immediate control
      → investigate cause
      → revise operation or mitigation
      → disclose result
      → suspend/close if risk remains unacceptable
```

**Objection:** third-party consultants and auditors can increase technical capacity.

**Reply:** yes, if accreditation, transparent methods, rotation, random checks and sanctions preserve independence.

**Residual:** when the proponent pays the assessor, structural conflict remains. Public verification and regulatory capacity cannot be outsourced completely.

### UPSC application

Distinguish **report accreditation**, **appraisal independence** and **compliance enforcement**. None substitutes for the other two.

1. Appraisal scrutinises the final EIA, alternatives, consultation and residual impacts.
2. EAC/SEAC recommends; MoEFCC/SEIAA decides with reasons.

## Lesson 7 — NGT jurisdiction, benches and standing

Progress: 7/12 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Statutory gateway and access to environmental justice

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Book context: The National Green Tribunal Act, 2010 defines a specialised forum rather than a regulator or universal environmental court.

CA search: "official National Green Tribunal jurisdiction benches standing 2026"

CA found: No material statutory change alters the jurisdictional foundation.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Jurisdiction gate

```text
IS IT A CIVIL CASE?
      ↓ yes
IS THERE A SUBSTANTIAL QUESTION RELATING TO ENVIRONMENT
or enforcement of an environmental legal right?
      ↓ yes
DOES IT ARISE FROM IMPLEMENTATION OF A SCHEDULE I ENACTMENT?
      ↓ yes
SECTION 14 ORIGINAL JURISDICTION MAY OPEN

Separate route:
Is the challenged order one listed in Section 16?
      ↓ yes
NGT APPELLATE JURISDICTION MAY OPEN
```

### 1. Institutional character

The NGT was established under the **National Green Tribunal Act, 2010** for effective and expeditious disposal of environmental cases, enforcement of environmental legal rights and grant of relief and compensation. It combines Judicial Members with Expert Members.

### 2. Composition: legal judgment plus environmental expertise

| Component | Statutory design |
|---|---|
| Chairperson | A person who is or has been a Judge of the Supreme Court or Chief Justice of a High Court |
| Judicial Members | Section 5(1) permits the Supreme Court Judge route and the High Court Chief Justice route stated for the Chairperson or Judicial Member; its proviso additionally makes a person who is or has been a Judge of a High Court eligible specifically as a Judicial Member |
| Expert Members — technical route | A Master of Science degree in physical sciences or life sciences **with a Doctorate degree**, or a Master of Engineering or Master of Technology, plus **15 years’ experience in the relevant field**, including **five years’ practical experience in environment and forests**—including pollution control, hazardous-substance management, EIA, climate-change management, biological-diversity management and forest conservation—in a reputed national-level institution |
| Expert Members — administrative route | **15 years’ administrative experience**, including **five years dealing with environmental matters**, in the Central Government, a State Government, or a reputed national- or State-level institution |
| Authorised complement | Not fewer than 10 and not more than 20 full-time Judicial Members, and the same range of full-time Expert Members; the authorised design must not be confused with posts actually filled at a given date |
| Hearing balance | Section 4 requires the number of Expert Members hearing an application or appeal to equal the number of Judicial Members |

Section 4 separately allows the Chairperson, when considered necessary, to invite one or more persons having specialised knowledge and experience in a particular case to assist the Tribunal in that case. The invited specialist assists in that case; the invitation does **not** appoint that person as a Judicial Member or Expert Member, and the Act does not state that the invitee acquires member or voting status.

The mixed composition is functional. Judicial Members determine jurisdiction, procedure, rights and legal consequence; Expert Members help test causation, ecological risk, monitoring design and restoration. Equal judicial–expert participation in the hearing design prevents either legal doctrine or technical inference from standing alone. Expertise does not displace hearing rights, and legal status does not convert an unsupported scientific claim into evidence.

### 3. Benches

Its ordinary sitting structure comprises:

- Principal Bench: New Delhi;
- Central Zone: Bhopal;
- Western Zone: Pune;
- Eastern Zone: Kolkata; and
- Southern Zone: Chennai.

Territorial access remains affected by distance, digital capability, legal assistance and bench capacity.

### 4. Original jurisdiction under Section 14

Section 14 covers civil cases involving a substantial question relating to environment, including enforcement of an environmental legal right, where the question arises from implementation of Schedule I enactments.

The statutory definition includes direct violation of a specific environmental obligation producing community-scale effects, substantial environmental/property damage or broadly measurable public-health damage, and environmental consequences linked to a specific activity or point source.

### 5. Schedule I discipline

The original Schedule named seven enactments:

1. Water Act, 1974;
2. Water Cess Act, 1977;
3. Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980, now titled the Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam, 1980;
4. Air Act, 1981;
5. Environment (Protection) Act, 1986;
6. Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991; and
7. Biological Diversity Act, 2002.

The Water Cess Act was repealed in 2017. Therefore, do not describe all seven as presently operative sources of fresh disputes. The Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 and Indian Forest Act, 1927 are not Schedule I enactments.

An environmental dispute may still involve wildlife facts, but the legal cause before the NGT must fit its statutory jurisdiction or an appeal provision.

### 6. Appellate jurisdiction under Section 16

Section 16 lists appealable orders and decisions under specified environmental laws, including orders granting or refusing environmental clearance under the Environment (Protection) Act framework. The NGT does not possess a free-floating appeal over every administrative act mentioning the environment.

### 7. Standing

Section 18 permits applications by injured persons, property owners, legal representatives, authorised agents, **any person aggrieved including a representative body or organisation**, governments, pollution-control boards, local authorities and specified environmental authorities.

The Supreme Court in *Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai v. Ankita Sinha* (2021) held that the NGT possesses **suo motu** power in discharge of its functions. Natural justice still requires notice and an opportunity to affected parties.

**Objection:** broad standing and suo motu cognisance risk an unelected tribunal entering policy.

**Reply:** environmental harm is diffuse, delayed and underrepresented; broad access prevents the absence of a conventional private plaintiff from defeating statutory protection.

**Residual:** proactive jurisdiction must remain tied to Schedule I, fair hearing, evidence and reasoned remedies.

### UPSC application

The quickest Prelims discriminator is:

```text
CPCB/SPCB = regulator and standard/consent/monitoring body
NGT       = adjudicatory tribunal
```

### Revision notes

1. NGT is a statutory specialised tribunal under the 2010 Act.
2. Its Chairperson must be or have been a Supreme Court Judge or Chief Justice of a High Court.
3. A Judicial Member may use the Supreme Court Judge or High Court Chief Justice route, while the Section 5 proviso also qualifies a serving or former High Court Judge.
4. The technical Expert route requires a Master of Science in physical or life sciences with a Doctorate, or a Master of Engineering or Master of Technology, plus 15 years’ relevant experience including five practical years in environment and forests at a reputed national-level institution.
5. The administrative Expert route requires 15 years’ administrative experience including five years dealing with environmental matters in government or a reputed national/State-level institution.
6. The Act authorises 10–20 full-time Judicial and 10–20 full-time Expert Members; authorised complement is not proof of posts filled.
7. Section 4 requires equal Judicial and Expert numbers at a hearing and separately permits invited specialists to assist a particular case without making them Tribunal Members.
8. Principal Bench is at New Delhi; zonal benches sit at Bhopal, Pune, Kolkata and Chennai.
9. Section 14 requires a civil case and substantial environmental question.
10. The question must arise from Schedule I implementation.
11. The original Schedule listed seven enactments; the Water Cess Act was repealed in 2017.
12. Wildlife Protection Act and Indian Forest Act are not Schedule I laws.
13. Section 16 contains a specific appellate list, including applicable EC orders.
14. Section 18 gives broad representative standing.
15. The Supreme Court recognised NGT suo motu power in 2021, bounded by natural justice and jurisdiction.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why can the NGT not assume jurisdiction merely because a dispute concerns wildlife?

**Model answer:** Environmental subject matter alone is insufficient. Original jurisdiction requires a civil case involving a substantial environmental question arising from implementation of a Schedule I enactment, or the matter must fit a listed appellate route. The Wildlife (Protection) Act is not itself in Schedule I.

**Misconception to avoid:** The NGT is specialised but not a court of unlimited environmental subject-matter jurisdiction.

### Original Mains practice — 10 marks, 150 words

**Mains prompt:** Explain the statutory gateways that prevent the National Green Tribunal from becoming a forum for every environment-related dispute.

**Model:** The NGT pairs Judicial and Expert Members so that legal rights and scientific causation can be assessed together, but specialisation is bounded by statute. Under Section 14, it hears civil cases involving a substantial environmental question only when that question arises from implementation of a Schedule I enactment. Section 16 separately identifies appealable orders, including specified environmental-clearance decisions. Environmental relevance alone is therefore insufficient. The Wildlife (Protection) Act and Indian Forest Act are not Schedule I laws, while the Water Cess Act entry has lost fresh operative significance after repeal. Standing is broad under Section 18, and the Supreme Court has recognised suo motu power, but neither enlarges substantive jurisdiction. The Tribunal must still give notice, follow natural justice and connect every remedy to its mandate.

## Lesson 8 — NGT procedure, limitation, principles, remedies and court relationship

Progress: 8/12 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: How the Tribunal decides and what follows

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Book context: Sections 14–22 of the NGT Act separate disputes, compensation, appeals, procedure, principles and further appeal.

CA search: "official NGT Act limitation appeal Supreme Court environmental principles 2026"

CA found: No material amendment changes the core section map used here.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Section-and-clock board

| Route | Subject | Ordinary limitation | Statutory extension |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Section 14 | environmental dispute | 6 months from first arising of cause | up to 60 days |
| Section 15 | relief, compensation, property/environment restitution | 5 years from first arising of cause | up to 60 days |
| Section 16 | appeal from listed order | 30 days from communication | up to 60 days |
| Section 22 | appeal from NGT to Supreme Court | 90 days from communication | delay may be condoned for sufficient cause |

Limitation is remedy-specific. Never apply the five-year compensation clock to a Section 16 clearance appeal.

### 1. Procedure

The NGT is not bound by the Code of Civil Procedure or the Indian Evidence Act. It is guided by natural justice and may regulate its procedure. It nevertheless possesses specified civil-court powers, including summoning, document production, affidavits, review, interim orders and cease-and-desist directions.

The Act asks the Tribunal to endeavour to dispose of matters within six months. This is an expeditious objective, not a licence to deny fair hearing.

### 2. Section 20 principles

```text
SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT
development and environmental protection integrated within ecological limits

PRECAUTIONARY PRINCIPLE
credible serious risk → preventive action despite incomplete certainty

POLLUTER PAYS
polluter bears prevention, remediation and compensatory environmental cost
```

Polluter Pays is not a fee for permission to pollute. Precaution is not displaced merely because compensation may later be recovered.

### 3. Relief and compensation

Section 15 authorises:

- relief and compensation to victims;
- restitution of damaged property; and
- restitution of the environment.

Section 17 addresses liability for death, injury, property or environmental damage and permits equitable apportionment where several activities combine to cause harm. In accident cases it applies no-fault liability.

Environmental compensation should be linked to restoration, deterrence and proven harm rather than used as an unexplained lump sum.

### 4. Appeal and courts

Section 22 provides appeal to the Supreme Court on grounds specified in Section 100 of the Code of Civil Procedure. This is not an automatic factual rehearing.

Section 29 bars civil-court jurisdiction over matters the NGT may determine. It does not abolish the constitutional judicial-review powers of High Courts under Articles 226 and 227 or the Supreme Court’s constitutional powers. Constitutional courts normally respect the specialised statutory route, but their basic judicial-review role remains.

### 5. Implementation

An NGT order may direct closure, remediation, compensation, monitoring or authority action. Its effectiveness depends on:

- clear operative directions;
- identified implementing agency;
- timeline and reporting;
- scientific measurement;
- budget and administrative capacity; and
- follow-up for non-compliance.

**Objection:** flexible procedure weakens evidentiary rigour.

**Reply:** freedom from strict procedural codes enables scientific and preventive adjudication.

**Residual:** flexibility must not become opacity. Parties need disclosure, an opportunity to contest expert material and intelligible reasons.

### UPSC application

Draw the four-clock table. It prevents the common error of writing “all NGT applications have a six-month limitation.”

2. Section 15 claims carry five years plus up to sixty days.
3. Section 16 appeals carry thirty days plus up to sixty days.
4. Section 22 appeals go to the Supreme Court within ninety days.
5. NGT is not bound by CPC procedure.
6. NGT is not bound by Evidence Act rules.
7. Natural justice remains binding.
8. The Tribunal has specified civil-court powers.
9. Six-month disposal is an endeavour.
10. Section 20 mandates sustainable development, precaution and polluter pays.
11. Section 15 covers victim, property and environmental restitution.

### Concept check

**Question:** A community challenges an environmental-clearance order after four months and asks for compensation for continuing harm. Why must the two requests be analysed separately?

**Model answer:** The clearance challenge is a Section 16 appeal with a 30-day period and a maximum statutory extension of 60 days. Compensation or restitution falls under Section 15 with a five-year period plus possible 60-day extension. One factual dispute can therefore contain legally different remedies and limitation clocks.

**Misconception to avoid:** A longer compensation limitation does not revive a time-barred statutory appeal against the clearance order.

### Original Mains practice — 15 marks, 250 words

**Mains prompt:** Assess how the NGT Act combines procedural flexibility with legal discipline.

**Model:** The NGT is designed for technically complex and urgent environmental disputes. Section 19 frees it from the Code of Civil Procedure and formal Evidence Act rules, enabling affidavits, expert material, interim protection and preventive directions. Yet flexibility is bounded by natural justice, reasoned orders and statutory jurisdiction. Sections 14, 15 and 16 distinguish original disputes, compensation/restoration and listed appeals, each with a different limitation period. Section 20 further disciplines outcomes by requiring sustainable development, precaution and Polluter Pays. Remedies may compensate victims, restore property and environment, stop violations or apportion liability among multiple contributors. Appeals lie to the Supreme Court under Section 22 on Section 100 CPC grounds, while constitutional judicial review remains with High Courts and the Supreme Court. The model’s weakness appears when flexible expert procedure becomes opaque or implementation is left to agencies without timelines and capacity. Disclosure of expert material, hearing rights and monitorable directions are therefore essential.

## Lesson 9 — Cumulative impact, carrying capacity and strategic assessment

Progress: 9/12 | Stage: Advanced | Subtopic: Moving beyond one project inside one boundary

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Book context: Ecosystem function and regional planning expose impacts that project-by-project appraisal can miss.

CA search: "official India cumulative impact carrying capacity strategic environmental assessment 2026"

CA found: No single current event is used; these are enduring analytical extensions.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Four assessment scales

| Tool | Unit of analysis | Central question |
|---|---|---|
| Project EIA | one proposal | What will this project do? |
| Cumulative impact assessment | interacting past, present and foreseeable actions | What will all pressures together do? |
| Carrying-capacity assessment | airshed, watershed, coast, city or ecosystem | What load can the system sustain without losing function? |
| Strategic environmental assessment (SEA) | policy, plan or programme | Which development pathway should be chosen before projects are fixed? |

### 1. Cumulative impact assessment

Suppose a river basin contains five mines. Each proponent models its own abstraction and discharge. The ecosystem, however, experiences the sum of water withdrawal, roads, blasting, dust, sediment, labour colonies and waste. Cumulative assessment asks whether individually conditioned projects create an unacceptable combined burden.

It must consider:

- additive effects;
- synergistic effects, where combined harm exceeds simple addition;
- repeated disturbance over time;
- shared infrastructure and induced development;
- ecological thresholds; and
- distribution of burden across communities.

### 2. Carrying capacity

Carrying capacity is the population, activity or pollution load an environmental system can sustain over time without eroding regenerative, assimilative and supporting functions. It is conditional, not a timeless fixed number.

```text
capacity depends on
resource renewal + waste assimilation + habitat connectivity
+ technology + consumption pattern + governance quality
```

Technology may raise efficiency but cannot abolish ecological limits. An airshed already exceeding health-based pollution levels has little assimilative space for another source.

### 3. Strategic environmental assessment

SEA operates upstream:

```text
ENERGY POLICY / TRANSPORT PLAN / MINING PROGRAMME
        ↓ compare pathways and regional constraints
PROJECT PIPELINE
        ↓
individual EIAs
```

India’s EIA Notification is principally project-based; it does not establish a comprehensive general SEA regime for all policies, plans and programmes. Regional and sectoral studies occur, but they should not be mistaken for a universal statutory SEA system.

### 4. Risk assessment within the wider scale

Project risk may be acceptable in isolation but unacceptable when several hazardous units share a floodplain, emergency route or water source. Cumulative risk assessment therefore examines common-cause events, domino effects and emergency-response capacity.

### 5. Why these tools matter

**Objection:** regional assessment is too uncertain to decide individual projects.

**Reply:** uncertainty grows when interacting projects are ignored. Regional analysis identifies thresholds and no-go constraints that individual EIA cannot derive.

**Residual:** carrying-capacity numbers can be manipulated through chosen boundaries, averaging and optimistic technology assumptions. Methods and uncertainty ranges must be disclosed.

### UPSC application

Use the phrase **“project EIA optimises a proposal; SEA first questions the development pathway.”**

### Revision notes

1. Project EIA studies one proposal.
2. Cumulative assessment studies interacting activities.
3. Effects may be additive, synergistic or repeated.
4. Shared infrastructure and induced development matter.
5. Carrying capacity concerns sustained load without functional decline.
6. Regenerative and assimilative capacities are distinct.
7. Capacity varies with technology, consumption and governance.
8. Technology cannot abolish ecological thresholds.
9. SEA assesses policies, plans and programmes upstream.
10. SEA can compare pathways before project sites are locked.
11. India lacks a comprehensive general statutory SEA regime.
12. Regional studies do not automatically equal SEA.
13. Cumulative risk includes domino and common-cause events.
14. Boundary choice can distort capacity estimates.
15. Uncertainty ranges should accompany threshold claims.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why may five projects that each satisfy an individual pollution standard still exceed an ecosystem’s carrying capacity?

**Model answer:** Individual compliance controls each source, while carrying capacity concerns the receiving system’s total regenerative and assimilative ability. Combined withdrawals, emissions, habitat fragmentation and induced growth may cross a regional threshold even when no single project exceeds its own limit.

**Misconception to avoid:** Source-wise compliance does not prove system-wide sustainability.

### Original Mains practice — 20 marks, 250 words

**Mains prompt:** Compare project EIA, cumulative impact assessment, carrying-capacity assessment and strategic environmental assessment. Why does India need all four?

**Model:** Project EIA asks how one defined proposal will affect its receiving environment and what conditions can make it acceptable. Cumulative impact assessment enlarges the frame to past, present and reasonably foreseeable activities whose combined abstraction, emissions, roads and habitat disturbance may cross a threshold. Carrying-capacity assessment estimates the load an airshed, watershed or ecosystem can sustain without eroding regenerative and assimilative functions; its result varies with technology, consumption and governance but is not infinitely elastic. Strategic environmental assessment works further upstream by comparing policies, plans or programmes before a project pipeline and sites become fixed. India needs all four because a sound mine-level EMP cannot decide whether an entire mineral belt is already saturated, while a regional plan cannot replace site-specific prediction. The EIA Notification remains mainly project-centred and India lacks a comprehensive general SEA regime. Reform should connect regional capacity limits and strategic alternatives to project screening, while disclosing boundaries, assumptions and uncertainty.

**Unique quantified rubric (20):** project EIA 3; cumulative assessment 4; carrying capacity 4; SEA 4; complementarity 3; India-specific qualification 2.

## Lesson 10 — Ex post facto clearance: the current jurisprudence

Progress: 10/12 | Stage: Advanced | Subtopic: Prior-clearance rule, exceptional statutory window and invalid perpetual amnesty

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Book context: The case line distinguishes the mandatory prior-clearance norm, fact-specific relief and the legal source of any exceptional regularisation regime.

CA search: "Supreme Court Vanashakti 29 July 2026 ex post facto environmental clearance"

CA found: **Vanashakti v. Union of India, 29 July 2026 — the Supreme Court upheld the prior-EC rule, sustained the closed 2017 notification and prospectively quashed the open-ended 2021 Office Memorandum.**

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Current-affairs linkage: one judgment, three legal propositions

```text
RULE
Prior EC under EIA Notification, 2006 is mandatory.

NARROW EXCEPTION
A valid, time-bound delegated notification under Section 3 may address
an exceptional closed class in supervening public interest.

INVALID ROUTE
An executive Office Memorandum cannot create a perpetual post-facto amnesty.
```

### 1. Why ex post facto clearance is controversial

Ex post facto clearance means appraisal after a project has begun construction, expanded or operated without the prior EC required by law. It reverses the sequence:

```text
lawful sequence: assess → decide → construct
post-facto sequence: construct → create sunk cost → ask to regularise
```

The second sequence weakens alternatives, public participation and precaution.

### 2. Jurisprudential timeline

| Decision/instrument | Precise contribution |
|---|---|
| *Common Cause v. Union of India* (2017) | Prior EC treated as mandatory in the mining context; ex post facto EC described as alien to the notification framework, while relief was moulded with compensation and compliance directions |
| S.O. 804(E), 14 March 2017 | One-time violation-management window for an existing closed class; applications were time-bound |
| *Alembic Pharmaceuticals v. Rohit Prajapati* (2020) | Executive circular route enabling retrospective clearance held contrary to environmental jurisprudence; existing operations were not mechanically demolished and environmental compensation was ordered |
| *Electrosteel Steels v. Union of India* | Fact-specific exceptional approach to an otherwise permissible project; the 2026 Court rejected its broader reading that the existing Act/notification generally accommodated post-facto EC |
| *Pahwa Plastics* and *D. Swamy* | Fact-specific public-interest/closure reasoning; 2026 judgment approved their treatment of the 2017 notification but overruled them insofar as they treated the 2021 OM as valid |
| *Vanashakti-I*, 16 May 2025 | Struck down the 2017 notification and 2021 OM and restrained future regularisation instruments, while protecting ECs already granted |
| Review judgment, 18 November 2025 | Recalled *Vanashakti-I* by 2:1 and restored the matters |
| *Vanashakti-III*, 29 July 2026 | Final controlling clarification set out below |

### 3. Controlling position after 29 July 2026

The Supreme Court held:

1. prior EC under the 2006 Notification is mandatory unless lawfully amended;
2. *Common Cause* and *Alembic* reflected the statutory context then governing and do not disable all valid delegated amendment;
3. the 2017 notification is valid, narrowly tailored, time-bound delegated legislation traceable to Section 3 of the Environment (Protection) Act read with Section 21 of the General Clauses Act;
4. the 2021 OM is an administrative order creating a perpetual regime, impermissibly supplanting delegated legislation and failing proportionality under Articles 14 and 21;
5. the 2021 OM was quashed prospectively;
6. ECs already granted under the 2017 notification or 2021 OM remain valid unless independently challenged;
7. pending and specified previously rejected applications are to be dealt with under the judgment’s directions;
8. no fresh applications under those instruments may be entertained;
9. the Union cannot create future ex post facto clearance through administrative orders; any exceptional regime requires a valid Section 3 notification; and
10. the Supreme Court’s Article 142 power in an appropriate case was expressly preserved.

### 4. The correct exam formulation

Do not write either extreme:

- “Post-facto clearance is always lawful if compensation is paid.”
- “The Supreme Court has imposed an absolute constitutional ban incapable of any statutory exception.”

Write instead:

> Prior EC remains the mandatory norm. Routine or perpetual administrative regularisation is invalid. A narrowly tailored, time-bound exception for a closed class may be created only through valid delegated legislation in supervening public interest, subject to precaution, proportionality, remediation and accountability.

**Objection:** any exception rewards deliberate violators.

**Reply:** the 2026 judgment limits the exception’s legal form, class and purpose and preserves penalties, remediation and merits challenges.

**Residual:** distinguishing a genuine supervening public interest from economic pressure created by illegal construction remains the hardest future issue.

### UPSC application

Always date the proposition. A 2025 statement that both instruments were struck down became incomplete after review and was superseded by the 29 July 2026 final clarification.

### Revision notes

1. Prior EC remains the mandatory rule.
2. Post-facto appraisal loses the preventive decision window.
3. *Common Cause* and *Alembic* remain central prior-clearance authorities.
4. The 2017 notification was a closed, time-bound delegated measure.
5. The 2021 OM created an open-ended administrative route.
6. *Vanashakti-I* struck down both instruments in May 2025.
7. The November 2025 review recalled that judgment by majority.
8. *Vanashakti-III* was decided on 29 July 2026.
9. It upheld the 2017 notification.
10. It prospectively quashed the 2021 OM.
11. Existing ECs remain open to independent merits challenge.
12. Pending applications receive the judgment’s protected treatment.
13. No fresh applications under the two instruments may be entertained.
14. Future executive OMs cannot create post-facto EC.
15. A valid, narrow Section 3 notification remains legally possible.

### Concept check

**Question:** After the 29 July 2026 judgment, is payment of environmental compensation sufficient authority to regularise any project started without prior EC?

**Model answer:** No. Compensation addresses consequences; it does not itself create legal authority for clearance. Prior EC remains mandatory. Any exceptional future regime must be created through a valid, narrowly tailored and time-bound Section 3 notification, not an administrative office memorandum.

**Misconception to avoid:** Polluter Pays repairs liability; it does not automatically validate the underlying unlawful commencement.

### Original Mains practice — 15 marks, 250 words

**Mains prompt:** Trace the evolution of Supreme Court doctrine on ex post facto environmental clearance and state the law after *Vanashakti* (2026).

**Model:** *Common Cause* and *Alembic Pharmaceuticals* treated prior environmental clearance as integral to precaution and described retrospective clearance as alien to the governing notification framework, although each Court moulded relief rather than ordering automatic demolition. The 2017 notification then created a one-time, closed violation-management window, while the 2021 Office Memorandum established a continuing administrative route. *Vanashakti-I* struck down both in May 2025, but a majority review recalled that judgment in November. On 29 July 2026, *Vanashakti-III* restored doctrinal precision: prior EC remains mandatory; the 2017 notification survives as narrowly tailored, time-bound delegated legislation under Section 3; the 2021 OM is prospectively quashed because an executive instruction cannot create a perpetual amnesty that supplants delegated legislation. Existing clearances and protected pending applications receive specified treatment, but no fresh application under those instruments is permitted. The result rejects routine regularisation without imposing an absolute bar on a valid, exceptional statutory scheme serving supervening public interest.

### 1. Fragmented responsibility

A major project may involve environmental clearance, forest approval, wildlife approval, pollution consent, land acquisition, local planning and rights recognition. Specialisation is useful, but fragmented files can produce contradictory assumptions and no single cumulative picture.

### 2. Capacity and workload

EACs, SEACs, SEIAAs, pollution boards, regional offices and NGT benches need scientific staff, legal support, field capacity and continuity. A vacancy or overloaded committee can cause delay; rushed appraisal can cause weak scrutiny. Speed and rigour are not opposites when early completeness checks and stable expertise exist.

### 3. Information asymmetry

The proponent knows the project best and pays the consultant. Regulators may lack raw data or resources for independent replication. Communities may know the landscape but lack technical assistance. Open data and adversarial scrutiny reduce this imbalance.

### 4. Clearance-condition inflation

Long lists of conditions can create the appearance of protection while dispersing responsibility. Conditions should be:

- few enough to monitor;
- specific to the predicted impact;
- assigned to an authority and project officer;
- connected to measurable triggers; and
- backed by consequences.

### 5. Tribunal-to-ground gap

NGT’s specialised orders still depend on ministries, State departments, pollution boards, district administrations, local bodies and project operators. Repeated committees and status reports may be necessary, but they also reveal an implementation system that activates only after litigation.

### 6. Access and remedy

Short appeal limitation, distance from benches, technical costs and unequal legal capacity may burden affected communities. At the same time, indefinite challenge would undermine legal certainty. Early disclosure, local assistance and prompt grievance channels are better than simply extending every limitation period.

**Objection:** the principal problem is excessive environmental litigation.

**Reply:** some litigation reflects poor initial appraisal, non-disclosure or non-compliance. Better first-instance decisions reduce both ecological harm and litigation.

**Residual:** litigation can also delay compliant projects or be strategically used. Cost orders against false or vexatious claims and transparent early records help separate accountability from obstruction.

### UPSC application

```text
INFORMATION + EXPERTISE + FIELD ENFORCEMENT + REMEDIAL FOLLOW-THROUGH
```

**Question:** Why can adding more clearance conditions reduce rather than improve environmental accountability?

**Model answer:** Numerous generic conditions diffuse attention and responsibility. Inspectors cannot verify them all, proponents cannot identify priority triggers and violations become difficult to prove. A smaller set of impact-linked, measurable, assigned and consequence-backed conditions is more enforceable.

### Original Mains practice — 10 marks, 150 words

**Mains prompt:** Why does specialised environmental adjudication not by itself guarantee environmental compliance?

**Model:** The NGT can interpret law, apply environmental principles and order compensation, closure or restoration, but implementation remains executive. Pollution boards must sample and enforce; district and local authorities must act on waste, land and public-health directions; departments must fund restoration; and project operators must change conduct. Weak staffing, fragmented mandates, vague orders, poor baseline data and self-reported compliance can therefore dilute a legally sound judgment. Repeated monitoring committees may improve follow-through but also show that routine administration failed before litigation. Specialised adjudication is necessary because it supplies expert, reasoned remedies, yet it must be connected to named implementing agencies, measurable milestones, public reporting and consequences for default. Environmental justice is complete only when the ecological condition changes on the ground.

### Reform architecture: improve the weakest decision point

```text
UPSTREAM
SEA + regional carrying capacity + transparent project pipeline
       ↓
ENTRY
location-sensitive screening + complete applications
       ↓
ASSESSMENT
independent baseline + alternatives + cumulative and risk analysis
       ↓
PARTICIPATION
local-language disclosure + technical assistance + response matrix
       ↓
DECISION
reasoned appraisal + few measurable conditions
       ↓
COMPLIANCE

       ↓

accessible NGT + restoration science + tracked implementation
```

### 1. Faster without weaker

Time can be saved by:

- rejecting incomplete applications early;
- standardising data formats and geospatial disclosure;
- maintaining stable sectoral expert panels;
- deciding ToR and clarification together where possible;
- using parallel inter-agency data exchange; and
- publishing reasoned decisions promptly.

Time should not be saved by eliminating material alternatives, baseline seasons, consultation or expert scrutiny.

### 2. Better evidence

Create interoperable public datasets for air, water, biodiversity, land and compliance. Require geotagged raw data, laboratory audit trails and disclosure of model assumptions. Independent public funding for critical baseline verification can reduce exclusive reliance on the proponent.

```text
concern raised → evidence supplied → proponent reply
→ committee finding → design/condition change or reason for rejection
```

This makes participation decision-relevant without converting appraisal into a vote count.

National rules should secure minimum safeguards and data standards. State institutions should retain contextual appraisal, but capacity support, transparent appointments, common digital records and conflict protocols are necessary. Local governments and Gram Sabhas should be involved according to their distinct statutory mandates.

### 5. Better compliance and justice

Use risk-based inspection frequency, automatic alerts for critical indicators, independent sampling and restoration escrow where long-term liability exists. NGT orders should name the implementing agency, ecological endpoint, budget source, timeline and verification method.

| Reform goal | Guardrail |
|---|---|
| Speed | no loss of material evidence or hearing |
| Digitalisation | raw data and reasons, not portal-only opacity |
| Third-party expertise | independence, rotation and sanctions |
| State flexibility | national minimum safeguards |
| Compensation | no substitution for prior permission |
| Adaptive management | no licence to approve an unsuitable site |

These measures reduce administrative repetition while preserving baseline quality, alternatives, consultation, appraisal and enforceable monitoring.

**Misconception to avoid:** Streamlining means removing redundant delay, not removing the decision-relevant evidence that justifies prior clearance.

### Original Mains practice — 20 marks, 250 words

**Mains prompt:** Design a reform package that makes India’s EIA–NGT system simultaneously preventive, participatory, federal and enforceable.

**Model:** Reform should start before individual applications. Strategic assessment and regional carrying-capacity studies can identify no-go areas and cumulative limits, reducing later conflict. At project entry, location-sensitive screening and strict completeness checks should replace repeated document exchange. Scoping must require representative baseline data, genuine alternatives, accident risk and cumulative effects. Geotagged raw data, model assumptions and laboratory trails should be public. Consultation needs local-language material, accessible venues and an issue-response matrix showing how concerns changed design or conditions. National rules should protect minimum safeguards, while capable SEIAAs and SEACs provide contextual appraisal; local bodies and Gram Sabhas must perform their separate statutory roles. Clearances should contain a limited set of measurable obligations with officers, budgets and correction triggers. Risk-based inspections, independent sampling and restoration finance should support compliance. NGT orders should identify the implementing agency, ecological endpoint and verification schedule. Such a system is faster because it gives early certainty, not because it postpones scrutiny until after construction.

**Unique quantified rubric (20):** upstream tools 3; screening/scoping 3; evidence integrity 3; participation 3; federal/local design 3; compliance/NGT execution 3; speed-with-rigour verdict 2.

## Check 1 — Sequence

**Question:** Arrange the decision logic: appraisal, baseline, scoping, mitigation, screening.

**Model answer:** Screening → scoping → baseline → mitigation design after prediction/alternatives → appraisal. The exact statutory four-stage list is screening, scoping, public consultation and appraisal, while baseline, prediction, alternatives and mitigation occur within the EIA study framed by scoping.

## Check 2 — Public role

**Question:** A proponent says that because an Entry 8 township is B1, a public hearing is automatically required. Identify the error.

**Model answer:** B1 ordinarily signals an EIA report, but public consultation has separate exemptions. Entry 8 follows a special route and ordinary consultation does not automatically follow merely from the B1 label.

## Check 3 — NGT clock

**Question:** Distinguish the limitation periods for a clearance appeal and a compensation claim.

**Model answer:** A Section 16 clearance appeal ordinarily has 30 days from communication, extendable by up to 60 days. A Section 15 compensation or restitution claim ordinarily has five years from when the cause first arose, extendable by up to 60 days.

## Check 4 — Post-facto law

**Question:** State the narrow difference between the valid 2017 notification and invalid 2021 OM after *Vanashakti-III*.

**Model answer:** The 2017 measure was time-bound delegated legislation addressing a closed class; the 2021 OM was an administrative instruction creating a perpetual route that supplanted the prior-clearance framework.

## Check 5 — Draft versus current law

**Question:** Name three Draft EIA 2020 proposals that must not be stated as operative merely because they appeared in S.O. 1199(E).

**Model answer:** Examples include prior Environment Permission for specified non-appraised B2 projects, a 20-day public-hearing notice period, annual default compliance reporting, the draft’s standing violation-processing route and its proposed validity ceilings. ## Check 6 — State-level continuity

**Question:** Distinguish SEIAA/SEAC from SAEIA/SCEIA after S.O. 3862(E).

**Model answer:** SEIAA decides and SEAC appraises Category B proposals in the regular State structure. # ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS MODEL PRACTICE

## 10 marks — EIA and NGT as a pair

**Question:** Explain why EIA and the NGT should be understood as complementary rather than interchangeable environmental institutions. Answer in 150 words.

**Model:** EIA is primarily preventive. Before a scheduled project begins, it identifies baseline conditions, predicts impacts, compares alternatives, incorporates public concerns and enables MoEFCC or SEIAA to grant, condition or reject prior environmental clearance. The NGT is adjudicatory. Under the NGT Act, it hears qualifying environmental disputes and listed appeals, applies sustainable development, precaution and Polluter Pays, and may award compensation or restitution. EIA cannot finally adjudicate a legal challenge to its own clearance; the NGT does not ordinarily prepare the proponent’s baseline or choose the project design. They interact when a clearance is challenged, conditions are violated or environmental damage requires relief. Their common weakness is implementation: vague EIA conditions generate litigation, while an NGT order without capable executive follow-through may not restore the environment. Effective governance therefore requires credible prevention and enforceable correction.

**Unique quantified rubric (10):** EIA role 3; NGT role 3; interaction 2; implementation qualification 2.

## 15 marks — Participation and expertise

**Question:** “Public participation improves environmental expertise rather than displacing it.” Discuss in the context of EIA. Answer in 250 words.

**Model:** EIA combines scientific prediction with knowledge of the affected landscape. Experts model emissions, hydrology, habitat and accident risk, but residents may identify seasonal streams, flood routes, grazing areas, fisheries, sacred sites and health patterns that a short survey misses. The statutory hearing and written-response process can therefore correct baseline gaps, expose distributional impacts and test whether mitigation is practical. Participation becomes technically useful only when documents are timely, intelligible and locally accessible; vulnerable groups can speak freely; and appraisal records an issue-wise response. It does not mean that every local preference is ecologically sound or that a hearing is automatically a veto. Consent or rights determination may arise separately under forest-rights, Scheduled Area or land-acquisition law. Conversely, experts cannot dismiss place-based evidence merely because it is not presented as a model. Independent technical assistance, open raw data and reasoned committee treatment can connect both forms of knowledge. The correct relationship is complementary: science tests causal claims, while participation reveals receptors, lived exposure and values that determine significance.

**Unique quantified rubric (15):** scientific role 3; local knowledge 3; procedural quality 3; legal boundary 2; mutual critique 2; institutional reform and verdict 2.

## 20 marks — Prevention, regularisation and remedy

**Question:** Critically examine the proposition that environmental compensation can reconcile development with projects commenced without prior environmental clearance. Answer in 250 words.

**Model:** Environmental compensation is necessary but insufficient. Polluter Pays can finance remediation, compensate victims and prevent the public from bearing private environmental cost. It also allows courts to mould relief where automatic demolition would create greater harm. Yet compensation cannot recreate the preventive choices lost when construction begins without prior EIA: another site, technology, scale or no-project option may no longer be realistic. It may also convert illegality into a calculable business expense. The Supreme Court’s 29 July 2026 *Vanashakti* judgment preserves this distinction. Prior EC remains mandatory; the closed, time-bound 2017 delegated notification survives, but the perpetual 2021 administrative OM was prospectively quashed. Existing and protected pending cases receive specified treatment, while no fresh applications under those instruments are allowed. Any future exceptional regime must come through a valid, narrowly tailored Section 3 notification serving supervening public interest. Compensation should therefore accompany, not replace, legality, remediation, merits scrutiny and deterrence. Development and environmental protection are reconciled by prior choice and accountable exceptions, not routine retrospective pricing.

**Unique quantified rubric (20):** compensation value 3; preventive deficit 4; business-cost critique 2; 2026 doctrine 5; exceptional-regime safeguards 3; balanced verdict 3.

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

## Preventive core

- EIA is a decision-forcing process before irreversible commitment.
- The key logic is `source → pathway → receptor → significance → decision`.
- Prior timing preserves alternatives; retrospective study cannot restore the lost choice set.
- Precaution addresses credible serious risk despite incomplete certainty.
- Compensation and restoration do not always reproduce lost ecological function.

## Legal architecture as at 3 October 2026

- EIA Notification, 2006, S.O. 1533(E), remains the general operative framework as amended.
- Draft EIA 2020 is not the governing replacement.
- Draft S.O. 1199(E) split B2 into EMP-based prior-EC and non-appraised prior-EP routes, proposed a 20-day hearing notice, annual default reporting and a continuing violation process.
- Its violation clause required closure recommendation where the site or operation was impermissible or unsustainable; otherwise it required damage assessment, remediation and natural/community-resource augmentation.
- Against the 2020 comparator of 30 years for mining, 10 years for river-valley projects and 7 years for other projects, the draft proposed construction/installation windows of up to 50 years/project life for mining, 15 years for river-valley/irrigation/nuclear projects and 10 years for other projects.
- Schedule and thresholds decide coverage.
- Category A: MoEFCC decision on EAC recommendation.
- Category B: SEIAA decision on SEAC recommendation.
- B1 requires EIA; B2 follows the screened reduced route.
- S.O. 3862(E) gives EACs, SEIAAs and SEACs constituted after 13 July 2026 four-year tenure; earlier bodies retain their original three-year term.
- The former additional extension of up to twelve months was removed; State reconstitution must begin six months before expiry.
- SAEIA and SCEIA temporarily discharge the functions of unavailable SEIAA and SEAC under the activation rules in S.O. 3863(E).
- The fallback is six months initially, extendable once by up to six months; it does not erase the substantive appraisal stages.
- SAEIA's notified offices are: Chief Secretary or Advisor to the Administrator or Lt. Governor as Chairperson; Principal Chief Conservator of Forest (Head of Forest Force) as Member; and an Environment Department Special Secretary, Joint Secretary or Deputy Secretary as Member Secretary.
- SCEIA's notified offices are: Environment Department Administrative Secretary as Chairperson; Chief Wildlife Warden; Director General or Director, State Health Services; Member Secretary of the State Pollution Control Board or Union territory Pollution Control Committee; the Director or Principal of an Indian Institute of Technology or National Institute of Technology located in the State/UT or their nominee as required, with a domain expert nominated by the Director of the National Environmental Engineering Research Institute where neither institution exists; and an Environment Department Special Secretary, Joint Secretary or Deputy Secretary as Member Secretary.
- The SCEIA Chairperson may co-opt expert(s) or institutions of repute to assist in particular matters; this does not alter the notified composition.
- General Condition elevates only entries to which it applies.
- It does not apply to entries 8(a) and 8(b).
- Entry 8(a): at least 20,000 and below 150,000 square metres built-up area.
- Entry 8(b): at least 50 hectares and/or at least 150,000 square metres; EIA and B1 appraisal.
- Supreme Court quashed the 2025 Note exempting industrial sheds, schools, colleges and educational hostels.
- Statute outranks notification; notification outranks inconsistent OM/circular.

## Study design

- Screening asks whether a Category B project is B1 or B2.
- Scoping creates the ToR.
- Baseline is the pre-project counterfactual.
- Representative data require correct season, station, duration and frequency.
- Traceability requires methods, units, laboratories, raw records and detection limits.
- Prediction covers direct, indirect, induced, cumulative, accident and closure impacts.
- Significance combines magnitude with receptor sensitivity.

## Alternatives, mitigation and risk

- Alternatives: no-project, site, technology, scale, layout and timing.
- Hierarchy: avoid → minimise → restore → offset residual harm.
- EMP: impact, measure, officer, budget, indicator, frequency, disclosure and trigger.
- Risk assessment: hazard × likelihood × exposure × consequence.
- Routine pollution assessment and catastrophic-risk assessment are complementary.

## Participation and rights

- Consultation ordinarily combines local hearing and written responses.
- Minimum notice and intelligible disclosure are essential.
- Material concerns must receive an issue-wise response.
- Consultation is not universal.
- Hearing is not automatically consent.
- Forest, wildlife, land, pollution-consent and tribal-rights processes remain legally distinct.
- Gram Sabha authority depends on the applicable statute and territorial context.
- Local knowledge can reveal seasonal and livelihood evidence missed by consultants.
- Mopa: disclosure/appraisal defects led to suspension and fresh expert review, not permanent cancellation.
- Niyamgiri: Gram Sabhas determined forest, cultural and religious rights before the forest-clearance decision.
- POSCO: segmented 4-MTPA appraisal of a proposed 12-MTPA complex led to suspension and full-project reconsideration.
- Kudremukh: protected-area litigation produced a controlled phase-out, end-2005 closure and reclamation.

## Appraisal and monitoring

- EAC/SEAC recommends; MoEFCC/SEIAA decides.
- Appraisal tests ToR compliance, data, alternatives, consultation and enforceable conditions.
- Accredited consultants must work within approved scope.
- Accreditation does not guarantee truth or independence.
- Material concealment or false data can support rejection/cancellation after due process.
- Proponent remains responsible.
- Half-yearly compliance reports are public.
- Monitoring needs independent checks and corrective triggers.
- PARIVESH—Pro-Active and Responsive facilitation by Interactive, Virtuous and Environmental Single-window Hub—supports common application data, approval guidance, GIS decision support, workflow tracking and compliance management.
- PARIVESH does not change legal thresholds or replace hearing and appraisal.
- Environment Audit Rules, 2025 took effect through S.O. 3973(E), 29 August 2025.
- EADA certifies, registers and monitors REAs; registration is valid for five years subject to renewal.
- REAs may audit, sample, verify self-compliance and report violations; random assignment applies to allocated audits.
- Third-party audit supplements rather than replaces government inspection and enforcement.

## NGT jurisdiction and institution

- Statutory tribunal under NGT Act, 2010.
- Chairperson: serving or former Supreme Court Judge, or serving or former Chief Justice of a High Court.
- Judicial Member: the Supreme Court Judge or High Court Chief Justice routes in Section 5(1), plus the proviso's serving-or-former High Court Judge route.
- Technical Expert Member: Master of Science in physical or life sciences with a Doctorate, or Master of Engineering or Master of Technology, plus 15 years’ relevant experience including five practical years in environment and forests at a reputed national-level institution.
- Administrative Expert Member: 15 years’ administrative experience including five years dealing with environmental matters in the Union/State Government or a reputed national/State-level institution.
- The Act authorises 10–20 full-time Judicial Members and 10–20 full-time Expert Members; actual vacancies are a separate capacity question.
- An application or appeal must be heard by equal numbers of Judicial and Expert Members.
- Section 4 permits the Chairperson to invite specialised persons to assist in a particular case; an invitee is not thereby appointed a Member or granted voting status.
- Principal Bench Delhi; zonal benches Bhopal, Pune, Kolkata and Chennai.
- Section 14: civil case + substantial environmental question + Schedule I implementation.
- Section 16: appeals from specifically listed orders.
- Original Schedule named seven enactments; Water Cess Act was repealed in 2017.
- Wildlife Protection Act and Indian Forest Act are not Schedule I enactments.
- Section 18 gives broad standing, including representative bodies.
- *Ankita Sinha* (2021) recognises suo motu power subject to natural justice.

## NGT clocks, principles and remedies

- Section 14: 6 months + up to 60 days.
- Section 15: 5 years + up to 60 days.
- Section 16: 30 days + up to 60 days.
- Section 22: Supreme Court appeal within 90 days; sufficient-cause condonation available.
- NGT is not bound by CPC or Evidence Act, but follows natural justice.
- Section 20: sustainable development, precautionary principle and Polluter Pays.
- Relief: victim compensation, property restitution and environmental restitution.
- Combined harm can be equitably apportioned.
- Civil-court bar does not extinguish constitutional judicial review.

## Advanced regional tools

- Cumulative assessment studies interacting activities.
- Carrying capacity measures sustained load without functional decline.
- SEA compares policies, plans and programmes before projects are fixed.
- India’s EIA regime is mainly project-based; no comprehensive general SEA statute exists.
- Regional assessment and project EIA cannot substitute for each other.

## Ex post facto law after 29 July 2026

- Prior EC is mandatory.
- 2017 notification: valid narrow, time-bound delegated legislation.
- 2021 OM: invalid perpetual administrative route; quashed prospectively.
- Existing ECs remain valid unless independently challenged.
- Protected pending applications continue under judgment directions.
- No fresh applications under those instruments.
- Future post-facto route cannot be created by administrative order.
- A valid, narrow Section 3 notification remains legally possible in exceptional supervening public interest.
- Supreme Court Article 142 power remains preserved.

## Reform spine

- SEA and carrying-capacity evidence upstream.
- Location-sensitive screening and complete applications.
- Representative, open and geotagged baseline data.
- Genuine alternatives and cumulative/risk assessment.
- Local-language participation with technical assistance.
- Reasoned appraisal and few measurable conditions.
- Risk-based inspections and restoration finance.
- NGT orders with agency, endpoint, budget, timeline and verification.
- Faster decisions through certainty and evidence, not weaker scrutiny.
