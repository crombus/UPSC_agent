# Sports Governance, Institutions and Major Tournaments — Live Session Edition

## Roadmap

Who may make a sporting rule, who can exclude an athlete, and who corrects an unlawful exclusion? Follow the athlete's path from a national federation to an international event, then test the institutions that are meant to protect that path. The statutory transition is taught **after** the Indian institutional map; tournament entry is taught **after** the distinction between eligibility and selection. The first eight lessons complete the Basic foundation before the optional advanced governance critique.

```text
Olympic Movement / IOC ─── Olympic Games framework
                  │
International federation ─── sport-specific international rules
                  │
National Olympic committee / national sport federation
                  │
Tournament organiser ─── event-specific entry and conduct
                  │
Athlete ─── eligibility → selection → entry → participation
        ╲ WADA Code / NADA India (integrity)
         ╲ internal review / applicable tribunal / CAS (remedy)
```

*The horizontal integrity and remedy paths do not replace the vertical chain of sport administration.*

Imagine an Indian athlete who satisfies a sport's technical rules but is not selected by the national federation. Asking the International Olympic Committee (IOC) to select her confuses the levels. ✅ The IOC governs the Olympic Movement and Games; the relevant international federation frames sport-specific international rules; a **national sports federation (NSF)** administers its sport nationally, and an event organiser applies its competition's entry rules. Neither international recognition nor a government grant automatically gives a body control of another layer. The **Indian Olympic Association (IOA)** is India's National Olympic Committee, not a universal sport federation; an event host does not become the sole owner of its sport.

The sequence is authority → published rule → decision → affected athlete → available review. A rule may be international, but its implementation may be national: a federation's exclusion from a selection list raises a different issue from an organiser's refusal of event entry. The **World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA)** supplies the common anti-doping code; India's **National Anti-Doping Agency (NADA)** is part of the implementation system; the **Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS)** adjudicates disputes within its jurisdiction. ❌ Neither WADA nor CAS organises the Olympic Games.

Major multi-sport events add a second institutional chain:

| Event family | Governing rights-holder | Delivery distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Olympic Games | **IOC** | A host organising committee delivers the edition; international federations govern their sports |
| Paralympic Games | **IPC** | Paralympic governance and classification remain distinct even where host delivery is shared |
| Asian Games | **Olympic Council of Asia (OCA)** | OCA governs the event; the host organising committee stages the awarded edition |
| Commonwealth Games | **Commonwealth Sport** | Commonwealth Sport governs the Games; the host body delivers under the host arrangement |

```text
rights-holder's host process
          ↓
national / city bid and public guarantees
          ↓
host award ≠ ownership of every sport
          ↓
organising committee: venues + operations + accessibility + security
          ↓
legacy audit: use + maintenance + access + fiscal/environmental burden
```

A successful bid is not a completed legacy. New stadia can become under-used liabilities if maintenance, community access, transport and para-accessibility are not planned before construction. A host can instead create value through reusable venues, trained officials and wider participation. Ask who awards, who guarantees, who delivers and who bears the post-event cost.

**Indian example:** An athlete may obtain domestic federation selection yet fail a tournament's qualifying route. This is an illustrative sequence, not an allegation about a named athlete. ⚠️ The attractive objection is that one supreme authority would make accountability easier. **Reply:** a central body cannot simultaneously supply each sport's technical rules, every national selection decision and each event's entry conditions without losing specialist competence. The accountability problem is therefore to publish the *decision-maker at each step* and a corresponding remedy, not to pretend the chain is unitary.

**UPSC use:** For an institution-matching stem, name the function before the name of the body. For GS-II, separate international autonomy, national recognition, event control, integrity and adjudication. Trap: treating the IOC as the rule-maker of every sport.

**Revision:**

1. IOC governs the Olympic Movement and Olympic Games framework.
2. International federations govern the technical rules of their own sports.
3. An NSF governs one sport nationally; an organiser controls a particular event.
4. OCA governs the Asian Games; Commonwealth Sport governs the Commonwealth Games.
5. IPC governance and para-sport classification are distinct from ordinary Olympic administration.
6. Host award, organising-committee delivery and sport ownership are separate.
7. Test legacy through use, maintenance, access and fiscal/environmental burden.
8. WADA writes the Code; NADA implements the Indian integrity layer.
9. CAS is an arbitral forum, not a tournament organiser.
10. Recognition, selection, admission and participation are not synonyms.

### Concept check

**Question:** A player is selected by an Indian federation but is not accepted into an international event. Identify the two distinct decisions that must be checked.

**Model answer:** Check the federation's selection decision and the event organiser's entry rules separately. Domestic selection is not a guarantee of acceptance under the international event's ranking, qualifying or eligibility criteria.

**Misconception to avoid:** Selection by one body automatically binds every organiser.

### Original Mains practice — 10 marks, 150 words

**Mains prompt:** Distinguish the governance roles of an international federation, an Indian national federation and an event organiser. Why does the distinction matter to an excluded athlete? Answer in 150 words.

**Model (110 words):** Sporting authority is layered, not delegated wholesale to one administrator. An international federation sets a sport's common rules; a recognised Indian national federation administers the sport and makes national decisions; an organiser applies the entry and conduct rules of its event. For example, a player selected nationally may still need ranking acceptance or qualification for a tournament. The athlete must identify which decision excluded her: selection criteria require a reasoned federation review, whereas event admission requires examination of the organiser's rule and its appeal route. WADA's integrity standards and a possible CAS appeal are additional, not substitute, layers. Technical autonomy is defensible only where the decision-maker and remedy are identifiable.

**Scoring (10):** accurate institutional distinctions 4; applied two-decision example 3; proportionate remedy and qualification 3.

| Indian actor | Legal/organisational character | What it does not become |
|---|---|---|
| Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports | Union executive ministry; policy, funding and recognition framework | National team selector for every event |
| Sports Authority of India (SAI) | Autonomous society registered under the Societies Registration Act, 1860; training and athlete support | Statutory corporation under the 2025 Act |
| IOA | Private National Olympic Committee within Olympic Movement | Ministry or all-sport technical federation |
| NSFs | Sport-specific associations subject to applicable recognition framework | Government departments merely because recognised |
| BCCI | Cricket governing body, with distinct legal/recognition questions | Automatically a statutory NSF |
| State associations | Federated domestic sporting layer | Automatically reformed by a Union notification |
| State governments/local bodies | Land, schools, local facilities, State competitions and implementation | Passive recipients of a Union scheme |
| Private academies/CSR partners | Supplement coaching, facilities, technology or finance under applicable terms | Substitutes for public standards, access and audit |

*Form, recognition and function are three separate questions: a publicly funded private association need not be a government office.*

An athlete trains at a SAI facility, is considered by an NSF for national selection and may participate in an Olympic event through arrangements involving the IOA. These are cooperating bodies, not interchangeable desks in one ministry. ✅ SAI's society form is different from NADA's statutory status under the National Anti-Doping Act, 2022; a statute regulating national sports bodies does not retroactively turn SAI into a statutory corporation. Recognition of an association is a conditional public relationship; incorporation tells you its form, not whether its decisions are fair.

```text
school / community / State competition
                 ↓
transparent talent identification
                 ↓
district-State-national progression
                 ↓
coaching + sports science + nutrition + competition exposure
                 ↓
NSF / SAI / academy pathway
                 ↓
senior performance, education and post-career support
```

Talent identification needs published, repeated opportunities rather than one urban trial. Infrastructure is a **service system**, not a construction total: creation must be followed by trained staff, equipment, maintenance, safe access and regular use. Union institutions can set policy and support programmes; States and local bodies control much of the school, land and facility interface; NSFs provide sport-specific pathways; private academies, leagues and CSR can add capacity but require transparent access, safeguarding and conflict controls.

Inclusion changes design. Women's participation needs safe facilities and complaint channels; rural, tribal and low-income athletes need reachable trials and continuing support; para-sport needs accessible venues, appropriate equipment, trained coaches and legitimate classification. Merely allowing a person with disability into a common trial does not create an equal sporting pathway.

⚠️ **Why regulation is needed:** ordinarily an unhappy consumer can switch provider. An elite athlete often cannot switch to a rival nationally recognised federation for the same sport. A private body's decisions thus have public consequences for access to competition and livelihood. **Objection:** treating an NSF as answerable invites the ministry to decide who plays. **Reply:** government may demand accounts, fair elections and reasons without writing a coach's tactical decision; scrutiny of process differs from replacement of sporting judgment.

**UPSC use:** First identify form (ministry/society/private association/statutory body), then authority (recognition/contract/charter/statute), and finally function. ❌ Do not call IOA or SAI a 2025-Act statutory regulator. Do not claim BCCI has the identical government-recognition status of every other sport federation.

**Revision:**

1. MYAS supplies Union policy, funding and recognition architecture.
2. SAI is a registered autonomous society delivering training and athlete support.
3. IOA is India's National Olympic Committee; an NSF is sport-specific.
4. BCCI's legal and recognition position must be identified rather than assumed.
5. States and local bodies matter for schools, land, venues and local competitions.
6. Talent identification must connect scouting to sustained coaching and competition exposure.
7. Infrastructure means usable, staffed, maintained and accessible facilities.
8. Private academies, leagues and CSR supplement capacity but not access, audit or safeguarding.
9. Inclusion covers women, rural/tribal/low-income athletes and persons with disabilities.
10. Para-sport needs accessibility, equipment, coaching and classification.
11. Public funding creates accountability but does not determine corporate form.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why does financial support from the Union not by itself settle whether SAI and a sports federation have the same legal status?

**Model answer:** Funding describes a relationship, not corporate form. SAI is a registered society under the ministry; an NSF is a sport-specific association whose recognition and duties must be separately established.

**Misconception to avoid:** “Public money makes every recipient a statutory corporation” mistakes oversight for incorporation.

### Original Mains practice — 10 marks, 150 words

**Mains prompt:** Explain why legal character, recognition and public funding should be separated when evaluating Indian sports bodies. Answer in 150 words.

**Model (110 words):** Legal character identifies how a body exists; recognition identifies the framework under which it represents a sport; funding brings additional public-accountability expectations. SAI is an autonomous registered society, whereas NADA has statutory standing under the 2022 anti-doping law. An NSF may be a private association yet hold a practically exclusive pathway to national representation. Consequently, neither calling an NSF “private” removes the case for transparent selection, nor does a grant convert it into a ministry. The IOA's place in the Olympic Movement adds charter obligations, without displacing applicable national law. Reform should attach published conditions and review to the actual power exercised while leaving technical selection to competent sporting decision-makers.

**Scoring (10):** three distinctions 4; two accurate Indian examples 3; autonomy/accountability conclusion 3.

```text
2025: Bill → Parliament → assent (18 August; Act 25 of 2025)
                                  ↓
1 January 2026: specified sections commence [S.O. 6152(E)]
                                  ↓
12 May 2026: additional specified sections [S.O. 2406(E)]
                                  ↓
May 2026: subordinate rules published ≠ every substantive power commenced
                                  ↓
Actual exercise of recognition / tribunal jurisdiction?
           Requires commenced power + functioning institution;
           NOT established by those two notifications alone.
```

*Each arrow is an additional legal or practical threshold; publishing a rule cannot independently commence an uncommenced parent power.*

✅ The **National Sports Governance Act, 2025** was introduced in the Lok Sabha on 23 July 2025, passed there on 11 August and by the Rajya Sabha on 12 August; it received assent on 18 August as Act 25 of 2025. Its architecture includes a **National Sports Board** for statutory oversight of recognised sports bodies and a **National Sports Tribunal** for sports disputes, with conditions concerning athlete representation, ethics, gender representation, transparency and public funding. A design description is not proof of current operational power.

Read commencement at the level of provisions. The 31 December 2025 Gazette notification brought named sections into force from **1 January 2026**; the 12 May 2026 notification S.O. **2406(E)** commenced **section 5(3)–(5), section 7, section 17(8)–(9), and sections 18, 19, 21, 28 and 29**. The May notification did **not** include the Board's principal recognition-power provision or the Tribunal's substantive jurisdiction/appeal provisions. ✅ The ministry also lists 2026 Board, Tribunal and national-sports-bodies rules. **Status at the last verified tranche, 12 May 2026:** the 2011 **National Sports Development Code of India**, an executive/administrative instrument, remains the operative recognition pathway alongside the Act's continuity for existing recognition. The Board and Tribunal are statutory creations; there is no evidence here that either has already exercised uncommenced recognition or adjudicatory powers. A later commencement or constitution would need a further dated notification before this status is updated.

The Act's framework addresses national Olympic/Paralympic bodies, federations and regional bodies. ✅ **Section 14(2)**, commenced in January 2026, treats a *recognised sports organisation receiving Central or State financial assistance* as a public authority under the **Right to Information Act, 2005**, **with respect to utilisation of that assistance**. Do not turn this into “every private sporting body is subject to RTI for every matter.” ⚠️ The potential benefit is an enforceable basis for recognition and remedies instead of reliance on a ministry's code; the potential cost is regulation of technical sport through poorly independent appointments.

**Indian example:** Suppose an athlete challenges a federation's 2026 exclusion. A lawyer should first ask which rule governed selection and which appeal route was actually effective *on the decision date*, rather than file at a Tribunal solely because its name appears in the Act. **Objection:** “The Act has been passed, so all litigation must move to the new Tribunal.” **Reply:** parliamentary assent, commencement, rule-making, constitution, conferred jurisdiction and actual functioning are distinct. ⚠️ Conversely, phased commencement does not imply the Act is wholly inoperative.

**UPSC use:** For “reform” distinguish the new statutory source of authority from the incomplete migration of recognition and disputes. Avoid invented Board decisions, Tribunal caseload, membership or penalties. The May Gazette is a recent, directly relevant dated example, not proof of completed implementation.

**Revision:**

1. The 2011 Code is executive; the 2025 Act is statutory.
2. Act 25 of 2025 received assent on 18 August 2025.
3. Specified provisions first commenced on 1 January 2026.
4. S.O. 2406(E) commenced a second specified tranche on 12 May 2026.
5. Rules and parent-section commencement are separate legal steps.
6. Board design does not prove operational recognition power.
7. Tribunal design does not prove operational case jurisdiction.
8. Earlier recognition has continuity during transition.
9. Section 14(2) RTI coverage is limited to use of public assistance.
10. Test a dispute against the law and forum available on its decision date.

### Concept check

**Question:** The 2026 Tribunal rules have been published. What additional legal fact is needed before claiming the Tribunal hears an athlete's appeal?

**Model answer:** Verify commencement of the relevant jurisdiction and appeal provisions, plus the applicable forum's operational constitution; publication of procedural or staffing rules alone does not supply substantive case jurisdiction.

**Misconception to avoid:** A rule made under a partly commenced statute automatically activates every provision of the Act.

### Original Mains practice — 15 marks, 250 words

**Mains prompt:** Examine how phased commencement affects India's transition from the 2011 Sports Development Code to the National Sports Governance Act, 2025. Answer in 250 words.

**Model (194 words):** India has changed the *source* of national sports governance but not automatically every route by which a federation is recognised or an athlete appeals. The 2011 National Sports Development Code operated as an administrative recognition framework. Act 25 of 2025 creates a statutory architecture with a National Sports Board, a National Sports Tribunal and governance duties concerning representation, transparency and athlete protection. Its assent on 18 August 2025 did not commence all provisions. Notifications commencing specified sections on 1 January and 12 May 2026 establish a phased, not universal, transition. The latter Gazette lists sections 5(3)–(5), 7, 17(8)–(9), 18, 19, 21, 28 and 29; it does not establish commencement of the Board's principal recognition power or the Tribunal's merits jurisdiction. Publishing 2026 rules is yet another distinct step. Existing recognition has statutory continuity; on the verified tranche record the 2011 Code remains the live administrative route. Regulation can protect athletes against opaque monopoly decisions, but premature claims of a functioning appellate tribunal risk sending time-sensitive selection disputes to the wrong forum. Answer any concrete case by checking the provision, its commencement notice, institution and actual remedy on the relevant date.

**Scoring (15):** code/statute contrast 4; accurate tranche and powers distinction 5; continuity and rights consequence 4; qualified conclusion 2.

*Accountability extends from who controls the federation to how one athlete receives a reasoned decision.*

Imagine a national trial held under criteria altered only after its results are known. Even if selectors are excellent coaches, post-hoc standards defeat procedural fairness. ✅ The 2025 Act establishes a framework for sports bodies and athlete representation; the 2011 Code remains relevant during the incomplete recognition transition. ⚠️ A defensible selection process publishes criteria early, records comparable performances and exceptional grounds, discloses selectors' conflicts, gives a reasoned decision and permits a review fast enough to matter before the event. **Athlete representation** means athletes have meaningful voice in governance, not that every selection must be decided by an athlete vote.

✅ **Section 4(1)** is in the January 2026 commenced tranche. Its design for a National Sports Body includes:

| Executive/participatory provision | Statutory design | What cannot yet be inferred |
|---|---|---|
| Executive Committee size | Not more than **15** members | Every existing NSF has already changed its committee |
| Outstanding-merit sportspersons | At least **two** members | All athletes have identical voting rights |
| Athletes Committee voice | **Two** Executive Committee members elected from the Athletes Committee | Their voting rights are uniform: the Act refers this to body bye-laws |
| Women's representation | At least **four** Executive Committee members | Numerical presence by itself secures safe reporting |
| Ethics, dispute and athletes committees | Each National Sports Body's organisational design includes them, subject to the Act's charter/bye-law conditions | An internal committee is automatically an independent external tribunal |

*A commenced governance prescription is stronger than an aspirational slogan; it is still different from evidence of each federation's actual implementation.*

Separate **recognition** (the body's standing), **internal governance** (elections and finance), **selection** (choice under published criteria) and **safeguarding** (independent prevention and complaint handling for abuse). The short sporting career and a coach's power over future selection magnify fear of retaliation. Gender representation on paper is weak if a complaint returns to the same alleged decision-maker. Public funding also makes transparent expenditure important; a public ledger alone cannot show that a complaint was resolved.

⚠️ **Objection:** Mandatory review makes selectors timid and destroys speed. **Reply:** a reviewer need not substitute its view of sporting merit; it can check notice, application of published criteria, conflict and reasons. Limit: subjective team-composition judgments still demand a margin of technical discretion, provided the authority explains why it departed from measurable performance.

**UPSC use:** Write a six-test audit: source of authority, recognition terms, elections/finance, selection rules, prompt remedy, athlete safety. Differentiate a testable procedural failure from a claim that every disappointed athlete must be selected. ❌ Neither government interference in team tactics nor federation immunity is the desired reform.

**Revision:**

1. Recognition, incorporation, funding and fair decisions are separate questions.
2. Commenced section 4(1) sets an Executive Committee ceiling of 15.
3. It provides at least two outstanding-merit sportspersons.
4. It provides two members elected from the Athletes Committee.
5. It provides at least four women without proving completed NSF compliance.
6. Athlete voice does not abolish specialist selection.
7. Selection criteria should precede trials and apply to recorded evidence.
8. Departures require reasons and conflict disclosure.
9. Review must be independent and timely.
10. Safeguarding needs a route outside the coach's practical control.
11. Audit participation, safety and expenditure outcomes separately.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why is an athlete seat on a federation committee insufficient to make an opaque selection decision fair?

**Model answer:** Representation can improve rule design and oversight, but individual decisions also need advance criteria, consistent application, reasons and prompt independent review. One seat cannot cure post-hoc standards.

**Misconception to avoid:** A representational entitlement automatically proves every selection complied with due process.

### Original Mains practice — 15 marks, 250 words

**Mains prompt:** Discuss how athlete representation and procedural fairness can strengthen federation accountability without compromising sporting expertise. Answer in 250 words.

**Model (157 words):** A federation needs coaches and selectors who can evaluate specialised performance; it also controls the athlete's limited opportunity to compete. Accountability should constrain arbitrary process rather than dictate results. Athlete representation in governing bodies can expose unsuitable calendars, unsafe training and exclusionary rules before a season begins. For an individual selection, the NSF should publish criteria before trials, disclose conflicts, record comparable evidence and give reasons for a departure. A prompt review can test consistency and bias without forcing an external forum to redesign team tactics. The 2025 sports-governance statute envisages athlete-facing governance, but on the verified phased-commencement record the 2011 Code and existing recognition still matter; one cannot assume every statutory power is available. An independent safeguarding channel protects athletes who complain against retaliation by coaches. The main objection is delay before events: a time-bound review with limited grounds answers it better than abolishing review. Voice without remedies is ceremonial; review without technical deference is intrusive.

**Scoring (15):** voice and governance 4; selection procedure 4; credible objection/reply 4; accurate statutory qualification and safety 3.

```text
WADA → World Anti-Doping Code and international standards
                       ↓
India: 2022 Act in force 15 July 2026
       2025 Amendment in force 16 July 2026
       2026 anti-doping + DG-service rules notified 17 July
                       ↓
NADA India: testing and results management
                       ↓
alleged violation → Disciplinary Panel hearing / decision
                       ↓
domestic Appeal Panel OR specifically authorised CAS route
```

*A standard, a test, a decision and an appeal are distinct stages; a sample is not a conviction.*

✅ WADA coordinates the global **World Anti-Doping Code** and related standards, but does not arrange tennis draws or select Indian teams. NADA India previously operated as a registered society; the **National Anti-Doping Act, 2022** gave it statutory standing, and the government reported that the Act's provisions **came into force on 15 July 2026**. The **National Anti-Doping (Amendment) Act, 2025 (Act 26 of 2025)** came into force on **16 July 2026**, as Gazette **S.O. 3929(E)** confirms. The Ministry's **6 August 2026** parliamentary answer further confirms that the **National Anti-Doping Rules, 2026** and the rules on appointment and service conditions of NADA's Director General were **notified on 17 July 2026**. These are different stages; neither a 2022 title nor a 2025 assent date establishes the July 2026 commencement by itself.

Testing checks compliance; **results management** handles an alleged violation before a reasoned adjudication. The domestic **National Anti-Doping Disciplinary Panel** determines the violation/consequences under the applicable law and rules; the **National Anti-Doping Appeal Panel** reviews decisions within its statutory route. The commenced amendment assigns constitution of the Appeal Panel to the **Central Government** and expressly protects operational independence of NADA's Director General and staff from sports federations and bodies with sporting or anti-doping responsibilities. That is a legislative safeguard, not proof that no conflict can occur in practice.

| Appealed decision | Who may invoke CAS under the amended Act? | First question to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Appeal Panel decision | WADA, IOC, IPC and the concerned international federation may appeal under amended section 23(9), subject to CAS rules and the Code | Is the appellant within that specified class? |
| Disciplinary Panel decision in a case involving an international-level athlete or international event | The affected athlete/person and the specified anti-doping/international bodies may appeal directly under section 23(10), within its conditions | Is this an international-level/event case and is the appellant eligible? |
| Agency's final decision against which no party has filed an appeal | WADA's exceptional route under section 23(11), if its Code Article 13 right applies | Has **any party filed an appeal** against that final decision, and does WADA have the Code right? |

*The usual domestic hearing/appeal and the conditional CAS pathways cannot be collapsed into “every athlete always appeals directly to CAS.”* CAS remains an arbitral forum, not a tester.

**Strict liability** in this context means the athlete bears responsibility for a prohibited substance in the body without the regulator having to prove intent to dope. It does **not** mean every positive test warrants an identical sanction or that notice, evidence review, contamination claims or appeal disappear. Consider an athlete who alleges an unintentionally contaminated supplement. The anti-doping rule cannot be made ineffective just by asserting good intentions, yet the source and degree of fault remain relevant under the applicable procedure. This is a hypothetical, not a real athlete's case.

⚠️ **Objection:** strict liability imposes unreasonable risk on athletes who rely on support staff. **Reply:** an intent-only rule is difficult to enforce; accessible product education, reliable sample handling, reasons and a fair hearing are essential safeguards. ✅ The 2025 amendment's commencement and the revised appeal routes are now verifiable; ⚠️ effective independence and timely relief for any particular athlete still require evidence beyond the legislation.

Excessive ministry direction into technical selection would threaten autonomy, but private control of public money and athlete safety without scrutiny would be equally unsafe. Effective governance means that each actual decision-maker can be named, challenged in time and evaluated against outcomes; statutory design is a beginning, not proof of those results.

**Scoring (20):** actor chain and tournament distinction 5; Indian statutory precision 4; selection, welfare, integrity and remedies 6; autonomy counter-case 3; evidence-based verdict 2.

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

### Who decides what

- IOC: Olympic Movement/Games; OCA: Asian Games; Commonwealth Sport: Commonwealth Games; IPC: Paralympic governance. International federations retain sport-specific technical rules, while host organising committees deliver awarded editions. Bid award, delivery and post-event legacy are distinct.
- Legacy must be tested through venue use, maintenance, community and para-accessibility, transport, trained personnel and fiscal/environmental burden—not the bid announcement alone.
- International federation: technical rules of its sport; NSF: domestic sport-specific administration/selection; organiser: its own event entry; IOA: Indian National Olympic Committee. One institution's approval does not bind all the others.
- MYAS: Union policy and recognition framework. SAI: autonomous registered society for training/support, **not** a 2025-Act statutory corporation. IOA and NSFs are not ministry departments. BCCI's cricket role must not be casually equated with the statutory status of a regulator.
- Talent governance links open identification to sustained coaching, sports science, nutrition, competition exposure, education and post-career support. States/local bodies are central to schools, land, venues and local competitions; transparent private/CSR participation can supplement but not replace access, audit and safeguarding.
- Infrastructure is a maintained service, not a construction count. Inclusion requires safe pathways for women and reachable support for rural, tribal and low-income athletes; para-sport additionally requires accessible venues, equipment, trained coaches and valid classification.
- WADA: Code and international standards; NADA: statutory Indian anti-doping body since the **2022 Act commenced on 15 July 2026**. The **2025 Amendment (Act 26 of 2025)** commenced **16 July 2026**; anti-doping and NADA Director General service rules were notified **17 July 2026**. CAS: international arbitral forum, not the domestic first-instance tester or universal appeal.

### Statute: six distinct statuses

- National Sports Governance Act, **Act 25 of 2025**: introduced **23 July**, passed **11 and 12 August**, assented **18 August 2025**.
- S.O. **6152(E)** dated 31 December 2025 commenced specified provisions on **1 January 2026**. S.O. **2406(E)** dated **12 May 2026** commenced sections **5(3)–(5), 7, 17(8)–(9), 18, 19, 21, 28 and 29**.
- 2026 Board, Tribunal and national-sports-bodies rules are listed by the ministry. Board/Tribunal statutory design ≠ commenced recognition/jurisdiction ≠ appointed decision-makers ≠ actual decisions.
- On the verified two-tranche record, 2011 National Sports Development Code recognition and the Act's continuity of earlier recognition remain material; do not assert that the Board has derecognised a federation or that the Tribunal has adjudicated an appeal.
- **Section 4(1)**, commenced January 2026: National Sports Body Executive Committee maximum **15**; at least **two** outstanding-merit sportspersons, **two** elected Athletes Committee representatives and **four** women; athlete-representative voting follows bye-laws. This is design, not verified NSF-level compliance. Ethics and safeguarding require implementation.
- **Section 14(2)**, already commenced, deems a *recognised sports organisation receiving Central/State financial assistance* a public authority under RTI **only for utilisation of that assistance**; do not extend it to all private bodies or all records. Later commencement requires a fresh Gazette check.

### Athlete's route and its protections

- Recognition of a federation → transparent elections/finance/conflicts → criteria published before selection → recorded application → reasons → independent, timely review.
- Safeguarding must permit reporting beyond a coach's influence; athlete representation must be meaningful but does not replace individual remedy.
- Strict liability means intent need not be shown for presence of a prohibited substance, **not** that laboratory evidence is a final penalty or that contamination, degree of fault and appeal are irrelevant. NADA results management is followed by the **Disciplinary Panel** determination and applicable **Appeal Panel** review; the amended Act assigns Appeal Panel constitution to the Central Government and protects NADA staff's operational independence.
- Anti-doping CAS routes in amended **section 23** differ: specified international bodies against an Appeal Panel decision (subsection 9); eligible parties, including an affected athlete, directly against a Disciplinary Panel decision **in an international-level athlete/event case** (subsection 10); WADA's special route when **no party has filed an appeal against the Agency's final decision**, provided its Code Article 13 right applies (subsection 11). This last condition is **not** “no appeal is available.” Always check the party, decision and Code/CAS conditions.
- Before naming a forum, check applicable decision rule, commencement, jurisdiction, constitution, deadline and interim relief. The **sports-governance Act's** Tribunal design in section 20 excludes specified international-event disputes and doping matters assigned exclusively to panels under the **now-operative** 2022 anti-doping Act; the *sports-governance section 20* was not among its verified commenced tranches.

### Grand Slam distinction bank and PYQ route

- Australian Open, Roland-Garros, Wimbledon and US Open: shared Grand Slam governance/rule book; **separate organisers**.
- Age/other eligibility → *possibility* of seeking entry; ranking acceptance, qualifying, a valid wild card and applicable special/protected routes are **distinct**; main draw ≠ qualifying.
- Rules on discretionary *draw places* and on an individual *player* have different subjects; find the actual provision before saying which restrictions exist. A wild card does not waive conduct and anti-doping conditions.
- **2026 Prelims GS-I Set A Q82** (unsolved, scan-checked **printed p. 45**; demand paraphrased): shared partnership, entry for all internationally ranked players older than **14**, and limitation on wild cards for a player. Independently verify exact institutional wording, qualifying conditions and restriction scope under the applicable rules. Do not assign a statement truth value, infer an option or treat a provisional Set-A key as official.

### Answer spine and balanced verdict

- Name authority → identify recognition and law in force → test internal governance → examine selection/eligibility → demand timely review and safety → evaluate integrity and actual outcome.
- Counter-case: undue government direction can imperil international sporting autonomy; reply: regulate money, governance process and rights without substituting for technical expertise.
- For a 10-marker use two distinctions and one consequence; 15-marker add actor chain, example and objection; 20-marker apply six-test audit, jurisdiction/status ladder, strongest counter-case and evidence that could reverse your verdict.
- ⚠️ Statutory design may improve accountability; proof of successful reform requires functioning powers, independent appointments, reasoned athlete decisions and accessible remedies. Never infer medals, corruption, actual sanctions or tribunal activity from a legal instrument.
