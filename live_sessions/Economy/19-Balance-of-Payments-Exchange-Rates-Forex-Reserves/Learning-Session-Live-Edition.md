## Lesson 1 - The BoP boundary and the double-entry ledger

**Progress: 1/20 | Stage: Foundation | Subtopic: Residence, period, credits, debits and balancing**

### Visual - one transaction, two entries

```text
Indian exporter ships software worth USD 100

CURRENT ACCOUNT                    FINANCIAL COUNTERPART
service export: credit +100   <->  foreign bank deposit/claim: debit -100

No money "appears from nowhere".
Every recorded economic value has a counterpart.
```

### Start with the boundary

The **balance of payments (BoP)** is a statistical statement of economic
transactions between an economy's **residents** and **non-residents** during a
specified period. RBI's BPM6-aligned manual, retrieved on 24 September 2026, defines
residence by the centre of predominant economic interest, not nationality alone.

That distinction solves common puzzles:

- An Indian citizen settled abroad can be a non-resident for BoP purposes.
- A foreign-owned company producing through an Indian resident subsidiary is part of
  India's resident economy.
- A student temporarily studying abroad normally remains resident in the home economy
  under the BPM6 convention.
- A transaction between two residents involving a foreign asset is not automatically
  an international transaction; classification depends on whether the external asset or
  liability position changes.

### The double-entry rule

Every transaction is recorded twice with equal value:

| Entry | Typical meaning |
|---|---|
| **Credit** | Export, income receivable, transfer receipt, reduction in foreign assets, or increase in foreign liabilities |
| **Debit** | Import, income payable, transfer payment, increase in foreign assets, or reduction in foreign liabilities |

**Accounting drill 1.** An Indian firm imports machinery worth USD 50 million and
receives a five-year loan from the foreign supplier.

1. Machinery import: current-account goods **debit 50**.
2. Increase in a liability to a non-resident: financial-account **credit 50**.
3. The entries balance even though India has imported more goods.

**Accounting drill 2.** RBI buys USD 2 billion from banks and pays rupees.

1. RBI acquires an external reserve asset: increase in foreign assets.
2. Under the financial-account sign convention, an acquisition of assets is recorded
   as a debit.
3. Domestic rupee liquidity rises; that monetary effect belongs to the intervention
   mechanism taught later.

### Why "the BoP always balances" does not mean "there is no problem"

The accounting statement balances because counterpart financing, reserve change and
errors and omissions close it. Economic pressure can still be severe. A country may
balance its books by exhausting reserves, taking expensive short-term debt, or
compressing imports through recession. Accounting balance is an identity; external
health is a behavioural and balance-sheet judgment.

**Errors and omissions** reconcile timing, valuation, source and coverage differences.
They are not a magical source of foreign exchange and should not be treated as proof of
fraud whenever non-zero.

### UPSC integration

- **Syllabus use:** external sector, BoP and macroeconomic stability.
- **PYQ linkage:** 2019 Prelims Q63 and Q65 require the learner to distinguish debt
  composition from the broader question of currency-crisis resilience.
- **Trap:** nationality is not the BoP residence test.
- **Answer use:** begin any BoP answer by defining the resident-non-resident and period
  boundary, then distinguish identity from sustainability.

### Revision notes

1. BoP is a period statement, not a stock statement.
2. International investment position is the related stock statement.
3. Residence follows predominant economic interest.
4. Nationality and residence can diverge.
5. Credits broadly supply foreign exchange or increase external liabilities.
6. Debits broadly demand foreign exchange or increase external assets.
7. Every transaction has a counterpart entry.
8. A CAD is not an unpaired cash loss.
9. Errors and omissions reconcile statistical gaps.
10. Accounting balance does not prove economic sustainability.

### Practice - post both sides of the ledger

**MCQ 1.** An Indian company imports equipment and finances it through a loan from the
foreign seller. Which pairing is correct?

A. Goods debit and increase in external liabilities as a financial-account credit
B. Goods credit and increase in reserve assets as a debit
C. Primary-income debit and capital-transfer credit
D. Services debit and reduction in external liabilities as a debit

**Answer: A**

- **A is correct:** the machinery import is a goods debit and supplier credit creates an external liability.
- **B is wrong:** an import is not a goods credit, and no reserve acquisition is stated.
- **C is wrong:** neither investment income nor a capital transfer is involved.
- **D is wrong:** equipment is a good, and the liability increases rather than falls.

**MCQ 2.** Which statement best explains why the BoP can balance during an external
crisis?

A. Current-account deficits are automatically erased at year-end.
B. Financing entries, reserve changes and statistical adjustment provide counterparts.
C. The exchange rate is legally required to remain unchanged.
D. Every import necessarily creates an equal export in the same quarter.

**Answer: B**

- **A is wrong:** a deficit is recorded; it is not erased.
- **B is correct:** double-entry accounting records the financing counterpart and reconciliation items.
- **C is wrong:** exchange-rate stability is not an accounting requirement.
- **D is wrong:** the counterpart may be a liability or reserve change, not an export.

**MCQ 3.** For BoP purposes, which test is decisive in the standard case?

A. Passport nationality
B. Currency used for payment
C. Centre of predominant economic interest
D. Location of the commercial bank alone

**Answer: C**

- **A is wrong:** nationality is not the governing BPM6 test.
- **B is wrong:** a rupee transaction can still involve a resident and non-resident.
- **C is correct:** residence is assigned by the unit's centre of predominant economic interest.
- **D is wrong:** the bank's location does not by itself determine the transactors' residence.

**Mains micro-model - 10 marks:** *"The balance of payments always balances, yet a
country can face a BoP crisis." Explain.*

**Model answer:** The BoP uses double-entry accounting, so every current, capital or
financial transaction has a counterpart. A current-account deficit can therefore be
matched by FDI, portfolio inflows, loans, deposit flows, reserve depletion or errors and
omissions. This accounting equality says nothing about financing quality. A crisis
arises when required financing is unavailable, short-lived or costly; reserves become
inadequate; debt service is concentrated; or currency depreciation damages unhedged
balance sheets. Thus, the BoP balances ex post, but the route through which it balances
determines vulnerability.

**Transition:** Once the ledger boundary is clear, the next task is to classify the
transactions that describe production, income and transfers.

## Lesson 2 - Current-account classification: goods, services and incomes

**Progress: 2/20 | Stage: Foundation | Subtopic: The four current-account components**

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual - the current account has four drawers

| Drawer | What enters | Typical Indian example |
|---|---|---|
| Goods | Physical merchandise, normally on BoP valuation rules | crude-oil import, pharmaceutical export |
| Services | Transport, travel, software, finance, consulting and others | software-service export |
| Primary income | Compensation and investment income | interest paid on external debt |
| Secondary income | Current transfers without a quid pro quo | personal remittance received |

### Classification before interpretation

The current account records flows that affect current production, income or transfers.
It is not synonymous with the **trade balance**. In common usage, trade balance often
means merchandise exports minus merchandise imports. The current-account balance adds
services, primary income and secondary income.

Use a simple equation:

```text
Current-account balance
= goods balance
 + services balance
 + net primary income
 + net secondary income
```

**India case, Q1 FY2026-27.** ✅ RBI's release dated 1 September 2026 reported:

- merchandise trade deficit: USD 86.1 billion;
- net services receipts: USD 51.6 billion;
- net primary-income outgo: USD 10.5 billion;
- personal transfer receipts: USD 42.9 billion;
- resulting current-account deficit: USD 4.2 billion, or 0.5% of GDP.

The four headline components do not add mechanically to the final balance without all
sub-items and sign conventions, but they reveal the structure: a very large goods gap
was substantially cushioned by services and transfers.

### Primary versus secondary income

**Primary income** is earned because labour or capital contributed to production:
employee compensation, interest, dividends and reinvested earnings. **Secondary
income** is a current transfer: one party provides value without receiving equivalent
economic value in return.

A worker's remittance to family is therefore not:

- a merchandise export;
- an FDI inflow;
- government tax revenue; or
- a capital transfer merely because it crosses a border.

Debt forgiveness linked to asset ownership or exceptional capital formation can enter
the capital account; ordinary recurring personal remittances belong to secondary
income.

### The invisibles idea

Older Indian discussion often groups services, income and transfers as **invisibles**.
The label is useful as a shorthand but less precise than the BPM6 categories. In an
answer, name the exact component before using "invisibles".

1. Current account has goods, services, primary income and secondary income.
2. Merchandise balance is not the entire current account.
3. Software exports are services.
4. Interest and dividends are primary income.
5. Personal remittances are secondary-income transfers.
6. Transfers lack an equivalent quid pro quo.

### Practice - classify the flow

**MCQ 4.** An Indian resident receives a recurring remittance from a family member
working abroad. Under BPM6, it is normally recorded under:

A. merchandise exports
B. primary income
C. direct investment
D. secondary income

**Answer: D**

- **A is wrong:** no physical good is exported.
- **B is wrong:** the recipient did not earn it by supplying labour or capital to the sender.
- **C is wrong:** no lasting ownership interest in an enterprise is created.
- **D is correct:** an ordinary personal remittance is a current transfer under secondary income.

**MCQ 5.** If a country has a merchandise deficit of 100, a net services surplus of 45,
net primary-income outgo of 20 and net secondary-income receipts of 30, its
current-account balance is:

A. -45
B. -15
C. +5
D. +95

**Answer: A**

- **A is correct:** -100 + 45 - 20 + 30 = -45.
- **B is wrong:** it omits or reverses one of the income components.
- **C is wrong:** the positive service and transfer balances do not fully offset the two deficits.
- **D is wrong:** it adds receipts without deducting merchandise and primary-income outgo.

**Mains micro-model - 10 marks:** *Why can a large merchandise deficit coexist with a
modest current-account deficit in India?*

**Model answer:** The current account is wider than merchandise trade. It adds net
services, primary income and secondary income to the goods balance. India frequently
runs a large merchandise deficit because of imports such as crude oil, electronics and
gold, while software and business-service exports generate substantial net receipts.
Personal remittances add a further secondary-income cushion, although net investment
income is often an outgo. RBI's Q1 FY2026-27 release dated 1 September 2026 illustrates
this composition: a USD 86.1 billion merchandise deficit coexisted with a current-account
deficit of only USD 4.2 billion. The cushion improves resilience but also creates
concentration risk if services demand or remittance corridors weaken.

**Transition:** Current flows explain what India earns and spends now. Asset transfers
and financing require two different accounts that older textbooks often merge.

## Lesson 3 - Capital account versus financial account

**Progress: 3/20 | Stage: Foundation | Subtopic: The modern BPM6 split**

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual - the narrow account and the large account

```text
CAPITAL ACCOUNT                       FINANCIAL ACCOUNT
capital transfers                     direct investment
non-produced non-financial assets     portfolio investment
                                      financial derivatives
                                      other investment
                                      reserve assets
```

### Why learners get this wrong

Older Indian textbooks and some RBI statistical tables used **capital account** as a
broad umbrella for most cross-border financial flows. BPM6 uses a narrower capital
account and a separate financial account. The correct response is not to call the old
source "useless"; it is to state its vintage and translate it into the modern framework.

### The narrow capital account

It contains:

1. **Capital transfers** - for example, certain debt forgiveness and investment grants
   that affect asset ownership or capital formation.
2. **Acquisition or disposal of non-produced non-financial assets** - for example,
   transactions in certain leases, licences, contracts or natural-resource rights.

It does **not** normally contain FDI, portfolio equity, external loans or reserve
assets under BPM6.

### The financial account

It records transactions in financial assets and liabilities between residents and
non-residents:

- direct investment;
- portfolio investment;
- financial derivatives and employee stock options;
- other investment such as loans, trade credit, currency and deposits;
- reserve assets.

### Sign discipline

Suppose an Indian investor buys a foreign bond for USD 10 million:

- India's foreign asset increases.
- It is recorded as a net acquisition of financial assets.
- In the traditional credit/debit language used in elementary BoP drills, it is a
  debit-side financial transaction because foreign exchange is used to acquire the
  asset.

Suppose a foreign fund buys an Indian bond:

- India's external liability increases.
- That is a financial inflow/credit in the elementary presentation.

Always check the table's published sign convention because modern statistical
presentations may display net acquisition and net incurrence with their own signs.

### A classification grid

| Transaction | Correct account |
|---|---|
| Migrant's ordinary remittance | Current - secondary income |
| Interest paid to foreign lender | Current - primary income |
| Foreign company acquires lasting interest in Indian enterprise | Financial - direct investment |
| Foreign fund buys listed Indian shares without control | Financial - portfolio investment |
| Debt forgiveness treated as capital transfer | Capital |
| RBI acquires reserve currency asset | Financial - reserve assets |

**MCQ 6.** Which statement correctly applies the modern BPM6 distinction?

A. FDI is part of the narrow capital account.
B. A capital transfer belongs to the capital account, while an ECB belongs to the financial account.
C. Personal remittances belong to the capital account because they cross a border.
D. Reserve assets are outside the international accounts.

**Answer: B**

- **A is wrong:** FDI is a financial-account category.
- **B is correct:** it separates the narrow capital account from financial borrowing.
- **C is wrong:** ordinary personal remittances are secondary income in the current account.
- **D is wrong:** reserve-asset transactions are recorded in the financial account.

**MCQ 7.** When an older Indian table places FDI under a broad "capital account",
what is the safest analytical response?

A. Reject all figures in the table.
B. Move FDI into the current account.
C. State the table's convention and translate FDI into the BPM6 financial account.
D. Treat every historical sign as identical to current BPM6 presentation.

**Answer: C**

- **A is wrong:** rejecting sound data solely because the terminology is older discards usable evidence.
- **B is wrong:** FDI is a financial-account flow under BPM6, not a current-account item.
- **C is correct:** a source's date and stated convention must be checked before interpreting "capital account".
- **D is wrong:** historical and current presentations can differ in grouping and sign convention.

**MCQ 8.** A foreign mutual fund buys listed shares of an Indian company without
acquiring managerial control. The flow is best classified as:

A. capital transfer
B. primary income
C. reserve-asset acquisition
D. portfolio investment in the financial account

**Answer: D**

- **A is wrong:** there is an exchange of a financial claim, not a transfer without quid pro quo.
- **B is wrong:** dividends later paid may be primary income, but the purchase itself is financial.
- **C is wrong:** the investor is not the monetary authority acquiring reserve assets.
- **D is correct:** tradable equity without a direct-investment relationship is portfolio investment.

**Mains micro-model - 10 marks:** *Distinguish the capital account from the financial
account under BPM6.*

**Model answer:** Under BPM6, the capital account is narrow. It records capital
transfers and transactions in non-produced non-financial assets. The financial account
records cross-border acquisition and incurrence of financial assets and liabilities:
direct investment, portfolio investment, derivatives, loans, deposits, trade credit and
reserve assets. Confusion arises because older Indian material often used "capital
account" broadly for financial flows. A precise answer should acknowledge that vintage
usage but apply the modern split. Thus, FDI and ECBs are not entries in the narrow
capital account; they are financial-account transactions.

**Transition:** The financial account is not one homogeneous pipe. Its instruments
differ sharply in control, maturity, reversibility and balance-sheet risk.

### Visual - each inflow carries a different promise

| Flow | Investor receives | Typical risk to host |
|---|---|---|
| FDI | lasting interest and influence/control | profit outflow, concentration, failed project |
| FPI equity | tradable ownership claim | rapid reversal and price volatility |
| Portfolio debt | tradable debt claim | rate, rollover and outflow risk |
| ECB | contractual foreign borrowing | currency mismatch and refinancing risk |
| NRI deposit | bank deposit claim | maturity and rollover risk |
| Derivative | contingent payoff | leverage, valuation and counterparty risk |

### FDI is not "good money" by definition

Direct investment normally reflects a lasting interest and significant influence. It
is often more stable than portfolio flows because factories and subsidiaries cannot be
sold as instantly as listed securities. Yet stability is not guaranteed. An FDI
project can import heavily, repatriate profits, crowd out rivals or fail to generate
technology spillovers. Evaluate its sector, domestic linkages and financing structure.

### FPI is liquid, useful and reversible

Portfolio investment deepens markets and lowers financing costs. Its liquidity is also
the source of **sudden-stop risk**. Global interest-rate changes, risk aversion or index
rebalancing can generate large outflows without a corresponding change in India's
long-run productivity.

### ECB transmission drill

An Indian firm borrows USD 100 million when USD 1 = ₹80.

- Initial rupee value of principal = ₹8,000 million.
- If the rupee depreciates to ₹88 per dollar, unhedged principal becomes ₹8,800
  million.
- Rupee burden rises by ₹800 million, or 10%, before considering interest.

Fed tightening can affect the borrower through three channels:

```text
higher global benchmark rate
        + rupee depreciation
        + tighter rollover liquidity
        = interest + currency + refinancing stress
```

✅ RBI's ECB Master Direction was updated on 8 September 2026 and retrieved on
24 September 2026. It confirms that ECB and trade-credit transactions are governed
under FEMA and remain subject to permitted framework, reporting and prudential rules.
Specific limits can change; therefore, this lesson does not freeze a remembered limit.

### Gross versus net flows

A country may receive large gross inflows and still record a small net inflow if
residents acquire foreign assets or foreign investors repatriate funds. Vulnerability
analysis should inspect both gross exposure and the net balance.

- **PYQs:** 2020 Q51 on FDI characteristics; 2021 Q7 on eligible FDI instruments;

### Revision notes

1. FDI creates a lasting-interest relationship.
2. FPI is a tradable claim without equivalent control.
3. Equity does not promise fixed repayment.
4. Debt creates contractual service obligations.
5. ECB may be foreign-currency or rupee-denominated within the applicable framework.
6. Unhedged foreign-currency debt creates currency mismatch.
7. NRI deposits are liabilities of banks, not remittances.
8. Derivatives can hedge or amplify risk.
9. Gross and net capital flows answer different questions.
10. Financing quality matters as much as financing quantity.

### Practice - read the risk signature

**MCQ 9.** Which borrower is most directly exposed to currency mismatch?

A. An Indian firm with dollar debt and mainly rupee revenue, left unhedged
B. An exporter with dollar revenue and matched dollar debt service
C. A firm financed entirely by rupee equity
D. A government department receiving a domestic budget grant

**Answer: A**

- **A is correct:** depreciation raises rupee debt service while revenue remains in rupees.
- **B is wrong:** dollar revenue provides a natural hedge, though other risks remain.
- **C is wrong:** no foreign-currency liability is created.
- **D is wrong:** the transaction is domestic fiscal finance.

**MCQ 10.** Which statement best distinguishes FDI from FPI?

A. FDI can never be withdrawn, whereas FPI must be withdrawn within a year.
B. FDI is associated with lasting interest and influence; FPI is generally a tradable financial claim without equivalent control.
C. FDI belongs to the current account and FPI to the capital account.
D. FPI is always debt while FDI is always equity.

**Answer: B**

- **A is wrong:** FDI can be sold and FPI has no universal one-year maturity.
- **B is correct:** control-linked lasting interest is the core distinction.
- **C is wrong:** both are financial-account categories.
- **D is wrong:** portfolio investment can include equity and debt securities.

**Mains micro-model - 15 marks:** *Why should a CAD financed by FDI be assessed
differently from one financed by short-term foreign debt?*

**Model answer:** FDI usually represents a lasting ownership relationship and shares
enterprise risk, whereas short-term foreign debt creates fixed repayment and rollover
obligations. FDI may therefore be less prone to an abrupt exit and can bring technology,
management and market access. Short-term debt can become destabilising when global
rates rise, lenders refuse renewal or the domestic currency depreciates. However, the
comparison is not moral: FDI can create profit outflows and weak domestic linkages,
while well-hedged debt used for productive exports can be sustainable. Assessment must
consider maturity, currency, hedging, use of funds, earning capacity and concentration.

**Transition:** Knowing the instruments lets us ask the central policy question: when
is a current-account deficit productive financing, and when is it a warning?

### Visual - the same CAD can tell four different stories

```text
CAD
 |
 +-- financed by FDI into export capacity --------> potentially productive
 +-- financed by long-term hedged debt -----------> conditionally manageable
 +-- financed by short-term unhedged debt --------> rollover/currency risk

```

### The saving-investment lens

At the macro level:

```text
Current-account balance = national saving - domestic investment
```

A CAD means domestic investment exceeds national saving. That can be desirable if
foreign saving finances productive capacity whose future returns exceed the servicing
cost. It can be dangerous if it finances consumption booms, overvalued assets or
projects without foreign-currency earning capacity.

### A sustainability scorecard

| Question | Safer pattern | Riskier pattern |
|---|---|---|
| What caused the CAD? | capital-goods imports, productivity investment | consumption surge, structural export weakness |
| Who finances it? | diversified stable investors | concentrated leveraged borrowers |
| In what instrument? | equity/long maturity | short-term debt |
| In what currency? | domestic currency or naturally hedged | foreign currency against rupee revenue |
| Can it roll over? | staggered maturities | maturity wall |
| What is the buffer? | adequate liquid reserves | falling usable reserves |
| What is the growth response? | future exports/productivity | weak cash flow and no capacity |

### Financing arithmetic

Suppose the CAD is USD 30 billion. Available net financing is:

- FDI: +18
- FPI: -5
- ECB and other loans: +12
- NRI deposits: +3

Net private financial inflow = 18 - 5 + 12 + 3 = USD 28 billion.
Ignoring other items, the remaining USD 2 billion must be met through reserve use or
another recorded adjustment.

### CAD, fiscal deficit and the twin-deficit hypothesis

A larger fiscal deficit can reduce national saving and widen the CAD, especially when
domestic demand spills into imports. But the relation is not mechanical. Private
saving, private investment, exchange rates and global demand can offset or amplify it.
Treat "twin deficits" as a possible transmission, not an identity.

### Current status and caution

✅ RBI reported a Q1 FY2026-27 CAD of 0.5% of GDP on 1 September 2026. That low ratio is
a dated observation, not a permanent safe threshold. ⚠️ Sustainability still depends on
the composition of imports and financing, reserve adequacy, debt service and the global
funding environment.

### Why the goods gap persists

India's merchandise deficit reflects both constraints and development demand.
Petroleum imports expose the economy to world prices and geopolitical supply risk.
Electronics and components reveal incomplete domestic value chains. Gold imports have
savings, cultural and portfolio dimensions. Capital-goods imports can expand productive
capacity and should not be read like pure consumption imports.

This is why a sectoral diagnosis is superior to the slogan "reduce imports". The correct
question is whether imports are:

- unavoidable in the short run;
- substitutable at competitive cost and quality;
- inputs into exports;
- productivity-enhancing capital goods; or
- non-essential demand sensitive to prices and incomes.

### The services cushion

Software and business services generate foreign-exchange receipts without appearing in
merchandise customs data. A services surplus can therefore offset a large goods deficit.
Yet concentration matters. Dependence on a few markets, digital-trade rules, automation,
cybersecurity restrictions or global recession can weaken the cushion.

### Remittances are resilient, not free

Personal transfers support household consumption, education, health and savings.
Macroeconomically they add secondary-income receipts. They are not a return on an Indian
external asset and do not create a repayment obligation. However, they depend on migrant
employment, host-country policy and corridor conditions.

### A decomposition drill

| Component | USD billion |
|---|---:|
| Goods balance | -120 |
| Services balance | +70 |
| Net primary income | -25 |
| Net secondary income | +45 |

Now suppose oil prices lift the goods deficit by 15 while other components are
unchanged. CAD becomes **-45**. If services receipts simultaneously rise by 10, CAD is
**-35**. The exercise shows why a shock must be traced through all components rather
than attached mechanically to the headline.

## Lesson 7 - Current-account and capital-account convertibility under FEMA

**Progress: 7/20 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Convertibility is a legal permission architecture**

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual - convertibility is not one switch

```text
RUPEE CONVERSION REQUEST
          |
          +-- CURRENT transaction?
          |      trade, travel, education, income, remittance
          |      -> generally permitted subject to FEMA rules,
          |         prohibitions, approvals, documentation and limits
          |
          +-- CAPITAL transaction?
                 changes assets/liabilities across borders
                 -> calibrated routes, sectoral rules, limits,
                    maturity, pricing and prudential safeguards
```

### Meaning

**Convertibility** is the ability to convert domestic currency into foreign currency
and vice versa for specified transactions at market-related rates, subject to the legal
framework. It does not mean:

- the currency is accepted everywhere as legal tender;
- every transaction is anonymous;
- the exchange rate is fixed;
- residents may move unlimited funds for any purpose; or
- all reporting and anti-money-laundering rules disappear.

### Current-account convertibility

India accepted IMF Article VIII obligations in 1994 and generally permits payments for
current international transactions. ✅ RBI's relevant Master Direction, updated
6 May 2026, explains that FEMA Section 5 and the Foreign Exchange Management (Current
Account Transactions) Rules, 2000 govern these transactions. The architecture still
contains prohibited categories, government-approval categories and transactions
subject to regulatory limits or documentation.

So the safe statement is: **India has current-account convertibility within the FEMA
rule framework**, not "every current transaction is unrestricted".

### Capital-account convertibility

Capital transactions change cross-border assets or liabilities. India follows a
calibrated approach because rapid two-way capital mobility can transmit global shocks,
enable leverage and constrain monetary/exchange-rate policy. Rules differ across:

- FDI and portfolio routes;
- resident overseas investment;
- ECB and trade credit;
- deposits;
- property and other asset transactions;
- individuals and financial institutions.

### Tarapore logic without a checklist fetish

Committees on capital-account convertibility stressed preconditions such as fiscal and
financial-sector strength, low inflation, prudent debt and effective supervision.
The enduring lesson is sequencing: liberalisation should follow risk-management
capacity rather than assume it.

### Convertibility case

An Indian importer buying dollars to pay for machinery undertakes a current payment
for the goods import, even though the machinery is a capital good in national accounts.
An Indian resident buying a foreign bond undertakes a capital transaction because an
external financial asset changes. "Capital good" and "capital transaction" are not
synonyms.

### UPSC integration

- **Trap:** current-account convertibility is broad, not literally limitless.
- **Trap:** an import of machinery remains a current-account goods transaction.
- **Policy link:** impossible trinity later explains why capital mobility affects
  monetary autonomy.
- **Stable/current boundary:** the legal source was updated 6 May 2026; exact limits
  should always be checked in the prevailing direction.

### Revision notes

1. Convertibility concerns permitted currency conversion for transactions.
2. Current transactions include trade, services, income and transfers.
3. Capital transactions change external assets or liabilities.
4. India broadly permits current payments within FEMA rules.
5. Prohibitions, approvals and documentation can still apply.
6. India's capital convertibility is calibrated.
7. Liberalisation differs by instrument and resident category.
8. Capital goods are not automatically capital-account entries.
9. Full convertibility is not the same as a fixed exchange rate.
10. Sequencing requires fiscal, financial and supervisory resilience.

## Lesson 8 - Exchange-rate quotation and percentage-change calculations

**Progress: 8/20 | Stage: Foundation | Subtopic: Direct quotes, inverses and appreciation arithmetic**

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Canonical exchange-rate calculations, Ramesh Singh exchange-rate chapter and RBI reference-rate conventions queried
CA search: "RBI reference rate rupee market determined exchange rate methodology 2026"
CA found: RBI official material retrieved 24 September 2026 continues to describe the rupee as market-determined; calculations below are stable and use hypothetical rates
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual - name the denominator before naming the movement

```text
₹80 per USD  ->  ₹84 per USD

More rupees are needed for one dollar.
Therefore:
rupee depreciates; dollar appreciates.

Never say "the rate rose, so the currency appreciated"
until the quotation is stated.
```

### Direct and indirect quotation

From India's viewpoint:

- **Direct/home-currency quote:** rupees per unit of foreign currency, such as
  ₹83/USD.
- **Indirect/foreign-currency quote:** foreign currency per rupee, such as
  USD 0.012048/₹.

They are reciprocals:

```text
If ₹83/USD, then USD per ₹ = 1/83 = 0.012048
```

### Percentage depreciation is not symmetric

If the direct quote changes from ₹80/USD to ₹88/USD:

```text
rupee depreciation against USD
= (88 - 80) / 80 x 100
= 10%
```

But to return from ₹88 to ₹80:

```text
rupee appreciation required in the direct-quote convention
= (88 - 80) / 88 x 100
= 9.09%
```

The bases differ. A 10% depreciation is not reversed by a 10% appreciation.

### Appreciation under the inverse quote

At ₹80/USD, one rupee buys USD 0.0125.
At ₹88/USD, one rupee buys USD 0.01136.
The rupee's dollar value falls by about 9.09%. This is consistent with the 10% rise in
the rupee-per-dollar quote; the apparent mismatch comes from reciprocal measurement.

### Bid, ask and spread

A dealer quote of ₹83.00/83.10 per USD means:

- dealer buys dollars at ₹83.00;
- dealer sells dollars at ₹83.10;
- spread = ₹0.10.

The customer buying dollars pays the ask; the customer selling dollars receives the
bid. This prevents the common exam error of choosing the cheaper side regardless of
transaction direction.

### UPSC integration

- **Trap:** quote direction controls the language of appreciation.
- **Trap:** depreciation and the later reversal are not percentage-symmetric.
- **Application:** import costs, export receipts and foreign-currency debt must be
  translated using the relevant quote.
- **Stable lesson:** hypothetical calculations need no mutable current claim.

### Revision notes

1. Always write both currencies and the unit.
2. ₹/USD is a home-currency-per-foreign-currency quote.
3. A rise in ₹/USD means rupee depreciation.
4. Its inverse is USD/₹.
5. Reciprocal percentage changes are asymmetric.
6. Use the initial value as the percentage-change base.
7. Dealer bid is the price at which the dealer buys.
8. Dealer ask is the price at which the dealer sells.
9. A dollar buyer pays the ask.
10. "The exchange rate rose" is incomplete without quotation direction.

### Practice - read before calculating

## Lesson 9 - Cross-rates, spreads and triangular consistency

**Progress: 9/20 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Deriving one currency pair from two others**

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Canonical numerical gaps, exchange-rate workbook and standard cross-rate mechanics queried
CA search: "RBI foreign exchange market cross currency reference rates methodology"
CA found: No separate mutable claim is required; the cross-rate identities are stable and checked against official-market conventions retrieved 24 September 2026
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual - cancel the common currency like a unit

```text
₹83        USD 1
----   x   ----      = ₹89.64 per EUR
USD 1      EUR 0.9259

Equivalent shortcut:
₹83/USD x USD 1.08/EUR = ₹89.64/EUR
```

### Unit-first method

Suppose:

- ₹83 per USD
- USD 1.08 per EUR

Then:

```text
₹/EUR = ₹/USD x USD/EUR
      = 83 x 1.08
      = ₹89.64 per EUR
```

Writing units makes the dollar cancel. If units do not cancel, the formula is upside
down.

### Triangular consistency

If the market simultaneously quotes:

- ₹83/USD;
- USD 1.08/EUR; and
- ₹92/EUR,

the implied ₹/EUR rate is ₹89.64, not ₹92. Ignoring transaction costs, a triangular
arbitrage opportunity exists. Actual dealers compare bid and ask rates, funding costs,
settlement risk and execution speed before concluding that profit is available.

### Bid-ask cross-rate

Suppose:

- USD/₹ is represented for simplicity as ₹83.00-83.10 per USD;
- EUR/USD is USD 1.0790-1.0810 per EUR.

The conservative customer-facing cross bid and ask require combining the correct sides,
not multiplying midpoints. At UPSC level, the essential insight is that spreads widen
the no-arbitrage band.

### Currency conversion chain

An Indian tourist needs EUR 1,000. At ₹83/USD and USD 1.08/EUR:

1. Dollar requirement = 1,000 x 1.08 = USD 1,080.
2. Rupee requirement = 1,080 x 83 = ₹89,640.
3. Fees and dealer spreads would raise the actual amount.

### UPSC integration

- **Numeracy use:** cross-rates connect quote direction, units and real transactions.
- **Trap:** multiplying two numbers without writing units.
- **Inference:** persistent large discrepancies are normally closed quickly in liquid
  markets, but controls and transaction frictions can create segmentation.
- **Stable lesson:** all rates are hypothetical.

### Revision notes

1. A cross-rate is derived from two other currency pairs.
2. Write currencies as units.
3. Multiply when the common currency cancels.
4. Invert a quote when units do not cancel.
5. Triangular consistency links three bilateral rates.
6. Apparent arbitrage must exceed spreads and costs.
7. Bid and ask cannot be replaced casually by the midpoint.
8. A customer conversion includes dealer margins.
9. Cross-rate mistakes are usually orientation mistakes.
10. Check the final unit before accepting the number.

### Practice - make the units cancel

B. ₹84.00/EUR

**Answer: C**

## Lesson 10 - Fixed, floating, pegged and managed exchange-rate regimes

**Progress: 10/20 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Rules, discretion and India's managed float**

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Canonical exchange-rate regimes, Ramesh Singh, RBI exchange-rate policy statements and 1991/1993 history queried
CA search: "RBI India market determined exchange rate no target band excessive volatility 2026"
CA found: RBI official material retrieved 24 September 2026 describes a market-determined rupee with intervention for orderly conditions rather than a pre-announced target or band
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual - who promises what?

| Regime | Price formation | Central-bank commitment | Main adjustment bearer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hard peg/fixed | official parity | defend a declared rate | reserves, rates, demand |
| Crawling/adjustable peg | rule or periodic changes | guide a path/band | mixed |
| Free float | market | little routine price defence | exchange rate |
| Managed float | market plus intervention | smooth disorderly conditions; no fixed parity | exchange rate plus policy |

### Fixed exchange rate

The authority commits to a parity or narrow band. If private demand for foreign
currency exceeds supply at that price, the central bank must sell reserves, raise
interest rates, restrict transactions or change the parity. A **devaluation** is an
official downward change in the currency's parity under such a regime.

### Floating exchange rate

Demand and supply determine the rate. A market-driven fall is **depreciation**, not
devaluation. Floating reduces the need to defend one exact price but can generate
overshooting, pass-through and balance-sheet stress.

### India's transition

India moved from a heavily administered system through the 1991 crisis reforms, the
Liberalised Exchange Rate Management System in 1992, and a unified market-determined
rate in 1993. The rupee is now commonly described as a **managed float**.

✅ RBI's official position, as retrieved on 24 September 2026, is that the exchange
rate is market-determined and intervention seeks to contain excessive volatility and
maintain orderly market conditions, without a predetermined target or band.

### Intervention is not proof of a peg

A central bank may intervene under a managed float to:

- restore market liquidity;
- damp self-reinforcing volatility;
- build reserves during inflows;
- prevent disorderly overshooting; or
- manage transmission to inflation and financial stability.

The test is whether it promises a fixed parity. Repeated intervention can influence the
path, but does not automatically transform the regime into a legal peg.

### Regime trade-offs

- A peg can reduce transaction uncertainty but imports the anchor's monetary conditions
  when capital is mobile.
- A float absorbs shocks but may harm unhedged borrowers.
- A managed float balances flexibility and discretion but can create uncertainty about
  the reaction function.

### UPSC integration

- **Trap:** depreciation is market-driven; devaluation is an official parity change.
- **History:** 1991, LERMS 1992 and unified rate 1993.
- **Policy:** India's regime is not "RBI fixes the rupee every day".
- **Forward link:** the impossible trinity explains the constraints behind these
  choices.

### Revision notes

1. A fixed regime promises a parity or band.
2. A float lets market demand and supply move the rate.
3. A managed float combines market pricing with intervention.
4. Devaluation applies to official parity reduction.
5. Depreciation is a market fall.
6. Intervention does not automatically imply a peg.
7. India unified its market-determined rate in 1993.
8. RBI does not announce a predetermined rupee target or band.
9. Pegs use reserves and policy adjustment to defend the rate.
10. Floats shift more adjustment into the currency price.

## Lesson 11 - Depreciation pass-through, inflation and balance-sheet effects

**Progress: 11/20 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: From currency price to domestic outcomes**

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

CA search: "RBI exchange rate pass through imported inflation India 2026"

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual - depreciation moves through several gates

```text
RUPEE DEPRECIATION
       |
       +--> foreign-currency invoice rises in rupees
       |          |
       |          +--> importer margin absorbs part
       |          +--> quantity/source changes
       |          +--> wholesale price adjusts
       |          +--> retail price adjusts
       |
       +--> export rupee revenue changes
       |
       +--> foreign-currency debt burden changes
```

### Pass-through is a chain, not a switch

If a barrel of oil costs USD 80:

- at ₹80/USD, rupee invoice = ₹6,400;
- at ₹88/USD, rupee invoice = ₹7,040;
- currency movement alone raises the rupee invoice by 10%.

Retail fuel need not rise by exactly 10%. The final effect depends on:

- the dollar commodity price;
- hedging and contract lags;
- taxes and administered pricing;
- inventory acquired at older rates;
- importer and retailer margins;
- substitution and demand conditions;
- the share of imported input in final cost.

The **exchange-rate pass-through coefficient** is therefore rarely one-for-one and can
differ by product, horizon and inflation regime.

### Export gain is also conditional

At a weaker rupee, a dollar export receipt converts into more rupees. But the exporter's
margin may not improve if:

- imported inputs become costlier;
- overseas buyers demand a lower dollar price;
- capacity is already constrained;
- a foreign-currency loan becomes more expensive; or
- competitors' currencies also depreciate.

### Balance-sheet effect

An unhedged airline with dollar lease payments and rupee ticket revenue is harmed by
depreciation even if some exporters benefit. A software exporter earning dollars and
paying mainly rupee wages may gain. The net macro effect depends on **who holds which
currency exposure**, not merely on the trade balance.

### Inflation-policy feedback

```text
depreciation -> imported inputs/fuel -> production and transport costs
            -> broader prices/wages/expectations
            -> monetary-policy response
            -> domestic demand and exchange rate
```

The chain can weaken if demand is soft or firms compress margins. It can strengthen
when inflation expectations are unanchored.

### UPSC integration

- **PYQ:** 2021 Prelims Q8 tests possible effects of devaluation.
- **Trap:** depreciation does not guarantee export-volume expansion.
- **Trap:** invoice pass-through, wholesale pass-through and retail pass-through are
  separate.
- **Answer use:** split trade-price, inflation and balance-sheet channels.

### Revision notes

1. Depreciation raises the rupee price of a fixed dollar invoice.
2. Retail pass-through need not be one-for-one.
3. Hedging delays or redistributes the effect.
4. Taxes and margins mediate retail prices.
5. Imported inputs can erode exporter gains.
6. Foreign-currency liabilities rise in rupee value.
7. Foreign-currency revenues create a natural hedge.
8. Sectoral balance sheets matter.
9. Inflation expectations affect second-round transmission.
10. Relative currency movements affect competitiveness.

## Lesson 12 - Marshall-Lerner condition and the J-curve

**Progress: 12/20 | Stage: Advanced | Subtopic: When depreciation improves the trade balance**

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Canonical trade-adjustment gap, Ramesh Singh OCR and local external-sector reference queried
CA search: "India rupee depreciation J curve export import elasticity recent RBI"
CA found: No current elasticity is asserted; the condition and lag logic are stable and sources were checked 24 September 2026
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual - price moves now, quantities later

```text
TRADE BALANCE
   better |                         ______
          |                    ____/
 initial  |-------------------/
          |       \__________/
   worse  |
          +--------------------------------> time after depreciation
             contract and quantity lags

                    J-CURVE
```

### Marshall-Lerner condition

Under simplifying assumptions, depreciation improves the trade balance if the sum of
the absolute price elasticities of demand for exports and imports exceeds one:

```text
|export-demand elasticity| + |import-demand elasticity| > 1
```

The condition is about **quantity response**. A cheaper currency does not produce more
exports if foreign demand is unresponsive or domestic firms cannot expand supply. Nor
does it cut import expenditure quickly when imports are essential.

### Why the balance can worsen first

Immediately after depreciation:

- import contracts may remain fixed in foreign currency;
- shipment quantities are predetermined;
- consumers need time to substitute;
- exporters need time to find buyers and expand output;
- invoicing currency may delay price adjustment.

The domestic-currency value of imports can therefore rise before volumes fall. Later,
if elasticities are sufficient, export volumes rise and import volumes fall, producing
the upward arm of the J.

### Numerical intuition

Initially, imports are USD 100 at ₹80/USD = ₹8,000.
After a 10% depreciation to ₹88/USD, suppose import volume initially remains unchanged
and dollar price remains USD 100. Rupee import cost becomes ₹8,800.

After adjustment, suppose substitution cuts the dollar import bill to USD 85:

```text
USD 85 x ₹88 = ₹7,480
```

The same exchange rate is associated first with deterioration and later improvement
because quantities changed with a lag.

### Reasons the J may be weak or absent

- energy and critical inputs have low short-run import elasticity;
- exports use imported inputs;
- capacity and logistics constrain supply;
- global demand is weak;
- competitors also depreciate;
- exporters retain the currency gain as margin rather than cut foreign prices;
- dominant-currency invoicing delays relative-price adjustment.

### UPSC integration

- **Trap:** Marshall-Lerner is neither automatic nor instantaneous.
- **Diagram use:** draw the J and label contract/quantity lags.
- **Mains value:** combine elasticities with supply capacity and import composition.
- **Stable lesson:** no mutable numerical claim is required.

### Revision notes

1. Depreciation changes prices before quantities.
2. Marshall-Lerner concerns export and import demand elasticities.
3. The elasticity sum must exceed one under simplifying assumptions.
4. Essential imports are inelastic in the short run.
5. Existing contracts delay adjustment.
6. Import bills may rise initially in domestic currency.
7. Export response requires spare capacity and market access.
8. Imported inputs weaken the export-cost advantage.
9. The J-curve is a time-path hypothesis.
10. A weaker currency does not guarantee a stronger trade balance.

**Mains micro-model - 15 marks:** *Why is exchange-rate depreciation an incomplete

**Model answer:** Depreciation improves price competitiveness only if export and import

creating a J-curve. The eventual result also depends on export capacity, infrastructure,
imported inputs, global demand, invoicing currency and competitors' exchange rates.

## Lesson 13 - NEER, REER and competitiveness calculations

**Progress: 13/20 | Stage: Advanced | Subtopic: Trade-weighted nominal and real currency indices**

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual - bilateral price becomes a weighted competitiveness index

```text
bilateral nominal rates
        x trade weights
        = NEER

NEER adjusted for
domestic prices / foreign prices
        = REER
```

### NEER

The **nominal effective exchange rate** combines the rupee's movement against a basket
of partner currencies using weights. It answers: has the rupee moved nominally against
important trading partners as a group?

A simplified geometric form is:

```text
NEER = product of (bilateral-rate index ^ trade weight)
```

Official construction is more detailed. ✅ RBI's revised methodology, retrieved on
24 September 2026, uses a 40-currency basket with 2015-16 as base.

### REER

The **real effective exchange rate** adjusts the nominal index for relative prices:

```text
REER = NEER x (domestic price index / weighted foreign price index)
```

This formula assumes a convention in which a higher index represents appreciation.
Some datasets may orient the nominal quote differently. Always read the methodology
before declaring that a rise means appreciation.

### Numerical drill

Assume:

- NEER index = 96;
- domestic CPI index = 125;
- weighted foreign CPI index = 120;
- chosen convention: higher REER = appreciation.

```text
REER = 96 x (125/120) = 100
```

Nominal depreciation to 96 is offset by India's higher relative price level. The real
index returns to 100, so no real competitive gain remains under this simplified
example.

### Interpretation

A real appreciation can result from:

- nominal currency appreciation;
- domestic inflation exceeding partner inflation; or
- both.

It can make exports relatively less competitive and imports relatively cheaper, but
REER is not a complete competitiveness index. Productivity, quality, logistics,
tariffs, contracts and non-price factors matter.

### PYQ anchor

2022 Prelims Q2 directly asks about NEER and REER. The safe approach is:

1. identify nominal versus price-adjusted;
2. inspect the index convention;
3. distinguish bilateral from basket movement;
4. avoid claiming that a real depreciation automatically raises every export.

### Revision notes

1. NEER is trade-weighted and nominal.
2. REER adjusts NEER for relative prices.
3. Bilateral movement can differ from basket movement.
4. RBI uses a 40-currency methodology.
5. The current RBI base is 2015-16 in the retrieved methodology.
6. CPI is used for relative-price adjustment.
7. Index orientation must be stated.
8. Domestic inflation can erase nominal depreciation.
9. REER indicates price competitiveness, not total competitiveness.
10. Productivity and logistics remain important.

## Lesson 14 - The impossible trinity and India's policy mix

**Progress: 14/20 | Stage: Advanced | Subtopic: Choosing two-and-a-half corners rather than three absolutes**

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Canonical trilemma analysis, convertibility framework and RBI managed-float policy queried
CA search: "India monetary policy exchange rate capital flows impossible trinity RBI 2026"
CA found: Current RBI regime evidence retrieved 24 September 2026 supports market determination with intervention; the trilemma itself is a stable macroeconomic constraint
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual - the policy triangle

```text
               FIXED EXCHANGE RATE
                      /\
                     /  \
                    /    \
                   /      \
                  /        \
 FREE CAPITAL MOBILITY ---- MONETARY AUTONOMY

Choose any two fully.
With all three, arbitrage pressure breaks one corner.
```

### The mechanism

Assume:

- the rupee is credibly fixed to the dollar;
- capital moves freely;
- RBI tries to keep India's interest rate below the US rate.

Investors shift toward the higher dollar return. To defend the fixed rupee rate, RBI
sells dollars and absorbs rupees. Unless policy changes or controls bind, reserves fall.
The attempt to maintain all three commitments cannot persist indefinitely.

### Three pure corners

| Pair chosen | Corner sacrificed | Example logic |
|---|---|---|
| fixed rate + free capital | monetary autonomy | interest conditions follow anchor |
| fixed rate + monetary autonomy | free capital mobility | controls limit arbitrage |
| free capital + monetary autonomy | fixed rate | currency must adjust |

Real economies operate between corners. Controls are partial, exchange rates are
managed, and monetary autonomy is constrained rather than zero.

### India's calibrated middle

India combines:

- a market-determined but managed exchange rate;
- substantial but not complete capital mobility;
- an independent inflation-targeting monetary framework.

Intervention, macroprudential regulation, prudential limits and reserve buffers create
room, but they do not abolish the trilemma. ⚠️ India's configuration is therefore best
understood as a managed compromise rather than a violation of the theorem.

### Policy case

Global rates rise sharply while domestic growth weakens.

- Cutting domestic rates may intensify capital outflow and rupee pressure.
- Defending an exact rate may require reserve sales and tighter conditions.
- Allowing orderly depreciation preserves more monetary autonomy.
- Temporary liquidity, prudential and flow-management measures may reduce disruption.

The correct answer identifies the trade-off instead of claiming one instrument can
simultaneously maximise every objective.

### UPSC integration

- **PYQ:** 2022 Q61 provides a Fed-tightening setting.
- **Trap:** reserves postpone and smooth adjustment; they do not repeal arbitrage.
- **Diagram:** draw the triangle and place India's calibrated mix inside it.
- **Answer use:** distinguish full corner solutions from real-world intermediate
  regimes.

### Revision notes

1. The trilemma has exchange-rate stability, capital mobility and monetary autonomy.
2. All three cannot be fully achieved together.
3. A fixed rate plus free capital constrains domestic rates.
4. Controls can preserve autonomy under a peg.
5. A float allows more monetary autonomy with open capital.
6. Real regimes lie between pure corners.
7. India manages the exchange rate without a fixed target.
8. India's capital account is calibrated, not fully open.
9. Reserves buy adjustment time.
10. Macroprudential tools can reduce, not erase, the trade-off.

### Practice - identify the sacrificed corner

**Block checkpoint - transmission and policy space:** The next item integrates

**MCQ 39.** Which description best fits India's practical trilemma management?

B. No capital flows and no exchange-rate movement

**Mains micro-model - 15 marks:** *How does the impossible trinity illuminate India's

**Model answer:** The trilemma states that a country cannot fully combine a fixed

**Progress: 15/20 | Stage: Advanced | Subtopic: Spot intervention, liquidity and reserve change**

### Visual - intervention is an asset swap

```text
RBI BUYS USD FROM BANKS

RBI assets:       foreign assets +100
RBI liabilities: bank reserves / rupee liquidity +100

Market effect: dollars absorbed; rupees injected
```

```text
RBI SELLS USD TO BANKS

RBI assets:       foreign assets -100
RBI liabilities: bank reserves / rupee liquidity -100

Market effect: dollars supplied; rupees absorbed
```

### Purchase case

During strong inflows, RBI may buy dollars:

1. A bank transfers foreign currency to RBI.
2. RBI credits the bank's rupee reserve account.
3. RBI foreign assets rise.
4. Banking-system rupee liquidity rises.
5. Absent offset, easier liquidity can affect money-market rates and domestic demand.

### Sale case

During disorderly outflow or dollar shortage, RBI may sell dollars:

1. RBI transfers foreign currency to the market.
2. Banks pay with rupee balances.
3. RBI foreign assets fall.
4. Domestic liquidity is absorbed.
5. Money-market conditions can tighten unless liquidity is re-injected separately.

### Intervention objective

The stated Indian framework is not a promise to defend a specific number. Intervention
can address disorderly market conditions, liquidity gaps and excessive volatility.
Persistent defence against fundamentals can consume reserves and invite one-way bets.

### Spot, forward and swaps

Spot transactions affect immediate delivery. Forward positions commit future purchase
or sale. Swaps combine legs across dates and can manage liquidity or forward-market
conditions. A complete exposure analysis therefore looks beyond the headline spot
reserve number to forward obligations and liquidity.

### Income versus balance-sheet position

The 2025 Prelims Q2 cross-link asks about RBI income. RBI can earn interest and trading
income from government securities and foreign assets. Currency notes are RBI
liabilities; printing notes is not itself the direct income line asserted by the
official key. The question is cross-linked, not a direct BoP-accounting PYQ.

### UPSC integration

- **Balance-sheet trap:** buying dollars injects rupees; selling dollars absorbs them.
- **Stock-flow trap:** a reserve stock change can include transactions and valuation.
- **PYQ cross-link:** 2025 Prelims Q2, official key A as recorded in the local official
  key ledger.
- **Answer use:** always draw both sides of RBI's balance sheet.

### Revision notes

1. Intervention exchanges foreign assets and rupee liabilities.
2. Dollar purchase raises RBI foreign assets.
3. Dollar purchase injects rupee liquidity.
4. Dollar sale lowers RBI foreign assets.
5. Dollar sale absorbs rupee liquidity.
6. Intervention need not defend a fixed rate.
7. Spot and forward exposures differ.
8. Swaps have two delivery legs.
9. Headline reserves do not show every forward commitment.
10. Sterilisation is a separate offsetting domestic operation.

### Practice - draw the balance sheet

## Lesson 16 - Sterilisation instruments, limits and costs

**Progress: 16/20 | Stage: Advanced | Subtopic: Separating exchange-market action from liquidity policy**

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Canonical sterilisation mechanism, Ramesh Singh reserve-cost discussion and RBI liquidity instruments queried
CA search: "RBI sterilisation forex intervention liquidity government securities standing deposit facility 2026"
CA found: RBI operating instruments were checked on 24 September 2026; no mutable amount is required for this mechanism lesson
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual - two operations, two objectives

```text
STEP 1: RBI buys USD
foreign assets +100 | rupee liquidity +100
                         |
                         v
STEP 2: sterilise
sell/issue domestic instrument or absorb deposits
domestic liquidity -100

Exchange-market purchase remains; liquidity impulse is offset.
```

### Sterilisation defined

**Sterilised intervention** combines a foreign-exchange transaction with a separate
domestic-liquidity operation. If RBI buys dollars and injects rupees, it may absorb the
rupees through:

- sale of government securities from its portfolio;
- market stabilisation securities under the applicable arrangement;
- variable-rate reverse repo or deposit absorption;
- standing deposit facility balances;
- other liquidity-management operations.

The exact instrument mix changes with the operating framework; the balance-sheet logic
does not.

### Worked balance-sheet map

Initial dollar purchase:

| RBI balance sheet | Change |
|---|---:|
| Foreign assets | +100 |
| Bank reserves/liquidity liability | +100 |

Sterilising domestic-security sale:

| RBI balance sheet | Change |
|---|---:|
| Domestic securities | -100 |
| Bank reserves/liquidity liability | -100 |

Net result: foreign assets replace domestic securities on RBI's asset side while
banking liquidity returns to its starting level.

### Why sterilise?

Without sterilisation, prolonged dollar purchases can leave excess liquidity, push
short-term rates below the policy target and amplify credit or inflation. Dollar sales
can create the reverse problem: excessive liquidity absorption may tighten money
markets, requiring an offsetting injection.

### Limits and costs

1. **Instrument constraint:** the central bank needs suitable assets or deposit tools.
2. **Interest cost:** the return on reserve assets may be below the cost of domestic
   absorption instruments.
3. **Quasi-fiscal exposure:** valuation and carry changes affect central-bank income
   and capital.
4. **Persistence:** repeated one-way flows continuously recreate liquidity.
5. **Market signal:** heavy intervention may encourage one-way expectations.
6. **Incomplete effectiveness:** sterilisation can alter domestic rates and attract
   further inflows, partially offsetting itself.

Ramesh Singh's external-sector chapter correctly flags sterilisation, forward premium
and opportunity costs as reserve-management considerations; its historical numerical
examples are not treated as current.

### UPSC integration

- **Trap:** sterilisation does not reverse the reserve purchase; it offsets liquidity.
- **Trap:** sale of domestic securities is not sale of foreign exchange.
- **Trilemma link:** sterilisation creates room but cannot indefinitely insulate a
  fixed rate with open capital.
- **Answer use:** show the two transactions in separate balance-sheet panels.

### Revision notes

1. Intervention and sterilisation are distinct operations.
2. Dollar purchase injects rupee liquidity.
3. Sterilisation absorbs the liquidity separately.
4. Domestic-security sale can absorb bank reserves.
5. Deposit facilities can also absorb liquidity.
6. Sterilisation can carry an interest cost.
7. One-way flows can exhaust instruments or create repeated costs.
8. Dollar sale may require later liquidity injection.
9. Sterilisation does not create a permanent exchange-rate guarantee.
10. Net balance-sheet composition changes even when liquidity is restored.

### Composition

**Foreign Currency Assets (FCA)** include securities and deposits denominated across
major currencies but reported in US-dollar terms. They are not simply physical dollars
held in a vault. Gold is valued at prevailing prices under the reporting method. SDR
holdings and the reserve-tranche position are separate IMF-related components.

### Transaction versus valuation

Published reserves can change through:

1. RBI purchase or sale of foreign exchange;
2. income earned on reserve assets;
3. government external receipts and payments;
4. changes in the dollar value of euro, yen, sterling and other assets;
5. changes in gold prices;
6. IMF-related transactions.

RBI's reserve-variation release published 1 September 2026 provides a clean example for

- reserves fell USD 8.1 billion on a BoP basis, excluding valuation;
- valuation loss was USD 14.4 billion;
- nominal reserves therefore fell USD 22.5 billion.

The valuation loss primarily reflected lower gold prices and US-dollar appreciation

```text
transaction change       -8.1
valuation change        -14.4
                         -----
nominal stock change    -22.5 USD billion
```

A fall of USD 22.5 billion does not mean RBI sold exactly USD 22.5 billion. The
transaction component is the closer measure of active BoP use.

### Reserve-management objectives

Reserves are managed for:

- safety;
- liquidity;
- reasonable return subject to the first two objectives;
- intervention capacity;
- confidence and external-payment continuity.

There is an opportunity cost: highly liquid safe assets may yield less than alternative
domestic uses or the cost of sterilisation. That does not make reserves wasteful;
insurance has a cost.

## Lesson 18 - SDR, reserve-tranche position and IMF-liquidity distinctions

**Progress: 18/20 | Stage: Advanced | Subtopic: Reserve assets are not all spendable currency balances**

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual - four concepts, four legal meanings

| Concept | What it is | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| IMF quota | member's financial/voice base in IMF | ordinary commercial-bank deposit |
| SDR allocation | distribution of SDRs broadly in proportion to quota | fiscal grant with no counterpart |
| SDR holding | reserve asset held in SDR system | circulating currency for households |
| Reserve-tranche position | liquid claim on IMF linked to quota resources used | same thing as SDR holding |

### SDR

✅ IMF official material verified on 24 September 2026 states:

- the Special Drawing Right is an international reserve asset;
- it is **not a currency**;
- it is a potential claim on freely usable currencies of IMF members;
- its value is based on a basket containing the US dollar, euro, Chinese renminbi,
  Japanese yen and pound sterling;
- SDRs are held by members, the IMF and prescribed official holders, not ordinary
  private individuals.

An SDR allocation increases a member's SDR holdings and creates a corresponding
allocation position. Using or exchanging SDRs can change net interest flows. Calling
the allocation "free foreign currency" hides this balance-sheet structure.

### Reserve-tranche position

The **reserve-tranche position (RTP)** is the part of a member's quota-related position
that it can draw readily because the IMF has used the member's currency or because of
the reserve-asset portion of quota arrangements. It is a liquid claim on the IMF and a
reserve asset. It is distinct from:

- an SDR holding;
- an IMF loan under a programme;
- the full quota amount;
- an ordinary foreign-currency deposit.

### Quota functions

Quota broadly influences:

- the member's financial contribution;
- voting power;
- normal access to IMF financing; and
- share in general SDR allocations.

The exact formulas and review outcomes are mutable institutional details; this lesson
does not state an unverified current quota number.

### Use case

If India exchanges SDR holdings for freely usable currency, its SDR holdings fall and
its usable currency assets rise. The transaction does not mean the SDR itself became a
private currency. If India draws on the reserve tranche, it uses a liquid claim linked
to its IMF position, not a conventional conditional loan tranche.

### UPSC integration

- **Trap:** SDR is neither a currency nor a claim on the IMF itself.
- **Trap:** SDR holding, allocation, quota and RTP are not synonyms.
- **Answer use:** define instrument, holder, claim and balance-sheet counterpart.
- **Source limitation:** IMF pages blocked direct fetch with HTTP 403; propositions
  above were verified through official IMF search results retrieved 24 September 2026.

2. SDR is not a currency.
3. It is a potential claim on freely usable currencies.
4. Its basket has five currencies.
5. Private individuals do not hold SDRs.
6. Allocation and holding are distinct.
7. Quota influences voting, contribution, access and allocation share.
8. RTP is a liquid claim on the IMF.
9. RTP is separate from SDR holdings.
10. Neither is identical to a conditional IMF programme loan.

### Visual - the reserve adequacy dashboard

| Metric | Shock it approximates | Blind spot |
|---|---|---|
| Months of imports | loss of trade receipts | ignores capital outflow |
| Reserves/short-term debt | rollover freeze | ignores resident flight and imports |

### Import cover

```text
Import cover in months
= reserves / average monthly imports
```

If reserves are USD 600 billion and annual imports are USD 720 billion, average monthly
imports are USD 60 billion and cover is 10 months. The measure is intuitive but weak
for financially open economies because a capital outflow can occur alongside imports.

### Greenspan-Guidotti rule

A common benchmark asks whether reserves cover short-term external debt falling due
within one year. The strongest version uses **residual maturity**, which includes:

- debt originally short term; and
- long-term debt whose repayment falls within the next twelve months.

Original maturity can understate immediate refinancing need.

### IMF ARA approach

IMF guidance verified on 24 September 2026 uses a composite risk approach for emerging
markets. It considers potential drains linked to:

- short-term debt;
- broad money;
- exports; and
- other external liabilities.

The weights vary with exchange-rate regime and the framework. ARA is a structured
stress proxy, not a divine optimum.

### Three Indian stress episodes

#### 1991 - reserve and structural constraint

Low usable reserves, oil-price pressure, fiscal and current-account weakness, and
external-payment difficulty culminated in emergency adjustment and reform. The lesson
is not merely "reserves were low": macro credibility, financing access and trade
capacity had weakened together.

#### 2013 - taper tantrum

Expected US monetary tightening triggered portfolio outflows and rupee pressure across
emerging markets. India's then-large CAD and inflation concerns increased sensitivity.
The episode illustrates global-rate transmission, sudden stops and the value of
reducing near-term external imbalance.

#### 2020 - pandemic shock

Trade, travel and capital flows were disrupted, but lower oil demand/prices, compressed
imports, policy support and reserve buffers changed the transmission. A global shock
does not produce the same BoP result in every episode; initial composition matters.

### Integrated stress scenario

An Indian infrastructure company has:

- USD 1 billion floating-rate debt;
- 40% due within twelve months;
- rupee revenue;
- only 25% currency hedge;
- project delays.

Then US rates rise 200 basis points, the rupee depreciates 10%, and lenders refuse
rollover. The firm faces:

```text
higher interest
 + higher rupee principal/service
 + refinancing gap
 + delayed cash flow
 = solvency/liquidity pressure
```

The sovereign may still have adequate reserves; the private balance sheet can remain
fragile. Macro buffers and micro hedging must be assessed together.

### Policy architecture

- deepen stable equity and local-currency financing;
- monitor residual maturity and unhedged exposure;
- require transparent derivative and debt reporting;
- build exports and natural hedges;
- maintain usable reserve and swap-line buffers;
- use macroprudential tools against concentrated short-term borrowing;
- allow orderly two-way exchange-rate adjustment;
- avoid socialising private currency risk without a systemic rationale.

# ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS MODEL PRACTICE

## 10 marks - 150 words

### Question 1

**"A current-account deficit is an accounting outcome; vulnerability is a financing
outcome." Explain.**

**Model answer:** A current-account deficit records that an economy's current external
payments exceed receipts. Double-entry accounting ensures that it is matched by
financial flows, reserve use or statistical adjustment. Vulnerability depends on the
quality of that counterpart. FDI and long-duration equity share risk and may create
productive capacity. Short-term foreign-currency debt creates fixed repayment,
currency mismatch and rollover exposure. Persistent reserve depletion narrows the
insurance buffer. The use of funds also matters: machinery that raises exports differs
from consumption without future foreign-exchange earnings. Therefore, the same CAD
ratio can be sustainable in one economy and dangerous in another. Policy should
evaluate maturity, currency, hedging, borrower, concentration, reserve adequacy and
future earning capacity rather than target one numerical threshold.

### Question 2

**Distinguish depreciation, devaluation and REER appreciation.**

**Model answer:** Depreciation is a market-driven fall in a currency under a flexible
or managed regime. Devaluation is an official reduction in parity under a fixed or
pegged system. REER appreciation is a rise in the price-adjusted effective exchange
index under the convention where a higher index means appreciation. It can occur
through nominal appreciation or because domestic inflation exceeds partner inflation.
Thus, the rupee can depreciate bilaterally against the dollar while appreciating in
real effective terms if relative prices and other partner currencies offset the
movement. Each concept answers a different question: market price, official parity and
trade-weighted real competitiveness.

## 15 marks - 250 words

### Question 3

**Analyse the channels through which rupee depreciation affects the Indian economy.**

**Model answer:** Rupee depreciation raises the domestic-currency price of a fixed
foreign invoice. The first channel is imported inflation through energy, electronics,
fertiliser and other inputs. Pass-through depends on hedges, inventories, taxes,
margins, demand and the imported share of final cost. Second, exporters receive more
rupees per dollar, but the gain can be offset by imported inputs, capacity constraints
and competitor currencies. Third, unhedged firms with foreign-currency debt and rupee
revenue face a balance-sheet loss; exporters with matched foreign earnings possess a
natural hedge. Fourth, the trade balance may initially deteriorate because contract and
quantity lags create a J-curve; later improvement requires the Marshall-Lerner
elasticity condition and supply response. Fifth, higher inflation or capital outflow
can alter monetary policy. A managed-float response may smooth disorderly movement but
cannot permanently override fundamentals. Depreciation is therefore distributive and
time-dependent, not a simple export subsidy.

### Question 4

**Evaluate the role and limitations of forex reserves in India's external-sector
management.**

**Model answer:** Reserves provide foreign-payment liquidity, support orderly exchange
markets, reassure creditors and cover imports and maturing debt. India's official stock
contains multi-currency FCA, gold, SDR holdings and the IMF reserve-tranche position.
RBI can sell foreign exchange during disorderly pressure, but the sale absorbs domestic
liquidity and may need an offsetting operation. Adequacy must be assessed through import
cover, residual-maturity debt, broad-money and volatile-liability exposure, together
with an ARA-style stress test. Headline stocks can mislead because valuation changes
move the dollar total without intervention, forward commitments affect usable
liquidity, and private unhedged debt can remain fragile despite ample sovereign buffers.
Accumulation also has opportunity, sterilisation and carry costs. Reserves buy time and
policy space; they cannot substitute for export capacity, prudent debt composition,
credible macro policy and flexible adjustment.

## 20 marks - 250 to 300 words

### Question 5

**Compare the external-sector transmission of India's 1991 crisis, the 2013 taper
tantrum and the 2020 pandemic shock. What policy lessons follow?**

**Model answer:** The three episodes demonstrate that the initial external balance
sheet determines how a shock propagates. In 1991, very low usable reserves interacted
with oil-price pressure, fiscal and current-account weakness and loss of financing
confidence, producing an acute external-payments crisis and structural reform. In
2013, anticipated US monetary tightening triggered portfolio reassessment across
emerging markets. India's large then-current-account deficit and inflation concerns
intensified rupee and funding pressure, illustrating sudden-stop and global-rate
channels. In 2020, the pandemic disrupted trade, travel and capital flows, but import
compression and lower energy demand improved the current account for a period, while
policy support and existing buffers altered the reserve outcome.

The comparison yields five lessons. First, reserves must be judged against likely
drains, not celebrated in gross terms. Second, CAD composition and financing quality
matter more than one threshold. Third, residual maturity, currency mismatch and hedging
must be monitored at firm and system level. Fourth, a managed float should permit
orderly two-way adjustment rather than defend an artificial parity. Fifth, services,
remittances, diversified exports and local-currency finance strengthen structural
resilience. India's goal should be shock absorption with adjustment, not the illusion
that reserves can prevent every movement.

### Question 6

**"India's exchange-rate framework is a practical negotiation with the impossible
trinity." Critically examine.**

**Model answer:** The impossible trinity prevents a country from fully combining a
fixed exchange rate, unrestricted capital mobility and independent monetary policy.
India seeks monetary autonomy under an inflation-targeting framework, permits
substantial but calibrated capital flows, and allows a market-determined rupee while
RBI intervenes against disorderly conditions. This is a negotiated middle rather than
a pure corner.

The framework has advantages. Exchange flexibility absorbs global shocks; reserves
provide intervention capacity; and prudential rules temper volatile borrowing.
Sterilisation can offset the liquidity effect of intervention. Yet each instrument has
limits. Reserve defence can be exhausted, sterilisation carries interest and instrument
costs, controls can distort allocation, and opaque intervention may encourage one-way
expectations. Global rate increases still affect portfolio flows, ECB costs and the
domestic policy trade-off.

India therefore cannot simultaneously promise a rupee level, complete openness and an
interest rate chosen solely for domestic conditions. The durable framework is a
credible no-target managed float, two-way currency risk, deeper hedging markets,
sequenced capital liberalisation, sound banks, prudent residual maturity and
transparent reserve metrics. The trilemma is managed, not abolished.

**MCQ 69.** An Indian importer pays for machinery from current earnings. Where is the
machinery transaction recorded?

A. Goods debit in the current account
B. Capital transfer in the capital account
C. FDI in the financial account
D. Primary-income debit

**Answer: A**

- **A is correct:** machinery is a merchandise import despite being a capital good.
- **B is wrong:** no transfer without equivalent value occurs.
- **C is wrong:** no lasting ownership relationship is created.
- **D is wrong:** the payment is for a good, not interest or dividend.

**MCQ 70.** The quote rises from ₹80/USD to ₹84/USD. Which statement is correct?

A. Rupee appreciates by 5%.
B. Rupee depreciates by 5%.
C. Dollar depreciates by 4.76%.
D. No bilateral value changes.

**Answer: B**

- **A is wrong:** a higher rupee cost per dollar means a weaker rupee.
- **B is correct:** 4/80 x 100 = 5%.
- **C is wrong:** the dollar appreciates; the inverse percentage uses a different base.
- **D is wrong:** the relative price has changed.

**MCQ 71.** RBI buys dollars and takes no offsetting liquidity action. What happens
immediately?

A. Rupee liquidity falls.
B. RBI foreign assets fall.
C. RBI foreign assets and rupee liquidity rise.
D. Private external debt is extinguished.

**Answer: C**

- **A is wrong:** banks receive rupee balances.
- **B is wrong:** RBI acquires the foreign currency.
- **C is correct:** both sides of RBI's balance sheet expand.
- **D is wrong:** intervention does not cancel private liabilities.

**MCQ 72.** Which statement correctly distinguishes SDR holdings from RTP?

A. Both are household currencies.
B. Both are identical to programme loans.
C. RTP is the market value of India's gold.
D. SDR holdings and the quota-related liquid claim on IMF are separate reserve components.

**Answer: D**

- **A is wrong:** neither circulates as household currency.
- **B is wrong:** neither is simply a conditional programme loan.
- **C is wrong:** gold is another reserve component.
- **D is correct:** the legal source and balance-sheet nature differ.

**MCQ 73.** Which action most directly reduces a firm's currency mismatch?

A. Matching dollar debt service with dollar export revenue
B. Shortening maturity while leaving revenue in rupees
C. Increasing floating-rate exposure
D. Removing all debt disclosure

**Answer: A**

- **A is correct:** a natural hedge aligns liability and cash-flow currency.
- **B is wrong:** it increases rollover pressure without fixing currency exposure.
- **C is wrong:** it adds interest-rate risk.
- **D is wrong:** opacity does not remove risk.

**MCQ 74.** Which item belongs to secondary income?

A. Dividend paid to a foreign shareholder
B. Personal remittance received without an equivalent current service
C. Purchase of a foreign bond
D. Debt-financed machinery import

**Answer: B**

- **A is wrong:** dividends are primary investment income.
- **B is correct:** it is a current transfer.
- **C is wrong:** it is a financial-asset transaction.
- **D is wrong:** it combines a goods debit and financial liability.

**MCQ 75.** Which fact most weakens the claim that a large gross reserve stock is
automatically adequate?

A. Reserves are reported in dollars.
B. Gold is a reserve component.
C. Near-term residual debt and volatile outflows may be large relative to usable reserves.
D. RBI publishes reserve data.

**Answer: C**

- **A is wrong:** reporting currency creates valuation issues but does not alone prove inadequacy.
- **B is wrong:** composition matters, but gold's presence alone is not decisive.
- **C is correct:** adequacy is the stock relative to plausible drains.
- **D is wrong:** publication improves transparency.

**MCQ 76.** Which statement about sterilised intervention is correct?

A. It fixes the exchange rate permanently.
B. It removes all interest costs.
C. It prevents the reserve asset from appearing on RBI's balance sheet.
D. It offsets the domestic-liquidity effect through a separate operation.

**Answer: D**

- **A is wrong:** sterilisation cannot guarantee a parity.
- **B is wrong:** absorption instruments may carry costs.
- **C is wrong:** the foreign asset remains unless sold.
- **D is correct:** monetary impact is offset without reversing the FX leg.

## Argument map - should India accumulate more reserves?

| Case for more | Qualification |
|---|---|

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

## 1. Core definitions

- **BoP:** period statement of resident-non-resident economic transactions.
- **IIP:** stock statement of external financial assets and liabilities at a date.
- **Residence:** centre of predominant economic interest, not passport alone.
- **Credit:** export/receipt, liability increase or foreign-asset reduction in the
  elementary debit-credit presentation.
- **Debit:** import/payment, foreign-asset increase or liability reduction.
- **CAD:** current-account debits exceed credits; macro identity is investment minus
  national saving.

## 2. Current account

| Component | Includes | Common trap |
|---|---|---|
| Goods | merchandise exports/imports | capital good is still a goods import |
| Services | software, travel, transport, finance | not in customs merchandise balance |
| Primary income | wages, interest, dividends, reinvested earnings | remittance is not automatically primary income |
| Secondary income | current transfers, personal remittances | not FDI or capital transfer |

## 3. Capital and financial accounts

- Narrow BPM6 capital account = capital transfers + non-produced non-financial assets.
- Financial account = FDI + FPI + derivatives + other investment + reserve assets.
- Older Indian sources may use "capital account" broadly; state the vintage.
- FDI implies lasting interest/influence; FPI is a tradable claim without equivalent
  control.
- ECB vulnerability = global rate + currency mismatch + rollover + cash-flow risk.

## 4. CAD diagnosis

> **Memory chain:** **Cause -> Composition -> Financing -> Currency -> Maturity -> Hedge
> -> Buffer -> Return.**

- Productive CAD: machinery/technology, stable equity, future export capacity.
- Fragile CAD: consumption boom, short-term debt, unhedged currency exposure, reserve
  depletion.
- No universal CAD/GDP safety threshold.
- Twin deficits are a possible causal link, not an identity.

## 5. India's current-account structure

- Merchandise deficit reflects energy, electronics, gold and capital-goods demand.
- Services and personal transfers provide recurring cushions.
- Net primary-income outgo partly offsets those cushions.
- Q1 FY2026-27, RBI release dated 1 September 2026:
  - CAD USD 4.2 billion or 0.5% GDP;
  - merchandise deficit USD 86.1 billion;
  - net services receipts USD 51.6 billion;
  - primary-income outgo USD 10.5 billion;
  - personal-transfer receipts USD 42.9 billion.

## 6. Convertibility

- Current-account convertibility operates within FEMA rules, not without any
  restriction.
- RBI current-remittance Master Direction was updated 6 May 2026.
- Capital convertibility is calibrated by instrument, resident, sector and prudential
  condition.
- A machinery import is current-account goods; a foreign-bond purchase is a capital
  transaction.

## 7. Exchange-rate calculations

```text
₹/USD rises -> rupee depreciates
₹/USD falls -> rupee appreciates

percentage change = (new - old) / old x 100

cross rate:
₹/EUR = ₹/USD x USD/EUR
```

- Bid: dealer buys base/foreign currency.
- Ask: dealer sells it.
- Percentage depreciation and reversal are asymmetric.
- Devaluation is official parity reduction; depreciation is market-driven.

## 8. Pass-through and trade adjustment

- Invoice effect precedes retail effect.
- Margins, taxes, hedges, inventory and demand mediate pass-through.
- Export gains depend on capacity, imported inputs and competitor currencies.
- Foreign-currency debt can create balance-sheet contraction.
- Marshall-Lerner: absolute export plus import demand elasticities exceed one under
  simplifying assumptions.
- J-curve: trade balance may worsen before quantity adjustment improves it.

## 9. NEER and REER

- NEER = trade-weighted nominal basket index.
- REER = NEER adjusted for relative prices.
- RBI methodology retrieved 24 September 2026: 40-currency basket, base 2015-16, CPI
  price adjustment.
- State index orientation.
- REER measures price competitiveness, not logistics, quality or productivity.

## 10. Impossible trinity

```text
fixed rate + free capital -> constrained monetary autonomy
fixed rate + monetary autonomy -> constrained capital mobility
free capital + monetary autonomy -> flexible exchange rate
```

- India: managed flexibility + calibrated capital mobility + constrained but real
  monetary autonomy.
- Reserves, intervention and macroprudential rules buy room; they do not abolish the
  trade-off.

## 11. Intervention and sterilisation

- Buy dollars -> foreign assets up, rupee liquidity up.
- Sell dollars -> foreign assets down, rupee liquidity down.
- Sterilise purchase -> absorb rupee liquidity through separate domestic operation.
- Sterilise sale -> inject liquidity separately if needed.
- Costs: interest differential, instrument limits, repeated inflows, market signals.

## 12. Reserve composition and change

- Components: FCA + gold + SDR holdings + RTP.
- FCA are multi-currency assets expressed in dollars.
- Nominal reserve change = BoP transaction change + valuation change.
- Q1 FY2026-27, release dated 1 September 2026:
  - BoP-basis reserve decline USD 8.1 billion;
  - valuation loss USD 14.4 billion;
  - nominal decline USD 22.5 billion.
- End-March 2026 reserves: USD 691.11 billion in RBI's 46th half-yearly report.

## 13. IMF distinctions

- SDR = international reserve asset, not currency.
- SDR = potential claim on freely usable currencies.
- Basket = USD, euro, renminbi, yen, sterling.
- Allocation creates holdings and an allocation position.
- Quota influences contribution, vote, access and allocation share.
- RTP = separate liquid quota-related claim on IMF.

## 14. Reserve adequacy

- Import cover = reserves / average monthly imports.
- Greenspan-Guidotti = cover debt due within one year.
- Prefer residual maturity for near-term obligations.
- IMF ARA considers short-term debt, broad money, exports and other liabilities.
- Add usable-reserve haircut, forward positions and simultaneous stress.
- No one ratio is sufficient.

## 15. External debt

> **Mnemonic:** **Who - Currency - Rate - Maturity - Hedge - Use - Lender.**

- DEA December 2025 report, March 2026 release:
  - total debt USD 765.5 billion;
  - debt/GDP 20.4%;
  - short-term original maturity 19.7% of debt;
  - residual-maturity debt due within year 43.2% of debt;
  - residual-maturity debt/reserves 48.1%;
  - currency: USD 54.8%, INR 30.1%, yen 6.3%, SDR 4.3%, euro 3.7%.
- Rupee-denominated debt remains external if owed to non-resident.
- Reserves are macro insurance, not an automatic firm-level hedge.

## 16. Crisis comparison

| Episode | Dominant transmission | Core lesson |
|---|---|---|
| 1991 | reserve/payment and structural constraint | financing confidence and macro fundamentals interact |
| 2013 | global-rate repricing and portfolio pressure | CAD and volatile financing amplify sudden stops |
| 2020 | trade/demand collapse with import compression | identical global shock can yield different BoP outcome |
