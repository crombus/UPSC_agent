Raw activity -> sector boundary -> output -> subtract intermediate inputs -> GVA
      |               |              |                  |
 informal work   bundled product  price/volume       double counting

### Intuition: classify the act, not the signboard

A service changes a consumer's condition, facilitates exchange or supplies knowledge, access, risk-pooling or coordination. Storage, transport, finance, software and legal advice remain services even when a tangible object is involved. A restaurant combines manufactured food inputs with preparation, ambience and delivery; its name does not settle the national-accounts boundary.

The core equation is:

`GVA = value of output - intermediate consumption`.

If a courier produces delivery output worth Rs 100 and purchases Rs 30 of fuel and outsourced inputs, its GVA is Rs 70. Turnover is not contribution to GDP because purchased inputs embody value added elsewhere. GDP at market prices adds product taxes and subtracts product subsidies from aggregate GVA.

### Productivity laboratory

Services may be distributive (trade, transport, storage), producer-facing (finance, logistics, professional and IT), social (health, education), personal, or public. The grouping is functional, not a ranking. Logistics can raise manufacturers' productivity by reducing inventory and uncertainty; the gain is economy-wide.

Labour productivity is commonly real GVA per worker or per hour. If real GVA rises from 100 to 112 while hours rise from 100 to 104, output per hour rises from 1.00 to 1.077, about **7.7 per cent**. That is not automatically total factor productivity: capital deepening, skill and composition may explain part.

Services are hard to measure because many are customised, non-storable and co-produced with the user. Free digital services create consumer surplus without a market price. Ad-funded platforms record advertising revenue rather than the full user benefit. Quality-adjusting a safer payment or faster search tool is difficult. Informality weakens establishment data. Outsourcing may shift a task from a manufacturer's payroll into measured services without changing the task.

Economic Survey 2025-26 treats services as a major source of growth and trade, but an aggregate share hides high-productivity software and low-productivity personal services. Read GVA with employment, hours, wages, firm size and informality.

### Output, employment and the structural-transformation test

```text
HIGH OUTPUT SHARE + LOWER EMPLOYMENT SHARE
                |
                +--> high average productivity in some services
                +--> skill and city concentration
                +--> coexistence with a low-productivity informal tail

Policy test: output -> jobs -> real earnings -> security -> diffusion
```

Economic Survey 2025-26 reports services at **53.6 per cent of GDP in H1 FY26**, services GVA growth of **9.1 per cent in the FY26 first advance estimates**, about **30 per cent of total employment**, and **61.9 per cent of urban employment**. These use different denominators. Their gap signals composition and average productivity, not proof that every service job is productive.

IT, finance and professional services can create high value per worker. Trade, transport, tourism, care and personal services can absorb more labour but vary greatly in wages, formality and capital intensity. Employment quality therefore needs five tests: participation, job numbers, real earnings, productivity and protection. MoSPI's revamped PLFS improves labour frequency, while ASISSE is designed to improve incorporated-services evidence; neither fully closes the informal-services gap.


### Strongest criticism, reply and verdict

**Criticism:** services-led growth is statistical relabelling and cannot create broad employment. **Reply:** tradable and enabling services earn foreign exchange and reduce coordination costs. **Qualified verdict:** India needs diffusion from high-productivity islands into labour-absorbing tourism, logistics, care and professional services; services and manufacturing are complements.

### UPSC application and verified linkage

**2024 Prelims GS-I Set A Q45** classified storage, dairy, mineral exploration and weaving. Status: official paper and official Set-A key available locally; this session records demand only, not the answer. It tests activity-based classification.

### High-risk traps

- Turnover is not GVA.
- A nominal service-share rise need not mean real volume growth.
- GVA per worker is not automatically TFP.
- Outsourcing may alter sector labels without creating a new task.

### Register-ready revision points

- Classify by economic activity, not technology use.
- GVA subtracts intermediate consumption.
- GDP at market prices adjusts GVA for product taxes and subsidies.
- Use real output for productivity growth.
- Per-worker and per-hour productivity can diverge.
- Quality change is especially hard in digital services.
- Free services may create unpriced surplus.
- Informality weakens measurement coverage.
- Producer services can raise manufacturing productivity.
- Disaggregate before judging employment quality.

### Lesson-specific Mains micro-model

**Question (10 marks, maximum 150 words):** Why can a rising services share coexist with weak employment quality?

**Complete micro-model:** Define services share in GVA, then disaggregate. High-productivity tradable services raise output and exports; enabling services reduce costs elsewhere; low-productivity informal services absorb labour with weak wages. Add outsourcing, quality adjustment and free-digital-surplus caveats. Use a productivity-employment matrix to distinguish IT-BPM, logistics, tourism and personal services. Conclude with skills, formalisation, urban infrastructure and improvement of labour-intensive services rather than treating an aggregate share as proof of inclusive transformation.

**Unique scoring rubric (10 marks):** 2 definition/GVA logic + 3 heterogeneity and employment + 3 evidence/measurement + 2 qualified verdict.

### Concept check

**Question:** A services share rises after manufacturers outsource logistics. What must be checked before calling this a productivity gain?

**Model answer:** Check real output and hours, subtract intermediate inputs, separate reclassification from efficiency, and examine jobs, earnings and service quality. Outsourcing can change measured composition without changing the physical task, though specialisation may still create genuine gains.

**Misconception to avoid:** A larger services share is not proof of higher productivity or better employment.

### The territorial test

GATS Article I:2 classifies service supply by the location of supplier and consumer.

- **Mode 1:** cross-border delivery, such as remote software or architectural drawings from India.
- **Mode 2:** consumption abroad, such as a foreign tourist or patient consuming in India.
- **Mode 3:** commercial presence, such as an Indian firm serving through an overseas subsidiary.
- **Mode 4:** temporary presence of a natural person supplying a service. It is not general migration or citizenship access.

A contract may combine modes: remote design under Mode 1, specialists under Mode 4 and a local affiliate under Mode 3. Balance-of-payments recording is residence-based and does not perfectly match GATS, especially where Mode 3 transactions occur between host-economy residents.

### Commitments and current scale

GATS schedules specify sectors, modes and limitations. **Market access** concerns listed quantitative/legal restrictions. **National treatment** compares foreign and domestic like services or suppliers, subject to scheduled limitations. MFN generally compares treatment across trading partners. Mutual recognition can reduce qualification barriers without eliminating licensing.

The Commerce Ministry reported on **24 July 2026** that FY2025-26 services exports reached **USD 421.3 billion**, led by telecommunications/computer/information and other business services. Reporting year, publication date and retrieval date must travel with the figure. Gross export receipts may exceed domestic value added when imported licences or professional inputs are used.

### Four-mode case

A hospital earns USD 2 million from foreign patients in India: Mode 2. Its India-based tele-radiology team bills abroad: Mode 1. A doctor goes temporarily to a partner hospital: Mode 4. An Indian-owned hospital incorporated abroad supplies under Mode 3, but its local sales may appear in affiliate rather than conventional cross-border statistics.

### Objection and balanced position

**Criticism:** liberalisation may expose providers and regulators to concentration, data and prudential risk. **Reply:** schedules retain limitations and legitimate domestic regulation. **Verdict:** India should negotiate mode-specific access while building skills, recognition, data safeguards and regulatory cooperation; services strategy is wider than visa access.

### UPSC application and verified linkage

No locally owned exact objective PYQ through 2026 asks all four modes together. **2025 GS-III Q2** on protectionism/bilateralism and **2023 GS-III Q2** on digitalisation supply verified Mains linkage. No actual answer is supplied.

### High-risk traps

- Mode 4 is temporary service supply, not free migration.
- Inbound tourism is an export under Mode 2.
- Mode 3 is broader than ordinary cross-border BOP exports.
- Market access and national treatment differ.

### Register-ready revision points

- Ask what crosses the border.
- Mode 1: service; Mode 2: consumer; Mode 3: establishment; Mode 4: supplier-person.
- One value chain may use multiple modes.
- Schedules are sector- and mode-specific.
- MFN differs from national treatment.
- Domestic regulation can remain, subject to commitments.
- Recognition matters for professional services.
- Mode 3 is imperfectly captured by BOP exports.
- Services surplus can offset merchandise deficit.
- Date every current export figure.

### Lesson-specific Mains micro-model

**Question (10 marks, maximum 150 words):** Explain why GATS modes are economically linked but legally distinct.

**Complete micro-model:** Open with Article I:2's territorial test. Give one India-centred example for each mode. Show complementarity through software using remote delivery, temporary specialists and an affiliate. Distinguish scheduled market access/national treatment from BOP recording. Discuss India's interest in Mode 1 scale and Mode 4 mobility, balanced by data protection, prudential regulation and qualification standards. Conclude that negotiation must address the full service value chain.

**Unique scoring rubric (10 marks):** 2 mode definitions + 3 territorial application + 3 India/trade constraint + 2 conclusion.

### Concept check

**Question:** An Indian architect emails designs abroad and later travels briefly to supervise. Which GATS modes operate?

**Model answer:** Remote delivery is Mode 1 because the service crosses the border; temporary travel by the architect is Mode 4 because the natural-person supplier moves.

**Misconception to avoid:** Mode follows who or what crosses the border, not the label digital or professional.

### Cloud and AI change the service-production function

```text
compute + data + models + domain knowledge
                 |
        automate routine components
                 |
 augment design, diagnosis, coding and decisions
                 |
new exports/productivity -- concentration, bias and job redesign
```

Cloud computing supplies infrastructure, platforms or software on demand. It lowers fixed cost and speeds scaling, but dependence on a few hyperscale providers creates switching, outage, data-location and bargaining risks. AI is layered over data, compute and domain processes. In IT-BPM and GCCs it can automate routine coding, testing and support while raising demand for model evaluation, cyber security, engineering, domain expertise and accountable human review.

The correct labour inference is task transformation, not the claim that an occupation either survives unchanged or disappears. India's upgrading objective is to own more architecture, intellectual property, research, product management and client responsibility while reskilling workers displaced from routine tasks.

### Value-ladder visual

```text
transaction processing -> managed operations -> analytics -> engineering/R&D
 cost arbitrage          process knowledge      data assets     product mandate
                         GCC and vendor ecosystem
```

### Three institutions, not one label

IT services sell technology capability; BPM reorganises repeatable business processes; a **Global Capability Centre (GCC)** is an offshore unit established by a multinational for its own global technology, analytics, engineering or business operations. A third-party vendor serves clients under contract; a GCC is captive to its corporate group, although hybrid sourcing is common. A startup normally owns a scalable product or business model.

Economic Survey 2025-26 reports, using industry estimates, FY2025 IT-ITeS revenue of **USD 283 billion including hardware** and more than **1,700 GCCs employing over 19 lakh professionals as of FY2024**. These figures retain their vintages; they are not silently converted into September 2026 counts.

### Upgrading mechanism

Value per worker rises through task complexity, reliability and scarce knowledge. India scaled codified tasks through skills, telecommunications and wage differentials. Process knowledge generated trust and switching costs. GCCs then internalised sensitive data and long-term capability, enabling product management, cybersecurity, AI, engineering and R&D. Agglomeration creates thick labour markets, specialised vendors and peer learning.

Revenue and employment can diverge. Automation may raise output per worker while slowing routine hiring. Exchange rates affect dollar revenue. A shift from maintenance to engineering can raise domestic value added without proportional headcount.

AI substitutes for some routine coding and back-office tasks but complements architecture, domain knowledge, verification and client trust. The Survey's finding of faster export growth in AI-exposed categories after a defined diffusion period is evidence within its econometric design, not proof that every occupation gains.

### Spillover test

GCCs transform the wider economy when workers move, domestic vendors learn, universities collaborate and Indian management receives global mandates. They remain enclaves when intellectual property, senior decisions and suppliers stay external. City congestion and narrow elite hiring can limit inclusion.

**Strongest criticism:** mandates are foreign-demand dependent and footloose. **Reply:** talent depth, vendor ecosystems and institutional reliability create durable location advantages. **Verdict:** retain cost competitiveness while investing in research links, managerial depth, cyber assurance, reskilling and tier-2-city diffusion.

### UPSC application and verified linkage

**2023 GS-III Q2** asks the status, problems and improvements of digitalisation in the Indian economy. Official-question OCR is locally verified. GCC/IT-BPM evidence belongs in the status and opportunity limbs; no model answer to the actual question appears here.

### High-risk traps

- A GCC is not a third-party outsourcing vendor.
- Revenue growth need not equal job growth.
- Automation may complement high-skill work while substituting routine tasks.
- A location count without vintage is unsafe.

### Register-ready revision points

- Distinguish IT services, BPM, GCCs and startups.
- GCCs are captive centres of multinational groups.
- Agglomeration lowers matching and vendor costs.
- Process knowledge supports value-ladder movement.
- Exports are not identical to domestic value added.
- Exchange rates affect dollar revenue.
- AI first changes tasks, then occupations.
- Enclave risk depends on local linkages.
- Tier-2 expansion needs skills and city infrastructure.
- Cyber reliability is an export capability.

### Lesson-specific Mains micro-model

**Question (10 marks, maximum 150 words):** Can GCC expansion produce broad structural transformation in India?

**Complete micro-model:** Define GCCs and distinguish vendors. Present high-value exports, skilled jobs, supplier learning, research links and managerial capabilities. Test breadth through city concentration, routine-task automation, foreign-demand dependence and enclave ownership of IP. Use the value ladder and dated Survey evidence. Recommend university-industry research, tier-2 infrastructure, apprenticeship, cyber standards and supplier development. Conclude that GCCs transform only when captive mandates create transferable capability beyond the enclave.

**Unique scoring rubric (10 marks):** 2 value ladder + 3 cloud/AI mechanism + 3 spillovers/limits + 2 upgrading remedy.

### Concept check

**Question:** When does a GCC become a domestic capability engine rather than an enclave?

**Model answer:** When higher-value mandates, worker mobility, domestic suppliers, research links and transferable knowledge diffuse beyond the captive centre. Headcount alone does not prove spillovers.

**Misconception to avoid:** Foreign ownership or scale does not automatically create domestic technology diffusion.

### A destination is a chain

```text
visa/search -> travel -> local transport -> stay -> food -> experience -> payment
       \________ logistics and professional enablers ___________/
One broken link lowers the value of the whole bundle.
```

### Composite output and the weakest link

Tourism is a Mode 2 export when a foreign visitor consumes in India. Its output is a bundle of connectivity, safety, sanitation, accommodation, heritage/ecology management, payments and local experiences. Public goods and coordination matter because the weakest link shapes willingness to visit and spend.

Logistics coordinates transport, storage, warehousing, freight information, customs and last-mile delivery. Total cost includes freight, inventory financing, uncertainty, damage, paperwork and time. If goods worth Rs 10 lakh are delayed 20 days and capital costs 12 per cent annually, simple financing cost is approximately:

`10,00,000 x 0.12 x 20/365 = Rs 6,575`.

This excludes spoilage and lost sales. Reliability may matter more than a small nominal freight-price reduction.

Professional services--law, accounting, architecture, engineering, health and education--are knowledge-intensive and often licence-bound. They can use all GATS modes. Trade depends on qualification recognition, liability rules, ethics, data protection and trust.

### Tourism and professional-services evidence

Tourism is jointly produced by transport, accommodation, food, local mobility, guides, culture, safety, sanitation, digital discovery and payments. The Ministry of Tourism dashboard reports **9.15 million foreign tourist arrivals in 2025** and foreign-exchange earnings of about **Rs 2.7 lakh crore (approximately USD 31.69 billion)**. These are dated indicators, not permanent capacities; interpretation also needs length of stay, domestic tourism, prices and local retention.

Professional services--legal, accounting, architecture, engineering, consulting, health and education--depend on trust, licensing and recognised qualifications. Mode 1 can deliver advice remotely; Mode 4 can move a professional temporarily. Mutual Recognition Agreements can lower qualification barriers without erasing public-interest quality regulation.

### Domestic multipliers and leakage

Tourism receipts can leak through imported inputs, foreign booking intermediaries and external ownership. Local procurement, guides, crafts, food and community enterprises raise domestic value added. Visitor volume without carrying-capacity control can destroy the ecological or heritage asset.

Logistics productivity spreads to farms and factories through lower loss, smaller buffer inventories and wider markets. Professional entry restrictions may protect quality or simply protect incumbents; the correct test is transparent proportionality to a public-interest risk.

### Counterargument and limit

**Criticism:** labour-intensive services generate seasonal, precarious work and ecological pressure. **Reply:** standards, skilling, urban services, formal contracts and carrying-capacity plans improve quality. **Qualified verdict:** maximise local value and resilience per visitor or shipment, not gross volume.

### UPSC application and verified linkage

**2024 GS-III Q12** on UDAN tests regional connectivity; **2024 GS-III Q6** tests electronic toll collection. Both are official-paper demands. Topic 18 owns infrastructure detail; this lesson uses them for service-chain productivity only.

### High-risk traps

- Tourism receipts are not equal to local income.
- Logistics cost includes time and uncertainty.
- Professional regulation is not automatically protectionism.
- More tourists can lower long-run value after carrying-capacity breach.

### Register-ready revision points

- Tourism is a composite experience service.
- Inbound tourism is Mode 2 export.
- Local sourcing determines the multiplier.
- Logistics includes information and coordination.
- Inventory delay has financing cost.
- Reliability may dominate freight price.
- Professional services depend on credentials and trust.
- Recognition differs from deregulation.
- Seasonality affects job quality.
- Measure value, resilience and local retention.

### Lesson-specific Mains micro-model

**Question (10 marks, maximum 150 words):** How can India expand labour-intensive services without sacrificing quality and sustainability?

**Complete micro-model:** For tourism, improve destination public goods, local supply chains and carrying-capacity governance. For logistics, integrate multimodal infrastructure, information and predictable customs to reduce total landed cost. For professional services, combine standards with transparent licensing and mutual recognition. Add worker skills, contracts and social protection. Use the delay-cost calculation. End with domestic value added and decent jobs per unit of ecological/logistical resource, not gross traffic alone.

**Unique scoring rubric (10 marks):** 2 chain definition + 3 tourism/logistics mechanism + 3 professional-service evidence + 2 qualification.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why can better tourism logistics raise income even if the ticket price does not fall?

**Model answer:** Reliability lowers buffers, waiting and uncertainty; longer stays and local sourcing can raise retained value. The gain lies in the whole service chain, not only the fare.

**Misconception to avoid:** Tourism is not a single hotel output, and visitor spending is not identical to local value added.

### What counts as the digital economy?

```text
Digital inputs             Digital processes              Digital outputs
connectivity/devices --> cloud/software/data/AI --> apps, content, platforms
             \________ transaction and welfare effects ________/
```

The digital economy is not the value of every electronic payment. It includes digitally produced or enabled value: ICT goods/services, online platforms, cloud/software, data-driven processes and digitally delivered products. The MeitY-ICRIER report estimated it at **11.74 per cent of national income in 2022-23**, employing **14.67 million workers**, and projected a share approaching **20 per cent of GVA by 2029-30**. This is a dated model-based estimate and projection, not an achieved 2026 share.

Data are non-rival in use, but collection, cleaning, compute, rights and access are scarce. Scale can improve models while exclusive data can entrench firms. Cloud lowers entry cost but may transfer dependence to infrastructure providers. AI can reduce search and prediction costs, but biased proxies, opacity and errors require accountable human review.

E-commerce is a method of ordering and coordinating trade, not an exemption from tax, consumer, competition or foreign-investment rules. A marketplace connects third-party sellers; an inventory model owns or controls goods sold to consumers. The distinction affects liability and India's foreign-invested e-commerce perimeter.

### Stack, not super-app

```text
identity/discovery -> consent/message -> payment/data rails
       |                    |                 |
 private and public applications compete above shared standards
       +--- access, security, remedy, audit and accountability ---+
```

Digital public infrastructure is population-scale shared capability built around interoperable standards, registries or rails. It is not synonymous with government ownership of every application, one central database, or compulsory use. Identity, payment and consent-based data exchange perform different functions and should not be collapsed into one stack.

Architecture separates foundational functions, transactional messages and applications. Governance determines who participates, what data are collected, how authentication occurs, who bears loss and how exclusion is remedied.

### Aadhaar-DBT: the identity rail becomes an economic service only through a delivery chain

```text
verified beneficiary record
        -> Aadhaar-enabled identity/authentication where applicable
        -> mapped bank account
        -> government payment instruction
        -> bank credit and cash-out/payment access
        -> correction and grievance route
```

Aadhaar is an identity layer; Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) is a delivery arrangement; the bank account holds the deposit claim. Joining the three can reduce duplicate records, physical handling and some intermediary leakage, but the chain works only when the beneficiary record is correct, authentication succeeds, the account is usable and a failed payment can be repaired. Authentication therefore proves a credential match at a point in time; it is not consent for unrelated data use and it is not proof that the intended person received usable value.

This is a stable mechanism rather than a new September 2026 scale claim. Its analytical limit matters: a cleaner transfer ledger can coexist with exclusion caused by seeding error, biometric failure, dormant accounts, distance to cash-out points or weak grievance handling. The policy reply is not to abandon digital delivery, but to retain alternative identification, assisted/offline channels, auditable exception handling and time-bound correction.

### Network mechanism

Direct network effects raise value with users on the same network. Cross-side effects connect groups: more merchants attract users and more users attract merchants. Open standards can pool network effects at the rail while preserving app competition. A closed platform controlling discovery, data and ranking can instead turn the same effect into lock-in.

Let user benefit be `B = b + nN - p`, where `b` is stand-alone benefit, `N` network size, `n` sensitivity to network size and `p` effective adoption cost. Adoption occurs when B is positive. DPI can lower p through common rails and raise usable N through interoperability. Authentication failure, weak connectivity or poor remedy raises effective p for vulnerable users.

Interoperability reduces the need for all users to join one proprietary platform. It does not guarantee equal visibility, low concentration or safety. Control may migrate to default apps, interfaces, data analytics or cloud infrastructure.

### Metaverse: a networked environment, not a headset

The **metaverse** is the idea of persistent, shared and potentially interoperable three-dimensional virtual environments in which users can interact, create or transact through digital identities. A virtual-reality headset is one access device; a game is one application; neither by itself establishes an interoperable metaverse. Economically, the same DPI/platform questions reappear: identity portability, digital-asset rights, switching costs, moderation, payment interoperability and control over discovery.

The analogy stops at the word “world.” A virtual environment remains governed by code, contracts, platforms and ordinary law; calling an item virtual does not settle ownership, taxation or consumer remedy. The **2024 Prelims Q48** route therefore tests the definition and its boundaries, not a claim that one current commercial platform has already created a universal metaverse.

### Surveillance and resilience objection

**Strongest criticism:** shared rails create surveillance, cyber and single-point governance risk. **Reply:** data minimisation, federated design where suitable, tokenisation, redundancy, open audits and purpose limitation reduce risk. **Qualified verdict:** call a system infrastructure only when openness is matched by enforceable governance, meaningful alternatives and grievance redress.

### UPSC application and verified linkage

**2023 GS-III Q2** on digitalisation asks status, problems and improvements. DPI supplies an architecture paragraph; inclusion, privacy and cyber risk supply critique. The actual PYQ is not solved.

### High-risk traps

- DPI does not mean government-owned apps.
- Interoperability does not eliminate concentration.
- Authentication is not consent.
- Aadhaar, DBT and a bank account perform different functions.
- A headset or a single game is not synonymous with the metaverse.
- Aggregate scale is not universal access.

Fintech is a delivery/technology model, not a category that replaces lender regulation. Digital credit unbundles acquisition, KYC, data analysis, underwriting, disbursal, servicing and collection across a regulated entity (RE) and lending service provider (LSP). Always identify who books the loan.

### BNPL arithmetic

Buy Now Pay Later is credit even when checkout language is soft. Revenue can come from merchant subsidy, interest, mandatory fees, late charges or cross-selling. For Rs 10,000 due in 30 days with a mandatory Rs 300 fee, simple one-month cost is 3 per cent; mechanical simple annualisation is about 36 per cent before compounding. Governing APR methodology decides formal disclosure, but “only Rs 300” is economically incomplete.

Multiple small facilities can produce invisible leverage. Credit reporting, affordability and dark-pattern control matter even where each loan is small.

### Account Aggregator roles

An NBFC-AA manages consent-based transmission between Financial Information Providers (FIPs) and Financial Information Users (FIUs). The artefact specifies purpose, data, frequency and duration. The AA is not data owner, credit bureau or lender; consent to share does not compel approval. The lender must still establish lawful processing, ability to repay and security.

The **Open Credit Enablement Network (OCEN)** is a distinct protocol idea for standardising parts of digital credit discovery and origination so that loan-service providers and lenders can communicate through common specifications. AA answers, “How may consented financial information move?” OCEN answers, “How may a credit request and product journey be expressed across participants?” Neither is the lender, neither guarantees sanction, and neither removes the regulated entity's underwriting and consumer-protection duties.

No current OCEN adoption-volume claim is made here because coverage and implementation are evolving and require a separately dated official source. The stable exam distinction is functional: AA is consented data transmission; OCEN is an enablement protocol for the credit-origination workflow.

Alternative data may expand inclusion by lowering verification cost, yet opaque scoring can reproduce discrimination and trigger over-lending. Purpose limitation, explainability, audit, proportional data and human grievance review are the answer.

**Strongest risk:** an app-led chain fragments responsibility. **Reply:** attach accountability to the regulated balance-sheet lender and control points. **Verdict:** technology may decentralise functions but must not decentralise accountability into invisibility.

### What is the legal-economic claim?

```text
cash/e-rupee -> direct RBI liability
bank deposit -> commercial-bank liability
UPI -> payment instruction/rail, usually moving deposits
crypto/VDA -> private digital asset; no sovereign par promise
tokenisation -> representation technique; rights depend on the underlying claim
```

Physical currency and e-rupee are RBI liabilities. A bank deposit is a commercial-bank liability under banking regulation. UPI is a payment means, not the liability. A crypto-asset may be transferable without a sovereign promise of par redemption. A token may represent money, securities, property rights or nothing enforceable; technology does not determine legal character.

RBI's FAQ updated **29 April 2026** states that e-rupee remains in pilot in retail and wholesale segments. Retail e-rupee is held in wallets offered by participating banks/non-banks, is legal tender and an RBI liability, pays no interest, and supports wallet-to-wallet payments. Scanning UPI QR from an e-rupee app is enabled. Offline and programmability uses are being explored. Nineteen banks were listed as wallet providers on that update date. These are dated pilot facts, not final universal rollout.

### Settlement and banking effects

A wallet-to-wallet e-rupee payment transfers the central-bank liability without passing through user bank accounts. Conventional UPI changes deposit claims and creates interbank settlement obligations. CBDC may reduce some settlement exposures, but large migration from deposits could affect bank funding.

Programmability can constrain purpose, geography, merchant category or expiry. It can reduce leakage but weaken fungibility, privacy and autonomy. Programmable bank payments are not identical to programmable sovereign money.

### VDA and token boundaries

Sections 115BBH and 194S tax specified VDA transfers; this does not make VDAs legal tender. PMLA coverage imposes anti-money-laundering obligations but is not a full prudential/market-conduct regime. NFT token uniqueness does not prove uniqueness, copyright or value of the underlying work.

### Blockchain before crypto: ledger architecture is not the asset

A **blockchain** is a distributed ledger in which participants hold replicated records and new blocks are linked through cryptographic hashes under a consensus/governance process. Altering an earlier record becomes detectable because it changes the hash chain, but this does not make input data true, confer legal title or guarantee that every network is decentralised. A public permissionless chain, a permissioned consortium chain and an ordinary centralised database therefore differ in who may validate, read, write and change governance rules.

Cryptocurrency is one blockchain application: a cryptographically represented private digital asset transferred under the network's rules. Blockchains may also support tokenised securities, supply-chain records or NFTs, while some digital ledgers and CBDCs need not use a public blockchain at all. This directly serves the **2026 provisional Prelims blockchain route**: distributed replication and cryptographic linkage are architectural properties; “automatic truth,” “automatic legal validity” and “complete decentralisation” are traps.

Tokenisation may improve fractionalisation and transfer records, but “on blockchain” does not prove decentralisation or legal title. Ask: issued by whom, redeemable for what, held where, governed by which law?

### Cryptocurrency and society: benefits, harms and distribution

The strongest economic case is that programmable digital assets can stimulate payment, settlement and tokenisation experiments and may reduce some cross-border frictions where conventional correspondent chains are slow or costly. The limitation is decisive: a volatile private token is a poor unit of account and an unreliable store of value for households that cannot absorb losses. A privately maintained stablecoin peg also depends on reserves, redemption and governance; it does not acquire sovereign legal-tender status.

The societal effects are wider than investment returns:

| Channel | Possible gain | Principal harm or qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Payments/remittances | faster programmable or cross-border transfer experiments | volatility, conversion cost, congestion and uncertain final legal claim |
| Innovation | tokenisation and new settlement designs | technology label can conceal weak governance or no enforceable asset |
| Household finance | wider access to a new asset class | speculation, fraud, custody loss and unequal ability to bear volatility |
| Illicit finance | transparent public ledgers may aid tracing in some cases | pseudonymous addresses, mixers and cross-border service providers can obstruct enforcement |
| Environment | proof-of-stake and permissioned designs can use less energy | proof-of-work validation can consume substantial electricity |
| Sovereignty/stability | competitive pressure may improve payment systems | currency substitution, capital-flow and monetary-sovereignty concerns can arise at scale |

The strongest criticism is that the innovation narrative socialises fraud, energy and financial-stability risks while privatising speculative gain. The reply is that the risks differ by design and activity; a permissioned settlement token is not analytically identical to an unbacked retail crypto-asset. The balanced verdict is activity-based regulation: enforce AML/KYC, custody, disclosure, advertising, tax and consumer rules without treating taxation as endorsement or assuming every distributed-ledger use should be prohibited.

**Verdict:** classify by issuer, liability, redemption and function before discussing technology. CBDC should scale where it delivers a distinct public purpose with privacy, resilience and controlled banking effects.

### Divide and measurement map

```text
ACCESS: device/connectivity/electricity
   -> CAPABILITY: literacy/language/disability/trust
      -> OUTCOME: income, credit, learning and bargaining power
DIGITAL OUTPUT = priced core + enabled value added + unpriced welfare indicators
```

Coverage is only the first gate. A connected household may lack an affordable private device, literacy, local-language interface, accessibility or trust after fraud. Gender, disability, age, place and income interact. Assisted and offline channels are productive infrastructure, not backward leftovers.

The January 2025 MeitY-ICRIER report develops a broad digital-economy measurement framework. Start with digital industries' GVA, then estimate digitally enabled activity transparently through supply-use or satellite-account methods. Do not add transaction values: a Rs 1,000 payment is not Rs 1,000 of new GDP.

If a shop shifts Rs 10 lakh of existing sales from cash to UPI with unchanged output, GDP does not rise by Rs 10 lakh. If payment time falls and the same inputs serve 5 per cent more customers, measured output may rise. Convenience can improve welfare even where market output does not fully capture surplus.

### Tax and legal boundaries

Section 115BBH specifies treatment of income from VDA transfer; section 194S imposes 1 per cent TDS subject to statutory conditions/thresholds. Taxation does not confer legal-tender status. GST treatment follows the underlying supply and platform role, not the payment method. Cross-border digital taxation must distinguish income-tax nexus, consumption tax, customs and treaty allocation. Do not quote a historical equalisation-levy rate as current without the operative year and provision.

### Productivity or cost shifting?

A platform may automate matching while shifting search, data entry or dispute work to users and suppliers. Separate real resource saving from unpaid task transfer, market rent and privacy loss. Average gains can coexist with exclusion.

**Verdict:** use a dashboard of digital GVA, firm productivity, adoption, consumer-surplus proxies, job quality, concentration, cyber loss and inclusion gaps, each with method and vintage.

- Taxation is not legal-tender recognition.
- Payment method alone does not determine GST.
- Productivity may hide cost shifting.

- Keep assisted/offline alternatives.
- Digital GVA is value added, not throughput.
- Satellite accounts broaden measurement transparently.
- Free-service surplus is partly outside GDP.
- Digital payment enables but does not mechanically create output.

### 1. Measurement beyond headline productivity

Real GVA per worker is useful, but service quality is hard to deflate. Safer payments, better diagnosis or free search may create welfare not fully captured in revenue; platform transaction value may expand without equal domestic value added. Advanced answers distinguish production, consumer surplus, rents, imported cloud/compute inputs and distribution. Baumol's cost disease is a boundary case: some personal services cannot raise output per hour without changing their nature.

### 2. Data, cloud and AI bottlenecks

Data are non-rival but access can be exclusive. Feedback economies can improve a model as use generates more data. Cloud lowers startup fixed cost yet concentration among hyperscale providers creates egress fees, technical dependence and correlated outage risk. AI governance must separate developer, deployer, data fiduciary, professional user and affected person. A lender cannot outsource regulatory responsibility to a model; human review must be meaningful for adverse credit, allocation or deactivation decisions.

### 3. Dynamic platform contestability

A market-share snapshot can miss tipping. Ask whether users can multihome, move history/reputation, reach counterparties and obtain fair discovery. Self-preferencing, tying, parity clauses, exclusive data and default placement can preserve power despite legal entry. Interoperability reduces coordination cost but badly governed standards can freeze incumbent choices or enlarge attack surfaces. Use participatory standard-setting, versioning, security certification and proportionate access.

### 4. Distributed fintech accountability

Digital lending unbundles lead generation, data, underwriting, funding, servicing and recovery. Specialisation can lower cost, but the consumer experiences one journey. RBI's Digital Lending Directions keep the regulated entity responsible for LSPs and require direct fund flows, Key Fact Statements, grievance arrangements and data discipline. Account Aggregator lowers verification cost, but consent fatigue and bargaining inequality require minimisation, purpose limits, revocation, adverse-decision explanation and non-digital alternatives.

### 5. CBDC design matrix

| Axis | Choice | Benefit | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| Access | retail / wholesale | public use / settlement efficiency | distinct inclusion and operational needs |
| Record | token-like / account-like | cash resemblance / identity control | recovery, anonymity and fraud differ |
| Connectivity | online / offline | speed / resilience | double-spend and device-security challenge |
| Function | general / programmable | fungibility / targeted use | surveillance and restriction concerns |
| Distribution | direct / intermediated | central control / bank-fintech reach | burden / intermediary dependence |
| Privacy | cash-like / traceable tiers | autonomy / AML and recovery | no design maximises all objectives |

CBDC is not automatically superior to UPI. It needs a distinct public purpose such as resilient sovereign digital cash, offline use or settlement finality. Deposit migration can affect bank funding; caps, tiering and intermediated distribution can moderate this risk.

### 6. Regulatory perimeter and phased data governance

Digital markets cut across RBI, NPCI, SEBI, IRDAI, PFRDA, CCI, MeitY and consumer authorities. Regulate by function and harm while preserving a clear lead authority and complaint path. A crypto tax rule is not a market-conduct licence. The DPDP Act, 2023 and Rules notified in November 2025 have phased commencement. Through 3 October 2026, the safe formulation is **enacted and partly commenced**; verify a specific duty before calling it operative.

This is the principal live linkage: scale has shifted the frontier from adoption alone to safe, portable and accountable use. The September 2026 fintech docket's focus on agentic AI and tokenisation reinforces the need to judge novelty through inclusion, explainability, liability and resilience.

# CUMULATIVE CONCEPT CHECKS

## Check 1 - structural transformation

**Question:** How can India combine high-productivity services with broad employment?

**Model answer:** Upgrade IT-BPM, GCCs, professional services and digital exports while raising productivity, formality and local retention in tourism, logistics, care, retail and personal services. Judge progress through output, jobs, real earnings, protection and diffusion.

**Misconception to avoid:** Services-led growth is neither automatically jobless nor automatically inclusive.

## Check 2 - digital economy boundary

**Question:** Distinguish digital output, digital transaction and digital welfare.

**Model answer:** Digital output is measured value added; a digital transaction is an electronic exchange mechanism; digital welfare includes surplus, convenience and distribution. They overlap but are not equal.

**Misconception to avoid:** Payment value cannot be added to GDP as newly produced output.

## Check 3 - fintech accountability

**Question:** Why must a regulated lender remain accountable when an LSP or AI model performs key tasks?

**Model answer:** The consumer receives one regulated credit product. Specialisation does not transfer statutory responsibility; the lender must govern partners, fund flow, disclosures, data, model risk and grievance remedy.

**Misconception to avoid:** Outsourcing a process does not outsource legal responsibility.

## Check 4 - money and payments

**Question:** Place cash, bank deposits, UPI, CBDC and private crypto on one liability map.

**Model answer:** Cash and CBDC are central-bank liabilities; deposits are commercial-bank liabilities; UPI mainly moves deposits; private crypto lacks a sovereign liability.

**Misconception to avoid:** A digital transfer method does not determine who issued the money.

## Check 5 - platform contestability

**Question:** When do network effects become a competition concern?

**Model answer:** When feedback combines with switching costs, exclusive data, non-portable reputation, default control or weak multihoming so efficient rivals cannot reach users.

**Misconception to avoid:** Neither scale nor low price alone proves abuse.

## Check 6 - inclusion and trust

**Question:** Why does digital access not equal digital capability?

**Model answer:** Capability also requires devices, affordability, literacy, language/accessibility, fraud awareness, usable authentication, confidence and timely remedy.

**Misconception to avoid:** Connections and registrations are inputs, not complete outcomes.

# ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS MODEL PRACTICE

## 10-mark practice

### Question 1 - 150 words

**Explain why digital-payment scale should not be confused with either monetary sovereignty or inclusive digital transformation.**

**Model answer:** Digital-payment scale measures throughput, not the issuer of money or distribution of capability. A UPI transfer usually reallocates commercial-bank deposits; it is not a transfer of RBI-issued CBDC. Monetary sovereignty concerns the unit of account, legal tender and central-bank liability. Inclusion asks whether citizens possess affordable devices, connectivity, literacy, local-language access, accessibility, trust and remedy. High transaction counts can coexist with exclusion, fraud and concentration. Policy should therefore report unique active users, merchant acceptance, rural/gender gaps, failure and complaint resolution alongside volume/value. It should preserve cash, assisted and offline routes, enforce liability and invest in capability. The correct conclusion is that interoperable payment scale is valuable infrastructure, but sovereign-money status follows the claim/issuer and inclusion follows effective access and safe use.

**Unique scoring rubric (10 marks):** 2 liability/payment distinction + 3 inclusion mechanism + 3 safeguards/evidence + 2 qualified conclusion.

### Question 2 - 150 words

**How do cross-side network effects influence platform pricing?**

**Model answer:** In a two-sided platform, participation by one group raises value for another. More consumers attract merchants; more merchants improve consumer choice. The platform therefore optimises a price structure, not merely one aggregate price. It may subsidise the price-sensitive or network-creating side and monetise the other through commission, advertising or ancillary service. Such cross-subsidy can solve a coordination failure and is not inherently predatory. Yet strong feedback, switching cost and exclusive data can tip the market. Competition analysis must examine multihoming, portability, ranking, parity clauses, data access, foreclosure and recoupment rather than call every zero price benign or abusive. Interoperability and portable reputation can preserve the network benefit while lowering lock-in.

**Unique scoring rubric (10 marks):** 2 two-sided definition + 3 pricing mechanism + 3 competition remedies + 2 verdict.

## 15-mark practice

### Question 3 - 250 words

**India's services-export success needs a domestic capability strategy, not only market access. Discuss.**

**Model answer:** Market access under GATS and FTAs matters across Mode 1 remote supply, Mode 2 tourism/health/education, Mode 3 commercial presence and Mode 4 temporary professional mobility. India's FY2025-26 services exports were reported at USD 421.3 billion by the Commerce Ministry on 24 July 2026, but gross receipts do not by themselves measure domestic value added or resilience.

A capability strategy has five elements. First, domain, technical, language and managerial skills permit movement from routine BPM to analytics, engineering and R&D. Second, reliable cities, digital rails, logistics and cyber assurance lower delivery cost. Third, research links and domestic suppliers convert GCCs from enclaves into spillover engines. Fourth, transparent professional licensing and mutual recognition protect quality while widening opportunity. Fifth, labour-intensive tourism, logistics, care and professional services spread jobs beyond elite technology clusters.

Risks include automation of routine tasks, foreign-demand dependence, city concentration, data restrictions and protectionism. Responses should diversify markets, support standards, strengthen privacy/cyber institutions, facilitate Mode 4 mobility and measure domestic value added, wage quality and supplier linkages. Thus market access is the external door; capability determines how much value India retains after entering it.

**Unique scoring rubric (15 marks):** 3 GATS and market-access framing + 5 domestic capability pillars with named India evidence + 4 risks and targeted responses + 3 capability-led qualified conclusion.

### Question 4 - 250 words

**Evaluate Account Aggregator architecture as an inclusion tool and a data-governance challenge.**

**Model answer:** An Account Aggregator is an RBI-regulated consent manager that transmits financial information from a Financial Information Provider to a permitted Financial Information User. The consent artefact specifies purpose, data, frequency and duration. It neither owns the information nor grants credit.

Its inclusion mechanism is reduced information and verification cost. A thin-file borrower can consent to share bank, tax or other financial records, enabling cash-flow underwriting and product comparison. Interoperable consent can weaken data silos and reduce repeated document submission.

The same architecture creates risks. Formally obtained consent may be non-comprehending; lenders may demand excessive history; scores may reproduce bias; breach or secondary use can expose sensitive patterns. Consent may also be mistaken for guaranteed approval, while aggressive digital lending creates over-indebtedness.

Safeguards should include data minimisation, plain-language and revocable consent, purpose/retention limits, encryption, audit trails, explainable adverse decisions, human grievance review and strict accountability of the regulated lender. Alternative non-digital routes are necessary. AA can widen credit only when the reduction in information asymmetry does not become a transfer of bargaining power from borrower to opaque scoring systems.

**Unique scoring rubric (15 marks):** 3 AA roles and consent architecture + 4 inclusion and information-cost mechanism + 4 data-governance and over-lending risks + 4 safeguards with a balanced verdict.

## 20-mark practice

### Question 5 - 250 words

**“Open protocols can lower entry barriers, but they cannot by themselves create contestable digital markets.” Analyse with reference to ONDC and platform economics.**

**Model answer:** Contestability means that incumbents face a credible threat of entry and expansion. ONDC separates buyer applications, seller applications, logistics and other participants through common specifications. This can pool network reach without requiring every seller or buyer to join one vertically integrated marketplace. Interoperability lowers the coordination barrier; multihoming and portability can lower switching cost.

However, protocol openness is only one layer. Discovery may remain concentrated in buyer apps; rankings may favour affiliates; ratings may not travel; fraud and returns may expose fragmented liability; smaller sellers may lack catalogue, fulfilment and working-capital capability. Subsidised incumbent ecosystems can still make entry uneconomic. Data gathered at interfaces can recreate a bottleneck above the protocol.

A complete contestability package therefore needs portable reputation, transparent sponsored ranking, non-discriminatory access, common security standards, clear seller-of-record and grievance responsibility, rapid refunds, audit of protocol governance and competition scrutiny of tying/exclusivity. MSME onboarding, logistics quality and sustainable unit economics matter as much as technical connection.

ONDC should not be described as one government marketplace or as replacement of private firms. Its promise is architectural separation. Its success should be measured through seller reach, switching/multihoming, repeat purchase, complaint resolution, concentration at interface layers and subsidy-adjusted economics. Thus an open protocol supplies the road; fair discovery, trust, capability and enforceable rules determine whether rivals can actually travel it.

**Unique scoring rubric (20 marks):** 4 contestability mechanism + 6 ONDC evidence/counterpoint + 5 remedies/metrics + 5 conclusion.

### Question 6 - 250 words

**Design an integrated policy framework for India’s digital services economy that reconciles innovation, inclusion, competition, worker welfare and security.**

**Model answer:** The framework should use the CLAIMS test. **Capability:** fund foundational literacy, domain skills, accessibility, local-language design, research links and tier-2 city infrastructure. **Liability:** assign clear responsibility to regulated lenders, payment participants, platforms and data fiduciaries, with one complaint entry point. **Access/architecture:** maintain interoperable rails, portability, offline/assisted options and open standards. **Incentives:** disclose cross-subsidies and APR, measure net gig earnings, and avoid pretending zero user price means zero cost. **Market structure/measurement:** monitor ranking, self-preferencing, multihoming and data bottlenecks; measure GVA rather than transaction value. **Security/sustainability:** require minimisation, cyber baselines, redundancy, incident reporting, recovery drills and privacy-preserving design.

Sectorally, India should upgrade IT-BPM/GCCs through R&D and supplier spillovers; expand labour-intensive tourism/logistics with quality and carrying-capacity control; regulate fintech through accountable REs and consent boundaries; use UPI/DPI to widen markets; and scale CBDC only for distinct public purposes. Platform workers need expense-aware pay transparency, portable protection and deactivation due process, cross-linked to labour law.

Internationally, use all GATS modes, recognition arrangements and diversified trade partnerships. Every pilot should publish hypothesis, safeguards, metrics and review. The governing principle is neither state control nor laissez-faire: population-scale public rails should support plural private/social innovation within enforceable rights and contestable markets.

**Unique scoring rubric (20 marks):** 4 integrated thesis + 6 CLAIMS instruments + 5 sector/trade evidence + 5 conclusion.

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

## 1. Services: production and measurement

- A service changes condition, provides access/knowledge, coordinates exchange or pools risk.
- Classify the activity; storage and transport are tertiary even when goods are handled.
- `GVA = output - intermediate consumption`; turnover is not contribution to GDP.
- GDP at market prices adjusts aggregate GVA for product taxes and subsidies.
- Real GVA per worker/hour is labour productivity; TFP additionally controls multiple inputs.
- Outsourcing may change sector shares without changing the physical task.
- Digital quality, free services, informality and bundled goods-services create measurement gaps.
- Services-led growth must be disaggregated by productivity, employment intensity, wages and formality.

## 2. Services trade

- GATS Mode 1: service crosses; Mode 2: consumer crosses; Mode 3: commercial presence; Mode 4: supplier-person temporarily crosses.
- One contract can combine modes.
- GATS coverage and BOP recording differ, especially for Mode 3.
- Market access and national treatment are separate scheduled commitments; MFN compares partners.
- Professional exports also require recognition, licensing, liability and data safeguards.
- Commerce Ministry, 24 July 2026: FY2025-26 services exports USD 421.3 billion; keep date and reporting period.

## 3. IT-BPM and GCCs

- IT services provide technology; BPM reorganises processes; GCC is a multinational's captive offshore unit.
- Economic Survey 2025-26: industry estimate of USD 283 billion FY2025 IT-ITeS revenue including hardware; over 1,700 GCCs and over 19 lakh professionals as of FY2024.
- Value ladder: processing -> managed operations -> analytics -> engineering/R&D -> product mandate.
- AI substitutes routine tasks and complements architecture, domain expertise and verification.
- Spillovers require worker mobility, local suppliers, university links and Indian managerial mandates.
- Revenue, domestic value added and employment are not interchangeable.

## 4. Tourism, logistics and professional services

- Inbound tourism is Mode 2 export and a composite chain of public/private services.
- Domestic gain depends on local retention; gross receipts can leak.
- Logistics cost includes freight, time, uncertainty, damage and inventory financing.
- Professional-service restrictions should be transparent and proportionate to quality/public risk.
- Optimise local value and decent jobs subject to ecological carrying capacity.

## 5. Digital economy, data, cloud, AI and DPI

- Digital economy output is not the value of every electronic transaction.
- MeitY-ICRIER estimated 11.74 per cent of national income and 14.67 million workers in 2022-23; the 20 per cent GVA figure for 2029-30 is a projection.
- Data are non-rival in use, but collection, compute, legal access and control are scarce.
- Cloud lowers fixed costs while creating switching, outage and infrastructure-concentration risks.
- AI can automate routine tasks and augment judgement; accountable human review remains necessary for consequential decisions.
- Marketplace and inventory e-commerce differ by stock/control and seller liability.
- DPI is shared interoperable capability, not one government super-app.
- Separate foundational/discovery, transactional/consent and application layers.
- Cross-side network effects can be pooled at an open rail while apps compete.
- Interoperability reduces lock-in but does not guarantee competition, privacy or security.
- Governance requires minimisation, redundancy, alternatives, audit, clear liability and remedy.
- Adoption equation: `B = b + nN - p`; lower effective cost and larger usable network promote adoption.

## 6. UPI and payment mechanics

- Separate funding asset, message/switch, clearing and settlement.
- Ordinary UPI generally moves commercial-bank deposits; UPI is not money or CBDC.
- NPCI operates UPI and NFS; RBI regulates payment systems.
- NFS is ATM switching.
- MDR is merchant-side acceptance price; interchange is an inter-participant transfer.
- Zero MDR reallocates cost rather than eliminating it.
- RBI Annual Report 2025-26, released 29 May 2026: UPI volume 24,16,169 lakh; value Rs 314.2 lakh crore; nearly 86 per cent of retail-payment volume.
- Rough annual average transaction value: Rs 1,300; not a user count.
- Authentication does not defeat social engineering; liability and rapid remedy remain essential.

## 7. Fintech lending and AA

- Fintech unbundles acquisition, KYC, underwriting, disbursal, service and collection.
- Identify the regulated balance-sheet lender; outsourcing to LSP does not remove RE responsibility.
- RBI Digital Lending Directions issued 8 May 2025 govern disclosure, fund flow, data, cooling-off and grievance.
- BNPL is credit; mandatory short-tenor fees can imply high annualised cost.
- AA transmits information through a scoped consent artefact; it is not data owner, bureau or lender.
- FIP supplies data; FIU uses it for authorised purpose.
- Consent to share does not guarantee approval.
- Inclusion requires minimisation, affordability, explainability, audit and human grievance review.

## 8. CBDC, crypto, VDA and tokenisation

- E-rupee: RBI liability, legal tender, non-interest bearing in current retail design.
- Deposit: commercial-bank liability. UPI: payment rail. Crypto/VDA: private digital asset without general sovereign par promise.
- RBI FAQ updated 29 April 2026: retail/wholesale pilots continue; wallet-to-wallet payments, UPI-QR scanning, offline/programmability exploration; 19 listed banks.
- Wallet-to-wallet e-rupee may settle without passing through user bank accounts.
- CBDC can affect bank funding if deposits migrate.
- Programmability trades targeting against fungibility, privacy and autonomy.
- VDA taxation/PMLA obligations do not confer legal-tender status or a complete conduct regime.
- Tokenisation is a representation technique; inspect issuer, claim, custody, redemption and governing law.
- NFT uniqueness does not automatically transfer copyright/title.

## 9. Platform-market economics

- Two-sided platforms choose a price structure because each side creates value for another.
- Zero user price can be funded by merchant fees, ads, data or cross-selling.
- Tipping rises with network effects, data feedback, scale and switching cost; falls with multihoming, portability and interoperability.
- Data can improve matching and create an entry barrier; assess relevance, exclusivity and replicability.
- Market share alone does not prove abuse.
- Predation requires foreclosure and recoupment/durable-harm logic.
- Competition tools should target ranking, tying, parity, self-preferencing, switching and data bottlenecks while preserving efficiency.

## 10. Contestability and digital-commerce models

- Interoperability = communicate; portability = move data/reputation; multihoming = use several networks.
- ONDC is open-network architecture across participant applications, not one marketplace.
- Protocol openness needs discoverability, standards, secure messages, clear roles and common grievance routing.
- Dropshipping: seller controls storefront/pricing, supplier holds and ships inventory.
- Affiliate: primarily refers traffic for commission.
- Crowdfunding may be donation, reward, debt or equity; rights differ.
- 2026 official-paper numbering used here: Q87 dropshipping, Q89 ONDC, Q90 UPI/CBDC, Q97 crowdfunding; official key provisional.

## 11. Gig/platform work

- Use net earnings after commission, fuel, data, equipment, maintenance and insurance.
- Divide by work-related logged-in hours including waiting.
- Schedule flexibility differs from control over price, ranking and deactivation.
- Portable benefits solve continuity, not every classification or wage issue.
- Ratings can reduce information asymmetry and transmit bias.
- Require pay transparency, portable cover, notice/reasons/appeal and worker data/reputation access.
- Topic 22 owns complete labour-law treatment.

## 12. Competition, privacy, consumer and cyber governance

- Competition protects the market process; privacy governs data; consumer law addresses unfair terms/loss; cyber and sector rules govern system integrity.
- CCI January 2020 study concerns are theories for evidence, not blanket findings.
- DPDP Rules notified 13 November 2025 with phased commencement; check each provision on the relevant date.
- Consent is insufficient without minimisation, retention/security controls and rights.
- Cyber externalities justify network baselines, audits, incident reporting, recovery and common-provider resilience.
- Map liability to control, but provide consumers one complaint entry point.

## 13. Divide, tax and measurement

- Divide layers: access, capability and outcome.
- Assisted/offline alternatives remain necessary.
- Digital transaction value is not digital GVA.
- Core-industry GVA, digitally enabled activity and welfare satellite indicators should be reported separately.
- Free consumer surplus is partly outside GDP.
- VDA tax is separate from monetary recognition.
- GST follows the underlying supply/platform role, not payment method.
- Do not repeat stale equalisation-levy rates without operative provision and year.
- Track inclusion, job quality, concentration and cyber loss alongside productivity.

## 14. Integrated answer spine

```text
Define and classify
-> explain technical/economic mechanism
-> attach dated India evidence
-> identify distribution and market structure
-> present strongest criticism
-> propose proportionate safeguards
-> give qualified verdict and measurable outcomes
```

Apply **CLAIMS**: Capability, Liability, Access/architecture, Incentives, Market structure/measurement, Security/sustainability. India's strategy should combine high-productivity tradable services with labour-intensive domestic services; population-scale interoperable rails with plural applications; innovation with rights; and exports with domestic supplier, worker and research spillovers.

## 15. Optional Advanced recall

- Quality-adjusted service productivity can diverge from revenue-based productivity.
- Baumol's cost disease remains relevant where human time is intrinsic to service quality.
- Data feedback, cloud dependence and default interfaces can relocate bottleneck power above an open protocol.
- Dynamic contestability asks whether users can multihome, move reputation and obtain fair discovery.
- Interoperability needs secure standard-setting, versioning and proportionate access.
- Digital-lending unbundling does not remove the regulated entity's responsibility.
- Formal AA consent can still be weak when bargaining power and literacy are unequal.
- CBDC design varies across retail/wholesale, token/account, online/offline, general/programmable, direct/intermediated and privacy choices.
- DPDP implementation is phased; verify the commencement of the specific obligation.
- Payments Vision 2028 reflects the shift from adoption alone to portability, fraud responsibility, resilience and trust.
