### Authority ladder

```text
SURVEY: diagnosis/projection -> BUDGET: BE/RE and authority -> OFFICIAL OUTTURN: NSO/RBI/CGA/Commerce -> revision
```

*The visual fixes the boundary before interpretation.*

### Why evidence status comes before inference

The first macro question is documentary: who produced the claim, for which period, and with what legal or statistical status? The Survey can diagnose and recommend but cannot appropriate money. A Budget Estimate is authorised intention, not proof of execution.

### Who can claim what?

- Survey=analysis and conditional projection
- Budget=BE/RE and fiscal proposal
- NSO=estimates and revisions
- RBI=policy/finance/external data
- CGA=cash accounts
- Commerce=trade release

### Survey-to-outturn vintage drill

The Survey FY26 real-growth figure of 7.4% was a First Advance Estimate and its FY27 6.8-7.2% was a projection. Neither is a September 2026 realised outcome.

### Objection: “official is enough”

Metadata seems cumbersome, but a compact parenthesis—source, period, status—prevents a much larger error.

### Exam verbs that preserve authority

Use verbs precisely: the Survey argues, the Budget provides, CGA provisionally records and MoSPI estimates.

### Authority recall card

- Survey=analysis and conditional projection
- Budget=BE/RE and fiscal proposal
- NSO=estimates and revisions
- RBI=policy/finance/external data
- CGA=cash accounts
- Commerce=trade release
- Every mutable figure needs period, unit/base, release, source and status.
- A headline requires composition and a counter-signal.
- A dated estimate should not become a timeless claim.

The same economy can have a high level but low growth, a rising share but weak contribution, and a large reserve stock beside an adverse trade flow. Most dashboard failures begin as grammar failures.

- Level=absolute magnitude
- growth=change against comparator
- share=component/total
- ratio=numerator/denominator

### Price-volume laboratory

```text
NOMINAL=current price x quantity | REAL=base price x quantity | DEFLATOR=(nominal/real)x100
```

*The visual fixes the boundary before interpretation.*

### The wheat-value intuition

If the same wheat quantity sells at a higher price, nominal output rises but real output does not. Real accounts seek the volume movement; nominal accounts matter for tax bases, incomes and debt ratios.

### Real, nominal and deflator identities

- Nominal=current-price value
- real=constant-price volume
- implicit deflator=nominal/real
- exact price-volume split is multiplicative, not simple subtraction

### Exact deflator calculation

Nominal rises 110 to 121 (10%); real 100 to 108 (8%); deflator 110 to 112.04 (about 1.85%). The 2-point growth gap is only an approximation.

### Residual-measure limitation

The deflator is broad domestic-output pricing and revision-sensitive; it is not household CPI. Use each index for its own boundary.

### Using Q1 FY27 without overclaim

Q1 FY27’s nominal-real gap does not mean every price rose by 2.5%.

### Formula recall card

### Why rebound is not recovery

A 20% fall from 100 gives 80; a 20% rise gives 96. A rebound rate can look spectacular while the level remains below its starting point.

### Base, season, pace and carry-over

- Base effect=unusual comparator
- seasonality=recurring calendar pattern
- annualisation=compounded short-period pace
- carry-over=prior exit level affects annual average
- momentum=recent sequential movement

### 100-80-96 recovery drill

Show an index path beside rates. A high year-on-year rate against a lockdown quarter can coexist with weak sequential momentum.

### Seasonal-adjustment limitation

Seasonal adjustment is method-dependent. Where official adjustment is absent, use same-quarter comparisons, multi-month averages and multiple indicators rather than pretending precision.

### V-shape trap control

UPSC traps: rebound is not level recovery; annualised pace is not realised annual outcome.

### Why revisions are necessary

Revision is the price of timely statistics. Policy cannot wait for final benchmarks, but early estimates must never be presented as permanent truths.

### FAE-to-rebasing taxonomy

- FAE=first incomplete annual estimate
- SAE=more information but advance
- provisional=post-year still revisable
- revised=fuller data
- rebasing=changed reference year/weights/sources/methods

### FY21 7.3-to-6.6 drill

The FY21 contraction was 7.3% in the May 2021 provisional estimate and 6.6% in the January 2022 revision. Cite both when the revision itself matters.

### Confidence and revision transparency

Documented revision strengthens integrity; opaque cherry-picking weakens it. A new base needs a bridge/back series for strict comparison.

### Expenditure identity map

```text
GDP=PFCE+GFCE+GFCF+stocks+valuables+exports-imports+discrepancies | contribution ~= prior share x growth
```

*The visual fixes the boundary before interpretation.*

### Who bought the output?

Expenditure decomposition asks who demanded final output. It is an accounting map, not proof that components are causally independent.

### Demand-component boundaries

- PFCE=private consumption
- GFCE=government consumption
- GFCF=fixed investment
- stocks=inventories
- valuables=store-of-value assets
- net exports=exports-imports
- discrepancies=balance

### Contribution arithmetic lab

A 60% component growing 5% contributes about 3 points; a 30% component growing 10% also contributes about 3. Exact chain-volume contribution needs official methods.

### Accounting is not causation

GFCF is not identical to private corporate capex. Imports subtract because GDP is domestic production, though machinery imports may support future capacity.

### PFCE-GFCF answer use

Write the identity, calculate approximate contribution, then test durability, distribution and institutional composition.

### Demand-side recall card

# ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS MODEL PRACTICE

## 10 marks - Why must a macro dashboard distinguish Survey, Budget and realised data?

**Model answer:** A macro dashboard combines documents with different functions. The Economic Survey is an analytical review: its projections and recommendations are evidence, not legal authority. The Union Budget supplies Budget and Revised Estimates and, after parliamentary approval, fiscal authority; an allocation still does not prove expenditure. MoSPI, RBI, CGA and Commerce publish statistical or cash outturns at different frequencies and revision stages. Thus the Survey’s 29 January 2026 FY26 FAE and FY27 projection must not be described as September 2026 realised outcomes. Likewise, the FY27 fiscal-deficit BE of 4.3% of GDP differs from CGA’s FY26 provisional unaudited 4.40% outturn. A revision-safe answer tags every number by period, unit/base, release, source and status. The distinction protects both legal accuracy and statistical comparability.

Externally, reserves were US$780.782 billion for 11 September, but August merchandise imports substantially exceeded exports. August CWS unemployment was 5.0% for age 15+, requiring LFPR, real-wage and job-quality checks. The dated picture is resilient in aggregate momentum and buffers but exposed to food-price incidence, execution, employment quality, external financing and revision risk.

## 20 marks - Explain how apparently contradictory macro signals should shape the policy mix rather than be averaged away.

**Model answer:** Contradictory signals often cover different periods, baskets, populations or statistical objects. First align metadata; then decompose causally. Strong GDP with weak jobs may reflect sector mix, productivity, participation or informality. Low headline inflation with high food inflation reflects weights and unequal incidence. High public capex with weak private investment can arise from execution lags, excess capacity or uncertainty. Strong reserves beside a wider trade deficit is a stock-flow combination, not an arithmetic contradiction. Credit growth with tight marginal liquidity can reflect legacy stock expansion, uneven funding and the difference between system liquidity and individual institutions. Policy must match the shock. Demand weakness supports stabilisation; food or oil supply shocks require supply action, targeted relief and guarded monetary response; external shocks call for exchange-rate flexibility, buffers and diversification; financial stress requires liquidity-solvency separation and macroprudential tools. Coordination should preserve mandates and include exit conditions. A qualified verdict names the dominant mechanism, counter-signal and next release capable of falsifying it instead of manufacturing one synthetic score.

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

## 1. Evidence discipline

- Survey = analysis; Budget = estimate/authority; official outturns arrive later.
- Minimum tag: value, period, unit/base, release, source, status and caveat.
- Publication date differs from reference period.
- BE, RE, provisional and final are not interchangeable.
- January Survey figures require Survey-vintage labels in September.

## 2. Measurement and national accounts

- Level, growth, share, ratio, index, stock, flow and per-capita answer different questions.
- Nominal=current prices; real=constant prices; deflator=nominal/real x100.
- GVA + product taxes - product subsidies = GDP.
- Contribution depends on component weight and growth.
- FAE -> SAE -> provisional -> revised; rebasing may break comparability.
- FY21 contraction: 7.3% provisional in May 2021; 6.6% revised in January 2022.
- Q1 FY27 release 31 August 2026, base 2022-23: real GDP 7.8%, nominal 10.3%, real GVA 8.2%.

## 3. Prices, fiscal and monetary

- August 2026 CPI: headline 4.82%, food 5.95%, provisional, base 2024=100, release 14 September.
- CPI, WPI and deflator differ by basket, stage, imports, services and weighting.
- FY27 Budget BE: FD 4.3% GDP; capex Rs 12,21,821 crore; effective capex Rs 17,14,523 crore.
- FY26 CGA provisional unaudited FD: 4.40% GDP/Rs 15,19,169 crore, 97.5% RE.
- RBI 5 August: repo 5.25%, SDF 5.00%, MSF 5.50%, neutral; WACR 5.31%; LAF surplus Rs 1.0 lakh crore.

## 4. Finance, labour and external

- Financial safety needs capital, liquidity, asset quality, profit, concentration and networks.
- PLFS August 2026 CWS age 15+: UR 5.0%, rural 4.1%, release 15 September.
- UR denominator is labour force; LFPR/WPR denominator is population.
- August total exports/imports US$82.68/92.09 bn; merchandise US$43.81/70.67 bn; release 16 September.
- Reserves US$780.782 bn for 11 September, provisional release 18 September: a stock, not a flow substitute.

## 5. Synthesis algorithm

- Align period, base, unit and vintage.
- Decompose composition and distribution.
- Trace mechanism and lag.
- Add an independent counter-signal.
- Distinguish authority from observation.
- Name the next release that could change inference.
- Conclude with a qualified, falsifiable verdict.
