### Current dashboard cut-off

## Lesson 1 - What the Economic Survey is, and what it is not

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Start with the document's job

```text
DATA FROM MANY AGENCIES
        ↓
ECONOMIC SURVEY
diagnosis + interpretation + reform argument + outlook
        ↓
PARLIAMENT / PUBLIC DEBATE
        ↓
UNION BUDGET
tax and spending proposals with legal-financial authority
```

The Survey is the Union Government's annual analytical review of the economy. It is prepared in the Ministry of Finance under the Chief Economic Adviser and tabled in Parliament. It is neither a statute nor the Union Budget. Its projections are reasoned assessments, not appropriations, guarantees or final outcomes.

### Structure is an argument, not a fact dump

Economic Survey 2025-26 moves from the state of the economy to fiscal, monetary-financial, external, inflation, agriculture, services, industry, infrastructure, climate, education-health, employment-skills and longer-term reform questions. The statistical appendix supplies series; chapters explain mechanisms and policy choices.

### A five-step reading method

1. **Find the claim:** growth is resilient, inflation is contained, or external buffers are adequate.
2. **Identify the measure:** GDP growth, CPI inflation, fiscal-deficit ratio or reserve stock.
3. **Attach status:** FAE, projection, BE, provisional estimate or realised observation.
4. **Test composition:** consumption versus investment; food versus core; services versus manufacturing.
5. **Read the qualification:** global risk, implementation constraint, distributional weakness or data revision.

### Example and limit

The Survey's FY26 real-GDP figure of **7.4%** was an FAE. It supported the January 2026 diagnosis, but it did not freeze FY26 forever. Later national-accounts releases and the February 2026 base revision changed the evidentiary environment.

### Objection and reply

**Objection:** A government document will always be optimistic.
**Reply:** Institutional authorship should prompt scrutiny, not dismissal. The correct method is to separate data, interpretation and recommendation, then cross-check the data with the primary statistical agency.

### UPSC integration

- Use the Survey for framing, synthesis and reform logic.
- Use primary releases for the latest value.
- Never write "the Survey proves"; write "the Survey assesses", followed by the relevant data and caveat.

### Revision notes

1. The Survey is an annual analytical document of the Ministry of Finance.
2. It is not the Budget, a law or an appropriation.
3. A chapter combines data, interpretation, risks and recommendations.
4. The statistical appendix is not identical to the narrative chapter.
5. A projection is conditional, not realised.
6. A Survey vintage remains historically valid but may cease to be current.
7. Primary statistical releases control the newest official value.
8. Good answers separate claim, measure, status, composition and qualification.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why can a Survey projection be useful without being treated as an official outcome?

**Model answer:** It is useful because it states the government's evidence-based assessment and assumptions. It is not an outcome because the future path depends on shocks, policy transmission and later data; it must retain its projection label and vintage.

**Misconception to avoid:** Calling every official number an actual. Official sources publish estimates, projections and plans as well as realised observations.

### Mains drill

**Question (10 marks / 150 words):** Explain the analytical role of the Economic Survey and distinguish it from the Union Budget.

**Model:** The Economic Survey is the Ministry of Finance's annual diagnosis of growth, prices, public finance, finance, external resilience and structural constraints. It synthesises official data, identifies transmission mechanisms, discusses risks and proposes a reform direction. The Union Budget performs a different function: it presents constitutionally required financial statements and tax-expenditure proposals for legislative approval. Thus, a Survey growth projection is an analytical assessment, while a Budget Estimate is a fiscal plan; neither is a realised outturn. A sound answer uses the Survey for context and reform logic, the Budget for authorised proposals, and MoSPI, RBI or CGA releases for subsequent outcomes. The Survey is therefore best treated as a connected macroeconomic argument whose claims must be decomposed and dated.

**Unique scoring rubric:** 2 marks role; 3 marks Survey-Budget distinction; 3 marks evidence-status discipline; 2 marks qualified conclusion.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Authority ladder

| Question | Primary authority | Typical product |
|---|---|---|
| Output and prices | MoSPI/NSO | National accounts, CPI, IIP |
| Monetary and financial conditions | RBI | MPC statement, liquidity data, FSR |
| Union fiscal outturn | CGA | Monthly Union accounts |
| Fiscal plan | Ministry of Finance | Union Budget BE/RE |
| External account and reserves | RBI | BoP, external debt, weekly reserves |
| Goods and services trade | Commerce Ministry/RBI estimate | Monthly trade release |
| Labour market | MoSPI/NSO | PLFS |
| Sector operations | Line ministries/regulators | Capacity, output, scheme or network data |

### The evidence sequence

```text
SURVEY DIAGNOSIS
      ↓
BUDGET PLAN: BE
      ↓
IN-YEAR RELEASES: monthly / quarterly
      ↓
REVISED ESTIMATE: RE
      ↓
PROVISIONAL / ACTUAL ACCOUNTS
      ↓
LATER REVISIONS AND REBASING
```

An answer should use the newest compatible official release, not simply the newest-looking number. Compatibility requires the same concept, unit, price basis, period and series.

### Release-calendar intuition

GDP is quarterly and revised. CPI is monthly. IIP is monthly and initially a quick estimate. Fiscal accounts accumulate during the year. BoP is quarterly. Reserves are a weekly stock. PLFS may be monthly, quarterly or annual depending on the product and status concept.

### Strongest caution

The newest source can still be the wrong source. Monthly merchandise trade is not the current account. Weekly reserves cannot establish annual external sustainability. A Budget target cannot answer how much was actually spent.

### UPSC integration

Use verbs that preserve authority: **estimated, projected, budgeted, recorded, reported, revised, stood at**. Avoid "achieved" unless the period is complete and the source reports an outcome.

### Revision notes

1. MoSPI controls national accounts, CPI, IIP and PLFS.
2. RBI controls MPC, BoP, reserves and financial stability.
3. CGA reports Union fiscal outturns.
4. Budget documents provide BE and RE, not final accounts.
5. Line ministries explain sector operations.
6. Frequency does not guarantee completeness.
7. The latest compatible release beats an older synthesis.
8. Citation verbs should reveal evidence status.

### Concept check

**Question:** Which source should control a claim about the Union fiscal deficit realised up to August 2026?

**Model answer:** The CGA's cumulative Union Government accounts should control it. The Budget gives the annual BE, while the Survey supplies interpretation and historical context.

**Misconception to avoid:** Using the most prestigious document rather than the institution responsible for the exact series.

### Mains drill

**Question (10 marks / 150 words):** Why must macroeconomic answers distinguish institutional authority from publication recency?

**Model:** Recency and authority solve different problems. A recent commentary may discuss the economy but not compile the tested series. MoSPI is authoritative for GDP and CPI; RBI for monetary policy, BoP and reserves; CGA for Union fiscal outturns. Recency matters only after the correct institutional and conceptual perimeter is chosen. For example, an August trade release cannot replace quarterly BoP data for the current account, and a Budget Estimate cannot replace CGA accounts for expenditure realised. The examiner therefore rewards a source chain: identify the variable, cite the responsible institution, state the period and release status, and then compare it with an earlier Survey or Budget benchmark. This prevents false precision and category errors.

**Unique scoring rubric:** 3 marks authority principle; 3 marks two correct examples; 2 marks recency qualification; 2 marks answer protocol.

## Lesson 3 - The grammar of every macro number

Progress: 3 / 22 | Stage: Foundation | Subtopic: Statistical objects, units, denominators and effective dates

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: queried - national-income and dashboard measurement owners
CA search: "India official macro data latest GDP CPI reserves fiscal deficit August September 2026"
CA found: The latest dashboard mixes quarterly growth, monthly inflation, cumulative fiscal flows and a weekly reserve stock.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Statistical-object switchboard

| Object | Answers | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Level | How much? | GDP in lakh crore |
| Growth rate | How fast changed? | real GDP +7.8% year-on-year |
| Share | What fraction of a total? | PFCE as % of GDP |
| Ratio | One variable relative to another | fiscal deficit/GDP |
| Index | Movement relative to base | CPI 2024=100 |
| Stock | Balance at a date | reserves on 25 September |
| Flow | Activity over a period | CAD during Q1 |
| Per-capita value | Amount per person | per-capita income |

### Label before interpretation

```text
VALUE
 + UNIT
 + DENOMINATOR
 + REFERENCE PERIOD
 + PRICE / BASE
 + ESTIMATE STATUS
 + RELEASE DATE
 = USABLE EVIDENCE
```

Thus, "reserves were US$747.557 billion on 25 September 2026, provisional RBI weekly data released 2 October" is usable. "Reserves are high" is incomplete.

### Effective-date discipline

Three dates may differ:

1. **reference date/period** - when the activity occurred;
2. **release date** - when the source published it;
3. **effective date** - when a rule, base or policy became operative.

A Budget announcement may have a future effective date. A statistical base can be introduced in one release while the reference observations cover earlier periods.

### Comparison test

Only compare figures after asking: same unit? same denominator? same price basis? same season? same series? comparable period? A stock-flow comparison can be analytically useful, but it cannot be arithmetically merged.

### Revision notes

1. Level and growth are not interchangeable.
2. Share and contribution are not interchangeable.
3. Stock is measured at a date; flow over a period.
4. An index requires a base and basket.
5. A ratio requires its denominator.
6. Reference, release and effective dates may differ.
7. Nominal and real series answer different questions.
8. Comparability must precede causal interpretation.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why is "reserves cover the CAD" potentially misleading?

**Model answer:** Reserves are a point-in-time stock, while the CAD is a period flow. They may be compared for resilience, but one should not imply that a large reserve stock permanently solves the underlying trade, income or financing mechanism.

**Misconception to avoid:** Treating analytically related quantities as if they shared the same accounting dimension.

### Mains drill

**Question (10 marks / 150 words):** Show how weak data labelling can distort macroeconomic diagnosis.

**Model:** A macro number is meaningful only with its statistical identity. A nominal GDP level cannot be compared directly with a real growth rate; a reserve stock cannot be averaged with a current-account flow; a component's GDP share does not equal its contribution to growth. Distortion also arises when the reference period, base year or estimate status is missing. For instance, FY26 Survey growth was an FAE under an older vintage, whereas Q1 FY27 growth was released under the 2022-23-base series. Correct diagnosis therefore follows a label-first rule: variable, unit, denominator, period, base/price concept, status and source. Once labels are compatible, composition and mechanism can be analysed. Data discipline is not cosmetic; it determines whether the inference is valid.

**Unique scoring rubric:** 2 marks label components; 4 marks three category errors; 2 marks dated example; 2 marks methodological verdict.

## Lesson 4 - Reading GDP without confusing production, prices and demand

Progress: 4 / 22 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: GDP, GVA, real, nominal and expenditure composition

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: queried - Economy 01 Basic/Advanced and Topic 26 owners
CA search: "MoSPI Q1 2026-27 GDP GVA 2022-23 base official 31 August 2026"
CA found: Q1 FY27 real GDP grew 7.8%, nominal GDP 10.3% and real GVA 8.2% under the 2022-23-base series.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Two bridges

```text
PRODUCTION BRIDGE
GVA at basic prices
 + product taxes
 - product subsidies
 = GDP at market prices

EXPENDITURE BRIDGE
GDP = PFCE + GFCE + GFCF + change in stocks + valuables + exports - imports
```

GDP measures final production within domestic territory during a period. GVA measures producer value added. Their growth can diverge because net product taxes change.

### Price-volume split

Nominal GDP values current output at current prices. Real GDP uses constant prices to isolate volume change. The implicit GDP deflator is:

```text
GDP deflator = nominal GDP / real GDP × 100
```

It covers domestically produced final output and has a changing composition; it is not CPI.

### Current official growth card

| Measure | Q1 FY27 | Status |
|---|---:|---|
| Real GDP growth | 7.8% | MoSPI quarterly estimate, base 2022-23 |
| Nominal GDP growth | 10.3% | current prices |
| Real GVA growth | 8.2% | constant 2022-23 prices |
| Nominal GVA growth | 11.5% | current prices |

The difference between nominal and real growth signals price effects, but it is not itself a household inflation rate.

### Example

If a component has a large share but grows slowly, it may contribute more absolute growth than a tiny component growing rapidly. Contribution is approximately the previous-period weight multiplied by the component's growth rate, with chain-linking and residual qualifications.

### UPSC traps

- GDP is territorial; GNI/GNP is resident-income based.
- Financial-asset trades and transfer payments are not current production.
- A high GDP rate does not prove higher median welfare.

### Revision notes

1. GDP is at market prices; GVA is at basic prices.
2. Net product taxes bridge GVA and GDP.
3. Real growth removes price change using a stated method.
4. Nominal growth matters for tax, debt and market-size ratios.
5. The GDP deflator is not CPI or WPI.
6. Expenditure shares do not equal growth contributions.
7. Q1 FY27 uses the 2022-23-base series.
8. Growth must be checked against jobs, distribution and sustainability.

### Concept check

**Question:** Can real GVA grow faster than real GDP without statistical contradiction?

**Model answer:** Yes. GDP equals GVA plus product taxes minus product subsidies. Changes in net product taxes can make their growth rates differ even when both series are correctly measured.

**Misconception to avoid:** Assuming two related aggregates must have identical growth because they describe the same economy.

### Mains drill

**Question (15 marks / 250 words):** Explain how GDP, GVA and expenditure components should be combined to diagnose growth.

**Model:** GDP and GVA provide complementary views. GVA identifies where producer value was created, while GDP at market prices incorporates net product taxes and records final output valued by purchasers. The expenditure identity then asks who bought that output: households through PFCE, government through GFCE, investors through capital formation and the external sector through net exports. A rigorous diagnosis begins by separating real from nominal values and attaching the base year and estimate vintage. Q1 FY27 illustrates this method: real GDP grew 7.8%, real GVA 8.2%, nominal GDP 10.3% and nominal GVA 11.5% under the 2022-23-base series. The GDP-GVA gap points to net-tax effects; the nominal-real gap points to price effects. Next, expenditure shares and growth rates must be distinguished from contribution. Finally, the headline must be tested against employment, inflation, fiscal quality, external balance and sector dispersion. GDP is therefore the starting aggregate, not a self-sufficient welfare verdict.

**Unique scoring rubric:** 3 marks accounting bridges; 3 marks real-nominal discipline; 3 marks current evidence; 3 marks composition; 3 marks welfare qualification.

## Lesson 5 - Why the same year can have several official growth numbers

Progress: 5 / 22 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Base year, estimate vintage, revision and comparator effects

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: queried - Topic 26 revision unit and Economy 01 measurement owner
CA search: "MoSPI new GDP series base 2022-23 methodology 27 February 2026 official"
CA found: MoSPI introduced the 2022-23-base national-accounts series on 27 February 2026 with updated sources and methodology.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Vintage ladder

```text
FAE → SAE → PROVISIONAL → FIRST REVISED → SECOND REVISED → THIRD REVISED
         fuller annual, corporate, survey and administrative data arrive
```

Revisions are a feature of timely statistics, not proof of manipulation. The question is whether methods, sources and revision policies are transparent.

### Rebasing is broader than changing "100"

A new base can update:

- relative prices and sector weights;
- classifications and product baskets;
- corporate, GST and survey databases;
- informal-sector proxies;
- deflators and quarterly benchmarking.

The 2022-23-base GDP series replaced 2011-12. Its methodology includes richer administrative sources, updated classifications and greater sectoral detail. Comparisons across the break require an official back series or explicit caution.

### Base effect and recovery level

```text
100 → 80 = -20%
80 → 96 = +20%
```

The second period shows strong growth but remains below the original level. Growth rate, recovered level and lost cumulative output are separate questions.

### FY21 vintage drill

The NSO's May 2021 provisional estimate put FY21 GDP contraction at **7.3%**. A January 2022 revision narrowed it to **6.6%**. Both are valid descriptions of different information vintages. The 2021 V-shaped-recovery answer should identify which figure was contemporaneous and which is revised.

### FY21 recovery path: V in aggregate, K beneath it

| Quarter | Calendar period | Real-GDP growth, year on year | Estimate vintage and exact official source | Reading |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| Q1 FY21 | Apr-Jun 2020 | **−23.9%** | First quarterly estimate, NSO, **31 August 2020**: `https://mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/press_release/PRESS_NOTE-Q1_2020-21.pdf` | lockdown collapse |
| Q2 FY21 | Jul-Sep 2020 | **−7.5%** | First quarterly estimate, NSO, **27 November 2020**; official PIB-hosted NSO press note: `https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/userfiles/PRESS_NOTE-Q2_2020-21.pdf` | contraction narrowed |
| Q3 FY21 | Oct-Dec 2020 | **+0.5%** | Then-current revised estimate, NSO provisional release, **31 May 2021**: `https://www.mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/press_release/Press%20Note_31-05-2021.pdf`; the first Q3 estimate was +0.4% on 26 February 2021 | return to positive growth |
| Q4 FY21 | Jan-Mar 2021 | **+1.6%** | First Q4 estimate, NSO provisional release, **31 May 2021**: `https://www.mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/press_release/Press%20Note_31-05-2021.pdf` | positive growth continued |

✅ **Direct fact with vintage control:** The canonical contemporaneous V-path combines the first-release Q1 and Q2 estimates (**−23.9%**, **−7.5%**) with the then-current Q3 estimate and first Q4 estimate (**+0.5%**, **+1.6%**). The **31 May 2021** note does **not** support −23.9% or −7.5%: it revised those quarters to **−24.4%** and **−7.4%**, while reporting Q3 at +0.5% and Q4 at +1.6%. The first Q3 estimate had been **+0.4%** in the NSO release of **26 February 2021**, `https://mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/press_release/PRESS%20NOTE%20SAE%2026-02-2021.pdf`. The steep fall followed by narrowing contraction and positive growth supplies the factual basis for calling the **aggregate output path V-shaped**. The Economic Survey 2020-21 also supported the rebound diagnosis with high-frequency indicators such as power demand, rail freight, GST collections, steel consumption and e-way bills.

⚠️ **Inference and qualification:** “K-shaped” is an analytical description, not an NSO national-accounts category. It means that branches of the economy recovered at different speeds: the canonical Basic owner records employment/informality evidence of movement from salaried work towards self-employment, daily-wage and informal work, slower recovery for women and younger workers, and weaker contact-intensive services relative to manufacturing or agriculture. Thus, an aggregate V can coexist with divergent sector, worker and income paths. A defensible verdict is: **V-shaped in aggregate GDP growth, but uneven or K-shaped in incidence**.

The quarterly rates are year-on-year comparisons, not sequential quarter-on-quarter changes. The deeply depressed Q1 base also magnified later rebound rates; a positive rate does not by itself prove that the pre-pandemic output path, jobs or incomes had been fully restored.

### Seasonality and annualisation

Quarterly activity may follow crop, festival, monsoon or fiscal-spending patterns. A sequential quarterly rate should not be annualised mechanically when seasonality and volatility are high.

### Revision notes

1. Advance estimates trade completeness for timeliness.
2. Revisions use fuller information.
3. Rebasing changes weights, data and methods.
4. A back series is needed for clean long-run comparison.
5. Base effects affect rates, not the underlying level automatically.
6. Seasonal patterns can distort quarter comparisons.
7. FY21 7.3% was provisional; 6.6% was later revised.
8. The contemporaneous FY21 V-path used first-release Q1/Q2 values of −23.9% and −7.5%, followed by the then-current Q3/Q4 values of +0.5% and +1.6%; the 31 May 2021 note later showed Q1/Q2 at −24.4% and −7.4%.
9. Aggregate V-shaped growth can coexist with K-shaped sector and employment outcomes.
10. Cite the vintage that answers the question.

### Concept check

**Question:** Does the revision of FY21 contraction from 7.3% to 6.6% make the earlier figure false?

**Model answer:** No. The 7.3% figure was the May 2021 provisional estimate based on information then available; 6.6% was the later revised estimate. It becomes wrong only if cited as the final settled figure without its vintage.

**Misconception to avoid:** Treating revision as erasure rather than a documented update to the information set.

### Mains drill

**Question (15 marks / 250 words):** Discuss why base-year and estimate-vintage discipline is central to current macroeconomic analysis.

**Model:** Macroeconomic statistics are constructed from incomplete and evolving information. Advance estimates support timely policy; provisional and revised estimates incorporate fuller corporate, survey and administrative data. A base-year revision goes further by updating prices, weights, classifications, data sources and deflation methods. India's 2022-23-base national-accounts series therefore should not be spliced casually with the old 2011-12 series. Vintage discipline also changes historical interpretation. The contemporaneous FY21 V-path combined first-release Q1 and Q2 growth of −23.9% and −7.5% with the then-current Q3 and Q4 values of +0.5% and +1.6%. The 31 May 2021 release subsequently recorded Q1 and Q2 at −24.4% and −7.4%; it must not be cited for the earlier values. The full-year contraction was reported at 7.3% in that provisional estimate and revised to 6.6% in January 2022. Base effects add another caution: a rebound from a depressed denominator need not restore the earlier output, employment or income path. A rigorous answer states series, base, period, release status and comparator.

**Unique scoring rubric:** 3 marks vintage sequence; 3 marks rebasing content; 3 marks FY21 example; 3 marks base/season effect; 3 marks conclusion.

## Lesson 6 - Growth and demand: who bought the output?

Progress: 6 / 22 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Consumption, government, investment and net exports

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: queried - Topic 26 demand-composition units and Economic Survey Chapter I
CA search: "Economic Survey 2025-26 PFCE GFCF domestic demand official"
CA found: The Survey reported PFCE at 61.5% of FY26 GDP and GFCF at 30.5% of GDP in H1 FY26.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Demand wheel

```text
HOUSEHOLDS ─ PFCE ┐
GOVERNMENT ─ GFCE ├─ domestic demand
FIRMS/STATE ─ GFCF┘
INVENTORIES + VALUABLES
EXPORTS - IMPORTS ─ net external demand
```

Consumption supports present demand. Investment creates future capacity. Government consumption supplies public services but is distinct from public capital formation. Net exports can subtract from expenditure GDP even when exports are rising if imports rise faster.

### Survey benchmark, correctly dated

The January 2026 Survey reported PFCE's FY26 share at **61.5%**, the highest since FY12, and GFCF at **30.5% of GDP in H1 FY26**. These are shares, not proof that consumption or investment caused every unit of growth.

### Four tests

1. **Breadth:** rural and urban, durable and non-durable consumption.
2. **Quality:** machinery and infrastructure versus inventory accumulation.
3. **Financing:** income, credit, fiscal borrowing or foreign saving.
4. **Sustainability:** capacity, productivity, leverage and import intensity.

### Strong consumption: strength and caution

Consumption can stabilise growth and improve capacity utilisation. Yet consumption financed by falling savings or unsustainable credit can weaken future resilience. Likewise, high investment is not automatically productive if projects are delayed or misallocated.

### Revision notes

1. PFCE is private final consumption expenditure.
2. GFCE is government final consumption expenditure.
3. GFCF measures fixed-capital formation.
4. Share is not contribution.
5. Imports subtract in the identity, not because imports are intrinsically bad.
6. Investment quality matters as much as its level.
7. Financing determines sustainability.
8. Demand analysis should connect today to future capacity.

### Concept check

**Question:** Why can imports rise during a healthy investment cycle even though imports subtract from expenditure GDP?

**Model answer:** Machinery, technology and intermediate imports may support future productive capacity. Their negative sign prevents double counting foreign production; it is not a welfare judgement on every import.

3. a **survey/labour** signal.

1. Leading does not mean causal.
2. Coincident does not mean complete.

**Question:** Why should a PMI increase not be treated as an official output-growth estimate?

**Model answer:** PMI is a diffusion survey of business conditions and direction, not a national-accounts measure of value added. It is a timely signal to be triangulated with production and transaction data.

### Mains drill

High-frequency data should therefore guide provisional diagnosis and scenario preparation, not be presented as a final causal or national-accounts result.

Climate policy should integrate adaptation, grids, storage and just-transition support.

This mix matches the October 2026 starting point: strong output momentum, higher food inflation, mixed capital flows, substantial reserves and weather risk. Its aim is not to maximise one indicator, but to raise productive capacity while keeping inflation, debt, external exposure and distributional stress manageable.

**Unique scoring rubric:** 4 marks instrument assignment; 8 marks six policy arms; 4 marks current-context responsiveness; 2 marks mandate protection; 2 marks integrated verdict.

# ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS MODEL PRACTICE

## Practice 1 - 10 marks / 150 words

**Question:** Why is an Economic Survey figure not automatically the latest official macroeconomic figure?

**Model answer:** The Survey is released at one date and synthesises the information then available. National accounts, prices, fiscal accounts, labour, external data and reserves continue to be released and revised by their primary agencies. Thus, the Survey's FY26 7.4% real-GDP FAE remains a valid January 2026 vintage, while later MoSPI releases under the 2022-23-base series control newer growth claims. The correct method is to retain the Survey for diagnosis and reform logic, then update the value from MoSPI, RBI, CGA or the relevant line ministry. Every citation should state variable, period, base, estimate status and release date. "Official" identifies authority; it does not mean final or permanently current.

**Scoring note:** Reward the Survey-primary-source distinction, one correct example, vintage language and a concise rule.

## Practice 2 - 15 marks / 250 words

**Question:** Examine whether India's current growth is broad-based.

**Model answer:** Q1 FY27 real GDP growth of 7.8% and real GVA growth of 8.2% indicate strong aggregate momentum under the 2022-23-base series. Supply-side breadth is visible: manufacturing grew 10.2%, construction 11.2%, trade-hotels-transport-communication 9.8% and agriculture 3.6%. August IIP growth of 8.0%, led by 9.0% manufacturing, supports industrial strength, though mining contracted 5.6%.

Breadth must also be tested beyond sectors. August PLFS CWS data showed LFPR 55.6%, WPR 52.8% and UR 5.0%, but job quality, real wages and formality remain necessary. August CPI inflation of 4.82%, with food at 5.95%, reduced real-income comfort unevenly. Monsoon and sowing risks affected agriculture, while Q1 external financing included positive FDI but FPI outflow. Fiscal-capex and infrastructure can widen capacity, yet outcomes depend on completion and utilisation.

Thus, current growth is sectorally broad but not automatically inclusive or risk-free. Its durability depends on food stability, job quality, investment productivity, export capability and implementation.

**Scoring note:** Reward sectoral evidence, labour-price-external tests, explicit qualification and a degree-based verdict.

## Practice 3 - 20 marks / 250-300 words

**Question:** "A macro dashboard is diagnostic, not causal." Discuss.

**Model answer:** A dashboard organises signals; it does not by itself identify why they moved. GDP, CPI, fiscal deficit, credit, employment, CAD and reserves differ in units, periods and mechanisms. Strong GDP can coexist with weak job quality because growth is sectorally concentrated or labour adjustment lags. Higher food inflation may reflect weather and supply rather than excessive aggregate demand. Large reserves can coexist with a wide trade deficit because a stock buffer and a flow imbalance answer different questions.

Causal analysis therefore requires six steps. First, define the variable. Second, align period, base and vintage. Third, decompose the headline. Fourth, identify transmission and lag. Fifth, test distribution and balance sheets. Sixth, compare alternative explanations. Current evidence illustrates the method: Q1 FY27 real GDP grew 7.8%; August CPI was 4.82%, with food 5.95%; Q1 CAD was 0.5% of GDP; and reserves stood at US$747.557 billion on 25 September. These values describe a resilient but uneven configuration. They do not prove one common cause.

Policy follows mechanism. Supply inflation needs supply and targeted fiscal action; broad demand pressure calls for monetary restraint; financial stress requires liquidity and supervision; structural weakness needs productivity reform. A dashboard becomes analytically valuable only when it guides questions, not when its boxes are mistaken for causal answers.

**Scoring note:** Reward conceptual thesis, three reconciliations, six-step method, current evidence, instrument matching and a qualified conclusion.

## Error-to-action set

| Common error | Why it fails | Repair action |
|---|---|---|
| writing a number without period | destroys comparability | add reference and release date |
| treating FAE as final | upgrades evidence status | use "estimated" and name vintage |
| mixing old/new base series | creates a false trend | use official back series or qualify |
| equating GDP with GVA | ignores net product taxes | write the bridge identity |
| equating trade deficit with CAD | omits services, income, transfers | reconstruct current account |
| citing UR alone | hides participation | add LFPR and WPR |
| equating capex with service | skips implementation | trace completion-utilisation-outcome |
| equating capacity with generation | confuses MW and MWh | state unit and system role |
| calling correlation causation | skips mechanism | add transmission and alternatives |
| dumping data | weakens argument | use claim-evidence-analysis-qualification |
