### The practical problem

A household may be above a monetary line today yet one illness away from distress. Another may have adequate consumption but no safe water or schooling. A third may be poor in relative social position although its basic calories are met. The first discipline is therefore to ask the right question before choosing a number.

### Six questions, six concepts

**✅ Fact/canonical concept:** Absolute poverty compares resources with a fixed real minimum. Relative poverty compares a person with the contemporary distribution or social norm. Inequality describes dispersion, not a minimum threshold. Deprivation is a shortfall in a specific functioning or service. Vulnerability is the ex-ante probability or risk of future poverty. Social exclusion concerns blocked participation, voice or access.

**⚠️ Analytical inference:** These can overlap without being identical. A village can record falling headcount poverty but rising land inequality; a migrant can be non-poor by annual consumption yet highly vulnerable because income is seasonal and benefits are not portable.

### Units, denominators and time

Always identify the unit: person, household, worker, child or region. Then identify the welfare variable: income, consumption, wealth, capability or access. Finally identify time: current status, a spell over several years, or expected future shortfall. Per-capita household consumption may hide unequal allocation within the household; an annual average may hide a hungry lean season.

### A four-household diagnosis

Suppose the poverty line is ₹1,000 per person per month. A has ₹700 and repeated deprivation; B has ₹950 after a crop shock but normally ₹1,300; C has ₹1,100 but unsafe water and a child out of school; D has ₹5,000 while the top group has ₹50,000. A is currently and plausibly chronically poor; B is currently poor but perhaps transiently so; C is not monetarily poor yet is multidimensionally deprived; D illustrates inequality rather than poverty.

### Comparison that matters

Poverty needs a threshold; inequality needs a distribution. Vulnerability is forward-looking; observed poverty is backward- or present-looking. Deprivation can be indicator-specific; social exclusion adds institutions and power.

### Strongest criticism, reply and balanced residual

**Strongest criticism:** A neat taxonomy fragments lived hardship into separate indices. **Reply:** Without separation, policy mistakes follow: a transfer aimed at a temporary shock is not the same as long-term capability building. **Balanced residual:** Diagnose separately, then combine evidence at household, group and place levels.

### UPSC use and verified linkage

This vocabulary is the foundation for the 2019, 2020, 2022, 2024 and 2026 Mains demands developed later. No direct owned PYQ is solved here.

**Common traps**
- ❌ poverty and inequality are not synonyms
- ❌ an average is not a distribution
- ❌ being above a line does not prove resilience
- ❌ household status does not reveal intra-household allocation

### Revision notes
- Absolute = shortfall from a fixed real minimum.
- Relative = position against a contemporary norm.
- Inequality measures dispersion across the distribution.
- Deprivation names a missing functioning, service or condition.
- Vulnerability asks about future risk.
- State the unit, welfare variable, threshold and date.
- Incidence, depth and severity answer different questions.
- Disaggregate by person, group, place and lifecycle.
- A single indicator can guide policy but cannot describe all welfare.
- Combine monetary, multidimensional and risk evidence.

### Mains micro-model

**Question:** Distinguish poverty, inequality, vulnerability and deprivation, and show why policy design requires all four.

**Model:** Begin with one-line definitions. Use a two-axis frame: present outcome versus future risk, and resources versus capabilities. Show that a poverty line identifies minimum-resource failure; distributional measures identify relative concentration; vulnerability models exposure and coping capacity; deprivation indicators identify concrete capability failures. Apply this to a shock-prone rural household, an urban migrant and a child facing nutrition and learning deficits. Conclude that targeted relief, insurance, universal services and structural opportunity reforms answer different diagnoses.

### The practical problem

A poverty line is not a timeless rupee number. It is a method for translating a normative minimum into prices and then applying it to a particular survey distribution. Change the basket, price index, reference period or survey design and the resulting headcount can change even if lived welfare does not.

### The method clock

**✅ Fact/canonical history:** The 1962 Working Group began an explicit minimum-consumption approach; Alagh retained calorie-linked poverty-line baskets; Lakdawala used state-specific lines and price adjustment; Tendulkar (report 2009) moved away from direct calorie anchoring and used a broader consumption basket with health and education; Rangarajan (report released 7 August 2014) recommended revised norms and a generally higher line. Rangarajan was a recommendation, not a newly adopted timeless current line.

### Prices and spatial variation

A common real minimum costs different nominal rupee amounts across states and between rural and urban areas. The price-adjustment problem is: choose the basket, observe prices faced by relevant households, select indices, and update consistently. State variation is therefore compatible with a nationally comparable real-welfare idea. The 2019 Prelims GS-I Q24 tests this logic; the official key is unavailable locally, so no answer letter is inferred.

### Comparability laboratory

Tendulkar-based historical 2011-12 lines were ₹816 rural and ₹1,000 urban per person per month, with an all-India headcount of 21.9%. Rangarajan's 2011-12 recommendation used ₹972 rural and ₹1,407 urban, yielding 29.5%. **✅ Fact:** the higher estimate follows a different basket and threshold. **⚠️ Inference:** it does not prove that millions became poorer between two measurements of the same year.

### HCES is not itself a poverty line

HCES 2023-24 reported all-India average MPCE without imputation of free welfare items of ₹4,122 rural and ₹6,996 urban. These are averages, not poverty thresholds. Survey redesign and recall/reference-period choices require caution in long-run comparison. Do not derive a current official poverty rate merely by comparing these averages with old nominal lines.

### Comparison that matters

Tendulkar and Rangarajan differ in normative basket and method; neither number should be stripped of year and status. Domestic lines serve national diagnosis; international PPP lines serve cross-country comparison.

### Strongest criticism, reply and balanced residual

**Criticism:** Any line creates a sharp poor/non-poor boundary around a continuous hardship distribution. **Reply:** Administration and tracking often need thresholds. **Residual:** publish sensitivity bands and depth measures, update prices transparently, and avoid letting eligibility depend on one stale scalar alone.

### UPSC use and verified linkage

**2019 Prelims GS-I Q24:** official poverty-line variation across states. Demand: understand state/rural-urban price variation and methodology. Status: objective key unavailable locally; no answer letter inferred.

**Common traps**
- ❌ average MPCE is not a poverty line
- ❌ old nominal lines cannot be compared without price and method controls
- ❌ different estimates need not imply changed welfare
- ❌ committee recommendation is not automatically operative policy

### Revision notes
- A line combines normative minimum, basket, prices and survey data.
- Always attach committee/method and year.
- State-specific nominal lines can preserve real comparability.
- Rural and urban prices and baskets differ.
- Tendulkar report: 2009; historical 2011-12 estimate 21.9%.
- Rangarajan report released 7 August 2014; 2011-12 recommendation 29.5%.
- ₹816/₹1,000 and ₹972/₹1,407 are historical monthly per-capita lines.
- HCES MPCE is consumption, not income or wealth.
- HCES 2023-24 no-imputation averages: ₹4,122 rural; ₹6,996 urban.
- Never announce a current official line without adoption/status evidence.

### Mains micro-model

**Question:** Why can two technically defensible poverty lines produce different poverty ratios for the same year?

**Model:** Define a poverty line as a method-dependent threshold. Explain four sources of divergence: normative basket, treatment of non-food essentials, price indices/spatial adjustment, and survey/reference-period choices. Use the Tendulkar and Rangarajan 2011-12 illustration with dates and status. State that a higher headcount under a higher line is a reclassification, not evidence of sudden welfare loss. End by recommending transparent vintage labels, sensitivity analysis, poverty-gap reporting and periodic basket/price review.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual entry

```text
FGT(alpha) = (1/N) sum over poor [(z-y_i)/z]^alpha
alpha=0 -> headcount/incidence
alpha=1 -> poverty gap/depth
alpha=2 -> squared gap/severity; gives extra weight to the poorest
```

*The visual fixes the question and the mechanism before technical detail.*

### The practical problem

Counting poor people treats someone ₹1 below the line like someone with almost nothing. The Foster-Greer-Thorbecke family repairs this by changing the power applied to each normalised shortfall.

### Full calculation laboratory

Let poverty line z=₹1,000 and incomes/consumption be ₹400, ₹700, ₹900, ₹1,200 and ₹1,800; N=5. The poor are the first three, q=3. Normalised gaps are 0.60, 0.30 and 0.10.

|Person|y|Poor?|Gap ratio|Squared gap|
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
|A|400|Yes|0.60|0.36|
|B|700|Yes|0.30|0.09|
|C|900|Yes|0.10|0.01|
|D|1,200|No|0|0|
|E|1,800|No|0|0|

FGT(0)=3/5=0.60. FGT(1)=(0.60+0.30+0.10)/5=0.20. FGT(2)=(0.36+0.09+0.01)/5=0.092. The average normalised gap among the poor is 0.20/0.60=1/3.

### A policy comparison headcount misses

Give A ₹300: A rises from ₹400 to ₹700 but remains poor. Headcount stays 60%, yet FGT(1) falls because depth falls. Give C ₹100: C reaches the line and headcount falls to 40%. If the sole target is headcount, policy may prefer the cheapest-to-lift person near the line; squared gap warns against neglecting the poorest.

### Axioms and limits

FGT with alpha>0 is monotonic: worsening a poor person's shortfall worsens poverty. With alpha>1 it is transfer-sensitive among the poor. But all FGT measures still depend on the chosen line and welfare variable; they do not capture non-monetary deprivation by themselves.

### Comparison that matters

Headcount is easiest to communicate. Gap measures aggregate resource shortfall relative to the line. Squared gap adds distribution among the poor. None tells who lacks sanitation, schooling or health protection.

### Strongest criticism, reply and balanced residual

**Criticism:** Gap measures can suggest a hypothetical transfer bill while ignoring identification and behavioural/administrative costs. **Reply:** They are diagnostic, not literal budget calculators. **Residual:** combine FGT with multidimensional profiles and delivery evidence.

### UPSC use and verified linkage

Supports analytical treatment of poverty measurement and the 2019 state-line Prelims demand; no direct solved answer.

**Common traps**
- ❌ divide by total population N in the standard FGT formula, not only q
- ❌ non-poor gaps are set to zero
- ❌ headcount can remain unchanged after a large gain below the line
- ❌ squared gap is not simply the square of the aggregate poverty gap

### Revision notes
- FGT unifies incidence, depth and severity.
- alpha 0 counts the poor.
- alpha 1 averages normalised gaps over all persons.
- alpha 2 squares individual gaps before averaging.
- Normalise by z for scale comparability.
- Set non-poor shortfalls to zero.
- Average gap among poor = FGT1/H when H>0.
- Headcount ignores improvement below the threshold.
- Severity prioritises larger shortfalls.
- Threshold and survey choices still matter.

### Mains micro-model

**Question:** Show, with a numerical example, why headcount poverty alone can create a policy bias.

**Model:** Write the FGT formula and define z, y, N and alpha. Use three poor persons with gaps 0.6, 0.3 and 0.1 among five persons. Derive H=0.6, gap=0.2 and severity=0.092. Compare a transfer to the poorest that leaves headcount unchanged with a smaller transfer to the near-poor that reduces headcount. Conclude that dashboards should report incidence, depth and severity together so political incentives do not favour only those easiest to lift above the line.

*The visual fixes the question and the mechanism before technical detail.*

### The practical problem

Today's poverty status is a photograph. Vulnerability is a weather forecast. A household just above the line may face greater future risk than a securely employed household with the same current consumption.

### Spell versus state

Transient poverty is a temporary spell caused by drought, illness, job loss or price shock. Chronic poverty persists because low assets, weak capabilities, discrimination, remoteness or repeated shocks reproduce the shortfall. Vulnerability-to-poverty estimates the probability of falling below a line in a future period.

### The poverty-trap mechanism

A shock cuts income. With savings or insurance, consumption is smoothed. Without them, the family sells livestock, withdraws a child from school, reduces nutrition or takes high-cost debt. These coping choices lower future earning capacity, making the next shock more damaging. A temporary event thereby becomes a chronic trap.

### Resilience portfolio

Prevention reduces exposure through public health, irrigation, climate-resilient assets and safe housing. Protection cushions loss through food, cash, insurance and employment guarantees. Promotion restores earning capacity through credit, skills, childcare, connectivity and productive assets. Shock-responsive systems pre-register likely beneficiaries, use triggers, permit rapid scale-up and preserve appeal/offline channels.

### Comparison that matters

Chronic-poverty policy is structural and long-horizon; transient-poverty policy needs speed and reversibility. Vulnerability analysis must include covariance: an entire region may be hit together, defeating informal mutual support.

### Strongest criticism, reply and balanced residual

**Criticism:** Predictive targeting can misclassify families and create opaque automated exclusion. **Reply:** Early warning enables faster support. **Residual:** use transparent triggers, minimal necessary data, human review, portability and post-crisis audits.

### UPSC use and verified linkage

Builds the resilience and sustainability arm of 2019 GS-III Q11 and the inter-generational mechanism of 2020 GS-III Q1.

**Common traps**
- ❌ currently non-poor does not mean non-vulnerable
- ❌ insurance is not the same as assistance
- ❌ covariate shocks overwhelm local risk-sharing
- ❌ coping can protect current consumption while damaging future capability

### Revision notes
- Transient poverty is temporary; chronic poverty persists.
- Vulnerability is forward-looking risk.
- Exposure, sensitivity and coping capacity shape vulnerability.
- Asset sales can convert shocks into traps.
- Idiosyncratic and covariate shocks need different pooling.
- Prevention reduces probability or impact.
- Protection smooths consumption.
- Promotion rebuilds livelihoods.
- Shock-responsive systems must scale quickly.
- Appeal and offline access remain essential.

### Mains micro-model

*The visual fixes the question and the mechanism before technical detail.*

### The practical problem

Monetary poverty asks whether resources cross a line. MPI asks whether deprivations overlap in the same household. The overlap is the key: two districts with the same average deprivation may differ sharply in how deprivation clusters.

### Alkire-Foster identification

Choose dimensions and indicators; set a deprivation cutoff for each indicator; assign weights; compute each household's weighted deprivation score; choose poverty cutoff k, nationally one-third; identify households with score at least k; censor deprivations of the non-poor for aggregation.

### Weight logic

The three dimensions carry equal weight. With two education indicators, each is 1/6. With three health indicators, each is 1/9. With seven living-standard indicators, each is 1/21. Weights make explicit the value judgement that a deprivation contributes to identification.

### Calculation lab

Four equally weighted toy indicators each weigh 1/4; k=1/3. Household scores are 0.50, 0.25, 0.75 and 0.00. Two of four are MPI-poor, so H=0.50. Their average intensity is (0.50+0.75)/2=0.625. MPI=H×A=0.3125. If the first poor household loses one deprivation, its score becomes 0.25 and it exits MPI poverty; H and A both change.

### Decomposition

MPI can be decomposed by subgroup and indicator contribution. This makes it policy-useful: high sanitation contribution needs a different response from high nutrition contribution. But contribution is not causal proof; indicators may co-move and administrative boundaries differ.

### Comparison that matters

A dashboard lists separate averages; MPI identifies joint deprivation. Monetary and multidimensional poverty can identify different households. MPI is not HDI: HDI uses population-level achievements, while MPI identifies household deprivation overlap.

### Strongest criticism, reply and balanced residual

**Criticism:** Weights and cutoffs are normative, household identification can hide intra-household inequality, and correlated indicators may double-count a common disadvantage. **Reply:** Transparent choices and decomposability are strengths over an unstructured list. **Residual:** publish robustness checks, uncensored indicator rates and group-level disaggregation.

### UPSC use and verified linkage

**2026 Prelims Q100:** MPI is a necessary cross-link routed primarily to Topic 2; provisional key only, so no answer letter is stated here.

**Common traps**
- ❌ H is not the MPI
- ❌ A averages intensity only among identified poor
- ❌ non-poor deprivations are censored in MPI aggregation
- ❌ 12 national indicators are not the same as every global-MPI version

### Revision notes
- MPI uses Alkire-Foster identification and aggregation.
- National MPI has health, education and living-standard dimensions.
- It uses 12 indicators.
- Each dimension has equal total weight.
- k=one-third identifies multidimensionally poor households.
- H is the proportion identified poor.
- A is average weighted deprivation among the poor.
- MPI=H×A.
- Decomposition aids diagnosis, not causal attribution.
- Check household and intra-household blind spots.

*The visual fixes the question and the mechanism before technical detail.*

### The practical problem

A Lorenz curve does not ask who is poor. It asks how cumulative resources are distributed from the poorest to the richest. The Gini compresses the curve into one number, gaining convenience but losing location-specific detail.

### Constructing the curve

Sort persons or households from lowest to highest welfare. Compute cumulative population shares and cumulative income or consumption shares. Plot the points from (0,0) to (1,1). The curve cannot lie above perfect equality for non-negative ordered values. A more bowed curve generally indicates greater inequality when curves do not cross.

### Discrete Gini laboratory

For incomes [1,2,3,4], mean=2.5 and n=4. Using G = sum_i sum_j |y_i-y_j| /(2 n² mean), the ordered-pair absolute-difference sum is 20. Denominator=2×16×2.5=80, so G=0.25. If all incomes double, G remains 0.25: it is scale-invariant.

### When curves cross

Distribution X may favour the bottom relative to Y but have a more concentrated top. Their Lorenz curves can cross. Then Lorenz dominance is absent, and a single Gini ranking depends on how differences across the distribution aggregate. Show percentile shares or quantile ratios rather than forcing a simple verdict.

### Data-base discipline

Income Gini, consumption Gini and wealth Gini are different objects. Household surveys may undercapture top incomes and assets. Consumption is often smoother than income, so consumption inequality can appear lower. **✅ Fact:** HCES measures consumption/MPCE. This lesson deliberately does not quote an HCES 2023-24 Gini because directly accessible records conflict.

### Comparison that matters

Lorenz is a whole curve; Gini is a scalar summary. Anonymity means identities do not matter, but group injustice may still matter. Relative Gini can be unchanged even when absolute rupee gaps widen.

### Strongest criticism, reply and balanced residual

**Criticism:** Gini hides where inequality occurs and misses top wealth when surveys under-sample the rich. **Reply:** It is comparable, decomposable in some frameworks and sensitive to transfers across much of the distribution. **Residual:** pair it with percentile shares, Palma, wealth evidence and group gaps.

### UPSC use and verified linkage

Supports 2019/2020 inclusive-growth distribution analysis and the 2024 expenditure-incidence question.

**Common traps**
- ❌ Gini does not measure poverty
- ❌ 0 means equality and 1 maximal inequality in the idealised range
- ❌ same Gini can arise from different distributions
- ❌ do not mix income, consumption and wealth Ginis

### Revision notes
- Sort units before constructing Lorenz coordinates.
- Axes are cumulative population and cumulative resource shares.
- Equality line is the 45-degree diagonal.
- More bowing usually means more inequality absent crossing.
- Gini is a normalised area measure.
- Pairwise-difference formula is useful for small samples.
- Gini is scale-invariant.
- Absolute gaps can rise while relative Gini stays unchanged.
- Crossing curves defeat simple Lorenz dominance.
- State welfare variable, survey and year.

### The practical problem

A whole-distribution average can remain calm while the top tail changes dramatically. The Palma ratio deliberately focuses on the top 10% and bottom 40%, leaving the middle 50% implicit.

### Palma calculation

If the top 10% receive 36% of income and the bottom 40% receive 18%, Palma=36/18=2. If progressive gains raise the bottom share to 24% while the top falls to 32%, Palma=1.33. It is a ratio of shares, not a percentage-point gap.

### Complementary diagnostics

P90/P10 compares high and low quantiles; mean/median signals skew; top 1% shares target concentration; wealth shares capture accumulated stock; intergenerational mobility asks whether origin predicts destination; absolute gaps measure rupees, not proportions. Each answers a distinct distributional question.

### Four bases

Income is a flow and can be volatile. Consumption is a flow of use and can be smoothed by savings or debt. Wealth is a stock conferring collateral, security and power. Opportunity/capability inequality concerns access and conversion, not merely observed money. Never substitute one for another.

### Top-tail blindness

Household surveys can miss very rich households, under-report capital income and omit offshore or hard-to-value assets. Tax and wealth-list data have their own coverage problems. Triangulation is better than pretending one source is complete.

### Comparison that matters

Gini uses the entire distribution; Palma highlights tails; poverty measures focus below a threshold; wealth concentration captures accumulated advantage. A complete answer uses the measure suited to the causal claim.

### Strongest criticism, reply and balanced residual

**Criticism:** Palma ignores changes within the middle 50%. **Reply:** That is intentional when policy concern is polarisation. **Residual:** report Palma alongside Gini and percentile/wealth evidence.

- ❌ a stable median can coexist with top concentration
- ❌ survey precision does not cure coverage bias

### Revision notes
- Palma focuses on tails.
- Palma=top-10 share/bottom-40 share.
- A higher Palma indicates greater tail inequality.
- P90/P10 is a quantile ratio.
- Mean above median suggests right skew but is not a full index.
- Income, consumption and wealth are non-interchangeable.
- Wealth affects collateral and resilience.
- Absolute and relative inequality can diverge.
- Top-tail undercoverage biases survey measures.
- Use a dashboard, not one sacred coefficient.

### The practical problem

GDP growth has no direct pipe to every household. It passes through employment, wages, self-employment returns, prices, assets, taxes and public services. The same growth rate can therefore produce very different poverty reductions.

### Growth elasticity of poverty

A simple elasticity is percentage change in poverty divided by percentage change in mean income or consumption. If poverty falls 12% while mean consumption rises 10%, elasticity is -1.2. It is descriptive, not a structural constant: line, period, initial inequality, prices and sectoral composition matter.

### Sector and job content

Labour-intensive agriculture, construction, manufacturing and services transmit growth through jobs and wages differently from capital-intensive enclaves. Informality, hours, security, gender access and productivity determine whether employment is a durable exit or merely low-paid absorption.

### Prices and assets

Food inflation can erase nominal wage gains for poor households because food has a high budget share. Land, housing, skills, digital access and finance shape who can exploit new opportunities. Asset-price booms can widen wealth inequality even while employment growth reduces poverty.

### Three scenarios

Broad job-rich growth can reduce poverty and inequality. Top-heavy growth can reduce absolute poverty but raise inequality. A shock with food inflation can raise poverty despite positive aggregate GDP. Therefore always trace quantities, distribution and prices.

### Comparison that matters

Redistribution changes post-market resources; predistribution changes wages, assets and bargaining before taxes/transfers. Capability services affect both present welfare and future productivity.

### Strongest criticism, reply and balanced residual

**Criticism:** Growth-first arguments assume eventual trickle-down. **Reply:** Without productivity and revenue growth, durable inclusion is fiscally and economically harder. **Residual:** pursue growth whose sectoral, employment, price and asset channels are designed for broad participation.

### The practical problem

Inclusion is broader than giving money after unequal market outcomes appear. It asks whether people can participate, convert resources into valued lives, withstand shocks and influence institutions.

### Four policy moments

Predistribution shapes initial assets, education, market power, labour standards, competition and access to productive infrastructure. Redistribution uses taxes and transfers after market outcomes. Public services supply capabilities that cash cannot reliably purchase in thin or unequal markets. Inclusion adds access, non-discrimination, voice, dignity and agency.

### Capability approach

**✅ Canonical concept:** Amartya Sen's capability approach evaluates substantive freedoms to be and do, not resources alone. Functionings are realised states such as being nourished or educated; capabilities are genuine opportunities to achieve them. Conversion factors—health, gender norms, disability, public infrastructure and environment—mean equal income need not yield equal freedom.

### A wheelchair example

Two people receive the same cash transfer. One faces inaccessible transport and buildings and therefore must spend more to achieve mobility. Resource equality is not capability equality. The example does not imply cash is useless; it shows why accessibility and services must accompany it.

### Inclusion test

Ask: opportunity to enter markets? capability to use opportunity? security against shocks? voice and remedy? dignity without stigma? ecological durability? A programme that satisfies only one test is partial.

### Comparison that matters

Redistribution can reduce immediate disposable-income inequality. Predistribution can alter the market-income distribution. Capability policy can change conversion and inter-generational mobility. Inclusion assesses participation and power as well as outcomes.

### Strongest criticism, reply and balanced residual

**Criticism:** Capability space is too broad to measure completely. **Reply:** Narrow monetary metrics are measurable but incomplete. **Residual:** use transparent proxy dashboards while acknowledging omitted freedoms and participatory valuation.

### UPSC use and verified linkage

- ❌ redistribution is not the whole of inclusion
- ❌ predistribution is not a synonym for pre-election transfer

### Revision notes
- Predistribution shapes pre-tax market outcomes.
- Redistribution alters disposable resources.
- Capabilities are substantive freedoms.
- Functionings are achieved beings/doings.
- Conversion factors differ across persons.
- Public services cannot always be replaced by cash.
- Inclusion includes voice, dignity and remedy.
- Intra-generational equity concerns contemporaries.
- Inter-generational equity concerns future cohorts and transmitted advantage.
- A complete strategy uses all four policy moments.

### Cash and DBT

### Results chain

Outlay authorises resources; release makes funds available; expenditure records use; input buys staff/infrastructure; output records delivered units; access and utilisation show reach; quality tests what was delivered; outcome records changed welfare. Benefit-incidence analysis combines unit subsidy/cost with service use to ask which income or social groups receive public spending. Statutory incidence asks who formally bears a tax; economic incidence asks whose real income changes after behavioural and price responses. Fiscal-incidence analysis combines taxes, transfers and in-kind services to estimate distributional change.

Health: effective coverage, continuity, financial hardship, avoidable mortality. Education: attendance, learning, progression and equity gaps. A state with harder geography or poorer baseline may need more resources to achieve the same output. Mechanical outcome-linked funding can punish weak capacity. Use need-adjusted benchmarks, process diagnostics and capacity grants, while preserving accountability.

### Comparison that matters

Expenditure incidence asks who receives spending; outcome incidence asks who gains. A formally progressive programme may be regressive in use if better-off groups capture high-quality facilities.

### The practical problem

### Portfolio, not silver bullet

### Sustainability tests

Fiscal sustainability asks whether recurrent commitments have credible revenue and efficient composition. Ecological sustainability asks whether today's inclusion depletes water, soil, air or climate stability and burdens future cohorts. # ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS MODEL PRACTICE

## 10 marks: Poverty measurement

**Question:** Why should India report poverty incidence, depth and multidimensional deprivation together? (150 words)

**Model answer:** A headcount identifies how many persons fall below a line but ignores their shortfall and distribution. The poverty gap captures average normalised shortfall; squared gap gives greater weight to severe shortfalls. MPI identifies overlapping health, education and living-standard deprivations that money alone may miss. Together they prevent three errors: rewarding only those nearest the line, overlooking the poorest, and treating resource adequacy as capability adequacy. Each measure must retain threshold, survey, price/PPP basis, geography and vintage. A useful dashboard should add vulnerability and group/place disaggregation. Thus plural measures are not statistical clutter; they align relief, capability investment and resilience policy with different diagnoses.

## 10 marks: Financial inclusion

**Question:** Assess the usefulness and limits of a composite financial-inclusion index. (150 words)

**Model answer:** A composite index improves monitoring by moving beyond account counts to Access, Usage and Quality. It can reveal whether physical/digital reach translates into active, suitable services and can guide regional or sub-index diagnosis. RBI reported its FI-Index at 70.0 for March 2026 against 67.0 for March 2025, mainly reflecting Usage improvement (release 16 July 2026). Yet the index does not establish causality with income, poverty or empowerment; national aggregation can hide gender, regional and product-level gaps; and measured usage may not imply affordability or consumer protection. It should therefore be paired with distributional data, financial-health outcomes, grievance evidence and impact evaluation.

## 15 marks: Targeting

**Question:** “The fiscal saving from targeting must be compared with the social cost of exclusion.” Discuss. (250 words)

**Model answer:** Targeting concentrates limited resources on high-need households and may enlarge benefit adequacy within a fixed budget. However, it requires data, thresholds and proof. Informal earnings, migration, family change and documentation errors create false negatives; application and authentication impose transaction costs; stigma can suppress take-up. Exclusion is especially costly for food, health and early-childhood needs because missed support can cause irreversible harm. Universal provision reduces identification error and builds political support but raises gross fiscal cost and may subsidise those able to pay. A better design is layered: universal primary services and emergency floors; targeted top-ups for identifiable high need; self-targeted employment where appropriate; simple presumptive inclusion in disasters; periodic dynamic updating; portability; offline alternatives; reasoned rejection, appeal and compensation. Evaluate net administrative and social cost, not leakage alone. Targeting is justified only when identification is sufficiently accurate, the benefit is usable and wrongful exclusion has an effective remedy.

## 15 marks: Social-sector expenditure

**Question:** Examine the conditions under which higher social-sector expenditure becomes inclusive growth. (250 words)

**Model answer:** Spending creates inclusion only through conversion. Allocation must become timely release and actual expenditure; expenditure must finance appropriate staff, facilities and supplies; outputs must be accessible and used; and use must deliver quality outcomes. Benefit incidence must favour underserved groups, while fiscal incidence should not impose disproportionate burdens on them. Health spending should improve effective coverage and financial protection; education spending should improve learning and progression; nutrition spending should improve validated anthropometric and diet outcomes; protection should be adequate, timely and portable. Weak procurement, vacancies, inaccessible design, elite capture, poor local capacity and behavioural barriers can break the chain. Outcome-linked finance alone may penalise difficult regions, so need-adjusted grants and capacity support are necessary. Independent measurement, social audit and grievance feedback reduce gaming. Expenditure is therefore necessary but becomes inclusive growth only when its incidence, quality and capability effects are equitable and fiscally sustainable.

## 20 marks: Inequality and growth

**Question:** Analyse the channels through which growth may reduce poverty yet increase inequality. Suggest a balanced strategy. (250-300 words)

**Model answer:** Poverty is threshold-based; inequality concerns the distribution. If all incomes rise but top incomes rise faster, absolute poverty may fall while relative inequality increases. The result depends on sectoral composition, labour intensity, wages, food and housing prices, asset ownership, geography and fiscal policy. Capital-intensive growth may raise profits and asset prices with limited jobs. Labour-intensive growth can raise broad earnings, but informality and low job quality may limit resilience. Poor households' high food share makes inflation decisive. Unequal land, skills, digital access and credit shape participation; unequal tax compliance and service quality shape post-market outcomes.

A balanced strategy needs job-rich productivity, competitive markets and MSME opportunity; broad asset and skill access; universal primary health, education, nutrition and infrastructure; progressive and administratively feasible taxation; portable assistance and insurance; and gender/group/place-sensitive correction. Track headcount, poverty gap, Gini/Palma, top shares, capability outcomes and vulnerability rather than GDP alone. Ecological limits matter because resource depletion shifts costs to poor and future cohorts. The objective is not equal outcomes mechanically, but fair opportunity, rising floors, bounded concentration, genuine agency and resilience.

## 20 marks: Capability and protection

**Question:** Design an inclusive social-policy portfolio across the lifecycle, with attention to shocks and federal variation. (250-300 words)

**Model answer:** Begin before birth with maternal health and nutrition; protect early childhood through nutrition, immunisation, WASH and stimulation; convert school access into learning; connect skills to productive work; provide maternity, disability, unemployment/livelihood and health protection; and secure pensions, long-term care and accessible health in old age. Universal primary services create a floor. Targeted transfers address unequal needs. MGNREGA supplies a rural household work guarantee and asset creation; NFSA protects food entitlements; insurance pools specified health risks; assistance serves those unable to contribute or work.

For shocks, predefine triggers and contingency finance, expand benefit amount and coverage, preserve portability and offline access, and avoid distress exits. Federal design should combine national rights and data standards with need-based transfers and local adaptation for tribal, remote, slum and migration contexts. Outcome metrics must cover adequacy, timeliness, quality, financial hardship, learning, nutrition, grievance and group gaps. Privacy, anti-discrimination, social audit and appeal protect agency. The portfolio should be fiscally credible and climate-resilient so current protection does not create future exclusion.

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

## A. Poverty and vulnerability

- Poverty is threshold shortfall; inequality is distributional dispersion; deprivation is dimension-specific; vulnerability is future risk.
- Always state unit, welfare variable, threshold, price/PPP basis, survey and year.
- FGT(alpha)=(1/N)Σ[(z-y)/z]^alpha over poor persons. Alpha 0=headcount; 1=depth; 2=severity.
- Headcount can be gamed toward near-poor households; gap and severity reveal gains below the line.
- Transient shocks become chronic traps through debt, asset sale, nutrition loss and school withdrawal.
- Resilience combines prevention, protection, promotion and rapid shock scale-up.

## B. Poverty-line and estimate discipline

- India method clock: 1962 Working Group -> Alagh (1979) -> Lakdawala (1993) -> Tendulkar (2009) -> Rangarajan recommendation (2014).
- Historical Tendulkar 2011-12: ₹816 rural, ₹1,000 urban, HCR 21.9%.
- Rangarajan 2011-12 recommendation: ₹972 rural, ₹1,407 urban, HCR 29.5%.
- Different method does not imply changed welfare. No timeless current line should be asserted without adoption evidence.
- HCES 2023-24 no-imputation average MPCE: ₹4,122 rural, ₹6,996 urban; published factsheet 27 December 2024, detailed material 30 January 2025. MPCE average is not a poverty line.
- World Bank 2022-23: 5.3% at USD3.00 and 23.9% at USD4.20 under 2021 PPP after June 2025 line revision; international comparator, not domestic eligibility.

## C. Multidimensional poverty

- National MPI uses 12 indicators across health, education and living standards.
- Health: nutrition, child/adolescent mortality, maternal health.
- Education: years of schooling, school attendance.
- Living standards: cooking fuel, sanitation, drinking water, electricity, housing, assets, bank account.
- Identification cutoff k=one-third; H=incidence; A=intensity among poor; MPI=H×A.
- NITI 2023 observed HCR: 24.85% (NFHS-4, 2015-16) to 14.96% (NFHS-5, 2019-21).
- NITI January 2024 value 11.28% for 2022-23 is an estimate, not fresh household observation.
- Weights/cutoffs are normative; household identification can hide intra-household deprivation.

## D. Inequality

- Lorenz plots cumulative population against cumulative income/consumption share.
- Gini is a relative whole-distribution summary; it is scale-invariant and can hide curve shape.
- Crossing Lorenz curves prevent a simple dominance claim.
- Palma=top-10 share/bottom-40 share; useful for tail polarisation.
- Income and consumption are flows; wealth is a stock. Consumption inequality often differs because of smoothing and survey design.
- Add top shares, quantile ratios, absolute gaps, mobility and horizontal/spatial gaps.

## E. Inclusive-growth transmission

- Growth works through jobs, wages, self-employment, profits, assets, prices, tax revenue, services and transfers.
- Poverty elasticity depends on line, period, initial inequality, sector, job intensity and prices.
- Predistribution changes pre-tax opportunity and market income; redistribution changes disposable resources.
- Capability is substantive freedom; functioning is realised achievement; conversion factors explain why equal resources can yield unequal freedom.
- Vertical, horizontal, spatial, gender and intersectional inequalities need different remedies.
- Test participation, capability, security, voice, dignity, fiscal credibility and ecological/inter-generational sustainability.

## F. Social sector and protection

- Health: prevention -> primary care -> referral -> hospitalisation -> rehabilitation/long-term care.
- PM-JAY: launched 23 September 2018; specified secondary/tertiary hospitalisation, up to ₹5 lakh per eligible family per year; not every health expense.
- Education: access -> attendance -> retention -> learning -> progression -> skills.
- Nutrition: stunting=height-for-age; wasting=weight-for-height; underweight=weight-for-age; administrative data need validation.
- MGNREGA: Act 2005; demand-driven, rural household, up to 100 days per financial year.
- NFSA 2013: PHH 5 kg/person/month; AAY 35 kg/household/month; separate statutory entitlement from mutable free-grain status.
- Assistance, insurance, labour guarantees, services and promotion are distinct protection pillars.

## G. Delivery and fiscal incidence

- JAM is infrastructure; DBT is delivery mode.
- Universal provision reduces identification error; targeting concentrates resources but risks exclusion; self-targeting imposes participation cost.
- Grievance design requires reasoned decisions, offline exception, correction, appeal and audit.
- Results chain: allocation -> release -> spend -> input -> output -> access/use -> quality -> outcome -> impact.
- Benefit incidence asks who receives spending value; economic tax incidence may differ from statutory liability.
- Track adequacy, timeliness, portability, quality, financial hardship, learning, nutrition and group/place gaps.

## H. Final answer mnemonic

> **🔑 Mnemonic: M-D-T-I-S**
>
> **M**easure correctly -> **D**isaggregate -> **T**race mechanism -> **I**nterrogate delivery -> test **S**ustainability.
