```text
Who lacks several basic freedoms?  -> multidimensional deprivation

```

*The visual fixes the question and the mechanism before technical detail.*

### The practical problem

A household may be above a monetary line today yet one illness away from distress. Another may have adequate consumption but no safe water or schooling. A third may be poor in relative social position although its basic calories are met. The first discipline is therefore to ask the right question before choosing a number.

### Six questions, six concepts

**✅ Fact/canonical concept:** Absolute poverty compares resources with a fixed real minimum. Relative poverty compares a person with the contemporary distribution or social norm. Inequality describes dispersion, not a minimum threshold. Deprivation is a shortfall in a specific functioning or service. Vulnerability is the ex-ante probability or risk of future poverty. Social exclusion concerns blocked participation, voice or access.

**⚠️ Analytical inference:** These can overlap without being identical. A village can record falling headcount poverty but rising land inequality; a migrant can be non-poor by annual consumption yet highly vulnerable because income is seasonal and benefits are not portable.

### Units, denominators and time

Always identify the unit: person, household, worker, child or region. Then identify the welfare variable: income, consumption, wealth, capability or access. Finally identify time: current status, a spell over several years, or expected future shortfall. Per-capita household consumption may hide unequal allocation within the household; an annual average may hide a hungry lean season.

### A four-household diagnosis

Suppose the poverty line is ₹1,000 per person per month. A has ₹700 and repeated deprivation; B has ₹950 after a crop shock but normally ₹1,300; C has ₹1,100 but unsafe water and a child out of school; D has ₹5,000 while the top group has ₹50,000. A is currently and plausibly chronically poor; B is currently poor but perhaps transiently so; C is not monetarily poor yet is multidimensionally deprived; D illustrates inequality rather than poverty.

### Comparison that matters

Poverty needs a threshold; inequality needs a distribution. Vulnerability is forward-looking; observed poverty is backward- or present-looking. Deprivation can be indicator-specific; social exclusion adds institutions and power.

### Strongest criticism, reply and balanced residual

**Strongest criticism:** A neat taxonomy fragments lived hardship into separate indices. **Reply:** Without separation, policy mistakes follow: a transfer aimed at a temporary shock is not the same as long-term capability building. **Balanced residual:** Diagnose separately, then combine evidence at household, group and place levels.

### UPSC use and verified linkage

This vocabulary is the foundation for the 2019, 2020, 2022, 2024 and 2026 Mains demands developed later. No direct owned PYQ is solved here.

**Common traps**
- ❌ poverty and inequality are not synonyms
- ❌ an average is not a distribution
- ❌ being above a line does not prove resilience
- ❌ household status does not reveal intra-household allocation

### Revision notes
- Absolute = shortfall from a fixed real minimum.
- Relative = position against a contemporary norm.
- Inequality measures dispersion across the distribution.
- Deprivation names a missing functioning, service or condition.
- Vulnerability asks about future risk.
- State the unit, welfare variable, threshold and date.
- Incidence, depth and severity answer different questions.
- Disaggregate by person, group, place and lifecycle.
- A single indicator can guide policy but cannot describe all welfare.
- Combine monetary, multidimensional and risk evidence.

### Mains application

**Question (10 marks / 150 words):** Distinguish poverty, inequality, vulnerability and deprivation, and show why policy design requires all four.

**Model (ceiling: 150 words):** Begin with one-line definitions. Use a two-axis frame: present outcome versus future risk, and resources versus capabilities. Show that a poverty line identifies minimum-resource failure; distributional measures identify relative concentration; vulnerability models exposure and coping capacity; deprivation indicators identify concrete capability failures. Apply this to a shock-prone rural household, an urban migrant and a child facing nutrition and learning deficits. Conclude that targeted relief, insurance, universal services and structural opportunity reforms answer different diagnoses.

**Unique scoring guidance:** Four exact distinctions; present/future and resources/capabilities frame; one example; policy-matching conclusion.


### Concept check

**Question:** A household is above a consumption poverty line but lacks safe water and schooling. What is the correct diagnosis?

**Model answer:** It is not monetarily poor under the stated line, but it may be multidimensionally deprived. Monetary poverty, capability deprivation, inequality and vulnerability answer different questions.

**Misconception to avoid:** Treating every deprivation as proof that the household is below a monetary line.

*The visual fixes the question and the mechanism before technical detail.*

### The practical problem

A poverty line is not a timeless rupee number. It is a method for translating a normative minimum into prices and then applying it to a particular survey distribution. Change the basket, price index, reference period or survey design and the resulting headcount can change even if lived welfare does not.

### The method clock

**✅ Fact/canonical history:** The 1962 Working Group began an explicit minimum-consumption approach; Alagh retained calorie-linked poverty-line baskets; Lakdawala used state-specific lines and price adjustment; Tendulkar (report 2009) moved away from direct calorie anchoring and used a broader consumption basket with health and education; Rangarajan (report released 7 August 2014) recommended revised norms and a generally higher line. Rangarajan was a recommendation, not a newly adopted timeless current line.

### Prices and spatial variation

A common real minimum costs different nominal rupee amounts across states and between rural and urban areas. The price-adjustment problem is: choose the basket, observe prices faced by relevant households, select indices, and update consistently. State variation is therefore compatible with a nationally comparable real-welfare idea. The 2019 Prelims GS-I Q24 tests this logic; the official key is unavailable locally, so no answer letter is inferred.

### Comparability laboratory

Tendulkar-based historical 2011-12 lines were ₹816 rural and ₹1,000 urban per person per month, with an all-India headcount of 21.9%. Rangarajan's 2011-12 recommendation used ₹972 rural and ₹1,407 urban, yielding 29.5%. **✅ Fact:** the higher estimate follows a different basket and threshold. **⚠️ Inference:** it does not prove that millions became poorer between two measurements of the same year.

### HCES is not itself a poverty line

HCES 2023-24 reported all-India average MPCE without imputation of free welfare items of ₹4,122 rural and ₹6,996 urban. These are averages, not poverty thresholds. Survey redesign and recall/reference-period choices require caution in long-run comparison. Do not derive a current official poverty rate merely by comparing these averages with old nominal lines.

### Comparison that matters

Tendulkar and Rangarajan differ in normative basket and method; neither number should be stripped of year and status. Domestic lines serve national diagnosis; international PPP lines serve cross-country comparison.

### Strongest criticism, reply and balanced residual

**Criticism:** Any line creates a sharp poor/non-poor boundary around a continuous hardship distribution. **Reply:** Administration and tracking often need thresholds. **Residual:** publish sensitivity bands and depth measures, update prices transparently, and avoid letting eligibility depend on one stale scalar alone.

### UPSC use and verified linkage

**2019 Prelims GS-I Q24:** official poverty-line variation across states. Demand: understand state/rural-urban price variation and methodology. Status: objective key unavailable locally; no answer letter inferred.

**Common traps**
- ❌ average MPCE is not a poverty line
- ❌ old nominal lines cannot be compared without price and method controls
- ❌ different estimates need not imply changed welfare
- ❌ committee recommendation is not automatically operative policy

### Revision notes
- A line combines normative minimum, basket, prices and survey data.
- Always attach committee/method and year.
- State-specific nominal lines can preserve real comparability.
- Rural and urban prices and baskets differ.
- Tendulkar report: 2009; historical 2011-12 estimate 21.9%.
- Rangarajan report released 7 August 2014; 2011-12 recommendation 29.5%.
- ₹816/₹1,000 and ₹972/₹1,407 are historical monthly per-capita lines.
- HCES MPCE is consumption, not income or wealth.
- HCES 2023-24 no-imputation averages: ₹4,122 rural; ₹6,996 urban.
- Never announce a current official line without adoption/status evidence.

### Mains application

**Question (10 marks / 150 words):** Why can two technically defensible poverty lines produce different poverty ratios for the same year?

**Model (ceiling: 150 words):** Define a poverty line as a method-dependent threshold. Explain four sources of divergence: normative basket, treatment of non-food essentials, price indices/spatial adjustment, and survey/reference-period choices. Use the Tendulkar and Rangarajan 2011-12 illustration with dates and status. State that a higher headcount under a higher line is a reclassification, not evidence of sudden welfare loss. End by recommending transparent vintage labels, sensitivity analysis, poverty-gap reporting and periodic basket/price review.

**Unique scoring guidance:** Basket, price/spatial adjustment, survey period and adoption status; deduct for calling average MPCE a poverty line.


### Concept check

**Question:** Why can two poverty estimates for the same year differ without any household becoming poorer?

**Model answer:** Committees may use different baskets, price adjustments, survey reference periods and thresholds. A higher line can reclassify unchanged household consumption.

**Misconception to avoid:** Reading methodological reclassification as a time-series welfare fall.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual entry

```text
FGT(alpha) = (1/N) sum over poor [(z-y_i)/z]^alpha
alpha=0 -> headcount/incidence
alpha=1 -> poverty gap/depth
alpha=2 -> squared gap/severity; gives extra weight to the poorest
```

*The visual fixes the question and the mechanism before technical detail.*

### The practical problem

Counting poor people treats someone ₹1 below the line like someone with almost nothing. The Foster-Greer-Thorbecke family repairs this by changing the power applied to each normalised shortfall.

### Full calculation laboratory

Let poverty line z=₹1,000 and incomes/consumption be ₹400, ₹700, ₹900, ₹1,200 and ₹1,800; N=5. The poor are the first three, q=3. Normalised gaps are 0.60, 0.30 and 0.10.

|Person|y|Poor?|Gap ratio|Squared gap|
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
|A|400|Yes|0.60|0.36|
|B|700|Yes|0.30|0.09|
|C|900|Yes|0.10|0.01|
|D|1,200|No|0|0|
|E|1,800|No|0|0|

FGT(0)=3/5=0.60. FGT(1)=(0.60+0.30+0.10)/5=0.20. FGT(2)=(0.36+0.09+0.01)/5=0.092. The average normalised gap among the poor is 0.20/0.60=1/3.

### A policy comparison headcount misses

Give A ₹300: A rises from ₹400 to ₹700 but remains poor. Headcount stays 60%, yet FGT(1) falls because depth falls. Give C ₹100: C reaches the line and headcount falls to 40%. If the sole target is headcount, policy may prefer the cheapest-to-lift person near the line; squared gap warns against neglecting the poorest.

### Axioms and limits

FGT with alpha>0 is monotonic: worsening a poor person's shortfall worsens poverty. With alpha>1 it is transfer-sensitive among the poor. But all FGT measures still depend on the chosen line and welfare variable; they do not capture non-monetary deprivation by themselves.

### Comparison that matters

Headcount is easiest to communicate. Gap measures aggregate resource shortfall relative to the line. Squared gap adds distribution among the poor. None tells who lacks sanitation, schooling or health protection.

### Strongest criticism, reply and balanced residual

**Criticism:** Gap measures can suggest a hypothetical transfer bill while ignoring identification and behavioural/administrative costs. **Reply:** They are diagnostic, not literal budget calculators. **Residual:** combine FGT with multidimensional profiles and delivery evidence.

### UPSC use and verified linkage

Supports analytical treatment of poverty measurement and the 2019 state-line Prelims demand; no direct solved answer.

**Common traps**
- ❌ divide by total population N in the standard FGT formula, not only q
- ❌ non-poor gaps are set to zero
- ❌ headcount can remain unchanged after a large gain below the line
- ❌ squared gap is not simply the square of the aggregate poverty gap

### Revision notes
- FGT unifies incidence, depth and severity.
- alpha 0 counts the poor.
- alpha 1 averages normalised gaps over all persons.
- alpha 2 squares individual gaps before averaging.
- Normalise by z for scale comparability.
- Set non-poor shortfalls to zero.
- Average gap among poor = FGT1/H when H>0.
- Headcount ignores improvement below the threshold.
- Severity prioritises larger shortfalls.
- Threshold and survey choices still matter.

### Mains application

**Question (10 marks / 150 words):** Show, with a numerical example, why headcount poverty alone can create a policy bias.

**Model (ceiling: 150 words):** Write the FGT formula and define z, y, N and alpha. Use three poor persons with gaps 0.6, 0.3 and 0.1 among five persons. Derive H=0.6, gap=0.2 and severity=0.092. Compare a transfer to the poorest that leaves headcount unchanged with a smaller transfer to the near-poor that reduces headcount. Conclude that dashboards should report incidence, depth and severity together so political incentives do not favour only those easiest to lift above the line.

**Unique scoring guidance:** Correct FGT logic, worked transfer effect, headcount incentive problem and dashboard conclusion.


### Concept check

**Question:** A transfer raises a poor person's consumption but leaves it below the line. Which measures can improve?

**Model answer:** Headcount remains unchanged, while the poverty gap falls and the squared gap may fall more because the shortfall narrows.

**Misconception to avoid:** Assuming no improvement occurs until the line is crossed.

*The visual fixes the question and the mechanism before technical detail.*

### The practical problem

Today's poverty status is a photograph. Vulnerability is a weather forecast. A household just above the line may face greater future risk than a securely employed household with the same current consumption.

### Spell versus state

Transient poverty is a temporary spell caused by drought, illness, job loss or price shock. Chronic poverty persists because low assets, weak capabilities, discrimination, remoteness or repeated shocks reproduce the shortfall. Vulnerability-to-poverty estimates the probability of falling below a line in a future period.

### The poverty-trap mechanism

A shock cuts income. With savings or insurance, consumption is smoothed. Without them, the family sells livestock, withdraws a child from school, reduces nutrition or takes high-cost debt. These coping choices lower future earning capacity, making the next shock more damaging. A temporary event thereby becomes a chronic trap.

### Resilience portfolio

Prevention reduces exposure through public health, irrigation, climate-resilient assets and safe housing. Protection cushions loss through food, cash, insurance and employment guarantees. Promotion restores earning capacity through credit, skills, childcare, connectivity and productive assets. Shock-responsive systems pre-register likely beneficiaries, use triggers, permit rapid scale-up and preserve appeal/offline channels.

### Comparison that matters

Chronic-poverty policy is structural and long-horizon; transient-poverty policy needs speed and reversibility. Vulnerability analysis must include covariance: an entire region may be hit together, defeating informal mutual support.

### Strongest criticism, reply and balanced residual

**Criticism:** Predictive targeting can misclassify families and create opaque automated exclusion. **Reply:** Early warning enables faster support. **Residual:** use transparent triggers, minimal necessary data, human review, portability and post-crisis audits.

### UPSC use and verified linkage

Builds the resilience and sustainability arm of 2019 GS-III Q11 and the inter-generational mechanism of 2020 GS-III Q1.

**Common traps**
- ❌ currently non-poor does not mean non-vulnerable
- ❌ insurance is not the same as assistance
- ❌ covariate shocks overwhelm local risk-sharing
- ❌ coping can protect current consumption while damaging future capability

### Revision notes
- Transient poverty is temporary; chronic poverty persists.
- Vulnerability is forward-looking risk.
- Exposure, sensitivity and coping capacity shape vulnerability.
- Asset sales can convert shocks into traps.
- Idiosyncratic and covariate shocks need different pooling.
- Prevention reduces probability or impact.
- Protection smooths consumption.
- Promotion rebuilds livelihoods.
- Shock-responsive systems must scale quickly.
- Appeal and offline access remain essential.

### Mains application

**Question (10 marks / 150 words):** How can a temporary shock turn into inter-generational poverty?

**Model (ceiling: 150 words):** Open with the shock-to-trap causal chain. Explain income loss, weak savings/insurance, distress debt, asset sale, nutrition reduction and school withdrawal. Show how these reduce productivity and children's capabilities, transmitting disadvantage. Distinguish idiosyncratic illness from covariate drought or pandemic. Propose prevention, portable protection, employment support, primary care, nutrition continuity and livelihood restoration. Conclude that resilience policy must protect both current consumption and future assets.

**Unique scoring guidance:** Shock-to-coping-to-capability chain, risk distinction, resilience portfolio and prediction qualification.


### Concept check

**Question:** What converts a temporary income shock into a chronic poverty trap?

**Model answer:** Debt, asset sales, poorer nutrition, school withdrawal and lower future productivity can make a short shock persist across time or generations.

**Misconception to avoid:** Calling all current poverty chronic without duration or asset evidence.

### Visual entry

```text
12 INDICATORS
Health: nutrition | child/adolescent mortality | maternal health
Education: years of schooling | school attendance
Living standard: cooking fuel | sanitation | drinking water | electricity
                 housing | assets | bank account
weighted deprivation score >= 1/3 -> MPI poor
MPI = H x A
```

*The visual fixes the question and the mechanism before technical detail.*

### The practical problem

Monetary poverty asks whether resources cross a line. MPI asks whether deprivations overlap in the same household. The overlap is the key: two districts with the same average deprivation may differ sharply in how deprivation clusters.

### Alkire-Foster identification

Choose dimensions and indicators; set a deprivation cutoff for each indicator; assign weights; compute each household's weighted deprivation score; choose poverty cutoff k, nationally one-third; identify households with score at least k; censor deprivations of the non-poor for aggregation.

### Weight logic

The three dimensions carry equal weight. With two education indicators, each is 1/6. With three health indicators, each is 1/9. With seven living-standard indicators, each is 1/21. Weights make explicit the value judgement that a deprivation contributes to identification.

### Calculation lab

Four equally weighted toy indicators each weigh 1/4; k=1/3. Household scores are 0.50, 0.25, 0.75 and 0.00. Two of four are MPI-poor, so H=0.50. Their average intensity is (0.50+0.75)/2=0.625. MPI=H×A=0.3125. If the first poor household loses one deprivation, its score becomes 0.25 and it exits MPI poverty; H and A both change.

### Decomposition

MPI can be decomposed by subgroup and indicator contribution. This makes it policy-useful: high sanitation contribution needs a different response from high nutrition contribution. But contribution is not causal proof; indicators may co-move and administrative boundaries differ.

### Comparison that matters

A dashboard lists separate averages; MPI identifies joint deprivation. Monetary and multidimensional poverty can identify different households. MPI is not HDI: HDI uses population-level achievements, while MPI identifies household deprivation overlap.

### Strongest criticism, reply and balanced residual

**Criticism:** Weights and cutoffs are normative, household identification can hide intra-household inequality, and correlated indicators may double-count a common disadvantage. **Reply:** Transparent choices and decomposability are strengths over an unstructured list. **Residual:** publish robustness checks, uncensored indicator rates and group-level disaggregation.

### UPSC use and verified linkage

**2026 Prelims Q100:** MPI is a necessary cross-link routed primarily to Topic 2; provisional key only, so no answer letter is stated here.

**Common traps**
- ❌ H is not the MPI
- ❌ A averages intensity only among identified poor
- ❌ non-poor deprivations are censored in MPI aggregation
- ❌ 12 national indicators are not the same as every global-MPI version

### Revision notes
- MPI uses Alkire-Foster identification and aggregation.
- National MPI has health, education and living-standard dimensions.
- It uses 12 indicators.
- Each dimension has equal total weight.
- k=one-third identifies multidimensionally poor households.
- H is the proportion identified poor.
- A is average weighted deprivation among the poor.
- MPI=H×A.
- Decomposition aids diagnosis, not causal attribution.
- Check household and intra-household blind spots.

### Mains application

**Question (10 marks / 150 words):** Explain the identification and aggregation stages of the Alkire-Foster method.

**Model (ceiling: 150 words):** Draw the sequence: indicator cutoffs, weights, household score, poverty cutoff k, censored matrix, H, A and H×A. Add a four-household calculation. Explain that H captures incidence while A captures intensity. Show decomposition by indicator and subgroup. Balance with normative weights, household-level concealment and data-vintage limits. Conclude that MPI complements rather than replaces monetary poverty.

**Unique scoring guidance:** Cutoffs, weights, k, H, A and decomposition; retain national/global and income/MPI boundaries.


### Concept check

**Question:** In an MPI, what is the difference between identification and aggregation?

**Model answer:** Identification applies indicator cutoffs, weights and poverty cutoff k. Aggregation combines incidence H and poor-only intensity A as MPI = H x A.

**Misconception to avoid:** Using an average deprivation rate as though it identifies the same households.

### Visual entry

```text
Cumulative income share
100 |                         equality line
    |                      /
    |                 ____/

    |       ____/
  0 +------------------------------ 100 cumulative population
Gini = area between equality and Lorenz / total triangle
```

*The visual fixes the question and the mechanism before technical detail.*

### The practical problem

A Lorenz curve does not ask who is poor. It asks how cumulative resources are distributed from the poorest to the richest. The Gini compresses the curve into one number, gaining convenience but losing location-specific detail.

### Constructing the curve

Sort persons or households from lowest to highest welfare. Compute cumulative population shares and cumulative income or consumption shares. Plot the points from (0,0) to (1,1). The curve cannot lie above perfect equality for non-negative ordered values. A more bowed curve generally indicates greater inequality when curves do not cross.

### Discrete Gini laboratory

For incomes [1,2,3,4], mean=2.5 and n=4. Using G = sum_i sum_j |y_i-y_j| /(2 n² mean), the ordered-pair absolute-difference sum is 20. Denominator=2×16×2.5=80, so G=0.25. If all incomes double, G remains 0.25: it is scale-invariant.

### When curves cross

Distribution X may favour the bottom relative to Y but have a more concentrated top. Their Lorenz curves can cross. Then Lorenz dominance is absent, and a single Gini ranking depends on how differences across the distribution aggregate. Show percentile shares or quantile ratios rather than forcing a simple verdict.

### Data-base discipline

Income Gini, consumption Gini and wealth Gini are different objects. Household surveys may undercapture top incomes and assets. Consumption is often smoother than income, so consumption inequality can appear lower. **✅ Fact:** HCES measures consumption/MPCE. This lesson deliberately does not quote an HCES 2023-24 Gini because directly accessible records conflict.

### Comparison that matters

Lorenz is a whole curve; Gini is a scalar summary. Anonymity means identities do not matter, but group injustice may still matter. Relative Gini can be unchanged even when absolute rupee gaps widen.

### Strongest criticism, reply and balanced residual

**Criticism:** Gini hides where inequality occurs and misses top wealth when surveys under-sample the rich. **Reply:** It is comparable, decomposable in some frameworks and sensitive to transfers across much of the distribution. **Residual:** pair it with percentile shares, Palma, wealth evidence and group gaps.

### UPSC use and verified linkage

Supports 2019/2020 inclusive-growth distribution analysis and the 2024 expenditure-incidence question.

**Common traps**
- ❌ Gini does not measure poverty
- ❌ 0 means equality and 1 maximal inequality in the idealised range
- ❌ same Gini can arise from different distributions
- ❌ do not mix income, consumption and wealth Ginis

### Revision notes
- Sort units before constructing Lorenz coordinates.
- Axes are cumulative population and cumulative resource shares.
- Equality line is the 45-degree diagonal.
- More bowing usually means more inequality absent crossing.
- Gini is a normalised area measure.
- Pairwise-difference formula is useful for small samples.
- Gini is scale-invariant.
- Absolute gaps can rise while relative Gini stays unchanged.
- Crossing curves defeat simple Lorenz dominance.
- State welfare variable, survey and year.

### Mains application

**Question (10 marks / 150 words):** Why should a Gini coefficient never be cited without its data base and distributional context?

**Model (ceiling: 150 words):** Define Lorenz and Gini. Demonstrate scale invariance with a simple distribution. Explain that income, consumption and wealth differ; surveys smooth consumption and can miss top tails. Note curve crossing and identical-Gini/different-shape possibilities. Recommend pairing Gini with percentile shares, Palma, poverty depth and group disaggregation. Conclude that the coefficient is a summary, not a self-interpreting verdict.

**Unique scoring guidance:** Lorenz construction, Gini interpretation, scale invariance, crossing/top-tail limits and companion measure.


### Concept check

**Question:** If every income doubles, what happens to relative Gini and absolute gaps?

**Model answer:** Relative Gini is unchanged because proportional scaling preserves relative dispersion, while rupee gaps double.

**Misconception to avoid:** Concluding that unchanged Gini means no distributional change.

### The practical problem

GDP growth has no direct pipe to every household. It passes through employment, wages, self-employment returns, prices, assets, taxes and public services. The same growth rate can therefore produce very different poverty reductions.

### Growth elasticity of poverty

A simple elasticity is percentage change in poverty divided by percentage change in mean income or consumption. If poverty falls 12% while mean consumption rises 10%, elasticity is -1.2. It is descriptive, not a structural constant: line, period, initial inequality, prices and sectoral composition matter.

### Sector and job content

Labour-intensive agriculture, construction, manufacturing and services transmit growth through jobs and wages differently from capital-intensive enclaves. Informality, hours, security, gender access and productivity determine whether employment is a durable exit or merely low-paid absorption.

### Prices and assets

Food inflation can erase nominal wage gains for poor households because food has a high budget share. Land, housing, skills, digital access and finance shape who can exploit new opportunities. Asset-price booms can widen wealth inequality even while employment growth reduces poverty.

### Three scenarios

Broad job-rich growth can reduce poverty and inequality. Top-heavy growth can reduce absolute poverty but raise inequality. A shock with food inflation can raise poverty despite positive aggregate GDP. Therefore always trace quantities, distribution and prices.

### Comparison that matters

Redistribution changes post-market resources; predistribution changes wages, assets and bargaining before taxes/transfers. Capability services affect both present welfare and future productivity.

### Strongest criticism, reply and balanced residual

**Criticism:** Growth-first arguments assume eventual trickle-down. **Reply:** Without productivity and revenue growth, durable inclusion is fiscally and economically harder. **Residual:** pursue growth whose sectoral, employment, price and asset channels are designed for broad participation.

### UPSC use and verified linkage

**2019 GS-III Q11:** inclusive-growth strategy for inclusiveness and sustainability; Comment, 15 marks, 250 words. Approach: define inclusion, trace job/asset/service/redistribution channels, test ecological and fiscal sustainability, then give a balanced portfolio.

**Common traps**
- ❌ elasticity is period- and method-specific
- ❌ nominal wages must be deflated by relevant prices
- ❌ employment quantity is not job quality
- ❌ aggregate growth does not identify beneficiaries

### Revision notes
- Growth reaches households through several channels.
- Job intensity and wage quality matter.
- Initial inequality changes poverty responsiveness.
- Food and housing prices alter real gains.
- Assets determine participation and resilience.
- Taxes fund services and transfers.
- Elasticity is descriptive, not universal.
- Poverty can fall while inequality rises.
- Positive GDP can coexist with a poverty shock.
- Inclusive growth joins production and distribution.

### Mains application

**Question (10 marks / 150 words):** Explain why identical GDP growth rates can produce different poverty outcomes.

**Model (ceiling: 150 words):** Use the transmission map. Compare a labour-intensive, food-price-stable expansion with a capital-intensive, asset-price-led expansion. Discuss initial inequality, female participation, informality, regional concentration, taxation and service conversion. Add elasticity as a summary, not causal proof. Conclude that policy should evaluate employment, real consumption, assets, capabilities and resilience alongside GDP.

**Unique scoring guidance:** Sector, jobs, prices, assets and fiscal channels; India example; policy balance.


### Concept check

**Question:** How can poverty fall while inequality rises?

**Model answer:** Broad gains may lift households above a line while top incomes, profits or assets grow faster. Poverty is threshold-based; inequality concerns the whole distribution.

**Misconception to avoid:** Assuming falling headcount proves proportionate sharing.

### The practical problem

Inclusion is broader than giving money after unequal market outcomes appear. It asks whether people can participate, convert resources into valued lives, withstand shocks and influence institutions.

### Four policy moments

Predistribution shapes initial assets, education, market power, labour standards, competition and access to productive infrastructure. Redistribution uses taxes and transfers after market outcomes. Public services supply capabilities that cash cannot reliably purchase in thin or unequal markets. Inclusion adds access, non-discrimination, voice, dignity and agency.

### Capability approach

**✅ Canonical concept:** Amartya Sen's capability approach evaluates substantive freedoms to be and do, not resources alone. Functionings are realised states such as being nourished or educated; capabilities are genuine opportunities to achieve them. Conversion factors—health, gender norms, disability, public infrastructure and environment—mean equal income need not yield equal freedom.

### A wheelchair example

Two people receive the same cash transfer. One faces inaccessible transport and buildings and therefore must spend more to achieve mobility. Resource equality is not capability equality. The example does not imply cash is useless; it shows why accessibility and services must accompany it.

### Inclusion test

Ask: opportunity to enter markets? capability to use opportunity? security against shocks? voice and remedy? dignity without stigma? ecological durability? A programme that satisfies only one test is partial.

### Comparison that matters

Redistribution can reduce immediate disposable-income inequality. Predistribution can alter the market-income distribution. Capability policy can change conversion and inter-generational mobility. Inclusion assesses participation and power as well as outcomes.

### Strongest criticism, reply and balanced residual

**Criticism:** Capability space is too broad to measure completely. **Reply:** Narrow monetary metrics are measurable but incomplete. **Residual:** use transparent proxy dashboards while acknowledging omitted freedoms and participatory valuation.

### UPSC use and verified linkage

**2020 GS-III Q1:** intra- and inter-generational equity in inclusive growth; Explain, 10 marks, 150 words. Approach: distinguish fairness within the present generation from opportunity/resource transmission across generations; integrate fiscal and ecological sustainability.

**Common traps**
- ❌ equal resources do not guarantee equal capabilities
- ❌ redistribution is not the whole of inclusion
- ❌ predistribution is not a synonym for pre-election transfer
- ❌ capability is opportunity, functioning is realised achievement

### Revision notes
- Predistribution shapes pre-tax market outcomes.
- Redistribution alters disposable resources.
- Capabilities are substantive freedoms.
- Functionings are achieved beings/doings.
- Conversion factors differ across persons.
- Public services cannot always be replaced by cash.
- Inclusion includes voice, dignity and remedy.
- Intra-generational equity concerns contemporaries.
- Inter-generational equity concerns future cohorts and transmitted advantage.
- A complete strategy uses all four policy moments.

### Mains application

**Question (10 marks / 150 words):** Differentiate predistribution, redistribution and capability expansion in an inclusive-growth strategy.

**Model (ceiling: 150 words):** Define each term. Give predistribution examples—quality schooling, asset access, competition, labour institutions; redistribution examples—progressive taxation and transfers; capability examples—primary care, nutrition, accessibility and safe public goods. Add voice and grievance as inclusion. Explain complementarities and trade-offs. Conclude that after-the-fact transfers cannot alone repair unequal opportunity, while market reforms without security leave people exposed.

**Unique scoring guidance:** Predistribution, redistribution, capabilities and agency; conversion-factor example; qualified verdict.


### Concept check

**Question:** Why is redistribution alone insufficient for inclusive growth?

**Model answer:** Transfers protect consumption, but durable inclusion also requires productive opportunity, quality services, assets, agency and shock protection.

**Misconception to avoid:** Equating inclusion with post-market cash redistribution.

### Primary-care economics

Prevention, vaccination, screening, maternal care, health promotion and early NCD management can avert high-cost episodes. Primary care also coordinates referrals and continuity. Markets underprovide public-health goods and information-intensive preventive care, justifying public action.

### Health and Wellness Centres to Ayushman Arogya Mandirs

**✅ Current official evidence:** The Health and Wellness Centre platform is now presented officially as **Ayushman Arogya Mandir (AAM)**. **✅ Current official evidence:** The Registrar General of India's **SRS Abridged Life Tables 2017-21** reported life expectancy at birth at **69.8 years** for India. Community health must shift from episodic infection treatment to longitudinal management, rehabilitation, palliative care and caregiver support. Healthy life expectancy matters alongside life expectancy.

### Financial hardship channels

Out-of-pocket spending includes medicines, diagnostics, outpatient visits, transport, lost wages and hospital bills. Catastrophic expenditure is relative to household capacity to pay; impoverishing expenditure pushes resources below a poverty threshold. Insurance can reduce covered hospital bills but may leave outpatient and indirect costs.

### PM-JAY boundary

**✅ Fact:** AB-PMJAY, launched 23 September 2018, provides up to ₹5 lakh per eligible family per year for specified secondary and tertiary hospitalisation on a family-floater basis. ### The practical problem

Social protection is not a synonym for cash transfer. It is an architecture spanning assistance, insurance, labour-market guarantees, public services and systems that expand during shocks.

### Assistance and insurance

Social assistance is normally tax-financed and need/category based: pensions or transfers do not require an actuarial contribution record. Social insurance pools specified risks and may involve contributions or public subsidy. Insurance works best for uncertain losses; predictable chronic needs may require assistance or services.

### MGNREGA boundary

**✅ Fact:** The Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act, enacted in 2005, creates a demand-driven statutory guarantee of up to 100 days of wage employment per rural household in a financial year for adult members volunteering for unskilled manual work. It is household-based, rural and demand-driven—not a universal individual job guarantee. Timely work, measurement and wage payment determine realisation.

### NFSA boundary

**✅ Fact:** The National Food Security Act, 2013 provides a statutory food-security architecture. Priority Household entitlement is 5 kg per person per month; Antyodaya Anna Yojana entitlement is 35 kg per household per month. Current free-grain status through December 2028 is a mutable policy status retrieved 3 October 2026; statutory category and current issue price/status must not be conflated.

### Strongest criticism, reply and balanced residual

### The practical problem

An allocation is a promise of fiscal space. It is not a teacher in a classroom, a medicine taken, or a learning gain. The outlay-to-outcome chain tells us where conversion can fail.

### Results chain

Outlay authorises resources; release makes funds available; expenditure records use; input buys staff/infrastructure; output records delivered units; access and utilisation show reach; quality tests what was delivered; outcome records changed welfare. Final impact requires a credible counterfactual because other forces also change outcomes.

### Benefit incidence

Benefit-incidence analysis combines unit subsidy/cost with service use to ask which income or social groups receive public spending. Statutory incidence asks who formally bears a tax; economic incidence asks whose real income changes after behavioural and price responses. Fiscal-incidence analysis combines taxes, transfers and in-kind services to estimate distributional change.

### Metrics by sector

Health: effective coverage, continuity, financial hardship, avoidable mortality. Education: attendance, learning, progression and equity gaps. Nutrition: stunting/wasting/underweight with survey/administrative caveats. Protection: eligible coverage, adequacy, timeliness, portability, exclusion and grievance resolution. ### Attribution and federal capacity

A state with harder geography or poorer baseline may need more resources to achieve the same output. Mechanical outcome-linked funding can punish weak capacity. Use need-adjusted benchmarks, process diagnostics and capacity grants, while preserving accountability.

### Comparison that matters

Expenditure incidence asks who receives spending; outcome incidence asks who gains. A formally progressive programme may be regressive in use if better-off groups capture high-quality facilities.

### The practical problem

A whole-distribution average can remain calm while the top tail changes dramatically. The Palma ratio deliberately focuses on the top 10% and bottom 40%, leaving the middle 50% implicit.

### Palma calculation

If the top 10% receive 36% of income and the bottom 40% receive 18%, Palma=36/18=2. If progressive gains raise the bottom share to 24% while the top falls to 32%, Palma=1.33. It is a ratio of shares, not a percentage-point gap.

P90/P10 compares high and low quantiles; mean/median signals skew; top 1% shares target concentration; wealth shares capture accumulated stock; intergenerational mobility asks whether origin predicts destination; absolute gaps measure rupees, not proportions. Each answers a distinct distributional question.

### Four bases

Income is a flow and can be volatile. Consumption is a flow of use and can be smoothed by savings or debt. Wealth is a stock conferring collateral, security and power. Opportunity/capability inequality concerns access and conversion, not merely observed money. Never substitute one for another.

### Top-tail blindness

Household surveys can miss very rich households, under-report capital income and omit offshore or hard-to-value assets. Tax and wealth-list data have their own coverage problems. Triangulation is better than pretending one source is complete.

### Comparison that matters

Gini uses the entire distribution; Palma highlights tails; poverty measures focus below a threshold; wealth concentration captures accumulated advantage. A complete answer uses the measure suited to the causal claim.

### Strongest criticism, reply and balanced residual

**Criticism:** Palma ignores changes within the middle 50%. **Reply:** That is intentional when policy concern is polarisation. **Residual:** report Palma alongside Gini and percentile/wealth evidence.

### UPSC use and verified linkage

Cross-link to 2025 GS-III HDI versus IHDI: IHDI adjusts human-development achievements for inequality but remains owned by Topic 2.

**Common traps**
- ❌ Palma numerator is top 10 share, denominator bottom 40 share
- ❌ wealth is a stock; income and consumption are flows
- ❌ a stable median can coexist with top concentration
- ❌ survey precision does not cure coverage bias

### Revision notes
- Palma focuses on tails.
- Palma=top-10 share/bottom-40 share.
- A higher Palma indicates greater tail inequality.
- P90/P10 is a quantile ratio.
- Mean above median suggests right skew but is not a full index.
- Income, consumption and wealth are non-interchangeable.
- Wealth affects collateral and resilience.
- Absolute and relative inequality can diverge.
- Top-tail undercoverage biases survey measures.
- Use a dashboard, not one sacred coefficient.

### Mains application

**Question (10 marks / 150 words):** Compare Gini and Palma as inequality measures and explain why wealth evidence is also needed.

**Model (ceiling: 150 words):** Define both measures and compute a Palma example. State that Gini aggregates the whole distribution while Palma emphasises top/bottom shares. Explain survey top-tail weakness and the distinction between income flow, consumption smoothing and wealth stock. Add opportunity and mobility measures. Conclude that no index dominates for every purpose: select by question and triangulate.

**Unique scoring guidance:** Palma formula, Gini contrast, flow-stock distinction, top-tail limitation and companion indicators.


### Concept check

**Question:** What does Palma reveal that a single Gini may obscure?

**Model answer:** It compares the top 10 per cent share with the bottom 40 per cent share, making tail polarisation visible; it still needs data-base labels.

**Misconception to avoid:** Treating Palma as a complete substitute for distribution and wealth evidence.

### The practical problem

Vertical inequality ranks individuals by resources. Horizontal inequality asks whether culturally or socially salient groups face systematic gaps. The second can damage dignity, trust and political stability even when national inequality is unchanged.

### Dimensions that should not be collapsed

Vertical inequality concerns the distribution ladder. Horizontal inequality compares groups. Spatial inequality compares places and access costs. Gender inequality includes paid work, assets, unpaid care, safety and intra-household allocation. Intersectionality studies how disadvantages combine rather than merely add.

### Decomposition logic

A national gap may contain between-state, within-state, rural-urban and within-city components. Group averages can hide the poorest subgroup. Use cross-tabs where sample size permits: for example, female school completion by caste, disability and district, not one all-India female average.

### Mechanisms

Discrimination changes returns to equal qualifications; residential segregation changes school and network access; unpaid care constrains time; inaccessible design raises effective prices for disabled persons; domicile and documentation rules burden migrants; historical asset deprivation affects collateral and inheritance.

### Policy matching

Vertical gaps invite progressive taxes/transfers and broad asset access. Horizontal gaps require anti-discrimination, representation, tailored outreach and recognition. Spatial gaps require fiscal equalisation, infrastructure and local capacity. Intersectional disadvantage needs convergent case management and usable grievance systems.

### Comparison that matters

Equal treatment can reproduce unequal outcomes when starting conditions and conversion costs differ. Targeted accommodation is not necessarily a departure from equality; it can create substantive equality.

### Strongest criticism, reply and balanced residual

**Criticism:** Group targeting can harden categories or miss poor persons outside designated groups. **Reply:** Identity-blind policy can ignore documented structural barriers. **Residual:** combine universal floors, individual need and group/place-sensitive corrective action.

- ❌ intersectionality is not a checklist of identities
- ❌ national averages can improve while lagging districts worsen
- ❌ formal access does not prove effective accessibility

### Revision notes
- Vertical inequality ranks resources.
- Horizontal inequality compares salient groups.
- Spatial inequality includes access and service quality.
- Gender analysis includes unpaid care and intra-household allocation.
- Intersectionality examines interacting disadvantages.
- Between-group and within-group gaps both matter.

- Disaggregate without stereotyping.

```text
DELIVERY CHOICE
```

### The practical problem

The question is not 'cash or food?' in the abstract. It is: what market exists, what risk is being covered, who is likely to be excluded, how volatile are prices, and what remedy exists when the system fails?

### Cash and DBT

Direct Benefit Transfer is a delivery mode into an identified account; JAM is enabling infrastructure, not the benefit itself. Cash preserves choice and can reduce handling, but adequacy erodes with prices and supply may be missing. Account ownership does not ensure access, control, authentication or cash-out.

### In-kind provision

Food, medicines, school meals or services can protect a merit/basic consumption floor and pool procurement. They can also suffer leakage, quality problems, rigidity and logistics costs. In-kind value may be highest where markets are thin or prices spike.

### Targeting choices

Universal provision minimises identification error and stigma but has a larger gross fiscal footprint. Targeting concentrates resources but creates inclusion error (non-eligible included) and exclusion error (eligible excluded). Self-targeting—such as demanding manual work—uses participation conditions but imposes time, information and dignity costs.

### Grievance as core design

Publish eligibility and rejection reasons; allow offline exception; provide assisted access; ensure time-bound correction and appeal; separate authentication failure from substantive ineligibility; log reversals; audit by gender, disability, migration and geography. A benefit without remedy is a fragile entitlement.

### Comparison that matters

Cash is not automatically more efficient; in-kind is not automatically paternalistic. Universalism and targeting can be layered: universal primary services plus targeted income support and self-targeted employment.

### Strongest criticism, reply and balanced residual

**Criticism:** Digital targeting reduces leakage but can turn documentation errors into silent exclusion. **Reply:** Digital trails can improve portability and audit. **Residual:** design for failure—fallback authentication, human review, privacy and compensation for wrongful denial.

### UPSC use and verified linkage

Supports 2022 GS-III Q2 and 2026 GS-III Q2 by distinguishing financial rails from substantive inclusion.

**Common traps**
- ❌ JAM is infrastructure; DBT is a transfer mode
- ❌ inclusion error and exclusion error point in opposite directions
- ❌ universal does not mean costless
- ❌ authentication success is not proof of benefit receipt or adequacy

### Revision notes
- Choose delivery by objective and market context.
- Cash offers choice but needs functioning markets.
- In-kind can secure specified consumption.
- Universalism reduces identification errors.
- Targeting concentrates resources but risks exclusion.
- Self-targeting has participation costs.
- DBT is not the scheme objective.
- Portability matters for migrants.
- Offline exception and appeal are essential.
- Audit receipt, control, adequacy and outcome.

### Mains application

**Question (10 marks / 150 words):** Evaluate DBT against in-kind delivery for basic social protection.

**Model (ceiling: 150 words):** Avoid a binary verdict. Compare objective, market supply, inflation, household control, transaction cost, leakage, portability and exclusion. Use food as a case where in-kind protects consumption and cash as useful for flexible shock support. Explain JAM/DBT boundaries. Propose a layered model with indexation, choice where feasible, fallback channels, time-bound grievance and outcome audits. Conclude that delivery efficiency includes the cost of exclusion, not only fiscal leakage.

**Unique scoring guidance:** Five delivery designs, market/agency conditions, exclusion costs, appeal and context verdict.


### Concept check

**Question:** When may an in-kind benefit be preferable to cash?

**Model answer:** Where markets are thin, prices spike, control is unequal or a specific merit good must be secured, direct provision may protect the minimum better.

**Misconception to avoid:** Claiming either cash or in-kind is universally superior.

# CUMULATIVE CONCEPT CHECKS

## Check 1 - Measurement choice

**Question:** A district's headcount falls, its poverty gap barely changes and child nutrition remains poor. What does the combination imply?

**Model answer:** Some households near the line may have crossed it, while deep shortfalls changed little and capability deprivation persisted. Report incidence, depth, severity and multidimensional outcomes separately.

**Misconception to avoid:** One favourable headline validates every poverty and development claim.

## Check 2 - Distribution

**Question:** A state's average MPCE rises and relative Gini is unchanged. Can absolute inequality still rise?

**Model answer:** Yes. Proportional growth can leave relative Gini unchanged while rupee gaps widen. Percentile shares, prices and group/place distribution remain necessary.

**Misconception to avoid:** Stable relative inequality means stable absolute gaps.

## Check 3 - Social expenditure

**Question:** What evidence is needed between a larger education allocation and a claim of inclusive growth?

**Model answer:** Verify release and spending, teachers and materials, equitable access and attendance, learning, progression, group gaps and fiscal sustainability.

**Misconception to avoid:** Budget authorisation is an outcome.

## Check 4 - Financial inclusion

**Question:** Why is RBI's FI-Index useful but insufficient?

**Model answer:** It integrates Access, Usage and Quality and permits trend diagnosis. It does not prove affordable credit, user control, consumer protection, higher income or lower poverty.

**Misconception to avoid:** A composite index establishes causal impact.

## Check 5 - Delivery design

**Question:** Should a climate-shock benefit use a pre-existing targeted list only?

**Model answer:** Not by default. Newly affected households may be absent. Use presumptive or open inclusion, rapid updating, portability, contingency finance, offline access and appeal.

**Misconception to avoid:** Static targeting remains accurate during a covariate shock.

# ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS MODEL PRACTICE

## 10 marks / 150 words - Poverty dashboard

**Question:** Why should India report poverty incidence, depth and multidimensional deprivation together?

**Model answer (ceiling: 150 words):** Headcount shows the share below a stated line but not how far below or how deprivation is distributed among the poor. The poverty gap records normalised shortfall, while the squared gap gives greater weight to severe shortfalls. National MPI adds overlapping health, education and living-standard deprivations through incidence H and poor-only intensity A. Together these measures prevent policy from concentrating only on households nearest a line or assuming that adequate consumption guarantees schooling, nutrition, sanitation or resilience. Each value must retain its survey, threshold, price or PPP basis, geography and vintage. A complete dashboard should also disaggregate social groups and places and add vulnerability to shocks. Plural measurement is not statistical clutter: it matches relief, capability investment and resilience policy to different diagnoses.

**Unique scoring rubric (10):** definition/measure separation 2; depth/severity mechanism 2; MPI H x A 2; vintage/disaggregation 2; policy conclusion 2.

## 15 marks / 250 words - Social-sector expenditure

**Question:** Examine the conditions under which higher social-sector expenditure becomes inclusive growth.

**Model answer (ceiling: 250 words):** Public expenditure works through a conversion chain. Allocation must become timely release and actual spending. Spending must buy the right mix of staff, facilities, medicines, teaching time, nutrition inputs and local capacity. Services must be geographically, physically, socially and financially accessible. Use must produce quality outcomes: learning rather than enrolment alone; effective coverage and financial protection rather than insurance cards alone; validated nutrition improvement rather than registration alone.

Distribution is decisive. Benefit-incidence analysis asks which groups receive spending value, while fiscal incidence asks who ultimately bears financing. Remote districts, migrants, persons with disabilities, women and disadvantaged groups may require need-adjusted grants, portability and additional delivery cost. Outcome-linked funding may encourage attention to results but can also reward easy cases, invite gaming or penalise difficult starting conditions. Independent measurement, social audit, grievance redress and disaggregated dashboards reduce these risks.

Thus expenditure becomes inclusive growth when adequate finance is converted into equitable, good-quality capabilities and resilience without an unsustainable fiscal burden. The test is incidence, implementation, outcome and durability, not outlay alone.

**Unique scoring rubric (15):** conversion chain 4; sector evidence 3; incidence/disaggregation 3; capacity/gaming counterpoint 2; verdict 3.

## 20 marks / 250 words - Inclusive growth portfolio

**Question:** Analyse how India can reduce poverty without reproducing inequality and vulnerability. Suggest an integrated policy portfolio.

**Model answer (ceiling: 250 words):** Poverty is threshold shortfall, inequality is distributional dispersion and vulnerability is exposure to future loss. They may move differently: job-rich growth can lift households above a line, while asset prices and high-skill incomes raise top concentration; a health or climate shock can return a non-poor household to poverty.

The first pillar is productive participation: labour-intensive, productivity-enhancing growth, MSME opportunity, skills linked to demand, care infrastructure and safe mobility for women. The second is predistribution: broad asset access where feasible, quality schooling, primary health, nutrition, digital and transport infrastructure, competition and anti-discrimination. The third is redistribution and universal basics: progressive, administrable revenue; equitable federal transfers; reliable education, health, WASH and nutrition; and targeted top-ups where needs differ. The fourth is social protection: labour guarantees, food security, pensions, maternity/disability support, risk pooling and portable shock-responsive systems. The fifth is financial inclusion that moves from Access to Usage and Quality.

Measurement must combine compatible monetary poverty series, gap/severity, MPI, Gini/Palma/top shares, learning, nutrition, health financial protection and social/regional gaps. NITI's 11.28 per cent MPI value for 2022-23 must be labelled an estimate; RBI's FI-Index 70.0 for March 2026 is a monitoring fact, not proof of poverty impact. Ecological and fiscal limits protect future cohorts.

The aim is not mechanical equality but rising floors, fair opportunity, bounded concentration, agency and resilience across groups and generations.

**Unique scoring rubric (20):** divergent concepts 3; five-pillar mechanism 8; dated evidence 3; measurement architecture 3; qualification/verdict 3.

## Poverty diagnosis, lines and committees

- Poverty is threshold shortfall; inequality is dispersion; deprivation is dimension-specific; vulnerability is future risk.
- State unit, welfare variable, threshold, price/PPP basis, survey and year.
- Method clock: 1962 Working Group -> Alagh (1979) -> Lakdawala (1993) -> Tendulkar (2009) -> Rangarajan recommendation (2014).
- Tendulkar 2011-12: Rs 816 rural, Rs 1,000 urban, HCR 21.9%; Rangarajan recommendation: Rs 972/Rs 1,407 and 29.5%.
- Different methods do not prove changed welfare. India has no timeless successor line that can be quoted without status.
- HCES 2023-24 average MPCE without welfare-item imputation: Rs 4,122 rural and Rs 6,996 urban; averages are not poverty lines.

## Monetary depth, vulnerability and resilience

- FGT(alpha) = (1/N) sum[((z-y)/z)^alpha] over poor persons.
- Alpha 0 is headcount; 1 is depth; 2 gives greater weight to severe shortfall.
- Headcount may prioritise near-line crossing; gap/severity reveal changes below the line.
- Transient shocks become traps through debt, asset sale, nutrition loss and school withdrawal.
- Resilience combines prevention, protection, promotion and rapid shock scale-up.

## Multidimensional poverty and estimate discipline

- National MPI: health, education and living standards; 12 indicators.
- Health: nutrition, child/adolescent mortality, maternal health. Education: schooling years and attendance.
- Living standards: fuel, sanitation, water, electricity, housing, assets and bank account.
- Poverty cutoff k = one-third; H = incidence; A = intensity among poor; MPI = H x A.
- NITI observed HCR: 24.85% in 2015-16 to 14.96% in 2019-21.
- NITI 11.28% for 2022-23 is an estimate, not a fresh NFHS observation.
- World Bank 2022-23 estimates: 5.3% at USD 3.00 and 23.9% at USD 4.20, 2021 PPP; international comparators, not domestic eligibility lines.
- UNDP HDR 2025 reports India HDI 0.685, rank 130/193, using 2023 data; HDI/IHDI construction remains Topic 2.

## Inequality toolkit

- Lorenz plots cumulative population against cumulative income/consumption share.
- Gini is relative and scale-invariant; crossing curves prevent simple Lorenz dominance.
- Palma = top 10 share / bottom 40 share; useful for tail polarisation.
- Income and consumption are flows; wealth is a stock.
- Add top shares, quantile ratios, absolute gaps, mobility and group/place gaps.
- Vertical, horizontal, spatial, gender and intersectional inequalities need different remedies.

## Inclusive-growth transmission

- Growth works through jobs, wages, self-employment, profits, assets, prices, revenue, services and transfers.
- Poverty elasticity depends on line, period, initial inequality, sector, labour intensity and prices.
- Predistribution changes pre-tax opportunity; redistribution changes disposable resources.
- Capability is substantive freedom; functioning is realised achievement; conversion factors create unequal freedom from equal resources.
- Test participation, capability, security, voice, dignity and fiscal/ecological sustainability.

## Health, education and nutrition

- Health continuum: prevention -> primary care -> referral -> hospitalisation -> rehabilitation/long-term care.
- PM-JAY, launched 23 September 2018, covers specified secondary/tertiary hospitalisation up to Rs 5 lakh per eligible family per year; not every expense.
- Ayushman Arogya Mandirs are the renamed Health and Wellness Centre platform for comprehensive primary care; PIB reported 1,82,944 operational as of 31 December 2025.
- SRS Abridged Life Tables 2017-21 reported Indian life expectancy at birth at 69.8 years; period and healthy-life-expectancy qualifications are mandatory.
- NFHS-5 all-India context: stunting 35.5%, wasting 19.3%, underweight 32.1%, TFR 2.0 and institutional births 88.6%; retain 2019-21 reference period.
- Survey 2025-26 stresses ageing, NCDs, obesity, mental health and the shift from enrolment to learning quality.
- Education chain: access -> attendance -> retention -> learning -> progression -> skills.
- Samagra Shiksha integrates school-system support from pre-school to Class XII; judge it by equitable learning and progression, not approval or infrastructure alone.
- Stunting is low height-for-age; wasting low weight-for-height; underweight low weight-for-age.

## Protection, expenditure and delivery

- Protection pillars: assistance, insurance, labour guarantees, public services and promotion.
- MGNREGA: Act 2005, demand-driven rural-household guarantee up to 100 days per financial year.
- NFSA 2013: PHH 5 kg/person/month; AAY 35 kg/household/month; separate statutory entitlement from mutable free-grain policy.
- NSAP's IGNOAPS, IGNWPS and IGNDPS provide non-contributory lifecycle assistance; adequacy, state top-ups, payment regularity, access and grievance correction determine protection.
- Results chain: allocation -> release -> spend -> input -> output -> access/use -> quality -> outcome -> impact.
- Benefit incidence asks who receives spending value; economic tax incidence can differ from statutory liability.
- JAM is infrastructure; DBT is a delivery mode.
- Universal provision reduces identification error; targeting concentrates resources but risks exclusion; self-targeting imposes participation cost.
- Grievance design needs reasons, offline exception, correction, appeal and audit.

## Financial inclusion, group gaps and final answer spine

- Financial inclusion requires Access, Usage and Quality, not account ownership alone.
- PMJDY dashboard on 23 September 2026: 59.37 crore beneficiaries, Rs 3,18,033.60 crore deposits and 41.56 crore RuPay cards; these are cumulative access/platform indicators.
- RBI FI-Index: 70.0 in March 2026 versus 67.0 in March 2025; improvement mainly from Usage.
- An index monitors reach/depth/quality but does not prove income, empowerment or poverty impact.
- NITI urban MPI incidence fell from 8.65% in 2015-16 to 5.27% in 2019-21, but an urban aggregate can hide slum, migrant, housing and service-quality deprivation.
- Disaggregate women, children, elderly, disability, migrants, SC/ST, rural-urban, regions and slums; cross-tab where evidence permits.
