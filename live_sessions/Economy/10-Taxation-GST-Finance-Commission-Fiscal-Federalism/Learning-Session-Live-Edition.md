## The first question: what is a tax supposed to achieve?

```text
PRIVATE ECONOMIC ACTIVITY
        |
        +-- creates taxable income, profit, property or consumption
        v
     TAX REVENUE
        |
        +-- public goods
        +-- redistribution
        +-- stabilisation
        `-- correction of harmful externalities
```

A **tax** is a compulsory levy imposed under law for public purposes. It is not a
price paid for an exactly proportionate personal service. A person may pay income tax
without receiving a government service of equal rupee value, while a person with little
taxable income may receive schooling, policing or public health.

### Part A - The six tests of a good tax

| Test | Question |
|---|---|
| Revenue adequacy | Does it raise stable resources? |
| Equity | Is the burden fairly distributed? |
| Efficiency or neutrality | Does it avoid unnecessary distortion? |
| Certainty | Can the taxpayer know liability, time and method? |
| Simplicity | Is the rule understandable and compliance proportionate? |
| Administrability | Can government enforce it at reasonable cost? |

These tests can conflict. A finely differentiated rate structure may target equity but
increase classification disputes. A very simple uniform rate may lower compliance cost
but burden households differently because their consumption baskets differ.

### Part B - Base, rate and liability

```text
Tax liability = tax base x applicable rate
```

The **base** is what is taxed: income, profit, value added, property, transaction or a
specific quantity. The **rate** may be ad valorem, such as a percentage of value, or
specific, such as an amount per unit.

**Example.** If taxable income is Rs 8 lakh, the entire Rs 8 lakh is not necessarily
taxed at one rate. A slab system may apply different marginal rates to different
portions. The **average tax rate** is total tax divided by total income; the
**marginal tax rate** applies to the next unit of taxable income.

The example explains rate structure, not the current statutory slabs, which are
time-sensitive.

### Part C - Benefit and ability-to-pay principles

- The **benefit principle** links payment to benefit received. It is useful for fees,
  tolls and some local services but weak for redistribution and public goods.
- The **ability-to-pay principle** distributes burden according to economic capacity.
  It supports vertical equity between unequal taxpayers and horizontal equity between
  similarly placed taxpayers.

**Strong objection:** Taxation reduces disposable income and can weaken work, saving or
investment incentives.

**Reply:** The relevant comparison is not "tax versus no tax." It is one tax design
against another, including the productive effect of public goods, infrastructure and
human capital financed by revenue.

**Balanced verdict:** A tax is efficient only relative to the public purpose it finances
and the alternative methods available.

### UPSC use

- Distinguish tax from fee, penalty and borrowing.
- Do not infer fairness from a high rate alone.
- In Mains, test both the collection side and the spending side.

### Revision notes

1. Tax is compulsory and legally imposed.
2. Tax is not a quid pro quo for an equal individual service.
3. Base and rate jointly determine gross liability.
4. Marginal and average rates are different.
5. Equity includes horizontal and vertical equity.
6. Neutrality means avoiding unnecessary distortion, not zero behavioural effect.
7. Simplicity and fine targeting may conflict.
8. Tax-financed public goods can improve efficiency.
9. Benefit taxation cannot alone finance redistribution.
10. Evaluate the tax-and-expenditure system together.

### Practice

**MCQ 1.** Which statement best describes a tax?

A. A compulsory public levy under law without exact quid pro quo

B. A user charge linked to a measured government service

C. A government security carrying a repayment obligation

D. A punitive payment triggered by a legal breach

**Answer: A**

- **A is correct:** it captures compulsion, law and public purpose.
- **B is wrong:** a measured user charge has a service-linked quid pro quo.
- **C is wrong:** taxation does not create an individual repayment claim.
- **D is wrong:** a penalty responds to breach; a tax applies to a lawful taxable event.

**MCQ 2.** A tax concession delivered through a deduction should primarily be evaluated as:

A. an administrative simplification without a fiscal cost

B. a benchmarked tax expenditure

C. concealed tax evasion by the beneficiary

D. a recoverable loan recorded as a financial asset

**Answer: B**

- **A is wrong:** forgone revenue has an opportunity cost.
- **B is correct:** a concession is analogous to spending through the tax code.
- **C is wrong:** a lawful concession is not evasion.
- **D is wrong:** no repayment obligation is created.

**Mains micro-drill - 10 marks:** "A good tax system is a balance, not a maximisation of
one objective." Explain.

**Model answer:** A good tax must raise adequate and predictable revenue, distribute
burden fairly, minimise avoidable distortions, remain certain and simple, and be
administratively feasible. Maximising one objective can damage another. Numerous
concessions may improve targeting but narrow the base and create litigation; a single
rate may simplify compliance but affect poor and rich consumption baskets unequally.
Very high rates may encourage avoidance, while very low rates may underfinance public
goods. The correct test therefore combines the tax's design, behavioural effects,
administrative cost and the distribution of the expenditure it finances. Broad bases,
moderate rates, transparent concessions and capable administration generally offer a
better balance than either punitive rates or indiscriminate exemptions.

## The person who pays government may not bear the burden

```text
STATUTORY LIABILITY
seller remits tax
        |
        v
price, wage, rent or return adjusts
        |
        v
ECONOMIC INCIDENCE
consumer / worker / owner bears real-income loss
```

**Statutory incidence** identifies who must remit the tax. **Economic incidence**
identifies whose purchasing power ultimately falls. Direct and indirect taxes describe
the legal route; they do not mechanically settle the final burden.

### Elasticity rule

The side of a market that is less able to change behaviour tends to bear more burden.

| Market condition | Likely burden |
|---|---|
| Demand relatively inelastic | Consumers bear more through price |
| Supply relatively inelastic | Producers or factor owners bear more |
| Many substitutes and mobile base | Greater behavioural response and avoidance risk |
| Short run with fixed choices | Burden may differ from long-run burden |

**Numerical illustration.** A Rs 10 tax is imposed on a product. If the consumer price
rises by Rs 7 and the producer's net receipt falls by Rs 3, consumers bear Rs 7 and
producers Rs 3. The seller may remit all Rs 10, but remittance is not burden.

The split is illustrative; real incidence depends on elasticities, competition,
informality and time.

### Progressive, proportional and regressive

| Pattern | Average burden as income rises |
|---|---|
| Progressive | Rises |
| Proportional | Remains constant |
| Regressive | Falls |

A consumption tax can be regressive relative to income because poorer households spend
a larger share of income. Yet exemptions, lower rates on essentials and transfers can
alter the system-wide result. Likewise, a direct tax is not automatically progressive:
a flat-rate direct tax is proportional.

### Efficiency-equity frontier

```text
More redistribution
      |
      +-- may improve social welfare and opportunity
      `-- may increase avoidance or reduce some incentives

More neutrality
      |
      +-- may widen the base and simplify compliance
      `-- may ignore unequal capacity and consumption
```

The **Laffer curve** is only a possibility: beyond some very high rate, revenue may fall
because the base contracts or compliance weakens. It does not prove that every tax cut
raises revenue.

### Criticism and reply

**Objection:** Incidence theory is too abstract for an informal and regulated economy.

**Reply:** That is a reason to qualify the model, not discard it. Informality, price
controls, market power and delayed adjustment are additional channels to analyse.

### UPSC use

- Separate legal payer, collection point and final burden.
- Never write "indirect taxes are always fully shifted."
- Judge progressivity across taxes and transfers where the question asks about equity.

### Revision notes

1. Remittance is not final burden.
2. Less elastic demand shifts burden toward consumers.
3. Less elastic supply shifts burden toward producers or factor owners.
4. Short-run incidence can differ from long-run incidence.
5. Direct does not necessarily mean progressive.
6. Indirect does not necessarily mean fully regressive.
7. Average burden determines progressive/proportional/regressive classification.
8. Differentiated rates and transfers can modify consumption-tax incidence.
9. Market power complicates competitive incidence.
10. The Laffer curve is not a universal case for tax cuts.

### Practice

**MCQ 3.** A seller legally remits a tax, but demand is highly inelastic and supply is
relatively elastic. Which outcome is most likely?

A. Sellers, because legal remittance fixes final incidence

B. Both sides equally, because the statutory rate is common

C. Consumers, through a higher market price

D. Workers, because wage adjustment precedes price adjustment

**Answer: C**

- **A is wrong:** legal remittance does not determine economic incidence.
- **B is wrong:** a common statutory rate does not imply equal economic burden.
- **C is correct:** the less elastic demand side changes quantity less and bears more.
- **D is wrong:** the stated demand-supply elasticities point to price shifting toward
  buyers, not a prior wage adjustment.

**MCQ 4.** Which statement is correct?

A. Direct-tax classification guarantees a progressive burden

B. A single consumption-tax rate establishes proportionality to income

C. Regressivity means the marginal schedule declines with each transaction

D. Average burden rises with income or ability to pay

**Answer: D**

- **A is wrong:** a direct tax can be proportional.
- **B is wrong:** one transaction rate does not make burden proportional to household
  income.
- **C is wrong:** regressivity may arise from burden as a share of income.
- **D is correct:** it states the distributional test.

**Mains micro-drill - 10 marks:** Explain why statutory incidence is an unsafe guide to
tax equity.

**Model answer:** Statutory incidence identifies the person required to remit tax, while
economic incidence identifies whose real income falls after prices, wages, rents and
returns adjust. A GST-registered seller remits output tax, but consumers may bear part
through higher prices and producers part through lower margins. The division depends
on relative elasticities, market power, informality and the adjustment period.
Therefore, equity cannot be inferred from the tax label or legal payer. A direct
corporate tax may partly affect workers or consumers, while an indirect tax may be
moderated by exemptions and transfers. Sound analysis traces the adjustment mechanism
and evaluates the complete tax-and-transfer system.

## Why can tax revenue grow faster than the economy?

```text
Income growth
   -> automatic base growth -----------------> TAX ELASTICITY
   -> rate/base/compliance policy changes ---> additional effect
                                                |
                                                v
                                           TAX BUOYANCY
```

**Tax buoyancy** is the percentage change in tax revenue divided by the percentage
change in the relevant income base, including discretionary policy changes.
**Tax elasticity** attempts to remove the effect of discretionary changes and isolate
the automatic response. **Tax effort** compares actual mobilisation with taxable
capacity; it is not another name for a high rate.

If nominal GDP rises 10 per cent and tax revenue rises 12 per cent, observed buoyancy is
1.2. That does not prove elasticity is 1.2 because rates, exemptions or enforcement may
have changed.

### Tax expenditure and effective rates

An exemption, deduction, credit, preferential rate or deferral can reduce the
**effective tax rate** below the statutory rate. Its estimated revenue forgone is a
**tax expenditure** relative to a defined benchmark.

**Limit:** removing a concession will not necessarily recover the full estimated amount;
behaviour and the benchmark itself matter.

### Administration is part of tax design

| Institution | Primary domain |
|---|---|
| CBDT | Union direct taxes |
| CBIC | Customs and Union indirect-tax/GST administration |
| State GST administrations | State GST administration |
| DGGI | Serious GST intelligence and investigation |
| DRI | Customs intelligence and anti-smuggling domain |
| ED | Statutory foreign-exchange and money-laundering enforcement domain |

All three named enforcement bodies are under the Department of Revenue, Ministry of
Finance, but their legal mandates are not interchangeable.

### Digitalisation and formalisation

Third-party reporting, e-filing, e-invoicing and invoice matching can:

```text
create information trail
 -> reduce concealment
 -> widen effective base
 -> improve buoyancy
```

But technology can also impose fixed costs on small firms and create false positives.
The correct reform is risk-based administration plus prompt correction and appeal, not
maximum friction.

### International and boundary examples

- The **Equalisation Levy** historically addressed specified non-resident digital
  services. The remaining levy on specified online-advertisement services was
  withdrawn for consideration received or receivable from 1 April 2025. The 2018 PYQ
  therefore tests the law then in force; earlier-period assessments, appeals or other
  proceedings remain governed by the law applicable to their period.
- **Indirect-transfer rules** can tax an offshore share transfer when substantial value
  is derived from Indian assets, subject to statutory conditions.
- Agricultural-income and rural-land boundaries are legal definitions, not everyday
  descriptions of "rural activity."

### Current anchor

The official Economic Survey 2025-26 reports average revenue receipts of about 9.1 per
cent of GDP in FY22-FY25, against 8.5 per cent in FY16-FY20, and links stronger direct
tax performance to compliance, formalisation and profitability. These are dated
aggregates, not proof that every tax is more efficient or equitable.

### Revision notes

1. Buoyancy includes policy changes.
2. Elasticity attempts to remove discretionary changes.
3. Tax effort compares collection with capacity.
4. Tax expenditure is benchmark-dependent revenue forgone.
5. Statutory and effective rates can differ.
6. Digital trails can widen the effective base.
7. Compliance costs can be regressive across firm size.
8. CBDT and CBIC have different domains.
9. DGGI, DRI and ED are not substitutes.
10. International tax rules require year-specific status.

### Practice

**MCQ 5.** Tax revenue rises 15 per cent while nominal income rises 10 per cent after a
major compliance reform. Which conclusion is safest?

A. Buoyancy is 1.5; elasticity needs reform adjustment

B. Tax elasticity is 1.5 despite the compliance reform

C. Tax effort also equals the observed buoyancy ratio

D. The statutory rate rose in the same proportion as revenue

**Answer: A**

- **A is correct:** buoyancy uses observed revenue; elasticity removes discretionary change.
- **B is wrong:** the compliance reform contaminates the automatic response.
- **C is wrong:** effort compares actual mobilisation with capacity.
- **D is wrong:** revenue growth does not reveal the rate change.

**MCQ 6.** Which pairing is correct?

A. DRI - personal income-tax assessment

B. DGGI - GST intelligence and investigation

C. CBDT - customs anti-smuggling intelligence

D. ED - routine State GST return administration

**Answer: B**

- **A is wrong:** DRI's principal domain is customs intelligence.
- **B is correct:** DGGI is the specialised GST intelligence body.
- **C is wrong:** CBDT administers Union direct taxes.
- **D is wrong:** ED has distinct statutory enforcement functions.

**Mains micro-drill - 10 marks:** Distinguish tax buoyancy, tax elasticity and tax
effort.

**Model answer:** Tax buoyancy measures the observed responsiveness of tax revenue to
growth in the relevant income base and therefore includes rate changes, base
rationalisation and enforcement reform. Tax elasticity estimates the automatic
responsiveness after removing such discretionary changes. Tax effort is different: it
asks how far a government uses its taxable capacity, given income structure,
administrative ability and assigned bases. A digital compliance drive may raise
buoyancy without changing underlying elasticity; a poorer State may show strong effort
despite a low tax-GSDP ratio. Comparisons must use consistent periods, nominal bases,
refund treatment and legal coverage.

## Before asking who receives tax, ask who may impose it

```text
Article 246 + Seventh Schedule
        |
        +-- Union tax fields
        +-- State tax fields
        |
        +-- Article 246A: special shared GST competence
        `-- Article 248: residuary power, subject to Article 246A
```

Taxing power must have constitutional authority. Article 246 ordinarily works through
the Seventh Schedule. Article 246A creates a special GST arrangement: Parliament and
State legislatures have simultaneous power over GST, while Parliament has exclusive
power for inter-State supplies.

### Stable allocation examples

| Union domain | State/local domain or boundary |
|---|---|
| Income tax other than agricultural income | Taxes on agricultural income |
| Customs duties | Land and building/property taxation |
| Corporation tax | State excise on alcoholic liquor for human consumption |
| Inter-State GST under Article 269A | State GST on intra-State supplies |

This table is a conceptual map, not a complete reproduction of every constitutional
entry.

### Direct-tax architecture after 1 April 2026

The Income-tax Act, 2025 and Income-tax Rules, 2026 commenced on 1 April 2026.
Earlier assessment periods and proceedings remain governed by the applicable earlier
law. The enactment of a new code must not be confused with the annual Finance Act that
sets or changes rates.

### Agricultural-income and rural-land boundary

The 2025 Prelims question tested two traps:

- poultry farming and wool rearing do not become agricultural income merely because
  they occur in rural areas;
- rural agricultural land is excluded from the capital-asset definition only under
  statutory conditions.

The safe approach is to apply the legal definition, not common-language labels.

### Cross-border base protection

Digitalisation and offshore holding structures challenge tax sovereignty. The
Equalisation Levy is now a historical response: the remaining levy on specified
online-advertisement services was withdrawn from 1 April 2025. This does not rewrite
the law applicable to earlier periods or the 2018 PYQ. Indirect-transfer rules remain a
separate source-nexus response. In an answer, explain the tax-base problem before
quoting a year-specific provision.

### Criticism and reply

**Objection:** Constitutional division becomes obsolete in a digital economy.

**Reply:** Digital activity complicates location and nexus, but constitutional
competence remains the starting point. Reform must adapt statutes and coordination
without bypassing legislative authority.

### Revision notes

1. Tax requires constitutional and statutory authority.
2. Article 246A is a special GST power, not an ordinary Concurrent List entry.
3. Parliament has exclusive GST competence over inter-State supply.
4. Article 248 residuary power is subject to Article 246A.
5. Income tax other than agricultural income is a Union field.
6. Agricultural-income status is definition-based.
7. Rural agricultural land has statutory conditions.
8. A new tax code and annual rate choices are different.
9. Earlier periods may remain governed by earlier law.
10. Equalisation Levy answers must state its historical period and withdrawal from
    1 April 2025.

### Practice

**MCQ 7.** Which statement best describes Article 246A?

A. It creates an ordinary Union-only GST taxing field

B. It makes each Council recommendation self-executing law

C. Concurrent GST power, except Parliament over inter-State supply

D. It limits States to GST administration without legislative power

**Answer: C**

- **A is wrong:** States retain intra-State GST competence.
- **B is wrong:** the Council recommends; legislatures enact.
- **C is correct:** this is the special constitutional design.
- **D is wrong:** non-GST State taxes and SGST competence remain.

**MCQ 8.** Which proposition is safest?

A. Rural location is sufficient to establish agricultural income

B. Agricultural use alone settles capital-asset status

C. The Income-tax Act, 2025 fixes rates without annual Finance legislation

D. Both treatments depend on statutory definitions and conditions

**Answer: D**

- **A is wrong:** allied business is not automatically agricultural income.
- **B is wrong:** qualifying rural agricultural land may be excluded.
- **C is wrong:** annual Finance legislation and applicable provisions matter.
- **D is correct:** legal boundaries control.

**Mains micro-drill - 10 marks:** Why is GST's constitutional design different from
ordinary tax assignment?

**Model answer:** Ordinary tax competence is primarily distributed by Article 246 and
the Seventh Schedule. GST instead rests on Article 246A, a special provision that gives
Parliament and State legislatures simultaneous power over intra-State GST while
reserving inter-State GST to Parliament. Article 269A then provides Union levy and
collection with apportionment, and Article 279A creates the coordinating Council. Thus
GST pools authority over a common base rather than merely transferring a State tax to
the Union. The design promotes a national market but makes legislative coordination,
settlement and federal trust indispensable.

## The pre-GST problem was not simply "too many taxes"

```text
Manufacturer -> wholesaler -> retailer
 excise base      VAT base      VAT base
       \________ incomplete cross-credit ________/
                    |
                    v
              cascading + border friction
```

GST sought a common supply base and a continuous input-credit chain. The 101st
Constitutional Amendment received assent in 2016; the principal GST laws became
operational on 1 July 2017.

### Taxes substantially subsumed

| Union-side levies | State-side levies |
|---|---|
| Service tax | State VAT/sales tax |
| Central excise on covered supplies | Central Sales Tax as collected |
| Additional excise duties | Purchase tax |
| CVD and SAD on imports | Entry tax/octroi |
| Relevant supply-related cesses/surcharges | Luxury and specified entertainment taxes |
| | Taxes on lotteries, betting and gambling |

The list should always carry the qualification "on covered supplies" because GST did
not swallow every levy.

### Major outside-GST boundaries

- basic customs duty;
- stamp duty;
- property tax;
- electricity duty;
- State excise on alcoholic liquor for human consumption;
- petroleum crude, petrol, diesel, natural gas and aviation turbine fuel until the
  GST Council recommends an operative date.

Outside GST is not the same as exempt under GST. An exempt supply lies within the GST
framework but bears no output tax under the applicable rule; a non-GST item lies outside
the levy.

### What GST can and cannot achieve

**Potential gains**

- reduced cascading;
- common market and fewer internal tax barriers;
- better invoice trail;
- destination-based revenue assignment;
- easier comparison of effective rates.

**Limits**

- multiple classifications can create disputes;
- excluded sectors break the credit chain;
- refunds can lock working capital;
- small firms face fixed compliance costs;
- a destination shift can concern producing States.

### Current status caution

Official 56th GST Council material dated 3 September 2025 described a broad 5 per cent
merit rate, 18 per cent standard rate and 40 per cent special demerit rate, with most
changes intended from 22 September 2025. A Council recommendation is not the charging
notification. Item-level liability must always be checked against the applicable
notification and date.

### Revision notes

1. GST is a supply-based value-added tax.
2. Its constitutional foundation is the 101st Amendment.
3. Rollout began on 1 July 2017.
4. GST unified many, not all, indirect taxes.
5. Basic customs duty remains outside.
6. Alcohol for human consumption is constitutionally excluded.
7. Specified petroleum products await a Council-recommended date.
8. Outside-GST and exempt supply differ.
9. A common base supports but does not guarantee a common market.
10. Current rates are notification- and date-specific.

- **B is wrong:** basic customs duty remains outside.

**Mains micro-drill - 10 marks:** Explain why "One Nation, One Tax" is an incomplete
description of GST.

**Model answer:** GST created a harmonised destination-based tax on supply and subsumed
many Union and State indirect taxes. Its dual design, common credit chain and IGST
settlement reduced cascading and internal barriers. Yet it is not literally one tax:
intra-State supplies carry CGST and SGST or UTGST, inter-State supplies use IGST, and
rates and exemptions vary by notification. Basic customs duty, stamp duty, property
tax, electricity duty and State excise on alcoholic liquor remain outside; specified
petroleum products await inclusion. "One Nation, One Tax" is therefore a political
shorthand for a coordinated base and market, not an exact constitutional description.

**Progress: 6/14 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: How dual GST moves across India: supply, CGST-SGST-UTGST, IGST and destination**

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Economy 10 GST mechanism, IGST Act concepts and constitutional owner queried
CA search: Not forced; place-of-supply rules are taught as stable mechanism
CA found: No separate current claim required (stable theory)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

## One sale, two governments, one credit chain

```text
INTRA-STATE SUPPLY
value Rs 1,000; assumed total GST 18%
        -> CGST Rs 90
        -> SGST Rs 90

INTER-STATE SUPPLY
value Rs 1,000; assumed IGST 18%
        -> IGST Rs 180
        -> settlement toward destination
```

The rates are illustrative. An intra-State taxable supply ordinarily attracts CGST plus
SGST, or CGST plus UTGST in the applicable Union Territory framework. An inter-State
supply ordinarily attracts IGST.

### Supply before tax

GST liability requires a statutory **supply**. The analysis proceeds:

```text
Supply? -> time -> value -> supplier/recipient location
        -> place of supply -> intra/inter-State character
        -> rate, person liable and credit
```

Turnover or payment alone does not prove a taxable supply.

### Article 269A and the IGST bridge

The Union levies and collects IGST on inter-State supplies and the proceeds are
apportioned between the Union and States. Imports are treated as inter-State supplies.
IGST is not a permanent extra layer over CGST and SGST; it is a clearing mechanism that
keeps credit moving across borders.

### Destination principle

Revenue follows the jurisdiction of final consumption rather than remaining solely
where production occurs.

**Example.** A Gujarat manufacturer sells to a Maharashtra dealer, who sells to a final
consumer in Maharashtra. The IGST and credit-settlement chain moves the consumption-tax
revenue toward Maharashtra.

**Limit:** place of supply is a legal rule. Billing address, factory location and
physical movement do not always provide the answer, especially for services, immovable
property and digital transactions.

### Origin versus destination

| Origin principle | Destination principle |
|---|---|
| Revenue linked to production/seller jurisdiction | Revenue linked to consumption jurisdiction |
| Can favour producing regions | Gives greater relevance to consuming regions |
| Risks tax exporting | Supports import-export neutrality |

Destination taxation can improve consumption-side fairness but creates settlement,
data and classification dependence.

### Revision notes

1. Supply is the taxable-event gateway.
2. Intra-State supply ordinarily uses CGST plus SGST/UTGST.
3. Inter-State supply uses IGST.
4. Article 269A governs IGST apportionment.
5. Imports are treated as inter-State supplies.
6. IGST preserves cross-border credit.
7. Destination means final-consumption jurisdiction.
8. Place of supply is statutory, not intuitive.
9. Union collection does not mean Union retention of all IGST.
10. Producing and consuming States can face different incentives.

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Ramesh Singh VAT/GST evidence and Economy 10 ITC sessions queried

CA found: Economic Survey 2025-26, released 29 January 2026 - GST reform and digital transaction trails are linked with compliance and formalisation
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

## GST taxes value added only when the credit chain survives

```text
Farmer/producer sells input:      Rs 100 + Rs 18 tax
Processor sells output:          Rs 200 + Rs 36 tax
Processor's eligible ITC:        Rs 18
Processor's net payment:         Rs 36 - Rs 18 = Rs 18
```

The processor added Rs 100 of value and pays Rs 18 net at the assumed rate. **Input-tax
credit (ITC)** allows eligible input tax to offset output-tax liability. It reduces
cascading; it is not unconditional cash income.

### Conditions and broken chains

Credit depends on statutory eligibility, business use, documents, receipt and other
conditions. Blocked credit, exempt use and non-compliance can turn input tax into cost.

```text
eligible credit -> neutral value-added chain
blocked credit  -> embedded cost -> possible higher price
```

Anti-abuse restrictions protect revenue, but overly broad restrictions can recreate
cascading and working-capital pressure.

### Four categories that UPSC mixes

| Category | Output GST | Input-credit consequence |
|---|---:|---|
| Zero-rated | Zero on qualifying outward supply | Eligible credit/refund route generally preserved |
| Exempt | No output tax | Attributable credit generally restricted/reversed |
| Nil-rated | Rate notified at zero | Generally treated within exempt-supply consequences |
| Non-GST | Outside GST levy | Not an exempt GST supply |

Exports and authorised SEZ supplies are important zero-rated categories. Imports are
taxed within the inter-State framework so that domestic and imported consumption face
comparable destination taxation.

### Inverted duty structure

An **inverted duty structure** exists when the input tax rate exceeds the output tax
rate. Credit accumulates and may require a statutory refund route.

**Example:** inputs taxed at 18 per cent feed an output taxed at 5 per cent. Even a
profitable firm can face cash-flow stress because credit accumulates faster than output
liability.

### Criticism and reply

**Objection:** Invoice matching converts businesses into tax-enforcement agents.

**Reply:** The credit chain creates self-reporting incentives, but fairness requires
reliable portals, proportionate documentation, supplier-default safeguards and timely
refunds.

### Revision notes

1. ITC offsets eligible input tax against output liability.
2. ITC approximates tax on value added.
3. Credit is conditional, not automatic.
4. Blocked credit can recreate cascading.
5. Zero-rated and exempt are not synonyms.
6. Zero rating generally preserves a credit/refund route.
7. Exemption generally breaks attributable credit.
8. Non-GST lies outside the levy.
9. Inverted duty creates credit accumulation.
10. Refund delay is a working-capital cost.

**Mains micro-drill - 10 marks:** Explain how ITC supports efficiency but creates a

**Model answer:** ITC allows eligible tax paid on business inputs to offset output-tax

supports production neutrality and creates an invoice trail. The same mechanism,

Blocked credits, supplier default, classification disputes and delayed refunds can
embed tax or lock working capital, especially for small firms and inverted-duty
sectors. Efficiency therefore requires both an intact credit chain and proportionate,
reliable administration.

**Progress: 8/14 | Stage: Advanced | Subtopic: GST Council and cooperative federalism: voting, law-making and *Mohit Minerals***

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Economy 10 and Polity GST Council owners queried
CA search: "GST Council 2026 latest official decisions compensation cess"
CA found: 56th GST Council recommendations dated 3 September 2025 - recommendations required the relevant notifications, with most changes effective 22 September 2025
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

## A common tax needs a forum, but the forum is not a legislature

```text
ARTICLE 279A GST COUNCIL
Union Finance Minister - Chair
Union Minister of State - member
One nominated minister from each State - members

Vote weight: Union 1/3 | States together 2/3
Decision: at least 3/4 of weighted votes present and voting
Quorum: one-half of total membership
```

The design prevents either side from carrying a formal vote alone. The Union can block
a proposal because one-third exceeds the one-quarter blocking threshold, but cannot
pass it without State support. States collectively cannot reach three-fourths without
the Union.

### What the Council recommends

The Council makes recommendations on rates, exemptions, model laws, thresholds,
special provisions and the GST framework. It does not:

- enact Parliament's or a State's GST law;
- administer every return;
- distribute the divisible pool;
- replace the Finance Commission;
- adjudicate every tax dispute.

### The legal-effect ladder

```text
Constitutional competence
       -> Council recommendation
          -> legislation or delegated notification
             -> effective date
                -> administration and adjudication
```

A press release may announce policy, but tax liability follows the valid legal
instrument.

### *Mohit Minerals* (2022)

In *Union of India v. Mohit Minerals Pvt Ltd*, decided on 19 May 2022, the Supreme
Court held that GST Council recommendations have persuasive value and are not binding
on Union and State legislatures. Article 246A gives both levels legislative authority;
Article 279A supplies a forum for collaborative dialogue.

**What the judgment does not mean**

- taxpayers may ignore enacted GST law;
- harmonisation is unconstitutional;
- the Council has become irrelevant.

It means political coordination cannot be converted into legal subordination without
constitutional or statutory authority.

### Cooperative, accommodative and competitive federalism

| Idea | GST expression |
|---|---|
| Cooperative | joint deliberation and common rules |
| Accommodative | mutual adjustment and transition support |
| Competitive | States still compete through administration, infrastructure and non-GST policy |

**Strong criticism:** the Union's blocking weight, superior fiscal capacity and control
over much of the administrative agenda can create asymmetry.

**Reply:** States collectively possess two-thirds of vote weight, Council practice has
usually preferred consensus, and *Mohit Minerals* preserves legislative autonomy.

**Residual problem:** legal autonomy cannot by itself prevent fragmentation; a common
market still depends on reason-giving and negotiated consistency.

### Revision notes

1. The GST Council is constitutional under Article 279A.
2. It is chaired by the Union Finance Minister.
3. Union vote weight is one-third.
4. States together hold two-thirds.
5. Decision threshold is at least three-fourths.
6. Quorum is one-half.
7. Recommendations are not self-executing tax law.
8. *Mohit Minerals* calls them persuasive, not binding.
9. Enacted statutes and valid notifications remain binding.
10. Cooperative federalism requires continuing dialogue.

**Progress: 9/14 | Stage: Advanced | Subtopic: GST compensation: transition insurance, the pandemic shock and federal trust**

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Economy 10 compensation evidence and GST Council official material queried

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

## Why would a State surrender a familiar tax base?

```text
State gives up major indirect taxes
        |
        v
uncertain GST transition revenue
        |
        v
five-year statutory compensation promise
        |
        v
political consent to reform
```

The GST (Compensation to States) Act, 2017 used 2015-16 as the base year and protected
defined State revenue at 14 per cent annual growth for the five-year transition ending
in June 2022. A compensation cess on specified luxury and demerit goods financed the
fund.

### Four concepts that must stay separate

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Compensation entitlement | Time-bound statutory protection of defined revenue |
| Compensation cess | Financing levy on specified supplies |
| Back-to-back borrowing | Pandemic-era bridge used to transfer resources to States |
| Tax devolution | States' share of the Article 270 divisible pool |

Compensation was not a permanent constitutional share. Cess continuation for servicing
liabilities was not an extension of the five-year entitlement.

After the original entitlement ended in June 2022, cess collections continued during
the separate post-2022 debt-servicing phase. Notification No.
03/2025-Compensation Cess (Rate), dated 31 December 2025, substituted nil rates for the
listed compensation-cess entries with effect from 1 February 2026. This dated rate
notification should not be described as extending the former State compensation
entitlement, cancelling the pandemic-era borrowing retrospectively or deciding any
earlier-period liability.

### COVID-19 stress test

```text
economic shutdown
 -> GST collection falls
 -> protected compensation gap rises
 -> cess fund insufficient
 -> dispute over who should borrow
 -> back-to-back Union borrowing transfers funds to States
```

The shock exposed an incomplete contingency design. States relied on a statutory
promise; the Union faced a financing gap in a nationally synchronised downturn.

### Producing-State concern

Destination taxation can redirect revenue toward consuming States. Compensation made
the initial bargain credible for States expecting losses from the change in tax
geography.

### Reform lesson

A durable transition mechanism needs:

1. a transparent base and formula;
2. independent and timely reconciliation;
3. an explicit shock-financing rule;
4. a credible sunset;
5. clear separation between entitlement, levy and debt servicing.

**Objection:** Compensation encouraged States to expect revenue without strengthening
their own administration.

**Reply:** Temporary insurance can enable a welfare-improving reform; the answer is
time-bound support with effort incentives, not denial of transition risk.

### Revision notes

1. The Compensation Act was statutory, not a permanent constitutional entitlement.
2. Base year was 2015-16.
3. Protected growth was 14 per cent annually.
4. Original transition lasted five years.
5. It ended in June 2022.
6. Compensation cess financed the fund.
7. COVID widened the gap between claims and cess proceeds.
8. Back-to-back borrowing was a financing bridge.
9. Loan servicing and compensation entitlement differ.
10. Notification No. 03/2025 made the listed cess rates nil from 1 February 2026 after
    the separate debt-servicing phase.

```text
registration
```

### Registration is not the taxable event

Turnover thresholds provide conditional relief from registration. Specified persons may
still face compulsory registration, and threshold rules vary by supply type and
jurisdiction. Registration does not itself create a taxable supply; supply does.

### Composition scheme

The composition scheme offers eligible small taxpayers simplified payment and returns,
but ordinarily restricts the normal ITC chain and comes with activity and turnover
conditions.

| Normal scheme | Composition scheme |
|---|---|
| ordinary invoice-credit chain | simplified payment |
| input credit subject to conditions | no ordinary ITC collection chain |

```text
buyer wants ITC
 -> asks supplier for valid invoice
 -> transaction enters digital trail
```

transactions, legal protections and productive scale. Excessive fixed compliance costs
can push micro firms in the opposite direction.

Fake invoices can create wrongful credit without genuine supply. Because the credit
network is connected, one false invoice can propagate across firms. Risk analytics,
e-invoicing and cross-verification help, but flags are not final guilt.

**Enforcement principle**

```text
risk signal -> verification -> reasoned notice -> adjudication -> appeal
```

### Administration and trust

Good administration requires:

- stable and clear forms;
- reliable portals;
- prompt refunds;
- proportionate penalties;
- coordination between CBIC and State administrations;
- distinction between mistake, aggressive avoidance and fraud.

6. Formalisation is wider than tax registration.
7. Refund delay creates working-capital cost.
8. Fake invoices can transmit wrongful credit.

**Progress: 11/14 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Fiscal federalism: assignment, vertical imbalance, horizontal imbalance and equalisation**

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Ramesh Singh public finance and Economy 10 fiscal-federal sections queried

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

## The government that spends is not always the government with the strongest taxes

```text
UNION
strong broad tax bases
        |
        | devolution and grants
        v
STATES
health, police, agriculture, infrastructure and services
        |
        | State-local transfers
        v
LOCAL BODIES
water, sanitation, local roads and civic services
```

**Fiscal federalism** assigns expenditure responsibilities, revenue powers, transfers
and borrowing across levels of government.

### Assignment principles

- **Subsidiarity:** place a service near citizens when local information and
  accountability matter.
- **Economies of scale:** centralise where one system is cheaper or more coherent.
- **Spillovers:** higher-level finance may be justified when benefits cross borders.
- **Redistribution and stabilisation:** national capacity may be stronger for broad
  redistribution and macroeconomic shocks.

### Vertical fiscal imbalance

Vertical imbalance arises when one level has stronger revenue powers while another has
large expenditure responsibilities. It is addressed through tax devolution, grants and
borrowing space.

### Horizontal fiscal imbalance

States differ in:

- income and taxable capacity;
- population and demographic structure;
- area and cost of delivery;
- forest/ecological constraints;
- historical infrastructure;
- revenue effort and governance.

Horizontal devolution seeks **equalisation**: enabling reasonably comparable public
services at reasonably comparable tax effort. It does not guarantee identical spending
or outcomes.

### Need versus incentive

| Need/equity logic | Incentive/contribution logic |
|---|---|
| income distance | tax/fiscal effort |
| population | demographic performance |
| area and cost disability | contribution to output |
| forest/ecology constraints | accountable spending |

No formula is politically neutral. A stronger need weight benefits poorer-capacity
States; a stronger contribution weight rewards richer or better-performing States.

### Criticism and reply

**Objection:** Equalisation rewards weak tax effort.

**Reply:** Capacity gaps are partly structural. A sound formula can combine dominant
equalisation with bounded incentives and transparent performance measures.

**Objection:** Contribution criteria punish citizens of poorer States.

**Reply:** Some incentive is defensible, but it should not defeat the constitutional
goal of comparable service capacity.

### Revision notes

1. Fiscal federalism includes spending, revenue, transfers and borrowing.
2. Subsidiarity is not automatic decentralisation.
3. Spillovers can justify higher-level finance.
4. Vertical imbalance is between levels.
5. Horizontal imbalance is among States.
6. Devolution and grants are different tools.
7. Equalisation seeks comparable capacity, not identical outcomes.
8. Income distance is an equity criterion.
9. Performance criteria create incentives but may favour stronger States.
10. Accountability requires predictable resources and clear responsibility.

**Mains micro-drill - 15 marks:** Why must horizontal devolution balance equity with
incentives?

**Model answer:** Horizontal devolution addresses unequal fiscal capacity and service
costs among States. Income distance, population, area and forest constraints support
equalisation so that residence in a poorer State does not permanently determine access
to basic services. Yet transfers that ignore tax effort, demographic performance or
accountability may weaken incentives and face resistance from contributing States.
Conversely, excessive performance weight rewards States already possessing stronger
bases and institutions. A balanced formula should keep equalisation central, use
measurable and controllable incentive criteria, avoid volatile indicators and preserve
State responsibility for own revenue and spending quality.

## Forty-one per cent of what?

```text
GROSS UNION TAX RECEIPTS
 - refunds and collection adjustments
 - Article 271 surcharges
 - applicable specific-purpose cesses
 = SHAREABLE NET PROCEEDS / DIVISIBLE POOL
          |
          v
   vertical devolution percentage
```

The **divisible pool** consists of shareable net proceeds under Article 270. Article 279
defines net proceeds and gives final certification authority to the CAG. A devolution
percentage is applied to this pool, not to all gross Union tax receipts.

### Cess and surcharge

- A **cess** is generally imposed for a specified purpose.
- A **surcharge** is an additional charge on a tax under Article 271.
- Applicable cesses and Article 271 surcharges are outside the divisible pool.

**Numerical illustration.** Suppose gross relevant collections are Rs 100, of which
Rs 12 are excluded cesses/surcharges and Rs 3 represent refunds/collection adjustments.
The illustrative pool is Rs 85. A 41 per cent State share applies to Rs 85, not Rs 100.

This is arithmetic for understanding, not actual budget data.

### Why the issue is federal

More non-shareable levies give the Union earmarked flexibility but reduce the base for
untied State devolution. The headline percentage can therefore remain stable while the
effective share of gross revenue changes.

### Devolution versus grants

| Transfer | Character |
|---|---|
| Tax devolution | Formula-based share of net tax proceeds |
| Article 275 grants | Statutory grants-in-aid linked to Finance Commission principles |
| Article 282 grants | Discretionary grants for any public purpose |
| Centrally sponsored scheme transfer | Often conditional and tied to programme design |

Article 275 and Article 282 are not interchangeable. Tied grants may address spillovers
or minimum standards, but excessive conditions can blur accountability and restrict
State priorities.

### Strongest arguments

**For cesses:** earmarking can protect nationally important expenditure and improve
political acceptability.

**Against overuse:** it can fragment the tax system, weaken legislative scrutiny of
outcomes and narrow the shareable pool.

**Balanced reform:** sunset clauses, transparent utilisation statements and a broader
shareable base can preserve purpose without hollowing out devolution.

### Revision notes

1. Article 270 governs shareable net proceeds.
2. Article 279 defines net proceeds and CAG certification.
3. Article 271 covers Union surcharges.
4. Devolution is not applied to gross tax receipts.
5. Cesses and surcharges can narrow the pool.
6. Tax devolution is generally untied.
7. Article 275 grants differ from Article 282 grants.
8. Conditional grants can correct spillovers.
9. Excessive conditions can weaken State autonomy.
10. Earmarking needs utilisation transparency and sunset review.

**Mains micro-drill - 15 marks:** Explain why rising cesses and surcharges can weaken
fiscal federalism even when vertical devolution remains unchanged.

**Model answer:** Vertical devolution is a percentage of the Article 270 divisible pool,

trust. Reform should favour broad shareable taxes, transparent cess accounts, periodic
sunsets and targeted grants only where spillovers or minimum standards justify
conditions.

### State borrowing and Article 293

Article 293 governs State borrowing. A State indebted to the Union may require Union
consent for further borrowing, and consent may carry conditions. The rule addresses
shared macroeconomic risk, but it also creates a federal-autonomy debate.

Borrowing is not inherently irresponsible. The relevant questions are:

- Is debt financing productive capital or recurring consumption?
- Is the interest burden sustainable?
- Are contingent and off-budget liabilities disclosed?
- Does the project create future revenue or social returns?

The RBI's *State Finances: A Study of Budgets 2025-26* reports consolidated State gross
fiscal deficit budgeted at 3.3 per cent of GDP and outstanding liabilities projected at

inter-State variation.

- delayed or irregular SFC constitution;

# ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS MODEL PRACTICE

## Question 1 - 10 marks, 150 words

**"The legal payer of a tax need not be its final bearer." Explain with reference to
elasticity.**

### Model answer

Tax impact falls initially on the person legally required to remit it, while incidence
is the final economic burden after prices, wages and returns adjust. Elasticity
determines the ability to escape that burden. If demand is relatively inelastic,
consumers reduce purchases only slightly after a price increase, so producers can pass
more tax forward. If supply is relatively inelastic, producers cannot readily withdraw
capacity and bear more through lower net receipts.

For example, a Rs 20 statutory tax on a seller need not reduce the seller's receipt by
Rs 20; the market price may rise by Rs 15, leaving consumers with most of the burden.
The reverse is possible when demand is elastic. Long-run incidence may also differ as
firms relocate or consumers substitute.

Hence progressivity cannot be inferred merely from the statute. Sound tax design must
analyse market structure, substitution possibilities and time horizon.

## Question 2 - 15 marks, 250 words

**Analyse GST's destination principle as both an economic reform and a federal
redistribution mechanism.**

### Model answer

GST taxes consumption broadly at destination rather than production at origin. Within
a State, CGST and SGST apply concurrently; on an inter-State supply, IGST acts as a
clearing mechanism. Eligible input credit follows the invoice chain and settlement
moves the State component toward the jurisdiction of final consumption.

Economically, this reduces origin-based cascading and the incentive to locate activity
merely for tax arbitrage. It supports a common market, improves production neutrality
and allows exports to be zero-rated so domestic indirect tax is not exported.

Federally, however, destination changes the geography of revenue. Consuming States may
gain relative to producing States that earlier relied on origin-linked levies.
Place-of-supply rules therefore become distribution rules, especially for services and
digital transactions. Transitional compensation made State consent credible, while
the GST Council provides continuing coordination.

The model faces limitations: blocked credit, petroleum exclusions, classification
disputes, delayed refunds and inaccurate place-of-supply rules can break neutrality.
Moreover, destination cannot by itself equalise State capacity, which remains a task
for Finance Commission transfers.

Thus GST is simultaneously a value-added tax architecture and a negotiated
redistribution of tax jurisdiction. Its legitimacy depends on accurate settlement,
stable credit rules and credible federal consultation.

## Question 3 - 20 marks, 250 words

**"India's fiscal federalism must reconcile autonomy, equalisation and macroeconomic
stability." Discuss with reference to the Finance Commission, grants and State
borrowing.**

### Model answer

India's Constitution assigns the Union broad tax bases while States bear major
service-delivery responsibilities, producing vertical imbalance. Unequal income,
population, area and ecological constraints also create horizontal imbalance.

The Finance Commission under Article 280 addresses both. It recommends the States'
collective share in Article 270 net proceeds, horizontal allocation and Article 275
grant principles, while supporting State-fund augmentation for local bodies. Income
distance advances equalisation; population and area reflect need and cost; demographic,
effort or contribution criteria preserve incentives. Formula-based devolution gives
States greater autonomy than tightly tied grants.

Grants remain necessary where spillovers, minimum standards or specific disabilities
cannot be handled by a general formula. Yet excessive Article 282 or scheme conditions
can blur accountability. Similarly, widespread cesses and surcharges narrow the
shareable base even when the headline devolution rate is stable.

Article 293 introduces macroeconomic discipline by permitting Union consent conditions
for borrowing by indebted States. Such oversight can contain common debt risk, but it
must be transparent and distinguish productive capital from fiscal concealment.
Off-budget liabilities and guarantees require full disclosure.

A durable settlement therefore needs a broad divisible pool, predictable untied
transfers, bounded outcome-oriented grants, transparent borrowing rules and regular
State Finance Commissions. Autonomy without discipline risks instability; discipline
without adequate resources reduces federalism to unfunded responsibility.

# REMEDIATION

## Error 1: A higher marginal rate applies to the whole income

**MCQ 37.** Which observation correctly distinguishes marginal and average tax rates?

A. A higher marginal rate on the next unit need not apply to the whole income

B. The average rate measures tax due on the next unit of income

C. The marginal rate equals total tax divided by total income

D. The rate schedule by itself establishes final economic incidence

**Answer: A**

- **A is correct:** slab rates apply to successive portions, not retrospectively to the
  entire base.
- **B is wrong:** that describes the marginal rate, not the average.
- **C is wrong:** total tax divided by total income is the average rate.
- **D is wrong:** incidence also depends on behavioural and market adjustment.

**Repair rule:** Write the slab calculation first, then distinguish marginal rate,
average rate and final incidence.

## Error 2: An inverted duty structure means output tax is higher

**MCQ 38.** Which rate relationship creates an inverted duty structure?

A. Output tax rates exceed input rates, rapidly using accumulated credits

B. Input tax rates exceed output rates, allowing eligible credit to accumulate

C. Export output is relieved while eligible input relief is preserved

D. The supply is legally outside the GST levy and credit framework

**Answer: B**

- **A is wrong:** that relationship ordinarily consumes rather than accumulates credit.
- **B is correct:** higher input taxation relative to output can create excess credit
  and refund pressure.
- **C is wrong:** that describes zero rating, not rate inversion.
- **D is wrong:** non-GST status is a different legal category.

**Repair rule:** Compare the input rate with the output rate before discussing refund
or working-capital effects.

## Error 3: All intergovernmental transfers provide equal State discretion

**MCQ 39.** Which transfer generally provides States the widest spending discretion?

A. An Article 282 grant tied to a centrally designed programme

B. A local-body grant tied to a specified service condition

C. Formula-based tax devolution from the divisible pool

D. Compensation-cess proceeds committed to servicing transition borrowing

**Answer: C**

- **A is wrong:** programme conditions constrain spending choice.
- **B is wrong:** the specified service condition makes the grant tied.
- **C is correct:** formula-based tax devolution is ordinarily untied.
- **D is wrong:** earmarked debt servicing is not discretionary State revenue.

**Repair rule:** Classify a transfer by source, formula and conditions before judging
fiscal autonomy.

## Error 4: A grant alone completes functional devolution

**MCQ 40.** A State transfers sanitation responsibility and a tied grant to a
municipality but retains the engineers and procurement authority. What is the best
diagnosis?

A. Excessive municipal reliance on own-source revenue

B. Horizontal fiscal imbalance between richer and poorer States

C. Incorrect apportionment of inter-State GST revenue

D. Incomplete devolution of functionaries and operational authority

**Answer: D**

- **A is wrong:** the facts describe retained State capacity, not excessive municipal
  taxation.
- **B is wrong:** the problem is within the State-local assignment, not disparities
  among States.
- **C is wrong:** IGST settlement has no connection with municipal sanitation staffing.
- **D is correct:** money and a formal function cannot deliver services when staff and
  operating control remain elsewhere.

**Repair rule:** Test decentralisation through the three Fs - functions, funds and
functionaries - plus accounts and audit.

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

## 1. Purpose and principles of taxation

- Taxes finance public goods, redistribution, stabilisation, demerit correction and
  State capacity.
- A good tax system seeks adequacy, equity, efficiency, certainty, convenience,
  simplicity and administrability.
- Benefit principle links payment to benefit; ability-to-pay supports vertical and
  horizontal equity.
- Efficiency cost includes compliance, administration, avoidance and deadweight loss,
  not only the amount collected.
- A broad base with moderate rates usually reduces distortion and discretion.

## 2. Classification and distribution

- Direct/indirect identifies the legal and institutional form; it does not by itself
  establish economic incidence.
- Progressive: average rate rises; proportional: constant; regressive: average rate
  falls.
- Specific tax is per physical unit; ad valorem tax is a percentage of value.
- Origin taxation follows production; destination taxation follows consumption.
- Equity needs incidence analysis across income groups, markets and time.

## 3. Incidence and elasticity

- Impact is initial liability; shifting is adjustment; incidence is final burden.
- Less elastic side generally bears more because it can escape less.
- Statutory collection from sellers can still burden buyers.
- Long-run elasticity may be greater, changing incidence over time.
- Salience, informality, market power and enforcement qualify the simple model.

## 4. Revenue diagnostics

- Tax buoyancy = percentage change in tax revenue / percentage change in nominal GDP.
- Elasticity removes estimated discretionary policy effects.
- Tax effort compares collection with taxable capacity.
- Tax expenditure is revenue forgone through concessions; it should face review like
  explicit expenditure.
- Sustainable mobilisation depends on base, administration, compliance and economic
  structure.

## 5. Constitutional tax map

- Parliament and State legislatures receive taxing competence through the
  Constitution; no tax can be imposed without authority of law under Article 265.
- Article 246A creates concurrent GST power, with Parliament's special role over
  inter-State supply.
- Article 269A governs inter-State GST levy and apportionment.
- Agricultural income and rural agricultural land questions require exact statutory
  definitions, not conversational meaning.
- Equalisation Levy is historical for current-law purposes: the remaining levy on
  specified online-advertisement services was withdrawn from 1 April 2025. The 2018
  PYQ and earlier-period proceedings must still be analysed under the law applicable
  to their period.

## 6. GST architecture

- The 101st Amendment pooled GST power and created the Council.
- Major subsumed levies included Union excise/service-tax and State VAT/sales-tax
  components within the GST base; exclusions and retained levies remain important.
- Intra-State supply generally produces CGST plus SGST/UTGST.
- Inter-State supply and imports use IGST.
- Destination principle moves revenue toward final consumption.
- Supply, time and place rules determine charge, timing and jurisdiction.

## 7. ITC and category boundaries

- Output tax minus eligible input credit approximates tax on value addition.
- Valid invoice, business use, receipt and legal conditions matter.
- Blocked credit embeds tax even in a nominal VAT.
- Zero rating preserves eligible input relief; exemption generally breaks or restricts
  it.
- Nil-rated, exempt and non-GST are not synonyms.
- Inverted duty can create accumulated credit and refund pressure.
- Delayed refunds are a working-capital tax in economic effect.

## 8. GST Council and law

- Article 279A: Union Finance Minister chairs; Union has one-third vote weight and
  States together two-thirds.
- Three-fourths weighted majority and one-half quorum apply.
- Council recommendations require legislation or valid delegated notification where
  the legal framework so requires.
- *Mohit Minerals* (19 May 2022): recommendations are persuasive, not binding on
  legislatures.
- The ruling preserves enacted-law compliance and recognises cooperative dialogue
  rather than hierarchy.

## 9. Compensation

- Compensation Act used 2015-16 base and 14 per cent protected annual growth.
- Original State entitlement lasted five years and ended in June 2022.
- Compensation cess, entitlement and back-to-back borrowing are distinct.
- COVID created simultaneous collection collapse and larger protected gaps.
- Notification No. 03/2025-Compensation Cess (Rate), dated 31 December 2025, made the
  listed compensation-cess rates nil from 1 February 2026 after the separate
  post-2022 debt-servicing phase.
- Transition insurance needs a clear base, shock rule, reconciliation and sunset.

## 10. Administration and formalisation

- Registration threshold is conditional; supply remains the taxable-event gateway.
- Composition simplifies compliance for eligible small taxpayers but limits ordinary
  credit-chain participation.
- Invoice demand, e-invoicing and data matching can widen the formal trail.
- Formalisation is durable record, finance and legal integration, not mere
  registration.
- Fake-invoice control requires analytics plus verification, reasoned notice,
  adjudication and appeal.
- DGGI, DRI and ED must not be casually merged; their tax/customs/enforcement mandates
  differ.

## 11. Fiscal federalism

- It allocates functions, taxes, transfers and borrowing across levels.
- Subsidiarity favours local information; scale, spillovers, redistribution and
  stabilisation can favour higher levels.
- Vertical imbalance is between levels; horizontal imbalance is among States.
- Equalisation seeks comparable service capacity at comparable effort, not identical
  outcomes.
- Formula design balances need, cost disability, ecology, demographic performance,
  effort and contribution.

## 12. Divisible pool and grants

- Article 270 covers shareable net proceeds; Article 279 addresses net proceeds and CAG
  certification.
- Devolution percentage applies to the divisible pool, not gross Union taxes.
- Article 271 surcharges and applicable cesses remain outside the pool.
- Article 275 grants follow constitutional grant-in-aid principles; Article 282 permits
  discretionary grants for public purposes.
- Untied devolution supports autonomy; tied grants require spillover or standard-based
  justification.
- Cesses need transparent use, sunset review and protection against permanent pool
  erosion.

## 13. Finance Commission

- Article 280: President constitutes every fifth year or earlier; Chairperson plus four
  members.
- It recommends vertical distribution, horizontal allocation, Article 275 principles
  and State-fund augmentation for local bodies.
- Article 281 requires the report and explanatory action memorandum before Parliament.
- Recommendations are advisory but institutionally influential.
- XV-FC (2021-26) recommended 41 per cent vertical devolution and used income distance,
  population, area, forest/ecology, demographic performance and tax/fiscal effort.
- XVI-FC (2026-31) retains accepted 41 per cent vertical share.
- XVI horizontal weights: income distance 42.5, population 17.5, demographic
  performance 10, area 10, forest 10, contribution to GDP 10 per cent.
- Weights distribute the States' collective share; they are not expenditure earmarks.

## 14. State and local finance

- State resources combine own taxes/non-tax revenue, devolution, grants and borrowing.
- Article 293 may require Union consent for further borrowing by a State indebted to
  the Union.
- Judge debt through sustainability, interest burden, capital quality, guarantees and
  off-budget disclosure.
- RBI's dated 2025-26 budget study placed consolidated State GFD at 3.3 per cent of GDP
  and liabilities at 29.2 per cent by end-March 2026; State variation remains large.
- Articles 243-I and 243-Y establish SFC review of Panchayat and Municipality finance.
- Article 280(3)(bb)-(c) connects Union Finance Commission support to State-fund
  augmentation on the basis of SFC recommendations.
- Local reform: timely SFCs, predictable transfers, property-tax capacity, fair user
  charges, accounts, audits, staff and outcome accountability.
