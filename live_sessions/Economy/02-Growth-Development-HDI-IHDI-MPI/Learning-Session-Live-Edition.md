*Growth creates resources; development depends on how effectively and equitably those resources improve people’s lives.*

## 1. Core distinction

| Economic growth | Economic development |
|---|---|
| ✅ Quantitative increase in **real output or real income** | ✅ Growth plus **structural and qualitative transformation** |
| Measured mainly through real GDP and per-capita income | Assessed through income, health, education, employment and distribution |
| Asks: **How much more is produced?** | Asks: **How have people’s lives and opportunities improved?** |
| May occur without equitable distribution | Requires broader capability expansion |
| Primarily an economic-output concept | Economic, social, institutional and environmental concept |

> 🔑 **Mnemonic:** **Growth enlarges the cake; development examines its size, ingredients, distribution and sustainability.**

## 2. Why growth is necessary but insufficient

✅ **Growth provides resources**

```text
Higher productivity
        ↓
Higher output
        ↓
Income + profits + tax revenue
        ↓
Capacity to finance jobs and public services
```

⚠️ **But conversion is not automatic.** Growth may be:

- concentrated in capital-intensive sectors;
- geographically limited to prosperous regions;
- accompanied by informal or insecure employment;
- distributed unequally;
- environmentally damaging;
- unsupported by quality health and education.

Therefore:

```text
Growth without conversion channels
              =
More output but limited development
```

## 3. Four tests of development

| Test | Question |
|---|---|
| **Employment** | Has growth generated productive and secure work? |
| **Distribution** | Have benefits reached poorer groups and lagging regions? |
| **Capabilities** | Have health, education and real opportunities improved? |
| **Sustainability** | Can progress continue without exhausting environmental and fiscal resources? |

## 4. Structural transformation

✅ Development generally involves movement:

```text
Low-productivity activity
          ↓
Higher-productivity agriculture, industry and services
          ↓
Better employment and wages
          ↓
Greater human capabilities
```

⚠️ Movement out of agriculture is not automatically development. If workers merely enter insecure, low-productivity urban work, the economy has changed sectors without achieving sufficient qualitative transformation.

## 5. Inclusive and sustainable development

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ✅ **Inclusive growth** | Growth whose opportunities and benefits are broadly shared |
| ✅ **Sustainable development** | Development meeting present needs without undermining future generations’ ability to meet theirs |
| ⚠️ **Productive inclusion** | Enabling people to participate through skills, assets, credit, infrastructure and decent employment—not merely receiving transfers |

Transfers can relieve immediate deprivation, but long-term development also requires productivity, capabilities and secure livelihoods.

## 6. India-focused example

✅ The World Bank’s **April 2026 India Development Update** reported that India’s real growth accelerated to **7.6% in FY2025–26**, while formal job creation strengthened.

⚠️ This establishes strong **economic growth**, but development must still be evaluated separately through:

- employment quality and labour productivity;
- women’s economic participation;
- health and learning outcomes;
- regional and social disparities;
- environmental resilience.

A high GDP-growth rate alone cannot answer these questions.

## UPSC traps

- ❌ **Growth and development are interchangeable.**
  ✅ Growth is one component and resource base of development.

- ❌ **Any movement from agriculture to services proves development.**
  ✅ Productivity, wages and employment quality must improve.

- ❌ **Redistribution alone constitutes inclusive growth.**
  ✅ Inclusion also requires participation in productive opportunities.

- ❌ **Development necessarily follows GDP growth.**
  ✅ Institutions and conversion channels determine the outcome.

## Mini recap

1. ✅ Growth means sustained increase in real output or income.
2. ✅ Development includes structural change, capabilities and distribution.
3. ⚠️ Growth creates resources but does not determine their use.
4. ⚠️ Jobs and public services are major conversion channels.
5. ✅ Inclusion and sustainability are essential tests of development.

*Equal resources do not necessarily produce equal freedom or well-being.*

## 1. Amartya Sen’s central argument

✅ The **capability approach** evaluates development through people’s substantive freedoms—the real opportunities available to live lives they have reason to value.

```text
Income ≠ Development itself
Income → one means of expanding capabilities
```

Economic policy must therefore ask:

- Can a person actually obtain healthcare?
- Can a child receive meaningful education?
- Can women participate safely in economic and public life?
- Can a person secure dignified employment?
- Can citizens exercise meaningful choice?

## 2. Functionings versus capabilities

| Concept | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ **Functionings** | Achieved states of being and doing | Being nourished, educated or employed |
| ✅ **Capabilities** | Real combinations of functionings a person can choose | Genuine opportunity to study, work or remain healthy |
| ✅ **Agency** | Ability to pursue goals one considers valuable | Choosing an occupation or participating in public decisions |

### Fasting versus starvation

```text
Person A has food but chooses not to eat → fasting
Person B has no access to food           → starvation
```

Both may display the same functioning—“not eating”—but their capabilities differ fundamentally:

- Person A possesses the option to eat.
- Person B lacks that option.

✅ Thus, the capability approach considers **freedom and choice**, not merely the observed outcome.

## 3. Why equal income may not mean equal capability

Suppose two people receive the same income:

| Person | Conversion condition | Capability outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Healthy urban adult | Nearby services and accessible transport | Converts income relatively easily |
| Person with disability in a remote area | Higher mobility costs and limited services | Same income produces fewer real opportunities |

⚠️ Income equality can coexist with capability inequality because people differ in:

- age and health;
- disability;
- gender and social norms;
- geographical location;
- household responsibilities;
- access to quality public services.

## 4. Means and ends of development

| Means | Ultimate developmental ends |
|---|---|
| Income | Healthy life |
| Infrastructure | Mobility and access |
| School buildings | Education and learning |
| Hospitals | Effective healthcare |
| Employment schemes | Livelihood security and agency |
| Legal rights | Ability to exercise those rights |

> 🔑 **Mnemonic:** **Resources → Conversion → Capabilities → Choice → Functionings**

## 1. What is HDI?

✅ The **Human Development Index** is a composite index published by the **United Nations Development Programme (UNDP)**.

✅ It evaluates average achievement in three fundamental dimensions:

| Dimension | Indicator |
|---|---|
| Health | Life expectancy at birth |
| Education | Mean years and expected years of schooling |
| Standard of living | Gross National Income per capita at purchasing-power parity |

> 🔑 **Mnemonic:** **Life – Learning – Living standard**

## 2. Education indicators

| Indicator | What it represents |
|---|---|
| ✅ **Mean years of schooling** | Average completed schooling of adults aged 25 years and above |
| ✅ **Expected years of schooling** | Years of schooling a child entering school can expect under prevailing enrolment patterns |

```text
Mean years = education already achieved
Expected years = prospective educational opportunity
```

⚠️ Neither indicator directly measures learning quality. A child can spend many years in school without acquiring corresponding literacy or numerical capabilities.

## 3. Why GNI rather than GDP?

✅ **Gross National Income (GNI)** measures income received by a country’s residents, including net primary income from abroad.

```text
GNI = GDP + net primary income from abroad
```

✅ HDI uses **GNI per capita**, adjusted for **purchasing-power parity (PPP)**, to improve comparison of actual purchasing capacity across countries.

⚠️ Per-capita GNI remains an average and does not show how income is distributed.

## 4. How HDI is calculated

### Step 1: Normalisation

The indicators have different units—years, schooling and money. Each is converted into a dimension index between **0 and 1**.

### Step 2: Education combination

Mean-years and expected-years indices are combined into one education index.

### Step 3: Geometric mean

```text
HDI = ∛(Health index × Education index × Income index)
```

✅ HDI uses a **geometric mean**, not a simple arithmetic average.

### Why does this matter?

Consider two hypothetical countries:

| Country | Health | Education | Income |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | High | High | High |
| B | Very high | Very low | High |

⚠️ The geometric mean penalises Country B’s imbalance. Very high income cannot completely compensate for extremely poor education.

> **Exam line:** HDI rewards balanced achievement across dimensions and restricts perfect substitutability among them.

## 5. Latest available India baseline

✅ The *Human Development Report 2025*, using **2023 data**, reported:

| Indicator | India |
|---|---:|
| HDI value | **0.685** |
| Rank | **130 out of 193 countries** |
| Category | **Medium human development** |

⚠️ These are not monthly or 2026 HDI statistics. HDI is released through periodic Human Development Reports, commonly using data from an earlier reference year.

## 6. What HDI captures—and misses

| HDI captures | HDI does not adequately capture |
|---|---|
| Average longevity | Inequality within dimensions |
| Schooling duration | Learning quality |
| Average national income | Income distribution |
| Cross-country comparability | Gender and regional disparities |
| Balanced basic achievements | Political freedom and personal agency |
| Broad development beyond GDP | Environmental sustainability |

This is why supplementary indices are required:

```text
HDI  → average achievement
IHDI → achievement adjusted for inequality
MPI  → overlapping household deprivation
```

## UPSC traps

- ❌ HDI uses GDP per capita.
  ✅ It uses **GNI per capita at PPP**.

- ❌ Education consists only of literacy rate.
  ✅ It uses mean and expected years of schooling.

- ❌ HDI is an arithmetic average.
  ✅ It is the geometric mean of three dimension indices.

- ❌ A high HDI proves equal distribution.
  ✅ HDI reports average achievement; inequality is addressed by IHDI.

- ❌ HDI directly measures political freedom.
  ✅ Political freedom is not one of its three formal dimensions.

## 1. What is IHDI?

✅ The **Inequality-adjusted Human Development Index** adjusts each HDI dimension for inequality in its distribution.

| HDI | IHDI |
|---|---|
| Average potential achievement | Achievement after accounting for inequality |
| Uses national health, education and income averages | Discounts these dimensions according to their unequal distribution |
| Does not show who receives the gains | Better reflects how widely human development is shared |
| Suitable for average human development | More useful for examining inclusive development |

> 🔑 **Mnemonic:** **HDI shows the height; IHDI shows how evenly that height is shared.**

## 2. Equality condition

✅ If health, education and income achievements are distributed equally:

```text
IHDI = HDI
Inequality loss = 0%
```

✅ As inequality increases:

```text
Adjusted dimension indices decline
              ↓
IHDI falls below HDI
              ↓
Inequality loss increases
```

The difference is not a separate monetary deduction. It represents the reduction in effective human development caused by unequal distribution.

## 3. Inequality-loss calculation

✅ The proportional loss can be expressed as:

```text
Inequality loss = 1 − (IHDI ÷ HDI)
```

### Hypothetical example

Suppose:

```text
HDI  = 0.700
IHDI = 0.560
```

Then:

```text
Loss = 1 − (0.560 ÷ 0.700)
     = 1 − 0.80
     = 20%
```

⚠️ Interpretation: unequal distribution reduces effective human-development achievement by 20% relative to the equality benchmark represented by HDI.

## 4. Why IHDI better reflects inclusive growth

Consider two countries with the same HDI:

| Dimension | Country A | Country B |
|---|---|---|
| Average health | Similar | Similar |
| Average education | Similar | Similar |
| Average income | Similar | Similar |
| Distribution | Relatively equal | Highly unequal |
| HDI | Same | Same |
| IHDI | Closer to HDI | Much lower than HDI |

✅ HDI may rank them similarly because their averages are equal.

✅ IHDI distinguishes them because Country B’s achievements are concentrated among fewer people.

```text
Growth in averages
        ≠
Broad sharing of opportunities
```

## 5. Dimensions adjusted

IHDI applies an inequality adjustment to all three HDI dimensions:

| Dimension | Relevant inequality |
|---|---|
| Health | Unequal longevity and survival outcomes |
| Education | Unequal years of schooling |
| Income | Unequal distribution of income |

❌ IHDI is not merely HDI adjusted for income inequality.

✅ Inequality in health and education also matters.

## 6. HDI, IHDI and inclusive growth

```text
Economic growth
      ↓
Higher average income and public resources
      ↓
Possible improvement in HDI
      ↓
Were improvements broadly distributed?
      │
   ┌──┴──┐
   │     │
  Yes    No
   │     │
Small   Large
HDI–    HDI–
IHDI    IHDI
gap     gap
```

⚠️ IHDI is a better indicator of inclusive growth because it combines:

1. average achievement;
2. distribution within each dimension;
3. the penalty caused by unequal access and outcomes.

## 7. Limitations

- ⚠️ IHDI still covers only health, education and income.
- ⚠️ It does not directly measure political freedom, employment quality or environmental sustainability.
- ⚠️ National IHDI can conceal caste, gender, tribal, disability and regional inequalities.
- ⚠️ Survey quality and data lags affect the inequality adjustment.
- ⚠️ It identifies unequal outcomes but does not independently explain their causes.

## 🖼️ Visual: How MPI works

```text
HOUSEHOLD
    │
    ↓
Check deprivation in each indicator
    │
    ↓
Add the assigned indicator weights
    │
    ↓
Weighted deprivation score
    │

    │

                            │
             ┌──────────────┴──────────────┐
             ↓                             ↓
      H = Incidence                  A = Intensity
     How many are poor?       How deprived are the poor?
             │                             │
             └──────────────┬──────────────┘
                            ↓
                       MPI = H × A
```

## 1. Why MPI is needed

An income poverty line asks:

> Is household income or consumption below a specified monetary threshold?

MPI asks:

> Does the household face overlapping deprivations in health, education and living conditions?

| Income poverty | Multidimensional poverty |
|---|---|
| Monetary threshold | Weighted deprivation cutoff |
| Primarily shows how many are below the line | Shows incidence and intensity |
| Cannot identify deprivation composition | Identifies specific overlapping deficits |
| Useful for cash-transfer adequacy | Useful for multidimensional policy targeting |

✅ MPI **complements** rather than replaces income-poverty measurement.

## 2. Global MPI dimensions and indicators

✅ The global MPI is produced by **UNDP** with the **Oxford Poverty and Human Development Initiative (OPHI)**.

```text
                         GLOBAL MPI
                            │
       ┌────────────────────┼─────────────────────┐
       ↓                    ↓                     ↓
     HEALTH              EDUCATION          LIVING STANDARDS
      1/3                   1/3                   1/3
```

| Dimension | Indicators | Individual weight |
|---|---|---:|
| Health | Nutrition; child mortality | `1/6` each |
| Education | Years of schooling; school attendance | `1/6` each |
| Living standards | Cooking fuel, sanitation, drinking water, electricity, housing and assets | `1/18` each |

✅ Each dimension has an overall weight of **one-third**.

> 🔑 **Mnemonic:** Health **2**, Education **2**, Living standards **6** → **10 global indicators**

## 3. India’s National MPI

✅ **NITI Aayog** publishes India’s National MPI.

✅ India’s national framework contains **12 indicators**, adding:

- **maternal health** under health;
- **bank account** under standard of living.

```text
Global MPI:   10 indicators
India MPI:    12 indicators
              + maternal health
              + bank account
```

❌ Do not assume the global and Indian indicator lists are identical.

## 4. Identifying an MPI-poor household

### Step 1: Indicator deprivation

For each indicator, determine whether the household crosses its specified deprivation threshold.

### Step 2: Weighted score

Add the weights of all indicators in which the household is deprived.

### Step 3: Poverty cutoff

✅ A household is classified as multidimensionally poor when its weighted deprivation score is **at least one-third**.

```text
Deprivation score = 0.25 → not MPI-poor
Deprivation score = 0.33 → MPI-poor
Deprivation score = 0.50 → MPI-poor
```

⚠️ Being deprived in one indicator does not automatically make a household MPI-poor. The **weighted total** matters.

## 5. Incidence: H

✅ **Incidence**, or the headcount ratio, is the proportion of the population classified as multidimensionally poor.

```text
H = Multidimensionally poor population
    ─────────────────────────────────────
             Total population
```

Example:

```text
30 out of 100 people are MPI-poor

H = 30/100 = 0.30 or 30%
```

It answers: **How many people are poor?**

## 6. Intensity: A

✅ **Intensity** is the average proportion of weighted deprivations experienced by multidimensionally poor people.

Suppose the poor experience these deprivation scores:

```text
40%, 50%, 45%, 45%
```

Then:

```text
A = average deprivation score among the poor
  = 45%
```

It answers: **How poor are the poor in multidimensional terms?**

❌ Non-poor households are not included while calculating `A`.

## 7. MPI formula

```text
MPI = H × A
```

Example:

```text
H = 30% = 0.30
A = 45% = 0.45

MPI = 0.30 × 0.45
    = 0.135
```

✅ A lower MPI can result from:

- fewer people remaining poor—a fall in `H`;
- poor people experiencing fewer deprivations—a fall in `A`;
- both occurring together.

## 8. Why H and A must be separated

```text
Region A                         Region B
20% are poor                     30% are poor
Poor face 60% deprivation        Poor face 40% deprivation

H = 0.20                         H = 0.30
A = 0.60                         A = 0.40
MPI = 0.12                       MPI = 0.12
```

Both regions have the same MPI but different poverty structures:

- Region A has fewer poor people, but their deprivation is deeper.
- Region B has more poor people, but their average deprivation is less intense.

⚠️ Policy should therefore examine `H`, `A` and dimension-wise deprivation—not MPI alone.

## 9. Limitations

- ⚠️ Results depend on selected indicators, weights and cutoffs.
- ⚠️ Household classification may conceal unequal deprivation among household members.
- ⚠️ Survey data may be released with a substantial time lag.
- ⚠️ MPI may not capture employment insecurity, urban service quality or sudden income shocks fully.
- ⚠️ It does not show the monetary amount required to eliminate poverty.

## 3. Major limitations of composite indices

### A. Choice dependence

✅ Composite results depend on:

- selected dimensions;
- indicators;
- weights;
- deprivation thresholds;
- aggregation formula.

⚠️ Changing the method can alter a country’s value even when actual living conditions have not changed.

### B. Average blindness

```text
High national average
         │
         ├── Prosperous region: very high outcome
         └── Lagging region: severe deprivation
```

⚠️ Even IHDI may not reveal every gender, caste, tribal, disability or district-level disparity.

### C. Quantity–quality gap

| Recorded indicator | Hidden quality issue |
|---|---|
| Years of schooling | Actual learning |
| Health facility access | Treatment quality |
| Water connection | Reliability and safety |
| Employment count | Wages, security and productivity |
| Bank account | Actual usage and financial control |

### D. Data lag

✅ HDI and MPI depend on national statistics and periodic household surveys.

⚠️ Consequently, a report released in one year may describe conditions from an earlier reference year.

For example, the **Human Development Report 2025** reports India’s HDI using **2023 data**.

### E. Omitted dimensions

These indices do not comprehensively measure:

- political freedom;
- personal security;
- subjective well-being;
- employment quality;
- unpaid care work;
- environmental sustainability;
- resilience to economic and climatic shocks.

### F. Indicator gaming

⚠️ When performance is judged narrowly through rankings, authorities may concentrate on improving measured variables rather than achieving deeper structural change.

### Remedial MCQ

A region’s MPI incidence falls from 30% to 20%, while intensity remains at 50%. Which statement is correct?

A. Fewer people are MPI-poor, but the average deprivation of those remaining poor is unchanged.
B. The number of poor is unchanged, but their deprivation has declined.
C. Both the incidence and depth of poverty have increased.
D. Unchanged intensity prevents MPI from falling.

---

**Learner question / doubt**

> so in mpi incidence failing and intensity remain unchanges that means poor people numbe increased.

No—the conclusion is reversed.

```text
Incidence (H) = MPI-poor population ÷ total population
Intensity (A) = average deprivation among MPI-poor people
```

If incidence falls from **30% to 20%** in a population of 100:

- MPI-poor people fall from **30 to 20**.
- If intensity remains 50%, those 20 remaining poor people still experience an average 50% deprivation.
- MPI falls from `0.30 × 0.50 = 0.15` to `0.20 × 0.50 = 0.10`.

An unchanged intensity says nothing about an increase in the number of poor. It says the **depth of deprivation among those remaining poor has not improved**.

⚠️ If total population changes substantially, a falling percentage does not by itself prove that the absolute number fell. But no increase can be inferred without population data.

### Check your understanding

Incidence falls while intensity remains unchanged. Which conclusion follows directly?

A. A smaller proportion is poor, while deprivation among the remaining poor is equally intense.
B. A larger proportion is poor, but each poor household has fewer deprivations.
C. The poor population is unchanged because intensity did not change.
D. Both the proportion and intensity of poverty declined.

---

**Learner question / doubt**

> that is the formula

Yes, the formula is:

```text
MPI = H × A
```

Where:

```text
H = Number of MPI-poor people ÷ Total population
A = Average weighted deprivation among MPI-poor people
```

Example:

```text
H = 20% = 0.20
A = 50% = 0.50

MPI = 0.20 × 0.50 = 0.10
```

So:

- Falling **H** → a smaller proportion is poor.
- Unchanged **A** → remaining poor face the same average deprivation.

---

**Correct — A.** ✅

A fall in incidence means a smaller proportion is MPI-poor. Unchanged intensity means the remaining poor experience the same average weighted deprivation.
