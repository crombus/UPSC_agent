## Lesson 1 — What does a notion of God actually contain?

Progress: 1/12 | Stage: Foundation | Subtopic: Conceptual grammar—nature, predicates, roles and relations

*The diagram shows why “Does God exist?” is premature until we know what kind of reality the word
is supposed to denote.*

### 1. Begin with the ordinary puzzle

Two people may both say “God,” yet mean radically different things. One means a knowing and loving
creator who can be addressed as “Thou.” Another means the infinite reality in which all finite
things exist. A third means the non-dual ground beyond every limiting predicate. Agreement on the
word therefore does not establish agreement on the concept.

✅ **Canonical doctrine:** A notion of God is a structured metaphysical profile. It specifies:

1. what kind of reality is ultimate;
2. which attributes may be predicated of it;
3. what explanatory or religious roles it performs;
4. how it relates to the world;
5. how persons relate to it.

⚠️ **Analytical inference:** Conceptual clarification comes before proof. A successful argument to
a “first cause” would not by itself establish personality, goodness, omniscience or worthiness of
worship. Those additions require separate reasoning.

### 2. Personal, impersonal and transpersonal

| Notion | Plain-language idea | Technical content | Typical religious consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Personal** | Ultimate reality can know, will, love and address persons | intelligence, purposive agency and relationality without requiring a body | prayer, command, grace and I–Thou devotion are intelligible |
| **Impersonal** | Ultimate reality is not a deliberating person among persons | substance, absolute, being-itself or non-dual consciousness | contemplation, knowledge or participation may displace interpersonal prayer |
| **Transpersonal** | Ultimate reality exceeds the personal/impersonal opposition | personal language may be valid but non-exhaustive | devotion can remain valid while metaphysical language is qualified |

✅ **Canonical doctrine:** “Personal” does not mean human-shaped, biologically sexed or spatially
bounded. It indicates knowledge, will, love, purposive agency and addressability. “Impersonal” does
not mean inert matter or non-existence. Spinoza’s substance is dynamically self-expressive;
Advaita’s Brahman is pure consciousness, though not a finite deliberating subject.

**Example with limit:** A judge is “personal” because reasons, intentions and responses matter, not
because the office is defined by the judge’s height or anatomy. The analogy clarifies agency but
stops at finitude: divine personhood, if coherent, cannot simply duplicate human psychology.

### 3. Transcendence and immanence

```text
TRANSCENDENCE                         IMMANENCE
"not reducible to the world"          "present and operative within it"
        |                                      |
ontological independence              sustaining / indwelling / expressing
        |                                      |
NOT spatial distance                  NOT automatically identity
```

✅ **Canonical doctrine:** Transcendence means that God exceeds or is not exhausted by the world.
Immanence means that God is present in, sustains, indwells or expresses the world. Classical theism
normally affirms both: God is distinct from creation yet continuously sustains it.

**Example with limit:** An author is not one of the characters in a novel, yet the story depends on
the author. This helps with transcendence and dependence. It fails if taken literally, because a
novel is an artefact external to the author, while many theologies claim a more intimate sustaining
relation.

### 4. Positive, negative and analogical God-talk

✅ **Canonical doctrine:**

- **Cataphatic predication** says positively that God is good, wise or powerful.
- **Apophatic predication** denies finite limitations: God is not dependent, composite or bounded.
- **Analogical predication** claims that “good” or “knows” has a real but not exactly identical
  meaning when said of God and creatures.

The balance matters. Purely literal projection risks anthropomorphism. Total negation risks an empty
referent. Analogy tries to preserve content without making God a magnified human.

### 5. Roles are not synonyms

| Role | What it explains | Main philosophical pressure |
|---|---|---|
| Creator | why the world depends for existence | freedom or necessity of creation |
| Sustainer | why dependence continues now | divine action and natural causation |
| Providence/ruler | order and history | freedom, hiddenness and suffering |
| Moral governor | moral consequence and justice | autonomy and apparently unequal suffering |
| Ground of being | actuality without treating God as one item | whether personality survives |
| Absolute | ontological self-sufficiency | whether relation and worship become secondary |

### 6. Strongest objection and reply

**Objection:** If each tradition changes the meaning of “God,” comparison becomes impossible.

**Reply:** Comparison is possible through constant questions—nature, attributes, world-relation,
person-relation and religious consequence—without pretending that all answers are equivalent.

⚠️ **Residual:** A common matrix enables comparison but can still distort a tradition whose own
categories do not begin with a creator-person. The matrix is an analytical aid, not a replacement
for internal doctrine.

### UPSC application

- **Syllabus use:** This grammar directly organises “Attributes; Relation to Man and the World.”
- **PYQ linkage:** 2019 Q8(b) and 2023 Q5(a) require personal/impersonal analysis; 2023 Q6(b)
  requires immanence/transcendence.
- **Answer move:** Define the axis before placing schools on it.
- **Trap:** Do not infer “physical body” from personhood or “God = world” from immanence.

### Revision notes

1. A God-concept includes nature, attributes, roles and two relations.
2. Conceptual clarification logically precedes proof.
3. Personal means intelligent and relational, not bodily.
4. Impersonal does not mean unconscious or unreal.
5. Transcendence is ontological independence, not distance.
6. Immanence is presence or operation, not necessarily identity.
7. Cataphatic language affirms; apophatic language removes limits.
8. Analogy seeks meaningful predication without anthropomorphic univocity.
9. Creator, sustainer, governor, ground and Absolute perform different work.
10. Comparison needs a fixed matrix but must respect internal vocabulary.

### Visual: identity, distinction and inclusion

```text
DEISM                 CLASSICAL THEISM          PANENTHEISM          PANTHEISM

God creates           God creates and           +---- GOD ----+      GOD/NATURE
     |                sustains                   |  + WORLD +  |      one reality
     v                     |                     |  +-------+  |      finite things
world/laws             world distinct            +------------+      are within it
     X                 yet dependent             God exceeds world

```

*The decisive question is not merely whether God exists, but whether world and God are externally
related, continuously dependent, included, or identical.*

### 1. Classical theism

✅ **Canonical doctrine:** Classical theism affirms one necessary, personal, perfect God who creates
and continuously sustains a world distinct from God. God is both transcendent and immanent.
Creation is not manufacture from pre-existing material; it is the dependence of the creature’s
whole being.

**Primary and secondary causation:** Divine causation need not compete with finite causes. A seed,
rain and farmer can be genuine causes within nature while the whole causal order remains dependent
on God. The model aims to avoid the picture of God as one more force pushing alongside other
forces.

**Limit:** If divine causation sustains every act, responsibility becomes difficult. The classical
reply distinguishes the cause of an agent’s existence and power from the agent’s own exercise of
that power. The residual problem is whether that distinction is sufficient for libertarian freedom.

### 2. Deism

✅ **Canonical doctrine:** Deism affirms a rational creator who establishes nature and its laws but
rejects or minimises continuing special intervention, miracle and revelation.

```text
RATIONAL CREATOR -> ORDERED LAWS -> SELF-OPERATING COSMOS
                          |
                     religion of reason
```

Its gain is respect for stable natural order. Its cost is religious remoteness: petitionary prayer,
providence and historical revelation become difficult to defend.

**Example with limit:** The clockmaker image captures law-governed independence after design. It
fails because a clock is made from independent material, while deistic creation may still claim
ontological dependence.

### 3. Pantheism

✅ **Canonical doctrine:** Pantheism denies an ultimate God–world separation. In Spinoza’s rigorous
form, God or Nature is the one infinite substance and finite things are modes. Pantheism is not the
claim that God is merely the sum of physical objects.

**Gain:** radical immanence and metaphysical unity.
**Cost:** personal purpose, providence and interpersonal devotion are transformed or weakened.

### 4. Panentheism

✅ **Canonical doctrine:** Panentheism holds that the world is in God but God exceeds the world.
It therefore joins deep immanence with a form of transcendence.

Process and relational models may describe God as affected by the world and acting persuasively
rather than coercively.

**Strong objection:** A changing or world-dependent God may no longer be ultimate or worthy of
unconditional worship.

**Reply:** Relational perfection may consist in perfect responsiveness rather than immunity from
relation. A God who shares creaturely becoming can appear religiously more adequate than an
impassible absolute.

⚠️ **Residual:** The reply improves relationality by revising classical immutability and
omnipotence. It is a different package, not a cost-free restatement.

### 5. Why the models disagree

| Prior commitment | Model it favours | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Ultimate reality must be freely personal and non-dependent | classical theism | preserves creator–creature distinction |
| Stable nature should need no later intervention | deism | minimises special action |
| Nothing can exist outside infinite reality | pantheism | excludes independent substances |
| God must be deeply relational yet exceed finite process | panentheism | combines inclusion and excess |

### UPSC application

- Use “distinct but sustaining,” “withdrawal,” “identity,” and “inclusion-with-excess” as four
  discriminating formulas.
- For 2023 Q6(b), classical theism is the central model: transcendence without deistic absence and
  immanence without pantheistic identity.
- For 2024 Q8(b), none of the four models logically requires a finite physical body.

### Revision notes

1. Classical theism affirms a necessary personal creator and sustainer.
2. Creation means total dependence, not rearrangement of prior matter.
3. Primary divine causation need not eliminate secondary natural causes.
4. Deism retains creation but weakens providence and revelation.
5. Pantheism denies ultimate separation between God and reality.
6. Spinozan pantheism is not material aggregation.
7. Panentheism means world-in-God plus divine excess.
8. Process models gain responsiveness by revising classical perfection.
9. Immanence is broader than identity.
10. Each model purchases one explanatory gain at a philosophical cost.

### Visual: perfection is a network, not a list

```text
                         NECESSITY / ASEITY
                         exists through itself
                                |
              +-----------------+-----------------+
              |                                   |
         SIMPLICITY                          ETERNITY
     no dependent parts              timeless or everlasting
              |                                   |
        IMMUTABILITY -------- PERSONHOOD -------- KNOWLEDGE
              |             knows/wills/loves          |
              +-----------------+----------------------+
                                |
                    POWER + PERFECT GOODNESS
                                |
                  Can all these coexist coherently?
```

*The attributes constrain one another. Repairing one apparent paradox may alter another attribute.*

### 1. Omnipotence

✅ **Canonical doctrine:** Omnipotence is maximal divine power. The classical restriction says God
can actualise every **absolutely possible** state of affairs. Contradictory descriptions—such as a
square circle—do not name possible tasks.

This is not verbal evasion. “Make a square circle” combines mutually excluding conditions, so
there is no coherent outcome whose non-production would show weakness.

**Moral impeccability:** The claim that God “cannot lie” is treated as perfection, not disability.
Power is capacity for actuality, not liability to defect.

### 2. Omniscience

✅ **Canonical doctrine:** Omniscience is knowledge of every truth, without error or dependence on
discursive learning.

Questions immediately arise:

- Are future free acts already truth-valued?
- Does timeless knowledge include tensed truths such as “it is now raining”?
- Can an incorporeal knower possess first-person or indexical knowledge—what “I” and “now” mean for
  embodied agents?

The attribute is therefore more demanding than “knows a very large number of facts.”

### 3. Perfect goodness

✅ **Canonical doctrine:** Omnibenevolence or perfect goodness means unsurpassable moral perfection.
Classical theism commonly says goodness belongs necessarily to the divine nature rather than being
an external rule God happens to obey.

**Pressure:** If “good” is defined only as whatever God wills, moral praise risks becoming empty.
If goodness is independent, divine ultimacy appears limited. The full Euthyphro debate belongs to
Religion and Morality, but Topic 01 must notice that “perfectly good” requires an intelligible
standard.

### 4. Eternity

| Model | Meaning | Gain | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Timeless eternity | all temporal reality is present to one non-successive divine act | protects transcendence and complete knowledge | response, sequence and action become difficult |
| Everlasting duration | God exists through every time without beginning or end | makes interaction easier to picture | God is more closely entangled with temporal change |

✅ **Canonical doctrine:** Boethian and Thomist classical theism often favours timelessness; some
modern theists prefer everlasting existence.

### 5. Necessity and aseity

**Necessity:** God cannot fail to exist.
**Aseity:** God exists “from itself” and derives being from nothing else.

The two support ultimacy but are not identical. Necessity concerns modal status; aseity concerns
non-derivation.

### 6. Immutability, impassibility and personhood

- **Immutability:** no intrinsic change or loss of perfection.
- **Impassibility:** God is not passively overpowered or involuntarily altered.
- **Personhood:** intelligence, will, love and addressability.

**Strongest objection:** A being incapable of any real response appears less personal, not more
perfect.

**Reply:** Constancy need not mean emotional inertness. New effects and new creaturely relations
can arise from an unchanged divine act and unwavering love.

⚠️ **Residual:** If every new relation is only on the creature’s side, the religious claim that God
responds may become thin.

### 7. Why the package is difficult

⚠️ **Analytical inference:** The more strongly we protect divine independence—simplicity,
immutability, timelessness—the harder it becomes to explain responsive personality. The more
strongly we protect relational responsiveness, the more we revise classical independence.

### UPSC application

- Never write an attribute list without one-line definitions and coherence pressures.
- 2026 Q8(c) demands criticisms against omniscience and omnipotence, not a generic description of
  God.
- A strong introduction: “The classical attributes form a compossibility claim: the question is not
  whether each can be defined separately, but whether one being can possess them together.”

### Revision notes

1. Omnipotence concerns all absolutely possible states of affairs.
2. Contradictions fail to specify tasks.
3. Omniscience means every truth, not merely vast information.
4. Future freedom and tensed knowledge pressure omniscience.
5. Perfect goodness requires more than arbitrary command.
6. Timeless eternity differs from everlasting duration.
7. Necessity is modal; aseity is non-derivation.
8. Immutability concerns intrinsic change.
9. Impassibility denies involuntary alteration, not necessarily love.
10. Strong independence can strain responsive personhood.
11. Attributes must be tested for joint coherence.

### Visual: four pressure-tests and their costs

```text
ATTRIBUTE CLAIM          OBJECTION                  STANDARD REPAIR             COST
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
omnipotence              stone paradox              logical possibility          narrows naive wording
omniscience              free act known infallibly  eternity / soft facts /       disputed metaphysics
                                                   middle knowledge
timelessness             how can God act now?       one timeless act,            responsiveness puzzle
                                                   temporal effects
simplicity               attributes become one      analogical identity /         modal-collapse worry
                                                   truthmaker reading
```

### 1. The paradox of the stone

The familiar question—“Can God create a stone God cannot lift?”—constructs a dilemma:

1. If God cannot create it, there is something God cannot do.
2. If God creates it but cannot lift it, there is something God cannot do.
3. Therefore unlimited power appears incoherent.

✅ **Canonical reply:** Aquinas restricts omnipotence to what is absolutely possible. “A stone an
omnipotent being cannot lift” specifies a state inconsistent with the stipulated subject. The
phrase does not identify a possible object.

**Mackie’s refinement:** First-order power is power over things; second-order power is power over
what powers exist, including one’s own. Unrestricted possession of both creates instability. The
question therefore tests the coherence of power over power, not mineral weight.

**Counter-objection:** The restriction may look like definition-by-retreat.
**Reply:** Every capacity is specified by coherent objects; inability to instantiate contradiction
is no deficiency.
**Residual:** The response handles logical paradox, not the religious question why maximal possible
power is not used to prevent suffering.

### 2. Foreknowledge and freedom

```text
t1: God infallibly knows "S will choose A at t2"
                          |
                          v
           the past and infallibility seem fixed
                          |
                          v
t2: Can S genuinely choose not-A?
```

The dilemma does not require divine knowledge to **cause** the act. It is modal:

1. God believed at an earlier point that S would do A.
2. The past is now accidentally necessary.
3. God cannot be mistaken.
4. S cannot now refrain from A.
5. Libertarian alternative possibility appears lost.

### 3. Four replies

| Reply | Premise challenged | Gain | Objection |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Boethian eternity** | there is earlier divine foreknowledge | all times are present to timeless God | entailment may remain even without temporal priority |
| **Ockhamism** | every past fact is hard and fixed | divine past belief is a “soft fact” partly about the future | hard/soft line can look ad hoc |
| **Molinism** | foreknowledge must cause or follow the act | middle knowledge covers counterfactuals of free choice | what grounds those counterfactual truths? |
| **Open theism** | future free acts are already settled truths | God knows the future correctly as open | revises classical exhaustive foreknowledge |

⚠️ **Analytical inference:** Compatibility depends on definitions. Compatibilist freedom needs
reason-responsive voluntary action, whereas libertarian freedom requires genuine alternatives.
An answer must state which freedom it defends.

### 4. Omnipotence and free will

The 2020 PYQ says “omnipotent God,” although the standard dilemma is often framed through
omniscient foreknowledge. Treat both:

- If omnipotence means that God must determine every act, freedom disappears.
- Classical theism can instead distinguish possessing power from exercising exhaustive
  determination.
- Creating free agents may be a higher-order exercise of power, not a surrender of power.
- Yet if God infallibly controls the total order, the sourcehood of finite agency remains disputed.

**Indian parity:**

- Nyāya treats Īśvara as administrator of karmic results according to each self’s *adṛṣṭa*, not as
  author of each choice.
- Rāmānuja uses divine *anumati*—permission or sustaining consent—while moral authorship remains
  with the *jīva*.
- Advaita relocates agency to the empirical self; liberation dissolves the ignorance behind the
  bondage-question rather than inserting an exception into causality.
- Bhagavad Gītā 18.61 depicts divine indwelling governance, while 18.63 ends with “act as you wish”;
  use the pair as a tension, not as a completed solution.

### 5. Divine simplicity

✅ **Canonical doctrine:** Divine simplicity denies composition in God: no matter/form,
substance/accident, essence/existence or separable property-parts. Wisdom, goodness and power are
one divine reality differently conceived.

**Argument:**

1. A composite depends on its parts and their unifier.
2. The ultimate source cannot be dependent.
3. Therefore the ultimate must be non-composite.

**Plantinga-style objection:** If God is identical with properties, God seems to become a property,
not a personal agent.

**Modal-collapse objection:** If God, God’s act and God’s will are identical and necessary, what
makes creation contingent?

**Replies:** Defenders interpret simplicity analogically or as a truthmaker thesis and distinguish
the necessary divine act from its contingent terminus.

⚠️ **Residual:** That distinction may reintroduce the differentiation that simplicity intended to
exclude.

### 6. Timeless action

**Objection:** A timeless being cannot first refrain and then create, answer a prayer, or know that
an event is happening now.

**Reply:** One timeless divine act can ground a succession of temporal effects, just as one complete
equation can describe values at many times.

**Limit of analogy:** An equation is not an agent and does not love or respond. The analogy explains
non-successive grounding, not personal interaction.

### Revision notes

2. Logical impossibility is not an object of action.

## Lesson 5 — Spinoza: God or Nature

### Visual: one substance, infinite expression, finite modes

```text
                    GOD OR NATURE
                 one infinite substance
                           |
             infinitely many ATTRIBUTES
           ways intellect grasps divine essence
                           |
          humans know THOUGHT and EXTENSION
                 /                     \
          finite minds             finite bodies
                 \                     /
                     finite MODES
             dependent ways substance exists
```

### 1. Plain-language intuition

Imagine rejecting the idea that reality is divided into a creator outside nature and created
substances inside it. If ultimate reality is genuinely infinite, Spinoza argues, nothing
independent can stand outside it. Minds, bodies and natural events must therefore be ways the one
reality is expressed.

The analogy of waves and ocean helps: waves are real patterns of the ocean, not independent liquids.
Its limit is crucial: an ocean is composed of parts and can exist without a particular wave;
Spinoza’s substance is not a physical aggregate assembled from modes.

### 2. Technical architecture

✅ **Canonical doctrine:**

- **Substance:** what is in itself and conceived through itself.
- **Attribute:** what intellect perceives as constituting the essence of substance.
- **Mode:** an affection or dependent modification of substance.
- **God:** absolutely infinite substance possessing infinite attributes.
- **Deus sive Natura:** “God, or Nature.”

Humans know two attributes—Thought and Extension—although infinite substance has infinitely many.
A human mind is a finite mode under Thought; its body is the corresponding finite mode under
Extension.

### 3. The one-substance argument

1. Substance is conceptually and ontologically self-sufficient.
2. Two substances sharing an attribute could not be distinguished as independent substances.
3. Absolutely infinite substance possesses every attribute expressing essence.
4. No rival substance can exist outside or alongside it.
5. Therefore God/Nature is the sole substance; finite things are modes.

**Presupposition:** Conceptual self-sufficiency accurately tracks ontological self-sufficiency.

**Objection:** Definitions alone may not establish that exactly one substance exists.
**Spinozist reply:** Infinite substance cannot be limited by another of the same nature, and a
finite substance would contradict substance’s self-sufficiency.
**Residual:** Critics may still charge that the transition from conceptual containment to existence
is too rapid.

### 4. Natura naturans and natura naturata

| Term | Meaning | Avoid this error |
|---|---|---|
| **Natura naturans** | Nature naturing: God considered as active, self-caused infinite reality | a personal artisan deciding among worlds |
| **Natura naturata** | Nature natured: the total modal order following from divine nature | an independently created external world |

The relation is one of necessary expression, not a first temporal act.

### 5. Attributes and freedom

Spinoza’s God does not choose whether to create. Everything follows from divine nature with
necessity. Divine freedom therefore means acting from the necessity of one’s own nature without
external constraint, not selecting among alternatives.

Human freedom follows the same pattern. A person becomes freer through adequate understanding of
the causes shaping action, not by becoming an uncaused exception to nature.

### 6. Is Spinoza a pantheist?

✅ **Canonical classification:** Spinoza is conventionally classed as a pantheist because finite
reality is within the one substance and no transcendent creator stands outside Nature.

⚠️ **Qualification:** “God = the sum of visible objects” is false. God/Nature includes infinite
intelligible reality; modes depend on substance. Some interpreters stress an acosmist or
panentheist reading, but the safe UPSC classification is impersonal, necessary and radically
immanent pantheism, with the interpretive caution noted briefly.

### 7. Religious gain and cost

**Gain:** Unity, intelligibility, rejection of arbitrary supernatural intervention, and a form of
intellectual love of God.

**Strongest objection:** The model removes a personal creator, providence, petitionary prayer and
libertarian freedom. It may preserve the word “God” while changing its religious meaning.

**Reply:** Spinoza rejects anthropomorphic religion as inadequate. Blessedness lies in adequate
knowledge and intellectual love, not bargaining with a cosmic ruler.

⚠️ **Residual:** The reply is coherent only if one accepts that ultimacy need not be personally
responsive. It does not recover classical devotion.

  necessity/immanence → loss of personality → verdict.

- **Directive:** Critically discuss.
- **Demand:** Show systemic dependence on substance-metaphysics before evaluation.
- **Approach only:** reconstruct substance–attribute–mode → explain *Deus sive Natura* and
  necessary expression → assess pantheism, freedom, personality and worship.

### Revision notes

1. Spinoza affirms one infinite substance.
2. Substance is in itself and conceived through itself.
3. Attributes express substance’s essence.
4. Humans know Thought and Extension.
5. Modes are dependent modifications, not parts.
6. God/Nature does not create by temporal choice.
7. *Natura naturans* is active infinite Nature.

## Lesson 6 — Śaṅkara: Brahman, Īśvara and the two levels of theism

### Visual: one reality read from two standpoints

```text
                         BRAHMAN
                            |
            +---------------+----------------+
            |                                |
   PARAMARTHIKA STANDPOINT          VYAVAHARIKA STANDPOINT
       ultimate truth                    empirical truth
            |                                |
    NIRGUNA BRAHMAN                  SAGUNA ISVARA
 beyond limiting predicates        creator, ruler, object of devotion
            |                                |
     ATMAN = BRAHMAN                 jiva, world, karma, worship
            \                                /
             \------ one reality, not two ---/
```

### 1. The learner’s puzzle

Advaita appears to say two things:

1. ultimate reality is non-dual Brahman beyond limiting attributes;
2. God creates, governs karma and receives worship.

The system does not posit two ultimate gods. It distinguishes standpoints. The personal Lord is
fully valid within empirical life; non-dual Brahman is the unsublatable truth disclosed by
liberating knowledge.

### 2. Nirguṇa Brahman

✅ **Canonical doctrine:** *Nirguṇa* means “without limiting qualities.” Brahman is non-dual
*sat-cit-ānanda*—being, consciousness and fullness/bliss—not a blank non-entity.

Apophatic methods such as *neti neti* (“not this, not this”) remove finite objectifying predicates.
*Yato vāco nivartante* marks the limit of speech that tries to make Brahman an object.

**Terminological control:** Do not technically call Brahman *anirvacanīya*. In Advaita,
*anirvacanīya*—indeterminable as absolutely real or unreal—properly characterises *māyā* and the
world-appearance. Brahman is *sat*, although beyond finite predication.

### 3. Saguṇa Īśvara

✅ **Canonical doctrine:** Saguṇa Īśvara is Brahman understood in relation to *māyā* as omniscient
creator, moral governor and object of worship at the empirical level.

Īśvara is not sheer illusion. The shared empirical order, scripture, ethical discipline, devotion
and liberation-path all operate at *vyāvahārika* reality. What is later sublated is ultimate
creator–creature duality.

### 4. Māyā, adhyāsa, mithyā and vivarta

| Term | Exact use |
|---|---|
| **Māyā/avidyā** | beginningless principle of appearance/ignorance that makes plurality intelligible |
| **Adhyāsa** | superimposition of self and not-self characteristics |
| **Mithyā** | dependent, experienced and sublatable—not absolutely real, not sheer nothing |
| **Vivarta** | apparent transformation without real change in Brahman |

**Rope–snake example:** A rope is misperceived as a snake. The snake is experienced and can cause
fear, yet is sublated by knowledge of the rope. The example shows dependent appearance.

**Limit:** The world is a shared, ordered field, not a private momentary error. The analogy does not
by itself explain cosmic appearance or the locus of ignorance.

### 5. The Advaitic argument

1. Every changing object is dependent and available to consciousness.
2. Consciousness is the presupposition of every known object and cannot be objectified in the same
   way.
3. Ordinary individuality superimposes body-mind limitations on consciousness.
4. Upaniṣadic instruction removes the superimposition.
5. The self is recognised as Brahman; plurality loses ultimate status.

**Presupposition:** Self-revealing consciousness is irreducible and *śruti* is a valid means for
non-objective ultimate reality.

### 6. Does Advaita leave room for theism?

**Yes, with qualification.**

- Worship of Īśvara purifies and disciplines the mind.
- Grace, devotion and moral governance are meaningful empirically.
- Īśvara is the highest personal form of reality within the world of relation.
- Ultimate knowledge sublates the duality between worshipper and worshipped.

**Strongest objection—Rāmānuja:** A reality without determinations is unknowable, unnameable and
unworshipable. If personal God is lower, devotion is ultimately provisional.

**Advaita reply:** Negation removes limitations, not reality. Provisional does not mean useless or
false; a valid means can be transcended after fulfilling its function.

⚠️ **Residual:** The reply preserves a path for devotion but not eternal I–Thou relation. A devotee
who regards relation as intrinsically final will find the hierarchy religiously costly.

- **Demand:** Explain Brahman first, then answer the explicit theism question.
- **Approach only:** nirguṇa doctrine → *sat-cit-ānanda* and apophasis → saguṇa Īśvara at the
  empirical level → role of devotion → Rāmānuja’s objection → qualified “yes, but not ultimately
  dualistic” verdict.

### Revision notes

1. Advaita distinguishes ultimate and empirical standpoints.
2. Nirguṇa denies limiting qualities, not reality.
3. *Sat-cit-ānanda* indicates Brahman’s nature, not detachable properties.
4. Saguṇa Īśvara is Brahman in relation to *māyā*.
5. Īśvara is creator and worship-object within empirical reality.
6. *Mithyā* is dependent and sublatable, not absolute non-being.
7. *Vivarta* is apparent transformation.
8. *Anirvacanīya* technically qualifies māyā/world-status, not Brahman.
9. Rope–snake clarifies sublation but not the entire cosmic theory.

## Lesson 7 — Rāmānuja: a personal Absolute with real selves and world

Progress: 7/12 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Viśiṣṭādvaita, cit–acit–Īśvara, body–self relation, causation and devotion

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: queried — Chatterjee–Datta, C. D. Sharma and Radhakrishnan Vol. II on Rāmānuja’s qualified non-dualism.
CA search: "Ramanuja Visishtadvaita devotion interreligious dialogue India 2026"
CA found: ⚠️ Search result — no doctrine-specific official development; the topic remains text- and argument-led.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual: one qualified whole

```text
                         BRAHMAN / ISVARA
                     personal, perfect, independent
                                |
                    inner ruler (antaryamin)
                                |
             +------------------+------------------+
             |                                     |
          CIT                                   ACIT
     conscious selves                         matter/nature
             |                                     |
             +---------- BODY OF BRAHMAN ---------+
                      real, dependent, inseparable
                                |
                       not numerically identical
```

### 1. Why Rāmānuja rejects an undifferentiated Absolute

Rāmānuja wants to preserve three scriptural affirmations simultaneously:

1. Brahman is one.
2. Souls and world are real.
3. Devotion is an ultimate relation, not a temporary educational device.

His solution is **qualified non-dualism**—*Viśiṣṭādvaita*. Reality is one Brahman, but Brahman is
internally qualified by real conscious selves (*cit*) and real non-conscious nature (*acit*).

**Example with limit:** A person is one living unity with a body containing many organs. The body
depends on and is governed by the self while remaining real. The analogy explains unity with
difference. It stops before anatomy: the cosmos is not a gross spatial body enclosing God.

### 2. Śarīra–śarīrī: body and indwelling self

✅ **Canonical doctrine:** World and selves constitute Brahman’s body; Brahman is their indwelling
self. Bodyhood here means:

- being supported by the indwelling self;
- being controlled by it;
- existing for its purpose.

This makes God radically immanent—present as inner controller—yet transcendent, because God is not
exhausted by any finite body or soul.

### 3. Apṛthaksiddhi

*Apṛthaksiddhi* means inseparable existence. A quality cannot exist apart from the qualified
substance, yet it is not numerically identical with the whole substance.

✅ **Canonical doctrine:** The *jīva* belongs inseparably to Brahman but never becomes the whole of
Brahman. Difference is real; independence is denied.

**UPSC trap:** Do not translate inseparability as identity. If self and God were simply identical,
devotion, dependence, service and grace would lose their structure.

### 4. God as material and efficient cause

Rāmānuja accepts Brahman as:

- **efficient cause**—intelligent director of manifestation;
- **material cause**—the real dependent *cit–acit* order exists inseparably within the divine whole.

The world is a real manifestation, commonly expressed through *pariṇāma*, rather than Advaita’s
*vivarta*.

**Objection:** If the changing and suffering world is God’s body, does defect enter God?

**Reply:** Change belongs to the dependent modes/body, while the indwelling controller remains
perfect. A self can remain a knower while the body changes.

⚠️ **Residual:** Because the body is genuinely God’s body, not external matter, the analogy cannot
remove every responsibility pressure. The reply distinguishes bearers of defect but does not make
the intimacy disappear.

### 5. God and self

| Dimension | Rāmānuja’s view |
|---|---|
| Reality of self | eternal and real |
| Dependence | complete ontological dependence on God |
| Identity | never the whole of God |
| Relation | body–indwelling-self; mode/qualification |
| Agency | finite but morally significant |
| Liberation | communion and service, retaining individuality |

### 6. Bhakti, prapatti and grace

✅ **Canonical doctrine:** Because God is supremely personal, devotion is metaphysically final.
*Bhakti* is sustained loving contemplation; *prapatti* is surrender or taking refuge. Grace does
not annihilate the self but fulfils its dependent nature in loving service.

This is why Rāmānuja’s metaphysics and religious practice fit tightly together. A real personal God
supports a real I–Thou relation; a real soul can love, surrender and remain in communion.

### 7. Comparison with Advaita

| Question | Advaita | Viśiṣṭādvaita |
|---|---|---|
| Ultimate | nirguṇa non-dual Brahman | personal, qualified Brahman |
| World | dependent appearance, *vivarta* | real body/mode, *pariṇāma* |
| Self | ultimately Brahman | real and inseparably dependent |
| Īśvara | empirically valid | absolutely ultimate |
| Devotion | valid means within empirical order | enduring and intrinsic relation |

They disagree because Advaita treats difference as ignorance-conditioned, while Rāmānuja treats
internal difference as necessary to a concrete unity.

### 1. The nature/proof firewall

The 2021 PYQ asks the **nature** of God in Nyāya, not merely how Nyāya proves God.

✅ **Canonical doctrine:** Mature theistic Nyāya presents Īśvara as:

- one eternal and incorporeal self;
- omniscient and free from ordinary error;
- intelligent efficient or ordering cause;
- not the material cause, because eternal atoms remain material constituents;
- administrator of karmic results;
- authoritative teacher or speaker in the theistic account.

Detailed Udayana proofs belong to “Proofs for God.” Topic 01 may state the reasoning only to clarify
the resulting concept.

### 2. Efficient cause, not material cause

```text
ETERNAL ATOMS + SOULS + KARMA
              |
       unconscious resources
              |
       ISVARA orders combination
       and allocates fruits
              |
          COSMIC ORDER
```

**Example with limit:** A potter intelligently arranges clay. This clarifies efficient versus
material causation. It fails if it makes God bodily, temporally ignorant or dependent on tools.
Nyāya must explain bodiless agency and knowledge.

### 3. Karma and freedom

Īśvara connects *adṛṣṭa*—unseen moral potency—with appropriate consequences. God administers an
order generated by agents’ deeds rather than inventing their moral desert.

**Objection:** If karma already works, God is redundant.
**Nyāya reply:** Unconscious atoms and *adṛṣṭa* cannot intelligently coordinate a morally ordered
cosmos.
**Counter-pressure:** Calling the allocator omniscient raises the question why unequal suffering
requires a personal administrator rather than impersonal law.

### 4. Yoga

Patañjali’s Īśvara is a *puruṣa-viśeṣa*—a special self untouched by affliction, action, fruition and
latent impression, and the unsurpassed teacher. Classical Yoga does not require Īśvara to create
*prakṛti* or direct its evolution.

**Lesson:** A religiously significant Lord need not be a creator.

### 5. Mīmāṃsā and Sāṃkhya

- **Mīmāṃsā:** Eternal authorless Veda, ritual duty and *apūrva* can explain normativity and delayed
  fruit without a divine author.
- **Sāṃkhya:** Many conscious *puruṣas* and unconscious primordial *prakṛti* explain experience and
  evolution without a creator.

Nyāya objects that unconscious principles cannot explain ordered purposiveness. These systems
reply that adding a creator either duplicates existing explanation or creates new problems about
divine motive and embodiment.

### 6. Buddhism and Jainism

Both sustain disciplined religious paths and cosmologies without an omnipotent creator:

- Buddhism uses dependent origination, karma and liberation from craving.
- Jainism affirms eternal *jīvas*, matter, karma and liberated perfected beings, but no creator who
  makes the universe.

⚠️ **Analytical inference:** “Non-theistic” is not one doctrine. It may reject a creator while
retaining liberation, sacred exemplars, karma, ritual and metaphysical ultimacy.

### 7. Strongest objection and reply

**Theistic objection:** Impersonal law cannot explain why there is an intelligible moral order.

**Non-creator reply:** A divine person does not end explanation unless divine knowledge, goodness
and action are themselves coherent. Karma or causal order may be basic without a supernatural
manager.

⚠️ **Residual:** The dispute turns on explanatory standards: whether intelligent order requires a
mind or whether invoking a mind merely relocates the unexplained.

### Visual: plurality can occur at different levels

### 1. Is Hinduism polytheistic?

The question cannot be answered by counting images or divine names. The criterion is whether the
tradition affirms many **independent ultimates**.

✅ **Canonical evidence:**

- Vedic hymns address Agni, Indra, Varuṇa and others with distinct functions.
- In henotheistic or kathenotheistic devotion, the deity presently invoked can receive supreme
  language without denial of others.
- Ṛgveda 1.164.46 speaks of one existent named in many ways.
- Upaniṣadic thought moves toward Brahman as one ultimate.
- *Iṣṭadevatā* permits concentrated devotion to a chosen form.
- Vaiṣṇava, Śaiva and Śākta traditions can be monotheistic in structure while accepting many sacred
  powers and manifestations.

⚠️ **Analytical inference:** Hindu traditions contain polytheistic practice, henotheistic
invocation, sectarian monotheism and metaphysical monism. No single label captures every level.

**Imported-category caution:** “Monotheism/polytheism” arose in a different religious dispute.
It remains useful only if the criterion is stated and indigenous structures are not erased.

### 2. Madhva’s Dvaita

✅ **Canonical doctrine:** Viṣṇu alone is *svatantra*—independent. Souls and matter are
*paratantra*—dependent.

**Pañca-bheda**, five real differences:

1. God–soul;
2. God–matter;
3. soul–soul;
4. soul–matter;
5. matter–matter.

The *bimba–pratibimba* relation describes God as original and soul as dependent reflection. It does
not mean identity. Liberation preserves difference and culminates in graded bliss and service.

**Strong objection:** Eternal difference and *taratamya*—gradation among souls—appear to compromise
equality and universal liberation.

**Reply:** Difference is constitutive rather than defective; fulfilment consists in perfect service
according to the soul’s nature, not becoming God.

⚠️ **Residual:** Accounts of intrinsic hierarchy and eternal damnation are morally and
interpretively contested. State them cautiously.

### 3. Śaiva Siddhānta

The triad is *pati–paśu–pāśa*: Lord, bound soul and bonds. Śiva is efficient cause; *māyā* is
material cause. The five acts are creation, maintenance, dissolution, concealment and grace.
Liberation makes the soul Śiva-like without making it the Lord.

This supplies a complete answer to “relationship between man and God”:

```text
SOUL bound by anava + karma + maya
                |
          Shiva's grace
                |
       knowledge and release
                |
   likeness/communion without identity
```

### 4. Kashmir Śaivism

✅ **Canonical doctrine:** Śiva is self-luminous consciousness (*prakāśa*) inseparable from
self-reflective awareness or power (*vimarśa/Śakti*). The world is a real self-manifestation
(*ābhāsa*) through divine freedom, not an illusory superimposition.

**Sharp contrast:** Advaita and Kashmir Śaivism are both non-dual, but Advaita uses *vivarta* and
sublation, while Kashmir Śaivism treats manifestation as real expression.

### 5. Śākta notion

The Goddess or Śakti is ultimate. Divine power is not a property added to an otherwise complete
God; it is the dynamic divine itself.

Contrast:

- Advaitic *māyā* is dependent and sublatable.
- Sāṃkhya *prakṛti* is unconscious.
- Śākta Śakti is conscious, ultimate and productive.

### 6. Why these positions matter

They prevent “Indian notion of God” from becoming shorthand for only Advaita and Rāmānuja.
They also show multiple possible person-relations: identity, body–self inseparability, eternal
dependence, master–servant relation, recognition and union with divine power.

### UPSC application and owned PYQs

- **Demand:** State a criterion and apply it across textual and lived levels.
- **Approach only:** define polytheism → evidence for many deities → henotheism and “one existent,
  many names” → monism/sectarian theism → imported-category caution → “not adequately captured by
  one label” verdict.

religions in India.”

- **Directive:** Discuss any one.
- **Demand:** Complete one relation rather than sampling many.
- **Approach only:** choose Viśiṣṭādvaita, Dvaita or Śaiva Siddhānta → define God and self → explain
  dependence/agency → bondage and devotion/grace → liberation → objection and verdict.

### Revision notes

1. Count independent ultimates, not images, when testing polytheism.
2. Henotheism gives contextual supremacy without denying others.
3. Kathenotheism gives successive supremacy.
4. Monolatry is exclusive worship without a necessary one-God ontology.
5. Monism concerns one reality, not automatically one personal deity.
6. Hindu traditions combine several levels of plurality and unity.
7. Madhva’s master axis is independent God versus dependent reality.
8. *Pañca-bheda* affirms five eternal differences.
9. *Bimba–pratibimba* means dependence without identity.
10. Śaiva Siddhānta preserves Lord–soul difference and grace.
11. Kashmir Śaivism treats world as real divine manifestation.

### 3. Immanence and transcendence revisited

**Classical theism:** God is transcendent as non-dependent creator and immanent as sustaining cause.

**Rāmānuja:** The body–self relation gives especially strong immanence; Brahman is *antaryāmin*, the
inner controller, yet exceeds each finite qualification.

**Spinoza:** Immanence becomes necessary expression in one substance; transcendent creatorhood is
rejected.

**Advaita:** Brahman is not an external being beyond the world. It is the reality upon which all
appearance depends, while itself exceeding finite predication.

⚠️ **Analytical inference:** “Both immanent and transcendent” does not have one univocal meaning
across systems. The precise relation must be named.

### 4. Must an ultimate cause be physically manifested?

The 2024 question tests a category mistake. A cause can be known through physical effects without
being a finite physical object.

Distinguish:

- **manifestation:** disclosure through an effect, symbol or form;
- **incarnation:** a specific religious claim that divinity assumes embodied existence;
- **embodiment:** possession of a body;
- **material causation:** being that from which a thing is constituted;
- **efficient causation:** directing or producing change;
- **metaphysical dependence:** the effect’s being rests on the cause.

**Argument against necessity of physical form:**

1. Ultimate causation concerns explanatory dependence.
2. Physical objects are conditioned, divisible and located.
3. If ultimacy means non-dependence, finite physical form cannot be necessary to it.
4. Effects may disclose a non-physical cause.

**Objection from artisan analogy:** Every observed maker acts bodily; therefore a cosmic cause must
have a body.

**Reply:** The inference improperly transfers a condition of intra-worldly manufacture to the cause
of the whole physical order. It also confuses epistemic access with ontology.

### 5. Does Rāmānuja require physical manifestation?

No. “World as God’s body” is a metaphysical dependence relation, not the claim that God is one
spatial organism. Incarnation may matter devotionally, but it is not logically required for
ultimate causation.

### 6. Strongest comparative objection

**Objection:** Every model either separates God too much to explain immanence or identifies God too
closely to preserve transcendence.

**Replies:**

- classical theism uses primary/secondary causation;
- panentheism uses inclusion-with-excess;
- Rāmānuja uses body–self unity;
- Advaita changes the question through levels of reality.

⚠️ **Residual:** Each repair depends on contested metaphysics. There is no neutral relation-model
that supplies all gains without cost.

  classical, Advaita, Rāmānuja and pantheist cases → answer artisan analogy → conclude that

1. God–world relations include distinction, identity, inclusion, appearance and embodiment.
2. Classical theism uses continuous dependence.
3. Spinoza uses substance–mode expression.
4. Advaita uses non-dual reality and dependent appearance.
5. Rāmānuja uses real body–self inseparability.

## Lesson 11 — Personal and impersonal ultimacy in the context of devotion

Progress: 11/12 | Stage: Advanced | Subtopic: I–Thou relation, bhakti, provisional devotion and impersonal religious practice

### Visual: what changes when ultimacy is personal?

```text
PERSONAL ULTIMATE                       IMPERSONAL / TRANSPERSONAL ULTIMATE
        |                                             |
   I–THOU address                              knowledge / contemplation
   prayer and response                         participation / realisation
   command and obedience                       transformation of awareness
   grace and surrender                         release from false separation
        |                                             |
   risk: anthropomorphism                       risk: devotional thinness
```

### 1. Why devotion changes the debate

The personal/impersonal distinction is not merely descriptive. It determines what worship can mean.
Devotion ordinarily includes:

- an intentional “I”;
- a real “Thou” capable of knowing and receiving devotion;
- love, trust, surrender or obedience;
- a relation that matters to liberation or fulfilment.

An impersonal Absolute strains this grammar because there may be no divine will that hears, answers
or reciprocates.

### 2. Classical personal theism

Prayer, worship and moral obedience are intelligible because God knows persons and wills their good.
The strongest danger is anthropomorphism: projecting finite psychology, gender and emotion onto
ultimate reality.

**Reply:** Personal predicates may be analogical. Divine knowledge and love are real but not
limited copies of human capacities.

### 3. Rāmānuja and bhakti

Rāmānuja offers the clearest metaphysical grounding for devotion:

1. God is supremely personal.
2. The soul is real and eternally dependent.
3. Difference is not abolished in liberation.
4. Bhakti and *prapatti* therefore culminate in communion and service.

Here devotion is not merely a means to a non-relational end. It expresses what the soul eternally
is in relation to God.

### 4. Madhva and devotion

Madhva intensifies difference. Viṣṇu alone is independent; the soul’s fulfilment is loving service
without identity. This protects the I–Thou structure most strongly but raises questions about
hierarchy and unequal capacities.

### 5. Advaita’s two-level answer

Advaita retains saguṇa Īśvara as a legitimate object of worship within empirical reality.
Devotion:

- disciplines desire;
- purifies the mind;
- supports concentration and surrender;
- prepares for knowledge.

But final knowledge recognises non-duality. The deepest truth is not eternal relation between two
independent centres.

**Strong objection:** If devotion is ultimately sublated, the devotee’s relation is downgraded.

**Advaita reply:** A means can be valid without remaining after fulfilment. A ladder is not false
because it is left after reaching the roof.

**Limit of analogy:** The ladder is external and discarded; devotion can transform the person’s
whole orientation. The analogy explains functional transcendence but may understate religious
value.

### 6. Spinoza and intellectual love

Spinoza replaces petitionary relation with the intellectual love of God: joy arising from adequate
understanding of one’s place within necessary Nature.

This is religiously serious but not interpersonal. God does not answer the lover as another person.

### 7. Tillich’s being-itself

Tillich warns against treating God as a highest being among beings. “Being-itself” or the ground of
being seeks to protect ultimacy from objectification.

**Pressure:** If God is not a being and not a person in ordinary grammar, what receives devotion?

**Reply:** Religious symbols participate in and disclose ultimate concern without literally
describing a finite person.

⚠️ **Boundary:** Full symbol theory belongs to Religious Language. Here it only shows how a
non-objectified ultimate can remain religiously significant.

### 8. Comparative verdict

| Model | Devotional strength | Metaphysical strength | Main cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Classical theism | reciprocal prayer and grace | creator-person distinction | anthropomorphism and attribute tensions |
| Rāmānuja | eternal bhakti and surrender | unity with real difference | divine-body suffering pressure |
| Madhva | maximal I–Thou difference | clear dependence | hierarchy and permanent distance |
| Advaita | valid devotional preparation | radical non-duality | relation is not ultimately final |
| Spinoza | contemplative intellectual love | total immanence and unity | no responsive Thou |
| Tillich | protects ultimacy from objectification | ground rather than highest object | personal address becomes symbolic |

### 1. How to compare without merely listing

A comparison is an explanation of disagreement. Use one constant:

- personality;
- world status;
- causal relation;
- self relation;
- freedom;
- devotion;
- attribute coherence.

Then identify the prior commitment producing each answer.

**Example:** Advaita and Rāmānuja disagree about devotion because they disagree earlier about the
status of difference. For Advaita, ultimate difference is ignorance-conditioned; for Rāmānuja,
difference is a real internal qualification of unity. Their devotional conclusions follow from
their metaphysics.

### 2. Directive decoder

| Directive | What the examiner requires | Failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| State | accurate doctrine in organised form | unsupported labels |
| Explain | relation and mechanism | mere definition |
| Elucidate | clarify a distinction with examples | historical narration |
| Discuss | balanced account plus position | one-sided assertion |
| Critically examine/evaluate | reconstruction, objection, reply and judgement | criticism-only answer |
| Give reasons and justifications | explicit thesis with ranked reasons | conclusion without criterion |
| Write an essay | coherent breadth around a central architecture | disconnected points |

**10 marks**


**15 marks**


**20 marks**


### 4. High-value judgement formulas

⚠️ **Analytical inference:**

- “The attributes remain coherent only after each is specified; the specification saves logical

- “Neither ‘polytheistic’ nor ‘monotheistic’ captures Hindu traditions without remainder, because
  plurality of forms, contextual supremacy and unity of ultimate reality operate at different
  levels.”
- “Advaita leaves room for theism as empirically valid religious practice, but not as an ultimately
  irreducible creator–creature duality.”
- “Physical manifestation may disclose an ultimate cause, but it is not a necessary condition of
  metaphysical causation.”
- “Personalism gives devotion a reciprocal object but risks anthropomorphism; impersonalism
  protects ultimacy but must explain how worship remains more than self-transformation.”

### 5. Error clinic

| Error | Repair |
|---|---|
| attributes listed without interaction | pair every attribute with one coherence pressure |
| “God is everywhere, so pantheism” | distinguish presence from identity |
| “nirguṇa means no reality” | say absence of limiting predicates |
| “world as body means physical God” | define support, control and purpose |
| Nyāya answer becomes proof-list | restore nature, cause-type and karmic relation |
| Hinduism gets a one-word label | state the criterion and layered verdict |
| PYQ answer narrates all schools | obey “any one,” “in context of devotion,” or named thinker |
| conclusion repeats introduction | state what survives and at what cost |

### 6. Whole-topic philosophical assessment

No single notion secures every desired feature:

- Classical theism gives personality, freedom and worship, but its attributes create coherence
  pressures.
- Spinoza gives unity and radical immanence, but loses reciprocal personality.
- Advaita gives non-dual ultimacy and a path beyond objectification, but makes theism non-final.
- Rāmānuja integrates metaphysics and devotion, but the world-as-body doctrine intensifies the
  relation between divine perfection and worldly defect.
- Nyāya gives intelligent order and moral administration, but faces redundancy and bodiless-agency
  objections.
- Madhva protects devotional difference, but hierarchy and permanent distance carry moral costs.

The best philosophical answer is therefore not “all are same” or “one is obviously true.” It
identifies the criterion—coherence, explanatory power, religious adequacy or devotional
intelligibility—and judges the models proportionately.

3. Directive words determine answer structure.
4. Ten-mark answers need economy, not superficiality.
5. Fifteen-mark answers need a developed objection and reply.
6. Twenty-mark answers need architecture and graded comparison.
7. A verdict must reuse the operative term in the question.
8. Logical coherence and religious adequacy are distinct criteria.
9. Every model gains something and pays a cost.
10. Indian/Western parity must be argumentative, not decorative.
11. PYQs require exact wording and demand control.
12. A strong conclusion states what survives after criticism.

# ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS MODEL PRACTICE

## Original 10-marker

**Question:** Distinguish between divine transcendence and divine remoteness. Can a transcendent God
also be immanent? Answer in about 150 words.

### Model answer

Divine transcendence means that God is ontologically independent of and not exhausted by the world;
remoteness means absence of continuing relation or action. The two are therefore not identical.
Deism tends toward remoteness because the creator establishes natural order but does not ordinarily
intervene. Classical theism, by contrast, combines transcendence with immanence: God is distinct
from creation yet continuously sustains its being and acts through genuine secondary causes.
Rāmānuja intensifies this combination through the *śarīra–śarīrī* model: world and selves form
Brahman’s real body, while Brahman as inner controller exceeds every finite mode. Pantheism should
not be inferred merely from immanence, because presence and sustaining activity do not entail
identity. The main difficulty is explaining how an immutable or timeless God can be genuinely
responsive. Classical theism replies that temporal effects may arise from one non-temporal divine
act, though the personal adequacy of this reply remains contested. Thus transcendence and
immanence are compatible when the former means non-dependence rather than spatial distance.

## Original 15-marker

**Question:** “A notion of God becomes religiously adequate only when it supports devotion.”
Critically examine. Answer in about 200 words.

### Model answer

Religious adequacy concerns whether a metaphysical ultimate can function as an object of worship,
trust and transformation. Personal theism supports devotion directly: an intelligent and loving
God can know the devotee, receive prayer and bestow grace. Rāmānuja gives this relation a strong
metaphysical base. The real but dependent *jīva* remains distinct from personal Brahman, so *bhakti*
and *prapatti* culminate in enduring communion. Madhva similarly preserves eternal I–Thou
difference.

The claim is nevertheless too strong if “devotion” is restricted to petitionary relation. Advaita
validates worship of saguṇa Īśvara as a transformative discipline while holding nirguṇa Brahman to
be ultimate. Spinoza replaces petition with intellectual love arising from adequate knowledge of
God/Nature. Tillich interprets religious symbols as directing ultimate concern toward being-itself.
These models retain religious orientation without a finite divine person.

Personalism faces anthropomorphism and attribute-coherence problems; impersonalism faces the
opposite danger of reducing worship to self-transformation without reciprocity. Religious adequacy
therefore requires an account of ultimate orientation and transformation, but not necessarily one
univocal form of personal devotion. A model is stronger when its practice follows coherently from
its metaphysics rather than being attached externally.

## Original 20-marker

**Question:** Compare the notions of God in classical theism, Spinoza, Advaita and Viśiṣṭādvaita
with reference to divine attributes and the relation to world and persons. Answer in about 300 words.

### Model answer

A notion of God should be compared through three constants: nature, world-relation and
person-relation. Classical theism affirms a necessary, personal, omnipotent, omniscient and
perfectly good creator. The world is real and distinct yet continuously dependent; persons are
created moral agents capable of prayer and grace. Its strength is religious personality, while
evil, foreknowledge and timeless response test the coherence of its attributes.

Spinoza identifies God with the one infinite substance, *Deus sive Natura*. Thought and Extension
are attributes; finite minds and bodies are modes. The world follows necessarily rather than from
free creation, and persons are modal expressions. The system secures radical immanence and unity
but abandons providential personality and libertarian choice.

Advaita makes nirguṇa Brahman—non-dual *sat-cit-ānanda* beyond limiting predicates—ultimate.
Saguṇa Īśvara is Brahman related through *māyā* as creator and object of devotion at the empirical
level. World plurality is *mithyā* and *vivarta*; the true self is Brahman. This protects
metaphysical absoluteness but makes theism and I–Thou devotion non-final.

Rāmānuja rejects that hierarchy. Personal Brahman is a qualified unity whose real body comprises
*cit* and *acit*. Through *apṛthaksiddhi*, selves are distinct yet inseparable; Brahman is inner
controller and real material-efficient cause. Bhakti and grace are therefore final. The pressure is
whether defect in God’s body affects divine perfection.

No model preserves all gains. Classical theism and Rāmānuja maximise personality; Spinoza and
Advaita maximise unity. Their comparative adequacy depends on whether ultimacy, coherence or
reciprocal devotion is given priority.

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

## A. Core conceptual grammar

1. A notion of God specifies nature, attributes, roles, world-relation and person-relation.
2. Personal means knowing, willing, loving and addressable; it does not entail body or gender.
3. Impersonal means beyond finite personal agency; it does not mean unconscious or unreal.
4. Transcendence is non-reducibility and independence, not spatial distance.
5. Immanence is sustaining, indwelling or expressive presence, not necessarily identity.
6. Cataphatic discourse affirms; apophatic discourse removes finite limits; analogy mediates.
7. Creator, sustainer, providence, moral governor, ground and Absolute are distinct profiles.

## B. Western models

1. Classical theism: necessary personal creator and sustainer, distinct yet immanent.
2. Deism: creator and lawgiver with minimal later intervention.
3. Pantheism: no ultimate God–world separation; Spinoza gives the rigorous substance form.
4. Panentheism: world in God, God exceeding world.
5. Process models gain responsiveness by revising immutability and coercive omnipotence.
6. Creation is total dependence, not manufacture from independent material.
7. Primary divine causation can coexist with real secondary causes.

## C. Attribute package

1. Omnipotence ranges over logically possible states of affairs.
2. Omniscience is knowledge of every truth and faces future, tensed and indexical pressures.
3. Perfect goodness must avoid arbitrary-command emptiness.
4. Timeless eternity differs from everlasting duration.
5. Necessity concerns inability not to exist; aseity concerns non-derivation.
6. Immutability and impassibility protect perfection but strain responsiveness.
7. Simplicity denies composition and faces modal-collapse and property-person objections.
8. Foreknowledge replies: Boethian eternity, Ockhamist soft facts, Molinist middle knowledge and
   open future.

## D. Spinoza

1. One infinite substance: *Deus sive Natura*.
2. Attribute expresses essence; mode is a dependent modification.
3. Humans know Thought and Extension.
4. *Natura naturans* is active infinite Nature; *natura naturata* the modal order.
5. The world follows necessarily, not by divine choice.
6. Freedom is adequate self-determination within necessity.
7. Unity and immanence are gained; personal providence is lost.

## E. Advaita

1. Nirguṇa Brahman is non-dual *sat-cit-ānanda* beyond limiting predicates.
2. Saguṇa Īśvara is Brahman relationally understood through *māyā*.
3. *Mithyā* means dependent and sublatable, not absolutely non-existent.
4. *Vivarta* is apparent transformation without change in Brahman.
5. *Anirvacanīya* technically applies to māyā/world appearance.
6. Īśvara, devotion and moral order are empirically valid.
7. Liberating knowledge sublates ultimate creator–creature duality.
8. Advaita therefore permits qualified, non-final theism.

## F. Rāmānuja

1. Brahman is personal qualified unity.
2. *Cit* and *acit* are real dependent qualifications.
3. World and selves are Brahman’s body; Brahman is inner controller.
4. *Apṛthaksiddhi* means inseparability without identity.
5. God is efficient and qualified material cause.
6. Real manifestation differs from Advaitic appearance.
7. Self remains real in liberation.
8. Bhakti, *prapatti* and grace culminate in service and communion.

## G. Nyāya and other Indian profiles

1. Nyāya Īśvara is one eternal omniscient incorporeal self.
2. God orders eternal atoms as efficient cause.
3. God administers karma but does not author each choice.
4. Yoga’s Īśvara is a special untouched self and teacher.
5. Mīmāṃsā uses authorless Veda and *apūrva* without a required creator.
6. Sāṃkhya uses *puruṣa–prakṛti* dualism without a creator.
7. Buddhism and Jainism show religion need not include an omnipotent creator.

## H. Hindu plurality and additional theisms

1. Polytheism means several genuine deities; henotheism means contextual supremacy.
2. Kathenotheism is successive one-at-a-time supremacy.
3. Monolatry is exclusive worship without necessary denial of other gods.
4. Monism and monotheism answer different numerical questions.
5. Hindu traditions combine plural practice, selected devotion, sectarian supremacy and metaphysical
   unity.
6. Madhva: Viṣṇu independent; souls/world dependent; five differences real.
7. Śaiva Siddhānta: Lord, bound soul and bonds; grace yields likeness without identity.
8. Kashmir Śaivism: real manifestation of Śiva-consciousness and Śakti.
9. Śākta: conscious divine power is ultimate.

## I. God–world–person and devotion

1. Identity, inclusion, appearance, embodiment, efficient causation and dependence must not be
   merged.
2. Physical manifestation is not required for metaphysical causation.
3. Rāmānuja’s divine body is not gross anatomy.
4. Personalism supports reciprocal prayer, grace and command.
5. Impersonal models reinterpret religion as knowledge, participation or transformation.
6. Rāmānuja and Madhva make devotion final.
7. Advaita makes devotion valid but ultimately transcended.
8. Spinoza retains intellectual love without a responsive Thou.
9. A final judgement should distinguish logical coherence from religious adequacy.
