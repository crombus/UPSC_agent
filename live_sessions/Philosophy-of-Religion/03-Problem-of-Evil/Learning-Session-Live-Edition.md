## Lesson 1 — Identify the problem before answering it

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Progress: 1 / 12 | Stage: Foundation | Subtopic: versions, burdens and evil-types

### Visual first — one word, several philosophical problems

```text
EVIL
 ├─ logical: can God and evil coexist?
 ├─ evidential: does observed suffering make God improbable?
 ├─ existential: how can a person trust or live after suffering?
 └─ moral protest: does explaining suffering risk excusing it?

EVIL-TYPE
 ├─ moral: culpable action
 ├─ natural: disease, disaster, predation
 ├─ structural: institutions and patterned injustice
 └─ metaphysical: finitude or lack, not automatically culpable harm
```

The classical target combines divine power, knowledge, goodness and creatorship. Evil does not generate the same accusation against every ultimate reality. The first answer-writing duty is therefore to name both the **version of the problem** and the **God-profile** under examination.

The familiar triad—God is omnipotent, God is perfectly good, evil exists—is not formally inconsistent by itself. A contradiction appears only after adding bridge claims such as: perfect goodness opposes every evil unless there is a morally sufficient reason, and unlimited power can prevent every logically preventable evil. Omniscience blocks the excuse that the evil escaped divine notice.

Three burdens must remain separate:

| Burden | What a successful reply must establish |
|---|---|
| Logical | A possible compatibility story defeats alleged contradiction |
| Evidential | The amount and distribution of suffering do not strongly lower theism’s probability |
| Existential-moral | The response does not erase lament, agency or duties of rescue |

A **defence** offers a possible reason and is enough against strict inconsistency. A **theodicy** claims to identify God’s actual reason or actual relation to evil. A pastoral response is different again: even a sound philosophical argument does not authorise explaining another person’s trauma to them.

### UPSC traps

- Do not call every deprivation “moral evil”; culpability matters.
- Do not infer from “privation” that experienced pain is unreal.
- Do not use a logical defence as if it explained the actual scale of suffering.
- Do not let existential protest substitute for a logical argument, or vice versa.

### Revision notes

1. State version and God-concept in the opening two lines.
2. Moral evil comes from culpable agency; natural evil need not.
3. Logical inconsistency needs bridge premises.
4. Omniscience is not in Mackie’s standard triad but strengthens responsibility pressure.
5. Defence = possible compatibility; theodicy = purported actual reason.
6. Explanation, justification and care are distinct tasks.
7. A revised God-concept may change the accusation without removing suffering.
8. Version-matching is the master examination skill.

### Local practice

Write a four-sentence introduction distinguishing logical, evidential and existential evil, then state why “God is good, God is powerful, evil exists” is not yet a contradiction.

**MCQ 1**

Which statement best distinguishes a defence from a theodicy?

A. A defence establishes possible compatibility; a theodicy proposes God’s actual morally sufficient reason.
B. A defence concerns natural evil, whereas a theodicy concerns moral evil.
C. A defence proves God exists, whereas a theodicy assumes atheism.
D. A defence is pastoral care, whereas a theodicy is formal logic.

**MCQ 1 — Answer and explanation**
**Correct answer: A**

- **A — Correct:** It preserves the canonical difference in argumentative burden: possibility is enough for a defence, while a theodicy makes the stronger explanatory claim.
- **B — Incorrect:** Both forms can address either evil-type; the distinction concerns what is claimed, not which suffering is discussed.
- **C — Incorrect:** Neither category is itself a proof of God, and theodicy is ordinarily attempted from within theism.
- **D — Incorrect:** Pastoral care is a third activity; philosophical defences need not be exclusively formal and theodicies need not be pastoral.

**MCQ 2**

Why are bridging premises required in the logical problem?

A. Because omniscience is identical with omnipotence.
B. Because the three propositions are not explicitly contradictory until claims about goodness and prevention are added.
C. Because natural evil is always produced by free creatures.
D. Because evidence can never affect the probability of theism.

**MCQ 2 — Answer and explanation**
**Correct answer: B**

- **A — Incorrect:** Knowledge and power are different attributes; omniscience removes ignorance as an excuse but does not collapse into omnipotence.
- **B — Correct:** The bridges connect perfect goodness with opposition to evil and power with preventability, creating the alleged inconsistency.
- **C — Incorrect:** Disease, predation and disaster show why natural evil cannot simply be assigned to creaturely choice.
- **D — Incorrect:** Evidential arguments exist precisely because observed suffering may alter comparative probability without yielding deduction.

## Lesson 2 — Mackie and the logical problem

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
📚 Book context: canonical §§9.1, 11–12 and coverage rows R03-01–02, R03-05
🔍 PYQ control: 2020 Q6(b), “What is the logical problem of evil? Discuss.”
📰 Live-source status: not relevant; no current-affairs evidence is needed
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Progress: 2 / 12 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: inconsistency, adequate solutions and residual pressure

### Visual first — where contradiction is alleged

```text
P1 God is omnipotent.
P2 God is perfectly good.
P3 Evil exists.
        +
B1 Perfect goodness prevents every evil it can,
   unless a morally sufficient reason exists.
B2 Omnipotence can prevent every logically possible evil.
        ↓
ALLEGED INCONSISTENCY
        ↓
Reply options: deny/qualify a bridge, revise an attribute,
or show a possible morally sufficient reason.
```

J. L. Mackie’s challenge is strongest when the theist wants to retain all three propositions and the unqualified bridges. A weak script merely repeats the triad. A strong script reconstructs why the propositions are supposed to clash.

An **adequate logical response** need not prove which reason God actually has. It must show that the conjunction is possibly coherent. A free-will defence, for example, adds the possibility that significant freedom is a great good that cannot be causally guaranteed while remaining freedom. If that possibility is coherent, strict contradiction fails.

Yet logical survival is not complete vindication:

```text
logical compatibility
        ≠
probable divine reason
        ≠
adequate explanation of this quantity and distribution
```

This transition explains why contemporary debate often moves from Mackie to evidential evil. A defence may show that some evil is compatible with God while leaving unexplained why suffering is so intense, apparently pointless, unevenly distributed and extended to non-human animals.

### Answer spine for 2020 Q6(b)

1. Define logical evil as an inconsistency claim.
2. Write the triad and both bridges.
3. Explain why the bare triad is not contradictory.
4. Present one compatibility defence, preferably significant freedom.
5. Test its scope: moral evil is addressed more directly than natural evil.
6. Conclude that the deductive challenge can fail while evidential pressure survives.

### Revision notes

1. Mackie’s target is a set of commitments, not merely emotional discomfort.
2. The bridge premises carry much of the argument.
3. Restricting omnipotence to the logically possible is not an arbitrary loss of power.
4. A possible reason defeats deduction.
5. Possibility does not prove actuality.
6. Logical and evidential evil require different verdicts.
7. Natural evil is the immediate residual after a moral-freedom reply.
8. End with a graded conclusion, not “problem solved.”

## Lesson 3 — Omnipotence, freedom and shared responsibility

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
📚 Book context: canonical §§9.1A, 9.2–9.2A and coverage rows R03-06, R03-16–17
🔍 PYQ control: 2021 Q5(e) and 2022 Q6(c)
📰 Live-source status: not relevant; the questions concern conceptual responsibility
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Progress: 3 / 12 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: power paradox and creator-agent distinction

### Visual first — four responsibility questions

```text
ABSOLUTE CREATOR
      │ creates/sustains capacity
      ├─ causal dependence: who enables action?
      ├─ moral authorship: who forms the wrongful intention?
      ├─ permission: who knowingly allows the risk?
      └─ system design: who chooses this world-order?
                         │
                    FINITE AGENT
                    intends and performs
```

Mackie’s power paradox asks whether unlimited power can create a power it cannot later control. If God can create such an independent power, future control appears limited; if God cannot, present creative power appears limited. The Aquinas-style reply says a contradictory specification is not a possible task. “Create an undefeated limit to unrestricted power while remaining unrestricted” describes no coherent action.

Plantinga then connects power to **significant freedom**. If a choice is causally determined by God, it is not libertarianly free in the relevant sense. The defence needs only the possibility of **transworld depravity**: perhaps every feasible world containing significantly free creatures includes some misuse. It does not assert that transworld depravity is actual, nor that natural evil follows from it.

The 2022 creator-responsibility question demands a finer analysis. “God creates the agent” does not entail “God authors the agent’s evil intention.” Conversely, creaturely authorship does not remove every question about foreknown permission and world selection.

| View | Human responsibility | Divine residual |
|---|---|---|
| Strong determinism | Difficult to isolate as ultimate authorship | God’s determination and world choice become central |
| Compatibilism | Action can be responsible if it issues from the agent’s motives | Who formed or selected those motives remains relevant |
| Libertarianism | Agent authors an undetermined culpable choice | God permits the risk and sustains the system |
| Theistic karma | Agent generates karmic desert | Divine administration still faces justice and permission questions |

### Revision notes

1. The power paradox is broader than the stone puzzle.
2. Logical impossibilities are not objects of power.
3. Freedom cannot be both causally guaranteed and libertarianly undetermined.
4. Transworld depravity is a possibility claim.
5. Foreknowledge does not by itself cause an act.
6. Permission and causation are distinct but both morally assessable.
7. Agent guilt need not imply complete creator innocence.
8. The free-will defence chiefly addresses moral evil.

## Lesson 4 — Augustine: privation, sin and punishment

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
📚 Book context: canonical §§9.3–9.3A and coverage rows R03-12–15
🔍 PYQ control: 2023 Q8(a) and verified 2026 Q6(a)
📰 Live-source status: not relevant; the authority is the controlled Markdown and PYQ wording
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Progress: 4 / 12 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: metaphysical status, moral origin and natural-evil strain

### Visual first — three claims that must not be collapsed

```text
WHAT IS EVIL?        WHY MORAL EVIL?          WHY NATURAL SUFFERING?
privation of a       mutable rational will    Fall/inherited disorder/
due good in a        defects from higher      punishment narrative
good created nature  to lesser goods          (contested extension)
```

Augustine rejects evil as a positive substance rival to God. Created natures are good insofar as they exist; corruption is the loss or disorder of a good that ought to be present. Blindness is not another substance added to an eye, but a real lack in a subject. **Privation therefore does not mean illusion.**

For moral evil, the will of a finite, mutable creature turns from immutable good toward a lesser good in a disordered manner. The defect belongs to the willing, not to a substance God created. This protects divine creative goodness and preserves finite culpability.

The printed phrase “all evil is either sin or punishment for sin” adds a second explanatory stage. Moral evil is the evil **of fault**; suffering and death are treated as evil **of punishment**, linked to the Fall and inherited disorder. This extension is far more vulnerable.

| Test | Augustinian strength | Residual objection |
|---|---|---|
| Moral wrongdoing | Locates fault in voluntary disorder | Why did an originally good but mutable will first defect? |
| Natural evil | Offers a unified fallen-order narrative | No demonstrated link from each disaster or disease to antecedent fault |
| Descendants | Inherited condition can replace crude personal guilt | Why should unequal harms follow an ancestor’s act? |
| Animals and pre-human pain | Cosmic disorder may be invoked | Historical and moral premises become increasingly strained |

Schleiermacher and Hick press the first-fall problem conditionally. If original perfection excludes defect, the fall is unintelligible. If creatures were created fallible, divine design and foreknown permission return. Augustine can reply that finite goodness is mutable without being evil and that foreknowledge does not cause choice. That reply strengthens creaturely responsibility but does not explain the scale of innocent and natural suffering.

### 2026 split-verdict method

```text
privation (status) → free defective will (moral cause)
→ Fall/punishment (natural-evil extension)
→ evaluate moral and natural evil separately
→ qualified success / contested extension
```

### Revision notes

1. Being is good; evil is parasitic on good.
2. Privation identifies status, not permission.
3. A mutable good creature need not be wicked at creation.
4. Moral fault is disordered willing.
5. Sin and punishment are different explanatory categories.
6. Natural evil requires additional Fall and penalty premises.
7. Inherited damage is easier to defend than inherited personal guilt.
8. Animal and pre-human suffering remain severe tests.
9. The 2026 answer must separate moral from natural evil.
10. Conclude with a split verdict.

## Lesson 5 — Irenaeus and Hick: soul-making

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
📚 Book context: canonical §§2(c), 9.4 and coverage rows R03-18–19
🔍 PYQ control: alternative required for evaluating Augustine in 2023 and 2026
📰 Live-source status: not relevant; current events do not control the doctrine
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Progress: 5 / 12 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: development, epistemic distance and stable order

### Visual first — developmental rather than punitive theodicy

```text
created in the image
        ↓ immature freedom
epistemic distance from overwhelming divine presence
        ↓
stable law-governed world with real danger
        ↓
freely formed courage, compassion, trust and responsibility
        ↓
growth toward likeness
```

The Irenaean-Hick approach begins from created immaturity rather than a perfect state ruined by a historical fall. Irenaeus supplies the image-to-likeness developmental pattern; John Hick develops the modern theodicy through **epistemic distance**, freedom and a law-governed environment. The phrase “vale of soul-making” belongs to John Keats; it should not be attributed to Irenaeus or treated as Hick’s coinage.

Ready-made virtue is not achieved virtue. Courage requires danger, compassion requires need, and responsible trust requires that God not be overwhelmingly obvious. A world that constantly suspended consequences whenever harm threatened would weaken stable agency and the intelligibility of action.

The position is stronger than “suffering makes people better.” It requires:

| Component | Philosophical work |
|---|---|
| Immature creation | Makes development rather than restoration central |
| Epistemic distance | Protects uncoerced moral and religious response |
| Natural regularity | Makes action predictable and responsibility possible |
| Real hardship | Supplies conditions in which virtues can be formed |
| Eschatological fulfilment | Prevents finite failure and suffering from being final |

Its objections are equally structured. Some suffering destroys rather than develops. The victim may supply another person’s opportunity for growth, making suffering instrumental. Animals do not plainly undergo moral formation. Universal fulfilment is a major metaphysical commitment, not a demonstrated fact. Stable order may explain why risk exists without explaining why suffering is so extreme or unequally distributed.

### Revision notes

1. Irenaean thought is developmental, not retributive.
2. Image and likeness must be distinguished.
3. Keats supplied the phrase; Hick supplied the modern theodicy.
4. Epistemic distance protects free response.
5. Natural law enables meaningful agency.
6. Virtue requires risk, not necessarily every actual horror.
7. Proportionality is the main objection.
8. Animal suffering resists moral-growth language.
9. Eschatology carries heavy argumentative weight.
10. Compare with Augustine by origin, purpose and moral cost.

## Lesson 6 — Rowe and skeptical theism

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
📚 Book context: canonical §§9.1, 9.8–9.9 and coverage rows R03-07, R03-25
🔍 PYQ control: evidential depth needed beyond the 2020 logical stem
📰 Live-source status: not relevant; the issue is epistemic access to reasons
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Progress: 6 / 12 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: apparently gratuitous evil and the noseeum inference

### Visual first — the evidential inference and its challenge

```text
intense suffering with no visible justifying good
                    ↓
        “probably no such good exists”
                    ↓
 perfect God probably would not permit it
                    ↓
      classical theism becomes less probable

SKEPTICAL-THEIST CHECK:
Would a divine justifying reason probably be visible to us if it existed?
```

William Rowe’s familiar case of apparently pointless animal suffering is evidential rather than deductive. It does not claim that God and suffering are impossible together. It argues that some intense suffering appears to serve no good that would justify its permission, and this appearance counts against classical theism.

Skeptical theism attacks the step from “I see no reason” to “there probably is no reason.” Stephen Wykstra’s **CORNEA** requires reasonable epistemic access: absence is evidence only when, if the relevant item existed, an observer in one’s position would probably detect it. Michael Bergmann’s representativeness point similarly denies that our known goods, evils and connections are a representative sample of all possible ones.

This is a **defence against an inference**, not a positive theodicy. It does not disclose God’s reason. Its strength is humility about limited cognition; its cost is moral corrosion.

| Pressure | Skeptical-theist reply | Counter-pressure |
|---|---|---|
| No visible reason | Human access may be radically limited | Unseen reasons can become unfalsifiable |
| Duty to prevent harm | Act on known local reasons | Unknown greater goods may still destabilise confidence |
| Divine goodness | Other grounds may support it | The same scepticism may affect revelation and promises |
| Positive theodicy | A reason may also be proposed | Claiming both knowledge and deep ignorance can sit awkwardly |

A disciplined answer never writes merely “God’s ways are mysterious.” It states the access principle, limits its domain and admits the ordinary-morality objection.

### Revision notes

1. Rowe argues probability, not contradiction.
2. Apparently gratuitous means no justifying good is visible.
3. CORNEA tests whether absence would be detectable.
4. The noseeum inference is “I see none, therefore none exists.”
5. Skeptical theism is not a theodicy.
6. Cognitive humility is its strength.
7. Moral and religious scepticism are its costs.
8. Domain restriction is necessary but can look ad hoc.
9. Logical compatibility does not answer Rowe automatically.
10. State the residual evidential burden.

Marilyn McCord Adams uses **horrendous evil** technically: participation can give a person prima facie reason to doubt that their life could be a great good to them on the whole. A world-total balance is insufficient. Divine goodness to persons requires **defeat within the individual life**, not merely unrelated compensation.

**Balancing off** means later or external goods outweigh the evil in an aggregate. **Defeat** means the evil is integrated into a valuable whole from the participant’s standpoint. Adams appeals to religion-specific resources such as divine identification and beatific intimacy. Critics question verifiability and whether later union can avoid making horror instrumental.

| Reply | Limited force | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Free will | Little direct application | Animals did not make the culpable choice |
| Stable natural law | Explains regular risk | Does not explain intensity and distribution |
| Soul-making | Can form human compassion | Risks using animals as instruments |
| Fallen order | Broadens the narrative | Adds contested historical and cosmic premises |
| Karma | Offers trans-life distribution | Unverifiable and open to desert abuse |

Existential protest insists that lament, resistance and solidarity may be morally prior to explanation. This is an ethical criticism of theodicy, not by itself a deductive disproof of God. A complete answer can therefore say both: argument remains necessary, and no argument cancels duties of rescue or the sufferer’s right to protest.

### Visual first — four ways to connect evil with a larger good

```text
GREATER GOOD: some goods require risk
STABLE ORDER: reliable laws permit agency and harm
BEST WORLD: God chooses the best compossible totality
ESCHATOLOGY: suffering is not the final word
                ↓
common test:
Does the reply explain type, amount, distribution,
and goodness to the particular sufferer?
```

A greater-good argument proposes that freedom, courage, compassion or reliable agency cannot exist without vulnerability and loss. Stable natural law strengthens this: predictable causation is necessary for action, learning and responsibility. But these points explain why **some risk** may exist more readily than why actual harms reach their observed extremes.

Leibniz’s best-world strategy concerns a total **compossible** order. God need not select every locally preferred good if those goods cannot coexist within the best whole. Two objections follow: perhaps there is no unique best world, because any good world could be improved; and world-total optimisation may neglect whether the order is good to each sufferer.

Eschatological fulfilment says no finite suffering is final. Restoration, union or transformed life can prevent a biography from being wasted. Yet later good may compensate without justifying permission. Adams’ person-level demand asks whether the evil is defeated within the life, not simply overbalanced after it.

| Question | Strong reply | Remaining gap |
|---|---|---|
| Why regular danger? | Stable agency requires regular consequences | Miracles need not be constant to reduce extremes |
| Why allow freedom? | Authored goodness may exceed programming | Not every consequence must be left unprevented |
| Why this world? | Total goods may be jointly optimal | Unique optimum is uncertain |
| Can suffering be redeemed? | Final fulfilment may transform biography | Compensation is not automatically justification |

The mature verdict is conditional: these arguments explain structural possibility and purpose better than individual distribution. They are strongest when used modestly and weakest when treated as proof that every harm was necessary.

## Lesson 9 — Process theodicy

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
📚 Book context: canonical §§7.4, 9.5 and coverage rows R03-20–21

📰 Live-source status: not relevant; doctrine is controlled by canonical Markdown
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Progress: 9 / 12 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: distributed power, persuasion and divine solidarity

### Visual first — process power

```text
CREATIVITY / BECOMING
        ↓
actual occasions possess some self-determination
        ↓
power is intrinsically distributed
        ↓
God supplies an initial aim by persuasion, not coercion
        ↓
finite occasions can deviate → evil
        ↓
God receives the world in the consequent nature
and is the “fellow-sufferer who understands”
```

Process theodicy answers evil by revising classical omnipotence, not by claiming that an unlimited God voluntarily refrains from coercion. For Alfred North Whitehead, actual reality consists in becoming occasions with some self-creative power. God’s **primordial nature** orders possibilities and offers each occasion an **initial aim**; the **consequent nature** receives and preserves what the world becomes.

Charles Hartshorne argues that monopoly of power is incoherent: if anything else is genuinely actual, it possesses some power. David Ray Griffin sharpens the evil-response: divine persuasion is a **metaphysical necessity**, not a chosen self-limitation. This separates process theodicy from Plantinga’s defence.

| Plantinga | Process thought |
|---|---|
| God has coercive power but values significant freedom | No actuality can wholly determine another actuality |
| Non-intervention is tied to the good of freedom | Persuasion is the only possible divine mode |
| Primarily a logical defence | A claimed account of God’s actual relation to evil |
| Classical omnipotence retained | Classical omnipotence rejected or redefined |

God is not indifferent: God lures toward richer value, receives every achievement and loss, and suffers with creation. The ethical attraction is solidarity. The metaphysical cost is severe: can such a God guarantee the defeat of evil, remain worthy of worship, or count as the sovereign God of classical religion?

### 2025 answer order

1. Becoming and self-creative occasions.
2. Dipolar God: primordial and consequent natures.
3. Initial aim and persuasive lure.
4. Distributed power as metaphysically necessary.
5. Evil as finite deviation, not divine choice.
6. Divine participation in suffering.
7. Criticisms: worship-worthiness, final victory and contested metaphysics.
8. Verdict: innocence purchased by revision of sovereignty.

### Revision notes

1. Creativity is fundamental in Whitehead’s scheme.
2. Actuality includes self-determining power.
3. Primordial nature orders possibilities.
4. Consequent nature receives the world.
5. Initial aim is persuasive, not coercive.
6. Hartshorne attacks monopoly power.
7. Griffin makes persuasion necessary, not voluntary.
8. Process is a theodicy, not merely a defence.
9. Divine solidarity is its moral strength.
10. Inability to guarantee victory is its central cost.

present duty remains:
compassion + protection + relief + non-injury

## Lesson 11 — Evil, profane and the foundation of religion

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
📚 Book context: canonical §§7.3, 9.6 and coverage rows R03-30–31
🔍 PYQ control: 2019 Q6(b), evil and profane as a firm foundation to religion
📰 Live-source status: not relevant; the stem is conceptual and functional
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Progress: 11 / 12 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: predicament, boundary, institution and soteriology

### Visual first — how negative categories can structure religion

```text
PREDICAMENT
sin / suffering / impurity / dukkha / avidyā / bondage
        ↓
BOUNDARY
sacred–profane / pure–impure / saved–lost
        ↓
PRACTICE AND INSTITUTION
ritual / prohibition / initiation / community discipline
        ↓
SOTERIOLOGY
ordered path of purification, release or transformation
```

The 2019 question is functional, not a request for another theodicy. It asks how evil and the profane help constitute religion.

For Émile Durkheim, religion classifies reality into **sacred** and **profane** and organises beliefs and practices around sacred things, forming a moral community. The profane means the ordinary or non-consecrated; it does **not** mean wicked.

Rudolf Otto analyses the holy through the **numinous**, *mysterium tremendum et fascinans*. Mircea Eliade describes **hierophany**, through which the sacred manifests and differentiates sacred space and recoverable sacred time from homogeneous profane existence. Their emphases differ: social classification, experiential category and phenomenological manifestation.

Evil supplies a predicament requiring diagnosis and overcoming. The profane supplies a boundary against which consecrated practices gain identity. Together they generate ritual, prohibition, institutional roles and a path of release.

Indian examples prevent the answer from becoming narrowly theistic:

| Tradition | Founding predicament | Religious response |
|---|---|---|
| Buddhism | *dukkha*, craving and ignorance | path ending suffering |
| Jainism | *āsrava* and *bandha* | *saṃvara*, *nirjarā*, non-injury |
| Advaita | *avidyā* | liberating knowledge |
| Ritual traditions | impurity and disorder | purification and disciplined practice |

The limitation is important. Religion can also arise from wonder, gratitude, devotion and experienced holiness. Sacred/profane is an ideal type, not an exceptionless law. Evil and the profane can found **religion structurally**, without proving theism.

4. Eliade stresses hierophany, space and time.
5. Predicament creates a need for transformation.
6. Boundary generates ritual and prohibition.
7. Institution stabilises practices.
8. Soteriology supplies the exit.
9. Indian predicaments often concern ignorance or bondage.
10. Structural foundation does not entail creator belief.

metaphysical proposal → epistemic limit → ethical independence

and never entails

# BOUNDED EXPERT REFERENCE — USE SELECTIVELY

## E1. Modal precision for the free-will defence

Three terms prevent overclaiming:

- **Possible world:** a complete possible way reality could be.
- **Feasible world for God:** a possible world God can actualise given the counterfactual free choices of creatures.
- **Transworld depravity:** the possible condition in which every feasible world containing significantly free agents includes some moral misuse.

This vocabulary blocks the naïve objection, “Why not create free beings who always choose well?” It does not prove the condition actual and does not explain natural evil.

**Expert stop rule:** define the distinction, apply it to logical compatibility, state the natural-evil remainder, and leave modal theory.

# ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS MODEL PRACTICE

## 10 marks — Distinguish a defence from a theodicy in the problem of evil

### Model answer

A defence and a theodicy respond to different burdens. A defence proposes a logically possible situation in which God’s perfection and evil coexist. It therefore defeats a claim of strict contradiction without identifying God’s actual reason. Plantinga’s appeal to significant freedom and possible transworld depravity is paradigmatic: it need only be possible that no feasible free world excludes all moral evil.

A theodicy makes a stronger claim about God’s actual reason or situation. Hick attributes suffering to soul-making in an epistemically distant, law-governed world. Process thought claims that God actually persuades but cannot coerce finite occasions.

The distinction prevents two errors. A defence cannot be criticised merely for failing to disclose providence, but it cannot claim to justify the observed amount of suffering. A theodicy can explain more, yet assumes a heavier evidential and moral burden. Thus logical compatibility is weaker than explanation, and explanation is weaker than person-level justification.

## 15 marks — Does free will absolve an absolute creator of responsibility for moral evil?

### Model answer

Free will gives a strong account of finite moral authorship but does not automatically absolve the creator. Four questions must be separated. The agent is causally dependent on God for existence and capacity; the agent may nevertheless author the wrongful intention. God may not cause that intention, yet knowingly permits its risk and selects a world-order in which it can produce grave harm.

Under strong determinism, exclusive creaturely responsibility is difficult because the complete history is divinely determined. Compatibilism locates responsibility in action issuing from the agent’s motives, but the origin and selection of those motives remain relevant. Libertarianism most clearly preserves creaturely culpability: causally guaranteeing a free choice would cancel the alternative possibility. Plantinga adds that possible transworld depravity blocks the assumption that God must have a feasible world of significant freedom without misuse.

Still, a possible defence does not explain why particular harms are left unprevented, and it says little about natural evil. The proper verdict is shared but differentiated responsibility: the agent can be morally guilty, while divine permission and system design remain open to evaluation.

## 20 marks — Can any major response answer both natural evil and goodness to particular sufferers?

### Model answer

The logical problem asks whether perfect God and evil can coexist; natural and person-level evil demand more than compatibility. Free will explains culpable action but does not directly reach disease, disaster or pre-human animal pain. Augustine’s privation protects divine creative goodness and his defective-will account handles moral fault, but the punitive extension to natural evil depends on a contested Fall and inherited disorder.

Hick offers the strongest classical-theist expansion. Epistemic distance, natural regularity and real danger make responsible growth possible. This explains why a risky environment may exist, yet not why its harms are so extreme. Animal suffering and lives damaged rather than formed resist the developmental rationale.

Greater-good and Leibnizian strategies evaluate the world-total. Adams shows the limitation: divine goodness must be goodness to persons. Unrelated goods cannot merely balance a life threatened by horror; defeat must occur within the participant’s life. Eschatological fulfilment may supply such a resource, but its truth and justificatory relation remain disputed.

Process thought handles natural processes without charging an omnipotent permitter: power is distributed and God persuades rather than coerces. Its cost is inability to guarantee victory. Indian non-creator systems also remove classical culpability and karma addresses distribution, but verification and victim-blaming remain.

No single response decisively answers type, amount, distribution and person-level defeat. A layered reply can explain structural risk, preserve local agency and hope for final defeat, but its adequacy depends on contested metaphysical commitments. The honest verdict is comparative and graded.
