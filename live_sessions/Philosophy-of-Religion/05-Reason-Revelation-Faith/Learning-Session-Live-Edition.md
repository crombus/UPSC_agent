## Lesson 1 — Three Epistemic Offices: Different Jobs, Shared Risk
Progress: 1/15 | Stage: Foundation | Subtopic: Roles, risks and relations

CA found: None with direct doctrinal relevance in the last six months; this lesson is conceptual.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
### Visual first — one inquiry, three offices

```text
REASON ── infer, test, interpret, criticise, regulate
   │                                      ↑
   ▼                                      │
REVELATION ── alleged divine self-disclosure ──► FAITH
              proposed content                 receives, trusts, commits

annexation risks: rationalism | circular authority | credulity/fanaticism
```

*Cooperation works only while no office pretends to do all three jobs.*

### Plain-language intuition

If a trusted person reports something you could not discover unaided, the report supplies content, reasoning tests credibility, and trust enables action. Religious epistemology radicalises this pattern. **Reason** is publicly assessable inference and criticism. **Revelation** is an alleged disclosure by the divine. **Faith** is assent, trust and commitment. The analogy stops short because a finite witness is easier to identify than an ultimate revealer.

### Doctrine and argument

Reason may be deductive, inductive, abductive, critical and hermeneutic. Revelation may be general or special, propositional or event-like. Faith includes cognition, will, affection and practice but cannot be reduced to one faculty. Disclosure allegedly provides what unaided inquiry lacks; faith makes it revelation *for* a recipient; reason tests whether reception is coherent, credible and ethically defensible.

**Strongest objection:** the division shelters religion by reserving inaccessible content for revelation. **Reply:** it is legitimate only if reason retains a real veto over contradiction and defeaters. **Residual:** interpretation partly shapes what is received, so reason can become partly constitutive.

### Presuppositions, example and comparison

The model presupposes that truth can be approached through more than one epistemic relation and that trust can be responsible without becoming proof. **Example:** accepting a surgeon's advice separates reported content, rational credibility checks and personal entrusting. **Limit:** divine disclosure lacks the surgeon's ordinary public credentials. Compared with strict rationalism, the three-office view admits new content; compared with fideism, it keeps criticism active.

### UPSC application

For 2019 Q5(a), choose faith as instrumental to reception, preserve reason as regulator, and distinguish occurrence from appropriation.

### Revision notes

1. Reason assesses; revelation discloses; faith appropriates.
2. They are offices, not synonyms.
3. Reason includes hermeneutics and criticism.
4. Revelation is a claim needing authentication.
5. Faith includes assent, trust, commitment and practice.
6. Reception does not create objective truth.
7. Complementarity needs a genuine rational veto.
8. Annexation produces characteristic distortions.
9. Testimony clarifies structure but not divine identification.
10. Interpretation makes boundaries porous.

### Local practice

**MCQ 1: A**
Which formula best distinguishes the three offices?
A. Reason assesses, revelation supplies alleged disclosure, and faith appropriates it.
B. Reason creates revelation and faith verifies it.
C. Revelation is intense feeling and reason is obedience.
D. Faith is merely leftover probability.

- **A — Correct:** Each term has a distinct epistemic job while remaining related.
- **B — Incorrect:** This reverses the offices: reasoning evaluates a disclosure-claim, while faith receives it rather than certifying it.
- **C — Incorrect:** It reduces revelation to affect and assigns obedience to the faculty whose role is critical assessment.
- **D — Incorrect:** Faith is not a remainder after probabilities are calculated; it adds trust, assent and practical commitment.

**MCQ 2: B**
When does complementarity become empty?
A. When revelation adds content unavailable to unaided inquiry.
B. When reason has no effective power to identify contradiction or defeaters.
C. When faith includes practical commitment.
D. When reason uses abduction as well as deduction.

- **A — Incorrect:** New content is precisely what makes revelation a distinct partner rather than an empty duplicate of reason.
- **B — Correct:** A real critical veto prevents division of labour from becoming insulation.
- **C — Incorrect:** Practical commitment is a genuine dimension of faith and does not disable rational scrutiny.
- **D — Incorrect:** Using abductive reasoning broadens rational assessment; it does not deprive reason of critical force.

**Remediation cue:** Re-state the central distinction in one sentence, then explain why each rejected option crosses that boundary.

## Lesson 2 — Reason's Five Roles and Experiential Limit
Progress: 2/15 | Stage: Foundation | Subtopic: Natural theology, rationalism and the limit of reason
━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Queried — canonical §§1, 9.1, 9.6; exact learner PDFs; Oxford religious epistemology.
CA search: "reason alone religion experience philosophy 2026"
CA found: None with direct doctrinal relevance; 2026 Q5(e) is an examination anchor.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
### Visual first — five roles and one boundary

```text
REASON: CONSTRUCT natural theology → TEST coherence → INTERPRET text
        → DEFEND/CRITICISE → REGULATE belief and identify defeaters
                                      ↓
LIMIT: inference can describe an ultimate without supplying encounter,
worship, dependence or transformation.
```

*Reason can map a house without making it a home.*

### Plain-language intuition

A complete theory of friendship is not friendship. Likewise, rational religion may establish consistency or probability without yielding prayer, awe or transformation. This is the force of the 2026 claim that reason alone lacks an experiential account.

### Doctrine and argument

**Natural theology** uses premises available without special disclosure. Aquinas's preambles and Nyāya inference to Īśvara are examples. Reason also tests contradiction, interprets scripture, defends claims and regulates belief when proof is inconclusive. Rationalism becomes excessive when only independently provable content is admitted.

Deism and Kantian religion preserve rational morality and purified religion. Schleiermacher's feeling of absolute dependence, Otto's numinous and James's noetic quality show what inferential description leaves out. Experience without reason risks projection; reason without experience describes religion from outside.

**Strongest objection:** experience is concept-laden, so reason has not been escaped. **Reply:** participation need not be interpretation-free to be irreducible to inference. **Residual:** traditions still disagree about the object experienced.

### Presuppositions, example and comparison

Natural theology presupposes an intelligible reality and reason capable of metaphysical inference. **Example:** a cosmological argument may infer dependence, while worship adds a lived relation not contained in the inference. **Limit:** failure of one proof does not show every rational role fails. Compared with Kant, Aquinas grants speculative reason greater reach; compared with Schleiermacher, he assigns less foundational weight to feeling.

### UPSC application

For 2024 Q5(d), write five roles and one limit. For 2026 Q5(e), contrast rational gains with encounter and appropriation, then agree with qualification.

### Revision notes

1. Natural theology uses public premises.
2. Philosophy of religion is wider.
3. Construction is one of five roles.
4. Coherence-testing survives failed proofs.
5. Interpretation is rational work.
6. Regulation identifies defeaters.
7. Rationalism mistakes a method for the whole.
8. Experience adds participation, not automatic truth.
9. Schleiermacher, Otto and James supply bounded contrasts.
10. The 2026 route needs qualified agreement.

### Local practice

**MCQ 3: C**
Which is reason's regulative rather than constructive role?
A. Inferring a first cause.
B. Receiving an event as providential.
C. Testing an interpretation for contradiction.
D. Committing life to an ultimate.

- **A — Incorrect:** A first-cause inference constructs a theological conclusion instead of testing an already proposed claim.
- **B — Incorrect:** Providential reception belongs to interpretive faith, not to reason’s negative task of regulation.
- **C — Correct:** Coherence-testing constrains content without generating it.
- **D — Incorrect:** Whole-life commitment is an act of faith and therefore occupies the practical rather than regulatory office.

**MCQ 4: D**
What is the best response to the claim that reason alone lacks experience?
A. Experience is self-authenticating and needs no reason.
B. Every experience is irrational.
C. Natural theology already contains worship.
D. Reason is necessary, but inference does not exhaust encounter and participation.

- **A — Incorrect:** Self-authentication ignores projection, rival experiences and the need for defeater-sensitive assessment.
- **B — Incorrect:** Concept-laden experience can still be rationally interpreted; irreducibility to inference is not irrationality.
- **C — Incorrect:** An argument about God can support belief, but worship adds encounter and commitment not contained in its premises.
- **D — Correct:** The qualified answer preserves discipline and irreducible participation.

**MCQ 5: A**
Why is natural theology narrower than philosophy of religion?
A. Philosophy of religion also studies language, experience and warrant.
B. Natural theology relies on special revelation.
C. Natural theology belongs only to medieval Christianity.
D. Natural theology rejects probability.

- **A — Correct:** Natural theology argues toward theological conclusions within a wider discipline.
- **B — Incorrect:** Dependence on special disclosure defines revealed theology, whereas natural theology begins from publicly available premises.
- **C — Incorrect:** The method appears beyond medieval Christianity, including modern arguments and Indian inferences to Īśvara.
- **D — Incorrect:** Natural theology often uses inductive or abductive probability; it is not confined to deductive certainty.

**Remediation cue:** Re-state the central distinction in one sentence, then explain why each rejected option crosses that boundary.

## Lesson 3 — Regulative Reason and Three Grades of Faith
Progress: 3/15 | Stage: Foundation | Subtopic: Regulative/constitutive; rational, supra-rational and irrational
━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Queried — canonical §§0A, 1, 9.5 and 16; exact learner PDFs.
CA search: "regulative reason faith philosophy 2026"
CA found: None with direct doctrinal relevance in the last six months.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
### Visual first — referee, author and three zones

```text
CONSTITUTIVE reason = writes content
REGULATIVE reason   = checks proposed content
RATIONAL: supported | SUPRA-RATIONAL: beyond proof, not contradiction
IRRATIONAL: violates coherence or resists decisive defeat
```

*The middle category prevents “not proved” from meaning “unreasonable”.*

### Plain-language intuition

A referee can disallow a goal without scoring one. Reason may reject contradiction and expose unreliable testimony without generating the mystery faith receives.

### Doctrine and argument

If reason had no regulatory force, any revelation could demand assent. If it had to constitute all content, revelation would add nothing. A viable harmony combines disclosed content with rational discipline. Critical rationalism marks the middle: commitment remains open to criticism.

**Strongest objection:** a universal veto is sovereignty in disguise. **Reply:** negative constraint differs from positive authorship. **Residual:** interpretation can reshape content, making regulation partly constitutive in practice.

### Presuppositions, example and comparison

The distinction presupposes that logical constraints can apply across traditions without deciding every first-order doctrine. **Example:** reason can reject “God both exists and does not exist in the same respect” while remaining unable to derive Trinity. **Limit:** ordinary religious paradox may require interpretation before contradiction is alleged. Aquinas embodies regulated supra-rationality; insulated fideism abandons it.

### UPSC application

The 2021 Q7(a) hinges on regulative versus constitutive. The 2023 Q6(c) rewards the supra-rational middle.

### Revision notes

1. Constitutive reason supplies content.
2. Regulative reason constrains it.
3. A veto is not authorship.
4. Rational faith has grounds.
5. Supra-rational faith exceeds demonstration.
6. Irrational faith violates coherence or evidence.
7. Critical rationalism preserves corrigibility.
8. Defeasibility is responsible faith's price.
9. Interpretation complicates the distinction.
10. Mystery never makes contradiction true.

### Local practice

C. A contradiction becomes true when sacred.

- **A — Incorrect:** Immunity to all counter-evidence marks insulated belief, not a mystery that responsibly exceeds demonstration.
- **B — Correct:** Exceeding proof differs from defeating reason.
- **C — Incorrect:** Sacred status cannot repeal non-contradiction; contradiction places a claim in the irrational category.
- **D — Incorrect:** Persistence after decisive defeat is epistemic obstinacy rather than supra-rational commitment.

**MCQ 7: C**

A. It derives every mystery from self-evident premises.
B. It becomes silent once faith begins.
C. It disciplines formulation without supplying every religious content.

- **B — Incorrect:** Silencing criticism abandons regulation exactly where faith remains vulnerable to contradiction and defeaters.
- **C — Correct:** Regulation constrains while constitution authors content.
- **D — Incorrect:** Vocabulary revision is only one interpretive activity and is far narrower than reason’s critical discipline.

**MCQ 8: D**

B. Religious claims lack content.

- **B — Incorrect:** The problem concerns how content is interpreted, not whether religious claims possess any content.
- **C — Incorrect:** Constitutive reason is defined by generating or inferring content; inability to infer is not the difficulty at issue.

- **A — Correct:** Corrigible commitment occupies the defensible middle.
- **B — Incorrect:** Immunity from revision reproduces the closure of fideism instead of mediating it.
- **C — Incorrect:** Refusing every commitment avoids fideism only by collapsing into the opposite extreme of paralysis.
- **D — Incorrect:** A voluntary act can adopt a belief but cannot manufacture the truth that would make it warranted.

## Lesson 4 — Revelation on Two Axes
Progress: 4/15 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: General/special; propositional/non-propositional; neighbouring concepts
━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Queried — canonical §§0A, 2, 9.2–9.3; Hick PDF pp. 67–75; exact learner PDFs.
CA search: "general special propositional non-propositional revelation 2026"
CA found: None with direct doctrinal relevance; classifications are timeless.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
### Visual first — two-axis grid

| Scope / nature | Propositional truths | Non-propositional presence/event |
|---|---|---|
| General | nature or conscience conveys truths | world experienced as gift/address |
| Special | prophet, scripture or person conveys doctrine | particular history encountered as divine act |

```text
SCOPE (general/special) × NATURE (proposition/event) = four possible combinations
```

*Scope asks where and to whom; nature asks what revelation is.*

### Plain-language intuition

“Public or particular?” differs from “message or meeting?” General/special concerns scope and medium; propositional/non-propositional concerns truth-content versus self-presence.

### Doctrine and argument

**Revelation** is disclosure. **Inspiration** concerns divine-human formation of a record. **Infallibility** concerns freedom from error. One can affirm disclosure while treating its record as historically conditioned.

**Strongest objection:** identifying an event as divine already asserts a proposition. **Reply:** encounter theory claims priority, not propositionlessness. **Residual:** later propositions are often the only public access to the encounter.

### Presuppositions, example and comparison

Classification presupposes that medium, scope and content can be analytically separated even when joined in practice. **Example:** nature can be read propositionally as evidence of order or non-propositionally as encountered gift. **Limit:** the same tradition may combine both readings. Propositional theories prioritise communicated truth; encounter theories prioritise personal presence.

### UPSC application

Use the grid before natural/revealed theology. Never merge the axes.

### Revision notes

1. General/special concerns scope.
2. Propositional/non-propositional concerns nature.
3. The axes cross.
4. General disclosure may use nature or conscience.
5. Special disclosure has a particular bearer.
6. Propositional revelation communicates truths.
7. Non-propositional revelation prioritises self-disclosure.
8. Revelation, inspiration and infallibility differ.
9. Encounter still needs interpretation.
10. The grid prevents category errors.

### Local practice

- **A — Incorrect:** It wrongly turns two classificatory axes into a truth-versus-error ranking and couples categories that can cross-combine.
- **B — Correct:** The two axes ask different classificatory questions.
- **C — Incorrect:** Natural theology is a route of access, while inspiration and infallibility concern mediation and error-status, not scope.
- **D — Incorrect:** Publicity does not measure certainty, and event versus doctrine concerns the form of disclosure rather than morality.

## Lesson 5 — Propositional Revelation and Aquinas's Two Routes
Progress: 5/15 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Natural/revealed theology; preambles and mysteries
━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Queried — canonical §§2, 9.1–9.2; Hick PDF pp. 67–70; exact learner PDFs.
CA search: "natural theology revealed theology philosophy 2026"
CA found: None with direct doctrinal relevance in the last six months.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
### Visual first — one truth, two routes

```text
PUBLIC REASON → NATURAL THEOLOGY → preambles: God exists, some attributes
DIVINE TESTIMONY → REVEALED THEOLOGY → mysteries: Trinity, Incarnation
                 both claim one truth; no genuine contradiction
```

*The distinction concerns access, not two unrelated realities.*

### Plain-language intuition

Some truths might be discoverable; others, if known, would have to be told. Aquinas calls the former preambles and the latter mysteries.

### Doctrine and argument

The model presupposes a personal communicating God, reliable mediation and harmony of truth. Revelation may repeat rationally accessible truths because people lack time, training or certainty. **Motives of credibility** make testimony reasonable without demonstrating mysteries.

**Strongest objection:** divine testimony warrants doctrine while doctrine identifies the witness. **Reply:** defenders appeal to cumulative evidence. **Residual:** credibility does not establish infallibility. Mīmāṃsā contrasts through an *apauruṣeya*, authorless Veda.

### Presuppositions, example and comparison

Aquinas presupposes divine truthfulness, an intelligible creation and reliable though limited human reason. **Example:** reason may argue that God exists, while Trinity remains accepted on testimony. **Limit:** the example illustrates access, not the success of any particular proof. Unlike fideism, Aquinas gives reasons a preparatory role; unlike rationalism, he preserves mysteries.

### UPSC application

For 2025 Q6(c), define propositional revelation, distinguish routes by access, give examples, separate the three neighbouring concepts and state circularity.

### Revision notes

1. Propositional revelation delivers truth-content.
2. Natural theology avoids special disclosure.
3. Revealed theology depends on testimony.
4. Preambles are rationally accessible.
5. Mysteries exceed but do not contradict reason.
6. Revelation may repeat accessible truths.
7. Credibility grounds are non-demonstrative.
8. Cumulative warrant is defeasible.
9. Mīmāṃsā authority is authorless.
10. Access controls the distinction.

- **D — Incorrect:** Support for testimony does not change a mystery into a conclusion reached from public premises.

## Lesson 6 — Self-Revelation, Experiencing-As and Reception
Progress: 6/15 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Non-propositional revelation, Hick and faith
━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Queried — canonical §§2, 3, 9.3; Hick assigned chapter; exact learner PDFs.
CA search: "self revelation experiencing as Hick 2026"
CA found: None with direct doctrinal relevance in the last six months.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
### Visual first — event to doctrine

```text
AMBIGUOUS EVENT
 ├─ secular causal description
 └─ experienced-as divine action → communal interpretation → doctrine and life
```

*An event does not wear its religious interpretation on its face.*

### Plain-language intuition

Two observers can receive the same rescue as chance or providence. Hick's **experiencing-as** shows perception is concept-laden. In non-propositional revelation, divine action or presence is primary; propositions are later interpretations.

### Doctrine and argument

Events are religiously ambiguous; faith receives them as divine; communities conceptualise the encounter; doctrinal diversity reflects differing schemes. Faith is indispensable to revelation *for a person*, though the event may occur without recognition.

**Strongest objection:** interpretation makes revelation projection. **Reply:** coherence, testimony, moral transformation and resistance by reality constrain projection. **Residual:** these tests are themselves tradition-shaped.

Propositional defenders add that objective truth can remain revealed when rejected. Distinguish occurrence from efficacious reception.

### Presuppositions, example and comparison

The account presupposes that experience is concept-laden and that personal disclosure cannot be reduced to information. **Example:** an historical deliverance can be narrated causally and received providentially. **Limit:** differing descriptions do not show the providential reading true. Compared with propositional revelation, Hick gains relational depth but loses determinate public content.

### UPSC application

For 2019 Q5(a), faith is primary to recognition while reason tests. For 2022 Q5(d), distinguish occurrence and reception before judging indispensability.

### Revision notes

1. Non-propositional revelation prioritises self-disclosure.
2. Doctrine interprets encounter.
3. Events are religiously ambiguous.
4. Experiencing-as is concept-laden perception.
5. Faith receives an event as revelation.

### Doctrine and argument

Faith is a centred, ecstatic act of intellect, will, emotion and unconscious. *Fides qua* and *fides quae* name act and content. A finite bearer elevated to ultimacy is idolatrous.

Only **existential doubt**—whether my concrete concern is truly ultimate—is intrinsic. Methodological doubt tests propositions; sceptical doubt questions certainty as a stance. Courage bears structural doubt; repression breeds fanaticism.

Technical reason calculates means. Ontological reason structures meaning, freedom and creativity. Reason is faith's precondition; faith is reason in ecstasy, fulfilled rather than destroyed. Tillich rejects reductions to low-evidence knowledge, will or feeling. Symbol theory enters only to explain why finite content is fallible.

**Strongest objections:** definition too broad; symbolic fallibility risks relativism; distinct dimensions may insulate religion. **Replies:** unconditional centring distinguishes preference; transparency diagnoses idolatry; religion may not dictate science. **Residual:** Tillich supplies an idolatry test, not a decisive selector of true revelation. Hick also notes ambiguity between concern about the Ultimate and whatever concerns us ultimately.

### Presuppositions, example and comparison

Tillich presupposes that every person has an ultimate concern, that reason can transcend its finite use and that ultimacy cannot be captured literally. **Example:** nationalism can organise sacrifice and promise fulfilment like religion. **Limit:** structural likeness does not make nation and God equally true. Compared with Aquinas, Tillich internalises doubt; compared with Kierkegaard, ecstasy fulfils rather than opposes reason.

### UPSC application

For 2026 Q5(c): definition → idolatry → three doubts → two reasons → objections and residual. Keep symbol theory bounded.

### Visual first — Hume's two-stage challenge

```text
PART I: uniform experience for laws > fallible experience of testimony
PART II: witnesses | wonder | cultural origin | contrary miracles
REPLIES: definition | credulity/testimony | Bayesian update | circularity
```

*Hume raises an evidential burden; he does not directly prove impossibility.*

### Plain-language intuition

An extraordinary claim needs unusually strong evidence because mistaken reports are common.

### Doctrine and argument

Hume defines miracle as violation of natural law. Part I compares bodies of experience. Part II adds witness quality, passion for surprise, “ignorant and barbarous” origins and incompatible miracle traditions.

Swinburne recasts miracle as a non-repeatable counter-instance and gives perception/testimony prima-facie credibility. Earman separates low prior probability from posterior probability after independent evidence. The circularity charge says uniform experience cannot be established by dismissing every contrary report in advance.

**Strongest objection to Hume:** a low prior probability does not settle the posterior probability after strong, genuinely independent testimony; otherwise sufficiently unusual evidence is excluded in advance. **Humean reply:** witness independence and reliability are rarely secure, reports of marvels have recurrent natural explanations, and an anomaly does not identify God. **Residual:** even a credible anomaly underdetermines which revelation, if any, it authenticates.

Indian contrast: Yoga-sūtra 3.37 calls powers obstacles; the *Kevaddha Sutta* prefers instruction; Mīmāṃsā needs no miracle-signing revealer.

### Presuppositions, example and comparison

Hume presupposes stable regularities and an empirically learned measure of human reliability. **Example:** ten genuinely independent reliable witnesses can alter probability more than ten copied reports. **Limit:** independence and reliability must be established, not counted. Swinburne challenges definition and prior weighting; Earman formalises updating; Hume's Part II remains the cautionary baseline.

### UPSC application

Use miracle only as one authentication test. Never claim Hume proved impossibility.

### Revision notes

1. Hume's definition is contestable.

3. His maxim demands exceptional evidence.
4. Part II supplies four cautions.
5. Contrary miracles pressure exclusivity.
6. Swinburne uses non-repeatable counter-instance.
7. Credulity/testimony are prima-facie principles.
8. Earman separates prior and posterior probability.
9. Circularity challenges automatic exclusion.
10. Anomaly does not identify cause.
11. India often declines miracle authentication.
12. Hume's caution survives.

### Plain-language intuition

### Doctrine and argument

Chatterjee–Datta presents Nyāya *śabda* as understanding a trustworthy person's statement; expectancy, fitness, proximity and intended meaning also matter. **Strongest objection:** modern categories distort Indian schools. **Reply:** compare functions, state disanalogy and avoid identity claims. **Residual:** all cross-tradition translation remains defeasible.

### Example and limit

**Example:** both Plantinga's properly basic belief and Mīmāṃsā's *svataḥ-prāmāṇya* resist the demand for a prior proof before initial entitlement. **Limit:** Plantinga explains warrant through properly functioning faculties within a theistic account, whereas Mīmāṃsā explains cognition's prima-facie validity and Vedic authority without turning the Veda into a creator God's testimony.

B. It may be supra-rational or defeasibly grounded; irrationality requires contradiction or defeat-resistance.
C. Faith is outside reason’s jurisdiction.
D. Proof and trust are identical.

- **A — Incorrect:** Many undemonstrated claims are hypotheses, memories or testimony-based beliefs, so lack of proof does not define faith.
- **B — Correct:** Lack of demonstration is not identical with lack of reasons.
- **C — Incorrect:** Responsible faith remains subject to coherence and defeaters even when its content exceeds demonstration.
- **D — Incorrect:** Demonstrative inference and personal entrusting perform different epistemic and practical functions.

**MCQ 55: C**
What is the deepest residual problem?
A. Faith cannot be distinguished from belief.
B. Reason has no roles.
C. Incompatible communities can claim internally rational and experientially grounded faith.
D. Science disproves every religious claim.

- **A — Incorrect:** Faith and belief can be distinguished by entrusting and commitment, so that local conceptual problem has already been answered.
- **B — Incorrect:** Reason retains constructive, interpretive and regulative roles throughout the inquiry.
- **C — Correct:** The diversity defeater survives the principal theories of warrant.
- **D — Incorrect:** Science challenges overlapping empirical claims but cannot issue a universal disproof of every religious proposition.

# ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS MODEL PRACTICE
These six questions are original practice, not PYQs.
## Original question — 10 marks

**Question:** “Reason can authenticate revelation only by ceasing to be its servant.” Comment.

### Model answer

Reason’s critical independence is necessary but not absolute sovereignty. Authentication classifies a disclosure-claim, checks consistency, assesses testimony and mediation, and compares fruits. These tests are genuine: mystery cannot make contradiction true. Yet reason does not originate the alleged content; a referee may reject a goal without scoring one.

The strongest objection is that interpretation changes what is assessed. This is partly right: hermeneutics prevents a perfectly external audit. Still, corrigible interpretation is better than self-authentication. Reason serves truth rather than belief or unbelief. It is regulative and capable of veto, while revelation remains a proposed source. Authentication is convergent and defeasible, not demonstration.
## Original question — 10 marks

**Question:** Is faith a virtue when doubt is present? Discuss with Tillich and one rival.

### Model answer

Tillich makes existential doubt intrinsic because a finite believer may mistake a concrete symbol for the ultimate. Courage carries structural doubt; repression generates fanaticism. Faith is whole-person ultimate concern, not weak knowledge.

Aquinas differs: faith has firmness without vision, and doubt is not constitutive in the same way. Motives of credibility make assent responsible while will and grace explain commitment. Tillich gains an internal critique of idolatry; Aquinas preserves determinate content more securely. Doubt is virtuous when it expresses finite self-criticism, not indifference or refusal to judge.
## Original question — 15 marks

**Question:** Compare propositional and non-propositional revelation as accounts of divine-human communication.

### Model answer

Propositional revelation treats disclosure as divinely authorised truth-content. It grounds natural/revealed theology and offers doctrinal clarity, while presupposing a personal revealer and reliable mediation. Circular authentication and rival infallible claims are its difficulties.

Non-propositional revelation makes divine action or presence primary. Hick’s experiencing-as explains why one event can be read naturally or providentially; doctrine interprets encounter. This honours personal relation but risks projection and public dependence on later propositions.

They are priorities, not exclusive boxes. Propositions without encounter can become inert information; encounter without propositions cannot be communicated or criticised. A balanced view gives event priority in reception and propositions priority in public articulation, both constrained by reason and diversity.
## Original question — 15 marks

**Question:** Can Reformed epistemology answer evidentialism without licensing arbitrariness?

### Model answer

Evidentialism proportions assent to evidence. Clifford makes irresponsible belief morally culpable and Flew assigns burden to the affirmative claim. Plantinga replies that classical foundationalism is self-undermining and too narrow because memory and other minds are rational without inference from its base.

Theistic belief may be properly basic when appropriately grounded. Warrant requires properly functioning faculties, suitable environment and truth-directed design; a functioning sensus divinitatis could satisfy these. This is conditional and defeasible.

The Great Pumpkin objection is answered by grounds and proper function, but diversity presses harder: rival communities can claim parallel warrant. Alston normalises some circularity but cannot uniquely vindicate a practice. Reformed epistemology defeats an over-strict de jure objection, not the de facto truth question.
## Original question — 20 marks

**Question:** “Rationally responsible faith is neither proof without trust nor trust without criticism.” Evaluate across Western and Indian traditions.

### Model answer

Reason infers, tests, interprets and regulates; revelation alleges disclosure; faith assents, trusts and commits. Rationalism reduces religion to unaided proof, while insulated fideism grants immunity.

Aquinas offers harmony: reason establishes preambles, revelation mysteries, and faith is intellectual assent under will and grace. Kierkegaard rightly insists that proof cannot replace appropriation, but Abraham exposes private-certainty danger. James permits only living, forced and momentous choice; critical rationalism preserves correction.

Plantinga shows rational belief need not be inferred, yet basicality stays defeasible. Tillich makes faith ultimate concern and ontological reason’s ecstatic fulfilment; existential doubt checks idolatry, though concrete truth remains underdetermined.

Indian accounts shift the frame. Nyāya accepts trustworthy testimony; Mīmāṃsā’s authorless Veda and intrinsic validity avoid a divine speaker; śraddhā enables inquiry and manana removes doubt. These are functional comparisons.

Diversity is the strongest objection: incompatible traditions can each claim warrant. Responsible faith may exceed coercive proof but forfeits standing when it contradicts reason or refuses correction.
## Original question — 20 marks

**Question:** Assess whether miracle testimony can authenticate revelation in a scientific age.

### Model answer

Hume defines miracle as violation of natural law. Part I sets uniform experience against fallible testimony; Part II adds witness quality, wonder, cultural origins and contrary miracles. The argument raises a burden but concerns evidence, not metaphysical impossibility.

Swinburne’s non-repeatable counter-instance avoids defining miracles away and gives testimony prima-facie standing. Earman shows low prior probability can be overcome by strong independent reports. Yet independence, background probability and alternatives must be assessed. An anomaly does not uniquely identify divine agency or validate a system.

A domain-sensitive Barbour model lets science judge empirical description while religious interpretation addresses meaning and remains accountable where empirical claims overlap. Indian traditions sharpen the point by declining display: Yoga treats powers as obstacles, Buddhism prefers instruction, and Mīmāṃsā needs no divine speaker.

Miracle testimony can offer cumulative support within an interpretive framework; it cannot independently authenticate a unique revelation. Humean caution survives while absolute exclusion does not.

**MCQ 56: D**

A. Every sacred book is non-propositional.
B. Infallibility means community approval.
C. Revelation occurs only through nature.

- **A — Incorrect:** A sacred book can contain propositions; the error lies in identifying every textual medium with revelation itself.
- **B — Incorrect:** Communal endorsement concerns reception and authority, not the stronger claim that a text cannot contain error.
- **C — Incorrect:** General revelation may work through nature, but special, historical and personal media remain possible.
- **D — Correct:** The repair restores disclosure, mediation and error-status distinctions.

**MCQ 57: A**

B. Tillich restricts faith to churches.

- **A — Correct:** Intensity alone omits unconditional demand, total promise and idolatry.
- **B — Incorrect:** Ultimate concern can structure secular lives as well as church membership, so Tillich’s category is not institution-bound.
- **C — Incorrect:** Conditional aims remain finite; calling each divine erases the idolatry test.
- **D — Incorrect:** Existential doubt is intrinsic because finite believers can misidentify what deserves unconditional concern.

**MCQ 58: B**

A. Only scriptural beliefs can be valid.
B. Cognition has prima-facie validity and can later be defeated.

D. Error is impossible.

- **A — Incorrect:** Svataḥ-prāmāṇya applies to cognition generally, not only to scriptural beliefs.
- **B — Correct:** Intrinsic validity is default entitlement, not incorrigibility.
- **C — Incorrect:** Demanding an inference before every cognition is valid creates the regress the doctrine is designed to avoid.
- **D — Incorrect:** Later defeating cognition explains error, so default validity never entails universal incorrigibility.

**MCQ 59: C**
“Hume proved miracles impossible.” Repair it.
A. Hume accepted virtuous witnesses automatically.
B. Hume defined miracles as coincidences.
C. Hume argued miracle testimony faces an exceptionally heavy evidential burden.

- **A — Incorrect:** Moral character can improve credibility but does not make testimony automatic or outweigh all contrary experience.
- **B — Incorrect:** Hume defines miracle through violation of natural law, not as an ordinary coincidence.
- **C — Correct:** The careful formulation preserves his epistemic rather than metaphysical target.
- **D — Incorrect:** His scrutiny of priors, witnesses and rival reports remains valuable even if absolute exclusion is rejected.

**MCQ 60: D**
“Kierkegaard permits any belief.” Repair it.
A. He requires scientific proof.
B. The leap removes ethical difficulty.
C. Faith becomes probability.
D. His leap concerns responsible appropriation under uncertainty, not blanket immunity.

- **A — Incorrect:** Kierkegaard opposes making faith dependent on detached scientific demonstration.
- **B — Incorrect:** Abraham’s case intensifies the ethical and publicity problem rather than dissolving it.
- **C — Incorrect:** Objective uncertainty and passionate commitment differ from calculating a probability.
- **D — Correct:** Commitment and risk do not erase responsibility.

**MCQ 61: A**
How should the Vatican AI message be used?
A. As a dated public-ethics example, not doctrinal or scientific proof.
B. Official status makes it infallible.
C. AI governance is solely theological.

- **A — Correct:** The source shows engagement with dignity and agency within strict evidential bounds.
- **B — Incorrect:** Institutional authorship gives provenance, not immunity from factual, ethical or theological error.
- **C — Incorrect:** AI governance requires technical, legal and democratic expertise alongside religious ethical contributions.
- **D — Incorrect:** The address advocates dialogue and cooperative pursuit of dignity rather than rejecting exchange.

**MCQ 62: B**
“Properly basic means groundless.” Repair it.
A. It is proved ontologically.
B. It is non-inferential yet grounded, conditioned by proper function and open to defeat.
C. It is true only for a community.
D. It is unrevisable.

- **A — Incorrect:** Ontological proof is inferential, whereas proper basicality is defined by not depending on an argument.
- **B — Correct:** Non-inferential status does not remove grounds or defeaters.
- **C — Incorrect:** Plantinga advances a warrant claim aimed at truth, not a relativist truth limited to communal acceptance.
- **D — Incorrect:** Defeaters can rationally overturn a basic belief, so non-inferential status is not incorrigibility.

**MCQ 63: C**
“Science and religion are separate, so conflict is impossible.” Repair it.
A. Barbour offers only conflict.
B. Technology raises no moral issues.
C. Some religious claims are empirical; total separation silences legitimate overlap.
D. Dialogue needs identical methods.

- **A — Incorrect:** Barbour distinguishes four relations; treating conflict as the sole model discards independence, dialogue and integration.
- **B — Incorrect:** Technologies distribute risk and power, making moral evaluation unavoidable even when mechanisms are scientifically understood.
- **C — Correct:** NOMA is one model with a real cost, not a universal conclusion.
- **D — Incorrect:** Dialogue requires contact between distinct methods and questions, not methodological identity.
