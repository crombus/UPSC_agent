## Lesson 1 — Three Offices and the Ambiguity of Self-Revelation
Progress: 1/15 | Stage: Foundation | Subtopic: distinct epistemic work and the 2019 double reading
━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Queried — canonical §§0A, 2–3 and the R05-01/R05-22 coverage rows.
CA search: Not required for this conceptual foundation.
CA found: Not applicable.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual first — one phrase, two routes

```text
REASON                    REVELATION                    FAITH
argues, tests,            alleged divine               assents, trusts,
interprets, criticises    disclosure                   commits, practises

"SELF-REVELATION"
   ├─ divine reading: God's self-disclosure → faith receives; reason regulates
   └─ human reading: self-knowledge/introspection → reflective reason has priority
```

*The examiner’s phrase must be disambiguated before the answer chooses faith or reason.*

### Core teaching

Reason, revelation and faith answer different questions. Reason asks what follows, what coheres and
what should be revised. Revelation names a purported disclosure whose source is divine. Faith is the
recipient’s assent, reliance and lived response. Their cooperation is possible only if none absorbs
the others: rationalism makes disclosure redundant, self-authenticating authority silences criticism,
and unregulated commitment becomes credulity.

The 2019 question — “What is instrumental to self-revelation: Faith or Reason?” — is linguistically
ambiguous. Within this syllabus clause, the more natural reading is **divine self-disclosure**. An event
or proposition may be offered as revelation, but faith is instrumental to recognising and appropriating
it; reason tests the interpretation, witnesses and consequences. If “self-revelation” instead means a
human subject coming to know oneself, disciplined introspection and rational self-examination have the
stronger claim. A good answer states both readings, selects one, and gives the alternate route in one
bounded paragraph.

**Objection:** faith cannot be needed for revelation because truth does not wait for acceptance.
**Reply:** distinguish the occurrence of an alleged disclosure from its recognition and efficacy for
a person. **Residual:** interpretation already shapes recognition, so the offices remain analytically
distinct but practically interwoven.

### UPSC application

For 2019 Q5(a): define the “self”; choose the divine reading; argue that faith receives while reason
regulates; then add: if human self-knowledge is intended, reasoned introspection is primary.

### Revision notes

1. Reason evaluates; revelation alleges disclosure; faith appropriates.
2. Reception cannot manufacture the truth of what is received.
3. Criticism cannot by itself generate every proposed mystery.
4. Divine self-disclosure and human self-knowledge are different questions.
5. Occurrence, recognition and transforming efficacy must be separated.
6. The 2019 answer needs an explicit ambiguity sentence.
7. Complementarity requires a genuine rational veto.
8. Interpretation prevents a perfectly sealed division of labour.

### Local practice

**MCQ 1: A**
Which opening best handles the 2019 self-revelation question?
A. State whether “self” means God disclosed to a recipient or a human subject known through introspection.
B. Assume without comment that self-revelation means mystical ecstasy.
C. Treat revelation and self-knowledge as exact synonyms.
D. Discuss proofs of God without choosing a reading.

- **A — Correct:** The wording supports two routes, and conceptual control begins by identifying them.
- **B — Incorrect:** Mystical ecstasy is only one possible experience and does not resolve whose self is revealed.
- **C — Incorrect:** Divine disclosure concerns an alleged transcendent source, whereas introspection concerns the human subject.
- **D — Incorrect:** A proof survey evades the instrumental choice between faith and reason.

**MCQ 2: B**
On the divine-disclosure reading, what is the most defensible division of work?
A. Faith proves the event and reason obeys it.
B. Faith recognises and appropriates; reason interprets and tests.
C. Reason creates the disclosure and faith records it.
D. Revelation eliminates both trust and criticism.

- **A — Incorrect:** Faith may receive a claim, but reception is not an independent demonstration of divine origin.
- **B — Correct:** It preserves the practical role of faith and the critical role of reason.
- **C — Incorrect:** Rational interpretation can shape content without becoming the alleged divine source.
- **D — Incorrect:** Disclosure still requires a mode of reception and remains open to epistemic appraisal.

## Lesson 2 — What Reason Can Do, and What Reason Alone Cannot Supply
Progress: 2/15 | Stage: Foundation | Subtopic: construction, regulation, interpretation and experience
━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Queried — canonical §§1, 9.1, 9.1A and 9.1B; R05-02–R05-05.
CA search: No live claim is needed for this doctrine.
CA found: Not applicable.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual first — four functions, one limit

| Rational function | Question asked | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Constructive | What can public premises support? | natural theology |
| Critical | Is the claim coherent and evidentially responsible? | contradiction or defeater test |
| Hermeneutic | What does a text or event mean in context? | historical interpretation |
| Regulative | What must be rejected or revised? | abusive or self-sealing reading |

```text
argument about the divine ≠ encounter with the divine
map of a relation          ≠ participation in that relation
```

### Core teaching

Reason in religion is wider than proof. It frames inferences, compares probabilities, interprets
texts, checks witness-claims, exposes moral harm and revises confidence. **Constitutive** reason
generates content from rational premises; **regulative** reason disciplines content received from
elsewhere. The latter can reject contradiction without deriving Trinity, and can condemn a violent
interpretation without proving an entire theology.

Natural theology begins from premises that do not presuppose a special revelation. Its conclusions,
even if sound, may underdetermine a particular creed. Kantian restrictions challenge speculative
reach but do not abolish coherence-testing or ethical criticism.

The 2026 reason-alone question targets sufficiency. A rational religion could retain arguments,
concepts and moral duties, yet inference alone does not yield prayer, awe, dependence or encounter.
Schleiermacher’s absolute dependence, Otto’s numinous and James’s noetic character supply bounded
examples of lived religion. They do not certify their own source. The defensible conclusion is mutual:
reason cannot replace experience, and experience cannot authenticate itself without reason.

### UPSC application

- 2024 Q5(d): organise the answer by functions, not by a list of proofs.
- 2026 Q5(e): agree with qualification; identify what reason preserves and what exclusive rationalism
  omits.

### Revision notes

1. Natural theology is only one use of reason.
2. Regulative does not mean constitutive.
3. Historical interpretation is rational activity.
4. Moral criticism can defeat an interpretation.
5. Public argument may underdetermine a creed.
6. Experience adds first-person participation.
7. Psychological force does not establish divine origin.
8. Strong conclusions preserve both correction and participation.

### Local practice

A. Deriving a first cause from contingency.
B. Formulating a doctrine from accepted revelation.
C. Rejecting an interpretation because it entails a contradiction.
D. Receiving a historical event as providential.

- **A — Incorrect:** This is a constructive natural-theology inference.
- **B — Incorrect:** Formulating content from premises is constitutive reasoning within a theological system.
- **C — Correct:** Regulation constrains a claim without supplying its entire content.
- **D — Incorrect:** Providential reception belongs primarily to faith-shaped interpretation.

A. Reason contributes nothing to religion.
B. Every experience is automatically revelatory.
C. Rational religion is logically impossible.
D. Reason can organise and criticise religion but cannot by itself supply lived encounter.

- **A — Incorrect:** Coherence, inference, interpretation and moral scrutiny remain indispensable.
- **B — Incorrect:** Experiences can be intense, misinterpreted or generated by non-divine causes.
- **C — Incorrect:** Deist and Kantian projects show that rationally reconstructed religion is conceptually possible.
- **D — Correct:** It answers the conditional without denying either rational religion or experience.

- **D — Incorrect:** Ethical consequences can test interpretations and institutions.

## Lesson 3 — Evidence, Commitment and Three Grades of Faith
Progress: 3/15 | Stage: Foundation | Subtopic: rationalism, faith-priority and failure modes
━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Queried — canonical §§4, 9.5, 9.5A and R05-06/R05-09–R05-12.
CA search: Not required.
CA found: Not applicable.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual first — the missing middle

```text
RATIONAL                SUPRA-RATIONAL                 IRRATIONAL
grounds and coherence   exceeds demonstration         contradiction, known fraud,
remain assessable       but not rational constraint   or immunity from decisive defeat
```

| Position | Legitimate insight | Characteristic danger |
|---|---|---|
| Evidentialism | belief carries epistemic responsibility | excessively narrow foundations |
| Faith-priority | proof cannot replace existential appropriation | insulation from criticism |
| Critical commitment | action can precede certainty | permanent provisionality |

### Core teaching

Clifford’s ethics of belief insists that accepting claims on inadequate grounds can harm others.
Kierkegaard insists that detached certainty cannot perform the existential act of commitment. The
opposition is not solved by calling every unproved claim irrational. Faith may be **rational** through
coherence, testimony and cumulative grounds; **supra-rational** where mystery and trust exceed what
demonstration compels; or **irrational** where a claim contradicts itself, rests on exposed deception
or declares itself immune to every possible correction.

Pascal’s wager supplies prudential motivation, not evidence for one deity. James permits a passional
decision only where the option is living, forced and momentous and evidence cannot settle it in time.
Neither proposal authorises choosing against decisive evidence. Critical rationalism offers the useful
middle: commit, act and remain corrigible.

**Objection:** allowing supra-rational faith creates a convenient refuge for error. **Reply:** the
category is bounded by consistency, defeater-sensitivity and ethical scrutiny. **Residual:** rival
traditions may all claim to occupy the middle.

### UPSC application

For 2023 Q6(c), define all three grades. A two-part “rational versus irrational” answer that omits the
supra-rational middle cannot explain responsible non-demonstrative faith.

### Revision notes

1. Lack of proof is not yet irrationality.
2. Rational faith remains accountable to grounds.
3. Supra-rational means beyond coercive demonstration.
4. Contradiction does not become mystery.
5. Clifford links belief to moral responsibility.
6. Kierkegaard denies that a system can replace appropriation.
7. Pascal is prudential.
8. James’s permission has three strict conditions.
9. Critical commitment stays revisable.

### Local practice

**MCQ 6: B**
Which claim is properly called supra-rational?
A. A doctrine known to be fabricated but retained for comfort.
B. A coherent mystery not demonstrable from public premises.
C. A contradiction protected from every objection.
D. A belief adopted after decisive disconfirmation.

- **A — Incorrect:** Known fabrication supplies a direct defeater rather than a mystery.
- **B — Correct:** It exceeds demonstration while remaining answerable to rational limits.
- **C — Incorrect:** Contradiction belongs to the irrational failure mode.
- **D — Incorrect:** Persistence after decisive defeat is obstinacy, not supra-rational faith.

**MCQ 7: C**
What does James’s genuine-option rule permit?
A. Any consoling belief.
B. A choice whenever evidence is inconvenient.
C. Passional decision when an option is living, forced, momentous and evidentially unresolved.
D. Belief that contradicts established facts.

- **A — Incorrect:** Consolation is not one of James’s limiting conditions.
- **B — Incorrect:** Avoidable inquiry cannot be bypassed merely because it is difficult.
- **C — Correct:** The permission is restricted to urgent options that evidence cannot decide in time.
- **D — Incorrect:** James does not license commitment against decisive evidence.

**Remediation cue:** Explain why “not demonstrated” and “irrational” are not coextensive.

## Lesson 4 — Augustine and Anselm: Faith Seeking Understanding
Progress: 4/15 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: *credo ut intelligam*, objection, Aquinas contrast and use
━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Queried — canonical §§0, 4–5 and coverage demand R05-07.
CA search: Not required for the historical-philosophical distinction.
CA found: Not applicable.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual first — two harmonies, different starting points

```text
AUGUSTINE / ANSELM
faith received first → inquiry from within → deeper understanding
        credo ut intelligam / fides quaerens intellectum

AQUINAS
public reason → preambles accessible without special revelation
revelation    → mysteries accepted on divine testimony
both belong to one truth and cannot genuinely conflict
```

| Question | Augustine–Anselm | Aquinas |
|---|---|---|
| Starting posture | believing participation | two distinguishable epistemic routes |
| Reason’s role | clarify and deepen what faith receives | prove preambles, organise mysteries, test compatibility |
| Main risk | circularity or inherited error | overconfidence in natural reason or testimony |

### Core teaching

The Augustinian–Anselmian formula **“I believe in order that I may understand”** presents inquiry as
faith seeking understanding. Faith is not the termination of thought; it opens a horizon within which
reason asks what the received commitment means, whether its claims fit together and how they illuminate
life. Anselm’s description of theology as *fides quaerens intellectum* preserves this ordered movement:
trust precedes fuller intellectual grasp.

This is not the same as saying evidence is irrelevant. The model assumes that some understanding
requires participation, just as friendship may be understood more deeply from within a relationship
than by detached observation. Yet participation can also reproduce communal error.

**Strong objection:** if faith supplies both premises and standards, “understanding” may become a
circular confirmation of what was already accepted. **Reply:** internal inquiry can still expose
inconsistency, refine interpretation and encounter external defeaters; faith-first need not mean
criticism-last. **Residual:** the model does not independently establish that the initial tradition is
true.

Aquinas differs in architecture. He distinguishes rationally accessible **preambles** from
revelation-dependent **mysteries**. Augustine and Anselm stress the epistemic order of the believer’s
journey; Aquinas provides a more explicit division between natural and revealed theology. Both reject
the inference from “beyond proof” to “against reason”.

### UPSC application

- 2021 Q7(a): place faith seeking understanding between evidentialist rationalism and insulated
  fideism; then ask whether reason can genuinely correct the inherited starting point.
- 2025 Q5(e): contrast this faith-first pedagogy with Aquinas’s two-route reconciliation before
  explaining intellectual assent.

### Revision notes

1. *Credo ut intelligam* means belief ordered toward understanding.
2. *Fides quaerens intellectum* makes inquiry internal to faith.
3. Faith-first does not logically entail reason-free.
4. Participation may disclose dimensions unavailable to detachment.
5. Circularity is the central objection.
6. External defeaters must remain possible.
7. Aquinas distinguishes preambles and mysteries more sharply.
8. Both traditions deny that mystery licenses contradiction.

### Local practice

## Lesson 5 — Revelation on Two Independent Axes
Progress: 5/15 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: general/special and propositional/non-propositional
━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Queried — canonical §§0A, 2, 9.2 and R05-13/R05-14.
CA search: Not required.
CA found: Not applicable.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual first — do not merge the axes

| Scope or medium | Truth-content model | Presence or event model |
|---|---|---|
| General | nature or conscience conveys claims | the world is encountered as gift, dependence or address |
| Special | prophet, scripture or person communicates doctrine | a particular event or history is received as divine action |

```text
AXIS 1: general / special = availability and bearer
AXIS 2: propositional / non-propositional = nature of disclosure
```

### Core teaching

**General revelation** is purportedly available through nature, conscience or universal history.
**Special revelation** is tied to a particular person, event, scripture or community. This is not the
same distinction as propositional versus non-propositional revelation. A general disclosure can be
described as conveying truths or as presenting the world as divine address; a special disclosure can
be doctrinal or event-centred.

Three neighbouring claims must also remain distinct. Revelation concerns disclosure. Inspiration
concerns the formation of a human record. Infallibility concerns freedom from error. A tradition may
affirm one without affirming all three in the same degree.

**Objection:** event-revelation cannot avoid propositions because calling an event “divine action” is
already a claim. **Reply:** non-propositional theories assert priority of encounter, not permanent
absence of language. **Residual:** public transmission almost always returns to propositions.

### UPSC application

Use the two-axis grid before discussing natural and revealed theology. The grid prevents “general
revelation” from becoming a loose synonym for “natural theology”.

### Revision notes

1. General/special concerns range and bearer.
2. Propositional/non-propositional concerns the character of disclosure.
3. The classifications cross.
4. Revelation is not identical with scripture.
5. Inspiration adds a mediation claim.
6. Infallibility adds an error-status claim.
7. Event priority does not mean propositionlessness.
8. Transmission reintroduces interpretation.

- **A — Incorrect:** Both scope categories can occur in either model of disclosure.
- **B — Incorrect:** The classification does not assign truth-values.

- **D — Correct:** The axes answer independent questions.

## Lesson 6 — Propositional Revelation and Two Theologies
Progress: 6/15 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: starting warrant, preambles, mysteries and authority
━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Queried — canonical §§9.2, 9.2B and R05-15/R05-16.
CA search: Not required.
CA found: Not applicable.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual first — same activity, different premises

```text
NATURAL THEOLOGY
publicly discussable premises → inference about God → limited conclusions

REVEALED THEOLOGY
purportedly disclosed propositions → rational interpretation → specific mysteries
```

| Axis | Natural theology | Revealed theology |
|---|---|---|
| Starting warrant | observation and public argument | accepted divine testimony |
| Reach | preambles and general attributes | particular doctrines and obligations |
| Main pressure | invalidity or underdetermination | authentication, rivalry and circularity |

### Core teaching

On the propositional model, revelation communicates truth-apt content. Natural theology reasons
without accepting a special disclosure as a premise; revealed theology reasons from propositions
claimed to have divine authority. Both employ thought. Their difference lies in starting warrant and
doctrinal reach.

Aquinas’s **preambles** may be approached by unaided reason; **mysteries** depend on revelation.
Disclosure may repeat accessible truths because not everyone has equal time, training or certainty.
“Beyond reason” therefore concerns access and demonstration, not permission to contradict.

The model presupposes a personal revealer, trustworthy mediation and defensible grounds for identifying
the witness. A text cannot establish unique divine authority merely by asserting it. Independent
testimony, coherence and other evidence can reduce circularity without producing certainty.

Mīmāṃsā offers a useful contrast: an *apauruṣeya* Veda is authorless, not dictation by a creator. This
shows that scriptural authority and personal divine speech are not identical concepts.

### UPSC application

For 2025 Q6(c), the phrase “in the propositional view” is controlling. Define that view first, compare
the two theologies by starting warrant and reach, and keep general revelation separate.

### Revision notes

1. Propositional revelation has truth-apt content.

## Lesson 7 — Event, Presence and Revelation-for-a-Person
Progress: 7/15 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Hick, experiencing-as, occurrence and reception
━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Queried — canonical §§3, 9.3 and R05-21/R05-22.
CA search: Not required.
CA found: Not applicable.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual first — one occurrence, rival construals

```text
historical event
   ├─ ordinary causal description
   └─ experienced-as divine action
             ↓
      communal narration
             ↓
       doctrine and practice
```

### Core teaching

Non-propositional revelation gives priority to divine presence, action or self-disclosure rather than
to dictated sentences. Hick’s “experiencing-as” explains how perception is shaped by concepts: the
same event can be described through ordinary causes and received as providential. Faith is therefore
important to the recognition of revelation.

Three levels prevent confusion:

1. **Occurrence:** something happens or a presence is alleged.
2. **Recognition:** a recipient perceives it as divine.
3. **Efficacy:** the interpretation reshapes belief and life.

A propositional theorist can maintain that disclosed truth obtains before anyone accepts it. The
non-propositional theorist replies that an unrecognised event is not yet revelation **for** a person.
Thus faith may be indispensable to reception without causing the event or guaranteeing the
interpretation.

**Projection objection:** if conceptual schemes supply the meaning, revelation may be human
construction. **Reply:** reality may resist expectation, and coherence, testimony and moral fruits can
constrain interpretation. **Residual:** those tests are themselves historically situated.

### UPSC application

- 2022 Q5(d): separate occurrence, recognition and efficacy before judging indispensability.
- 2019 Q5(a): on the divine reading, faith is instrumental to reception; reason remains the regulator.

3. An event has more than one possible description.
4. Faith contributes recognition.
5. Faith does not cause objective truth.
6. Occurrence differs from revelation-for-a-person.
7. Projection is the central objection.
8. Public tests remain defeasible.

## Lesson 8 — Can a Revelation-Claim Be Justified?
Progress: 8/15 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: cumulative tests, circularity and personal assessment
━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Queried — canonical §§2, 9.2A and R05-16–R05-20.
CA search: Not required.
CA found: Not applicable.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual first — convergent support without automatic divinity

| Test | Positive work | Built-in limit |
|---|---|---|
| Testimony | checks proximity, competence and independence | sincere witnesses may misread |
| Coherence | excludes contradiction | coherent fiction remains possible |
| Moral fruit | tracks transformation and harm | useful error is possible |
| Community | preserves and corrects memory | institutions preserve bias too |
| Experience or sign | supplies salience and first-person force | attribution remains underdetermined |
| Rival comparison | exposes parochial confidence | neutral criteria are scarce |

```text
several independent supports → stronger prima-facie case
same supports inside rival traditions → lower confidence and a diversity defeater
```

### Core teaching

The target of justification is not merely sincerity or moral usefulness. It is the claim that a
proposition or event originates in divine disclosure. No single test establishes that conclusion.

Witnesses must be competent, independent and suitably placed. Coherence is a negative filter, not a
proof of divine authorship. Beneficial transformation counts in favour of an interpretation, but false
beliefs can also produce discipline or solidarity. Communities preserve testimony and also exercise
power. Unusual events require an account of cause and meaning. Rival revelations force comparison
because incompatible claims cannot all be literally true in the same respect.

**Circularity:** “this text is divine because the text says so” is vicious when no independent
constraint enters. A broader web of testimony, history and coherence can be non-linear without being
conclusive.

**Personal assessment for 2024:** use independent testimony and coherence primarily as filters;
experience and fruits as limited positive reasons; competing revelations as a continuing reason for
modesty. The rational status is cumulative and defeasible.

### UPSC application

For 2024 Q7(b), the directive asks for “your own comments”. Rank the criteria and explain why
convergence improves warrant without proving a unique revealer.

### Revision notes

1. The target is divine attribution.
2. Witness number is not witness independence.
3. Coherence excludes some errors.
4. Fruit does not entail truth.
5. Communities transmit and distort.
6. Signs require interpretation.
7. Exclusive self-certification is vicious.
8. Rival revelations create comparative pressure.
9. Cumulative support remains defeasible.

## Lesson 9 — Faith, Belief and Aquinas’s Intellectual Assent
Progress: 9/15 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: tenets, *fides qua/quae*, intellect, will and grace
━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Queried — canonical §§3, 7.3, 9.4, 9.7 and R05-08/R05-23/R05-24.
CA search: Not required.
CA found: Not applicable.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual first — from proposition to entrusting

```text
belief-that: "p is true"
        +
belief-in: reliance, fidelity and practical commitment
        =
faith as cognitive + volitional + relational act
```

| Aquinas | Function |
|---|---|
| Intellect | performs assent |
| Will | moves the intellect under non-coercive evidence |
| Grace | orients the person toward divine truth |
| Motives of credibility | prevent assent from becoming sheer whim |

### Core teaching

A useful seven-part analysis of faith includes an ultimate object, some content assented to, trust,
commitment with risk, hope, communal formation and practical fruits. It is an exam framework, not a
claim that every religion endorses one canonical creed.

Belief-that is propositional assent. Faith adds believing-in: reliance, fidelity and a lived
orientation. Aquinas’s *fides quae* names the content believed; *fides qua* names the act or habit of
believing. The reductionist may say that believing-in is only a bundle of beliefs about existence and
reliability. The reply is that one may accept those propositions while refusing to entrust oneself.

For Aquinas, faith is intellectual assent to divine truth, commanded by will and moved by grace. It is
not demonstrative knowledge because the object is unseen; it is not opinion because assent is firm.
Reason supplies preambles and compatibility checks; revelation supplies mysteries.

**Voluntarism objection:** if will commands belief, desire may replace truth. **Reply:** the will does
not invent the proposition; it commits under credibility grounds that do not compel. **Residual:** the
adequacy of those grounds remains disputed across traditions.

### UPSC application

- 2020 Q7(c): give the tenets first, then distinguish faith and belief by object, grammar, mode,
  voluntariness and failure.
- 2025 Q5(e): reconstruct intellect–will–grace and firm assent without vision; contrast Augustine’s
  faith-first inquiry and Aquinas’s two-route harmony.

### Revision notes

1. Faith includes but exceeds propositional belief.
2. The seven tenets form an analytical scheme.
3. *Fides quae* is content.
4. *Fides qua* is the believing act.
5. Intellect formally assents.
6. Will moves under non-coercive grounds.
7. Grace orients the person.
8. Faith is firm without demonstrative sight.
9. Credibility limits arbitrariness.

## Lesson 10 — Kierkegaard, Pascal, James and Corrigible Commitment
Progress: 10/15 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: existential appropriation, prudence and permission
━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Queried — canonical §§4, 9.5 and R05-06/R05-10/R05-12.
CA search: Not required.
CA found: Not applicable.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual first — four different permissions

| View | What it permits | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Kierkegaard | existential appropriation under objective uncertainty | universal immunity from ethics or evidence |
| Pascal | prudential commitment under high stakes | truth of one specific religion |
| James | passional choice in a genuine option | belief against decisive evidence |
| Critical rationalism | committed action with revisability | final security |

### Core teaching

Kierkegaard challenges the idea that an objective system can substitute for becoming a committed
subject. Abraham dramatises the risk and also creates a publicity problem: private certainty can be
indistinguishable from dangerous fanaticism. The “leap” should therefore be presented as appropriation
under uncertainty, not as permission for contradiction.

Pascal’s wager changes the question from evidence to decision. Its limits are the many-gods problem,
the authenticity of strategically cultivated belief and the gap between expected utility and truth.
James is narrower: where an option is living, forced and momentous and evidence cannot decide, refusing
to choose is itself a practical choice.

Critical rationalism offers a defensible synthesis: commitment can be genuine while remaining exposed
to counter-evidence and moral criticism. Its price is that no human formulation gains absolute closure.

### UPSC application

Use the views comparatively. State each permission and its cost. Do not attribute the slogan “I believe
because it is absurd” as a safe verbatim quotation from Tertullian.

### Revision notes

1. Kierkegaard targets detached substitution for existence.
2. Objective uncertainty is not logical contradiction.
3. Abraham raises a public-justification problem.
4. Pascal gives prudential, not evidential, reasons.
5. The many-gods objection survives.
6. James’s option has three conditions.
7. Non-choice can itself decide a forced option.
8. Corrigibility constrains fanaticism.

## Lesson 11 — Evidentialism and Reformed Epistemology
Progress: 11/15 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: ethics of belief, proper basicality, warrant and practices
━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Queried — canonical §§7.5, 9.9; dossier promotion rule; R05-28–R05-30.
CA search: Not required.
CA found: Not applicable.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual first — rival standards of justification

```text
LOCKE / CLIFFORD / FLEW
assent proportioned to evidence; affirmative claimant bears a burden

PLANTINGA
some beliefs may be non-inferentially grounded and warranted

ALSTON
doxastic practices cannot be validated from a completely practice-free standpoint
```

### Core teaching

Evidentialism asks whether religious assent is proportioned to evidence. Clifford’s shipowner shows
that irresponsible belief can impose risk on others; Flew places the initial burden on affirmative
claims.

Plantinga argues that classical foundationalism is too restrictive and struggles to satisfy its own
criterion. Memory and belief in other minds are commonly rational without fresh inference. Theistic
belief may likewise be **properly basic**: non-inferential but grounded in appropriate circumstances.
**Warrant** requires proper cognitive functioning, a suitable environment, truth-directed design and
absence of undefeated defeaters. This is conditional; it does not prove that theism is true.

The Great Pumpkin objection asks why any belief may not claim basic status. Grounding, proper function
and defeaters answer simple arbitrariness, but religious diversity remains difficult. Alston adds that
perception itself cannot be non-circularly validated from nowhere; mature doxastic practices may be
rationally engaged in unless defeated. Practice parity still does not select among incompatible
traditions.

Indian comparison must be qualified. Mīmāṃsā’s *svataḥ-prāmāṇya* grants cognition prima-facie validity
until defeated; Nyāya’s *parataḥ-prāmāṇya* requires further certification. Neither is identical with
Plantingian proper-function warrant or Clifford’s moral evidentialism.

### UPSC application

For 2023 Q5(b), identify the standard of justification before listing thinkers. Conclude that lack of
inference alone does not entail irrationality, while truth, proper function, defeaters and diversity
remain open.

### Revision notes

1. Evidentialism is a standard, not a proof of atheism.
2. Clifford moralises belief responsibility.
3. Classical foundationalism may be self-undermining.
4. Properly basic means non-inferentially grounded.
5. Basic beliefs remain defeasible.
6. Warrant is stronger than mere acceptability.
7. Great Pumpkin tests arbitrariness.
8. Alston argues from practice parity.
9. Diversity remains a serious defeater.
10. Indian validity theories are analogues, not equivalents.

## Lesson 12 — Tillich: Ultimate Concern, Doubt and Ecstatic Reason
Progress: 12/15 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: centred act, idolatry, three doubts and two senses of reason
━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Queried — canonical §9.10 and verified 2026 Q5(c).
CA search: Not required.
CA found: Not applicable.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual first — the complete discriminator

```text
ULTIMATE CONCERN
unconditional demand + total promise + centring of the whole person
        ├─ transparent finite bearer → responsible faith
        └─ finite bearer made ultimate → idolatry → existential disappointment

DOUBT: methodological | sceptical | existential
                                  ↑ only this is intrinsic to finite faith

REASON: technical calculation | ontological/creative reason
faith = ontological reason reaching beyond finitude without self-destruction
```

### Core teaching

Tillich defines faith as the state of being ultimately concerned. It is a centred act involving
intellect, will, emotion and the whole personality, not a weak hypothesis on poor evidence. The same
structure can be occupied by nation, success or ideology. When a finite bearer claims unconditional
status, faith becomes idolatrous.

The three doubts must not be merged. Methodological doubt tests propositions. Sceptical doubt questions
the possibility of certainty as a stance. **Existential doubt** asks whether one’s concrete concern is
truly ultimate; this doubt is internal to finite faith and is carried by courage. Suppressing it
creates fanaticism.

Tillich also distinguishes technical reason, which calculates means, from ontological or creative
reason, which structures meaning, freedom and ends. Reason is the precondition of faith; faith is reason
in ecstasy — rationality fulfilled beyond its finite limits, not abolished. A faith that destroys
reason destroys itself.

**Objections:** the definition may be too broad; structural faith may include secular idolatries; the
account may not identify which concrete concern is true. **Replies:** unconditional centring separates
ultimate from ordinary preference, and the idolatry test exposes false ultimacy. **Residual:** the model
diagnoses distortion more successfully than it selects a uniquely true revelation.

### Bounded Indian comparison — deploy once, then stop

| Point | Tillich | Advaita | Exam boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Finite descriptions of the ultimate | No finite bearer or image may itself be absolutised | *Nirguṇa* Brahman is not exhausted by limiting predicates; *neti neti* removes such determinations | Limited analogy only: both resist reducing ultimacy to a finite description |
| Route to the claim | Existential analysis of faith and idolatry | Scriptural exegesis and non-dual inquiry | Do not present the theories as identical |

> **Mandatory trap warning:** *Anirvacanīya* describes *māyā* or the status of sublatable appearance,
> **not Brahman**. For R05-27, write one analogy sentence, state the different routes, add this warning,
> and return to Tillich; full symbol theory belongs to Religious Language.

### UPSC application

For 2026 Q5(c): definition → idolatry → three doubts → two senses of reason → one objection and
qualified verdict. A bounded *nirguṇa* comparison may sharpen the limit of finite descriptions, but
must include the *anirvacanīya–māyā* warning. Keep Tillich’s full symbol theory outside this owner.

### Revision notes

1. Faith is ultimate concern.
2. It is a whole-person centred act.
3. Ultimate concern is not automatically true faith.
4. Idolatry absolutises a finite bearer.
5. Methodological doubt belongs to inquiry.
6. Sceptical doubt is a general stance.
7. Existential doubt is intrinsic.
8. Courage carries doubt.
9. Technical reason calculates means.
10. Ontological reason concerns meaning and creativity.
11. Ecstasy fulfils rather than destroys reason.
12. Concrete truth remains underdetermined.
13. *Nirguṇa* is only a limited analogy: *anirvacanīya* qualifies *māyā*, not Brahman.

## Lesson 13 — Miracle-Testimony and the Burden of Proof
Progress: 13/15 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Hume, replies, contrary miracles and Indian contrasts
━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: Queried — canonical §9.8 and R05-17/R05-19/R05-31/R05-37.
CA search: Not required.
CA found: Not applicable.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual first — three evidential steps

```text
Did an unusual event occur?
            ↓
Is divine action the best explanation?
            ↓
Does that action authenticate this message or whole tradition?

success at one step does not guarantee the next
```

### Core teaching

Hume defines miracle as violation of natural law and compares the weight of regular experience with
fallible testimony. His further cautions concern witness quality, attraction to wonder, cultural
conditions and contrary miracle traditions. The conclusion is an exceptionally high evidential burden,
not a logical demonstration that miracles cannot occur.

Swinburne challenges a definition that makes violation do too much work and gives perception and
testimony prima-facie standing. Bayesian criticism notes that a low prior probability may be overcome
by sufficiently strong independent evidence. Yet independence must be demonstrated, and a credible
anomaly still does not identify divine agency or validate an entire creed.

Contrary miracles weaken exclusive inference because rival traditions appeal to structurally similar
reports. Indian materials add a different pressure: Yoga acknowledges powers but treats them as
obstacles to absorption; Buddhist criticism downgrades display in favour of instruction; Mīmāṃsā does
not need a miracle-certified creator-speaker.

### UPSC application

Use miracles as one limited strand in revelation-authentication. Never write that Hume proved
metaphysical impossibility.

### Revision notes

1. Hume’s target is testimony.
2. Regular experience establishes a high prior burden.
3. Witness quality and independence matter.
4. Wonder encourages exaggeration.
5. Contrary miracles challenge exclusivity.
6. Low prior is not zero posterior.
7. A credible anomaly underdetermines agency.
8. A divine attribution underdetermines a whole system.
9. Indian traditions often downgrade apologetic display.

Progress: 14/15 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Barbour, Haught, empirical overlap and normative significance
━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual first — models and prices

| Barbour model | Relation | Intellectual price |
|---|---|---|
| Conflict | competing claims in one domain | turns local disputes into universal warfare |
| Independence | separate questions or methods | may silence genuine overlap |
| Dialogue | exchange at boundaries | can remain inconclusive |

```text
mechanism and prediction → empirical inquiry leads

```

### Core teaching

Barbour’s four models prevent a single verdict from controlling every claim. The historical warfare
story is too crude, but conflict is real where religious statements make empirical assertions.
Independence protects methodological difference yet may evade boundary questions. Dialogue compares
models, intelligibility and values. Integration seeks a unified picture but can tie theology to
provisional science. Haught’s Conflict, Contrast, Contact and Confirmation offers a related vocabulary.

Technology intensifies the question. Scientific expertise can tell us what a system does, with what
accuracy and risk. It does not alone decide which ends are worth pursuing, how burdens should be
distributed or why persons possess dignity. Religion may contribute moral traditions, communities and
criticism of technological ultimacy, but it has no monopoly on public ethics and cannot settle
mechanisms by authority.

Vivekananda, Radhakrishnan and Aurobindo offer Indian integration claims. Report them as philosophical
proposals, not as scientific certification.

### UPSC application

For 2020 Q5(a), use all four models and their costs, then conclude with a domain-sensitive mixture:
methodological independence, dialogue on boundaries and values, and local conflict where factual claims
overlap.

1. No single model fits every claim.
2. Universal warfare history is overdrawn.
3. Independence can become mutual silence.
4. Dialogue preserves contact without identity.
5. Integration is exposed to scientific change.
6. Technology separates “can” from “ought”.
7. Religion can contribute without monopolising ethics.
8. Public claims require publicly discussable reasons.
9. Indian syntheses remain contestable proposals.

# OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

> **Entry rule:** Advanced material may refine a complete Core answer. It may not replace a missing
> definition, argument, objection or directive-specific verdict.

## A1. Personal warrant and public portability

```text
first-person disclosure
        ↓ strong existential force
testimony and records
        ↓ public translation
historical and comparative testing
        ↓
defeasible public credibility, not transferred certainty
```

A private experience may rationally matter greatly to its subject while possessing limited force for
an outsider. Public testimony is more shareable, yet it loses immediacy and may introduce dependence,
institutional selection and interpretive distortion. The correct contrast is not “conclusive or
worthless”. Personal warrant and public portability are different epistemic achievements.

**Advanced objection:** incompatible private disclosures cancel one another. **Reply:** they defeat
unqualified universalisation, not necessarily every first-person entitlement. **Stop rule:** after
stating the asymmetry, return to the printed criterion or revelation claim.

## A2. Doxastic voluntarism and indirect control

One normally cannot create sincere belief by command. Nevertheless, agents can control attention,
inquiry, company, practices and willingness to trust. These choices alter which grounds are noticed
and how commitment develops.

| Form | Plausible use | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| instant belief at will | generally implausible | sincerity resists command |
| assent under non-coercive grounds | Aquinas | wishful thinking if credibility is weak |
| passional permission | James | only genuine options |
| formation through practice | prayer, study, *śravaṇa–manana* | practices can educate or bias |

Indirect control explains how faith may be responsibly cultivated without implying that will creates
truth.

## A3. Finer Reformed-epistemology residue

| Distinction | Advanced use | Remaining pressure |
|---|---|---|
| justification / warrant | rational acceptability is not yet knowledge | truth remains necessary |
| *de jure* / *de facto* | rational status differs from truth | conditional theistic model may be rejected |
| grounded / inferred | non-inferential need not mean arbitrary | rival grounds compete |
| practice circularity / vicious circularity | every practice uses some of its outputs | parity cannot choose one practice |

Plantinga’s conditional point is: if theism is true and relevant faculties function properly in the
right environment without undefeated defeaters, theistic belief may possess warrant. The conditional
does not establish theism.

## A4. Advanced deployment table

| Printed demand | One safe refinement | Immediate return |
|---|---|---|
| self-revelation | first-person force versus public portability | faith receives; reason regulates |
| Aquinas | indirect rather than instant doxastic control | intellect–will–grace |
| justified religious belief | justification/warrant once | diversity and defeaters |
| faith versus belief | commitment is more voluntary than proposition formation | entrusting versus assent |

## Advanced retrieval checks

### Check 1
**Question:** Why can private disclosure be rationally significant without becoming public proof?

**Model answer:** The recipient has first-person access, existential force and possibly sustained
transformation. Outsiders possess only reports and consequences, so they must test transmission,
alternative explanations and rival experiences. Personal warrant may therefore exceed public
portability without becoming either universal evidence or nothing.

### Check 2
**Question:** How can will influence faith if belief is not directly voluntary?

**Model answer:** Will can govern attention, inquiry, trusted practices and commitment under
non-coercive grounds. These activities shape belief formation without allowing a person to make a
proposition true or sincerely believe anything on command.

### Check 3
**Question:** What does the *de jure/de facto* distinction prevent?

**Model answer:** It prevents a debate about rational or warranted status from being mistaken for a
proof of truth. A belief may be defensible under a conditional warrant model while the truth of its
object remains contested.

## Advanced revision notes

1. Personal force and public portability differ.
2. Public records trade immediacy for shareability.
3. Rival disclosures demand comparative modesty.
4. Direct belief at will is generally implausible.
5. Indirect control operates through attention and practice.
6. Warrant and justification are not synonyms.
7. Conditional warrant does not prove theism.
8. Practice parity does not select a unique tradition.
9. One Advanced move is normally sufficient.

# ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS MODEL PRACTICE

These are original exercises, not PYQs.

## Original question — 10 marks

**Question:** Does faith seeking understanding escape the charge of circularity? Answer in about
150 words.

### Model answer

Augustine and Anselm treat faith not as the end of thought but as its initiating horizon: *credo ut
intelligam*, or faith seeking understanding. A participant may grasp the practical and existential
meaning of a commitment more deeply than a detached observer. Reason can clarify doctrine, test
consistency and refine interpretation from within.

The circularity objection is nevertheless serious. If the tradition supplies both premises and
standards, inquiry may merely reproduce inherited conclusions. Faith-first reasoning becomes
epistemically responsible only if contradiction, historical evidence, moral criticism and external
defeaters can force revision.

Aquinas provides a useful contrast. His distinction between rational preambles and revealed mysteries
gives public reason a more independent starting point, although revealed testimony still needs
authentication. Faith seeking understanding therefore escapes vicious circularity only in a qualified
sense: participation may deepen comprehension, but it cannot make the original commitment self-validating.

**Scoring note:** Reward the formula, participatory gain, circularity objection, external correction and
an explicit Aquinas contrast.

## Original question — 10 marks

**Question:** How should a philosopher answer an ambiguously worded question on self-revelation?
Answer in about 150 words.

### Model answer

The first task is semantic discipline. “Self-revelation” may mean God’s self-disclosure to a human
recipient, or a human subject’s disclosure to oneself through introspection.

Within a syllabus on reason, revelation and faith, divine self-disclosure is the stronger contextual
reading. Faith is instrumental to recognising and appropriating an event or proposition as revelation;
reason remains regulative by testing coherence, testimony, interpretation and moral fruits. Faith does
not cause the disclosure, so occurrence must be distinguished from revelation-for-a-person.

If the phrase instead means human self-knowledge, reasoned introspection has priority: critical
reflection tests self-deception, integrates memory and action, and makes implicit motives explicit.
Faith may orient a person but is not the general instrument of self-knowledge.

A sound answer should state both routes, select the contextually justified one, and devote one bounded
sentence to the alternative rather than silently changing the question.

**Scoring note:** Require both readings, a justified contextual choice, occurrence/recognition and a
brief introspection route.

## Original question — 15 marks

**Question:** Compare faith seeking understanding with Aquinas’s reconciliation of reason and
revelation. Answer in about 200 words.

### Model answer

Both models reject the crude opposition between proof-only rationalism and reasonless fideism, but they
organise the harmony differently.

They also agree that a truth genuinely established by reason cannot be overturned merely by being
labelled a mystery; harmony requires intellectual discipline as well as religious commitment.

Augustine and Anselm begin with believing participation. *Credo ut intelligam* and *fides quaerens
intellectum* mean that faith generates a task of understanding. Reason clarifies received commitments,
draws implications and seeks coherence. Its strength is insight into the participatory character of
religious life. Its weakness is circularity: inquiry may merely confirm inherited authority unless
external defeaters retain force.

Aquinas distinguishes two epistemic routes within one truth. Natural reason approaches preambles such
as God’s existence; revelation communicates mysteries unavailable to demonstration. Faith is firm
intellectual assent to divine testimony, commanded by will and moved by grace. Reason can show
compatibility and supply motives of credibility without deriving the mysteries.

The Aquinas model gives public reason greater independence, but it still depends on contested
authentication of divine testimony and faces a voluntarism objection. The Augustinian–Anselmian model
better depicts inquiry from within commitment, while Aquinas better specifies limits and division of
labour. A defensible synthesis treats participation as a route to understanding, not a substitute for
critical testing.

**Scoring note:** Credit shared harmony, different starting points, one objection to each and a
comparative verdict.

## Original question — 15 marks

**Question:** Can cumulative criteria justify a claim to revelation? Answer in about 200 words.

### Model answer

A revelation-claim alleges divine origin, not merely sincerity or moral usefulness. Its justification
therefore requires several distinct tests.

Competent and independent testimony can support the occurrence of an event. Coherence excludes
contradictory readings, while historical interpretation tests transmission. Moral and spiritual fruits
give limited positive support; communal endurance may preserve memory; unusual signs may increase
salience. None is individually sufficient. A coherent fiction is possible, beneficial transformation
can arise from error, and an anomaly does not identify its cause.

Cumulative convergence is stronger when the supports are genuinely independent. It can also reduce
vicious circularity, because the source is not validated solely by its own claim. Yet rival revelations
create a diversity defeater: incompatible traditions may present testimony, experience and fruit of
similar strength.

The assessment must also distinguish credibility of occurrence from divine attribution. Historical
evidence may establish that a community encountered an unusual event without showing that God caused
it or endorsed every doctrine later attached to it.

The justified verdict is therefore graded. Independent evidence and coherence function best as
filters; experience and fruit provide defeasible positive reasons. Convergence can make a disclosure
rationally credible, but certainty about unique divine authorship remains unwarranted while rival
claims and alternative explanations survive.

**Scoring note:** Require the target of justification, multiple tests with limits, independence,
diversity and a graded conclusion.

## Original question — 20 marks

**Question:** “Responsible religious commitment begins before certainty but never after criticism
ends.” Evaluate through Western and Indian traditions. Answer in about 300 words.

### Model answer

Religious commitment often begins where evidence is significant but non-coercive. This does not make
criticism dispensable. Reason, revelation and faith perform different tasks: reason evaluates,
revelation alleges disclosure, and faith trusts and acts.

Augustine and Anselm make faith the starting point of understanding. Their insight is participatory:
some meanings appear only from within a practice. Their danger is circular confirmation. Aquinas
responds through a clearer division. Reason approaches preambles; faith assents firmly to revealed
mysteries through intellect, will and grace. Credibility grounds constrain assent, though rival
testimony keeps them contestable.

Kierkegaard shows why objective description cannot replace decision, but Abraham exposes the danger of
private certainty. James therefore permits passional choice only for living, forced and momentous
options that evidence cannot decide. Critical rationalism captures the norm: commitment remains
revisable.

Pascal adds prudential urgency but cannot identify the true religious option; the many-gods objection
shows why expected utility cannot substitute for comparative truth assessment. Thus decision theories
may explain why commitment begins, while evidential and moral criticism still govern whether it should
continue.

Evidentialists rightly connect belief with responsibility. Plantinga replies that rationality need not
always be inferential: a belief can be properly basic, grounded and defeasible. Alston’s practice
argument weakens the demand for completely non-circular validation, while religious diversity remains
a serious test. Tillich internalises this fallibility: existential doubt checks the idolatrous elevation
of finite objects, and faith must not destroy reason.

Indian traditions widen the comparison. Nyāya tests trustworthy testimony; Mīmāṃsā grants cognition
prima-facie validity while allowing defeat. Vedāntic *śraddhā* initiates hearing, but *manana* examines
doubt; Buddhist *saddhā* is directed toward practice and verification. These are not creator-centred
versions of Western faith.

Their shared lesson is procedural rather than doctrinal: initial trust can make inquiry possible, but
later reflection, experience and correction determine whether that trust matures into knowledge or
collapses under defeat.

Responsible commitment may therefore precede certainty, but it loses epistemic standing when it
forbids correction, ignores exposed fraud, embraces contradiction or suppresses moral criticism.

**Scoring note:** Reward integrated use of Augustine–Anselm, Aquinas, one existential/pragmatic view,
Plantinga or Tillich, and at least two distinct Indian positions.

## Original question — 20 marks

**Question:** Assess reason’s authority over revelation without reducing revelation to natural
theology. Answer in about 300 words.

### Model answer

The problem requires a distinction between constitutive and regulative authority. Constitutive reason
derives religious content from public premises; regulative reason tests content claimed to have been
received through revelation.

Reason legitimately examines logical consistency, witness competence, historical transmission,
interpretive context, moral consequences and rival claims. It can reject a self-contradictory reading,
an exposed fraud or a doctrine authenticated solely by its own assertion. Such criticism does not imply
that reason generated the alleged disclosure.

Augustine and Anselm treat faith as seeking understanding: reason works from within received commitment.
The model recognises participation but risks circularity. Aquinas supplies a sharper division. Natural
theology reaches preambles, while revealed theology accepts mysteries on divine testimony. Reason
clarifies and checks compatibility; it does not demonstrate the mystery itself.

Non-propositional theories complicate the picture because interpretation partly constitutes an event
as revelation-for-a-person. Hick’s experiencing-as shows that regulation can reshape the content it
claims merely to assess. Moreover, reason is culturally situated and may present one tradition’s
standards as universal.

This limitation does not justify relativism. Standards can be criticised through internal coherence,
cross-tradition comparison, historical evidence and consequences for persons, even when no standpoint
is wholly tradition-free. Fallible regulation remains preferable to authority insulated from every
possible correction.

Indian comparisons confirm both authority and limit. Nyāya subjects testimony to speaker and sentence
conditions. Mīmāṃsā gives the authorless Veda prima-facie authority while still requiring
interpretation; Vedāntic *manana* removes doubts after hearing rather than independently producing the
teaching.

These examples also block a Western monopoly on the debate: rational scrutiny can operate within a
received discipline without either constituting all content or surrendering the power to identify
defects.

Reason should therefore possess veto, revision and interpretation rights without claiming authorship
of every religious content. Revelation is not reduced to natural theology when its distinctive source
claim remains intact; it becomes responsible only when that claim is open to rational and moral defeat.

**Scoring note:** Require regulative/constitutive authority, Augustine–Anselm, Aquinas, the hermeneutic
complication, Indian comparison and a bounded verdict.
