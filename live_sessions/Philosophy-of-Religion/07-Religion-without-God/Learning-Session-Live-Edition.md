# MUST-NEEDED / CORE LEARNING SESSION

## Lesson 1 — Identify the Missing God before Classifying the Religion

Progress: 1/8 | Stage: Foundation | Subtopic: Creator, Lord, exemplar, ultimate, and four attitudes toward belief

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Permitted basis: canonical Topic 07 §§0–1, 9.1A; Clause 7 demands R07-01–05.
PYQ use: establishes the vocabulary required by the 2018, 2020, and 2021 stems.
Tier ruling: Core, because an unspecified use of “God” makes every later claim equivocal.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual — four different removals

```text
PERSONAL CREATOR        NON-CREATING LORD       PERFECTED EXEMPLAR       IMPERSONAL ULTIMATE
makes the world         guides practice         has attained freedom     orders meaning/reality
      |                       |                         |                         |
remove this             may still remain        may still be revered      may still orient life
      \_______________________|_________________________|_________________________/
                                      |
                           "without God" is ambiguous
```

The syllabus is best read as excluding a **personal creator**, not as banning every sacred object,
deity, liberated being, or ultimate principle.

### 1. Four stances that answer four questions

| Stance | Question answered | Minimal claim |
|---|---|---|
| Non-theism | Must a path employ a creator? | No creator is structurally required |
| Atheism | Does God exist? | God does not exist |
| Agnosticism | Is God known? | Knowledge is suspended or denied |
| Indifference | Does the issue matter here? | The question does not organise practice |

Non-theism concerns the design of a religious path. Atheism is an ontological denial. Agnosticism
is an epistemic restraint. Indifference is a practical lack of concern. A Buddhist can therefore be
non-theistic without asserting that every kind of divine being is unreal.

### 2. The classification problem

A definition may be:

- **substantive**, identifying religion by its object;
- **functional**, identifying it by what it does;
- **multidimensional**, identifying a profile of doctrine, narrative, practice, ethics,
  experience, institution, and material expression;
- **family-resemblance based**, admitting overlapping similarities without one universal essence.

A definition that includes “relation to a personal God” makes godless religion impossible by
stipulation. That may describe theistic religion accurately, but it cannot decide a cross-cultural
dispute without confronting Buddhism and Jainism.

The opposite danger is unlimited functionalism. Armies, political movements, and sports crowds can
generate ritual, loyalty, symbols, and sacrifice. These similarities do not automatically make
them religions.

### 3. A usable examination test: the four-axis profile

Ask whether the candidate has a sufficiently integrated pattern across four axes:

1. **Orientation:** an organising sacred or ultimate focus;
2. **Formation:** disciplines that reshape desire, conduct, or understanding;
3. **Transmission:** durable communal or textual practices;
4. **Release:** a promised transformation from a diagnosed human predicament.

No axis is by itself a universal necessary condition. Together they provide a defensible profile.
Buddhism scores strongly on all four. A momentary patriotic ceremony does not. Cārvāka is a harder
case because it attacks the unseen order and liberative scheme found in the classical examples.

### Objection, answer, remainder

**Objection:** Replacing God with an “ultimate focus” merely hides God behind abstract language.

**Answer:** Similar practical importance does not confer personality, will, creative power, or
providence. A doctrine can orient life without becoming an agent.

**Remainder:** The profile still requires judgement at the edges. It is a reasoned test, not a
mechanical definition.

### Revision notes

1. Specify that the disputed necessity is normally a personal creator.
2. A non-creating Lord is not identical to a creator.
3. A liberated exemplar is achieved perfection, not cosmic authorship.
4. An impersonal ultimate need not possess will.
5. Non-theism and atheism answer different questions.
6. Agnosticism concerns knowledge.
7. A substantive definition can exclude by stipulation.
8. A functional definition can become too inclusive.
9. Smart’s dimensions are a comparative profile, not seven individually mandatory tests.
10. Use orientation, formation, transmission, and release as a disciplined answer profile.
11. Functional importance does not establish ontological identity.

### Local practice

Which opening move gives the cleanest answer to “religion without God”?

A. Clarify whether “God” means a personal creator and state a cross-cultural test of religion.
B. Assume that every sacred object is a creator.
C. Treat agnosticism and atheism as synonyms.
D. Define religion as any strongly held commitment.

**MCQ 1: A**

- **A — Correct:** It prevents equivocation about God and supplies a criterion that can be applied to actual traditions.
- **B — Incorrect:** Sacred status, exemplary perfection, and world-creation are separate predicates.
- **C — Incorrect:** Withholding knowledge differs from affirming non-existence.
- **D — Incorrect:** Intensity alone would classify nationalism and fandom too easily.

Why is a family-resemblance approach useful here?

A. It proves that all religions have identical doctrines.
B. It permits overlapping religious features without requiring creator-belief in every case.
C. It makes every social institution a religion.
D. It removes the need to defend borderline judgements.

**MCQ 2: B**

- **A — Incorrect:** Family resemblance explains variation rather than doctrinal uniformity.
- **B — Correct:** Membership can rest on a patterned overlap of features instead of one theistic essence.
- **C — Incorrect:** A disciplined profile is still needed to exclude weak analogies.
- **D — Incorrect:** Borderline cases remain contestable and must be argued.

## Lesson 2 — Audit the Work Religion Does

Progress: 2/8 | Stage: Foundation | Subtopic: Deity-dependent goods and non-deity pathways

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Permitted basis: canonical Topic 07 §§1.2, 7.4, 9.1A; Clause 7 demands R07-06–12.
PYQ use: supplies the analysis for 2019 and the “religious life” part of 2021.
Tier ruling: Core, because the necessity claim must be tested function by function.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual — do not make one office perform every task

```text
PERSONAL-AGENCY TASKS                    PATH-AND-ORDER TASKS
petition -> someone hears                meditation -> attention is trained
covenant -> parties relate               vow -> conduct is constrained
grace -> a giver bestows                 ritual -> an act is performed
providence -> an agent guides            community -> memory is transmitted
revelation -> a speaker discloses        liberation -> a condition is transformed

Conclusion: some tasks require a person; the whole religious pattern need not.
```

### 1. The separability test

Instead of asking whether “religion needs God” in one undivided sentence, separate the alleged
functions:

| Religious good | Why personhood matters—or does not |
|---|---|
| Petition | A request is addressed to a possible responder |
| Covenant | Reciprocal commitment presupposes parties |
| Grace as gift | Giving is an act of agency |
| Providence | Purposive guidance belongs to an agent |
| Revelation from God | A disclosure requires a divine discloser by definition |
| Veneration | Worth can be acknowledged without asking for intervention |
| Moral discipline | Compassion, vows, insight, or causal consequences can guide action |
| Ritual | Performance need not be a conversation with a creator |
| Community | Institutions can preserve a non-creator path |
| Liberation | A diagnosis and effective discipline may suffice |

This yields a precise concession: a godless religion cannot preserve petition or grace **in their
original interpersonal senses**. It need not preserve them in order to remain religious.

### 2. Religious life is thicker than attendance

The 2021 question asks about a **life**, not a label. Robust religious life involves repeated
practice, inward appropriation, ethical formation, communal accountability, and an orienting end.
Occasional participation in a family ceremony may be culturally meaningful while falling short of
such a life.

A Buddhist practitioner who cultivates the path, accepts discipline, participates in the Saṅgha,
and directs life toward cessation provides stronger evidence than a person who merely checks a
religious identity box.

### 3. Authority without a commander

Non-theistic traditions distribute authority among teaching, community, causal order, vow, and
scriptural injunction. This does not automatically solve the problem of normativity. It shows only
that “binding” and “commanded by a creator” are not equivalent descriptions.

### Objection, answer, remainder

**Objection:** Practices without a divine “Thou” are therapy or ethics, not worship.

**Answer:** The objection correctly identifies a special interpersonal texture. It does not show
that doctrine, sacred orientation, disciplined transformation, and inherited community disappear.

**Remainder:** Non-theistic religion is not theistic religion with one item erased. Its devotional
shape is genuinely different.

### Revision notes

1. Petition has an addressee.
2. Covenant is reciprocal.
3. Grace as gift presupposes a giver.
4. Divine revelation presupposes a divine speaker.
5. Veneration need not be petition.
6. Ritual can be organised impersonally.
7. Moral formation can arise through vows, compassion, and insight.
8. Community is a vehicle of continuity.
9. Liberation is a generic religious function; grace is one account of it.
10. Religious life requires more than inherited attendance.
11. Removing God redistributes functions rather than simply deleting religion.

### Local practice

Which item most directly depends on a responsive personal reality?

A. Communal memory
B. Ethical self-discipline
C. Petition for freely given help
D. Insight into impermanence

**MCQ 3: C**

- **A — Incorrect:** Institutions and texts can preserve memory without a divine hearer.
- **B — Incorrect:** A vow or training rule may discipline conduct without personal command.
- **C — Correct:** The act is a request whose point includes the possibility of an agent’s response.
- **D — Incorrect:** Insight is a cognitive achievement rather than a reciprocal exchange.

What best distinguishes religious life from occasional cultural attendance?

A. Possession of a religious surname
B. Presence at one annual festival
C. Agreement that religion has social value
D. Sustained formation through practice, commitment, community, and an orienting end

**MCQ 4: D**

- **A — Incorrect:** An inherited name establishes neither belief nor disciplined participation.
- **B — Incorrect:** A single ceremony can express heritage without constituting a way of life.
- **C — Incorrect:** Sociological approval is compatible with complete personal non-participation.
- **D — Correct:** The combination displays an enduring practical orientation rather than a nominal affiliation.

What follows from the separability of religious functions?

A. Creator-belief may be unnecessary for religion even though some interpersonal goods require God.
B. Every function formerly attributed to God is meaningless.
C. Non-theistic and theistic devotion have identical structures.
D. Moral obligation becomes impossible.

**MCQ 5: A**

- **A — Correct:** It preserves the category-wide counterexample while conceding genuinely deity-dependent practices.
- **B — Incorrect:** Petition and providence remain intelligible inside personal theism.
- **C — Incorrect:** The absence of reciprocal divine agency changes the devotional relation.
- **D — Incorrect:** Non-theistic systems offer alternative, though contestable, grounds of discipline.

## Lesson 3 — Buddhism: A Causal Path Instead of a Cosmic Maker

Progress: 3/8 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Full statement and evaluation for the 2024 question

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Permitted basis: canonical Topic 07 §§2, 9.2, 9.2A; Clause 7 demands R07-13–19.
PYQ use: directly owns the 2024 twenty-marker and supports the 2018–2021 necessity questions.
Tier ruling: Core, because Buddhism is the strongest historical counterexample.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual — the Buddhist answer is a treatment sequence

```text
CONTACT WITH A CONDITIONED WORLD
                |
                v
        dukkha is diagnosed
                |
       craving + ignorance traced
                |
        dependent arising mapped
                |
   ethics + meditation + wisdom practised
                |
        conditions of dukkha cease
                |
             nirvana

No stage needs an originating creator or final divine judge.
```

### 1. State the religious structure

The Four Noble Truths provide the grammar of problem, origin, cessation, and way. The Eightfold Path
organises wisdom, ethical conduct, and mental discipline. Intentional karma gives conduct causal
significance. The Saṅgha carries teaching and discipline across generations. Nirvāṇa names the
liberating end.

This is not merely an ethical code. It links a diagnosis of ordinary existence to a specialised
practice, communal transmission, and a final transformation.

### 2. Why a creator is not a missing premise

Dependent origination explains events through conditions. For Buddhism’s governing question—how
suffering arises and ceases—the relevant explanatory work is done by causal dependence, craving,
ignorance, and their removal. A first maker would not add a necessary therapeutic instruction.

This is an argument about **redundancy within the Buddhist project**, not a universal proof that a
creator is impossible.

### 3. Three textual cautions

1. Devas and Brahmā figures may occur within saṃsāra. Their existence would not make them eternal
   creators or final liberators.
2. DN 1 treats a creator-Brahmā claim as arising from ignorance of prior conditions.
3. The undeclared questions include issues such as whether the world is eternal and what happens to
   a Tathāgata after death. They are not an official list suspending judgement about a creator.

The poisoned-arrow illustration teaches urgency: remove suffering before demanding answers that do
not advance release. It does not deductively establish atheism.

### 4. Evaluation grid

| Challenge | Buddhist resource | Open pressure |
|---|---|---|
| What authorises morality? | precepts, compassion, intention, karmic consequence, communal correction | trans-life karma remains philosophically disputed |
| Who is liberated without a permanent self? | causally connected continuity need not be an eternal substance | the identity of the subject of release still invites analysis |
| Is there worship? | recollection, refuge, veneration, and later devotion | assistance in some Mahāyāna settings complicates a purely self-effort picture |
| Is Dharma a disguised God? | Dharma lacks creative will, personhood, and providential agency | functional centrality can still invite theological comparison |

Mahāyāna devotion to Buddhas and bodhisattvas refutes the slogan that non-theism means absence of
devotion. Such figures can be revered or invoked without becoming a single omnipotent first maker.

### 5. Twenty-mark judgement

Buddhism defeats the thesis that every religion requires a personal creator because it is an
historically developed pattern of doctrine, discipline, community, experience, and release. It
does not establish that karma or nirvāṇa is true, nor does it preserve petition, covenant, or
creator-grace unchanged.

### Revision notes

1. Buddhism addresses dukkha through a causal-therapeutic scheme.
2. Dependent origination replaces no deity; it performs a different explanatory role.
3. Karma concerns intentional action and consequence.
4. Four Noble Truths supply diagnosis and route.
5. Eightfold Path combines conduct, attention, and understanding.
6. Saṅgha makes the path socially durable.
7. Nirvāṇa is a liberating end, not a creator.
8. Devas are conditioned beings.
9. DN 1 and the undeclared questions must not be conflated.
10. The poisoned arrow supports priority, not atheistic proof.
11. No-self creates a continuity question, not a creator requirement.
12. Mahāyāna devotion qualifies, rather than cancels, Buddhist non-theism.

- **A — Incorrect:** The doctrine does not need a global denial of every divine being.
- **B — Correct:** The causal map completes Buddhism’s practical explanation without a world-making agent.
- **C — Incorrect:** The community transmits discipline; it does not originate reality.
- **D — Incorrect:** A liberating goal is not thereby a willing person.

C. To prioritise release over speculation that does not alter the cure

- **A — Incorrect:** Refusing delay is not a semantic theory about all metaphysical language.
- **B — Incorrect:** The illustration does not decide the world’s temporal status.
- **C — Correct:** Its force is practical and soteriological: treatment should not wait upon irrelevant answers.
- **D — Incorrect:** The parable neither confirms nor installs a creator.

- **B — Incorrect:** A counterexample does not absorb other traditions into itself.

- **D — Incorrect:** Buddhist redundancy is not a formal contradiction in creator-theism.

## Lesson 4 — Three Indian Designs beyond Creator-Centred Religion

Progress: 4/8 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Jainism, Sāṃkhya, and Yoga

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Permitted basis: canonical Topic 07 §§6A, 9.3; Clause 7 demands R07-20, R07-21, R07-26.
PYQ use: provides comparative support for 2018, 2020, and 2021.
Tier ruling: Core, because the alternatives must not collapse into one undifferentiated “atheism.”
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual — same absence, different machinery

| System | What binds? | What orders the world? | Religious response | End |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jainism | karmic matter binds *jīva* | beginningless plurality of souls and matter | vows, non-injury, austerity, veneration | liberated *siddha* |
| Sāṃkhya | confusion of *puruṣa* and *prakṛti* | evolution of *prakṛti* through the guṇas | discrimination | *kaivalya* |
| Yoga | affliction and mental fluctuation | Sāṃkhya framework with a special *puruṣa* | disciplined Yoga and optional Īśvara-orientation | *kaivalya* |

### 1. Jainism: perfection without government

Jainism retains souls, karmic accretion, ethical vows, austerity, and liberation while rejecting a
creator. A *siddha* is a soul that has attained freedom; it does not administer the universe.
Veneration directs the practitioner toward an achieved ideal rather than toward a deity expected to
rewrite causal law.

Two anti-creator pressures are especially useful:

- A perfect being seems to lack an unmet motive for creating.
- Causal action upon matter appears to demand instruments; embodiment would limit a creator, while
  bodiless action requires further explanation.

These arguments create pressure. They do not settle the complete proofs-for-God debate.

### 2. Sāṃkhya: liberation through discrimination

Classical Sāṃkhya explains manifestation through *prakṛti* and the guṇas in relation to plural
*puruṣas*. Bondage is misidentification; liberating knowledge distinguishes consciousness from
nature. The milk-and-calf analogy is used to make purposive-looking activity by a non-conscious
principle conceivable.

The analogy is illustrative, not demonstrative. A critic may still ask how unconscious nature
produces ordered outcomes.

### 3. Yoga: neither ordinary theism nor simple godlessness

Yoga accepts Īśvara as a special *puruṣa*, untouched by affliction, action, and karmic deposit.
Īśvara-praṇidhāna can assist concentration. Yet classical Yoga’s religious practice and liberation
do not straightforwardly require a creator who makes *prakṛti*.

Thus Yoga is a **qualified non-creator case**, whereas Sāṃkhya is more directly non-theistic.

### Objection, answer, remainder

**Objection:** Unconscious karma or *prakṛti* cannot explain fitting outcomes or apparent purpose.

**Answer:** These systems reject the assumption that order must resemble an artefact planned by a
mind. They model regularity as intrinsic to the relevant causal structure.

**Remainder:** Whether impersonal order is ultimately adequate remains an open metaphysical dispute.

### Revision notes

1. Jain *jīva* is bound by karmic matter.
2. Non-injury and vows structure Jain formation.
3. A *siddha* is liberated, not creative.
4. Jain veneration is not ordinary petition.
5. The motive argument targets divine perfection.
6. The instrument argument targets bodiless action.
7. Sāṃkhya employs *prakṛti* and plural *puruṣas*.
8. Discriminative knowledge ends misidentification.
9. The milk analogy does not prove non-conscious design.
10. Yoga’s Īśvara is a special *puruṣa*.
11. Yoga retains devotional assistance without straightforward creatorhood.

### Local practice

D. It grants exemptions from vows.

- **A — Incorrect:** Jain order is not periodically remade by perfected souls.
- **B — Correct:** Exemplarity and veneration do not entail authorship or governance.
- **C — Incorrect:** *Prakṛti* belongs to Sāṃkhya, not Jain cosmology.
- **D — Incorrect:** The liberated state does not operate by discretionary suspension of discipline.

B. Every natural process is divine action.
C. Apparent purposiveness can be conceived without a conscious planner.
D. Matter is unreal.

- **A — Incorrect:** Conscious planning would defeat the analogy’s intended use.
- **B — Incorrect:** The example is offered to reduce, not establish, dependence on a designer.
- **C — Correct:** It provides a model of orderly contribution by a non-conscious process.
- **D — Incorrect:** Sāṃkhya treats *prakṛti* as real.

## Lesson 5 — Mīmāṃsā: Sacred Authority without a Divine Speaker

Progress: 5/8 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Injunction, intrinsic validity, delayed fruit, and intelligent allocation

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Permitted basis: canonical Topic 07 §9.7; Clause 7 demands R07-22–25.
PYQ use: central comparative evidence for the 2020 prerequisite question.
Tier ruling: Core, because it separates several jobs usually bundled into God.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual — the unbundled architecture

```text
QUESTION                         MIMAMSA LOCATION
Who wrote the sacred text?  ->   no person: apauruseya Veda
How is duty known?          ->   codana, the Vedic injunction
Why trust cognition?        ->   prima-facie intrinsic validity
How can a past rite bear
fruit later?                ->   postulated apurva
Who chooses the recipient?  ->   disputed: law-like link or intelligent allocator?
```

### 1. Authorlessness is not revelation

Classical Mīmāṃsā calls the Veda *apauruṣeya*: it is not authored by a human or divine person.
Therefore its authority is not grounded in the reliability of a revealer.

Jaimini’s formula, *codanā-lakṣaṇo ’rtho dharmaḥ*, identifies dharma through injunction. The point
relevant here is that duty is disclosed by the Vedic imperative, not issued as a creator’s command.

### 2. Be precise about intrinsic validity

*Svataḥ-prāmāṇya* is a thesis about **cognitions**: cognition begins with prima-facie standing and is
defeated when later knowledge exposes error. It is inaccurate to say that an injunction is a
“self-validating cognition.” Textual authority and the epistemic status of cognition must be kept
distinct.

### 3. Why *apūrva* is introduced

The rite finishes now; the promised result is remote. If the vanished act cannot directly produce
that result, Mīmāṃsā posits a generated, unseen capacity linking performance and fruit. That link is
*apūrva*. It is explanatory postulation, not perceptual discovery.

Kumārila places emphasis on a potency associated with act or agent. Prabhākara gives priority to
the injunction’s *niyoga* or *kārya*—the obligatory “to-be-done.” The first is closer to a mechanism
of result; the second foregrounds duty.

### 4. Nyāya’s allocator challenge

Nyāya can ask how an unconscious link matches the right fruit, person, and time. Mīmāṃsā may answer
that fixed relations need not deliberate: determinate causation can be law-like.

Neither side wins by definition. The critic can call *apūrva* an opaque placeholder; the defender
can charge that an intelligent superintendent adds more ontology than the causal bridge requires.

### Revision notes

1. *Apauruṣeya* means without personal authorship.
2. It should not be described as divine revelation.
3. *Codanā* discloses dharma through injunction.
4. Intrinsic validity belongs to cognition.
5. Defeat explains how error is later recognised.
6. *Apūrva* answers a temporal gap in ritual causation.
7. The link is postulated, not observed.
8. Kumārila stresses potency.
9. Prabhākara stresses obligation.
10. Nyāya demands intelligent allocation.

## Lesson 6 — Cārvāka: Challenge the Bridge to the Unseen

Progress: 6/8 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Perception, universal concomitance, supersensible entities, and replies

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Permitted basis: canonical Topic 07 §9.6; Clause 7 demands R07-27–32.
PYQ use: directly owns the 2025 ten-marker.
Tier ruling: Core, because the examiner asks for an epistemological critique, not a lifestyle slogan.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual — where the inference is challenged

```text
observed smoke beside observed fire
                 |
                 v
claim: "wherever smoke, there fire"
                 |
        how is the universal known?
        /             |              \
 perception       inference        testimony
 finite cases     needs another     not independently
 only             universal rule    authorised
        \             |              /
                 hidden conditions?
                 |

Can the rule reach God, soul, karma, rebirth, apurva?
```

### 1. Perception-first epistemology

The strict Cārvāka position admits perception as the valid means of knowledge and rejects entities
that cannot be perceptually warranted: God, an immaterial surviving soul, karmic residue, rebirth,
heaven, ritual potency, and liberation. Its reported ontology recognises earth, water, fire, and air;
ether is excluded as imperceptible.

The 2025 answer should begin with *pramāṇa*, not with the popular verse about borrowing ghee. The
surviving slogans are doxographic reports preserved by opponents.

### 2. The *vyāpti* problem

Inference needs an invariant relation between a sign and what it indicates. Finite perception gives
co-occurrences, not every unobserved case. If another inference establishes the universal relation,
that supporting inference itself needs a universal relation, producing regress or circularity.
Testimony cannot help a theory that denies it independent authority.

An *upādhi* is a limiting condition that has gone unnoticed. Wet fuel qualifies the rash
generalisation **fire produces smoke**; it is not a counterexample to **smoke indicates fire**.
The larger sceptical point is that unknown restrictions may survive repeated observation.

### 3. Religious applications

- A world-to-maker inference inherits the uncertainty of its universal bridge.
- *Dehātmavāda* identifies the living person with the embodied organism.
- The fermentation example illustrates a property emerging from combined ingredients.
- *Svabhāva* attributes regularity to things’ own nature rather than to design.
- Karma, rebirth, and *apūrva* are rejected as imperceptible explanatory additions.

The emergence analogy makes bodily consciousness conceivable; it is not modern neuroscience.

### 4. Replies and qualified defence

**Self-refutation:** A universal rejection of inference is not itself perceived.

**Practical inconsistency:** Ordinary action relies on expectations about unobserved outcomes.

**Nyāya response:** repeated observation, absence of counterinstances, and *tarka* can establish
reliable concomitance.

**Cārvāka options:** The critique may function dialectically, exposing the opponent’s proof without
asserting a global thesis. A fragmentarily reported Purandara strand permits ordinary empirical
inference while denying inference to what cannot be perceptually checked.

### 5. Is this a religion without God?

The reported school does not provide a comparable sacred discipline, post-mortem liberation, or
devotional community. It is therefore a limiting critic, not the leading positive case. This
historical observation does not prove that every possible religion requires unseen entities;
religious naturalism remains a separate disputed proposal.

### Revision notes

1. The 2025 question is about epistemic warrant.
2. Strict Cārvāka privileges perception.
3. *Vyāpti* is the universal bridge required by inference.
4. Particular observations do not survey all cases.
5. Inferential proof of inference risks regress.
6. *Upādhi* means an unnoticed restricting condition.
7. Wet fuel qualifies fire-to-smoke.
8. *Dehātmavāda* rejects a separate surviving soul.
9. Fermentation illustrates emergence.
10. *Svabhāva* offers order without a maker.
11. Global anti-inference faces self-refutation.
12. *Prasaṅga* supplies a dialectical reading.
13. Purandara is a reported, not uniform, school position.
14. Cārvāka is a boundary critic rather than an established godless religion.

## Lesson 7 — Agnosticism: What Can Practice Survive without Knowledge?

Progress: 7/8 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: Huxley, weak and strong forms, faith, participation, Kant, and Spencer

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Permitted basis: canonical Topic 07 §§4, 9.5A; Clause 7 demands R07-33–36.
PYQ use: directly owns both halves of the 2023 ten-marker.
Tier ruling: Core, because definition alone answers only half of the printed question.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual — knowledge, belief, and practice can diverge

```text
KNOWLEDGE CLAIM          BELIEF / HOPE             PRACTICE
"I know God exists"      assent / hope / suspense  prayer / meditation / rites
       |                        |                         |
agnosticism directly     may be withheld, weak,     may continue, change,
limits this column       or cautiously retained     or become impossible
```

### 1. Huxley’s restraint

Huxley introduced “agnostic” in 1869. The central method is refusal to claim knowledge beyond
adequate grounds. Applied to God, this does not itself assert non-existence.

- **Open or weak agnosticism:** the matter is not presently known.
- **Strong agnosticism:** the matter cannot be known in principle.

The second thesis bears a heavier burden because it makes a claim about every possible route to
knowledge.

### 2. How agnostics can relate religion and God

| Agnostic posture | Religious possibility | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|
| Suspended assent | meditation, ethics, inherited ritual, inquiry | attendance may be shallow rather than committed |
| Hope without claimed knowledge | cautious theistic participation | some prayers seem to require actual trust in an addressee |
| Strong unknowability | reverence toward mystery or an impersonal ultimate | calling the unknown “God” may conceal disagreement |
| Non-creator practice | full participation in a Buddhist path | this is religious through the path, not through agnosticism itself |

Agnosticism is not a religion. It can coexist with religious life when some independent pattern of
practice, community, and orientation remains.

### 3. Bounded contrasts

Kant rejects speculative knowledge of God while allowing practical faith through moral reasoning.
That is not simple suspension of belief. Spencer’s “Unknowable” makes a stronger limit claim but
does not provide evidence for a personal creator. Huxley’s evidential caution should not be turned
into approval of confident faith despite inadequate evidence.

### Objection, answer, remainder

**Objection:** Religious commitment requires trust, while agnosticism refuses commitment.

**Answer:** It refuses or limits a knowledge claim; it need not cancel every ethical, contemplative,
or communal commitment.

**Remainder:** Practices built around direct address to God fit agnosticism less easily than
meditation, disciplined inquiry, or non-creator traditions.

4. Strong agnosticism says unknowable in principle.
5. Strong unknowability requires reasons.
6. Huxley’s method is evidential restraint.
7. Knowledge, belief, and practice need not coincide.
8. Practice without assent can be sincere.
9. Mere attendance is not automatically religious commitment.
10. Hopeful faith differs from knowledge.
11. Kant permits practical faith after limiting speculative reason.
12. Spencer’s Unknowable is not a creator proof.
13. Agnosticism can accompany religion but does not constitute it alone.

### Visual — courtroom structure

```text

          |
          v

          |
```

### 1. Construct the denial

The religious theist may argue:

1. **Definition:** Religion is an I–Thou relation with the personal transcendent; removing the Thou
   changes the category.
2. **Worship:** Reverence for an idea or exemplar is admiration, not worship in the proper sense.
3. **Obligation:** Commands bind in a way that impersonal regularities do not.
4. **Salvation:** Radical bondage requires forgiveness or grace, not self-administered technique.
5. **Ultimacy:** An impersonal order cannot answer, love, judge, or ground hope.

This is the answer’s starting case in 2019, not a caricature to dismiss in one paragraph.

### 2. Cross-examination

The definition is too narrow if it erases historically substantial traditions solely because they
lack a creator. Buddhist discipline, Jain vows, and Mīmāṃsā ritual show that sacred authority,
ethical formation, inherited practice, and liberation can be organised differently.

The worship objection wins a partial concession. Petition and reciprocal covenant require a
personal partner. Veneration and refuge need not. The obligation objection shifts the burden:
compassion, vow, injunction, and karma each require defence, but none is identical to divine
command. The salvation objection applies strongly to grace-centred traditions, not automatically to
every account of liberation.

### 3. Necessity logic for the three related PYQs

| Stem | What must be established |
|---|---|
| 2018: justify | criterion + actual case + objection answered |
| 2020: prerequisite | one defended counterexample defeats universal necessity |
| 2021: religious life | show sustained practice and formation, not only category membership |

Creator-belief may be central, widespread, or sufficient for many religions without being necessary
for all religion.

### 4. Final Core verdict

> Religion without a personal creator is both coherent and actual where an integrated tradition
> orders life through sacred orientation, formation, transmission, and release. A creator remains
> indispensable to specifically personal forms such as petition, covenant, providence, and grace.
> The result is a differentiated judgement, not the claim that God is irrelevant.

- Seven owned questions are answerable without Lessons 9–10.
- Buddhism supplies the primary positive case.
- Jainism and Mīmāṃsā provide independent comparative routes.
- Cārvāka supplies the epistemic limit.

- The theist’s personal-relation objection receives a real concession.

knowingly withhold belief? Narratives can transform without deception, but inherited believers may
reject the revision as a different activity.

### 3. Indian graded truth is not make-believe

Advaitic worship at the empirical level is treated as valid within that level and later sublated.
Buddhist conventional practice likewise need not be self-conscious pretence. These graded-truth
models preserve operative sincerity and should not be relabelled as Western fictionalism.

### 1. Four negative diagnoses

Feuerbach reads theology as projected human excellence. Marx sees religion as consolation in a
damaged social world and as a possible mask of material conditions. Freud interprets religion
through wish-fulfilment and a magnified father-image. Nietzsche’s death-of-God announcement diagnoses
the loss of credibility of the Christian-metaphysical horizon and the resulting danger of nihilism.

A causal explanation of belief is not yet a refutation. It becomes a defeater only when connected
to unreliability, contradiction, or independent evidence of falsity.

### 2. Reconstruction after removal

Comte’s Religion of Humanity preserves calendars, ceremonies, solidarity, and reverence while
redirecting them toward humanity. Secular spirituality and humanistic ritual similarly imitate
some religious forms.

The boundary question remains: is communal admiration enough, or must the practice also reshape
life in relation to an ultimate concern and a diagnosed condition? The four-axis profile requires
more than moral approval or public ceremony.

### 3. Nietzsche’s special caution

“God is dead” is neither a biological report nor a deductive atheistic proof. It announces a
cultural crisis of inherited value. Revaluation and self-overcoming attempt a response, but the
standard by which new values escape arbitrariness remains disputed.

Indian non-theistic traditions should not be narrated as responses to Europe’s post-Christian loss
of faith. Their conceptual histories are different.

# ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS MODEL PRACTICE

## Original 10-marker

**Question:** Why does denial of a creator not by itself establish either religion or irreligion?
Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer**

Denial of a creator is one proposition about the architecture of reality; religion is a patterned
way of orientation and practice. The same denial can therefore occur in different forms.

Buddhism retains a diagnosis of *dukkha*, dependent origination, intentional karma, ethical and
meditative discipline, the Saṅgha, and nirvāṇa. Jainism retains souls, karmic bondage, vows, and
liberated exemplars. Their non-creator position belongs inside a sacred-liberative structure.

Cārvāka, by contrast, rejects God together with an immaterial soul, karma, rebirth, ritual efficacy,
and liberation. Its reported philosophy chiefly challenges the epistemic warrant for the
supersensible. It is not thereby a positive religion.

Thus creator-denial is neither sufficient for religion nor sufficient for irreligion. Classification
depends upon what else remains: sustained orientation, transformative discipline, transmission,
and an end. Modern religious naturalism tests whether these features can be retained without any
unseen order, but it requires an independent argument rather than a conclusion borrowed from
Cārvāka.

## Original 15-marker

**Question:** Examine the claim that removing God redistributes religious functions instead of
destroying religion. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer**

In creator-centred theism, one divine person may author revelation, command duty, hear prayer,
govern history, grant grace, and save. The claim of redistribution separates these functions and
asks whether alternative institutions or causal schemes can perform some of them.

Mīmāṃsā provides the clearest analysis. Vedic authority is *apauruṣeya*, so no divine speaker is
required. *Codanā* discloses dharma. Cognition has prima-facie validity rather than awaiting divine
certification. *Apūrva* is postulated to link ritual performance and delayed fruit. Nyāya objects
that an unconscious potency cannot allocate a fitting result; Mīmāṃsā replies with law-like
determination, though the postulate remains contestable.

Buddhism distributes the functions differently. Dependent origination and karma map conditioned
suffering; the Four Noble Truths diagnose it; the Eightfold Path trains conduct and understanding;
the Saṅgha transmits the discipline; nirvāṇa is release. No creator performs an omitted step.

The thesis has limits. Petition needs a hearer, covenant needs reciprocal parties, providence needs
purposeful guidance, and grace-as-gift needs a giver. Their disappearance is a genuine change, not
an illusion. Therefore God’s functions are substantially separable, but not interchangeable.
Religion can survive through a different arrangement of authority, practice, community, and
liberation, while specifically interpersonal theistic goods cannot be preserved without a personal
deity.

## Original 20-marker

**Question:** “Religion without God is coherent only if religion is neither defined by God nor
reduced to social usefulness.” Critically discuss with Indian examples. Answer in about 350 words.

**Model answer**

The statement identifies two opposite errors. A substantive definition that makes relation to a
personal creator essential excludes Buddhism before considering it. A purely functional definition
that counts every source of solidarity or meaning admits nations, clubs, and ideologies. A better
test examines an integrated pattern of ultimate orientation, formation, transmission, and release.

Buddhism strongly meets that test. Dependent origination and intentional karma explain the arising
of suffering; the Four Noble Truths and Eightfold Path provide diagnosis and training; the Saṅgha
maintains continuity; nirvāṇa gives direction. Devas remain conditioned, and DN 1’s treatment of a
creator-Brahmā is separate from the undeclared questions. Mahāyāna devotion shows that non-theism
does not entail an absence of worship.

Jainism offers another architecture: *jīva* is bound through karmic matter, vows and non-injury form
the practitioner, and a *siddha* is an attained exemplar rather than a creator. Classical Sāṃkhya
locates bondage in misidentification of *puruṣa* and *prakṛti*, while Yoga adds a special *puruṣa*
without making cosmic creation the indispensable route to *kaivalya*.

Mīmāṃsā is the strongest ritual case. Authorless Veda supplies authority, injunction discloses
dharma, and *apūrva* is posited for delayed fruit. Its vulnerability is the Nyāya question whether
an unconscious link can allocate results.

Cārvāka clarifies the boundary. Its attack on *vyāpti* challenges God, soul, karma, and rebirth, but
its reported doctrine lacks the sacred-liberative pattern of the positive cases. Creator-denial
alone is therefore insufficient.

The theist can still claim that petition, grace, covenant, and providence require a personal
ultimate. This is correct about those goods, not about religion universally. Religion without a
creator is coherent and actual; religion defined as any useful commitment is not.
