#### Evidence ledger — establish the bounded regional claim

The map shows why “the North-East insurgency” is an unsafe singular. ✅ MHA's 23 April 2026 paper says that, compared with 2014, insurgency incidents in the region fell 82% in 2025, security-force casualties 90% and civilian deaths 97%. A sound answer asks four questions: **who is the actor, what is the demand, what instrument is operating, and what is its verified stage?** Identity claims may concern language, customary law, territory, land, representation or resources. Some are pursued constitutionally; some acquire armed organisations; some produce conflict among communities rather than between a group and the State.

### Visual first — classify claim and method separately

```text
POLITICAL CLAIM
├─ autonomy within India
├─ separate State within India
├─ secession from India
└─ inter-community security claim

METHOD
├─ election / protest / litigation / negotiation
└─ organised armed coercion → insurgency
```

#### Comparison matrix debrief — separate political end from armed method

✅ **Insurgency** is a sustained organised armed political challenge seeking influence over authority, people or territory. **Separatism** describes an intended political end; it does not by itself prove an armed method. **Autonomy** redistributes authority within the Constitution. **Ethnic conflict** concerns insecurity or violence between communities and can occur without an underground organisation.

#### Misconception pre-emption — one label cannot select every remedy

The two-axis distinction changes policy. A council demand requires competence, representation and fiscal design. An armed organisation under ceasefire requires monitored camps, weapons rules, prohibition of recruitment and extortion, and a political channel. Inter-community conflict requires civilian protection, justice, return and reconciliation. Calling every identity claim “terrorism” closes democratic channels; excusing coercion as identity expression weakens rule of law and third-party rights.

#### Application — test a ceasefire edge case

An organisation may accept a ceasefire without abandoning its political objective. This is why actor, claim, means and current instrument must all be stated.

### Revision notes

1. Insurgency describes organised armed political method.
2. Separatism describes an end, not necessarily a method.
3. Autonomy reallocates authority within the Constitution.
4. Ethnic conflict may exist without an insurgent group.
5. Demand and method are separate analytical axes.
6. A ceasefire need not erase the political claim.
7. Policy must protect third parties as well as address grievance.
8. Misclassification produces either over-securitisation or permissiveness.

### Concept check

**Question:** [10 marks; answer in no more than 150 words] Distinguish insurgency from autonomy and ethnic conflict. Why does the distinction matter?

**Model answer:** Insurgency is an organised, sustained armed challenge using political violence to influence authority, people or territory. Autonomy is a constitutional distribution of power through devices such as councils or special safeguards; it can be demanded peacefully. Ethnic conflict is violence or insecurity between communities and may exist without an underground programme. Instruments therefore differ. Insurgency requires lawful intelligence, policing and monitored cessation rules; autonomy requires representative, fiscal and jurisdictional design; ethnic conflict requires civilian protection, justice and reconciliation. ⚠️ Treating all three as terrorism suppresses political diagnosis, while treating armed coercion as merely an identity claim weakens public authority. Policy must identify the claim, method, affected communities and competent institution before selecting enforcement, negotiation or accommodation.

**Model: 116 words; ceiling: 150 words; marks: 10.**

**Scoring rubric:** 3 accurate distinctions + 3 policy consequences + 2 named instruments + 2 qualification = 10.

**Misconception to avoid:** Every strong identity claim is not secessionist, and every autonomy movement is not insurgent.

#### Timeline — date the federal changes first

✅ Nagaland became a State in 1963. The North-Eastern Areas (Reorganisation) Act, 1971 reshaped the region from 1972; Mizoram and Arunachal Pradesh became States in 1987. Statehood improved recognition and administrative proximity. ⚠️ It could also create internal minorities, new boundary disputes and fresh claims. The Mizo experience is powerful because negotiation, disarmament, constitutional change and electoral incorporation aligned; its clauses are not a universal template.

#### Checkpoint synthesis — what reorganisation did and did not solve

Statehood and autonomy converted some recognition claims into constitutional politics, but neither erased internal minorities nor guaranteed administrative capacity. The checkpoint prevents a chronology from becoming a claim that boundary change alone produced peace.

#### Evidence ledger — interpret historical distance without determinism

✅ Singh's OCR pages describe colonial isolation and limited freedom-struggle contact. This is a historical interpretation, not cultural determinism. Post-Independence, the Sixth Schedule supplied self-rule, but the 1960 sole-official-language decision intensified fears of “Assamisation” among several hill communities.

### Concept check

**Question:** [10 marks; answer in no more than 150 words] How did federal reorganisation function as conflict management in the North-East?

**Model answer:** Federal reorganisation converted some identity demands into constitutionally recognised political units. The Sixth Schedule first offered district and regional self-government. Nagaland's statehood in 1963, the 1972 formation of Meghalaya, Manipur and Tripura as States, and Mizoram's 1987 statehood widened representation and administrative proximity. This allowed language, customary authority and fear of assimilation to be negotiated institutionally. Mizoram shows the strongest mechanism: accord, disarmament, constitutional follow-through and electoral politics reinforced one another. ⚠️ Reorganisation was not a universal cure. New borders can create internal minorities, inter-State claims and resource disputes. Its security value depends on inclusive design, minority safeguards, fiscal capacity and implementation—not on boundary drawing alone.

#### Constitutional text — begin with Article 371A

⚠️ Territorial or jurisdictional concessions can affect neighbouring States and non-Naga communities. A durable settlement therefore needs representative authority, precise competencies, third-party safeguards, enforceable sequencing and transparent implementation. Security can contain violence; it cannot decide a legitimate final allocation of power.

#### Stakeholder map — identify claimant, signatory and affected resident

The relevant stakeholders are not exhausted by the armed signatory: councils, State institutions, non-signatory factions, residents inside a proposed jurisdiction and neighbouring communities can carry different interests. This is why the unit of analysis must remain agreement-specific.

#### Counterfactual — what surrender figures cannot establish

Official surrender figures are important but bounded to named signatories. This is a design challenge, not an argument against autonomy.

#### Synthesis chain — prevent an accord dump

Use the chain: **grievance → actor → instrument → verified outcome → unresolved implementation**. It prevents an “accord dump” and keeps Bodo, Karbi, Adivasi, Dimasa and ULFA trajectories distinct.

#### Evidence ledger — list drivers only to connect them

✅ Singh's book-period list includes alienation, border proximity, demographic anxiety, corruption, underdevelopment, arms and Myanmar instability. A strong answer explains interaction, not a catalogue.

#### Causal synthesis — alignment, not one cause

**Answer line:** North-East conflicts persist where political recognition, capable governance and credible security guarantees fail to align.

#### Stress test — development without recognition

Underdevelopment is neither necessary nor sufficient for insurgency. Development may reduce isolation and recruitment opportunities, but cannot decide sovereignty, justice or representation. Infrastructure without customary-land consultation, fair compensation or benefit sharing can create a new grievance. #### Constitutional text — read the statutory activation chain

✅ Section 3 authorises a disturbed-area notification where armed forces in aid of civil power are considered necessary. Section 4 provides specified force, arrest and search powers. Section 5 requires handover of an arrested person to the nearest police station with the least possible delay. ⚠️ **Mandatory current-status caution:** any claim that Article 356 is presently operating in Manipur or any other State requires a fresh, date-specific direct Gazette, Parliament or MHA record. Horizontal reconciliation concerns relations among communities contesting land, representation, resources or return. Manipur demonstrates the gap: agreements with particular organisations cannot alone restore inter-community safety. Assam's council settlements require safeguards for residents outside the principal claimant group; a Naga settlement must consider neighbouring communities and States. Horizontal work needs civilian protection, credible justice, local dialogue, women's and victim participation, safe return and minority guarantees. ⚠️ Community bodies add legitimacy but cannot replace impartial police, courts or constitutional government. Vertical bargaining without horizontal peace risks an elite accord and new spoilers; horizontal dialogue without controlling armed coercion lacks security. Durable transformation synchronises both through monitored implementation and lawful politics.

**Model: 123 words; ceiling: 250 words; marks: 20.**

**Scoring rubric:** 4 vertical logic + 4 horizontal logic + 5 evidence + 4 integration + 3 verdict = 20.

# ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS MODEL PRACTICE

## 10-mark practice

**Question:** [10 marks; answer in no more than 150 words] Differentiate constitutional accommodation from conflict appeasement.

**Model answer:** Constitutional accommodation recognises legitimate difference through law-bound institutions; appeasement grants advantage without clear authority, accountability or protection of others. The Sixth Schedule, Articles 371A and 371C, State reorganisation and negotiated councils illustrate accommodation because they define competencies within the Constitution. They become vulnerable to an appeasement critique when powers are vague, coercion determines benefits, minorities are excluded or funds evade audit. The answer is not to reject accommodation but to improve its design through representative negotiation, cessation of violence, precise jurisdiction, minority rights, transparent finance and elections. ⚠️ Lawful security remains necessary against violence and extortion. Durable accommodation integrates difference while maintaining equal citizenship and rule of law.

**Model: 108 words; ceiling: 150 words; marks: 10.**

**Scoring rubric:** 2 definition + 3 evidence + 3 risks/design + 2 verdict = 10.

**Why this earns marks:** It defines both terms, uses constitutional evidence and gives a qualified design test.

## 15-mark practice

**Question:** [15 marks; answer in no more than 250 words] “Signing an accord and settling a conflict are different achievements.” Examine.

**Model answer:** Signature creates formal commitment; settlement requires the commitment to change institutions, incentives and lived security. The 2015 NSCN(IM) Framework Agreement establishes principles but is not a final Naga settlement. Manipur's CoO/SoO arrangements manage violence yet leave political and inter-community questions. Bodo, Karbi, DNLA, Adivasi and pro-talks ULFA settlements specify deeper commitments, and MHA records bounded surrender or disbandment outcomes. These gains do not establish legal creation of councils, fiscal delivery, minority protection or social legitimacy. Implementation needs legislation, elections, audited funds, DDR, victim support and grievance mechanisms. It must manage non-signatory factions and neighbouring communities affected by territory or authority. Mizoram remains instructive because political incorporation and implementation reinforced agreement. ⚠️ Success is non-recurrence, functioning institutions, protected rights and lawful competition. A signature opens a pathway; delivery and acceptance complete it.

**Model: 131 words; ceiling: 250 words; marks: 15.**

**Scoring rubric:** 3 distinction + 4 evidence + 4 implementation + 2 limits + 2 verdict = 15.

**Why this earns marks:** It compares different rungs, uses named cases and evaluates implementation.

## 20-mark practice

**Question:** [20 marks; answer in no more than 250 words] Critically evaluate India's approach to insurgency and peace processes in the North-East.

**Model answer:** India's approach combines calibrated force, ceasefires, political negotiation, asymmetric federalism, rehabilitation and development. Its strength is differentiation. Statehood and the Sixth Schedule supplied early accommodation; Articles 371A and 371C preserve distinct arrangements; Bodo, Karbi, DNLA, ULFA, NLFT/ATTF, TIPRA and FNTA instruments address different constituencies. MHA's April 2026 update reports major regional reductions in incidents and casualties, indicating security gains. Time-bound ceasefires create negotiating space, while AFSPA notifications use differentiated footprints.

Limits remain. Frameworks and ceasefires may persist without final settlement; implementation evidence is uneven; negotiated benefits may exclude minorities; border restrictions may burden kinship and trade; unlawful force erodes intelligence and legitimacy. Manipur shows that vertical agreements cannot substitute for horizontal reconciliation, protection and justice. Development improves access but cannot resolve status or representation alone.

Reform requires clause-wise public monitoring, stronger DDR and victim support, plural participation including women, conflict-sensitive connectivity and continuous rights review. ⚠️ Durable peace is not fewer incidents plus more signatures; it is legitimate institutions managing conflict without armed coercion.

**Model: 164 words; ceiling: 250 words; marks: 20.**

**Scoring rubric:** 4 architecture + 5 achievements/evidence + 5 limitations + 4 reforms + 2 verdict = 20.

**Why this earns marks:** It is critical rather than celebratory, evidence-linked and ends with a measurable peace standard.

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

## First principles

- North-East conflicts are State-, community-, faction- and stage-specific.
- Insurgency is an armed method; separatism an end; autonomy constitutional authority-sharing.
- Regional decline in violence does not prove political closure.
- Always write actor → demand → instrument → status → implementation.

## Historical-federal spine

- Colonial distance contributed to identity insecurity but did not predetermine conflict.
- Sixth Schedule was an early autonomy response.
- 1960 language politics sharpened assimilation fears.
- Nagaland: 1963; Meghalaya, Manipur, Tripura: 1972; Mizoram and Arunachal Pradesh: 1987.
- Statehood offers recognition but may generate internal-minority and boundary issues.

## State map

- **Nagaland:** Article 371A; 2015 NSCN(IM) framework; parallel ceasefires; final settlement unresolved.
- **Assam:** Bodo 2020, Karbi 2021, Adivasi 2022, DNLA and ULFA 2023.
- **Manipur:** Article 371C; ZUF 2022, UNLF 2023, UPF/KNO 2025 term only to 3 September 2026 in cited record.
- **Tripura:** NLFT(SD) 2019; TIPRA and NLFT/ATTF 2024.
- **Meghalaya:** ANVC 2014; council/boundary questions remain distinct.
- **Arunachal Pradesh:** current selective AFSPA footprint, not a uniform regional profile.

## Drivers

- Identity: language, land, customary authority, representation and recognition.
- Governance: corruption, weak services and parallel taxation.
- Development: opportunity and access, but not a substitute for political settlement.
- Resources: land and benefit disputes can intensify identity conflict.
- Border: sanctuary, arms and narcotics alongside lawful kinship and trade.
- In July 2012, violence beginning in Kokrajhar spread to Chirang and Dhubri and displaced more than 4.85 lakh people; provocative SMS circulation amplified fear and contributed to a wider exodus panic.

## Development-governance architecture

- DoNER began as a department in 2001 and became a ministry in 2004.
- MDoNER coordinates and monitors regional development; sector ministries retain implementation responsibility.
- NEC is a statutory regional body under the NEC Act, 1971, amended in 2002.
- The current cited report records 10% GBS monitoring, PM-DevINE, NESIDS and Schemes of NEC.
- NLCPR was restructured into NESIDS; older Special Category/NLCPR descriptions require fiscal-vintage qualification.
- Sanction, release, physical completion, functionality and social outcome are separate stages.
- Development spending supports peace only when access, land safeguards, fairness and trust improve.

## Constitutional architecture

- Sixth Schedule: Articles 244(2)/275(1), specified areas in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram.
- Article 371A: named Nagaland safeguards.
- Article 371C: Manipur hill-area arrangement.
- FNTA agreement, 5 February 2026: commitment to create authority; implementation separately verified.
- Union List Entry 2A enables legislation on Union-force deployment in aid of civil power while State constitutional government remains intact.
- Article 356 addresses failure to carry on State government in accordance with the Constitution and can reallocate executive/legislative functions subject to parliamentary controls.
- Entry 2A deployment, AFSPA notification and Article 356 proclamation are not interchangeable.
- Never state current President's Rule in Manipur or elsewhere without a fresh date-specific Gazette, Parliament or MHA record.

## AFSPA

- Sections 3, 4, 5 and 6: notification, powers, handover and prosecution sanction.
- 1997 judgment: constitutionality with safeguards.
- EEVFAM: no absolute immunity.
- From 1 October 2026: differentiated Nagaland, Manipur and Arunachal Pradesh footprints.
- De-notification is not repeal; necessity does not eliminate accountability.
- Irom Sharmila's 2000–2016 fast is a bounded legitimacy illustration; it does not alone settle the statute's legality or operational case.

## Peace ladder and dated map

- Ceasefire/SoO/CoO → framework → MoS → implementation → consolidation.
- 2014 ANVC; 2015 NSCN(IM); 2019 NLFT(SD); 2020 Bru/Bodo; 2021 Karbi; 2022 Adivasi/ZUF; 2023 DNLA/UNLF/ULFA; 2024 TIPRA/NLFT-ATTF; 2025 UPF-KNO; 2026 FNTA.
- Do not describe expired time-bound instruments as current.

## Implementation

- Security: weapons, recruitment, extortion and camps.
- Political: law, authority, elections and minority protection.
- Fiscal: releases, audit and delivery.
- DDR: weapons, command dissolution, livelihoods and acceptance.
- Social: victims, return, women, trust and reconciliation.
- Durable peace means disputes recur without armed remobilisation.

## Final answer lines

- Accommodation and coercion are parallel but actor-specific tracks.
- Vertical armed-group peace must be paired with horizontal community peace.
- Connectivity is useful only when land and benefit governance are fair.
- **Verdict:** durable peace is differentiated accommodation under law, backed by precise security, inclusive institutions and verifiable delivery.
