### Visual first — compare purpose, source and proof

| Question | Money laundering | Terror financing |
|---|---|---|
| Core wrong | Handling proceeds of crime to project legitimacy | Raising, providing or collecting value for terrorist purpose |
| Source | Necessarily linked to criminal proceeds | May be lawful or unlawful |
| Concealed feature | Criminal origin or ownership | Destination, beneficiary or intended use |
| Typical legal route | PMLA and scheduled offence | UAPA offences/listing controls; PMLA where crime proceeds are laundered |
| Evidentiary focus | proceeds → transaction → possession/use | funder → knowledge/intention → beneficiary/use |

### Definition gate — origin is not purpose

✅ PMLA treats money-laundering as involvement with proceeds of crime and projecting or claiming them as untainted property. ✅ UAPA Section 17 covers raising, providing or collecting funds from legitimate or illegitimate sources with the specified terrorist knowledge or purpose. A lawful salary donation can therefore become terror financing without first becoming “dirty money.”

### Overlap map — when both offences arise

Crime proceeds may be laundered and then diverted to a terrorist network. Conversely, a network may collect small lawful donations and move them through ordinary accounts without a classic placement stage. ⚠️ “Clean-clean” finance is difficult because neither generation nor first transfer necessarily carries an illegal marker; intent and network context become decisive.

### Ownership boundary

Topic 02 owns terrorism’s wider recruitment and operational architecture; Topic 11 owns organised crime and narcotics as full ecosystems. This topic owns the financial source-channel-control chain.

### Revision notes

1. Money laundering conceals criminal proceeds.
2. Terror financing supplies value for terrorist purpose.
3. Terror funds may have lawful origin.
4. Laundering and financing can overlap without being identical.
5. Origin is central to PMLA; purpose and knowledge are central to UAPA financing offences.
6. “Clean-clean” finance may lack an obvious predicate marker.
7. The same transaction may trigger several legal and intelligence chains.
8. Financial disruption is not proof of guilt.
9. General terrorism belongs mainly to Topic 02.

### Concept check

**Question:** [10 marks; answer in no more than 150 words] Why is the proposition “terror financing is merely a form of money laundering” legally and analytically unsafe?

**Model answer:** Money laundering and terror financing overlap but have different organising ideas. Laundering concerns proceeds generated by crime and their concealment, possession, use or projection as untainted property. Terror financing concerns value raised, provided or collected for a terrorist purpose and may begin with lawful income. Hence a small lawful donation knowingly routed to a terrorist organisation may constitute financing without a classic placement-layering-integration sequence. Conversely, laundered fraud proceeds need not fund terrorism. The distinction changes proof and intervention: PMLA follows proceeds of a scheduled offence, while UAPA addresses fund-raising and listing-linked controls. Agencies should coordinate transaction, identity and intelligence evidence, but courts must still establish the elements of each offence.

**Model: 110 words; ceiling: 150 words; marks: 10.**

**Scoring rubric:** 3 definitions + 3 lawful/unlawful-source distinction + 2 legal consequence + 2 conclusion = 10.

**Misconception to avoid:** Lawful origin does not immunise money knowingly supplied for a terrorist purpose.

### Visual first — source is only the first node

SOURCE               CHANNEL                 USE
state support ─┐      cash courier ─┐         weapons/logistics

### Evidence ledger — book-period variation

✅ Singh’s book-period account differentiates external sponsorship and donation routes in Jammu and Kashmir from extortion or parallel taxation prominent in North-East and Left-Wing Extremist settings. It also identifies narcotics, counterfeit currency, organised crime and front entities as possible sources or facilitators. These examples are historical typologies, not proof of a current named network.

### Diagnostic sequence

Start with five questions: **who generates value, how is it moved, where is it stored, who controls it, and what is it used for?** The same source can use several channels, and one channel can serve lawful and unlawful customers. Hawala is a settlement mechanism; cash is an instrument; a charity is an organisational form; none proves terrorist purpose without corroboration.

### Counterfactual test

If authorities stop one remittance corridor, does the network lose resources or merely substitute cash, trade mispricing, mule accounts or virtual assets? ⚠️ Effective disruption raises cost, delay and detection risk across the portfolio rather than celebrating one seizure.

### Revision notes

2. Funding models vary by threat ecosystem.
3. External support is not the only source.
4. Extortion can create recurring local revenue.
5. Crime proceeds may require laundering before use.
6. Donations and business income may look lawful at origin.
7. Counterfeit currency may finance activity and damage monetary confidence.
8. A channel is not itself proof of terrorist purpose.
9. Substitution risk follows narrow enforcement.
10. Book-period group claims must not be stated as current networks.

### Concept check

**Question:** [10 marks; answer in no more than 150 words] Construct a source–channel–use framework for analysing terror finance without stereotyping a region or financial instrument.

**Model answer:** Analysis should begin with the actor and value source: external sponsorship, extortion, crime proceeds, donations or legitimate business income. It should then identify movement through cash, hawala, formal banking, trade, front entities or virtual assets; storage in accounts, commodities, wallets or businesses; control by intermediaries and beneficial owners; and final use for logistics, recruitment, propaganda or violence. Each claim needs transaction, identity and contextual evidence. A historical regional pattern cannot prove a present network, and use of hawala, charity or cryptocurrency cannot by itself prove terrorism. The framework should also test substitution: closing one channel matters only if the network’s total cost, speed, secrecy and access are impaired.

**Misconception to avoid:** Every suspicious financial channel is not a terrorist-financing channel.

## Lesson 3: Placement, layering and integration — useful but incomplete

Progress: 3 / 20 | Stage: Foundation | Subtopic: The classic laundering model and its limits

━━━ PRE-TEACH CHECKLIST ━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Book context: queried — Basic owner; Singh PDF pp. 91–92
CA search: "site:fatf-gafi.org money laundering stages placement layering integration limitations"
CA found: FATF standards are risk- and outcome-based rather than dependent on a mandatory three-stage sequence
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### Visual first — model and exceptions

```text
CLASSIC: cash → PLACEMENT → LAYERING → INTEGRATION → apparently lawful wealth

EXCEPTIONS:
direct asset purchase ───────────────► concealment/use
trade mispricing ─────► value transfer without bank placement
lawful terror funds ──► collection/use without criminal-origin cleaning
digital transfer ─────► rapid layering across services and borders
```

### Build the model

✅ Placement introduces criminal proceeds into a financial or commercial system. ✅ Layering uses transactions, conversions, accounts or assets to obstruct tracing. ✅ Integration returns value in apparently legitimate form. The model helps organise an answer and locate controls.

### Stress test — sequence is not an element checklist

⚠️ Real conduct may compress, repeat or bypass stages. Cash can buy property directly; shell entities can disguise ownership from the beginning; TBML moves value through manipulated commerce; virtual assets can combine conversion and layering. Terror financing may involve lawful-origin funds and therefore no “dirty-money placement.”

### Decision rule

Use placement-layering-integration as a heuristic, then name the actual transaction mechanism and evidence. Never acquit analytically merely because all three stages are not visible.

### Revision notes

1. Placement introduces criminal proceeds.
2. Layering obstructs tracing.
3. Integration creates apparently legitimate usability.
4. The stages may overlap or repeat.
5. Some schemes bypass cash placement.
6. TBML moves value through trade representations.
7. Virtual assets can compress stages.
8. Lawful-origin terror finance may not fit the model.
9. The model guides investigation; it is not a universal factual sequence.
10. Actual transactions and statutory elements control the case.

### Concept check

**Question:** [10 marks; answer in no more than 150 words] Evaluate the usefulness and limitations of placement–layering–integration in analysing illicit finance.

**Model answer:** Placement, layering and integration provide a clear heuristic. They show where criminal proceeds enter a system, how transactions obscure origin or ownership, and how value returns in apparently legitimate form. Controls can then be matched to stages: cash reporting and customer due diligence, transaction and beneficial-ownership analysis, and scrutiny of assets or businesses. However, the sequence is not universal. Trade mispricing transfers value through invoices, direct purchases may bypass placement, digital services may compress several stages, and lawful-origin terror finance may require concealment of destination rather than cleaning of origin. Investigators should therefore use the model to form hypotheses, not substitute it for transaction tracing and statutory proof.

**Model: 108 words; ceiling: 150 words; marks: 10.**

**Scoring rubric:** 3 stages + 3 control value + 3 limitations + 1 verdict = 10.

**Misconception to avoid:** Failure to identify three neat stages does not disprove laundering or terror finance.

### Visual first — channel comparison

| Channel | Concealment advantage | Investigative opportunity | Main false-positive risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bulk cash/courier | little account trail | seizure, travel, fingerprints, CCTV | cash-intensive lawful activity |
| Hawala | settlement outside matching bank transfer | communications, ledgers, counterparties | remittance communities treated as suspect |
| Formal banking | scale and speed | KYC, STR, account and wire records | alert mistaken for offence |
| Mule accounts | distance from controller | device, beneficiary and cash-out graph | exploited account holder |
| Commodity/value store | portable wealth | purchase, ownership and resale trail | ordinary investment |

### Scenario — one payment, several ledgers

A sender pays a hawaladar in one city; an associate pays the recipient elsewhere while obligations are later netted through trade, cash or banking. No matching cross-border transfer need appear. Yet communications, cash deposits, travel, device links and settlement accounts may create corroboration.

### Formal-system paradox

Formal finance can be abused, but it also creates customer, device, beneficiary and transaction data. ⚠️ De-risking every customer linked to a risky geography may drive activity into less visible channels. Targeted enhanced due diligence is generally more informative than blanket exclusion.

### Revision notes

1. Hawala transfers value through linked settlement obligations.
2. A matching bank transfer may not exist.
3. Cash reduces account trails but creates physical evidence.
4. Formal banking offers scale and an auditable trail.
5. Mule accounts separate controller from named holder.
6. STRs are intelligence inputs, not findings of guilt.
7. Channel substitution follows blanket closures.
8. Geography alone is not suspicion.
9. Enhanced due diligence should follow assessed risk.

### Concept check

**Question:** [10 marks; answer in no more than 150 words] Compare hawala and formal banking as terror-finance channels from an investigator’s perspective.

**Model answer:** Hawala can move value through brokers and later settlement without a matching bank transfer between sender and recipient. This obscures the payment path, but communications, broker ledgers, cash deposits, travel and counterparties may expose the network. Formal banking offers rapid scale and layering through accounts, yet creates KYC, device, beneficiary, wire and transaction-monitoring records. The investigator should combine financial and non-financial evidence rather than infer guilt from channel choice. Blanket exclusion of communities or remittance corridors can displace activity into cash and weaken visibility. A risk-based response uses targeted enhanced due diligence, graph analysis and lawful information sharing while protecting legitimate customers and testing who ultimately controlled and used the value.

**Model: 111 words; ceiling: 150 words; marks: 10.**

**Scoring rubric:** 3 hawala mechanics + 3 formal-system comparison + 2 evidence + 2 proportional response = 10.

**Misconception to avoid:** Hawala leaves no evidence; it leaves different evidence.

### Visual first — invoice-value gap

```text
REAL GOODS / SERVICE VALUE: 100
DECLARED VALUE:             160
                              │
                              └─► 60 transferred through apparent trade

Other patterns: under-invoice • multiple invoice • phantom shipment
                over/under-shipment • false quality/description
```

### Mechanism ledger

✅ TBML disguises criminal proceeds or moves value through trade transactions. Over-invoicing can justify excess payment abroad; under-invoicing can transfer value to the importer; multiple invoicing seeks several payments for one shipment; false description, quantity or quality manipulates valuation.

### Evidence triangle

Customs data alone is rarely enough. Compare invoice, shipping document, payment, insurance, tax, related-party ownership, market price and physical examination. ⚠️ Price variation may reflect quality, timing, transport or contract terms; anomaly is a lead, not proof.

### Institutional handoff

Customs/DRI may detect valuation or trade anomalies; banks see payment patterns; FIU-IND can link reports; police, NIA or another agency supplies predicate/terror context; ED follows proceeds under PMLA. No single database establishes the whole offence.

### Revision notes

1. TBML transfers value through trade misrepresentation.
2. Over-invoicing can justify excess outward value.
3. Under-invoicing can leave value with the foreign buyer.
4. Multiple invoicing seeks repeated settlement.
5. Phantom or misdescribed trade hides the real transaction.
6. Market-price variation is not automatically crime.
7. Related ownership can reveal control.
8. Customs, bank and ownership data must be reconciled.
9. TBML is distinct from ordinary smuggling.
10. An anomaly becomes evidence through corroboration.

### Concept check

**Question:** [15 marks; answer in no more than 250 words] Explain TBML and design an evidence-led response that avoids treating every valuation difference as laundering.

**Model answer:** Trade-based money laundering moves or disguises value through apparently legitimate commerce. Techniques include over- or under-invoicing, multiple invoices, false descriptions, phantom shipments and mismatches in quantity or quality. Detection begins with anomalies, but price divergence may also reflect grade, timing, freight, insurance or contract terms. Authorities should therefore reconcile customs declarations, shipping and insurance documents, bank payments, tax records, market benchmarks, beneficial ownership and physical examination. Related-party transactions and repeated counterparties deserve contextual scrutiny. Customs or DRI can identify trade anomalies; reporting entities and FIU-IND connect payment patterns; the predicate-offence agency establishes criminal or terrorist context; ED traces proceeds under PMLA. International requests may be needed for foreign invoices and ownership. The goal is a corroborated value-transfer narrative, not an algorithmic presumption based on price alone.

**Model: 126 words; ceiling: 250 words; marks: 15.**

**Scoring rubric:** 4 techniques + 4 evidence design + 4 institutional chain + 3 qualification = 15.

**Misconception to avoid:** A customs valuation dispute is not automatically TBML.

### Case trigger

A genuine relief organisation may work in a high-risk location through local partners. The risk lies in diversion, coercion, false beneficiaries or hidden control—not in charitable status itself. Crowdfunding adds speed, reach and fragmented donors, while platform records can improve traceability.

### Proportionality rule

✅ FATF’s revised Recommendation 8 approach is targeted and risk-based. ✅ The 2024 India evaluation called for stronger NPO outreach on terrorist-financing risk. ⚠️ Blanket account closure can suppress lawful civil society, reduce humanitarian access and push transfers underground.

### Safeguard design

Use governance checks, segregated duties, documented partners, sanctions screening, transaction monitoring, audit and a review route. Preserve humanitarian exceptions and reasoned decisions.

Controls should combine governance and partner due diligence, segregated duties, sanctions screening, traceable payments, audit and field verification. Platforms can retain campaign and beneficiary records for crowdfunding. FATF’s Recommendation 8 requires focused, proportionate measures, while the 2024 India evaluation called for better risk outreach. Blanket account closure can obstruct humanitarian work and push finance into informal channels. Reasoned restrictions, humanitarian safeguards and accessible review therefore improve both rights protection and financial visibility.

Virtual assets change speed and investigative technique; they neither guarantee anonymity nor justify treating all users as suspects.

### Evidence ladder

✅ FIU-IND is the central national agency for receiving, analysing and disseminating information relating to suspect financial transactions. A cash report follows prescribed reporting rules; an STR reflects assessed suspicion; neither is a judicial finding.

### Follow-the-money workflow

Begin with identities and accounts, then reconstruct timing, counterparties, devices, beneficial owners, cash points, trade documents and end use. Compare financial chronology with communications, travel, seizures and operational events. Strategic analysis identifies patterns; operational intelligence supports a particular inquiry.

### Feedback problem

⚠️ Weak feedback can produce defensive reporting: many alerts with little prioritisation. Agencies should return typologies and quality signals without exposing investigations or personal data unnecessarily.

### Revision notes

1. FIU-IND receives and analyses prescribed reports.
2. CTR and STR are not synonyms.
3. An STR records suspicion, not guilt.
4. Dissemination begins investigation; it does not finish proof.
5. Financial chronology should be matched with non-financial evidence.
6. Beneficial ownership identifies real control.
7. Strategic and operational analysis serve different purposes.
8. Report quality matters more than volume alone.
9. Feedback improves reporting behaviour.
10. Information sharing must remain lawful and secure.

### Concept check

**Question:** [10 marks; answer in no more than 150 words] Explain why an STR is valuable financial intelligence but insufficient proof of money laundering.

**Model answer:** An STR records a reporting entity’s reasoned suspicion based on transaction pattern, customer profile or contextual indicators. FIU-IND can combine it with other reports, accounts and databases, identify networks and disseminate intelligence to a competent agency. Its value lies in early detection and prioritisation. It remains insufficient proof because unusual conduct may have a lawful explanation, the reporter may lack the full context, and the statutory elements of proceeds of crime, control, knowledge and laundering activity require admissible corroboration. Investigation must connect account and beneficial-owner data with the predicate offence, communications, trade or asset records and end use. Treating suspicion as guilt damages due process and encourages low-quality defensive reporting.

**Model: 110 words; ceiling: 150 words; marks: 10.**

### Statutory anchor

✅ PMLA is the Prevention of Money-Laundering Act, 2002, enacted as Act 15 of 2003 and brought into force on 1 July 2005. Section 3 links laundering to proceeds of crime derived from a scheduled offence. Reporting entities perform due diligence, preserve records and furnish prescribed information; FIU-IND receives financial reports; ED investigates PMLA offences.

| Control point | Statutory text | Judicial interpretation | Operational significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Section 19 arrest | The Director, Deputy Director, Assistant Director or another authorised officer needs material-based “reason to believe,” recorded in writing, that the person is guilty; the person must be informed of the grounds, and the order/material must be forwarded to the Adjudicating Authority in the prescribed manner | *Vijay Madanlal Choudhary* (27 July 2022) rejected the constitutional challenge because Section 19 contains stringent safeguards; *Pankaj Bansal* (3 October 2023) required a copy of written grounds to be furnished henceforth | Arrest is reviewable disruption, not proof of guilt; production before the competent court within the statutory 24-hour rule remains necessary |
| ECIR | PMLA does not create an FIR-equivalent statutory document called an ECIR | *Vijay* held ECIR to be ED's internal document, not equivalent to an FIR, and its supply in every case not mandatory; the arrested person must nevertheless receive the grounds of arrest | Do not claim that ECIR registration, FIR registration, arrest and prosecution complaint are the same procedural event |
| Section 50 inquiry | Authorities may summon persons, compel attendance, evidence and records; persons summoned must attend, state the truth and produce required records | *Vijay* characterised the process as an inquiry concerning proceeds of crime, held Section 48 authorities not to be police officers as such, and held statements recorded by them not hit by Articles 20(3) or 21 | A compulsory statement is an investigative input with legal consequences, but its weight, context and connection to proved proceeds remain matters for adjudication |
| Section 45 bail | Prosecutor gets an opportunity to oppose; if opposed, the court needs reasonable grounds to believe the accused is not guilty and is unlikely to commit an offence while on bail, subject to statutory provisos | *Vijay* upheld the post-2018 form and its twin conditions; *V. ### Property-status discipline

✅ Section 5 permits provisional attachment subject to statutory conditions and reasons. ✅ Section 8 supplies adjudication and later confiscation or release through the statutory process. Attachment restrains property; it does not establish guilt or final State title.

### Practice boundary

⚠️ Predicate investigation by police, NIA, CBI, customs or another competent agency affects the laundering case. ED powers do not cure weak proof of the underlying proceeds, ownership or transaction.

✅ Section 19 requires forwarding the arrest order and supporting material to the Adjudicating Authority and production of the arrested person before the competent court within 24 hours, excluding journey time. Under *Pankaj Bansal*, post-judgment compliance requires furnishing written grounds rather than merely reading or showing them. These safeguards permit the person to challenge the arrest and seek legal advice; they do not require automatic supply of the ECIR.

| Question | FEMA, 1999 | PMLA, 2002 |

| Trigger | Contravention of FEMA, its rules, regulations, notifications, directions, orders or an authorisation condition | Property derived or obtained from criminal activity relating to a scheduled offence plus Section 3 laundering conduct |

✅ ED administers both statutes, but common agency ownership does not merge their triggers, burdens or outcomes. ⚠️ Calling every overseas remittance “money laundering,” or every laundering case a FEMA matter, erases the exact legal element UPSC expects.

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

## Core definitions and distinctions

- Money laundering concerns proceeds of crime; terror financing concerns value supplied for terrorist purpose and may use lawful funds.
- Placement, layering and integration are a heuristic, not a mandatory factual sequence.
- TBML uses invoice, shipment, description or quantity manipulation to move or disguise value.
- Hawala is a broker-settlement mechanism; its use does not itself prove crime.
- Beneficial ownership asks which natural person ultimately owns or controls.
- CTR is a prescribed cash report; STR expresses assessed suspicion.

## Sources and channels

- Sources may include external sponsorship, extortion, crime and narcotics proceeds, counterfeit currency, donations, business income and diverted charitable funds.
- Channels include cash, courier, hawala, accounts, mule networks, trade, commodities, NPOs, crowdfunding, fintech and VDA services.
- Source, channel, controller and use must be proved separately.
- Narrow enforcement can cause substitution; portfolio disruption is the relevant outcome.

## Indian legal and institutional architecture

- PMLA 2002 came into force on 1 July 2005 and depends on proceeds of a scheduled offence.
- Reporting entities conduct CDD, preserve records and furnish prescribed information.
- FIU-IND receives, analyses and disseminates financial intelligence.
- ED investigates PMLA offences and can provisionally attach under statutory conditions.
- Adjudicating Authority review, Special Court adjudication and final confiscation/release must not be collapsed.
- Section 19 arrest requires material-based reasons recorded in writing, communication of grounds, prescribed forwarding of the order/material and production before the competent court within the statutory period.
- ECIR is ED's internal document, not an FIR; *Vijay Madanlal Choudhary* held universal ECIR supply unnecessary, while *Pankaj Bansal* requires written grounds of arrest to be furnished for subsequent arrests.
- Section 50 authorises compulsory attendance, truthful evidence and document production; *Vijay* treated it as a proceeds inquiry and Section 48 authorities as not police officers as such.
- Section 45's post-2018 twin bail conditions remain operative after *Vijay*; Article 21 still controls unreasonably prolonged pre-trial custody.
- FEMA is a primarily civil/adjudicatory foreign-exchange contravention regime; PMLA is a criminal proceeds-of-scheduled-crime regime. Common ED administration does not merge them.
- UAPA Section 17 covers specified fund-raising from lawful or unlawful sources.
- UAPA Section 51A supplies listing-linked asset and service controls.
- NIA or State police investigates terrorist offences; customs/DRI supplies trade and border evidence; RBI and SEBI supervise sectoral preventive controls.

## FATF and cooperation

- FATF standards cover AML, CFT and proliferation financing through preventive, enforcement, transparency and cooperation measures.
- WMD Act Section 12A, inserted by the 2022 amendment, prohibits proliferation financing and enables targeted freezing, seizure, attachment and resource-denial measures.
- Technical compliance asks whether the framework exists; effectiveness asks whether it works.
- Grey list means increased monitoring; call for action is the high-risk category; regular follow-up is a different mutual-evaluation process.
- UN sanctions, Egmont intelligence exchange, MLA and NMFT perform different functions.
- Intelligence exchange cannot automatically substitute for admissible foreign evidence.

## Evaluation and safeguards

- Separate alert, investigation, arrest, freeze, attachment, confirmed restraint, charge, conviction, confiscation and restitution.
- FATF’s India assessment credited several capabilities but prioritised faster ML/TF case completion and risk-based NPO outreach.
- Privacy, correction, due process, inclusion and legitimate humanitarian access are effectiveness variables.
- A strong answer ends with timely lawful disruption plus fair adjudication—not maximal surveillance.
