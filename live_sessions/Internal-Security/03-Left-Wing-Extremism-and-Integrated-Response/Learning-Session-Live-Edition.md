✅ Naxalism takes its name from the May 1967 uprising around Naxalbari in Darjeeling district, associated with Charu Majumdar and Kanu Sanyal. ✅ CPI (Maoist) was formed in 2004 through merger of the People’s War Group and Maoist Communist Centre of India; this is historical organisational context, not proof of current strength.

LWE is a Maoist armed project seeking capture of State power through protracted struggle, rather than a secessionist demand for a separate sovereign State. “Naxalism,” “Maoism” and “LWE” overlap in usage, but answers should define the term used and avoid treating every radical Left belief, tribal protest or land movement as armed extremism.

**Question:** Why is it analytically unsafe to label every radical land protest as LWE? **(10 marks; answer in no more than 150 words.)**

**Model-answer ceiling:** Actual 92 words; governed ceiling **150 words**.

Premature labelling can criminalise grievance, damage intelligence quality and close channels for lawful settlement. ### 🖼️ Five-phase expansion model — visual

```text
PREPARATORY → PERSPECTIVE → GUERRILLA → BASE → “LIBERATED”
 study/control     organise       attack      parallel     displaced
```

The canonical owner describes Maoist “Protracted People’s War” as combining armed insurgency, mass mobilisation and strategic alliances. The five phases are a doctrinal model, not a reliable current map. Real districts may show mixed or reversed characteristics. State response should identify actual capability rather than assume a linear march.

### 🖼️ Ecosystem rings — visual

```text
        armed command and squads
      logistics / intelligence / finance
   coerced support ─ recruited support
 lawful organisations and ordinary residents

Evidence threshold must rise before moving inward.
```

Recruitment can exploit grievance, kinship, fear, prestige, coercion, revenge or livelihood insecurity. An “overground ecosystem” may include couriers, shelter, intelligence, procurement, propaganda and finance, but the phrase becomes dangerous when used to collapse lawful advocacy, journalism or tribal organisation into criminal facilitation.

The correct chain is **person → conduct → knowledge/intent → link → offence**. Residents may provide food or information under duress. Youth may encounter propaganda without joining. Civil groups may criticise operations or land acquisition lawfully. Protection, exit opportunities and evidence-based investigation are therefore as important as disruption.

### Attribution guardrail

An ecosystem assessment should record what is known, inferred and disputed. Labels that cannot be translated into specific conduct and evidence are poor investigative tools.

1. Recruitment can be voluntary, manipulated or coerced.
2. Armed command differs from peripheral support.
3. Logistics, intelligence and finance perform different functions.
4. Lawful advocacy is not armed facilitation.
5. Conduct and intent require evidence.
6. Coerced support needs protection and exit routes.
7. Stigmatisation damages intelligence and trust.
8. Youth prevention should preserve lawful political participation.
9. Topic 11 owns detailed criminal-finance networks.
10. Topic 12 owns detailed investigation architecture.

### Concept check

**Question:** Villagers supplied food after armed threats, while one trader knowingly arranged repeated logistics for payment. Distinguish the governance response. **(15 marks; answer in no more than 250 words.)**

**Model-answer ceiling:** Actual 98 words; governed ceiling **250 words**.

**Model answer:** The villagers’ conduct must be assessed for coercion, immediacy and realistic ability to refuse. They may require protection, confidential reporting and restoration of State access rather than collective prosecution. The trader’s repeated, paid and knowing logistical arrangement presents a different evidentiary pathway: investigators should establish transactions, communications, intent and links through lawful evidence. Both groups need due process, but culpability cannot be inferred from the same outward act of supply. Public messaging should avoid branding the village, while security planning should reduce armed control over markets and transport. Differentiated response improves justice, cooperation and disruption of deliberate support.

**Misconception to avoid:** All material reaching an armed group reflects equally voluntary and culpable support.

The canonical account links LWE intensity in mineral and infrastructure belts to extortion opportunities. Finance supports food, mobility, communications, weapons, recruitment and local coercive administration. It can arise through levies on contractors, mining, transport, forest produce or projects; exact current scale requires dated investigation evidence.

### Continuity dilemma

IED capability combines material, technical knowledge, reconnaissance, concealment, triggering and target selection. Security response should reduce access and opportunity while protecting workers and preserving evidence. Generic claims about explosives, routes or tactics can be operationally unsafe and belong in specialist training, not public study notes.

### Evidence boundary

Finance disruption requires contractor safeguards, transparent payments, reporting protection, financial investigation and prosecution. Road or mining suspension may reward coercion; unprotected project expansion may expose workers. Risk-informed continuity is the middle path.

### 🖼️ Self-reinforcing deprivation loop — visual

```text
governance absence → grievance and low trust → recruitment/coercion
       ↑                                         ↓
blocked roads/services ← extortion and sabotage ← parallel control

Break loop at multiple points: protection + administration + rights + delivery
```

The canonical owner makes a crucial distinction: underdevelopment can help produce vulnerability, while Maoist organisation can deliberately maintain it by obstructing roads, electricity, water and administration. Poverty is therefore neither a sufficient explanation nor a passive background.

Governance deficit includes vacant posts, inaccessible offices, delayed justice, corruption, weak panchayats, missing records and officials unable to stay in remote areas. Armed groups may exploit this absence through taxation, dispute settlement, propaganda and punishment. The State must restore lawful presence without reproducing extraction or humiliation.

Root causes and operational capability interact. Better services alone cannot neutralise armed coercion; security operations alone cannot make administration legitimate. The holding phase—resident policing, revenue, welfare, health, education and grievance systems—links the two.

### REVISION NOTES

1. Poverty alone does not mechanically produce LWE.
2. Organisation converts grievance into coercive mobilisation.
3. Development obstruction can be deliberate strategy.
4. Extortion benefits from project and mineral activity.
5. Governance deficit includes absence, delay and corruption.
6. Parallel authority competes through coercion, not consent alone.
7. Security enables access but does not equal governance.
8. Service delivery needs rights and accountability.
9. Holding capacity connects clearing and development.
10. Root-cause and operational metrics should remain separate.

### Concept check

**Question:** A road project is completed, but officials visit villages only in escorted monthly camps and grievances remain unresolved. Explain why the governance loop persists. **(15 marks; answer in no more than 250 words.)**

**Model-answer ceiling:** Actual 99 words; governed ceiling **250 words**.

**Model answer:** The road improves physical access but has not created routine, trusted administration. Monthly camps make the State episodic; residents still lack predictable records, welfare, justice and appeal. Escort dependence may be necessary temporarily, yet it signals incomplete holding capacity. The district should phase in resident or frequent multidisciplinary teams, local recruitment, protected digital and physical records, fixed grievance timelines and monitored service standards. Police should secure access and respond to coercion without controlling civil decisions. Community feedback should test whether people can use the road and offices safely. Infrastructure breaks the loop only when lawful institutions become continuously accessible.

**Adjacent rubric:** Governance diagnosis 4 + hold-capacity distinction 4 + institutional measures 4 + outcome test 3 = **15 marks**

### Convergence test

Procurement should therefore follow a defined task, local testing, human verification, cybersecurity, fallback and outcome audit. Technology multiplies competent institutions; it does not replace them.

**Adjacent rubric:** Capability uses 4 + implementation limits 4 + adaptation/governance 4 + reasoned verdict 3 = **15 marks**

# ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS MODEL PRACTICE

## Original 10-mark question

**Question:** Explain why LWE cannot be reduced either to poverty or to policing alone. **(10 marks; answer in no more than 150 words.)**

**Model-answer ceiling:** Actual 91 words; governed ceiling **150 words**.

**Model answer:** Poverty and exclusion create vulnerability but do not automatically produce armed extremism. Maoist organisation actively converts grievance into recruitment, extortion, coercion and parallel authority, and may obstruct development that threatens its control. Policing is necessary to protect people and lawful access, yet operations alone cannot resolve land, forest, justice, service and trust deficits. India’s official approach therefore combines security, development and rights of local communities. The durable end-state is low armed capability plus functioning, accountable administration. Treating only poverty ignores organisation; treating only violence ignores the conditions that make coercion sustainable.

**Adjacent rubric:** Dual reduction critique 4 + organisational variable 2 + integrated response 2 + end-state 2 = **10 marks**

## Original 15-mark question

**Question:** Assess clear–hold–develop as a framework for integrated LWE response. **(15 marks; answer in no more than 250 words.)**

**Model-answer ceiling:** Actual 111 words; governed ceiling **250 words**.

**Model answer:** Clear–hold–develop distinguishes three capabilities. Security forces disrupt imminent armed coercion and open access; ordinary police and civil administration then hold the area through persistent lawful presence; infrastructure, rights, livelihoods and services develop durable alternatives. Its strength is sequencing: roads or claims processes cannot function under intimidation, while tactical gains reverse if institutions do not arrive. Its limit is a falsely linear reading. Rights, accountability, grievance and essential services must operate during security phases, and protection may continue during development. Thresholds should track violence, network capability, staff presence, service use, FRA/PESA quality and trust. The framework succeeds when exceptional deployment tapers into capable constitutional governance, not when territory is merely declared cleared.

**Adjacent rubric:** Framework explanation 5 + strength 3 + limitation/rights 4 + metrics/end-state 3 = **15 marks**

## Original 20-mark question

**Question:** Critically evaluate India’s current LWE position and propose a consolidation strategy. **(20 marks; answer in no more than 250 words.)**

Strategy should preserve targeted intelligence and inter-State coordination; transition surge forces against capability thresholds; sustain roads, telecom, banking and services; and strengthen FRA/PESA, justice and grievance delivery. Surrender policy needs protection, livelihood and recidivism tracking. District averages should be replaced by block-level security, governance, rights and trust ledgers. Independent audits must test displacement and metric gaming. The goal is not literal zero risk but irreversible loss of coercive parallel authority and durable constitutional governance.

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

## 1. Concept and history

- Naxalism began around Naxalbari in May 1967.
- LWE is a Maoist armed project to capture State power, not secession.
- CPI (Maoist) formed in 2004 through merger of PWG and MCCI.
- Historical organisational detail does not establish present capability.

## 2. Causation and political economy

- Structural causes include land/forest exclusion, displacement, unemployment, service and justice gaps.
- Organisation converts vulnerability into armed mobilisation.
- Mineral and infrastructure activity can create extortion opportunity.
- Development obstruction can deliberately preserve coercive control.
- Root cause, trigger, organisation and operational capability need separate evidence.

## 3. Geography and networks

- “Red Corridor” is a historical analytical term, not a current fixed map.
- MHA reported zero affected districts in March/April 2026.
- Thirty-seven Legacy & Thrust districts and one District of Concern retained support in July 2026.
- Inter-State boundaries and trijunctions can enable displacement.
- District classification is not root-cause resolution.

## 4. Integrated response

- The 2015 National Policy combines security, development and rights/entitlements.
- SAMADHAN adds leadership, strategy, training, intelligence, dashboards, technology, theatre plans and finance denial.
- Clear, hold and develop are distinct capabilities.
- Rights and accountability operate throughout the sequence.
- District convergence is more important than national scheme totals.

## 5. Institutions and capability

- States lead police and public order; the Union supplements with policy, CAPFs and schemes.
- Local police and district administration supply persistent knowledge and governance.
- Intelligence must be actionable, verified and lawfully shared.
- Special forces require ordinary police, investigation and civil handover.
- Topic 12 owns full institutional detail.

## 6. Development and rights

- FRA and PESA are central to lawful forest and Scheduled Area governance.
- Enactment or approval counts do not prove fair implementation.
- MHA reported 15,194 km of LWE-specific roads by August 2026.
- Approximately ₹4,289.20 crore SCA had been released since 2017.
- PIB reported more than 9,600 mobile towers in June 2026.
- Infrastructure must be usable, inclusive, maintained and protected.

## 7. People, rehabilitation and trust

- Civilians face violence, coercion, extortion, displacement and service denial.
- Poorly accountable State action can also impose costs.
- Surrender is an event; rehabilitation is a multi-year outcome.
- Protection, livelihood, documents, health and community acceptance matter.
- Trust grows through fair administration, verified communication and grievance.

## 8. Current status and evaluation

- MHA reported 218 incidents and 93 deaths in 2025: declines of 89% and 91% from 2010.
- PIB described India as “effectively free from Naxalism” from 31 March 2026.
- The qualification does not mean zero risk or completed grievance resolution.
- Security, governance/rights and reintegration need separate ledgers.
- Durable success means loss of coercive parallel authority plus trusted constitutional governance.
