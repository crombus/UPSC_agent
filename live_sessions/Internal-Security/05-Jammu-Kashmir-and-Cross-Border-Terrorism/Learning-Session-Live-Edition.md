✅ The canonical owners describe J&K militancy as a historically prominent case of externally sponsored proxy violence involving groups such as Lashkar-e-Taiba, Jaish-e-Mohammed and Hizbul Mujahideen. Those names are historical illustrations, not a timeless current order of battle.

Deniability serves several purposes: it raises the cost of attribution, permits calibrated escalation, creates multiple fronts and mixes external direction with local grievances. ⚠️ Analysis must therefore distinguish the sponsor, armed organisation, operative, voluntary facilitator and coerced civilian. ### Why attribution quality changes strategy

Weak attribution encourages indiscriminate blame and escalatory error. Strong attribution supports targeted disruption, diplomatic presentation, prosecution and proportionate response. Confidence should be graded, sources corroborated and uncertainty stated.

### 🖼️ Infiltration kill-chain and interruption points — visual

```text
selection → training → launch support → border/LoC crossing
                                      ↓
terrain guide → safe house → transport → weapon/finance pickup
                                      ↓
local reconnaissance → target selection → attack → exfiltration/merger

Interrupt at: intelligence | surveillance | route denial | guide network |
              logistics | finance | communications | community warning
```

Infiltration is not a single fence-crossing event. It is a chain requiring preparation, route knowledge, weather and terrain exploitation, communication, reception and onward logistics. LoC sectors have military characteristics; International Border sectors involve a different deployment environment. Topic 05 owns the terror mechanism; Topic 06 owns full border-management design.

✅ MHA's Annual Report 2024–25 describes multi-tier deployment along the International Border, LoC and changing infiltration routes; fencing, technology, improved intelligence, operational coordination and proactive action within the Union Territory. The list shows why a barrier-only answer is incomplete.

### Metric guardrail

| Metric | Correct use | Common error |
|---|---|---|
| attempt detected | pressure on the system | equating it with successful entry |
| successful infiltration estimate | possible network reinforcement | treating estimate as exact census |
| encounter | operational contact | treating all encounters as infiltrations |
| weapon/drone recovery | delivery effort interrupted | assuming every drop reached a cell |
| quiet firing sector | reduced immediate border harm | declaring proxy activity ended |

Launch support can include staging, reconnaissance, guides, covering fire, forged identity, communications and reception arrangements. ⚠️ The exact form varies; doctrine should focus on functions rather than assume one permanent route.

### Interruption sequence

1. fuse military, police and technical indicators without erasing source caveats;
2. protect likely routes and communities without immobilising civilian life;
3. separate immediate pursuit from later evidence collection;
4. track displaced routes after each interdiction;
5. measure downstream network weakness, not seizures alone.

### REVISION NOTES

1. Infiltration is a chain, not only a crossing.
2. Launch support precedes physical movement.
3. Reception networks connect the border to the hinterland.
4. LoC and International Border environments differ.
5. Topic 06 owns detailed border infrastructure.
6. Attempts and successful infiltration are different metrics.
7. Covering fire and infiltration can interact but are not identical.
8. Technology complements human and terrain intelligence.
9. Route denial may displace rather than eliminate movement.
10. Downstream network weakness is the real outcome test.

### Concept check

**Question:** A sector reports fewer detected crossings but more weapon recoveries fifty kilometres inland. Diagnose three plausible explanations and the next intelligence task. **(15 marks; answer in no more than 250 words.)**

**Model-answer ceiling:** Actual 102 words; governed ceiling **250 words**.

**Model answer:** First, routes may have shifted outside the monitored sector, making the decline a detection artefact. Second, earlier infiltrators or local cells may be drawing from pre-positioned caches, so current crossing counts do not explain present capability. Third, drones, couriers or cross-border smuggling may be substituting for person-based infiltration. Analysts should geolocate recoveries, compare weapon batches and communication signatures, map transport and safe-house links, and test whether recoveries reflect better policing rather than greater supply. Border and hinterland units need a shared hypothesis ledger. The aim is to identify the delivery architecture; neither lower detections nor higher recoveries alone proves improvement or deterioration.

**Adjacent rubric:** Three hypotheses 6 + intelligence tasks 4 + metric caution 3 + integrated conclusion 2 = **15 marks**

**Misconception to avoid:** Fewer detected crossings necessarily mean fewer externally supplied weapons inside the theatre.

### 🖼️ Recruitment and facilitation spectrum — visual

| Position | Typical condition | State response |
|---|---|---|
| target of propaganda | exposure without action | credible information and resilience |
| vulnerable youth | grievance, isolation or status search | prevention, education and mentoring |
| coerced helper | threat and low ability to refuse | protection and differentiated liability |
| knowing facilitator | shelter, guide, courier or finance with intent | evidence-led investigation |
| armed operative | participation in violence | lawful operational and criminal response |

An OGW is not a legal shortcut for any non-combatant whom authorities distrust. Facilitation may involve reconnaissance, shelter, transport, communication, recruitment, propaganda, finance or procurement, but each allegation requires evidence of conduct and intent. The same outward act—providing food, for example—may reflect voluntary support, ordinary commerce or coercion.

Recruitment can combine political grievance, humiliation, kinship, fear, thrill, revenge, online influence and material inducement. No single profile is sufficient. Prevention therefore requires credible local policing, education, family support, grievance access, digital literacy and protection for those resisting coercion.

✅ The canonical advanced owner records a book-period post-2008 shift toward crowd mobilisation and stone-pelting as an information and legitimacy tactic. That historical observation shows strategic adaptation; it must not be converted into an unsupported claim about every protest or the current toolkit.

### Attribution guardrail

NIA's March and June 2025 releases described searches concerning alleged logistics, infiltration support, hybrid operatives, finance, drones and digital evidence. Search and seizure are investigative steps. They justify study of the ecosystem; they do not justify branding a community or presuming guilt.

### REVISION NOTES

1. OGW is a functional allegation, not a social identity.
2. Conduct and intent must be proved.
3. Coercion changes culpability and response.
4. Lawful dissent is not logistical support.
5. Recruitment has multiple pathways.
6. Family and community protection can reduce vulnerability.
7. Evidence-led targeting preserves intelligence cooperation.
8. Search and seizure do not equal conviction.
9. Indiscriminate labelling strengthens hostile narratives.
10. Prevention and prosecution address different points in the pathway.

### Concept check

**Question:** Two shopkeepers repeatedly supplied the same armed group; one acted under threats to his family and the other arranged coded deliveries for payment. Design differentiated responses. **(15 marks; answer in no more than 250 words.)**

**Model-answer ceiling:** Actual 109 words; governed ceiling **250 words**.

**Model answer:** Both cases require evidence preservation, but their intent and freedom to choose differ. The coerced shopkeeper should receive immediate threat assessment, family protection, confidential reporting and a realistic exit route; investigators should test whether any conduct exceeded compulsion. The paid organiser requires financial, communication and delivery evidence, lawful search and prosecution where intent is established. Authorities should avoid public identification that exposes witnesses or brands the market. A protected reporting channel and secure supply alternatives can reduce future coercion. The response should punish deliberate facilitation, protect coerced civilians and deny the armed group control over everyday commerce. Equal treatment of unequal circumstances would be both unjust and operationally damaging.

**Adjacent rubric:** Intent/coercion distinction 4 + coerced-person response 4 + deliberate-facilitator response 3 + community protection 2 + objective 2 = **15 marks**

The Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019 created the two Union Territories from the appointed day and excludes public order and police from the J&K Assembly's legislative competence. This creates a Lieutenant-Governor/Union-centred security chain even with an elected Assembly.

Notification is not implementation. A statute allocates authority; it does not prove intelligence fusion, police capacity, grievance resolution or trust. Likewise, revocation of President's Rule and formation of an elected government change representative governance without converting J&K into an ordinary State security chain.

⚠️ Operational advantages may include shorter vertical coordination and clearer Union responsibility. Risks include blurred political accountability, poor civil-security consultation and local perceptions of distance. The remedy is not to deny the statute but to design transparent coordination, defined roles and measurable delivery.

### 🖼️ Mission-chain swimlane — visual

| Stage | Primary contribution | Required handover |
|---|---|---|
| border/LoC warning | Army, BSF and technical sources by sector | time, route and confidence |
| hinterland prevention | J&K Police, intelligence and local administration | suspect/network picture |

Coordination fails when information is shared without confidence levels, when tactical units disturb evidence, when agencies duplicate witnesses, or when no owner follows finance and digital trails. A common room is not coordination unless it changes decisions.

### Handover protocol

```text
warning received
  → threat graded and owner named
  → operation authorised with command boundary
  → scene secured and civilians protected
  → evidence/custody log opened
  → network leads assigned
  → after-action gaps owned and retested
```

The Army's role at the LoC, CAPF support, J&K Police's local knowledge and investigative continuity, and NIA's central investigation role in assigned cases are complementary. Topic 12 owns their complete legal mandates. Here the exam focus is the seam between warning, force and proof.

### Debrief from a coordination failure

If one agency protects its source by withholding all context, another may deploy blindly. If every detail is shared indiscriminately, sources may be exposed. The solution is graded access: share what a recipient needs for action, preserve originator caveats and create a mechanism for rapid clarification.

### REVISION NOTES

1. Intelligence, operations and investigation are different functions.
2. A lead is not admissible proof.
3. Tactical success can damage prosecution if evidence is mishandled.
4. Confidence and source caveats should travel with intelligence.
5. Local police provide continuity and community context.
6. Central agencies add specialised and inter-State capability.
7. Command must be explicit before action.
8. Victim and civilian protection belong in the operational plan.
9. Graded access balances utility and source protection.
10. After-action learning needs named owners and retesting.

### Concept check

**Question:** A high-confidence intelligence alert reaches three agencies, but each assumes another will verify the safe house. Build a four-step protocol that prevents both delay and evidentiary contamination. **(15 marks; answer in no more than 250 words.)**

**Model-answer ceiling:** Actual 102 words; governed ceiling **250 words**.

**Model answer:** First, the receiving coordination node should name one operational lead, one verification lead and a deadline, while recording source confidence and caveats. Second, the operational lead should prepare proportionate surveillance or intervention with civilian-safety and command rules. The NIA chargesheet in the Pahalgam case provides a later investigative account; it should not be projected backwards as if every detail was known on day one. This is the core time-discipline of crisis analysis.

### REVISION NOTES

1. Life safety and scene integrity begin together.
2. Victim liaison is an operational responsibility.
3. Early public certainty can outrun evidence.
4. Attribution confidence should be recorded.
5. Communal protection prevents attack multiplication.
6. Tactical defence and strategic response have different timelines.
7. Agencies need one network-lead allocation board.
8. Emergency measures require review dates.
9. Later chargesheets should not rewrite what was known initially.
10. Strategic credibility depends on evidence and proportionality.

### Concept check

**Question:** Within six hours of a civilian attack, officials receive conflicting claims of responsibility and public demands for mass detention. Draft the decision priorities. **(20 marks; answer in no more than 250 words.)**

**Model-answer ceiling:** Actual 109 words; governed ceiling **250 words**.

**Model answer:** First protect survivors, secure hospitals and likely follow-on targets, preserve the scene and establish family liaison. Create one verified-facts cell that records competing claims, source confidence and unresolved questions; public communication should state only confirmed information and counter communal rumours. Tactical pursuit may proceed on actionable intelligence with command and civilian-safety rules. Assign forensic, digital, finance and logistics leads across agencies without duplicate witness contact. Record emergency restrictions with geographic scope, expiry and review. Strategic attribution and external response should follow corroborated evidence, while immediate protection does not wait for complete proof.

### 🖼️ Evaluation scorecard — visual

| Dimension | Indicator family | Counterfactual question |
|---|---|---|
| hostile capability | attacks, recruitment, supply, regeneration | what would likely occur without intervention? |
| civilian security | deaths, displacement, fear and continuity | did risk fall for all groups? |

### REVISION NOTES

1. Inputs, outputs, capabilities and outcomes are different.
2. Evaluation needs a counterfactual.
3. District averages can hide local deterioration.
4. Security gains may displace across geography or vector.
5. Civilian harm must be independently measured.
6. Ordinary institutional capacity is a normalisation indicator.
7. Trust surveys need privacy and credible sampling.
8. Rehabilitation outcomes require longitudinal tracking.
9. Tapering should be reversible and threshold-based.
10. Durable peace means low coercive capability plus legitimate institutions.

### Exit-and-reversal protocol

```text
baseline → quarterly multi-pillar review → independent challenge
   ↓
threshold met for sustained period?
   ├─ no → adapt support and diagnose failure
   └─ yes → taper exceptional support in stages
               ↓
          monitor rebound indicators
               ├─ stable → continue transition
               └─ rebound → predefined restoration
```

Evaluation should compare places, periods and interventions without pretending random assignment is always possible. Interrupted trends, matched areas, process tracing and independent case audits can strengthen inference. Definitions must be stable or changes disclosed.

The desired transition is not from security to no security. It is from exceptional, force-heavy dependence toward capable ordinary policing, civil administration, representative governance, justice and resilient communities, with specialised support retained against defined risks.

### Concept check

**Question:** Design a three-year evaluation of whether reduced terrorist incidents reflect durable capability loss rather than temporary suppression or displacement. **(20 marks; answer in no more than 250 words.)**

**Model-answer ceiling:** Actual 104 words; governed ceiling **250 words**.

**Model answer:** Establish a baseline for attacks, attempted and successful infiltration, recruitment, finance, weapons, network regeneration and civilian harm, with definitions fixed. Track former hotspots, neighbouring districts and digital/drone substitution to detect displacement. Add ordinary police reach, service continuity, grievance quality, rights complaints, participation, rehabilitation stability and confidential trust surveys. Use interrupted trends, matched localities and process-traced cases to test whether interventions plausibly caused change. Independent audits should examine under-reporting and classification shifts. Pre-declared thresholds should govern staged tapering, while rebound indicators trigger support restoration. Durable capability loss means hostile networks cannot readily recruit, supply, command or regenerate and ordinary legitimate institutions can contain residual risk.

**Adjacent rubric:** Security baseline 5 + displacement/regeneration 4 + governance/rights measures 4 + evaluation method 3 + taper/reversal rule 2 + durability test 2 = **20 marks**

**Misconception to avoid:** A downward incident line alone reveals whether the cause was durable network loss.

# CUMULATIVE CONCEPT CHECKS

## Cumulative check 1 — history and mechanism

**Question:** Explain why a territorial-dispute narrative alone cannot diagnose contemporary cross-border terrorism in J&K. **(10 marks; answer in no more than 150 words.)**

**Model-answer ceiling:** Actual 93 words; governed ceiling **150 words**.

**Model answer:** The territorial dispute explains historical and interstate context, but contemporary terrorism operates through a distinct mechanism: external direction or support, deniable armed groups, infiltration or remote delivery, local recruitment and facilitation, finance, digital narratives and attacks on civilians or institutions. These elements require counter-infiltration, intelligence, police investigation, financial disruption, governance and rights-compatible prevention. Political history remains relevant because actors exploit identity and legitimacy, yet it cannot substitute for mapping present capabilities. A sound diagnosis therefore links the external objective to the internal operational chain while keeping diplomatic, constitutional and criminal-security questions analytically separate.

**Adjacent rubric:** Limitation of dispute frame 3 + current mechanism 4 + response implications 2 + synthesis 1 = **10 marks**

## Cumulative check 2 — evidence and attribution

**Question:** Build an attribution standard for a suspected externally directed terror module. **(15 marks; answer in no more than 250 words.)**

**Model-answer ceiling:** Actual 109 words; governed ceiling **250 words**.

**Model answer:** Attribution should combine authenticated communications, finance, travel and training history, recovered material, digital and forensic signatures, handler identity, operational similarity and corroborated witness evidence. Each item should be assessed for reliability, alternative explanation and chain of custody. The analysis must distinguish external origin, organisational affiliation, state-institution linkage and responsibility for the specific act. Intelligence can justify protection and surveillance at a lower threshold than criminal conviction, while prosecution requires admissible proof. Public and diplomatic statements should express confidence and unresolved gaps. Independent challenge reduces confirmation bias. The result should be a graded finding supported by converging evidence, not a label inferred from one weapon, slogan or claim of responsibility.

**Adjacent rubric:** Evidence categories 5 + actor distinctions 3 + threshold distinction 3 + uncertainty/communication 2 + challenge mechanism 2 = **15 marks**

## Cumulative check 3 — operations and proof

**Question:** Why must counter-infiltration planning include an investigation plan before an interdiction begins? **(10 marks; answer in no more than 150 words.)**

**Model-answer ceiling:** Actual 89 words; governed ceiling **150 words**.

**Model answer:** Interdiction protects life and stops movement, but mishandled scenes can destroy the evidence needed to identify handlers, receivers, finance and wider modules. Pre-designating an investigation lead clarifies photography, seizure, device isolation, biometric and material recovery, custody and witness procedures without constraining urgent tactical decisions. It also enables immediate exploitation of phones, routes, caches and logistics while leads remain fresh. Command boundaries should keep investigators out of tactical control and operators from unrecorded evidence handling. The objective is simultaneous safety and network dismantling, not choosing between operational success and prosecution.

**Adjacent rubric:** Evidence rationale 3 + pre-planning elements 4 + command boundary 2 + objective 1 = **10 marks**

## Cumulative check 4 — governance and participation

**Question:** Assess whether electoral participation can be treated as an internal-security outcome. **(15 marks; answer in no more than 250 words.)**

**Model-answer ceiling:** Actual 102 words; governed ceiling **250 words**.

**Model answer:** Electoral participation is a relevant outcome because safe candidacy, campaigning and voting can show institutional access and reduced coercive veto. It may strengthen representation, grievance articulation and non-violent competition. Yet turnout is shaped by mobilisation, contest quality, geography and local conditions; it does not independently prove trust, uniform inclusion or political settlement. Evaluation should disaggregate participation, threats, rejected nominations, campaign freedom and post-election responsiveness. It should be read with attacks, recruitment, rights, service access and local-body capacity. Electoral participation is therefore a bounded security-governance indicator: important evidence of democratic capability, but neither a substitute for other metrics nor a final settlement certificate.

**Adjacent rubric:** Security significance 4 + limitations 4 + evidence needed 4 + balanced verdict 3 = **15 marks**

## Cumulative check 5 — rights and effectiveness

**Question:** Show how accountability can improve rather than merely constrain counter-terrorism effectiveness. **(15 marks; answer in no more than 250 words.)**

**Model-answer ceiling:** Actual 105 words; governed ceiling **250 words**.

**Model answer:** Accountability improves target accuracy by requiring reasons, evidence and command ownership. Complaint, judicial and internal review can detect mistaken identity, coercion, evidence contamination and repeated operational failure. Remedy for wrongful harm reduces fear and hostile narratives, while credible discipline strengthens community reporting and witness cooperation. Clear force, detention, search and data rules protect personnel by defining lawful action. Transparency cannot disclose sensitive methods, but classified records, audit trails and authorised oversight can preserve review. The design challenge is timely review that does not paralyse urgent protection. Effectiveness should be measured through reduced hostile capability and civilian harm together; unreviewed activity counts can conceal strategic damage.

**Adjacent rubric:** Accuracy/learning 4 + legitimacy/cooperation 4 + personnel/rule clarity 2 + secrecy-review balance 3 + outcome standard 2 = **15 marks**

## Cumulative check 6 — ceasefire and settlement

**Question:** Frame a balanced policy for preserving a LoC ceasefire while responding to continuing covert supply. **(15 marks; answer in no more than 250 words.)**

**Model-answer ceiling:** Actual 106 words; governed ceiling **250 words**.

**Model answer:** Preserve hotline, flag-meeting and local de-escalation mechanisms because reduced firing protects civilians and lowers accidental escalation. Treat covert supply as a separate problem: strengthen intelligence fusion, counter-drone and route surveillance, forensic exploitation, receiver-network investigation and lawful financial tracing. Responses should be based on attributed evidence and calibrated to avoid turning every incident into uncontrolled military escalation. Border communities need warning, shelters, compensation and continuity support. Diplomatic communication should document violations or sponsorship without claiming that the ceasefire itself has failed whenever a module is found. The policy protects the humanitarian gain while denying adversaries the ability to use restraint in one domain as cover in another.

**Adjacent rubric:** Ceasefire preservation 4 + covert-supply response 4 + escalation discipline 3 + community protection 2 + synthesis 2 = **15 marks**

## Cumulative check 7 — rehabilitation

**Question:** Distinguish assistance, rehabilitation and reconciliation for conflict-displaced families. **(10 marks; answer in no more than 150 words.)**

**Model-answer ceiling:** Actual 93 words; governed ceiling **150 words**.

**Model answer:** Assistance meets immediate needs through cash, ration, shelter, documents and services. Rehabilitation rebuilds durable agency through safe housing, livelihood, education, property remedy, protection and voluntary return or integration. Reconciliation addresses recognition, accountability, memory, inter-community confidence and the conditions for coexistence. The stages overlap but cannot substitute for one another. A family receiving relief may remain displaced; a family employed in the Valley may still feel unsafe; a safe return may occur without social repair. Policy should measure each layer separately and preserve family choice rather than treating physical return as the only success.

**Adjacent rubric:** Three distinctions 5 + examples/overlap 3 + choice and measurement 2 = **10 marks**

## Cumulative check 8 — durable transition

**Question:** Propose conditions for shifting from exceptional security support to ordinary institutional management. **(20 marks; answer in no more than 250 words.)**

**Model-answer ceiling:** Actual 113 words; governed ceiling **250 words**.

**Model answer:** Transition should require sustained low attack, recruitment, supply and regeneration capability across several review periods, not one quiet season. Ordinary police must handle residual threats with reliable intelligence, investigation, prosecution and community access; civil services, courts and local bodies must function routinely. Civilian harm, rights complaints, displacement and trust should improve across regions and groups. Rehabilitation and victim support must remain stable. Independent audit should test under-reporting, route displacement and definition changes. Exceptional forces or restrictions should taper in stages against published operational thresholds, with clear command transfer and rebound triggers. Transition succeeds when specialised support becomes a reserve rather than the foundation of order, while legitimate institutions retain the capacity to respond.

**Adjacent rubric:** Security conditions 5 + ordinary capacity 4 + rights/distribution 4 + audit safeguards 3 + staged transition/reversal 4 = **20 marks**

# ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS MODEL PRACTICE

## Final 10-mark practice

**Question:** Explain why cross-border terrorism in Jammu and Kashmir must be analysed as both an external strategy and an internal governance challenge. **(10 marks; answer in no more than 150 words.)**

**Model-answer ceiling:** Actual 101 words; governed ceiling **150 words**.

**Model answer:** Cross-border terrorism has an external strategic layer: sponsorship, training, finance, weapons, handlers and deniable proxy groups seek effects below conventional-war thresholds. It becomes an internal governance challenge when it uses infiltration, local facilitation, recruitment, narratives and grievances to attack civilians and institutions. External disruption therefore needs border intelligence, attribution and diplomatic or strategic measures, while internal resilience needs evidence-led policing, service delivery, political participation, rehabilitation, rights protection and accountable communication. Treating it only as foreign aggression ignores local vulnerability; treating it only as domestic alienation ignores organised external capability. Durable security requires dismantling the proxy chain while strengthening legitimate ordinary institutions.

**Adjacent rubric:** External mechanism 3 + internal mechanism 3 + integrated response 3 + conclusion 1 = **10 marks**

## Final 15-mark practice

**Question:** Analyse the operational internal-security consequences of the post-2019 administrative structure in Jammu and Kashmir. **(15 marks; answer in no more than 250 words.)**

**Model-answer ceiling:** Actual 122 words; governed ceiling **250 words**.

**Model answer:** The 2019 Act created J&K as a Union Territory with a legislature while excluding public order and police from Assembly competence. Security authority is therefore Lieutenant-Governor and Union centred even after the 2024 election and revocation of President's Rule. This can clarify Union responsibility, ease deployment and support common intelligence and counter-terror coordination. It also creates a seam: elected institutions manage many services and represent citizens, but do not control policing as a State government would. Poor consultation can blur accountability and weaken local ownership. Effective design needs formal civil-security forums, shared district assessments, clear public communication, grievance escalation and legislative scrutiny within lawful competence. The operational test is not centralisation itself but whether coordination becomes faster, locally informed, rights-compatible and answerable.

**Adjacent rubric:** Correct legal position 4 + operational advantages 3 + coordination/accountability risks 4 + institutional remedies 3 + verdict 1 = **15 marks**

## Final 20-mark practice

**Question:** “A quieter Line of Control is a security and humanitarian gain, but not proof that Jammu and Kashmir's proxy-war system has ended.” Evaluate and propose a durable strategy. **(20 marks; answer in no more than 250 words.)**

Yet proxy capacity can persist through infiltration, drones, tunnels, finance, online direction, local recruitment and hinterland cells. These require separate metrics. Durable strategy should preserve military hotlines and community protection; integrate border and hinterland intelligence; exploit recoveries for network evidence; target finance and logistics lawfully; and maintain J&K Police, CAPF, armed-force and NIA handovers. Governance must add safe elections, local bodies, services, grievances, victim support and voluntary rehabilitation. Exceptional powers need necessity, proportionality and review. Evaluation should track hostile regeneration, civilian security, ordinary institutional capacity, trust and displacement. The end-state is neither perpetual exceptionalism nor premature closure, but low coercive capability contained by legitimate institutions.

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

## 1. Historical and conceptual foundation

- Instrument of Accession: 26 October 1947.
- UNSC Resolution 47 prescribed a sequenced process; Pakistan-side withdrawal came first.
- LoC terminology followed the 1971 conflict and Shimla framework.
- The late-1980s insurgency combined internal triggers with external support.
- Conventional-war constraints encouraged low-intensity proxy strategy.
- The Afghan-war aftermath strengthened trained-cadre availability.
- Article 370 constitutional doctrine and judgment analysis belong to Polity.

## 2. Proxy and infiltration system

- Deniability separates sponsor, organisation, operative and facilitator.
- Attribution uses converging material, communication, finance, training and forensic evidence.
- Infiltration is a chain: launch, crossing, guide, reception, logistics and attack.
- LoC firing, infiltration, recruitment and hinterland incidents are separate.
- Drones, tunnels, couriers and digital channels permit vector substitution.
- A recovery or chargesheet is not a judicial conclusion about every accused.

## 3. Local ecosystem and prevention

- OGW describes alleged facilitation functions, not a community identity.
- Coercion, knowledge and intent must be differentiated.
- Recruitment pathways include grievance, fear, kinship, status, revenge and online influence.
- Prevention combines fair policing, family/community support, education, grievance remedy and credible communication.
- Indiscriminate labelling reduces trust and actionable reporting.

## 4. Current constitutional-administrative position

- The 2019 Act created J&K UT with legislature and Ladakh UT without legislature.
- Public order and police are excluded from J&K Assembly competence.
- The security chain remains Lieutenant-Governor/Union centred.
- President's Rule was revoked on 13 October 2024.
- Elected government and UT security competence coexist.
- Notification, institutional implementation and outcomes are different.

## 5. Security and investigative architecture

- MHA identifies multi-tier deployment, fencing, technology, intelligence and coordination.
- Tactical operations need command, civilian-safety and evidence plans.
- J&K Police contributes local continuity; CAPFs and armed forces add assigned capability.
- NIA or other investigators require lawful case allocation and admissible evidence.
- Topic 12 owns detailed mandates; Topic 06 owns border-engineering depth.
- After-action learning should track route and network adaptation.

## 6. Governance, participation and development

- The official 2024 Assembly election turnout was 63.88%.
- Turnout is participation evidence, not proof of settlement.
- Development counts when infrastructure and services are usable and fairly distributed.
- PMRP 2004 and UDAAN are book-period policy examples, not current-status evidence.
- Local bodies, civil society and communities support feedback, warning and victim care.
- Participation must remain voluntary and free from collective suspicion.
- Ordinary governance is a security capability when it is accessible and accountable.

## 7. Victims and rehabilitation

- Relief, rehabilitation and reconciliation are distinct.
- MHA reported 47,466 registered migrant families on 26 February 2026.
- The canonical book-period account records nearly four lakh Kashmiri Pandits displaced during the 1990s; this is not the current registered-family metric.
- On 25 March 2026, MHA reported 6,000 transit units approved, 4,112 constructed and 3,257 allotted.
- Construction, allotment, occupancy and durable voluntary return are different.
- Return and local integration should remain informed choices.
- Victims need protection, information, compensation access, remedy and psychosocial support.

## 8. Rights and special powers

- Authority, lawful purpose, current material, necessity, proportionality and review form the control chain.
- Preventive detention is not conviction.
- The J&K AFSPA 1990 is distinct from the 1958 North-East Act.
- A statute does not decide whether a particular action was lawful.
- Complaint, judicial, command and legislative accountability improve learning and legitimacy.
- Rights-compatible targeting can strengthen intelligence and prosecution.

## 9. Ceasefire, normalisation and resolution

- The 2003 ceasefire understanding was reaffirmed by DGMOs on 25 February 2021.
- Strict observance applied from midnight 24/25 February.
- Hotline and flag meetings support de-escalation.
- Ceasefire restraint has humanitarian value.
- Normalisation requires low hostile capacity and functioning ordinary institutions.
- Political resolution remains a separate end-state.

## 10. Evaluation

- MHA Annual Report 2024–25 recorded TII declining from 228 in 2018 to 28 in 2024.
- Current claims must note the 2025 high-impact Pahalgam attack and later investigation.
- Track frequency, consequence, regeneration, displacement and distribution.
- Use independent audits and stable definitions.
- Taper exceptional support in reversible stages.
- Durable security combines low coercive capability with trusted constitutional governance.
