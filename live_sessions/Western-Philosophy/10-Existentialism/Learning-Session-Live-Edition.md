## Lesson 1 — A Family of Three Projects, Not One Doctrine

**Progress: 1/12 · Stage: Foundation · Core demands: W10-19–W10-22, W10-49–W10-51, W10-76–W10-82**

### Visual first — the boundary map

```text
                    CONCRETE, FINITE EXISTENCE
                               |
          +--------------------+--------------------+
          |                    |                    |
   KIERKEGAARD              SARTRE              HEIDEGGER
   existing individual      for-itself          Dasein
   before God               without blueprint   asks about Being
          |                    |                    |
   choice / faith           situated freedom     thrown projection
          |                    |                    |
 anti-system critique     atheistic humanism    fundamental ontology

SARTRE'S SLOGAN: "existence precedes essence"
  - Kierkegaard: does not use it
  - Heidegger: rejects its humanist reversal
```

*Caption: the shared concern with concrete existence does not erase different questions, methods or conclusions.*

### Core concept

“Existentialism” is a retrospective family label. Kierkegaard asks how a finite individual exists ethically and religiously; Sartre asks what follows when consciousness has no fixed human blueprint; Heidegger uses an analysis of Dasein to reopen the question of Being. Only Sartre accepts the syllabus's famous slogan in its atheistic-humanist form.

### Argument reconstruction and presuppositions

**Sartre's paper-knife argument**

1. An artefact is designed for a function before it is produced.
2. Its essence or blueprint therefore precedes its existence.
3. Traditional theism treats humanity as the product of a divine designer.
4. Sartre begins from atheism: there is no divine artisan and hence no prior human blueprint.
5. Human beings first exist in situations and subsequently define themselves through projects.
6. Therefore, for human reality, **existence precedes essence**.

**Presuppositions:** a fixed essence would function as a determining blueprint; Sartre's atheism is a premise here, not a demonstrated conclusion; absence of a blueprint does not remove bodies, histories or social constraints.

**Heidegger's correction**

“The essence of Dasein lies in its existence” concerns Dasein's **manner of being**, not a sovereign subject manufacturing itself. Reversing essence and existence still leaves the human subject central; Heidegger instead asks how Being becomes intelligible. Being is not the highest being.

### Distinctions that must survive

| Axis | Kierkegaard | Sartre | Heidegger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Governing problem | How must the existing individual live? | What follows from non-self-identical consciousness without a blueprint? | What is the meaning of Being? |
| God | Central to religious existence | Denied as premise of the slogan | Not settled by fundamental ontology |
| Freedom | Qualitative choice before God | Radical but situated project | Thrown projection, not voluntarism |
| Authenticity route | Faith and inward appropriation | Critique of bad faith; positive account remains thin | Owning finite possibilities |
| Slogan | Not his | Explicitly his | Rejected as humanist inversion |

### Bounded example and limit

The paper knife clarifies an artefact's blueprint. It does **not** prove that persons can choose bodies, pasts or available opportunities. It establishes a denial of exhaustive predetermination, not omnipotence.

### Strongest objection → reply → residual

- **Objection:** if no common essence exists, responsibility and shared moral judgement become arbitrary.
- **Reply:** Sartre argues that choices project an image of humanity and therefore carry universal weight.
- **Residual:** the source of that universalisability remains under-explained once pre-given values are denied.

### UPSC use

- In a slogan question, open with ownership: **Sartre affirms; Kierkegaard does not formulate; Heidegger repudiates the humanist reading.**
- In a family-label question, compare governing problems before comparing terms.
- Never make “existentialist” mean “one who believes that life is meaningless.”

### Revision notes

1. Existentialism here is a family resemblance, not a unified doctrine.
2. Sartre's paper knife distinguishes designed artefacts from unplanned human existence.
3. Atheism is Sartre's premise in the no-blueprint argument.
4. Facticity remains after essence is denied.
5. Kierkegaard's centre is the existing individual, not Sartre's slogan.
6. Heidegger's centre is the Being-question, not human self-creation.
7. Being is not one more being or a supreme entity.
8. Dasein can be authentic or inauthentic.
9. The three accounts of freedom are non-identical.
10. A good comparison preserves thinker boundaries before offering a common thread.

### Retrieval and application drill

**Prompt:** Why is “existence precedes essence” an unsafe definition of all existentialism?

**Model route:** identify Sartre's atheistic blueprint argument; contrast Kierkegaard's theistic existing individual; explain Heidegger's ontological claim and humanism criticism; conclude that the phrase is one position within a disputed family.

## Lesson 2 — Kierkegaard: The Existing Individual and Subjective Truth

**Progress: 2/12 · Stage: Core · Core demands: W10-01–W10-06, W10-14–W10-16, W10-18, W10-79, W10-81**

### Visual first — from system to appropriation

```text
OBJECTIVE RESULT
fact / doctrine / system
        |
        | can be known without changing how I exist
        v
LIMIT OF OBJECTIVITY FOR ETHICAL-RELIGIOUS LIFE
        |
        v
SUBJECTIVE APPROPRIATION
how I relate: passion + risk + commitment + temporality
        |
        v
INDIRECT COMMUNICATION
pseudonymous standpoint -> reader must choose -> no result is handed over
```

*Caption: “truth is subjectivity” concerns the existing subject's mode of relation, not the replacement of facts by preference.*

### Core concept

Johannes Climacus's statement “truth is subjectivity” distinguishes an objective **what** from an existential **how**. Accurate doctrine can remain externally possessed; ethical or religious truth must be appropriated in lived commitment. Kierkegaard's target is not logic or science but the claim that a completed objective system can substitute for the temporal act of existing.

### Argument reconstruction and presuppositions

1. A system presents truth from an impersonal standpoint.
2. The system-builder nevertheless exists in time, chooses, risks and dies.
3. No completed description can perform those acts for the individual.
4. Ethical-religious truth is therefore not only a proposition but a mode of existing in relation to it.
5. Direct communication tends to turn such truth back into detachable information.
6. Pseudonymous indirect communication stages possible ways of life and leaves appropriation to the reader.

**Presuppositions:** some truths have an existential condition of possession; individual freedom should not be cancelled by authorial authority; a first-person demand is not fully captured by a third-person description.

### Distinctions

| Do not confuse | Correct distinction |
|---|---|
| Subjectivity with factual relativism | Objective truths remain objective; existential truth concerns appropriation |
| Individuality with isolation | The issue is non-substitutability of decision, not absence of relations |
| Anti-system with anti-reason | A logical system may be possible; a completed existential system is the target |
| Kierkegaard with every pseudonym | Climacus, de Silentio, Haufniensis and Anti-Climacus occupy different standpoints |
| “Subjectivity is truth” with immediate self-certainty | Subjectivity is also “untruth” through finitude and sin |

### Pseudonymous attribution

| Voice | Work | Bounded standpoint |
|---|---|---|
| Johannes Climacus | *Philosophical Fragments*; *Concluding Unscientific Postscript* | asks how one becomes a Christian; not presented as a Christian authority |
| Johannes de Silentio | *Fear and Trembling* | admires but does not claim to comprehend faith |
| Vigilius Haufniensis | *The Concept of Anxiety* | psychological observer of anxiety |
| Anti-Climacus | *The Sickness unto Death* | demanding Christian standpoint |
| Victor Eremita / Judge William | *Either/Or* | editor and ethical voice within staged alternatives |

### Bounded example and limit

Knowing every proposition about courage does not itself make a person courageous in a risky choice. The example shows a gap between information and appropriation. It does not show that sincere commitment makes a false proposition true.

### Strongest objection → reply → residual

- **Hegelian reply:** Kierkegaard's anti-system polemic can caricature mediation. On a Hegelian account, individuality need not disappear into an abstract universal: concrete freedom and particularity can be preserved and developed through recognition, social practices and ethical institutions.
- **Kierkegaardian rejoinder:** social mediation can form a person's language, roles and available commitments, but it cannot appropriate an ethical-religious truth, decide or choose in the existing individual's place.
- **Residual limit:** Kierkegaard identifies a genuine non-substitutability of first-person commitment, yet this does not show that such commitment is socially unmediated or that Hegel simply erases individuality.
- **Objection:** indirect communication makes Kierkegaard's position impossible to identify and shields it from criticism.
- **Reply:** the double authorship and explicit staging of voices make non-identity part of the method; the reader is meant to evaluate standpoints rather than receive a final system.
- **Residual:** retrospective authorial explanations cannot eliminate every attribution dispute.

### UPSC use

For “subjectivity is truth,” write **Climacus** at least once, define appropriation, deny relativism, connect the existing individual to the anti-system argument, then evaluate fideism and attribution.

### Revision notes

1. The existing individual is particular, temporal, passionate and deciding.
2. A system cannot exist in the individual's place.
3. “Truth is subjectivity” is attributed to Johannes Climacus.
4. The thesis concerns the how of ethical-religious relation.
5. Logic and science retain objective validity.
6. “Subjectivity is untruth” qualifies immediate self-sufficiency.
7. Indirect communication protects the reader's act of appropriation.
8. Pseudonyms are staged existence-possibilities, not interchangeable masks.
9. Kierkegaard's anti-Hegelian point is strongest against systematic closure.
10. Hegel's mediated particularity preserves concrete freedom through recognition and ethical life.
11. Kierkegaard's residual claim is that mediation cannot appropriate, decide or choose for the existing individual.

### Retrieval and application drill

**Prompt:** Defend Kierkegaard against the charge of relativism in four moves.

**Model route:** attribute the thesis to Climacus; preserve objective factual truth; define passionate appropriation; concede that the religious criterion may still face a fideism objection.

## Lesson 3 — Kierkegaard: Spheres of Existence, Faith and Qualitative Transition

**Progress: 3/12 · Stage: Core · Core demands: W10-07–W10-11, W10-17**

### Visual first — not a chronological ladder

```text
AESTHETIC
interest / immediacy / pleasure
        | limit: boredom, fragmentation, despair
        | qualitative choice, not automatic maturation
        v
ETHICAL
commitment / vocation / universal duty
        | limit: guilt and the singular case
        | leap, not Hegelian mediation
        v
RELIGIOUS
single individual before God
faith + paradox + absolute relation to the Absolute

Possible relapse at every level; no guaranteed developmental timetable.
```

*Caption: the spheres are modes of existence separated by qualitative decisions, not age-based stages.*

### Core concept

The aesthetic, ethical and religious spheres organise different ways of existing. The aesthetic avoids binding commitment; the ethical accepts universal responsibility; the religious places the singular individual before God. Transition is neither deduction nor inevitable development.

### Argument reconstruction and presuppositions

1. Aesthetic immediacy seeks the interesting but cannot integrate the self through durable commitment.
2. Ethical choice gives continuity through obligation, vocation and responsibility.
3. Yet universal duty confronts guilt and the singularity dramatised by Abraham.
4. Religious existence claims a relation to God that cannot be mediated into a universal formula.
5. Therefore movement between spheres requires qualitative appropriation or leap.

**Presuppositions:** a person can inhabit a way of life rather than merely hold beliefs; qualitative change cannot be generated by more information alone; religious faith is irreducible to ethical universalism.

### Distinctions

| Distinction | Examination-safe formulation |
|---|---|
| Stage / sphere | Prefer “sphere” when avoiding a fixed chronology |
| Leap / irrational whim | The leap marks non-deductive commitment; it is not a licence for arbitrary action |
| Faith / ethical exemption | *Fear and Trembling* stages a paradox through de Silentio; it does not issue a general permission to violate morality |
| Infinite resignation / faith | Resignation relinquishes the finite; the knight of faith receives it again “by virtue of the absurd” |
| Religious existence / church conformity | Singular responsibility cannot be outsourced to the crowd |

### Bounded example and limit

Abraham illustrates the conflict between universal ethics and the claimed absolute relation to God. The example tests whether faith can be mediated by public reason; it must not be used as an open warrant for private revelation.

### Strongest objection → reply → residual

- **Objection:** the teleological suspension of the ethical legitimises fanaticism.
- **Reply:** de Silentio presents Abraham as an incommunicable paradox, not a reproducible public rule; the text itself withholds authorial endorsement.
- **Residual:** precisely because no public criterion settles the case, the danger of self-authorising fanaticism remains.

### UPSC use

In a three-spheres answer, give each sphere its organising aim and internal limit, then explain transition. A list without the qualitative-transition argument cannot answer the question on subjectivity.

### Revision notes

1. The aesthetic sphere seeks immediacy and the interesting.
2. Its limits are boredom, dispersion and despair.
3. The ethical sphere centres commitment, vocation and universal duty.
4. Its limits appear in guilt and singularity.
5. The religious sphere places the individual before God.
6. The spheres are not an automatic chronology.
7. Transition is qualitative and decisional.
8. “Leap of faith” is a standard English summary; source-sensitive writing should speak of the leap and faith.
9. De Silentio is the voice of *Fear and Trembling*.
10. Abraham's paradox is not a general moral permission.

### Retrieval and application drill

**Prompt:** Why does Kierkegaard's account require both the ethical sphere and its limit?

**Model route:** show that commitment repairs aesthetic fragmentation; explain universal duty; introduce guilt and singularity; conclude that religious transition depends on, rather than merely ignores, the ethical problem.

## Lesson 4 — Fear, Anxiety, Anguish and Despair

**Progress: 4/12 · Stage: Core · Core demands: W10-12, W10-13, W10-39, W10-68, W10-78**

### Visual first — one contrast, three disclosures

| | Fear | Kierkegaardian anxiety | Sartrean anguish | Heideggerian anxiety |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Object | determinate threat | no determinate entity | one's freedom | being-in-the-world as such |
| Reveals | danger | possibility and capacity to choose/fail | absence of inner guarantee | collapse of familiar significance and finitude |
| Error to avoid | calling every anxiety fear | reducing it to pathology alone | confusing it with Heidegger's ontology | treating it as a moral emotion |

*Caption: related vocabulary does not entail a shared doctrine.*

### Core concept

Fear is directed at something within the world. Existential anxiety or anguish lacks such a determinate object and is treated as disclosing a structure: possibility for Kierkegaard, freedom for Sartre, and being-in-the-world or finitude for Heidegger.

### Distinctions

- **Fear / anxiety:** entity-directed threat versus disclosure without a determinate threatening object.
- **Kierkegaard / Sartre / Heidegger:** possibility versus freedom versus being-in-the-world and finitude.
- **Description / inference:** the mood's experienced structure must be stated before any conclusion about freedom or Being.
- **Kierkegaardian / Sartrean despair:** misrelated self before God versus disciplined action without reliance on uncontrollable outcomes.

### Argument reconstruction and presuppositions

1. Moods are modes through which a situation is disclosed, not merely private colour added to neutral facts.
2. Fear discloses a determinate threatening entity.
3. Anxiety lacks such an entity but still has a distinctive phenomenal structure.
4. The thinkers infer that it discloses a condition of existence rather than another object.
5. The inferential target differs by thinker and must remain bounded.

**Presuppositions:** moods can possess cognitive or disclosive significance; absence of a determinate object is philosophically meaningful; clinical explanation and phenomenological description answer different questions.

### Despair must also be separated

- **Kierkegaardian despair:** the self misrelates to itself and to the power that established it; forms include not willing to be oneself and defiantly willing to be oneself.
- **Sartrean despair in the 1946 lecture:** act without relying on outcomes beyond one's control.
- These are not interchangeable doctrines.

### Bounded examples and limits

- The cliff distinguishes fear of falling from Sartrean anguish that nothing within the self guarantees one will not jump.
- The gambler discovers that yesterday's resolution does not mechanically bind the present project.
- Heidegger's anxiety suspends the familiar network of significance.

These examples illuminate structure; they do not establish that every clinical anxiety state yields ontological knowledge.

### Strongest objection → reply → residual

- **Objection:** neurochemical or psychological accounts explain anxiety without granting it philosophical authority.
- **Reply:** phenomenology asks what the experience discloses and how it is structured, not which causal mechanism produces it.
- **Residual:** moving from a description of mood to a claim about freedom or Being remains an inference requiring independent defence.

### UPSC use

Write “phenomenological description” before “ontological or ethical inference.” This prevents a mood from being treated as infallible revelation.

### Revision notes

1. Fear has a determinate object.
2. Existential anxiety lacks a determinate entity.
3. Kierkegaard links anxiety to possibility.
4. Sartre links anguish to freedom without guarantee.
5. Heidegger links anxiety to being-in-the-world and finitude.
6. The three diagnoses are analogous, not identical.
7. Kierkegaardian despair is a self-relation before God.
8. Sartrean despair concerns controllable action and outcomes.
9. A causal account need not refute a phenomenological description.
10. Description alone does not prove the thinker's further ontology.

### Retrieval and application drill

**Prompt:** Give a qualified defence of existential anxiety as disclosure.

**Model route:** distinguish fear; describe objectlessness; state the thinker-specific structure revealed; answer the naturalist objection by separating causal and phenomenological questions; retain the limit on ontological inference.

## Lesson 5 — Sartre: In-itself, For-itself and Nothingness

**Progress: 5/12 · Stage: Core · Core demands: W10-23–W10-28**

### Visual first — plenitude and non-coincidence

```text
BEING-IN-ITSELF (en-soi)              BEING-FOR-ITSELF (pour-soi)
"is what it is"                       "is what it is not and is not what it is"
self-identical / full / opaque         intentional / nihilating / projective
no distance from itself               distance from facticity and present identity
                                       |
                                       v
                              QUESTIONS · ABSENCE · POSSIBILITY
                                       |
                                       v
                                  SITUATED FREEDOM
```

*Caption: the distinction is ontological, not a Cartesian equation of matter with mind.*

### Core concept

Being-in-itself is full, self-identical being. Being-for-itself is consciousness as non-coincidence: it is directed beyond itself, related to possibilities not yet actual, and unable to become identical with its past or role. **Nihilation** names consciousness's introduction of absence and negation into the experienced field.

The desired fusion of these modes—a self-identical being that is also fully conscious and self-grounding—is the impossible **in-itself-for-itself**. Sartre identifies this contradictory ideal as “God”: human reality desires the impossible plenitude of being its own foundation while remaining consciousness.

### Argument reconstruction and presuppositions

1. Consciousness is intentional: it is consciousness of something beyond itself.
2. To question, imagine or expect is to distance oneself from what is simply given.
3. This distance permits absence and alternatives to organise experience.
4. Therefore consciousness is not a full thing among things; it lacks self-identical plenitude.
5. Its “nothingness” is this lack and power of nihilation, not non-existence.
6. Because no factual state fuses with consciousness, project and freedom become structurally possible.

**Presuppositions:** being-in-itself is fully positive; intentionality does not require an inner content-container; pre-reflective consciousness can be aware without a substantial ego.

### Distinctions

| Term | Safe meaning | Unsafe shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| In-itself | self-identical being | “matter” in a mind–body dualism |
| For-itself | non-self-identical consciousness | a soul-substance |
| Nothingness | lack of plenitude and capacity for negation | literal non-existence |
| “Is what it is not” | projects toward the not-yet | already possesses the future |
| “Is not what it is” | exceeds its past/facticity | has no past or constraints |
| In-itself-for-itself / “God” | contradictory ideal of self-grounding conscious plenitude | an achieved synthesis or a standard theological proof |

**Trap and limitation:** “God” here names the impossible synthesis internal to Sartre's ontology. It is neither a state humans attain nor, by itself, a standard proof or disproof concerning the God of philosophical theology.

### Bounded example and limit

Expecting Pierre in a café, one experiences the room around his absence. This shows that negation can structure a field of experience before an explicit negative sentence. It does not prove that consciousness creates Pierre or the café.

### Strongest objection → reply → residual

- **Objection:** “non-self-identity” sounds contradictory.
- **Reply:** Sartre is not saying that one thing has and lacks the same fixed property in the same respect; he distinguishes present facticity from projected possibility.
- **Residual:** the sharp opposition between full in-itself and nihilating for-itself can appear too dualistic and leaves their relation difficult.

### UPSC use

For the 2023 formula, unpack both halves separately, then connect them through nihilation to temporality, freedom and bad faith. For the 2025 distinction, begin with English terms and give French once.

### Revision notes

1. In-itself is full and self-identical.
2. For-itself is intentional and non-self-identical.
3. The contrast is ontological, not simply mental versus material.
4. “Is what it is not” points to future project.
5. “Is not what it is” points to transcendence of facticity.
6. Nihilation introduces absence, questioning and alternatives.
7. Pierre's absence illustrates experienced negation.
8. Nothingness does not mean consciousness is unreal.
9. Freedom follows from non-coincidence, but only in situation.
10. Sartre calls the contradictory ideal of complete self-grounding “God”; it is neither achieved synthesis nor a standard theological proof.

### Retrieval and application drill

**Prompt:** Explain why the for-itself cannot become a completed in-itself-for-itself.

**Model route:** define both modes; show that self-identical plenitude excludes the distance constitutive of consciousness; identify their impossible synthesis as the ideal Sartre calls “God”; clarify that this is neither achieved nor a standard theological proof.

## Lesson 6 — Sartre: Facticity, Bad Faith, Alienation and Being-for-others

**Progress: 6/12 · Stage: Core · Core demands: W10-29–W10-33, W10-46–W10-48**

### Visual first — the double flight

```text
                    HUMAN REALITY
                         |
          +--------------+--------------+
          |                             |
      FACTICITY                    TRANSCENDENCE
 body / past / role / situation    projection / possibility / surpassing
          |                             |
          +--------------+--------------+
                         |
                    BAD FAITH
      "I am only my role"  OR  "my situation means nothing"
                         |
               inauthentic self-relation
                         |
          fixed by role or by the Other's Look
                         |
                      alienation
```

*Caption: bad faith evades the tension of facticity and transcendence; it is not merely telling a lie.*

### Core concept

Bad faith is self-deception in which the for-itself treats itself as only facticity or only transcendence. Alienation follows when the self lives through a fixed role or an objectifying judgement as though that exhausted it. The Look adds a social dimension: another perspective gives me an outside I cannot control.

### Argument reconstruction and presuppositions

1. Human reality is both given situation and self-surpassing project.
2. Neither pole alone is adequate.
3. A person may flee responsibility by becoming a thing-like role.
4. A person may also deny bodily, social or historical givenness by claiming pure choice.
5. Because consciousness is pre-reflectively aware yet non-self-identical, avowal and evasion can coexist without two separate minds.
6. Under the Look, shame reveals oneself as an object for another subject; the Other is lived as present rather than reached through a separate argument from analogy.
7. Thus freedom is socially exposed and cannot control every meaning of its acts.

**Presuppositions:** pre-reflective awareness is possible; self-deception need not involve an unconscious censor; shame is irreducibly relational; subject and object positions generate tension.

### Distinctions and examples

| Example | What it can show | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Over-scripted waiter | a role may be performed as though it exhausts the person | competent role-performance is not automatically bad faith |
| Ambiguous date gesture | simultaneous evasion of a gesture's meaning and one's response | the example reflects a dated social framing and must be used cautiously |
| Keyhole and footsteps | shame reveals being seen and an uncontrolled outside | it does not prove every relation is conflict |
| Fixed social label | the Other's judgement can harden facticity | social identification can also support solidarity |

### Bounded example and limit

The waiter and keyhole scenes diagnose possible attitudes, not whole classes of people or every social relation. Their force lies in displaying fixed self-description and exposure to another perspective; their limit is that work, love and recognition can also sustain non-alienated agency.

### Strongest objection → reply → residual

- **Objection:** self-deception requires knowing and not knowing the same truth.
- **Reply:** Sartre distinguishes pre-reflective, non-positional awareness from reflective avowal; bad faith is an unstable project rather than a completed inner lie.
- **Residual:** critics can still ask whether this redescribes rather than solves the paradox.

### UPSC use

For 2018, establish facticity/transcendence before moving from bad faith to alienation. Add the Look only as a bounded social pressure, not as a replacement for the self-deception argument.

### Revision notes

1. Facticity includes body, past, social location and situation.
2. Transcendence is projection beyond the given.
3. Bad faith can deny either pole.
4. The waiter is an attitude example, not proof that roles are inherently false.
5. Self-deception is explained through pre-reflective non-coincidence.
6. Inauthenticity is evasion of the for-itself's unstable freedom.
7. Alienation can arise through fixed roles and external judgement.
8. The Look is disclosed in relational shame.
9. Being-for-others gives a perspective one cannot master.
10. Sartre's critique of bad faith is clearer than his positive ethics of authenticity.

### Retrieval and application drill

**Prompt:** How can bad faith lead to alienation without making every social role inauthentic?

**Model route:** define the double structure; explain identification with a role as exhaustive; introduce external fixation through the Look; qualify that role participation becomes bad faith only when used to deny transcendence or facticity.

## Lesson 7 — Sartre: Freedom, Responsibility and Determinism

**Progress: 7/12 · Stage: Core · Core demands: W10-34–W10-41**

### Visual first — freedom is not power

```text
UNCHOSEN SITUATION
body + past + coercion + institutions + available alternatives
        |
        v
MEANING-CONFERRAL WITHIN A PROJECT
what counts as motive, obstacle, opportunity or reason
        |
        v
SITUATED CHOICE / NON-CHOICE / CONFORMITY
        |
        v
RESPONSIBILITY

STRUCTURAL FREEDOM ≠ EQUAL OPTIONS ≠ GUARANTEED SUCCESS
```

*Caption: Sartre denies exhaustive determination, not the existence of constraints.*

### Core concept

The for-itself cannot become a completed causal thing. Motives guide action only within a project that gives them significance. Even deferral or conformity takes up a stance. Yet structural freedom must be distinguished from practical power, available alternatives and fair conditions.

### Distinctions

- **Freedom / power:** capacity to take a stance versus capacity to secure an outcome.
- **Motive / mechanical cause:** a reason interpreted within a project versus a force acting on a thing.
- **Non-choice / incapacity:** evasive deferral versus coercive or psychological inability.
- **Responsibility / blame:** agency can remain while attribution of blame is graded by facticity.

### Argument reconstruction and presuppositions

1. A motive is not a self-interpreting force.
2. It becomes action-guiding through its place in a project.
3. The project is a mode of the for-itself's transcendence.
4. Therefore motives do not mechanically determine a thing-like self.
5. Non-choice is still a way of taking up the situation.
6. Responsibility follows because no fixed essence can decide in one's place.
7. Choice projects an image of humanity and produces anguish.

**Presuppositions:** interpretation is not itself mechanically fixed; agency persists under constraint; responsibility can be graded without disappearing.

### Three lecture-moods

| Mood | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Anguish | awareness of unsupported responsibility |
| Abandonment | no God or pre-given moral order supplies a decision |
| Despair | rely only on action and reasonably controllable possibilities |

### Bounded example and limit

A prisoner may retain a stance toward captivity but lacks the power and options of the jailer. The example preserves a difference between consciousness and causal objecthood while blocking the inference that both are equally free in any politically useful sense.

### Strongest objection → reply → residual

- **Marxist objection:** early radical freedom can abstract from class power and the material organisation of labour, property and scarcity. These structures do not merely obstruct chosen projects; they distribute the options, risks and costs through which projects become thinkable and effective.
- **Later Sartrean partial reply:** the analyses of scarcity, practico-inert structures, seriality and collective praxis make agency historically and materially mediated rather than socially weightless.
- **Marxist residual:** the later account qualifies the rhetoric of absolute freedom but does not fully repair early formulations or show that formal ontological freedom yields meaningful practical agency under domination.
- **Feminist / de Beauvoir-style objection:** an abstract chooser can conceal gendered situation. Socialisation, embodiment, dependency, violence and unequal relations constrain horizons, assign meanings to bodies and make freedom relational rather than an isolated act of transcendence.
- **Later Sartrean / de Beauvoir-style partial reply:** situated and embodied freedom can distinguish oppression from mere bad faith, analyse how horizons are socially formed, and connect one's freedom with the conditions of others' freedom.
- **Feminist residual:** retaining transcendence prevents reduction to social roles, but a formal capacity to exceed facticity does not by itself establish equal voice, safety, recognition or substantive freedom.

### UPSC use

In a freedom/determinism answer, never write “circumstances do not matter.” Use the sequence: for-itself → motive meaning → facticity → situated project → structural criticism.

### Revision notes

1. Freedom is the for-itself's mode of being, not a detachable faculty.
2. “Condemned” means existence is unchosen but stance is inescapable.
3. Motives gain force within projects.
4. Non-choice can be a choice of deferral or conformity.
5. Coercion and incapacity qualify responsibility.
6. Facticity limits outcomes and alternatives.
7. Freedom is not omnipotence.
8. Anguish tracks responsibility.
9. Abandonment tracks absence of pre-given value.
10. Marxist criticism shows how class and material structures distribute options, risks and costs.
11. Feminist and de Beauvoir-style criticism foregrounds gendered horizons, embodiment, relations and relational freedom.
12. Later social mediation supplies partial replies; formal transcendence alone does not secure substantive freedom.

### Retrieval and application drill

**Prompt:** Critically assess “You can always make something out of what you have been made into.”

**Model route:** distinguish being made into a situation from taking it up; explain facticity/transcendence; defend non-reducibility to the past; introduce oppression and constrained horizons; conclude with graded situated agency.

## Lesson 8 — Sartre's Consciousness as “Nothing” and the Husserl Comparison

**Progress: 8/12 · Stage: Core · Core demands: W10-42–W10-45, W10-80**

### Visual first — shared floor, divergent architecture

| Axis | Shared phenomenological floor | Husserl | Sartre |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intentionality | consciousness is of something | analyses constitution of sense | radicalises outward direction |
| Self-awareness | pre-reflective awareness | pure ego as non-object pole in mature work | non-egological field; ego appears in reflection |
| Unity | experience has temporal and intentional unity | constituting subjectivity, habituality and inner time | object and intentional structure; no inner inhabitant |
| Negation | acts can intend absence or frustration | analysed within intentional life | made ontological as nihilation |
| Aim | describe givenness | constitution of objectivity | freedom, contingency and self-deception |

*Caption: Sartre retains intentionality and pre-reflective awareness while rejecting an ego inside consciousness.*

### Core concept

“Consciousness is nothing” means that consciousness has no thing-like essence, content-container or self-identical inhabitant. It exists as directed openness and nihilation. Sartre's disagreement with Husserl concerns the status of the ego and the task of phenomenology, not intentionality itself.

### Argument reconstruction and presuppositions

1. If consciousness is exhaustively intentional, it points beyond itself rather than containing copies.
2. A transcendental ego inside it would duplicate the unity already supplied by intentional acts and objects.
3. Such an ego would introduce opacity into consciousness.
4. Sartre therefore treats the ego as a transcendent object constituted in reflection.
5. Pre-reflective consciousness remains non-positionally aware of itself.
6. Its lack of thing-like identity is “nothingness,” which grounds nihilation and freedom.

**Presuppositions:** intentionality is exhaustive; consciousness is translucent; reflection modifies rather than neutrally observes experience; unity need not require a substantial or quasi-substantial pole.

### Distinctions

- Sartre's nothingness is neither non-existence nor Buddhist emptiness.
- Mature Husserl's pure ego is not an inner empirical object.
- Early Husserl's position complicates a simple ego/no-ego opposition.
- Sartre reorients phenomenology; “refutation” overstates the result.

### Bounded example and limit

Pierre's absence supports the phenomenological reality of negation. Husserl can reply that intentional fulfilment and frustration already describe such givenness without converting consciousness into ontological nothingness.

### Strongest objection → reply → residual

- **Objection:** without a unifying pole, Sartre cannot explain identity, habituality or the continuity of a stream over time.
- **Reply:** intentional objects, temporal structure and reflective constitution can provide unity without an inner ego.
- **Residual:** whether these resources fully replace Husserlian constituting subjectivity remains contested.

### UPSC use

For 2026 Q2(c), begin with shared intentionality, describe Sartre's six-step radicalisation, compare ego/unity/negation/aim, then state the early-Husserl qualification and a balanced verdict.

### Revision notes

1. Both thinkers accept intentionality.
2. Both recognise pre-reflective self-awareness.
3. Sartre denies a thing-like interior to consciousness.
4. The ego is an object of reflection for Sartre.
5. “Nothing” means lack of self-identical plenitude.
6. Nihilation links nothingness to freedom.
7. Husserl's mature project analyses constituting subjectivity.
8. A pure ego is not an empirical object.
9. Early Husserl complicates a simple contrast.
10. The fairest conclusion is reorientation, not refutation.

### Retrieval and application drill

**Prompt:** State Sartre's two strongest objections to a transcendental ego and one Husserlian reply.

**Model route:** superfluity and opacity; reply that the pure ego is a non-object pole needed to explain habitual and temporal unity; conclude that the dispute concerns function, not an inner homunculus.

## Lesson 9 — Heidegger: Ontological Difference and Being-in-the-world

**Progress: 9/12 · Stage: Core · Core demands: W10-49, W10-52–W10-60, W10-80**

### Visual first — from detached subject to involved world

```text
TRADITIONAL PICTURE
inner subject -> representation -> external object

HEIDEGGER'S REVERSAL
Dasein = already being-in-the-world
        |
        +-> worldhood: referential "in-order-to" network
        +-> ready-to-hand: equipment in use
        +-> breakdown: network becomes conspicuous
        +-> present-at-hand: thematic object-property stance
        |
        v
CARE: ahead-of-itself + already-in + alongside
```

*Caption: being-in-the-world is one unitary constitution, not a subject, a container and a relation added together.*

### Core concept

The ontological difference separates Being from beings. Dasein is selected because its own Being matters to it and it already understands Being in practice. Its basic constitution is being-in-the-world: engaged involvement within a meaningful referential whole.

### Distinctions

- **Being / beings:** intelligibility of entities versus particular entities.
- **World / universe:** meaningful referential context versus total collection of physical things.
- **Ready-to-hand / present-at-hand:** equipment in practical use versus thematised object.
- **Being-in / spatial containment:** constitutive involvement versus location inside a container.
- **Priority / truth:** practical involvement is claimed to be more primordial; scientific description is not thereby false.

### Argument reconstruction and presuppositions

1. Detached theoretical awareness is not the only or primary mode of encountering entities.
2. In ordinary activity, equipment shows up through use, purpose and relations.
3. A hammer is intelligible through nails, wood, building and dwelling.
4. Breakdown makes this normally unobtrusive network conspicuous.
5. The subject/object split abstracts from prior involvement.
6. Therefore being-in-the-world is more primordial than a detached subject confronting an external realm.

**Presuppositions:** practical intelligibility is not reducible to explicit judgement; world means a context of significance, not merely the universe; Dasein is not a biological label.

### Epoché and the priority dispute

Husserlian epoché suspends the natural attitude's existential posit to study how objects are given; it neither denies the world nor repeats ordinary Cartesian doubt. Heidegger's objection concerns **priority**: reflective constitution presupposes already involved, temporal being-in-the-world.

### Existentialia and care

| Existentiale | Function |
|---|---|
| Attunement | finds Dasein already situated |
| Understanding | projects possibilities and practical know-how |
| Discourse | articulates disclosed meaning |
| Care | unifies projection, thrownness and dealings |

Care is formally: **ahead-of-itself — already-in — alongside**.

### Bounded example and limit

The broken hammer shows how an equipment-network becomes explicit. It does not show that scientific description is false; present-at-hand analysis is derivative in priority, not useless.

### Strongest objection → reply → residual

- **Objection:** Husserl's transcendental ego is not a Cartesian substance, and reduction studies world-correlation rather than a worldless mind.
- **Reply:** Heidegger's claim is that reflective correlation still presupposes practical involvement and temporality.
- **Residual:** whether transcendental phenomenology can include this prior involvement remains a live comparison, not a settled refutation.

### UPSC use

For 2018, define epoché accurately before criticism. For 2022, explain worldhood and equipment before authenticity. The hyphenated unity is the doctrine.

### Revision notes

1. Being is not a being.
2. Dasein's own Being is at issue for it.
3. Being-in-the-world is unitary.
4. “In” means involvement, not spatial containment.
5. Worldhood is a referential context of significance.
6. Ready-to-hand is equipment in use.
7. Present-at-hand is thematic object-description.
8. Breakdown makes the equipment-network conspicuous.
9. Attunement, understanding and discourse disclose Dasein's “there.”
10. Care unifies ahead-of-itself, already-in and alongside.

### Retrieval and application drill

**Prompt:** Why is the hammer example an argument against the priority of a detached subject rather than against science?

**Model route:** show practical encounter within a referential whole; explain breakdown and thematisation; conclude that theoretical description is derivative, not false.

*Caption: authenticity modifies how Dasein owns possibilities; it does not transport Dasein outside everyday life.*

### Core concept

Dasein is not automatically authentic. Inauthenticity is absorption in anonymous public possibilities, not sin or moral vice. The silent call of conscience and anticipation of death can retrieve Dasein from dispersion into anticipatory resoluteness.

### Argument reconstruction and presuppositions

1. Dasein is originally being-with others.
2. Everyday understanding is often governed by what “one” does, thinks and says.

### Bounded example and limit

Public career expectations can be taken over anonymously or owned reflectively. The same outward action may occur in either mode. This illustrates form rather than proving the action morally good.

### Strongest objection → reply → residual

- **Objection:** authenticity is formally empty; resoluteness can attach to unjust projects.
- **Reply:** Heidegger is offering fundamental ontology, not an ethical code.
- **Residual:** the reply explains the intention but does not provide a criterion for criticising resolute injustice.

# VERIFIED PRIMARY-OWNED PYQS — EXACT WORDING AND SOLVED APPROACHES

## PYQ 1 — 2018 Q1(d), 10 marks

**Question:** How does Sartre connect inauthenticity with bad faith? Why does Sartre show that inauthenticity and bad faith lead to alienation? Discuss.

**Demand:** connect three terms through one mechanism; do not merely define them separately.

**Model answer:** Sartrean human reality is both **facticity**—body, past, role and situation—and **transcendence**, the projection beyond what is given. Bad faith is the unstable project of denying this tension. A person may act as though a social role completely fixes the self, or as though pure choice cancels every constraint. In either case consciousness evades its non-self-identical freedom.

This evasion is inauthentic because the for-itself treats itself as an in-itself, or denies the facticity it must take up. Alienation follows in two connected ways. First, the self becomes estranged from its own structure by living through a fixed description. Second, under the Other's Look it can accept an external judgement as an exhaustive nature. The over-scripted waiter illustrates the attitude, but competent role-performance is not automatically bad faith.

Sartre explains self-deception through pre-reflective awareness: one need not posit two inner persons, one deceiving and one deceived. Yet the account remains contested because non-thetic awareness may redescribe rather than fully solve the paradox. Thus bad faith explains alienation powerfully, but Sartre's positive criterion of authentic social existence remains comparatively thin.

## PYQ 2 — 2018 Q4(a), 20 marks

**Question:** What is 'Epoché'? How does Heidegger reject this method of phenomenological reduction? Explain Heidegger's concept of 'being in the world' as opposed to the concept of a transcendental ego.

**Demand:** define Husserl fairly, identify Heidegger's priority objection, then explain the positive alternative.

**Model answer:** Epoché is Husserl's suspension of the natural attitude's unexamined existential posit so that phenomenology can study how objects are given and acquire sense. It is neither denial of the world nor ordinary Cartesian doubt. Transcendental reduction redirects inquiry toward constituting subjectivity and world-correlation.

Heidegger's objection concerns priority. If Dasein is always already practically involved in a meaningful world, reflective constitution cannot be the first phenomenon. The idea of a detached ego that subsequently secures a world abstracts from the involvement that makes reflection possible.

Being-in-the-world is therefore a unitary constitution. “In” means concernful involvement, not spatial containment. Entities first show up as ready-to-hand equipment within an “in-order-to” network: hammer, nail, wood, building and dwelling. Breakdown makes this network conspicuous and can permit a present-at-hand, property-based stance. Attunement, understanding and discourse disclose Dasein's “there,” while care unifies its being as ahead-of-itself, already-in and alongside.

A Husserlian reply is that the transcendental ego is not a Cartesian substance and that reduction studies intentional correlation rather than a worldless mind. Heidegger's stronger claim is that even this reflection presupposes practical and temporal disclosedness. Hence he redirects, rather than merely abandons, phenomenology. The unresolved issue is whether Husserlian constitution can incorporate the priority of engaged being-in-the-world.

## PYQ 3 — 2019 Q3(a), 20 marks

**Question:** Is Dasein authentic existence for Heidegger? How does he relate temporality with Dasein? Discuss.

**Demand:** deny the equation of Dasein with authenticity, then derive temporality from care.

**Model answer:** Dasein is not identical with authentic existence. It is the entity whose own Being is at issue and which exists as being-in-the-world. Proximally and for the most part it is absorbed in the “they,” where idle talk, curiosity and ambiguity organise everyday understanding. Inauthenticity is an existential mode, not moral depravity.

The silent call of conscience can retrieve Dasein from anonymous dispersion. Being-toward-death discloses death as ownmost, non-relational, certain and indefinite. In anticipatory resoluteness Dasein owns its thrown possibilities; authenticity is thus a modification of the same social and practical existence, not escape from it.

Dasein's Being is care: ahead-of-itself, already-in and alongside. Temporality is the meaning of this unity. Future or coming-toward corresponds to projection; having-been to thrownness; making-present to practical engagement. The future has existential priority because anticipation opens Dasein's finite possibilities, not because it occurs chronologically first. Ordinary clock time is a derivative public sequence of nows, useful but less primordial.

The account illuminates how identity is projective and inherited. Its limits are the formality of authenticity and the unfinished larger programme relating Being and time. Therefore Dasein can exist authentically, but it is not by definition “authentic existence.”

## PYQ 4 — 2019 Q4(c), 15 marks

**Question:** How does Sartre look at the problem of freedom of choice and determinism? Explain.

**Demand:** state Sartre's anti-determinist mechanism and qualify it through situation.

**Model answer:** Sartre rejects determinism when it treats the for-itself as an in-itself—a completed thing moved by causes. Consciousness is nihilating and projective: it can distance itself from a given state, interpret it and envisage alternatives. A motive does not mechanically produce an act; it becomes a reason within a project that gives it significance.

Freedom is therefore structural and inescapable. Even deferral or conformity is a way of taking up a situation. “Condemned to be free” means that existence was not chosen, yet no essence can choose in one's place.

This is not freedom from facticity. Body, past, coercion, social institutions and available options shape the field in which projects are intelligible. Freedom is not power, equal opportunity or guaranteed success. Critics therefore argue that early Sartre under-describes trauma and structural oppression. His later attention to scarcity and collectivity partially answers that pressure while qualifying absolute rhetoric.

Sartre's strongest conclusion is anti-reductionist: causes and motives do not exhaust the meaning of action. His weakest formulation appears when this truth is converted into undifferentiated responsibility under radically unequal conditions.

## PYQ 5 — 2020 Q3(a), 20 marks

**Question:** What does Kierkegaard mean by saying “Subjectivity is the truth” in the context of the problem of 'the single individual'?

**Demand:** connect subjective appropriation, individual existence, anti-system argument and non-relativism.

**Model answer:** The statement belongs to Johannes Climacus's pseudonymous *Concluding Unscientific Postscript*. It does not mean that belief becomes true through sincerity. Kierkegaard preserves objective truth in logic, history and science; his concern is the **how** of ethical and religious relation.

The single individual exists in time, chooses, risks and dies. A completed system may describe the individual as a moment in a whole, but it cannot perform the individual's commitment. Thus an objectively correct doctrine about God can remain existentially false for one who never appropriates it, while a relation marked by passionate inwardness addresses the demand of existing.

This also explains indirect communication. If existential truth were delivered as a result, it could be possessed without transformation. Pseudonymous voices instead stage possibilities and require the reader to decide. “Subjectivity is untruth” prevents easy self-certainty: finitude and sin mean that inwardness is not self-validating.

The doctrine exposes what an impersonal system omits, but risks fideism if passionate relation becomes insulated from reasons. Its defensible form is not relativism but the claim that some truths have conditions of lived appropriation that objective description alone cannot satisfy.

## PYQ 6 — 2020 Q4(a), 20 marks

**Question:** “You can always make something out of what you have been made into.” Critically discuss this statement by Sartre with reference to his views on existentialism.

**Demand:** explain facticity/transcendence, then judge the scope of “always.”

**Model answer:** The statement condenses Sartre's account of situated freedom. What one has been “made into” is facticity: body, history, social position, past decisions and the meanings imposed by others. What one “makes” is the project through which the for-itself interprets and surpasses that givenness.

Because consciousness is not self-identical, no past description can exhaust its present relation to possibilities. Bad faith occurs when one claims to be only a role or, conversely, pretends that pure choice abolishes the given. Authentic responsibility therefore requires owning both facticity and transcendence.

The word “always” is strongest as an ontological denial of complete thinghood: even constrained consciousness takes some stance. It is weakest as a political or psychological claim. Coercion, trauma, illness, poverty and oppressive institutions can shrink options and even the intelligibility of alternatives. Freedom of stance is not equal power to transform circumstances.

Later Sartrean attention to social mediation partially concedes this problem. The statement is persuasive if read as graded, situated agency and implausible if read as equal responsibility or unlimited self-making. Human beings exceed what has been done to them, but never from nowhere.

## PYQ 7 — 2021 Q4(b), 15 marks

**Question:** “I can always choose, but I ought to know that if I do not choose, I am still choosing”. Critically discuss Sartre’s conception of choice and responsibility in the light of above statement.

**Demand:** explain non-choice as stance, then distinguish evasion from inability.

**Model answer:** For Sartre the for-itself is a project rather than a fixed essence. It must take up a situation, and refusing an explicit decision can itself mean choosing delay, conformity or another person's judgement. Non-choice is therefore often a form of choice.

Responsibility follows because no divine command or predetermined nature can decide in one's place. This produces anguish: choice projects an image of humanity without guaranteed justification. Bad faith tries to escape the burden by treating the self as a thing controlled by role, passion or circumstance.

The thesis needs qualification. Deliberate deferral differs from incapacity produced by coercion, severe illness or absence of intelligible alternatives. Facticity can constrain both action and awareness. Sartre's structural freedom is not equal practical power.

Thus the quotation correctly exposes ordinary evasion, but it should not erase graded responsibility. Its defensible core is that silence and conformity have meaning within a project; its limit is the tendency to redescribe every inability as avoidable bad faith.

## PYQ 8 — 2022 Q2(a), 20 marks

**Question:** Provide a critical account of Heidegger’s Being-in-the-world and discuss the problem of ‘authenticity’ in the context of Dasein.

**Demand:** explain the unitary world-structure before treating authenticity as a mode within it.

**Model answer:** Being-in-the-world rejects the picture of an inner subject first existing and then inferring an external realm. Dasein is constitutively involved in a meaningful world. “World” is a referential whole in which equipment appears through purposes. A hammer is ready-to-hand within building practices before it is present-at-hand as an object with properties. Breakdown makes this network explicit.

Attunement, understanding and discourse disclose Dasein's situation and possibilities. Care unifies this existence as ahead-of-itself, already-in and alongside. Since being-with is original, Dasein's everyday self is commonly organised by the “they,” expressed through idle talk, curiosity and ambiguity.

Authenticity is not moral virtue. Conscience silently recalls Dasein to its thrown projection; being-toward-death discloses an ownmost, non-relational, certain and indefinite possibility. Anticipatory resoluteness owns finite possibilities without leaving the shared world.

The analysis powerfully dissolves Cartesian abstraction and shows that theoretical cognition is derivative from practical significance. Yet authenticity remains formally empty: ownership alone supplies no criterion of just choice. Heidegger can reply that fundamental ontology is not ethics, but that reply does not remove the normative concern. Being-in-the-world is the constitutive structure; authenticity is one non-moral modification of how it is lived.

## PYQ 9 — 2022 Q4(a), 20 marks

**Question:** How does Soren Kierkegaard define the notion of ‘subjectivity’? Explain it with reference to three stages of existence as propounded by him.

**Demand:** integrate appropriation with the three spheres and their transitions.

**Model answer:** Kierkegaardian subjectivity is not private opinion but the existing individual's passionate appropriation of ethical and religious truth. Johannes Climacus contrasts objective possession of a doctrine with the way a finite person commits to it under risk. The truth at issue concerns the mode of existing.

The aesthetic sphere seeks immediacy, pleasure and the interesting while avoiding binding commitments; boredom and fragmentation reveal its limit. The ethical sphere chooses continuity through vocation, marriage, duty and universal responsibility; guilt and the singular case expose its limit. The religious sphere places the single individual before God in faith and paradox.

The movement is not chronological maturation or Hegelian deduction. A qualitative leap is required. *Fear and Trembling*, voiced by Johannes de Silentio, stages Abraham's paradox and the teleological suspension of the ethical; it does not grant a public licence to ignore morality.

The spheres therefore intensify appropriation: from dispersed immediacy, through responsible universality, to singular God-relation. The account illuminates existential transformation, but its refusal of public mediation creates a fideism and fanaticism problem. Subjectivity is defensible as lived appropriation, not as immunity from rational criticism.

## PYQ 10 — 2023 Q1(d), 10 marks

**Question:** How does Kierkegaard argue against Hegel’s idea of universal spirit in favour of the individual as the essence of spirit? Critically discuss.

**Demand:** present structural opposition and avoid caricaturing Hegel.

**Model answer:** Kierkegaard objects that a completed system views individuals as moments within universal Spirit, while the thinker who constructs the system still exists as a finite person who chooses, risks and dies. Objective mediation cannot perform the individual's either/or. Hence the “single individual” is irreducible as an existential standpoint.

The contrast appears in method: Hegelian mediation seeks systematic reconciliation; Kierkegaard stresses qualitative choice, leap and indirect communication. A logical system may be possible, but an existential system cannot contain the act of the one who builds it.

The criticism powerfully identifies the first-person cost of systematic closure. Yet Hegel need not deny all individuality; he can argue that concrete freedom is realised through mediated social forms rather than isolated inwardness. Kierkegaard therefore demonstrates an irreducible dimension of appropriation, not the complete falsity of universal explanation.

## PYQ 11 — 2023 Q3(c), 15 marks

**Question:** “Consciousness is what it is not and is not what it is.” In the light of this statement bring out the chief features of Sartre’s conception of consciousness.

**Demand:** unpack both clauses through the for-itself, nihilation, temporality and bad faith.

**Model answer:** Sartre contrasts the for-itself with the self-identical in-itself. Consciousness “is what it is not” because it projects toward possibilities not yet actual: its future project helps define its present orientation. It “is not what it is” because no past, role or bodily fact exhausts it.

This non-coincidence is **nihilation**. Consciousness can question, imagine absence and distance itself from the given. Pierre's absence can organise an experienced café, showing that negation is not merely a later sentence. Facticity remains, but it is taken up through transcendence.

The structure grounds situated freedom and also bad faith. One may pretend to be only a role, or deny the given through fantasies of pure freedom. Sartre's account avoids a substantial ego, treating selfhood as project.

The main objection is coherence: can one being both know and evade its condition without an unconscious division? Sartre appeals to pre-reflective awareness, but the solution remains disputed. The formula is therefore not a logical contradiction; it is an ontological claim about temporal, projective consciousness.

## PYQ 12 — 2024 Q1(e), 10 marks

**Question:** How does Kierkegaard define truth in terms of subjectivity? Critically discuss.

**Demand:** define, delimit and evaluate.

**Model answer:** Johannes Climacus's “truth is subjectivity” means that ethical-religious truth requires passionate inward appropriation. The “how” of the existing subject's relation cannot be replaced by merely accurate information about the “what.”

The thesis does not abolish objective truth in logic or science. Nor does sincerity make false belief true. Indirect communication and pseudonymous standpoints protect the reader's responsibility to exist rather than merely repeat a doctrine.

Its strength is to expose the gap between description and commitment. Its weakness is that intense inwardness can seem insulated from public reasons, producing a fideism objection. The most defensible verdict is that subjectivity is a condition for possessing some existential truths, not a universal criterion of propositional truth.

## PYQ 13 — 2024 Q4(a), 20 marks

**Question:** What do the existentialist thinkers mean by the slogan “existence precedes essence”? How is human existence related to human freedom according to them? Discuss.

**Demand:** identify Sartrean ownership, then compare—not homogenise—the three accounts of freedom.

**Model answer:** Strictly, “existence precedes essence” is Sartre's atheistic slogan. A designed artefact has a blueprint before production; without a divine artisan there is no prior human nature that exhaustively determines individuals. Persons exist in situations and define themselves through projects.

Kierkegaard does not use the slogan. His existing individual resists replacement by a completed Hegelian system and becomes a self through qualitative choice, ultimately in a God-relation. Heidegger's statement that Dasein's essence lies in existence is ontological: Dasein is defined by its manner of being and openness to Being. He rejects Sartre's humanist reversal as still centred on a self-creating subject.

Their freedoms consequently differ. Kierkegaardian freedom is the anxious possibility of choice and leap before God. Sartrean freedom is the for-itself's situated non-coincidence with facticity, bringing responsibility and anguish. Heideggerian freedom is thrown projection: owning inherited possibilities through anticipatory resoluteness, not unlimited will.

The family resemblance lies in resisting a fixed abstract account of the person. Yet the theistic, atheistic-humanist and ontological projects are incompatible in important respects. The slogan is illuminating only when retained as Sartre's thesis and used as a controlled comparison, not a common creed.

## PYQ 14 — 2025 Q1(c), 10 marks

**Question:** Explain the difference between being-for-itself and being-in-itself as presented by Sartre.

**Demand:** define both modes, show the relation and add one consequence.

**Model answer:** Being-in-itself (*en-soi*) is full, self-identical and opaque: it is what it is. It is not simply “matter,” but the mode of being that contains no distance from itself.

Being-for-itself (*pour-soi*) is consciousness. It is intentional, nihilating and non-self-identical: it is what it is not through future project, and is not what it is because no past or role exhausts it. This distance from facticity grounds situated freedom.

The for-itself desires complete self-grounding, yet a synthesis with the plenitude of the in-itself would destroy the non-coincidence that makes it conscious. The distinction therefore explains freedom and bad faith while generating the unresolved tension between being and nothingness.

## PYQ 15 — 2026 Q2(c), 15 marks

**Question:** Describe Sartre's notion of consciousness as 'nothing'. How is it different from Husserl's notion of consciousness? Discuss.

**Demand:** establish the shared floor, describe Sartre, compare ego and task, then qualify.

**Model answer:** Sartre inherits Husserlian intentionality: consciousness is consciousness of something and is pre-reflectively aware of itself. “Nothing” does not mean non-existence. It means that consciousness has no thing-like essence, inner content-container or self-identical plenitude.

Sartre removes the ego from consciousness. The ego is a transcendent object constituted in reflection, while the pre-reflective field is non-egological. As for-itself, consciousness nihilates the given, experiences absence and projects possibilities. Pierre's absence in the café illustrates negation as a feature of experience. Nothingness thus becomes the ontological ground of freedom.

Mature Husserl instead analyses constituting subjectivity, a pure ego as non-object pole, habituality and inner time-consciousness. His task is the constitution of objective sense; Sartre's is to explain contingency, freedom and self-deception. Yet the contrast must be qualified: early Husserl did not posit an ego as an immanent item, and the mature ego is not an inner object.

Sartre therefore radicalises and reorients phenomenology rather than simply refuting Husserl. He keeps intentionality but turns consciousness's emptiness into an ontology of freedom.

## PYQ 16 — 2026 Q3(b), 15 marks

**Question:** Explain Heidegger's conception of authenticity and inauthenticity as modes of Dasein's being.

**Demand:** treat both as non-moral modes and explain the modification.

**Model answer:** Dasein is the entity whose own Being is at issue. Its everyday mode is commonly inauthentic: it understands itself through the anonymous “they,” where idle talk, curiosity and ambiguity circulate public possibilities. Inauthenticity is neither sin nor moral wickedness; it is absorption in an unowned way of being.

The silent call of conscience recalls Dasein to its thrown projection without giving a moral rule. Being-toward-death discloses death as ownmost, non-relational, certain and indefinite. In anticipatory resoluteness Dasein owns finite possibilities instead of treating them merely as what “one” does.

Authenticity is therefore a modification of the same being-in-the-world. It does not abolish being-with, social roles or everyday practices. Nor does it guarantee moral goodness. This formality is both the doctrine's precision and its weakness: Heidegger can explain ownership, but not which projects deserve commitment.

# ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS PRACTICE

## Original 10-marker

**Question:** Distinguish Sartrean anguish from Heideggerian anxiety. Answer in about 150 words.

### Model answer

Both moods lack the determinate object characteristic of fear, but they disclose different structures. Sartrean anguish reveals the for-itself's unsupported freedom. The cliff and gambler examples show that no fixed nature or past resolution guarantees present choice. Anguish therefore accompanies responsibility.

Heideggerian anxiety concerns being-in-the-world as such. The familiar network of practical significance recedes, Dasein is individualised and its finite thrown possibilities become visible. The mood serves fundamental ontology rather than a moral psychology of choice.

Thus Sartre moves from phenomenological non-guarantee to freedom, while Heidegger moves from world-collapse to finitude and possible authenticity. The similarity is formal—objectless disclosure; the conclusions are not interchangeable.

## Original 15-marker

**Question:** “Bad faith is possible only because consciousness never fully coincides with itself.” Examine.

### Model answer

Sartre's for-itself is non-self-identical. It “is what it is not” through projected possibility and “is not what it is” because past facticity never exhausts it. This distance makes freedom possible, but also permits evasion.

Bad faith absolutises one pole. The over-scripted waiter treats a role as a complete essence; the opposite error treats pure choice as though body, past and situation were unreal. Because consciousness has pre-reflective awareness without necessarily making that awareness an explicit reflective judgement, Sartre believes avowal and concealment can coexist.

The account explains why bad faith is not an ordinary lie between two persons. It also links self-deception to inauthenticity and alienation under fixed social judgement. However, the knowing/not-knowing paradox persists: non-thetic awareness may simply rename the divided attitude. Moreover, role examples require caution because social participation need not deny freedom.

The claim is therefore structurally central: non-coincidence is a necessary condition of bad faith. Whether it is a sufficient explanation of self-deception remains disputed.

## Original 20-marker

**Question:** Compare Kierkegaardian subjectivity, Sartrean freedom and Heideggerian authenticity as three responses to the problem of impersonal abstraction. Critically discuss.

### Model answer

All three resist accounts that dissolve concrete existence into impersonal abstraction, but they identify different losses.

Kierkegaard's target is the completed system that cannot perform the temporal choice of the single individual. Climacus's “truth is subjectivity” makes ethical-religious truth a matter of passionate appropriation, while indirect communication preserves the reader's responsibility. The strength is its account of lived commitment; the cost is possible fideism.

Sartre rejects a predetermined human essence. The for-itself nihilates the given and projects itself within facticity. Freedom produces responsibility, anguish and the possibility of bad faith. This preserves agency against causal reduction, but early radical formulations under-describe oppression and unequal power.

Heidegger's target is the detached subject/object picture. Dasein is already being-in-the-world, governed by care and ecstatic temporality. Authenticity is an owned modification of thrown social possibilities through conscience and being-toward-death. Its ontological precision is also its normative weakness: resoluteness gives no moral content.

The comparison therefore yields no single existentialist solution. Kierkegaard answers impersonal objectivity through religious appropriation, Sartre through situated self-surpassing and Heidegger through finite world-disclosure. Each recovers a first-person or involved dimension; none alone supplies a complete account of rational justification, social structure and ethical norm.

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

## 1. Boundary sheet — three projects

- **Kierkegaard:** the existing individual, subjective appropriation, qualitative choice, religious faith and resistance to systematic closure.
- **Sartre:** atheistic no-blueprint thesis, for-itself/in-itself, nothingness, situated freedom, responsibility, bad faith and being-for-others.
- **Heidegger:** ontological difference, Dasein, being-in-the-world, care, everyday modes, death and temporality.
- **Common thread:** finite concrete existence resists reduction to fixed essence, impersonal totality or detached subject.
- **Non-negotiable limit:** family resemblance is not doctrinal identity.

## 2. Kierkegaard rapid register

- **Existing individual:** a system cannot choose, risk, live or die in the individual's place.
- **“Truth is subjectivity”:** Climacus; the how of ethical-religious appropriation, not factual relativism.
- **Subjectivity as untruth:** finitude and sin block immediate self-sufficiency.
- **Indirect communication:** existential truth cannot be transferred as a neutral result.
- **Pseudonyms:** cite the voice; de Silentio, Climacus, Haufniensis and Anti-Climacus are not interchangeable.
- **Aesthetic:** immediacy and the interesting → boredom, fragmentation, despair.
- **Ethical:** commitment, vocation, universal duty → guilt and singularity.
- **Religious:** single individual before God → paradox and faith.
- **Transition:** qualitative leap, not automatic chronology or dialectical deduction.
- **Abraham:** tests universal ethics; no general permission for private revelation.
- **Anxiety:** possibility and the capacity to choose/fail.
- **Despair:** misrelation of the self to itself and its grounding God-relation.
- **Core criticism:** fideism, attribution difficulty and under-described community.
- **Qualified verdict:** Kierkegaard recovers appropriation from impersonal abstraction but leaves public justification precarious.

## 3. Sartre rapid register — existence and consciousness

- **Paper knife:** artefact has prior blueprint; humanity lacks one on Sartre's atheistic premise.
- **Existence precedes essence:** Sartre's thesis only; situation remains.
- **In-itself:** full, opaque, self-identical being.
- **For-itself:** intentional, nihilating, non-self-identical consciousness.
- **“Is what it is not”:** project toward future possibility.
- **“Is not what it is”:** past and role do not exhaust consciousness.
- **Nihilation:** questioning, absence and projection introduce negation into experience.
- **Pierre:** absence can organise the café; consciousness does not create the café.
- **Nothingness:** lack of thing-like plenitude, not non-existence.
- **Impossible synthesis:** complete self-grounding would cancel the for-itself's non-coincidence.

## 4. Sartre rapid register — freedom, bad faith and others

- **Facticity:** body, past, social location, situation.
- **Transcendence:** projection beyond the given.
- **Bad faith:** flight to either pure facticity or pure transcendence.
- **Waiter:** bounded attitude-example; role competence alone is not bad faith.
- **Self-deception reply:** pre-reflective awareness without reflective avowal.
- **Freedom:** structural non-coincidence within situation.
- **Freedom is not power:** options, capabilities and outcomes remain unequal.
- **Non-choice:** often a stance of delay or conformity; incapacity must be distinguished.
- **Responsibility:** no essence or God decides in one's place.
- **Anguish:** weight of unsupported choice.
- **Abandonment:** no pre-given divine moral order.
- **Despair:** act without relying on uncontrollable outcomes.
- **Look:** shame reveals oneself as object for another perspective.
- **Alienation:** fixed role or judgement can harden an external nature.
- **Core criticism:** early radicalism under-describes social structure; positive authenticity remains thin.
- **Qualified verdict:** Sartre protects agency against reduction but requires graded responsibility under constraint.

## 5. Sartre–Husserl comparison register

- **Shared:** intentionality, anti-representationalism and pre-reflective awareness.
- **Sartre:** non-egological field; ego is a reflective worldly object.
- **Husserl:** mature constituting subjectivity, pure ego as non-object pole, habitual and temporal unity.
- **Sartre's objections:** superfluity, opacity, priority and reification.
- **Husserlian reply:** the pure ego is not a homunculus and answers unity over time.
- **Qualification:** early Husserl complicates a simple ego/no-ego contrast.
- **Verdict:** Sartre radicalises and reorients phenomenology; “refutes Husserl” is too strong.

## 6. Heidegger rapid register — world and care

- **Ontological difference:** Being is not a being.
- **Dasein:** the entity whose own Being is at issue.
- **Being-in-the-world:** unitary practical involvement; “in” is not containment.
- **Worldhood:** referential context of significance.
- **Ready-to-hand:** equipment encountered in use.
- **Present-at-hand:** thematic object-property stance.
- **Breakdown:** makes the network conspicuous; does not erase practical meaning.
- **Attunement:** finds Dasein situated.
- **Understanding:** projects possibilities.
- **Discourse:** articulates disclosed meaning.
- **Care:** ahead-of-itself, already-in, alongside.
- **Epoché comparison:** suspension is not world-denial; Heidegger disputes reflective priority.
- **Qualified verdict:** involvement is more primordial than detached cognition, but Husserlian correlation may contest the claimed priority.

## 7. Heidegger rapid register — authenticity and death

- **Being-with:** sociality is original.
- **The they:** anonymous public self-understanding.
- **Idle talk, curiosity, ambiguity:** modes of falling, not moral sins.
- **Inauthenticity:** unowned absorption in public possibilities.
- **Conscience:** silent call; no substantive moral rule.
- **Death:** ownmost, non-relational, certain, indefinite possibility.
- **Anticipatory resoluteness:** takes over finite thrown possibilities.
- **Authenticity:** modification of the same being-in-the-world.
- **Not isolation:** being-with remains.
- **Not moral goodness:** ownership gives no ethical guarantee.
- **Core criticism:** formal emptiness and political under-determination.
- **Qualified verdict:** the ontology explains ownership but not worthiness of projects.

## 8. Heidegger rapid register — temporality

- **Temporality is the meaning of care.**
- **Future / coming-toward:** projection and anticipation.
- **Having-been:** thrownness that Dasein still is.
- **Present / making-present:** practical engagement.
- **Ecstatic unity:** not three containers on a line.
- **Future priority:** existential organisation, not chronological precedence.
- **Primordial time:** finite way Dasein exists.
- **Clock time:** derivative public sequence of nows, neither false nor dispensable.
- **Historicality:** inherited possibilities projected anew.
- **Limit:** the larger Being/time project remains incomplete.

## 12. Last-page qualified conclusions

- **Kierkegaard:** subjective appropriation reveals what impersonal system cannot live for the individual, but its religious form strains public justification.
- **Sartre:** non-self-identical consciousness secures situated agency against reduction, but radical responsibility must be graded under social and psychological constraint.
- **Heidegger:** being-in-the-world and care overturn detached subjectivity, but formal authenticity supplies no independent ethical criterion.
- **Whole topic:** existentialism is most philosophically useful as a contested family of methods for recovering finite existence, not as a single doctrine or a collection of slogans.
