# MUST-NEEDED / CORE LEARNING SESSION

## Lesson 1 — What project is phenomenology undertaking?

**Progress: 1/11 | Stage: Foundation | Subtopic: rigorous description, development and philosophical level**

### Visual: three questions that must not be collapsed

```text
WHAT APPEARS, AND HOW?
descriptive phenomenology
        |
        | identifies structures of givenness
        v
WHAT MUST AN OBJECT OF THIS KIND BE?
eidetic / ontological inquiry
        |
        | after the transcendental turn asks:
        v
HOW CAN SENSE AND OBJECTIVITY COUNT FOR A SUBJECT?
transcendental constitution

NOT THE SAME CLAIM:
"describe appearing" ≠ "only appearances exist"
```

*The diagram separates a method of description from an ontology and from the stronger transcendental thesis about conditions of sense.*

### Concept

Husserl seeks philosophy as a **rigorous science**: inquiry should return to matters as they are given rather than begin from inherited metaphysical pictures, natural-scientific explanations or psychological causal stories. “To the things themselves” means return to the subject matter in its modes of presentation; it does not mean naïve access to physical things stripped of all mediation.

Phenomenology changes across Husserl’s work:

| Phase | Marks-relevant role | Do not import backwards |
|---|---|---|
| *Logical Investigations* (1900–01) | anti-psychologism, intentionality, descriptive and eidetic analysis | mature *Ideas I* noesis–noema terminology |
| “Philosophy as a Rigorous Science” (1911) | programmatic opposition to naturalism and historicist relativism | a complete later life-world theory |
| *Ideas I* (1913) | natural attitude, suspension, reduction, transcendental turn and noesis–noema | late genetic and life-world emphases |
| *Cartesian Meditations* (French first publication, 1931; German posthumous, 1950) | transcendental ego and intersubjectivity | a proof of other minds in the natural attitude |
| *Crisis* (Parts I–II, 1936; complete text posthumous, 1954) | life-world, sedimented sense and critique of scientific objectivism | a rejection of science |

### Argument and presuppositions

1. Every theory appeals, directly or indirectly, to evidence.
2. Evidence is available only through ways in which a matter is presented, intended or fulfilled.
3. Therefore philosophy must first clarify modes of givenness instead of silently presupposing a ready-made subject confronting a ready-made world.
4. Essential and transcendental analysis can then ask what structures make such givenness and validity possible.

This argument presupposes that reflective description can disclose structures rather than merely add another causal psychological report. It also presupposes that givenness can constrain judgement.

### Essential distinctions

| Level | Governing question | Legitimate claim | Illegitimate inflation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Descriptive | How is the object experienced or meant? | perception presents through profiles | nothing exists beyond experience |
| Eidetic/ontological | What invariant belongs to this kind? | spatial perception has horizon-structure | every invariant is a separated entity |
| Transcendental | How do sense and validity arise for subjectivity? | objectivity has constitutive conditions | an empirical mind manufactures reality |
| Natural-scientific | What causal process occurs in the world? | optics and neurology explain conditions | causal explanation replaces clarification of meaning |

Husserl’s later position is commonly described as **transcendental idealism**, but it is not the Berkeleyan thesis that existing is merely being perceived. The world’s factual existence is bracketed during the inquiry; the transcendental claim concerns the dependence of sense and validity on structures of subjectivity.

### Bounded example and limit

A neuroscientist can explain which neural pathways are active when a red cube is seen. Phenomenology asks a different question: how is one cube given as identical through changing profiles, and how is the unseen back co-intended? The phenomenological description does not compete with the neural explanation. Its limit is that descriptive priority alone does not prove that transcendental constitution exhausts the ontology of the object.

### Objection → reply → residual

**Objection:** Reflection turns lived experience into an artificial object and therefore cannot be presuppositionless.

**Reply:** Phenomenology need not claim a view from nowhere. It institutes a disciplined change of attitude and submits descriptions to fulfilment, correction and repeatable analysis.

**Residual:** The ideal of complete presuppositionlessness remains aspirational. Reflection may reveal structures while still carrying linguistic, embodied and historical assumptions.

### UPSC use

- **Definition line:** Phenomenology is a disciplined description of modes of givenness that develops into eidetic and transcendental inquiry; it is not the assertion that only private appearances exist.
- **W09 anchors:** W09-01, W09-52, W09-58.
- **Answer use:** open method answers by identifying the level of claim before introducing technical operations.
- **Trap:** do not write one timeless “Husserlian system” that makes the *Logical Investigations*, *Ideas I* and *Crisis* use an unchanged vocabulary.

### Revision notes

1. Rigour means accountable evidence, not imitation of physics.
2. “Things themselves” means subject matters as given, not naïve physical objects.
3. Description asks how; eidetics asks what invariant; transcendental inquiry asks under what constitutive conditions.
4. Description does not entail that only descriptions or appearances exist.
5. The early anti-psychologistic project precedes the mature transcendental vocabulary.
6. *Ideas I* is central for suspension, reduction and noesis–noema.
7. *Cartesian Meditations* supplies the intersubjectivity problem and reply.
8. The *Crisis* adds life-world and scientific-objectivism themes.
9. Transcendental idealism is not empirical mental fabrication.
10. Phase and edition discipline prevents false historical claims.

### Retrieval and application drill

**Retrieval:** State in one sentence each the descriptive, eidetic and transcendental questions.

**Model:** Description clarifies how something is given; eidetic inquiry seeks the invariant structure of that kind of givenness or object; transcendental inquiry asks how its sense and validity are constituted for subjectivity.

**Application:** A candidate writes, “Because phenomenology studies appearances, Husserl denies an external world.” Repair the sentence.

**Model:** Phenomenology suspends commitment to the world’s existence so that its modes of givenness can be analysed; suspension is methodological and does not by itself deny a world.

**Misconception to avoid:** “Transcendental” does not mean supernatural; it concerns conditions under which object-sense and validity are possible.

## Lesson 2 — How do suspension and reduction change inquiry?

**Progress: 2/11 | Stage: Foundation | Subtopic: natural attitude, epoché and distinct reductions**

### Visual: the methodological switch

```text
NATURAL ATTITUDE
"the world is simply there"
        |
        | suspension (epoché)
        | neutralise the existence-posit
        v
EXPERIENCE RETAINED IN FULL
colour • profile • judgement • doubt • memory • horizon
        |
        | phenomenological reduction
        v
HOW THE OBJECT IS GIVEN
        |
        +--------------------------+
        |                          |
        v                          v
EIDETIC VARIATION             TRANSCENDENTAL INQUIRY
invariant structure           constituting subjectivity
```

*Suspension removes a commitment from play; reduction positively redirects attention. Eidetic and transcendental inquiries have different tasks.*

### Concept

The **natural attitude** is the ordinary and scientific stance in which the world is taken as existing independently, already furnished with objects and properties. Its **general thesis** is not a sentence repeatedly uttered but the operative positing of the world as simply there.

The natural attitude is neither foolish nor false. It is indispensable for life and empirical science. Its philosophical limitation is that it leaves unexamined how things acquire sense, evidence and objectivity.

Suspension (**epoché**) places the existence-posit out of action. Bracketing (**Einklammerung**) does not erase the experienced content. The seen tree remains coloured, spatial and perspectival; phenomenology refrains from using “the tree exists independently” as a premise while describing its appearance.

### Argument and presuppositions

1. If the world’s independent existence is presupposed, inquiry begins inside the very thesis it must clarify.
2. Suspension prevents that thesis from deciding the analysis in advance.
3. Phenomenological reduction redirects inquiry to the intentional field in which the world is meaningful.
4. Eidetic variation can disclose invariants.
5. Transcendental reduction asks how object-sense and validity are constituted for transcendental subjectivity.

The method presupposes that the natural posit can be neutralised without destroying experience and that the resulting reflection is not merely another natural psychological event.

### Essential distinctions: four operations

| Operation | Exact function | What remains | Main error |
|---|---|---|---|
| Suspension (*epoché*) | withhold use of the world-existence thesis | the experienced world as appearing | “Husserl disproves the world” |
| Phenomenological reduction | redirect attention to intentional givenness | acts, senses, profiles, horizons | “only inner experiences exist” |
| Eidetic reduction | vary factual features to seek invariants | essential possibility | “collect many cases inductively” |
| Transcendental reduction | disclose constituting subjectivity | subject–world correlation | “discover a mental substance” |

These operations are related but should not be rendered as one compulsory staircase. Eidetic variation can operate within descriptive work and is not merely the automatic third step of every reduction.

### Epoché and Cartesian doubt

| Axis | Descartes | Husserl |
|---|---|---|
| Immediate aim | suspend assent to what can be doubted and find certainty | neutralise the natural existence-posit to study givenness |
| Status of world | doubted, then recovered through further argument | neither denied nor proved during reduction |
| Residue | cogito interpreted as thinking thing | intentional and eventually transcendental field |
| Danger | substance dualism and need for a world-bridge | transcendental idealism and solipsism charge |

### Bounded example and limit

Imagine examining a cup. In ordinary use, you reach for a real cup. Under suspension, you do not pretend the cup vanishes; you attend to how one side is seen, other sides are anticipated, and identity persists through movement.

**Limit:** the method can clarify the cup’s mode of givenness but does not by that alone settle every realist question about what exists independently of experience.

### Why bracketing and reduction matter

1. They create a changed evidential attitude rather than merely adding another theory.
2. They avoid immediately inferring a mental substance from self-givenness.
3. They open essential and constitutive questions obscured by natural assumptions.
4. They distinguish causal explanation from clarification of meaning and validity.

### Objection → reply → residual

**Objection:** A subject who brackets the world is already embodied, speaking and engaged in that world; complete reduction appears impossible.

**Reply:** Suspension is a methodological neutralisation, not physical withdrawal. Its success can be graded: it reveals previously unnoticed commitments even if no finite reflection is absolutely assumption-free.

**Residual:** The objection limits claims of completeness. It does not show that every local act of bracketing is useless.

### UPSC use

- **2021:** natural attitude must be criticised as unexamined, not condemned as factually false.
- **2023:** “significance” requires purposes and consequences, not a glossary.
- **W09 anchors:** W09-02–W09-11, W09-57.
- **Answer-grabbing line:** Suspension is negative only in form; its philosophical result is the positive redirection to how a world can be given.

### Revision notes

1. The natural attitude posits a world as simply there.
2. Common sense and natural science both normally operate within it.
3. Natural attitude is practically valid but philosophically unexamined.
4. Suspension brackets the existence-posit, not the appearing content.
5. Phenomenological reduction redirects inquiry to intentional givenness.
6. Transcendental reduction asks about constituting subjectivity.
7. Eidetic reduction uses variation to disclose invariants.
8. The reductions are distinguishable operations, not one automatic chronology.
9. Epoché differs from Cartesian doubt in aim and result.
10. Complete suspension is contested, but partial clarification can remain methodologically valuable.

### Retrieval and application drill

**Retrieval:** What exactly is bracketed?

**Model:** The operative thesis that the world and its objects exist independently is withheld from use; the experienced content, including its profiles, relations and horizons, remains available for description.

**Application:** Give three distinct reasons for reduction in a 15-marker.

**Model:** It changes the evidential attitude, blocks an unargued substance inference, and opens eidetic and transcendental analysis.

**Misconception to avoid:** “Reduction” does not mean reducing the world to a smaller set of entities; it means leading inquiry back to the field of givenness.

## Lesson 3 — Why is consciousness always consciousness of something?

**Progress: 3/11 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: intentionality, correlation and fulfilment**

### Visual: act, intended sense and worldly fulfilment

```text
NOESIS                         NOEMATIC CORRELATE
perceiving / imagining  --->  object-as-intended in that mode
remembering / judging          "the tree as seen / recalled / doubted"
       |                                  |
       +---------------+------------------+
                       |
                 INTENTIONALITY
                 directedness
                       |
             fulfilment / disappointment
                       |
        worldly presence is possible but not required
```

*Intentionality is directedness, not deliberate intention and not guaranteed successful reference.*

### Concept

Husserl transforms Brentano’s insight that mental life is directed. Seeing is seeing-of, remembering is remembering-of, judging is judging-that, and imagining is imagining-of. Consciousness is not first a sealed container that later reaches outward.

In the vocabulary of *Ideas I*:

- **Noesis** is the act-mode: perceiving, recalling, doubting, valuing.
- **Noematic correlate** is the object-as-intended or sense-structure in precisely that mode.

The noema is not safely described as a private picture between mind and world. In ordinary veridical perception, the worldly object may itself be intended under a determinate profile and sense. Competing interpretations remain, so the exam-safe formulation is “object-as-intended/sense-correlate.”

### Argument and presuppositions

1. Different acts can intend the same object differently.
2. The same act-type can be fulfilled, disappointed or remain empty.
3. Therefore directedness and existence must be distinguished.
4. The act–correlate structure lets phenomenology describe both veridical and non-veridical experience without treating the latter as non-conscious noise.

This analysis presupposes that identity across acts and the difference between empty and fulfilled intending are phenomenologically accessible.

### Essential distinctions: profiles, horizons and synthesis

A spatial object appears through **profiles** or adumbrations. The front of a house is directly seen; the back is co-intended as available through possible movement.

| Structure | Function |
|---|---|
| Internal horizon | anticipated further profiles of this object |
| External horizon | meaningful relations to surrounding objects and world |
| Synthesis of identification | unifies changing profiles as appearances of one object |
| Empty intention | object or feature is meant without adequate presence |
| Fulfilment | further givenness confirms or enriches the intention |
| Disappointment | later givenness corrects the prior sense |

Evidence is consequently dynamic and graded. A transcendent spatial object is never exhausted in a single profile.

### Bounded example and limit: hallucination

A hallucinated pink elephant can be determinate in colour, size and location. The act is perceptual-seeming and directed even if no corresponding elephant exists. It is therefore intentional.

The crucial limit is this: shared intentional structure does not make hallucination and perception epistemically equal. Perception may receive harmonious fulfilment through further profiles and intersubjective confirmation; hallucination may fail those tests.

### Objection → reply → residual

**Objection:** If nonexistent objects can be intended, phenomenology seems committed to a realm full of impossible entities.

**Reply:** Intentionality requires a structured “of” relation in experience, not a separately existing object for every act. The analysis concerns the sense and mode of intending.

**Residual:** The ontological status of noematic sense is interpretively disputed. Calling it either a physical object or a mental image without qualification oversimplifies the correlation.

### UPSC use

- **2019:** answer “yes” immediately, then distinguish directedness from existence and fulfilment.
- **2024:** profiles and horizons provide the worked example for the consciousness–being correlation.
- **W09 anchors:** W09-12–W09-18, W09-55.
- **Trap:** intentionality is not voluntary intention.

### Revision notes

1. Intentionality means directedness of consciousness.
2. Brentano is a source, but Husserl transforms the doctrine.
3. Noesis is the act-mode.
4. Noema is safest expressed as object-as-intended/sense-correlate.
5. Noema is not automatically a private image.
6. Intentionality does not entail existence of a referent.
7. Hallucination can therefore be intentional.
8. Profiles give one object incompletely.
9. Horizons co-intend further possible givenness.
10. Fulfilment and disappointment distinguish directedness from epistemic success.

### Retrieval and application drill

**Retrieval:** Why can a hallucination be intentional?

**Model:** Because intentionality is determinate directedness, not successful reference; the hallucination has an act-mode and an object-as-intended even when no matching worldly object exists.

**Application:** Explain why “noema = mental image” weakens a 2024 essence answer.

**Model:** It inserts a private intermediary and obscures the structural correlation between how a worldly object is given and the act that intends it.

**Misconception to avoid:** A non-existent intentional object is not automatically an existent object in a special mental realm.

## Lesson 4 — How are sensory material and temporal unity constituted?

**Progress: 4/11 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: hyle–morphe and inner time-consciousness**

### Visual: two internal analyses of experience

```text
SENSORY SIDE                         TEMPORAL SIDE
hyletic material                     retention
     |                               just-past still held
     v                                      |
intentional formation                       v
     |                               primal impression
     v                                  now-phase
object presented                           |
                                             v
                                         protention
                                      just-about-to-come

Neither diagram describes a pile of inner objects.
```

*Hyletic analysis asks how sensory material is intentionally formed; temporal analysis asks how a temporally extended object is experienced as one.*

### Concept: hyletic material and intentional formation

In *Ideas I*, Husserl distinguishes lived sensuous material from the intentional formation through which an object is presented.

| Side | Meaning | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| Hyletic material (*hylē*) | non-intentional sensuous content lived through | a perceived object or a veil from which the world is inferred |
| Intentional formation (*morphē*) | apprehensional organisation that gives directed form | a second physical ingredient added to sensations |

The same broadly similar colour-field may be taken as a house-front, a patch in a painting or a stain. The distinction attempts to explain why sensory material alone does not fix intentional sense.

### Concept: the living present

The experience of a melody cannot be a sequence of isolated now-points. Husserl analyses a threefold temporal structure:

1. **Retention:** the just-past remains held within the present experience.
2. **Primal impression:** the current phase is originarily present.
3. **Protention:** the next phase is anticipated and may fulfil or disappoint expectation.

Retention is not recollection. Recollection represents again something no longer present; retention belongs to the ongoing perception itself.

### Essential distinctions

| Pair | Exact distinction |
|---|---|
| Hyle–morphe / noesis–noema | the first analyses sensory material and intentional formation within the act-side; the second names the act–correlate structure |
| Retention / recollection | retention holds the just-past within present perception; recollection re-presents what is no longer present |
| Primal impression / objective instant | primal impression is the now-phase within temporal experience, not a self-standing point measured by a clock |
| Protention / prediction | protention is the operative openness to the next phase; explicit prediction is a higher-order judgement |

### Argument and presuppositions

1. A melody is heard as a sequence with identity, not as unrelated sounds.
2. The present experience must therefore include a fading hold on what has just elapsed and an openness to what is coming.
3. Temporal synthesis is a condition for experiencing enduring or successive objects.

The account presupposes that the temporal flow can be described without treating it as one more object in objective time.

### Bounded example and limit

When the final note of a familiar phrase is delayed, surprise reveals a prior protention. When the note arrives, it fulfils or modifies the anticipation.

**Limit:** time-consciousness is supporting depth for Topic 09, not an independent printed limb. Use it when explaining synthesis, transcendental ego or the difference from a static Cartesian substance; do not turn every method answer into a manuscript history.

### Objection → reply → residual

**Objection:** If a temporal flow constitutes experienced time, what constitutes the flow? A regress threatens.

**Reply:** Husserl treats the flow as self-unifying rather than as an object requiring another higher-order stream.

**Residual:** “Self-constitution” names the required structure but may not completely dissolve the reflexive difficulty.

A second pressure targets the hyle–morphe model: if hyletic data are wholly non-intentional, how are they identified as data at all? The model explains formation but risks introducing inert material that later embodied phenomenology disputes. That later positive doctrine is not imported here.

### UPSC use

- **W09 anchors:** W09-19–W09-20.
- **2025 use:** temporal synthesis helps show why Husserl’s ego is not a static thinking substance.
- **Answer economy:** use the melody in two or three sentences; do not overdevelop editorial history unless source precision is relevant.
- **Trap:** retention is not memory in the ordinary recollective sense.

### Revision notes

1. Hyletic material is lived sensory content, not the perceived object.
2. Intentional formation gives object-directed organisation.
3. Hyle–morphe analyses the noetic side; it is not identical with noesis–noema correlation.
4. The model is not a sense-datum inference to an outer world.
5. A melody requires temporal synthesis.
6. Retention holds the just-past within ongoing perception.
7. Primal impression is the now-phase.
8. Protention anticipates what is about to occur.
9. Retention differs from later recollection.
10. Self-constitution of the flow remains a critical difficulty.

### Retrieval and application drill

**Retrieval:** Distinguish retention from recollection.

**Model:** Retention is the fading just-past phase still belonging to present perception; recollection re-presents an event that is no longer present.

**Application:** Why does time-consciousness matter in a Descartes comparison?

**Model:** It shows that Husserl analyses subjectivity as a temporally synthesising field rather than immediately inferring a timeless or static mental substance.

**Misconception to avoid:** Hyletic material is not what one ordinarily sees; one sees the intended object.

## Lesson 5 — What is the transcendental ego, and can it reach other subjects?

**Progress: 5/11 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: transcendental subjectivity, Descartes and intersubjectivity**

### Visual: three selves and one danger

```text
EMPIRICAL PERSON
body + biography + psychological events
        |
        | bracketed as worldly
        v
TRANSCENDENTAL SUBJECTIVITY
field in which object-sense and validity are constituted
        |
        | NOT
        +--> Cartesian mental substance
        +--> empirical psyche
        +--> Kant's formal "I think" without qualification
        |
        v
DANGER: transcendental solipsism
        |
        v
pairing + appresentation + intersubjective verification
```

*The transcendental turn does not discover a hidden soul; it changes the question from what the person is to how objectivity has sense for subjectivity.*

### Concept

The transcendental reduction brackets the empirical psychophysical person as one object in the world and discloses **transcendental subjectivity**: the field for which meaning, identity and validity arise.

This ego is not:

- a Cartesian *res cogitans*;
- the empirical psyche studied by psychology;
- merely Kant’s formal unity of apperception repeated unchanged.

Husserl’s analyses include intentional life, temporal synthesis, habitualities and intersubjective objectivity. “Ego” therefore names a centre or field of constitution, not a little entity enclosed inside the body.

### Argument: Descartes and Husserl

Both seek a radical first-person beginning and refuse to treat inherited belief as self-validating. The difference lies in what follows.

| Axis | Descartes | Husserl |
|---|---|---|
| Procedure | methodic doubt | suspension and reduction |
| Immediate result | cogito, then thinking substance | intentional/transcendental field without mundane substance inference |
| World | recovered through further proof, including divine non-deception | never first denied; its existence-posit is bracketed |
| Subject | thinking thing | constituting, temporal, intentional subjectivity |
| Objectivity problem | bridge from mind to world | correlation and intersubjective validity |

The 2025 question demands both common aspiration and structured divergence. A mere “Descartes is rationalist, Husserl is phenomenologist” answer is too shallow.

### Essential distinctions

| Pair | Distinction |
|---|---|
| empirical person / transcendental subjectivity | worldly psychophysical object / field of constitution |
| Cartesian cogito / Husserlian ego | thinking substance / intentional-temporal subjectivity |
| original presentation / appresentation | my stream given as mine / the other co-intended as irreducibly foreign |
| proof of another mind / constitution of alterity | natural-attitude existence argument / analysis of the sense and evidence “other subject” |

### Intersubjectivity: the Fifth Meditation route

1. Husserl abstracts a sphere of ownness.
2. My lived body is uniquely given both as physical and as a centre of sensation and action.
3. Another body appears with behaviour structurally like mine.
4. Passive **pairing** associates the two bodies.
5. The other’s subjectivity is **appresented**: co-intended but never originally given as my stream is.
6. Continuing behaviour may confirm or disappoint the appresentation.
7. The world gains the sense of being available for perspectives other than mine.

This is not presented as an inference proving that another mind exists. It analyses how the sense “another subject” and “world for anyone” is constituted.

### Presuppositions

- A sphere of ownness can be isolated by abstraction.
- My lived body is originally available before the full constitution of others.
- Pairing is passive rather than a disguised argument from analogy.
- Non-original givenness can still support valid otherness.

### Bounded example and limit

Seeing another person reach around an obstacle presents the body as oriented from a “there” that would be a “here” for that person. I do not feel the other’s sensation originally, yet behaviour appresents a foreign perspective.

**Limit:** the account clarifies the form of access but begins from ownness. It may therefore presuppose social sense while claiming to constitute it.

### Objection → reply → residual

**Objection:** The ownness-first account is circular. A body is recognised as another lived body only because social meaning is already available.

**Reply:** The reduction to ownness is an abstractive analysis of layers of validity, not a historical claim that a solitary infant first constitutes itself and later invents others.

**Residual:** The reply blocks a crude chronological objection but does not fully remove the concern that alterity is measured from the ego’s own structure.

### UPSC use

- **2025:** substance, world-access and intersubjectivity are the three comparison axes.
- **2022:** empirical psyche versus transcendental subjectivity is central to the anti-psychologism reply.
- **W09 anchors:** W09-21–W09-24, W09-56.
- **Trap:** never say Husserl proves other minds exist.

### Revision notes

1. Transcendental reduction brackets the empirical person as worldly.
2. Transcendental subjectivity is a field of constitution.
3. It is not a Cartesian mental substance.
4. It is not the empirical psyche.
5. Husserl and Descartes share a radical first-person aspiration.
6. Descartes infers a thinking substance; Husserl suspends that inference.
7. Husserl does not lose and then prove the world in the Cartesian way.
8. The other is appresented, not originally given as my own stream.
9. Pairing and behavioural verification support intersubjective sense.

### Retrieval and application drill

**Application:** Why is the inaccessible character of the other’s stream not automatically a failure?

*Scientific idealisation is legitimate; the crisis begins when its abstractions conceal the lived horizon that gives them sense.*

### Concept

The **life-world** (*Lebenswelt*) is the pre-theoretical world of practical and perceptual familiarity. Scientific inquiry presupposes this world: instruments are handled, marks are read, colleagues communicate, and ideal measurements arise from lived operations.

The late *Crisis* argues that mathematical natural science abstracts exact structures from this horizon. The achievement is powerful. The philosophical error is **objectivism**—treating the idealised result as the sole reality while forgetting the operations and meanings through which it arose.

### Argument and presuppositions

1. Scientific objects are reached through embodied, linguistic and communal practices.
2. Idealisation converts approximate lived appearances into exact formal structures.
3. These structures can be objectively valid without being self-grounding.
4. Phenomenology retrieves their sense-origin in the life-world.

The argument presupposes that genesis of sense can be distinguished from causal genesis and that uncovering a pre-scientific horizon does not relativise scientific truth to local preference.

### Essential distinctions: intersubjectivity and knowledge

Objectivity means more than private stability. An object is experienced as available to other possible subjects under appropriate conditions. Intersubjective confirmation therefore guards against the equation of constitution with private fabrication.

| Claim | Correct reading | Overstatement to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| science presupposes life-world | scientific meaning begins in lived practices | science is merely subjective |
| idealisation transforms givenness | exact objects arise through operations | equations are fictional |
| sedimentation inherits achievements | methods become taken for granted | history makes truth arbitrary |
| world-for-anyone is intersubjective | objectivity involves possible perspectives | consensus creates truth |

### Bounded example and limit

Geometry and measurement idealise shapes and lengths that are encountered approximately in practice. A surveyor’s exact coordinates depend on instruments, conventions and embodied operations, yet the result is not reducible to the surveyor’s feelings.

**Limit:** life-world analysis can expose forgotten origins, but it does not by itself decide a specific scientific dispute or replace empirical testing.

### Objection → reply → residual

**Objection:** By grounding science in lived experience, Husserl makes scientific validity anthropocentric.

**Reply:** Grounding concerns intelligibility and access, not manufacture. Scientific validity exceeds any one act because meanings and procedures are repeatable and open to intersubjective fulfilment.

**Residual:** The route from historically sedimented practice to universal validity requires more argument than the slogan “life-world grounds science” supplies.

### UPSC use

- **Natural-attitude answer:** science belongs to the natural attitude when it leaves its own meaning-conditions unexamined.
- **Psychologism answer:** the life-world must not be used to make logic a sociological habit.
- **W09 anchor:** W09-25.
- **Source caution:** write “*Crisis*, Parts I–II 1936; complete text posthumous 1954.”

### Revision notes

1. The life-world is pre-theoretical, not pre-human chaos.
2. Science begins from lived operations and shared practices.
3. Idealisation is an achievement, not an error.
4. Objectivism forgets the grounding horizon of that achievement.
5. Phenomenology clarifies science; it does not abolish it.
6. Sedimentation explains inherited, taken-for-granted meanings.
7. Objectivity includes availability to possible other subjects.

**Application:** How can the life-world be used without reviving psychologism?

**Misconception to avoid:** “Pre-scientific” means prior in grounding, not intellectually inferior.

## Lesson 7 — What is an essence, and how is it disclosed?

**Progress: 7/11 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: invariants, imaginative variation and ontological regions**

### Visual: from instance to invariant

```text
START WITH ONE EXAMPLE
        |
        v
VARY FREELY IN IMAGINATION
colour • size • material • orientation • context
        |
        +--> feature changes, kind survives = accidental
        |
        +--> feature removed, kind collapses = candidate invariant
        |
        v
COUNTER-VARIATION + REPEATABILITY
        |
        v
EIDETIC CLAIM
graded evidence, not mystical certainty
```

*An essence is approached by testing what can and cannot vary while the kind remains intelligible.*

### Concept

An **essence** (*eidos*, *Wesen*) is the invariant structure or whatness without which an item would cease to be of that kind. Essences are not datable psychological events and not causally produced by individual acts. They are also not safely treated as entities housed in a separated Platonic realm.

They are disclosed through instances and acts of **free imaginative variation**:

1. begin with an example;
2. alter features in imagination;
3. test whether the item remains intelligible as the same kind;
4. identify the invariant;
5. challenge the proposed invariant through counter-variation.

This differs from induction. Induction generalises from many observed cases and remains empirical; variation explores essential possibility and can begin with one example.

### Argument and presuppositions

1. Empirical fact alone states what happens to be the case.
2. Phenomenology seeks what must hold for a phenomenon to count as the kind it is.
3. Imaginative variation separates accidental from necessary structure.
4. Eidetic intuition names the fulfilled disclosure of the invariant, not an unexplained hunch.

The method presupposes disciplined imagination, a stable criterion of kind-identity and intersubjective checkability.

### Bounded example and framework limit

Within Euclidean geometry, a triangle can vary in size, colour, orientation and material inscription while remaining a closed three-sided plane figure. The familiar angle-sum property depends on the geometrical framework and should not be announced as the invariant of every possible geometry.

The example teaches two controls:

- variation is not arbitrary fantasy; the governing sense of the kind matters;
- a candidate essence must state its framework.

### Essential distinctions: eidetic and categorial intuition

**Eidetic intuition** (*Wesensschau*) is the claimed seeing of invariant structure through fulfilled variation. It must not be described as mystical.

**Categorial intuition**, developed in the Sixth *Logical Investigation*, concerns the givenness of states of affairs and categorial formations beyond isolated sensory items. It is related to but not simply identical with the later variation-based account.

### Formal and regional ontology

| Domain | Concern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Formal ontology | forms applying across domains | object, property, relation, whole and part |
| Regional/material ontology | essential structures of a specific region | physical thing, consciousness, cultural object |

Phenomenology aims to clarify how such domains are given and structured. It does not thereby prove that every proposed regional essence is infallible.

### Comparison controls

- **Against separated Platonism:** the invariant is disclosed through instances and acts, not located in another world.
- **Against crude Kant-assimilation:** Husserl does not merely accept a fixed inventory of categories; he uses intuitive and variational analysis of diverse regions.
- **Against psychologism:** the act of grasping is temporal; the essence or ideal content is not identical with that act.

### Objection → reply → residual

**Objection:** Different investigators may vary examples differently and claim incompatible essences.

**Reply:** Eidetic evidence is tested through explicit variation, counterexamples, clarification of the governing framework and intersubjective repeatability.

**Residual:** These controls improve accountability but do not yield an algorithm guaranteeing one uncontested essence.

### UPSC use

- **2024:** explain essences as correlational invariants, not detached objects.
- **Theory-of-essences answer:** the marked content is the procedure, not merely the term *Wesensschau*.
- **W09 anchors:** W09-27–W09-34, W09-39–W09-40.
- **Trap:** a felt certainty is not sufficient evidence.

### Revision notes

1. Essence is invariant whatness, not an empirical average.
2. An act discloses an essence but does not causally create its validity.
3. Husserlian essence is not safely equated with a separated Platonic Form.
4. Free imaginative variation tests accidental and necessary features.
5. Variation differs from induction.
6. One example can begin eidetic inquiry.
7. Framework dependence must be stated.
8. Eidetic intuition is claimed fulfilment, not mysticism.
9. Categorial intuition and later variation should not be collapsed.
10. Formal ontology differs from regional/material ontology.

### Retrieval and application drill

**Retrieval:** Give the four-step variation procedure.

**Model:** Select an example, vary features freely, test whether kind-identity survives, and retain as candidate essence what cannot be removed without collapse—then counter-test it.

**Application:** Why is “all triangles have a 180-degree angle sum” a careless essence example?

**Model:** The claim is framework-dependent; it holds in Euclidean geometry and cannot be treated as invariant across every possible geometry.

**Misconception to avoid:** Eidetic necessity is not statistical frequency.

*The essence of a kind of object includes lawful modes in which it can be given, while the act is described through the kind of object it intends.*

### Concept

The **correlational a priori** is the claim that structures of intending and structures of the object-as-intended belong together. An object is not reduced to an act, and an act is not turned into the object. The relation is one of essential correlation.

For a spatial thing:

- consciousness proceeds through limited profiles;
- the thing is given as possessing more than the currently visible profile;
- internal horizons prescribe possible further appearances;
- synthesis identifies the profiles as belonging to one thing.

Thus the essence of “perception of a spatial thing” specifies both a mode of consciousness and a type of being-as-given.

### Argument and presuppositions

1. A spatial object cannot be perceived from every side at once.
2. Yet it is intended as one object with more sides than are currently visible.
3. Perspectival acts and horizonal object-structure therefore imply one another.
4. Eidetic description articulates this relation.
5. Continuity between consciousness and being means structural correlation, not numerical identity.

The argument presupposes that “being” in the question is being as phenomenologically disclosed, not a complete inventory of mind-independent reality apart from every possible mode of access.

### Essential distinctions: three claims to keep separate

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Descriptive | this object appears through profiles and horizons |
| Eidetic/ontological | a spatial thing is essentially available through indefinitely further profiles |
| Transcendental-idealist | the sense and validity of “objective spatial thing” are constituted within subjectivity |

The first does not automatically prove the third. A high-quality answer states the transition rather than smuggling it in.

### Bounded example and limit

Walking around a building discloses new sides while confirming one identity. The visible front, anticipated back and possible viewpoints form a lawful unity.

**Limit:** this account clarifies the conditions under which a building is experienced as objective. It does not by itself decide whether its existence is wholly dependent on actual consciousness.

### Objection → reply → residual

**Objection:** Correlation makes being dependent on consciousness and therefore collapses into idealism.

**Reply:** Constitution concerns the articulation and validation of object-sense, not fabrication of empirical existence. The object’s resistance, inexhaustibility and availability to other subjects prevent reduction to private content.

**Residual:** The reply establishes distance from subjective idealism, but debate remains over whether transcendental constitution leaves sufficient independence to being.

- **Trap:** do not make noema a third object mediating two otherwise sealed substances.

1. Correlational a priori links act-structure and object-as-intended structure.
2. Correlation is not identity.
3. Spatial perception is necessarily perspectival.
4. The spatial thing exceeds every current profile.

## Lesson 9 — What is wrong with psychologism?

**Progress: 9/11 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: logical validity, ideal content and four arguments**

### Visual: the category mistake

```text
REAL ACT OF THINKING                    IDEAL CONTENT / VALIDITY
datable • causal • fallible             repeatable • normative • non-datable
"I judge p at 3 p.m."                   proposition p / inference form
            \                              /
             \                            /
              X  PSYCHOLOGISM CONFUSES  X

CAUSE OF BELIEF ≠ GROUND OF TRUTH
```

*Psychologism confuses contingent facts about thinking with standards governing whether thought is valid.*

### Concept

Psychologism reduces logical laws to empirical laws of how human beings actually think. It may claim that non-contradiction expresses a psychological inability, that truth is what our species must believe, or that logic is a branch of descriptive psychology.

Husserl’s early *Philosophy of Arithmetic* (1891) pursued a psychologistic route through acts of collecting and counting. Frege’s 1894 review sharply criticised the slide from objective content to subjective presentation. It is historically unsafe to claim that this review alone caused Husserl’s reversal. By the *Prolegomena to Pure Logic* in *Logical Investigations* (1900–01), Husserl had mounted a systematic attack.

### Argument: the four anti-psychologistic arguments

| Argument | Reconstruction | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Necessity versus probability | empirical psychological laws are inductive and defeasible; logical validity claims necessity | the necessary cannot be grounded in merely probable regularity |
| Ideal content versus real act | the same proposition can be thought in many datable acts | content is not identical with any occurrence |
| Relativism and self-refutation | species-relative logic makes truth relative, yet the thesis is asserted as valid | psychologism undermines its own claim |
| Cause versus ground | causal explanation of why one believes does not justify the belief | genesis of belief differs from norm of validity |

### Argument and presuppositions

The whole case depends on a distinction between:

- a real act in time;
- an ideal meaning that can be repeated;
- a normative relation of validity.

Husserl must also explain how ideal contents are accessible without placing them in an inaccessible second world. Phenomenology studies acts of meaning and fulfilment while refusing to reduce validity to those acts.

### Essential distinctions

| Pair | Distinction |
|---|---|
| psychological regularity / logical law | contingent description of thinking / necessary norm of valid inference |
| real act / ideal content | datable event / repeatable meaning |
| causal compulsion / justification | why belief occurs / why it is warranted |
| access / validity | a subject grasps a meaning / the meaning is not true because this subject grasps it |

### Bounded example and limit

Two people can judge “2 + 2 = 4” at different times. Their acts differ causally and psychologically, but the proposition and its validity are not two private products.

**Limit:** this establishes that validity is not identical with empirical psychology. It does not yet provide a complete metaphysics of ideal objects.

### Objection → reply → residual

**Objection:** Logic still has to be grasped by minds. Why not say its laws depend on mental constitution?

**Reply:** Conditions of access differ from grounds of validity. A proof may be understood in a temporal act, but what makes the inference valid is not the causal history of that act.

**Residual:** Husserl must later show that transcendental constitution does not reintroduce dependence in a stronger form. That is Lesson 10.

### UPSC use

- **2020:** present all four arguments and connect them to phenomenological analysis of acts and ideal meanings.
- **2022:** define psychologism, then move to the transcendental worry.
- **W09 anchors:** W09-41–W09-48.
- **Trap:** avoiding psychologism does not mean excluding consciousness from philosophical study.

### Revision notes

1. Psychologism makes logic dependent on empirical mental facts.
2. Husserl’s early arithmetic project had psychologistic features.
3. Frege’s review sharpened the problem, but sole causal responsibility is disputed.
4. Psychological laws are inductive; logical laws claim necessity.
5. A proposition is not identical with any datable act of judging.
6. Species-relative truth threatens self-refutation.
7. Causes of belief differ from grounds of validity.
8. Ideal objectivity does not require a naïve separate realm.
9. Phenomenology studies access without reducing validity to access.
10. The transcendental turn creates a further objection that must be answered separately.

### Retrieval and application drill

**Retrieval:** State the four arguments in eight words or fewer each.

**Model:** probability cannot ground necessity; act differs from content; relativism self-refutes; cause differs from justification.

**Application:** A psychologist discovers that humans systematically commit a fallacy. What follows for logic?

**Model:** The discovery explains a recurrent cognitive cause; it does not convert the fallacy into a valid inference.

**Misconception to avoid:** Anti-psychologism is not the claim that thinking has no psychological conditions.

## Lesson 10 — Does transcendental constitution revive psychologism?

**Progress: 10/11 | Stage: Core | Subtopic: constitution, ideal validity and critical verdict**

### Visual: the objection and the three-part defence

```text

                    |
                    v

                    |

      |             |             |

      \             |             /
       \            |            /

                    v

```

*Husserl’s defence distinguishes the constitutive field from an empirical mind, but the success of that distinction remains contestable.*

### Concept

The transcendental turn appears to create a reflexive danger. Early Husserl insists that ideal truths are not products of empirical mental acts. Later Husserl says that every object-sense and validity is constituted in transcendental subjectivity. Does the later claim restore subject-dependence at a higher level?

### The objection

1. Psychologism makes validity depend on subjectivity.
2. Transcendental phenomenology locates constitution in subjectivity.
3. Therefore transcendental phenomenology appears to be a refined psychologism.

The argument is forceful only if “subjectivity” and “constitution” keep the same meanings in both premises.

### Husserl’s three replies

**1. Empirical psyche is not transcendental subjectivity.**
The psyche is an object in the world, studied through empirical causal laws. Transcendental subjectivity is the field in which “world,” “psyche,” “truth” and “objectivity” have sense.

**2. Constitution is not fabrication.**
To constitute is to articulate, disclose and validate sense through lawful modes of givenness. It is not for a private subject to invent that 2 + 2 = 4.

**3. Objectivity is intersubjective.**
Validity is not grounded in one person’s preference. The object is available in principle to other subjects, and meanings can be fulfilled, corrected and repeated.

### Essential distinctions

| Pair | Distinction |
|---|---|
| empirical psyche / transcendental subjectivity | causal object in the world / condition-field of worldly sense |
| constitution / fabrication | articulation and validation / private invention |
| intersubjectivity / consensus | possible shared access and correction / whatever a group happens to agree |
| anti-psychologism / realism | rejection of empirical reduction / a further ontological thesis not automatically proved |

### Argument and presuppositions

The reply presupposes:

- a defensible difference between transcendental and empirical subjectivity;
- a non-creative sense of constitution;
- an account of other subjects strong enough to secure world-for-anyone;
- ideal meanings whose validity is not exhausted by any act of access.

### Bounded example and limit

A proof is understood through acts of reading and judging. Its steps acquire explicit sense in those acts, yet the proof is not valid because this reader happens to feel compelled.

**Limit:** “constitution is disclosure” can become a verbal assurance unless the specific structures of fulfilment, repeatability and intersubjective correction are shown.

### Objection → reply → residual

**Objection:** The distinction between empirical and transcendental subjectivity simply renames the problem. If all objectivity is constituted, independence has still been subordinated to subjectivity.

**Reply:** Phenomenology does not reduce objects to private states. Objects exceed each profile, resist expectations, support correction and are intended as available to others.

**Residual:** This defeats a crude subjective-idealism charge. It does not force every realist to accept transcendental idealism, especially while the ownness-first account of others remains disputed.

### UPSC use

- **2022:** the critical half begins here; definition plus four early arguments alone is incomplete.
- **2020:** mention the worry briefly only after the basic anti-psychologism case is established.
- **W09 anchors:** W09-49–W09-53.
- **Verdict line:** Husserl coherently distinguishes empirical causal psychology from transcendental conditions of sense, but the intersubjective and realist adequacy of the constitutive framework remains open.

### Revision notes

1. The transcendental turn creates a reflexive anti-psychologism problem.
2. Empirical psyche is a worldly causal object.
3. Transcendental subjectivity is a condition-field of sense and validity.
4. Constitution must not be equated with creation.
5. Access to ideal meaning does not cause its validity.
6. Intersubjective repeatability blocks private preference.
7. Fulfilment and disappointment constrain intending.
8. The ownness-first route to others remains contested.
9. The defence is stronger than subjective idealism but not an automatic proof of realism.
10. A graded verdict must state both coherent distinction and unresolved residual.

### Retrieval and application drill

**Retrieval:** Give the three-part defence against transcendental psychologism.

**Model:** Transcendental subjectivity is not empirical psyche; constitution is disclosure rather than fabrication; objectivity is intersubjective and corrigible.

**Application:** Why is “truth is constituted” an unsafe standalone sentence?

**Model:** Without distinguishing sense-disclosure from causal creation, it sounds as though a subject manufactures truth and therefore invites the psychologism objection.

**Misconception to avoid:** The empirical/transcendental distinction is a reply to be defended, not a magic label that ends criticism.

### 2018 boundary: Heidegger

The exact 2018 question is primarily owned by Existentialism because it asks how Heidegger rejects phenomenological reduction and replaces the transcendental ego with being-in-the-world. Topic 09 supplies only the Husserlian premise:

- suspension brackets the world-existence posit;
- reduction redirects inquiry to constituting subjectivity;
- the method does not literally deny the world.

**Bounded doctrinal objection:** Heidegger’s pressure on that premise is that Dasein is constitutively **being-in-the-world**, not a self-sufficient subject that subsequently enters a relation with a world. On this objection, transcendental reduction abstracts from an existential condition of inquiry and then redescribes world-involvement from the standpoint of constituting subjectivity.

**Fair Husserlian reply:** Epoché is not an attempt to remove the inquirer from every worldly involvement. It suspends the unexamined use of the world-posit so that worldhood, embodiment, horizons and intersubjective validity can themselves become themes. The reduction can therefore be defended as a reflective change of attitude rather than the production of a worldless subject.

**Residual problem:** Even if being-in-the-world remains available as a phenomenon, the critic may still ask whether making it intelligible through transcendental subjectivity preserves the subject-priority under challenge. That residual identifies the limit of the reduction; it does not license a broad exposition of Heidegger’s positive ontology here.

The complete Heideggerian account is not needed to make Husserl’s Core complete, and no further Topic 10 doctrine is imported.

### 2026 boundary: Sartre

The 2026 comparison is primarily owned by Existentialism. The Husserlian half is:

- consciousness is intentional;
- mature transcendental phenomenology retains a constituting ego or ego-pole;
- intentional life includes synthesis, habituality and world-correlation.

Sartre’s consciousness-as-nothing, non-egological position and full comparison belong to the paired owner. They are not imported as the solution to Husserl’s solipsism or psychologism problems.

### Bounded example and limit

In a 2026 comparison, Topic 09 may state that mature Husserl retains a constituting ego-pole and that consciousness is intentional. It should then stop before reconstructing Sartre’s full non-egological theory. The limit is ownership, not relevance: enough comparison is included to identify Husserl accurately, but not enough to convert this session into Existentialism.

### Objection → reply → residual

**Objection:** Later phenomenology shows that Husserl’s project fails because reduction cannot isolate a pure ego from world, body, history or language.

**Reply:** A criticism of completeness need not invalidate every descriptive achievement. Profiles, horizons, fulfilment, act/content distinction and variation can remain illuminating even if transcendental idealism is rejected.

**Residual:** The method may survive more securely as disciplined clarification than as a fully presuppositionless foundational science.

## A1. Static versus genetic phenomenology and passive synthesis

Static analysis asks how an object and its act-correlates are structured in a constituted field. Genetic analysis asks how such sense, familiarity and availability become established through temporal, habitual and passive syntheses. The distinction is not “description versus causal psychology.”

| Question | Static emphasis | Genetic refinement |
|---|---|---|
| What is analysed? | act, object-as-intended, profile, horizon and fulfilment as a structured correlation | the temporal and habitual formation through which a correlation becomes familiar and available |
| Explanatory direction | display the dependencies within a constituted sense | trace the genesis and sedimentation of that sense within intentional life |
| Agency | explicit acts can be isolated for description | not every synthesis is initiated by an active judgement |
| Safe relation | gives a structural cross-section | deepens the account by adding formation without cancelling structure |

**Passive synthesis** names unbidden unifications and associations operating beneath explicit acts. Temporal synthesis and habituality show why consciousness is not a sequence of isolated decisions. The pairing involved in appresentation is one bounded Core example: association can organise a field before a deliberate inference is made.

**Objection:** Once phenomenology explains genesis, habit and passive association, it appears to import empirical psychology and lose its claim to transcendental clarification.

**Reply:** The genetic question remains phenomenological when it describes how sense is passively constituted and sedimented, rather than giving a natural-scientific causal history of a psyche.

**Residual:** Calling the genesis “transcendental” does not automatically prove that every psychological presupposition has been removed. The relation between lived formation and empirical causation still requires discipline.

**Deployment/use:** Add one paragraph after a complete method account in a 15- or 20-marker asking about the development of phenomenology, passive synthesis, habit or the limits of active constitution. State: static analysis gives the structure; genetic analysis explains its formation.

**Stop/omit rule:** Omit this refinement in a short method answer unless genesis is directly relevant. Do not inventory manuscript debates, and do not claim that genetic phenomenology simply replaces the earlier method.

## A2. Competing interpretations of the noema

Core remains fixed: noesis is the act-mode, and noema is safely rendered as the **object-as-intended/sense-correlate**, not a private picture. Optional depth begins only with the question of how that correlate should be interpreted.

| Interpretation | What it stresses | Explanatory gain | Pressure it faces |
|---|---|---|---|
| ideal-sense reading | an ideal sense through which an object is intended | explains how one object can be meant under different determinations and how sense is repeatable | may appear to insert a third entity between act and world |
| object-as-meant reading | the object itself precisely as intended in this act | resists a representational veil and preserves worldly directedness | must explain non-veridical cases and act-specific differences without assuming successful reference |
| appearance-structure reading | the organised manner of presentation, including profile and horizon | keeps analysis close to the experienced structure of appearing | may narrow noema to appearance when judging and other acts intend more than a sensory profile |

A single tree can be perceived as nearby, remembered as formerly present or doubted as misidentified. The interpretations agree that the acts differ in noetic mode; they disagree over whether the noematic pole is best explained as ideal sense, object-as-meant or structured appearance.

**Objection:** Any noema distinct from the act recreates a veil of representation.

**Reply:** None of the three readings requires a private image. Each attempts to specify the correlate within intentionality, and the object-as-meant reading explicitly treats worldly directedness as primary.

**Residual:** The interpretations distribute the risks differently; no neutral formula decides every dispute. Hence the Core wording should remain interpretation-aware rather than pretending consensus.

**Deployment/use:** Use one interpretation contrast only after intentionality and noesis/noema have been defined. It is most useful when rebutting “noema = inner image” or qualifying a 15/20-marker on correlation.

**Stop/omit rule:** Omit the dispute in a 10-marker unless noema is directly asked. Do not turn the answer into a catalogue of commentators or technical noematic components; give the contrast, its consequence and return to the directive.

## A3. Does the late life-world deepen or redirect the project?

The late life-world analysis generates a continuity dispute because it gives greater weight to pre-scientific experience, historical sedimentation and practical horizons while retaining a transcendental concern with the conditions of sense.

| Reading | Central claim | What it explains | Main pressure |
|---|---|---|---|
| continuity reading | life-world analysis deepens transcendental phenomenology | the forgotten horizon presupposed by scientific objectivism becomes a new field of constitutive clarification | can understate the new historical and worldly emphasis |
| late worldly/pragmatic turn | the life-world corrects an overly abstract ego-centred starting point | practice, inherited meaning and historical situation become philosophically central | risks portraying Husserl as simply abandoning reduction |
| mixed reading | the transcendental task continues but its reach expands to sedimented and historical sense | preserves continuity while recognising a genuine shift of emphasis | leaves the precise relation between transcendental ego and historical world contested |

**Objection:** Returning to a taken-for-granted world reverses epoché and restores the natural attitude.

**Reply:** The life-world is not naïve realism reinstated. It is the pre-given horizon whose meanings and operations science presupposes, now made thematic rather than used unexamined.

**Residual:** The reply shows compatibility in aim, but it does not conclusively settle whether the late historical emphasis merely extends or materially revises the earlier transcendental framework.

**Deployment/use:** In a method, science or life-world 15/20-marker, use this as one final evaluative paragraph: state the continuity debate, defend the life-world against the “reversal” charge, and preserve the residual.

**Stop/omit rule:** Omit this debate when the stem is confined to anti-psychologism or a short technical definition. Do not expand it into a general philosophy of history, and do not call the late work either a total abandonment or a completed reconciliation.

## PYQ 1 — 2019 Q1(b), 10 marks

**Exact question:** “Can hallucination be regarded as an intentional act by Husserl? Explain.”

### Demand decoding

- **Can:** give an immediate yes/no thesis.
- **Intentional act:** define directedness, not voluntary purpose.
- **Explain:** show why non-existence does not cancel intentional structure.

### Model answer

Yes. For Husserl, intentionality means that consciousness is always consciousness **of** something. Its defining feature is directedness, not the actual existence of the intended object. A hallucination therefore has a genuine intentional structure: there is a perceptual-seeming act and a determinate object-as-intended, such as a coloured figure at an apparent location.

The point becomes clearer through the distinction between intention and fulfilment. A veridical perception may be confirmed through coherent further profiles, practical interaction and intersubjective checking. A hallucination can fail these fulfilment conditions while still remaining directed. Hence intentionality is broader than successful reference.

This does not require a special hallucinated entity to exist in a private realm. The noematic correlate is safest understood as the object-as-intended or sense-structure, not as an inner picture inserted between subject and world. Hallucination is therefore intentional, though epistemically defective when compared with fulfilled perception.

### Why this earns marks

It answers “yes” directly, defines intentionality, uses noesis/noematic correlation cautiously, distinguishes existence from fulfilment and blocks the private-image mistake.

## PYQ 2 — 2020 Q1(c), 10 marks

**Exact question:** “What, according to Husserl, is wrong with psychologism? How does Husserl address the problems with psychologism in his phenomenological method?”

### Demand decoding

- Define the error.
- Give arguments, not a slogan.
- Show how phenomenology studies acts without reducing validity to acts.

### Model answer

Psychologism treats logical laws as empirical generalisations about how human beings in fact think. Husserl objects that this confuses real psychological acts with ideal meanings and norms.

First, psychological laws are inductive and at best probable, whereas logical laws claim necessity. Second, a datable act of judging differs from the repeatable proposition judged. Third, species-relative logic produces relativism: the thesis itself is asserted as valid beyond the species-bound habits it describes. Fourth, a causal account of why a person believes something is not a ground showing that the belief is true.

Phenomenology addresses the problem by analysing intentional acts and their fulfilment while preserving ideal objectivity. Meaning is accessed through consciousness but not manufactured by an empirical psyche. The method therefore avoids both crude Platonism and reduction to mental events.

The later transcendental turn creates a further worry because constitution occurs in subjectivity. Husserl’s reply is that transcendental subjectivity is not empirical psychology and constitution means disclosure of sense, not invention. This reply is coherent, though its full success depends on intersubjective objectivity.

### Why this earns marks

It covers both limbs, reconstructs all four objections, connects method to ideal validity and ends with the exact transcendental qualification.

## PYQ 3 — 2021 Q4(a), 20 marks

**Exact question:** “Present a critical exposition of Husserl’s criticism of ‘natural attitude’. How does Husserl propose to address the problems involved in natural attitude through his phenomenological method?”

### Demand decoding

- **Critical exposition:** explain, reconstruct, object and assess.
- Treat the natural attitude as unexamined, not simply false.
- Explain the methodological repair and its limits.

### Model answer

The natural attitude is the ordinary and scientific stance in which the world is tacitly posited as existing independently and already containing determinate objects. Its “general thesis” is not a constantly repeated proposition but the practical commitment that the world is simply there. Husserl does not call this stance false: everyday action and empirical science require it. His criticism is philosophical. The attitude presupposes the subject–world relation whose sense and validity foundational inquiry must clarify.

Husserl first employs suspension (*epoché*). The world-existence posit is placed out of use, but the experienced content is retained. The tree still appears coloured, spatial and perspectival; phenomenology neither asserts nor denies its independent existence. Phenomenological reduction then redirects attention to intentional givenness—acts, object-senses, profiles, horizons and syntheses.

Further operations must be distinguished. Eidetic variation seeks invariant structures by imaginatively changing factual features. Transcendental reduction asks how object-sense and validity are constituted for subjectivity. The method thereby prevents naturalism from reducing meaning to causal events and prevents an immediate Cartesian inference from self-givenness to a mental substance.

The repair has three gains. It institutes a changed evidential attitude, opens inquiry into essential structures, and makes constitution itself a problem. Yet it faces objections. Reflection may presuppose the embodiment, language and world-involvement it attempts to bracket. The transcendental ego can also appear to relocate rather than overcome subject-centred idealism. Husserl can answer that suspension is a repeatable methodological neutralisation, not physical withdrawal, and that constitution is constrained by fulfilment and intersubjective correction.

The result is best graded. Husserl successfully shows that ordinary and scientific objectivity carries unexamined meaning-conditions. His claim to a completely presuppositionless reduction is less secure. The natural attitude is therefore philosophically surpassed as an uncritical starting point, not abolished as the indispensable horizon of life and science.

### Why this earns marks

It addresses both question limbs, distinguishes four operations, gives philosophical gains, presents the strongest objections and closes with a qualified rather than total verdict.

## PYQ 4 — 2022 Q1(d), 10 marks

**Exact question:** “What is psychologism? Critically discuss the way Edmund Husserl avoids the problem of psychologism in the discourse of transcendental phenomenology.”

### Demand decoding

- Define psychologism.
- Explain early anti-psychologism.
- Make the transcendental-turn objection central to the critical discussion.

### Model answer

Psychologism is the reduction of logical validity to empirical facts about how human beings think. Husserl’s *Prolegomena* rejects it because psychological laws are probable rather than necessary, real acts differ from ideal contents, species-relative truth is self-undermining, and causes of belief are not grounds of validity.

Transcendental phenomenology nevertheless seems exposed to the same problem. If all sense and objectivity are constituted in subjectivity, truth may again appear subject-dependent.

Husserl replies, first, that transcendental subjectivity is not the empirical psyche investigated by psychology. It is the field in which “world,” “object” and “validity” have sense. Second, constitution is not fabrication: an act discloses and fulfils an ideal meaning but does not create its validity. Third, objectivity is intersubjective; meanings are repeatable, corrigible and available in principle to other subjects.

The reply successfully distinguishes empirical causal explanation from transcendental clarification. Yet its final adequacy depends on whether Husserl can constitute other subjects without circularly beginning from an ego-centred sphere. He avoids crude psychologism, but the charge that transcendental idealism retains a deeper subject-priority is not decisively eliminated.

### Why this earns marks

It defines the doctrine, compresses the four arguments, confronts rather than evades the transcendental objection and gives a balanced final judgement.

## PYQ 5 — 2023 Q3(b), 15 marks

**Exact question:** “Elucidate the significance of bracketing and reduction in Husserl’s phenomenological method.”

### Demand decoding

- “Significance” asks why the operations matter.
- Distinguish the operations.
- State the philosophical gain and operational limit.

### Model answer

Bracketing and reduction are significant because they convert an unexamined worldly stance into a philosophical inquiry into givenness. In the natural attitude, common sense and science posit the world as simply existing. Suspension (*epoché*) neutralises this existence-posit without denying the world or deleting the experienced content.

The phenomenological reduction is the positive redirection that follows: attention moves to intentional experience, including act-modes, object-senses, profiles, horizons and fulfilment. It therefore permits inquiry into how objectivity is meaningful rather than treating subject and object as ready-made substances.

Two further operations must not be collapsed into a mechanical sequence. Eidetic reduction uses free imaginative variation to disclose invariants. Transcendental reduction asks how sense and validity are constituted for transcendental subjectivity. Together, these operations matter in three ways. They establish a changed evidential attitude, block the immediate Cartesian inference from consciousness to a mental substance, and open eidetic and constitutive questions.

The method remains contested. A subject attempting reduction is already embodied, linguistic and situated; complete suspension may therefore be impossible. Husserl can reply that reduction is a disciplined, repeatable change of attitude rather than literal exit from the world. Its success can be partial yet philosophically revealing.

Thus bracketing is not sceptical destruction but methodological neutralisation, while reduction is the constructive route to intentional, eidetic and transcendental analysis. Their significance survives even if absolute presuppositionlessness remains an ideal rather than a completed achievement.

### Why this earns marks

It converts definitions into purposes, distinguishes the reductions, supplies the required critical limit and concludes with a graded methodological verdict.

## PYQ 6 — 2024 Q4(b), 15 marks

**Exact question:** “Why does Husserl think that essences exhibit a kind of continuity between consciousness and being? Discuss.”

### Demand decoding

- “Why” requires the correlational argument.
- “Continuity” must not become identity.
- Use one worked case and assess idealist pressure.

### Model answer

Husserl treats an essence as an invariant structure disclosed through free imaginative variation. Essences are neither empirical mental events nor separated Platonic objects. Their distinctive role is to articulate the lawful relation between a mode of consciousness and the kind of object given through it.

This is the correlational a priori. Consider perception of a spatial thing. Consciousness can see only one profile at a time, while co-intending unseen sides through an internal horizon. A synthesis identifies changing appearances as profiles of one enduring object. The essence of spatial perception therefore specifies both how consciousness must proceed—perspectivally and synthetically—and what the spatial thing is as given—an identity exceeding each appearance and open to further profiles.

Continuity consequently means structural correlation. Consciousness and being are distinguishable, but neither pole is adequately described in isolation. Eidetic analysis does not manufacture the empirical object; it clarifies the invariant relation between object-sense and modes of givenness.

The account improves upon a private-image theory because the object resists expectations, supports correction and is available to other possible subjects. Yet it does not conclusively dissolve the realism–idealism dispute. If all object-sense is constituted in transcendental subjectivity, critics may still ask whether being retains sufficient independence.

Husserl’s continuity is thus neither identity nor causal production. It is the a priori correlation through which a kind of object and its possible modes of appearance belong together, with transcendental idealism remaining the unresolved critical horizon.

### Why this earns marks

It explains imaginative variation, reconstructs the profile–horizon example, defines continuity negatively and positively, and addresses the idealism objection.

## PYQ 7 — 2025 Q4(a), 20 marks

**Exact question:** How is Husserl’s account of “I think” different from that of Descartes? Critically discuss.

### Demand decoding

- Begin with the common radical first-person aspiration.
- Compare on fixed axes rather than writing two mini-essays.
- Make substance, world-access, temporality and intersubjectivity explicit.

### Model answer

Descartes and Husserl both seek a radical beginning in first-person self-givenness. Each suspends ordinary confidence in the world to expose what cannot be eliminated from inquiry. Yet their accounts of “I think” differ in procedure, ontology and relation to objectivity.

Descartes’ methodic doubt withholds assent from what can be doubted. The cogito establishes the thinker, which is characterised as a *res cogitans*, a thinking substance distinct from extended substance. Knowledge of the external world is subsequently rebuilt through clear and distinct ideas and divine non-deception.

Husserl’s suspension has another purpose. It brackets the natural attitude’s existence-posit without denying or testing the world. Phenomenological reduction redirects attention to intentional life: every cogito is a cogito of something. Transcendental reduction therefore discloses not a worldly mental substance but the constituting field in which object-sense and validity arise.

The Husserlian subject is also temporally structured. Retention, primal impression and protention synthesise an enduring stream; habitualities and sedimented meanings exceed a bare formal instant. Further, objectivity requires intersubjectivity. In the Fifth *Cartesian Meditation*, another subject is appresented through bodily pairing and continuing behavioural confirmation. Descartes’ central bridge is from mind to world; Husserl’s is from constituting ego to world-for-anyone.

Husserl gains by avoiding an immediate substance inference and by retaining the intentional world within the very structure of consciousness. However, the transcendental turn creates its own difficulty. If every other and every object-sense is constituted for my ego, phenomenology risks transcendental solipsism. Appresentation explains why the other is not originally given as my own stream, but the ownness-first route may circularly presuppose social meaning.

Husserl’s “I think” is therefore intentional, temporal and intersubjectively oriented rather than a self-enclosed thinking thing. He moves beyond Cartesian substance dualism, but the constitutive priority of transcendental subjectivity leaves a refined idealist problem rather than eliminating every form of subject-centredness.

### Why this earns marks

It uses stable comparison axes, includes commonality and difference, gives named Husserlian structures, treats intersubjectivity critically and returns a graded ruling.

# ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS PRACTICE

## Original 10-marker

**Question:** Distinguish phenomenological description from the ontological claim that only appearances exist. Answer in about 150 words.

### Model answer

Phenomenological description asks how an object is given: through which act-mode, profile, horizon and possible fulfilment. Suspension places the ordinary existence-posit out of use so that this structure can be examined without assuming a ready-made subject–object relation.

The procedure does not entail that only appearances exist. The seen tree retains colour, spatial location and anticipated unseen sides; phenomenology neither proves nor disproves its independent existence during the reduction. Description is therefore methodologically neutral about the factual thesis it brackets.

Husserl’s later transcendental idealism is a stronger claim. It holds that object-sense and validity are constituted within transcendental subjectivity. Even this should not be reduced to subjective idealism, because the object exceeds each act, can disappoint expectation and is intended as available to other subjects.

The distinction is consequently threefold: description clarifies appearing; ontology concerns what exists; transcendental inquiry concerns conditions of meaningful objectivity. The first does not automatically establish either a realist or an idealist ontology.

### Why this earns marks

It defines both sides, explains suspension, distinguishes a method from the later transcendental thesis and avoids equating idealism with private fabrication.

## Original 15-marker

**Question:** “Phenomenological reduction clarifies science but cannot replace it.” Discuss. Answer in about 200 words.

### Model answer

Phenomenological reduction and natural science answer different questions. Science operates within the natural attitude: it posits a world and explains events through causal laws, measurement and experiment. Reduction brackets the use of that existence-posit and asks how objects, evidence and validity are given.

The method clarifies science in three ways. First, intentional analysis distinguishes an object from any single profile and explains the horizonal structure presupposed by observation. Second, anti-psychologism protects logical validity from reduction to contingent cognitive habits. Third, life-world analysis shows how scientific idealisations arise from embodied and intersubjective operations such as measuring, recording and communicating.

This clarification does not replace empirical inquiry. Eidetic variation cannot determine a particle’s mass, and life-world analysis cannot decide between rival experimental hypotheses. Conversely, a causal account of neural processing does not by itself explain what it means for one object to be intended as identical across profiles.

The main objection is that grounding science in subjectivity makes validity anthropocentric. Husserl replies that constitution is not fabrication: scientific meanings are repeatable, corrigible and open to other subjects. The reply preserves a distinction between access and validity, though the movement from historically sedimented practices to universal objectivity remains contested.

Reduction therefore supplies philosophical clarification of scientific sense and evidence, while science retains autonomous empirical methods.

### Why this earns marks

It compares functions, gives three named contributions, sets a firm operational limit and evaluates the subjectivity objection.

## Original 20-marker

**Question:** Critically examine the claim that Husserl’s phenomenology secures objectivity by making consciousness and being internally correlated. Answer in about 300 words.

### Model answer

Husserl begins from intentionality: consciousness is always consciousness of something. In *Ideas I* vocabulary, a noetic act is correlated with an object-as-intended or noematic sense. This correlation is not a relation between an inner picture and a hidden external object. It identifies the lawful unity of mode of intending and mode of givenness.

Spatial perception illustrates the claim. Only one profile of a building is originally seen, while unseen sides are co-intended through a horizon. A synthesis identifies changing profiles as appearances of one object. Eidetic analysis therefore discloses a correlational a priori: perspectival consciousness and a spatial thing that exceeds every present appearance imply one another.

The theory supports objectivity in several ways. An object resists arbitrary intention, can fulfil or disappoint anticipation, and remains inexhaustible by one act. Intersubjectivity adds availability to perspectives other than mine. Anti-psychologism further separates ideal meanings and logical validity from datable empirical acts.

Yet correlation raises two objections. First, if every object-sense is constituted in transcendental subjectivity, being may seem subordinated to consciousness. Husserl replies that constitution articulates and validates sense; it does not fabricate empirical existence. Second, intersubjectivity is reached from a sphere of ownness through pairing and appresentation. The account explains why another stream is non-originally given, but it may presuppose the social sense it seeks to constitute.

A graded verdict should distinguish descriptive and transcendental success. The analyses of profiles, horizons, fulfilment and repeatable meanings show why objectivity cannot be reduced either to one sensation or to private preference. They do not conclusively establish that transcendental constitution supplies a complete ontology of independent being.

Husserl thus secures a robust phenomenological objectivity: object-sense is lawful, resistant, corrigible and intersubjective. Whether this amounts to a final reconciliation of consciousness and being remains the contested idealist residue of the programme.

### Why this earns marks

It reconstructs the correlational argument, connects intentionality, eidetics, intersubjectivity and anti-psychologism, gives two objections with replies and distinguishes descriptive success from ontological closure.

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

## A. Project, phases and claim-levels

- Syllabus spine: **Method · Theory of Essences · Avoidance of Psychologism**.
- Aim: philosophy as rigorous science through accountable givenness.
- “To the things themselves” = return to subject matters as presented, not naïve physicalism.
- Descriptive claim: how something appears.
- Eidetic/ontological claim: invariant structure of a kind.
- Transcendental claim: conditions under which object-sense and validity arise.
- Description alone does not prove that only appearances exist.
- Transcendental idealism is not Berkeleyan private fabrication.
- *Logical Investigations* (1900–01): anti-psychologism and early intentional analysis.
- *Ideas I* (1913): suspension, reduction, transcendental turn, noesis–noema.
- *Cartesian Meditations*: French first publication 1931; German posthumous 1950.
- *Crisis*: Parts I–II 1936; unfinished; complete text 1954.

## B. Natural attitude and reductions

- Natural attitude = world tacitly posited as simply there.
- It includes common sense and normal natural science.
- It is not false; it is philosophically unexamined.
- Suspension (*epoché*) withholds use of the existence-posit.
- Experienced content remains fully describable.
- Phenomenological reduction redirects inquiry to modes of givenness.
- Eidetic reduction uses imaginative variation to disclose invariants.
- Transcendental reduction asks about constituting subjectivity.
- These are related but not one mechanical chronology.
- Epoché differs from Cartesian doubt: neutralisation for description versus doubt in a search for certainty.
- Operational limit: complete presuppositionlessness is contested.
- Safe verdict: change of attitude succeeds more securely than total withdrawal.

## C. Intentionality and objectivity

- Intentionality = consciousness-of, not deliberate intention.
- Noesis = act-mode.
- Noematic correlate = object-as-intended/sense-correlate.
- Noema is not safely equated with a private picture.
- Non-existent objects can be intended.
- Hallucination is intentional but may lack coherent fulfilment.
- Profiles give the object incompletely.
- Internal horizon = further possible profiles of this object.
- External horizon = surrounding meaningful world.
- Synthesis identifies changing appearances as one object.
- Fulfilment confirms or enriches; disappointment corrects.
- Evidence is graded and dynamic.

## D. Material and temporal synthesis

- Hyletic material = lived non-intentional sensuous content.
- Intentional formation = apprehensional organisation into object-directed experience.
- Hyle–morphe is not the same distinction as noesis–noema.
- Hyletic data are not perceived intermediary objects.
- Retention = just-past still held in perception.
- Primal impression = current now-phase.
- Protention = anticipation of the about-to-come.
- Retention is not recollection.
- Melody example demonstrates temporal unity.
- Residual: self-constitution of the temporal flow risks reflexive difficulty.

## E. Transcendental subjectivity and intersubjectivity

- Empirical person = worldly body, biography and psyche.
- Transcendental subjectivity = field of sense-constitution.
- It is neither *res cogitans* nor empirical psychology.
- Descartes and Husserl share a radical first-person aspiration.
- Descartes infers thinking substance; Husserl suspends mundane ontology.
- Descartes rebuilds world-knowledge; Husserl analyses world-givenness.
- Husserlian ego is intentional and temporal.
- Other subject is appresented through bodily pairing.
- The other’s stream is necessarily non-originally given.
- Continuing behaviour confirms or disappoints appresentation.
- Objectivity gains the sense “available for anyone.”
- Residual: ownness-first constitution may be circular or ego-centred.

## F. Life-world and science

- Life-world = pre-theoretical world of practical, embodied and shared sense.
- Science abstracts and idealises from it.
- Idealisation is legitimate; objectivist forgetting is the target.
- Phenomenology clarifies scientific meaning and evidence.
- It cannot replace measurement, experiment or causal explanation.
- Sedimentation = inherited meanings and methods becoming taken for granted.
- Sense-origin differs from causal production of truth.
- Intersubjective repeatability protects against private relativism.
- Safe verdict: science is grounded in lived operations without being reduced to preference.

## G. Theory of essences

- Essence = invariant whatness.
- It is not an empirical average or datable act.
- It is disclosed through instances but not fabricated by acts.
- It is not a separated Platonic object.
- Procedure: example → variation → survival/collapse test → invariant → counter-variation.
- Variation differs from induction.
- One example can begin eidetic inquiry.
- Triangle claims require geometrical framework qualification.
- Eidetic intuition = claimed fulfilled disclosure of invariant.
- It is not mystical or self-certifying.
- Categorial intuition and later variation are related but phase-distinct.
- Formal ontology concerns cross-domain forms.
- Regional ontology concerns domain-specific essences.

## H. Consciousness–being continuity

- Correlational a priori links act-structure and object-as-intended structure.
- Continuity does not mean identity.
- Spatial thing example: profiles + horizons + synthesis.
- The object exceeds every current appearance.
- Essence articulates lawful togetherness of mode of intending and kind of object.
- Constitution concerns sense and validity, not simple empirical creation.
- Intersubjective availability strengthens objectivity.
- Residual: correlation reframes but may not settle realism versus idealism.
- 2024 answer line: “The continuity is structural correlation, not merger.”

## I. Anti-psychologism

- Psychologism = logic reduced to empirical laws of thinking.
- Early *Philosophy of Arithmetic* had psychologistic features.
- Frege’s 1894 review criticised act/content confusion.
- Do not claim the review alone caused Husserl’s turn.
- Argument 1: probable psychological law cannot ground necessary validity.
- Argument 2: real datable act differs from ideal repeatable content.
- Argument 3: species-relative truth is self-undermining.
- Argument 4: causal conditions of belief differ from grounds of truth.
- Phenomenology studies acts while preserving ideal objectivity.
- Anti-psychologism is not anti-psychology.

## J. Transcendental psychologism objection

- Worry: if all sense is constituted in subjectivity, truth again appears subject-dependent.
- Reply 1: transcendental subjectivity ≠ empirical psyche.
- Reply 2: constitution ≠ fabrication.
- Reply 3: objectivity is intersubjective, repeatable and corrigible.
- Evidence through fulfilment constrains intending.
- Strong result: crude empirical psychologism is avoided.
- Residual: transcendental subject-priority and ownness-first intersubjectivity remain contested.
- 2022 verdict: coherent distinction, incomplete final escape.

**Final qualified verdict:** Husserl establishes a powerful method for describing intentional givenness, identifying invariant structures and defending logical validity against empirical reduction. The programme is strongest when it distinguishes acts from contents, profiles from objects and sense-constitution from fabrication. It is least settled where complete reduction, transcendental idealism and the constitution of other subjects are asked to carry the full burden of a presuppositionless foundation.
