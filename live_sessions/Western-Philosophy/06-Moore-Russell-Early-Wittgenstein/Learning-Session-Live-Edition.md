## Lesson 1 — Three analytic projects, not one undifferentiated “analytic philosophy”

**Progress: 1/12 | Stage: Foundation | Core demands: W06-74–W06-78**

### Visual — the attribution firewall

```text
PHILOSOPHICAL TROUBLE
        │
        ├── Moore asks: Is the philosophical denial more certain than what I know?
        │      tools: truisms · comparative certainty · act/object distinction
        │
        ├── Russell asks: What logical form is hidden by surface grammar?
        │      tools: analysis · quantification · descriptions · constructions
        │
        └── Early Wittgenstein asks: What makes any proposition capable of representing?
               tools: picture · shared logical form · logical space · say/show limit

BOUNDARIES
early Wittgenstein ──not── later meaning-as-use/private-language philosophy
picture-theoretic sense ──not── Vienna Circle verificationism
```

*Caption: The thinkers share an analytic revolt, but their questions, methods and limits remain different.*

### Concepts at stake

- **Moore’s common-sense realism** begins from ordinary world-claims that he says are known, not from a completed metaphysical system.
- **Russell’s logical analysis** reconstructs a proposition so that apparent subjects may disappear and quantified structure becomes visible.
- **Early Wittgenstein’s transcendental-logical inquiry** asks what a proposition and reality must share for representation to be possible.
- “Analysis” therefore has three meanings here: clarification of ordinary commitments, revelation of logical form, and investigation of the conditions of representation.

### Arguments and presuppositions

1. Moore presupposes that some ordinary judgements possess greater credibility than premises used to deny them.
2. Russell presupposes that grammatical form may conceal genuine logical form and that contextual paraphrase can reveal ontological commitment.
3. Early Wittgenstein presupposes that representation requires a structural correspondence between proposition and possible reality.
4. The common analytic impulse is opposition to inflated metaphysics and obscure inference; it is not one common doctrine of meaning.

### Distinctions that must survive every answer

| Do not collapse | Exact distinction |
|---|---|
| Moore into Russell | Moore compares certainty and distinguishes act/object; Russell quantifies away misleading grammatical subjects. |
| Russell into early Wittgenstein | Russell combines logical analysis with acquaintance and phase-specific atoms; Wittgenstein characterises objects through their place in possible combinations. |
| Early into later Wittgenstein | The *Tractatus* seeks a general form of representation; language-games, family resemblance and private-language critique belong to the later syllabus owner. |
| Picture theory into verificationism | Early Wittgenstein asks whether a proposition pictures a possible fact; positivists later offer an empirical criterion of significance. |

### Bounded example and limit

The sentence “The present King of France is bald” invites Russell’s quantified analysis. “Here is one hand” invokes Moore’s epistemic burden shift. “The book is on the table” can illustrate Wittgensteinian picturing only at the abstract level of names arranged as objects might be arranged. None of these examples licenses transfer of the whole method to another thinker.

### Objection → reply → residual

| Objection | Reply | Residual |
|---|---|---|
| All three merely analyse language. | Their objects of analysis differ: ordinary certainty, quantified logical form, and conditions of representation. | “Analytic philosophy” remains a useful family label, not a single theory. |
| Early Wittgenstein naturally culminates in verificationism. | Possible factual picturing is not the same as empirical verification. | The *Tractatus* influenced positivism, but influence is not identity. |
| Later Wittgenstein solves every early problem. | The later work changes the project rather than supplying an internal premise of the early one. | A bounded contrast may close an answer; it must not replace Core exposition. |

### UPSC use

Open comparison answers by fixing an axis: **epistemic certainty**, **logical reconstruction**, or **condition of representation**. Then compare on that axis. Never write three independent biographies.

### Revision notes (10)

1. Moore defends ordinary knowledge; he does not begin with quantified paraphrase.
2. Russell exposes logical form hidden by grammar.
3. Early Wittgenstein explains the possibility and limits of propositional representation.
4. Moore’s realism is not a complete theory of perception.
5. Russell’s phases must not be fused into one invariant ontology.
6. Wittgensteinian objects are not simply Russellian sense-data.
7. Meaning-as-use belongs to later Wittgenstein.
8. Verificationism belongs to Logical Positivism.
9. A comparison needs one explicit criterion.
10. Attribution errors destroy otherwise knowledgeable answers.

### Retrieval and application drill

1. **Retrieve:** Which thinker owns the hierarchy-of-languages proposal?
   **Answer:** Russell proposes it in his Introduction to the *Tractatus*; it is not Wittgenstein’s solution.
2. **Apply:** A question asks why a meaningful proposition can be false. Which project controls?
   **Answer:** Early Wittgenstein’s picture theory and bipolarity.
3. **Discriminate:** Is “surface grammar may mislead” enough to identify the thinker?
   **Answer:** No. It points strongly to Russell, but Wittgenstein also distinguishes sign from represented possibility; name the exact mechanism.

## Lesson 2 — Moore’s defence of common sense: truisms and comparative certainty

**Progress: 2/12 | Stage: Core | Core demands: W06-01–W06-05**

### Visual — Moore’s reversal of philosophical burden

```text
ORDINARY TRUISM
“I have a body” / “The earth existed before this moment” / “Other persons exist”
        ↓ known with very high certainty, Moore claims
PHILOSOPHICAL ARGUMENT DENIES IT
        ↓
COMPARATIVE TEST
Are the sceptic’s premises more certain than the truism denied?
        ├── No → reject the philosophical conclusion
        └── Yes → Moore would owe reconsideration

KEY FIREWALL:
knowing P ≠ possessing a complete philosophical analysis of P
```

*Caption: Moore does not derive the world from neutral premises; he ranks the certainty of competing claims.*

### Concepts at stake

**Common sense** does not mean popularity, inherited custom, or whatever most people say. It names ordinary propositions Moore claims each person knows: that a body exists, that the earth has existed for years, that other embodied persons exist, and that ordinary events occurred.

**Comparative certainty** is the method. An argument that concludes “I do not know that I have a body” must rely on premises more credible than the ordinary knowledge it overturns. Moore doubts that scepticism can meet that standard.

### Argument and presuppositions

1. There are ordinary propositions which Moore claims to know with certainty.
2. Philosophical reasoning sometimes yields conclusions inconsistent with them.
3. Rational acceptance should track the more certain side.
4. The truisms are more certain than the abstract premises used to deny them.
5. Therefore, the philosophical argument rather than the ordinary judgement should be rejected.

**Presuppositions:** certainty can be comparatively assessed; ordinary perceptual and memorial judgement can count as knowledge without an antecedent anti-sceptical proof; philosophical premises receive no automatic priority merely because they are general.

### The knowledge/analysis distinction

| Knowing that P | Analysing P |
|---|---|
| “I have a body” can be known. | What “body,” “external,” or “material object” ultimately means may remain disputed. |
| Epistemic achievement | Conceptual or philosophical achievement |
| Need not wait for a theory | May remain incomplete without cancelling the knowledge |

The sceptic commits a level-confusion if failure to analyse “material object” is treated as proof that no material object is known.

### Bounded example and limit

Suppose a student cannot define “memory” without circularity but still remembers attending yesterday’s class. Moore’s point is not that every memory belief is infallible. It is that conceptual incompleteness alone does not defeat a particular claim to know.

### Objection → reply → residual

| Objection | Moorean reply | Residual |
|---|---|---|
| Listing truisms simply assumes what the sceptic denies. | The sceptic also needs premises, and none has been shown more certain than the ordinary claims. | This is a burden shift, not a neutral derivation accepted by both sides. |
| Common sense has often been wrong. | Moore’s list is not every popular belief; it is a restricted set of ordinary world-claims. | The criterion for membership in the privileged set still needs defence. |
| Certainty is psychological confidence. | Moore treats it as epistemic credibility, not intensity of feeling. | A full account of warrant is underdeveloped. |

### UPSC use

For “Why is Moore a common-sense realist?” write: **truisms → knowledge/analysis distinction → comparative certainty → burden reversal → qualified criticism**. “He believed what common people believe” earns almost no marks.

### Revision notes (10)

1. Moore’s truisms include earth, body and other persons.
2. Common sense is not opinion polling.
3. The method compares certainty.
4. Philosophical abstraction receives no automatic epistemic privilege.
5. Knowledge of P need not await analysis of P.
6. Moore rejects conclusions less certain than what they deny.
7. His realism is commitment to ordinary world-claims.
8. He does not provide a complete physical-world theory.
9. The strongest objection is dialectical question-begging.
10. The best defence is a burden-of-proof reversal.

### Retrieval and application drill

1. **Retrieve:** Name three classes of Moorean truism.
   **Answer:** One’s body, the long existence of the earth, and other embodied persons.
2. **Apply:** “You cannot define matter; therefore you cannot know that the desk exists.” Diagnose.
   **Answer:** It conflates knowledge with philosophical analysis.
3. **Evaluate:** Does Moore refute every possible sceptical argument?
   **Answer:** No. He claims the sceptic has not supplied premises more certain than the ordinary knowledge denied.

## Lesson 3 — “Here is one hand”: proof, burden shift and the paradox of analysis

**Progress: 3/12 | Stage: Core | Core demands: W06-06–W06-08, W06-16**

### Visual — the proof and its pressure point

```text
Moore raises RIGHT hand: “Here is one hand.”
                         +
Moore raises LEFT hand: “Here is another.”
                         ↓
At least two material things exist.
                         ↓
At least two things “to be met with in space” exist.
                         ↓
An external world exists.

VALIDATION GATE
[premise differs from conclusion]
[premise is known]
[conclusion follows]
                         ↓
SCEPTICAL PRESSURE: “But how do you know the premises?”
MOOREAN RESPONSE: knowledge need not be preceded by another proof.
```

*Caption: The inference can be valid while the dispute survives at Moore’s claim to know its premises.*

### Concepts at stake

Moore’s 1939 proof is distinct from the 1925 list of truisms. It offers an ostensive argument for external objects: first the right hand, then the left; therefore at least two external things exist.

### The three proof conditions

| Condition | Application |
|---|---|
| Premise and conclusion must differ | “Here is a hand” is not identical to “an external world exists.” |
| Premise must be known | Moore insists he knows that the presented items are hands. |
| Conclusion must follow | If hands are material things met with in space, external things exist. |

This is more than “I can see my hands.” Moore offers a structured proof with an explicit standard of rigour.

### Argument and presuppositions

1. Here is one hand.
2. Here is another.
3. Hands are material things encountered in space.
4. Therefore at least two external objects exist.
5. Therefore an external world exists.

The argument presupposes perceptual knowledge of the premises and the classification of hands as external objects. Moore does not derive those premises from a sceptic-neutral foundation.

### Bounded example and limit

Showing two books can prove that at least two books are in the room if one knows that they are books. It cannot persuade an interlocutor who suspends every perceptual entitlement. Moore’s achievement is to expose that radical standard as more demanding than ordinary proof, not to satisfy it on its own terms.

### The paradox of analysis — bounded method context

```text
“Brother” analysed as “male sibling”
        ├── exactly same meaning → apparently trivial
        └── different meaning → apparently not a correct analysis
```

The paradox shows why analysis cannot be mere word substitution. It is useful for explaining the analytic project, but it is not a separate printed syllabus limb and has no settled Moorean solution.

### Objection → reply → residual

| Objection | Reply | Residual |
|---|---|---|
| The proof begs the question because knowing the hand is exactly what scepticism disputes. | Moore denies that every known premise requires a further proof and demands a more certain sceptical premise. | No common starting point is created for the radical sceptic. |
| The argument is circular. | Premise and conclusion are different, so it is not formally circular. | Dialectical question-begging may remain even without formal circularity. |
| Ostension proves only appearances. | Moore claims knowledge of material hands, not merely hand-like appearances. | The perceptual warrant remains the contested point. |

### UPSC use

In the 2026 question, narrate the hands only briefly. Spend marks on the **three conditions**, the valid inference, the premise-knowledge challenge, and the qualified verdict: rigorous by Moore’s standard, not neutral between Moore and radical scepticism.

### Revision notes (10)

1. The hands proof belongs to 1939, not the 1925 paper.
2. Moore raises the right hand first and the left second.
3. Two hands imply at least two material objects.
4. Material objects establish an external world.
5. Premise and conclusion are distinct.
6. Moore claims the premises are known.
7. The conclusion follows if hands count as external things.
8. The sceptic attacks premise-knowledge, not merely validity.
9. Moore shifts the burden rather than deriving from neutral premises.
10. The paradox of analysis is bounded methodological context.

### Retrieval and application drill

1. **Retrieve:** List Moore’s three conditions of rigorous proof.
   **Answer:** Distinct premise/conclusion, known premise, valid consequence.
2. **Apply:** Is “An external world exists; therefore an external world exists” Moore’s proof?
   **Answer:** No. It fails the distinctness condition.
3. **Judge:** “The proof is invalid because a sceptic rejects the premise.”
   **Answer:** Too quick. Rejection of a premise challenges soundness or dialectical force, not the formal inference from hands to external objects.

## Lesson 4 — Moore’s refutation of idealism: awareness is not its object

**Progress: 4/12 | Stage: Core | Core demands: W06-09–W06-15, W06-76**

### Visual — blue, green and transparent awareness

```text
EXPERIENCE OF BLUE                    EXPERIENCE OF GREEN
object: BLUE                          object: GREEN
          \                           /
           \                         /
            shared relation: AWARENESS
                      ↓
awareness is common; objects differ
                      ↓
object of awareness ≠ act of awareness
                      ↓
“blue exists” ≠ “blue and consciousness exist”
                      ↓
esse cannot simply be identified with percipi
```

*Caption: Moore separates what is presented from the conscious relation in which it is presented.*

### Concepts at stake

Moore targets the idealist move expressed through *esse est percipi*—to be is to be perceived—and especially the alleged identity of the presented object with the act of consciousness.

Every sensation contains a distinction:

1. **Act or relation of awareness:** the subject’s being conscious.
2. **Object of awareness:** blue, green, a sound, or another presented quality.

Blue and green differ; awareness is what their experiences share. Therefore the objects cannot simply be identical with the common act.

### Transparency of consciousness

Awareness is “diaphanous” or transparent: when attention turns to an experience of blue, it seems to encounter blue rather than an additional inner object called “consciousness.” This explains the temptation to conflate act and object. Transparency is used to diagnose the idealist mistake, not to deny consciousness.

### Argument and presuppositions

1. Experiences of blue and green have a common element: awareness.
2. They differ in their presented objects: blue and green.
3. Therefore awareness is distinguishable from either presented object.
4. “Blue exists” does not contain the same assertion as “blue and consciousness exist.”
5. Hence existence cannot simply be defined as being perceived.

**Presupposition:** introspective discrimination can reveal the relational structure of experience. That claim is powerful but contestable.

### Moore’s two routes must remain separate

| Route | Target | Positive result | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Truisms/hands | Scepticism about external objects | Ordinary material things are known; burden shifts | Premise-knowledge remains disputed |
| Blue/awareness | Idealist identity of object and awareness | Act and object are distinct | Does not alone prove the object exists unperceived |

### Berkeley–Moore–Russell comparison half

| Position | Core move owned here |
|---|---|
| Berkeley | The pressure arises from tying sensible object to perception and mind. |
| Moore | Break the alleged identity of perceived object and conscious act. |
| Russell | Reconstruct external-object discourse from phase-specific actual and possible sense-data through logical constructions. |

Moore attacks an idealist inference. Russell offers a reductive/structural strategy for object-talk. They are not two versions of the same reply.

### Bounded example and limit

The same awareness relation can present a red patch and then a green patch. The difference in object does not establish that either colour exists when unperceived. It establishes only that the object is not identical with the act by which it is presented.

### Objection → reply → residual

| Objection | Moorean reply | Residual |
|---|---|---|
| Introspective distinguishability does not prove mind-independent existence. | Correct; the narrower conclusion is that object and awareness are not identical. | Realism requires further support, supplied separately by common-sense/external-world arguments. |
| “Blue exists” might still entail consciousness under an idealist theory. | Entailment is not identity of meaning; Moore attacks the claim that the two propositions are the same. | The idealist may reformulate dependence without strict identity. |
| The paper refutes Hegel’s whole Absolute Idealism. | No. Its immediate target is the act/object conflation associated with *esse est percipi*. | Broader idealisms need separate treatment. |

### UPSC use

For the 2020 or 2025 quotation, proceed: **name the target → distinguish act/object → explain blue/green → explain transparency → state anti-identification result → qualify the realist conclusion**.

### Revision notes (10)

1. Moore’s target is the identity of object and awareness.
2. Blue and green are different objects of sensation.
3. Awareness is common to both experiences.
4. Therefore awareness differs from either object.
5. “Blue exists” is not the same proposition as “blue and consciousness exist.”
6. Transparency explains why consciousness is easily overlooked.
7. The argument blocks a route to *esse est percipi*.
8. It does not alone prove unperceived existence.
9. It does not refute every Hegelian thesis.
10. Moore’s anti-idealist route differs from Russell’s construction strategy.

### Retrieval and application drill

1. **Retrieve:** What is shared by the sensations of blue and green?
   **Answer:** The conscious relation or act of awareness, not the presented object.
2. **Apply:** “Moore proves that blue exists outside every mind.” Correct it.
   **Answer:** Moore proves only that blue as object is not identical with awareness; mind-independent persistence needs further argument.
3. **Compare:** What does Russell add to the external-world debate?
   **Answer:** A phase-specific reconstruction of object-talk through actual and possible sense-data, rather than Moore’s introspective act/object distinction.

## Lesson 5 — Russell’s logical atomism: facts, propositions and analytical residues

**Progress: 5/12 | Stage: Core | Core demands: W06-17–W06-25**

### Visual — from misleading complexity to logical residue

```text
ORDINARY SENTENCE
surface subject + predicate + hidden connectives/quantifiers
        ↓ logical analysis
MOLECULAR PROPOSITION
truth-functional structure built from simpler propositions
        ↓ continued analysis
ATOMIC PROPOSITION
represents one atomic fact
        ↕ structural correspondence
ATOMIC FACT
particular–quality or relational configuration

“ATOMIC” = final logical residue in the analysis
           ≠ microscopic physical particle
```

*Caption: Russell’s atoms are logical endpoints—structured facts and their representing propositions—not tiny pieces of matter.*

### Concepts at stake

- A **fact** is what makes a proposition true or false; it is not itself true or false.
- A **proposition** represents how things stand and is a truth-bearer.
- An **atomic fact** contains no other facts as constituents, although it can contain particulars, qualities and relations.
- An **atomic proposition** represents an atomic fact without a logical connective.
- A **molecular proposition** is formed from propositions through logical operations.

### Atomic and molecular

| Feature | Atomic | Molecular |
|---|---|---|
| Form | No propositional connective | Built through “and,” “or,” “not,” “if” |
| Example | “This patch is red” | “This patch is red and that patch is blue” |
| Truth-dependence | Compared with an atomic fact | Determined by component truth-values and connective |
| Ontological lesson | Reveals a candidate simple configuration | Need not add an “and-object” or “or-object” to reality |

### Relational atoms and universals

Atomic does not mean monadic. “A is to the left of B” can represent a relational atomic fact containing two particulars and a relation. Russell’s logical residues may therefore include universals such as qualities and relations; they are not restricted to point-like sense-data.

### Structural correspondence

For a proposition to represent a fact, the proposition’s articulated constituents and arrangement must correspond to the fact’s constituents and arrangement. The sentence is not identical with the fact. It is a structured representation whose truth or falsity depends on the relevant fact.

### Negative facts

Russell’s account faces the truth of negative propositions. If “Socrates is not alive” is true, what makes it true? Russell accepts negative facts as a possible truth-making cost. Critics object that ontology now contains negative complexes in addition to positive facts.

### Method of logical analysis

Analysis is not the physical dissection of an object. It is the reconstruction of a proposition so that quantified form, genuine constituents and truth-dependencies replace misleading grammar. The endpoint is provisional: description theory can expose pseudo-constituents without proving that analysis terminates in one final inventory.

### Presuppositions and bounded example/limit

Russell presupposes that surface grammar can conceal objective logical articulation, that analysis can identify genuine constituents, and that molecular truth is grounded in simpler truth-conditions. “A is to the left of B” is a bounded relational example: it may be atomic without being constituent-less. The limit is methodological—nothing in the example proves that every proposition reaches one stable ultimate base.

### Russell and *Tractatus* atomism

| Axis | Russell | Early Wittgenstein |
|---|---|---|
| Atoms | Phase-specific acquainted particulars plus qualities/relations | Objects left unspecified, characterised by possible combinations |
| Ground | Logical analysis joined to epistemology | Conditions of propositional sense |
| Independence | Important but comparatively flexible | Strict independence of elementary propositions |
| Risk | Unstable simples and negative facts | Missing examples, colour incompatibility and self-application |

### Objection → reply → residual

| Objection | Russellian reply | Residual |
|---|---|---|
| “Atom” misleadingly suggests physics. | The atoms are results of logical analysis. | The actual final residues remain disputed. |
| Negative facts inflate ontology. | True negative propositions appear to require truth-makers. | Alternative accounts may avoid negative entities. |
| Description analysis proves atomism. | It removes apparent constituents and supports the search for genuine ones. | It neither proves termination nor establishes that all reality is atomic. |

### UPSC use

For the 2020 quotation, define “logical, not physical,” distinguish fact/proposition, include relational facts and universals, then assess negative facts. For 2022, begin with grammar/logical form and show why analysis only **supports**, rather than deductively establishes, atomism.

### Revision notes (10)

1. Russell’s atoms are logical, not physical.
2. Facts make propositions true or false; propositions, not facts, bear truth-values.
3. Atomic facts may contain particulars and universals.
4. Relational facts can be atomic.
5. Atomic propositions contain no propositional connective.
6. Molecular propositions are truth-functions of simpler propositions.
7. Sentence and fact are not identical.
8. Negative facts are Russell’s controversial truth-making cost.
9. Analysis reveals logical form beneath grammar.
10. Atomism is a provisional endpoint, not a proven exhaustive ontology.

### Retrieval and application drill

1. **Retrieve:** Why can an atomic fact have constituents?
   **Answer:** “Atomic” means that it contains no other facts as constituents, not that it has no particulars, qualities or relations.
2. **Apply:** Is “A loves B” necessarily molecular?
   **Answer:** No. It can represent one relational atomic fact if no propositional connective is involved.
3. **Evaluate:** What is the safest verdict on descriptions and atomism?
   **Answer:** Description analysis removes pseudo-constituents and supports the atomist search, but does not prove final simples or exhaustive atomism.

## Lesson 6 — Incomplete symbols and the theory of descriptions

**Progress: 6/12 | Stage: Core | Core demands: W06-26–W06-35**

### Visual — making an apparent subject disappear

```text
SURFACE GRAMMAR
“The present King of France is bald.”
      subject-like phrase       predicate
                    ↓ contextual analysis
∃x [Kx ∧ ∀y(Ky → y=x) ∧ Bx]
 │       │                    │
existence  uniqueness         predication
                    ↓
existence condition fails
                    ↓
whole proposition = FALSE
not meaningless · no ghostly king · no denoting object required
```

*Caption: A definite description contributes through the complete proposition and disappears as an independently denoting constituent.*

### Incomplete symbol

An **incomplete symbol** has no independent denotation or meaning in isolation but contributes systematically to the propositions in which it occurs. This does **not** mean “meaningless.” Its role is given by contextual analysis.

### The three-part analysis

“The F is G” means:

1. **Existence:** at least one F exists.
2. **Uniqueness:** at most one F exists.
3. **Predication:** that unique F is G.

Formal schema:

`∃x [Fx ∧ ∀y(Fy → y=x) ∧ Gx]`

For “The present King of France is bald,” the existence condition fails; therefore Russell classifies the analysed proposition as false.

### Presuppositions and bounded limit

The analysis presupposes that quantificational paraphrase captures the sentence’s truth-conditions and that apparent subject-position need not mark a genuine constituent. The King and golden-mountain cases establish the power of this method for empty descriptions; they do not prove that every ordinary definite description has only one pragmatic or semantic use.

### The golden mountain

“The golden mountain is very high” expands to: exactly one golden mountain exists and it is very high. Since no such mountain exists, the conjunction is false. The phrase does not force a nonexistent golden mountain into the ontology.

### Negative existentials and scope

“The present King of France does not exist” is analysed as denial that there is exactly one present King of France. It can therefore be true without referring to a nonexistent subject.

| Scope | Paraphrase | When no F exists |
|---|---|---|
| Description wide, negation narrow | Exactly one F exists and it is not G | False |
| Negation wide, description inside | It is not the case that exactly one F exists and is G | True |

The distinction protects excluded middle by showing that superficially similar negations have different logical forms.

### George IV and Scott

“George IV wished to know whether Scott was the author of *Waverley*” is not equivalent to “George IV wished to know whether Scott was Scott.” “The author of *Waverley*” is quantificational, not a logically proper name. Substitution therefore does not preserve the attitude-context in the simple way surface grammar suggests.

### Strawson’s bounded challenge

Strawson argues that in ordinary use the King-of-France sentence may presuppose, rather than assert, a unique king. Failed presupposition may yield a truth-value gap rather than Russellian falsehood. This is a genuine rival diagnosis, but its positive theory belongs to the Quine–Strawson owner.

### Objection → reply → residual

| Objection | Russellian reply | Residual |
|---|---|---|
| Empty descriptions are meaningless. | Their quantified analyses are intelligible and evaluable. | Ordinary-language use may involve presupposition rather than simple assertion. |
| The theory only handles existence. | It includes existence, uniqueness and predication, plus scope. | Natural-language descriptions may be pragmatically more varied. |
| Contextual elimination is a trick. | It resolves negative existential, excluded-middle and substitution puzzles without nonexistent objects. | The semantic completeness of the analysis remains contestable. |

### UPSC use

Always write all three components. In a 10-marker, one formula plus its verbal translation is enough. In a 15/20-marker, add scope and one bounded Strawson objection. Never call the sentence meaningless.

### Revision notes (10)

1. Incomplete symbols function only in propositional context.
2. “Incomplete” does not mean meaningless.
3. Definite descriptions are not ordinary logical names.
4. Analysis requires existence, uniqueness and predication.
5. The King-of-France sentence is false for Russell.
6. No nonexistent king need be denoted.
7. The golden-mountain sentence receives the same structure.
8. Negative existentials negate an existential-unique analysis.
9. Scope resolves the excluded-middle puzzle.
10. Strawson offers presupposition as a rival account.

### Retrieval and application drill

1. **Retrieve:** State the three clauses in “The F is G.”
   **Answer:** At least one F, at most one F, and that unique F is G.
2. **Apply:** Why can “The present King of France does not exist” be true?
   **Answer:** It denies the whole existential-unique condition rather than predicating nonexistence of a denoted object.
3. **Evaluate:** What is Strawson’s narrow challenge?
   **Answer:** Failed reference may produce presupposition failure and no truth-value, not Russellian falsehood.

## Lesson 7 — Acquaintance, description and logical constructions

**Progress: 7/12 | Stage: Core | Core demands: W06-36–W06-46**

### Visual — Russell’s epistemic and ontological economy

```text
DIRECTLY PRESENTED ITEMS
sense-data · introspective data · memory data · universals
        ↓ acquaintance
CONSTITUENTS AVAILABLE FOR UNDERSTANDING
        ↓ contextual/quantified construction
DISCOURSE ABOUT UNMET OR COMPLEX ITEMS
Bismarck · physical objects · scientific objects
        ↓
KNOWLEDGE BY DESCRIPTION / LOGICAL CONSTRUCTION

MAXIM:
substitute constructions from known entities
for inferences to unknown entities wherever possible
```

*Caption: Description explains indirect knowledge; construction seeks economy by rebuilding discourse from an accepted base.*

### Acquaintance versus description

| Acquaintance | Description |
|---|---|
| Direct, presentational and non-inferential | Indirect knowledge through a uniquely identifying condition |
| Phase-specific candidates: sense-data, introspective and memory data, universals | Physical objects, other minds, historical persons, scientific unobservables |
| Only `this`/`that` used for items present in acquaintance count as logically proper names in Russell’s 1910s view | “The F,” and ordinary names treated as disguised descriptions, are contextually analysed |

### Logically proper names versus ordinary names

In Russell’s 1910s view, only `this` and `that`, when used for items presently given in acquaintance, count as logically proper names. Ordinary names such as `Bismarck` or `Aristotle` are abbreviated or disguised descriptions: understanding a proposition containing them does not require the bearer himself to be an acquainted constituent.

### Principle of Acquaintance

Russell’s principle states that every proposition we can understand must be composed wholly of constituents with which we are acquainted. Yet we understand propositions about Bismarck without direct acquaintance with him. The theory of descriptions preserves the principle by showing that “the first Chancellor of the German Empire” need not contain Bismarck himself as a constituent.

### The Bismarck gradient

```text
Bismarck himself → acquaintance with his own presented self-consciousness
person who met him → acquaintance with relevant sense-data
reader of history → knowledge by description only
```

The example shows why description is not defective knowledge; it is the ordinary route to absent, historical or theoretical objects.

### Presuppositions and bounded limit

The Principle of Acquaintance presupposes directly presented items and a constituent-based model of understanding. The Bismarck series illustrates Russell’s 1910s reduction of ordinary names to descriptions; it does not prove that reduction or show that private presentation can by itself secure public objectivity.

### Logical construction

Russell’s maxim is to substitute constructions from known entities for inferences to unknown entities wherever possible. In the relevant phase, a physical object is reconstructed through ordered actual and possible sense-data from different perspectives. A construction is not a magical creation and not a mere fiction. It allows ordinary object-statements to remain true without adding an independently posited entity beyond the construction-base.

### Atom, construction and incomplete symbol

| Logical atom | Logical construction | Incomplete symbol |
|---|---|---|
| Treated as basic in the relevant analysis | Derived structure built from a base | Expression whose contribution is defined only in context |
| Candidate final constituent | Adds no extra independent entity | Disappears under analysis |
| Ground level | Reconstructed discourse | Linguistic mechanism enabling reconstruction |

### Russell’s phases

The acquaintance/sense-data/construction programme belongs chiefly to the 1910s. Russell’s later neutral monism must not be projected backward as if every work expressed one stable ontology.

### Objection → reply → residual

| Objection | Russellian reply | Residual |
|---|---|---|
| Private sense-data make public objects impossible. | Construction is a systematic theoretical reconstruction, not denial of science or common objects. | The route from private presentation to public objectivity remains strained. |
| A name such as `Aristotle` can retain its bearer even if every associated description is false. | Russell can retreat to logically proper names: only `this`/`that` used for present acquaintance name directly. | Description logic survives, but the description theory of ordinary names is weakened. |
| Eliminating apparent names proves a sufficient atomist base. | It secures economy and removes pseudo-constituents. | Sufficiency of sense-data or simples remains unproved. |

### UPSC use

For incomplete-symbol-to-atomism questions, write the chain: **acquaintance principle → contextual elimination of descriptions → logical constructions → genuine residues → qualified atomist support**. The qualification is indispensable.

### Revision notes (10)

1. Acquaintance is direct and non-inferential.
2. Description is indirect and condition-based.
3. The Principle of Acquaintance constrains understandable propositions.
4. Description prevents an unmet object from being a direct constituent.
5. Bismarck is Russell’s canonical graded example.
6. Logical constructions implement ontological economy.
7. Physical objects are reconstructed from actual/possible sense-data in a phase-specific programme.
8. Constructions are not fictions.
9. Atoms are basic; constructions are derived; incomplete symbols are contextually defined expressions.
10. Russell’s later phases must not be folded into the 1910s system.

### Retrieval and application drill

1. **Retrieve:** Which expressions count as logically proper names in Russell’s 1910s view, and how are ordinary names treated?
   **Answer:** Only `this`/`that` used for present acquaintance name directly; ordinary names such as `Bismarck` are treated as disguised descriptions.
2. **Apply:** Why does the theory of descriptions support the Principle of Acquaintance?
   **Answer:** It removes the described object as a constituent and retains acquainted universals plus logical form.
3. **Evaluate:** Does construction make tables imaginary?
   **Answer:** No. It analyses table-talk through a more economical base; ordinary claims may remain true.

## Lesson 8 — Early Wittgenstein: the world of facts and the picture theory

**Progress: 8/12 | Stage: Core | Core demands: W06-47–W06-52**

### Visual — how a proposition pictures

```text
OBJECTS
possible combinatorial capacities
        ↓ arranged in a possible combination
STATE OF AFFAIRS (Sachverhalt)
        ├── obtains → FACT
        └── does not obtain → possible but non-actual situation

PROPOSITION AS PICTURE
names/elements  ───────── correspond to ───────── objects
arrangement of names ─── mirrors possible ────── arrangement of objects
shared condition ──────── LOGICAL FORM ───────── shared structure

obtains → proposition true
does not obtain → proposition false but meaningful
```

*Caption: Sense precedes truth: a proposition can meaningfully depict a possibility that reality does not actualise.*

### World, objects, states of affairs and facts

“The world is the totality of facts, not of things” means that a mere inventory of objects does not tell us how the world is. Objects possess possibilities of combination. A **state of affairs** is a possible combination; when it obtains, it contributes to the facts constituting the world.

Translation must be controlled. *Sachverhalt* is rendered “atomic fact” in the Ogden/Ramsey tradition and “state of affairs” by Pears–McGuinness. Declare a convention and use it consistently.

### Proposition as logical picture

1. Elements of the picture correspond to objects.
2. Their arrangement presents a possible arrangement of objects.
3. The picture and the possible situation share pictorial form.
4. Every possible picture and reality share the most abstract representational form: logical form.
5. Reality decides truth or falsity; structure makes truth-assessment possible.

### Presuppositions

The account presupposes simple objects, determinate possibilities of combination and a shared structure sufficient for representation. A model-railway example can illustrate arrangement, but it must not be inflated into visual resemblance or treated as an example of the final simple-object analysis.

### True and false meaningful pictures

A false proposition is not meaningless merely because the depicted situation does not obtain. It has sense if it presents a possible configuration. This yields the decisive order:

```text
possible representation → sense
comparison with reality → truth or falsity
```

### Pictorial form and logical form

| Pictorial form | Logical form |
|---|---|
| The specific form a picture must share with what it depicts | The universal structural form shared by any representation and possible reality |
| A spatial model needs spatial representational resources | A proposition, diagram or model must share logical possibility-structure |
| Medium-sensitive | Condition of representation as such |

Logical form cannot be represented as one more contingent fact, because every act of representation already presupposes it. It therefore **shows itself** in meaningful propositions.

### Bounded example and limit

A model railway can spatially picture the arrangement of trains. A proposition is not necessarily a visual likeness; its elements and arrangement logically articulate a possibility. The analogy must not reduce logical form to physical shape.

### Objection → reply → residual

| Objection | Tractarian reply | Residual |
|---|---|---|
| False propositions picture nothing. | They picture a possible state that does not obtain. | The account requires a defensible space of possibilities. |
| A sentence does not resemble a fact. | Picturing is structural correspondence, not visual similarity. | The nature of simple objects and final analysis remains unspecified. |
| Logical form can simply be described. | Any description is itself a representation already using the form. | This produces the saying/showing and self-application problem. |

### UPSC use

For the 2022 question, first distinguish object, state of affairs and fact. Then explain correspondence and false meaningful pictures. Only after that distinguish pictorial and logical form and derive why logical form is shown rather than said.

### Revision notes (10)

1. The world is the totality of facts, not a heap of things.
2. Objects have possibilities of combination.
3. A state of affairs is a possible combination.
4. An obtaining state contributes to fact.
5. Names correspond to objects.
6. Arrangement of names presents a possible arrangement of objects.
7. False propositions can still possess sense.
8. Pictorial form is representation-specific.
9. Logical form is the universal condition of picturing.
10. Logical form shows itself and cannot be pictured as another fact.

### Retrieval and application drill

1. **Retrieve:** What decides whether a meaningful picture is true?
   **Answer:** Whether the pictured state of affairs obtains in reality.
2. **Apply:** Why is a false map-like representation still intelligible?
   **Answer:** Its elements and arrangement present a possible configuration even though reality does not match it.
3. **Discriminate:** Is logical form a further object named by “logical form”?
   **Answer:** No. It is the structural condition that every meaningful representation already manifests.

## Lesson 9 — Bipolarity, logical space and the general form of the proposition

**Progress: 9/12 | Stage: Core | Core demands: W06-53–W06-59**

### Visual — the machinery of sense

```text
ELEMENTARY PROPOSITIONS
postulated as mutually independent pictures
        ↓ occupy alternatives in
LOGICAL SPACE
the field of possible combinations
        ↓ each genuine proposition has
BIPOLARITY
capable of truth AND capable of falsity
        ↓ complex propositions generated truth-functionally
GENERAL FORM
informal: “This is how things stand.”
formal: [p̄, ξ̄, N(ξ̄)]
        ↓ boundary cases
tautology / contradiction = sinnlos (senseless, factually empty)
pseudo-proposition = unsinnig (nonsensical)
```

*Caption: Picture theory becomes an account of sense only when possibility, polarity and truth-functional generation are joined.*

### Bipolarity, not mere bivalence

**Bivalence** says a proposition has one of two truth-values. **Bipolarity** says a proposition with sense must be capable of being true and capable of being false. A representation that cannot possibly fail does not depict a contingent situation.

Early Wittgenstein’s bipolarity and picture conditions influenced verificationist exclusion of non-factual discourse, but they are not themselves the later Vienna Circle verification principle.

Consequences:

1. Truth and falsity require comparison with reality.
2. Tautologies cannot be false and contradictions cannot be true; they say nothing factual.
3. Elementary propositions are postulated as logically independent so that truth-value combinations represent possible configurations.

### Logical space

Logical space is not a physical region. It is the structured field of possibilities in which a proposition fixes a location by excluding alternatives. To understand `p` is also to grasp the possibility `not-p`.

Objects determine possibilities through their combinatorial capacities. A proposition’s sense locates one possible arrangement among alternatives.

### Negation and logical constants

Negation does not name a negative object. Logical constants operate on propositions rather than representing additional worldly entities. Wittgenstein’s “fundamental idea” is that logical constants are not representatives.

### Truth-functional construction and N

Complex propositions are generated from elementary propositions through truth-operations. The general form at proposition 6 is:

`[p̄, ξ̄, N(ξ̄)]`

- `p̄`: the elementary proposition base.
- `ξ̄`: a selected set of already formed propositions.
- `N(ξ̄)`: joint negation—none of the selected propositions is true.

The informal form at 4.5 is “This is how things stand” in the Pears–McGuinness rendering.

### Pressures on the programme

| Presupposition | Pressure |
|---|---|
| Elementary propositions exist | No uncontested example is supplied. |
| They are mutually independent | “This patch is red all over” excludes “This patch is blue all over.” |
| Every proposition is a truth-function of the base | Quantification, modality and belief-contexts create difficulties. |
| N can generate the needed forms | Infinite or multiply general cases remain disputed. |

Colour exclusion is an internal pressure on elementary independence. It does not license importing later meaning-as-use as if it were already the *Tractatus* solution.

### *Sinnlos* and *unsinnig*

| Status | Meaning | Example-type |
|---|---|---|
| *Sinnlos* | Senseless: well-formed limiting case that says nothing contingent | Tautology or contradiction |
| *Unsinnig* | Nonsensical: fails to picture a possible factual situation | Metaphysical pseudo-proposition |

### Objection → reply → residual

| Objection | Tractarian reply | Residual |
|---|---|---|
| Tautologies are meaningless gibberish. | They show logical scaffolding though they say nothing factual. | “Showing” must be explained without turning it into another factual claim. |
| Colour incompatibility refutes every possible atomism. | It refutes an easy identification of apparent colour statements as independent elementary propositions. | A deeper analysis might be attempted, but no settled Core rescue is supplied. |
| N establishes complete expressive power. | It presents a unifying truth-functional ambition. | Quantification and intensionality pressure completeness. |

### UPSC use

Whenever picture theory is critically examined, do not stop at “propositions picture facts.” Add bipolarity, logical space and the general form; then identify one internal pressure.

### Revision notes (10)

1. Bipolarity is capacity for truth and falsity.
2. It is stronger than bivalence and is not the Vienna Circle verification principle.
3. Logical space is a space of possibilities, not a physical area.
4. Sense locates a proposition among alternatives.
5. Logical constants do not name objects.
6. Complex propositions are truth-functions of an elementary base.
7. N is joint negation.
8. The informal general form is “This is how things stand.”
9. Colour exclusion pressures elementary independence.
10. *Sinnlos* must not be confused with *unsinnig*.

### Retrieval and application drill

1. **Retrieve:** Why is a tautology *sinnlos*?
   **Answer:** It cannot be false and therefore says nothing about which contingent possibility obtains.
2. **Apply:** “Logical space is outside the world.” Correct it.
   **Answer:** Logical space is the field of possible configurations articulated by objects and propositions, not an external location.
3. **Evaluate:** What does colour exclusion directly pressure?
   **Answer:** The claimed logical independence of apparently elementary propositions.

*Caption: Silence follows from the picture-theoretic limit and the ladder, not from dislike of difficult subjects.*

### What factual language can say

Propositions say how things contingently stand. They can be true or false because they picture possible facts. They cannot step outside representation to state the form that every representation and possible fact share.

### What shows itself

- Logical form manifests in meaningful propositions.
- Internal relations are exhibited through symbolism.
- Tautologies show logical structure without stating facts.
- On a standard reading, ethics, aesthetics, value and the mystical are not facts in the world and cannot be propositionally stated.

The unsayable is not simply empirically false. Nor is the distinction the logical positivist verification principle.

### Presuppositions, bounded example and limit

The distinction presupposes that genuine saying is bipolar factual representation and that logical form is a condition rather than another represented item. A tautology is the bounded case: it can display logical scaffolding while saying nothing contingent. The limit is the book’s own apparent attempt to state this distinction, which generates the ladder problem.

### Standard and resolute readings

| Reading | Treatment of *Tractatus* remarks |
|---|---|
| Standard/ineffability | The remarks guide apprehension of genuine but unsayable form or value. |
| Resolute/therapeutic | There is no body of ineffable truths; the reader recognises the apparent doctrines as nonsense and abandons the temptation. |
| Shared minimum | Ethics and logical form are not ordinary factual propositions in logical space. |

### The ladder and self-application

The book appears to state the limits of stating. Proposition 6.54 acknowledges the tension: its propositions serve as elucidations to be recognised as nonsensical and thrown away after use. The ladder does not erase the problem; it makes the problem methodologically explicit.

### Proposition 7

The Ogden rendering is: “Whereof one cannot speak, thereof one must be silent.” The Pears–McGuinness rendering is: “What we cannot speak about we must pass over in silence.” Name the translation when quoting.

Proposition 7 has no 7.1. It follows the ladder and stands without subordinate comments.

### Objection → reply → residual

| The doctrine is self-refuting. | 6.54 presents the sentences as elucidatory steps rather than final factual theses. | Critics may still ask how nonsense can elucidate. |
| Ethics is dismissed as worthless. | Value is not classified as a contingent fact; on the standard reading its importance exceeds factual statement. | Resolute readings resist treating this as an ineffable doctrine. |
| Silence is a verificationist ban. | It follows from picturing and logical form, not an empirical test of meaning. | Positivists later transform some Tractarian pressures. |

### UPSC use

For 2018 or 2021, derive the distinction rather than listing examples: **picture → shared logical form → form cannot be pictured → saying/showing → self-application → ladder → qualified verdict**.

## Lesson 11 — Can a hierarchy of languages meet Wittgenstein’s difficulty?

**Progress: 11/12 | Stage: Advanced Core | Core demands: W06-66–W06-73**

### Visual — Russell’s ascent and Wittgenstein’s recurring limit

```text
L1: object language
cannot state its own structure
        ↓ described by
L2: metalanguage
can formalise L1 syntax/truth
but cannot finally state L2’s own form
        ↓ described by
L3: meta-metalanguage
        ↓
... no final language

NARROW SUCCESS: local syntax, semantic definition, self-reference control
GLOBAL FAILURE: no standpoint outside representation as such
VALUE FAILURE: ethics/mystical are not higher-level syntax
```

*Caption: The hierarchy solves a formal-semantic problem at each level but does not secure a final view of representational form as such.*

### Wittgenstein’s difficulty

1. A proposition can picture because it shares logical form with reality.
2. A picture cannot depict its own pictorial form; it displays it.
3. Therefore propositions say contingent situations while logical form shows itself.
4. The *Tractatus* nevertheless appears to state the limit.
5. The ladder acknowledges the self-application problem.

This is structural, not a mere shortage of vocabulary.

### Presuppositions and bounded example

Wittgenstein presupposes that shared form is internal to representation; Russell’s hierarchy presupposes that languages can be treated as formal levels and that ascent preserves the subject. A truth-definition for a specified L1 is the bounded success-case. It cannot be generalised into a view from outside every representational form.

### Russell’s proposal

Russell suggests that although L1 cannot say its structure, L2 may describe L1, L3 may describe L2, and so on without a final language. The proposal belongs to Russell’s Introduction, not to Wittgenstein as his accepted resolution.

### Narrow success

A richer metalanguage can formalise the syntax and semantic conditions of a specified object language. Tarski’s later, independent object-language/metalanguage discipline strengthens the formal case for separating levels. It must not be presented as Wittgenstein’s own answer or as contemporary with the *Tractatus*.

### Remaining global limit

Each metalanguage has a structure it does not finally characterise from outside itself. Because the hierarchy is open, no last language states the limit of representation as such. The problem is displaced upward.

Ethics, value and the mystical are not pieces of object-language syntax waiting to be redescribed in L2. A hierarchy of factual languages does not make non-factual value into a fact.

### Verdict matrix

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Can L2 describe L1 syntax? | Yes |
| Can a richer language define truth for a formalised lower language? | Yes, as later formal semantics shows |
| Is there a final language of all language? | No |
| Can the hierarchy represent logical form as such from nowhere? | No |
| Does it turn ethics/value into factual propositions? | No |
| Does it remove the ladder problem? | At most partially |

### Internal criticisms of the early programme

- No uncontested examples of simple objects or elementary propositions.
- Colour exclusion pressures elementary independence.
- The general truth-functional programme faces quantification and intensional contexts.
- The ladder produces a self-application paradox.

These are internal criticisms. Later meaning-as-use is only a bounded contrast, not a Core repair.

### Objection → reply → residual

| Objection | Reply | Residual |
|---|---|---|
| What is shown in L1 is simply said in L2. | Some local structural facts can be said in L2. | L2’s own form remains manifested; no final standpoint appears. |
| Infinite hierarchy is harmless. | It may be harmless for local formal work. | It is fatal to the claim of a completed statement of language’s total limit. |
| Tarski proves Wittgenstein wrong. | Tarski addresses truth-definition and paradox in formalised languages. | He does not show that value or logical form as such becomes factual. |
| Later Wittgenstein accepts the hierarchy. | Later work abandons the demand for one general representational form. | That later dissolution is outside Core ownership. |

### UPSC use

The 2026 directive demands a declared stand. Use: **partial agreement**. The hierarchy is a genuine solution to a formal-semantic problem and an evasion of the transcendental problem. State both before concluding.

### Revision notes (10)

1. Wittgenstein’s difficulty arises from shared logical form.
2. A picture cannot depict its own form.
3. Russell proposes L1, L2, L3 and no final language.
4. The hierarchy is Russell’s proposal, not Wittgenstein’s resolution.
5. Metalanguage can formalise lower-language syntax.
6. Tarski is later and independent.
7. Every metalanguage has its own unfinalised form.
8. No final language states representation’s global limit.
9. Ethics and mystical value are not higher-order syntax.
10. Best verdict: formal-semantic success, transcendental failure.

### Retrieval and application drill

1. **Retrieve:** What exactly does L2 gain over L1?
   **Answer:** Resources to state or formalise selected syntactic and semantic features of L1.
2. **Apply:** Does L2 escape all representational form?
   **Answer:** No. It has a form of its own and remains within representation.
3. **Evaluate:** Give the one-line 2026 verdict.
   **Answer:** The hierarchy meets the local formal difficulty but displaces rather than dissolves the global Tractarian limit.

*Caption: Each thinker has a distinctive starting point, transformation and qualified conclusion.*

### Master comparison

| Axis | Moore | Russell | Early Wittgenstein |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem | Scepticism and idealist conflation | Misleading grammar and ontological inflation | Conditions and limits of propositional sense |
| Starting point | Ordinary knowledge and experience | Propositions requiring analysis | Possibility of representation |

### Russell versus Wittgenstein

Russell asks what genuine constituents remain after analysis and ties the answer to acquaintance. Wittgenstein asks what must be possible for any proposition to picture and leaves simple objects unspecified. Russell’s atoms are epistemically and ontologically phase-bound; Wittgenstein’s objects play a combinatorial logical role.

### Moore versus Russell

Moore does not eliminate “hand” through quantification. He claims to know that hands exist and tests the sceptic’s premises. Russell does not defend the table by simply asserting it; he reconstructs object-talk from an accepted base in a particular phase.

### Berkeley comparison control

When the 2018 cross-owned question asks how Moore and Russell react to Berkeley:

- Moore: reject identity of object and conscious act; combine this with common-sense realism.
- Russell: reconstruct external objects as logical constructions from actual/possible sense-data.
- Difference: Moore’s argument is anti-identification and epistemic; Russell’s is analytic and reductive/structural.

### Presuppositions and bounded deployment

Every synthesis presupposes a fixed axis of comparison; otherwise it becomes three biographies. The Berkeley case is a bounded application of that rule. Use it only to identify the distinct Moorean and Russellian response-halves, because the full Berkeley exposition remains cross-owned.

### Later Wittgenstein boundary

Family resemblance, language-games, meaning-as-use and private-language critique do not belong inside an exposition of the *Tractatus*. A final contrast may say that later Wittgenstein abandons the quest for a single general form rather than repairing it by a hierarchy.

### Positivist boundary

Picture-theoretic meaningfulness concerns representing a possible fact. Verificationism concerns empirical verification or confirmation as a criterion of cognitive significance. The latter is not the doctrine of the *Tractatus*.

### Objection → reply → residual

## Solved PYQ 01 — 2018 Q3(c), 15 marks

**Question:** How does Wittgenstein apply the distinction between 'saying' and 'showing' to point to a single way of apprehending the most decisive problems of philosophy? Is he justified? Give reasons for your answer.

**Demand decode:** “How” requires derivation from picture theory; “most decisive problems” requires logical form, value and the mystical; “is he justified?” requires a reasoned verdict.

### Model answer

Early Wittgenstein distinguishes what propositions can **say** from what meaningful symbolism can only **show**. A proposition says a possible contingent fact because its elements are arranged as objects might be arranged. Yet this representation is possible only because proposition and reality share logical form. That common form cannot itself be pictured as one more fact: any attempted proposition already presupposes it. Thus logical form and internal relations show themselves in meaningful propositions.

The distinction also places ethics, aesthetics, the meaning of life and the mystical outside factual discourse. On the standard reading these concerns are manifested but ineffable; on the resolute reading the temptation to state ineffable doctrines must itself be discarded. Proposition 6.54 applies the limit reflexively: the book’s elucidations are a ladder to be thrown away.

Wittgenstein is justified in exposing that conditions of representation cannot be treated as additional represented objects. However, the ladder leaves a residual problem: it is unclear how recognised nonsense can elucidate without covertly communicating content. The distinction is therefore philosophically powerful as a limit on factual saying, but not an unproblematic final doctrine.

**Why this earns marks:** It derives say/show from picture theory, includes the ladder and interpretive split, and gives a graded verdict rather than merely listing sayable/showable items.

## Solved PYQ 02 — 2018 Q4(c), 15 marks

**Question:** How is Russell's theory of definite description related to his Logical Atomism? Discuss and give reasons for your answer.

**Demand decode:** The relation—not two isolated summaries—is central. The conclusion must distinguish support from entailment.

### Model answer

Russell’s theory of definite descriptions is a model of the logical analysis that supports his atomist programme. Surface grammar treats “the present King of France” as a name. Russell instead analyses “The present King of France is bald” as asserting existence, uniqueness and predication: exactly one present King of France exists and that individual is bald. Since the existence clause fails, the proposition is false without denoting a nonexistent king.

This contextual elimination matters for logical atomism because it separates apparent constituents from genuine logical constituents. If every grammatical subject were treated as a named entity, ontology would be inflated by golden mountains and nonexistent kings. Analysis replaces the apparent subject with quantified structure and leaves the particulars, qualities, relations and logical apparatus required by the complete proposition.

Nevertheless, description theory does not prove that analysis terminates in final simples, identify those simples, or establish that reality is exhaustively atomic. It is the **scalpel** that removes pseudo-constituents; logical atomism is the qualified programme concerning what may remain. Hence the theory of descriptions is methodologically and ontologically supportive of atomism, but not a deductive proof of it.

**Why this earns marks:** It gives the formal mechanism, explains the precise bridge to atomism, and avoids the common overclaim that descriptions establish the whole ontology.

## Solved PYQ 03 — 2019 Q3(c), 15 marks

**Question:** Why is Moore’s philosophy called common-sense realism?

**Demand decode:** Explain both “common-sense” and “realism,” then defend and qualify the label.

### Model answer

Moore is called a common-sense realist because he begins with ordinary propositions about a mind-independent world that he claims we know with greater certainty than philosophical premises used to deny them. In “A Defence of Common Sense,” such truisms include that the earth has existed for years, that he has a body and that other embodied persons exist.

His central method is comparative certainty. If a sceptical or idealist argument concludes that such propositions are not known, its premises must be more credible than the knowledge rejected. Moore argues that they are not. He also distinguishes knowing a proposition from possessing its complete analysis: uncertainty about the analysis of “material object” does not cancel knowledge that one has hands.

The later hands proof dramatises the realism: one hand and another are known material things, therefore external objects exist. Yet the label must be qualified. Moore supplies neither a full theory of perception nor a sceptic-neutral derivation of the premises. His realism lies in defending ordinary world-commitments and reversing the burden of proof, not in constructing a complete metaphysical system.

**Why this earns marks:** It defines the label through truisms, comparative certainty and the knowledge/analysis distinction, then adds the correct limitation.

## Solved PYQ 04 — 2020 Q2(b), 15 marks

**Question:** Critically discuss the following statement by Moore: “If anyone tells us that to say 'Blue exists' is the same thing as to say that 'Both blue and consciousness exist', he makes a mistake and a self-contradictory mistake.”

**Demand decode:** Explain the quotation inside Moore’s anti-idealist argument and assess how much it proves.

### Model answer

Moore’s claim attacks the idealist tendency to identify the object of awareness with the conscious act. In an experience of blue, one must distinguish **blue**, the object presented, from **awareness**, the relation in which it is presented. Experiences of blue and green differ in their objects while sharing consciousness. Therefore consciousness cannot be identical with either object.

Moore calls awareness transparent or diaphanous: introspection appears to disclose blue rather than an inner object called consciousness. This transparency explains why idealists conflate object and act. But “blue exists” asserts the object, whereas “blue and consciousness exist” adds a distinct relation or act. Treating the two propositions as identical therefore erases a distinction that the comparison of blue and green reveals.

The argument blocks the simple equation *esse est percipi*: being cannot merely mean being perceived. Yet introspective distinguishability does not by itself prove that blue exists unperceived or establish a complete realist theory of perception. Moore successfully refutes an identity claim central to one idealist route, but further argument is needed for full mind-independent realism.

**Why this earns marks:** It explains blue/green, transparency, the self-contradictory conflation and the bounded conclusion.

## Solved PYQ 05 — 2020 Q2(c), 15 marks

**Question:** “The reason that I call my doctrine logical atomism is because the atoms that I wish to arrive at as the sort of last residue in analysis are logical atoms and not physical atoms.” Write a note on the nature of atomic facts according to Russell in the light of the above statement.

**Demand decode:** Define “logical,” explain atomic facts and propositions, and distinguish them from physical particles.

### Model answer

Russell’s “atoms” are the last residues sought by logical analysis, not microscopic pieces of matter. An atomic fact contains no other facts as constituents, though it can contain particulars, qualities and relations. “This is red” may represent a particular exemplifying a universal; “A is to the left of B” may represent a relational atomic fact.

An atomic proposition represents such a fact without a propositional connective. Molecular propositions are built from atomic propositions through operations such as conjunction, disjunction and negation; their truth-values depend on their components and connectives. Thus the analysis concerns the articulated structure of truth and representation.

Facts make propositions true or false and are not themselves truth-bearers. Russell’s structural correspondence between proposition and fact does not identify the sentence with reality. A difficulty arises with true negative propositions: Russell’s willingness to admit negative facts appears to multiply ontology with negative complexes. Moreover, his candidates for final simples changed across phases. Logical atomism is therefore a disciplined analytical programme, but its final inventory remained provisional.

**Why this earns marks:** It directly explains the quotation, includes relational facts and negative-fact criticism, and keeps logical atoms distinct from physical particles.

## Solved PYQ 06 — 2021 Q3(a), 20 marks

**Question:** What are the main arguments put forward by Moore in his paper “A Defence of Common Sense” to prove that there are possible propositions about the world that are known to be true with certainty? Do you think Moore’s arguments provide a sufficient response to objections presented by the sceptic against the possibility of knowledge? Give reasons in support of your answer.

**Demand decode:** Reconstruct the 1925 argument, then assess sufficiency against scepticism.

### Model answer

Moore’s defence begins from truisms: the earth has existed for many years, he has a body, other embodied persons exist, and ordinary events have occurred. “Common sense” here is not majority opinion but a restricted class of ordinary world-propositions that Moore claims each person knows.

His first argument is comparative certainty. Any philosophical argument concluding that such propositions are unknown must employ premises more certain than the propositions denied. Moore argues that sceptical premises never attain that credibility. His second move separates **knowledge** from **analysis**. One may know “I have a body” without possessing a final analysis of “body” or “material object”; conceptual difficulty does not by itself defeat knowledge.

The method powerfully exposes an asymmetry in radical scepticism: the sceptic demands proof from ordinary knowledge while granting abstract doubt unearned priority. It also preserves the actual practice of proof, which normally begins from known premises rather than proving every premise recursively.

However, the response is not sufficient on neutral ground. The sceptic disputes precisely Moore’s entitlement to call the truisms knowledge. Comparative certainty may restate Moore’s conviction rather than supply an independent criterion of warrant. Moore changes the dialectical burden but does not compel the radical sceptic. His argument is therefore a strong defence of ordinary epistemic practice and a limited rebuttal of sceptical overreach, not a universally accepted refutation.

**Why this earns marks:** It answers both halves, distinguishes knowledge from analysis, and evaluates Moore as a burden-shifter rather than pretending he offers a neutral foundational proof.

## Solved PYQ 07 — 2021 Q4(c), 15 marks

**Question:** What does Wittgenstein mean by the statement — “Whereof one cannot speak, thereof one must be silent?” Critically discuss.

**Demand decode:** Explain proposition 7 through the factual limit and ladder, not as a free-standing slogan.

### Model answer

Proposition 7 concludes the *Tractatus* because meaningful propositions picture possible contingent facts. Names correspond to objects and their arrangement presents a possible state of affairs; reality determines whether the picture is true or false. What makes depiction possible—logical form—cannot itself be pictured as one more fact, for every picture already presupposes it.

Hence propositions can say how things stand, while logical form and internal relations show themselves. Ethics, aesthetics, the meaning of life and the mystical are not additional empirical facts. Silence therefore does not mean that such matters are unimportant or scientifically false; it marks the boundary of factual saying.

The difficulty is self-application. The *Tractatus* itself appears to state the nature and limit of language. Proposition 6.54 responds with the ladder: its elucidations are used and then recognised as nonsensical and discarded. Standard readers see an apprehension of unsayable form or value; resolute readers deny that any ineffable doctrine remains.

The statement is justified as a warning against treating the conditions of representation as represented facts. Yet the elucidatory role of acknowledged nonsense remains unstable. Silence is a powerful methodological conclusion, not an uncontroversial final theory.

**Why this earns marks:** It derives proposition 7 from picture theory, controls the ethics/verificationism trap, and evaluates the ladder.

## Solved PYQ 09 — 2022 Q3(c), 15 marks

**Question:** Is there any difference between pictorial form and logical form in Ludwig Wittgenstein’s picture theory of language? How does the logical form define the relation between language and reality? Explain.

**Demand decode:** Distinguish the forms, then explain how logical form grounds representation.

### Model answer

In early Wittgenstein’s picture theory, a proposition represents a possible state of affairs because its elements correspond to objects and their arrangement presents a possible arrangement of those objects. **Pictorial form** is the form a particular representation must share with what it depicts: a spatial model, for example, requires spatially relevant organisation.

**Logical form** is more fundamental. It is the most abstract structure shared by every possible picture and reality. Because names can be combined as objects can be combined, the proposition presents a place in logical space. If the pictured state obtains, the proposition is true; if it does not, the proposition is false but still meaningful.

Logical form therefore defines the language–reality relation by making comparison possible. It is not an additional fact represented by a proposition. Any attempt to picture logical form would already use it; hence it shows itself in meaningful symbolism but cannot be said as one more contingent proposition.

The account explains false yet meaningful propositions and avoids treating representation as mere visual resemblance. Its weakness is that simple objects and elementary propositions remain unspecified, while colour incompatibility pressures the independence of the elementary base.

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes the two forms, connects logical form to truth and falsity, and adds the showing conclusion and one internal criticism.

## Solved PYQ 10 — 2023 Q4(b), 15 marks

**Question:** Why according to Russell is the proposition — “The present king of France is bald” problematic? Critically discuss.

**Demand decode:** State the puzzles, give the full analysis, and critically assess Russell’s truth-value verdict.

### Model answer

The sentence is problematic because its grammar presents “the present King of France” as a denoting subject although no such king exists. If the phrase were a name, the sentence might appear meaningless, committed to a nonexistent entity, or exempt from ordinary truth-value logic.

Russell treats the description as an incomplete symbol. The sentence asserts: at least one present King of France exists; at most one exists; and that unique individual is bald. Formally, `∃x[Kx ∧ ∀y(Ky → y=x) ∧ Bx]`. Since the existence clause is false, the whole proposition is false—not meaningless and not about a ghostly king.

The analysis also clarifies negative existentials and scope. “It is not the case that exactly one present King of France is bald” may be true even though “Exactly one present King exists and is not bald” is false.

Strawson objects that ordinary use presupposes a unique king; when the presupposition fails, truth or falsity may not arise. This limits Russell as a complete pragmatics of natural language. Nevertheless, his quantified analysis remains a decisive account of logical form and ontological economy.

**Why this earns marks:** It presents all three clauses, includes scope, and evaluates Russell through a bounded presupposition objection.

## Solved PYQ 15 — 2026 Q1(e), 10 marks

**Demand decode:** Reconstruct the proof, state Moore’s three proof conditions, and assess the sceptical objection.

Moore raises his right hand and says, “Here is one hand,” then raises his left and says, “Here is another.” If these premises are known, at least two material things encountered in space exist; therefore an external world exists.

## Solved PYQ 16 — 2026 Q4(a), 20 marks

**Question:** "Wittgenstein's difficulty about not being able to say anything within a given language about the structure of that language can be met by the idea of a hierarchy of languages." Do you agree with this statement? Give reasons and justifications for your answer.

**Demand decode:** Declare agreement/disagreement, explain the Tractarian difficulty and Russell’s hierarchy, then test formal and transcendental adequacy.

I agree only partially. A hierarchy of languages meets a **local formal-semantic** difficulty but not Wittgenstein’s **global transcendental** limit.

The difficulty arises from picture theory. A proposition represents a possible fact because proposition and reality share logical form. The *Tractatus* itself appears to state this limit and responds through the ladder at 6.54.

# ORIGINAL 10-, 15- AND 20-MARK MAINS PRACTICE

## Original 10-marker

**Question:** Distinguish Moore’s proof of an external world from his refutation of idealism. Answer in about 150 words.

**Directive decode:** “Distinguish” requires a common criterion—target, method, result and limit—not two separate summaries.

### Model answer

Moore’s two arguments support realism through different routes.

| Axis | External-world proof | Refutation of idealism |
|---|---|---|
| Target | Scepticism about external objects | Identity of object and act in *esse est percipi* |
| Method | “Here is one hand … another”; known premises and valid inference | Blue/green differ while awareness is common |
| Result | At least two material things, hence an external world | Object of awareness is not identical with awareness |
| Limit | Sceptic contests knowledge of premises | Distinction does not alone prove unperceived existence |

The hands proof reverses the sceptical burden: no abstract premise appears more certain than the perceptual knowledge denied. The anti-idealist argument uses the transparency of consciousness to expose an act/object conflation. Thus the former is epistemic and ostensive; the latter is conceptual and phenomenological. Together they strengthen common-sense realism, but neither supplies a complete theory of perception.

**Why this earns marks:** The answer fixes comparison axes, states both mechanisms and ends with a qualified relation between them.

## Original 15-marker

**Question:** Critically examine how incomplete symbols and logical constructions serve Russell’s ideal of ontological economy.

**Directive decode:** “Critically examine” requires mechanism, gain, objection and residual—not praise alone.

### Model answer

Russell’s ontological economy begins from the thought that grammatical nouns need not name independent entities. A definite description is an incomplete symbol: “The present King of France is bald” is analysed into existence, uniqueness and predication. The apparent subject disappears, and the false proposition needs no nonexistent king.

Logical constructions generalise this contextual strategy. Russell’s maxim recommends substituting constructions from known entities for inferences to unknown entities. In his 1910s phase, a physical object can be reconstructed through ordered actual and possible sense-data. A construction is not a fiction; it permits true ordinary discourse without adding a further entity beyond the construction-base.

The gain is twofold. Logically, analysis reveals structure hidden by grammar. Ontologically, it prevents commitment to pseudo-entities. Yet economy does not guarantee adequacy. Private sense-data create a public-object problem; ordinary names need not be disguised descriptions; and eliminating apparent constituents does not prove that the retained base is sufficient.

Incomplete symbols and constructions are therefore powerful instruments of disciplined commitment. They show how to say more while positing less, but they do not settle the identity or sufficiency of Russell’s final atoms.

**Why this earns marks:** It links the two devices through contextual definition, explains their distinct roles and evaluates the reduction-base.

## Original 20-marker

**Question:** Compare Russell’s logical atomism with the atomism of early Wittgenstein. Does their shared language of facts and propositions conceal a deeper methodological difference? Discuss.

**Directive decode:** “Compare” requires fixed axes; “does … conceal?” requires a declared thesis and reasoned evaluation.

### Model answer

Russell and early Wittgenstein share the ambition to clarify the relation between propositions and facts through analysis, but their shared vocabulary conceals a deeper difference. Russell’s programme is simultaneously logical and epistemological; Wittgenstein’s is primarily an inquiry into the conditions of representation.

For Russell, analysis seeks atomic facts structured by particulars, qualities and relations. Atomic propositions represent these facts; molecular propositions are truth-functions of simpler propositions. The theory of descriptions removes pseudo-subjects, and acquaintance supplies a phase-specific epistemic base. Negative facts and changing candidates for logically proper names reveal the programme’s ontological instability.

In the *Tractatus*, the world is the totality of facts. Objects are unspecified simples whose combinatorial possibilities determine logical space. A proposition pictures a possible state of affairs because names and their arrangement share logical form with objects and their possible arrangement. Sense requires bipolarity, and complex propositions are generated from an elementary base. Logical form itself can only show itself.

| Axis | Russell | Early Wittgenstein |
|---|---|---|
| Simples | Acquaintance-linked particulars and universals in a phase | Unspecified objects required for determinate sense |
| Method | Analyse grammar to discover genuine constituents | Derive conditions any picture must satisfy |
| Independence | Important but less rigidly systematised | Strict elementary independence |
| Principal problem | Which residues remain after analysis? | How can any proposition represent at all? |
| Central weakness | Negative facts and unstable base | Missing simples, colour exclusion and ladder paradox |

The difference is therefore methodological and not merely terminological. Russell moves from propositions toward an epistemically constrained ontology. Wittgenstein moves from the possibility of sense toward the form and limit of language. Their programmes intersect, and Russell acknowledged Wittgenstein’s influence, but they should not be treated as one atomism.

**Why this earns marks:** It compares on five axes, uses named doctrines as evidence, and answers the second sentence with a clear methodological thesis.

## 2. Moore — common-sense realism

### Truisms

- Earth existed long before the present moment.
- I have a body.
- Other persons with bodies exist.
- Ordinary events and relations are known.
- “Common sense” = restricted ordinary knowledge, not majority opinion.

### Comparative-certainty argument

```text
truism known with high certainty
        versus
philosophical premise denying it
        ↓
accept the more credible side
```

- A sceptical argument must have premises more certain than the proposition denied.
- Moore finds no such premise.
- Philosophical generality alone earns no priority.

### Knowledge versus analysis

- Knowing `P` does not require a final analysis of `P`.
- “I have a body” may be known even when “body/material object” remains philosophically difficult.
- Trap: conceptual incompleteness ≠ epistemic failure.

### Qualified verdict

- Strength: defends actual epistemic practice and reverses the burden.
- Weakness: radical sceptic disputes the premise-entitlement itself.
- Best closing line: **Moore offers a dialectically forceful burden shift, not a neutral foundational proof.**

## 3. Moore — two hands and proof conditions

### Proof

1. Right hand: “Here is one hand.”
2. Left hand: “Here is another.”
3. Therefore at least two material objects exist.
4. Therefore an external world exists.

### Three conditions

1. Premise differs from conclusion.
2. Premise is known, not merely believed.
3. Conclusion follows from premise.

### Criticism

- Valid inference can coexist with disputed soundness.
- Sceptic attacks knowledge of premises.
- Formal non-circularity does not eliminate dialectical question-begging.

### Paradox of analysis

```text
equivalent analysis → apparently trivial
non-equivalent analysis → apparently incorrect
```

Use only as bounded method context.

## 4. Moore — refutation of idealism

### Target

- *Esse est percipi*: being is being perceived.
- Specific error: identity of presented object with conscious act.

### Blue/green argument

1. Blue and green are different objects.
2. Awareness is common to both experiences.
3. Therefore awareness differs from either object.
4. “Blue exists” ≠ “blue and consciousness exist.”
5. Hence object cannot simply be identified with perception.

### Transparency

- Attention seems to reveal blue, not consciousness as a further inner object.
- Transparency explains conflation; it does not erase the act/object distinction.

### Limit

- Establishes non-identity.
- Does **not** establish unperceived persistence by itself.
- Does **not** refute every Hegelian idealism.

### Two-route comparison

| Moore route | Main task | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Truisms/hands | Defend external objects against scepticism | Premise-knowledge contested |
| Blue/awareness | Block idealist act/object identity | Full mind-independence not proved |

## 5. Russell — logical atomism

### Core grammar

- Fact: makes a proposition true or false; not itself a truth-bearer.
- Proposition: represents and bears truth-value.
- Atomic fact: no other facts as constituents; can include particulars, qualities and relations.
- Atomic proposition: no propositional connective.
- Molecular proposition: truth-functional combination.
- Logical atom: analytical residue, not physical particle.

### Structural correspondence

```text
proposition constituents/arrangement
        ↕ correspond
fact constituents/arrangement
```

- Representation is structural, not identity.
- Relational fact such as “A is left of B” can be atomic.

### Negative facts

- Proposed truth-makers for true negatives.
- Cost: ontology appears to include negative complexes in addition to positive facts.

### Provisional endpoint

- Description analysis removes pseudo-constituents.
- It does not prove final simples, termination or exhaustive atomism.

## 6. Russell — definite descriptions and incomplete symbols

### Definition

An incomplete symbol:

- lacks independent denotation in isolation;
- contributes systematically within a complete proposition;
- disappears as an independent constituent under contextual analysis;
- is not thereby meaningless.

### Formula

`The F is G`:

1. at least one F exists;
2. at most one F exists;
3. that unique F is G.

`∃x [Fx ∧ ∀y(Fy → y=x) ∧ Gx]`

### King of France

- Existence fails.
- Whole conjunction = false.
- No ghostly king.
- Sentence remains meaningful.

### Golden mountain

- Exactly one golden mountain exists and is very high.
- Existence fails → false, not nonsense.

### Scope

| Form | Result if no F exists |
|---|---|
| Exactly one F exists and is not G | False |
| It is not the case that exactly one F exists and is G | True |

### Three puzzle-payoffs

- Negative existentials: deny existential-unique condition.
- Excluded middle: logical scope distinguishes the relevant negations.
- George IV/Scott: description is quantificational, so substitution as a simple name fails.

### Criticism

- Strawson: failed presupposition may produce a truth-value gap.
- Best verdict: Russell’s logic remains powerful even if it is not a complete account of ordinary use.

## 7. Russell — acquaintance, description and constructions

### Acquaintance

- Direct, presentational, non-inferential.
- Phase-specific candidates: sense-data, memory/introspective data, universals.
- In the 1910s view, only `this`/`that` used for present acquaintance count as logically proper names.

### Description

- Indirect knowledge through a unique condition.
- Ordinary names such as `Bismarck` are treated as abbreviated or disguised descriptions.
- Handles historical persons, physical objects and unobservables.

### Principle of Acquaintance

Every understandable proposition must be composed of constituents with which the knower is acquainted.

### How descriptions preserve it

- “Bismarck” as described object need not occur as direct constituent.
- Quantified form plus universals can be grasped.
- Theory of descriptions joins logic to epistemology.

### Construction maxim

“Substitute constructions out of known entities for inferences to unknown entities wherever possible.”

### Physical objects

- Phase-specific reconstruction from ordered actual and possible sense-data.
- Construction ≠ atom.
- Construction ≠ fiction.
- It is derived discourse without an extra independent entity.

### Limits

- Public-object problem from private sense-data.
- Aristotle-style objection: a name can retain its bearer even if every associated description is false.
- Russell can retreat to logically proper `this`/`that`; description logic survives, but the description theory of ordinary names is weakened.
- Economy does not prove sufficiency of the base.
- Later Russell must not be retrofitted into the 1910s.

## 8. Russell and early Wittgenstein — atomism comparison

| Axis | Russell | Early Wittgenstein |
|---|---|---|
| Atom | Acquaintance-linked particular/universal in a phase | Unspecified object with combinatorial possibilities |
| Main route | Analysis of actual propositions | Conditions required for representation |
| Epistemology | Central and changing | Not a sense-datum identification |
| Independence | Present but less strict | Elementary propositions postulated independent |
| Main residual | Negative facts; unstable base | Missing simples; colour exclusion; ladder |

> **Verdict:** shared language of facts hides a methodological difference—Russell moves toward an epistemically constrained ontology; Wittgenstein moves toward the form and limit of sense.

## 9. Early Wittgenstein — world, states and pictures

### Ontological sequence

```text
objects → possible combinations → states of affairs
obtaining states → facts
totality of facts → world
```

- World is not a mere inventory of things.
- *Sachverhalt*: declare translation as “state of affairs” or “atomic fact” and remain consistent.

### Picture theory

1. Names/elements correspond to objects.
2. Arrangement of names presents possible arrangement of objects.
3. Shared pictorial form permits depiction.
4. Logical form is the universal form of representation.
5. Obtaining possibility → true.
6. Non-obtaining possibility → false but meaningful.

### Pictorial versus logical form

- Pictorial form: medium/representation-specific shared form.
- Logical form: most abstract structure common to any picture and reality.
- Logical form cannot be pictured as an additional fact.

## 10. Bipolarity, logical space and general form

### Bipolarity

- Sense requires capacity for truth and capacity for falsity.
- Stronger than bivalence.
- Historically influential on verificationist exclusion, but not itself the Vienna Circle verification principle.
- A representation that cannot fail says nothing contingent.

### Logical space

- Field of possible configurations.
- Proposition fixes a place by including/excluding alternatives.
- Not a physical space outside the world.

### Logical constants

- Operate on propositions.
- Do not name worldly objects.

### General form

- Informal 4.5: “This is how things stand” (Pears–McGuinness).
- Formal proposition 6: `[p̄, ξ̄, N(ξ̄)]`.
- `N` = joint negation.

### Presuppositions under pressure

- Elementary propositions exist.
- They are logically independent.
- All propositions are truth-functions of them.
- N handles required formation.

### Colour exclusion

- “Red all over” excludes “blue all over.”
- Pressures apparent elementary independence.
- Does not authorise importing later meaning-as-use.

### *Sinnlos* / *unsinnig*

| Term | Status |
|---|---|
| *Sinnlos* | Tautology/contradiction: well-formed, factually empty limit |
| *Unsinnig* | Pseudo-proposition failing to picture a possible fact |

## 11. Saying, showing and silence

### Can be said

- Contingent possible states.
- Natural-scientific propositions.

### Shows itself

- Logical form.
- Internal relations.
- Logical scaffolding.
- On standard reading: ethics, value, aesthetics and mystical significance.

### Interpretive control

- Standard: genuine but ineffable form/value.
- Resolute: no ineffable doctrine; apparent theses are therapeutic nonsense.
- Shared minimum: neither is ordinary factual saying.

### Ladder

- Proposition 6.54: elucidations used and discarded.
- Self-application is acknowledged, not accidentally overlooked.
- Residual: how can nonsense elucidate?

### Proposition 7

- Ogden: “Whereof one cannot speak, thereof one must be silent.”
- Pears–McGuinness: “What we cannot speak about we must pass over in silence.”
- Name translation.
- There is no proposition 7.1.

## 12. Hierarchy of languages

### Russell’s proposal

```text
L1 cannot state own structure
L2 describes L1
L3 describes L2
...
no final language
```

### What it solves

- Selected lower-language syntax.
- Local semantic definition.
- Self-reference control.
- Later formal object-language/metalanguage discipline supports this narrow gain.

### What it does not solve

- Form of each metalanguage.
- Final statement of representation’s total limit.
- Ethics/value/mystical as non-factual.
- Full ladder/self-application problem.

### 2026 verdict

**Partial agreement:** formal-semantic success, transcendental failure.
