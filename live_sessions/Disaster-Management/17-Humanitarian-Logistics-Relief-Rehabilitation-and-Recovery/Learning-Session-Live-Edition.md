### 🖼️ Phase staircase

```text
RECONSTRUCTION  ┌ durable services + safer development
REHABILITATION  ├ temporary function + livelihood/social recovery
RELIEF          └ rescue + survival + dignity
                 impact ───────────────→ development ownership
```

*Three overlapping jobs rise from survival to temporary function and then durable risk reduction.*

### Phase boundary test

✅ **Phase fact:** Relief protects life and dignity. Rehabilitation restores minimum function. Reconstruction is durable and risk-informed.

⚠️ **Overlap judgement:** Phases overlap but objectives differ. Section 12 anchors relief.

### Handover rule

**Handover application:** BBB belongs to recovery. Handover must be documented. Livelihoods start early. Indicators differ by phase. Premature exit creates gaps.

### Concept check

**Question:** (10 marks; answer in ≤150 words) Distinguish relief, rehabilitation and reconstruction, and explain how phase-specific handover prevents recovery gaps.

**Model-answer label:** 107 words; ceiling ≤150 words for this 10-mark question.

**Model answer:** Relief addresses immediate threats through rescue, rapid assessment, food, water, medical care and temporary shelter. Rehabilitation restores minimum functioning—such as temporary roads, schools, livelihoods and psychosocial support—so routine life can resume. Reconstruction provides durable, safer infrastructure and integrates recovery into long-term development. These phases may overlap, but their objectives and indicators differ. A documented handover should transfer unresolved households, inventories, contracts, service gaps and risk information from emergency command to line departments and local bodies. Minimum relief must continue until usable alternatives exist. Reconstruction decisions should then apply hazard-resistant siting and standards rather than reproduce exposure. Thus, speed is protected without confusing visible distribution with completed recovery.

**Adjacent rubric:** Phase distinctions 3 + overlap/examples 2 + handover design 3 + risk-informed conclusion 2 = **10 marks**

**Misconception to avoid:** Treating the first reopening of roads or distribution of kits as proof that reconstruction is complete.

**Transition:** Lesson 2 now supplies the command architecture that moves work across those phase boundaries.

### Command authority before activity

**Transfer application:** Logistics moves resources. Eoc supports rather than commands. Structure scales with complexity. Demobilisation needs records. Unresolved cases need named owners.

✅ **IRS rule:** India uses irs. Ics is only a comparator. Command sets objectives.

### Scale, demobilise and transfer

⚠️ **Scaling judgement:** Operations acts. Planning maintains the picture.

### Exercise the system, not the ceremony

✅ **Current official architecture:** NDMA’s *National Guidelines on Disaster Management Exercises (DMEx)*, released in **October 2024**, treats an exercise as a planned test of policies, plans, procedures and response capability—not a public demonstration. It requires stated objectives, scenario events and injects, continuous evaluation, a hotwash and debrief, documentation, lessons and follow-up action.[^DMEX]

⚠️ **Operational application:** An IRS–EOC exercise should force realistic decisions: a failed communication link, a competing resource request, an inaccessible relief site and an unresolved case at handover. Observers should record whether command, EOC support, logistics and information functions worked; each gap then needs an owner, deadline and later re-test.

### Concept check

**Question:** (15 marks; answer in ≤250 words) Explain how IRS, EOC support, demobilisation and handover should be organised for accountable transition from response to recovery.

**Model-answer label:** 113 words; ceiling ≤250 words for this 15-mark question.

**Model answer:** India’s IRS should establish one set of incident objectives, with Operations directing field action, Planning maintaining the common operating picture and Logistics mobilising personnel, equipment and supplies. The EOC should support—rather than displace—field command by consolidating information, coordinating departments and escalating resource requests. The structure must expand for multiple sites and contract as objectives stabilise. Demobilisation should record personnel release, equipment return, contracts, expenditure, injuries and lessons. Handover to normal administration must be accepted in writing and include unresolved households, relief entitlements, inventories, damaged lifelines, protection risks and recovery works with named owners and deadlines. Community and local-body representatives should verify whether services are actually usable. This design combines rapid command with continuity.

**Adjacent rubric:** IRS functions 4 + EOC relationship 3 + demobilisation controls 3 + handover/verification 4 + conclusion 1 = **15 marks**

**Misconception to avoid:** Assuming the EOC is a parallel field commander, creating conflicting orders and obscuring accountability.

### Concept check

**Question:** (10 marks; answer in ≤150 words) Design a rapid-needs assessment and prioritisation method for an isolated block with modest physical damage but failed health and water services.

**Model-answer label:** 110 words; ceiling ≤150 words for this 10-mark question.

**Model answer:** The block should not be ranked by physical damage alone. **Question:** (15 marks; answer in ≤250 words) Construct an accountable humanitarian supply chain from needs assessment to verified household use, including reverse logistics.

**Question:** (10 marks; answer in ≤150 words) Compare logistics design for a forecast cyclone and a no-warning earthquake, identifying trigger and staging safeguards.

**Model-answer label:** 109 words; ceiling ≤150 words for this 10-mark question.

**Model answer:** For a forecast cyclone, authorities can use agreed thresholds to pre-position specialist teams, relief stocks, mobile health capacity and evacuation support before landfall. The cyclone design manages forecast error through reversible, low-regret action; the earthquake design manages uncertainty through redundancy and geographic dispersion. Hazard-specific readiness is superior to one generic logistics plan.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

### 🖼️ Section 12 entitlement frame

| Statutory branch | Included floor |
|---|---|
| relief camp | shelter, food, drinking water, medical cover, sanitation |
| special protection | widows and orphans |
| assistance | death ex gratia, house damage, livelihood restoration |
| residual need | other relief as may be necessary |

*The statute joins camp services with special protection, death, housing and livelihood assistance.*

### What section 12 actually covers

✅ **Legal text:** Section 12 directs the National Authority to recommend guidelines for minimum standards covering **(i)** shelter, food, drinking water, medical cover and sanitation in relief camps; **(ii)** special provisions for widows and orphans; **(iii)** ex gratia assistance for loss of life, assistance for house damage and assistance for restoration of means of livelihood; and **(iv)** other relief as may be necessary.[^DMACT]

⚠️ **Usability inference:** The February 2016 NDMA Guidelines translate this legal floor into service standards. Compliance therefore concerns safe and continuous use, not a warehouse or camp stock count. Water, food, sanitation, medical referral, shelter protection and assistance claims also interact.

### Convert law into usable service

**District application:** A district should assign each statutory head to a department, publish eligibility and service benchmarks, record interruptions, provide disability and protection accommodations, and operate claim review and grievance channels. Equal inputs may be inequitable where barriers differ. Minimum standards are a floor, not a ceiling on evidenced context-specific relief.

### REVISION NOTES

1. Section 12 directs NDMA to recommend minimum-relief guidelines.
2. Camp heads include shelter, food, drinking water, medical cover and sanitation.
3. The section requires special provisions for widows and orphans.
4. It covers ex gratia assistance for loss of life.
5. It covers assistance for damage to houses.
6. It covers assistance to restore means of livelihood.
7. It allows other necessary relief based on need.
8. The NDMA Minimum Standards Guidelines date to February 2016.
9. A standard must be accessible and usable, not merely stocked.
10. Minimum standards are a dignity floor, not a maximum entitlement.

### Concept check

**Question:** (10 marks; answer in ≤150 words) State the full architecture of Disaster Management Act section 12 and explain how a district should convert it into usable minimum relief.

**Model-answer label:** 115 words; ceiling ≤150 words for this 10-mark question.

**Model answer:** Section 12 requires the National Authority to recommend minimum standards covering relief-camp shelter, food, drinking water, medical cover and sanitation; special provisions for widows and orphans; ex gratia assistance for loss of life; assistance for house damage and restoration of livelihoods; and other necessary relief. A district should translate these heads into measurable service plans, named departmental responsibility and accessible claim procedures. Camp monitoring must test safe water, suitable food, privacy, disability access, referral and continuity rather than count stocks alone. House, death and livelihood assistance needs verification, communication, time limits, grievance and review. Disaggregated feedback should reveal whether affected groups can actually use services. Section 12 creates an operational dignity floor beyond commodity distribution.

**Adjacent rubric:** Full statutory heads 4 + service operationalisation 3 + assistance/accountability 2 + dignity-floor conclusion 1 = **10 marks**
