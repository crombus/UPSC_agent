# The Earth's Crust, Rocks / India Geological Structure — Mains-Focused Live Edition

This Mains edition of Geography Topic 02 teaches the crust as a General Studies Paper I object: what it is made of, how its three rock families are produced and recycled, how it is folded, faulted and rebuilt through geological time, why continents and ocean basins are transient configurations, and how those processes appear in India's cratons, mobile belts, basins, Deccan volcanics, Himalaya and foreland.

**One topic, two halves.** The world half supplies rock classification, deformation and plate processes; the India half applies them to cratons, mobile belts, basins, volcanic provinces and the Himalayan collision. The same process vocabulary must explain both.

**The honest shape of this topic.** Seven verified demands connect directly or substantially to it. Both Mains demands sit in the world half — primary rocks and crustal tectonics, worth twenty-five marks — while the India half carries the 2023 hill-range and provisional 2026 Peninsular-Block objective demands. The world half therefore earns depth from mark value; the India half earns equal depth from conceptual load, chronology and mapwork.

**Modern chronology controls.** The operative sequence is **Archaean cratonic basement → Proterozoic mobile belts and basins → Gondwana rifts and Mesozoic marine-margin basins → Deccan flood-basalt pulse near 66 Ma → Cenozoic Himalaya and foreland basin → Quaternary deposits and continuing deformation**. The older **Archaean / Purana / Dravidian / Aryan** ladder is retained only as historiography when interpreting older textbooks; it does not control modern geological chronology.

**Answer-neutrality.** Objective PYQ references display year, paper, number and a neutral demand label only. No answer letter, statement verdict, option elimination or corrective mapping is attached to them. Static concepts are taught independently rather than presented as solutions. No solved previous-year model answer appears; all practice models are original.

**What is deliberately left out.** Era-period-epoch time-scale tables, exhaustive mineral-occurrence district inventories, book-era production and reserve tables, volcano and earthquake case files, Himalayan physiographic sub-division detail, compiled four-option objective corpora and mineral crystallography. Each is either low-yield memorisation, stale data or another topic's property. Nothing that is a definition, a causal step, an objection, a reply, a qualification or a practice item has been removed.

### 1. The boundary of the lesson

The lesson asks a foundational question: *what is the outer solid shell made of, how does it behave, and how do we infer its boundaries?*

⚠️ The lesson treats the crust and upper mantle as material and mechanical objects: composition, behaviour, boundaries and evidence. Three adjacent processes are named only to prevent confusion.

- Condensation, differentiation, the geodynamo and magnetosphere are established in Topic 01.
- Earthquake events, zonation, tsunami and eruption styles are developed in Topic 03.
- Here seismic waves are used only as evidence for internal structure.

❗ That restraint is itself examinable discipline. A fifteen-mark tectonics answer that drifts into seismic zonation has spent marks on a demand it was not asked.

### 2. Historical sial/sima and the modern crustal distinction

✅ Leong's **sial/sima** contrast is retained as historical terminology: lighter granitic continental material against denser basaltic material. The accompanying imperial measurements and shell labels are mid-century textbook statements, not current geophysical constants.

⚠️ Modern answers should instead distinguish **continental crust**, **oceanic crust**, **lithosphere** and **asthenosphere**. Sial-over-sima is not a universal two-layer model of every crustal column.

✅ A safe first-order contrast is oceanic crust commonly about **5-10 km** thick against continental crust commonly about **30-40 km**, with substantial local variation.

### 3. The two discontinuities worth naming, and the one distinction worth more than both

✅ The **Mohorovičić discontinuity** is the crust-mantle boundary; its depth varies strongly by tectonic setting. ✅ The **Gutenberg discontinuity** lies near **2,900 km** at the mantle-outer-core boundary. ❗ Lithosphere and asthenosphere do not have one universal depth bracket: the lithosphere-asthenosphere boundary varies with age, temperature and setting.

⚠️ **Crust is compositional; lithosphere is mechanical.** The lithosphere contains crust plus rigid uppermost mantle. The asthenosphere is hotter, weaker and ductile but remains predominantly solid, flowing slowly over geological time.

❗ This is not pedantry. **Plates are lithospheric, not crustal.** An answer that says "the crust is broken into plates" has described the wrong object, and at Lesson 8 that error propagates into every statement about what subducts and what does not.

### 4. The evidence chain, stated as a chain

✅ **P-waves** are compressional and travel through solids and liquids. **S-waves** are shear waves and cannot propagate through liquids. **Surface waves** travel near the surface and are chiefly relevant to shaking hazards.

⚠️ The chain runs: *S-waves are absent beyond a certain angular distance from an earthquake, producing a **shadow zone*** → *a liquid shell must begin at the depth that produces that shadow* → *P-waves do continue but are sharply refracted at that same depth* → *a sharp refraction requires a sharp change in material properties, so the boundary is real and not gradual*. ✅ Majid draws **four** inferences from the wave data, not one: a **solid inner core**, a **liquid outer core**, a **soft asthenosphere** and a **rigid lithosphere**.

⚠️ Seismic velocities constrain density, elastic properties and likely composition, but do not uniquely identify chemistry. Core composition is inferred by combining seismology with density, high-pressure experiments, cosmochemical evidence and geomagnetism.

### 5. 🇮🇳 India hook — the same Moho, at two very different depths

⚠️ The Moho is not horizontal. The **Peninsular shield** is comparatively stable but structurally heterogeneous; beneath the **Himalaya**, continent-continent convergence has shortened and thickened the crust, deepening the crust-mantle boundary relative to ordinary continental crust.

❗ Do not attach an uncited all-India number. The defensible contrast is heterogeneous cratonic crust beneath the Peninsula versus collisionally thickened crust beneath the Himalaya.

### Comparison, objection and the residual

| | What it establishes | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| Continental/oceanic crust contrast | Continental crust is generally thicker and more buoyant than oceanic crust | Where any boundary actually lies |
| Seismic-wave behaviour | Depths, states and the sharpness of internal boundaries | The chemical identity of any shell |
| Thickness measurements | That crust is thin under oceans and thick under mountains | Why it is thick there — that is isostasy and orogeny, at L5 and L9 |

**Strongest objection.** If planetary layering and earthquake hazards are developed elsewhere, what remains here?

**Reply.** The **crust** remains the object: what the outer shell contains, how it behaves and how its boundary is inferred. Rock families, deformation, plate kinematics and India's geological provinces all depend on that foundation.

⚠️ **Residual.** Crustal thickness and the lithosphere-asthenosphere boundary vary with tectonic setting, age and thermal structure; first-order values are not local constants.

Revision notes:

- **Crust** = compositional unit ending at the **Moho**; **lithosphere** = variable-thickness mechanical unit containing crust plus rigid uppermost mantle.
- **Plates are lithospheric, not crustal.** Highest-value terminology point in the lesson.
- **Asthenosphere:** weaker, hotter, ductile and predominantly solid; its top and thickness vary.
- **Sial/sima:** historical shorthand only; modern answers use continental/oceanic crust and lithosphere/asthenosphere.
- Oceanic crust is commonly about **5-10 km**; continental crust commonly about **30-40 km**, with large local variation.
- **Moho** = crust/mantle; **Gutenberg** = mantle/outer core near **2,900 km**; inner-core boundary near **5,150 km**.
- **P-waves** compressional, fastest, cross solids and liquids. **S-waves** shear and cannot cross a liquid.
- **Shadow zones and velocity changes** constrain a liquid outer core, solid inner core and mechanical layering.
- Strong P-wave refraction near 2,900 km indicates a marked mantle-core discontinuity.
- Seismic evidence constrains **depth, state and elastic properties**; composition is inferred from multiple evidence classes.
- 🇮🇳 Himalayan convergence thickens crust and deepens the Moho relative to ordinary continental crust. ❗ No unsupported Indian thickness figure.

### Concept check

**Question:** A candidate writes: "The Earth's crust is divided into seven major plates which float on the molten mantle." Identify the three separate errors and give the corrected sentence.

**Model answer:** First, plates are **lithospheric**, not merely crustal: each includes crust and rigid uppermost mantle. Second, the mantle is not a molten ocean. The asthenosphere is hotter, weaker and predominantly solid, deforming slowly over geological time; the seismic evidence for a liquid shell applies to the outer core below about 2,900 km. Third, “float” confuses horizontal plate motion with isostatic buoyancy. A safer correction is: *Rigid lithospheric plates move relative to one another over a mechanically weaker, mostly solid asthenospheric zone whose depth varies by setting.*

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not call the mantle molten merely because it flows over geological time. The mantle is predominantly solid; S-waves fail to propagate through the liquid outer core.

**Original Mains practice [10 marks]:** "Our picture of the Earth's interior is an inference from wave behaviour rather than a description of an observation." Examine, with reference to the crust and its boundaries. *(Examine — answer in about 150 words.)*

**Model (about 150 words):** The layered interior is inferred from wave behaviour. P-waves cross solids and liquids; S-waves require shear strength and cannot cross a liquid. Their shadow zone supports a liquid outer core beginning near 2,900 km, while P-wave refraction indicates a marked boundary. Velocity changes also identify the Moho, whose depth varies by setting. Mechanical behaviour distinguishes rigid lithosphere from the weaker, mostly solid asthenosphere. Wave data constrain depth, state, density and elastic properties but do not uniquely determine chemistry; core composition is inferred by combining seismology with density, experiments, cosmochemistry and geomagnetism. The section is therefore a constrained model, not direct observation.

**Rubric (quantified, 10 marks):** 2 for P/S contrast; 2 for shadow-zone reasoning; 2 for refraction/velocity discontinuity; 2 for multi-evidence composition inference; 1 for Moho/Gutenberg; 1 for crust/lithosphere distinction. Deduct 2 for a molten mantle.

The loop is closed and it has **no privileged starting point**. ⚠️ That is the first analytic claim of the lesson: calling igneous the "primary" rock is a statement about *genesis of material*, not a statement about sequence in time, because igneous rock is itself produced by the remelting of earlier rock.

### A. The frame before the families

Most candidates begin with the three families. Begin one step earlier, with the question *on what dimensions can two rocks differ at all?*

✅ Leong supplies six: rocks differ in **texture, structure, colour, permeability, mode of occurrence and degree of resistance to denudation**.

⚠️ Use the six dimensions as an answer skeleton: texture, structure, colour/composition, permeability, occurrence and resistance. They turn loose description into ordered analysis.

| Criterion | What it actually asks | Why an examiner rewards it |
|---|---|---|
| **Texture** | Crystal size and arrangement | It encodes **cooling rate**, so it is a genetic statement disguised as a descriptive one |
| **Structure** | Stratified, foliated, massive or jointed | It separates the three families at a glance in the field |
| **Colour** | Light or dark | It proxies composition — acid rocks are pale, basic rocks dark |
| **Permeability** | Whether water passes | It is the bridge to aquifers, soils and relief — the whole of L14 |
| **Mode of occurrence** | Batholith, sill, flow, bed, band | It is the map-scale expression of the rock |
| **Resistance to denudation** | Whether it stands up or wears down | It converts petrology into **landscape**, which is where the marks are |

### B. What a rock is, and why the definition matters

✅ A **rock is an aggregate of minerals**. ✅ The principal rock-forming minerals are the **feldspars, micas, amphiboles, pyroxenes, olivine, quartz, calcite, dolomite, the clays and gypsum**.

⚠️ Two consequences follow immediately and both are examinable. First, a rock **name** is a statement about an *assemblage*, not about a single substance; this is why "granite" covers a range of compositions and why the same parent can yield different metamorphic products. Second, because minerals have different hardness, solubility and cleavage, the **behaviour of a rock under weathering is a weighted average of its minerals** — which is why a quartz-rich rock resists and a feldspar-rich rock decays to clay.

### C. The three families, defined by process rather than by appearance

✅ **Igneous** rock forms from the cooling and solidification of molten material. ✅ Within it, **plutonic or intrusive** rock cools slowly at depth and therefore grows **large, easily recognised crystals** — granite is the type example — and is exposed only later by denudation; **volcanic or extrusive** rock pours out as lava and solidifies fast, so its crystals are **tiny**, and basalt forms lava sheets and plateaux. ✅ Majid classifies igneous rock by **texture and composition** — basalt, gabbro, andesite, diorite, rhyolite, granite — and by **mode of occurrence** into intrusive and extrusive. ✅ Igneous rock is the **chief original source of the inorganic mineral matter** from which sedimentary rock is later built, which is the precise sense in which it is "primary".

✅ **Sedimentary** rock forms from clastic, organic and/or chemical material. It is commonly stratified, though massive exceptions occur; transport may be by water, wind, ice or gravity before deposition and lithification.

✅ **Metamorphic** rock is pre-existing rock altered by temperature, pressure and fluids, changing texture and mineral assemblage. Common pathways include shale/mudstone → slate/phyllite/schist, limestone or dolostone → marble, sandstone → quartzite, and felsic protoliths → some gneisses.

> 🔑 **Trap, and it is the one most often set.** ❌ "Foliation and stratification are the same thing." They are not. **Stratification** is sedimentary layering produced by deposition. **Foliation** is a metamorphic alignment of minerals produced by directed pressure. A schist splits along its **foliation**, not along a bedding plane — and in the field that single difference tells you which family you are holding.

> 🔑 **Second trap.** ❌ "Coal → graphite" is often written as a universal one-step rule. ⚠️ Graphite **may** form by metamorphism of carbon-rich material, but the transformation is not universal. Do not extend it to a simplistic coal-to-diamond sequence.

### D. Permeability, which is the family trait that does the most later work

✅ Primary porosity is generally **low** in crystalline metamorphic rock, but **joints, fractures and weathered zones supply secondary permeability** — and that secondary route, not the pore space of the fresh rock, is what matters for groundwater and for foundations.

⚠️ Hold that thought for three later lessons. At Lesson 3 it explains why a resistant, impermeable granite forms high ground. At Lesson 11 it explains why the Cuddapah limestones behave differently from the Vindhyan sandstones. At Lesson 14 it is the step that converts "the Peninsula is crystalline" into "the Peninsula has limited groundwater in the rock mass but useful storage in fractures", which is an argument rather than a label.

### Comparison, objection and the residual

**Strongest objection.** The three-family classification is school geography. Why spend a full Mains lesson on it?

**Reply.** Because the 2022 demand is literally a classification demand — *characteristics and types of primary rocks* — and because the India half of this topic is nothing but a classification exercise carried out in time instead of in substance. ⚠️ A candidate who holds only the three names has a label set; a candidate who holds the **six criteria plus the closed cycle** has a generative structure that produces paragraphs on demand. The difference shows up in the third sentence of the answer.

⚠️ **Residual.** The parent of a metamorphic rock is not always recoverable. For quartzite and marble it may be clear; for schist and gneiss it can be difficult. An answer that claims every metamorphic rock announces its protolith has overstated the discipline.

- **Six criteria** (Leong): **texture · structure · colour · permeability · mode of occurrence · resistance to denudation**. Use as the scoring skeleton for any "characteristics and types" directive.
- **Rock = an aggregate of minerals.** Rock-forming minerals: **feldspars, micas, amphiboles, pyroxenes, olivine, quartz, calcite, dolomite, clays, gypsum**.
- **Igneous** = cooled magma. **Plutonic/intrusive** → slow cooling at depth → **large crystals** (granite). **Volcanic/extrusive** → fast cooling at surface → **tiny crystals** (basalt, lava sheets and plateaux).
- Majid classifies igneous rock by **texture + composition** (basalt, gabbro, andesite, diorite, rhyolite, granite) **and** by mode of occurrence.
- "**Primary**" means igneous is the **chief original source of inorganic mineral matter**, not that it comes first in time.
- **Sedimentary** = accumulated sediment, **stratified**, layers from inches to many feet; transport by stream, glacier, wind or organisms.
- **Metamorphic** = pre-existing rock altered by **temperature, pressure and chemically active fluids**; **both texture and mineral composition** change.
- Standard pairs: **shale/mudstone → slate or schist · limestone → marble · sandstone → quartzite · granite → gneiss**.
- ❌ **Foliation ≠ stratification.** Foliation is metamorphic mineral alignment; stratification is sedimentary layering. A schist splits along foliation.
- ⚠️ "Coal → graphite" is an example, not a universal rule; ❗ do not extend it to diamond.
- **Secondary permeability** — joints, fractures, weathered mantle — governs water in crystalline rock, not primary pore space.
- The **rock cycle is closed and has no fixed start**; metamorphic rock can itself be metamorphosed, and any rock can be remelted.
- 🇮🇳 Exemplars: Deccan basalt (extrusive) · Closepet granite (intrusive) · Vindhyan sandstone (mechanical sedimentary) · Gondwana coal (continental sedimentary) · Eastern Ghats khondalite suite, Bundelkhand gneiss and Nilgiri charnockitic terrain (distinct high-grade metamorphic associations).

### Concept check

**Question:** Why is it more accurate to call igneous rock the "primary" rock on grounds of material rather than on grounds of time?

**Model answer:** Because the rock cycle is closed. Igneous rock is produced by the cooling of molten material, and molten material is itself produced by the melting of pre-existing rock at depth or in a subduction setting — so igneous rock is continuously being *re*made from sedimentary and metamorphic parents and cannot be the first rock in any temporal sequence. What is true is the material claim: igneous rock is the chief original source of the inorganic mineral matter that weathering, transport and cementation later assemble into sedimentary rock, and that sedimentary rock in turn supplies many metamorphic parents. "Primary" therefore identifies a position in a chain of *derivation of substance*, which holds at every moment of geological time, rather than a position in a chronology, which holds at none.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not read the familiar textbook arrow igneous → sedimentary → metamorphic as a one-way timeline. It is one traverse of a loop; metamorphic rock can be metamorphosed again, and any family can be remelted and returned to the start.

**Original Mains practice [10 marks]:** Explain how the physical properties of a rock, rather than its name, determine its influence on landscape and water availability. *(Explain — answer in about 150 words.)*

**Model (about 150 words):** A rock name records an assemblage of minerals; landscape responds to the properties that assemblage produces. Four of the six standard criteria do the work. Texture encodes cooling rate and therefore crystal size, which governs the surface available for chemical attack. Structure determines whether water enters along bedding, along foliation or only along joints. Permeability decides whether rainfall runs off and erodes or percolates and is stored — which is why a permeable limestone can stand as a prominent steep-sided hill while a shale of comparable hardness is worn into low rounded ground. Resistance to denudation, the net outcome of the others, decides which units stand as uplands. Hence the causal variable is permeability and mineral stability rather than hardness alone. The limit is that relief, climate, structure and time act with the rock, so lithology sets a tendency rather than a determined outcome.

Deduct 2 for an answer that only lists the three families.

### 2. Composition: historical acid/basic and modern felsic/mafic

⚠️ **Acid/basic** is legacy terminology. Use **felsic/intermediate/mafic/ultramafic** where precision matters. Felsic rocks are generally silica-rich, pale and less dense; mafic rocks are richer in ferromagnesian minerals, darker and denser. Aluminium is not a diagnostic “basic oxide”.

### 3. The second division: plutonic against volcanic, and the texture rule

✅ **Plutonic or intrusive** rock cools slowly at depth and grows **large, easily recognised crystals**; it reaches the surface only when denudation strips the cover above it. ✅ **Volcanic or extrusive** rock is erupted and chilled quickly, so crystals are **tiny**.

⚠️ Crystal size generally increases as cooling slows. Depth matters, but body size, emplacement geometry, composition and thermal surroundings also affect cooling.

> 🔑 **Trap.** ❌ "Basalt cools slowly to form large crystals." Basalt is extrusive; it cools fast and is fine-grained. Granite, plutonic, carries the large crystals. The error survives because both rocks are familiar; the cure is to ask *where did it stop?* before asking *what is it called?*

### 4. The forms magma takes — the part that separates a 7 from a 10

Key intrusive forms:

- ✅ **Batholith** — a very large, commonly composite plutonic body whose base is not exposed.
- ✅ **Stock** — a smaller exposed plutonic mass; do not rely on one universal area threshold.
- ✅ **Laccolith** — a dome-shaped intrusion that **arches the overlying strata** into a blister.
- ✅ **Sill** — a sheet injected **along** bedding or foliation, therefore concordant.
- ✅ **Dike** — a sheet that **cuts across** pre-existing structure, therefore discordant.
- ✅ **Phacolith** — a lens-shaped body occupying the **crest or trough of a fold**; the source associates it with **saddle reefs and gold-quartz veins**, which is why it carries economic weight out of proportion to its size.

✅ On the extrusive side the source separates **explosive** products — **bombs, lapilli, dust and ash** — from **quiet or fissure** eruptions of mobile lava.

⚠️ Flood-basalt provinces are built by repeated fissure-fed lava flows rather than a single cone. Eruption styles and hazards are developed in Topic 03; here the point is the emplacement of a rock body.

> 🔑 **Trap.** ❌ "The Deccan Trap is a sedimentary formation." It is **igneous — flood basalt, extrusive volcanic rock**. ### I. Sedimentary rock sorted by origin

✅ Leong's classification is by **origin of the material**, and each class carries worked examples that an answer can name.

- ✅ **Mechanically formed.** Fragments of pre-existing rock, transported and cemented. **Sandstone** is the type; **grit** is the coarser variety; **conglomerate** is made of **rounded** pebbles and **breccia** of **angular** ones; finer products are **clay, shale and mudstone**.
- ✅ **Organically formed**, and it splits in two. **Calcareous** rocks — **limestone and chalk** — are built from **corals and shellfish**. **Carbonaceous** rocks — **peat, lignite and coal** — are built from **vegetative matter**.
- ✅ **Chemically formed.** Precipitates from solution, typically by evaporation: **rock salt**, **gypsum** of the Dead Sea type, **potash** and **nitrates**.

⚠️ Clast rounding can suggest transport and reworking, though lithology and process also matter. Carbonaceous accumulation helps explain coal in Gondwana basins; carbonate sedimentation helps explain limestone resources in several Proterozoic basins.

✅ Majid cuts the same material a second way, **by transporting agent**. Water-laid, **argillaceous or aqueous**, types are **marine, lacustrine or riverine**. Wind-laid material gives **loess**. Ice-laid material gives **moraines**. ✅ Sub-aerially exposed riverine sedimentary rock develops **polygonal desiccation cracks** on drying.

✅ **Loess** is wind-deposited silt, commonly porous, weakly cemented and massive rather than clearly bedded. Absence of obvious bedding may support aeolian origin but does not alone prove it; grain size, sorting, mineralogy and regional context also matter.

### II. Metamorphic rock read backwards

✅ Metamorphic rock is produced from pre-existing rock by changes in **temperature, pressure and the chemical action of fluids**, which alter **texture and mineral composition together**. ✅ The common products named are **phyllite, quartzite, schist and slate**, with a figure devoted to the origins of common metamorphic rocks. ✅ **Gneiss** shows granular quartz-feldspar banding; **slate** shows slaty cleavage; both are field-identifiable on that basis.

| Parent | Product | What the change does | Reversibility of the inference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Limestone or dolostone | **Marble** | Recrystallisation of carbonate | Parent obvious |
| Sandstone | **Quartzite** | Quartz grains fused; hardness rises sharply | Parent obvious |
| Shale or mudstone | **Slate** (low grade) → **phyllite** → **schist** (higher grade) | Progressive alignment of platy minerals | Parent usually recoverable |
| Granite or other felsic rock | **Gneiss** | Segregation into light and dark bands | Parent often **not** recoverable |
| Carbon-rich material | **Graphite** | Loss of volatiles, ordering of carbon | ⚠️ An example, not a universal rule |

⚠️ For quartzite and marble the parent is often clear; for schist and gneiss it may be difficult to determine without mineralogical and structural evidence.

> 🔑 **Trap.** ❌ "Marble is an igneous rock." It is metamorphic, from limestone under heat and pressure, and the error is caused by its crystalline sparkle, which looks igneous. ⚠️ The cure is structural: marble is **massive and recrystallised but carries no igneous texture and no bedding**, and its chemistry is carbonate, not silicate.

> 🔑 **Second trap.** ❌ "Metamorphic rock permeability follows from the rock's name." It does not. ✅ **Fracturing, jointing and weathering control secondary permeability more than the unfractured matrix does** — the point installed at Lesson 2 and spent at Lesson 14.

### III. The chronology toolkit — the real reason this lesson exists

⚠️ Everything in the India half is a claim about **order in time**, and order in time is read from rock by four devices. Three of them come straight out of the two families just taught.

1. **Stratification and superposition.** Sedimentary rocks are **commonly stratified**, but bedding may be massive, bioturbated, deformed, metamorphosed or otherwise obscured and is not universal. Where a bedded sequence is preserved and not overturned, the lower bed was deposited first, so superposition establishes relative order.
2. **Fossils.** Sedimentary strata may preserve fossils useful for correlation; igneous rocks do not contain a primary depositional fossil assemblage. Sparse fossils in many Proterozoic successions increase reliance on radiometric, chemostratigraphic and palaeomagnetic evidence.
3. **The unconformity.** A surface between older and younger rocks can record deposition, deformation, uplift/erosion and renewed deposition, representing missing time. Indian examples must be stated for named sectors rather than as one universal basement-basin break.
4. **Cross-cutting relations.** ⚠️ A dike is younger than everything it cuts; an intrusion is younger than its host. ⚠️ This is the one device that works in igneous and metamorphic terrain where neither bedding nor fossils are available — which is to say, across most of the Indian shield.

⚠️ These devices establish **relative** order. Absolute ages come from radiometric dating and must be checked against current ICS boundaries and modern GSI/lithostratigraphic publications.

### IV. 🇮🇳 India hook — two Indian structures that are nothing but this lesson applied

⚠️ Two Indian cases are the chronology toolkit made visible, and both are developed later.

- ⚠️ **Vindhyan successions.** Older regional accounts, particularly for selected Rajasthan and northern sectors, contrast lower successions with stronger carbonate/marine influence and local deformation against younger, often sandstone-rich and more gently disposed successions. Mapped unconformities or disconformities can establish relative order, but facies, structure and boundary expression vary across the Rajasthan, Son Valley and other Vindhyan sub-basins; no single Lower/Upper contrast controls the entire basin.
- ✅ **Khondalite and charnockitic terrains.** The **Eastern Ghats khondalite suite** is a high-grade metasedimentary granulite association within the polyphase Eastern Ghats Mobile Belt; “Bengal Gneiss” is a separate historical/regional term, not a universal synonym. **Charnockitic rocks** occur in the Nilgiris and other high-grade terrains, but petrogenesis and age vary by body. Neither khondalite nor charnockite should be assigned a blanket Archaean age.

| Produced by | Deposition and cementation | Heat, pressure and chemically active fluids |

**Strongest objection.** Does the 2022 primary-rock question make the other two families unnecessary?

**Reply.** No. A classification answer must situate igneous rocks against sedimentary and metamorphic families. India's structure also requires all three: ancient crystalline/metamorphic terrains, Proterozoic and Gondwana basins, Deccan volcanics and Cenozoic foreland sediment.

⚠️ **Residual.** The three-way origin classification is clean in the textbook and messy in the field: many real rocks are mixed, a limestone can be partly chemical and partly organic, and an "unconformity" can be a complex surface rather than a single plane. Use the classification as an organising device and avoid asserting that any particular Indian bed is purely one class unless a source says so.

Revision notes:

- **Three sedimentary origins:** **mechanically formed** (sandstone; **grit** coarser; **conglomerate** rounded; **breccia** angular; clay, shale, mudstone) · **organically formed** — **calcareous** (limestone, chalk, from corals and shellfish) and **carbonaceous** (peat, lignite, coal, from vegetative matter) · **chemically formed** (rock salt, gypsum, potash, nitrates).
- **By agent:** water → **marine, lacustrine, riverine**; wind → **loess**; ice → **moraines**. Desiccation cracks mark sub-aerial riverine exposure.
- **Loess:** coherent but **not cemented**, **permeable**, **absence of stratification proves aeolian origin**, **absent in the equatorial tropics**; type case northern China.
- **Conglomerate vs breccia = distance of transport.** Rounding takes travel.
- **Metamorphic pairs:** limestone → **marble** · sandstone → **quartzite** · shale → **slate → phyllite → schist** by rising grade · granite → **gneiss**. ⚠️ Coal → graphite is an example, not a universal rule; ❗ never extended to diamond.
- Parent **recoverable** for marble and quartzite; **often not** for schist and gneiss.
- **Gneiss** = granular quartz-feldspar banding; **slate** = slaty cleavage.
- **Four chronology devices:** superposition · fossil correlation where available · unconformities as missing time/event sequences · cross-cutting relations in sedimentary and crystalline terrain.
- These give **relative** order; absolute ages are **quoted from named authors**, never asserted.
- 🇮🇳 **Vindhyan successions:** Lower/Upper facies, deformation and unconformity contrasts are regional and formation-specific; use the mapped Rajasthan, Son Valley or other sub-basin context rather than a universal division.

### Concept check

**Question:** An unconformity is sometimes described as "a gap in the record". Explain why it is better described as a recorded event, and say what sequence of events a single unconformity surface actually preserves.

**Model answer:** An unconformity is missing rock but preserved evidence. Beds below it were deposited, deformed, uplifted or exposed, and eroded; younger beds were then deposited across the truncated surface after renewed subsidence or inundation. The surface therefore records at least five events even though no sediment represents part of the elapsed time. Its value is chronological: it proves deformation and erosion occurred between two depositional episodes. The duration and regional expression may vary along the same surface, so it should not be treated as one uniform time gap.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not treat the magnitude of the time break as constant along the surface. The Vindhyan case shows the opposite: the same unconformity is prominent in the north and almost absent in parts of southern Mewar, Chittaurgarh and the Son valley, so the length of the erased interval varies regionally.

**Original Mains practice [10 marks]:** "Sedimentary rocks are classified twice over — once by the origin of their material and once by the agent that carried it." Discuss, with examples, and state what each classification explains that the other does not. *(Discuss — answer in about 150 words.)*

**Model (about 150 words):** Both classifications are in use because they answer different questions. By origin of material, sedimentary rocks are mechanically formed, from fragments of older rock — sandstone, grit, conglomerate of rounded pebbles, breccia of angular ones, clay and shale; organically formed, either calcareous from corals and shellfish, as limestone and chalk, or carbonaceous from vegetation, as peat, lignite and coal; or chemically formed by precipitation, as rock salt, gypsum, potash and nitrates. By agent, the same material is aqueous — marine, lacustrine or riverine — aeolian, or glacial. Origin explains the rock's composition and therefore its economic value: carbonaceous accumulation is why coal exists where it does. Agent explains its texture, sorting and structure: loess is coherent but uncemented, permeable, and identified by the *absence* of stratification, while glacial moraine is unsorted. Neither alone is sufficient, since a limestone's value follows from origin and its bedding from agent.

**Rubric (quantified, 10 marks):** 3 for the three-way origin classification with at least five named examples; 2 for the agent classification with the aqueous sub-types; 2 for the explanatory division of labour stated explicitly; 2 for loess used correctly, including the absence-of-stratification criterion; 1 for the conglomerate-breccia transport inference. Deduct 2 for presenting the two classifications as rivals rather than as complements.

### Part A — The two ways the crust deforms

⚠️ Ductile rocks may fold; brittle rocks fault. Compression commonly produces folds and thrusts, extension normal faults and grabens, and shear strike-slip faults. Temperature, pressure, strain rate, fluids and lithology influence the response.

**Folding, as a progression rather than a pair of terms.** ✅ Leong's figures run the whole sequence: a **simple fold**, with an up-arch (**anticline**) and a down-arch (**syncline**); then an **asymmetric fold**, where one limb is steeper; then an **overfold**, where one limb is pushed over the other; then a **recumbent fold**, lying nearly flat; and finally an **overthrust fold**, where the fold breaks and the over-riding portion is driven forward as a **nappe**. ✅ Because this sequence builds mountains by raising rock, fold mountains are called **mountains of elevation**.

⚠️ These forms are a descriptive sequence, not a universal stress meter; geometry also depends on mechanical layering, temperature and deformation history.

**Faulting.** A **horst** is relatively uplifted or left standing between normal faults while adjoining blocks are downthrown; a graben is relatively downthrown. Fault scarps and inherited surfaces may create steep margins and subdued summits. Hunsruck, Vosges and Black Forest are standard European examples.

> 🔑 **Trap, and it is tested.** ❌ Classifying the **Vosges** as a fold mountain. It is a **block mountain**, and the mechanism is **tension**, not compression. ⚠️ The error is natural — European ranges are assumed to be Alpine — and the cure is the profile: a fold mountain has a **sharp, folded, structurally continuous crest**, while a block mountain has **steep fault scarps and a flat top**.

### Part B — Four kinds of mountain, and two that candidates never reach

✅ Leong's typology has **four** classes, not two.

| Class | Mechanism | Named examples | The give-away in the field |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fold mountains** — *mountains of elevation* | Compression; folding through to overthrusting and **nappes** | Alps, Himalayas, Andes, Rockies | Folded strata; structural continuity along the chain |
| **Block mountains** | **Tension**; the surrounding land subsides along faults | **Hunsruck, Vosges, Black Forest** | Steep fault scarps, almost level summit |
| **Volcanic mountains** — *mountains of accumulation* | Build-up of erupted material | **Fuji, Mayon, Merapi, Agung, Cotopaxi** | Conical form built outward from a vent |
| **Residual mountains** — *mountains of denudation* | What is **left** after prolonged erosion of a former upland | **Mt Monadnock**; dissected plateaux including the **Highlands of Scotland**, **Scandinavia** and the **Deccan Plateau** | Accordant summit levels; no coherent structural crest |

⚠️ The fourth class carries the lesson's hidden bridge. ✅ The source names the **Deccan Plateau** among the dissected plateaux that constitute residual uplands — which means the Indian half of this topic is already present in the world half's typology. 🇮🇳 The Deccan's relief is not the product of an orogeny; it is **what denudation has left of a flood-basalt province**, and Lesson 12 supplies the magnitude of that denudation.

**Plateau types, because a "mountain" question is often a relief question.** ✅ Leong divides plateaux three ways. **Tectonic** plateaux are continental blocks, such as the **Deccan**; they may be **tilted**, like the **Meseta** of central Iberia, or **faulted**, like the **Harz**. **Intermont** plateaux lie **between** mountain ranges — the **Tibetan** plateau between the Himalaya and the Kunlun, and the **Bolivian** plateau between two Andean ranges. **Volcanic** plateaux are built of lava — **Antrim**, the **north-western Deccan**, the **Columbia-Snake**.

⚠️ Note that the Deccan appears in **two** of those classes, as a tectonic continental block and, for its north-western part, as a volcanic plateau. That is not sloppiness; it is the difference between the block's **structural** identity and its **surface** material, and an answer that distinguishes the two has earned a mark that an answer naming one of them has not.

### Part C — Orogenic history without a false universal clock

⚠️ **Caledonian**, **Variscan/Hercynian** and **Alpine** remain useful regional historical labels, but each spans multiple events and locations. Do not assign one universal age to every range grouped under a label. Present height and seismicity also depend on reactivation, uplift, lithology, climate and denudation, not age alone.

Use the labels comparatively:

- **Caledonian** — chiefly early Palaeozoic deformation in parts of the North Atlantic region.
- **Variscan/Hercynian** — chiefly late Palaeozoic deformation across parts of Europe.
- **Alpine-Himalayan** — Cenozoic belts produced through several subduction and collision histories.

### Part D — 🇮🇳 India hook — three Indian ranges, three different mechanisms

⚠️ India supplies one example of each major mechanism, and keeping them apart is the whole of the 2023 demand at Lesson 14.

- ✅ The **Aravalli-Delhi Fold Belt** is a polyphase **Proterozoic** mobile/orogenic belt resting on older basement. “One of the oldest surviving fold belts” is safer than “the world's oldest mountain”, and no single 600-700 Ma date represents the whole belt.

### §1. Who made the claim, and what exactly the claim was

✅ Alfred Wegener proposed continental displacement in **1912** and published the developed book-length case in **1915**. He argued that the continents had once formed Pangaea before separating.

⚠️ Be precise about what is being claimed, because the precision is examinable. The claim is of **horizontal displacement of continental blocks**. It is not a claim that the continents changed shape, nor that the ocean floors were fixed and the continents ploughed across them — although Wegener's critics read him that way, which is why the "tracks" objection below bit so hard.

### §2. The five classes of evidence, each with its logic attached

⚠️ Listing the five earns little; attaching the *inferential move* to each earns the paragraph.

| Class | What is observed | Why it supports drift | The refinement that upgrades it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jig-saw fit** | The Atlantic margins, especially South America and Africa, match | Two blocks that fit were once joined | The match is **better at the continental-shelf edge than at the shoreline**, because coastlines are erosional and shelf edges are the true structural margins |
| **Geological continuity** | Mountain belts and old rock provinces line up across the ocean on reassembly | Identical structures cannot be produced independently on two separated blocks | ✅ The source's figure pairs the fit with the distribution of **major basalt plateaus** |
| **Fossil distribution** | Identical terrestrial floras and faunas on now-separated southern continents | Land organisms cannot cross an ocean basin | ✅ A dedicated figure of palaeontological evidence; the southern continents are the decisive set |
| **Palaeoclimatic evidence** | Glacial deposits in today's tropics; coal-forming, warm, swampy deposits in today's high latitudes | The rock records a **different latitude** for the same block | 🇮🇳 India's own Gondwana record sits here — see §5 |
| **Palaeomagnetism** | Magnetic minerals in old rocks point to poles that appear to have wandered, and to have wandered **differently** on different continents | The poles are broadly fixed; divergent apparent paths mean the **continents** moved | This class arrived **after** the hypothesis was rejected and is what revived it — Lesson 7 |

⚠️ Note the structure of the fifth row. It is the only class whose evidential force is **comparative**: a single continent's apparent polar wander path proves nothing on its own, because the pole could have moved. **Two** continents with **different** paths cannot both be right about a single fixed pole, so at least one of them moved. That is a stronger logical form than any of the first four, and it is why palaeomagnetism and not fossils broke the deadlock.

### §3. Why the hypothesis failed — and the precise form of the failure

✅ Wegener proposed **two driving forces**: a **centrifugal "pole-fleeing" force** generated by the Earth's rotation, and the **tidal force of the Moon and Sun**. ✅ Both were judged physically inadequate to move continental masses, and ✅ the hypothesis was **rejected for want of a viable mechanism**.

✅ Two secondary criticisms are recorded and both are worth naming. The **'wakes' or 'tracks' critique** asked where the trails were that continental blocks ploughing through the ocean floor should have left. And Wegener was accused of **selecting his evidence**, using what supported the case and passing over what did not.

> 🔑 **Trap, and it is the single most valuable sentence in this lesson.** ❌ "Wegener's drift was rejected because the evidence was wrong." It was not. ✅ The evidence was assembled convincingly; what was missing was an **adequate driving mechanism**. ⚠️ Write that distinction explicitly in any drift answer: a hypothesis can be well-evidenced and still be refused, and the thing that secures acceptance in the earth sciences is a **mechanism that the physics permits**.

⚠️ **The reply that history supplied.** The 'tracks' objection assumed that continents move *through* a static ocean floor. Lesson 7's answer is that the ocean floor is **not** static: it is created at ridges and consumed at trenches, and continents are carried **with** it rather than ploughing across it. The objection was therefore a good objection to the wrong model, and it dissolved the moment the model changed.

### §4. The rehabilitation, dated

✅ Palaeomagnetic and ocean-floor evidence accumulated through the **1950s and 1960s**. Plate tectonics emerged through a **mid-to-late-1960s synthesis**, not one universal establishment date.

⚠️ Drift was incorporated into a broader theory rather than accepted unchanged. Sea-floor spreading describes creation and recycling of oceanic crust; plate tectonics generalises motion and interaction of rigid lithospheric plates.

### §5. 🇮🇳 India hook — India's own place in Gondwana, and the glacial proof beneath it

⚠️ India is not a bystander in the drift argument; it is one of the principal pieces of evidence.

- ✅ India belonged to the **southern assembly, Gondwana**, whose fragmentation and the **northward drift of the Indian subcontinent to collide with Eurasia** is an explicit step in the chronology of India's own geological history.
- ✅ Talchir diamictite/tillite records late Palaeozoic glacial conditions in Gondwana basins. Its occurrence in present tropical India supports a very different past continental position and climate.

Here Talchir is used only as palaeoclimatic evidence; coal geology appears at Lesson 12.

### Comparison, objection and the residual

| Moving object | **Continents**, through the ocean floor | **Lithospheric plates**, carrying continents and ocean floor together |
| Driving force offered | Pole-fleeing centrifugal force; tidal force of Moon and Sun | Slab pull, ridge push, gravitational potential and mantle flow |
| Status of the ocean floor | Passive and essentially permanent | **Created at ridges, destroyed at trenches** |
| Fate | Chiefly rejected for inadequate mechanism | Incorporated displacement into a broader testable framework |
| What survives | Fit, geological, fossil and palaeoclimate evidence | Reinterpreted with palaeomagnetic and ocean-floor tests |

**Strongest objection.** If drift was absorbed into plate tectonics, why teach it separately at all? Why not begin at Lesson 8?

**Reply.** Three reasons, and the first is the examinable one. The 2025 objective demand is specifically on the **evidences of continental drift**, which is a question about the older theory's evidential base and not about plate kinematics. Second, the five evidence classes are **still the evidence**: they did not become redundant when the mechanism arrived, they became explained. Say "chiefly" rather than "solely" when assigning the cause of rejection.

### Stage 1 — Palaeomagnetism: how a rock remembers

⚠️ Certain iron-bearing minerals, while a lava is still hot, are free to align with the ambient magnetic field; as the lava cools past a critical temperature they lock in that alignment and retain it afterwards. A cooled basalt therefore carries a **frozen record of the direction of the field at the moment it solidified**.

⚠️ Two independent kinds of information come out of that record. The **direction** gives the position of the magnetic pole relative to the rock; collected across many rocks of different ages on one continent, it traces an **apparent polar wander path**. The **polarity** — whether the field pointed as it does now or the opposite way — gives a sequence of **reversals**.

The geodynamo and magnetosphere are established in Topic 01; here the field is used as a signal recorded by cooling rock.

⚠️ The first kind of information is what revived drift, by the comparative argument set out at Lesson 6. The second kind is what made spreading **testable**, and that is this lesson's business.

### Stage 2 — The ocean floor stops being a blank

✅ Systematic bathymetric, magnetic, seismic and age mapping converted the ocean floor from an assumed passive surface into testable evidence. Unverified one-line attributions for the first ridge map or the phrase “Ring of Fire” are not used.

✅ Mid-ocean ridges are elevated volcanic-tectonic systems; the **Mid-Atlantic Ridge rises above sea level in Iceland**. Transform boundaries offset ridge segments; the **San Andreas** is a continental transform example.

### Stage 3 — Early-1960s spreading models and their tests

✅ Hess's early-1960s sea-floor-spreading model proposed creation of oceanic crust at ridges and recycling at trenches. Modern plate motion is not reduced to basal conveyor-belt convection: **slab pull, ridge push, gravitational potential-energy differences and mantle flow** all contribute.

✅ The mechanism runs in sequence: mantle upwelling and decompression melting → magma rises at the **mid-ocean ridge** and new oceanic crust forms at the axis → cooling crust **records magnetic polarity** → crust moves away on both flanks → the floor becomes **older and more sediment-covered** away from the ridge → oceanic lithosphere is recycled at subduction zones.

| Ocean-floor observation | What spreading explains about it |
|---|---|
| **Symmetrical magnetic stripes** of alternating polarity parallel to the ridge | The floor is a tape recorder: made at the axis, carried outward on both sides, so the record is mirrored |
| **Age increases away from the ridge crest** | Because the crust was made at the crest |
| **Sediment thickness increases away from the ridge** | Older floor has had longer to accumulate sediment |
| **Deep trenches with inclined earthquake zones descending beneath arcs** | Old, cold, dense oceanic crust is being consumed |
| **The ocean floor is geologically young** compared with continental shields | Oceanic crust is continuously recycled; continental crust is buoyant and persists |

> 🔑 **Trap.** ❌ Describing spreading without subduction. **The Earth is not expanding**; creation at ridges is balanced over geological time by recycling at convergent margins. Continents move with plates rather than ploughing through a static ocean floor.

> 🔑 **Second trap.** Sea-floor spreading is not identical to plate tectonics. Spreading concerns ocean-floor creation and recycling; the mid-to-late-1960s plate-tectonic synthesis generalises rigid-plate motion and boundary kinematics.

### Stage 4 — What the mechanism did to the older theory

⚠️ The decisive effect was not that spreading proved drift. It was that spreading made the **ocean floor an object of evidence** rather than a passive gap between the interesting continents. Once the floor was young, striped, symmetric and recycled, every one of Wegener's distributional observations acquired a cause, and the fifty-year objection evaporated.

⚠️ Scientific acceptance changed because both the evidence base and explanatory framework changed: palaeomagnetism, ocean-floor mapping, spreading and boundary seismology supplied tests and mechanisms unavailable to Wegener.

### Stage 6 — 🇮🇳 India hook — two oceans that did not exist

⚠️ The spreading mechanism is not an Atlantic curiosity for India; it is the reason two of India's three maritime frontiers exist at all.

- ✅ The **Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal evolved during Mesozoic-Cenozoic breakup, rifting and sea-floor spreading** around the Indian plate. Their oceanic basins are younger than much of the Peninsular crust they border.

### Concept check

**Question:** Of the four classic ocean-floor observations, why is the symmetry of the magnetic stripes the decisive one rather than the increase of age away from the ridge?

**Model answer:** Because symmetry is the observation that no competing model reproduced. Increasing age away from the axis is consistent with spreading but could in principle be accommodated by other accounts of how floor is added or preserved, and it is a one-sided measurement that says nothing about the two flanks together. The magnetic record is different in kind. Each strip of new crust locks in the polarity of the field at the instant it cools; if crust is created at a single axis and carried away on both flanks at comparable rates, the same sequence of normal and reversed strips must appear on each side as a mirror image about the axis. Mirrored magnetic stripes record alternating polarity on both flanks; crustal age and sediment cover increase away from the axis; trenches and inclined earthquake zones mark recycling. Together these observations explain both creation and destruction without an expanding Earth. They also resolve Wegener's mechanism problem: continents move with lithospheric plates rather than ploughing through a static floor. ### Movement 1 — The plates themselves, stated with the right level of caution

✅ There are **seven major plates together with several minor ones**. ✅ **Shields are the oldest and most stable** portions of the plates. ✅ Three kinds of movement occur at boundaries. ✅ Oceanic crust averages about **5 km** against continental crust of **30-40 km**, which is the thickness contrast installed at Lesson 1.

❗ Do **not** quote a supposedly final global plate count. A safe formulation is: *"seven conventionally recognised major plates and numerous smaller plates whose enumeration varies by authority."*

⚠️ Oceanic lithosphere cools and densifies with age and commonly subducts beneath more buoyant continental lithosphere. At ocean-ocean convergence the older/colder plate often subducts, while continent-continent convergence mainly shortens and thickens crust.

### Movement 2 — Divergence, and what a constructive margin looks like

✅ At **divergent margins** plates move apart and new oceanic crust forms at ridges; continental extension may begin with rifting. The Mid-Atlantic Ridge is exposed in Iceland. Seismicity is shallow, and decompression melting commonly produces mafic volcanism.

### Movement 3 — Convergence, split three ways because the products differ

⚠️ Most scripts write "convergent boundaries produce fold mountains and volcanoes", which is true of one of the three sub-types and false of another. The three must be separated.

- **Ocean meets continent.** The ocean plate subducts. Products: a **trench**, a **fold-mountain chain with explosive andesitic volcanoes**, and **deep-focus earthquakes** along the descending slab. The Andes is the standing case.
- **Ocean meets ocean.** The older, denser plate subducts. Products: a **trench**, an **island arc**, and a **marginal or back-arc sea**.
- **Continent meets continent.** Buoyant continental crust resists deep subduction. Products include crustal shortening, thickening, fold-thrust belts, high plateaux and strong seismicity; a continuous Andean-style arc is absent, though regional/post-collisional magmatism can occur.

> 🔑 **Trap.** ❌ “The Himalaya is a volcanic arc.” The Himalaya is a continent-continent collision orogen, unlike the Andean subduction arc. This does not exclude all regional magmatism; it excludes classifying the range itself as an Andean-style volcanic arc.

### Movement 4 — The transform regime, which builds nothing and matters anyway

✅ At **transform or conservative** margins plates slide past each other; **crust is neither created nor destroyed**. Products: **long strike-slip fault zones** and **shallow but destructive earthquakes**, with **no volcanic arc**. ✅ The named case is the **San Andreas**; the source also calls these **shearing or neutral margins**.

⚠️ The analytic point: the transform regime demonstrates that **plate boundaries are defined by relative motion, not by what they build**. A boundary that produces no new rock is still a boundary, which is why a crust-budget column belongs in the matrix at all.

### Movement 5 — The life cycle, which is the paragraph that separates a top answer

⚠️ A list of boundary types is static. The question asked in 2025 was about **change**, and change is demonstrated by the cycle, not by the list. The six stages are set out in the rail above; what follows is why each one is worth its line.

| Stage | The process | Present-day case | Why this case and not another |
|---|---|---|---|
| Embryonic | Continental crust domes, stretches and rifts | **East African Rift** | A continent visibly in the act of splitting, on land where it can be observed |

| Suture | The ocean is consumed; a collision belt marks the join | 🇮🇳 **Himalaya**, marking the **closed Tethys** | The only stage where the ocean is gone and the evidence is a mountain range |

✅ The margin distinction that hangs off stage three and stage four is economically decisive. **Passive, Atlantic-type margins** sit *inside* a plate: wide shelves, thick sediment wedges, little seismicity — and therefore the world's principal **offshore hydrocarbon** provinces. **Active, Pacific-type margins** sit *at* a plate edge: narrow shelves, trenches, arcs, strong seismicity and volcanism.

The tectonic setting explains why passive margins favour thick sediment wedges; petroleum occurrence still requires source, reservoir, seal and trap.

⚠️ **The qualification that must accompany the cycle.** The six stages are a **conceptual ordering, not a timetable**. A basin can stall, and a rift can fail without becoming a sea. No universal rate or duration applies.

### Movement 6 — 🇮🇳 India hook — the one country that occupies two stages of the rail at once

⚠️ India is the most economical illustration of the whole cycle available anywhere, because its northern and its southern margins sit at **opposite ends of the same sequence**.

- ✅ Northward motion of India and convergence with Eurasia consumed Tethyan oceanic domains and built the Himalayan-Tibetan collision system. Use **Cenozoic**, not the obsolete blanket term “Tertiary”.
- ✅ On the south and west, the **development and expansion of the Arabian Sea and the Bay of Bengal** after Gondwanaland disintegrated is basin **creation** — the early stages of the same rail.
- ⚠️ So one country displays the closing of one ocean and the opening of two others, produced by a single event: the break-up of a supercontinent and the northward transit of the block between them. That is a complete answer to "change in continents and ocean basins" in three sentences, and it is India-centric without being parochial.

### Concept check

**Question:** Using only the ocean-basin life cycle, explain why the Atlantic has broad continental shelves and offshore oil while the Pacific rim has trenches, arcs and great earthquakes — and say what this implies about the permanence of either pattern.

**Model answer:** The two oceans occupy different stages of one sequence. The Atlantic is mature: it has a central spreading ridge and its continental margins lie *inside* plates rather than at their edges, so they are passive and aseismic, accumulate wide shelves and thick sediment wedges, and consequently host major offshore hydrocarbon provinces. The Pacific is declining: subduction has begun around its rim, so its margins are active plate edges with trenches, island arcs, arc volcanism and shallow-to-deep seismicity, and its shelves are narrow. The pattern in each case follows from the stage, not from any inherent property of the ocean. That is the implication: neither pattern is permanent. Subduction can initiate along a passive margin, which would convert an Atlantic-type margin into a Pacific-type one and begin the closing of that basin — which is exactly how a declining basin arises in the first place.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not treat "passive margin" as a synonym for "coastline". A passive margin is defined by its position inside a plate, not by the presence of a shore; an active margin also has a coastline, and the difference between them is tectonic position, not coastal form.

### Bridge A — Isostasy: why anything stands above anything else

⚠️ **Isostasy** describes gravitational balance among lithospheric columns over the denser, deformable mantle. It does not require a universal sial-over-sima shell.

⚠️ Keep the two conceptions separate:

- **Airy.** Columns have **similar density** but **different root depth**. A higher mountain has a **deeper root**, exactly as a taller iceberg has more ice below the waterline.
- **Pratt.** Columns rise to **different heights** because their **density differs** above a **common depth of compensation**. A column stands higher because it is lighter, not because it is more deeply anchored.

❗ **Why the distinction must survive.** The familiar argument that the Himalaya must possess a deep crustal root is an **Airy** argument; it has no force under a Pratt conception, where the same height would be explained by low density instead. An answer that writes "the mountains float, so they have roots" has assumed one of the two models without naming it. Writing "on the Airy conception the Himalayan elevation implies a deep root" costs four extra words and converts an assumption into an argument.

⚠️ **Isostatic adjustment is observable.** Removal of a heavy ice load produces post-glacial rebound; sediment loading can contribute to deltaic subsidence. The coastal-risk implications require additional evidence and are not developed here.

⚠️ **Orogeny** can involve convergence, shortening, thrusting, metamorphism, magmatism, crustal thickening, exhumation and erosion. Processes and timing vary by belt; no universal basin-to-mountain sequence or single uplift pulse applies.

### Bridge B — The three-fold relief hierarchy, which is the world's answer and India's question

⚠️ The standard hierarchy that follows from orogeny plus time has three terms:

| Term | Character | Seismicity | Mineral association | Indian instance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ancient shield/craton** | Comparatively stable, worn down, crystalline | Lower long-term deformation than a young fold belt, but not aseismic | Metallic-mineral associations | 🇮🇳 Peninsular cratons |
| **Relict or faulted upland** | Commonly subdued or fault-bounded | May be reactivated; not necessarily aseismic | Variable | 🇮🇳 Aravalli-Delhi relief; Satpura-Maikal uplands |
| **Active fold-thrust belt** | High relief and active deformation | Seismically active | Mixed sedimentary, metamorphic and crystalline units | 🇮🇳 Himalayan-Tibetan belt |

✅ **Shields are exposed ancient cratonic areas**, while platforms are cratonic basement covered by younger strata. ⚠️ India contains cratonic/mobile-belt terrain, a Himalayan-Tibetan orogenic belt and a Cenozoic foreland basin within one regional system.

### Bridge C — India's broad structural provinces

⚠️ Older texts commonly divide India into three broad regions:

1. ✅ **The Peninsular region**, including cratonic/mobile-belt terrain and fault-separated extensions such as Meghalaya.
2. ✅ **The Himalayan-Tibetan orogenic region** and its eastern continuation.
3. ✅ **The Indo-Gangetic plain** lying between them.

The Andaman-Nicobar island arc is tectonically distinct and should not be treated as a simple Himalayan extension.

Some schemes combine the plain with the Himalayan foreland system. Treat the division count as analytical, not natural.

⚠️ **Use the disagreement.** A named, citable disagreement is worth more in a Mains answer than a confidently asserted single scheme, because it shows that the candidate knows a division is an analytical decision. The two-division scheme has a real argument behind it: the plain is a **downwarp of the Himalayan foreland**, formed concomitantly with the rise of the range, so it is arguably part of the extra-Peninsular system rather than a third thing. ⚠️ The three-division scheme replies that the plain's **surface material, its age and its land use** are so distinct that treating it as an appendix of the mountains obscures more than it clarifies. Both positions are defensible; the edition teaches the three-fold division and names the alternative.

### Bridge D — The Peninsular block in modern terms

- ✅ Ancient cratons and Proterozoic mobile belts form a long-lived continental nucleus.
- ⚠️ Stability is comparative: major shear zones, rifts, intraplate earthquakes and reactivated faults remain.
- ✅ Prolonged denudation produces residual plateaux, subdued surfaces and resistant hill ranges.
- ❗ Reject the absolute “no marine submergence since the Cambrian” claim. Kachchh and east-coast basins record later marine incursions; parts of the western coast also record local tectonic submergence.
- ✅ Veliconda and Seshachalam are residual uplands of the southern Eastern Ghats; Mahendragiri is a prominent Eastern-Ghats summit, though not the highest.
- ⚠️ Old plateau surfaces commonly have mature, broad valleys, but structural gorges and rift-controlled valleys also occur. Do not generalise one valley form to the whole block.

⚠️ The **Meghalaya plateau** is commonly treated as a fault-separated extension of Peninsular basement despite its physiographic separation by the Ganga-Brahmaputra lowland. Geology follows crustal affinity; physiography follows present relief.

### Phase 1 — Why the Cenozoic is pivotal

⚠️ **Tertiary** is obsolete formal terminology. Use **Paleogene** and **Neogene** for the Cenozoic intervals during which India-Eurasia convergence, Himalayan deformation and foreland-basin sedimentation developed.

### Phase 2 — Himalayan and foreland successions

✅ Paleogene and Neogene sedimentary successions vary regionally along the Himalayan and north-eastern foreland. The **Siwalik Group** is a thick Neogene-to-early-Quaternary molasse succession derived from the rising orogen; ages, thicknesses and subdivisions require a named basin/sector and modern source.

⚠️ **The Siwaliks are largely the orogen's recycled debris.** Sediment eroded from the rising range accumulated in the foreland and was later deformed as the thrust front propagated southward. Hydrocarbon potential, where present, requires source, maturation, reservoir, seal and trap; thick sediment alone is insufficient.

### Phase 3 — Polyphase convergence and southward propagation

⚠️ Himalayan collision, metamorphism, exhumation, thrusting and uplift are polyphase and diachronous. The older three-date table is removed: **45 Ma is Eocene, not mid-Miocene**, and one date cannot be assigned to each physiographic range. The robust sequence is southward propagation of deformation into progressively younger foreland sediment, with continuing convergence today.

> 🔑 **Trap.** The Himalaya is not an Andean volcanic arc and does not consist only of young sediment. It contains sedimentary, metamorphic and crystalline units. Everest's summit limestone is the **Ordovician Qomolangma Formation**, not Upper Carboniferous.

### Phase 4 — The Quaternary, and the plain as a structure

✅ The Indo-Gangetic-Brahmaputra plain occupies a Himalayan **foreland basin** created by flexural subsidence during Cenozoic convergence. Much of its present surface alluvium is Quaternary, but basin development and sedimentation began earlier.

⚠️ **This is the claim that makes the plain a geological object rather than a depositional accident.** A plain that is simply "where sediment collected" invites no further question. A plain that is a **downwarp formed concomitantly with the mountains** is part of the orogenic system: the load of the rising range depressed the crust in front of it, and that depression was then filled by the range's own erosion products. ❗ An answer that describes the Indo-Gangetic plain merely as an alluvial surface has missed the structure beneath it.

✅ Under ICS 2026/06 the Quaternary begins at **2.58 Ma** and the Holocene at **0.0117 Ma**. Older four-glacial/three-interglacial schemes are historiographic and must not control modern Quaternary chronology.

✅ **Karewa** deposits are lacustrine-fluvial successions in the Kashmir basin; assigning the whole succession to one “second interglacial” is obsolete.

✅ ***Bhangar*** denotes older alluvial terrace/surface deposits and ***khadar*** younger floodplain alluvium. Their absolute ages vary regionally; do not assign one universal Pleistocene bracket.

> 🔑 **Trap.** ❌ Treating *bhangar* and *khadar* as universal soil-age boxes. They are relative geomorphic-alluvial categories: older/higher alluvial surfaces versus younger active-floodplain deposits. Their absolute ages vary regionally.

### Phase 5 — Glacial evidence

✅ Talchir diamictite/tillite is a key Gondwana palaeoclimatic indicator. Himalayan Quaternary glaciation is regionally complex and should be dated with modern local studies rather than inherited elevation limits or a three-ice-age textbook list.

### Phase 6 — Coastal structure without obsolete length

❗ No legacy coastline length is used. This lesson retains only the structural point that rifting, faulting, subsidence, sedimentation and sea-level change produced contrasting coastal sectors.

✅ **Evidence of emergence and submergence** follows, and it is the part that earns marks:

✅ Geological structure informs **land-use planning, irrigation potential, groundwater quality and quantity, transport alignment and hazard interpretation**.

### Branch 5 — Where the fan stops, and the restraint that is itself a teachable move

⚠️ Three limits must travel with every use of the fan.

1. **Association, not determination.** ✅ Geological association supports a **probability**, not a deterministic mineral or hazard prediction. ⚠️ A shield is a favourable host for metallic ores; it does not guarantee a deposit, and grade, depth, accessibility, price and policy decide what is mined.
2. **Multiple causation at every node.** ✅ Soil formation depends on climate, relief, organisms and time as well as on parent rock, so **parent rock alone does not determine soil**. The same is true of drainage, of groundwater and of settlement.
3. **Numbers are the weak point.** ❗ Do not quote peak heights, plain widths, plateau areas, coastline lengths or subdivision counts from memory. Use a current named map/source.

**Boundary:** lithology supplies the substrate for resource and carbon-cycle questions, but reserves, mining economics and sequestration chemistry require their own evidence.

**Strongest objection.** The causal fan looks like environmental determinism in modern dress. Does it not simply assert that geology decides how Indians live?

**Reply.** It does not, and the structure of the argument shows why. The fan claims that structure **sets the distribution of possibilities** at each node and that the possibilities narrow as one moves outward — rock to relief is tight, relief to drainage is tight, drainage to settlement is loose, and settlement to prosperity is looser still. ⚠️ At every node a second set of variables enters: climate and time at the soil node, technology and capital at the resource node, price and policy at the land-use node. **The Gangetic plain is not densely settled because it is alluvial; it is densely settled because alluvium plus monsoon water plus a long agrarian history plus modern irrigation made it so.** The honest formulation is that structure explains the **pattern of opportunity**, and the social and economic variables explain what was done with it.

### Concept check

**Question:** A candidate argues that India's mineral poverty in the north and mineral wealth in the east-central Peninsula "proves that geology determines economic geography". Assess the argument and restate it correctly.

**Model answer:** The evidence is real but the inference is overstated. Exposed cratonic and mobile-belt terrains in east-central and southern India host many primary metallic-mineral systems, whereas thick alluvium masks basement and favours agriculture and aquifers. Yet alluvial plains can contain placer and construction resources, and an ore-bearing host does not guarantee an economic deposit. Ore-forming history, grade, continuity, depth, accessibility, infrastructure, price, technology and regulation intervene. Geology therefore conditions the spatial opportunity for particular deposits; it does not determine economic geography or prosperity.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not infer from a strong spatial correlation that no other variable is operating. The correlation holds at the node where it is tightest — rock to possible mineral host — and weakens at every node further out along the fan.

**Original Mains practice [10 marks]:** "The Eastern Ghats are a misnomer." Examine this claim and state what the naming dispute reveals about describing relief. *(Examine — answer in about 150 words.)*

**Model (about 150 words):** The Eastern Ghats differ from the Western Ghats: they are a discontinuous Proterozoic mobile-belt and topographic system repeatedly breached by rivers, not one continuous western-style escarpment. The name remains useful geographically but must not imply uniform structure. Modern map control also matters: Jindhagada/Arma Konda, not Mahendragiri, is generally identified as the highest summit. Biligirirangan occupies the transition between the two Ghats, while Seshachalam belongs to the southern Eastern Ghats. The broader lesson is that a relief name may combine distinct geological and geomorphic units; answers should state whether they are using a tectonic belt, physiographic range or administrative map.

**Rubric (quantified, 10 marks):** 3 for discontinuity/mobile-belt complexity; 2 for contrast with the western escarpment; 2 for Biligirirangan and Seshachalam placement; 2 for distinguishing tectonic belt from topographic range; 1 for current summit qualification. Deduct 2 for treating the Ghats as structural equivalents.

### Instrument 4 — The three evidence units, ready to deploy

✅ Three evidence units use the form **claim → evidence → significance → limitation**.

1. ✅ **Claim:** ocean basins have a life cycle rather than a fixed identity. **Evidence:** the East African Rift, Red Sea, Atlantic, Pacific, Mediterranean and Himalayan suture occupy successive stages of one sequence **simultaneously**. **Significance:** it lets an answer *demonstrate* change using places that exist today. **Limitation:** the stages are a conceptual ordering, not a timetable; a basin can stall or reverse.
2. ✅ **Claim:** tectonic setting conditions resource and hazard patterns. **Evidence:** passive margins favour thick sediment wedges; active margins carry trenches, arcs and deep seismic zones. **Limitation:** hydrocarbons still require source, reservoir, seal and trap.
3. ✅ **Claim:** rock genesis governs the surface geography above it. **Evidence:** the extrusive basalt of the Deccan weathers to black soil underpinning a cotton belt, while crystalline shields store groundwater in fractures rather than pore space. **Significance:** it converts petrology into an agriculture and water-security argument. **Limitation:** soil formation also depends on climate, relief, organisms and time — parent rock alone does not determine soil.

⚠️ A fourth unit applies the form to India. **Claim:** substrate helps separate major primary-metallic belts from intensive alluvial agriculture. **Evidence:** crystalline east-central/southern belts host many ores; alluvial plains favour soils and aquifers, with placer exceptions. **Limitation:** grade, depth, access, infrastructure, price and policy control extraction.

# CUMULATIVE CONCEPT CHECKS

Five checks. Three consolidate a teaching block, one tests the chronology thread that runs across the whole India half, and the fifth spans the topic — justified because the chain from a classification frame to an Indian resource geography cannot be tested inside any single block.

### Cumulative check A — the classification foundation, Lessons 1 to 4

**Question:** Build the chain from "the crust is an aggregate of minerals" to "an unconformity records five events", naming every intermediate step, and identify the point at which description becomes chronology.

**Model answer:** A rock is an aggregate of minerals, so composition helps control weathering, density and permeability. Genesis divides rocks into igneous, sedimentary and metamorphic families; texture and structure then record formation conditions. Coarse igneous textures generally indicate slower cooling than fine textures. Bedding permits superposition, fossils may support correlation in suitable sedimentary units, and cross-cutting relations establish relative age. An unconformity truncating older deformed beds beneath younger strata records deposition, deformation, uplift/erosion and renewed deposition. Description becomes chronology when structures are placed in relative sequence.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not treat the chronology toolkit as an add-on to the classification. Superposition, fossils, unconformities and cross-cutting relations are all consequences of the family properties taught for identification; the same facts do both jobs.

### Cumulative check B — the tectonic argument, Lessons 5 to 8

**Question:** Trace the movement from Wegener's 1912/1915 drift case through early-1960s sea-floor spreading to the mid-to-late-1960s plate-tectonic synthesis. What did each stage add?

**Model answer:** Wegener's 1912 proposal and 1915 book assembled distributional evidence for continental displacement but lacked a physically adequate mechanism. Early-1960s sea-floor spreading made the ocean floor active and generated testable expectations: mirrored magnetic stripes, increasing age and sediment away from ridges, and crustal recycling at trenches. The mid-to-late-1960s plate-tectonic synthesis then generalised rigid-lithosphere motion and boundary kinematics. Modern plate dynamics combine slab pull, ridge push, gravitational potential energy and mantle flow. Regional mountain-belt histories remain necessary because one boundary mechanism may operate through several deformation and reactivation episodes; no single Caledonian, Variscan or Alpine date applies worldwide.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not assign acceptance to one year. Both evidence and mechanism changed across the 1950s-60s synthesis.

### Cumulative check C — the bridge, Lessons 8, 9 and 13

**Question:** Relate India's ancient continental nucleus, Himalayan-Tibetan collision system and foreland basin without treating them as three independent facts.

**Model answer:** The Peninsular nucleus consists of ancient cratons and Proterozoic mobile belts that long pre-date Himalayan collision, though they retain faults, rifts and intraplate activity. Northward plate motion and Cenozoic convergence consumed Tethyan oceanic domains and produced the Himalayan-Tibetan fold-thrust system through polyphase deformation, metamorphism and exhumation. Flexural subsidence in front of the growing orogen created a foreland basin, which Himalayan erosion progressively filled. Much of the present alluvial surface is Quaternary, but the basin began developing earlier. The three provinces are therefore linked by tectonic inheritance, convergence, flexure and sediment supply, not by one simple event or a fixed three-date uplift ladder.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not place the plain's origin alongside the other two as a parallel third fact. It is downstream of both, and dating it with them destroys the causal order that makes the three-province scheme an explanation rather than a list.

### Cumulative check D — the Indian chronology thread, Lessons 10 to 13

**Question:** Why must the historical Archaean/Purana/Dravidian/Aryan ladder be treated as historiography rather than as India's controlling modern chronology?

**Model answer:** The ladder groups unlike units and mixes age, lithology and tectonic setting. “Archaean” may mean basement or a broad old grouping; “Purana” is used inconsistently; “Dravidian” is an obsolete Palaeozoic umbrella; “Aryan” combines Gondwana sediments, Deccan volcanics, Cenozoic orogeny and Quaternary deposits. It also encourages false inferences from missing strata. Modern answers instead use ICS periods and named cratons, mobile belts, supergroups, basins and volcanic provinces. The fourfold scheme may still be mentioned to interpret older books, but it cannot control chronology.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not resolve disagreement by selecting one older fourfold scheme as modern truth. Translate observations into named units and ICS time.

### Cumulative check E — the whole topic, Lessons 1 to 15

**Question:** In one connected argument, state what this topic establishes about the relationship between a rock and a human geography, drawing on at least five lessons, and say exactly where the relationship stops holding.

**Model answer:** Lithology and tectonic setting constrain possibilities, but the relationship weakens along the chain to human outcomes. Mineral assemblage influences density, weathering and permeability; genesis influences texture and structure; boundary setting conditions basins, hazards and relief. In India, cratons/mobile belts favour metallic mineralisation, Gondwana rifts host major coalfields, and alluvial foreland deposits support productive aquifers and agriculture. Yet none is deterministic. Soil also depends on climate, organisms, relief and time; hydrocarbons require source, reservoir, seal and trap; extraction depends on grade, access, infrastructure, price and policy. Structure explains opportunity patterns, not economic outcomes.

**Misconception to avoid:** Do not read this as licence for determinism in its weak form either. The fan does not become determinism merely because its early nodes are tight; the honest claim is that each node narrows a distribution, and the number of nodes between rock and livelihood is large.

## 10-mark practice (about 150 words)

**Practice question 1 [10] — mechanism and classification.** "Texture is a historical record, not merely a descriptive property." Explain, with reference to igneous rocks. *(Explain — answer in about 150 words.)*

**Model answer (original):** Texture records crystallisation history. Slow cooling generally permits larger crystals, while rapid cooling yields fine-grained or glassy textures; cooling rate also depends on body size, emplacement geometry and thermal surroundings, not depth alone. Coarse granite exposed at the surface therefore indicates intrusive crystallisation followed by uplift and erosion, whereas fine-grained Deccan basalt records rapid cooling of erupted mafic lava. The inference is general rather than exact: porphyritic rocks record more than one cooling stage, and an intrusion may have a coarse interior but a fine chilled margin.

**Rubric (quantified, 10 marks):** 3 for cooling rate/crystal size; 2 for additional controls; 2 for Indian intrusive/extrusive examples; 2 for reconstruction of emplacement/exposure; 1 for porphyritic or chilled-margin qualification.

**Practice question 2 [10] — evidence and its limits.** Examine how absence of strata may inform geological history without proving continuous emergence. *(Examine — answer in about 150 words.)*

**Model answer (original):** Missing strata can indicate non-deposition, erosion, burial or limited exposure. Their absence becomes informative only when combined with basin geometry, unconformities, palaeogeography and dating. Peninsular India's patchy Palaeozoic record therefore supports regional contrasts with Himalayan-Tethyan successions but cannot prove universal emergence after the Cambrian. Later marine-margin and intracratonic basins independently demonstrate marine incursions. The safe conclusion is that preservation histories differ across provinces; absence narrows possibilities but does not identify one cause by itself.

**Rubric (quantified, 10 marks):** 3 for four causes of absence; 3 for combining absence with positive evidence; 2 for regional preservation contrast; 1 for later marine incursions; 1 for a qualified conclusion. Deduct 2 for “never submerged”.

## 15-mark practice (about 250 words)

**Practice question 3 [15] — chronology and causation.** "Cenozoic convergence reorganised India's northern structural framework." Discuss. *(Discuss — answer in about 250 words.)*

**Model answer (original):** Cenozoic convergence consumed Tethyan oceanic domains and produced the Himalayan-Tibetan fold-thrust system through polyphase deformation, metamorphism, thrusting and exhumation. Deformation propagated southward into younger foreland sediment, producing Siwalik molasse rather than three discrete mountain-building dates. This makes the plain a tectonic basin before it is a depositional surface. The reorganisation also affected drainage, sediment routing, seismicity and sedimentary-resource provinces. ⚠️ “Tertiary” should be replaced by Paleogene and Neogene, and uplift cannot be assigned to a false “mid-Miocene 45 Ma” phase. **Practice question 4 [15] — comparison across the two halves.** Compare the Deccan Trap and the Columbia-Snake province as expressions of one process, and assess what the comparison adds to an understanding of India's geological structure. *(Compare and assess — answer in about 250 words.)*

# COMPLETE CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

Compressed for retrieval. This section changes form and does not repeat the lessons' explanatory prose; where an exam-tight statement must recur, the explanation is stripped out so that recall density stays high. Fifteen headings, one per lesson.

## A. The Crust in Section

**Crust** is compositional and ends at the **Moho**; **lithosphere** is mechanical and contains crust plus rigid uppermost mantle. ❗ Plates are lithospheric, not merely crustal. The **asthenosphere** is a variable-depth, weaker, predominantly solid upper-mantle zone. Historical sial/sima language does not define a universal two-layer crust. Oceanic crust is commonly about **5-10 km**; continental crust commonly about **30-40 km**, with wide variation. **Gutenberg** lies near **2,900 km**. P-waves cross solids and liquids; S-waves cannot cross liquids. Seismic data constrain physical properties but not unique chemistry. Himalayan convergence thickens crust; no unsupported Indian crustal-thickness number.

## B. Three Families and One Cycle

**Six criteria:** texture · structure · colour/composition · permeability · occurrence · resistance. **Rock = mineral aggregate.** Igneous forms from melt; sedimentary from clastic, organic and/or chemical material; metamorphic from solid-state alteration by temperature, pressure and fluids. Sedimentary rock is commonly, not invariably, stratified. Foliation ≠ bedding. Crystalline groundwater storage is mainly secondary, through joints, fractures and weathered zones. The rock cycle is closed. 🇮🇳 Examples: Deccan basalt · Closepet granite · Vindhyan sandstone · Gondwana coal · Eastern Ghats khondalite suite · Nilgiri charnockitic terrain.

## C. Primary Rocks in Depth

**Felsic** (legacy acid) rocks are generally silica-rich, pale and less dense; **mafic** (legacy basic) rocks are richer in ferromagnesian minerals, darker and denser. Texture records cooling history: intrusive generally coarse, extrusive generally fine. Forms include batholith, stock, laccolith, sill, dike and phacolith; source-specific size thresholds must be attributed. 🇮🇳 Deccan basalt is mafic and extrusive, with the principal pulse near **66 Ma**; Closepet granite is felsic and intrusive. Basalt supplies soil parent material but does not alone determine regur.

## D. Sedimentary and Metamorphic Rocks

Sedimentary material may be clastic, biogenic/organic, chemical or mixed; agents include water, wind, ice and gravity. Loess is wind-deposited silt, but absence of bedding alone does not prove origin. Metamorphism changes mineral assemblage and texture; protoliths may be clear or uncertain. Relative chronology uses superposition, fossils, unconformities and cross-cutting relations. Absolute ages require radiometric evidence tied to a named unit and current ICS boundaries.

## E. Crustal Movement and Orogenic Chronology

Compression produces folds/thrusts and crustal thickening; extension produces normal faults, horsts and grabens. Fold, fault-block, volcanic and residual are process/form classes. Caledonian, Variscan/Hercynian and Alpine-Himalayan are broad regional historical labels, not three single global dates. 🇮🇳 Aravalli-Delhi is a polyphase Proterozoic belt; Satpura-Maikal includes fault-block uplands; Palghat follows a major shear/lineament but has debated origin. 2024 Prelims Q10 remains answer-neutral.

## F. Continental Drift

Wegener proposed displacement in **1912** and developed the case in **1915**. Evidence: continental fit, geological continuity, fossils, palaeoclimate and palaeomagnetism. His rotational/tidal mechanisms were inadequate. Ocean-floor and palaeomagnetic evidence in the 1950s-60s, sea-floor spreading in the early 1960s and the mid-to-late-1960s plate-tectonic synthesis supplied a physical framework. 🇮🇳 Talchir glacial deposits provide Gondwana palaeoclimate evidence. 2025 Prelims Q25 remains answer-neutral.

## G. Palaeomagnetism and Sea-Floor Spreading

Cooling igneous rock records magnetic direction/polarity. Sea-floor spreading predicts mirrored stripes, increasing age and sediment away from ridge axes, and recycling at trenches. Plate motion involves slab pull, ridge push, gravitational potential energy and mantle flow, not one asthenospheric conveyor. 2018 Prelims Q57 is cross-topic and answer-neutral; this topic supplies only the rock-recording mechanism.

## H. Plate Tectonics and the Ocean-Basin Cycle

Seven major plates is a convention; minor-plate counts vary. Divergent, convergent and transform regimes create characteristic crust budgets and structures. Continent-continent convergence thickens crust without producing an Andean arc. Wilson-cycle stages are conceptual, not a timetable. Passive margins favour thick sediment wedges but hydrocarbons require full petroleum-system elements. 🇮🇳 India records both margin opening after Gondwana breakup and later Cenozoic Himalayan-Tibetan convergence. 2025 GS-I Q16 supplies the Mains application.

## I. Isostasy and India's Three Structural Provinces

**Airy:** similar density, different root depth. **Pratt:** different density above a common compensation depth. India combines ancient but faulted cratonic/mobile-belt terrain, the active Himalayan-Tibetan fold-thrust system and a Cenozoic foreland basin with extensive Quaternary surface alluvium. Peninsular stability is comparative, not inert, and later marine incursions/local coastal submergence invalidate absolute post-Cambrian non-submergence. 2026 Prelims Q34 remains answer-neutral.

## J. Archaean Cratons and Dharwar Supracrustals

Modern answers use named cratons, granite-gneiss complexes, greenstone/supracrustal belts and mobile belts; the fourfold ladder is historiographic only. No one age or coverage fraction describes the whole basement. Ancient provinces are favourable hosts for metallic mineralisation, not guaranteed deposits. Eastern Ghats khondalite suites, Bundelkhand granite-gneiss and Nilgiri charnockitic terrain require separate descriptions. 📰 **NCMM:** 29 January 2025; ₹16,300 crore government expenditure and ₹18,000 crore expected PSU/other investment.

## K. Proterozoic Belts/Basins and Palaeozoic Control

Use Aravalli-Delhi and Eastern Ghats mobile belts plus Cuddapah and Vindhyan basins/supergroups, with unit-specific ages. Map shape alone cannot establish stress direction. Panna diamond geology is deposit-specific; “Golconda” is not one Vindhyan horizon. Patchy Palaeozoic preservation does not prove continuous emergence. ICS 2026/06: Cambrian 538.8-486.85 · Ordovician 486.85-443.1 · Silurian 443.1-419.62 · Devonian 419.62-358.86 · Carboniferous 358.86-298.9 Ma. **Everest summit = Ordovician Qomolangma Formation limestone.**

## L. Gondwana Basins and Deccan Volcanics

Gondwana successions accumulated in fault-controlled continental rift basins; Permian = **298.9-251.902 Ma**. They host the overwhelming share of Indian bituminous coal; any percentage requires current official data. ICS Mesozoic: Triassic 251.902-201.4 · Jurassic 201.4-143.1 · Cretaceous 143.1-66.0 Ma. Deccan Volcanic Province: repeated fissure-fed mafic flows, principal pulse near **66 Ma**; area/thickness estimates require named methods. 📰 **GSI FS 2025-26, PRID 2248494:** 458 projects; 230 critical-mineral; 92 rare-earth; 80 reports; ~22,000 sq km large-scale mapping; 28,000 sq km Deccan geochemical mapping; natural hydrogen in Andaman and Nicobar Islands, with no rock type/locality stated.

## M. Cenozoic Himalaya, Siwaliks and Quaternary

Paleogene **66-23.04 Ma**; Neogene **23.04-2.58 Ma**; Quaternary **2.58 Ma-present**. Himalayan convergence, metamorphism, thrusting, exhumation and uplift are polyphase; **45 Ma is Eocene, not mid-Miocene**. Siwaliks are Neogene-to-early-Quaternary foreland molasse. Everest summit = Ordovician Qomolangma limestone. The foreland basin developed during the Cenozoic; much present alluvium is Quaternary. Karewas are Kashmir-basin lacustrine-fluvial successions. *Bhangar* = older alluvial surfaces; *khadar* = younger floodplain deposits; ages vary regionally. Talchir glacial deposits remain key Gondwana palaeoclimate evidence. Current coastline length omitted; emergence/submergence requires dated local evidence.

## N. Structure, Relief and Resource

Geology conditions relief, drainage, groundwater, soil parent material and mineral association without determining economic outcomes. **Aravalli-Delhi:** polyphase Proterozoic belt. **Satpura-Maikal:** fault-block/upland system; Amarkantak at Vindhya-Satpura meeting zone. **Western Ghats:** near-continuous escarpment; Palghat lineament origin debated. **Eastern Ghats:** discontinuous Proterozoic mobile belt; Jindhagada/Arma Konda, not Mahendragiri, generally highest. **Biligirirangan:** Ghats transition/easternmost Western extension. **Seshachalam:** southern Eastern Ghats. 2023 Q9 answer-neutral.

## O. Mapwork and Answer Architecture

**Modern build order:** cratons/basement → Proterozoic mobile belts and basins → Gondwana/Mesozoic basins → Deccan/Rajmahal volcanics → Cenozoic Himalaya/foreland/coastal basins → Quaternary → lineaments. **Directive decoding:** primary rocks → criteria + felsic/mafic + intrusive/extrusive + forms; crustal tectonics → evidence + mechanism + boundary regimes + Wilson cycle; isostasy → Airy/Pratt plus observable adjustment. **Number protocol:** ICS chart values may be quoted; unit ages, thicknesses and elevations need named sources; spreading rates, precise plate totals, unattributed collision dates, reserves, crustal thicknesses, universal Deccan area correction and current coastline measurement are never supplied from memory.
