## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

Everything above is sufficient for a complete answer at every printed demand. The four contrasts below are **second-order debate**: they sharpen evaluation in a 15- or 20-marker and are never a prerequisite for understanding or writing the core answer. Add **one** advanced move to a 15-marker, or **one to two** to a 20-marker.

**[FACT] Promotion note.** The Advanced dossier's promotion ledger of **15 August 2026** records **deep disagreement about Hick's Real** as promoted out of Advanced and into the owner. It is therefore taught in Session 16 as core, and is not repeated here as optional enrichment.

### A1 — Hickian pluralism against the hard-incompatibility critique

**Competing positions.** Hick relocates religions as valid responses to the Real; critics stress that contradictory truth-claims cannot all be true in the same sense.
**Strongest extra objection → counter-reply.** *Objection:* the unknowable Real is too thin to ground determinate religions. *Reply:* Hick presents a second-order hypothesis about transformation rather than a new first-order creed — **though revisionism remains a live charge**.
**Safe use.** As an evaluative paragraph *after* the E-I-P triad has been laid out clearly. It does not replace the exposition.

### A2 — Vedāntic universalism against the assimilation critique

**Competing positions.** Vivekananda and Radhakrishnan read religions as converging paths; critics say this absorbs others into a neo-Vedāntic frame.
**Strongest extra objection → counter-reply.** *Objection:* "all paths" may covertly privilege Vedānta. *Reply:* defenders hold that convergence need not erase distinct practices, only deny monopoly over the Real.
**Safe use.** To show maturity in a 2025-style Vedāntic pluralism answer. Session 11 already carries the core form of this exchange; the advanced move is only the sharper statement of the privileging worry.

### A3 — Deep disagreement against comparative learning

**Competing positions.** One line holds that traditions are too conceptually different for neat synthesis; another holds that comparison can still produce disciplined mutual learning without forced sameness.
**Strongest extra objection → counter-reply.** *Objection:* translation across traditions distorts their self-understanding. *Reply:* comparative work can preserve disagreement while identifying partial overlap, analogy or shared ethical stakes.
**Safe use.** One sentence in a conclusion, to avoid superficial "all religions are the same" rhetoric.

### A4 — Many-sidedness against relativism, as a second-order contest

**Competing positions.** Jain many-sidedness allows standpoint-conditioned truth without collapsing into "anything goes".
**Strongest extra objection → counter-reply.** *Objection:* if every claim is only conditional, no claim has full authority. *Reply:* Jain logic regulates predication by standpoint and object-aspect; it disciplines absolutism rather than abolishing truth.
**Safe use.** Particularly safe in Indian-context answers on absolute truth and conflict. The core form of the doctrine is in Session 13; this is the second-order authority worry only.

### What not to over-study

Do not turn the answer into comparative-religion sociology or global conflict commentary detached from philosophical positions. The clause is about **theories of religious diversity and the problem of absolute truth**, not about the descriptive history or present evaluation of particular communities.

## CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

Twenty-one topic-specific cards, one per taught session. These are compressed revision notes, not a second run of the teaching prose.

### 1. Four axes, and the two illicit slides

- Diversity becomes a problem only where **determinate truth-claims + determinate salvation + a demand for consistency** meet.
- **Truth (alethic)** · **Salvation (soteriological)** · **Epistemic access** · **Political coexistence** — four independent questions.
- Slide A (✗): "we coexist peacefully" ⇒ "all religions are equally true".
- Slide B (✗): "there is one absolute Truth" ⇒ "therefore I am an exclusivist".
- Pluralism ≠ relativism: it can deny equal truth, retain non-contradiction, and still deny exhaustive possession.
- Tolerance is a civic ethic and is **available to an exclusivist**.
- Answer habit: name the axis in the first sentence.

### 2. Exclusivism, and the attribution chain

- One revelation carries decisive truth and/or salvific authority; appeals to **determinate revelation + non-contradiction**.
- Presupposition: **privileged warrant**, and salvation specifiable enough for comparison.
- Tag: *extra ecclesiam nulla salus* = **later standard phrasing**. Cyprian (d. 258), *Ep.* 73: *salus extra ecclesiam non est*; also *De Ecclesiae Catholicae Unitate* 6.
- Conciliar hardening: Lateran IV **1215** · *Unam Sanctam* **1302** · Florence **1442**.
- Qualification: Vatican II **1965** — *Lumen Gentium* 16, *Nostra Aetate* ⇒ decisive move toward inclusivism.
- Strength: determinate content preserved. Weakness: privileged warrant; **contingency of birth**.
- Exclusivism about truth ≠ intolerance about practice (Locke).

### 3. Inclusivism

- Grants **partial truth** and possible salvation beyond the home tradition, interpreted **through** it.
- Argument: determinate revelation **+ universality of grace**. Normative priority retained.
- Model: Rahner's **anonymous Christians** — a claim about grace, not covert membership.
- Differentia: *others are included on terms set by the privileged tradition*.
- Objection: patronising. Reply: every judgement uses a framework; the test is corrigibility and respect for self-descriptions.
- Residual cost: **asymmetry** — the home tradition is never redescribed in the other's categories.

### 4. Pluralism, defined by its denial

- Affirms: several traditions are **authentic responses**, or attain **different real ends**.
- Denies: any finite tradition **exhaustively possesses** the absolute.
- Not relativism (there *is* a truth of the matter) · not indifferentism · not syncretism · not perennialist identity.
- Acknowledged weakness: dilution of specifics; the **Real *an sich*** is unknowable.
- Relativism criticism → reply: **equal dignity / salvific efficacy ≠ identical propositional truth**.
- Self-reference criticism → reply: defeasible hypothesis; rejoinder: it still judges first-order traditions.

### 5. Hick I — the Copernican revolution

- Centre moves from a tradition or incarnation to the **Real**.
- Four steps: (1) persistent, birth-linked diversity; (2) transformation across traditions; (3) equal divine concern resists arbitrary exclusivism; (4) therefore one Real through different schemes.
- Premise 3 is **theological** ⇒ unavailable against a non-theistic exclusivist.
- Conclusion is a **possibility claim** by inference to the best explanation, not a proof.
- The Real is **transcategorial**: personal / non-personal predicates attach to manifestations.
- Sources: *An Interpretation of Religion*; *God and the Universe of Faiths*.

### 6. Hick II — the Kantian basis

- Real *an sich* : phenomena :: noumenon : appearance; conceptual schemes function as categories.
- **Departure from Kant:** the noumenal Real is treated as **causally related** to religious experience.
- Presuppositions: the Kantian distinction extends religiously; transformation is comparable across traditions.
- **Noumenal objection:** existence, causality and goodness become illicit predications.
- Reply: predication is **formal, not substantive** ⇒ consistency preserved, informativeness reduced.
- Indian comparator: Advaita has a **built-in** *nirguṇa*/*saguṇa* two-level semantics; Hick must construct one.

### 7. Hick III — the transformation criterion

- **Self-centredness → Reality-centredness**, evidenced by saints and moral fruits.
- Scope: authenticity of a **tradition**; it does **not** make contradictory doctrines jointly true.
- Proposed because doctrinal comparison lacks a neutral adjudicating framework.
- Three objections at three levels: (a) thin Real; (b) **self-referential / parity**; (c) **identity and mission** — hear it, do not declare it solved.
- Conflicting ends: communion, non-dual knowledge, cessation are not obviously one end. Reply: family resemblance / transformation; critics call it **flattening**.
- Coarse-grained: cannot discriminate between two traditions that both produce saints.

### 8. Plantinga and D'Costa

- Charges: **arbitrariness, arrogance, irrationality**.
- Replies: the pluralist holds a contested belief too; believing what others deny is the ordinary philosophical condition; disagreement **reduces** but does not extinguish warrant.
- Counter 1: establishes **permissibility, not truth**. Counter 2: **contingency of birth** untouched.
- **D'Costa:** pluralism is a **covert exclusivism** — its second-order account is held uniquely true and reinterprets traditions against their self-understanding. **Strongest single objection to Hick**; his reply does not fully escape it.
- Net result: no neutral standpoint; the live question is **corrigibility**.

### 9. Four varieties of pluralism

- Discriminating question: **one ultimate, or several ends?**
- **Hick — identist:** one Real, many conditioned responses; criterion soteriological.
- **Heim — differential** (*Salvations*, 1995): communion, non-dual realisation, cessation are **three destinations**; criterion fidelity to self-understanding; cost — **gives up a single ultimate**.
- **Panikkar / Cobb — mutual transformation:** dialogue changes both parties; a method, not a metaphysics.
- **Griffin — deep religious pluralism:** process-based, **plural ultimates** (creativity, God, cosmos).
- Verdict: the triad is a **map, not a verdict**; Hick most exposed to D'Costa, Heim most faithful to the traditions.

### 10. The Vedic verse, and its discipline

- *Ekaṃ sad viprā bahudhā vadanti* — **Ṛgveda 1.164.46**, *asya vāmasya* riddle-hymn, ascribed to **Dīrghatamas**.
- Native subject: **naming of Vedic deities** — Indra, Mitra, Varuṇa, Agni, Garutmān, Yama, Mātariśvan.
- Four disciplines: cite precisely · state the native subject · **argue** the modern extension · keep transliteration consistent.
- Structure supplied: **one referent, many namings** — which is what breaks the absolute-truth inference.
- Weakest point of the extension: within the pantheon the referent is agreed; across traditions it is contested.

### 11. Vivekananda's Universal Religion (2025 owner)

- Argument: capacities vary → disciplines vary → non-dual ground → **realisation**, not doctrinal uniformity, is the test.
- Four disciplines matched to dispositions: knowledge (*jñāna*), devotion (*bhakti*), action (*karma*), meditative discipline (*rāja*).
- **Chicago, 1893:** acceptance, **not mere toleration** — toleration still condescends.
- Harmony **without homogenisation**; "all religions say the same thing" is **not safe**.
- Presupposition: Vedānta as a **non-sectarian meta-framework**.
- Objection: **neo-Vedāntic assimilation**. Reply: distinctive forms preserved. Status: **contested** — say so.
- Advaita ground has a limit: it does not follow without argument that communion, creator-relation and cessation are one end.

### 12. Radhakrishnan and Gandhi

- **Radhakrishnan:** religions are diverse **interpretations of a common spiritual experience** (*anubhava*); criterion **integral experience**; risk **perennialist reduction**; *The Hindu View of Life*.
- **Gandhi:** **equal respect + fallibilism**; summarised by *sarva-dharma-samabhāva* — a **modern summary formula**, not an unverified verbatim slogan.
- Division of labour: Radhakrishnan **explains** difference (needs a contested uniformity claim); Gandhi **prescribes** how to live with it (needs only a claim about one's own limits).
- Gandhi's fallibilism is therefore the more **portable** resource — available to exclusivist, inclusivist and pluralist alike.

### 13. Many-sidedness as a logic

- **Anekāntavāda** (reality is many-sided) → **nayavāda** (name the standpoint) → **syādvāda** (*syāt* = "in a certain respect") → **saptabhaṅgī** (sevenfold predication).
- Not relativism: the respects are **stated and checkable**; predication is regulated, not abandoned.
- Two live uses: denies that a finite formulation exhausts the absolute; marks framework-relativity **inside** the proposition (deep disagreement).
- Owner's note: stating it as a **logic** is worth more than describing the elephant parable.
- Limit: same question, same respect ⇒ no index of standpoint helps.

### 14. Absolute truth without epistemic absolutism

- Suppressed premise of the 2022 inference: **a finite formulation can exhaust the absolute**.
- Three independent denials: Advaita's *nirguṇa* (with *saguṇa* structuring devotion) · Jain *syādvāda* · Hick's transcategorial Real.
- **Absolute referent + pluralist access.**
- Four separations: **referent · formulation · access · salvation**.
- Three independent theses: **ontological absolutism · epistemic fallibilism · political tolerance**.
- Presupposition made: truth admits **degrees of adequacy or non-literal mediation**.
- Template: "Ontological absolutism entails epistemic exclusivism only if one adds the premise that a finite formulation can exhaust the absolute."

### 15. Hard contradiction, and the 2023 charges

- Four legitimate reinterpretive moves: **level · symbol · standpoint · soteriological end**.
- Residue: a personal creator cannot both exist and not exist in the same respect; the self cannot be both permanent and denied in the same sense. **Acknowledge, do not harmonise.**
- 2023 contains **two** allegations: (a) invites conflict; (b) destroys the truth of religion.
- Reply (a): conflict is not caused by plurality alone — power, identity and exclusivist certainty mediate it.
- Reply (b): pluralism **relocates** absoluteness in the transcendent Real; coexistence ≠ "anything goes".
- Alethic exclusivity on one proposition is compatible with soteriological and political pluralism.

### 16. Deep and peer disagreement

- **Peer** = roughly equal in evidence and reasoning ability.
- **Conciliationism / equal weight:** Feldman, Christensen — reduce confidence. **Steadfastness:** Kelly, van Inwagen — one's own reasoning is part of one's evidence.
- **Independence principle** — conciliationism needs it; steadfastness denies it.
- **Fogelin (1985):** deep disagreement = no shared **framework propositions**; Wittgenstein's **hinges**, *On Certainty*. **Lynch:** extends to epistemic principles, where defence is circular.
- Religion differs over **sources · criteria · framework**.
- Four consequences: failure of convergence is predicted (weak evidence for relativism) · exclusivism not convicted of irrationality · pluralism not established by persistence · **procedural virtues** obligatory.
- Payoff: **unresolvability is epistemic, not alethic.**

### 17. Toleration

- Three components: **objection · acceptance · power**. Remove (i) ⇒ indifference; remove (iii) ⇒ endurance.
- Paradox: allowing what one holds wrong. Resolution **by levels** — first-order disapproval, second-order reasons against coercion.
- **Forst's ladder:** permission · coexistence · respect · esteem. Only respect and esteem fit **equal citizenship**; most historical tolerance was **permission**.
- **Locke 1689:** no commission over souls · force cannot produce belief · coerced conformity would not save.
- **Mill 1859:** harm principle + the epistemic cost of suppression. **Popper:** paradox of tolerance, restricted to those who answer argument with violence. **Rawls:** overlapping consensus.
- **Aśoka RE XII:** do not disparage other sects — you injure your own most; **concord (*samavāya*)**; earliest verifiable state-level argument. **Akbar:** *ṣulḥ-i kull* — a **policy**. **Gandhi**, **Vivekananda** (acceptance), **Jain** epistemic ground. Articles 25–28.
- Verdict: **indispensable but insufficient.**

### 18. Religious freedom

- Bundle: conscience (inviolable) · profess · practise · propagate · change or leave · freedom from compelled observance · associational right.
- Four justifications: **incoercibility · autonomy · epistemic · social**.
- Five hard problems: **practice/belief asymmetry** (public order, morality, health — contested terms) · **propagation and conversion** (the line is **coercion and fraud**) · **internal minorities** — Shachar's **paradox of multicultural vulnerability**, the most examinable tension · **neutrality** — wall of separation against **Bhargava's principled distance**, which permits reform · **judicial determination** — "essential religious practices" makes the state an arbiter of theology.
- Objection: a liberal value universalised. Reply: incoercibility is about the **psychology of belief**, and Aśoka, Sufi and Bhakti traditions reach comparable conclusions on their own grounds.
- Verdict: possible and more nearly necessary; never unlimited or perfectly neutral.

### 19. History of religions and conflict

- Three versions: **involvement** (trivial) · **significant cause** (the argument) · **defining engine** (false).
- For: absolute truth-claims + exclusivist soteriology + strong identity + sacralised authority. **Kimball's markers** — a typology, not a law.
- Against: **Cavanaugh** (2009) religious/secular as modern construct · **Armstrong** (2014) no separable pre-modern sphere · **Sen** (2006) singular-affiliation fallacy.
- The move: **cause · marker · mobiliser · legitimiser**; most cases are the last three.
- Counter-evidence: Aśoka after Kaliṅga · monastic learning · hospitals and famine relief · abolitionism · civil rights · *satyāgraha* · Bhakti and Sufi universalism · interfaith movement.
- Verdict: **historically selective and conceptually confused**, with the narrower truth conceded.

### 20. Religion as a uniting force

- For: **Durkheim** (collective effervescence; church as moral community) · universalist ethics (*vasudhaiva kuṭumbakam*, the four abodes, *ummah*, image of God) · **1893 and 1993 Parliaments**, *Declaration Toward a Global Ethic*, **Hans Küng** · transnational relief and diaspora networks.
- Against: Durkheim's integration is **in-group** · **Huntington** — cite as a **thesis under criticism** · **Roy** — deterritorialised, deculturated religiosity produces cosmopolitan **and** reactive forms · instrumentalisation.
- Three conditions: **universalist ethics · fallibilist epistemology · non-monopolised identity**.
- Verdict: **high-amplitude**, not intrinsically unifying — the same traditions produce the Parliament of Religions and communal violence.

### 21. The bench, the grid, the decoder, the ladder, the spine

- **Parity bench (six problems):** rival truth-claims · framework-relativity · grounds of toleration · tolerance versus acceptance · ultimate reality versus its descriptions · salvific ends. Discriminating points: the Vedic verse is about names of deities; Jainism marks relativity inside the proposition; Aśoka is the earliest verifiable state-level argument; Advaita's two-level semantics is built in; Heim fits Indian plurality better than Hick.
- **Inter-thinker grid (nine rows):** exclusivism · inclusivism · Hick · Vivekananda · Radhakrishnan · Jain · Heim · Plantinga · D'Costa, on one ultimate / status of traditions / criterion / main objection.
- **Directive decoder (eleven rows)** — fix the axis; keep the context clause; conditional verdict with conditions; name the central problem in one sentence; grade a sweeping claim; define before arguing importance; show the compatibility mechanism; face the hard cases; test the inference; separate two allegations; be faithful to the named thinker.
- **Verdict ladder:** weak · average · good · strong · top — the top band requires a discriminating distinction **and** an acknowledgement of hard contradictions.
- **Architecture:** 10 marks — define, one model, referent versus access, one objection, non-relativist verdict. 15 marks — E-I-P, thinker's argument and presupposition, comparator, objection and reply. 20 marks — frame, strongest model, referent versus formulation, hard contradictions, two objections and replies, graded conclusion.
