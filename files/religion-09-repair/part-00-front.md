## BASIC LEARNING SESSION

### Exact printed ownership and cross-topic firewall

The printed clause owns **alethic, epistemic and soteriological theories of religious diversity**, the problem of absolute truth, and their implications for dialogue, tolerance and coexistence.

| Owned here | Boundary that is not crossed |
|---|---|
| exclusivism, inclusivism, pluralism and the particularist replies to them | complete concepts of God belong to the Notions of God owner |
| conflicting revelation and conflicting experience **as evidence of diversity** | authenticating a revelation, and testing an experience for veridicality, belong to their own owners |
| Hick's Real and its humanly conditioned manifestations | the complete theory of religious experience, and the complete semantics of God-talk, belong to those owners |
| tolerance, religious freedom and dialogue **only where a printed question routes them here** | full secularism, multiculturalism and political-rights theory belong to Socio-Political Philosophy |
| Indian many-sidedness, many-paths and equal-respect arguments **as pluralism resources** | complete Jain, Vedāntic and individual-thinker doctrine belongs to the Paper I owners |
| the missionary and identity costs that pluralist theories impose on traditions | descriptive history and evaluation of particular present-day communities are outside scope |

⚠️ **[FACT]** The firewall that governs the whole clause: **social tolerance, epistemic humility, alethic pluralism and soteriological pluralism are four different theses.** Peaceful coexistence neither proves that rival claims are true nor requires treating every religion as equally true. Almost every weak script on this clause slides from "we should live together peacefully" to "therefore all religions are equally true", and never recovers.

### 0A. FOUR AXES THAT MUST NOT BE MERGED

```text
        MANY RELIGIONS, CONFLICTING TRUTH-CLAIMS — FOUR SEPARATE QUESTIONS
  ┌──────────────┬──────────────────┬──────────────────┬───────────────────┐
    1. TRUTH        2. SALVATION       3. ACCESS          4. COEXISTENCE
    (alethic)       (soteriological)   (epistemic)        (political)
    which claims    can another        what should        how should
    are true,       tradition really   disagreement do    citizens and
    false,          transform or       to my level of     institutions
    symbolic,       liberate its       confidence?        treat deep
    partly          adherents?                            disagreement?
    adequate?
  ──────────────  ──────────────────  ──────────────────  ───────────────────
    one Real +      several            fallibilism,        freedom,
    non-exhaustive  efficacious        humility,           reciprocity,
    formulations;   paths, possibly    steadfast           respect,
    or several      toward DIFFERENT   particularism,      non-coercion —
    irreducible     ends               or suspension       WITHOUT agreement
    truths/ends                                            about truth
  └──────────────┴──────────────────┴──────────────────┴───────────────────┘
        ↓ THE TWO ILLICIT SLIDES THAT COST THE MOST MARKS ↓
   "we coexist peacefully"  ==>  "all religions are equally true"   ✗ (4 → 1)
   "I hold the absolute truth" ==> "I must be an exclusivist"       ✗ (1 → 1)
```

*Read the four columns as four independent questions. A position may be pluralist on one axis and exclusivist on another, which is why the axis must be named before any thinker is discussed.*

> 🔑 **Mnemonic — "Truth · Salvation · Access · Coexistence" (T-SAC):** name the axis in your first sentence. Positions differ across these four axes **independently**, and a victory on one concedes nothing on the others.

**[FACT] Pluralism is not relativism.** A pluralist can deny that all propositions are equally true, can retain the law of non-contradiction, and can still reject any one tradition's claim to exhaustive possession of the absolute. **[FACT] Conversely, tolerance is a civic ethic and can be defended by an exclusivist** — Locke's argument that force cannot produce belief is available to someone who thinks every other religion false.

### Origin, chronology and anachronism control

A calendar timeline is not the natural shape of this clause: it is a dispute about *how to respond to disagreement*, not an institution with a founding date. What is required is the minimum chronology that prevents anachronism, plus the dated print anchors an examiner can be quoted at.

```text
CONCEPTUAL ORIGIN OF THE PROBLEM
  Stage 1  A tradition states determinate truth-claims and a way of salvation.
  Stage 2  It meets other traditions doing the same thing, incompatibly.
           ==> the problem is not diversity as such; it is diversity
               plus determinate claims plus a demand for consistency.
  Stage 3  First response - EXCLUSIVISM: one tradition is decisively true;
           the rest are, to that extent, mistaken.
  Stage 4  Second response - INCLUSIVISM: the others are partly true and are
           fulfilled in mine; their adherents may be saved through my truth.
  Stage 5  Third response - PLURALISM: several traditions are authentic
           responses to one ultimate Reality (Hick), or attain genuinely
           DIFFERENT real ends (Heim).
  Stage 6  The counter-question: does holding ONE absolute truth make
           exclusivism, and therefore conflict, INEVITABLE?
           ==> this is what 2021, 2022 and 2024 print.
  Stage 7  The epistemological floor: is the disagreement even the kind
           that argument could settle? ==> deep disagreement.

DATED PRINT ANCHORS  (assert these; invent no others)

                   "ekam sad vipra bahudha vadanti"; its own subject is the

ANACHRONISM CONTROL
  RV 1.164.46 is not a charter of inter-religious pluralism. Its native subject
  is the naming of Vedic deities - Indra, Mitra, Varuna, Agni, Garutman, Yama,
  Matarisvan as names of "the one existent". The extension to the world's
  religions is a nineteenth- and twentieth-century development (Vivekananda,
  Radhakrishnan, Gandhi) and must be ARGUED, not asserted by citation.
  "Extra ecclesiam nulla salus" is the later standard formula, not Cyprian's
  own wording, and Vatican II (1965) moved the Roman Catholic position
  decisively toward inclusivism - so it cannot be cited as "the Christian view".
  Asoka's Rock Edict XII is an ancient STATE toleration argument, not an ancient
  theory of alethic pluralism. Akbar's sulh-i kull is a HISTORICAL POLICY, not a
  philosophical doctrine; say so when you cite it.
  Sarva-dharma-samabhava should be used as a modern summary of Gandhi's
  fallibilist equal respect, not as an unverified verbatim slogan.
```
