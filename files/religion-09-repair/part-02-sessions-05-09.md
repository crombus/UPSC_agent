### SESSION 5 — HICK I: THE COPERNICAN REVOLUTION AND THE ARGUMENT FOR IT

#### DEFINITION

**Plain-language definition.** Hick's proposal is that we stop putting one religion — or one incarnation — at the centre of the religious universe and put ultimate Reality there instead, with the traditions in orbit around it. That is why he calls it a Copernican revolution.

**Technical definition.** Hick's **pluralistic hypothesis** holds that the major traditions are culturally conditioned human responses to the transcategorial **Real**, and are assessed **soteriologically** by the transformation they produce from self-centredness to Reality-centredness. It is offered as a **second-order philosophical hypothesis** about the traditions, not as a new first-order religion.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE OR ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Hick's move is structural rather than devotional: he does not argue that any particular doctrine is false, he argues that the centre of the system has been misplaced — and once the Real rather than a tradition occupies the centre, the persistence of several transforming traditions stops being an anomaly to be explained away and becomes exactly what the hypothesis predicts.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

**Copernican revolution · the Real · transcategorial · culturally conditioned response · post-axial traditions · second-order hypothesis · contingency of birth · equal divine concern · soteriological assessment · *An Interpretation of Religion* · *God and the Universe of Faiths***

**How to use them:** state the hypothesis in one sentence and then give the four-step argument as four numbered steps. Examiners reward a reconstructed argument far more than a described position.

#### VISUAL — THE COPERNICAN SHIFT AND THE FOUR-STEP ARGUMENT

```text
     BEFORE (Ptolemaic)                    AFTER (Copernican)
   ┌────────────────────┐              ┌────────────────────────┐
   │   MY TRADITION at  │              │      THE REAL at       │
   │     the centre     │              │       the centre       │
   │        ·           │   ═══════▶   │           ·            │
   │   others orbit as  │              │  traditions orbit as   │
   │   deficient        │              │  culturally conditioned│
   │   approximations   │              │  AUTHENTIC responses   │
   └────────────────────┘              └────────────────────────┘

   THE ARGUMENT, IN FOUR STEPS — write it numbered
   ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ (1) DIVERSITY IS PERSISTENT and is strongly correlated     │
   │     with birth context.                                    │
   │ (2) MULTIPLE TRADITIONS demonstrably produce moral and     │
   │     spiritual transformation.                              │
   │ (3) EQUAL DIVINE CONCERN resists an arbitrary exclusivism   │
   │     that would make salvation depend on birthplace.        │
   │ ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────── │
   │ (4) THEREFORE one Real may be authentically experienced     │
   │     through different conceptual schemes.                   │
   └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
     Note the shape: (1) is descriptive, (2) is evaluative,
     (3) is theological, (4) is the hypothesis they jointly support.
     Attack any premise and the conclusion is only a possibility claim.
```

*The two circles show the structural move; the numbered box is the argument you must be able to reproduce. Premises 1–3 come from three different domains, which is both the argument's strength and the place where it is most easily broken.*

#### TEACHING

**[FACT] The doctrine statement.** The major traditions are culturally conditioned human responses to the transcategorial Real, assessed soteriologically by the transformation from self-centredness to Reality-centredness.

**[FACT] Step 1 — persistent diversity.** Religious diversity is persistent and often linked to birth context. The observation is not merely sociological: it is doing argumentative work, because it makes any claim to unique access look like an accident of geography.

**[FACT] Step 2 — transformation occurs plurally.** Multiple traditions produce moral and spiritual transformation. Hick's evidence is the existence of saints, and the moral fruits of practice, across traditions — which is why the criterion he later adopts is soteriological rather than doctrinal.

**[FACT] Step 3 — equal divine concern.** A God of equal concern for all human beings resists an arbitrary exclusivism under which salvation tracks birthplace. **[INFERENCE]** Note that this premise is *theological*, so it is available against a theistic exclusivist but not against a non-theistic one — a discrimination worth one line in a 20-marker.

**[FACT] Step 4 — the conclusion.** Therefore one Real may be authentically experienced through different conceptual schemes. **[INFERENCE]** The conclusion is a **possibility** claim supported by an inference to the best explanation; it is not a demonstration, and Hick does not present it as one.

**[FACT] The Copernican analogy, stated exactly.** Hick shifts the centre from one religion or incarnation to the Real, analogously to a Copernican shift from an ecclesial centre to ultimate Reality. **[INFERENCE]** The analogy is doing rhetorical work as well as structural work: it casts exclusivism as pre-scientific. That is a rhetorical liability an examiner may reward you for noticing.

**[FACT] What the Real is.** The Real in itself is **transcategorial**: personal and non-personal predicates apply to its experienced manifestations, not literally to the Real as it is independently of human schemes. So "God is personal" and "the ultimate is beyond personality" are not, on this account, contradictories about the same object under the same description.

**[FACT] What the hypothesis is *not*.** It is a second-order philosophical hypothesis about the traditions, not a rival first-order creed. Hick's reply to the charge of arrogance depends on this distinction, and Session 8 shows why critics deny that it works.

**Objection.** The argument moves from "many traditions transform people" to "many traditions are authentic responses to the same Real" — but transformation could have a purely natural explanation and would then support no religious conclusion at all.
**Reply.** **[INFERENCE]** Hick's hypothesis is offered as the best explanation *given* that religious experience is taken to be, at least sometimes, cognitive; against a thoroughgoing naturalist it is not intended to be persuasive. Conceding that limitation openly is stronger than pretending the argument is a proof, and it locates the debate correctly — with the Religious Experience owner, where veridicality is decided.

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH

> **Claim:** Hick's pluralistic hypothesis relocates the centre of the religious universe from a tradition to the transcategorial Real, and defends that relocation by an inference to the best explanation rather than by a demonstration.
> **Named evidence:** The argument runs in four steps in *An Interpretation of Religion* — persistent diversity correlated with birth; transformation produced across traditions; equal divine concern telling against an arbitrary exclusivism; and therefore one Real authentically experienced through different conceptual schemes.
> **Analysis:** Because the premises are drawn from three different domains — descriptive, evaluative and theological — the argument is broad but breakable: a non-theist is untouched by premise 3, and a naturalist reads premise 2 differently.
> **Qualification:** The conclusion is a possibility claim about how diversity may be interpreted, offered as second-order philosophy rather than as a new creed, and it should be presented in that modest form because that is the form in which Hick's reply to his critics is available.

#### UPSC TRAPS

13. ⚠️ Do not describe Hick's hypothesis without reconstructing the argument. "Hick says all religions respond to the Real" is a description; the four steps are the answer.
14. ⚠️ Do not say Hick proves pluralism. He offers an inference to the best explanation supporting a possibility claim, and overstating it invites an easy refutation.
15. ⚠️ Do not use the Copernican analogy as though it were itself an argument. It is a picture of the structural move, and an examiner will notice if it is doing work that the premises should be doing.

#### MINI RECAP

Centre moves from tradition to the Real. Four steps: persistent birth-linked diversity; plural transformation; equal divine concern; therefore one Real through many schemes. Transcategorial Real. Second-order hypothesis, not a creed. Possibility claim, not proof.

### SESSION 6 — HICK II: THE KANTIAN BASIS, AND THE NOUMENAL OBJECTION

#### DEFINITION

**Plain-language definition.** Hick borrows Kant's distinction between the world as it is in itself and the world as it appears to us, and applies it to religion: the Real in itself is one thing, and the Gods and Absolutes of the traditions are how that one thing appears through different human conceptual schemes.

**Technical definition.** The **Kantian analogy** distinguishes the Real *in itself* (the Real *an sich*) from humanly experienced religious **phenomena**. Human conceptual schemes — theistic and non-theistic, personal and non-personal — function like categories that shape the appearance. Unlike Kant, however, Hick treats the noumenal Real as **causally related** to religious experience, and that departure is what generates the central objection.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE OR ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> The Kantian analogy is what makes Hick's pluralism a philosophical position rather than a pious hope — and it is also what makes it vulnerable, because a Real placed beyond every human category cannot then be described as existing, as causing experience or as good without the categories being smuggled back in.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

**Real *an sich* · noumenal / phenomenal · transcategorial · conceptual scheme · categories of religious experience · personal *personae* and non-personal *impersonae* · causal relation to experience · noumenal objection · illicit predication · *nirguṇa* parallel**

**How to use them:** state the analogy, state the *departure* from Kant, and then state the objection that the departure creates. That three-move sequence is the whole session, and it is what a 20-marker rewards.

#### VISUAL — THE ANALOGY, THE DEPARTURE AND THE OBJECTION

```text
  KANT                                HICK
  ───────────────────────────────     ────────────────────────────────
  thing-in-itself (noumenon)          THE REAL AN SICH
        │ unknowable                        │ transcategorial
        │ but causally affects us?          │ CAUSALLY related to
        │ (a famously disputed point)       │ religious experience
        ↓                                   ↓
  CATEGORIES of the understanding     RELIGIOUS CONCEPTUAL SCHEMES
  (space, time, causality)            (theistic / non-theistic;
        ↓                              personal / non-personal)
        ↓                                   ↓
  PHENOMENA — the world as            THE GODS AND ABSOLUTES of the
  experienced                         traditions, as experienced
  ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
  THE OBJECTION, STATED PRECISELY
  If the Real is beyond ALL categories, then:
     "the Real EXISTS"          ← existence is a category
     "the Real CAUSES experience"← causality is a category
     "the Real is GOOD"          ← evaluative predication
  ...are all illicit. The hypothesis needs the Real to do work that
  its own transcategoriality forbids it from doing.
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  THE INDIAN PARALLEL WORTH ONE LINE:
  nirguṇa Brahman (without qualities) is likewise beyond exhaustive
  predication, yet Advaita has a BUILT-IN two-level semantics
  (nirguṇa / saguṇa) that Hick has to construct.
```

*Read the two columns downward to see the analogy, then read the bottom box to see the price. The Indian parallel is not decoration: it is the discriminating point in the owner's parity bench.*

#### TEACHING

**[FACT] The analogy.** The Kantian analogy distinguishes the Real in itself from humanly experienced phenomena. The traditions' Gods and Absolutes are the Real as experienced through particular conceptual schemes, not the Real as it is independently of those schemes.

**[FACT] The departure from Kant.** Hick treats the noumenal Real as **causally related** to religious experience. That is a stronger commitment than the analogy strictly licenses, and it is deliberate: without it, the Real would explain nothing.

**[FACT] The presupposition being made.** Two things are presupposed: that the Kantian appearance/reality distinction **can be extended religiously**, and that soteriological transformation is **comparable across traditions**. Both are contestable; naming them is worth a mark.

**[FACT] The noumenal objection.** If the Real is unknowable and beyond categories, then even **existence**, **causality** and **goodness** appear to be illicitly predicated of it. The hypothesis needs the Real to be there, to be the source of experience, and to be worth being transformed toward — and each of those is a categorial claim.

**[FACT] Hick's line of reply.** The predicates are said to apply **formally** rather than substantively: the Real is that to which the traditions respond, and formal or purely referential characterisations do not import the substantive content that transcategoriality forbids. **[INFERENCE]** The reply is intelligible but thin, and the honest verdict is that the objection reduces the informativeness of the hypothesis rather than refuting it outright. An answer that says exactly this is at the top band.

**[FACT] The Advanced-dossier form of the same objection.** The dossier records the objection as: *the unknowable Real is too thin to ground determinate religions* — with the reply that Hick presents a second-order hypothesis about transformation, not a new first-order creed, and the note that **revisionism remains a live charge**.

**[FACT] The Indian comparator.** Advaita's **without-qualities (*nirguṇa*)** Brahman is likewise beyond exhaustive predication, and Advaita has a **built-in two-level semantics** — *nirguṇa* and *with-qualities* (*saguṇa*) — where Hick must construct one. **[INFERENCE]** This is the owner's discriminating point: the Indian resource already contains the device Hick needs, which is a genuinely comparative observation rather than a patriotic one.

**Objection.** If the Real is transcategorial, the traditions are not really talking about it, and Hick has replaced a plurality of religions with a single unknowable X that no one worships.
**Reply.** **[FACT]** Hick's answer is that the traditions' *personae* and *impersonae* are authentic — the worship is directed at something genuinely encountered, under a scheme. **[INFERENCE]** The residual worry is not that worship is misdirected but that the theory has purchased compatibility by making the shared object contentless; and the fair verdict is that this is a cost, honestly borne, not a knock-down refutation.

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH

> **Claim:** Hick's pluralism rests on a religious application of the Kantian distinction between reality in itself and reality as humanly experienced, and its central difficulty follows directly from that basis.
> **Named evidence:** The Real *an sich* is transcategorial, so personal and non-personal predicates attach to its manifestations rather than to it; yet Hick, departing from Kant, treats the noumenal Real as causally related to religious experience, and describes it as existing and as that toward which transformation is oriented.
> **Analysis:** If the Real lies beyond all categories, existence, causality and goodness cannot be literally predicated of it, so the hypothesis needs from the Real precisely the work its transcategoriality forbids; Hick's reply is that such predications are formal rather than substantive, which preserves consistency at the cost of informativeness.
> **Qualification:** The objection therefore thins the hypothesis rather than refuting it, and it is worth noting that Advaita's *nirguṇa*–*saguṇa* distinction supplies from within a tradition the two-level semantics Hick has to construct from outside.

#### UPSC TRAPS

16. ⚠️ Do not state the Kantian analogy without stating the departure. Hick's causal claim about the noumenal Real is exactly what the objection targets, and omitting it makes the objection look unmotivated.
17. ⚠️ Do not treat the noumenal objection as a refutation. It reduces informativeness; saying so is more accurate and scores higher than declaring Hick defeated.
18. ⚠️ Do not import the *nirguṇa* comparison as mere decoration. Its examinable point is structural — Advaita has the two-level semantics built in, and Hick must construct it.

#### MINI RECAP

Kant's noumenon/phenomenon applied to religion. Departure: the Real causes experience. Objection: existence, causality and goodness become illicit predications. Reply: formal, not substantive, predication. Verdict: thinner, not refuted. Indian comparator: *nirguṇa*/*saguṇa* built in.

### SESSION 7 — HICK III: THE SALVATION-AND-LIBERATION TRANSFORMATION CRITERION

#### DEFINITION

**Plain-language definition.** If we cannot settle which religion is doctrinally right, Hick proposes we look at what religions *do* to people: the test is whether a tradition actually moves people from being centred on themselves to being centred on ultimate Reality.

**Technical definition.** The **soteriological criterion** assesses traditions by the transformation they effect from **self-centredness to Reality-centredness**. It is a *practical* criterion, offered because doctrinal comparison is deadlocked. Crucially, it does **not** make contradictory doctrines jointly true; it is a criterion of authenticity for traditions, not of truth for propositions.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE OR ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Hick's criterion answers a question about *traditions* and leaves a question about *propositions* untouched, and the whole force of his position — and the whole force of the objection to it — turns on whether that division of labour is legitimate or merely convenient.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

**soteriological criterion · self-centredness to Reality-centredness · transformation · practical test · saints across traditions · fruits · authenticity of a tradition ≠ truth of a proposition · identity and mission objection · self-referential objection · parity objection**

**How to use them:** the sentence that must appear is "**the criterion does not make contradictory doctrines jointly true**". Writing it pre-empts the strongest quick objection and shows the examiner you know what the criterion is for.

#### VISUAL — WHAT THE CRITERION MEASURES, AND WHAT IT LEAVES ALONE

```text
  DOCTRINAL COMPARISON — DEADLOCKED
  "God is personal"  ⟷  "the ultimate is beyond personality"
  "one life, then judgement" ⟷ "beginningless rebirth"
        │  no neutral adjudicating framework available
        ↓
  HICK'S MOVE: change the QUESTION, not the answer
        ↓
  ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │  SOTERIOLOGICAL CRITERION                                 │
  │  self-centredness  ───────────────▶  Reality-centredness  │
  │  (grasping, ego,                    (compassion, justice, │
  │   fear, acquisition)                 detachment, love)    │
  │  measured by: saints, moral fruits, transformed lives     │
  └───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
        ↓ WHAT IT SETTLES        ↓ WHAT IT DOES NOT SETTLE
   whether a tradition is    which doctrines are TRUE;
   an AUTHENTIC response     whether the ends are the SAME
   ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
   THREE OBJECTIONS THAT ARRIVE TOGETHER
   (a) NOUMENAL   — the Real is too thin to be the terminus
                     of transformation (Session 6)
   (b) SELF-REFERENTIAL / PARITY — pluralism criticises first-order
                     superiority while claiming second-order superiority
   (c) IDENTITY / MISSION — traditions object to being redescribed
                     as culturally limited appearances
```

*The top shows why a practical criterion is proposed at all; the box states it; the two arrows show its exact scope; the bottom strip lists the three objections that must be answered together rather than one at a time.*

#### TEACHING

**[FACT] The criterion.** Transformation from self-centredness to Reality-centredness is the practical criterion. Traditions are assessed by whether they actually produce that reorientation.

**[FACT] Its exact scope.** It **does not** make contradictory doctrines jointly true. That sentence is the owner's, and it is the single most useful line in the session: the criterion establishes the *authenticity of a tradition as a response*, not the *truth of its propositions*.

**[FACT] Why the criterion is proposed.** Because doctrinal comparison across traditions lacks a neutral adjudicating framework — the point Session 16 makes technically as **deep disagreement**. A practical criterion is a way of getting traction where a doctrinal one cannot.

**[FACT] Objection (a) — the noumenal objection returns.** If the Real is transcategorial, what exactly is one becoming centred *on*? Session 6 gives the reply; here the point is that the thinness of the Real infects the criterion as well as the metaphysics.

**[FACT] Objection (b) — the self-referential or parity objection.** Pluralism presents itself as a superior second-order account while criticising first-order claims to superiority. Exclusivists ask why Hick's meta-framework escapes the contestability he attributes to everyone else. **[INFERENCE]** Note that this objection does not require the exclusivist to be right about anything else; it is a consistency challenge, which is why it is so difficult to shake off.

**[FACT] Objection (c) — the identity and mission concern.** Traditions may reasonably object that being redescribed as culturally limited appearances **erases their self-understanding** and weakens witness or mission. The owner's instruction is explicit: *dialogue requires hearing that objection, not declaring it solved.*

**[INFERENCE] How to hold the three together.** They are not three separate complaints but one complaint at three levels — metaphysical (the Real is thin), epistemic (the theory claims a standpoint it denies to others), and existential (the traditions do not recognise themselves in the description). An answer that says this has organised the objection better than the objection is usually organised.

**[FACT] The criticism the owner records about conflicting salvific goals.** Communion with God, non-dual knowledge and liberation-as-cessation (*nirvāṇa*) are not obviously **one** end. Hick's reply appeals to family resemblance or to the transformation criterion; **critics call this flattening**. Session 9 shows Heim taking exactly this criticism and building an alternative pluralism on it.

**Objection.** The criterion is unfalsifiable: any tradition can be said to produce transformation in some of its adherents, so the test excludes nothing.
**Reply.** **[INFERENCE]** It is not vacuous — it excludes traditions or movements whose observable fruit is systematically the intensification of self-assertion — but it is coarse-grained, and it cannot discriminate between two traditions that both produce saints. Conceding the coarseness is better than defending a precision the criterion does not have; and once conceded, the honest conclusion is that the criterion supports pluralism as an **ethic of engagement** rather than as a demonstrated metaphysics.

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH

> **Claim:** Hick assesses traditions soteriologically, by the transformation from self-centredness to Reality-centredness, because doctrinal comparison across traditions has no neutral adjudicating framework.
> **Named evidence:** The criterion is practical and observational — it appeals to saints and moral fruits across traditions — and Hick is explicit that it does not render contradictory doctrines jointly true.
> **Analysis:** Its scope is therefore the authenticity of a tradition as a response rather than the truth of its propositions, which lets it operate where doctrinal comparison is deadlocked; but it inherits the thinness of the Real, it claims a second-order standpoint whose superiority the theory itself criticises in first-order form, and it redescribes traditions in terms they reject.
> **Qualification:** The criterion is also coarse-grained, since it cannot discriminate between traditions that both produce transformed lives, so the defensible conclusion is that it supports pluralism as an ethic of engagement rather than as a demonstrated metaphysics.

#### UPSC TRAPS

19. ⚠️ Do not present the transformation criterion as settling the truth question. It does not, and Hick does not claim that it does.
20. ⚠️ Do not answer the parity objection by repeating that Hick's is a philosophical hypothesis. That reply is available but contested; Session 8 shows D'Costa pressing it, and a script must acknowledge the pressure.
21. ⚠️ Do not omit the identity and mission concern. It is the objection traditions themselves raise, and the owner requires that it be heard rather than declared solved.

#### MINI RECAP

Criterion: self-centredness → Reality-centredness. Scope: authenticity of traditions, not truth of propositions. Three objections at three levels: thin Real, parity, redescription. Conflicting ends: Hick says family resemblance, critics say flattening — and Heim builds on that.

### SESSION 8 — EXCLUSIVISM DEFENDED (PLANTINGA), AND PLURALISM ACCUSED (D'COSTA)

#### DEFINITION

**Plain-language definition.** This session turns the argument round. Plantinga argues that the exclusivist is not doing anything the pluralist is not also doing; D'Costa argues that pluralism is exclusivism wearing a different hat.

**Technical definition.** Plantinga's "A Defense of Religious Exclusivism" answers three charges — **arbitrariness**, **arrogance** and **irrationality** — by showing that each applies equally to the pluralist's own contested second-order belief. D'Costa's charge is that pluralism is a **covert exclusivism**: it makes its own second-order account of the Real uniquely true and reinterprets every tradition against that tradition's self-understanding.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE OR ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> An answer that presents exclusivism only as intolerance and pluralism only as generosity will not reach the top band, because both positions have serious defenders — and the sharpest exchange in the whole clause is between Plantinga, who shows that the pluralist holds a contested belief too, and D'Costa, who shows that the pluralist's contested belief is doing exactly the work the exclusivist's belief was accused of doing.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

**arbitrariness · arrogance · irrationality · warrant · defeater · meta-level parity · contingency of birth · covert exclusivism · second-order account · revisionism · self-understanding of traditions**

**How to use them:** give Plantinga's three replies as three replies, then give the two counters (permissibility ≠ truth; contingency of birth), then give D'Costa in one sentence. That sequence is a complete top-band paragraph.

*Read the table left to right for the exchange, then the two boxes below for what the exchange does and does not settle. The last box is the strongest single objection to Hick in the entire clause.*

#### TEACHING

**[FACT] Why the session is required.** An answer presenting exclusivism only as intolerance and pluralism only as generosity will not reach the top band. Both positions have serious defenders, and the graded ladder rewards stating Plantinga's defence and then meeting it.

**[FACT] Reply 1 — arbitrariness.** The pluralist too holds a belief — that no tradition is uniquely true — which the exclusivist rejects. At the meta-level the pluralist is therefore in exactly the same position: holding a contested belief without neutral adjudication.

**[FACT] Reply 2 — arrogance.** Believing *p* while knowing that others deny *p* is the ordinary condition of every philosopher, including the pluralist. If that is arrogance here, it is arrogance everywhere; so either the charge is universal and therefore harmless, or it is not a charge at all.

**[FACT] Reply 3 — irrationality.** Awareness of disagreement may reduce warrant somewhat, but it does not extinguish it. Session 16 gives this claim its technical setting in the steadfastness position.

**[FACT] Counter 1.** The replies establish **permissibility**, not **truth**. Showing that exclusivism is rationally permissible is not showing that it is correct — and a script that stops at Plantinga has answered a different question from the one printed.

**[FACT] Counter 2 — contingency of birth.** The replies do not address the fact that one's tradition is **overwhelmingly predicted by where one was born**. This is the objection that actually bites, and it is also premise 1 of Hick's argument, which is why Sessions 5 and 8 lock together.

**[FACT] D'Costa's charge.** Pluralism is a **covert exclusivism**: it makes its own second-order account of the Real the uniquely true one and reinterprets every tradition against its own self-understanding. The owner records this as **the strongest single objection to Hick**, and records that Hick's reply — that his is a philosophical hypothesis, not a rival creed — **does not fully escape it**.

**[INFERENCE] Why the reply does not fully escape.** A second-order theory that tells first-order traditions what they are *really* doing is exercising exactly the authority it denies them. The distinction between orders is real, but it does not by itself confer neutrality; it relocates the claim to privileged access rather than dissolving it.

**Objection.** If Plantinga and D'Costa are both right, the debate is a stalemate and no position is preferable.
**Reply.** **[INFERENCE]** Not a stalemate, but a change of question. What survives is that no position escapes holding a contested commitment — so the live question becomes which commitments are held **corrigibly**, with the procedural virtues that Session 16 shows deep disagreement makes obligatory. That reframing is the conclusion a top-band script draws, and it is defensible without conceding relativism.

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH

> **Claim:** The exclusivism–pluralism dispute cannot be settled by attributing bad epistemic character to either side, because each side's defenders show that the other holds an equally contested commitment.
> **Named evidence:** Plantinga's "A Defense of Religious Exclusivism" answers arbitrariness by noting that the pluralist's own denial of unique truth is contested, answers arrogance by observing that believing what others deny is the ordinary condition of philosophy, and answers irrationality by distinguishing reduced from extinguished warrant; D'Costa answers in the other direction, arguing that pluralism makes its own second-order account uniquely true and reinterprets traditions against their self-understanding.
> **Analysis:** Plantinga's replies establish permissibility rather than truth and leave the contingency-of-birth objection untouched, while D'Costa's charge shows that Hick's appeal to a second-order status relocates rather than removes the claim to privileged access.
> **Qualification:** The exchange therefore does not decide between the positions; it converts the question from *who is neutral* — nobody is — into *whose commitment is held corrigibly*, which is the form in which the dispute can still be argued.

#### UPSC TRAPS

22. ⚠️ Do not omit Plantinga's defence from an exclusivism-versus-pluralism answer. The verdict ladder places "Plantinga's defence stated and met" in the strong band; omitting it caps the script lower.

### SESSION 9 — FOUR VARIETIES OF PLURALISM, AND WHY THEY ARE INCOMPATIBLE

#### DEFINITION

**Plain-language definition.** "Pluralism" names at least four different theories. One says there is one Reality behind many faiths. One says the faiths are heading to genuinely different destinations. One says dialogue should change both sides rather than explain them. One says there are several ultimates.

**Technical definition.** **Identist pluralism** (Hick): one Real, many culturally conditioned responses, assessed soteriologically. **Differential pluralism** (S. Mark Heim, *Salvations*, 1995): traditions aim at and attain **genuinely different religious ends**. **Mutual transformation** (Raimon Panikkar; John Cobb): dialogue changes both parties rather than subsuming them under a neutral hypothesis. **Deep religious pluralism** (David Ray Griffin): a process-based version allowing **plural ultimates** — creativity, God, the cosmos.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE OR ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> The single most common structural error in this clause is to treat "pluralism" as one position and then attack or defend it wholesale; in fact identist and differential pluralism give incompatible answers to the very question the stem is asking, since one says the traditions are aiming at the same thing under different descriptions and the other says they are arriving at different things.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

**identist pluralism · differential pluralism · *Salvations* (1995) · plural religious ends · communion · non-dual realisation · liberation-as-cessation (*nirvāṇa*) · mutual transformation · Panikkar · Cobb · deep religious pluralism · Griffin · plural ultimates · fidelity to self-understanding**

**How to use them:** name at least two varieties and state the discriminating question — *one ultimate or several ends?* — because that question is what makes the classification analytical rather than a list.

#### TEACHING

**[FACT] Variety 1 — Hick's identist pluralism.** One Real, many culturally conditioned responses, assessed soteriologically by the shift from self-centredness to Reality-centredness. Sessions 5–7 give it in full.

**[FACT] Variety 2 — Heim's differential pluralism.** In *Salvations* (1995), the traditions aim at and attain **genuinely different religious ends**: communion, non-dual realisation and liberation-as-cessation (*nirvāṇa*) are **not three descriptions of one destination but three destinations**. The owner records that this **preserves the traditions' self-understanding far better than Hick does**.

**[FACT] Variety 3 — mutual transformation.** Raimon Panikkar and John Cobb hold that dialogue changes both parties rather than subsuming them under a neutral hypothesis. **[INFERENCE]** This is a methodological pluralism rather than a metaphysical one: it does not answer the one-or-many question, it changes what the encounter is *for*.

**[FACT] Variety 4 — Griffin's deep religious pluralism.** A process-based version that allows **plural ultimates** — creativity, God, the cosmos. **[INFERENCE]** It is the most metaphysically ambitious of the four, and it pays for its generosity by adopting a specific process metaphysics that most traditions do not share.

**[FACT] The comparative verdict.** The owner's verdict formula: "the E-I-P triad is a **map, not a verdict**. Exclusivism is rationally permissible but epistemically uncomfortable; inclusivism is generous but judges others in terms they do not accept; and 'pluralism' names at least four positions, of which **Hick's is the most exposed to D'Costa's charge and Heim's the most respectful of the traditions' own self-descriptions**."

**[FACT] The cost of Heim's position.** Heim gives up a **single ultimate**. That is recorded in the owner's inter-thinker grid as his main objection, and it matters: differential pluralism buys fidelity to the traditions at the price of the unity that made pluralism attractive in the first place.

**[INFERENCE] Why this session decides several PYQs.** The 2018 "distinguish E, I and P" question, the 2019 "central problem between pluralists and exclusivists" question and the 2023 "does pluralism destroy the truth of religion" question all change shape depending on which pluralism is in view. Naming the variety at the outset is therefore not pedantry; it is what makes the rest of the answer determinate.

**Objection.** Multiplying varieties is scholastic: the examiner asked about pluralism, not about a taxonomy of pluralisms.
**Reply.** **[INFERENCE]** The taxonomy is doing argumentative work, not display work. The objection that pluralism flattens the traditions' distinct ends is decisive against Hick and simply misses Heim; so a script that has not named the variety cannot tell whether the objection it is answering applies. One sentence — "on Hick's identist version…, whereas on Heim's differential version…" — is enough, and it converts a generic paragraph into a discriminating one.

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH

> **Claim:** "Pluralism" names at least four incompatible theories, and the discriminating question between them is whether there is one ultimate reality or several genuinely different religious ends.
> **Named evidence:** Hick's identist pluralism posits one transcategorial Real with culturally conditioned responses; Heim's *Salvations* (1995) holds that communion, non-dual realisation and liberation-as-cessation are three destinations rather than three descriptions; Panikkar and Cobb propose mutual transformation through dialogue; Griffin's deep religious pluralism admits plural ultimates on a process metaphysics.
> **Analysis:** The classification is load-bearing because the standard objections attach differently — the charge of flattening distinct ends is decisive against Hick and irrelevant to Heim, while D'Costa's covert-exclusivism charge presses hardest on the version that claims a single neutral second-order account.
> **Qualification:** Each variety pays a price: Heim surrenders the single ultimate that made pluralism attractive, Griffin buys generosity with a contested process metaphysics, and mutual transformation changes the method without answering the metaphysical question.

#### UPSC TRAPS

25. ⚠️ Do not treat "pluralism" as one position. Hick's identist pluralism and Heim's differential pluralism are **incompatible answers**, and this is trap 15 in the owner's list.
26. ⚠️ Do not present Heim as simply better than Hick. He is more respectful of self-understanding **and** he gives up the single ultimate; both halves must be written.
27. ⚠️ Do not use Panikkar or Cobb as a metaphysical answer. Mutual transformation is a claim about what dialogue is for, not about how many ultimates there are.

#### MINI RECAP

Four varieties: identist (Hick), differential (Heim, *Salvations* 1995), mutual transformation (Panikkar, Cobb), deep religious pluralism (Griffin, plural ultimates). Discriminating question: one ultimate or several ends. Hick most exposed to D'Costa; Heim most faithful to the traditions but without a single ultimate.
