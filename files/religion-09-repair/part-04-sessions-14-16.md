### SESSION 14 — ABSOLUTE TRUTH WITHOUT EPISTEMIC ABSOLUTISM: BREAKING THE 2022 INFERENCE

#### DEFINITION

**Plain-language definition.** The worry is: if I believe there is one absolute Truth, must I believe that my religion has it and yours does not? The answer is no — provided one separates *what is absolutely true* from *how completely any human formulation can capture it*.

**Technical definition.** One may affirm an **absolute referent** while denying that any **finite formulation** exhausts it. Ontological unity does not entail epistemic monopoly; conditioned perspectives may be **corrigible and partially adequate**. The three theses to keep apart are **ontological absolutism** (there is one absolute reality), **epistemic fallibilism** (no finite formulation of it is exhaustive or incorrigible) and **political tolerance** (coercion is impermissible). They are separate, and none entails the others.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE OR ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Ontological absolutism entails epistemic exclusivism only if one adds the premise that a finite formulation can exhaust the absolute; deny that premise — as the Advaitic *without-qualities* (*nirguṇa*) doctrine and Jain many-sidedness both do — and the inference from absolute truth to exclusivism fails.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

**absolute referent · finite formulation · epistemic access · salvific claim · ontological absolutism · epistemic fallibilism · political tolerance · exhaustive possession · partial and perspectival · corrigible · *nirguṇa* Brahman · *saguṇa* forms · suppressed premise**

**How to use them:** the four-way separation — **referent, formulation, access, salvation** — is the mark scheme for 2021 Q5(b), 2022 Q7(a) and 2024 Q5(b). Write the four words, then break the inference.

#### VISUAL — THE SUPPRESSED PREMISE, AND WHERE IT FAILS

```text
  THE ALLEGED INFERENCE (2022 stem)
  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ P1  There is exactly ONE Absolute Truth.                     │
  │ P2  [SUPPRESSED]  A finite formulation CAN EXHAUST it.       │
  │ P3  [SUPPRESSED]  MY tradition's formulation is that one.    │

  ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
```

*The top box exposes the two suppressed premises; the second box shows the traditions that already deny P2; the two lower bands give the four separations and the three independent theses that a top-band script names.*

#### TEACHING

**[FACT] The printed worry (2022).** *"An unquestionable acceptance of only one Absolute Truth will inevitably result in religious exclusivism"* — and exclusivism is then said to breed conflict and intolerance.

**[FACT] The analysis, in the owner's own terms.** If "absolute truth" means *my* religion's propositions are the sole truth, then yes, the position tends to exclusivism. **But** one can hold that there **is** an Absolute Truth — the Real, or Brahman — while recognising all religions as **partial, perspectival** approaches to it: an absolute **referent** with pluralist **access**. This **breaks the link** between believing in absolute truth and being exclusivist.

**[FACT] The Jain contribution.** *Anekāntavāda* and *syādvāda* are a powerful Indian tool here: reality is many-sided; every viewpoint is **partially true**; epistemic humility therefore dissolves dogmatic exclusivism.

**[FACT] The doctrine statement.** One may affirm an absolute referent while denying that any finite formulation exhausts it. The supporting argument: ontological unity does not entail epistemic monopoly, and conditioned perspectives may be corrigible and partially adequate.

**[FACT] The presupposition being made.** Truth permits **degrees of adequacy or non-literal mediation** without collapsing into relativism. **[INFERENCE]** This is a genuine philosophical commitment and should be stated as one: someone who insists that a proposition is simply true or simply false, with no room for partial adequacy or non-literal reference, will not accept the reply — and that is where the debate then goes.

**[FACT] The distinction to write.** **Ontological absolutism, epistemic fallibilism and political tolerance are separate theses.** Establishing one settles neither of the others.

**[FACT] The canonical example.** ***Nirguṇa* Brahman** — Brahman **without qualities** — as beyond exhaustive predication can support humility, **while *saguṇa* forms — Brahman with qualities — structure real devotional paths**. **[INFERENCE]** The example is doing double duty: it shows a tradition that already contains the referent/formulation distinction internally, and it shows that the distinction need not devalue devotion, since the *saguṇa* level is where actual religious life happens.

**[FACT] The 2024 stem, and what it asks.** "Write a note on the notion of absolute truth in the context of religion" is a **notion** question: it asks for the concept to be analysed, not for a verdict on a controversy. The four-way separation *is* the analysis, and the compatibility result is the note's conclusion.

**Objection.** If no formulation is exhaustive, then the claim "there is one absolute Truth" is itself a finite formulation and is therefore not exhaustive either — the position undercuts itself.
**Reply.** **[INFERENCE]** The self-reference is real but not fatal. The claim being made about the absolute is **formal** — that there is a single referent — rather than substantive, and a formal claim can be asserted without pretending to exhaust its object; this is the same move Hick makes in Session 6, and it carries the same cost, namely that the claim is thin. What the objection does establish is that the position must be held **corrigibly**, which is exactly what epistemic fallibilism asserts.

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH

> **Claim:** Belief in one absolute Truth entails religious exclusivism only on a suppressed premise that a finite formulation can exhaust the absolute, and that premise is denied by major traditions on both sides of the comparison.
> **Named evidence:** Advaita's *nirguṇa* Brahman is beyond exhaustive predication while *saguṇa* forms structure real devotional paths; Jain *syādvāda* indexes every assertion to a standpoint; Hick's Real *an sich* is transcategorial — three independent denials of the same premise.
> **Analysis:** With the premise denied, one may affirm an absolute **referent** while treating all human **formulations** as partial, perspectival and corrigible, so ontological absolutism, epistemic fallibilism and political tolerance come apart as three separate theses rather than one package.
> **Qualification:** The compatibility is genuine but conditional: it requires that truth admit degrees of adequacy or non-literal mediation, and someone who denies that will reinstate the inference — which is where the argument properly continues rather than where it ends.

#### UPSC TRAPS

40. ⚠️ In an absolute-truth answer, separate **referent, formulation, access and salvation**. This is trap 6 in the owner's list, and it is the single instruction that decides three of the twelve owned questions.
41. ⚠️ Do not answer the 2022 stem with a flat "no". The stem's inference *does* go through on one reading of "absolute truth" — name that reading, concede it, and then break the other.
42. ⚠️ Do not assume that absolute truth entails exclusivism, and do not assume the reverse either. The link is an inference with a suppressed premise, and the premise is what must be discussed.

#### MINI RECAP

Suppressed premise: a finite formulation can exhaust the absolute. Deny it — Advaita, Jainism and Hick each do — and the inference fails. Absolute referent, plural access. Three separate theses: ontological absolutism, epistemic fallibilism, political tolerance. Four separations: referent, formulation, access, salvation.

### SESSION 15 — WHAT PLURALISM CANNOT DISSOLVE: HARD CONTRADICTION AND THE 2023 CHARGES

#### DEFINITION

**Plain-language definition.** Some religious claims do not merely look different; they contradict each other about the same thing in the same sense. Pluralism has to admit that, and a pluralism that pretends otherwise stops being philosophy.

**Technical definition.** The **hard-contradiction control**: a personal creator cannot both exist and not exist in the same respect; the self cannot both be permanent and denied in the same sense. Pluralism may reinterpret **level, symbol, standpoint or end**, but it must **acknowledge any contradiction that remains**. The 2023 stem raises two distinct allegations — that pluralism **invites inter-religious conflict** and that it **destroys the truth of religion** — and they must be answered separately.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE OR ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Pluralism does not destroy religious truth; it relocates it — but what it cannot do is dissolve hard contradictions, because a personal creator either exists or does not, and a pluralism that pretends otherwise is rhetoric rather than philosophy.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

**hard contradiction · same respect · same sense · level · symbol · standpoint · soteriological end · literal conflict · relocation, not destruction · equal dignity ≠ identical truth · two separate allegations · flattening**

**How to use them:** name the **four reinterpretive moves** (level, symbol, standpoint, end), then name the **residue**. The residue is where the top band is.

#### TEACHING

**[FACT] The hard-contradiction control.** A personal creator cannot both exist and not exist in the same respect; the self cannot both be permanent and denied in the same sense. Pluralism may reinterpret level, symbol, standpoint or end, **but it must acknowledge any contradiction that remains**.

**[FACT] The 2023 question, and the reply it needs.** Critics say pluralism relativises truth. **Reply:** pluralism need not deny truth — it **relocates** absoluteness in the transcendent Real and treats religions as culturally conditioned but **genuine** responses; **coexistence need not mean "anything goes"**.

**[FACT] The conflict allegation and its reply.** Pluralism may be said to create rather than remove dispute by relativising commitment. **Reply:** conflict is **not caused by plurality alone**; power, identity and exclusivist certainty mediate it. Session 19 supplies the full apparatus — religion as cause, marker, mobiliser or legitimiser.

**[FACT] The relativism criticism, restated with its reply.** Equal validity erases truth. **Reply:** distinguish **equal dignity or salvific efficacy** from **identical propositional truth**.

**[FACT] The conflicting-ends criticism.** Communion with God, non-dual knowledge and liberation-as-cessation are **not obviously one end**. **Reply:** family resemblance, or the transformation criterion — **and critics call this flattening**. Session 9 shows Heim converting the criticism into an alternative pluralism.

**[INFERENCE] The four reinterpretive moves, and their limits.** *Level*: two claims may be true at different levels of description (the *nirguṇa*/*saguṇa* device). *Symbol*: a claim may be non-literal, which is the Religious Language owner's territory and is signposted rather than annexed. *Standpoint*: the claim may be indexed to a respect (*syāt*). *End*: the traditions may be describing different destinations rather than one. Each is legitimate where it can actually be specified. Where none can be specified, a straight contradiction remains — and the honest move is to say so.

**[INFERENCE] Why acknowledging the residue strengthens rather than weakens the answer.** A pluralism that concedes irreducible conflict at some points but maintains authentic plural response at others is a *determinate* position that can be argued for. A pluralism that harmonises everything is unfalsifiable and, by D'Costa's argument in Session 8, is also quietly imposing its own framework on traditions that assert contradictories deliberately.

**Objection.** If contradictions remain, then at most one tradition is right about them, and pluralism has collapsed back into exclusivism about those claims.
**Reply.** **[INFERENCE]** It has collapsed into exclusivism **about those claims** — and that is the correct result, not a defeat. Pluralism is a thesis about traditions as wholes and about the availability of transformation and truth beyond one's own; it is not the thesis that no religious proposition is false. Holding both together is exactly the discrimination the four axes of Session 1 were designed to permit: alethic exclusivity on a specific proposition is compatible with soteriological and political pluralism.

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH

> **Claim:** Pluralism relocates religious truth rather than destroying it, but it cannot dissolve contradictions that arise on the same question in the same respect.
> **Named evidence:** It may legitimately reinterpret by level (*nirguṇa* and *saguṇa*), by symbol, by standpoint (*syāt*-indexed assertion) or by soteriological end (Heim's plural destinations); it cannot reinterpret away the claim that a personal creator exists as against the claim that no such being exists.
> **Analysis:** Because those four moves are available, the charge that pluralism relativises truth fails as a general charge — absoluteness is relocated in the transcendent referent and the traditions remain genuine responses — while the residue shows that pluralism is a thesis about traditions rather than a guarantee that no religious proposition is false.
> **Qualification:** The 2023 stem in fact contains two allegations, conflict-generation and truth-destruction, and they require separate answers: the first is met by showing that power, identity and exclusivist certainty mediate conflict, the second by distinguishing equal dignity and salvific efficacy from identical propositional truth.

#### UPSC TRAPS

43. ⚠️ Do not dissolve hard contradictions rhetorically. The owner's control is explicit, and the graded ladder puts "explicit acknowledgement of hard contradictions that pluralism cannot dissolve" in the **top** band.
44. ⚠️ Do not answer the 2023 stem as a single question. **Conflict-generation and truth-destruction are different charges**, and the directive decoder requires them to be answered separately.
45. ⚠️ Do not meet the relativism charge with an assertion of harmony. Meet it by distinguishing equal dignity and salvific efficacy from identical propositional truth.

#### MINI RECAP

Four legitimate moves: level, symbol, standpoint, end. One residue: same question, same respect. Two allegations in 2023: conflict, and truth-destruction. Relocation, not destruction. Equal dignity ≠ identical truth. Conceding the residue is a top-band move.

### SESSION 16 — DEEP AND PEER DISAGREEMENT: WHY CONVERGENCE FAILS, AND WHAT FOLLOWS

#### DEFINITION

**Plain-language definition.** Before asking who is right, ask whether this is the kind of disagreement argument could settle. Some disagreements cannot be settled by argument because the parties do not share the background against which any evidence would count.

**Technical definition.** An **epistemic peer** is one roughly equal in evidence and reasoning ability. **Conciliationism** (equal weight) holds that on discovering peer disagreement one should reduce confidence, often toward suspension; **steadfastness** holds that one may retain one's belief, since one's own reasoning is part of one's evidence. The **independence principle** — assess the disagreement without relying on the reasoning under dispute — is what conciliationism needs and steadfastness denies. **Deep disagreement**, in Robert Fogelin's technical sense ("The Logic of Deep Disagreement", 1985), obtains when parties do not share the **framework propositions** — Wittgenstein's "hinge" commitments from *On Certainty* — against which evidence would count.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE OR ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Religious disagreement is deep in Fogelin's technical sense: it lacks a shared framework against which evidence could adjudicate — which explains the persistence of exclusivism without vindicating it, and supports pluralism as an **ethic of engagement** rather than as a demonstrated metaphysics.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

**epistemic peer · conciliationism / equal weight · Feldman · Christensen · steadfastness · Kelly · van Inwagen · independence principle · deep disagreement · Fogelin (1985) · framework propositions · hinge propositions · *On Certainty* · Michael Lynch · epistemic principles · epistemic ≠ alethic · procedural virtues**

**How to use them:** the payoff sentence is "**unresolvability is epistemic, not alethic**". It converts a vague appeal to humility into a precise epistemological claim, and it is the discriminating move the top band names.

#### VISUAL — THE TWO LAYERS, AND THE FOUR CONSEQUENCES

*Layer 1 is disagreement inside a shared framework; Layer 2 is disagreement about the framework. The four numbered consequences are what convert the module into marks, and they cut in both directions.*

#### TEACHING

**[FACT] Why the module is needed.** The pluralism debate assumes rival truth-claims *could* in principle be settled by argument. The epistemology of disagreement asks whether that assumption is true — and its answer reshapes the whole clause.

**[FACT] Peer disagreement.** An **epistemic peer** is one roughly equal in evidence and reasoning ability. Two rival positions: **conciliationism / equal weight** (Richard Feldman, David Christensen) — on discovering peer disagreement one should reduce confidence, often toward suspension; and **steadfastness** (Thomas Kelly; Peter van Inwagen's well-known claim that it is not irrational to retain one's belief even when one cannot show the other is wrong) — one may retain one's belief, since one's own reasoning is part of one's evidence.

**[FACT] The independence principle.** Assess the disagreement without relying on the reasoning under dispute. This is what conciliationism **needs** and steadfastness **denies**. **[INFERENCE]** Naming the principle is what shows an examiner that you understand *why* the two camps disagree rather than merely that they do.

**[FACT] Deep disagreement, technically.** Fogelin (1985): some disagreements are not resolvable by argument **in principle**, because the parties do not share the **framework propositions** — Wittgenstein's hinge commitments from *On Certainty* — against which evidence would count. When background frameworks diverge, the same evidence is **not evidence for the same thing**, and argument has no shared fulcrum. **Michael Lynch** extends this to disagreement about **epistemic principles** themselves — which sources are reliable — where any defence is circular.

**[FACT] Application to religion.** Inter-religious disagreement is a paradigm case: the parties differ over the **sources** of evidence (which scripture, which experience, which inference), the **criteria of adequacy** (salvation? liberation? cessation?), and the **framework** within which any datum would count.

**[FACT] Consequence 1.** The failure of rational convergence across traditions is **predicted**, not scandalous — and is therefore **weak evidence for relativism**.

**[FACT] Consequence 2.** Exclusivism **cannot be convicted of irrationality** merely for persisting. This is where Plantinga's third reply, in Session 8, gets its technical backing.

**[FACT] Consequence 3.** Pluralism **cannot be established** merely by pointing at persistence. The same fact that fails to convict the exclusivist also fails to acquit the pluralist.

**[FACT] Consequence 4.** What *can* be rationally required are **procedural virtues** — fallibilism, intellectual humility, epistemic charity, and willingness to make one's framework explicit.

**[FACT] The Indian resource.** Many-sidedness with its standpoint theory and conditional predication is, in effect, a formal apparatus for exactly this problem: it makes the **standpoint (*naya*)** explicit and prefixes every assertion with *syāt*, so that framework-relativity is marked **inside** the proposition rather than left implicit. The owner records this as a genuine Indian contribution to a live Western problem — **and states that putting it that way is worth more than describing the elephant parable**.

**Objection.** If deep disagreement is unresolvable, pluralism collapses into relativism.
**Reply.** **[FACT]** Unresolvability is **epistemic**, not **alethic** — one party may still be right; what fails is our ability to demonstrate which.

**Second objection.** This licenses complacency: if nothing can be settled, why engage?
**Reply.** **[FACT]** It licenses only the **retention** of belief, not the refusal of dialogue; and it makes the procedural virtues **obligatory rather than optional**.

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH

> **Claim:** Religious disagreement is deep in Fogelin's technical sense, and recognising that changes what the pluralism debate can be expected to deliver.
> **Named evidence:** Fogelin's "The Logic of Deep Disagreement" (1985) identifies disagreements unresolvable in principle because the parties do not share the framework or hinge propositions of Wittgenstein's *On Certainty*, and Lynch extends the analysis to disagreement about epistemic principles themselves; within a shared framework the dispute is instead between conciliationism (Feldman, Christensen) and steadfastness (Kelly, van Inwagen), turning on the independence principle.
> **Analysis:** Applied to religion, where the parties differ over sources of evidence, criteria of adequacy and the framework in which a datum would count, the failure of convergence is predicted rather than scandalous — so it neither convicts exclusivism of irrationality nor establishes pluralism, and what it does require is the procedural virtues of fallibilism, humility, charity and explicitness about one's framework.
> **Qualification:** Unresolvability here is epistemic rather than alethic — one party may still be right and we may be unable to show which — and the Jain apparatus of standpoint-indexed assertion is the most developed attempt to mark that framework-relativity inside the proposition itself.

#### UPSC TRAPS

46. ⚠️ Do not say religious disagreement is "just relativism". Use deep disagreement, and state that unresolvability is **epistemic, not alethic**. This is trap 16 in the owner's list.
47. ⚠️ Do not use deep disagreement as an argument **for** pluralism. Consequence 3 says explicitly that pluralism cannot be established merely by pointing at persistence.
48. ⚠️ Do not confuse peer disagreement with deep disagreement. The first assumes a shared framework and asks what to do about a peer; the second denies the shared framework.

#### MINI RECAP

Layer 1: peers — conciliationism (Feldman, Christensen) vs steadfastness (Kelly, van Inwagen), turning on the independence principle. Layer 2: Fogelin 1985, hinge propositions, *On Certainty*, Lynch on epistemic principles. Four consequences, cutting both ways. Payoff line: unresolvability is epistemic, not alethic.
