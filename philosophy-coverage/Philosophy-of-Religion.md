## Clause 1: Notions of God; attributes; relation to man and the world (Indian and Western)

**Syllabus control:** "Notions of God : Attributes; Relation to Man and the
World. (Indian and Western)." The challenge is to identify *which* God-concept
is at issue before arguing about its attributes or its relations. For example,
one God does not by itself entail a personal creator; the ultimate in Advaita
is not simply the Western classical-theist God under a different name.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| R01-01 | Monotheism, polytheism, henotheism, kathenotheism, monolatry | Distinguish one ultimate deity, several independent gods, contextual supremacy of one invoked deity, one-god-at-a-time Vedic invocation and exclusive worship without denial of others. | R01-02 | Classical theism versus deism | Necessary personal creator and continuing sustainer/providence versus creator who establishes laws without continuing revelatory intervention. | R01-03 | Pantheism versus panentheism | Spinoza: finite things are modes of one infinite God/Nature, not God as a pile of physical objects. Panentheism: world *in* God, but God exceeds it. Identity differs from inclusion-with-excess | §1.1, §8.2, §11 | Essential / Answer-ready |
| R01-04 | Personal versus impersonal; God with qualities (*saguṇa*) versus quality-transcending Brahman (*nirguṇa*) | Volition and addressability contrast with the impersonal absolute; neither pair is wholly interchangeable. | R01-05 | Immanence versus transcendence | Divine presence/sustenance and independence/beyondness are compatible under classical theism; neither means being physically inside or geographically far from the world. | R01-06 | Creator, sustainer, providential ruler, moral governor, ground of being, absolute | These are different *roles*: originating all creaturely being, continuously supporting it, directing history, allotting moral consequences, grounding actuality or standing as the self-sufficient ultimate. | R01-07 | Cause without physical manifestation | Distinguish physical embodiment, incarnation, being the material cause, being the efficient cause and the metaphysical dependence of created being.
