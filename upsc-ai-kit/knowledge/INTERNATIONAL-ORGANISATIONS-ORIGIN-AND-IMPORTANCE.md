# International Organisations: Origin and Importance

> **Coverage:** All 150 organisations, bodies, forums and regimes in
> [`INTERNATIONAL-ORGANISATIONS-AND-GROUPINGS-INDEX.md`](INTERNATIONAL-ORGANISATIONS-AND-GROUPINGS-INDEX.md).
> That companion remains the owner for current membership, headquarters and India-status updates.
>
> **How to revise:** remember the problem that produced the institution, not the year alone.
> Dates distinguish foundation, treaty adoption, entry into force, renaming and operational start.

## 1. United Nations principal organs and justice bodies

| Organisation / body | Origin | Why created and UPSC importance | India relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **United Nations** | UN Charter signed and effective in 1945 after World War II. | Prevent war and organise security, rights, development and cooperation; foundation of the post-war multilateral order. | India was an original member and major peacekeeping contributor. |
| **UN General Assembly** | Principal organ created by the 1945 Charter. | Universal deliberative, budgetary and electoral forum; one State, one vote, with mostly recommendatory resolutions. | Major platform for India's Global South and UN-reform diplomacy. |
| **UN Security Council** | Created by the 1945 Charter; expanded from 11 to 15 members in 1965. | Holds primary peace-and-security responsibility and can adopt binding Chapter VII decisions; veto reform is central. | India has served elected terms and seeks permanent membership. |
| **UN Economic and Social Council** | Created by the 1945 Charter; enlarged to 54 members in 1973. | Coordinates UN economic, social and development work and functional commissions. | India is periodically elected; it has no permanent seat. |
| **International Court of Justice** | Created by the 1945 Charter and ICJ Statute; began in 1946, succeeding the PCIJ. | Decides disputes between States and gives advisory opinions; jurisdiction generally requires consent. | India is party to the Statute and has appeared before the Court. |
| **UN Secretariat** | Principal organ created by the 1945 Charter. | International civil service that implements and supports UN mandates; headed by the Secretary-General. | India contributes personnel and seeks equitable representation. |
| **Trusteeship Council** | Created in 1945 to supervise Trust Territories; operations suspended in 1994. | Major decolonisation institution; it is suspended, not abolished. | Useful for decolonisation and UN-reform questions. |
| **International Criminal Court** | Rome Statute adopted in 1998 and effective in 2002. | Tries individuals for core international crimes under complementarity; distinct from the ICJ. | India is not a State Party. |
| **Permanent Court of Arbitration** | Established by the 1899 Hague Convention; revised in 1907. | Facilitates arbitration and fact-finding; despite its name, it is not a standing court of permanent judges. | India is a contracting party and has used PCA-administered proceedings. |
| **International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea** | Created under UNCLOS 1982; operational from 1996. | Specialised forum for law-of-the-sea disputes, prompt release and seabed matters. | India is an UNCLOS party. |

## 2. UN specialised agencies and related organisations

| Organisation / body | Origin | Why created and UPSC importance | India relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **World Health Organization** | Constitution adopted in 1946 and effective in 1948. | Coordinates global health standards, disease control and emergencies; World Health Assembly and IHR are key. | India was an original member and hosts WHO's South-East Asia office. |
| **International Labour Organization** | Created in 1919 by the Treaty of Versailles; UN specialised agency from 1946. | Pursues peace through social justice and labour standards; uniquely tripartite. | India is a founding member; conventions shape labour policy. |
| **Food and Agriculture Organization** | Constitution signed at Quebec in 1945. | Raises nutrition and agricultural productivity and combats hunger; owns FAOSTAT and co-owns Codex. | India is a founding member. |
| **UN Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization** | Constitution adopted in 1945 and effective in 1946. | Builds peace through education, science and culture; governs major heritage frameworks. | India joined in 1946 and has many designated sites. |
| **International Civil Aviation Organization** | Chicago Convention signed in 1944; ICAO began in 1947 after provisional PICAO. | Sets global civil-aviation standards and recommended practices. | India is a Convention party and elected Council participant. |
| **International Maritime Organization** | Convention adopted in 1948; body began as IMCO in 1959 and became IMO in 1982. | Creates uniform shipping-safety and pollution rules, including SOLAS and MARPOL. | India has been a member since 1959. |
| **International Telecommunication Union** | Telegraph Union founded in 1865; renamed ITU in 1932; UN agency from 1947. | Coordinates telecom standards, radio spectrum and satellite orbits. | India participates in standardisation and spectrum conferences. |
| **World Intellectual Property Organization** | WIPO Convention signed in 1967; effective in 1970; succeeded BIRPI. | Administers international IP treaties and registration systems; distinct from WTO-TRIPS. | India is a member; Indian patents remain nationally granted. |
| **International Fund for Agricultural Development** | Proposed after the 1974 World Food Conference; Agreement effective in 1977. | Concessional finance focused on rural poverty and food security. | India is a founding member and programme partner. |
| **UN Industrial Development Organization** | UN body from 1967; Constitution made it a specialised agency in 1985. | Promotes industrialisation, technology transfer and inclusive sustainable industry. | India is a founding participant and technical-cooperation partner. |
| **Universal Postal Union** | General Postal Union founded by the 1874 Treaty of Bern; renamed in 1878. | Created a single international postal territory and common exchange rules. | India participates through the Department of Posts. |
| **World Meteorological Organization** | Convention effective in 1950; succeeded the 1873 International Meteorological Organization. | Enables exchange of weather, climate and hydrological observations and early warnings. | India is a founding member represented by IMD. |
| **International Atomic Energy Agency** | Statute effective in 1957 after the 1953 Atoms for Peace proposal. | Promotes peaceful nuclear use while applying safeguards, safety and security standards. | India is a founding member with India-specific safeguards. |
| **World Bank Group** | IBRD arose at Bretton Woods in 1944; IFC, IDA, ICSID and MIGA were later additions. | Combines development lending, concessional finance, private investment, guarantees and dispute settlement. | India belongs to IBRD, IDA, IFC and MIGA, but not ICSID Convention. |
| **International Monetary Fund** | Articles adopted at Bretton Woods in 1944 and effective in 1945. | Supports monetary cooperation and temporary balance-of-payments financing; quotas shape voting and access. | India is a founding member. |
| **World Trade Organization** | Marrakesh Agreement signed in 1994; effective in 1995; institutional successor to GATT. | Permanent institution for trade rules, negotiations, review and dispute settlement. | India is a founding member and major developing-country voice. |
| **Organisation for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons** | Created when the Chemical Weapons Convention entered into force in 1997. | Verifies chemical-weapon prohibition and destruction through declarations and inspections. | India is an original State Party and destroyed its declared stockpile. |
| **Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty Organization Preparatory Commission** | Interim body established by CTBT signatories in 1996. | Builds the verification system pending treaty entry into force; not yet the final CTBTO. | India has not signed the CTBT. |

## 3. UN funds, programmes and functional bodies

| Organisation / body | Origin | Why created and UPSC importance | India relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **UN Development Programme** | Created in 1965 by merging two UN technical-assistance programmes; began in 1966. | Coordinates development, capacity, governance and SDG support; publishes Human Development Reports. | Long-standing India programme partner. |
| **UN Environment Programme** | Created after the Stockholm Conference by UNGA in 1972. | Coordinates global environmental assessment and norm-building; supports many treaty secretariats. | India engages through UNEA and environmental programmes. |
| **UN Children's Fund** | Emergency fund created in 1946; made permanent and renamed in 1953, retaining UNICEF. | Shifted from post-war relief to child survival, nutrition, education and protection. | Active in India since 1949. |
| **UN Population Fund** | Began in 1967 and became operational in 1969; renamed in 1987, retaining UNFPA. | Supports population data, reproductive health and voluntary family planning. | Works on maternal health, demography, ageing and gender. |
| **UN High Commissioner for Refugees** | Office created in 1949; operations began in 1951. | Protects refugees and addresses asylum and statelessness; originally temporary but became permanent. | India is outside the 1951 Convention but permits some UNHCR operations. |
| **World Food Programme** | Established experimentally in 1961; operations began in 1963; made continuing in 1965. | Delivers emergency food assistance and humanitarian logistics; distinct from FAO and IFAD. | Supports food-security knowledge and South-South cooperation with India. |
| **UN Women** | Created in 2010 and operational in 2011 by consolidating four UN gender entities. | Unified UN leadership on gender equality and women's empowerment. | Partners with Indian institutions and civil society. |
| **UN Human Settlements Programme** | Habitat centre created in 1977; transformed into UN-Habitat in 2002. | Coordinates housing and sustainable urbanisation; anchors the New Urban Agenda. | Relevant to housing, municipal finance and rapid urbanisation. |
| **UN Office on Drugs and Crime** | Formed in 1997 by merging drug-control and crime-prevention units; renamed UNODC in 2002. | Integrates work on drugs, organised crime, corruption, trafficking and criminal justice. | Works with India through its South Asia office. |
| **UN Conference on Trade and Development** | First conference and permanent UN organ established in 1964. | Gives development-centred treatment to trade, finance, investment and debt concerns. | India was a founding participant. |
| **Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights** | Created after the Vienna World Conference by UNGA in 1993. | Provides central leadership, monitoring and technical support for UN human-rights work. | India engages while emphasising sovereignty and non-selectivity. |
| **UN Human Rights Council** | Created in 2006 to replace the Commission on Human Rights. | Conducts UPR, special procedures and inquiries through 47 elected States. | India has served multiple terms and undergoes UPR. |
| **UN Commission on the Status of Women** | Established in 1946 as an ECOSOC functional commission. | Develops global standards on women's rights and follows the Beijing Platform. | India participates in its negotiations and reviews. |
| **UN Statistical Commission** | Established by ECOSOC in 1946. | Apex intergovernmental body for statistical concepts, classifications, SNA and SDG indicators. | India participates through MoSPI. |
| **UN Committee of Experts on Global Geospatial Information Management** | Established by ECOSOC in 2011. | Coordinates government geospatial expertise, standards and reference frameworks. | India participates through mapping and geospatial institutions. |
| **UN Office for Outer Space Affairs** | Expert unit began in 1958; became a division in 1968 and UNOOSA in 1992. | Services UN space-law bodies, registry functions and peaceful-use capacity-building. | India registers space objects and participates in its work. |
| **Committee on the Peaceful Uses of Outer Space** | Ad hoc committee created in 1958; made permanent in 1959. | Developed the five UN space treaties and promotes peaceful space cooperation. | India has been a member since 1959. |

## 4. Global economic, financial and development institutions

| Organisation / body | Origin | Why created and UPSC importance | India relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Asian Development Bank** | Constitutive agreement effective and operations opened in 1966. | Finances development and regional cooperation in Asia-Pacific. | India is a founding member and major borrower/shareholder. |
| **Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank** | Articles effective in 2015; operations began in 2016 after a 2013 proposal. | Addresses Asia's infrastructure-financing gap; important in new multilateralism. | India is a founding member and second-largest shareholder. |
| **New Development Bank** | Agreement signed at the 2014 BRICS Fortaleza Summit; operational in 2015. | Mobilises infrastructure and sustainable-development finance for emerging economies. | India is a founding shareholder; NDB is not BRICS's secretariat. |
| **African Development Bank** | Agreement signed in 1963, effective in 1964; operations began in 1966. | Regional development finance for Africa. | India became a non-regional member in 1983. |
| **European Bank for Reconstruction and Development** | Agreement signed in 1990; operations began in 1991 after communist collapse. | Supports democratic market transition and private enterprise. | India is a shareholder, not a country of operations. |
| **Bank for International Settlements** | Established in 1930 under Hague arrangements, initially for German reparations. | Oldest international financial institution; facilitates central-bank cooperation and hosts standard setters. | RBI is a member/shareholder. |
| **Financial Stability Board** | Created by G20 leaders in 2009 as successor to the 1999 Financial Stability Forum. | Coordinates financial regulation and monitors systemic vulnerabilities. | India is represented by Finance Ministry, RBI and SEBI. |
| **Basel Committee on Banking Supervision** | Formed by G10 central-bank governors in 1974 after international bank failures. | Develops global bank-prudential standards; norms require domestic implementation. | RBI is a member. |
| **Financial Action Task Force** | Established by the 1989 Paris G7 Summit. | Sets AML, counter-terror-financing and proliferation-financing standards and conducts evaluations. | India has been a full member since 2010. |
| **Asia/Pacific Group on Money Laundering** | Voluntary FATF-style regional body founded in Bangkok in 1997. | Implements FATF standards through regional evaluations and assistance. | India is a member of both APG and FATF. |
| **Egmont Group of Financial Intelligence Units** | Informal FIU network began at Egmont Palace in 1995. | Enables secure financial-intelligence exchange; not a police body. | FIU-India joined in 2007. |
| **Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development** | Convention effective in 1961; succeeded the Marshall Plan-era OEEC. | Coordinates economic policy, taxation, standards and development evidence. | India is a Key Partner, not a member. |
| **G24** | Established in 1971 as a G77 chapter for IMF-World Bank issues. | Coordinates developing-country positions on monetary and development finance. | India is a member. |
| **Paris Club** | Informal creditor forum originated in Argentina's 1956 Paris debt negotiations. | Coordinates official bilateral sovereign-debt treatment; distinct from the London Club. | India participates only as an ad hoc creditor, not permanent member. |
| **International Organization of Securities Commissions** | Became a global organisation in 1983 from a 1974 inter-American association. | Sets securities-regulation standards and enables regulator cooperation. | SEBI is a member. |
| **International Association of Insurance Supervisors** | Voluntary association founded in 1994. | Develops consistent insurance-supervision and financial-stability standards. | IRDAI is a member. |
| **International Organisation of Pension Supervisors** | Established in 2004 from an OECD-supported regulators' network. | Promotes pension-supervision standards and cooperation. | PFRDA participates. |

## 5. Global political forums and reform coalitions

| Organisation / body | Origin | Why created and UPSC importance | India relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **G20** | Finance-ministers' forum established in 1999 after financial crises; leaders' forum from 2008. | Includes systemically important advanced and emerging economies in global economic coordination. | India is a founding member and held the 2023 presidency. |
| **G7** | G6 leaders first met in 1975; Canada's 1976 entry made it G7. | Coordinated industrial democracies after monetary and oil shocks; informal with no permanent secretariat. | India is not a member but attends selected outreach sessions. |
| **BRICS** | BRIC foreign ministers began political coordination in 2006; first summit in 2009; South Africa joined in 2011. | Amplifies emerging economies and presses for global-governance reform; informal and presidency-led. | India is a founding BRIC participant and 2026 chair. |
| **BRICS partner-country category** | Created at the 2024 Kazan Summit; first partners took effect in 2025. | Provides structured participation below full membership. | India helped negotiate the category; partners lack full-member decision rights. |
| **IBSA Dialogue Forum** | Launched by the 2003 Brasilia Declaration. | Coordinates India, Brazil and South Africa as plural democracies and promotes South-South cooperation. | India is a founder; IBSA Fund works through UNOSSC. |
| **G4** | Coalition formed in 2004 by India, Brazil, Germany and Japan. | Pursues permanent UNSC seats and Council expansion; distinct from the agricultural trade G4. | India's principal permanent-seat coalition. |
| **L.69 Group** | Emerged around the 2007 UN draft resolution A/61/L.69. | Global South coalition seeking comprehensive UNSC reform and stronger developing-country representation. | India is a leading member. |
| **Uniting for Consensus** | Began as the 1995 Coffee Club; reorganised as UfC in the 2000s. | Opposes new national permanent UNSC seats and favours elected-seat expansion. | India is outside it; its model counters the G4 proposal. |
| **Non-Aligned Movement** | Founded at the 1961 Belgrade Summit, building on Bandung 1955. | Protected autonomy from Cold War blocs and supported anti-colonialism; no permanent secretariat. | India was a founding leader; relevant to strategic autonomy. |
| **Group of 77 and China** | Founded by 77 developing countries at UNCTAD I in 1964. | Coordinates developing-country economic interests in UN negotiations. | India is an original member; China associates with the group. |
| **Commonwealth of Nations** | Evolved from the 1931 Statute of Westminster; modern form shaped by the 1949 London Declaration. | Voluntary cooperation among independent States; republican India's continued membership transformed it. | India is a republic and major member. |
| **Alliance for Multilateralism** | Franco-German initiative announced and launched in 2019. | Flexible issue-based network defending rules-based multilateral cooperation. | India participates selectively; it is not a treaty member. |

## 6. South Asia and Bay of Bengal groupings

| Organisation / body | Origin | Why created and UPSC importance | India relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SAARC** | Charter signed at the first Dhaka Summit on 8 December 1985; Secretariat opened in 1987. | Promotes South Asian welfare, development and collective self-reliance; consensus and exclusion of bilateral disputes constrain it. | India is a founding member; India-Pakistan tensions have stalled summits. |
| **BIMSTEC** | Founded as BIST-EC by the 1997 Bangkok Declaration; renamed in 2004; Charter adopted in 2022 and effective in 2024. | Connects South and Southeast Asia through Bay of Bengal sectoral cooperation. | Founding member; central to Act East and Neighbourhood First. |
| **BBIN Initiative** | Informal subregional framework crystallised in the 2015 Motor Vehicles Agreement. | Advances transport, trade, energy and water cooperation below the SAARC level. | India drives implementation; Bhutan has not ratified the MVA. |
| **South Asia Subregional Economic Cooperation** | ADB-assisted programme established in 2001 from the 1996 South Asian Growth Quadrangle. | Develops transport, trade-facilitation and energy corridors; not a treaty organisation. | India is a founding participant and key transit market. |
| **Colombo Security Conclave** | Began as an India-Maldives-Sri Lanka dialogue in 2011; revived and named CSC in 2020; chartered in 2024. | Coordinates maritime, counter-terrorism, cyber and disaster security in the Indian Ocean. | India is a founder; Bangladesh became a full member in 2024. |
| **South Asian University** | Proposed at the 2005 SAARC Summit; agreement signed in 2007; teaching began in 2010. | Treaty-created regional centre for higher education and South Asian research. | India hosts and provides major support. |
| **SAARC Development Fund** | Predecessor fund began in 1996; SDF approved in 2005, chartered in 2008 and operational in 2010. | Finances regional social, economic and infrastructure projects. | India is its largest contributor. |

| Organisation / body | Origin | Why created and UPSC importance | India relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ASEAN** | Founded by the 1967 Bangkok Declaration. | Promotes Southeast Asian peace, stability and integration; consensus, non-interference and ASEAN centrality are core. | India is a Comprehensive Strategic Partner, not a member. |
| **East Asia Summit** | Inaugural leaders' summit held at Kuala Lumpur in 2005. | ASEAN-led forum for strategic, political and economic dialogue. | India is an original summit participant. |
| **ASEAN Regional Forum** | Inaugural ministerial meeting held in Bangkok in 1994. | Broad Asia-Pacific confidence-building and preventive-diplomacy forum; not an alliance. | India joined in 1996. |
| **ADMM-Plus** | Inaugural meeting held in Hanoi in 2010, extending the 2006 ADMM. | Enables practical defence cooperation between ASEAN and eight partners. | India is an original Plus participant. |
| **Mekong-Ganga Cooperation** | Launched through the 2000 Vientiane Declaration. | Links Mekong and Ganga regions through culture, education, tourism and connectivity. | India is a founder and only non-Mekong member. |
| **Quad** | Began with the 2004 tsunami Core Group; diplomatic meeting in 2007, revival in 2017 and leaders' format in 2021. | Coordinates Indo-Pacific maritime, technology, health and infrastructure cooperation; not a treaty alliance. | India is one of four participants. |
| **AUKUS** | Announced by Australia, UK and US in 2021. | Delivers nuclear-powered submarines and advanced defence capabilities through two pillars. | India is not a member but tracks its Indo-Pacific impact. |
| **Five Eyes** | Evolved from the 1946 UKUSA signals-intelligence agreement; expanded to five by 1956. | Institutionalises high-level intelligence sharing; not a conventional public treaty organisation. | India is not a member. |
| **I2U2** | Foreign-ministers' format began in 2021; first leaders' summit in 2022. | Mobilises joint investment in food, energy, water, transport and technology. | India is a founding participant. |
| **Indo-Pacific Economic Framework for Prosperity** | Launched by 14 partners in Tokyo in 2022. | Modular cooperation on trade, supply chains, clean economy and fair economy without a normal market-access FTA. | India joined three pillars, not the trade pillar. |
| **Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership** | Negotiations launched in 2012; agreement signed in 2020 and effective from 2022. | Consolidates ASEAN-centred trade arrangements with common regional rules. | India withdrew in 2019 and is not a party. |
| **Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership** | Signed in 2018 after the US left the 2016 TPP; effective in 2018. | Preserves high-standard Pacific trade and investment rules. | India is not a member. |
| **Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation** | Inaugural ministerial meeting held in Canberra in 1989. | Voluntary cooperation among "member economies" for open trade and investment. | India is not a member. |
| **Asia-Europe Meeting** | Inaugural Bangkok summit held in 1996. | Informal interregional political, economic and cultural dialogue. | India joined in 2006 and first participated in 2008. |
| **Conference on Interaction and Confidence Building Measures in Asia** | Proposed at UNGA in 1992; institutional milestones came in 1999 and 2002. | Pan-Asian confidence-building and cooperative-security forum. | India is a member. |
| **Asia Cooperation Dialogue** | Launched at Cha-Am, Thailand, in 2002. | Connects Asian subregions through a continent-wide informal dialogue. | India is a founding member. |

| **Shanghai Cooperation Organisation** | Shanghai Five began in 1996; SCO founded in 2001 and chartered in 2002. | Eurasian security and cooperation forum focused on the "three evils" and RATS. | India became a full member in 2017. |

| **Collective Security Treaty Organization** | Based on the 1992 Tashkent Treaty; organisation constituted by 2002 instruments. | Russia-led collective-defence alliance; distinct from SCO and EAEU. | India is not a member. |
| **Gulf Cooperation Council** | Created by the 1981 GCC Charter. | Coordinates six Gulf Arab monarchies on economic, political and security matters. | India is an external strategic partner with major energy and diaspora interests. |
| **Organization of Islamic Cooperation** | Founded at the 1969 Rabat Summit after the Al-Aqsa arson; charter followed in 1972. | Promotes solidarity and cooperation among Islamic States. | India is not a member; Kashmir statements are relevant. |

| **African Union** | OAU founded in 1963; AU arose through the 1999 Sirte Declaration, 2000 Act and 2002 launch. | Replaced an anti-colonial organisation with deeper integration and peace-security institutions. | India is an external partner and backed AU's permanent G20 entry. |
| **African Continental Free Trade Area** | AU process launched in 2012; agreement signed in 2018, effective in 2019; trading began in 2021. | Builds a continental single market and raises intra-African trade. | India is not a party but has major trade and investment interests. |
| **India-Africa Forum Summit** | Inaugural summit held in New Delhi in 2008. | Structures India-Africa political, development and capacity-building partnership; it is a summit process. | India is co-creator and development partner. |
| **Forum for India-Pacific Islands Cooperation** | Launched at the first India-Pacific Islands summit in Fiji in 2014. | India-led cooperation on development, climate, health and capacity. | India convenes the forum with 14 Pacific Island countries. |

## 9. Europe and trans-Atlantic institutions

| Organisation / body | Origin | Why created and UPSC importance | India relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **European Union** | Evolved from ECSC 1951 and EEC 1957; formally created by the Maastricht Treaty effective in 1993. | Deep supranational integration designed to secure peace and a common economic-political order. | India is a strategic partner, not a member. |
| **Council of Europe** | Statute signed in London in 1949. | Protects human rights, democracy and rule of law; separate from the EU. | India is not a member. |
| **European Court of Human Rights** | Convention adopted in 1950; Court established in 1959. | Judicially enforces the European Convention against Council of Europe States; not an EU court. | It has no jurisdiction over India. |
| **North Atlantic Treaty Organization** | Created by the 1949 Washington Treaty in the early Cold War. | Collective defence linking North America and Europe through Article 5. | India is neither a member nor formal NATO partner. |
| **Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe** | CSCE process began in 1973; Helsinki Final Act 1975; renamed OSCE from 1995. | Comprehensive political-military, economic and human-security cooperation. | India is not a participating State. |
| **European Free Trade Association** | Created by the 1960 Stockholm Convention by States outside the EEC. | Provides free trade without EU-style supranational integration. | India is not a member; TEPA does not confer membership. |
| **Nordic Council** | Inter-parliamentary body created in 1952; later cooperation anchored by the Helsinki Treaty. | Coordinates Nordic legislation, mobility and welfare; distinct from Nordic Council of Ministers. | India-Nordic summits are separate from membership. |

| **Leadership Group for Industry Transition** | India and Sweden launched it in 2019; LeadIT 2.0 followed in 2023. | Public-private platform for decarbonising hard-to-abate industries. | India is co-founder. |
| **Global Environment Facility** | Began as a World Bank-UNDP-UNEP pilot in 1991; restructured independently in 1994. | Grants finance for global environmental benefits and serves several conventions. | India receives grants and participates in governance. |
| **Green Climate Fund** | Established under UNFCCC at COP16 in 2010; governing instrument approved in 2011. | Finances mitigation and adaptation in developing countries. | Indian projects access it through accredited entities. |
| **Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change** | Established jointly by WMO and UNEP in 1988. | Assesses published climate science for policymakers; it does not conduct original research. | India nominates experts and uses findings in policy and negotiations. |
| **Intergovernmental Science-Policy Platform on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services** | Established by governments at Panama City in 2012. | Strengthens the biodiversity science-policy interface; often compared with IPCC. | India is a member. |

## 11. Security, policing and export-control regimes

| Organisation / body | Origin | Why created and UPSC importance | India relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **INTERPOL** | Founded in 1923 as the International Criminal Police Commission; reconstituted in 1946 and renamed under its 1956 Constitution. | Enables international police notices, databases and coordination; has no arrest force. | India joined in 1949; CBI is its National Central Bureau. |
| **Nuclear Suppliers Group** | Supplier consultations began after India's 1974 nuclear test; guidelines published in 1978. | Voluntary regime restricting nuclear exports that could aid proliferation. | India is not a member but received the 2008 waiver. |
| **Missile Technology Control Regime** | Established by the G7 in 1987. | Voluntary controls on missiles, UAVs and WMD-delivery technology. | India joined in 2016. |
| **Wassenaar Arrangement** | Established in 1996 as successor to COCOM after 1995 negotiations. | Promotes responsible transfers of conventional arms and dual-use items. | India joined in 2017. |
| **Australia Group** | Formed in 1985 after Iraq's chemical-weapons use exposed precursor-control gaps. | Harmonises chemical and biological weapon-related export controls. | India joined in 2018. |
| **Hague Code of Conduct against Ballistic Missile Proliferation** | Adopted at The Hague in 2002. | Voluntary declarations and launch notifications; it does not prohibit missiles. | India subscribed in 2016. |
| **Proliferation Security Initiative** | Announced in 2003; interdiction principles adopted that year. | Voluntary network for lawful interdiction of WMD-related trafficking. | India joined in 2016. |
| **Global Initiative to Combat Nuclear Terrorism** | US-Russia initiative launched at the 2006 G8 Summit. | Partnership for prevention, detection and response to nuclear terrorism. | India is a partner. |
| **Conference on Disarmament** | Recognised by the 1978 UN disarmament session; renamed CD in 1984. | UN-linked, autonomous multilateral disarmament negotiating forum; consensus causes deadlock. | India is one of 65 members. |

## 12. Oceans, polar regions, transport and science cooperation

| Organisation / body | Origin | Why created and UPSC importance | India relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **International Seabed Authority** | Created by UNCLOS 1982 and the 1994 Part XI Agreement; came into existence in 1994. | Regulates seabed activities in "the Area" under common heritage and environmental principles. | India is a member and sponsors exploration contracts. |
| **International Hydrographic Organization** | Bureau began in 1921; 1967 Convention created IHO, effective in 1970. | Standardises hydrographic surveys and nautical charts for safe navigation. | India participates through the Naval Hydrographic Department. |
| **Arctic Council** | Created by the non-binding 1996 Ottawa Declaration. | Forum of Arctic States and Indigenous participants for environment and development; excludes military security. | India is an observer. |
| **Antarctic Treaty Consultative Meeting** | Decision-making meetings arise under the 1959 Antarctic Treaty, effective in 1961; secretariat began in 2004. | Allows Consultative Parties to govern peaceful use, science and environment; not a separate organisation. | India has been a Consultative Party since 1983. |
| **Commission for the Conservation of Antarctic Marine Living Resources** | Created by the 1980 Canberra Convention, effective in 1982. | Applies ecosystem-based conservation to Southern Ocean living resources and fisheries. | India joined in 1985. |
| **International Whaling Commission** | Created by the 1946 Whaling Convention, effective in 1948. | Conserves whale stocks and regulates whaling; responsible for the commercial-whaling moratorium. | India is a member and supports conservation. |
| **International Union for Conservation of Nature** | Founded in 1948 as IUPN; renamed IUCN in 1956. | Hybrid government-civil-society conservation union; produces the Red List and is not a UN agency. | India is a State member; Indian institutions also participate. |
| **International Science Council** | Formed in 2018 by merging natural- and social-science councils. | Provides a unified global scientific voice and coordinates international research. | Indian academies and organisations participate. |
| **International Bureau of Weights and Measures** | Created by the 1875 Metre Convention. | Treaty-based core of global metrology and SI measurement comparability. | India participates through its national metrology system. |

## 14. Use rule

For Prelims, combine **origin + legal nature + membership + headquarters**. For Mains, use origin
only when it explains present design:

```text
FOUNDING PROBLEM -> INSTITUTIONAL DESIGN -> PRESENT IMPORTANCE -> INDIA'S INTEREST -> LIMITATION
```

Example: SAARC's consensus rule and exclusion of bilateral disputes were intended to enable
cooperation in a conflict-prone region, but they also help explain its present paralysis.
