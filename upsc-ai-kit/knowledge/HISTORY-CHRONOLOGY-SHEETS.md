# History Chronology Sheets

> **Purpose:** one canonical cross-period chronology for Prelims ordering, match-the-pair
> elimination and GS-I introductions. Detailed causes, debates, consequences and historiography
> remain in the Ancient, Medieval, Modern, World History and Art & Culture owner files.
>
> **Dating rule:** ancient dates are often approximate and source-dependent. `c.` means
> circa; BCE/CE is used throughout. A reign date is not automatically the date of every policy
> associated with that ruler.

## How to revise

1. Learn the four macro ladders first.
2. Then revise the thematic sheets: colonial Acts, associations, reform, resistance,
   Congress sessions, Gandhian phases and transfer of power.
3. For every event remember: **date -> actor -> place -> immediate result -> one trap**.
4. Bold entries are canonical owners. Backticked labels in comparison tables are references.

## 1. Ancient India: period and polity ladder

| Period/event | Approximate date | High-yield marker | Chronology trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lower Palaeolithic in India** | c. 600,000-150,000 BCE in the repository's R.S. Sharma chronology | Hand-axes and cleavers | Archaeological chronologies may be revised by new discoveries |
| **Middle Palaeolithic in India** | c. 150,000-35,000 BCE in the older textbook scheme | Flake and prepared-core tools | Attirampakkam research indicates a much earlier regional transition |
| **Upper Palaeolithic in India** | c. 35,000-10,000 BCE | Blades, burins and symbolic activity | Do not merge with Mesolithic microlith industries |
| **Mesolithic in India** | c. 9000-4000 BCE | Microliths, hunting-gathering and early domestication | It overlaps regionally with early farming cultures |
| **Mehrgarh Neolithic beginnings** | from c. 7000 BCE | Early farming and herding in the northwest | It predates the Mature Harappan cities |
| **Mature Harappan phase** | c. 2600-1900 BCE | Urban planning, script, craft specialisation and long-distance trade | Not contemporaneous with the standard Rig Vedic date range |
| **Late Harappan regionalisation** | after c. 1900 BCE | Deurbanisation and regional cultures | Decline was not one instantaneous invasion event |
| **Rig Vedic phase** | c. 1500-1000 BCE | Pastoral-tribal society in Sapta-Sindhu | Iron and territorial kingdoms belong mainly to the later phase |
| **Later Vedic phase** | c. 1000-600 BCE | Agriculture, iron, janapadas and more stratified varna order | Painted Grey Ware is an archaeological association, not proof of one ethnicity |
| **Second urbanisation** | from c. 600 BCE | Towns, punch-marked coins, NBPW and heterodox traditions | It is distinct from Harappan first urbanisation |
| **Mahajanapada age** | c. 600-321 BCE | Sixteen major states and Magadha's rise | Gana-sanghas coexisted with monarchies |
| **Mahavira's teaching career** | 6th century BCE | Twenty-fourth Tirthankara in Jain tradition | Mahavira did not found the entire Jain tradition ex nihilo |
| **Buddha's teaching career** | 6th-5th centuries BCE | Four Noble Truths, Sangha and middle path | Exact life dates vary among scholarly chronologies |
| **Achaemenid penetration of northwest India** | c. 516 BCE under Darius I | Northwest linked to Persian imperial system | Precedes Alexander by nearly two centuries |
| **Alexander's invasion** | 326 BCE | Battle of Hydaspes against Porus | Alexander did not conquer the Gangetic heartland |
| **Nanda ascendancy** | 4th century BCE | Powerful Magadhan state before Mauryas | Nandas precede Chandragupta Maurya |
| **Mauryan Empire** | c. 324-187 BCE | First virtually subcontinental empire | Chandragupta, Bindusara and Ashoka are sequential |
| **Ashoka's Kalinga war and Dhamma turn** | c. 3rd century BCE; war in ninth regnal year | Edicts and moral-political Dhamma | Dhamma was not identical to sectarian Buddhism |
| **Shunga dynasty** | c. 187-75 BCE | Post-Mauryan north Indian rule | “Brahmanical reaction” is debated, not a settled one-line fact |
| **Indo-Greek phase** | c. 2nd-1st centuries BCE | Menander, bilingual coinage and northwest interaction | Indo-Greeks precede the mature Kushana phase |
| **Shaka-Western Kshatrapa phase** | c. 1st century BCE-4th century CE | Rudradaman and Junagadh inscription | Founder of the Shaka era beginning 78 CE is debated |
| **Satavahana phase** | c. 1st century BCE-mid-3rd century CE | Deccan state, Prakrit and trade | Broadly overlaps Indo-Greek, Shaka and Kushana phases |
| **Kushana Empire** | c. 1st-3rd centuries CE | Silk Routes, Kanishka, Gandhara and Mathura art | Kanishka's accession is now often placed c. 127 CE, not automatically 78 CE |
| **Sangam age** | c. 300 BCE-300 CE | Chera-Chola-Pandya polities and Tamil literature | “Three Sangams” belongs to literary tradition, not secure event chronology |
| **Gupta era begins** | 319-320 CE | Imperial Gupta chronology and Sanskrit court culture | Gupta era date is not the birth date of the dynasty's founder |
| **Samudragupta's expansion** | 4th century CE | Allahabad pillar prasasti and differentiated conquest | Southern rulers were often reinstated after submission |
| **Chandragupta II's reign** | late 4th-early 5th century CE | Western expansion, trade and cultural patronage | “Golden age” requires social qualification |
| **Huna pressure and Gupta decline** | c. 5th-6th centuries CE | Toramana and Mihirakula | Huna invasions were one cause among several |
| **Harsha's reign** | 606-647 CE | Kannauj-centred hegemony, Bana and Xuanzang | Harsha did not recreate a Mauryan-scale pan-Indian empire |
| **Pallava-Chalukya rivalry** | c. 6th-8th centuries CE | Deccan-south warfare and temple architecture | Vatapi and Kanchi changed hands in different campaigns |
| **Tripartite struggle** | c. 8th-10th centuries CE | Palas, Gurjara-Pratiharas and Rashtrakutas contest Kannauj | It was not a single battle |
| **Imperial Chola phase** | c. 850-1279 CE | Kaveri state, temples, local institutions and maritime reach | Rajaraja I precedes Rajendra I |
| **Rajaraja I's reign** | 985-1014 CE | Brihadisvara temple and imperial expansion | Rajendra's Ganga and Southeast Asian campaigns came later |
| **Rajendra I's reign** | 1012/14-1044 CE | Ganga expedition and Srivijaya campaign | Maritime raid did not create permanent Chola rule over Southeast Asia |

## 2. Medieval India: dynasties, battles and cultural markers

| Event/phase | Date | High-yield marker | Chronology trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mahmud of Ghazni's raids** | c. 1000-1027 | Repeated raids including Somnath, 1025 | Raids did not establish the Delhi Sultanate |
| **First Battle of Tarain** | 1191 | Prithviraj Chauhan defeats Muhammad Ghuri | First Tarain is the Rajput victory |
| **Second Battle of Tarain** | 1192 | Ghuri defeats Prithviraj | The reversal occurs one year later |
| **Battle of Chandawar** | 1194 | Ghuri defeats Jayachandra of Gahadavala kingdom | Follows Tarain |
| **Mamluk dynasty** | 1206-1290 | Aibak, Iltutmish, Raziya and Balban | Iltutmish, not Aibak, is usually called the effective consolidator |
| **Iltutmish's reign** | 1211-1236 | Consolidation, iqta and Delhi's legitimacy | Precedes Raziya and Balban |
| **Raziya's reign** | 1236-1240 | First and only woman Sultan of Delhi | “Sultana” can misleadingly imply merely a consort |
| **Balban's reign** | 1266-1287 | Kingship, court discipline and frontier defence | Belongs to Mamluk, not Khalji dynasty |
| **Khalji dynasty** | 1290-1320 | Expansion and stronger military-fiscal state | Comes before Tughlaqs |
| **Alauddin Khalji's reign** | 1296-1316 | Market regulation, Mongol defence and Deccan campaigns | Price controls were linked to army maintenance |
| **Tughlaq dynasty** | 1320-1414 | Ambitious experiments, expansion and later decentralisation | Not founded by Muhammad bin Tughlaq |
| **Muhammad bin Tughlaq's reign** | 1325-1351 | Token currency and Daulatabad project | Failure of implementation does not mean every objective was irrational |
| **Vijayanagara founded** | c. 1336 | Sangama founders and southern imperial polity | The polity continued after the 1565 sack |
| **Bahmani kingdom founded** | 1347 | Independent Deccan sultanate | Later divided into five Deccan sultanates |
| **Firuz Shah Tughlaq's reign** | 1351-1388 | Canals, public works and greater hereditary tendencies | Comes after Muhammad bin Tughlaq |
| **Timur's invasion** | 1398 | Sack of Delhi and Sultanate weakening | Timur did not establish a durable Indian dynasty |
| **Sayyid dynasty** | 1414-1451 | Weak post-Timur Sultanate phase | Precedes Lodis |
| **Lodi dynasty** | 1451-1526 | Afghan dynasty ending at Panipat | Ibrahim Lodi was defeated by Babur |
| **Guru Nanak's life** | 1469-1539 | Foundational Sikh teachings | Sikh militarisation belongs mainly to later Gurus |
| **Vasco da Gama reaches Calicut** | 1498 | Direct Portuguese sea route to India | Portuguese Estado da India developed afterward |
| **Krishnadeva Raya's reign** | 1509-1529 | Tuluva apex, warfare and cultural patronage | Precedes Talikota |
| **First Battle of Panipat** | 1526 | Babur defeats Ibrahim Lodi | Starts Mughal rule, unlike 1556 and 1761 battles |
| **Battle of Khanwa** | 1527 | Babur defeats Rana Sanga | Follows Panipat I |
| **Battle of Chanderi** | 1528 | Babur defeats Medini Rai | Do not confuse with Chandawar, 1194 |
| **Battle of Ghaghra** | 1529 | Babur defeats Afghan-Bengal coalition | Final major battle of Babur's Indian campaigns |
| **Humayun's first reign** | 1530-1540 | Mughal succession and Afghan challenge | Interrupted by Sur rule |
| **Battle of Chausa** | 1539 | Sher Khan defeats Humayun | Precedes Kanauj |
| **Battle of Kanauj-Bilgram** | 1540 | Sher Shah expels Humayun | Begins Sur interregnum |
| **Sher Shah's reign** | 1540-1545 | Revenue, roads, sarais and silver rupee | Short reign, large administrative legacy |
| **Humayun's restoration** | 1555-1556 | Mughal return | Lasts only until his death in 1556 |
| **Second Battle of Panipat** | 1556 | Akbar-Bairam Khan forces defeat Hemu | Akbar's reign had just begun |
| **Akbar's reign** | 1556-1605 | Expansion, mansabdari, sulh-i-kul and imperial integration | Din-i-Ilahi was not a mass religion |
| **Battle of Talikota** | 1565 | Deccan coalition defeats Vijayanagara forces | Capital was devastated, but dynasty survived elsewhere |
| **Battle of Haldighati** | 1576 | Mughal forces confront Maharana Pratap | Did not end all Mewar resistance |
| **Ibadat Khana established** | 1575 | Interreligious debate at Fatehpur Sikri | Precedes the 1582 Tauhid-i-Ilahi phase |
| **Tauhid-i-Ilahi phase** | 1582 | Akbar's select ethical-disciple order | Better treated as a limited court order, not a popular faith |
| **Jahangir's reign** | 1605-1627 | Nur Jahan, Mewar settlement and painting | Comes before Shah Jahan |
| **Shah Jahan's reign** | 1628-1658 | Monumental architecture and imperial campaigns | Deposed before death |
| **Aurangzeb's reign** | 1658-1707 | Maximum territorial extent and prolonged Deccan wars | Jizya reimposed in 1679 |
| **Treaty of Purandar** | 1665 | Shivaji and Jai Singh settlement | Precedes Shivaji's coronation |
| **Shivaji's coronation** | 1674 | Formal Maratha kingship | Not the beginning of all Maratha activity |
| **Jizya reimposed by Aurangzeb** | 1679 | Reversal of Akbar-era abolition | Do not date it to Aurangzeb's accession |
| **Death of Aurangzeb** | 1707 | Accelerates Mughal succession and regional fragmentation | Mughal dynasty continued formally |
| **Successor-state consolidation** | c. 1720s-1740s | Hyderabad, Bengal and Awadh autonomy | They often retained nominal Mughal legitimacy |
| **Third Battle of Panipat** | 1761 | Abdali defeats Maratha coalition | Maratha power recovered regionally afterward |

## 3. Company conquest and colonial-policy ladder

| Event/policy | Date | Immediate significance | Chronology trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Carnatic Wars** | 1746-1763 | Anglo-French contest in south India | End before the Company's final Mysore and Maratha conquests |
| **Battle of Plassey** | 1757 | Company political leverage in Bengal through conspiracy | Buxar, not Plassey, establishes wider military supremacy |
| **Battle of Buxar** | 1764 | Company defeats Mir Qasim, Shuja-ud-Daula and Shah Alam II | Follows Plassey by seven years |
| **Diwani grant** | 1765 | Company receives Bengal, Bihar and Orissa revenue rights | Begins Dual Government, not direct Crown rule |
| **Bengal Dual Government** | 1765-1772 | Company revenue power with Nawab's nominal administration | Ended by Warren Hastings |
| **First Anglo-Mysore War** | 1767-1769 | Mysore checks Company expansion | Hyder Ali, not Tipu, dominates this phase |
| **Regulating Act** | 1773 | Parliamentary regulation and Governor-General of Bengal | Not Governor-General of India |
| **Second Anglo-Mysore War** | 1780-1784 | Hyder Ali and Tipu against Company alliance | Ends with Treaty of Mangalore |
| **Pitt's India Act** | 1784 | Board of Control and dual control in Britain | Does not end Company rule |
| **Third Anglo-Mysore War** | 1790-1792 | Tipu cedes territory after Treaty of Seringapatam | Tipu survives until fourth war |
| **Permanent Settlement** | 1793 | Zamindari revenue settlement in Bengal Presidency core | State demand fixed, cultivator burden not fixed |
| **Subsidiary Alliance system** | from 1798 | Wellesley's dependent-alliance strategy | Different from Dalhousie's Doctrine of Lapse |
| **Fourth Anglo-Mysore War** | 1799 | Tipu Sultan killed; Mysore subordinated | Final Anglo-Mysore War |
| **Second Anglo-Maratha War** | 1803-1805 | Company gains major territorial influence | Triggered after Treaty of Bassein, 1802 |
| **Vellore Mutiny** | 1806 | Early sepoy revolt in south India | Precedes 1857 by half a century |
| **Charter Act of 1813** | 1813 | Ends most Company trade monopoly and provides education grant | Tea and China trade exception remained |
| **Anglo-Nepal War** | 1814-1816 | Treaty of Sugauli and frontier settlement | Gurkha recruitment expands later |
| **Third Anglo-Maratha War** | 1817-1818 | Peshwa defeated and Company paramountcy consolidated | Final major Maratha war |
| **Ryotwari expansion** | early 19th century | Direct revenue settlement with cultivators in much of Madras/Bombay | Not a permanent zamindari settlement |
| **Mahalwari settlement** | from 1822/1833 | Village-estate revenue responsibility in northwestern regions | Distinct from individual ryot settlement |
| **Sati prohibition** | 1829 | Bentinck's regulation outlaws sati in Bengal Presidency | Social practice did not vanish immediately |
| **Charter Act of 1833** | 1833 | Governor-General of India and end of Company commercial role | First Governor-General of India was Bentinck |
| **Macaulay Minute** | 1835 | English higher-education policy turn | Wood's Despatch comes in 1854 |
| **First Anglo-Afghan War** | 1839-1842 | Failed forward-policy intervention | Not the 1878 war |
| **First Anglo-Sikh War** | 1845-1846 | Company influence over Punjab expands | Sikh kingdom survives under restrictions |
| **Second Anglo-Sikh War** | 1848-1849 | Punjab annexed | Occurs under Dalhousie |
| **Doctrine of Lapse** | 1848-1856 | Dalhousie's annexation policy toward adopted succession | Awadh was annexed on alleged misgovernment |
| **Wood's Despatch** | 1854 | Education departments, universities framework and graded system | Later than Macaulay Minute |
| **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act** | 1856 | Legalises widow remarriage | Vidyasagar's campaign did not ensure widespread social acceptance |
| **Annexation of Awadh** | 1856 | Annexed on alleged misgovernment | Not under Doctrine of Lapse |
| **Revolt of 1857** | 1857-1858 | Sepoy revolt plus broad regional civil uprisings | Uneven geography; not an all-India uniform movement |
| **Government of India Act and Queen's Proclamation** | 1858 | Crown rule replaces Company rule | Governor-General also becomes Viceroy |

## 4. Constitutional Acts and representation sequence

| Act/reform | Year | What changed | Must-not-confuse |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Indian Councils Act 1861** | 1861 | Legislative councils revived and Indians could be nominated | No responsible government |
| **Indian Councils Act 1892** | 1892 | Indirect electoral principle and budget discussion expanded | No separate electorates |
| **Morley-Minto Reforms** | 1909 | Enlarged councils and separate Muslim electorates | Dyarchy came in 1919 |
| **Montagu Declaration** | 1917 | Progressive realisation of responsible government stated | A policy declaration, not the 1919 Act |
| **Government of India Act 1919** | 1919 | Provincial dyarchy and expanded legislatures | Provincial autonomy came in 1935 |
| **Simon Commission appointed** | 1927 | All-British statutory commission | Arrived in India in 1928 |
| **Nehru Report** | 1928 | Dominion-status constitutional proposal | Jawaharlal's Lahore resolution came in 1929 |
| **Communal Award** | 1932 | Expanded communal representation, including separate electorate proposal for Depressed Classes | Modified for Depressed Classes by Poona Pact |
| **Poona Pact** | 1932 | Reserved seats in joint electorates for Depressed Classes | Not separate electorates for them |
| **Government of India Act 1935** | 1935 | Provincial autonomy and proposed federation | Federal part never fully operated |
| **August Offer** | 1940 | Expanded executive council and post-war constitution promise | Precedes Cripps Mission |
| **Cripps proposals** | 1942 | Post-war dominion status and constituent-making offer | Provinces could opt out; proposal rejected |
| **Wavell Plan** | 1945 | Proposed reconstituted Executive Council | Failed at Simla Conference |
| **Cabinet Mission Plan** | 1946 | Union, provincial grouping and Constituent Assembly | Rejected a sovereign Pakistan at that stage |
| **Mountbatten Plan** | 1947 | Accepted partition and accelerated transfer | Announced 3 June 1947 |
| **Indian Independence Act** | 1947 | Creates two Dominions and ends British paramountcy | Passed after the 3 June Plan |
| **Constitution adopted** | 26 November 1949 | Constituent Assembly adopts Constitution | Commencement mainly 26 January 1950 |
| **Republic inaugurated** | 26 January 1950 | Constitution in force and India becomes republic | Independence was 15 August 1947 |
