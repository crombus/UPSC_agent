### 1. Communication as a complete system

```text
SENDER / PURPOSE
      |
   ENCODE message
      |
choose CHANNEL -----> NOISE / BARRIER
      |                    |
   RECEIVER <--------- CONTEXT
      |
   DECODE meaning
      |
   FEEDBACK confirms, corrects or exposes misunderstanding
```

Communication succeeds only when the receiver understands the intended meaning sufficiently for
the purpose. Sending information is not proof that communication occurred.

| Element | Question to ask |
|---|---|
| **Sender** | Who initiates the communication and with what authority? |
| **Purpose** | Inform, consult, persuade, instruct, warn, negotiate or record? |
| **Message** | Is it accurate, relevant, complete and understandable? |
| **Channel** | Face-to-face, telephone, written note, email, public notice, form or digital platform? |
| **Receiver** | What language, knowledge, interest, vulnerability or accessibility need matters? |
| **Feedback** | How will understanding or compliance be confirmed? |
| **Context** | What institutional, cultural, emotional or time constraint affects meaning? |
| **Noise** | What physical, semantic, psychological, organisational or technological barrier interferes? |

> **Core distinction:** Information can be transmitted one-way. Effective communication normally
> requires interpretation and feedback.

### 2. Forms and channels

| Form | Strength | Limitation / trap |
|---|---|---|
| **Oral** | Fast clarification and immediate feedback | Memory and record may be weak |
| **Written** | Precision, consistency and durable record | Slower feedback; wording can be misread |
| **Non-verbal** | Conveys attention, confidence and emotion | Culture- and context-sensitive; cannot override clear words automatically |
| **Visual** | Simplifies patterns, procedures and warnings | Bad scales, labels or symbols can distort |
| **Digital** | Speed, reach and searchability | Access, privacy, overload, impersonation and context loss |
| **Formal** | Authority, accountability and traceability | Can become rigid or jargon-heavy |
| **Informal** | Speed and relationship-building | Rumour, exclusion and lack of accountability |

Choose the channel by **purpose + urgency + complexity + sensitivity + need for record + receiver
accessibility**, not by habit.

#### Direct-PYQ channel distinctions

- **Formal / informal** concerns authority, traceability and relationship context.
- **Firm** communication gives a clear instruction, boundary or decision; **open-ended**
  communication invites response or exploration. Neither pair is automatically better: match the
  stated relationship and purpose.
- For the supplied 2026 Q73 matching item, use purpose rather than the physical medium:
  **memorandum -> directive**, **flyer -> non-targeted dissemination**, **letter -> confidential
  intimation**, and **minutes -> record of decisions**. This is the paper's context-specific mapping,
  not a claim that each tool can never serve another lawful purpose.

### 3. Listening and feedback

```text
HEAR -> ATTEND -> CLARIFY -> PARAPHRASE -> RESPOND -> CONFIRM
```

- **Active listening:** give attention, avoid premature judgement and identify both facts and
  concerns.
- **Clarifying question:** asks for missing or ambiguous information without putting words into the
  speaker's mouth.
- **Paraphrasing:** restates the understood meaning so the speaker can correct it.
- **Open question:** invites explanation; useful for exploration.
- **Closed question:** obtains a specific fact or confirmation.
- **Feedback:** should be timely, specific, behaviour-focused and actionable.

> **Trap:** Listening is not passive agreement. A listener may understand accurately and still
> disagree lawfully and respectfully.

### 4. Barriers and the correct remedy

| Barrier | Example | Better remedy |
|---|---|---|
| **Physical** | Noise, distance, poor acoustics | Change place/channel; repeat and confirm |
| **Semantic** | Jargon, ambiguous wording, translation failure | Plain language, examples, shared definitions |
| **Psychological** | Fear, anger, prejudice, defensiveness | Psychological safety, neutral tone, private clarification |
| **Organisational** | Conflicting orders, long hierarchy, information silos | Single authoritative instruction, role clarity, escalation path |
| **Cultural/social** | Different norms, status effects, exclusion | Respectful adaptation; avoid stereotypes; include affected voices |
| **Technological** | Poor connectivity, inaccessible format, platform failure | Alternative channel, accessible design, redundancy |
| **Information overload** | Too many messages or irrelevant detail | Prioritise, structure and summarise action points |
| **Selective perception** | Hearing only what confirms an existing belief | Ask for evidence; paraphrase; invite contrary information |

**2026 matching vocabulary:** in supplied Q75, the tested pairings are semantic ->
misunderstanding a word's meaning; cognitive -> lack of feedback; organisational -> information
overload; and affective -> fear of social stigma. Learn that item-specific taxonomy while retaining
the wider diagnostic table above; labels can vary between sources, but the fact pattern controls.

Diagnose the barrier before selecting the response. Public blame cannot fix an ambiguous instruction;
more technical detail cannot fix a language mismatch.

### 5. Interpersonal skills

| Skill | Exam-ready meaning |
|---|---|
| **Empathy** | Understand another person's perspective and constraints without surrendering law or objectivity |
| **Respect** | Preserve dignity and avoid discrimination or humiliation |
| **Assertiveness** | State facts, needs and limits clearly without aggression or passivity |
| **Emotional regulation** | Prevent anger, fear or ego from controlling the response |
| **Cooperation** | Coordinate roles and information toward a legitimate shared objective |
| **Trustworthiness** | Keep commitments, protect justified confidentiality and correct errors |
| **Adaptability** | Adjust language/channel while preserving the substance |
| **Persuasion** | Use reasons, evidence and credible framing rather than coercion or deception |

#### Assertive, passive and aggressive responses

| Style | Pattern | Why it succeeds/fails |
|---|---|---|
| **Passive** | Avoids stating a legitimate concern | Can preserve short-term calm but leaves the problem unresolved |
| **Aggressive** | Threatens, humiliates or dominates | Damages dignity, trust and cooperation |
| **Assertive** | States the issue, evidence, boundary and next step respectfully | Protects both purpose and relationship |

### 6. Conflict resolution and negotiation

```text
PAUSE
  -> establish facts
  -> hear each side and identify interests
  -> separate person from problem
  -> identify lawful/non-negotiable constraints
  -> generate feasible options
  -> agree responsibility, timeline and review
  -> document when accountability requires it
```

- **Position** is what a person says they want; **interest** is why they want it.
- Prefer a fair process and problem-solving before coercive escalation.
- Escalate immediately when safety, serious illegality, abuse or evidence preservation requires it.
- Compromise is not automatically correct: unlawful, discriminatory or unsafe conduct cannot be
  legitimised by splitting the difference.

> **Trap:** "Talk privately first" is a useful default for ordinary misunderstanding, not a rule
> that overrides mandatory reporting, urgent protection or a serious conflict of interest.

### 7. Public-facing and administrative communication

| Situation | Better response architecture |
|---|---|
| Citizen does not understand a procedure | Use plain language, explain steps and documents, provide an accessible written reference, confirm understanding |
| Colleague receives conflicting instructions | Identify the controlling authority, issue one corrected instruction and remove obsolete versions |
| Stakeholders oppose a proposal | Hear interests, disclose constraints, explain evidence and record unresolved objections |
| Rumour or misinformation spreads | Verify facts, respond quickly through credible channels and correct without amplifying unnecessary detail |
| Sensitive personal matter | Use privacy, minimum necessary disclosure and an appropriate secure channel |
| Service failure | Acknowledge, explain known facts without speculation, provide remedy/timeline and update |

Good public communication combines **clarity, accessibility, accuracy, timeliness, dignity,
accountability and feedback**.

### 8. Questionnaires, interviews and consultation

| Defect | Why it is defective | Better design |
|---|---|---|
| **Leading** | Pushes the respondent toward an answer | Neutral wording |
| **Loaded** | Contains an accusation or disputed assumption | Separate the premise and ask factually |
| **Double-barrelled** | Asks two things in one item | One issue per question |
| **Ambiguous** | Terms/time period are unclear | Define scope and period |
| **Intrusive** | Seeks unnecessary sensitive information | Collect only what the purpose requires |
| **Unbalanced choices** | Omits plausible responses | Exhaustive, mutually intelligible options |
| **Social-desirability pressure** | Encourages the respectable rather than truthful answer | Privacy and neutral framing |

An interview adds the ability to probe and clarify but increases interviewer-bias risk. A
questionnaire improves standardisation and reach but may lack clarification.

**Objective-to-data rule:** start with the decision the survey must support, then retain only a
head that changes that decision. In supplied 2026 Q76, feasibility of a family-oriented resort is
directly informed by **family size** and **budget**; number of earners and dietary restrictions do
not answer that stated feasibility question. Do not select an intrusive or merely interesting datum.

### 9. CSAT scenario decision engine

When options describe possible responses, test them in this order:

1. **Facts:** does the option verify rather than assume?
2. **Legality and ethics:** does it reject deception, discrimination, retaliation and avoidable harm?
3. **Proportionality:** is it no more coercive than required?
4. **Communication quality:** does it clarify, listen and confirm?
5. **Feasibility:** can the actor actually do it within role and time?
6. **Accountability:** is documentation/escalation included where necessary?
7. **Follow-up:** does it prevent recurrence rather than merely hide the symptom?

The best option is not always the softest. It is the most **lawful, fair, clear, proportionate and
effective** response to the facts given.

#### Timed worked solution 1: Q73 tool-purpose matching (target: 40 seconds)

**Method:** write the unique purpose first. Minutes preserve a meeting's decisions, so `minutes ->
record decisions`. A flyer is mass/non-targeted dissemination, not confidential intimation. A
memorandum is the internal directive; the remaining letter is confidential intimation. The resulting
row is **option C** in the supplied provisional key.

**Why not shortcut by formality alone?** All four are written forms. The discriminating condition is
purpose and audience, so a generic “written = formal” rule cannot solve it.

#### Timed worked solution 2: Q76 questionnaire design (target: 35 seconds)

**Method:** underline the decision: *feasibility of a family-oriented resort*. Family size estimates
party composition and budget estimates willingness/ability to pay. Number of earners is an indirect
proxy and food allergies concern service design, not the stated feasibility choice. Select `1 and 2
only`, **option C** in the supplied provisional key.

**Verification:** each retained head changes the feasibility assessment directly; each rejected head
fails the stated-objective test. The error to log if missed is **classification/extraction**, not
lack of etiquette theory.

#### Fully reasoned core scenario: conflicting public-service instructions

A citizen with limited digital access receives an old office notice requiring a paper form and a new
portal message requiring online submission. A clerk proposes publicly blaming the citizen for not
following the portal; another proposes accepting both routes indefinitely without checking which is
controlling.

**Best response:** verify the controlling instruction, withdraw/correct the obsolete notice, provide
an accessible assisted route if required, explain the current steps privately and clearly, confirm
understanding, and record/escalate the correction.

**Why:** facts come before blame; accessibility prevents a technically correct but unusable remedy;
authority and a corrected record prevent contradictory instructions recurring. Public humiliation
fails dignity and communication quality. Indefinite dual processing preserves the confusion. This
uses the scenario engine in order: facts -> legality/equity -> proportionality -> clarity ->
accountability -> follow-up.

### 11. High-frequency traps

- Sending a message is not proof of understanding.
- Non-verbal cues are contextual, not universally decisive.
- Empathy does not mean accepting wrongdoing.
- Persuasion is not manipulation; deception and coercion are warning signs.
- Confidentiality is not absolute when law or immediate safety requires disclosure.
- Escalation can be necessary, but premature escalation can worsen an ordinary misunderstanding.
- A written channel creates a record but does not guarantee clarity.
- More information is not always better; relevance and structure matter.
- A technically correct message can still fail because it is inaccessible to the receiver.
- Consensus is not proof of correctness or legality.

### 1. Communication under complexity

Real administrative communication often contains several simultaneous failures:

```text
uncertain facts + unequal power + strong emotion + time pressure
             + multiple audiences + need for a record
```

Use a layered response:

1. stabilise immediate safety/service;
2. separate verified facts from allegations;
3. identify audiences and their information needs;
4. choose channel and spokesperson;
5. communicate what is known, unknown and being done;
6. create feedback and correction loops;
7. document decisions and update when facts change.

---

### 2. Stakeholder communication matrix

| Dimension | Questions |
|---|---|
| **Power** | Can the stakeholder authorise, block or materially alter the action? |
| **Interest** | How directly are they affected? |
| **Vulnerability** | Do they face language, disability, income, gender, geographic or digital barriers? |
| **Information need** | Decision detail, operational instruction, warning, consultation or reassurance? |
| **Channel** | What is accessible, credible, timely and recordable? |
| **Feedback** | How can objections, misunderstanding or implementation failure return to the decision-maker? |

High power does not erase the need to hear highly affected low-power groups.

---

### 3. Difficult-conversation architecture

```text
OBSERVATION (specific, verifiable)
     -> IMPACT (why it matters)
     -> PERSPECTIVE (invite explanation)
     -> REQUIREMENT / BOUNDARY
     -> AGREED ACTION + REVIEW
```

Avoid:

- attributing motive without evidence;
- global labels such as "always careless";
- public humiliation;
- false promises;
- threats outside lawful authority;
- debating while the other person is unable to engage safely.

---

### 4. Negotiation depth

| Concept | Application |
|---|---|
| **BATNA** | Best available alternative if no agreement is reached |
| **Reservation point** | Boundary beyond which agreement is worse than the alternative |
| **Zone of possible agreement** | Overlap, if any, between acceptable ranges |
| **Integrative negotiation** | Trades across differing interests to create mutual value |
| **Distributive negotiation** | Divides a fixed value; greater risk of positional conflict |
| **Objective criteria** | Law, evidence, precedent, cost or transparent standards |

The exam-safe response seeks interests and objective criteria but never bargains away mandatory law,
safety or equal treatment.

---

### 5. Mediation and de-escalation

A neutral facilitator:

1. sets respectful ground rules;
2. permits each account without interruption;
3. reframes accusations into issues/interests;
4. identifies common and disputed facts;
5. tests options for fairness and feasibility;
6. records agreement and review.

Mediation is unsuitable where urgent protection, severe power imbalance, coercion or a mandatory
legal process makes voluntary bargaining unsafe or improper.

---

### 6. Risk, crisis and misinformation communication

- communicate early enough to support protective action;
- distinguish confirmed fact, current assessment and uncertainty;
- give specific action instructions;
- use one credible source of updates;
- correct visibly and promptly;
- avoid both false reassurance and unsupported alarm;
- adapt format for language, disability and connectivity constraints.

Trust is preserved more by transparent correction than by pretending uncertainty never existed.

---

### 7. Behavioural and survey traps

| Bias/problem | Consequence | Countermeasure |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmation bias | Selective listening/evidence | Ask what would disprove the current view |
| Authority gradient | Juniors suppress risk information | Explicitly invite dissent and protect reporting |
| Groupthink | Premature consensus | Independent views before discussion |
| Framing effect | Different wording changes response | Neutral parallel wording |
| Acquiescence bias | Respondents agree by default | Balanced choices and reverse checks |
| Social desirability | Respondents conceal stigmatised views | Privacy and indirect/neutral questions |
| Non-response bias | Respondents differ from non-respondents | Follow-up and representativeness checks |

---

### 8. Advanced option elimination

Reject options that:

- confuse secrecy with justified confidentiality;
- promise certainty before facts are known;
- seek consensus before hearing affected parties;
- punish the messenger rather than test the information;
- use public disclosure when a narrower channel suffices;
- delay urgent protection in the name of consultation;
- treat documentation as a substitute for actual communication;
- provide a technically correct but inaccessible remedy.

When two options appear reasonable, prefer the one that combines **fact verification, appropriate
channel, proportional action, feedback and follow-up**.

---

### 9. Optional advanced scenario: dispute with safety implications

A contractor alleges that a junior engineer delayed a safety clearance for personal reasons. The
engineer says the inspection record is incomplete. A manager can either order an immediate public
apology, privately broker a compromise that permits work to resume, or pause the affected work,
secure the records, hear both accounts separately and use the authorised safety process.

**Best response:** pause the affected activity if the risk is material, preserve evidence, separate
verified facts from allegations, use the authorised process, communicate the interim status to those
who need it, and review after a recorded decision.

**Reasoning:** public apology attributes motive without proof; private compromise cannot bargain
away mandatory safety. The third route is proportionate because it protects people first while still
allowing fair hearing and feedback. This is an optional transfer exercise; Core Topic 07 already
contains the exam-essential scenario engine.

## CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

### Complete revision spine

- **Communication cycle:** Sender, encoding, message, channel, receiver, decoding and feedback.
- **Channel choice:** Match urgency, complexity, privacy, reach and record needs.
- **Verbal and non-verbal:** Align words, tone, posture and context.
- **Active listening:** Attend, clarify, paraphrase and confirm.
- **Feedback:** Make it timely, specific, behavioural and two-way.
- **Barriers:** Diagnose semantic, physical, psychological, cultural and organisational blocks.
- **Interpersonal styles:** Prefer assertive respect over passivity or aggression.
- **Conflict:** Separate positions from interests and facts from assumptions.
- **Negotiation:** Prepare interests, options, objective criteria and a lawful BATNA.
- **Public dealing:** Use accessible language, procedural fairness and documented follow-up.
- **Questionnaires:** Avoid leading, double-barrelled and ambiguous questions.
- **Scenario decision:** Choose the feasible response that reduces harm and preserves trust.
