## 1. Communication under complexity

Real administrative communication often contains several simultaneous failures:

```text
uncertain facts + unequal power + strong emotion + time pressure
             + multiple audiences + need for a record
```

Use a layered response:

1. stabilise immediate safety/service;
2. separate verified facts from allegations;
3. identify audiences and their information needs;
4. choose channel and spokesperson;
5. communicate what is known, unknown and being done;
6. create feedback and correction loops;
7. document decisions and update when facts change.

---

## 2. Stakeholder communication matrix

| Dimension | Questions |
|---|---|
| **Power** | Can the stakeholder authorise, block or materially alter the action? |
| **Interest** | How directly are they affected? |
| **Vulnerability** | Do they face language, disability, income, gender, geographic or digital barriers? |
| **Information need** | Decision detail, operational instruction, warning, consultation or reassurance? |
| **Channel** | What is accessible, credible, timely and recordable? |
| **Feedback** | How can objections, misunderstanding or implementation failure return to the decision-maker? |

High power does not erase the need to hear highly affected low-power groups.

---

## 3. Difficult-conversation architecture

```text
OBSERVATION (specific, verifiable)
     -> IMPACT (why it matters)
     -> PERSPECTIVE (invite explanation)
     -> REQUIREMENT / BOUNDARY
     -> AGREED ACTION + REVIEW
```

Avoid:

- attributing motive without evidence;
- global labels such as "always careless";
- public humiliation;
- false promises;
- threats outside lawful authority;
- debating while the other person is unable to engage safely.

---

## 4. Negotiation depth

| Concept | Application |
|---|---|
| **BATNA** | Best available alternative if no agreement is reached |
| **Reservation point** | Boundary beyond which agreement is worse than the alternative |
| **Zone of possible agreement** | Overlap, if any, between acceptable ranges |
| **Integrative negotiation** | Trades across differing interests to create mutual value |
| **Distributive negotiation** | Divides a fixed value; greater risk of positional conflict |
| **Objective criteria** | Law, evidence, precedent, cost or transparent standards |

The exam-safe response seeks interests and objective criteria but never bargains away mandatory law,
safety or equal treatment.

---

## 5. Mediation and de-escalation

A neutral facilitator:

1. sets respectful ground rules;
2. permits each account without interruption;
3. reframes accusations into issues/interests;
4. identifies common and disputed facts;
5. tests options for fairness and feasibility;
6. records agreement and review.

Mediation is unsuitable where urgent protection, severe power imbalance, coercion or a mandatory
legal process makes voluntary bargaining unsafe or improper.

---

## 6. Risk, crisis and misinformation communication

- communicate early enough to support protective action;
- distinguish confirmed fact, current assessment and uncertainty;
- give specific action instructions;
- use one credible source of updates;
- correct visibly and promptly;
- avoid both false reassurance and unsupported alarm;
- adapt format for language, disability and connectivity constraints.

Trust is preserved more by transparent correction than by pretending uncertainty never existed.

---

## 7. Behavioural and survey traps

| Bias/problem | Consequence | Countermeasure |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmation bias | Selective listening/evidence | Ask what would disprove the current view |
| Authority gradient | Juniors suppress risk information | Explicitly invite dissent and protect reporting |
| Groupthink | Premature consensus | Independent views before discussion |
| Framing effect | Different wording changes response | Neutral parallel wording |
| Acquiescence bias | Respondents agree by default | Balanced choices and reverse checks |
| Social desirability | Respondents conceal stigmatised views | Privacy and indirect/neutral questions |
| Non-response bias | Respondents differ from non-respondents | Follow-up and representativeness checks |

---

## 8. Advanced option elimination

Reject options that:

- confuse secrecy with justified confidentiality;
- promise certainty before facts are known;
- seek consensus before hearing affected parties;
- punish the messenger rather than test the information;
- use public disclosure when a narrower channel suffices;
- delay urgent protection in the name of consultation;
- treat documentation as a substitute for actual communication;
- provide a technically correct but inaccessible remedy.

When two options appear reasonable, prefer the one that combines **fact verification, appropriate
channel, proportional action, feedback and follow-up**.

## 9. Optional advanced scenario: dispute with safety implications

A contractor alleges that a junior engineer delayed a safety clearance for personal reasons. The
engineer says the inspection record is incomplete. A manager can either order an immediate public
apology, privately broker a compromise that permits work to resume, or pause the affected work,
secure the records, hear both accounts separately and use the authorised safety process.

**Best response:** pause the affected activity if the risk is material, preserve evidence, separate
verified facts from allegations, use the authorised process, communicate the interim status to those
who need it, and review after a recorded decision.

**Reasoning:** public apology attributes motive without proof; private compromise cannot bargain
away mandatory safety. The third route is proportionate because it protects people first while still
allowing fair hearing and feedback. This is an optional transfer exercise; Core Topic 07 already
contains the exam-essential scenario engine.
