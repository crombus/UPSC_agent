## 1. Mixed-mechanism problems

Hard GMA questions often combine:

```text
classification + representation + one quantitative/logical mechanism + verification
```

Examples:

- a seating condition followed by a counting question;
- a rate problem with a ratio change;
- a data table requiring weighted average and percentage-point distinction;
- a conditional chain followed by a sufficiency judgement.

Do not solve all visible mathematics. Identify the exact output the stem requires.

---

## 2. Method-selection hierarchy

Try methods in this order:

1. direct invariant/property;
2. option elimination;
3. smart values or scaling;
4. compact algebra/diagram;
5. full enumeration or long computation.

A shortcut is safe only when it preserves every condition and the option spacing makes the result
decisive.

---

## 3. Cognitive-load control

- chunk repeated information into a table or symbol;
- write intermediate results rather than rehearsing them mentally;
- standardise diagrams for relations, directions and rates;
- pause after the setup to check the question asked;
- separate solving from option marking to reduce transcription errors.

Working memory is limited; external notation is a performance tool.

---

## 4. Robustness under uncertainty

| State | Response |
|---|---|
| Method known, arithmetic long | Estimate or inspect options before calculating fully |
| Two options survive | Test the condition that distinguishes them |
| Stem appears inconsistent | Re-read qualifiers, units and shared directions |
| Diagram has branches | Seek a forced conclusion or counterexample rather than enumerate blindly |
| Confidence remains low | Park the item and protect time |

---

## 5. Advanced error analytics

Track errors by **mechanism and process stage**:

| Stage | Failure | Remediation |
|---|---|---|
| Classification | Chose wrong topic/method | Timed mixed-identification drills |
| Extraction | Missed condition or qualifier | Underline quantities and logical words |
| Representation | Wrong equation/grid/tree | Rebuild from the stem before calculating |
| Execution | Arithmetic or logical slip | Smaller steps and intermediate checks |
| Verification | Did not test result | Mandatory plug-back/magnitude habit |
| Decision | Over-attempted or abandoned too early | Confidence and time log |

Raw score alone cannot reveal which stage needs repair.

---

## 6. Mixed-session design

A useful advanced session contains:

- rapid classification without solving;
- one representative problem from each weak mechanism;
- a timed mixed block;
- error reconstruction without looking at the solution;
- a second attempt after a delay.

Advanced practice should increase transfer and decision quality, not merely increase difficulty.
