## 1. Communication as a complete system

```text
SENDER / PURPOSE
      |
   ENCODE message
      |
choose CHANNEL -----> NOISE / BARRIER
      |                    |
   RECEIVER <--------- CONTEXT
      |
   DECODE meaning
      |
   FEEDBACK confirms, corrects or exposes misunderstanding
```

Communication succeeds only when the receiver understands the intended meaning sufficiently for
the purpose. Sending information is not proof that communication occurred.

| Element | Question to ask |
|---|---|
| **Sender** | Who initiates the communication and with what authority? |
| **Purpose** | Inform, consult, persuade, instruct, warn, negotiate or record? |
| **Message** | Is it accurate, relevant, complete and understandable? |
| **Channel** | Face-to-face, telephone, written note, email, public notice, form or digital platform? |
| **Receiver** | What language, knowledge, interest, vulnerability or accessibility need matters? |
| **Feedback** | How will understanding or compliance be confirmed? |
| **Context** | What institutional, cultural, emotional or time constraint affects meaning? |
| **Noise** | What physical, semantic, psychological, organisational or technological barrier interferes? |

> **Core distinction:** Information can be transmitted one-way. Effective communication normally
> requires interpretation and feedback.

## 2. Forms and channels

| Form | Strength | Limitation / trap |
|---|---|---|
| **Oral** | Fast clarification and immediate feedback | Memory and record may be weak |
| **Written** | Precision, consistency and durable record | Slower feedback; wording can be misread |
| **Non-verbal** | Conveys attention, confidence and emotion | Culture- and context-sensitive; cannot override clear words automatically |
| **Visual** | Simplifies patterns, procedures and warnings | Bad scales, labels or symbols can distort |
| **Digital** | Speed, reach and searchability | Access, privacy, overload, impersonation and context loss |
| **Formal** | Authority, accountability and traceability | Can become rigid or jargon-heavy |
| **Informal** | Speed and relationship-building | Rumour, exclusion and lack of accountability |

Choose the channel by **purpose + urgency + complexity + sensitivity + need for record + receiver
accessibility**, not by habit.

## 3. Listening and feedback

```text
HEAR -> ATTEND -> CLARIFY -> PARAPHRASE -> RESPOND -> CONFIRM
```

- **Active listening:** give attention, avoid premature judgement and identify both facts and
  concerns.
- **Clarifying question:** asks for missing or ambiguous information without putting words into the
  speaker's mouth.
- **Paraphrasing:** restates the understood meaning so the speaker can correct it.
- **Open question:** invites explanation; useful for exploration.
- **Closed question:** obtains a specific fact or confirmation.
- **Feedback:** should be timely, specific, behaviour-focused and actionable.

> **Trap:** Listening is not passive agreement. A listener may understand accurately and still
> disagree lawfully and respectfully.

## 4. Barriers and the correct remedy

| Barrier | Example | Better remedy |
|---|---|---|
| **Physical** | Noise, distance, poor acoustics | Change place/channel; repeat and confirm |
| **Semantic** | Jargon, ambiguous wording, translation failure | Plain language, examples, shared definitions |
| **Psychological** | Fear, anger, prejudice, defensiveness | Psychological safety, neutral tone, private clarification |
| **Organisational** | Conflicting orders, long hierarchy, information silos | Single authoritative instruction, role clarity, escalation path |
| **Cultural/social** | Different norms, status effects, exclusion | Respectful adaptation; avoid stereotypes; include affected voices |
| **Technological** | Poor connectivity, inaccessible format, platform failure | Alternative channel, accessible design, redundancy |
| **Information overload** | Too many messages or irrelevant detail | Prioritise, structure and summarise action points |
| **Selective perception** | Hearing only what confirms an existing belief | Ask for evidence; paraphrase; invite contrary information |

Diagnose the barrier before selecting the response. Public blame cannot fix an ambiguous instruction;
more technical detail cannot fix a language mismatch.

## 5. Interpersonal skills

| Skill | Exam-ready meaning |
|---|---|
| **Empathy** | Understand another person's perspective and constraints without surrendering law or objectivity |
| **Respect** | Preserve dignity and avoid discrimination or humiliation |
| **Assertiveness** | State facts, needs and limits clearly without aggression or passivity |
| **Emotional regulation** | Prevent anger, fear or ego from controlling the response |
| **Cooperation** | Coordinate roles and information toward a legitimate shared objective |
| **Trustworthiness** | Keep commitments, protect justified confidentiality and correct errors |
| **Adaptability** | Adjust language/channel while preserving the substance |
| **Persuasion** | Use reasons, evidence and credible framing rather than coercion or deception |

### Assertive, passive and aggressive responses

| Style | Pattern | Why it succeeds/fails |
|---|---|---|
| **Passive** | Avoids stating a legitimate concern | Can preserve short-term calm but leaves the problem unresolved |
| **Aggressive** | Threatens, humiliates or dominates | Damages dignity, trust and cooperation |
| **Assertive** | States the issue, evidence, boundary and next step respectfully | Protects both purpose and relationship |

## 6. Conflict resolution and negotiation

```text
PAUSE
  -> establish facts
  -> hear each side and identify interests
  -> separate person from problem
  -> identify lawful/non-negotiable constraints
  -> generate feasible options
  -> agree responsibility, timeline and review
  -> document when accountability requires it
```

- **Position** is what a person says they want; **interest** is why they want it.
- Prefer a fair process and problem-solving before coercive escalation.
- Escalate immediately when safety, serious illegality, abuse or evidence preservation requires it.
- Compromise is not automatically correct: unlawful, discriminatory or unsafe conduct cannot be
  legitimised by splitting the difference.

> **Trap:** "Talk privately first" is a useful default for ordinary misunderstanding, not a rule
> that overrides mandatory reporting, urgent protection or a serious conflict of interest.

## 7. Public-facing and administrative communication

| Situation | Better response architecture |
|---|---|
| Citizen does not understand a procedure | Use plain language, explain steps and documents, provide an accessible written reference, confirm understanding |
| Colleague receives conflicting instructions | Identify the controlling authority, issue one corrected instruction and remove obsolete versions |
| Stakeholders oppose a proposal | Hear interests, disclose constraints, explain evidence and record unresolved objections |
| Rumour or misinformation spreads | Verify facts, respond quickly through credible channels and correct without amplifying unnecessary detail |
| Sensitive personal matter | Use privacy, minimum necessary disclosure and an appropriate secure channel |
| Service failure | Acknowledge, explain known facts without speculation, provide remedy/timeline and update |

Good public communication combines **clarity, accessibility, accuracy, timeliness, dignity,
accountability and feedback**.

## 8. Questionnaires, interviews and consultation

| Defect | Why it is defective | Better design |
|---|---|---|
| **Leading** | Pushes the respondent toward an answer | Neutral wording |
| **Loaded** | Contains an accusation or disputed assumption | Separate the premise and ask factually |
| **Double-barrelled** | Asks two things in one item | One issue per question |
| **Ambiguous** | Terms/time period are unclear | Define scope and period |
| **Intrusive** | Seeks unnecessary sensitive information | Collect only what the purpose requires |
| **Unbalanced choices** | Omits plausible responses | Exhaustive, mutually intelligible options |
| **Social-desirability pressure** | Encourages the respectable rather than truthful answer | Privacy and neutral framing |

An interview adds the ability to probe and clarify but increases interviewer-bias risk. A
questionnaire improves standardisation and reach but may lack clarification.

## 9. CSAT scenario decision engine

When options describe possible responses, test them in this order:

1. **Facts:** does the option verify rather than assume?
2. **Legality and ethics:** does it reject deception, discrimination, retaliation and avoidable harm?
3. **Proportionality:** is it no more coercive than required?
4. **Communication quality:** does it clarify, listen and confirm?
5. **Feasibility:** can the actor actually do it within role and time?
6. **Accountability:** is documentation/escalation included where necessary?
7. **Follow-up:** does it prevent recurrence rather than merely hide the symptom?

The best option is not always the softest. It is the most **lawful, fair, clear, proportionate and
effective** response to the facts given.

## 10. High-frequency traps

- Sending a message is not proof of understanding.
- Non-verbal cues are contextual, not universally decisive.
- Empathy does not mean accepting wrongdoing.
- Persuasion is not manipulation; deception and coercion are warning signs.
- Confidentiality is not absolute when law or immediate safety requires disclosure.
- Escalation can be necessary, but premature escalation can worsen an ordinary misunderstanding.
- A written channel creates a record but does not guarantee clarity.
- More information is not always better; relevance and structure matter.
- A technically correct message can still fail because it is inaccessible to the receiver.
- Consensus is not proof of correctness or legality.
