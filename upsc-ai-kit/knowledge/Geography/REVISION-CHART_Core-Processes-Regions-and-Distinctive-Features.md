> **Purpose:** One-place revision immediately before Prelims, GS-I or geography-linked GS-III questions.
> **Coverage:** Physical, climatological, oceanic, human, economic, regional, political and applied Geography, with systematic India application.
> **Two-pass design:** Core gives the complete process, classification, map and elimination foundation. Advanced adds India-specific depth, dated data, policy and richer analysis; no indispensable answer mechanism should exist only in Advanced.
> **Use rule:** Read processes first, then patterns, then India examples. Do not memorise isolated places without knowing the geographic mechanism that explains them.

## 1. The 60-second Geography system

```text
EARTH STRUCTURE + PLATE TECTONICS
                ->
        ROCKS + RELIEF + HAZARDS
                ->
 WEATHERING + RIVERS + ICE + WIND + WAVES
                ->
      LANDFORMS + SOILS + DRAINAGE
                |
INSOLATION -> PRESSURE -> WINDS -> MOISTURE -> RAINFALL
                ->
       CLIMATE + VEGETATION + WATER
                |
        OCEAN-ATMOSPHERE COUPLING
                ->
  MONSOON + CURRENTS + EXTREMES + REGIONAL CLIMATES
                |
                v
POPULATION -> MIGRATION -> SETTLEMENT -> ECONOMIC ACTIVITY
                ->
AGRICULTURE + RESOURCES + INDUSTRY + TRANSPORT + TRADE
                ->
 REGIONAL INEQUALITY + PLANNING + ENVIRONMENTAL STRESS
```

> **Memory line:** Structure creates relief; energy drives atmosphere and oceans; climate regulates water and vegetation; people organise production and settlement over this physical base; policy and technology modify constraints but do not erase geography.

## 2. Master diagnostic framework

| If the question shows... | First identify | Then ask | Typical conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fold mountains, trenches, volcanoes | Plate boundary and crust type | Convergent, divergent or transform? | Structure controls hazard and relief |
| Rock decay without transport | Weathering | Physical, chemical or biological? | Regolith forms in place |
| Downslope movement under gravity | Mass movement | Water saturation, slope and trigger? | Movement need not involve a transporting agent |
| Valley, meander or delta | Fluvial stage/process | Erosion, transport or deposition? | Gradient and discharge shape the landform |
| Cirque, arête or moraine | Glacial process | Erosional or depositional? | Ice erodes uplands and deposits till |
| Dune or mushroom rock | Aeolian process | Deflation/abrasion or deposition? | Aridity, sparse vegetation and wind matter |
| Sinkhole, cave or stalactite | Karst process | Soluble rock, joints and groundwater? | Solution and deposition operate underground |
| Cliff, spit or lagoon | Coastal process | Wave erosion, drift or sea-level change? | Sediment budget and wave energy matter |
| Warm/cold current pair | Ocean circulation | Wind, Coriolis, basin shape or density? | Currents redistribute heat and nutrients |
| Rainfall distribution | Lifting mechanism | Convectional, orographic or cyclonic? | Moisture alone is insufficient without uplift/cooling |
| Climate code | Köppen letter logic | Temperature group or dryness/seasonality? | Classification describes pattern, not cause |
| Crop/industry location | Physical and economic controls | Raw material, market, labour, energy, transport? | No single factor explains all locations |
| Dense/sparse settlement | Carrying conditions and access | Relief, water, jobs, disease, history, policy? | Avoid environmental determinism |
| Regional disparity | Unequal spatial accumulation | Initial advantage, connectivity, institutions? | Growth poles can spread or intensify inequality |

## 3. Earth, coordinates and time

| Concept | Core rule | UPSC trap |
|---|---|---|
| Rotation | West to east; approximately 24 hours | Causes day-night, not seasons |
| Revolution | Earth orbits Sun in approximately 365¼ days | Seasons require axial tilt plus revolution |
| Latitude | Angular distance north/south of Equator | Parallels differ in length |
| Longitude | Angular distance east/west of Prime Meridian | Meridians are equal-length semicircles |
| Local time | 1° longitude = 4 minutes; 15° = 1 hour | East is ahead, west is behind |
| International Date Line | Roughly follows 180°, with deviations | Crossing westward adds a day; eastward subtracts |
| Solstice | Maximum hemispheric tilt toward/away from Sun | Not caused by changing Earth-Sun distance |
| Equinox | Sun overhead at Equator; near-equal day and night | Does not mean identical climate worldwide |
| India location | Northern and Eastern hemispheres; central Indian Ocean position | Tropic of Cancer crosses India; Standard Meridian is 82°30′E |

## 4. Earth's interior, rocks and plate tectonics

| Topic | Classification / mechanism | Distinctive feature |
|---|---|---|
| Crust | Continental, thicker and less dense; oceanic, thinner and denser | Oceanic crust is generally younger |
| Mantle | Solid but capable of slow flow | Convection contributes to plate movement |
| Core | Liquid outer and solid inner core | Outer-core motion generates magnetic field |
| Igneous rocks | Intrusive or extrusive | Primary rocks formed from magma/lava |
| Sedimentary rocks | Mechanical, chemical or organic | Layering and fossils are common |
| Metamorphic rocks | Heat, pressure and fluids alter existing rocks | Marble from limestone; slate from shale |
| Divergent boundary | Plates move apart | Ridges, rifts and new crust |
| Convergent boundary | Plates move together | Trenches, fold mountains, volcanic arcs or collision mountains |
| Transform boundary | Plates slide past | Shallow earthquakes, little crust creation/destruction |
| Hotspot | Intraplate mantle upwelling | Volcanic chain may record plate movement |

```text
magma
  -> cooling -> igneous
  -> weathering/deposition/lithification -> sedimentary
  -> heat/pressure -> metamorphic
  -> melting -> magma
```

> **Trap:** A rock name indicates origin and transformation, not a fixed landscape. The same rock behaves differently under different climate, structure and slope conditions.

## 5. Volcano, earthquake and tsunami distinctions

| Phenomenon | Cause | Key terms | Safe inference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Volcano | Magma reaches surface through tectonic weakness/hotspot | Shield, composite, fissure, caldera | Most cluster near plate boundaries, not all |
| Earthquake | Sudden energy release along rupture/fault | Focus, epicentre, P and S waves | Damage depends on magnitude, depth, distance, geology and construction |
| Tsunami | Sudden displacement of large water volume | Submarine earthquake, landslide or volcano | Not every undersea earthquake generates one |

| Scale / wave | What it measures or does | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Magnitude | Energy released at source | Modern reporting commonly uses moment magnitude; not intensity |
| Intensity | Observed shaking/damage at a place | Varies spatially for one earthquake |
| P wave | Fast compressional wave through solids and liquids | Arrives first |
| S wave | Shear wave through solids only | Its absence through outer core supports liquid-core inference |
| Tsunami in deep ocean | High speed, long wavelength, low amplitude | May be barely noticeable offshore |
| Tsunami near coast | Slows, wavelength shortens and height rises | It is not a normal wind-generated tide |

> **India:** BIS seismic classification publicly supports Zones II-V. Do not invent a Zone VI without a verified official revision.

## 6. Weathering, mass movement and groundwater

| Process | Defining mechanism | Common settings / products |
|---|---|---|
| Physical weathering | Disintegration without chemical change | Freeze-thaw, thermal expansion, salt action |
| Chemical weathering | Mineral alteration/decomposition | Solution, carbonation, oxidation and hydrolysis |
| Biological weathering | Organisms physically or chemically alter rock | Roots, burrowing and organic acids |
| Mass movement | Gravity-driven downslope transfer | Falls, slides, slumps, flows and creep |
| Infiltration | Water enters soil | Depends on permeability, vegetation and saturation |
| Percolation | Water moves downward through soil/rock | Recharges aquifer |
| Aquifer | Stores and transmits usable groundwater | Unconfined or confined |
| Aquiclude/aquitard | Restricts groundwater movement | Storage and transmission differ |
| Water table | Upper surface of saturated zone | Rises/falls with recharge and withdrawal |
| Artesian condition | Confined water under pressure | Flow does not mean an unlimited supply |

> **Coastal aquifer chain:** excessive pumping -> freshwater head falls -> saline interface moves inland/upward -> wells become saline. Recharge, pumping regulation and barrier methods address different parts of the mechanism.

## 7. Geomorphic agents: master comparison

| Agent | Dominant erosion | Signature erosional landforms | Signature depositional landforms |
|---|---|---|---|
| Running water | Hydraulic action, abrasion, solution | V-shaped valley, gorge, waterfall and pothole | Floodplain, levee, alluvial fan and delta |
| Glacier | Plucking and abrasion | Cirque, arête, horn, U-shaped valley and hanging valley | Moraines, drumlins, eskers and outwash |
| Wind | Deflation and abrasion | Deflation hollow, yardang, zeugen and pedestal rock | Dunes and loess |
| Groundwater in limestone | Solution/carbonation | Sinkhole/doline, swallow hole, cave and limestone pavement | Stalactite, stalagmite and pillar |
| Sea waves/currents | Hydraulic action, abrasion and solution | Cliff, wave-cut platform, cave, arch and stack | Beach, spit, bar, tombolo and lagoon |

### Fluvial sequence

```text
steep gradient -> vertical erosion -> youthful valleys/waterfalls
lower gradient -> lateral erosion -> meanders/floodplains
loss of velocity -> deposition -> levees/deltas/alluvial fans
```

> **Trap:** An oxbow lake results from meander cut-off; a delta requires sediment deposition at a river mouth but does not form where waves/tides remove sediment faster than it arrives.

## 8. Landform look-alikes

| Pair | Correct distinction |
|---|---|
| Gorge vs U-shaped valley | River-cut narrow/deep valley versus glacier-widened trough |
| Alluvial fan vs delta | Fan at break of slope inland versus deposit at standing-water mouth |
| Moraine vs loess | Unsorted glacial till ridge versus wind-deposited silt |
| Stalactite vs stalagmite | Hangs from cave roof versus grows from floor |
| Spit vs bar | Spit remains attached at one end; bar may connect two sides/across bay |
| Lagoon vs lake | Lagoon commonly separated from sea by bar/spit; lake is a broader inland-water category |
| Atoll vs volcanic island | Ring reef enclosing lagoon versus island built by volcanic material |
| Weathering vs erosion | Breakdown in place versus removal/transport |
| Landslide vs soil creep | Rapid slope failure versus very slow downslope movement |
| Desertification vs desert | Land degradation process versus existing climatic region |

## 9. Lakes, coasts, islands and reefs

| Feature | Origin / condition | Examples or clue |
|---|---|---|
| Tectonic lake | Faulting or crustal movement | Deep elongated basins common |
| Glacial lake | Ice erosion or moraine damming | Cirque/tarn and moraine-dammed lakes |
| Volcanic lake | Crater/caldera occupation | Circular steep-sided basin |
| Oxbow lake | Meander cut-off | Crescent form on floodplain |
| Lagoon | Bar/spit isolates coastal water | Often shallow and brackish |
| Artificial lake | Dam/reservoir | Human-controlled storage |
| Emergent coast | Relative land uplift/sea-level fall | Raised beaches and marine terraces |
| Submergent coast | Relative sea-level rise/land subsidence | Rias and fjords |
| Fringing reef | Attached or close to shore | Narrow/absent lagoon |
| Barrier reef | Offshore parallel reef | Broad lagoon |
| Atoll | Ring reef around lagoon | Often linked to subsiding volcanic foundation |

### Coral-growth conditions

- Warm, shallow, clear, sunlit and generally saline tropical/subtropical water.
- Low sediment load and adequate circulation.
- Reef-building corals are animals living in symbiosis with photosynthetic algae.

> **Trap:** All tropical islands are not coral islands. Andaman-Nicobar includes tectonic/volcanic origins; Lakshadweep is coral.

## 10. Oceans: currents, tides and salinity

| Process | Main controls | High-yield effect |
|---|---|---|
| Surface currents | Prevailing winds, Coriolis and basin shape | Heat redistribution and coastal climate |
| Deep circulation | Temperature-salinity density differences | Global overturning and oxygen/nutrient transport |
| Upwelling | Divergence/offshore transport draws deep water upward | Nutrient-rich fisheries |
| Downwelling | Surface convergence/subduction | Oxygen transfer to depth |
| Spring tide | Sun-Earth-Moon aligned | Greatest tidal range |
| Neap tide | Sun and Moon at right angles | Smaller tidal range |
| High salinity | High evaporation, low rainfall/runoff and restricted exchange | Subtropical enclosed/arid seas |
| Low salinity | High rainfall, river inflow or ice melt | Equatorial/high-latitude/coastal dilution zones |

| Current pairing | Climatic/fishery clue |
|---|---|
| Warm western-boundary current | Warms and moistens nearby high-latitude western coasts |
| Cold eastern-boundary current | Stabilises air, promotes aridity/fog and coastal upwelling |
| Warm-cold current convergence | Fog and rich fishing, e.g. Newfoundland region |
| Indian Ocean currents | Seasonally reverse in northern basin with monsoon winds |

> **IOD:** A coupled Indian Ocean temperature-gradient pattern. Positive/negative phases influence regional rainfall probability but do not mechanically determine every monsoon outcome.

## 11. Atmosphere: the non-negotiable causal chain

```text
unequal insolation
      ->
temperature contrast
      ->
pressure gradient
      ->
wind modified by Coriolis and friction
      ->
moisture transport
      ->
uplift + adiabatic cooling
      ->
condensation/cloud
      ->
precipitation
```

| Concept | Core rule | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Insolation | Shortwave solar energy received | Temperature is an outcome, not insolation itself |
| Terrestrial radiation | Earth emits longwave radiation | Greenhouse gases mainly absorb outgoing longwave |
| Albedo | Fraction reflected | High albedo reduces absorbed energy |
| Pressure gradient | Drives air from high toward low pressure | Wind does not cross isobars unchanged because Coriolis/friction act |
| Coriolis | Right in Northern, left in Southern Hemisphere | Zero at Equator; does not initiate wind |
| Convectional rain | Heated air rises | Common in equatorial afternoons |
| Orographic rain | Moist air forced over relief | Windward wet; leeward rain shadow |
| Cyclonic/frontal rain | Air converges or warm air rises over cold | Common in temperate depressions |
| Temperature inversion | Temperature rises with height in a layer | Traps pollution and suppresses vertical mixing |
| Jet stream | Fast upper-tropospheric current | Influences weather systems; not a surface wind |
| Western disturbance | Extra-tropical system moving into South Asia | Important for winter rain/snow in north-west India/Himalaya |

## 14. India physical-geography application

| World process | India application | Essential distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Continental collision | Himalaya and ongoing seismicity | Young fold mountains remain tectonically active |
| Ancient shield geology | Peninsular plateau and mineral belts | Stable does not mean hazard-free |
| Monsoon drainage | Himalayan perennial and Peninsular mostly rain-fed rivers | Peninsular rivers are not universally seasonal |
| River deposition | Indo-Gangetic-Brahmaputra plains and deltas | Flood fertility and flood hazard coexist |
| Glaciation | Himalayan glaciers and high-altitude lakes | Snowline is not glacier snout |
| Aridity | Thar and rain-shadow interiors | Desertification may occur outside climatic desert |
| Karst | Limestone regions including Meghalaya and central/southern belts | Cave length/rank data are volatile |

| Coral/islands | Lakshadweep versus Andaman-Nicobar | Coral atolls versus tectonic-volcanic island arc |
| Ocean-atmosphere coupling | Monsoon, ENSO and IOD interaction | No index determines monsoon alone |
| Jet streams | Subtropical westerly and tropical easterly circulation | Seasonal shifts link to monsoon evolution |
| Western disturbances | Winter precipitation in north-west and western Himalaya | Extra-tropical, not monsoon systems |

## 15. Indian monsoon mechanism

```text
seasonal land-sea thermal contrast
          +
northward ITCZ shift + cross-equatorial flow
          +
Tibetan-Himalayan thermal/dynamic influence
          +
jet-stream changes + Mascarene High
          ->
Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal branches
          ->
relief-controlled rainfall distribution
          ->
breaks, variability and retreat
```

| Component | Role | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| ITCZ | Seasonal convergence shifts northward | Monsoon is not only land-sea breeze at continental scale |
| Cross-equatorial flow | South-east trades cross Equator and turn south-westerly | Coriolis modifies direction |
| Western Ghats | Orographic uplift | Windward wet, leeward rain shadow |
| Bay branch | Moves toward north-east and Ganga plain | Meghalaya maxima reflect relief orientation |
| Monsoon break | Rainfall weakens over much of core zone for a period | Does not mean all India is dry |
| Retreating monsoon | Seasonal withdrawal; north-east winds | Tamil Nadu receives important rain from Bay systems |
| ENSO/IOD | Ocean-atmosphere modulators | Relationships are probabilistic and interacting |

## 16. Population and demographic transition

| Stage | Birth rate | Death rate | Growth | Population structure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | High | High | Low/unstable | Young, low life expectancy |
| II | High | Falls rapidly | Very high | Youth bulge |
| III | Falls | Low | Slowing | Working-age share rises |
| IV | Low | Low | Low/stable | Ageing begins |
| V, where used | Very low | Low or exceeds births | Zero/negative | Advanced ageing and possible decline |

| Population pyramid | Shape | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Expansive | Broad base | High fertility and young population |
| Stationary | More column-like | Low birth/death rates and stable structure |
| Constrictive | Narrow base | Low fertility and ageing |

> **Trap:** Demographic dividend is a potential age structure, not an automatic economic benefit. Education, health, skills and employment determine its realisation.

## 17. Migration and settlement

| Concept | Core identity | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Push factor | Adverse condition at origin | Does not alone determine destination |
| Pull factor | Attraction at destination | Perception and networks mediate it |
| Intervening obstacle | Cost/barrier between origin and destination | Distance can be social/legal, not only physical |
| Step migration | Movement through hierarchy of places | Not necessarily permanent at each stage |
| Counter-stream | Return/reverse flow | Exists alongside dominant stream |
| Circular migration | Repeated movement without permanent relocation | Different from one-time permanent migration |
| Site | Actual ground occupied by settlement | Relief, water and defensibility |
| Situation | Relative location and connectivity | Route, market, hinterland and regional position |
| Nucleated settlement | Houses clustered | Often linked to water, defence or community organisation |
| Dispersed settlement | Houses separated | Common where farms/relief encourage spread |
| Urbanisation | Rising urban share and urban way of life | Not identical to city population growth alone |

| Model | Pattern |
|---|---|
| Concentric zone | Rings around central business district |
| Sector | Wedge-shaped growth along transport corridors |

## 18. Regional development

```text
initial advantage
   -> investment + infrastructure + skills + market access
   -> cumulative growth
   -> migration and agglomeration
   -> spread effects OR backwash effects
```

| Concept | Meaning | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Growth pole | Dynamic industry/node stimulates surrounding economy | Corridors and industrial clusters |
| Cumulative causation | Advantage reinforces itself | Explains persistent regional inequality |
| Spread effect | Benefits diffuse outward | Jobs, markets and infrastructure |
| Backwash effect | Core drains labour/capital from periphery | Uneven development |
| Planning region | Area with functional coherence for intervention | Need not match administrative border |
| Regional disparity | Unequal development outcomes | Distinct from cultural/geographic diversity |

> **Mains rule:** Compare physical endowment, historical investment, connectivity, human capital, institutions and policy implementation. Do not attribute disparity to geography alone.

## 19. Agricultural systems and crop diagnostics

| System | Labour/scale | Climate-location signature |
|---|---|---|
| Shifting cultivation | Small, temporary plots and long fallow | Humid tropical forest margins |
| Intensive wet rice | Small holdings, dense labour and water control | Monsoon Asian plains/deltas |
| Nomadic pastoralism | Mobile herding | Arid and semi-arid belts |
| Mixed farming | Crops plus livestock | Temperate Europe and North America |
| Plantation | Large estate, monoculture and export | Humid tropics with port/colonial linkage |
| Commercial grain | Large mechanised holdings | Temperate grasslands |
| Mediterranean farming | Horticulture, vines, olives and winter crops | Winter-rain western margins |
| Market gardening/dairy | High-value perishables | Peri-urban consumer belts |

| Crop | Broad geographic requirement | Elimination trap |
|---|---|---|
| Rice | Warm season and abundant water/irrigation | Not all rice is lowland flooded rice |
| Wheat | Cool growth and dry bright ripening | Not a humid tropical crop |
| Cotton | Long frost-free season; varied soils | Not confined to black soil |
| Jute | Hot-humid deltaic/alluvial belt and water for retting | Not a dry-plateau crop |
| Tea | Humid, acidic, well-drained slopes and labour | High rain does not mean waterlogging |
| Coffee | Shaded warm uplands and drainage | Not all humid tropics suit it |
| Millets | Lower/variable rainfall tolerance | "Coarse" does not mean nutritionally inferior |

### Von Thünen core

`Market -> perishables/dairy -> classical forest belt -> field crops -> extensive grazing`

> Distance, freight cost, perishability and land rent organise the model; refrigeration and transport modify but do not erase the logic.

## 20. Minerals, energy and industrial location

| Resource / industry | Geological or location logic | High-yield inference |
|---|---|---|
| Coal | Sedimentary basins | Heavy-industry and power linkage; grades differ |
| Petroleum/gas | Organic-rich sedimentary basins, reservoirs and traps | Offshore occurrence still requires basin geology |
| Metallic minerals | Often igneous/metamorphic belts or hydrothermal processes | Shield regions are important |
| Hydropower | Relief plus reliable discharge | Renewable but not impact-free |
| Solar | Insolation, land, grid and storage | High radiation alone is insufficient |
| Wind | Wind regime, terrain, grid and demand | Capacity factor matters |
| Raw-material-oriented industry | Bulk/weight loss or perishability | Sugar, pulp and some mineral processing |
| Market-oriented industry | Finished product demand or perishability | Consumer goods and urban services |
| Footloose industry | High value-to-weight, skills/connectivity | Still depends on labour, infrastructure and networks |

### Weber's least-cost model

```text
transport cost + labour cost + agglomeration economies
                         ->
                   industrial location
```

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Material index | Weight of localised inputs relative to final product |
| Agglomeration | Firms gain by clustering |
| Deglomeration | Congestion and high costs push activity outward |
| Industrial inertia | Industry remains after original location factor weakens |

## 21. Transport, trade and network geography

| Mode | Strength | Limitation / best use |
|---|---|---|
| Road | Flexible door-to-door | Costly for very bulky long-distance freight |
| Rail | Bulk inland movement | Fixed routes and high capital cost |
| Inland waterway | Cheap heavy freight | Requires navigability and terminals |
| Sea | Lowest-cost intercontinental bulk transport | Port dependence and slower delivery |
| Air | Fast, high-value and urgent cargo | Expensive and low bulk suitability |
| Pipeline | Continuous liquids/gases | Product-specific and fixed |

| Network concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Node | Junction or activity centre |
| Link | Connection between nodes |
| Hinterland | Inland service/catchment area |
| Foreland | Overseas area linked through port |
| Chokepoint | Narrow route whose disruption has wider effects |
| Entrepôt | Trading/trans-shipment centre |
| Containerisation | Standardised transfer lowers time/cost |

> **Space geography:** Launch-site latitude, eastward launch, tracking, orbital choice and communication networks are geographic variables, not only technological facts.

## 23. Political geography and boundaries

| Term | Meaning | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Boundary | Legally defined line | Different from broad frontier zone |
| Frontier | Transitional borderland | May be poorly demarcated |
| Delimitation | Boundary described in treaty/map | Not physical marking |
| Demarcation | Boundary marked on ground | Follows delimitation |
| Antecedent boundary | Drawn before dense cultural landscape develops | Later settlement adapts to it |
| Subsequent boundary | Develops with existing cultural pattern | Not necessarily conflict-free |
| Superimposed boundary | Imposed without respecting local patterns | Common colonial legacy |
| Relic boundary | No longer functional but visible in landscape | Political function has ended |

| India line/status | Geographic-political identity |
|---|---|
| Radcliffe Line | Partition boundary with Pakistan/Bangladesh context |
| McMahon Line | Eastern-sector alignment associated with India-China boundary history |

> **Trap:** Boundary history, legal claim, demarcation and current control are separate layers. Do not collapse them into one statement.

## 24. Contemporary-issue answer engine

```text
LOCATION
  -> physical base
  -> resource / population pressure
  -> exposure + vulnerability
  -> spatial impact
  -> institutions and policy
  -> adaptation / mitigation / regional solution
```

| Issue | Geographic dimensions to cover |
|---|---|
| Urban flooding | Drainage basin, impervious surface, wetland loss, intensity and governance |
| Heatwave | Latitude, continentality, urban heat island, humidity, vulnerable groups and cooling access |
| Landslide | Lithology, slope, rainfall, seismicity, road cutting, drainage and land use |
| Groundwater decline | Aquifer type, recharge, cropping, energy incentives, extraction and food-security link |
| Coastal erosion | Waves, currents, sediment supply, structures, sea-level change and settlement exposure |
| Desertification | Climate variability, vegetation loss, grazing, irrigation salinity and soil erosion |
| Regional disparity | Resource base, historical investment, infrastructure, skills and governance |
| Seawater intrusion | Pumping-recharge imbalance, coastal geology, sea level and land use |

> **Rule:** A hazard becomes a disaster through exposure and vulnerability. A scheme name cannot substitute for process analysis.

## 25. Cultural and social geography

| Concept | Meaning | Correct use |
|---|---|---|
| Cultural trait | Individual element such as language, food or practice | Smallest analytical unit |
| Cultural complex | Interrelated traits | Explains patterned cultural life |
| Cultural region | Area sharing significant traits | Boundaries are often transitional |
| Formal region | Defined by measurable uniformity | Language or crop region |
| Functional region | Organised around node and flows | Commuter or market region |
| Perceptual region | Based on shared image/identity | Boundaries may be disputed |
| Cultural landscape | Visible imprint of human activity | Built form, fields and sacred spaces |
| Diffusion | Spread of traits | Relocation, contagious, hierarchical or stimulus |

> **Trap:** Cultural regions are not sealed or eternally homogeneous. Migration, networks, state policy and technology reshape them.

## 26. Optional Advanced enrichment: India-specific depth

| Topic | Core foundation | Optional Advanced correction |
|---|---|---|
| Earth science | Plate and rock-process foundation | Apply to India's cratons, sedimentary basins, Himalaya and source-era nomenclature carefully. |
| Seismicity | Boundary and fault processes | Hazard zoning is a planning tool, not a deterministic prediction of exact future damage. |
| Weathering and groundwater | Process and aquifer logic | Crystalline aquifers often depend on secondary fractures/weathered zones; avoid primary-porosity assumptions. |
| Drainage | River-process framework | Himalayan/Peninsular contrast needs exceptions, tectonic inheritance and basin-specific analysis. |
| Glaciers | Glacial landforms and mass balance | Separate glacier retreat, lake growth, GLOF trigger, downstream exposure and uncertainty. |
| Aridity | Climatic and geomorphic causes | Distinguish Thar desert ecology from wider land-degradation/desertification processes. |
| Coasts and islands | Erosion/deposition and island types | Use coastal cells, sediment budgets, CRZ and island-specific geology rather than one coast-wide rule. |
| Ocean-monsoon | Currents, ENSO and IOD | Treat teleconnections probabilistically and identify the season, index and region before claiming an effect. |
| Climate regions | World type and vegetation response | India's climatic boundaries are transitional and modified by monsoon, relief and continentality. |
| Population | DTM and pyramid | Census 2011 remains the completed all-India baseline; label surveys and projections by source/date. |
| Migration | Push-pull and networks | Gendered, seasonal, circular and distress migration require different indicators and policies. |
| Urbanisation | Settlement models | Indian urban form includes informality, peri-urban growth and governance fragmentation not captured fully by classical models. |
| Regional development | Growth-pole logic | Physical diversity is not itself disparity; distinguish endowment, access, capacity and outcomes. |
| Agriculture | Climate-location systems | India requires monsoon risk, irrigation, holdings, procurement, markets and agro-ecological sustainability. |
| Resources | Geology and location | Reserve/resource/production are different; use dated official figures and strategic-import dependence. |
| Industry | Weber/agglomeration | Corridors and PLI do not erase labour, logistics, land, power and regional-capacity constraints. |
| Transport | Network and chokepoint logic | Completion, announcement and operational capacity must be distinguished for corridors and ports. |
| Political geography | Boundary concepts | Legal line, claim line, actual control and lived borderland geography are separate layers. |
| Contemporary issues | Hazard-pressure framework | Add institution, finance, distributional impact and locally appropriate adaptation. |

> **Firewall rule:** Core alone must support correct Prelims elimination and a complete Mains causal answer. Advanced improves India specificity and nuance but remains optional.

## 27. Evidence-to-inference rules

| Evidence | Safe inference | Unsafe overreach |
|---|---|---|
| Contour map | Relative relief, slope and drainage clues | Rock type without geological evidence |
| Satellite image | Surface pattern/change for that sensor/date | Cause without field or time-series evidence |
| Climate normal | Long-period average for stated baseline | Weather for a particular day |
| Forecast | Modelled future condition | Observed outcome |
| Census | Counted demographic pattern for reference date | Current-year population without update |
| Projection | Scenario-based estimate | Completed Census result |
| Resource reserve | Economically/geologically assessed stock under stated criteria | Annual production |
| Installed power capacity | Maximum rated capacity | Actual generation or reliability |
| Administrative designation | Official category/boundary | Uniform ground reality |
| Correlation map | Spatial association | Proof of causation |
| Hazard zonation | Relative planning risk | Exact prediction of event date/magnitude |
| Köppen boundary | Threshold-based climate classification | Sharp ecological wall on ground |
