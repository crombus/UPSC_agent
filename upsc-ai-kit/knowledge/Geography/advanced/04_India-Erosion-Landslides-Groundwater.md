## 1. Snapshot & core idea

**Advanced — India's Soil Erosion, Landslides & Groundwater Stress.**

- ✅ Sheet, rill and gully erosion describe different runoff organisations. Rills can enlarge into
  gullies and ravines, but **sheet → rill → gully → ravine is not a compulsory sequence at every site**.
- ✅ Wind erosion moves particles by suspension, saltation and surface creep in dry, poorly protected
  terrain. Treat old national area/tonnage claims as historiography unless a current survey supplies
  method, year and units.
- ⚠️ Landslides are concentrated in the Himalaya, north-east and parts of the Western Ghats/Nilgiris,
  but the causal mixtures differ. The Himalaya combines active deformation, fractured rock, steep relief,
  old slide/glacial debris and intense rain; the Ghats commonly combine deeply weathered regolith,
  escarpment relief, extreme rain and slope modification.
- ⚠️ A trigger is not a complete cause. Terrain/material, discontinuities, antecedent moisture,
  drainage, toe removal and loading must be tested before attribution.
- ⚠️ **Subsidence is predominantly vertical ground deformation, not a downslope mass-movement class.**
  It may reflect compaction, groundwater withdrawal, dissolution, mining or several interacting causes.
- ⚠️ Joshimath is a multi-agency investigated case of ground displacement in complex old
  landslide/debris terrain. Do not reduce it to construction loading, one project or one trigger.
- ⚠️ India's groundwater problem is both quantitative and qualitative. Use current CGWB assessment
  terminology and NAQUIM aquifer characterisation; do not let a 1971 province scheme control modern
  hydrogeology.

## 2. Key classification / data

| Process / issue | Modern control | Indian application |
|---|---|---|
| Sheet erosion | Diffuse soil loss by overland flow; often difficult to observe directly | Cleared/cultivated slopes; requires field or remote-sensing evidence |
| Rill/gully/ravine erosion | Concentrated flow incises channels; enlargement is possible, not inevitable | Chambal-Yamuna badlands and Shiwalik *chos* as regional examples |
| Wind erosion | Suspension, saltation and surface creep | Arid north-west; depends on erodibility, cover, moisture and wind |
| Landslide susceptibility | Terrain propensity from lithology/material, structure, slope, drainage, geomorphology and land cover | GSI NLSM baseline at 1:50,000; finer studies for priority sectors |
| Landslide hazard | Susceptibility plus trigger probability/intensity in time | Rainfall-threshold/forecasting work is distinct from susceptibility mapping |
| Subsidence | Vertical ground lowering/compaction; mechanism must be diagnosed | Joshimath requires report-specific, multi-causal treatment |
| Groundwater occurrence | Primary pores plus weathered/fractured/conduit systems; aquifer geometry is local | CGWB NAQUIM 2.0, not a single national verbal province verdict |
| Groundwater stress | Extraction relative to annual extractable resource, with assessment year/method | CGWB GWRA-2025: national stage **60.63%**, not a district diagnosis |
| Groundwater quality | Salinity, arsenic, fluoride, nitrate and other contaminants are spatially specific | Use CGWB quality monitoring/report year; quantity status does not establish quality |
| Coastal intrusion | Reduced freshwater head permits saline interface movement/mixing | Pumping, recharge and density effects require local monitoring/model evidence |

> ⚠️ **Risk vocabulary:** susceptibility concerns terrain; hazard adds a time/intensity dimension;
> exposure identifies people/assets; vulnerability concerns damage propensity. Risk arises from their
> interaction, not from one universal literal equation.

## 4. Must-Know Facts

- ⚠️ **2021 GS-I Q4 is primarily Geography:** compare physical causes of landslides in the Himalaya
  and Western Ghats. Disaster Management supports mitigation/response; it does not own the causal core.
- 📰 GSI's NLSM provides a national **1:50,000 susceptibility** baseline. Its FS 2025-26 project page
  lists **1:10,000 meso-scale** work and **micro-scale mapping finer than 1:2,000**.
- 📰 NRSC/ISRO's *Landslide Atlas of India* reports about **80,000 mapped landslides (1998-2022)**,
  covering vulnerable regions in **17 states and 2 UTs**, and ranks **147 districts by exposure**.
- 📰 CGWB's *National Compilation on Dynamic Ground Water Resources of India, 2025* reports:
  recharge **448.52 BCM**, annual extractable resource **407.75 BCM**, extraction **247.22 BCM** and
  national stage of extraction **60.63%**.
- 📰 Of **6,762** GWRA-2025 assessment units: **73.14% Safe, 11.21% Semi-critical, 2.97% Critical,
  10.80% Over-exploited and 1.88% Saline**. These national shares do not diagnose one aquifer.
- 📰 CGWB **NAQUIM 2.0** is the current aquifer-mapping/management control, including hard-rock,
  basaltic and alluvial settings at user-oriented scales.
- 📰 Atal Bhujal Yojana used community water budgets and Gram-Panchayat Water Security Plans in
  selected water-stressed areas. Treat coverage/impact figures as dated programme results, not
  universal evidence that recharge occurred.
- 📰 **WDC-PMKSY 2.0** is the current central watershed framework relevant to soil/water conservation
  and rainfed/degraded-land treatment; use its current guidelines rather than Plan-era programme totals.

## 5. UPSC Traps

- ❌ Every eroding surface passes through sheet → rill → gully → ravine.
  → These are process/forms that can be related, not a compulsory national ladder.
- ❌ Any old four-zone landslide map proves an area is “safe”.
  → Susceptibility is scale-, inventory- and method-dependent; no Indian region is guaranteed landslide-free.
- ❌ Subsidence is simply a slow landslide.
  → Subsidence is predominantly vertical deformation; establish its mechanism independently.
- ❌ Rainfall “caused” a landslide because failure followed rain.
  → Rain may trigger; test material, structure, antecedent saturation, drainage and human modification.
- ❌ Deccan basalt is uniformly impermeable and groundwater exists only above it.
  → Flow-unit architecture, weathering, fractures, vesicular/interflow zones, dykes and sedimentary
  interbeds create strong local variability.
- ❌ A saturated aquifer cannot be recharged.
  → Surface infiltration and recharge wells can add water where hydraulic capacity and water quality permit.
- ❌ One recharge-pit/trench specification works nationally.
  → MAR design must follow aquifer, catchment yield, clogging risk, source-water quality and monitoring.
- ❌ Saline and sodic soils have the same remedy.
  → Salinity needs drainage/leaching; suitable sodic soils may require a calcium amendment such as gypsum.
