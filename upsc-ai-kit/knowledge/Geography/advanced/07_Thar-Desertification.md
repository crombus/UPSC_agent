## 1. Snapshot & core idea


**Advanced — The Thar: a transboundary, polygenetic and time-variable dryland.**

✅ Khullar places the arid/semi-arid Thar chiefly west of the Aravalli in Rajasthan, extending into
adjoining Punjab, Haryana, Gujarat and Pakistan. Older regional descriptions link it with the Rann of
Kachchh, but the Rann is a **distinct seasonally inundated saline mudflat/marsh system**, not another
field of Thar dunes. Thar sediment cannot be assigned to one source: local rock breakdown, older
fluvial and palaeochannel deposits, Indus/Himalayan sediment and repeated aeolian recycling contribute
in different sectors and periods.
- ✅ The Thar lies west of the Aravalli, between the Indus and the Aravalli, spilling into Punjab, Haryana and northern Gujarat.
- ✅ Book estimates of desert area vary with the boundary used; retain them as source-era figures, not
  as a single current official area. Rainfall grades from semi-arid margins to an arid western core.
- ⚠️ Treat the historical “Rann as an extension of the Thar” description as regional geography,
  not controlling geomorphology. Jaisalmer and Barmer show strong aeolian dune topography.
- ✅ Economy (Majid): bajra, pulses, fodder; livestock is central; scope for date-palm, water-melon and rain-water harvesting.
- ✅ It is described as the Western Dry Region — an arid tract (Marwar/Mewar margins) with extreme temperature range.

**CA Anchor — direct SAC-ISRO condition mapping and current restoration reporting.**

✅ The direct 2021 SAC Atlas maps **2018-19 condition**, not a 2021 or 2026 measurement:
- ✅ **97.85 million hectares (29.77%)** of India's total geographical area was under
  desertification/land degradation; **83.69 million hectares** was desertification within drylands.
- ✅ National **wind erosion was 17.94 million hectares (5.46%)** of total geographical area.
  Water erosion (**11.01%**) and vegetation degradation (**9.15%**) were larger national classes.
- ✅ Rajasthan recorded **21.237665 million hectares, or 62.06% of state area**, under
  desertification/land degradation; **wind erosion covered 43.37% of state area**. The latter
  denominator is the state's total geographical area, not “43.37% of Rajasthan's degraded land.”
- ⚠️ Total national mapped DLD increased from **94.53 million hectares (28.76%)** in 2003-05 to
  **97.85 million hectares (29.77%)** in 2018-19, while national wind erosion declined from
  **5.58% to 5.46%**. Rajasthan's total mapped DLD declined from **63.19% to 62.06%** over those
  endpoints. Do not compress these divergent process trends into one “rising desertification” rate.

## 5. UPSC Traps

> 🔑 Trap: Do not confuse similar-looking landforms, processes, scales or Indian locations; UPSC often tests the exact mechanism and setting.


**Advanced — The Thar (Great Indian Desert): Extent & Aridity (Khullar / Majid).**
- ❌ The Thar lies east of the Aravalli. → The Thar is WEST of the Aravalli range.
- ❌ The Rann is either unrelated to the Thar or simply another Thar dune field. → It is regionally
  linked but geomorphically a saline mudflat/marsh with marine and terrestrial influences.
- ❌ The Thar receives moderate rainfall (~75 cm). → It receives < 50 cm (Khullar); the arid core is < 25 cm (Majid).
- ❌ Thar sand has one local-rock origin or the desert formed at one date. → Provenance and
  luminescence evidence show multiple sources and recurrent phases of dune accumulation/reworking.

**Ca Anchor — ISRO-SAC Desertification & Land Degradation Atlas.**
- ❌ Desertification means only the spread of sand dunes. → Under UNCCD Article 1 it is land
  degradation in arid, semi-arid and dry sub-humid areas. Reversibility, cost and recovery time are
  site- and pathway-specific.

## 6. 📰 Current link


**Advanced — The Thar (Great Indian Desert): Extent & Aridity (Khullar / Majid).**
📰 The Thar's current significance lies in the contrast between mobile/stabilised dunes, canal-irrigated
tracts, renewable-energy expansion and land degradation. Quote area and rainfall only with the source boundary/year.

**Ca Anchor — ISRO-SAC Desertification & Land Degradation Atlas.**
📰 The SAC 2021 Atlas supplies the latest national **condition map** used here. Its separate
**Land Degradation Vulnerability Assessment** atlas, dated November 2024, develops vulnerability
methods and demonstrations for selected districts; it is not a replacement national status estimate.

📰 **UNCCD COP17** met in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia, **17-28 August 2026**, under the theme
**“Restoring Land. Restoring Hope.”** India's 17 June and 24 August 2026 releases report
**21.76 million hectares brought under restoration during 2011-2020** and about **1.22 billion
person-days** generated. Keep the reporting period attached.

📰 India launched its first guide to **Grasslands and Other Open Natural Ecosystems** at COP17.
Restoration in the Thar and Aravalli landscape must therefore protect native grassland, scrub,
savanna and pastoral systems; tree planting is not a universal proxy for ecological restoration.

## 8. Dated Thar origin, dune and provenance evidence

- ✅ Zeeden et al. (2020) document extensive **Kashmir Valley loess-palaeosol sequences**:
  Indian aeolian archives are not confined to Thar sand or upper-Ganga *bhur*.
- ✅ Nitundil et al. (2023) use portable luminescence calibrated against laboratory ages for rapid
  age assessment of western-Thar dune accumulation. The related EGU 2024 synthesis describes a
  dominance of Holocene dune dynamics in the preserved record, vegetated linear dunes and modern
  dune modification by irrigated agriculture.
- ✅ Usman et al. (2024) find that transboundary Thar sands record spatially varying Himalayan,
  Indus/Karakorum-Kohistan, palaeochannel and recycled deltaic contributions, with stronger sediment
  supply during parts of the early Holocene.
- ⚠️ These studies support recurrent construction, stabilisation and reworking. They do not establish
  one origin date, one source or a single natural-versus-human percentage for the entire Thar.

## 9. Current Aravalli judicial and restoration sequence

- ✅ **20 November 2025:** the Supreme Court accepted a committee's mining-context definition based
  on landforms rising at least 100 metres above local relief and ranges formed by qualifying hills
  within 500 metres.
- ✅ **29 December 2025:** the Supreme Court kept the committee recommendations and its
  20 November findings/directions **in abeyance** pending holistic review. It also directed that,
  until further orders, no new or renewed mining permission in the Aravalli Hills and Ranges as
  defined in the Forest Survey of India report of 25 August 2010 be granted without prior Court
  permission.
- ✅ **25 May 2026:** the Court continued the expert-review process through a High-Powered Committee
  and required consideration of ecological and stakeholder evidence. Therefore the 100-metre/
  500-metre definition is not a settled current mapping rule.
- 📰 The 2025 Detailed Action Plan and later official reporting treat the Aravalli Green Wall as
  landscape restoration across Rajasthan, Gujarat, Haryana and Delhi. By 28 September 2026,
  **about 6.45 million hectares of degraded land had been identified for restoration**; this is an
  identified programme area, not measured ecological recovery.
