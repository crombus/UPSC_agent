## 1. Snapshot & core idea


India's structure is best read through a **modern chronostratigraphic and tectonic sequence**:

```text
ARCHAEAN CRATONIC BASEMENT
  -> PROTEROZOIC mobile belts and sedimentary basins
  -> PALAEOZOIC-MESOZOIC Gondwana rifts and marine-margin basins
  -> DECCAN flood-basalt pulse near 66 Ma
  -> CENOZOIC India-Eurasia convergence, Himalaya and foreland basin
  -> QUATERNARY alluvium and continuing deformation
```

⚠️ Khullar's **Archaean / Purana / Dravidian / Aryan** ladder is retained only as a
**historiographic textbook scheme**. It is not the controlling modern geological time scale and
must not replace ICS eons/eras/periods or GSI lithostratigraphic units.

- ✅ Ancient cratons and associated mobile belts form the Peninsular shield and are strongly
  associated with metallic-mineral provinces; association does not guarantee an economic deposit.
- ✅ Gondwana basins host the overwhelming share of India's bituminous coal resources; book-era
  percentages require current official verification before use.
- ✅ The Deccan Volcanic Province is a continental flood-basalt province whose principal eruptive
  pulse lies near the **Cretaceous-Paleogene boundary (~66 Ma)**.
- ✅ The Himalaya is a polyphase Cenozoic fold-and-thrust system containing sedimentary,
  metamorphic and crystalline units. Collision, exhumation and uplift cannot be reduced to a false
  three-date ladder such as “mid-Miocene, 45 Ma”.
- ⚠️ The Peninsular shield is comparatively stable, not tectonically inert; faults, rifts, shear
  zones, intraplate earthquakes and local coastal subsidence remain possible.

## 2. Key classification / data

| Modern unit / stage | Age / character | Economic / geographic note |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ Archaean cratons and basement complexes | Ancient gneiss-granite and greenstone/supracrustal terrains | Iron, manganese, gold and other metallic-mineral associations |
| ✅ Proterozoic mobile belts and basins | Aravalli-Delhi and Eastern Ghats belts; Cuddapah and Vindhyan basins | Base metals, limestone, building stone; Panna diamond province requires deposit-specific explanation |
| ✅ Gondwana rift basins | Mainly Permian-Mesozoic continental sedimentation | Major coal-bearing basins; not part of a modern “Aryan” time unit |
| ✅ Mesozoic marine-margin basins | Kachchh, Jaisalmer and east-coast basins | Evidence against an absolute post-Cambrian non-submergence claim |
| ✅ Deccan Volcanic Province | Principal pulse near **66 Ma** | Flood-basalt structure; later weathering contributes to regur with climate, relief and drainage |
| ✅ Cenozoic Himalaya and foreland basin | Polyphase India-Eurasia convergence and continuing deformation | Himalayan relief, Siwalik molasse, foreland-basin sedimentation, petroleum-bearing basins |
| ✅ Quaternary | **2.58 Ma-present** under ICS 2026-06 | Alluvium, terraces and continuing geomorphic adjustment |

### Historical fourfold scheme — use only when a source or question invokes it

| Historical label | Rough modern correspondence | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Archaean | Basement and older Precambrian units | Do not collapse all Dharwar supracrustals into undifferentiated basement |
| Purana | Proterozoic Cuddapah and Vindhyan successions in the older scheme | Use modern basin/supergroup names and source-specific ages |
| Dravidian | Older Palaeozoic grouping | Historiographic label, not a modern system; do not infer Peninsular non-submergence from its patchy preservation |
| Aryan | Gondwana through Cenozoic/Quaternary in the older scheme | Far too heterogeneous to function as a modern chronostratigraphic unit |

### Hill-range controls for the 2023 objective demand

- ✅ **Amarkantak** lies in the Maikal upland at the meeting zone of the Vindhya-Satpura systems;
  it is not a Sahyadri junction.
- ✅ **Biligirirangan Hills** lie in south-eastern Karnataka at the transition between the Western
  and Eastern Ghats and are commonly treated as an easternmost Western-Ghats extension.
- ✅ **Seshachalam Hills** are a southern Eastern-Ghats range in Andhra Pradesh.
- ✅ The **Aravalli-Delhi Fold Belt** is polyphase and Proterozoic; avoid the absolute “world's
  oldest mountain” and any single blanket age for the entire belt.
- ⚠️ The **Palghat Gap** aligns with a major shear/lineament zone, but a simple rift-valley origin
  is not settled.
- ✅ The Eastern Ghats are discontinuous. **Jindhagada/Arma Konda**, not Mahendragiri, is generally
  identified as their highest summit; elevations require a current named map.

## 4. Must-Know Facts (Prelims)


- ✅ Modern sequence: Archaean cratons → Proterozoic belts/basins → Gondwana rifts and Mesozoic
  marine basins → Deccan volcanism near 66 Ma → Cenozoic Himalaya/foreland → Quaternary.
- ✅ Historical fourfold scheme = historiography, not the controlling modern time scale.
- ✅ Dharwar/greenstone and other ancient shield belts are associated with iron, manganese and gold.
- ✅ Gondwana basins host most Indian bituminous coal; north-eastern coal is mainly Cenozoic.
- ✅ Basalt contributes parent material to regur, but climate, relief, drainage, organisms and time
  also control the soil.
- ✅ Everest summit limestone belongs to the **Ordovician Qomolangma Formation**, not the Upper
  Carboniferous.
- ✅ **Tertiary** and **Recent** are historical terms; use Paleogene/Neogene and Holocene.

## 5. UPSC Traps

> 🔑 Trap: Do not confuse similar-looking landforms, processes, scales or Indian locations; UPSC often tests the exact mechanism and setting.


- ❌ The Deccan Trap is a sedimentary formation. → The Deccan Trap is IGNEOUS (flood basalt / extrusive volcanic rock).
- ❌ The Himalaya consists only of young Tertiary sedimentary rocks. → It contains sedimentary,
  metamorphic and crystalline units assembled in a young fold-thrust belt.
- ❌ Most Indian coal is in the Deccan Trap. → Almost all Indian coal is in GONDWANA rocks, not the Deccan Trap.
- ❌ The Peninsula has had no marine submergence since the Cambrian. → Later marine incursions are
  recorded in Peninsular-margin and intracratonic basins; stability is regional and comparative.
- ❌ Himalayan uplift occurred in a “mid-Miocene 45 Ma” phase. → 45 Ma is Eocene; uplift and
  exhumation were polyphase and regionally variable.
- ❌ The asthenosphere is molten at a fixed depth. → It is predominantly solid, weak upper mantle;
  the lithosphere-asthenosphere boundary varies spatially.
