## 1. Snapshot & core idea


**Advanced — India's Earthquake Zones, Plate Collision & Liquefaction (D.R. Khullar).**

⚠️ Older books divide India by historical earthquake frequency and intensity; those divisions are
historiographic aids, not current design zoning. Modern control comes from tectonics, instrumental
catalogues, BIS standards and site-specific hazard studies.
- ⚠️ The Indian continental lithosphere **underthrusts Eurasia beneath the Himalayan-Tibetan system**.
  Much damaging Himalayan seismicity occurs on or near the **Main Himalayan Thrust (MHT)**, the
  regional décollement; MFT/MBT/MCT are related mapped thrust systems but are not interchangeable
  with the principal locked interface.
- ⚠️ The Himalaya and north-east form the main plate-boundary seismic belt. Kachchh is a major
  intraplate/rift-reactivation pocket. Peninsular India is comparatively stable, not aseismic; Koyna,
  Latur and Jabalpur demonstrate distinct intraplate or triggered mechanisms.
- ⚠️ Indo-Gangetic alluvium can amplify selected frequencies and may contain liquefaction-susceptible
  deposits, but response varies with basin geometry, sediment properties, groundwater and input motion.
- ⚠️ Liquefaction involves pore-pressure rise and loss of effective stress/stiffness in susceptible
  saturated cohesionless or low-plasticity soils, including sands, silty sands and some silts. It is
  not restricted to uniformly graded sand and the soil does not become an ordinary liquid.
- ✅ BIS zonation is a regional design/hazard generalisation. Earthquake size is commonly reported as
  **moment magnitude (Mw)** for large events; intensity and local site response remain separate.

## 2. Key classification / data

| BIS zone | Risk / MSK intensity | Representative areas |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ Zone V | Very high damage risk; MSK IX and above | Mapped parts of the Himalayan/north-eastern belt, Rann of Kachchh and Andaman-Nicobar |
| ✅ Zone IV | High damage risk; MSK VIII | Delhi and other mapped Himalayan, foothill and adjoining sectors |
| ✅ Zone III | Moderate damage risk; MSK VII | Broad mapped sectors; consult the operative BIS map for a location |
| ✅ Zone II | Low damage risk; MSK VI or lower | Broad mapped sectors; “low” does not mean aseismic |
| ⚠️ Himalayan source system | Continental collision and underthrusting | Strain release chiefly associated with the MHT and connected thrust/fault systems |
| ⚠️ Indo-Gangetic site response | Foreland alluvium plus dense exposure | Amplification/liquefaction susceptibility is local, not uniform across the plain |
| ⚠️ Peninsular seismicity | Stable continental region with inherited faults and local triggers | Hazard varies; do not assign the whole interior to one zone from memory |

## ➕ 2004 Indian Ocean Tsunami & India's Warning System (PYQ — Mains 2025 GS-1)

> **Modern control:** USGS event science and INCOIS/ITEWC official system description.

- ✅ On **26 December 2004** the **USGS Mw 9.1 Sumatra-Andaman megathrust earthquake** generated
  a destructive Indian Ocean tsunami. Travel and warning time varied greatly between near-source
  Andaman-Nicobar coasts and more distant mainland coasts.
- ⚠️ India's heaviest losses occurred in the Andaman-Nicobar Islands and on the Tamil Nadu coast, with
  impacts also in Andhra Pradesh, Kerala and Puducherry. Avoid an undated basin-wide fatality estimate
  when the analytical focus is formation, warning and vulnerability.
- ✅ **India's response:** the [**Indian Tsunami Early Warning Centre (ITEWC)**](https://tsunami.incois.gov.in/TEWS/abouttsunamiready.jsp) at **INCOIS,
  Hyderabad**, under the Ministry of Earth Sciences, has been operational since **October 2007**.
  It combines real-time seismic and sea-level observations, including tsunami buoys/BPRs and tide
  gauges, with numerical modelling and round-the-clock advisories. The [official architecture page](https://tsunami.incois.gov.in/TEWS/searlywarnings.jsp)
  directly documents the BPR/tide-gauge sensor and communications design.
- ✅ ITEWC provides regional tsunami services under the UNESCO-IOC Indian Ocean framework.
- ✅ Natural warning signs include strong or long coastal shaking, an unusual roar, or rapid sea-level
  change. Evacuate to officially identified high ground/inland routes; a single elevation cut-off is not
  a universal safety rule because run-up varies with bathymetry and coastal form.

| Element | Fact |
|---|---|
| ✅ Trigger event | 26 Dec 2004 Sumatra (Sunda Trench) megathrust quake |
| ✅ Indian warning system | INCOIS/ITEWC, Hyderabad; operational since October 2007 |
| ✅ System | Seismic + sea-level observations, numerical modelling and communications |
| ⚠️ High-risk setting | Low-lying, gently sloping or funnel-shaped coasts near tsunamigenic sources |

> 🔑 Trap: tsunami warning detects and evaluates an event after it begins; it does not predict an
> earthquake, and near-field coasts may have very little response time.

## 4. Must-Know Facts (Prelims)


**Advanced — India's Earthquake Zones, Plate Collision & Liquefaction (D.R. Khullar).**
- ✅ BIS seismic zones: II (low) to V (very high) — IS:1893.
- ✅ Zone V is highest under IS 1893 (Part 1):2016; use the mapped boundary, not state-wide shorthand.
- ✅ **Delhi** is in Zone IV; the wider NCR and Peninsular interior must be checked by exact location.
- ⚠️ Himalayan earthquakes reflect continental underthrusting and strain release on the MHT/fault system.
- ⚠️ Liquefaction = pore-pressure rise and effective-stress loss in susceptible saturated soils.
- ⚠️ Peninsular exceptions: Latur (1993) is intraplate; Koyna (1967) is widely studied as
  reservoir-triggered/reservoir-associated seismicity acting on a pre-stressed fault system.

## 5. UPSC Traps

> 🔑 Trap: Do not confuse similar-looking landforms, processes, scales or Indian locations; UPSC often tests the exact mechanism and setting.


**Advanced — India's Earthquake Zones, Plate Collision & Liquefaction (D.R. Khullar).**
- ❌ The Peninsular shield is completely aseismic. → It is relatively stable but not immune; Latur is
  intraplate, while Koyna requires the more precise reservoir-associated/triggered qualification.
- ❌ Highest Indian seismic zone is Zone I. → The BIS map runs Zone II–V; Zone V is the HIGHEST-risk (there is no Zone I in the current map).
- ❌ Alluvial plains are uniformly safest or uniformly most dangerous. → Some alluvial basins amplify
  damaging frequencies and contain liquefiable deposits; response requires local microzonation.

## 6. 📰 Current link


**Advanced — India's Earthquake Zones, Plate Collision & Liquefaction (D.R. Khullar).**
📰 **BIS status, exact official record.**
- Gazette notification **HQ-PUB013/1/2020-PUB-BIS (1361)**, dated **6 November 2025**
  ([official PDF](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/267696.pdf)), established
  IS 1893 (Part 1):2025 on 3 November 2025 and provided that the 2016 standard would remain
  concurrently in force until 3 May 2026.
- Gazette notification **HQ-PUB-BIS (1452)**, dated **3 March 2026**
  ([official PDF](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2026/270643.pdf)), withdrew the November
  notification with immediate effect.
- MoES/PIB Release **2247533**, 1 April 2026, explicitly states that
  **IS 1893 (Part 1):2016 remains the current standard** after withdrawal of the revised zonation.
- The Gazette notices establish and withdraw the standards notification; they do **not themselves
  state Zone VI boundaries, PGA values, construction-cost effects or land-area shares**. Do not
  attribute such details to the Gazettes without the official standard or another first-party source.

📰 **Monitoring and EEW, dated official controls.**
- NSN counts: **169** observatories on 25 March 2026 (Release **2244887**), **170** on
  1 April 2026 (Release **2247533**) and **174** on 13 August 2026 (Release **2298817**).
- The April release states national capability to detect and locate earthquakes of magnitude
  **3.0 and above**. The August release states that earthquake early warning remains in a
  **pilot phase**, using initial P-wave detection to provide only a few seconds of lead time,
  depending on source distance. Monitoring/rapid information and EEW are not prediction.
