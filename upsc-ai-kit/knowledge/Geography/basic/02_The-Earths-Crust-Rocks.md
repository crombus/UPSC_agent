## 1. Snapshot & core idea


**Foundation — The Three Rock Families (G.C. Leong).**

✅ The crust is built of three rock families defined by how they form: igneous (from cooled magma), sedimentary (from accumulated sediment) and metamorphic (pre-existing rock altered by heat & pressure). Igneous is the 'primary' rock; its debris builds sedimentary rock; heat & pressure convert either into metamorphic rock — the essence of the rock cycle.
- ✅ Plutonic (intrusive) rocks cool slowly deep in the crust → large, easily recognised crystals (granite); exposed later by denudation.
- ✅ Volcanic (extrusive) rocks pour out as lava and solidify fast → tiny crystals; basalt forms lava sheets/plateaux (Deccan, Columbia-Snake, Iceland).
- ✅ Sedimentary rocks are stratified (layered) — usually deposited under water; layers vary from inches to many feet.
- ✅ Sediment is transported by streams, glaciers, wind or even animals before deposition and cementation.
- ✅ Common metamorphic pairs: shale/mudstone→slate or schist, limestone→marble, sandstone→quartzite,
  and granite or other felsic protoliths→gneiss. Graphite may form by metamorphism of carbon-rich material,
  but “coal→graphite” is not a universal one-step rule.

**Standard — Rock Classification, Characteristics & the Rock Cycle (Majid Husain).**

✅ Majid Husain classifies igneous rocks by texture and composition — basalt, gabbro, andesite, diorite, rhyolite, granite — and by mode of occurrence into intrusive (plutonic) and extrusive (volcanic). Igneous rock is the most important original source of the inorganic mineral matter that later forms sedimentary rock — so the three families are genetically linked in the rock cycle.
- ✅ Sedimentary characteristics: derived from pre-existing rock (igneous the chief source) and/or
  organic or chemical material; commonly layered/stratified, with recognised massive exceptions.
- ✅ Sub-aerially exposed riverine sedimentary rocks develop polygonal desiccation cracks (mud cracks) on drying.
- ✅ Glacial sedimentary deposits = moraines; wind-laid fine silt = loess (e.g. northern China).
- ✅ Metamorphic rocks form from pre-existing rock by change in temperature, pressure and chemical action of fluids — altering texture AND mineral composition.
- ✅ Primary porosity is generally low in crystalline metamorphic rocks, but joints, fractures and
  weathered zones may provide **secondary permeability**—important for groundwater and foundations.
- ✅ Gneiss shows granular quartz-feldspar banding; slate shows slaty cleavage — both aid field identification.

### Modern terminology and state-of-matter controls

- ✅ The **mantle is predominantly solid silicate rock**. The asthenosphere is a hotter, weaker,
  ductile part of the upper mantle that flows slowly over geological time; it is not a global magma
  layer. The lithosphere-asthenosphere boundary varies with age and tectonic setting, so a single
  universal asthenosphere depth bracket must not be memorised.
- ⚠️ **Sial** and **sima** are useful historical compositional shorthand. Modern answers should
  distinguish **continental crust**, **oceanic crust**, **lithosphere** and **asthenosphere** rather
  than present sial-over-sima as a universal two-layer crust.
- ⚠️ **Acid/basic** is legacy igneous terminology. Use **felsic/intermediate/mafic/ultramafic**
  where precision matters; silica and ferromagnesian mineral content are the main discriminators.
  Aluminium is not, by itself, a diagnostic “basic” component.
- ⚠️ Sedimentary rocks are **commonly**, not invariably, stratified. Limestone may be biogenic,
  chemical or mixed in origin; “limestone = organic” is therefore only a classroom example.

## 4. Must-Know Facts (Prelims)


**Foundation — The Three Rock Families (G.C. Leong).**
- ✅ Igneous = cooled magma; plutonic (large crystals) vs volcanic (small crystals).
- ✅ Basalt = extrusive → Deccan Traps; columnar basalt = Giant's Causeway (Antrim).
- ✅ Sedimentary rocks = STRATIFIED (layered), mostly water-laid.
- ✅ Limestone/chalk may be strongly biogenic (corals, shells), while limestone may also be
  chemical or mixed in origin; sandstone is mechanically formed.
- ✅ Metamorphic key pairs: limestone→marble, sandstone→quartzite; carbon-rich material may yield
  graphite, but coal→graphite is not a universal one-step rule.
- ✅ Granite→gneiss; shale→schist; clay→slate.

**Standard — Rock Classification, Characteristics & the Rock Cycle (Majid Husain).**
- ✅ Igneous rocks classified by TEXTURE + COMPOSITION (basalt, gabbro, andesite, diorite, rhyolite, granite).
- ✅ Igneous rock = chief original source of sedimentary mineral matter.
- ✅ Slate = low-grade metamorphism of shale (slaty cleavage).
- ✅ Gneiss = high-grade regional metamorphism; banded quartz-feldspar.
- ✅ Crystalline rocks commonly store groundwater in joints, fractures and weathered mantles rather than
  in abundant primary pore space.
- ✅ Loess = wind-deposited silt; moraine = glacial sediment.

## 5. UPSC Traps

**Foundation — The Three Rock Families (G.C. Leong).**
- ❌ Basalt cools slowly to form large crystals. → Basalt is EXTRUSIVE → rapid cooling → small crystals; granite (plutonic) has large crystals.
- ❌ All sedimentary rocks come from river sand. → They can be mechanical (sandstone), organic (limestone/chalk) or chemical; sediment may be river-, wind-, glacier- or organism-derived.
- ❌ Marble is an igneous rock. → Marble is METAMORPHIC — from limestone under heat & pressure.

**Standard — Rock Classification, Characteristics & the Rock Cycle (Majid Husain).**
- ❌ Foliation/cleavage and stratification are the same. → Foliation (slate, schist) is a metamorphic alignment; stratification is sedimentary layering — different origins.
- ❌ Metamorphic-rock permeability is fixed by rock name alone. → Fracturing, jointing and weathering
  often control secondary permeability more than the unfractured rock matrix.
- ❌ Source rock of every metamorphic rock is obvious. → For quartzite/marble it is clear; for schist and gneiss the parent is often hard to determine.
- ❌ Tectonic plates float on a molten mantle at one fixed depth. → Plates are rigid lithosphere;
  the underlying mantle is mostly solid, and the mechanically weak asthenosphere has a variable top
  and thickness.
- ❌ “Acid/basic” is a complete modern igneous classification. → Treat it as historical shorthand;
  use felsic/intermediate/mafic/ultramafic and name silica/mineral assemblage.

## 8. Crustal dynamics: drift, sea-floor spreading and the changing map

> **Why this section exists:** this file is the routed owner for the demand *change in continents
> and ocean basins due to crustal tectonics* and for *evidences of continental drift*, but it
> previously taught **only petrology**. Rock families alone cannot explain why continents and ocean
> basins change. That mechanism is supplied here.

### 8.1 Continental drift: the claim and its evidence

⚠️ Alfred Wegener proposed continental displacement in **1912** and developed the book-length
case in **1915**. The hypothesis held that today's continents were once
assembled into a single landmass (**Pangaea**), which fragmented into a northern **Laurasia** and a
southern **Gondwana** and drifted apart.

| Evidence class | What is observed | Why it supports drift |
|---|---|---|
| ⚠️ Jig-saw fit | The Atlantic margins, especially South America and Africa, match — better still at the continental-shelf edge than at the shoreline | Coastlines are erosional; shelf edges are the true structural margins |
| ⚠️ Geological continuity | Mountain belts and old rock provinces line up across the ocean when the continents are reassembled | Structures cannot be created identically on two separated blocks |
| ⚠️ Fossil distribution | Identical terrestrial fossil floras and faunas occur on now-separated southern continents | Land organisms cannot cross an ocean basin |
| ⚠️ Palaeoclimatic evidence | Glacial deposits in today's tropics, and coal-forming (warm, swampy) deposits in today's high latitudes | The rocks record a *different* latitude for the same block |
| ⚠️ Palaeomagnetism | Magnetic minerals in old rocks point to poles that appear to have wandered — and to have wandered *differently* on different continents | The poles are broadly fixed; the continents moved |

> 🔑 **Trap:** Wegener assembled the evidence convincingly but had **no adequate mechanism** — his
> proposed driving forces were physically inadequate. The hypothesis was accepted only after
> ocean-floor evidence supplied the engine. In an answer, say this explicitly: it is the standard
> discriminating point between a good and an average script.

### 8.2 Sea-floor spreading: the missing ocean-floor mechanism

```text
MANTLE CONVECTION / UPWELLING
        |
        v
MID-OCEAN RIDGE: magma rises, new oceanic crust is created at the axis
        |
        +--> the new crust records the magnetic polarity of its moment of cooling
        |
        v
CRUST MOVES AWAY SYMMETRICALLY ON BOTH FLANKS
        |
        +--> ocean floor gets OLDER, THICKER-SEDIMENTED and DEEPER away from the ridge
        |
        v
DESTROYED AT SUBDUCTION ZONES (trenches) - so the Earth does not expand
```

> ⚠️ **Driver qualification:** do not reduce plate motion to a conveyor belt driven only by
> asthenospheric convection. Modern plate dynamics combine **slab pull, ridge push, gravitational
> potential-energy differences and mantle flow**, with their relative importance varying by plate
> and boundary. The mantle and asthenosphere remain mostly solid while deforming slowly.

| Ocean-floor observation | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ Symmetrical magnetic stripes of alternating polarity parallel to the ridge | The floor is a tape recorder of reversals; it is being made at the axis and carried outward |
| ⚠️ Ocean-floor age increases away from the ridge crest | Crust is created at the ridge |
| ⚠️ Sediment thickness increases away from the ridge | Older floor has had longer to accumulate sediment |
| ⚠️ Deep trenches with inclined earthquake zones descending under arcs | Old, cold, dense oceanic crust is being consumed |
| ⚠️ The ocean floor is geologically **young** compared with continental shields | Oceanic crust is continuously recycled; continental crust is buoyant and persists |

> 🔑 **Trap:** the Earth is **not** expanding. Creation at ridges is balanced by destruction at
> trenches. Any answer that describes spreading without subduction has left the mass balance open.

### 8.3 Plate boundaries: the three regimes and what each produces

| Boundary | Relative motion | Crust budget | Characteristic products |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⚠️ Divergent / constructive | Plates move apart | Crust created | Mid-ocean ridge, rift valley, shallow-focus earthquakes, fissure basaltic vulcanism, new ocean basins |
| ⚠️ Convergent / destructive - ocean-continent | Ocean plate subducts beneath continent | Crust destroyed | Trench, fold-mountain chain with explosive andesitic volcanoes, deep-focus earthquakes |
| ⚠️ Convergent - ocean-ocean | Older, denser ocean plate subducts | Crust destroyed | Trench and **island arc**, marginal/back-arc sea |
| ⚠️ Convergent - continent-continent | Neither block subducts easily | Crust thickened, not destroyed | Great fold mountains, crustal shortening, high plateau, shallow-to-intermediate seismicity, **little or no volcanism** |
| ⚠️ Transform / conservative | Plates slide past each other | Crust neither created nor destroyed | Long strike-slip fault zones, shallow but destructive earthquakes, no volcanic arc |

> 🔑 **Trap:** a continent-continent collision belt is **seismically very active but volcanically
> quiet**. Writing "the Himalaya is volcanic" is a standard and fatal error. The Himalaya is a
> collision orogen; the Andes is a subduction orogen; the two are not interchangeable examples.

### 8.4 The life cycle of an ocean basin

⚠️ Ocean basins are not permanent features; they open and close. The sequence, with a present-day
example of each stage, is the cleanest way to answer any question on *changing* ocean basins:

| Stage | Process | Present-day example |
|---|---|---|
| ⚠️ Embryonic | Continental crust domes, stretches and rifts | East African Rift system |
| ⚠️ Juvenile | The rift floods; a narrow sea with a young spreading axis appears | Red Sea and Gulf of Aden |
| ⚠️ Mature | A wide basin with a central ridge and passive, aseismic margins | Atlantic Ocean |
| ⚠️ Declining | Subduction begins around the rim; the basin starts to close | Pacific Ocean, ringed by trenches and arcs |
| ⚠️ Terminal | Continents approach; the remaining sea narrows | Mediterranean between Africa and Eurasia |
| ⚠️ Suture / relic | The ocean is consumed; a collision mountain belt marks the join | Himalaya, marking the closed Tethys |

- ⚠️ **Passive (Atlantic-type) margins** sit *inside* a plate: wide shelves, thick sediment wedges,
  little seismicity — and, as a direct consequence, the world's principal **offshore hydrocarbon**
  provinces (see `31_Mineral-Energy-Resources-World-and-India.md`).
- ⚠️ **Active (Pacific-type) margins** sit *at* a plate edge: narrow shelves, trenches, arcs,
  strong seismicity and volcanism.

### 8.5 Isostasy and orogeny: why relief exists at all

- ⚠️ **Isostasy** is the buoyant equilibrium between the lighter crust and the denser substratum
  beneath it: crustal blocks adjust vertically toward gravitational equilibrium.
  - ⚠️ In the **Airy** conception, columns have similar density but different *root* depth — a
    higher mountain has a deeper root.
  - ⚠️ In the **Pratt** conception, columns rise to different heights because their *density*
    differs above a common depth of compensation.
- ⚠️ **Isostatic adjustment** is observable: removal of a heavy ice load causes long-continued
  uplift of the deglaciated region (post-glacial rebound), while heavy sediment loading of a delta
  causes subsidence — which is exactly why deltas are so vulnerable to relative sea-level rise
  (see `10_Coastal-Landforms.md`).
- ⚠️ **Orogeny** is the mountain-building process itself: sediment accumulation in a subsiding
  basin, compression at a converging margin, folding, thrusting and crustal thickening, followed by
  isostatic uplift and prolonged denudation. The result is the standard three-fold relief hierarchy
  UPSC tests: **ancient shields** (stable, worn down, mineral-rich), **old block/relict mountains**
  (rounded, non-seismic) and **young fold mountains** (high, sharp, seismically active).

> ⚠️ **Factual caution:** do not quote a numerical spreading rate, a crustal age in millions of
> years, a plate count beyond the standard "seven major plates" convention, or a collision date
> unless a cited source is in front of you. The *mechanism* earns the marks; an invented number
> loses them.

## 9. India's physiographic divisions: structure expressed as relief

> **Why this section exists:** the official syllabus clause this folder directly owns is
> *"Indian and World Geography — **Physical**, Social, Economic Geography of India and the World"*,
> yet no Core file named India's physiographic divisions. A hostile Core-only stress test found
> that questions on India's relief and its consequences could not be answered from Core at all.
> The Indian stratigraphic sequence, rock systems and plateau nomenclature remain in
> `advanced/02_India-Geological-Structure.md`; what is added here is the **structure-to-relief-to-

### 9.1 Three structural provinces of very different ages

```text
1. THE PENINSULAR BLOCK   - an ancient, rigid, stable SHIELD of crystalline and
                            metamorphic rock; long denuded; comparatively stable
                            but NOT aseismic -- intraplate deformation occurs
                                     |
                                     |  (collision closes the Tethys)
                                     v
2. THE HIMALAYA           - YOUNG FOLD MOUNTAINS from continent-continent collision:
                            highly seismic, VOLCANICALLY QUIET, still rising, and
                            supplying enormous sediment
                                     |
                                     |  (sediment fills the trough between the two)
                                     v
3. THE INDO-GANGETIC PLAIN - a FOREDEEP / trough between the rising range and the
                            stable block, filled to great depth with alluvium
```

- ⚠️ **This is a single causal story, not three separate facts.** The collision that raised the
  Himalaya created the depression in front of it, and the erosion of the rising range filled that
  depression. Writing the three divisions as one chain is what converts a description into an
  explanation. Mechanism: the crustal-dynamics section above.

### 9.2 The six divisions and what each determines

| Division | Structural character | Relief and drainage consequence | Land-use and settlement consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⚠️ **The Himalaya and associated ranges** | Young fold mountains in parallel longitudinal ranges, rising northward, cut by deep transverse gorges | Snow and glacier storage feeding **perennial** rivers; extreme relief; high seismic and mass-movement hazard; heavy orographic rainfall on the southern face | Terraced and horticultural agriculture; vertical zonation of forest, pasture and settlement; costly transport; hydropower potential with hazard exposure |
| ⚠️ **The Northern (Indo-Gangetic-Brahmaputra) Plain** | Deep alluvial fill in a foredeep | Extremely low gradient; meandering, shifting channels; **khadar and bhangar** surfaces; highly productive shallow aquifers | India's densest settlement and most intensive cultivation; flood exposure; groundwater dependence (see `04`, `37`) |
| ⚠️ **The Peninsular Plateau** | Ancient shield with block-faulted uplands, escarpments and rift valleys; extensive lava cover in the north-west | Moderate relief; **seasonal** rivers on hard rock with little alluvium; limited groundwater in crystalline terrain but good storage in fractures | Mineral wealth in the shield; rain-fed and drought-prone farming; black-soil cotton belt on the lava tract; sparser rural density than the plains |
| ⚠️ **The Western and Eastern Ghats** | Faulted escarpment on the west; discontinuous, dissected hills on the east | Western Ghats form a near-continuous **orographic barrier** and the water divide of the Peninsula; the Eastern hills are breached by large rivers | Heavy windward rainfall and plantation crops on the west; rain-shadow drylands to the east; the Ghats are the biodiversity core delegated to `Environment-and-Ecology` |
| ⚠️ **The Coastal Plains** | Narrow on the west, broad on the east | Western coast is narrow with **short, swift, estuary-forming** streams; eastern coast is wide and **deltaic**, built by large peninsular rivers; contrasting continental-shelf widths | Fishing, ports, plantations and backwater economy on the west; deltaic rice and dense settlement on the east, with cyclone and surge exposure (see `10`, `13`) |
| ⚠️ **The Islands** | Two contrasting origins — a submarine ridge/arc group in the Bay of Bengal, and coral-built atoll and reef islands in the Arabian Sea | Volcanic and tectonically active in the east; low, reef-derived and sediment-limited in the west | Very different vulnerability profiles: seismic and tsunami exposure versus sea-level and freshwater-lens fragility (see `11`) |

> 🔑 **Trap:** the two Ghats are **not** a matched pair. The Western Ghats are a continuous faulted
> escarpment forming the Peninsula's water divide; the Eastern Ghats are discontinuous, lower and
> repeatedly cut through by rivers. This asymmetry explains the entire drainage pattern of
> Peninsular India (see `05_Landforms-by-Running-Water.md`).

> 🔑 **Trap:** the Himalaya is **seismically very active but lacks an Andean-style volcanic arc**,
> because it is a continent-continent collision orogen, not an ocean-continent subduction arc.

### 9.3 Why this division set explains so much

| Question type | The physiographic explanation |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ Why is population density so uneven? | Level alluvium with perennial water versus rugged shield and mountain terrain (`37`) |
| ⚠️ Why are minerals concentrated in the east-central Peninsula? | Ancient shield geology hosts metallic ores; Gondwana basins host coal (`31`) |
| ⚠️ Why is the Deccan interior drought-prone? | Rain-shadow position behind the Western Ghats escarpment (`14`, `18`) |
| ⚠️ Why do the two coasts differ economically? | Narrow shelf with estuaries and ports on the west; wide shelf with deltas and dense rice farming on the east (`30`) |
| ⚠️ Why is the north more flood-prone and the Peninsula more drought-prone? | Perennial snow-fed rivers on a near-flat plain versus seasonal rivers on hard rock with high runoff variability |

### 9.4 Peninsular-block and hill-range controls

- ⚠️ **Peninsular stability is comparative, not absolute.** The shield contains major shear zones,
  rifts and intraplate earthquake provinces. Do not call it tectonically inert.
- ❗ Do not use the absolute claim that the Peninsula has had **no marine submergence since the
  Cambrian**. Peninsular-margin and intracratonic basins, including Kachchh and east-coast basins,
  preserve later marine incursions. Distinguish long-lived cratonic stability from local
  transgression, subsidence and coastal submergence.
- ✅ The **Aravalli-Delhi Fold Belt** is a polyphase **Proterozoic** orogenic/mobile belt resting on
  older basement. “One of the oldest surviving fold belts” is safer than “the world's oldest
  mountain”; no single 600–700 Ma date represents the whole belt.
- ⚠️ The **Palghat Gap** coincides with the Palghat-Cauvery shear/lineament zone, but its present
  low corridor has multiple proposed tectonic and erosional controls. Do not present a simple rift
  between two faults as settled fact without a modern cited study.
- ✅ The **Eastern Ghats** are a discontinuous, polyphase Proterozoic mobile belt rather than a
  continuous escarpment like the Western Ghats. **Jindhagada/Arma Konda**, not Mahendragiri, is
  generally identified as their highest summit; use a named current map before quoting elevation.
- ✅ **Amarkantak** lies in the Maikal upland at the meeting zone of the Vindhya-Satpura systems;
  it is not a Sahyadri junction. **Biligirirangan Hills** occupy the transition between the Western
  and Eastern Ghats and are commonly treated as an easternmost Western-Ghats extension.
  **Seshachalam Hills** form part of the southern Eastern Ghats in Andhra Pradesh.

> **Answer-neutral PYQ ownership:** 2023 Prelims GS-I Q9 named Amarkantak,
> Biligirirangan and Seshachalam; 2026 Prelims GS-I Q34 tested features associated with the
> Peninsular Block. The static placements and structural distinctions above are taught
> substantively, but no statement truth value, option, answer letter or elimination mapping is
> recorded here.

> ⚠️ **Factual caution:** do **not** quote peak heights, plain widths, plateau areas, coastline
> lengths or the number of physiographic sub-divisions from memory. Different textbooks divide the

### 10.2 Reusable 15-mark spine — "Change in continents and ocean basins due to crustal tectonics"

1. **Thesis:** continents and ocean basins are not permanent geographic containers; they are
   transient configurations produced by continuous creation of oceanic crust at ridges and its
   destruction at trenches, with continental crust rearranged as a by-product.
2. **Evidence that the map changes:** the five drift-evidence classes (fit, geological continuity,
   fossils, palaeoclimate, palaeomagnetism).
3. **Mechanism:** sea-floor spreading with its four ocean-floor proofs; the creation-destruction
   balance.
4. **Structured expression:** the three boundary regimes and what each builds.
5. **The cycle, with living examples:** East African Rift -> Red Sea -> Atlantic -> Pacific ->
   Mediterranean -> Himalayan suture. *This paragraph is what distinguishes a top answer:* it
   converts a static list into a demonstrated process.
6. **Consequence for human geography:** passive margins -> shelves and offshore hydrocarbons;
   active margins -> seismic and volcanic hazard belts and island-arc fisheries.
7. **Qualification:** rates are slow relative to human time and are not uniform; the "seven major
   plates" scheme is a convention, and minor plates matter locally.
8. **Conclusion:** the present distribution of land, sea and mountain is a snapshot of an ongoing
   cycle — which is precisely why hazard zones, resource belts and coastline stability are
   spatially organised rather than random.

### 10.3 Evidence units available in this file

> **Claim:** ocean basins have a life cycle rather than a fixed identity. **Evidence:** the East
> African Rift, Red Sea, Atlantic, Pacific, Mediterranean and Himalayan suture occupy successive
> stages of one sequence simultaneously. **Significance:** it lets an answer *demonstrate* change
> using places that exist today, instead of asserting it. **Limitation:** the stages are a
> conceptual ordering, not a timetable; a given basin can stall or reverse.

> **Claim:** tectonic setting, not chance, fixes where hydrocarbons and where earthquakes are.
> **Evidence:** wide passive margins accumulate thick sediment wedges and host offshore oil, while
> active margins carry trenches, arcs and deep-focus seismicity. **Significance:** it links this
> file directly to resource geography and to hazard zoning. **Limitation:** a sedimentary basin is
> necessary but not sufficient — source, reservoir, seal and trap must all be present.

> **Claim:** rock genesis governs the surface geography above it. **Evidence:** the extrusive
> basalt of the Deccan weathers to black soil that underpins a cotton belt, while crystalline
> shields store groundwater in fractures rather than in pore space. **Significance:** it converts
> petrology into an agriculture and water-security argument. **Limitation:** soil formation also
> depends on climate, relief, organisms and time — parent rock alone does not determine soil.
