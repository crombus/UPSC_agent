#### 1. Purpose and scope

This topic is the illustration bank other topics point to for functional
examples. It does not develop mechanism/argument itself (`05`, `08`) —
it curates recallable, verifiable Indian illustrations in a disciplined
format and links each to the prompt(s) and lens(es) it serves.

#### 2. Core terms in plain language

- **Source-status-tagged illustration:** a non-statistical institutional,
  historical, constitutional, statutory or policy example whose row states
  whether it rests on a primary text, durable owner or a refreshed official
  source.
- **Essay function:** the specific claim the example is used to
  demonstrate — never used as a standalone fact.
- **Limitation:** the boundary of what the example does *not* prove, so
  it is not overstated.
- **Source-module link:** the GS folder owning the full depth of this
  fact, for further study.

#### 3. ✅ Evidence and source basis

- The bank uses durable constitutional, statutory, institutional and
  historical facts. Every row names a **source status** and an owning
  module; it is not permission to invent a number, outcome, or quotation.
- ✅ A primary-text row may be used qualitatively as written. Check the
  linked owner before quoting a provision or adding a current outcome.
- 📰 A live-policy row must be refreshed against the named official source
  immediately before a live attempt. Its date is part of the fact.
- ❌ No percentage, ranking, funding figure, beneficiary count, or claimed
  implementation outcome belongs in this bank without a dated authoritative
  source.

#### 4. The central idea and common misreading

⚠️ **Central idea:** every illustration must be usable in the exact
chain **claim → fact/source status → essay function → limitation →
owner** — an example without a stated function and
limitation is a fact dump, not an essay illustration. ❌ **Common
misreading:** memorising a list of "good examples for Essay" and
inserting them wherever convenient, regardless of whether they actually
demonstrate the specific claim at hand.

#### 5. Basic decomposition questions

1. What specific claim does this example support?
2. Is the example a widely documented fact I can state without
   inventing a number?
3. What is this example's essay function here (illustrating a mechanism,
   a value conflict, a counter-case)?
4. What does this example *not* prove — where does its relevance stop?
5. Which GS module owns the full depth of this fact, if the reader wants
   more?

#### 7. Selection, reuse and refresh protocol

```text
PROMPT CLAIM -> choose a cross-domain example -> state its mechanism
     -> state the limitation -> record owner/source status
     -> live or dated policy? refresh official source and date
     -> reused example? give it a demonstrably different function
```

Reuse is allowed only when the paragraph claim and stated function differ.
Do not use one familiar example in both essays merely because it is easy
to recall. For 📰 entries, re-check the owning official source immediately
before writing; remove the entry if status, date or claimed implementation
cannot be confirmed.

#### 8. Thesis options and selection

⚠️ Match the illustration to the specific mechanism in your thesis (`05`)
— e.g. use the Chipko illustration only where your thesis actually
concerns collective ecological restraint, not merely because the
prompt mentions forests.

#### 9. Simple essay architecture

⚠️ Budget one illustration per argument-map cycle (`07`) drawn from this
bank or your own verified recall — avoid clustering two illustrations in
one paragraph and leaving another paragraph without any.

#### 10. Opening, body and closure moves

⚠️ Reserve your most vivid, well-verified illustration for a body
paragraph where it does real argumentative work; avoid spending the
opening or closing paragraph on an illustration instead of the prompt's
own tension (`06`).

#### 11. ❌ Traps and repair moves

- ❌ **Fact-dumping** (stating the Chipko movement or ISRO's programme
  without connecting it to a specific claim). → Repair: always state the
  claim and function before or after the example (Section 6's format).
- ❌ **Overstating an example's proof value** (treating one institution's
  success as proof of a general claim). → Repair: always state the
  limitation explicitly.
- ❌ **Inventing a statistic to make an example sound more impressive.**
  → Repair: state the fact qualitatively and verifiably; omit any number
  you cannot confirm (`09`).
- ❌ **Reusing the same illustration in both required essays** with no
  new function stated. → Repair: prefer a fresh, functionally distinct
  illustration for each essay to demonstrate range.

#### 12. Timed micro-drill and self-check

**5-minute drill:** Pick a row from a domain you normally avoid. For an
unmapped prompt, write its full chain (claim → fact/source status →
function → limitation → owner) from memory, then verify the source status.

**Self-check:** For any illustration you plan to use, can you state its
limitation as confidently as its function? If you can only state the
impressive part and not the limitation, you may be overstating its
proof value.

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

> Studies Paper — Essay, Mains).
> **Core area:** Extending the illustration bank with multi-claim
> examples, cross-prompt reuse discipline, and a refresh protocol for any
> illustration that touches a live policy debate.
> **Grounded in:** the source-status-tagged Core illustration bank
> (Constitution/Act texts, official institutional owners and dated official
> sources), linked GS subject modules for full depth, and audited 2024–2025
> UPSC Essay paper corpus (see `../README.md`) for prompt mapping.
> **Research cutoff:** 18 July 2026.
> **Tags:** ✅ verified fact | ⚠️ strategy/inference | 📰 dated anchor | ❌ trap/boundary.
> **Companion:** `../basic/12_India-Centric-Illustration-Bank.md`

#### 1. Advanced proposition and boundary

**Proposition:** ⚠️ a mature illustration bank is organised by
**function**, not by subject label — the same verified example can serve
different essay functions across different prompts, provided each use
states its own claim, function and limitation afresh. **Boundary:** ❌
this topic does not replace the GS subject modules' factual depth
(Section 12 links, not duplicates) — it governs how illustrations are
selected, reused across prompts, and refreshed for currency.

#### 2. Concept definition and taxonomy

- **Single-function example:** used for one specific claim only (most
  basic-file entries).
- **Multi-function example:** the same underlying fact, reused across
  different prompts with a genuinely different function each time (see
  Section 3).
- **Illustration reuse risk:** using the same example in both required
  essays, or repeatedly across practice attempts, without varying its
  stated function — signals a shallow illustration repertoire.
- **Refresh flag:** a marker on any illustration whose currency depends
  on an evolving policy situation, requiring a check against its named
  official source and recording its access date before live use.

#### 3. Tension pairs and hidden assumptions

⚠️ Multi-function reuse, worked example — the **Right to Information
Act, 2005**:

| Prompt/context | Function |
|---|---|
| 2024-B6 "Power tests character" | Illustrates institutional accountability constraining discretionary power |
| 2025-A1 "Truth knows no color" | Illustrates a legal mechanism for equal access to official information regardless of the requester's status |
| 2024-A4 "The doubter is a true man of science" | Illustrates institutionalised scrutiny/verification as a civic analogue to scientific doubt |

⚠️ **Hidden assumption to avoid:** that reusing a familiar example
signals efficiency; if the *function* is not restated distinctly for
each prompt, the reuse reads as recycling rather than range.

#### 4. Levels of analysis and temporal-spatial scale

⚠️ Classify each bank entry by the scale at which its function operates
— individual (a documented personal-conduct episode), institutional (an
Act, agency, or programme), state (a national policy stance), or
civilisational (a long-run historical pattern) — and match this to the
scale identified for the prompt in `11`, to avoid using an
institutional-scale example to illustrate a claim pitched at the
individual scale.

#### 5. Stakeholder map and distributional effects

⚠️ For each illustration, note which stakeholder group is centred by the
example and which is not — e.g. the Chipko-movement illustration centres
forest-dependent community agency; it says less about, and should not be
overstated as resolving, state-level industrial-policy trade-offs. An
advanced essay states this explicitly rather than letting the example
imply a broader resolution than it supports.

#### 6. Causality, mechanisms and feedback loops

```text
SELECT CORE-BANK EXAMPLE (source status known)
        |
        v
STATE ITS MECHANISM  (what causal/structural link does it demonstrate?)
        |
        v
MATCH MECHANISM TO SPECIFIC PARAGRAPH CLAIM (05/07)
        |
        v
STATE THE LIMITATION explicitly (what it does NOT prove)
        |
        v
IF THE EXAMPLE TOUCHES A LIVE POLICY SITUATION -> refresh the named
official source, record the date, then apply the refresh flag (13)
```
⚠️ Skipping the "match mechanism to specific claim" step is the most
common source of fact-dumping even among advanced writers who have
already memorised good examples.

#### 7. Necessary/sufficient conditions and exceptions

⚠️ A verified, functionally matched illustration is necessary for a
strong body paragraph but not sufficient on its own — the mechanism and
qualification (`05`) must still be argued, not merely implied by the
example's presence. **Exception:** for very tightly reasoned aphorism
essays (e.g. 2025-A3, "thought finds a world and creates one also"), a
single, deeply developed illustration may outperform two shallower ones.

#### 8. Paradox, dialectic and counterfactual test

**Paradox:** the examples that most impress on paper are often the least
"famous" ones — a precisely matched, honestly limited example
outperforms a grand but loosely connected one (e.g. invoking a major
global institution vaguely, versus a smaller, precisely relevant Indian
episode). **Counterfactual test:** "If I swapped this example for any
other broadly similar one, would my specific claim still be equally
well-supported?" If yes, the example was interchangeable filler, not a
precisely matched illustration.

#### 9. Ethical conflict and synthesis

⚠️ Where an illustration involves a contested or sensitive episode,
present it with due limitation and without partisan framing — the essay
should use the example's structural/functional lesson, not take a side
in an unrelated political controversy the essay is not actually arguing
about.

#### 10. Evidence/source-risk and India application

✅ `basic/12` now records a source status and owner for every entry:
primary Constitution/Act text, a durable institutional/historical owner,
or a 📰 official current source. Use the factual core only at the
precision its status supports. A durable row does not license a recalled
statistic; a 📰 row must be refreshed against its named official source
immediately before live use. Do not add a funding amount, ranking,
percentage or implementation claim without a dated authoritative source
(`09`).

#### 11. Advanced structure, paragraph sequencing and style

⚠️ When using a multi-function example (Section 3) across the same
essay's two argument-map cycles (an unusual but sometimes valid choice),
make the distinct function explicit each time it reappears, so the
repetition reads as a deliberate callback rather than as running out of
material.

#### 12. ❌ Failure modes and revision protocol

- ❌ **Reuse without restated function** (Section 3). → Protocol: always
  state the specific claim being illustrated on each reuse.
- ❌ **Overstated proof value** (Section 5). → Protocol: name the
  stakeholder/scale the example does not speak to.
- ❌ **Stale refresh-flagged example used without a currency check.** →
  Protocol: verify against the linked source module or a dated source
  before using in a live attempt (Section 13).
