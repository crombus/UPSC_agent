# MAJOR GOVERNANCE COMMITTEES, COMMISSIONS AND REPORTS

> **Use:** connect each administrative problem to the panel that diagnosed it. A report is
> a reform framework, not proof that its recommendations were implemented.

## Administrative reform and civil services

| Priority | Panel and chair/author | Year | Assigned task | Major contribution |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| A | **Paul H. Appleby Reports** | 1953, 1956 | Assess post-Independence public administration | Supported creation of IIPA and Organisation and Methods machinery; stressed professional administration |
| A | **First Administrative Reforms Commission - Morarji Desai, later K. Hanumanthaiya** | 1966-70 | Comprehensive review of Union administration | Twenty reports on machinery, personnel, finance, redress and Centre-State administration; proposed Lokpal/Lokayuktas |
| A | **Second Administrative Reforms Commission - Veerappa Moily** | 2005-09 | Prepare a blueprint for revamping public administration | Fifteen reports covering RTI, ethics, e-governance, local governance, crisis management, personnel and citizen-centric administration |
| A | **Kothari Committee on Recruitment Policy and Selection Methods** | 1974-76 | Reform recruitment to higher civil services | Common Civil Services Examination with preliminary, main and interview stages |
| A | **Hota Committee on Civil Service Reforms** | 2004 | Review recruitment, careers, performance and accountability | Fixed tenure, domain specialisation, performance appraisal, disciplinary reform and civil-service ethics |
| B | **Surinder Nath Committee** | 2003 | Review performance appraisal, promotion and empanelment in higher civil services | More objective assessment and systematic competency/performance evaluation |
| B | **Yugandhar Committee** | 2003 | Examine training needs of All India and Central Services | Mid-career training and continuous capacity development |

## Transparency, accountability and digital governance

| Priority | Panel/report and chair | Year | Assigned task | Major contribution |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| A | **H. D. Shourie Working Group on Right to Information** | 1997 | Review secrecy laws and prepare access-to-information legislation | Drafted a Freedom of Information framework and advanced the shift toward statutory transparency |
| A | **Second ARC First Report - Right to Information: Master Key to Good Governance** | 2006 | Review implementation and institutional design of transparency | Proactive disclosure, record management, whistle-blower linkage and repeal/reform of conflicting secrecy provisions |
| A | **Second ARC Twelfth Report - Citizen Centric Administration** | 2009 | Improve service delivery and grievance redress | Sevottam, enforceable service standards, citizen charters and grievance systems |
| A | **Committee of Experts on Data Protection - Justice B. N. Srikrishna** | 2017-18 | Develop a data-protection framework and draft law | Privacy principles, fiduciary duties, rights and an independent data-protection authority model |
| B | **Nandan Nilekani Technology Advisory Group for Unique Projects** | 2010-11 | Recommend technology architecture for major financial-governance systems | Common platforms, standards and scalable digital public infrastructure |

## High-yield distinctions

| Do not confuse | Correct distinction |
|---|---|
| Appleby and ARC | Appleby supplied early expert diagnoses; the ARCs were Government commissions issuing sector-wide reform reports |
| First and Second ARC | First ARC built the post-1960s reform agenda; Second ARC addressed contemporary transparency, ethics, digital and citizen-centric governance |
| Kothari and Hota | Kothari redesigned entry recruitment; Hota addressed careers, tenure, performance and accountability |
| National Police Commission and Sorabjee | NPC supplied the reform principles; Sorabjee converted many into a Model Police Act |
| Citizen charter and Sevottam | A charter states commitments; Sevottam combines standards, grievance redress and service-delivery capability |

> **Cross-links:** constitutional decentralisation and federalism are owned by Polity; integrity
> panels are owned by Ethics; Kargil and national-security task forces are owned by Internal
> Security.
