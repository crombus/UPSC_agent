## 1. Snapshot & core idea

**Foundation — Bengal was rich, strategic and vulnerable.**

- ✅ Bengal was one of the richest Mughal provinces, with fertile agriculture, textile production and active trade.
- ✅ Murshid Quli Khan, Alivardi Khan and Siraj-ud-Daula made Bengal autonomous in practice while still linked to Mughal legitimacy.
- ✅ The **1717 farman** granted the Company customs privileges for its own trade in return for an annual payment; conflict grew when Company servants extended **dastaks** to private trade, injured Bengal's revenue and Indian merchants, and fortified Calcutta without permission.
- ⚠️ The "Black Hole" episode is historically contested and should not be used as the main cause of conquest.

**Foundation — Plassey was a coup; Buxar was the decisive military victory.**

- ✅ **Plassey (23 June 1757)** was won by Clive through conspiracy with Mir Jafar, Rai Durlabh, Jagat Seth and others; it was near-bloodless.
- ✅ Mir Jafar became Nawab but failed to satisfy Company demands; Mir Qasim replaced him and tried to reform Bengal from Munger.
- ✅ Mir Qasim abolished inland duties to counter Company privilege, leading to open conflict.
- ✅ **Buxar (22 Oct 1764)** was a decisive battle where Hector Munro defeated Mir Qasim, Shuja-ud-Daula of Awadh and Shah Alam II.
- ✅ The **Treaty of Allahabad (1765)** gave the Company Diwani, turning it into the revenue power of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa.

## 4. Must-Know Facts (Prelims)

- ✅ **Siraj-ud-Daula** was the Nawab defeated at **Plassey (23 June 1757)**.
- ✅ Plassey was won by conspiracy involving **Mir Jafar**, **Rai Durlabh**, **Jagat Seth** and **Amichand**.
- ✅ **Mir Qasim** shifted his capital to **Munger**, reformed administration and abolished inland duties.
- ✅ **Buxar (22 Oct 1764)**: Hector Munro defeated **Mir Qasim + Shuja-ud-Daula + Shah Alam II**.
- ✅ **Treaty of Allahabad (1765)**: Company got **Diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa**.
- ✅ **Dual Government** lasted from **1765 to 1772** and was ended by **Warren Hastings**.
- ✅ **Bengal Famine of 1770** occurred amid crop failure, epidemic and Company revenue dominance; avoid reducing a complex catastrophe to one cause while ignoring the extractive administrative setting.

## 5. UPSC Traps

> 🔑 Trap: Plassey gave the Company political entry through conspiracy; Buxar gave it military supremacy and legal revenue rights.

- ❌ Plassey was a major battlefield victory. → It was largely a **conspiracy-backed coup**.
- ❌ Buxar was less important than Plassey. → Buxar established **real military supremacy** over Bengal, Awadh and the Mughal emperor.
- ❌ Diwani meant the Company became Nawab. → Diwani meant **revenue rights**; administration nominally remained with the Nawab under Dual Government.
- ❌ Awadh was annexed in 1765. → Awadh was preserved as a **buffer state**.
- ❌ The Black Hole episode alone caused the conflict. → The deeper causes were trade privileges, fortification and Company private trade.

### 8.2 Qualified thesis options

- **Sovereignty-conflict thesis:** *The Anglo-Bengal conflict was not a trade dispute but a sovereignty dispute: a Nawab could not concede duty-free private trade, unauthorised fortification and independent jurisdiction and still remain a ruler.*
- **Collaboration thesis:** *Plassey was decided in Murshidabad before it was decided at the battlefield; the Company's decisive asset was not firepower but the alignment of the banking house of the Jagat Seths, disaffected commanders and the mercantile elite.*
- **Two-stage thesis (best for Plassey/Buxar wordings):** *Plassey gave the Company a client; Buxar gave it a monopoly of force; the Treaty of Allahabad gave it a legal title — political entry, military supremacy and constitutional sanction were three separate acquisitions.*
- **Fiscal-transformation thesis:** *The Diwani converted a trading company into a revenue state, and by financing purchases and armies from Indian land revenue it removed the Company's dependence on bullion imports — the structural pivot of British India.*

### 8.4 Named evidence units (claim → evidence → significance → limit)

**A. Fiscal privilege as the root of conflict**
- **Claim:** The dispute began where the Company's commercial privilege collided with the Nawab's revenue and jurisdiction.
- **Evidence:** The 1717 farman granted duty-free trade for Company commerce against an annual payment; Company servants extended *dastaks* to their own private trade; Calcutta was fortified without permission.
- **Significance:** Extending a corporate exemption to private commerce transferred customs revenue from the Nawab's treasury to individual Englishmen and undercut Indian merchants who paid duty — an unsustainable position for any ruler.
- **Limit/caution:** Do not present this as pure Nawabi grievance; Siraj-ud-Daula's own accession disputes weakened his ability to enforce a settlement.

**B. Elite collaboration at Plassey**
- **Claim:** The Company won Bengal through a purchased realignment of the Nawab's own elite.
- **Evidence:** Plassey (23 June 1757) turned on the conspiracy involving Mir Jafar, Rai Durlabh, the banking house of the Jagat Seths and Amichand; the engagement was near-bloodless.
- **Significance:** Explains why an economically dominant province fell so quickly — the fiscal and military elite had a stake in a change of Nawab, not in resisting a foreign company.
- **Limit/caution:** The "Black Hole" episode is contested and must not be used as the causal centrepiece.

**C. Mir Qasim and the limits of reform**
- **Claim:** Bengal's most capable response failed because the asymmetry was structural, not tactical.
- **Evidence:** Mir Qasim shifted his capital to Munger, reorganised the army and finances, and abolished inland duties altogether so that Indian traders would face no disadvantage against duty-free Company traders.
- **Significance:** His abolition of duties was a logically complete answer to the privilege problem — and precisely because it equalised competition, it produced war rather than settlement.
- **Limit/caution:** Do not romanticise the reforms; his fiscal pressure and reliance on new commanders also alienated existing elites.

**D. Buxar as the real military verdict**
- **Claim:** Buxar, not Plassey, established the Company's supremacy over the whole northern political order.
- **Evidence:** At Buxar (22 October 1764) Hector Munro defeated the combined forces of Mir Qasim, Shuja-ud-Daula of Awadh and the Mughal emperor Shah Alam II.
- **Significance:** Defeating a Nawab, a neighbouring state and the emperor simultaneously left no superior authority in northern India to which Bengal could appeal.
- **Limit/caution:** Buxar's importance lies in what it settled politically; avoid claims about troop numbers or casualties.

**E. The Diwani and the buffer settlement**
- **Claim:** The Company preferred legal title and indirect control to outright annexation.
- **Evidence:** The Treaty of Allahabad (1765) granted the Company the Diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa; Awadh was restored to Shuja-ud-Daula as a buffer rather than annexed.
- **Significance:** Establishes the template of British expansion — acquire revenue and control while retaining an Indian ruler as the visible authority and an allied state as a strategic shield.
- **Limit/caution:** Diwani was a revenue right; the Company did not become Nawab in 1765, and answers must keep *diwani* and *nizamat* distinct.

**F. Dual Government — power without responsibility**
- **Claim:** Splitting revenue power from administrative accountability produced a predictable governance failure.
- **Evidence:** Between 1765 and 1772 the Company held the Diwani while the Nawab's establishment retained administrative responsibility; ⚠️ collection operated through Indian deputies while the Company controlled the purse. The Bengal famine of 1770 fell within this period; Warren Hastings ended the arrangement in 1772.
- **Significance:** The classic exam formulation — the Company had power without responsibility and the Nawab responsibility without power — is analytically accurate about the incentive structure.
- **Limit/caution:** The 1770 famine had crop failure and epidemic dimensions; the defensible claim is that extractive revenue administration aggravated it, not that Company policy alone caused it. Do not cite mortality figures.

**G. Bengal as the engine of empire**
- **Claim:** The conquest of Bengal changed the financial basis of British power in Asia.
- **Evidence:** Bengal land revenue funded the Company's export purchases and its sepoy armies instead of imported bullion; those armies fought the Mysore, Maratha and later campaigns.
- **Significance:** Converts Bengal from a regional episode into the pivot of the whole conquest narrative, and connects this owner to the drain and land-revenue arguments.
- **Limit/caution:** Quantified drain estimates belong to the economic owner and remain contested; keep the claim mechanism-based here.

### 8.5 Causal mechanism (for "how" and "why did they lose" demands)

```text
1717 farman privilege -> private-trade abuse via dastaks
   -> Nawab's customs revenue erodes; Indian merchants undercut
   -> Nawab asserts sovereignty (fortification, jurisdiction)
   -> Company buys the disaffected elite (Jagat Seths, Mir Jafar)
   -> Plassey 1757: regime change, indemnities, client Nawab
   -> client cannot meet Company demands -> Mir Qasim reform -> war
   -> Buxar 1764: Bengal + Awadh + Emperor defeated together
   -> Allahabad 1765: Diwani = legal revenue sovereignty
   -> Dual Government 1765-72: extraction without accountability
   -> 1770 famine; 1772 Hastings assumes direct administration
```

### 8.6 Counter-evidence, variation and caution

- **Against a purely economic reading:** the immediate triggers were jurisdictional and military; economics explains the stakes, not the sequence.
- **Against a purely conspiratorial reading:** collaboration worked because the Company already had a fortified base, naval supply and access to credit; treachery alone does not explain durability of control.
- **Against inevitability:** Mir Qasim's reforms and the Buxar coalition show that resistance was attempted at the highest level and was not foreordained to fail.
- **Regional variation:** Bihar and Orissa experienced the Diwani differently from the Bengal delta; zamindari and revenue arrangements were not uniform across the three provinces.

### 8.7 Verdict scaffolds

- **Turning-point verdict:** "If the question is when the Company gained Bengal, the answer is 1757; if it is when the Company could not be removed from Bengal, the answer is 1764–65."
- **Institutional verdict:** "Dual Government was not a failure of individuals but of design: it rewarded extraction and penalised nobody for its consequences."
- **Consequence verdict:** "Plassey and Buxar mattered less as battles than as the moment Indian revenue began to finance Indian conquest."

### 8.8 Factual-risk cautions

- Plassey was 23 June 1757 and Buxar 22 October 1764; keep the order and the distinction between coup and conquest.
- Awadh was **not** annexed in 1765; it was retained as a buffer.
- Diwani ≠ Nawabship; nizamat responsibility remained formally with the Nawab until 1772.
- Do not assign mortality, indemnity or revenue figures for 1757–72 without a verified source.
- Do not use the "Black Hole" as an established causal fact.
- Warren Hastings ended Dual Government in 1772; parliamentary regulation follows in 1773 and belongs to `basic/06`.
