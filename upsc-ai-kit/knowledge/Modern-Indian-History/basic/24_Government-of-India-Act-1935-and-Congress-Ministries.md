## 1. Snapshot & core idea

**Foundation — the 1935 Act was the largest colonial constitutional framework but not freedom.**

- ✅ The **Government of India Act 1935** drew on the Simon Report, Round Table Conferences, the 1933 White Paper and Joint Select Committee work.
- ✅ It proposed an **All-India Federation** of British Indian provinces and princely states, but the federation never came into being because the princes did not accede.
- ✅ It introduced **provincial autonomy** by abolishing dyarchy in the provinces, while **dyarchy was to be introduced at the Centre** — but only under the federal scheme, which never came into force.
- ✅ It enlarged the franchise to roughly **14%** of the population while keeping power under major safeguards and governor/viceroy controls.
- ✅ Separate electorates were extended, keeping communal representation at the heart of colonial constitutional politics.
- ✅ It provided for a **Federal Court**, which began functioning in **1937**. *(The Reserve Bank of India was a separate creation of the RBI Act 1934, not of this Act.)*

**Foundation — Congress used office as a field of struggle, then resigned over war.**

- ✅ Congress criticised the Act as inadequate; Jawaharlal Nehru described it as **"a machine with strong brakes but no engine"**.
- ✅ Congress still contested the **1937 elections** and formed ministries in **eight of eleven provinces**: Bombay, Madras, United Provinces, Bihar, Central Provinces, Orissa, and later NWFP and Assam.
- ✅ Congress ministries released political prisoners, expanded civil liberties, promoted education and attempted agrarian and tenancy reforms within limited powers.
- ✅ Muslim League propaganda about alleged **"Congress atrocities"** deepened Congress–League estrangement.
- ✅ Congress ministries resigned in **October–November 1939** after Viceroy Linlithgow declared India at war without consulting Indian leaders.

## 5. UPSC Traps

> 🔑 Trap: The 1935 Act is famous for both **provincial autonomy** and an **unrealised federation**; do not say the federation actually functioned.

- ❌ The Act introduced dyarchy in provinces. → ✅ It **abolished provincial dyarchy** and proposed dyarchy at the Centre.
- ❌ The All-India Federation came into being in 1937. → ✅ It never came into being because princely states did not accede.
- ❌ Congress boycotted the 1937 elections. → ✅ Congress contested and formed ministries in eight provinces.
- ❌ Congress ministries resigned because they lost elections. → ✅ They resigned over the unilateral war declaration in 1939.
- ❌ Lahore Resolution came before Deliverance Day. → ✅ Deliverance Day was **Dec 1939**; Lahore Resolution was **Mar 1940**.

### 8.2 Qualified thesis options

- **Safeguards thesis:** *The 1935 Act's substantive concession — responsible government in the provinces — was systematically neutralised by discretionary and emergency powers vested in governors and the Viceroy, so that Indians received administration without ultimate authority.*
- **Constituency thesis:** *The Act's franchise was politically engineered: by enfranchising the rich and middle peasantry it sought to bind the very classes on whom Congress depended, making the reforms a bid for the allegiance of dominant rural communities rather than a step toward democracy.*
- **Office-as-struggle thesis:** *Congress's acceptance of office in 1937 was a calculated extension of the movement into administration; it produced real if limited gains and one large unintended cost — it demonstrated to the Muslim League that provincial power meant Congress power.*
- **Federation-failure thesis:** *The federal scheme collapsed because it required the princes to surrender treaty rights voluntarily; the Act asked its most conservative beneficiaries to dissolve their own privileged position, which they predictably declined to do.*

### 8.4 Bank A — Provincial autonomy and the safeguards

- **Claim:** Provincial responsible government was real and reversible at will.
- **Evidence:** ✅ In the provinces the Act **replaced dyarchy with responsible government in all departments**; ✅ this was balanced by **wide discretionary powers given to governors** over summoning legislatures, assenting to bills and administering tribal regions, plus **special powers to safeguard minority rights, the privileges of civil servants and British business interests**, and finally the power to **take over and run a province's administration indefinitely** under a special provision.
- **Significance:** The last power is decisive — a constitution in which the executive can suspend responsible government indefinitely does not transfer sovereignty; it delegates administration. This is the strongest evidence for the safeguards thesis and the origin of the debate over emergency provisions in independent India.
- **Limit/caution:** Concede the genuine advance: Indian ministers now held **all** departments, including law and order, which dyarchy had reserved (`basic/18`).

### 8.5 Bank B — The federal scheme and reserved subjects (routed demand)

- **Claim:** The federal half of the Act was designed so that its Indian beneficiaries would never control the state's core functions.
- **Evidence:** ✅ At the centre the Act provided a **federal structure that would come into effect only if more than 50 per cent of the princely states formally acceded by signing Instruments of Accession**, which would override their previous treaties with the Crown. ✅ The Act **introduced dyarchy at the centre subject to various safeguards**, and **departments such as foreign affairs, defence and internal security remained completely under the control of the Viceroy**. ✅ In the proposed bicameral central legislature, **members nominated by the princes would constitute 30 to 40 per cent of the seats**, thus permanently eliminating the possibility of a Congress majority.
- **Significance:** Two mechanisms — reserved control of defence and external affairs, and a built-in princely bloc — meant that even a fully operational federation would have left the Congress permanently in a minority and the empire's essential functions untouched. This answers the routed demand precisely.
- **Residuary powers:** ⚠️ Under the 1935 scheme **residuary legislative power was vested in the Governor-General** rather than in the federal centre or the provinces — the arrangement that the Constituent Assembly deliberately reversed in 1950 by vesting residuary powers in the Union Parliament. Present this as the constitutional-history contrast; detailed doctrine belongs to the Polity owner.
- **Limit/caution:** ✅ The federation **never came into being** because the princes did not accede; always state that the central provisions remained on paper.

### 8.6 Bank C — Franchise, communal representation and fiscal change

- **Evidence:** ✅ The electorate was enlarged to **30 million**, but high property qualifications enfranchised only about **10 per cent of the Indian population**; ✅ in rural India the vote went to the **rich and middle peasants**, presumed to be the main constituency of Congress politics. ⚠️ **D.A. Low** suspects the Act was a ploy to corrode the Congress's support base and tie these classes to the Raj, writing that a "competition for the allegiance of the dominant peasant communities" lay at the heart of the Congress–Raj conflict at this stage. ✅ **Separate electorates were provided for Muslims** and **reserved seats for the Scheduled Castes** — a new term for the "depressed classes" — in the provincial and central legislatures. ✅ Another feature was the **transfer of financial control from London to New Delhi**, meeting a long-standing demand for fiscal autonomy.
- **Significance:** The franchise design is the topic's most analytically rewarding fact — it shows constitutional reform being used as a competitive political instrument rather than as a concession of principle.
- **Limit/caution:** ⚠️ The Low interpretation is an argument about intention; present it as a historian's reading. Note the file's own figure of roughly 14 per cent alongside the source's 10 per cent of population — cite the range or the source you are using, and do not present either as an uncontested single number.

### 8.7 Bank D — The Congress ministries, 1937–39

- **Evidence:** ✅ Congress criticised the Act — Nehru described it as **"a machine with strong brakes but no engine"** — and still contested the **1937 elections**, forming ministries in **eight of eleven provinces**: Bombay, Madras, United Provinces, Bihar, Central Provinces, Orissa, and later NWFP and Assam. ✅ The ministries released political prisoners, expanded civil liberties, promoted education and attempted agrarian and tenancy reform within limited powers. ✅ The **Federal Court began functioning in 1937**; **Burma was separated from India** under the Act's scheme in the same year.
- **Significance:** Two years of office gave the Congress its first administrative experience, an argument for its national representativeness, and a demonstrable record on civil liberties — assets it carried into the endgame and into 1947.
- **Limit/caution:** ✅ Achievements were bounded by finance, by governors' powers and by the Act's protections for civil servants and business; and ✅ Muslim League propaganda about alleged **"Congress atrocities"** in these provinces deepened Congress–League estrangement. State both.

### 8.8 Bank E — Resignation and the road to 1940

- **Evidence:** ✅ Congress ministries **resigned in October–November 1939** after Viceroy **Linlithgow declared India at war without consulting Indian leaders**; ✅ the Muslim League observed **"Deliverance Day" in December 1939**; ✅ the **Lahore Resolution of March 1940** demanded separate Muslim states.
- **Significance:** The causal chain is tight and examinable — resignation removed the Congress from provincial power, the League filled the political space, and within four months the demand shifted from safeguards within India to separation from it.
- **Limit/caution:** ⚠️ Do not present the resignation as the cause of Partition; it is one link in a chain that includes 1909, 1916, 1932, 1937 and the war years. And note the Lahore Resolution's wording — it spoke of "independent states", not of "Pakistan" (`basic/17`).

### 8.9 Counter-evidence, balance and variation

- **Genuine advances:** all-department responsible government in provinces, a much larger electorate, an Indian Federal Court, fiscal control moved to New Delhi, and new provinces (Sind, Orissa).
- **Against a purely negative reading:** the 1935 Act supplied the administrative and much of the textual framework that the Constituent Assembly later adapted, including the federal division of powers and emergency architecture.
- **Variation:** ministry performance differed markedly between provinces; NWFP and Assam came later and on different terms, and non-Congress ministries elsewhere had their own records.
- ⚠️ The Act "could hardly satisfy anybody and was criticised equally by Congress as well as the Muslim League" — use this to show that dissatisfaction was general, not sectional.

### 8.10 Verdict scaffolds

- **Statute verdict:** "The 1935 Act gave Indians the provinces and kept the state: defence, external affairs, internal security, residuary power and the right to suspend the constitution all remained where they had been."
- **Federation verdict:** "The federation failed by design, not by accident — it required the princes to legislate away their own treaty privileges in exchange for a share of a legislature that would have protected them anyway."
- **Ministries verdict:** "Two years in office proved the Congress could govern and simultaneously taught the League what Congress government would mean — the same evidence produced opposite conclusions in the two parties."

### 8.11 Factual-risk cautions

- The Act **abolished** provincial dyarchy and **proposed** central dyarchy; the federation **never came into force**.
- **Defence, external affairs and internal security** remained under the Viceroy's control; **residuary powers** vested in the Governor-General.
- Federation required accession by **more than 50 per cent** of the princely states via **Instruments of Accession**.
- Princely nominees would have held **30–40 per cent** of central legislature seats.
- Electorate = **30 million**, roughly **10–14 per cent** of the population depending on the measure used; do not assert a single figure as certain.
- Congress ministries: **eight of eleven** provinces, **1937**; resignation **October–November 1939**; Deliverance Day **December 1939**; Lahore Resolution **March 1940**.
- The **Reserve Bank of India** came from the RBI Act **1934**, not from this Act.
