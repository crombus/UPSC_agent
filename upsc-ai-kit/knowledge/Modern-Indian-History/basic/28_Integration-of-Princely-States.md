## 1. Snapshot & core idea

**Foundation — political unity had to be built after legal independence.**

- ✅ At independence there were **more than 560 princely states**; exact counts vary with the classification of estates and states. British paramountcy **lapsed** rather than passing automatically to India.
- ✅ **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel**, as Home and States Minister, and **V.P. Menon**, through the States Department, led the integration process.
- ✅ The **Instrument of Accession** allowed states to accede on three subjects: **Defence, External Affairs and Communications**.
- ✅ Chandra presents integration as a **two-stage process**: rapid accession by August 1947, followed by merger, administrative consolidation and democratisation.
- ⚠️ Patel's method is often described as an appeal to patriotism backed by firm pressure, or an **"iron fist in a velvet glove"**.

**Foundation — Junagadh, Hyderabad and Kashmir were the key problem states.**

- ⚠️ **Junagadh** acceded to Pakistan despite being Hindu-majority and geographically non-contiguous; a plebiscite in February 1948 favoured India.
- ⚠️ **Hyderabad** under the Nizam sought independence; Razakar violence and stalemate led to **Operation Polo** in September 1948.
- ⚠️ **Kashmir** under Maharaja Hari Singh hesitated; after Pakistani tribal invasion in October 1947, the Instrument of Accession was signed and Indian troops were sent.
- ⚠️ The **Constituent Assembly**, presided over by Dr Rajendra Prasad with B.R. Ambedkar chairing the Drafting Committee, adopted the Constitution on 26 November 1949.

## 5. UPSC Traps

> 🔑 Trap: **Paramountcy lapsed**; it did not pass automatically to the Indian Union.

- ❌ All princely states joined India automatically on 15 August 1947. → They had to sign instruments of accession and later merge/integrate.
- ❌ Instrument of Accession transferred all subjects immediately. → It initially covered **Defence, External Affairs and Communications**.
- ❌ Junagadh, Hyderabad and Kashmir had identical problems. → Each involved a different mix of ruler's choice, people's composition, geography and security.
- ❌ Operation Polo relates to Kashmir. → It relates to **Hyderabad**.
- ❌ India became a republic on 15 August 1947. → India became independent then; it became a **Republic on 26 January 1950**.

### 8.2 Qualified thesis options

- **Sequencing thesis:** *Integration succeeded because it was sequenced correctly — accession on three subjects first, when the princes' bargaining position was weakest and the alternative was chaos; merger and democratisation afterwards, when refusal was no longer an option.*
- **Leverage thesis:** *Patel's achievement was to convert a moment of maximum uncertainty into maximum leverage: with paramountcy lapsing and no state able to survive alone, the offer of a limited accession with privy purses and dignities was more attractive than an independence no one could defend.*
- **Two-pressures thesis:** *Accession was not purely a transaction between the States Department and the princes; the States' Peoples' movements (`basic/23`) had already created popular pressure inside many states, so rulers negotiated with the Union while facing their own subjects.*
- **Differentiated-crisis thesis:** *Junagadh, Hyderabad and Kashmir were not three versions of one problem but three different failures of the accession formula — a ruler at odds with his population, a ruler seeking sovereignty, and a ruler paralysed by choice — and each required a different instrument.*

### 8.4 Named evidence units (claim → evidence → significance → limit)

**A. The problem created by lapse**
- **Claim:** Independence created a territorial crisis that independence alone could not solve.
- **Evidence:** ✅ At independence there were **more than 560 princely states** — totals such as 562 and 565 differ by classification, not by fact — and **British paramountcy lapsed** rather than passing to India.
- **Significance:** Lapse meant that roughly a third of the subcontinent's territory had, in strict law, no successor sovereign; the Union's claim had to be constructed rather than inherited.
- **Limit/caution:** ✅ Do not treat 562 and 565 as rival historical claims; state "more than 560" and note that counts vary with the classification of estates.

**B. The accession formula**
- **Claim:** The instrument's genius was its narrowness.
- **Evidence:** ✅ The **Instrument of Accession** allowed states to accede on only three subjects — **Defence, External Affairs and Communications**; ✅ **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel** as Home and States Minister and **V.P. Menon** through the **States Department** conducted the process; ⚠️ Patel's method is often described as an appeal to patriotism backed by firm pressure — an "iron fist in a velvet glove".
- **Significance:** By demanding only what no state could exercise anyway — defence, foreign relations and communications — the formula made accession look like a small concession while transferring everything that constitutes sovereignty in practice.
- **Limit/caution:** ⚠️ The privy-purse and dignity guarantees that accompanied accession were a real cost, resolved only much later (`basic/34`); note that the settlement was not free.

**C. Two stages, not one**
- **Claim:** Accession and integration were distinct achievements.
- **Evidence:** ✅ Chandra presents integration as a **two-stage process** — rapid accession by August 1947, followed by **merger, administrative consolidation and democratisation**.
- **Significance:** The second stage is what converted a patchwork of acceding units into administrable provinces with elections, courts and civil services — and it is the stage most answers omit.
- **Limit/caution:** Democratisation inside former states was uneven and often slower than in British India.

**D. The three hard cases compared**

| | ✅ Junagadh | ✅ Hyderabad | ✅ Kashmir |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ruler's choice | Acceded to Pakistan | Sought independence | Hesitated |
| Population | Hindu-majority, at odds with the ruler | Largely Hindu, ruled by the Nizam | Muslim-majority under a Hindu Maharaja |
| Geography | Non-contiguous with Pakistan | Landlocked inside India | Contiguous with both dominions |
| Precipitating factor | Ruler–subject mismatch | Razakar violence and stalemate | Tribal invasion from Pakistan, **October 1947** |
| Instrument used | **Plebiscite, February 1948**, favouring India | **Operation Polo / Police Action, September 1948** | Instrument of Accession signed; Indian troops sent |
| Residue | Settled | Settled, with a contested aftermath | India–Pakistan war and the UN issue |

- **Analytical use:** the four-variable grid (ruler, population, contiguity, security) is the comparison engine; it explains why three different instruments were used and blocks the "all three were the same" answer.
- **Limit/caution:** ⚠️ Handle Kashmir with strict factual restraint — record the invasion, the accession and the ensuing war and UN reference; do not narrate contested political claims or later constitutional controversies here.

**E. Completion in the Constitution**
- **Claim:** Territorial integration was completed by constitutional rather than administrative means.
- **Evidence:** ⚠️ The **Constituent Assembly**, presided over by **Dr Rajendra Prasad** with **B.R. Ambedkar** chairing the Drafting Committee, adopted the Constitution on **26 November 1949**; it came into force on **26 January 1950**.
- **Significance:** A single citizenship, a single judiciary and uniform fundamental rights dissolved the legal distinction between subjects of princes and subjects of the Union — the point at which accession became nationhood.
- **Limit/caution:** Constitutional doctrine belongs to the Polity owner; keep this file's treatment historical.

### 8.5 Mechanism — why integration succeeded

```text
Paramountcy lapses -> no state can guarantee its own defence, currency or communications
   + States' Peoples' movements (basic/23) create internal democratic pressure
   + Partition violence makes isolation dangerous
   -> narrow three-subject accession offered, with dignities and privy purses
   -> rapid accession by August 1947 removes the option of collective princely resistance
   -> merger and administrative consolidation, then elections
   -> Constitution 1950: single citizenship dissolves princely subjecthood
```

### 8.6 Counter-evidence, balance and caution

- **Against a purely heroic reading:** integration used coercion where persuasion failed (Hyderabad), and the settlement's financial concessions to princes drew criticism from the outset.
- **Against a purely coercive reading:** the overwhelming majority acceded without force, and popular movements inside many states favoured accession.
- **Variation:** large states negotiated individually; small states were grouped into unions or merged into neighbouring provinces — the process was not uniform.
- ⚠️ Distinguish sharply between the **historical fact** of accession and later **constitutional or political controversies**; this owner records the former.

### 8.7 Verdict scaffolds

- **Statecraft verdict:** "Integration succeeded because it asked for little at the moment when refusing anything was impossible, and asked for the rest once refusal had ceased to be an option."
- **Comparative verdict:** "Junagadh was solved by counting people, Hyderabad by using force and Kashmir by neither — which is why only the third remained a question."
- **Constitutional verdict:** "The princes acceded to a Union in 1947 and their subjects became citizens of a Republic in 1950; the second event, not the first, completed integration."

### 8.8 Factual-risk cautions

- Paramountcy **lapsed**; it was not transferred.
- The Instrument of Accession covered **Defence, External Affairs and Communications** only.
- **Operation Polo = Hyderabad, September 1948**; the **Junagadh plebiscite = February 1948**; the **Kashmir tribal invasion = October 1947**.
- India became **independent** on 15 August 1947 and a **Republic** on 26 January 1950.
- State "**more than 560**" princely states; do not assert 562 or 565 as the correct figure.
- Do not state privy-purse amounts, troop numbers or casualty figures for any accession episode.
