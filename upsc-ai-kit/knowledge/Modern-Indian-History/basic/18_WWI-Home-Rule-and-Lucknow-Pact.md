## 1. Snapshot & core idea

**Foundation — war created both hope and hardship.**

- ✅ India supported Britain in **World War I** with men, money and materials; Indian leaders initially expected that loyalty would be rewarded with constitutional reforms.
- ✅ Wartime conditions produced discontent: rising prices, heavier taxation, war loans, shortages and recruitment pressure made British rule appear more exploitative.
- ⚠️ The war internationalised Indian politics: slogans like democracy and self-determination sharpened the contradiction between British liberal claims and colonial rule in India.

**Foundation — Home Rule revived national agitation before Gandhi.**

- ✅ The **Home Rule Leagues** demanded self-government for India on the Irish Home Rule model, not immediate complete independence.
- ✅ Tilak's League and Besant's League used public meetings, pamphlets, newspapers, branches and local workers to carry nationalist politics beyond annual Congress sessions.
- ✅ The **Lucknow Pact** brought a double unity: reunion of Moderates and Extremists inside Congress, and cooperation between Congress and the Muslim League.
- ⚠️ The institutional cost of Congress-League unity was Congress acceptance of **separate electorates and weightage**. Historians dispute whether this was a necessary tactical bargain or a step that entrenched communal representation.

## 5. UPSC Traps

> 🔑 Trap: Do not merge the two Home Rule Leagues; UPSC likes the leader-date-place distinction.

- ❌ Tilak and Besant founded one common league. → ✅ They founded **separate leagues** in 1916, with different organisational zones.
- ❌ Home Rule meant Purna Swaraj. → ✅ It meant **self-government within the Empire**, inspired by the Irish model.
- ❌ Lucknow Pact only meant Hindu-Muslim unity. → ✅ It also meant **Moderate-Extremist reunion** inside Congress.
- ❌ Separate electorates began with the Lucknow Pact. → ✅ They were introduced by **Morley-Minto reforms, 1909**; Congress accepted them in the Pact.
- ❌ Montagu Declaration granted immediate responsible government. → ✅ It promised **gradual** development of responsible government.

### 8.2 Qualified thesis options

- **Contradiction thesis:** *The war destroyed the moral basis of British rule in India: a government fighting for self-determination in Europe while conscripting resources from a subject population could no longer justify indefinite tutelage, and Indian politics moved from petition to demand.*
- **Organisational-bridge thesis:** *The Home Rule Leagues mattered less for what they demanded than for what they built — permanent branch networks, continuous propaganda and local political workers between Congress sessions — which is precisely the machinery Gandhi inherited in 1920.*
- **Designed-to-fail thesis (for 1919):** *Dyarchy conceded responsibility without capacity: the transferred departments were those with the least political weight and the smallest funds, while law and order and finance stayed under bureaucratic control, so Indian ministers were set up to be discredited by their own failures.*
- **Ambivalent-pact thesis:** *Lucknow was simultaneously the movement's greatest early unification and its deepest concession, and the two cannot be separated — the unity was real, and it was purchased in the currency of communal representation.*

### 8.4 Named evidence units (claim → evidence → significance → limit)

**A. War: expectation and extraction**
- **Claim:** Indian support was conditional and the condition was not met.
- **Evidence:** ✅ India supplied men, money and materials, and leaders expected constitutional reward; wartime **inflation, heavier taxation, war loans, shortages and recruitment pressure** produced widespread discontent.
- **Significance:** The gap between contribution and concession is the causal engine of 1919–22; it explains why the post-war repression of Rowlatt was experienced as betrayal rather than as ordinary coercion (`basic/19`).
- **Limit/caution:** Do not quantify Indian troop numbers, war contributions or price rises without verified figures.

**B. Home Rule Leagues: two organisations, one method**
- **Claim:** The Leagues professionalised nationalist propaganda.
- **Evidence:** ✅ **Tilak's League (April 1916)**, strong in Maharashtra, Karnataka, the Central Provinces and Berar; ✅ **Besant's League (September 1916)** from Adyar/Madras with **George Arundale** and **B.P. Wadia**; both used newspapers, pamphlets, lectures, public meetings and **branch networks**; ✅ Besant's **internment in June 1917** intensified sympathy, and she became **Congress president in 1917**.
- **Significance:** Internment converted a moderate demand into a national cause and demonstrated that repression could enlarge a movement — a lesson directly applied after 1919.
- **Limit/caution:** Home Rule meant **self-government within the Empire** on the Irish model, not Purna Swaraj; and the two Leagues had separate organisations and zones.

**C. Lucknow 1916: the double unity**
- **Claim:** Lucknow reunited two divisions at once.
- **Evidence:** ✅ The **Lucknow Congress (December 1916)** reunited Moderates and Extremists, readmitting **Tilak**; the **Congress–League Pact** produced a joint reform scheme; ✅ the price was Congress acceptance of **separate electorates and weightage**.
- **Significance:** For the first time the two largest political organisations presented a common constitutional demand, which is what made the Montagu Declaration politically necessary.
- **Limit/caution:** ⚠️ Historians dispute whether the bargain was a necessary tactical concession or a step that entrenched communal representation; present the dispute rather than resolving it silently.

**D. The Montagu Declaration, 20 August 1917**
- **Claim:** The Declaration changed the terms of debate permanently.
- **Evidence:** ✅ It promised increasing association of Indians in administration and the **gradual development of responsible government** in India as an integral part of the Empire.
- **Significance:** Once "responsible government" was an avowed British objective, every later argument was about pace and scope, not about principle — the constitutional ratchet on which 1919, 1935 and 1947 all turn.
- **Limit/caution:** It promised gradual development, not immediate responsible government; and it kept India within the Empire.

**E. Government of India Act 1919 — dyarchy (routed Prelims demand)**
- **Claim:** Dyarchy was a mechanism for conceding the appearance of responsibility while retaining the substance of control.
- **Evidence:** ✅ Certain provincial functions were **transferred** to ministers responsible to the legislative assemblies while others were **reserved** for bureaucratic control. ✅ The departments actually transferred were of **less political weight — education, health, agriculture and local bodies — with limited funds**, which "would invariably discredit the Indian ministers", while **more vital departments such as law and order and finance were kept under official control**. ✅ Provincial governors held **veto and certificate powers**, as did the Viceroy through the certificate procedure for pushing through rejected bills; ✅ there was **parity of representation between Europeans and Indians in the provincial executive councils**; ✅ revenue resources were divided, with **land revenue going to the provinces and income tax remaining with the centre**.
- **Significance:** This is the exact content a "reserved and transferred subjects" question requires, and it also supplies the analytical point — the *selection* of transferred subjects, not the principle of dyarchy, is what guaranteed failure.
- **Limit/caution:** Do not list every 1919 provision; the marks lie in the funding-and-blame mechanism. Detailed constitutional mechanics belong to the Polity owner.

**F. Franchise, community and women's representation (routed Prelims demand)**
- **Claim:** The 1919 reforms enlarged the electorate and simultaneously hardened its communal segmentation.
- **Evidence:** ✅ Electorates were **considerably enlarged — to 5.5 million for the provinces and 1.5 million for the imperial legislature**; ✅ despite theoretical criticism of separate electorates in the **Montagu–Chelmsford Report**, communal representation and reservation were **retained and considerably extended** — **Sikhs** received separate electorates, **non-Brahman seats were reserved in Madras**, and the **"depressed classes" were offered nominated seats** at all levels. ⚠️ On women's political rights, the 1919 settlement did **not itself enfranchise women**; it left the removal of the sex disqualification to provincial legislatures, which acted progressively over the following years, and the substantial extension of the women's franchise came with the **Government of India Act 1935** (see `basic/24`) — still on restricted property, educational and status qualifications rather than on universal adult suffrage.
- **Significance:** Explains why "representation" advanced along two contradictory axes at once — wider in numbers, narrower in the identity through which the vote was exercised — and gives a precise, non-fabricated answer on women's franchise.
- **Limit/caution:** Distinguish **separate electorates** (Muslims, Sikhs) from **reserved seats** (non-Brahmans in Madras) and from **nominated seats** (depressed classes). ⚠️ Do not state a year or a figure for women's enfranchisement in any specific province without verified evidence.

**G. Renaming of the Home Rule League (routed Prelims demand)**
- **Evidence:** ⚠️ The routed demand records that the **All-India Home Rule League was renamed *Swarajya Sabha* in 1920**. The 2018 official key is not held locally and no source book in this repository narrates the renaming.
- **Safe handling:** Treat the renaming and its date as recorded by the routing ledger, and connect it analytically to what *is* verified — that by 1920 the Home Rule organisations were being absorbed into the Gandhian Congress and the language of "Home Rule" was giving way to "swaraj" (`basic/20`). Do not assert the presiding personality or the constitutional details of the renaming without verified evidence.

### 8.6 Counter-evidence, balance and variation

- **Genuine gains to concede:** a much larger electorate, elected majorities in provincial legislatures, Indian ministers holding portfolios for the first time, and financial devolution to provinces.
- **Against a purely cynical reading:** provincial politics under the 1919 Act produced a generation of Indian legislators and administrators whose experience mattered in 1937 and after 1947.
- **Regional variation:** dyarchy operated very differently between provinces depending on the governor, the ministry and the strength of Swarajist obstruction (`basic/21`).
- **Nationalist response:** the reforms satisfied few — ⚠️ Bipan Chandra records that the 1919 scheme of dyarchy "satisfied few" — which is why the post-war agitation moved outside the councils.

### 8.7 Verdict scaffolds

- **War verdict:** "The First World War did not create Indian nationalism; it removed the arguments with which Britain had been postponing it."
- **Home Rule verdict:** "The Leagues built the roads on which Gandhian mass politics travelled — permanent branches, continuous propaganda and local workers — even though their own demand fell far short of independence."
- **Lucknow verdict:** "The Pact solved the problem of nationalist division by institutionalising the problem of communal representation; both halves of that sentence are required for a critical answer."
- **1919 verdict:** "Dyarchy failed not because Indians could not govern but because they were given the departments that could not succeed."

### 8.8 Factual-risk cautions

- Tilak's League = **April 1916**; Besant's = **September 1916**; they were **separate** organisations.
- The Montagu Declaration is **20 August 1917** and promised **gradual** responsible government.
- Separate electorates began in **1909**; the Lucknow Pact **accepted** them; the 1919 reforms **extended** the principle to Sikhs and added reserved and nominated seats for other groups.
- Transferred subjects = education, health, agriculture, local bodies (low weight, low funds); reserved = law and order, finance (high weight, official control).
- The 1919 Act did **not** enfranchise women directly; the substantial extension came under the **1935** Act, and neither introduced universal adult suffrage.
- The electorate figures (**5.5 million** provincial, **1.5 million** imperial) are the ones attested in the repository's source material; do not substitute other numbers.
