## 1. Snapshot & core idea

**Foundation — the Cabinet Mission scheme collapsed under communal mistrust.**

- ✅ The Cabinet Mission Plan had tried to preserve unity through a weak centre and provincial grouping, but Congress and the League interpreted grouping differently.
- ✅ **Direct Action Day (16 August 1946)** led to severe communal violence in Calcutta and widened distrust between Congress, League and communities.
- ✅ Violence in **Noakhali, Bihar and Punjab** made the demand for quick settlement more urgent and more tragic.
- ⚠️ By early 1947, the question shifted from whether India would remain united to how power could be transferred while limiting civil war.

**Foundation — Partition was accepted as the price of transfer and a strong centre.**

- ✅ **Attlee's statement of 20 February 1947** declared that Britain would transfer power not later than June 1948.
- ✅ **Mountbatten's 3 June Plan** accepted partition, the division of Punjab and Bengal, referendums in NWFP and Sylhet, and boundary commissions.
- ✅ The **Indian Independence Act (July 1947)** legally created two dominions, India and Pakistan, from **15 August 1947** and ended British paramountcy over princely states.
- ⚠️ Partition produced massive migration and communal carnage; estimates commonly place displacement around **10–15 million**, but exact numbers should not be stated with false precision.

## 4. Must-Know Facts (Prelims)

- ✅ **Attlee's statement** came on **20 February 1947**. ⚠️ Distinguish it from **Attlee's earlier statement of 15 March 1946** ("a minority will not be allowed to place a veto on the progress of the majority", `basic/26` §8.6): the 1946 statement rejected the League's veto and preceded the Cabinet Mission; the 1947 statement set the withdrawal deadline.
- ✅ **Lord Mountbatten** was the last Viceroy and arrived in March 1947.
- ✅ **3 June Plan** is the Mountbatten Plan that accepted Partition.
- ✅ **Indian Independence Act (July 1947)** legally created the dominions of India and Pakistan.
- ✅ **15 August 1947** is the date of Indian independence and transfer of power.
- ✅ **Cyril Radcliffe** headed the boundary commissions for Punjab and Bengal.
- ✅ The Act provided for the **lapse of British paramountcy** over princely states.
- ✅ Nehru delivered the **"Tryst with Destiny"** speech at independence; Gandhi worked for communal peace in Bengal.

## 5. UPSC Traps

> 🔑 Trap: **3 June Plan = political plan for Partition**; **Indian Independence Act = legal enactment**; **Radcliffe = boundary line**.

- ❌ Cabinet Mission itself partitioned India. → It tried to preserve a united India through grouping.
- ❌ Mountbatten Plan was passed by Parliament as the Act. → The plan preceded the **Indian Independence Act**.
- ❌ Radcliffe was an Indian nationalist leader. → Cyril Radcliffe was a British lawyer appointed to chair boundary commissions.
- ❌ British paramountcy passed automatically to India. → Paramountcy **lapsed**, creating the princely-state problem.
- ❌ Gandhi celebrated independence in Delhi. → Gandhi was in Bengal working for communal peace.

### 8.2 Qualified thesis options

- **Cumulative-causation thesis:** *Partition was not decided in 1947; it became the residual option after four decades in which the political system was progressively organised around religious community — separate electorates in 1909, their acceptance in 1916, their extension in 1919 and 1932, and their conversion into a claim to nationhood after 1940.*
- **Contingency thesis:** *Structure made partition possible and contingency made it happen: the Congress's absence from politics after 1942, the League's transformation into a mass party by 1946, the collapse of the Cabinet Mission's ambiguity, and the accelerated timetable of 1947 each closed an option that had been open.*
- **Strong-centre thesis:** *The Congress leadership accepted partition because the alternative on the table — the Cabinet Mission's weak centre with compulsory grouping — was judged to make an ungovernable India, so partition was chosen as the price of a state capable of acting.*
- **Two-registers thesis:** *August 1947 must be assessed in two registers that do not reconcile: a constitutional triumph without precedent in the colonial world, and a humanitarian catastrophe of displacement and communal killing that the transfer's speed and its unpoliced boundary directly aggravated.*

### 8.4 Bank A — Causes at three levels

| Level | Factor | Evidence anchor |
|---|---|---|
| **Long-term structure** | Political representation organised by religious community | Separate electorates **1909**, accepted at **Lucknow 1916**, extended **1919**, **Communal Award 1932** (`basic/17`, `basic/18`, `basic/22`) |
| **Long-term structure** | Uneven modern development and elite competition for office and representation | `basic/17` |
| **Medium-term politics** | The 1937 ministries and the League's exclusion from power; "Congress atrocities" propaganda | `basic/24` |
| **Medium-term politics** | The **Lahore Resolution, March 1940** — a claim to separate states, not initially to a named "Pakistan" | `basic/17`, `basic/24` |
| **Medium-term politics** | The Congress's absence from legal politics, 1942–45, while the League organised | `basic/25` |
| **Short-term contingency** | The **Cabinet Mission's** grouping ambiguity and its collapse; the 1945–46 elections | `basic/26` §8.6–8.8 |
| **Short-term contingency** | The **failed negotiations of 1944–45** — the C.R. Formula and the Gandhi–Jinnah talks (September 1944) and the Wavell Plan/Simla Conference (25 June – 14 July 1945) — which exhausted the pre-electoral options for a united settlement | `basic/26` §8.6 |
| **Short-term contingency** | The **electoral verdict of 1945–46**: ✅ Congress **over 90 per cent of the general seats** provincially and **80.9 per cent of general-constituency votes**; ✅ Muslim League **74.7 per cent of the votes in Muslim constituencies**, **93 per cent of Muslim votes and 119 of 250 seats in Bengal**, **75 of 175 seats in Punjab**; ✅ franchise restricted to about **10 per cent of the population** | `basic/26` §8.7 |
| **Short-term contingency** | Communal violence in **Noakhali, Bihar and Punjab**, which made a rapid settlement seem urgent | ✅ This file, §1 |
| **Short-term contingency** | ⚠️ The accelerated transfer timetable of 1947 and the decision to publish the boundary award only after the transfer | ✅ Radcliffe's role, this file, §5 |

- **Analytical use:** the three-level structure is the single most reusable device in this topic; it prevents both the "ancient hatred" answer and the "British conspiracy" answer.

**Electoral mandate is not inevitability — the discipline required in every causal answer:**

| Claim a weak answer makes | What the evidence actually supports (`basic/26` §8.7) |
|---|---|
| "Indians voted for Partition in 1946" | ✅ Voting was by **separate electorates** on a franchise of about **10 per cent of the population**; there was no territorial question on any ballot |
| "The 1946 result was a plebiscite for Pakistan" | ✅ This was the **League's own framing**, reported by **Anita Inder Singh** as a claim the League *presented*; it is a fact about campaign strategy, not about the constitutional character of the poll |
| "The result settled what Pakistan would be" | ✅ **Ayesha Jalal** holds that the demand was never coherently defined between 1940 and the Cabinet Mission; no boundary, constitution or population arrangement was decided by the election |
| "Muslim opinion was uniform after 1946" | ✅ The League won only **75 of 175 Punjab seats** and was excluded from office there by a Congress–Akali–Unionist coalition; **Azad** led the Congress; nationalist Muslims contested; the KPP and Unionists had been marginalised by political pressure |
| "Partition became inevitable in 1946" | ✅ **Both parties accepted the Cabinet Mission scheme in some form in June–July 1946**, *after* the elections; unity remained constitutionally available until the grouping interpretations were made irreconcilable and the Interim Government was formed without the League |

- **What the verdict *did* change (state this to avoid the opposite error):** ⚠️ it removed the Congress's argument that the League did not represent Muslims — the argument on which Simla had turned in 1945 — and made the League the only electorally certified representative of the Muslim electorate. After 1946 no scheme that excluded the League could be implemented, which is why the Cabinet Mission's compromise took the grouping form and why its collapse left no third option. **Mandate, not inevitability, is the correct formulation.**

### 8.5 Bank B — The three instruments of 1947 (routed distinction)

| Instrument | Nature | Function | Common error to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| ✅ **3 June Plan** | Political plan | Set out the terms on which partition and transfer would proceed | It was **not** an Act of Parliament |
| ✅ **Indian Independence Act (July 1947)** | Legal enactment | Created the two dominions of India and Pakistan from **15 August 1947** and provided for the **lapse of British paramountcy** over the princely states | It followed, and did not precede, the political plan |
| ✅ **Radcliffe boundary commissions** | Administrative/technical | Drew the boundary lines in Punjab and Bengal under **Cyril Radcliffe**, a British lawyer | Radcliffe was **not** an Indian leader; the award was a boundary decision, not the partition decision |

- **Significance:** keeping the three distinct answers a common exam demand precisely, and it also carries an argument — the political decision, the legal transfer and the boundary determination were separated in time and authority, which is one reason the boundary took effect in an administrative vacuum.
- **Paramountcy point:** ✅ paramountcy **lapsed** rather than passing to India, which created the princely-state problem that `basic/28` resolves.

### 8.6 Bank C — Consequences

- **Constitutional:** ✅ two dominions from **15 August 1947**; ✅ **Nehru's "Tryst with Destiny"** speech marked the transfer, while ✅ **Gandhi was in Bengal working for communal peace**, not celebrating in Delhi — a fact that itself expresses the two registers of that day.
- **Human:** ⚠️ massive migration and communal carnage; displacement is **commonly estimated at around 10–15 million**, and **exact numbers must not be stated with false precision**. Deaths are contested across a wide range and should be described qualitatively.
- **Institutional (⚠️, developed elsewhere):** refugee rehabilitation as the new state's first administrative test; minority questions on both sides; a divided army, civil service and treasury; disrupted river, rail and commercial systems; and the beginning of a permanent security relationship with Pakistan (see `basic/29`, `basic/32`).
- **Political:** ⚠️ the experience of partition strengthened the constitutional case for a strong centre and for secularism as a state commitment rather than a mere sentiment.

### 8.7 Responsibility — how to handle it safely

| Actor | Contribution to the outcome | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| ⚠️ British policy | Created and repeatedly extended communal representation; accelerated the 1947 timetable | Do not reduce Partition to a British plot; the demand had Indian political force |
| ⚠️ Muslim League | Converted minority safeguards into a claim to separate nationhood after 1940 | Do not attribute the demand to a religious community as such |
| ⚠️ Congress | Accepted separate electorates in 1916; underestimated the League after 1937; accepted partition in 1947 to secure a strong centre | Do not present acceptance as enthusiasm; the leadership faced a collapsing situation |
| ⚠️ Communal organisations on all sides | Hardened community boundaries and legitimised violence | Assign agency to organisations and leaderships, never to communities |

> **Answer rule:** a "who was responsible" question is best answered by refusing single-actor blame and specifying each actor's contribution and constraint.

### 8.8 Counter-evidence, balance and caution

- **Against inevitability:** the Cabinet Mission scheme was accepted in principle by both parties in 1946 — ✅ the League on **6 June 1946** and the Congress in going into the Constituent Assembly — so unity remained constitutionally available within eighteen months of partition, *after* the electoral verdict. The chain that closed it is dated and specific (`basic/26` §8.7): ✅ Nehru's AICC statement of **7 July 1946** → ✅ Jinnah's withdrawal on **29 July 1946** → ✅ Direct Action and the Calcutta violence of **16 August 1946** → ✅ the Congress-only Interim Government of **2 September 1946**.
- **Against contingency alone:** four decades of communal representation had already made a common electorate politically unimaginable to the League, and the pre-electoral negotiations had already failed on the same question — ✅ the **Gandhi–Jinnah talks (September 1944)** and the ✅ **Simla Conference (25 June – 14 July 1945)**, which broke on Jinnah's claim that the League alone could nominate all Muslim members of the Executive Council (`basic/26` §8.6).
- **Regional variation:** Punjab and Bengal experienced partition as territorial division and mass violence; the United Provinces, Bombay and the south experienced it as a political settlement and a minority question.
- ⚠️ **Numerical discipline is non-negotiable here.** Do not state death tolls. Cite displacement only as a broad range with the range explicitly flagged as an estimate.

### 8.9 Verdict scaffolds

- **Causal verdict:** "Partition was made possible by decades of communal representation, made likely by the political vacuum of 1942–45, made unavoidable in negotiation by the 1945–46 electoral verdict that certified the League as the Muslim electorate's representative, and made certain by the failure of the one scheme designed to avoid it."
- **Instruments verdict:** "The 3 June Plan decided it, the Independence Act enacted it, and the Radcliffe award located it — three different acts of authority whose separation contributed to the disorder of August 1947."
- **Assessment verdict:** "Independence and Partition are the same event described from two positions; an answer that records only one of them has answered only half the question."

### 8.10 Factual-risk cautions

- **3 June Plan ≠ Indian Independence Act ≠ Radcliffe Award** — political, legal and boundary instruments respectively.
- The Act is of **July 1947** and took effect on **15 August 1947**; paramountcy **lapsed**.
- The **Cabinet Mission did not partition India**; it attempted to preserve unity through grouping.
- The **Lahore Resolution (1940)** spoke of independent states, not of a named "Pakistan".
- **Radcliffe** was a British lawyer chairing the boundary commissions.
- **The 1945–46 elections were not a plebiscite on Partition.** Use the figures only as given in `basic/26` §8.7; do not state central-legislature seat totals (not supported by the source books held here), do not describe the franchise as universal, and do not convert a vote share into a seat share.
- **Gandhi was in Bengal**, not in Delhi, at independence.
- Displacement: **broad estimates of about 10–15 million**; deaths: **do not quantify**.
