## 1. Snapshot & core idea

**Foundation — the war crisis exposed the limits of colonial constitutionalism.**

- ✅ In **1939**, India was declared a belligerent in the Second World War without the consent of elected Indian representatives, proving that provincial autonomy under the 1935 Act was subordinate to imperial authority.
- ✅ The **Congress ministries resigned** because they would not support a war for democracy abroad while India remained denied democracy at home.
- ✅ The **August Offer (1940)** promised dominion status as an eventual goal, expansion of the Viceroy's Executive Council and protection of minority interests, but it did not concede immediate responsible government.
- ⚠️ The Congress rejection reflected a strategic shift: Indians were no longer satisfied with future promises; the demand was for real power in the present.

**Foundation — Quit India was the final great mass movement before freedom.**

- ✅ The **Cripps Mission (1942)** offered dominion status after the war and a constitution-making body; a British Indian province could refuse the new constitution and retain its existing position, while princely states would join through their rulers' decisions. Congress rejected the lack of immediate responsible government and the danger to unity.
- ✅ Gandhi criticised the offer as a **"post-dated cheque"** because it promised freedom after the war but retained British control during the crisis.
- ✅ On **8 August 1942**, the AICC at Bombay adopted the Quit India resolution; Gandhi gave the slogan **"Do or Die"**.
- ✅ With top leaders arrested on **9 August 1942**, the movement became a leaderless upsurge marked by strikes, protests, attacks on symbols of authority, underground activity and parallel governments.

## 4. Must-Know Facts (Prelims)

- ✅ **India was declared at war in 1939** without consulting Indian representatives.
- ✅ **Congress ministries resigned in 1939** over the war issue.
- ✅ **August Offer (1940)** was made by Viceroy Linlithgow and was rejected by Congress.
- ✅ **Individual Satyagraha** began in October 1940; **Vinoba Bhave** was first satyagrahi and **Jawaharlal Nehru** second.
- ✅ **Cripps Mission (1942)** was led by Sir Stafford Cripps and offered post-war dominion status.
- ✅ **Quit India** was adopted at **Gowalia Tank, Bombay, on 8 August 1942**.
- ✅ **Aruna Asaf Ali** is associated with hoisting the flag at Gowalia Tank; **Usha Mehta** is associated with the secret Congress Radio.
- ✅ **Parallel governments** emerged at Ballia, Tamluk and Satara.

## 5. UPSC Traps

> 🔑 Trap: **Cripps = 1942 offer before Quit India**; **Quit India = 8 August 1942 Bombay**; do not merge them into one event.

- ❌ Cripps offered immediate full independence. → It offered **post-war dominion status**, not immediate transfer of power.
- ❌ Gandhi's "post-dated cheque" remark refers to the August Offer. → It is associated with the **Cripps proposals**.
- ❌ Quit India was centrally directed throughout. → Leaders were arrested early; much of the movement became **spontaneous and local**.
- ❌ Only peaceful protest occurred in Quit India. → The movement included strikes, sabotage and attacks on state symbols, though Congress ideology remained non-violent.
- ❌ Muslim League and Communists led Quit India jointly with Congress. → The League stayed aloof and the Communists, after the Nazi attack on the USSR, adopted the **People's War** line.

### 8.2 Qualified thesis options

- **Compressed-causation thesis:** *Quit India was the product of a four-year accumulation — the unilateral declaration of war in 1939, the inadequacy of the August Offer, the failure of the Cripps Mission, the collapse of British prestige in Asia after the fall of Singapore and Burma, and severe wartime economic distress — reaching the point at which further negotiation appeared pointless.*
- **Decapitation thesis:** *The character of Quit India was determined by the government's decision to arrest the entire leadership within hours: what had been planned as a Gandhian mass campaign became a leaderless, regionally uneven, partly violent upsurge with underground direction and local parallel governments.*
- **Political-victory thesis:** *Quit India was defeated as an insurrection and decisive as a political fact — after 1942 the British governed India by force alone, and the question in London ceased to be whether to leave and became when and on what terms.*
- **Isolation thesis:** *The movement's most consequential weakness was political rather than military: with the League aloof, the Communists committed to the People's War line, and the princes and much of the propertied class loyal, the Congress fought 1942 alone — which shaped the balance of forces in 1946.*

### 8.4 Bank A — The causal chain (for "events that led to Quit India")

| Step | Evidence | Why it mattered |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ 1939 | India declared a belligerent **without the consent of elected Indian representatives**; Congress ministries resigned | Proved provincial autonomy was subordinate to imperial authority |
| ✅ 1940 | **August Offer** — dominion status as an eventual goal, expansion of the Viceroy's Executive Council, protection of minority interests, but no immediate responsible government | Congress rejection marked the shift from accepting promises to demanding present power |
| ⚠️ 1941–42 | Japanese advance; fall of Singapore and Burma; refugee influx and fear of invasion | Destroyed the assumption that British rule guaranteed security |
| ✅ 1942 | **Cripps Mission** — dominion status after the war and a constitution-making body; a British Indian province could **refuse the new constitution and retain its existing position**, and princely states would join through their rulers' decisions | Congress rejected both the absence of immediate responsible government and the danger to unity; Gandhi called it a **"post-dated cheque"** |
| ⚠️ 1942 | Wartime inflation, shortages, requisitioning and the "denial policy" in coastal Bengal | Converted political grievance into material distress |

- **Compression tip for 150 words:** name 1939, the August Offer, Cripps and the collapse of British prestige in Asia — four steps, one clause each — then move to results. Do not narrate.

### 8.5 Bank B — The resolution and the movement's character (routed demand)

- **Evidence:** ✅ On **8 August 1942** the AICC at **Bombay** adopted the **Quit India resolution**; Gandhi gave the call **"Do or Die"**. ✅ With the top leaders arrested on **9 August 1942**, the movement became a **leaderless upsurge** marked by strikes, protests, attacks on symbols of authority, underground activity and **parallel governments**.
- **Significance:** The 24-hour gap between resolution and arrests is the analytically decisive fact — it explains why a movement authorised as disciplined mass civil disobedience took the form of sabotage, communications disruption and local seizure of authority.
- **Phases to name (⚠️):** an initial urban phase of strikes, hartals and student action; a rural phase of attacks on railways, telegraph lines, post offices and police stations; and a prolonged underground phase of clandestine organisation and, in some districts, **parallel governments** functioning where colonial authority had collapsed.
- **Limit/caution:** ✅ Congress ideology remained non-violent even though the movement included sabotage and attacks on state property — state the distinction between official method and actual events rather than choosing one.

### 8.6 Bank C — The alignment of political forces

- **Evidence:** ✅ The **Muslim League stayed aloof**; ✅ the **Communists**, after the Nazi attack on the USSR, adopted the **People's War** line and opposed the movement; ⚠️ the princes, most large landholders and much organised business remained loyal or neutral, while the Hindu Mahasabha and other groups also stayed out.
- **Significance:** The Congress's four-year absence from provincial politics after 1939, combined with its total absence from legal politics after August 1942, left the field to the League — the direct link between Quit India and the League's transformation into a mass party by 1946 (`basic/26`, `basic/27`). ⚠️ The link must be argued, not asserted: while the Congress leadership was imprisoned the League built the organisational base in Bengal and Punjab that the 1945–46 results then registered — ✅ **93 per cent of Muslim votes and 119 of 250 seats in Bengal**, and ✅ **75 of 175 seats in Punjab**, where the Unionist Party fell to 18 (`basic/26` §8.7).
- **Qualification (do not overstate the causal chain):** ⚠️ the Congress's absence created the opportunity but did not by itself produce the League's mandate; the sources attribute that to a sustained mobilisation drawing in professionals, business groups, students and the *ulama*, *pirs* and *sajjada nishins*, and to wartime scarcity and procurement grievances. ✅ Nor was the Congress destroyed by the absence: it emerged in 1945–46 with **80.9 per cent of the general-constituency vote**, campaigning on the 1942 repression and the INA trials. The correct formulation is that 1942 changed the *balance* between two mandates, not that it handed the League an uncontested field.
- **Limit/caution:** The Communists' position was strategic, arising from the changed character of the war after June 1941; present it as a political calculation, not as collaboration.

### 8.7 Bank D — Results (for "point out its results")

| Register | Result | Evidence/reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Immediate | Comprehensive suppression | Mass arrests, military deployment, firing, collective fines; the movement was broken within months as an open campaign |
| Political | The end of governance by consent | ⚠️ After 1942 the Raj rested on coercion alone; the loss of Indian administrative and political cooperation was structural, not episodic |
| Political | Congress removed from legal politics for three years | Leadership imprisoned until mid-June 1945, leaving the field to the League — a decisive factor in the 1945–46 elections (`basic/26` §8.7, which supplies the named results: ✅ Congress over 90% of general seats provincially and 80.9% of general-constituency votes; ✅ League 74.7% of votes in Muslim constituencies) |
| Political | Demonstration of mass willingness to act | Rural and small-town participation on a scale beyond CDM, including in districts with no Congress organisation |
| Long-term | Britain's post-war calculation shifted | ⚠️ Combined with wartime exhaustion, the Labour government's arrival and the post-war upsurge (`basic/26`), 1942 made continued rule appear unaffordable |
| Long-term | Radicalisation of the movement's rank and file | Underground networks and militant cadres fed into the 1945–46 upsurge and into post-war Left politics (`basic/23`) |

### 8.8 Comparison engine — the three Gandhian mass movements

| Dimension | NCM 1920–22 | CDM 1930–34 | Quit India 1942 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Demand | Swaraj and Khilafat redress | Purna Swaraj | **Immediate British withdrawal** |
| Method | Withdrawal of cooperation | Breaking specific laws | Open rebellion in effect, after leadership removal |
| Leadership | Continuous | Continuous with negotiated pauses | **Removed within 24 hours** |
| Muslim participation | High | Low outside NWFP | Very low; League aloof |
| State response | Restrained, then repressive | Mass arrests and ordinances | **Immediate, comprehensive, military** |
| Ending | Unilateral withdrawal | Negotiated suspension, later withdrawal | Suppression, then negotiation from 1945 |

### 8.9 Counter-evidence, balance and historiographical caution

**The changing social base of nationalism, 1885–1942, and what each broadening cost:**

| Phase | Base added | Gain | ⚠️ Cost of that broadening |
|---|---|---|---|
| ✅ 1885–1905 (`basic/14`) | English-educated professional middle class | An all-India platform, an economic critique, organisational continuity | Method limited to petition; no leverage on the state |
| ✅ 1905–08 (`basic/15`) | Urban students, women, shopkeepers, the vernacular-reading public | Boycott, national education, mass symbolism | The Hindu-inflected mobilising idiom narrowed Muslim participation |
| ✅ 1916–18 (`basic/18`) | Provincial branch networks and the Muslim League | Double unity; a joint constitutional demand | Congress accepted **separate electorates and weightage** — the price of unity was communal representation |
| ✅ 1920–22 (`basic/20`) | Peasants, small-town India, Muslims via Khilafat, artisans | The first genuine mass movement; a reorganised Congress | ✅ Autonomous agrarian movements (**Eka**, Malabar) escaped Congress discipline; Chauri Chaura forced withdrawal; the alliance rested on an issue India did not control |
| ✅ 1930–34 (`basic/22`) | Middle peasantry, women on a wide scale, the NWFP, forest-dependent communities | Deep rural penetration; no-tax campaigns | Muslim participation fell sharply outside the NWFP; business support was ambivalent |
| ✅ 1930s (`basic/23`) | Workers, tenants, the Left, states' peoples | A social-economic programme; the Karachi and Faizpur resolutions | Internal ideological conflict — the **Tripuri crisis (1939)** and Bose's exit |
| ✅ 1942 (`basic/25`) | Villages and districts with no Congress organisation at all | The widest rural mobilisation of the struggle | ✅ The League stayed aloof and the Communists opposed it; the Congress fought alone and vacated legal politics for three years |

- ⚠️ **The argument to make:** every broadening of the social base widened the movement's reach and narrowed its cohesion. The Congress could add peasants only by tolerating agrarian demands it could not fully endorse, add Muslims only by conceding communal representation, and add the Left only by absorbing a programme its landed supporters resisted. That trade-off — reach purchased with coherence — is the single most useful analytical frame for any question on the movement's social composition, and it also explains why the endgame of 1945–47 was fought between a broad but internally divided Congress and a narrow but unified League (`basic/26`, `basic/27`).

- **Against overstating success:** the movement did not compel a single British concession in 1942 or 1943, and was over as an open campaign within months.
- **Against understating it:** rural penetration and the parallel governments show a depth of mobilisation no earlier movement reached, in districts where the Congress had little formal presence.
- **Regional variation:** eastern United Provinces and Bihar, parts of Bengal and coastal Andhra, and the western Indian districts with parallel administrations each experienced a different Quit India.
- ⚠️ Do not claim that Quit India "won" independence, and do not cite arrest, casualty or sabotage figures; the movement's importance is best argued through changes in the *conditions* of British rule.

### 8.10 Verdict scaffolds

- **2024-style verdict:** "Quit India was provoked by four years of constitutional refusal and imperial crisis, suppressed within months, and decisive within five years — it converted the British question from whether to concede to when to leave."
- **Character verdict:** "The movement's form was chosen by the government, not by the Congress: arresting the leadership on 9 August guaranteed that Do-or-Die would be interpreted locally."
- **Cripps verdict:** "Cripps failed because it offered a future constitution to people demanding a present government, and offered provincial opt-out to a movement whose central premise was national unity."

### 8.11 Factual-risk cautions

- **Cripps = 1942, before Quit India**; the Quit India resolution = **8 August 1942, Bombay AICC**; arrests = **9 August 1942**.
- The **"post-dated cheque"** remark is associated with **Cripps**, not the August Offer.
- The August Offer is **1940**; it promised dominion status as a goal, not immediate responsible government.
- The Cripps opt-out applied to **British Indian provinces**; princely states would have acceded through their rulers.
- The League stayed aloof and the Communists followed the **People's War** line — do not describe 1942 as a united national movement.
- Do not state numbers arrested, killed or of parallel governments established.
