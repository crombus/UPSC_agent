## 1. Snapshot & core idea

**Foundation — the "Congress system": one-party dominance with internal pluralism.**

- ✅ For its first two decades Congress was **dominant** but not dictatorial — it was **broad, plural and consensual**, absorbing many social groups and tolerating open dissent.
- ✅ Congress won large **seat** majorities while polling **under 50% of votes** — non-Congress candidates together often polled more, but were divided.
- ✅ **Patel's rule (1948)** barring dual party membership pushed the Socialists out and defined Congress's organisational boundary.
- ⚠️ Congress functioned as a **"party of consensus"** with a left-of-centre orientation, co-opting the agendas of its rivals.

**Foundation — a fragmented opposition could not yet displace Congress.**

- ✅ The **Socialists / PSP** (JP, Narendra Dev, Lohia, Asoka Mehta) split repeatedly over whether to cooperate with or oppose Congress.
- ✅ The **Communists (CPI)** moved from insurrection (1948–51) to elections, won **Kerala (1957)**, then **split in 1964** (CPI vs CPM).
- ✅ On the right, the **Jana Sangh** (1951, RSS-linked) and the **Swatantra Party** (1959, pro-free-market, anti-planning) offered conservative alternatives.
- ⚠️ Because the opposition was **ideologically scattered and often ex-Congress**, it could challenge but not yet defeat the ruling party nationally.

## 4. Must-Know Facts (Prelims)

- ✅ The **"Congress system"** = one-party dominance with internal factional pluralism (Rajni Kothari's phrase).
- ✅ Congress won large seat majorities while polling **less than half the popular vote**.
- ✅ The **PSP (Praja Socialist Party)** formed in **1952** from the KMPP and the Socialist Party.
- ✅ The **CPI won Kerala in 1957** and **split into CPI and CPM in 1964**.
- ✅ The **Jana Sangh** was founded in **1951** by **Shyama Prasad Mookerjee**.
- ✅ The **Swatantra Party** was founded in **1959** by **C. Rajagopalachari** (with Masani and Ranga).
- ✅ **Lohia's socialists** stressed backward-caste reservation and Hindi as the link language.

## 5. UPSC Traps

> 🔑 Trap: One-party *dominance* is not one-party *rule* — the "Congress system" contained a functioning opposition and internal dissent.

- ❌ Congress won majorities of the popular vote. → It won **seat** majorities on **under 50%** of votes.
- ❌ The CPM was founded in 1947. → The CPI **split** into CPI and CPM in **1964**.
- ❌ The Swatantra Party was left-wing. → It was **pro-free-market and anti-planning**.
- ❌ The Jana Sangh had no organisational parent. → It was closely linked to the **RSS**.
- ❌ The opposition was united against Congress. → It was **ideologically fragmented** and often composed of ex-Congressmen.

### 8.2 Qualified thesis options

- **Party-of-consensus thesis:** *The Congress system worked because the Congress was not one party but a coalition of factions and interests operating as a party — it contained the ideological spectrum internally, so that opposition parties functioned as "parties of pressure" whose agendas were co-opted rather than defeated.*
- **Arithmetic thesis:** *Congress dominance was a product of the electoral system as much as of popular support: it won large seat majorities while polling under half the votes, because a divided opposition could not convert its combined majority into seats.*
- **Democratic-dominance thesis:** *One-party dominance was not one-party rule — dissent was tolerated inside the Congress, opposition parties governed states, and a Communist government took office in Kerala in 1957; the system was competitive in principle long before it became competitive in outcome.*
- **Ex-Congress thesis:** *The opposition's central weakness was that most of it came out of the Congress — Socialists, KMPP, Swatantra — so it shared the ruling party's vocabulary and personnel while lacking its organisation, which made differentiation difficult and coalition-building unstable.*

### 8.4 Bank A — How the Congress system worked

| Pillar | Evidence | Function |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ Breadth and plurality | Congress was **dominant but not dictatorial** — broad, plural, consensual, absorbing many social groups and tolerating open dissent | Made exit unattractive: a dissenter could win inside the party |
| ✅ Organisational boundary | **Patel's 1948 rule** barring members from belonging to rival parties with their own organisation pushed the **Socialists out** | Defined the party's limits and set the terms of the first split |
| ✅ Internal contest | The **Tandon–Nehru contest for the Congress presidency, 1950–51**, resolved in Nehru's favour | Showed that leadership and ideological direction were settled internally, not by opposition pressure |
| ✅ Co-option of agendas | The **Avadi resolution (1955)** on the socialistic pattern | Absorbed the Socialists' programme, leaving them without a distinctive appeal |
| ✅ Electoral arithmetic | Large **seat** majorities on **under 50 per cent of votes**; non-Congress candidates together often polled more but were divided | Converted plurality support into commanding majorities |

- **Analytical use:** ⚠️ **Rajni Kothari's** "Congress system" and the idea of a **"party of consensus"** with a left-of-centre orientation are the standard analytical vocabulary — attribute the concept and do not quote it.

### 8.6 Bank C — Why the opposition could not displace Congress

- **Claim:** The failure was structural, arithmetical and ideological at once.
- **Evidence:** ⚠️ The opposition was **ideologically scattered** — running from Swatantra's free-market conservatism through Jana Sangh's cultural nationalism to the CPI's communism — and **often composed of ex-Congressmen** sharing the ruling party's personnel and language; ✅ its combined vote frequently exceeded the Congress's while its seat count did not.
- **Significance:** This explains why the breakthrough of 1967 required **anti-Congress coalition arithmetic** rather than any single party's growth — the opposition's problem was aggregation, not support.
- **Limit/caution:** Do not describe the opposition as ineffective; it governed Kerala from 1957 and shaped Congress policy through co-option.

### 8.7 Erosion — why the system weakened

```text
Nehru's authority (to 1964) held factions in balance
   -> succession contests weaken central arbitration
   -> co-option requires policy space; economic crisis of the mid-1960s narrows it (basic/34)
   -> Congress split-prone at state level; defections become viable
   -> Lohia-style anti-Congressism supplies a coalition formula across ideologies
   -> 1967: Congress loses several states; the era of dominance ends (basic/34)
```

### 8.8 Counter-evidence, balance and caution

- **Democratic credit:** opposition parties contested freely, won states and criticised openly; internal Congress dissent was public and consequential.
- **Democratic debit:** ⚠️ central intervention in state governments and the use of Congress organisational power against elected opponents qualify the picture; state this without over-claiming.
- **Regional variation:** Kerala, West Bengal, Tamil Nadu and parts of the north developed strong non-Congress currents far earlier than the national picture suggests.
- ⚠️ Do not present vote shares or seat numbers for any election without verified figures; the safe formulation is "large seat majorities on under half the popular vote".

### 8.9 Verdict scaffolds

- **System verdict:** "Congress dominance rested less on the party's strength than on its capacity to be several parties at once, and it ended when the factional balance it managed internally began to be fought externally."
- **Opposition verdict:** "The opposition's votes were always sufficient and its unity never was; 1967 is what happened when that changed."
- **Democracy verdict:** "One-party dominance in India coexisted with genuine competition — the Kerala government of 1957 is the proof, and its later dismissal is the qualification."

### 8.10 Factual-risk cautions

- **Jana Sangh = 1951, S.P. Mookerjee, RSS-linked**; **Swatantra = 1959, Rajagopalachari with Masani and Ranga, pro-market**; **PSP = 1952 from KMPP + Socialist Party**.
- The **CPI split into CPI and CPM in 1964**; the CPM was not founded in 1947.
- The **1948 Congress rule** on dual membership caused the Socialists' exit.
- Congress won **seat** majorities on **less than half** the popular vote — never claim popular-vote majorities.
- "Congress system" and "party of consensus" are **Rajni Kothari's** analytical terms — attribute the concept, do not quote.
- Do not assign election vote shares or seat totals without verified figures.
