# Modern India: Phases, Movements and Distinctive Features - Last-Minute Revision Chart

> **Purpose:** One-place revision immediately before Prelims or GS-I.
> **Coverage:** Eighteenth-century transition, Company conquest, colonial economy and state, reform, 1857, nationalism, Gandhian mass movements, independence, integration and post-independence consolidation to 2000.
> **Source base:** Every relevant Core and Advanced Modern Indian History file, the Master Chronology, Bipan Chandra and the supplementary interpretive checks identified in the subject README.
> **Two-pass design:** The master chart gives the non-negotiable, exam-complete Core identity of every phase. The optional Advanced layer adds source criticism, debates and nuance; skipping it must not affect the ability to answer Prelims or Mains.

---

## 1. The 60-second chronology ladder

```text
Mughal imperial contraction
    -> regional states and European companies
    -> Plassey political entry
    -> Buxar military supremacy
    -> Diwani and Company revenue-state
    -> territorial conquest through war, alliance and annexation
    -> colonial economy + centralised bureaucracy
    -> reform and resistance
    -> Revolt of 1857
    -> Crown rule
    -> Moderate nationalism
    -> Swadeshi, revolutionary and communal politics
    -> Home Rule and Lucknow Pact
    -> Gandhi's local satyagrahas
    -> Non-Cooperation
    -> Civil Disobedience
    -> provincial autonomy
    -> Quit India
    -> INA/RIN/post-war upsurge
    -> Independence and Partition
    -> princely-state integration
    -> republic, planning and linguistic reorganisation
    -> Nehruvian consolidation
    -> Indira, Emergency and democratic recovery
    -> Mandal, coalition politics and 1991 reforms
```

> **Memory line:** Company trade -> Company rule -> colonial extraction -> reform and revolt -> organised nationalism -> mass nationalism -> transfer of power -> nation-building -> democratic and economic transformation.

## 2. Master phase-identification chart

| Phase / era | Approximate period | Political signature | Economic / social signature | Fastest identification clue |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| **Eighteenth-century transition** | 1707-1765 | Mughal contraction; successor states, Marathas, Sikhs and regional competition | Economy and trade continue despite political fragmentation | Regional reordering, not a simple "dark age" |
| **European commercial competition** | 1498-1763, politically decisive in 18th c. | Portuguese, Dutch, English and French coastal establishments; Carnatic Wars | Fortified factories, maritime trade and naval rivalry | Wandiwash 1760 ends French political challenge |
| **Company conquest of Bengal** | 1757-1772 | Plassey coup, Buxar victory, Diwani and Dual Government | Bengal revenue finances Company expansion; power without responsibility | Plassey = conspiracy; Buxar = real military supremacy |
| **Company territorial expansion** | 1765-1856 | Mysore, Maratha and Sikh wars; Subsidiary Alliance; Doctrine of Lapse | Dependent alliances and annexations redirect Indian revenues to imperial war | Wellesley = Subsidiary Alliance; Dalhousie = Lapse |
| **Early colonial constitutional state** | 1773-1858 | Parliament increasingly regulates the Company and centralises rule | Company changes from trader to governed territorial sovereign | 1773 GG of Bengal; 1833 GG of India |
| **Colonial economic transformation** | late 18th-early 20th c. | State protects imperial commercial interests | Drain, deindustrialisation, commercialisation, land settlements and famines | Colonial infrastructure without autonomous development |
| **Colonial administrative state** | late 18th-19th c. | Civil service, army, police, judiciary and codified law | Racially exclusive higher services; centralised coercive institutions | Rule of law coexists with racial inequality |
| **Education, press and cultural policy** | 19th century | Orientalist-Anglicist debate, English education and press regulation | New intelligentsia and public sphere develop within colonial limits | 1835 Macaulay; 1854 Wood's Despatch |
| **Socio-religious reform** | c. 1820s-early 20th c. | Reform and revival respond to colonial modernity and internal hierarchy | Women's education, anti-caste critique, religious reinterpretation and modern associations | Match founder + organisation + year + region |
| **Civil, tribal and peasant resistance** | 18th-19th c. | Recurrent local opposition before and after 1857 | Revenue pressure, dispossession, outsider penetration and customary rights | Movement must be tied to its specific grievance and region |
| **Revolt of 1857** | 1857-58 | Sepoy revolt becomes broad civil uprising in several north/central regions | Political annexation, agrarian distress, religious fear and military grievances converge | Cartridge = trigger, not complete cause |
| **Crown rule and controlled association** | 1858-1885 | Secretary of State, Viceroy and cautious legislative inclusion | Army reorganised; princes and landlords courted; divide-and-rule deepens | Company ends, empire remains |
| **Moderate nationalism** | 1885-1905 | INC creates all-India platform using constitutional agitation | Drain theory and economic nationalism expose colonial exploitation | Limited method, foundational ideological achievement |
| **Swadeshi and militant nationalism** | 1905-1908/11 | Anti-partition agitation, boycott, passive resistance and Surat split | Indigenous enterprise, national education and samitis | Partition of Bengal transforms political method |
| **Revolutionary nationalism I** | c. 1907-1917 | Secret societies, targeted attacks and overseas networks | Youth radicalisation under repression | Revolutionary action supplements, not replaces, mass politics |
| **Communalism and League politics** | late 19th c.-1947 | Separate political identities, Muslim League and constitutional safeguards | Colonial representation policies institutionalise community categories | Separate electorates begin in 1909 |
| **Home Rule and wartime nationalism** | 1914-1918 | Tilak and Besant revive agitation; Lucknow unity | Wartime inflation, taxation and political expectation | Two Home Rule Leagues + Lucknow double unity |
| **Gandhi's local-to-national rise** | 1917-1919 | Champaran, Ahmedabad, Kheda; Rowlatt Satyagraha | Local grievances joined to disciplined non-violent resistance | 1917 -> 1918 -> 1919 sequence |
| **Non-Cooperation-Khilafat** | 1920-1922 | First all-India mass movement; Congress becomes mass organisation | Boycott plus khadi, charkha, national education and panchayats | Withdrawn after Chauri Chaura |
| **Swarajists and 1920s revolutionary phase** | 1922-1929 | Council entry, constructive work, HSRA and Bhagat Singh | Labour, peasant and youth politics expand | No-changers, pro-changers and revolutionaries coexist |
| **Civil Disobedience phase** | 1927-1934 | Simon boycott, Purna Swaraj, Salt March, RTCs and Poona Pact | No-tax, salt, forest and boycott struggles widen participation | Salt is opening symbol, not entire movement |
| **Left, peasant, labour and states' peoples' politics** | 1920s-1940s | Congress Socialists, Communists, Kisan Sabha, trade unions and princely-state movements | Class and agrarian questions enter nationalism | National movement becomes socially more differentiated |
| **1935 Act and provincial ministries** | 1935-1939 | Provincial autonomy under safeguards; Congress ministries in eight provinces | Limited reforms and political training under colonial constraints | Federation never operated; provincial autonomy did |
| **WWII and Quit India** | 1939-1942 | India taken into war without consent; Cripps fails; Quit India demands withdrawal | Wartime shortages and repression; spontaneous local upsurge | Cripps before Quit India; "Do or Die" |
| **Post-war upsurge** | 1945-1946 | INA trials, RIN revolt and Cabinet Mission expose weakened Raj | Military loyalty and imperial legitimacy come under pressure | Political effect exceeds direct military result |
| **Independence and Partition** | 1946-1947 | Cabinet Mission fails; Direct Action and violence; 3 June Plan; Independence Act | Mass displacement and communal catastrophe accompany freedom | Plan -> Act -> Radcliffe boundary |
| **Princely-state integration and Republic** | 1947-1950 | Accession, merger, democratisation and Constitution | Territorial unity converted into constitutional citizenship | Patel-Menon; Junagadh-Hyderabad-Kashmir differ |
| **Foundations of the Republic** | 1947-1952 | Parliamentary democracy, secularism and federal institutions survive Partition | Refugee rehabilitation, planning and universal adult franchise | Independence did not automatically complete nation-building |
| **Linguistic reorganisation** | 1948-1966 | Andhra 1953; SRC; 1956 Act; Maharashtra-Gujarat and Punjab-Haryana later | Linguistic identity accommodated within federal democracy | Reorganisation strengthened unity |
| **Tribal integration** | post-1947 | Protective integration rather than forced assimilation | Autonomy, development and cultural preservation remain in tension | Nehru's tribal Panchsheel |
| **Nehru era** | 1950-1964 | Democratic institutionalisation, non-alignment and federal consolidation | Mixed economy, planning, public sector, science and education | Non-alignment is active independence, not neutrality |
| **Congress system and opposition** | 1947-1967 | Congress dominance with internal factional competition; opposition survives | Elections normalise democratic conflict | Dominance is not one-party dictatorship |
| **Shastri-Indira consolidation** | 1964-1973 | Succession, 1965 war, Congress split, 1971 victory | Green Revolution, bank nationalisation and Garibi Hatao | Centralisation and populism rise together |
| **JP Movement and Emergency** | 1974-1977 | Total Revolution challenge; national Emergency under Article 352 | Censorship, detention, sterilisation and slum-clearance excesses | 1977 election restores democratic competition |
| **Janata and regional crises** | 1977-1984 | First non-Congress Centre; Indira returns; Punjab and Assam crises | Coalition instability and regional mobilisation | Alternation proves democratic resilience |
| **Rajiv and late-century transition** | 1984-1991 | Modernisation agenda, identity conflicts, Bofors and political decline | Technology push, anti-defection law and early liberalisation impulses | Large mandate eroded by scandal and social conflict |
| **Mandal, reforms and coalition era** | 1990-2000 | OBC assertion, Hindutva mobilisation and coalition governments | 1991 liberalisation transforms the economic model | Political fragmentation plus deeper social representation |
| **Post-independence social-economic synthesis** | 1950-2000 | Democratic state manages development, caste, gender and regional demands | Land reform, Green Revolution, public sector, Mandal and liberalisation | Planning-to-market shift without abandoning democracy |

## 3. Colonial conquest fingerprint

| Instrument / event | Associated person / date | What it did | Common trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plassey | Clive, 1757 | Conspiracy-backed political entry into Bengal | Not the decisive open military victory |
| Buxar | Hector Munro, 1764 | Defeated Mir Qasim-Awadh-Mughal emperor coalition | More militarily decisive than Plassey |
| Diwani | 1765 | Revenue rights in Bengal, Bihar and Orissa | Did not make Company the Nawab |
| Dual Government | 1765-72 | Company revenue power; Nawab administrative liability | Ended by Warren Hastings |
| Subsidiary Alliance | Wellesley, from 1798 | Troops, subsidy, Resident and loss of independent diplomacy | Not Dalhousie's policy |
| Doctrine of Lapse | Dalhousie | Annexation where natural heir absent and adoption rejected | Awadh was not annexed under it |
| Awadh annexation | 1856 | Alleged misgovernment | Distinct from Doctrine of Lapse |
| Mysore Wars | 1767-1799 | Defeated Haidar-Tipu challenge | Tipu died in Fourth War |
| Maratha Wars | 1775-1818 | Ended confederacy and deposed Peshwa | Salbai ended First War |
| Sikh Wars | 1845-49 | Punjab annexed after Ranjit Singh's death | Ranjit Singh did not fight these wars |

## 4. Constitutional development ladder

| Measure | Signature provision | Elimination clue |
|---|---|---|
| **Regulating Act 1773** | Governor-General of Bengal; Supreme Court framework | First parliamentary regulation |
| **Pitt's India Act 1784** | Board of Control + Court of Directors | Dual control in Britain |
| **Charter Act 1813** | Ends monopoly except tea/China; education grant | Missionaries and Rs 1 lakh |
| **Charter Act 1833** | Governor-General of India; Law Member | Bentinck and Macaulay |
| **Charter Act 1853** | Legislative-executive separation; civil-service competition | Last Charter Act |
| **Government of India Act 1858** | Crown rule; Secretary of State; Viceroy | Company rule ends |
| **Indian Councils Act 1861** | Portfolio system; nominated non-officials | Controlled association, not election |
| **Indian Councils Act 1892** | Budget discussion and indirect election principle | Moderate-era limited reform |
| **Morley-Minto Act 1909** | Separate electorates for Muslims | Communal electorate begins |
| **Government of India Act 1919** | Provincial dyarchy | Montagu-Chelmsford |
| **Government of India Act 1935** | Provincial autonomy; proposed federation; central dyarchy | Federation never implemented |
| **Indian Independence Act 1947** | Two dominions; paramountcy lapses | Legal transfer of power |

> **Trap:** 1773 created the **Governor-General of Bengal**; 1833 created the **Governor-General of India**.

## 5. Colonial land and economic systems

| System / concept | Region / architect | Unit and feature | Main consequence / trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Permanent Settlement** | Cornwallis, 1793; Bengal-Bihar-Orissa | Zamindar made proprietor; state demand fixed permanently | Not a direct ryot settlement |
| **Ryotwari** | Munro and Read; Madras and Bombay | Direct settlement with cultivator/ryot; periodically revised | State demand could remain heavy |
| **Mahalwari** | Holt Mackenzie / R.M. Bird; north-western regions | Village/mahal-based settlement | Not confined to individual peasants |
| **Drain of wealth** | Naoroji, R.C. Dutt | Unrequited transfer through home charges, salaries, profits and expenditure | More than private corruption |
| **Deindustrialisation** | 19th-century colonial economy | Machine imports and discriminatory trade damage handicrafts | English industrialisation mattered through colonial policy |
| **Commercialisation** | Colonial agriculture | Cash crops and market dependence | Not synonymous with peasant prosperity |
| **Colonial infrastructure** | Railways, telegraph, canals | Imperial movement, exports and capital | Had unintended integration effects without benevolent intent |
| **Famines** | Especially 1770, 1876-78, 1896-1900 | Crop failure interacts with revenue, markets and policy | Avoid single-cause explanations |

## 7. National movement phase comparison

| Phase | Goal / political language | Main method | Social reach | Core limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Moderates, 1885-1905 | Constitutional reform and economic critique | Petitions, press, councils and deputations | Educated middle class | Narrow base and faith in British justice |
| Swadeshi, 1905-08 | Anti-partition, self-reliance and self-government | Boycott, swadeshi, national education and passive resistance | Strong in Bengal; wider echoes | Regional concentration and repression |
| Revolutionary I | Direct anti-colonial action | Secret societies, assassinations and overseas networks | Committed youth minorities | Could not substitute for mass mobilisation |
| Home Rule, 1916-18 | Self-government within Empire | Leagues, branches, lectures and political education | Revived all-India agitation | Did not demand immediate complete independence |
| Non-Cooperation, 1920-22 | Swaraj through withdrawal of cooperation | Boycott and constructive programme | First broad all-India mass movement | Withdrawn after violence |
| Civil Disobedience, 1930-34 | Purna Swaraj; defiance of unjust law | Salt, no-tax, boycott and forest-law resistance | Wider women, peasant and regional participation | Negotiation, repression and uneven momentum |
| Quit India, 1942 | Immediate British withdrawal | Mass uprising, underground work and parallel governments | Broad but leaderless after arrests | Severe repression and limited coordination |

## 8. Gandhi's movement sequence

| Movement | Date | Grievance / symbol | Distinctive result |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Champaran | 1917 | Indigo and tinkathia | First successful civil-disobedience experiment in India |
| Ahmedabad | 1918 | Mill-worker wage dispute | Gandhi's first hunger strike in India |
| Kheda | 1918 | Revenue remission after crop failure | Vallabhbhai Patel emerges |
| Rowlatt Satyagraha | 1919 | Detention without trial | First nationwide satyagraha; exposed discipline problem |
| Non-Cooperation | 1920-22 | Punjab wrongs, Khilafat and Swaraj | Congress reorganised for mass politics |
| Salt / Civil Disobedience | 1930-34 | Salt monopoly and colonial law | Purna Swaraj converted into mass defiance |
| Quit India | 1942 | Failure of wartime transfer; British must leave | Last great mass upsurge |

> **Sequence trap:** Champaran 1917 -> Ahmedabad/Kheda 1918 -> Rowlatt/Jallianwala 1919 -> Non-Cooperation 1920-22 -> Civil Disobedience 1930 -> Quit India 1942.

## 12. Optional Advanced enrichment: deeper interpretation

| Phase / issue | Core identification | Optional Advanced correction |
|---|---|---|
| Eighteenth century | Mughal decline and regional powers | Avoid treating political fragmentation as economic or cultural collapse; regional states retained vitality. |
| European conquest | English naval-financial advantage | English success also depended on Indian alliances, Bengal resources and contingent political crises. |
| Plassey-Buxar | Political entry then military supremacy | "Black Hole" claims are contested and should not replace structural causes such as fortification, dastak abuse and private trade. |
| Colonial economy | Drain and deindustrialisation | English industrialisation harmed Indian crafts through the colonial power structure; avoid both monocausal and benevolent-modernisation narratives. |
| Reform movements | Modernising social critique | Upper-caste reform, anti-caste movements and women's agency had different social locations; do not merge them into one elite awakening. |
| 1857 | Broad, regionally uneven uprising | It was more than a mutiny but not yet a fully modern national revolution; region, class and leadership varied. |
| INC foundation | Hume assisted Congress formation | Safety-valve theory is insufficient; Indian political associations and nationalism created the need for an all-India platform. |
| Communalism | Separate organisations and electorates | Treat communalism as historically constructed through politics, colonial institutions and elite competition, not primordial hostility. |
| Swadeshi | Boycott and self-reliance | Its creative achievements coexisted with regional limits, social exclusions and communal tensions. |
| Gandhian strategy | Non-violent mass mobilisation | Movement withdrawal, truce and constructive work formed a long-term strategy of controlled mobilisation, not simple inconsistency. |
| Revolutionary nationalism | Heroic sacrifice and anti-colonial action | Its political influence exceeded organisational capacity; Bhagat Singh's later socialist thought should not be reduced to bomb action. |
| Quit India | Final mass upsurge | Its long-term result was political: British legitimacy and governability weakened, even though the movement was suppressed militarily. |
| INA-RIN | Coercive apparatus under strain | Important contributors to the endgame, but not sole causes of British withdrawal. |
| Partition | League demand, British policy and political deadlock | Avoid single-blame explanations; communal mobilisation, institutional choices, violence and Congress-League-British calculations interacted. |
| Princely integration | Patel-Menon accession strategy | Integration combined consent, pressure, popular movements, security action and later administrative merger. |
| Nehru era | Democracy, planning and non-alignment | Balance major institutional achievements against literacy, inequality, Kashmir/China and organisational weaknesses. |
| Emergency | Authoritarian suspension and democratic recovery | Explain both pre-Emergency economic-political crisis and the constitutional/extra-constitutional abuse of power. |
| Post-1990 transition | Mandal, markets and coalitions | Democratisation of representation, identity mobilisation and economic liberalisation unfolded together rather than as separate stories. |

> **Firewall rule:** Core alone must support a complete Prelims decision and a complete Mains answer. Advanced may improve sophistication, but no indispensable fact, distinction or answer mechanism may exist only in Advanced.

## 13. Evidence-to-inference rules

| Evidence | Safe inference | Unsafe overreach |
|---|---|---|
| Colonial official report | State priorities, categories and recorded administration | Neutral or complete account of Indian society |
| Nationalist writing | Political critique, ideology and mobilisation | Unquestioned statistical precision |
| Memoir / autobiography | Participant experience and self-understanding | Complete explanation of the movement |
| Police/intelligence record | Surveillance targets and official fears | Proof that every allegation was true |
| Newspaper / pamphlet | Public argument, mobilisation and political language | Universal popular opinion |
| Commission report | Investigated issue and official recommendation | Automatic implementation or impartiality |
| Act / proclamation | Formal constitutional rule or promise | Effective democratic practice on the ground |
| Movement resolution | Declared programme and leadership intent | Uniform conduct by every participant |
| Election result | Political support under given institutions | Permanent social consensus |
| Economic series | Trend in prices, output, trade or revenue | Complete lived experience without regional/social context |

> **Elimination rule:** First distinguish **formal provision**, **actual implementation**, **political motive** and **historical consequence**. UPSC frequently makes one statement jump incorrectly between these levels.
