**A. The 1905 sequence and the 7 August anchor (routed Prelims demand)**
- **Claim:** Swadeshi has a precise political birth date, and it matters.
- **Evidence:** ✅ The partition was announced in July 1905; **Surendranath Banerjea called for boycott of British goods and institutions at a Calcutta meeting on 17 July 1905**; the **formal proclamation of the Swadeshi Movement came on 7 August 1905 at a mass meeting in the Calcutta Town Hall, where the famous Boycott Resolution was passed**; the partition took effect on **16 October 1905**, observed as a day of protest.
- **Significance:** ⚠️ **National Handloom Day is observed on 7 August**, and the historical reason is exactly this — the 1905 Boycott Resolution that launched Swadeshi. Citing the Town Hall meeting rather than only the date shows the link is understood, not memorised.
- **Limit/caution:** Distinguish the 17 July call (a Moderate leader's initiative) from the 7 August formal resolution; do not merge them.

**B. Effectiveness of boycott — the economic evidence**
- **Claim:** Boycott had measurable commercial effect, at least initially and locally.
- **Evidence:** ✅ Even Moderate leaders such as Surendranath Banerjea toured urging boycott of **Manchester cloth and Liverpool salt**; ✅ Bipan Chandra records that the value of British cloth sold in some mofussil districts **fell sharply between September 1904 and September 1905**, and that protest meetings such as the one at **Barisal drew crowds of ten to twelve thousand**.
- **Significance:** Converts "boycott was popular" into "boycott worked, briefly and regionally" — a far stronger analytical claim, and the basis for judging both its achievement and its unsustainability.
- **Limit/caution:** ⚠️ The source gives a striking magnitude for the fall in specified mofussil districts; cite it as a sharp district-level decline rather than as an all-India statistic, and do not extend it beyond the year given.

**C. Constructive programme — industry and education (routed Prelims demand)**
- **Claim:** Swadeshi's constructive side is what distinguishes it from mere protest.
- **Evidence:** ✅ Encouragement of indigenous enterprise and use of Indian goods; the **National Council of Education (1906)** and the **Bengal National College**, associated with **Aurobindo Ghosh**; ⚠️ swadeshi enterprises in textiles, soap, matches, banking, insurance and small manufacturing, and swadeshi stores as the retail arm of boycott.
- **Significance:** National education and swadeshi enterprise gave the movement institutions that outlived the agitation and supplied the model for Gandhi's constructive programme and for later economic nationalism.
- **Limit/caution:** ⚠️ Many swadeshi ventures were undercapitalised and short-lived; do not name specific firms, output or capital figures without verified evidence.

**D. Cultural mobilisation and its ambivalence**
- **Claim:** Swadeshi succeeded by making politics emotional and popular, and paid a price for the idiom it chose.
- **Evidence:** ✅ **Vande Mataram**, festivals, songs, processions and **samitis** carried politics beyond petitions; ⚠️ the religious and cultural symbolism drew heavily on Hindu tradition.
- **Significance:** Explains both the unprecedented reach (students, women, urban crowds) and the difficulty of drawing Muslim participation — the strongest evidence for the "energised and narrowed" reading.
- **Limit/caution:** Do not assert that Swadeshi caused the Muslim League's formation; state the two as connected developments in a shared context (`basic/17`).

**E. Repression and the Surat split**
- **Claim:** The movement was contained by state coercion and by its own organisational division.
- **Evidence:** ✅ Official bans, arrests, prosecutions and restrictions on activists and publications; ✅ the **Surat Split (1907)** divided Moderates and Extremists; ✅ **Morley–Minto reforms (1909)** introduced **separate electorates for Muslims**; ✅ the partition was **annulled in 1911** with the capital moved from Calcutta to Delhi.
- **Significance:** The annulment is a genuine achievement — sustained agitation reversed a major administrative decision — and the sequence 1907→1909→1911 shows the state's twin response of division and concession.
- **Limit/caution:** Annulment came six years later and alongside the capital shift; present it as a qualified victory, not a triumph.

**F. Transmission to Gandhian politics**
- **Claim:** Gandhi inherited the technique and supplied what Swadeshi lacked.
- **Evidence:** ⚠️ Boycott, passive resistance, self-reliance, national education and constructive work all pass forward into `basic/20` and `basic/22`; what Swadeshi lacked was an organisation reaching the villages, a discipline of non-violence and a leadership able to include Muslims and peasants.
- **Significance:** This is the highest-value paragraph for "precursor" questions because it states both the continuity and the specific deficit that Gandhi corrected.
- **Limit/caution:** Do not describe Swadeshi as non-violent in principle; passive resistance coexisted with revolutionary violence (`basic/16`).

4. **Partition announcement:** Curzon's partition of Bengal was announced in July 1905 and was presented officially as an administrative reorganisation.
5. **Divide-and-rule reading:** Nationalists interpreted Bengal partition as a political attempt to divide a growing Bengali public and weaken anti-colonial mobilisation.
6. **Boycott Resolution:** A mass meeting at Calcutta Town Hall adopted the Boycott Resolution on 7 August 1905, a key launch point of the Swadeshi movement.
7. **Partition takes effect:** The partition took effect on 16 October 1905, which was marked by hartals, processions, mourning and public solidarity rituals.
8. **Swadeshi:** Swadeshi promoted indigenous goods, enterprise and self-reliance as both an economic programme and a political discipline.
9. **Boycott:** Boycott rejected foreign goods and, in more militant programmes, extended toward official schools, courts and functions where feasible.
10. **National education:** The National Council of Education was founded in 1906, and Bengal National College was associated with Aurobindo Ghosh.
11. **Samitis and cultural mobilisation:** Samitis, songs, festivals, processions and Vande Mataram carried anti-partition politics beyond petitions into everyday public action.
12. **Lal-Bal-Pal:** Lal-Bal-Pal refers to Lala Lajpat Rai, Bal Gangadhar Tilak and Bipin Chandra Pal, leading symbols of assertive nationalism.
13. **Congress programme, 1905-06:** The Banaras Congress of 1905 supported Swadeshi and Bengal boycott; the Calcutta Congress of 1906 under Dadabhai Naoroji adopted a compromise programme of self-government, Swadeshi, boycott and national education.
14. **Passive resistance:** The militant programme used passive resistance, self-help and withdrawal of cooperation to reduce dependence on colonial institutions.
15. **Regional spread:** Swadeshi spread beyond Bengal through Tilak in Maharashtra, Lajpat Rai and Ajit Singh in Punjab, and V.O. Chidambaram Pillai in Madras, but intensity and social reach varied.
16. **Social reach:** Students, women, urban middle classes and selected workers and rural groups participated, while peasant and Muslim participation remained uneven.
17. **Surat Split:** The Congress split at Surat in 1907 over leadership, methods and control between Moderate and militant currents.
18. **Repression and decline:** Bans, arrests, prosecutions, deportations, press restrictions and Congress division weakened the open Swadeshi movement after 1907.
19. **Annulment and reorganisation:** Annulment and the transfer of the imperial capital to Delhi were announced in 1911; the 1912 reorganisation reunited Bengali-speaking areas but separately arranged Bihar-Orissa and Assam.
20. **Long-term legacy:** Swadeshi made boycott, constructive work, national education, self-reliance and emotionally resonant mobilisation part of later politics.

#### High-risk UPSC traps

- The 7 August 1905 Boycott Resolution, 16 October implementation and 1911 annulment are different chronological anchors.
- Swadeshi meant constructive indigenous production; boycott meant withdrawal from foreign goods or colonial institutions.
- The Surat split occurred in 1907 after Swadeshi began; it did not cause the anti-partition movement.
- Annulment in 1911 and reorganisation in 1912 did not simply restore the entire pre-1905 province or leave Calcutta as the imperial capital.
- Militant nationalists radicalised the Moderate economic critique rather than discarding it.
- Vande Mataram's mobilising power and Hindu-inflected idiom should both be assessed without reducing the movement to one cultural symbol.

#### Current-affairs boundary
