# UPSC ENVIRONMENT CONVENTIONS AND PROTOCOLS - QUICK REVISION INDEX

> **Cut-off:** 14 September 2026.
> A declaration, COP decision, voluntary pledge and treaty are not legally equivalent. India's
> signature, ratification and domestic implementation are stated separately.

## Legal-status legend

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Binding treaty** | Creates international legal obligations for Parties |
| **Protocol / amendment** | Supplementary binding instrument; separate consent may be required |
| **COP decision** | Binding institutionally/procedurally within treaty system but not a new treaty |
| **Soft law** | Political or normative commitment without treaty enforcement |
| **Negotiation** | No adopted treaty text yet |

## 1. Climate change regime

| Instrument / mechanism | Legal force and governing body | Objective / key rules | India status and implementation | UPSC trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **UN Framework Convention on Climate Change, 1992** | Binding treaty; Conference of Parties | Stabilise GHG concentrations; equity and common but differentiated responsibilities | India ratified in 1993; MoEFCC nodal ministry; national communications/BURs/BTRs | Framework Convention does not itself impose Kyoto-style identical targets |
| **Kyoto Protocol, 1997** | Binding protocol; COP serving as Meeting of Parties, CMP | Binding targets for Annex I Parties; CDM, JI and emissions trading | India ratified in 2002; no binding Annex I target; major CDM host | Developed-country target architecture, not universal NDC system |
| **Doha Amendment, 2012** | Binding amendment to Kyoto; second commitment period 2013-2020 | Extended targets and revised annexes for second period | India accepted in 2017; period has ended | Entry into force in 2020 did not revive future Kyoto periods |
| **Paris Agreement, 2015** | Binding treaty under UNFCCC; CMA | NDC cycle, transparency, adaptation, finance and temperature goals | India ratified on 2 October 2016; updated NDC 2022 and LT-LEDS 2022 | NDC content is nationally determined, but procedural duties are binding |
| **Nationally Determined Contributions** | Paris procedural obligation; CMA guidance | Every Party prepares, communicates and maintains successive progression-oriented NDCs | India targets emissions-intensity reduction, non-fossil electricity capacity share and carbon sink | NDC is not identical to a domestic statute or carbon budget |
| **Enhanced Transparency Framework** | Paris Agreement and CMA decisions | Common reporting, technical expert review and facilitative consideration | India transitions from BUR to Biennial Transparency Report framework | Flexibility exists for developing countries needing it |
| **Global Stocktake** | Paris Agreement five-year process under CMA | Assesses collective progress on mitigation, adaptation and means of implementation | India participates and uses equity/finance positions | It evaluates collective progress, not individual legal compliance |
| **Article 6.2 cooperative approaches** | Paris treaty plus CMA guidance | Internationally transferred mitigation outcomes with accounting to avoid double counting | India may authorise eligible activities/credits under domestic framework | Bilateral transfers are not the same as Article 6.4 mechanism |
| **Article 6.4 mechanism** | UN-supervised Paris mechanism | Credits emission reductions/removals with sustainable-development and corresponding rules | India developing domestic carbon-market alignment | Successor-like mechanism but not simply renamed CDM |
| **Article 6.8 non-market approaches** | Paris framework | Cooperation without transfer of mitigation credits | India supports technology, finance and capacity approaches | No tradable carbon unit |
| **Warsaw International Mechanism for Loss and Damage** | COP decision under UNFCCC | Knowledge, coordination and support for climate loss and damage | India participates as vulnerable developing country | Loss and damage is distinct from adaptation |
| **Santiago Network** | COP/CMA mechanism | Technical assistance for averting, minimising and addressing loss and damage | India eligible to seek technical support | Network is not the Loss and Damage Fund |
| **Fund for Responding to Loss and Damage** | COP/CMA-created financial mechanism with governing Board | Finance for particularly vulnerable developing countries facing climate losses | India supports adequate grant-based finance; access rules continue evolving | A COP-created fund, not a separate climate treaty |
| **Green Climate Fund** | UNFCCC/Paris financial-mechanism operating entity | Mitigation and adaptation finance through accredited entities | NABARD and other Indian entities access/project-participate | GCF is not the Global Environment Facility |
| **Global Environment Facility** | Financial mechanism for multiple conventions | Grants for agreed global environmental benefits | India accesses through national focal points and implementing agencies | Serves several conventions, not only climate |
| **Adaptation Fund** | Kyoto-created fund serving Paris Agreement | Finances concrete adaptation projects in developing countries | NABARD has acted as India's national implementing entity | Initially linked to CDM levy; governance later shifted to Paris service |
| **Technology Mechanism** | UNFCCC COP mechanism | Technology Executive Committee plus Climate Technology Centre and Network | India participates and can seek technical assistance | Policy arm and implementation network are distinct |
| **Climate Technology Centre and Network** | Operational arm of Technology Mechanism | Technical assistance, knowledge and networks | Indian institutions participate in network/requests | Not a financing bank |
| **Global Goal on Adaptation framework** | Paris/CMA decisions | Collective adaptation targets, dimensions and assessment | India stresses finance, national circumstances and equity | It is not a nationally uniform quantified target |
| **Just Transition Work Programme** | CMA decision process | Dialogue and pathways for equitable low-emission transition | India links transition with development and climate finance | COP work programme, not a treaty or compensation fund |

## 2. Ozone layer and cooling

| Instrument / mechanism | Legal force and governing body | Objective / key rules | India status and implementation | UPSC trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Vienna Convention for Protection of Ozone Layer, 1985** | Binding framework treaty; Conference of Parties | Research, monitoring and cooperation on ozone protection | India acceded in 1991 | Does not itself list phase-out schedules |
| **Montreal Protocol, 1987** | Binding protocol; Meeting of Parties | Controls production/consumption of ozone-depleting substances | India became Party in 1992; Ozone Depleting Substances Rules 2000 | Widely successful; differentiated schedules for developing Parties |
| **London Amendment, 1990** | Binding amendment | Added chemicals and created Multilateral Fund | India Party | Multilateral Fund assists eligible developing countries |
| **Copenhagen Amendment, 1992** | Binding amendment | Accelerated phase-outs and added HCFC/methyl bromide controls | India Party | Not the 2009 Copenhagen climate accord |
| **Montreal Amendment, 1997** | Binding amendment | Licensing systems and methyl-bromide trade controls | India Party | Amendment name matches city/protocol name |
| **Beijing Amendment, 1999** | Binding amendment | Added bromochloromethane and HCFC production controls | India Party | Separate consent from original Protocol |
| **Kigali Amendment, 2016** | Binding amendment to Montreal Protocol | Phases down climate-warming HFCs | India ratified in 2021; phasedown schedule begins later for India, from 2028 group | HFCs are generally not ozone-depleting but are potent GHGs |
| **Multilateral Fund for Montreal Protocol** | Protocol financial mechanism | Pays incremental costs and capacity support for developing Parties | India receives support through Ozone Cell programmes | Not the climate GCF |

## 3. Biodiversity, biosafety and access-benefit sharing

| Instrument / mechanism | Legal force and governing body | Objective / key rules | India status and implementation | UPSC trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Convention on Biological Diversity, 1992** | Binding treaty; CBD COP | Conservation, sustainable use and fair/equitable benefit sharing | India ratified in 1994; Biological Diversity Act 2002 and Rules | CBD covers genetic resources but not human genetic resources |
| **Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety, 2000** | Binding CBD protocol; COP-MOP | Safe transfer, handling and use of living modified organisms | India Party since 2003; 1989 biosafety Rules and regulatory committees | LMO focuses living organisms, not all processed GM products |
| **Nagoya Protocol, 2010** | Binding CBD protocol; COP-MOP | Access to genetic resources, prior informed consent and benefit sharing | India ratified in 2012; NBA/SBB/BMC and ABS regulations | Access and benefit sharing differs from biosafety |
| **Nagoya-Kuala Lumpur Supplementary Protocol, 2010** | Binding Cartagena supplementary protocol | Liability and redress for biodiversity damage from LMOs | India is a Party; domestic biosafety/liability framework applies | It supplements Cartagena, not Nagoya ABS Protocol |
| **Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework, 2022** | CBD COP15 decision, soft-law target framework | Four goals and 23 targets including 30-by-30, restoration and finance | India aligns National Biodiversity Strategy/Targets and reports through CBD | Not a separate treaty; "30x30" is one target, not entire framework |
| **Aichi Biodiversity Targets, 2010-2020** | CBD COP10 strategic-plan decision | Twenty global biodiversity targets | Historical predecessor to KMGBF | Expired target period; not current 2030 framework |
| **Global Strategy for Plant Conservation** | CBD COP framework | Plant-diversity conservation targets | India implements through botanical/forest/agricultural institutions | COP strategy, not separate convention |
| **Biosafety Clearing-House** | Cartagena institutional mechanism | Exchanges LMO laws, decisions and risk information | India submits national biosafety information | Information mechanism, not approval authority |
| **Access and Benefit-Sharing Clearing-House** | Nagoya institutional mechanism | Publishes permits, checkpoints and national ABS information | India reports through national authorities | Not a commercial marketplace |

## 4. Wildlife, wetlands, forests and plant resources

| Instrument / mechanism | Legal force and governing body | Objective / key rules | India status and implementation | UPSC trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CITES, 1973** | Binding treaty; Conference of Parties | Regulates international trade in listed wild fauna/flora through permits | India Party since 1976; Wildlife Protection Act 2022 created management/scientific authority framework | Appendix I is strictest; listing does not automatically prohibit every transaction |
| **Convention on Migratory Species, 1979** | Binding treaty; CMS COP | Conserves migratory species and habitats through appendices/agreements | India Party since 1983; hosted COP13 in 2020 | Appendix I endangered; Appendix II needs international agreements |
| **Ramsar Convention, 1971** | Binding treaty; Ramsar COP | Conservation and wise use of wetlands; Ramsar List | India Party since 1982; Wetlands Rules 2017 and national/state authorities | Ramsar listing does not make site a UNESCO World Heritage Site |
| **Montreux Record** | Ramsar administrative register | Flags listed wetlands where ecological character has changed/is changing | Indian sites may enter/leave through Ramsar process | Not a separate convention or blacklist |
| **World Heritage Convention, 1972** | Binding UNESCO treaty; World Heritage Committee | Protects cultural and natural heritage of outstanding universal value | India ratified in 1977; domestic heritage/wildlife/forest laws protect sites | UNESCO listing does not transfer sovereignty |
| **International Treaty on Plant Genetic Resources for Food and Agriculture, 2001** | Binding FAO treaty; Governing Body | Conservation, farmers' rights and multilateral access-benefit sharing for listed crops | India is a Party; PPVFR Act and gene-bank systems support implementation | Multilateral System covers Annex I crops, not every species |
| **International Plant Protection Convention, 1951** | Binding FAO treaty; Commission on Phytosanitary Measures | Prevents spread of plant pests through phytosanitary standards | India is a contracting party; Plant Quarantine Order and NPPO implement | Plant-health treaty, not biodiversity-access treaty |
| **International Convention for Regulation of Whaling, 1946** | Binding treaty; International Whaling Commission | Conservation and management of whale stocks | India is a member and supports commercial-whaling moratorium | IWC predates modern biodiversity conventions |
| **International Tropical Timber Agreement, 2006** | Binding commodity/environment treaty; International Tropical Timber Council | Sustainable tropical-forest management and timber trade | India is a member of ITTO | Trade/development agreement, not logging ban |
| **UN Forest Instrument, 2007/2015** | Non-legally binding UN instrument; UN Forum on Forests | Sustainable forest management and global forest goals | India reports through UNFF and domestic forest programmes | Not a forest convention |
| **Bonn Challenge, 2011** | Voluntary global restoration initiative | Restore degraded/deforested landscapes | India pledged restoration, linked with land-degradation commitments | Not a treaty or only afforestation target |
| **New York Declaration on Forests, 2014** | Voluntary declaration | Halt forest loss and restore landscapes | India did not endorse original 2014 declaration | Not legally binding |
| **Glasgow Leaders' Declaration on Forests and Land Use, 2021** | Voluntary political declaration | Halt/reverse forest loss and land degradation by 2030 | India did not sign | Distinct from Glasgow Climate Pact |

## 7. Hazardous wastes, chemicals and pollution

| Instrument / mechanism | Legal force and governing body | Objective / key rules | India status and implementation | UPSC trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Basel Convention, 1989** | Binding treaty; Basel COP | Controls transboundary hazardous/other waste movement and disposal | India Party since 1992; Hazardous and Other Wastes Rules 2016 | Prior informed consent does not mean unrestricted trade |
| **Basel Ban Amendment, 1995** | Binding amendment for accepting Parties | Prohibits specified hazardous-waste exports from Annex VII to non-Annex VII States | India has not ratified Ban Amendment | Convention Party status does not automatically equal Ban Amendment Party |
| **Basel Plastic Waste Amendments, 2019** | Binding annex amendments | Bring most mixed/contaminated plastic waste under control procedure | Applies through Basel obligations and Indian waste/import rules | Not a global ban on all plastic trade |
| **Basel E-waste Amendments, 2022** | Binding annex amendments effective for Parties unless opted out | Expand control of transboundary e-waste movements | Indian Hazardous/E-Waste Rules and trade controls implement | E-waste can be hazardous or "other waste"; controls expanded |
| **Rotterdam Convention, 1998** | Binding treaty; Rotterdam COP | Prior informed consent for listed hazardous chemicals/pesticides in trade | India Party; chemicals/pesticides/import-export laws implement | PIC procedure does not automatically ban listed chemicals |
| **Stockholm Convention, 2001** | Binding treaty; Stockholm COP | Eliminate/restrict persistent organic pollutants and reduce releases | India ratified in 2006; EPA rules, pesticide and waste controls | India has used acceptable-purpose exemptions such as DDT for vector control |
| **Minamata Convention on Mercury, 2013** | Binding treaty; Minamata COP | Controls mercury supply, products, processes, emissions, releases and waste | India ratified in 2018; sector, product and waste rules implement | Named after Japanese mercury-poisoning disaster |
| **Global Framework on Chemicals, 2023** | Voluntary multi-stakeholder framework | Life-cycle sound management of chemicals and waste beyond 2020 | India participates through chemicals/waste governance | Successor to SAICM; not a binding convention |
| **Strategic Approach to International Chemicals Management** | Voluntary policy framework, 2006-2020 transition | Sound chemicals management | Superseded by Global Framework on Chemicals | Not Stockholm Convention |
| **Bamako Convention, 1991** | Binding African regional treaty | Bans hazardous-waste import into Africa | India is not a Party | Stronger regional response to waste dumping |
| **Waigani Convention, 1995** | Binding South Pacific regional treaty | Bans hazardous/radioactive waste imports and controls movement | India is not a Party | Regional Pacific instrument |
| **Convention on Long-Range Transboundary Air Pollution, 1979** | Binding UNECE regional treaty with protocols | Controls transboundary air pollutants in Europe/North America | India is not a Party | Not a global air-pollution convention |

## 8. Marine environment and law of the sea

| Instrument / mechanism | Legal force and governing body | Objective / key rules | India status and implementation | UPSC trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **UN Convention on the Law of the Sea, 1982** | Binding treaty; Meeting of States Parties plus institutions | Maritime zones, navigation, resources, marine environment and dispute settlement | India ratified in 1995; Maritime Zones Act and sector laws | EEZ gives sovereign rights, not full territorial sovereignty |
| **BBNJ Agreement / High Seas Treaty, 2023** | Binding UNCLOS implementing agreement; COP and subsidiary bodies; in force 17 January 2026 | Marine genetic resources/benefit sharing, area-based tools, EIAs and capacity/technology | India signed but had not ratified by cutoff; domestic ratification/implementation pending | Applies to areas beyond national jurisdiction, not national EEZ |
| **MARPOL 73/78** | Binding IMO convention; MEPC | Prevents pollution from ships through six technical annexes | India Party to MARPOL and annexes; Merchant Shipping law/rules implement | Ship-source pollution, not land-based pollution generally |
| **MARPOL Annex I** | Binding annex | Oil pollution | India Party | Includes operational and accidental oil controls |
| **MARPOL Annex II** | Binding annex | Noxious liquid substances in bulk | India Party | Bulk liquid chemicals, not packaged harmful goods |
| **MARPOL Annex III** | Binding annex | Harmful substances in packaged form | India Party | Links with International Maritime Dangerous Goods Code |
| **MARPOL Annex IV** | Binding annex | Sewage from ships | India Party | Separate from garbage rules |
| **MARPOL Annex V** | Binding annex | Garbage from ships, including strict plastics prohibition | India Party | Plastics discharge prohibition is not the global plastics treaty |
| **MARPOL Annex VI** | Binding annex | Air pollution, sulphur, NOx, energy efficiency and GHG measures | India Party | Covers ship air emissions, not aviation |
| **London Convention, 1972** | Binding IMO treaty; Consultative Meeting | Controls dumping of wastes at sea | India is a Party | Dumping differs from normal operational ship discharges under MARPOL |
| **London Protocol, 1996** | Binding modern protocol using reverse-list approach | Generally prohibits dumping except listed materials | India is not a Party | Stricter than London Convention |
| **Ballast Water Management Convention, 2004** | Binding IMO treaty | Prevents spread of invasive species through ballast-water standards | India was not a contracting Party at cutoff; draft domestic rules were under preparation | Global entry into force does not make India a Party |
| **Hong Kong Ship Recycling Convention, 2009** | Binding IMO treaty; entered into force 26 June 2025 | Safe and environmentally sound ship design, inventory and recycling facilities | India acceded in 2019; Ship Recycling Act 2019 implements | Basel and Hong Kong regimes address different waste/shipping stages |
| **Anti-Fouling Systems Convention, 2001** | Binding IMO treaty | Prohibits harmful organotin anti-fouling systems and controls future substances | India is a Party; merchant-shipping rules implement | Targets coatings, not ballast water |
| **OPRC Convention, 1990** | Binding IMO treaty | Oil-pollution emergency planning, reporting and cooperation | India is a Party; Coast Guard is central response actor | Preparedness/response treaty, not compensation convention |
| **Civil Liability Convention, 1992** | Binding IMO liability treaty | Strict shipowner liability and compulsory insurance for tanker oil pollution | India is a Party; Merchant Shipping law implements | First compensation tier |
| **Fund Convention, 1992** | Binding compensation treaty | Additional compensation financed by oil receivers | India is a Party to 1992 Fund system | Supplementary Fund is a separate optional protocol |
| **Bunkers Convention, 2001** | Binding IMO treaty | Liability/insurance for bunker-oil pollution from non-tankers | India is a Party | Distinct from tanker cargo-oil CLC |
| **UN Fish Stocks Agreement, 1995** | Binding UNCLOS implementing agreement | Conservation of straddling and highly migratory fish stocks | India signed but has not ratified | Signature does not equal Party status |
| **FAO Port State Measures Agreement, 2009** | Binding FAO treaty | Uses port controls to deter illegal, unreported and unregulated fishing | India was not a Party at cutoff | Fisheries enforcement treaty, not IMO port convention |

## 14. High-yield comparison traps

| Pair | Correct distinction |
|---|---|
| UNFCCC vs Paris Agreement | Framework treaty vs implementing treaty with universal NDC cycle |
| Kyoto vs Paris | Developed-country binding targets vs nationally determined contributions for all Parties |
| COP vs CMP vs CMA | UNFCCC Parties vs Kyoto Parties vs Paris Parties |
| Vienna vs Montreal vs Kigali | Ozone framework vs ODS controls vs HFC climate phasedown |
| CBD vs Cartagena vs Nagoya | Biodiversity framework vs LMO biosafety vs access-benefit sharing |
| CITES vs CMS | Wildlife trade vs migratory-species conservation |
| Ramsar vs World Heritage | Wetland wise use/listing vs outstanding universal natural/cultural heritage |
| Basel vs Rotterdam vs Stockholm | Waste movement vs hazardous-chemical trade information vs POP elimination |
| Stockholm Declaration vs Stockholm Convention | 1972 soft-law environment principles vs 2001 binding POP treaty |
| MARPOL vs London Convention | Operational ship-source pollution vs deliberate dumping at sea |
| Ballast Water vs Anti-Fouling | Invasive organisms in ballast vs toxic hull coatings |
| UNCLOS vs BBNJ | Ocean constitutional framework vs high-seas biodiversity implementing agreement |
| Sendai vs Paris | Disaster-risk framework vs climate treaty |
| KMGBF vs CBD | COP-adopted target framework vs binding parent convention |
| Global plastics treaty vs plastic-waste rules | Negotiation not yet adopted vs existing Basel/domestic controls |

## Verification hierarchy

1. UN Treaty Collection or depositary status page for signature/ratification.
2. Convention secretariat for amendments, annexes and COP decisions.
3. Indian treaty database, MoEFCC/MEA/line-ministry notification for national status.
4. Parent Act and current rules for domestic implementation.
5. Never infer Party status merely because India attends negotiations or implements similar rules.
