### EVIDENCE, PYQ AND CURRENT-STATUS CONTROL

- **Archaeological inference:** material context supports bounded reconstruction; absence is not automatic proof of non-existence.
- **Textual testimony:** genre, authorship, redaction, patronage and temporal distance control what a text can prove.
- **Inscription and coin evidence:** contemporary names, titles, claims and circulation are powerful but do not by themselves map uniform territorial control.
- **Scholarly interpretation:** historian labels are arguments to test, not facts to memorise without evidence.

[FACT] Upinder's South-east Asia discussion, drawing on O. W. Wolters, stresses diversity within the region and argues that Indian materials were 'fractured and restated' through localisation. That is the strongest built-in corrective available in the repository's OCR base.

[ANALYSIS] 'Cultural interaction', 'localisation' and 'connected histories' are safer UPSC phrases because they acknowledge Indian influence while preserving the agency of Khmer, Cham, Javanese, Sri Lankan, Chinese and Tibetan actors.

[DEBATE] The 'Sanskrit cosmopolis' model is useful only in a bounded way: it explains the spread of elite political-literary idioms, but not the whole field of trade, Pali monasticism, vernacular performance, local cult integration or material circulation.

### SESSION 3 — ROUTE ECOLOGY: LAND CORRIDORS, MARITIME BASINS AND MONSOON TIMING

- **Study link**

**How to use them:** Frame the answer through Route ecology; define land corridors, connect maritime basins with monsoon timing to explain the mechanism, and use Mains angle for the decisive comparison or qualification.

[FACT] Interaction ran through multiple ecological corridors: the north-west and Central Asian Silk Roads; Himalayan and Tibetan passes; the Arabian Sea; the Bay of Bengal; the Straits of Malacca; and the South China Sea maritime world.

[FACT] Geography Topic 16 and related notes define the monsoon as a seasonal reversal of winds. This mattered historically because shipping windows, convoy timing and port-seasonality depended on winds, currents and storm risk rather than on abstract 'ocean routes' alone.

[ANALYSIS] Ports only worked as nodal systems. Bharuch, Tamralipti, Arikamedu, Puhar, Sri Lankan ports, Kedah, Oc-Eo and later Srivijaya mattered because they linked hinterlands, shipping, storage, taxation and intermediaries.

[LIMIT] No single route dominated every century. Overland, Himalayan and maritime paths waxed and waned with political security, patronage, climate and regional demand.

- ⚠️ The term "Indianisation" must be used carefully: local rulers adapted Indian ideas for their own contexts.

✅ Upinder notes that special importance was attached to **Buddha relics**, believed to be corporeal remains and coveted across the Asian Buddhist world.

- ✅ Relics, monasteries and texts created sacred geographies linking India to Asia.
- ✅ Cultural flows were multi-directional: pilgrims came to India, texts travelled out, and Asian patrons reinterpreted Indian forms.
- ✅ The Ashoka–Mahinda–Sanghamitta narrative is specifically associated with the Sri Lankan `Mahavamsa`; distinguish chronicle memory from Ashoka's own inscriptions.
