### Canonical Basic owner

### Vocabulary, Spelling, Word Formation, Idioms and Proverbs — Core

Vocabulary questions test exact meaning, grammar and register. A “use in a
sentence” answer earns credit only if the context makes the intended sense
unmistakable.

### 1. Learn a word as a usable entry

For every new word record:

```text
headword | part of speech | plain meaning | common collocation
one near-synonym | one contrast/antonym | one original formal sentence
```

Example: **mitigate** (verb): make less severe; *mitigate a risk*; near
synonym *alleviate*; contrast *aggravate*. *Early warning systems mitigate
flood risk; they do not eliminate it.*

A synonym is rarely interchangeable in every setting. `Childish` and
`childlike` both relate to a child, but the first is usually disapproving and
the second often approving. Prefer a plain accurate word to an impressive
near-synonym with the wrong register.

| Register | Prefer | Avoid when formality needs precision |
|---|---|---|
| formal neutral | *assist, obtain, demonstrate, purchase* | casual *help out, get hold of, show off, buy up* |
| precise contrast | *economic* (economy) / *economical* (not wasteful) | treating them as synonyms |
| intensity | *alleviate* (reduce) / *eradicate* (remove completely) | claiming eradication when only reduction occurred |

### 2. Confusables and collocations

| Word 1 | Word 2 | Distinction/example |
|---|---|---|
| accept | except | receive/agree; excluding |
| advice (noun) | advise (verb) | *give advice; advise a candidate* |
| affect (usually verb) | effect (usually noun; also verb = bring about) | *affect outcomes; have an effect; effect change* |
| all ready | already | fully prepared; by now |
| altar | alter | religious table; change |
| appraise | apprise | assess value; inform: *apprise her of* |
| assure | ensure | reassure a person; make certain an outcome |
| complement | compliment | complete; praise |
| conscience | conscious | moral sense; aware |
| credible | credulous | believable; too ready to believe |
| discreet | discrete | tactful/unobtrusive; separate |
| economic | economical | relating to economy; saving money |
| eminent | imminent | distinguished; about to happen |
| farther | further | physical distance; additional/abstract (modern use overlaps) |
| principal | principle | chief/head or capital sum; rule |
| stationery | stationary | writing material; not moving |
| than | then | comparison; time/result |
| their/there/they're | possession/place/they are | *their files are there; they're ready* |
| who’s | whose | who is/who has; possession |
| climatic | climactic | climate-related; relating to climax |

### 3. Spelling: patterns, not guesses

| Pattern | Rule | Examples / caution |
|---|---|---|
| `-able/-ible` | no fully reliable sound rule; learn word families | *reliable, manageable; visible, permissible* |
| final silent `e` | usually drop before vowel suffix; retain before consonant suffix | *making, hopeful; hoped, safely* |
| `y` after consonant | change `y` to `i` before most suffixes, but not `-ing` | *carry → carried; happy → happier; carry → carrying* |
| doubled final consonant | one stressed vowel + one consonant before vowel suffix | *admit → admitted; prefer → preferred; travel → travelled/traveled* |
| `-cede/-ceed/-sede` | `-ceed` mainly *proceed, exceed, succeed* | *precede, recede; supersede* |

High-frequency spellings to retrieve exactly: **accommodate, accommodation,
achievement, argument, calendar, committee, definitely, discipline,
embarrass, environment, government, independent, maintenance, necessary,
noticeable, occurrence, privilege, receive, recommend, separate, successful,
tomorrow**. British and American variants may both be correct when used
consistently: *colour/color, organisation/organization, travelled/traveled*.

### 4. Word formation and grammatical fit

Identify the required part of speech from the slot before choosing a related
word. A verb after `to` is base form; an adjective commonly follows a linking
verb; a noun commonly follows a determiner or possessive.

| Base | Noun | Verb | Adjective | Adverb |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| decide | decision | decide | decisive | decisively |
| succeed | success | succeed | successful | successfully |
| explain | explanation | explain | explanatory | — |
| benefit | benefit | benefit | beneficial | beneficially |
| differ | difference | differ | different | differently |
| govern | government | govern | governmental | — |
| create | creation | create | creative | creatively |
| approve | approval | approve | approved/approving | — |

Common negative prefixes are not freely interchangeable: *inaccurate,
illegal, impossible, irregular, non-essential*. Learn the completed word.
Common suffix signals: `-tion/-ment/-ness` (noun), `-ify/-ise/-ize` (verb),
`-ful/-less/-ive/-al` (adjective), `-ly` (often adverb). Check meaning:
`economic` is not the same as `economical`, and `historic` is not the same as
`historical`.

### 5. Synonyms, antonyms and definition questions

Give an antonym of the same grammatical class unless the question says
otherwise: *scarce → abundant; rigid → flexible; temporary → permanent;
transparent → opaque; voluntary → compulsory*. Several answers can be valid
when they preserve the required sense. For a one-word question, a key must
state an accepted set or accept a defensible equivalent; do not demand one
dictionary synonym where the stem gives no context.

For “use in a sentence,” show the word’s exact sense:

| Word | Weak | Creditworthy |
|---|---|---|
| impartial | *The judge was impartial.* | *The impartial judge heard both sides before giving reasons.* |
| feasible | *The plan is feasible.* | *The engineer showed that the bridge was feasible within the approved budget.* |
| reluctant | *She was reluctant.* | *Reluctant to sign without evidence, she asked for the full report.* |

### 6. Idioms and proverbs

An idiom has a conventional non-literal meaning; a proverb states a general
lesson. Use an idiom grammatically and a proverb only where it supports the
point.

| Expression | Meaning | Natural use |
|---|---|---|
| at sea | confused/uncertain | *Without the map, I was at sea.* |
| a thorn in the flesh | persistent irritation/problem | *The leak became a thorn in the administration's flesh.* |
| move heaven and earth | make every possible effort | *Officials moved heaven and earth to restore supply.* |
| step into someone's shoes | take over a role | *She stepped into her predecessor's shoes.* |
| know the ins and outs | know all details | *The clerk knows the ins and outs of the process.* |
| cut adrift | abandon or leave unsupported/isolated | *Small suppliers were cut adrift when credit stopped.* |
| cut corners | save time/money improperly by lowering standards | *The contractor cut corners on safety.* |
| a blessing in disguise | apparent misfortune later proves beneficial | *The delay was a blessing in disguise because it exposed the defect.* |
| A stitch in time saves nine. | early action prevents a bigger problem | — |
| Look before you leap. | consider consequences first | — |
| Actions speak louder than words. | conduct is stronger evidence than promises | — |
| All that glitters is not gold. | appearance can mislead | — |

#### Mock-usage expressions

- **go berserk** — an informal verb phrase meaning *to become violently
  uncontrollable or behave wildly*, often from extreme anger, fear or
  excitement. Use it intransitively: `a person/crowd goes, went, has gone
  berserk`; do not use it for ordinary annoyance or as a transitive verb.
  *When the alarm malfunctioned, the frightened crowd went berserk and staff
  called for help.*
- **Where there is a will, there is a way.** — a fixed proverb meaning that
  determined effort and resolve can help a person find a solution. Use the
  complete sentence (or quote it); it is a motivating generalisation, not a
  guarantee that every obstacle can be overcome. *After the student mastered
  typing through persistent practice, her teacher said, “Where there is a
  will, there is a way.”*

### 7. Acquisition and timed retrieval

Use a **5–10–1** cycle: learn five entries, test after ten minutes without
looking, then use each once in a sentence the next day. Keep a two-column log:
`word confused` → `contrast and corrected sentence`. Do not rely on rereading.

In **8 minutes**, supply the requested form or choice:

1. The measure may ___ hardship. (alleviate/alleviation)
2. The committee reached a ___ after debate. (decide/decision)
3. The officer acted ___ during negotiations. (discreet/discrete)
4. The storm did not ___ the inland district. (affect/effect)
5. Correct: *The accomodation was definately insufficient.*
6. Give an antonym of *scarce*.
7. Use *cut corners* in a sentence.

**Key:** 1 *alleviate* [WF]; 2 *decision* [WF]; 3 *discreet* [CONF]; 4
*affect* [CONF]; 5 *The accommodation was definitely insufficient.* [SPL];
6 *abundant* (also *plentiful* in suitable context) [SYN]; 7 must mean
improperly reducing standards to save resources [IDIOM].

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

### Register-sensitive refinements

- Synonymy is contextual: *mitigate* reduces severity, whereas *eradicate* removes the thing.
- Some dictionary senses differ in regional frequency; accept standard British and American spellings consistently.
- Idioms may be informal even when grammatically correct. In an essay, a plain literal expression is often safer.
- A proverb supports an argument only after its relevance is explained; it is not evidence by itself.

### Adversarial checks

Test affect/effect as both verb and noun, farther/further overlap, historic/historical, economic/economical, principal/principle, complement/compliment, derivational false friends and context-dependent antonyms.

- **Register:** Prefer plain contemporary formal English over ornamental or colloquial substitution.
- **Collocation:** Learn words with their normal partners.
- **Confusables:** Separate similar spelling or sound by meaning and grammar.
- **Spelling patterns:** Use families and suffix rules while remembering genuine exceptions.
- **British and American variants:** Accept consistent standard variants unless the paper specifies a house style.
- **Word formation:** Use the grammatical slot to choose noun, verb, adjective or adverb.
- **Synonyms:** Match denotation, intensity and register rather than treating near-synonyms as identical.
- **Antonyms:** Keep the grammatical class and contextual dimension.
- **Idioms and proverbs:** State conventional meaning and use the expression naturally.
- **Retrieval and editing:** Use spaced recall, sentence production and a confusable log under time.
