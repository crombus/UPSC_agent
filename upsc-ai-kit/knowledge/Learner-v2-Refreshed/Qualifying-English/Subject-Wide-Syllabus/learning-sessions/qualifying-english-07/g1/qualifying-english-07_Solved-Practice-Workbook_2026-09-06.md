B. End with a slogan only.
C. Repeat the prompt verbatim.
D. Return to the qualified thesis and give a reasoned final judgement.

**Correct answer: D.** The correct option states the governing rule without changing meaning or inventing an absolute.


### Constructed-response practice and models

### Transfer 1. Decode: “Efficiency is not the same as effectiveness.”

**Model answer:** Efficiency concerns resources or speed; effectiveness concerns whether the intended outcome is achieved. The tension is between process economy and actual result.

### Transfer 2. Write a qualified thesis for the same prompt.

**Model answer:** Efficiency is valuable, but it becomes meaningful only when faster or cheaper processes still deliver the intended and fair outcome.

### Transfer 3. Give four distinct body moves.

**Model answer:** Define the distinction; show where efficiency helps; explain proxy/measurement failure; propose outcome-based safeguards.

### Transfer 4. Repair the opening “Since ancient times, efficiency has been important.”

**Model answer:** Open with the present tension: “An office can process files quickly and still fail the people it serves.”

### Transfer 5. Turn an example into analysis.

**Model answer:** After the example, state the mechanism and link: rapid complaint closure can reward disposal rather than repair, showing why speed alone is not effectiveness.

### Transfer 6. Supply a genuine counterargument.

**Model answer:** Slow procedures can also harm citizens; therefore the answer is disciplined timeliness, not delay in the name of care.

### Transfer 7. State the revision priority if the essay is 690 words.

**Model answer:** Cut repetition and duplicate examples first; preserve the thesis, counterargument and conclusion.

### Transfer 8. Write a qualified conclusion sentence.

**Model answer:** Institutions should therefore measure efficiency by the resources saved and effectiveness by the fair outcomes actually delivered, treating neither as a substitute for the other.
