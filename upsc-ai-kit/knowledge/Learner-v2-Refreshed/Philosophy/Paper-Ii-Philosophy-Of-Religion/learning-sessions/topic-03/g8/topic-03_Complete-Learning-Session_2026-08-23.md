#### 1.1 The object of analysis

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **Recommended opening definition:** The problem of evil is not one argument but a family of challenges generated by the relation among a divine profile, the facts of suffering and wrongdoing, and norms governing what a good agent would permit.












[THESIS] The problem of evil is not one argument but a family of challenges generated by the relation among a divine profile, the facts of suffering and wrongdoing, and norms governing what a good agent would permit. It becomes especially acute for classical creator-theism: an omnipotent, omniscient and perfectly good God creates and sustains a world containing apparently preventable evil.

[QUALIFICATION] Topic 01 owns detailed analysis of omnipotence, omniscience, goodness, eternity and God-world relations. Here those attributes are imported only as premises under stress. Topic 02 owns arguments for existence. Evil can rebut or undercut a proof, but this session does not repeat the proofs.

#### 1.2 Four distinct challenges

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **Major criticism:** A modal defence can answer the first while leaving the other three largely untouched.












1. **Logical challenge:** Is there a contradiction between God and the existence of any evil?
2. **Evidential challenge:** Even if compatibility is possible, does the observed amount, intensity and distribution of suffering make God improbable?
3. **Existential-religious challenge:** Can a person continue to trust, pray or find meaning when suffering is personally devastating?
4. **Pastoral or moral-protest challenge:** Is it ethically acceptable to justify another person's suffering as part of a cosmic plan?

[VERDICT] A modal defence can answer the first while leaving the other three largely untouched. This is the master distinction of the topic.

#### 1.3 Defence, theodicy, explanation and justification

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **Major reply:** Compatibility is weaker than probability; probability is weaker than full explanation; explanation is different from moral justification.












![Defence, theodicy and anti-theodicy](assets/18_defence-theodicy.png)

| Term | Exact burden | Typical example | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|---|
| Defence | Supplies a logically possible reason sufficient to block contradiction | Plantinga's Free Will Defence | Demanding that it prove God's actual motive |
| Theodicy | Claims to identify God's actual or probable morally sufficient reason | Hick's soul-making; process theodicy | Praising mere possibility as a historical explanation |
| Explanation | Gives a causal or structural account of why evil occurs | Free choice; natural law; karma | Assuming a cause morally justifies permission |
| Justification | Shows permission was morally right or at least non-culpable | Greater-good or freedom claim | Treating compensation as automatic justification |
| Anti-theodicy | Questions the morality or possibility of comprehensive justification | Dostoevskian protest; post-Holocaust caution | Confusing refusal to justify with denial of meaning or action |

[THESIS] Compatibility is weaker than probability; probability is weaker than full explanation; explanation is different from moral justification. A high-scoring answer states which burden is being met.

#### 1.4 Kinds of evil

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **Recommended opening definition:** Suffering is an experiential harm; wrongdoing is a culpable act or disposition.












![Moral, natural and metaphysical evil](assets/03_evil-matrix.png)

- **Moral evil** consists in culpable wrongdoing by responsible agents.
- **Natural evil** consists in pain, disease, disaster and predation not directly attributable to a human choice.
- **Metaphysical evil** names finitude, limitation or imperfection as such. It does not follow that every limitation is morally evil.
- **Suffering** is an experiential harm; **wrongdoing** is a culpable act or disposition. Either can occur without the other.
- **First-order evils** are direct pains, losses and wrongs. **Second-order goods** such as courage, compassion and forgiveness arise in response to vulnerability. The availability of a second-order good does not by itself show that the first-order evil was necessary or proportionate.

> [MEMORY] Four controls: **C-P-J-T** - Compatibility, Probability, Justification, Target.

#### 2.1 Epicurean attribution caution, Hume and Mackie

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **High-value transition:** The familiar questions - whether God is willing but unable, able but unwilling, or both able and willing while evil exists - are commonly labelled the Epicurean paradox.












[QUALIFICATION] The familiar questions - whether God is willing but unable, able but unwilling, or both able and willing while evil exists - are commonly labelled the Epicurean paradox. The exact popular wording should not be presented as a verbatim quotation from Epicurus. The dilemma is transmitted in later discussion, prominently through Lactantius, and Hume's *Dialogues Concerning Natural Religion* gives a celebrated modern formulation through Philo.

[THESIS] J. L. Mackie's 1955 essay "Evil and Omnipotence" turns the inherited dilemma into a logical charge against the simultaneous affirmation of omnipotence, complete goodness and evil.

![Mackie's logical argument](assets/04_mackie-map.png)

#### 2.2 Formal reconstruction

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **Core argument:** Premises 4 and 5 are not mere definitions.












[ARGUMENT]

1. God is omnipotent.
2. God is wholly good.
3. Evil exists.
4. A wholly good being eliminates evil as far as it can.
5. An omnipotent being has no relevant limit on eliminating evil.
6. Therefore, if such a God exists, evil should not exist.
7. Evil exists; hence the set appears inconsistent.

[QUALIFICATION] Premises 4 and 5 are not mere definitions. They require an account of logically possible action, morally sufficient reasons, freedom, stable laws and goods that may entail risk. The logical debate therefore turns on whether those bridging premises are unrestricted.

#### 2.3 What counts as a reply?

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **Major reply:** The theist need not show why every actual evil occurs in order to defeat a bare contradiction.












[THESIS] The theist need not show why every actual evil occurs in order to defeat a bare contradiction. It is enough to show a possible proposition consistent with God and evil, such as: God has a morally sufficient reason to permit some evil because significantly free creaturely agency is a great good that cannot be causally guaranteed.

[OBJECTION] This can look evasive: possibility is cheap, while actual horrors remain.

[REPLY] That objection changes the target from logical inconsistency to plausibility, evidence or moral adequacy. It is legitimate, but it must be named as a new stage.

#### 2.4 Omnipotence paradox and attribute control

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **Major criticism:** Omnipotence means power to do what is logically possible, not power to instantiate contradictions.












[THESIS] Omnipotence means power to do what is logically possible, not power to instantiate contradictions. God cannot make a free act both causally determined by God and libertarianly undetermined in the same respect.

[OBJECTION] Restricting omnipotence to the logically possible may merely define away the problem.

[REPLY] Logical possibility is not an ad hoc restriction invented for evil; it is part of the standard concept of power. Yet the critic can still ask why a logically possible, better feasible world was not actualized. That moves toward Plantinga's world-feasibility claim and then toward evidential comparison.

[VERDICT] Mackie's argument remains pedagogically decisive because it forces the theist to state the hidden moral and modal assumptions. Its original strict inconsistency charge is weaker once a coherent freedom-based possibility is admitted, but the evidential successor remains powerful.

> **Major reply:** The defence is strongest for moral evil.












![Plantinga's Free Will Defence](assets/05_plantinga.png)

#### 3.1 Significant freedom and possible worlds

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **High-value transition:** Alvin Plantinga's Free Will Defence aims at the logical problem.












[THESIS] Alvin Plantinga's Free Will Defence aims at the logical problem. A person is significantly free with respect to an action when the action is morally significant and the person is free to perform or refrain from it. A world containing such agency may be more valuable than a world of determined conformity.

[ARGUMENT]

1. Significant freedom is a great good.
2. If God causally determines every significantly free choice to be right, the choice is not libertarianly free in relation to God.
3. It is possible that every world feasible for God that includes significantly free creatures also includes at least some wrongdoing.
4. If so, God's inability to actualize freedom-with-guaranteed-goodness is not lack of omnipotence, because the proposed object is not a feasible world God can strongly actualize.
5. Therefore God's existence is logically compatible with moral evil.

#### 3.2 Transworld depravity accurately stated

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **High-value transition:** Transworld depravity is not the claim that every person is maximally wicked or always chooses evil.












[QUALIFICATION] **Transworld depravity** is not the claim that every person is maximally wicked or always chooses evil. It is a technical modal possibility: for any creaturely essence God might instantiate with significant freedom, in every feasible world where that essence is significantly free, it goes wrong in at least one relevant circumstance. Plantinga needs the possibility of this condition, not proof that it actually obtains.

[OBJECTION] Why could God not create free persons who always freely choose the good, as the blessed might in heaven?

[REPLY] A world-description in which creatures happen always to choose rightly may be logically describable, yet not strongly actualizable by God if the counterfactuals of their free choices are not under divine control. The heaven objection remains a plausibility pressure: if confirmed freedom without sin is coherent eschatologically, the theist must explain why earthly formation requires a different risk structure.

#### 3.3 Natural evil extension and residual limits

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **Recommended opening definition:** The defence is strongest for moral evil.












[THESIS] The defence is strongest for moral evil. Plantinga suggested that natural evil could possibly involve non-human free agents; this is enough for bare logical compatibility if coherent, but it is not an evidentially attractive explanation for disease, tectonics or evolution.

[OBJECTION] The actual scale of natural and animal suffering far exceeds what creaturely moral freedom explains.

[REPLY] The theist may combine freedom with stable-law, soul-making or finite-creation accounts. Combining theories, however, increases explanatory commitments and must not become an unfalsifiable list.

[VERDICT] Plantinga is widely taken to have neutralized the strongest deductive version of the problem. He does not establish that God probably exists, that transworld depravity is actual, or that observed natural evil is justified.

### SESSION 4 — EVIDENTIAL EVIL: ROWE, PROBABILITY AND SKEPTICAL THEISM

#### 4.1 Rowe and apparently gratuitous suffering

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **High-value transition:** William Rowe's evidential argument grants that God and evil may be logically compatible.












[THESIS] William Rowe's evidential argument grants that God and evil may be logically compatible. It argues that some intense suffering appears gratuitous: an omnipotent, omniscient being could prevent it without losing a greater good or permitting an equal or worse evil.

[ARGUMENT]

1. There exist instances of intense suffering that an omnipotent, omniscient being could have prevented without losing a greater good or permitting an equal or worse evil.
2. A perfectly good being would prevent any such suffering.
3. Therefore, probably, no omnipotent, omniscient, perfectly good being exists.

Rowe's well-known example is a fawn badly burned in a forest fire, suffering for days before death, with no visible connection to moral agency or a beneficiary. The case is illustrative, not a claim that one event alone deductively disproves God.

#### 4.2 The noseeum inference

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **Core argument:** Evidential force depends on a noseeum move: "We see no justifying reason; therefore there probably is none." Such reasoning is strong when observers would likely detect the relevant reason if it existed, as when looking for an elephant in a small room.












[THESIS] Evidential force depends on a noseeum move: "We see no justifying reason; therefore there probably is none." Such reasoning is strong when observers would likely detect the relevant reason if it existed, as when looking for an elephant in a small room. It is weak when the reason could exceed their access.

[OBJECTION] God's providential reasons, long causal chains and goods involving many lives may exceed human perspective.

[REPLY] Mere logical possibility of an unknown reason cannot erase all evidence. In ordinary reasoning, absence of expected evidence can be evidence of absence. The real question is whether humans should expect access to divine justifying reasons.

#### 4.3 Draper and conceptual Bayesian framing

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **Recommended opening definition:** Paul Draper's evidential strategy, where used, compares hypotheses: the biological distribution of pain and pleasure may be less surprising on an indifferent naturalistic hypothesis than on perfect theism.












[QUALIFICATION] Paul Draper's evidential strategy, where used, compares hypotheses: the biological distribution of pain and pleasure may be less surprising on an indifferent naturalistic hypothesis than on perfect theism. The point is comparative likelihood, not a numerical probability assignment.

[ARGUMENT] Conceptually:

```text
Posterior plausibility of theism
depends on prior plausibility
multiplied by how expected the evidence is if theism is true.

If evolutionary suffering is much more expected on indifferent nature
than on perfect providence, it counts against theism,
unless independent evidence or a strong auxiliary theodicy offsets it.
```

[QUALIFICATION] Bayesian language disciplines comparison but cannot manufacture agreed priors or likelihoods. Avoid pseudo-precision in an UPSC answer.

#### 4.4 Scale, distribution, intensity and hiddenness

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **High-value transition:** Divine hiddenness is a bounded cross-link, not a substitute topic.












| Evidential dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Scale | Why is there so much suffering rather than the minimum needed for freedom or growth? |
| Distribution | Why does suffering fall so unevenly, including on infants and animals? |
| Intensity | Why do some harms destroy agency rather than challenge it? |
| Duration | Why do harms continue when their lesson or causal role seems exhausted? |
| Hiddenness cross-link | Why does a loving God remain obscure to sincere sufferers? |

[QUALIFICATION] Divine hiddenness is a bounded cross-link, not a substitute topic. Here it matters because available comfort, guidance or unmistakable presence might reduce suffering without cancelling freedom.

#### 4.5 Skeptical theism

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **Recommended opening definition:** Skeptical theists challenge our entitlement to infer from "no reason we can see" to "probably no reason." Wykstra's CORNEA-style principle asks whether, if the proposed good existed, it is reasonable to expect that we would discern it.












![Skeptical-theism response tree](assets/07_skeptical-theism.png)

[THESIS] Skeptical theists challenge our entitlement to infer from "no reason we can see" to "probably no reason." Wykstra's CORNEA-style principle asks whether, if the proposed good existed, it is reasonable to expect that we would discern it. Bergmann-style representativeness theses deny that our sample of goods, evils and entailment relations is representative of the full moral order.

[OBJECTION] Broad skepticism threatens ordinary moral reasoning: if unknown goods may justify any horror, how can one condemn or prevent evil?

[REPLY] The view can be restricted to global inferences about what an omniscient governor may permit, while preserving local duties based on known reasons. We should prevent suffering because we lack authority to assume an unknown justifying plan.

[VERDICT] Skeptical theism weakens Rowe's inference but carries costs. It sits uneasily beside confident positive theodicies, can weaken providential interpretation, and must be bounded to avoid moral paralysis.

#### 5.1 Augustine: privation, fall and free will

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **Recommended opening definition:** Augustine treats evil as a privation of good arising through created freedom, not as a positive substance created by God.












![Augustinian privation and fall](assets/09_augustine.png)

#### Augustinian privation theory

[THESIS] Created being is good insofar as it has being; evil is not a positive substance made by God but a privation, corruption or disorder of a due good.

[ARGUMENT] An eye is a good organ; blindness is the lack of a function due to it. Likewise, a will is good as a created capacity but becomes evil by disordered attachment to a lesser good over the highest good. Moral evil originates in defective creaturely willing. In Augustine's larger narrative, natural disorder and suffering are connected with fall and punishment.

[QUALIFICATION] **What it preserves or explains:** It avoids a Manichaean dualism of two rival substances and locates culpability in creaturely agency.

[OBJECTION] A lack can be painfully real, so privation alone does not explain why God permits it. Pre-human predation, disease and extinction strain a historical account that treats natural evil as punishment for a human fall. The origin of a defect in an originally flawless creation is also difficult.

[REPLY] Privation is a metaphysical analysis, not denial of suffering. Defenders may read fall non-literally as the structural misuse of finite freedom, but this revision weakens the exact punitive claim.

[VERDICT] The theory is strong against the claim that God created an evil substance and strong on moral disorder; it is incomplete as a morally persuasive explanation of innocent, animal and evolutionary suffering.


#### 5.2 Irenaeus and Hick: developmental soul-making

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **Recommended opening definition:** Soul-making theodicy presents an epistemically distant and law-governed world as the arena in which immature persons develop virtue.












![Irenaean-Hick developmental model](assets/08_soul-making.png)

#### Irenaean-Hick soul-making

[THESIS] Humans are created as immature persons capable of development toward divine likeness; a world of epistemic distance, stable law and real danger permits freely achieved virtue.

[ARGUMENT] Ready-made innocence is not mature goodness. Courage presupposes danger, compassion another's need, forgiveness wrongdoing, and responsible action predictable consequences. Hick contrasts a soul-making world with a hedonistic paradise that continuously adjusts to desire. Irenaeus supplies the image-to-likeness developmental theology; John Keats supplies the phrase 'vale of soul-making'; Hick develops the modern theodicy.

[QUALIFICATION] **What it preserves or explains:** It addresses moral and natural evil within one developmental framework and explains why God is not overwhelmingly evident.

[OBJECTION] Much suffering destroys character, occurs before moral agency, or affects animals. The quantity needed for growth seems lower than the quantity observed. It also risks using victims as instruments for others' virtue.

[REPLY] Hick invokes eventual eschatological fulfilment so no life is finally wasted, and stresses that freedom and stable order require genuine risk.

[VERDICT] Soul-making explains a class of hardships better than horrendous or apparently pointless evils. Eschatology may compensate or defeat evil, but compensation alone does not prove permission was justified.


#### 5.3 Leibniz: best possible world

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **Recommended opening definition:** Leibniz argues that a perfect God actualizes the best feasible world as a total order, not a world containing no local evil.












![Leibniz best-world defence](assets/10_leibniz.png)

#### Leibnizian best-world reasoning

[THESIS] A perfectly wise and good God creates the best among possible worlds, where 'best' concerns the complete compossible order rather than the local pleasantness of each event.

[ARGUMENT] Possible goods can conflict: stable laws, freedom, regular causation and rich varieties of being may jointly entail permitted defects. God antecedently wills good and permits evil as part of an optimal totality rather than positively willing evil as evil.

[QUALIFICATION] **What it preserves or explains:** It highlights systemic trade-offs and rejects the naive inference that omnipotence can combine every isolated advantage.

[OBJECTION] The claim that this is the best feasible world is hard to verify, and global optimization can turn sufferers into expendable parts. Voltairean ridicule targets facile optimism rather than the bare logic of compossibility.

[REPLY] The defender can distinguish 'best total world' from 'best event' and insist that victims are included within final divine goodness, not sacrificed outside it.

[VERDICT] Compossibility is a valuable modal insight, but the best-world claim requires an ethically credible goodness-to-persons account.


#### 5.4 Greater-good, law-governed order and Swinburne-style responsibility

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **High-value transition:** A regular world is necessary for knowledge, prediction, action and moral responsibility.












[THESIS] A regular world is necessary for knowledge, prediction, action and moral responsibility. Fire must burn, gravity must operate and biological processes must be sufficiently stable; otherwise consequences would be arbitrary and agency trivial.

[ARGUMENT] Stable laws create both enabling goods and exposure to harm. If God constantly cancelled dangerous effects, agents could not learn causal structure, bear responsibility or help one another under genuine stakes. Some writers, including Swinburne in standard discussions, emphasize the value of serious responsibility and the opportunity to respond to others.

[OBJECTION] Regularity explains why some harms occur, not why preventable excess, extreme intensity or selective miracles are absent. A less dangerous law-system may still support agency.

[REPLY] Counterfactual comparison among entire law-systems is difficult, and complex life may require vulnerability. Yet the burden remains to explain why the actual distribution is not gratuitously severe.

[VERDICT] Natural-law reasoning is a plausible necessary-condition account, not a complete sufficient justification.

#### 5.5 Punishment, discipline and providence - use with caution

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **High-value transition:** Retributive punishment may explain a wrongdoer's suffering only where guilt, proportionality and legitimate authority are established.












[QUALIFICATION] Retributive punishment may explain a wrongdoer's suffering only where guilt, proportionality and legitimate authority are established. It cannot be casually applied to infants, disasters, animals or inherited conditions. Disciplinary suffering may sometimes reform, but uninvited observers lack warrant to label another's trauma a divine lesson.

[VERDICT] In an exam, present punishment and discipline as limited traditional motifs, then impose proportionality, innocence and victim-blaming constraints.

#### 6.1 Process theodicy: Whitehead, Hartshorne, Griffin and Cobb

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **High-value transition:** Whitehead is the metaphysical source; Hartshorne argues against classical omnipotence; David Ray Griffin develops process theodicy; John Cobb systematizes process theology.

[ARGUMENT]

1. Actual entities are partly self-creative; power is intrinsically distributed.

[REPLY] Process thinkers answer that coercive omnipotence is incoherent in a world of genuinely actual others; supreme persuasive power and perfect solidarity are more religiously adequate than total control.

[VERDICT] Process theodicy purchases divine innocence and solidarity at the price of classical sovereignty. That trade-off is its strength and its cost.

[VERDICT] Revising an attribute can dissolve the original inconsistent set, but it does not make suffering disappear. The problem is relocated to adequacy of worship, providence, hope and the metaphysics of divine-world interaction.

[THESIS] Evil and the profane can provide a structural foundation for religion by defining the predicament to be overcome and the boundary separating ordinary life from sacred order; however, profane does not mean evil, and religion also arises from wonder, gratitude and encounter.

[ARGUMENT] Durkheim's sacred-profane distinction is classificatory: sacred things are set apart and protected, while the profane is the ordinary sphere. Otto's holy as numinous and Eliade's analyses of hierophany further show how sacred space and time gain salience against ordinary homogeneity.

These cases show that the founding negative is not always moral wickedness.

[OBJECTION] Reducing religion to management of fear, evil or pollution ignores celebration, love, awe and gratitude. It can also confuse sociological function with truth.

[REPLY] "Foundation" need not mean sole origin or proof of truth. Evil and profane provide a structural problem and boundary through which practices become intelligible.

[VERDICT] They found religion functionally - predicament plus sacred boundary - but do not by themselves establish theism or exhaust religious life.

**Why this earns marks:** It separates evil from profane, uses Durkheim, Otto/Eliade and Indian soteriologies, answers the functional directive, adds a limitation and closes with a precise verdict.

#### PYQ 2 - 2020, Q6(b), 15 marks

#### Exact verified question

**What is the logical problem of evil? Discuss.**

#### Demand and structure

- **Directive:** Explain, analyse and judge at the level required by the printed wording.
- **Core route:** identify the exact logical, evidential, conceptual or functional demand.
- **Evidence:** named thinkers, technical distinctions and one controlled comparison.
- **Conclusion:** state what survives and the residual cost.

#### Examiner-grade model answer

[THESIS] The logical problem alleges inconsistency among an omnipotent God, a wholly good God and the existence of evil. The inconsistency arises only with bridge premises about what goodness would eliminate and what power can actualize.

[ARGUMENT] (1) God is omnipotent. (2) God is wholly good. (3) Evil exists. (4) A wholly good being eliminates every evil it can. (5) An omnipotent being can eliminate every evil. Therefore evil should not exist. Mackie argued that theism must surrender or qualify at least one proposition.

The theist replies by restricting the bridges. Omnipotence is power over the logically possible, not power to determine an act while leaving it libertarianly free. Plantinga's Free Will Defence proposes that significant freedom is a great good and that it is possible every feasible world with such freedom includes wrongdoing. Transworld depravity names this modal possibility; it does not mean universal constant wickedness.

[OBJECTION] The defence is weaker on natural evil and does not show why this world's scale of suffering is needed.

[REPLY] Those points transform the deductive problem into an evidential one. A logical defence needs compatibility, not a complete history.

[VERDICT] Plantinga substantially answers the strict contradiction, but Rowe-style evidential evil, animal suffering and horrendous evils preserve the larger challenge.

**Why this earns marks:** It reconstructs premises, identifies bridge principles, states Plantinga accurately, separates logical from evidential burden and offers a graded conclusion.

[THESIS] Evil creates an omnipotence paradox because an unlimitedly powerful creator appears able to prevent evil, while perfect goodness appears to require prevention. [VERDICT] The paradox does not prove omnipotence incoherent; it forces a distinction between logical power and unilateral control. It remains evidentially serious where suffering is unrelated to freedom.

**If God is the Absolute Creator, then the responsibility of the evil cannot belong to the human agent. Critically examine.**

#### Demand and structure

- **Directive:** Critically examine both attribution and responsibility.
- **Core route:** identify the exact logical, evidential, conceptual or functional demand.
- **Evidence:** named thinkers, technical distinctions and one controlled comparison.
- **Conclusion:** state what survives and the residual cost.

#### Examiner-grade model answer

[THESIS] Absolute creatorhood makes God causally responsible for the existence and powers of agents, but it does not automatically erase derivative human responsibility. The issue is whether creaturely agency is genuine and whether God's permission is morally justified.

[ARGUMENT] The critic argues: God knowingly creates each agent, circumstance and consequence; therefore the ultimate explanatory chain ends in God. If God could actualize a better world, shifting all blame to creatures is incomplete. Augustine's claim that evil originates in defective will and Plantinga's significant freedom reply by distinguishing primary creation from secondary self-determination. Causing a free agent to exist is not identical with causing the agent's wrongful choice.

[OBJECTION] God selected the world knowing the misuse and could limit consequences. Natural evil is not traceable to human choice.

[REPLY] Freedom may require non-control over choice, and stable law may require real consequences. Yet these claims establish at most a possible permission; they do not excuse every excess. Process thought instead limits unilateral divine power, while non-creator Indian systems remove the creator defendant.

[VERDICT] Responsibility is layered: humans can be culpable for freely chosen moral evil, while classical theism retains a burden regarding creation, permission, natural evil and world-selection.

**Why this earns marks:** It refuses the false choice between divine and human responsibility, distinguishes causal levels, uses Augustine/Plantinga/process comparison and gives a qualified judgement.

#### PYQ 5 - 2023, Q8(a), 20 marks

#### Exact verified question

**"All evil is either sin or punishment for sin." - St. Augustine. Critically discuss.**

#### Demand and structure

- **Directive:** Explain, analyse and judge at the level required by the printed wording.
- **Core route:** identify the exact logical, evidential, conceptual or functional demand.
- **Evidence:** named thinkers, technical distinctions and one controlled comparison.
- **Conclusion:** state what survives and the residual cost.

#### Examiner-grade model answer

[THESIS] Augustine's statement combines a moral genealogy of evil with a punitive account: culpable evil is sin, while suffered evil is punishment. Its privation and free-will elements are philosophically important, but the universal punitive formula is inadequate for innocent, animal and evolutionary suffering.

[ARGUMENT] Augustine holds that being as such is good because created by a good God. Evil is privatio boni - the corruption or absence of a due perfection - rather than a substance created by God. Moral evil begins when finite will turns from the highest good toward lesser goods. In the larger fall narrative, natural disorder and suffering follow sin, allowing Augustine to clear God of creating evil.

[STRENGTH] Privation defeats dualism and accurately describes many evils as parasitic on goods: blindness presupposes an eye, betrayal a relationship, corruption an ordered capacity. Free will locates culpability in agents.

[OBJECTION] A perfect creation's uncaused fall is difficult; an omniscient creator foreknows the result. Geological and evolutionary suffering precedes human agency. Punishment of descendants or animals violates proportionality, and eternal punishment would perpetuate rather than defeat evil.

[REPLY] Privation need not deny the reality of pain, and fall may be read as a structural account of creaturely defect rather than a scientific history. But this revision retreats from the literal universal punishment thesis.

[COMPARISON] Hick replaces retribution with development; process thought revises power; karma explains distribution but risks victim blame.

[VERDICT] "Sin or punishment" illuminates moral disorder within an Augustinian system but cannot serve as a complete theodicy for the actual history of suffering.

**Why this earns marks:** It explicates both privation and punishment, uses the blindness example, integrates modern evolutionary criticism, compares alternatives and gives a directive-responsive verdict.

#### PYQ 6 - 2024, Q6(c), 15 marks

#### Exact verified question

**"The problem of evil is a direct offshoot of how God is conceptualised in a system." Critically discuss.**

#### Demand and structure

- **Directive:** Explain, analyse and judge at the level required by the printed wording.
- **Core route:** identify the exact logical, evidential, conceptual or functional demand.
- **Evidence:** named thinkers, technical distinctions and one controlled comparison.
- **Conclusion:** state what survives and the residual cost.

#### Examiner-grade model answer

[THESIS] The problem of evil is directly concept-dependent: it is most acute where God is an omnipotent, omniscient, perfectly good creator and sustainer. Altering creatorhood, power, knowledge, goodness or world-relation changes or dissolves the formal contradiction, though suffering remains.

[ARGUMENT] Classical theism generates Mackie's triad because perfect goodness opposes evil, omnipotence can prevent it, omniscience knows it and creatorhood appears to make permission attributable. Free-will, soul-making and law-order responses try to preserve the profile by supplying morally sufficient reasons.

Process theism revises omnipotence: God persuades but cannot coerce finite actualities, buying innocence at the cost of sovereignty. Open theism revises the settled status of future free acts. A finite God faces struggle rather than culpable permission. Advaita locates evil within the empirical order of avidya and karma while ultimate non-duality alters the God-world dualism. Buddhism, Samkhya, Mimamsa and Jainism lack an omnipotent creator defendant, so the question becomes diagnosis of dukkha or bondage. Nyaya and Visistadvaita retain a divine governor and therefore must relate karma to just administration.

[OBJECTION] Concept-dependence may appear to solve the problem by definition, and impersonal or non-creator systems still face suffering, justice and consolation.

[REPLY] The claim is not that suffering vanishes, but that the logical accusation changes with the metaphysical defendant.

[VERDICT] Evil is a stress-test of a God-concept, not a uniform refutation of religion. Revising the concept dissolves one problem while relocating costs to worship-worthiness, providence or karmic justice.

**Why this earns marks:** It makes the God-concept the organizing axis, compares preserved and revised attributes, includes accurate Indian diversity and avoids saying non-theism eliminates suffering.

#### PYQ 7 - 2025, Q6(b), 15 marks

#### Exact verified question

**Explain the main tenets of the Process Theodicy as an explanation of the problem of evil.**

#### Demand and structure

- **Directive:** Explain, analyse and judge at the level required by the printed wording.
- **Core route:** identify the exact logical, evidential, conceptual or functional demand.
- **Evidence:** named thinkers, technical distinctions and one controlled comparison.
- **Conclusion:** state what survives and the residual cost.

#### Examiner-grade model answer

[THESIS] Process theodicy explains evil by rejecting classical unilateral omnipotence. God is perfectly persuasive and receptive but cannot wholly determine finite actualities, so evil does not arise from a preventable divine decree.

[ARGUMENT] In Whitehead's process metaphysics, reality is composed of becoming actual occasions with some self-determination; creativity and power are distributed. God's primordial nature orders possibilities and supplies each occasion an initial aim, while the consequent nature receives and preserves the world's achievements and suffering. Because finite occasions partly create themselves, divine action is persuasive lure rather than coercion. Evil occurs through discord, collision and failure to realize better possibilities.

Hartshorne argues that monopolistic omnipotence is incoherent if other beings are genuinely actual. Griffin develops the crucial theodicy: persuasion is a metaphysical necessity, not a voluntary self-limitation. Cobb provides theological systematization. Divine solidarity is expressed in the process image of God as fellow-sufferer.

[STRENGTH] The view directly addresses natural as well as moral evil and avoids blaming every victim or choice.

[OBJECTION] A non-coercive God may be unworthy of worship, unable to guarantee final victory, and dependent on a contested metaphysics.

[REPLY] Process thinkers answer that relational power and faithful persuasion are more coherent and morally admirable than total control.

[VERDICT] Process theodicy purchases divine innocence and solidarity by revising sovereignty. Its adequacy depends on whether persuasive power is enough for worship and hope.

**Why this earns marks:** It names distinct process thinkers, explains primordial/consequent nature, initial aim and necessary persuasion, and states the exact theological trade-off.

#### 10 marks

#### Question

Why is the distinction between logical and evidential evil indispensable?

#### Model solution

[THESIS] Logical evil alleges contradiction; evidential evil alleges improbability. The distinction fixes what counts as success.

[ARGUMENT] Mackie's set needs unrestricted bridge premises. Plantinga blocks contradiction by a possible freedom condition. Rowe can grant this and still argue that apparently gratuitous suffering, especially animal and horrendous evil, is unexpected on perfect theism.

[OBJECTION] If possibility is highly implausible, logical success seems empty.

[REPLY] It still defeats a deductive claim, but not the inductive case.

[VERDICT] The distinction prevents the mistaken conclusion that Plantinga solved every problem of evil.

**Why this earns marks:** Compact definitions, named arguments, burden control and graded verdict.

#### 15 marks

#### Question

Critically compare Augustinian and Irenaean-Hick accounts of evil.

#### Model solution

[THESIS] Augustine explains evil retrospectively through privation, defective freedom and fall; Hick explains it prospectively through development under epistemic distance.

[ARGUMENT] Augustine preserves creation's goodness by denying evil is a substance and locating sin in disordered will. Hick begins with immature persons who require a regular risky world to achieve virtue. Augustine is retributive where suffering is punishment; Hick is developmental.

[STRENGTH] Augustine is strong against dualism and on culpability. Hick integrates natural adversity and moral formation.

[OBJECTION] Augustine struggles with pre-human suffering and a flawless creation's fall. Hick struggles with crushing, animal and horrendous suffering and risks instrumentalization.

[REPLY] Augustine can read fall structurally; Hick invokes eschatological fulfilment. Neither reply fully settles proportionality.

[VERDICT] Hick better fits evolutionary history; Augustine offers sharper moral ontology. A complete answer requires Adams's goodness-to-persons test.

**Why this earns marks:** Clear axes, doctrine, objections, replies, modern evidence and reasoned comparison.

#### 15 marks

#### Question

Does karma solve the problem of evil?

#### Model solution

[THESIS] Karma can explain distribution across lives and remove arbitrary divine blame, but it does not constitute one uniform Indian solution and creates serious moral and identity problems.

[ARGUMENT] Nyaya combines karma with a divine administrator; Mimamsa supports non-creator karmic efficacy; Buddhism uses causal continuity without permanent self; Jainism treats karma as material bondage; Vedanta schools place karma within different God-world metaphysics.

[STRENGTH] The life-series expands moral causation beyond present innocence.

[OBJECTION] Victim blame, regress, memory, identity, opacity and social legitimation undermine justice.

[REPLY] Beginningless samsara avoids a first deed; memory is not necessary for all identity; compassion remains obligatory.

[VERDICT] Karma relocates the distribution problem but cannot justify retrospective certainty about victims.

**Why this earns marks:** It differentiates schools, gives the structural advantage, meets the strongest ethical objections and refuses homogenization.

#### 20 marks

#### Question

Has process theodicy solved the problem of evil or changed the concept of God?

#### Model solution

[THESIS] It solves the classical culpability problem only by changing the concept of God; whether this counts as solution depends on whether persuasive, non-coercive deity remains religiously adequate.

[ARGUMENT] Whitehead's actual occasions possess self-determination; God orders possibilities through the primordial nature and receives the world through the consequent nature. Hartshorne rejects monopolistic omnipotence. Griffin argues persuasion is metaphysically necessary. Evil therefore arises from distributed creativity, not preventable decree.

[STRENGTH] Moral and natural evil fall under one metaphysical model; divine solidarity is central.

[OBJECTION] Such a God cannot guarantee rescue or final victory, and the process metaphysics is contestable.

[REPLY] Process thinkers argue that coercive omnipotence is incoherent and relational power more worthy of worship.

[COUNTER-REPLY] Coherence of distributed power does not establish sufficient providential efficacy.

[VERDICT] Process theodicy is an internally powerful relocation, not a cost-free preservation of classical theism.

**Why this earns marks:** Exact process vocabulary, named thinker roles, objection-counter-reply and direct answer to both halves.

#### 20 marks

#### Question

"The greatest weakness of theodicy is not logical but moral." Discuss.

#### Model solution

[THESIS] Modern debate suggests that even logically coherent theodicies may fail morally by instrumentalizing sufferers, blaming victims or silencing protest.

[ARGUMENT] Plantinga can establish compatibility without interpreting each horror. Hick's development risks making one person's agony a means to another's compassion. Leibnizian total optimization may overlook goodness-to-persons. Punishment and karma can become victim blaming. Dostoevsky's Ivan rejects harmony bought with innocent suffering; post-Holocaust caution resists total explanation. Adams therefore distinguishes balancing-off from defeat within the sufferer's life.

[OBJECTION] Without some greater-good reasoning, permission by a good God remains unintelligible.

[REPLY] A restrained defence can state possible compatibility while pastoral practice prioritizes lament, prevention and solidarity. Positive accounts must pass consent, proportionality, distribution and personal-defeat tests.

[VERDICT] Theodicy must remain philosophically possible but morally non-triumphal. Its success is measured not only by consistency but by whether it preserves victims as ends.

**Why this earns marks:** It reframes the question at meta-level, uses multiple theories and protest thinkers, includes a constructive reply and ends with an ethical criterion.

#### 11. Modal logic without notation overload

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **High-value transition:** The logical problem is a question about whether the relevant propositions can be true.












[THESIS] The logical problem is a question about whether the relevant propositions can be true
together in at least one possible world. The critic alleges that no possible world contains both
the classical God and evil; the defender needs only one coherent possibility in which God has a
morally sufficient reason to permit evil.

[ARGUMENT] Three modal ideas must be kept separate. First, **logical possibility** means that a
description contains no contradiction. Second, **world feasibility** asks whether God can
actualize that description given truths about undetermined creaturely choices. Third,
**actuality** asks which world and history obtain. Plantinga argues at the first two levels. He
does not infer that the freedom story is the actual divine policy merely because it is possible.

[OBJECTION] The distinction between possible and feasible worlds appears to limit divine power by
facts God does not control. If counterfactuals of freedom are independently true, what grounds
them, and why cannot an omnipotent creator choose better agents or circumstances?

[REPLY] Libertarian freedom precisely denies that God determines the content of the free choice.
The defender can say that creating agents includes accepting truths about how those agents would
freely respond. The grounding question remains a serious objection to Molinist and feasible-world
accounts, but it is not a proof of contradiction.

[QUALIFICATION] In an UPSC answer, modal vocabulary should clarify rather than display technique.
Write: "A logically describable sinless free world may not be strongly actualizable by God if the
truth of free creaturely choices is not under divine causal control." Do not use symbols unless
they reduce ambiguity.

[VERDICT] Modal analysis is most successful as a negative result: the simple inconsistent-set
argument needs more than the bare triad. It leaves open whether the actual world is a reasonable
choice for a perfectly good creator.

#### 12. The moral premises behind the logical problem

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **Major criticism:** The logical problem is best understood as a stress-test of perfect goodness under the.












[THESIS] The dispute is not only about divine power. It is also about the content of goodness.
The premise "a good being eliminates evil" is too strong unless it includes "when elimination
does not sacrifice an equal or greater good, violate another's agency, or permit an equal or worse
evil."

[ARGUMENT] Ordinary morality permits painful surgery, dangerous education and enforcement of
just law when proportionate and necessary. The theist generalizes: divine permission may be
morally justified where a good is logically connected with risk. The critic answers that ordinary
permission is constrained by consent, necessity, proportionality, fair distribution and
accountability. A parent may allow a difficult examination but not torture a child for character.
Therefore "greater good" is not a blank cheque.

[OBJECTION] Applying human moral constraints to God may be anthropomorphic because creatorhood
changes the relation between giver of life and creature.

[REPLY] If divine goodness has no recognizable continuity with moral goodness, the theist loses
the very premise used to praise and worship God. Analogical predication may allow difference of
mode, not reversal of evaluative content.

[QUALIFICATION] This creates a dilemma for extreme skeptical theism. Too much confidence in human
moral standards makes evil evidentially forceful; too little makes "God is good" thin and removes
the basis for positive theodicy.

[VERDICT] The logical problem is best understood as a stress-test of perfect goodness under the
conditions of creator responsibility, not as a mechanical contradiction among three dictionary
definitions.

#### 13. Freedom: value, range and responsibility

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **High-value transition:** Free-will approaches require more than the assertion that freedom is valuable.












[THESIS] Free-will approaches require more than the assertion that freedom is valuable. They must
specify the kind of freedom, the range of consequences necessary for responsibility, and the
distribution of opportunities for meaningful choice.

[ARGUMENT] Libertarian freedom holds that at least some choices are not determined by prior
conditions. Compatibilist freedom identifies acting from one's own reasons without external
constraint even if those reasons are causally determined. Plantinga's defence needs a libertarian
relation to God: if God determines the choice, creaturely authorship cannot wholly transfer moral
responsibility. Yet significant freedom need not entail unlimited power to harm. Human societies
rightly restrict weapons and opportunities for catastrophic wrongdoing while preserving agency.
The critic therefore asks why God permits consequences far beyond what meaningful choice needs.

[OBJECTION] Continuous divine restriction would make the world a moral nursery, obscure causal
responsibility and prevent serious cooperation against evil.

[REPLY] The objection supports some regular consequences, not every scale of consequence. The
theist must explain a threshold: which harms are integral to responsible agency and which are
apparently excessive?

[QUALIFICATION] Social and structural evil complicates individualistic freedom. Poverty,
oppression and institutions are produced through many choices and inherited systems. No single
agent may intend the total harm, yet responsibility is distributed. A theodicy centered only on
isolated decisions misses this cumulative structure.

[VERDICT] Freedom plausibly explains the possibility of moral fault. It less clearly explains the
available magnitude of harm, unequal moral opportunity, structural evil and divine non-restraint.

#### 14. Natural evil: autonomy of creation and excess

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **Recommended opening definition:** A stable natural order is not merely a stage for morality; it is the condition of embodied.












[THESIS] A stable natural order is not merely a stage for morality; it is the condition of embodied
life, scientific understanding and purposive action. Some contemporary formulations describe
creation as having relative autonomy rather than being a theatre of continuous intervention.

[ARGUMENT] Regular tectonics, weather, mutation, immunity and ecological competition have
life-enabling and harm-producing aspects. A world in which harmful effects vanished whenever they
threatened a person would cease to be cognitively reliable. Agents could not infer causes,
coordinate plans or develop technologies. This supports a general permission of natural risk.

[OBJECTION] The critic distinguishes law from initial conditions, regularity from intensity, and
ordinary vulnerability from apparently pointless agony. An omniscient designer could allegedly
choose different constants, safer organisms, earlier warnings or selective interventions without
abolishing order.

[REPLY] Whole-system comparison is difficult: changes that remove one harm may remove goods or
produce unknown harms. Complex adaptive systems may require trade-offs that local observers
cannot calculate.

[COUNTER-OBJECTION] Difficulty of comparison is epistemic modesty, not positive evidence that the
actual balance is optimal. It blocks confident atheistic inference only to the extent that access
is genuinely poor; it also blocks confident theodicy.

[VERDICT] Creation-autonomy and law-order accounts explain why a serious world cannot be a
hedonistic paradise. The question of preventable excess remains the central evidential residue.

#### 15. Evolutionary evil and deep time

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **Recommended opening definition:** Deep time is a fairness test for every theodicy.












[THESIS] Evolutionary history intensifies the problem because pain, predation, parasitism and mass
extinction occur over immense pre-human periods. The data weaken any account that treats natural
evil chiefly as punishment for human sin.

[ARGUMENT] Evolution through variation, selection and ecological interaction generates adaptive
complexity but also waste, failed organisms and sentient suffering. A theist may value a creation
capable of bringing forth novelty through lawful processes rather than receiving every form by
direct fiat. Such fecundity can be a good. Nevertheless, the process contains beings who cannot
understand danger, consent to risk or acquire human-style virtue.

[OBJECTION] Calling evolutionary suffering the price of autonomy treats animals as disposable
means. If God values each sentient creature, a species-level good is insufficient.

[REPLY] A defender may extend eschatological or redemptive goodness to non-human creatures, or
argue that creaturely value is not exhausted by painless duration. These proposals are
philosophically possible but source-dependent and speculative.

[QUALIFICATION] Karmic rebirth across animal forms supplies a different continuity story, but it
can moralize animal pain without accessible evidence of desert. Jain concern for graded living
beings and radical non-violence provides an ethical response even where metaphysical explanation
is disputed.

[VERDICT] Deep time is a fairness test for every theodicy. A theory that mentions animals only as
instruments for human growth is incomplete.

#### 16. Rowe, Draper and cumulative evidence

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **High-value transition:** The evidential case is strongest when cumulative rather than dependent on one dramatic.












[THESIS] The evidential case is strongest when cumulative rather than dependent on one dramatic
example. It combines apparently gratuitous instances with patterns of biological pain, unequal
distribution, divine hiddenness and the failure of proposed reasons to cover all classes.

[ARGUMENT] Rowe asks whether at least one instance of intense suffering lacks a justifying
connection. Draper asks a comparative question: is the pattern of pleasure and pain more expected
if nature is indifferent than if guided by perfect benevolence? The two approaches can reinforce
each other. Rowe supplies local cases; Draper supplies pattern comparison. Neither requires the
claim that evil is logically incompatible with God.

[OBJECTION] Theism is not a single bare hypothesis. It may include freedom, stable law,
soul-making, eschatology and independent evidence from religious experience or cosmological
arguments. Evaluating one datum in isolation distorts a cumulative worldview.

[REPLY] Auxiliary theodicies must have independent motivation and predictive discipline. A view
that adds a new hidden purpose for every counterexample becomes resistant to evidence. Other
evidence for God can offset evil, but Topic 02 arguments must be weighed rather than presumed.

[QUALIFICATION] Do not assign invented numerical priors. State only the comparative structure:
evidence confirms a hypothesis to the extent that it is more expected on that hypothesis than on
its rivals.

[VERDICT] Evidential evil functions as an undercutting and rebutting consideration within a total
case. It rarely yields a stand-alone mathematical conclusion, but it can be philosophically
weighty.

#### 17. Skeptical theism: three boundaries

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **Recommended opening definition:** Skeptical theism is defensible only when bounded epistemically, morally and religiously.












[THESIS] Skeptical theism is defensible only when bounded epistemically, morally and religiously.

1. **Epistemic boundary:** Human ignorance concerns the full network of divine reasons; it does
   not imply ignorance of every local good, evil or causal relation.
2. **Moral boundary:** Agents must act on accessible moral reasons. Unknown divine permission
   never authorizes human infliction or neglect.
3. **Religious boundary:** If the view blocks all inference from worldly goods to providence, it
   cannot selectively block only negative evidence while retaining every positive sign.

[ARGUMENT] Wykstra's accessibility principle is plausible when the scale difference between
human and divine cognition is relevant. Bergmann's representativeness concern likewise warns
that sampled goods may not exhaust possible goods. But a complete suspension of inductive
judgement would damage ordinary reasoning.

[OBJECTION] The asymmetry between divine and human perspectives may itself justify selective
skepticism: evil-permission could involve remote entailments, while some positive religious
experiences are direct.

[REPLY] That distinction must be argued case by case. It cannot be assumed merely to protect a
favoured conclusion. Moreover, the more opaque divine purposes become, the harder it is to infer
that God is trustworthy in the recognizable moral sense.

[VERDICT] Skeptical theism is best used as a defeater-defeater against overconfident noseeum
reasoning, followed immediately by an affirmation of local moral responsibility.

#### 18. Soul-making under the proportionality test

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **High-value transition:** Soul-making succeeds for obstacles proportionate to agency and fails when it treats all.












[THESIS] Soul-making succeeds for obstacles proportionate to agency and fails when it treats all
suffering as development irrespective of outcome.

[ARGUMENT] A developmental environment needs danger, resistance, uncertainty and other persons'
needs. These conditions enable courage, patience, compassion and practical wisdom. Yet formation
requires an agent capable of learning, a challenge within some range of response, and an
opportunity for recovery. Horrendous evils can fragment memory, trust and agency. Infant death
and much animal pain offer no obvious subject of moral formation.

[OBJECTION] Goods may accrue to communities or other persons, and eschatological life may complete
development interrupted here.

[REPLY] Community goods do not automatically justify using one person as material for another.
Eschatology can widen the developmental horizon only if the individual sufferer is restored and
the evil defeated within that person's good.

[QUALIFICATION] The distinction between first-order evil and second-order good should never be
written as "evil is necessary for good." Some second-order virtues need vulnerability, but the
same virtue may be elicited by many degrees of hardship. Necessity and proportionality require
separate argument.

[VERDICT] Hick supplies a powerful account of why a world with no adversity might be morally
shallow. Adams supplies the needed correction: goodness must finally be goodness to persons.

#### 19. Augustine after evolutionary criticism

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **Major criticism:** Corruption remains dependent on an underlying good: disease is malfunction of living.












[THESIS] Augustine's metaphysical privation claim can survive even if the historical-punitive
account of natural evil is rejected.

[ARGUMENT] Corruption remains dependent on an underlying good: disease is malfunction of living
systems, cruelty is disorder of agency, injustice is corruption of social relation. This
ontological point does not require that earthquakes began after a first human sin. The historical
fall narrative, by contrast, makes empirical and moral claims about original perfection,
inherited disorder and punishment.

[OBJECTION] Separating privation from fall may save a fragment while abandoning the theodicy.
Even if evil is ontologically parasitic, God creates the subjects and conditions in which
privation occurs.

[REPLY] The defender can combine privation with finite creation and free development: a created
world is good yet not divine or incapable of defect. This avoids dualism without claiming that
every natural evil punishes sin.

[COUNTER-OBJECTION] If defect is an inevitable condition of finite being, either God cannot create
without evil or chooses a vulnerable order. The classical question returns at the level of
creation.

[VERDICT] Use Augustine in layers. Privation is the strongest philosophical layer; defective
freedom is strong for moral evil; universal punishment and a historical cosmic fall carry the
heaviest burden.

#### 20. Process theology compared with voluntary self-limitation

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **Major criticism:** Process thought gives the most systematic non-classical response to natural evil, but.












[THESIS] Process non-coercion and classical kenotic self-limitation must be distinguished by
modality. In a self-limitation view, God possesses unilateral power but refrains for the sake of
freedom or relationship. In Griffin-style process thought, such unilateral coercion is not a
metaphysical possibility.

[ARGUMENT] The distinction changes responsibility. Voluntary restraint still permits the
question, "Why did God not make an exception here?" Necessary non-coercion answers that no
exception was available. It therefore gives process thought a cleaner innocence claim but a
greater assurance problem. A God who voluntarily risks creation may remain sovereign; a process
God participates in a field of distributed creativity.

[OBJECTION] Process thought may redefine divine power until God resembles one influential cause
among others.

[REPLY] Hartshornean and process accounts distinguish supreme relational or persuasive power
from finite power. God offers the best possibility to every occasion and perfectly receives all
value; supremacy need not mean monopoly.

[QUALIFICATION] The answer must state that Whitehead's dipolar terminology, Hartshorne's critique
of omnipotence and Griffin's theodicy are related but not interchangeable. Do not attribute every
later theological conclusion directly to Whitehead.

[VERDICT] Process thought gives the most systematic non-classical response to natural evil, but
its success depends on whether worship and hope require guaranteed control.

#### 21. Karma: desert, causation and social ethics

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **Recommended opening definition:** A strict desert reading says present suffering is proportionate consequence of prior.












[THESIS] Karma can be interpreted as desert, impersonal moral causation, character formation or
bondage mechanics. The ethical evaluation changes with the interpretation.

[ARGUMENT] A strict desert reading says present suffering is proportionate consequence of prior
wrongdoing. An impersonal-causation reading treats deeds as producing tendencies without a divine
judge. A formative reading emphasizes how action shapes character and future possibility. Jain
karmic matter gives a more literal bondage mechanism, while Buddhist dependent continuity denies
a permanent owner. Nyaya adds an intelligent allocator; Mimamsa can retain ritual efficacy
without creatorhood.

[OBJECTION] Without memory, transparent proportionality or a stable person, retribution seems
unfair. Beginninglessness avoids a first-cause regress only by denying a first temporal point; it
does not show why the system is morally good. Socially, observers may infer desert from
misfortune and withhold reform.

[REPLY] Memory is not the sole ground of responsibility even within one life, and causal
continuity can support concern. More importantly, no school licenses a bystander to know a
victim's hidden karmic history. Compassion, dana, ahimsa and assistance are themselves morally
required actions.

[VERDICT] Use karma as a comparative model of trans-life moral causation, never as a warrant for
explaining a particular victim. Its strongest feature is distribution; its greatest risk is
retrospective moralization.

#### 22. Advaita and the reality-status objection

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **Major criticism:** Advaita (non-dual school) changes the problem by distinguishing empirical experience from ultimate.












[THESIS] Advaita changes the problem by distinguishing empirical experience from ultimate
non-duality, but it cannot responsibly answer suffering with the slogan "the world is unreal."

[ARGUMENT] At the empirical level, persons, Isvara, karma, dharma, ignorance and suffering are
operative. Knowledge removes the fundamental misidentification of self with finite body-mind and
reveals non-dual Brahman. At the ultimate level, no independent evil principle confronts Brahman.
The classical creator-versus-evil duality is therefore not final.

[OBJECTION] If empirical suffering is sublated, the view may appear to trivialize victims and
make moral action provisional.

[REPLY] Empirical validity is sufficient for duties, compassion, discipline and liberation
practice before realization. Sublatability does not mean non-occurrence at its operative level.
Dream pain can be real to the dreamer without being ultimate, though this analogy must not be used
pastorally to dismiss actual trauma.

[QUALIFICATION] Ramanuja's critique is relevant: if the world and selves are real modes dependent
on Brahman, their suffering cannot be assigned merely a lower ontological status. Visistadvaita
therefore retains a more direct theistic governance problem alongside karma and grace.

[VERDICT] Advaita dissolves the ultimate metaphysical dualism but leaves an empirical ethical and
soteriological problem that must be answered through knowledge, duty and compassion.

#### 23. Buddhist and Samkhya diagnoses compared

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **High-value transition:** This is a reframing, not a victory by default.












[THESIS] Buddhism and Samkhya both avoid a creator theodicy but disagree radically about self and
the cause of bondage.

[ARGUMENT] Samkhya locates suffering in purusha's apparent identification with prakriti's
evolutes. Discriminative knowledge separates consciousness from material-nature activity,
culminating in kaivalya. Buddhism denies a permanent purusha and analyses dukkha through
dependent origination, impermanence, craving and ignorance. Cessation is not isolation of a
substantial witness but ending the conditions that sustain appropriation and suffering.

[OBJECTION] Removing God avoids culpability but may not explain why the beginningless causal
order has its painful character. Samkhya must explain the conjunction of radically different
principles; Buddhism must explain responsibility and continuity without self.

[REPLY] Both systems present the "why" as a mistaken demand for a first creator-cause and redirect
attention to operative conditions and liberation. A causal diagnosis can be sufficient for
practice even without a cosmic justification.

[QUALIFICATION] This is a reframing, not a victory by default. Existential pain, compassion and
fairness remain, while the logical inconsistent triad disappears.

[VERDICT] Creatorless systems demonstrate that the problem of evil is not coextensive with the
problem of suffering. They exchange theodicy for diagnosis and soteriology.

#### 24. Protest, lament and moral knowledge

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **High-value transition:** Protest is philosophically important because it treats the negative moral appearance of.












[THESIS] Protest is philosophically important because it treats the negative moral appearance of
suffering as knowledge-bearing rather than as a defect of faith.

[ARGUMENT] Job's protest resists friends who infer guilt from suffering. Ivan Karamazov refuses
to endorse a harmony that uses innocent agony. Camus makes solidarity and revolt an ethical
answer without metaphysical reconciliation. These positions differ, but all reject the movement
from "a total explanation may exist" to "the sufferer should accept our explanation."

[OBJECTION] Protest without metaphysical resolution may express emotion rather than answer the
truth question.

[REPLY] Moral perception is part of the evidence. A theory that can preserve consistency only by
erasing the prima facie wrongness of cruelty has changed the meaning of goodness. Protest also
generates practical reasons to resist avoidable evil.

[QUALIFICATION] Anti-theodicy should not be represented as the claim that no meaning, redemption
or religious response is possible. Its central objection is to comprehensive third-person
justification and instrumentalization.

[VERDICT] Protest is not a logical refutation of theism, but it is a constraint on what may count
as a morally adequate theodicy.

#### 25. Pastoral reason and philosophical restraint

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **High-value transition:** The order appropriate to an examination answer is not always the order appropriate at a.












[THESIS] The order appropriate to an examination answer is not always the order appropriate at a
hospital bed, disaster site or bereavement. Philosophical competence includes knowing when an
explanation is morally mistimed.

[ARGUMENT] A sufferer may need safety, truthful presence, lament and practical assistance before
causal or theological interpretation. Offering karma, punishment or soul-making as a diagnosis of
a particular person's suffering exceeds available evidence and can add moral injury. Prayer can
include protest; trust can coexist with uncertainty; religious communities can embody
solidarity without claiming a hidden plan.

[OBJECTION] Refusing explanation may leave religious belief cognitively empty when it is most
challenged.

[REPLY] Restraint is not permanent evasion. General philosophical accounts can be assessed in
appropriate contexts. The immediate moral duty is not identical with the later intellectual
task.

[QUALIFICATION] The same rule protects against secular triumphalism. One should not use a
person's trauma as an occasion to score an atheistic conclusion either. Victim-centered reason
asks what the evidence supports and what the situation ethically permits.

[VERDICT] The practical implication of the problem of evil is active compassion under epistemic
humility. A theory that excuses preventable harm fails regardless of its abstract elegance.

#### 27. Essay laboratory - Is evil evidence against God or against a concept of God?

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **Recommended opening definition:** God is finite, processual, impersonal or not a creator, the inference changes.












[THESIS] Evil first tests a concept of God and only then contributes evidence about whether any
being answering to that concept exists. If "God" means an omnipotent, omniscient and perfectly
good creator, apparently preventable suffering bears directly on the concept's instantiation. If
God is finite, processual, impersonal or not a creator, the inference changes.

[ARGUMENT] The conceptual stage asks which attributes generate expectations. Perfect goodness
creates an expectation of opposition to evil; knowledge creates awareness; power creates capacity;
creatorhood creates responsibility for the framework. The evidential stage compares those
expectations with the world. A process God predicts struggle and partial achievement more readily
than classical control. Advaita predicts an empirically operative but ultimately sublatable order.
Buddhism predicts conditioned suffering without a providential distribution. Each view must be
tested against what it actually asserts.

[OBJECTION] This method lets believers escape evidence by revising God whenever a difficulty
arises.

[REPLY] Revision is legitimate only if independently motivated and if its religious cost is paid
openly. An ad hoc finite God introduced solely to avoid evil has weak explanatory value. Process
thought, by contrast, offers a wider metaphysics of power and becoming, though that metaphysics is
contestable.

[VERDICT] Evil does not address an empty word. It confirms or disconfirms profiles. The strongest
answer therefore moves in the order **concept -> expectation -> evidence -> revision cost**.

#### 28. Essay laboratory - Could God create virtue without suffering?

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **High-value transition:** Some virtue logically requires vulnerability, but it does not follow that the actual.












[THESIS] Some virtue logically requires vulnerability, but it does not follow that the actual
quantity and distribution of suffering is required.

[ARGUMENT] Courage needs perceived danger, compassion needs another's need, forgiveness
presupposes wrong, and responsibility needs consequential action. These are relation-dependent
goods. A world containing them cannot be wholly insulated from harm. Yet virtues differ:
creativity, friendship, gratitude and understanding do not require atrocity. Even danger-dependent
virtues may require only bounded challenge. The critic can therefore grant soul-making while
denying that horrors are necessary.

[OBJECTION] The total network of virtues, histories and free relations may require risks that no
local observer can calculate.

[REPLY] This possibility creates humility but not justification. The theist should avoid the
universal claim that every evil is necessary and defend only a general risk structure. Adams's
defeat condition must handle cases that exceed ordinary formation.

[QUALIFICATION] The question "Could God create virtue directly?" also concerns authenticity.
Implanted dispositions may be good qualities, but achieved virtues include a person's authorship
and history. That distinction supports development without proving every route to development
morally permissible.

[VERDICT] Soul-making explains why a frictionless world may lack important goods; proportionality
and goodness-to-persons decide whether the explanation reaches actual horrors.

#### 29. Essay laboratory - Is divine permission equivalent to causation?

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **High-value transition:** The 2022 PYQ is best answered through layered rather than exclusive responsibility:.












[THESIS] Permission and causation are not identical, but a creator's permission can still carry
responsibility where prevention is easy, knowledge complete and the stakes grave.

[ARGUMENT] A human wrongdoer can be the direct efficient cause of moral evil. God, on classical
theism, causes the agent and sustaining order but does not determine the free choice. This supports
derivative creaturely culpability. Nevertheless, knowingly selecting and sustaining a world
creates a responsibility of framework and permission. Moral analysis therefore needs at least
three levels: authorship of the act, design of available powers and consequences, and decision
whether to intervene.

[OBJECTION] If God intervened whenever misuse became serious, creaturely action would not be real.

[REPLY] Responsibility does not require unlimited consequence. Legal and social systems constrain
harm while retaining agency. The theist must identify why divine restraint is more limited than
responsible human restraint should be.

[QUALIFICATION] Process thought changes the third level by denying unilateral prevention. Karma
systems distribute responsibility across a life-series. Non-creator systems remove framework
selection by God but retain causal questions about bondage.

[VERDICT] The 2022 PYQ is best answered through layered rather than exclusive responsibility:
human authorship can be real while divine permission remains philosophically assessable.

#### 30. Essay laboratory - Can eschatology repair theodicy?

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **High-value transition:** Eschatology can prevent a life from being finally defeated by evil, but future bliss is.












[THESIS] Eschatology can prevent a life from being finally defeated by evil, but future bliss is
not automatically a moral warrant for earlier permission.

[ARGUMENT] Hick needs eventual fulfilment because earthly opportunities are radically unequal.
Adams needs a good powerful enough to defeat horrors within the participant's life. Augustine's
judgement framework seeks cosmic moral order. These uses differ: completion of development,
personal redemption and retributive balance are not interchangeable.

[OBJECTION] If eternal good infinitely exceeds finite suffering, the total life is good.

[REPLY] Aggregate outweighing does not settle consent, use and dignity. It would be wrong to harm
a person deliberately merely because one later supplies a larger benefit. The theist needs an
internal relation between redemption and the suffered evil, or a reason why permission respected
the person throughout.

[QUALIFICATION] Eschatology is a substantive religious commitment, not publicly established by
the problem of evil itself. It may belong in a cumulative theology, but should be labelled rather
than smuggled into a supposedly neutral proof.

[VERDICT] Eschatology is strongest as a theory of defeat and hope, weaker as a retroactive licence.
It deepens the response only when it remains victim-centered.

#### 31. Essay laboratory - Does prayer intensify the problem?

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **Major criticism:** Petitionary prayer can intensify evil's evidential problem because traditions portray God.












[THESIS] Petitionary prayer can intensify evil's evidential problem because traditions portray God
as able and willing to respond, while many urgent petitions appear unanswered. It can also
transform the existential response through lament, solidarity and moral mobilization.

[ARGUMENT] If God sometimes prevents harm in response to prayer, selective non-prevention demands
an account of distribution. If God never changes outcomes, petition requires reinterpretation.
Stable-law defenders may say prayer is not a mechanism for suspending causation; it can align the
agent with divine purpose and community action. Protest prayer preserves relationship without
pretending to understand permission.

[OBJECTION] Unanswered prayer is merely another instance of limited human perspective.

[REPLY] That answer is available but costly. It makes claims of answered providence harder to
interpret and can render petitionary expectations opaque. A consistent view must treat positive
and negative cases with the same epistemic standard.

[QUALIFICATION] This is a bounded link to religious experience, reason and faith. The Problem of
Evil owner uses prayer only to test lived implications, not to provide a full theory of prayer.

[VERDICT] Prayer does not solve evil. At its best it refuses isolation, voices protest and
generates compassionate action without claiming privileged knowledge of hidden reasons.

#### 32. Essay laboratory - The relation between evil and divine hiddenness

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **Recommended opening definition:** Do not turn this section into a full hiddenness essay or assert that hiddenness is.












[THESIS] Hiddenness aggravates suffering when a loving God could provide unmistakable presence,
guidance or consolation without removing all freedom.

[ARGUMENT] Hick values epistemic distance because overwhelming awareness might compromise free
relationship and development. The critic distinguishes coercive display from adequate availability:
a parent can remain non-coercive while being recognizably present to a terrified child. Sincere
nonbelief and divine silence during horror therefore add an interpersonal dimension to evidential
evil.

[OBJECTION] Religious growth may require trust under ambiguity, and divine presence may occur in
forms the sufferer cannot presently recognize.

[REPLY] Again, possibility is not proportionality. Some ambiguity may preserve freedom; extreme
absence may destroy the very trust and agency soul-making requires. The distribution of religious
awareness becomes relevant.

[QUALIFICATION] Do not turn this section into a full hiddenness essay or assert that hiddenness is
itself always evil. Use it as an auxiliary pressure on epistemic-distance explanations.

[VERDICT] Hiddenness shows that the problem concerns not only prevention of pain but the quality
of divine relationship under pain.

#### 33. Essay laboratory - Can theism learn from non-theistic traditions?

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **Recommended opening definition:** The most fruitful comparison is functional and critical: creator-theism supplies a.












[THESIS] Non-theistic traditions cannot be imported as ready-made theodicies, but they can correct
theism's tendency to prioritize cosmic justification over diagnosis and practice.

[ARGUMENT] Buddhism starts from dukkha, traces conditions and prescribes a path. Jainism links
bondage to disciplined non-violence. Samkhya diagnoses misidentification and seeks discriminative
release. These approaches ask what sustains suffering and how agency can respond, without first
vindicating a creator. Theistic traditions can likewise make lament, compassion and prevention
central even while retaining questions of providence.

[OBJECTION] Borrowing practice while retaining creator belief avoids the distinctive metaphysical
challenge.

[REPLY] The challenge remains; learning at the practical level is not a logical solution. It is a
test of whether a proposed belief produces responsible action. Comparative philosophy need not
erase doctrinal difference to identify shared ethical insight.

[QUALIFICATION] Karma should not be treated as the universal bridge. Buddhist no-self, Jaina jiva,
Samkhya purusha and Vedantic selves differ fundamentally.

[VERDICT] The most fruitful comparison is functional and critical: creator-theism supplies a
permission problem; non-creator systems supply alternative causal and soteriological grammars;
each can be evaluated for compassion, responsibility and explanatory cost.

#### 34. Essay laboratory - Final position options

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM**
> **Final verdict:** The most defensible synthetic judgement is that strict logical incompatibility is not.












[THESIS] A candidate need not claim a total solution. A reasoned final position can take one of
four forms, provided its cost is explicit.

1. **Classical preservation:** Free will, stable law, soul-making and eschatological defeat jointly
   make theism coherent and not unreasonable. Cost: multiple auxiliary commitments and a residue
   of apparently gratuitous evil.
2. **Epistemically modest preservation:** Plantinga defeats contradiction; skeptical theism blocks
   overconfident induction; no complete theodicy is claimed. Cost: opacity of goodness and
   providence.
3. **Non-classical revision:** Process or finite theism reduces culpability through distributed
   power. Cost: weakened sovereignty and assurance.
4. **Non-theistic reframing:** Buddhism, Jainism or Samkhya removes the creator defendant and
   prioritizes diagnosis and liberation. Cost: unresolved questions of causal order, identity or
   ultimate why.

[ARGUMENT] These are not equal answers to one fixed question. Each changes premises, explanatory
burdens and religious practices. The examiner should be shown exactly what has been preserved,
revised or abandoned.

[VERDICT] The most defensible synthetic judgement is that strict logical incompatibility is not
established, evidential evil remains substantial, and moral protest limits every universal
theodicy. Philosophical integrity requires continued action against suffering rather than
premature closure.
