**Core proposition:** ⚠️ Values are not innate; they are transmitted through family, education,

### SESSION 4 — Learning courage and calm from Indian exemplars

#### 5. Must-Know Facts for Prelims
- ✅ ARC **Box 2.8** reproduces Gandhi's Seven Social Sins and sources them to *Young India*, 1925,
  in this order: politics without principles; wealth without work; **leisure** without conscience;
  knowledge without character; commerce without morality; science without humanity; worship
  without sacrifice. ⚠️ The *Young India* note of **22 October 1925** (Collected Works, Vol. XXVIII)
  reads "**pleasure** without conscience", and Gandhi introduced the list as one his correspondent
  wanted readers to know — cite it as a list Gandhi published and endorsed, not one he is documented
  to have composed.
- ✅ Mahavir's five great vows (*Mahavratas*): Ahimsa, Satya, Asteya, Brahmacharya, Aparigraha;
  Anekantavada and Syadvada are the associated Jain doctrines of the many-sidedness of truth and
  conditional predication. ⚠️ Jain tradition contrasts Parshvanatha's four vows with Mahavir's
  five — brahmacharya is the vow Mahavir's tradition *separately enumerated*, having earlier been
  understood within aparigraha.
- ✅ Kautilya's *Arthashastra* is the classical Indian source the ARC quotes (Annexure-I(2)) for the
  structural, opportunity-driven — rather than purely moral — origin of official corruption.
- ⚠️ Value transmission channels commonly tested: family, school/teachers, religion, peer group,
  media, and direct exemplar/role-model learning.

### SESSION 5 — Mahavir: many-sided truth and non-possession

#### 6. UPSC traps
- ❌ Quotation questions only test memorisation of the quote. -> They test your ability to translate
  the quote into a governance/administrative lesson relevant to "the present context" (the standard
  phrasing in 2024-2025 papers).
- ❌ Values are fixed and unchangeable once formed. -> William James's own quoted teaching (2025
  GS-IV Q3(b)) explicitly holds that attitudes/values can be altered by conscious effort.
- ❌ Anekantavada means "no view is ever correct." -> It means truth is many-sided and no single
  perspective is complete — administratively, it supports multi-stakeholder consultation, not moral
  relativism or indecision.
- ❌ Every quotation UPSC prints is an authenticated saying of the named thinker. -> The paper only
  proves UPSC's attribution. The Vivekananda "learn everything that is good" line (2024) is
  traceable to the *Complete Works*; the Sardar Patel line (2024), the William James line (2025)
  and the Vivekananda "strength of a society" line (2025) are **not** traceable to primary
  sources. Answer the idea in the "present context" — never invent a citation for the quotation.

**Selectable evidence pool** (pick 1-2, do not exhaust the list in one answer):
1. *Claim:* Values are transmitted, not innate. *Evidence:* Gandhi's Seven Social Sins (ARC Box
   2.8). *Significance:* gives a checklist against which to test any policy/conduct. *Limitation:*
   the "leisure/pleasure" textual variance shows even canonical lists have transmission gaps.
2. *Claim:* Selective learning beats imitation. *Evidence:* Vivekananda's "learn... do not become
   others" (2024 GS-IV Q3(a)). *Significance:* supports adapting global e-governance models to
   local context. *Limitation:* over-applied, it can be used to resist legitimate reform.
3. *Claim:* Institution-building corrects structural inequality. *Evidence:* Savitribai Phule's
   Bhide Wada girls' school, 1848. *Significance:* models durable, structural correction over
   sentiment-only appeals. *Limitation:* one institution alone did not resolve 19th-century gender
   inequality — it required sustained, generation-spanning reform.
4. *Claim:* Personal integrity scales into national order. *Evidence:* Kalam's
   righteousness-to-peace chain (2019 GS-IV Q6(c), sourced by Kalam himself to Sathya Sai Baba).
   *Significance:* supports "why does individual ethics matter for national power" answers.
   *Limitation:* is a rhetorical chain, not an empirically demonstrated causal claim — flag it as
   illustrative, not proven.
5. *Claim:* Equanimity under adversity is administratively valuable. *Evidence:* Thiruvalluvar's
   Tirukkural 623 (2025 GS-IV Q3(a)). *Significance:* directly maps to composure in disaster/crisis
   leadership (see `05`). *Limitation:* equanimity must not be read as passivity — decisive action
   is still required alongside composure.

**Counterpoint and reasoned verdict (template):** "This teaching is administratively valuable
because [named mechanism], but its limitation is [X]; therefore it should be applied alongside
[named institutional safeguard], not relied on alone."

### ETHICS DEEP-REVIEW CORE CONTROL

- **Must remember:** Human values are learned through family, society, education, institutions, reflection and practice; lessons from Gandhi, Ambedkar, Buddha, Vivekananda, Savitribai Phule, Kalam and administrators require argument, context and limits rather than ornamental quotation.
- **Close distinction:** Anecdote is not doctrine, admired character is not infallibility, nishkama karma is not indifference to consequences, trusteeship is not a substitute for enforceable justice, and anekantavada supports epistemic humility rather than moral paralysis.

### 3. Detailed treatment of frequently tested leaders
#### Gandhi's Seven Social Sins — deeper reading
- ✅ Reproduced in ARC **Box 2.8** and sourced there to Gandhi in *Young India*, 1925 — a prescient
  warning against decoupling ends (wealth, pleasure, knowledge, politics, commerce, worship) from
  ethical means (work, conscience, humanity, character, principle, morality, sacrifice).
- ⚠️ Provenance nuance worth one line in an answer: in the *Young India* note of 22 October 1925
  Gandhi presented the list as one his correspondent wanted readers to know; the ARC's Box also
  prints "leisure without conscience" where the original reads "pleasure without conscience". Cite
  it as a list Gandhi published and endorsed.
- ⚠️ Administrative reading: each "sin" names a **means-ends decoupling** — e.g., "politics without
  principles" anticipates exactly the ARC's later (1.12) diagnosis that corrupt political markets
  displace rather than eliminate corruption.
- ⚠️ Limitation: as a list of prohibitions it does not itself supply an enforcement mechanism — it
  must be paired with institutional design (Nolan-type codes, `09`/`16`), consistent with the
  "values need institutions" synthesis in `01`.

#### Vivekananda's two tested quotations — synthesis
- ✅ "Learn everything that is good from others, but bring it in, and in your own way absorb it; do
  not become others" (2024) is traceable to the *Complete Works*, Vol. III, "The Common Bases of
  Hinduism" (Lahore, 1897).
- ⚠️ "The strength of a society is not in its laws, but in the morality of its people" (2025) is
  set by UPSC as Vivekananda's but is **not traceable** to the *Complete Works*. The nearest
  documented statement of the theme is from "Our Duty to the Masses" (23 June 1894): "No country on
  earth has so many laws, and in no country are they so little regarded." Answer the proposition;
  do not manufacture a citation.
- ⚠️ Read together they still form a coherent position: **selective institutional borrowing** plus
  **primacy of internalised societal morality over external law**.
- ⚠️ Advanced application: when India adapts a foreign anti-corruption model (Hong Kong's ICAC,
  Singapore's CPIB — see `23`), Vivekananda's principle argues for adaptation to Indian federal,
  political and cultural conditions rather than transplant — a caution directly relevant to why the
  Lokpal took decades longer to operationalise than the ARC's 2007 recommendation envisaged.

#### Kautilya — the earliest structural (not moral) theory of corruption
- ✅ Quoted in ARC **Annexure-I(2)** (National Colloquium speech of ARC Chairman M. Veerappa Moily,
  1 September 2006), which reproduces five *Arthashastra* propositions: officials handling the
  king's money can no more avoid tasting it than a tongue can avoid tasting honey or poison
  (Kangle 2.9.32); embezzlement is as unobservable as a fish drinking water (2.9.33); "it is
  possible to know even the path of birds flying in the sky, but not the ways of officers moving
  with their intentions concealed" (2.9.34); those who have amassed money wrongfully should be made
  to disgorge it and be moved to other work; and those who increase the king's wealth "in just
  ways" should be made permanent in their offices.
- ⚠️ This is a **structural/opportunity theory** of corruption predating modern institutional
  economics by roughly two millennia — the prescribed remedy (positive permanence for the honest,
  disgorgement and reassignment for the rest) anticipates today's performance-linked,
  rotation-based vigilance mechanisms (see `21`).
- ⚠️ Advanced distinction: Kautilya's realism (assume temptation is near-universal; design controls
  accordingly) sits in productive tension with Gandhi's character-based idealism — a strong Mains
  answer can use *both* registers depending on the question's framing (systemic design vs personal
  conduct).

#### Mahavir — Anekantavada as an administrative epistemology
- ✅ 2025 GS-IV Q4(b) tested "major teachings of Mahavir" and their "relevance in the contemporary
  world" — Ahimsa, Satya, Asteya, Brahmacharya, Aparigraha (five *Mahavratas*), plus Anekantavada
  and Syadvada.
- ⚠️ Precision point worth one line: Jain tradition contrasts Parshvanatha's four restraints with
  Mahavir's five. Brahmacharya is best described as the vow Mahavir's tradition *separately
  enumerated*, having earlier been understood as included within aparigraha — not as an ethical
  idea Mahavir is documented to have invented.
- ⚠️ Administrative translation: Anekantavada supports structured multi-stakeholder consultation
  (e.g., before a land-acquisition or environmental-clearance decision) as an epistemic discipline,
  not mere procedural box-ticking — it says complex public decisions are irreducibly many-sided.
- ⚠️ Aparigraha (non-possessiveness) maps onto conflict-of-interest and anti-nepotism norms in
  public office — an official's "non-attachment" to personal gain from office is the ethical core
  of probity (see `14`).

### 4. Institutional translation of value-education
- 📰 Mission Karmayogi / the National Programme for Civil Services Capacity Building (NPCSCB),
  approved by the Union Cabinet on **2 September 2020**, builds behavioural competencies (values,
  attitude, integrity) into training through the **FRAC** approach (Framework of Roles, Activities
  and Competencies) and the **iGOT-Karmayogi** platform, with the **Capacity Building Commission**
  established on **1 April 2021**. Official scale, as reported to Parliament with data as on
  **6 February 2026**: over 1.49 crore users, 4,342 courses, 7.26 crore course completions and 23
  languages. Do not assert a general APAR/appraisal linkage without a service-specific DoPT order.
- ⚠️ Value-education in Mains answers should therefore reference *both* the classical exemplar and
  the current institutional delivery mechanism, showing the values-plus-institutions synthesis in
  practice.

### 5. Indian applications and boundary cases
- ⚠️ A civil servant applying Vivekananda's selective-borrowing principle while localising a
  Bhoomi/Gyandoot-style e-governance model (see `23`) for a state with weaker digital literacy —
  the limitation is that "adaptation" must not become an excuse to dilute the reform's substance.
- ⚠️ A civil servant applying Kautilya's rotation/reward logic through a transparent, rule-bound
  transfer policy (see `21`) rather than ad hoc reassignment — the boundary is that rotation itself
  can be misused as a punitive tool if not rule-bound.

### 6. Limitations and trade-offs
- ⚠️ Exemplar-based reasoning risks selective quotation — a leader's teaching can be invoked to
  justify opposite administrative choices unless the specific institutional context is stated.
- ⚠️ Kautilya's assume-everyone-is-tempted realism, if taken alone, can justify excessive,
  demoralising surveillance of honest officials (see `21` for the ARC's explicit caution against
  this over-correction).
- ⚠️ Gandhi's character-first idealism, if taken alone, under-weights the ARC's own institutional
  diagnosis (`01`) that discretion and monopoly — not character alone — drive corruption.

### 7. Must-Know Facts for Advanced Prelims
- ✅ The "Seven Social Sins" list appears in the 4th Report as **Box 2.8**, sourced there to Gandhi
  in *Young India*, 1925 (original note dated 22 October 1925, Collected Works Vol. XXVIII).
- ✅ Mahavir's five Mahavratas: Ahimsa, Satya, Asteya, Brahmacharya, Aparigraha.
- ✅ Anekantavada and Syadvada are Jain doctrines of the many-sidedness and conditional
  predication of truth (*syat* = "in some respect / from a standpoint"), distinct from moral
  relativism.
- ✅ The Kautilya passages the ARC quotes are from the *Arthashastra*'s chapter on inspecting the
  work of officers — Kangle's numbering 2.9.32-34.

### 8. Advanced Prelims traps
- ❌ Anekantavada is a form of ethical relativism ("nothing is really right or wrong"). -> It is an
  epistemological doctrine about the complexity of truth-claims, not a denial of moral standards.
- ❌ Kautilya's Arthashastra advocates unrestrained state surveillance of all officials as a first
  resort. -> His remedy pairs vigilance with positive incentives (permanence in office, rewards) for
  officials who serve with integrity — a balanced, not purely punitive, design.
- ❌ Nishkama karma means indifference to outcomes/results in public service. -> It means non-
  attachment to personal credit/reward for the outcome, not indifference to whether the public
  duty is performed well.

### ONE-PAGE CONCEPT GRID

| Concept / thinker | Exam-ready formulation |
|---|---|
| Value formation is social and revisable | Values are enduring beliefs about what is desirable that are transmitted through family, education, culture, peers, role models and lived experience; they can be strengthened or corroded by repeated institutional incentives rather than being permanently fixed at birth. |
| Internalisation differs from performance | Value internalisation means that a value guides conduct even when no one is watching, whereas value performance displays approved conduct mainly for appraisal, publicity or fear of sanction; the former is more reliable under discretion and pressure. |
| Values are broader than attitudes | Values are relatively enduring beliefs about what is worthwhile across situations, while attitudes are object-specific evaluations; value education therefore develops judgement and habits, not merely a favourable response to a particular policy or person. |
| Mission Karmayogi is an institutional channel | Mission Karmayogi's NPCSCB approach links civil-service capacity building to functional and behavioural competencies, a citizen-government interface, a shift from rules to roles, and iGOT learning; it institutionalises learning but cannot by itself prove ethical internalisation. |
| Gandhi's social sins join means to ends | The Seven Social Sins published and endorsed by Gandhi warn against separating power, wealth, pleasure, knowledge, commerce, science and worship from their ethical restraints; their administrative force lies in testing whether apparently successful ends were pursued through principled means. |

### CORE REVISION SPINE

- **Aparigraha translates into probity:** Aparigraha, non-possessiveness among Mahavir's five Mahavratas, supports a public ethic of non-attachment to office-derived gain, conflict-of-interest control and resistance to nepotism; it does not demand indifference to legitimate public resources or personal responsibility.
- **Kautilya identifies opportunity and concealment:** The Arthashastra passages quoted in ARC Annexure-I(2) use the honey and fish analogies to show that officials handling public money face temptation and concealment; this is a structural account of corruption that calls for auditable controls, detection and fair incentives, not character sermons alone.
- **Kautilya is not surveillance alone:** Kautilya's ARC-cited account pairs action against wrongful accumulation with permanence for officials who increase public wealth in just ways; a modern translation therefore balances rule-bound vigilance, rewards and protection of honest officials rather than normalising punitive or arbitrary surveillance.
- **Nishkama karma rejects personal attachment:** Nishkama karma means performing one's duty without attachment to personal reward, credit or fruit; it requires conscientious effort and concern for public outcomes, not indifference to whether a programme succeeds or a citizen is harmed.
- **Duty joins responsibility and fulfilment:** Devotion to duty gives a civil servant responsibility for timely, fair and competent action and can yield personal fulfilment through meaningful service; it cannot legitimise burnout, illegal orders or a self-righteous refusal to learn from affected citizens.
- **Savitribai Phule models institution-building:** Savitribai Phule's work with Jyotirao Phule in co-founding the Bhide Wada girls' school in Pune in 1848 shows that gender inequality is confronted through durable institutions, access to education and support for excluded groups, not sentiment alone.
- **Kalam's chain is attributed with care:** A.P.J. Abdul Kalam used and endorsed a chain linking righteousness in the heart to peace in the world, while crediting it in his 2007 European Parliament address to Sathya Sai Baba; it illustrates a bottom-up ethical aspiration, not an empirically proven single-cause model of governance.
- **Exemplars need contextual translation:** Leader-based ethics is useful when a teaching is translated into a specific decision rule, Indian illustration and institutional safeguard; it becomes an exemplar fallacy when a revered name substitutes for evidence, ignores competing duties or pretends that personal virtue alone settles a complex case.
- **Quotation answers test translation:** A strong GS-IV quotation answer states the core idea, briefly situates it, translates it into a present administrative mechanism, supplies a concrete Indian illustration, acknowledges a relevant limitation or provenance caution, and closes with a qualified ethical verdict rather than a biography.
- **Values and institutions must work together:** Character-centred lessons such as Gandhi's and structural lessons such as Kautilya's are complementary: internalised values guide conduct where rules cannot observe everything, while transparent procedures, incentives and accountability reduce opportunities for values to be overwhelmed by discretion and pressure.
- **Brahmacharya requires textual precision:** Mahavir's five Mahavratas are Ahimsa, Satya, Asteya, Brahmacharya and Aparigraha; Jain tradition distinguishes Parshvanatha's four restraints from Mahavir's five, with Brahmacharya separately enumerated rather than being an ethical idea demonstrably invented by Mahavir.
- **Competency scale is not conduct proof:** As of 6 February 2026, Mission Karmayogi's iGOT ecosystem had 1.49 crore users, 4,342 courses, 7.26 crore completions and 23 languages; these dated figures show learning reach, not a verified general APAR linkage or automatic improvement in ethical conduct.

### SOURCE AND ATTRIBUTION DISCIPLINE

Use the local Basic owner as the controlling source. Gandhi's 1925 list was published and endorsed by Gandhi but presented as sent by a correspondent; its original wording has 'pleasure' where ARC Box 2.8 prints 'leisure'. The 2024 Patel line, 2025 William James line, and 2025 Vivekananda law-morality line are UPSC attributions not traced to primary sources in the owner. Thiruvalluvar's 2025 wording is a modern paraphrase of Tirukkural 623. Kalam used the righteousness-to-peace chain while crediting Sathya Sai Baba. Do not turn any caution into a fabricated citation.
