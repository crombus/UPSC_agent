## Practice diagnostics: turn an error into a revision action

### If the error is chronology

Rebuild the ladder rather than memorising an isolated date: Farid's local exposure and Bihar field -> Surajgarh (1534) -> Chausa (1539) -> Kannauj (1540) -> Kalinjar (1545) -> Islam Shah's death (1553) -> Humayun's return (1555). Then ask what each transition changed in resources, sovereignty or succession. This prevents a correct date from becoming an empty fact.

### If the error is an institution mix-up

Write the mechanism in five words. **Rai** is crop-rate calculation; **patta** records a stated demand; **dagh** brands a horse; **chehra** identifies a soldier; a **sarai** concentrates movement and information. Next attach one limit: patta did not abolish intermediaries; dagh-chehra had Sultanate roots; sarais were not welfare-only.

### If the error is an overclaim

Replace invention language with a sequence. Road policy means restoration/improvement of older corridors. The rupee means standardisation of a fine silver form in a trimetallic field. Revenue means a generally one-third claim shaped by local mediation, cash/kind choice and the Multan exception. These substitutions are not hedges: they are the evidence-based answer.

### If the error is a Mains-structure failure

Use one sentence of thesis, four named evidence units and one genuine qualification. For example, do not list roads, sarais, customs and policing. Explain that each reduced transactional uncertainty while increasing military, fiscal and informational reach. End by showing why personal supervision and short duration limited durability. That causal sentence is what changes a fact list into analysis.

### Q1. The safest description of Farid's early political formation is that he:
