## 1. Snapshot & core idea

**Foundation — two Deccan powers and three contested zones.**

✅ Vijayanagara and Bahmani kingdoms dominated south of the Vindhyas for more than two centuries. Their conflict centred on the **Tungabhadra doab**, the **Krishna-Godavari basin**, and the **Konkan/Goa coast**.
- ✅ The Tungabhadra doab was fertile and strategically placed between the two powers.
- ✅ The Krishna-Godavari basin had fertile land and ports controlling regional foreign trade.
- ✅ Goa/Konkan mattered because horses from Iran and Iraq were essential to southern armies.

**Foundation — high points and breakdown.**

✅ Vijayanagara became wealthy and powerful under Deva Raya II; foreign travellers such as Nicolo Conti and Abdur Razzaq described its size, population, cultivation and splendour.
- ✅ Deva Raya II strengthened the army by employing Muslim cavalrymen and archers and by learning from Sultanate military methods.
- ✅ Mahmud Gawan expanded Bahmani power, took Goa and Dabhol, and reformed administration.
- ✅ Krishnadeva Raya restored internal order, defeated rivals, patronised literature and built tanks/towns; after his death factional politics and Rama Raja's diplomacy led to the 1565 catastrophe.
- ✅ Krishnadevaraya maintained generally cooperative relations with the Portuguese; the exam-fact that he allowed a Portuguese fort at **Bhatkal** rests mainly on Portuguese accounts such as **Barbosa** and **Paes**, so the safe point is cooperation with provenance caution on exact wording.
- ✅ The 2021 official-paper wording attributes women's expertise in **wrestling, astrology, accounting and soothsaying** to **Nuniz**. An older repository note attached a similar list to Paes; use the bounded phrase **Portuguese accounts** in Mains unless authorship is independently checked.
- ✅ **Amaram** linked territorial revenue and **nayaka** military service. Many nayakas built hereditary local power bases, but practice changed by dynasty and region; it was not identical to the transferable Sultanate **iqta**.

## 11. Fourteenth-century Deccan context and origins

Delhi's unstable southern control interacted with surviving Hoysalas, the
Madurai Sultanate, Warangal/Velama and Reddi powers, Karnataka nayakas,
Tungabhadra-Krishna routes and coastal trade. Vijayanagara and Bahmani formation
was therefore regional recombination, not creation from an empty political
space.

### Vijayanagara

- c. 1336 is the conventional foundation/coronation anchor for Harihara and
  Bukka.
- Traditions connect the brothers to Warangal/Kampili, capture/conversion,
  reconversion and Vidyaranya.
- These biographies and Vidyaranya's exact founding role are debated; a later
  civilizational foundation narrative is not settled fact.
- Inscriptions, urban growth, conquest and institutional consolidation provide
  firmer evidence of a developing polity.

### Bahmani

- Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah established independent rule in 1347.
- "Hasan Gangu" and service under a Brahman named Gangu derive importantly from
  later tradition, especially Firishta.
- Claimed descent/title traditions enhanced legitimacy but do not securely
  establish biological origin.
- Gulbarga was the early capital; Ahmad Shah I later shifted the political
  centre to Bidar.

## 13. Bahmani rulers, capitals and breakup

| Ruler/phase | Significance |
|---|---|
| Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah, from 1347 | foundation and Gulbarga centre |
| Firuz Shah Bahmani, 1397–1422 | learning, ports, campaigns, alliances and political marriage |
| Ahmad Shah I, 1422–36 | Warangal expansion, association with Gesu Daraz memory and capital shift to Bidar |
| Muhammad Shah III phase | Mahmud Gawan served as leading minister/wakil-i-sultanat |
| Mahmud Gawan, c. 1463–1481/82 | coastal expansion, eight-taraf reform, khalisa/measurement and Bidar madrasa |

The breakup produced Ahmadnagar, Bijapur, Golconda, Berar and Bidar. It was a
process, not one simultaneous declaration. Mahmud Gawan was a minister, not a
Bahmani sultan.

## 14. Political geography, war and diplomacy

### 14.1 Recurrent strategic zones

```text
Tungabhadra/Raichur doab → forts + cultivation + frontier revenue
Krishna-Godavari region → agrarian basin + routes + eastern ports
Konkan/Goa-Dabhol coast → maritime trade + imported horses + western access
```

Raichur and other forts mattered because holding a doab required defended
routes, garrisons, supplies and local cooperation—not merely battlefield victory.

### 14.2 Coalitions, not permanent communal camps

- Vijayanagara fought southern Hindu rulers and employed Muslim cavalry/archers.
- Bahmani rulers allied with Warangal, Gujarat or other powers according to
  changing interests.
- Political marriage between Deva Raya I's house and Firuz Shah Bahmani is
  evidence of diplomacy, not proof that conflict disappeared.
- Mahmud Gawan's Gujarat cooperation against Malwa and Rama Raya's later
  balance-of-power diplomacy show shifting coalitions.
- Temple, mosque or religious rhetoric could be politically used, but doab,
  revenue, forts, horse supply, ports and elite interests explain recurrent war.

## 15. Talikota / Rakshasi-Tangadi / Bannihatti, 1565

The principal anti-Vijayanagara coalition is commonly associated with
Ahmadnagar, Bijapur, Golconda and Bidar; accounts differ on coordination,
forces and battlefield details. Berar should not be inserted automatically into
every participant list.

### Causal chain

1. Rama Raya repeatedly intervened in Deccan-sultanate rivalries.
2. Diplomatic humiliation, frontier contests and a temporary coalition altered
   the balance.
3. Cavalry, artillery/firearms, commanders, local knowledge and battlefield
   contingency interacted.
4. Rama Raya was captured and executed.
5. Hampi's metropolitan core was sacked and its imperial functions were badly
   disrupted.

### Continuity qualification

- 1565 ended the capital's great-power phase, not the polity in one instant.
- Tirumala and the Aravidu line continued from Penukonda and later centres.
- Nayaka powers in Madurai, Tanjavur, Gingee and elsewhere became more autonomous.
- Political, revenue, temple and commercial networks survived in altered forms.

Avoid exact troop/casualty figures unless the cited source and its rhetorical
limits are given.

## 16. Vijayanagara administration and state debate

| Level/institution | Function and caution |
|---|---|
| Raya | kingship, war, justice, patronage and protection; normative ideals ≠ uniform practice |
| ministerial council | great nobles and office-holders advised the ruler; influence varied |
| rajya/mandala | provincial scale, often mediated by princes, governors or powerful commanders |
| nadu, sthala, grama | lower territorial units; terminology/practice varied regionally |
| amaram/nayankara | revenue-service relationship supporting troops and local administration |
| nayaka/amaranayaka | military-intermediary power; many acquired hereditary bases but remained politically negotiated |
| ayagar/ayyangar village officers | local service/record/revenue functions; mature descriptions must not be projected uniformly across all reigns |

### Competing models

- **Segmentary interpretation:** ritual sovereignty and strong local units limit
  direct central control.
- **Centralized interpretation:** taxation, standing forces, court, records,
  capital and intervention show substantial royal capacity.
- **Military-fiscal interpretation:** revenue assignments, nayakas, forts and
  cavalry mobilization tie territory to war.
- **Integrative/negotiated interpretation:** core control and layered
  centre-periphery bargains changed by dynasty and region.

The safest verdict is variable, negotiated centralization—not European
feudalism, a timeless segmentary state or a Delhi-style bureaucracy.

## 17. Bahmani administration and elite politics

| Institution/group | Historical function |
|---|---|
| Sultan and court | sovereignty, appointments, military and cultural patronage |
| wakil-i-sultanat / leading minister | high executive coordination; Mahmud Gawan is the key case |
| taraf / tarafdar | provincial territorial administration and military-revenue power |
| khalisa | resources reserved more directly for the crown |
| forts/governors | frontier and provincial control; Gawan sought more direct appointment |
| local revenue personnel | Deccani Brahmans and other locally embedded groups linked court and countryside |

Mahmud Gawan subdivided four larger tarafs into eight, fixed salary/troop
obligations, sought land measurement and stronger fort control. These were
reform efforts, not proof of a completed uniform bureaucracy.

**Deccani** and **Afaqi/gharib** were changing faction labels involving
settlement, patronage, office, regional networks and sometimes sectarian
alignments. They were not pure races, sealed ethnic blocs or permanent parties.
Gawan's execution c. 1481/82 accelerated, but did not single-handedly cause,
Bahmani disintegration.

## 18. Agrarian base, irrigation and rural society

- Tanks, canals, wells and reservoirs supported cultivation, capitals and armies.
- Deva Raya I's Tungabhadra works are a routed PYQ anchor; later rulers and
  temples also invested in water.
- Assessment varied by crop, soil and irrigation; one rate must not be projected
  empire-wide.
- Land tax, profession/sale/property and other levies appear in evidence, but
  normative lists and local inscriptions do not establish universal incidence.
- Temples, chiefs, nayakas, village officers and cultivators had layered claims
  over produce, service and land.
- Agrarian expansion could enrich courts and intermediaries while imposing
  unequal burdens on peasants.
- Travellers knew capitals better than villages; spectacular prosperity cannot
  establish the condition of every cultivator.

## 19. Armies, horses, forts and technology

Both systems combined cavalry, infantry, elephants, forts and commanders.
Imported horses from Arabia/Iran through west-coast ports created a strategic
premium but did not alone determine victory.

| Factor | Exam-safe use |
|---|---|
| Cavalry and mounted archery | Deva Raya II's adaptation crossed religious boundaries |
| Elephants and infantry | important but source numbers are often inflated |
| Artillery/firearms | reported in Deccan warfare from the fourteenth century; capability changed over time |
| Forts | Raichur/Mudgal and coastal/interior forts linked frontier to supply |
| Horse trade | Goa, Dabhol and other ports connected maritime merchants to inland armies |
| Navy/coast | access varied; do not infer permanent maritime supremacy from tribute claims |

## 20. Towns, markets, trade and coinage

- Hampi/Vijayanagara was an urban region of enclosures, royal and sacred
  centres, bazaars, craft/labour spaces, settlements and hydraulic works.
- Temples were ritual, landed, redistributive and commercial institutions; they
  supported festivals, markets, craft and credit without becoming modern banks.
- Inland routes linked Raichur, capitals and agrarian zones to Goa, Dabhol,
  Bhatkal and eastern ports.
- Arab, Persian, Indian and later Portuguese merchants participated in horses,
  textiles, spices, foodgrains, precious goods and coastal exchange.
- Vijayanagara gold **varaha/pagoda** issues and Bahmani coinage illuminate
  sovereignty and monetization; coin finds do not prove total cash penetration.
- War and trade coexisted; neither uninterrupted prosperity nor permanent
  devastation describes the whole region.

## 21. Social groups, caste, dependence and women

The social field included royal households, Brahmans, warrior/nayaka groups,
Muslim cavalrymen and officials, merchants, artisans, cultivators, pastoral and
forest communities, temple/matha personnel, enslaved/captive/dependent people
and urban labourers.

- Varna and jati shaped hierarchy, occupation and residence, but did not produce
  immobile sealed communities.
- Warfare generated captives and enslavement; this cannot be mapped directly
  onto Atlantic racial chattel slavery.
- Gangadevi's Sanskrit authorship, royal women's patronage and Portuguese
  observations reveal elite/courtly agency.
- Women also worked in agrarian, artisanal, service, ritual and market settings,
  but traveller lists cannot measure all women's conditions.
- The 2021 PYQ follows Nuniz's attributed list; do not shift authorship to Paes.

## 22. Religion, institutions and composite cultures

- Vijayanagara rulers used temple patronage and titles to make sovereignty
  visible, while supporting different Hindu sects and employing Muslims.
- Bahmani rulers patronized mosques, madrasas and Sufi institutions while using
  Hindu officials and negotiating with non-Muslim chiefs.
- Temples, mosques and dargahs held property, organized ritual, attracted labour/
  markets and mediated legitimacy.
- Gesu Daraz and Ahmad Shah's saintly memory are politically relevant here;
  doctrinal Sufi history belongs to Topic 10.
- War damage, desecration, patronage and accommodation were event-specific.
- "Composite culture" should mean evidenced interaction in language, craft,
  court, military or institution—not an uncomplicated harmony claim.

## 23. Languages, literature and music

- Vijayanagara courts patronized Telugu, Kannada, Sanskrit and Tamil.
- Krishnadevaraya's Telugu *Amuktamalyada* combines literary devotion with
  normative reflections on kingship; it is not an administrative gazette.
- The Ashtadiggajas are an important Telugu court tradition, but later literary
  canonization should not be read as a complete contemporary roster without
  source checking.
- Gangadevi's Sanskrit *Madhuravijayam* links literary authorship, conquest
  memory and royal legitimacy.
- Bahmani and successor cultural worlds used Persian/Arabic and interacted with
  Dakhni, Marathi, Kannada and Telugu settings.
- Courtly music, performance and devotional/elite patronage crossed political
  boundaries; Topic 10 owns full Bhakti-Sufi intellectual history.
