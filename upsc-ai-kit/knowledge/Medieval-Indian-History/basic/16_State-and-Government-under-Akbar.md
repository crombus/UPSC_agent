## 1. Snapshot & core idea

**Foundation — revenue reform: from Sher Shah to Akbar.**

- ✅ Akbar adopted Sher Shah's measured revenue approach but found the central price schedule delayed assessment and hurt peasants when court prices were higher than countryside prices.
- ✅ Around **1580**, the **dahsala** schedule used ten-year produce/price data to frame annual cash rates; the commonly stated one-third norm and actual realization varied by crop, region and collection.
- ✅ Iron-linked bamboo measurement aimed to regularize area records; remission/relief appears in normative claims but implementation must be verified regionally.
- ✅ This measurement-based assessment was called the **zabti** system and is associated with **Raja Todar Mal**, though it was the work of a team.

**Foundation — mansabdari: rank, salary and army.**

- ✅ Many imperial service-holders received a **mansab** (rank) within an evolving hierarchy; not every state worker belonged to one modern civil service.
- ✅ The late-Akbar **zat-sawar** distinction broadly separated personal status/pay from cavalry obligation, but detailed ratios and meanings changed.
- ✅ The **dagh** system branded horses, and **chehra** recorded soldiers' descriptive rolls.
- ✅ Mansabdars were usually paid through **jagirs**, which gave revenue rights but no hereditary ownership.

## 2. Key classification / data

| Institution | Function |
|---|---|
| ✅ Dahsala | Ten-year average of produce and prices; one-third state share in cash |
| ✅ Zabti | Measurement-based assessment; applied from Lahore to Allahabad, Malwa and Gujarat |
| ✅ Batai/ghalla-bakhshi | Crop-sharing system; produce divided in fixed proportion |

## 4. Must-Know Facts (Prelims)

- ✅ Dahsala was introduced in **1580**.
- ✅ State share under dahsala = **one-third** of average produce, stated in cash.
- ✅ **Todar Mal's bandobast** is a common name, but Satish Chandra stresses team effort.
- ✅ Land categories: **polaj**, **parati**, **chachar**, **banjar**.
- ✅ Mansab ranks split later into **zat** and **sawar**.
- ✅ Akbar divided the empire into **twelve subas in 1580**.
- ✅ Main central officers: *diwan*, *mir bakhshi*, *mir saman* / household, and *sadr/qazi*.
- ✅ Zabt/dahsala did not cover every field in every province; batai, kankut and nasaq remained available, especially where measurement was difficult or crops failed.

## 5. UPSC Traps

> 🔑 Trap: Dahsala is not a permanent ten-year settlement; it used ten-year averages.

- ❌ Dahsala meant revenue was fixed permanently for ten years. → It was based on ten-year average produce/prices; the state could modify it.
- ❌ Todar Mal alone created the system. → He was crucial, but it was a team's work under Akbar.
- ❌ Jagir meant ownership of land. → It meant assignment of state revenue due from land.
- ❌ Zat and sawar existed in the same form from the start. → The system developed gradually.

## 11. Sovereignty, court and central departments

Akbar's **padshahship** centred appointment, transfer, justice, military command
and access on the ruler. Court ritual and personal audience made a hierarchy of
service visible; this was not an impersonal constitutional state.

| Office | Main function and chronology caution |
|---|---|
| wakil/vakil | regency/general authority under Bairam; later prestige and reduced concentration |
| wazir/diwan-i ala | finance, revenue, accounts, khalisa, jagir and expenditure; title/function evolved |
| mir bakhshi | mansab presentation, appointments, muster, pay verification and intelligence linkage; not simply defence minister |
| sadr-us-sudur | religious grants, learned appointments and charitable endowment; powers were curtailed/reorganized |
| qazi-ul-quzat/qazi | judicial/religious law functions alongside local/customary forums |
| mir saman/khan-i-saman | imperial household, karkhanas, stores and material supply |
| diwan-i buyutat/household accounts | scrutiny of karkhana/household expenditure in changing form |
| daroghas/news writers | specialized supervision, reports and communication |

Checks meant overlapping centrally appointed counterweights and direct reporting
to the emperor, not modern separation of powers.

## 12. Province to village

Akbar formed **twelve subas in 1580**; conquest and reorganization later changed
the number, commonly counted as fifteen by the end of the reign after Deccan
additions. Timing and attachment of frontier regions require record-specific
caution.

```text
EMPIRE
  └─ SUBA: subadar/sipahsalar + provincial diwan + bakhshi + sadr/qazi + news writer
       └─ SARKAR: faujdar + amalguzar/amil and record staff
            └─ PARGANA: shiqdar + amil/amin + qanungo + chaudhuri + fotedar + karkuns
                 └─ VILLAGE: muqaddam/headman + patwari + panchayat + cultivators
```

- Provincial diwan/bakhshi were centrally linked and could check the governor.
- **Faujdar** was a mobile military-police/route-order officer whose jurisdiction
  need not match every sarkar boundary.
- **Amil/amalguzar** supervised revenue; **qanungo** preserved local revenue/
  custom records; **chaudhuri** represented/mediated pargana-level rural elites.
- **Muqaddam**, **patwari** and panchayat connected village records, collection
  and local authority.
- Formal hierarchy did not erase zamindars, chiefs, customary law or regional
  bargaining.

## 13. Mansabdari: evolving rank and service

| Dimension | Akbar-period control |
|---|---|
| mansab | numerical rank locating service-holder in imperial hierarchy |
| zat | late-Akbar personal status/pay component, broadly |
| sawar | cavalry/horsemen obligation component, broadly |
| salary | cash or more often revenue assignment, adjusted by rank/obligation |
| service | military command, administration, court and provincial assignments |
| transfer/promotion | ruler-centred reward, discipline and anti-local-entrenchment tool |
| dagh | horse branding/verification with Sultanate/Sur precedents |
| chehra | descriptive roll of troopers/personnel |

- Numerical ranks are generally associated with the later 1560s; procedures
  changed.
- Dagh was strengthened in 1573–74 amid resistance and verification problems.
- Zat-sawar distinction is associated with 1595–96; earlier meanings cannot be
  read through the later dual formula automatically.
- Rank did not always equal actual troops present; muster, deductions,
  category/rate and jagir realization affected service.
- Mansabdars included Turani, Irani, Afghan, Indian Muslim, Rajput and other
  elites, but incorporation remained hierarchical and patronage-bound—not a
  modern meritocracy.
- **Du-aspa sih-aspa** is a later Jahangir-period refinement; use only as a
  forward boundary, not Akbar's normal system.
- Mansabdars were not salaried civilian bureaucrats only.

## 14. Jagir, khalisa and fiscal dependence

**Jagir** was an assignment of state revenue demand, not ownership of soil,
peasants or permanent hereditary estate.

| Term | Meaning and limit |
|---|---|
| tankhwah jagir | assignment in lieu of salary |
| mashrut/conditional obligation | additional condition/service is clearer in later Mughal evolution; do not universalize under Akbar |
| khalisa | revenue reserved for direct imperial/central expenditure |
| madad-i maash/inam | revenue-free/charitable grant categories under separate supervision |
| jama | assessed/expected revenue on paper |
| hasil | amount actually realized |

Transfers limited hereditary entrenchment but encouraged short-horizon
extraction and information problems. A high-paper **jama** could fail to meet
salary if **hasil** was lower.

Jagirdar, zamindar and peasant:

```text
imperial assessed demand
        ↓ assigned to jagirdar or kept khalisa
jagirdar/official seeks realization
        ↕ bargains/coerces
zamindar/chaudhuri/muqaddam/qanungo local rights and information
        ↕
peasants differentiated by crop, holding, caste, labour and risk
```

Jagirdars could collect state demand; zamindars possessed diverse hereditary/
customary claims, armed/local authority and shares. The categories were not
synonyms.

## 15. Revenue evolution and Todar Mal

### Stages

1. restoration and early provincial experiments;
2. central price schedules after Gujarat exposed delay/local price distortion;
3. karori/measurement experiments in the 1570s;
4. Muzaffar Khan Turbati, Todar Mal, Shah Mansur and other officials refined
   records/rates;
5. zabt-dahsala schedules implemented around 1580;
6. regional alternative methods and ongoing correction.

Todar Mal was a major organizer, not sole inventor. Sultanate/Sur measurement,
local qanungo knowledge and team administration were indispensable.

### Measurement and cultivation categories

- standardized **Ilahi gaz** and **bigha** traditions;
- bamboo **jarib** joined with iron rings to reduce stretching;
- **polaj**: regularly cultivated;
- **parauti/parati**: temporarily fallow;
- **chachar**: fallow for several years;
- **banjar**: long uncultivated.

These categories guided assessment but do not describe one fixed social class or
every region.

## 16. Zabt, dahsala and dastur

```text
measure cultivated area
        ↓
record crop/land category and local productivity
        ↓
compile ten-year produce and price information
        ↓
form regional crop cash schedules (dastur)
        ↓
state annual demand, often framed near one-third norm
        ↓
collect cash or use alternative method where unsuitable
```

**Dahsala** does not mean a ten-year tax paid at once or a permanent ten-year
settlement. Ten-year data helped calculate annual rates.

**Zabt** is measurement-based assessment; **dahsala** is the averaging/rate
mechanism within a developed zabt system. **Dastur** is a regional schedule/
circle of rates, not a universal empire price.

Cash demand aided treasury/jagir/army accounting but exposed cultivators to
price movements and monetization gaps.

## 17. Alternative revenue methods and geography

| Method | Exam-safe meaning |
|---|---|
| batai/ghalla-bakhshi | actual crop-sharing after harvest; variants differed in division timing |
| kankut | measurement plus appraisal/estimate of standing crop |
| nasaq | estimate based substantially on previous/local assessment rather than full fresh measurement |
| zabt | field measurement and scheduled cash demand |

Zabt was strongest in relatively settled, surveyed, monetized core regions and
was unsuitable or incomplete in Bengal river/delta zones, forest/hill/frontier
areas and uncertain-crop regions. It probably did not cover every field even
within a nominal zabt province.

Method choice could vary by crop, locality, cultivator/community and year.

## 18. Zamindars, peasants and agrarian capacity

**Zamindar** covered diverse village headmen, lineage chiefs, landed right-
holders, local rulers and armed intermediaries. They could:

- claim hereditary/customary shares and dues;
- maintain armed followers/forts;
- provide local crop, caste and boundary knowledge;
- collect/mediate revenue;
- resist, negotiate or enter imperial service.

Peasants were differentiated by landholding, crop, caste/jati, labour,
irrigation, debt and market access. One-third normative demand did not equal
total burden: cesses, collection costs, price conversion and intermediary claims
mattered.

Normative remission/loans after flood, drought or crop failure show a state
ideal. Famine, flight and arrears reveal implementation limits.

Fiscal aims combined extraction, cultivation expansion, military finance,
record-making and social control—not peasant welfare alone.

## 19. Army and logistics

| Component | Institutional role |
|---|---|
| mansab contingents | elite command/service tied to rank and jagir/cash |
| ahadis | individually enrolled cavalry directly associated with emperor, qualifying mansab-only army claims |
| artillery/matchlocks | specialized corps and siege/field support |
| infantry | varied matchlock, guard, labour and local service |
| elephants | transport, battle, ceremony and logistics |
| forts/garrisons | route/frontier and provincial control |
| naval/river forces | important in Bengal/coasts but no uniform blue-water navy |
| roads/supply | fodder, grain, carts, animals and seasonal movement |

Mir bakhshi/muster records could expose paper troops, but corruption, substitution
and harassment remained. Technology did not substitute for finance/logistics.

## 20. Information, post, justice and mints

- waqia-navis/news writers, messengers and intelligence reports linked province/
  court while remaining dependent on local access and patronage;
- postal/relay routes served military and administrative communication, not a
  modern public postal department;
- qazi, mir adl and local/customary forums produced legal pluralism; royal
  justice rhetoric did not guarantee impersonal equality;
- imperial mints standardized gold, silver and copper issues, building on Sur/
  Sultanate precedents;
- coin legends, mint/date and weight are primary evidence, but standard coinage
  did not create uniform monetization;
- Akbar's **Ilahi era/calendar** was introduced in the 1580s and back-reckoned
  to accession; it served fiscal/court ordering and should not be confused with
  Tauhid-i Ilahi religious terminology.

---

## 21. Governance aims and elite incorporation

The state sought:

1. fiscal extraction and predictable assessment;
2. military mobilization and verified cavalry;
3. elite integration through rank, jagir, promotion and transfer;
4. provincial oversight through paired officers/reports;
5. negotiated use of zamindars/local knowledge;
6. court-centred sovereignty and succession security.

Rajput and diverse elite incorporation widened the ruling group but remained
unequal. Rank reflected service, patronage, lineage, politics and imperial need,
not a neutral merit examination.

---

## 22. Akbar-period structural tensions

- **jama-hasil gap:** paper revenue could exceed realization;
- **jagir pressure:** transfer and short tenure encouraged extraction;
- **paper cavalry:** rank/records could diverge from actual men/horses;
- **corruption/information:** officials and local intermediaries could manipulate
  measurement, classification and accounts;
- **cash risk:** cultivators bore price and market-access problems;
- **regional variation:** one manual could not override ecology and local rights;
- **personal centre:** checks depended on imperial attention and appointments.

These tensions existed within Akbar's reign; later jagirdari crisis must not be
projected backward fully formed.

## 23. Continuity without blueprint

| Sultanate/Sur precedent | Akbar-period development | Later boundary |
|---|---|---|
| iqta/jagir, khalisa and accounts | transferable jagir within mansab hierarchy | later jama-hasil/jagir stress |
| dagh/chehra | renewed verification and record system | Jahangir/Shah Jahan adjustments |
| sarkar/pargana/local officers | suba and paired provincial checks | changing suba count |
| measurement/rai/patta traditions | jarib, dastur, zabt-dahsala and alternatives | later revisions |
| routes/news | expanded imperial reporting and logistics | not modern postal state |

Akbar was a major organizer and political centre, not sole inventor. Institutions
emerged through officials, precedents, resistance and regional practice.
