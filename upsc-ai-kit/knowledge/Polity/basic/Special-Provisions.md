## Core idea
✅ India practises **asymmetric federalism** — some states get **special provisions** to protect tribal
customs, backward regions, or law & order. These sit in **Part XXI**, alongside the (now inoperative)
**Article 370** for J&K.

> 🔑 **12 states with Art 371 provisions:** Maharashtra, Gujarat, Nagaland, Assam, Manipur, Andhra Pradesh,
> Telangana, Sikkim, Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh, Goa, Karnataka.

## Article 370 — the big story
📰 ✅ **5 Aug 2019:** J&K's special status was made inoperative; the **J&K
Reorganisation Act, 2019** created **J&K (with legislature)** and **Ladakh (without)**.
✅ In *In Re: Article 370* (11 Dec 2023), the Supreme Court upheld the constitutional
steps, directed Assembly elections by Sept 2024, and recorded the Union's assurance that
statehood would be restored at the earliest; it did **not set a judicial deadline for statehood**.

## Key Art 371 provisions (remember)
- ✅ **371-A Nagaland** & **371-G Mizoram** — Union laws on **customary law / land** don't apply unless the state
  Assembly consents (strongest protection).
- ✅ **371-D Andhra Pradesh/Telangana** — local cadres + Administrative Tribunal.
- ✅ **371-J Karnataka** — Hyderabad-Karnataka region reservation (98th Amdt).
- ✅ **371 Maharashtra/Gujarat** — development boards (Vidarbha, Marathwada, Saurashtra, Kutch).

## 📰 Current link
📰 ✅ **Status 21 Jul 2026:** Assembly elections were held in 2024; restoration of
statehood remains an unfulfilled Union assurance. Ladakh's Sixth-Schedule/statehood demands
remain political proposals, not enacted constitutional changes.

### 6.2 Qualified thesis options

- *Asymmetric federalism is India's method of holding together a diverse union: Articles 371–371-J grant graduated autonomy so that distinct regions integrate on acceptable terms, which strengthens rather than weakens the Union.*
- *The abrogation of Article 370 completed a long-signalled integration, and the 2023 Constitution Bench upheld it as the removal of a temporary provision — but the conversion of a State into Union Territories remains the more contested constitutional move.*
- *The special provisions protect identity at the price of differentiated rights; the tension with single, uniform citizenship is real but is managed as protection for the vulnerable rather than privilege for insiders.*
- *Article 370 was always drafted as temporary and consent-based, so its operation depended on interpretive machinery that the 2019 route and the 2023 verdict resolved in favour of the Union.*

### 6.4 Bank A — Article 370: operation before 2019 and the 2019 instruments

- **Claim:** Article 370 gave J&K a consent-based autonomy, not ordinary Statehood. → **Evidence:** **Art 370 (Part XXI)** limited Parliament's law-making for J&K to specified Union/Concurrent-List matters, with other constitutional provisions applying only by **Presidential Order under Art 370(1)(d) with the State Government's concurrence**; **Art 35A** (added by the **1954 Presidential Order**) let the J&K legislature define "permanent residents" and reserve land/employment. → **Significance:** the Union's writ ran in J&K only through concurrence and modification. → **Caution:** ✅ Art 35A came from a **Presidential Order**, not a constitutional amendment passed by Parliament.
- **Claim:** The 2019 route rendered Art 370 inoperative and reorganised the State. → **Evidence:** the **Constitution (Application to Jammu and Kashmir) Order 2019 (C.O. 272)** applied the whole Constitution to J&K; **C.O. 273** declared Art 370's clauses inoperative; Parliament (acting during President's Rule) passed the resolutions; the **Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act 2019** split the State into two UTs (**J&K with a legislature, Ladakh without**) w.e.f. **31 October 2019**. → **Significance:** integration by executive order plus statute rather than by classic amendment. → **Caution:** ✅ Art 370 was **rendered inoperative**, it still **exists in the text** — do not say it was "deleted/repealed."

### 6.5 Bank B — *In re Article 370* (2023) propositions (state exactly, with caution)

- **Claim:** The Court upheld the abrogation and characterised Art 370 as temporary. → **Evidence:** ✅ *In re Article 370* (Constitution Bench, **11 December 2023**) held Art 370 was a **temporary provision** and that J&K **retained no element of internal sovereignty** distinct from other States after accession. → **Significance:** removed the "special sovereignty" premise. → **Caution:** ✅ state it as the Court's holding, not as a political slogan.
- **Claim:** The President's abrogation power survived the Constituent Assembly's dissolution. → **Evidence:** ✅ the Court held the **President could declare Art 370 inoperative under Art 370(3)** even after the J&K Constituent Assembly ceased to exist, so its recommendation was not a continuing precondition. → **Significance:** resolved the interpretive knot at the heart of the case. → **Caution:** ⚠️ the Court found it **unnecessary to rule on the validity of the C.O. 272 amendment to Art 367**, since the result was achievable under Art 370(3) directly — do not assert it validated that specific route.
- **Claim:** The Court directed restoration of statehood and elections but did not fix a statehood deadline. → **Evidence:** ✅ it directed the Election Commission to hold **Assembly elections by 30 September 2024** and recorded the Union's assurance that **statehood would be restored "as soon as possible,"** while **not deciding the validity of carving J&K into UTs**. → **Significance:** the downgrade's constitutionality was left open. → **Caution:** 📰 statehood restoration is **pending**; do not describe it as completed or as subject to a judicial deadline.

### 6.6 Bank C — Article 371 to 371-J special provisions (own each)

- **Claim:** The Article 371 family gives 12 States tailored autonomy inside Part XXI. → **Evidence:** these were **added by later amendments**, not in the original text, for **Maharashtra, Gujarat, Nagaland, Assam, Manipur, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Sikkim, Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh, Goa and Karnataka**. → **Significance:** constitutional asymmetry by design. → **Caution:** ✅ located in **Part XXI**, not Part III.

| Article | State(s) | What it actually does |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ **371** | Maharashtra & Gujarat | Governor's special responsibility for **development boards** (Vidarbha, Marathwada / Saurashtra, Kutch) |
| ✅ **371-A** | Nagaland | Union laws on **Naga customary law, land, civil/criminal justice** apply **only if the Assembly so decides**; Governor's law-and-order role (**strongest protection**) |
| ✅ **371-B** | Assam | Committee of the Assembly for **Tribal Areas** |
| ✅ **371-C** | Manipur | Committee of the Assembly for **Hill Areas**; Governor's special responsibility |
| ✅ **371-D/E** | Andhra Pradesh & Telangana | Equitable employment/education ("**local cadres**") + **Administrative Tribunal** (371-D, extended to Telangana **2014**); Central University (371-E) |
| ✅ **371-F** | Sikkim | Assembly ≥ 30; one Lok Sabha seat; protection of population sections (**36th Amendment 1975**) |
| ✅ **371-G** | Mizoram | Union laws on **Mizo customary law, land, justice** apply **only if the Assembly so decides** (**strongest protection**) |
| ✅ **371-H** | Arunachal Pradesh | Governor's special law-and-order responsibility |
| ✅ **371-I** | Goa | Assembly ≥ 30 |
| ✅ **371-J** | Karnataka | Development board + **reservation** (education + jobs) for the **Hyderabad-Karnataka region** (**98th Amendment 2012**) |

- **Analytical point:** ⚠️ **371-A (Nagaland)** and **371-G (Mizoram)** are the strongest — Union laws on customary law/land need Assembly consent; these are **distinct from** the Fifth/Sixth Schedules (see `Scheduled-and-Tribal-Areas.md`).

### 6.7 Bank D — Asymmetric federalism and the differentiated-citizenship critique

- **Claim:** Asymmetric autonomy strengthens rather than weakens the Union. → **Evidence:** India is a **"holding-together" federation**; Articles 371–371-J and the Schedules let distinct regions integrate on acceptable terms, defusing separatist pressure. → **Significance:** flexibility is a unifying device, not a concession of weakness. → **Caution:** ⚠️ this is an analytical characterisation, mark it as such.
- **Claim:** Special provisions create differentiated rights that sit uneasily with uniform citizenship. → **Evidence:** land, employment and customary-law protections (historically **Art 35A** in J&K; 371-A/G land rules) give some residents rights others in India lack, against the backdrop of **single citizenship (Art 5–11)** and **equality (Art 14)**. → **Significance:** the core criticism that special provisions entrench a form of **differentiated/graded citizenship**. → **Caution:** ⚠️ present the counter too — these are protective of vulnerable communities and contextually justified, not simple privilege.

### 6.8 Mechanism, institutional incentives and consequences

- ⚠️ **Consent as the design principle:** 371-A/G make Union law conditional on Assembly assent, giving these States a veto over customary-law/land legislation — the mechanism by which identity is protected without secession.
- ⚠️ **Executive-order integration:** the 2019 route showed that where a State is under President's Rule, the Union can achieve far-reaching constitutional change through Presidential Orders plus parliamentary resolutions — a precedent with federal-stability implications.
- ⚠️ **Statehood is not entrenched:** the reorganisation demonstrated that a State's status can be altered by Parliament, and the Court left that specific question open — a consequential silence.

### 6.9 Criticism, counter-argument, State variation and implementation constraints

- **Erosion-of-consent critique:** ⚠️ critics argue the 2019 route bypassed the State's own consent that Art 370 had required, weakening federal-consent principles.
- **Integration counter:** ⚠️ defenders note Art 370 was textually temporary and the 2023 Bench upheld the abrogation, treating it as completing integration.
- **Differentiated-citizenship critique vs protection:** ⚠️ special provisions are attacked as entrenching insider/outsider distinctions and defended as safeguards for tribal and backward regions.
- **Variation across States:** ⚠️ the provisions differ sharply — from mere development boards (371 Maharashtra/Gujarat) to customary-law vetoes (371-A/G) — so "special provision" is not one model.
- **Implementation constraints:** 📰 pending **J&K statehood restoration**; **Ladakh's Sixth-Schedule/statehood** demands remain political proposals, not enacted changes — verify before dating.

### 6.10 Verdict scaffolds

- **Article 370 verdict:** "The 2023 Constitution Bench treated the abrogation as the lawful removal of a temporary provision; the sharper constitutional question — whether a State may be downgraded to Union Territories — the Court left for another day."
- **Asymmetry verdict:** "Articles 371–371-J show that Indian unity is built on calibrated difference: graduated autonomy is the price and the strength of holding a diverse union together."
- **Citizenship verdict:** "Special provisions test the promise of uniform citizenship, but their protective purpose — shielding tribal custom, land and backward regions — is what makes differentiated rights defensible rather than discriminatory."

### 6.12 Factual-risk and current-status controls

- ✅ Special provisions are in **Part XXI**; Art 371 family covers **12 States**; added by **later amendments**, not the original Constitution.
- ✅ **Art 370 rendered inoperative 5 Aug 2019** (C.O. 272/273 + resolutions); **J&K Reorganisation Act 2019** effective **31 Oct 2019** (J&K with legislature, Ladakh without).
- ✅ *In re Article 370* (**11 Dec 2023**): Art 370 **temporary**; **no internal sovereignty**; President's Art 370(3) power **survived** the Constituent Assembly; elections by **30 Sep 2024**; statehood "as soon as possible"; validity of the **UT reorganisation left open**.
- ⚠️ Do not assert the Court validated the **C.O. 272/Art 367 amendment route**; it found that unnecessary.
- 📰 **J&K statehood restoration is pending**; **Ladakh's Sixth-Schedule/statehood** demands are political proposals — verify before dating.
- ✅ Do not confuse **Art 371 provisions** with the **Fifth/Sixth Schedules** or place them in Part III; do not read **371-A/G** as letting Parliament override State customary law.
