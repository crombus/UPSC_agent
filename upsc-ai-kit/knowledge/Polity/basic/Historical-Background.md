## 2. Core idea

✅ Many features of our Constitution have **roots in British-era laws**. A chain of Acts built the
legal framework of government in India — the most important being the **Government of India Act, 1935**
(the biggest source of the present Constitution).

## 3. Company Rule — key Acts

| Act | Landmark |
|---|---|
| ✅ Regulating Act **1773** | GG of **Bengal** (Warren Hastings); Supreme Court Calcutta |
| ✅ Pitt's India Act **1784** | Board of Control → **double government** |
| ✅ Charter Act **1813** | Ended Company trade monopoly (except tea/China) |
| ✅ Charter Act **1833** | GG of **India** (William Bentinck); centralisation |
| ✅ Charter Act **1853** | Separated legislative & executive; open competition for civil service |

## 4. Crown Rule — key Acts

| Act | Landmark |
|---|---|
| ✅ GoI Act **1858** | Abolished Company; **Viceroy** (Canning); Secretary of State |
| ✅ Councils Act **1861** | Indians in law-making; portfolio system |
| ✅ Councils Act **1909** (Morley-Minto) | **Separate electorate for Muslims** (Minto = father of communal electorate) |
| ✅ GoI Act **1919** (Montagu-Chelmsford) | **Dyarchy** in provinces; bicameralism; direct elections |
| ✅ GoI Act **1935** | **Provincial autonomy**; federal scheme; RBI; Federal Court |
| ✅ Independence Act **1947** | Two dominions; end of British rule |

> 🔑 Mnemonic (reforms pairs): **1909 = communal electorate** · **1919 = dyarchy** ·
> **1935 = provincial autonomy**. (Don't swap them!)

## 5. Government of India Act, 1935 - complete Prelims core

| Feature | Exact distinction |
|---|---|
| **All-India Federation** | Proposed a federation of British Indian provinces and princely states |
| **Did the federation operate?** | **No.** Accession of the required princely states did not occur |
| **Provincial autonomy** | Dyarchy in provinces was abolished and responsible government expanded, subject to gubernatorial safeguards |
| **Dyarchy at the Centre** | Federal subjects were divided into reserved and transferred subjects, but this federal arrangement did not commence |
| **Reserved federal subjects** | **Defence, external/foreign affairs, ecclesiastical affairs and tribal areas** were administered by the Governor-General with councillors, not controlled by responsible ministers or the federal legislature |
| **Legislative lists** | Federal, Provincial and Concurrent Lists |
| **Residuary powers** | Vested in the Governor-General, not automatically in the federal or provincial legislature |
| **Bicameralism** | Introduced in six provinces |
| **Institutions** | Provided for the Federal Court; separated Burma; supported the institutional framework associated with the RBI |

> **2024 Prelims trap:** The Act **provided** for an All-India Federation of provinces and princely
> states, so that statement is correct even though the federation never came into operation.
> Defence and foreign affairs were **reserved under the Governor-General**, not kept under the
> control of the federal legislature. Therefore, for that two-statement question, **statement 1
> only** is correct.

## 6. Must-Know Facts (Prelims)

- ✅ First **GG of Bengal** = Warren Hastings (1773); first **GG of India** = William Bentinck (1833).
- ✅ First **Viceroy** = Lord Canning (1858).

- ✅ **Dyarchy** in provinces = GoI Act **1919**; abolished by **1935** (provincial autonomy).

### 7.2 Qualified thesis options

- *The 1950 Constitution did not begin on a blank page: its administrative spine, federal scheme, public services and judiciary were inherited from a century of colonial statutes — above all the Government of India Act 1935 — while its democratic soul, universal franchise, justiciable rights and single citizenship, was a decisive Indian departure.*
- *Constitutional development under Company and Crown moved in two opposite directions — relentless centralisation from 1773 to 1858 and cautious devolution from 1861 to 1935 — and the Constitution inherited both, producing a federation with an unmistakable unitary bias.*
- *The colonial reforms that most shaped 1950 matter as much for what the framers rejected — separate electorates, official majorities and gubernatorial "reserved" powers — as for what they retained.*
- *If the 1935 Act is the Constitution's largest single quarry, the reforms of 1909–1932 are its cautionary text, showing how community-based representation, once conceded, hardened into a claim to nationhood.*

### 7.4 Bank A — Centralisation strand (the executive / administrative inheritance)

- **Claim:** a single, centralised executive and legislature were built incrementally. **Evidence:** ✅ Regulating Act **1773** made the Governor of Bengal the **Governor-General of Bengal** and subordinated Bombay & Madras; ✅ Charter Act **1833** created the **Governor-General of India** (first, Bentinck) with sole all-India legislative authority (laws now "Acts"). **Significance:** ⚠️ antecedent of a single Union executive and law-making legislature. **Caution:** ✅ the GG of *India* dates from **1833**, not 1773.
- **Claim:** civilian/political control over the administration predates independence. **Evidence:** ✅ Pitt's India Act **1784** created the **Board of Control** → "double government"; ✅ Charter Act **1793** entrenched the GG's power to override the council. **Significance:** ⚠️ the principle that administration answers to a political authority foreshadows ministerial responsibility. **Caution:** ✅ 1784 did **not** abolish the Company; it split commercial and political functions.
- **Claim:** Crown sovereignty gave a unitary, centrally answerable top. **Evidence:** ✅ Charter Act **1813** asserted **Crown sovereignty** over Company territories; ✅ GoI Act **1858** abolished the Company, created the **Viceroy** (Canning) and the **Secretary of State + Council of India**. **Significance:** ⚠️ a centralised administration accountable to a higher political authority — a template the framers democratised. **Caution:** ✅ the Company was abolished in **1858**, not 1853.

### 7.5 Bank B — Representative / devolution strand

- **Claim:** Indian association with law-making began as nomination, not election. **Evidence:** ✅ Councils Act **1861** added Indian **non-official members** and recognised the **portfolio system** (Canning); restored legislative powers to Bombay & Madras. **Significance:** ⚠️ the portfolio system is the institutional seed of the cabinet/ministerial system; legislative restoration seeds federal devolution. **Caution:** ✅ members were **nominated**, not elected.
- **Claim:** legislative oversight was conceded before the franchise. **Evidence:** ✅ Councils Act **1892** enlarged councils, allowed **budget discussion** and **questioning of the executive**, and a **limited indirect election** (the word "election" was avoided). **Significance:** ⚠️ budget and question powers are antecedents of parliamentary accountability. **Caution:** ✅ an official majority was retained — this was not responsible government.
- **Claim:** communal representation was introduced as reform and later rejected as principle. **Evidence:** ✅ Councils Act **1909** (Morley–Minto) created **separate electorates for Muslims** (Minto = "father of the communal electorate") and enlarged councils; first Indian in the Viceroy's Executive Council = **S.P. Sinha**. **Significance:** ⚠️ this is the feature the Constitution consciously **rejected** — single citizenship, universal adult franchise and (barring temporary SC/ST seat reservation) joint electorates. **Caution:** ✅ 1909 did **not** introduce dyarchy.
- **Claim:** provincial self-government and the federal division of subjects were rehearsed before 1950. **Evidence:** ✅ GoI Act **1919** (Montagu–Chelmsford) separated **central and provincial subjects**, introduced **dyarchy** in provinces, **bicameralism** and **direct elections**, and led to the **Central Public Service Commission (1926)**. **Significance:** ⚠️ the split of subjects prefigures the Union/State Lists; the PSC prefigures the UPSC; direct elections prefigure the parliamentary form. **Caution:** ✅ **provincial autonomy** is **1935**, not 1919 (1919 = dyarchy).

### 7.6 Bank C — The 1935 and 1947 Acts (the decisive inheritances)

- **Claim:** the 1935 Act is the single largest source of the Constitution. **Evidence:** ✅ it provided an **All-India Federation** (provinces + princely states; never operational), **provincial autonomy**, the three legislative **Lists**, the **Federal Court (1937)**, PSCs, the office of **Governor**, and institutionally the **RBI**; residuary powers to the **Governor-General**. **Significance:** ✅ the federal scheme, Governor, judiciary, PSCs, emergency provisions and administrative detail passed into 1950 (book: "single largest source"). **Caution:** ✅ the federation **never came into operation**, and residuary powers went to the **Viceroy**, not the provinces.
- **Claim:** the 1947 Act converted the drafting body into a sovereign constituent power. **Evidence:** ✅ Indian Independence Act **1947** created two dominions from **15 Aug 1947**, provided for the **lapse of British paramountcy**, and let the Constituent Assembly frame any constitution and repeal British Acts; governance ran on the **GoI Act 1935** until 1950. **Significance:** ⚠️ this is why the Assembly could enact a wholly Indian document and why the princely states had to be integrated. **Caution:** ✅ paramountcy **lapsed**; it did not automatically pass to India.

### 7.8 Mechanism, incentives and consequences (how the reforms actually worked)

- ✅ **Official majorities and a limited franchise** (1892–1935) let councils criticise but not remove the executive → ⚠️ this taught the framers to insist on **responsible government** and universal franchise, not merely enlarged councils.
- ✅ **Separate electorates** (1909; extended 1919 to Sikhs, Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans; **Communal Award 1932** to depressed classes) → ⚠️ institutionalised community-based mobilisation; the ✅ **Poona Pact (1932)** substituted reserved seats within a joint electorate — the model the Constitution adopted for SC/ST.
- ✅ **Dyarchy** (1919 provinces; 1935 at the Centre, never operational) split responsibility between ministers and the Governor → ⚠️ its friction discredited divided responsibility and pushed the framers toward **full provincial autonomy** and collective ministerial responsibility.

### 7.9 Criticism, counter-argument and variation

- ⚠️ **"The Constitution is a mere colonial continuation"** overstates: universal adult franchise, justiciable Fundamental Rights, an independent Election Commission and single citizenship have **no colonial parent**.
- ⚠️ **"It is wholly original"** also fails: the Constitution retained substantial administrative detail and institutional architecture from the 1935 Act, while the parliamentary form drew on Westminster practice.
- ⚠️ **Temporal variation:** the centralising Acts (1773–1858) and the devolving Acts (1861–1935) pull in opposite directions; an answer that cites only one strand misreads the inheritance.

### 7.10 Verdict scaffolds

- **Culmination verdict:** "The Constitution is the culmination of colonial constitutional development in its structure and the repudiation of it in its politics — inheriting the 1935 federal-administrative frame while discarding official majorities, separate electorates and a restricted franchise."
- **Source-tracing verdict:** "No single Act 'made' the Constitution, but the 1935 Act came closest; the rest supplied the incremental machinery of a centralised state and a slowly widening representation."
- **Representative-evolution verdict:** "Representation advanced from nomination (1861) to consultation (1892) to communal election (1909) to provincial self-rule (1935) — a graded ascent the Constitution completed by grounding power in universal adult franchise."

### 7.11 Prelims close-option distinctions

- **Routed 2024 Prelims GS-I Q62 — GoI Act 1935, All-India Federation and reserved control of defence/foreign affairs:** ✅ the Act **provided for** an All-India Federation of provinces and princely states (that statement is correct **even though the federation never operated**, because the requisite princely states did not accede); ✅ **defence and external/foreign affairs were "reserved" subjects administered by the Governor-General**, not placed under the federal legislature. A statement claiming the federal legislature controlled defence/foreign affairs is therefore **wrong**.
- **1773 vs 1833:** GG of **Bengal** (1773) vs GG of **India** (1833).
- **1853 vs 1858:** 1853 separated the council's legislative and executive functions; the Company was **abolished in 1858**.
- **1909 vs 1919 vs 1935:** communal electorate (1909) · dyarchy in provinces (1919) · provincial autonomy (1935). Never swap these.
- **Residuary powers under 1935:** vested in the **Viceroy / Governor-General**, not the provinces.
- **First Indian on the Viceroy's Executive Council:** **S.P. Sinha (1909)**, not 1919.

### 7.12 Factual-risk and current-status controls

- Do **not** state that the Regulating Act 1773 created the Governor-General of **India** — it created the GG of **Bengal**.
- Do **not** state that the 1935 federation came into operation — ✅ it **never** did.
- Do **not** assign residuary powers under the 1935 Act to the provinces — ✅ they went to the **Governor-General**.
- Do **not** describe 1909 as introducing dyarchy, or 1919 as introducing provincial autonomy.
- Do **not** claim the Constitution "borrowed" separate electorates — ✅ it **rejected** them (single citizenship; universal adult franchise).
