## Core idea
The **NHRC** is the statutory **"watchdog of human rights"** — it inquires into violations by public servants,
visits jails, and promotes HR, but its recommendations are **advisory, not binding**, and it **cannot punish**.
The 1993 Act **permits a state government to constitute** an SHRC for State/Concurrent
List matters; it does not mean every state necessarily has a functioning commission.

> 🔑 **Statutory, not constitutional** — 1993 Act.
> 🔑 **SHRC:** Governor appoints, **President removes**. **NHRC:** can't touch cases **older than 1 year**.

## Must-Know Facts
- ✅ NHRC = chairperson + members + **7 ex-officio** (national commission chairs + Chief Commissioner PwD).
- ✅ Appointed by the **President** on a **6-member committee** (PM head).
- ✅ **Civil-court powers**; own investigating staff; recommendations only — govt responds in **1 month**.
- ✅ **Armed forces:** NHRC can only seek a **report** & recommend.
- ✅ **Human Rights Courts** in each district (with HC Chief Justice's concurrence).

- **2018 · GS-II · Q16** — Demand (neutral rendering): *whether the multiplicity of commissions for vulnerable sections, with overlapping jurisdiction and duplication, should be merged into a single umbrella Human Rights Commission.* Directive: **Argue your case** (take and defend a position) · **15 marks · 250 words**.
  - **Core routing supersedes the older `advanced/35_NHRC-and-SHRC.md` pointer for this demand.**
  - **Executable route:** (1) **Thesis (pick a side, as the directive demands)** — coordinate under a strengthened apex NHRC rather than dissolve the specialised commissions; a single merged body is *constitutionally impossible for the constitutional commissions and functionally undesirable for the rest.* (2) **Map the multiplicity** — statutory NHRC (**Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993**) alongside the **constitutional** NCSC (**Art 338**), NCST (**Art 338A**), NCBC (**Art 338B**, constitutionalised by the 102nd Amendment 2018) and the statutory NCW, NCM, NCPCR (**CPCR Act 2005**) and Chief Commissioner for Persons with Disabilities (**RPwD Act 2016**). (3) **Case FOR an umbrella** — overlapping mandates, duplication, forum-shopping, weak coordination and a single accountable voice; note the NHRC is *already* partly integrative because the **2019 Amendment made the NCBC and NCPCR chairpersons and the Chief Commissioner for PwD deemed members**. (4) **Case AGAINST merger** — NCSC/NCST/NCBC hold **constitutional** status and service-safeguard functions that a statutory NHRC cannot absorb without a constitutional amendment; specialised bodies carry group-specific expertise a mega-commission would dilute. (5) **Middle path** — retain specialised commissions but institutionalise coordination through the NHRC as apex clearing-house, using the existing **Section 36(1)** bar (the NHRC does not inquire into a matter already pending before another duly constituted commission) as the referral spine. (6) **Reform overlay** — Paris-Principles compliance, enforceable recommendations, filled vacancies. (7) **Verdict** — argue for a coordinated architecture with the NHRC as apex, not abolition.
- **2021 · GS-II · Q12** — Demand (neutral rendering): *Human Rights Commissions have protected rights yet fail against the powerful; analyse their structural and practical limitations and suggest remedies.* Directive: **Analyse + suggest remedial measures** · **15 marks · 250 words**.
  - **Core routing supersedes the older `advanced/35_NHRC-and-SHRC.md` pointer for this demand.**
  - **Executable route:** (1) **Thesis** — HRCs are *recommendatory by statutory design and resource-starved in practice*, so they document violations ably but cannot enforce; the cure is statutory teeth plus Paris-Principles independence, not abolition. (2) **Structural limitations (named)** — recommendations **advisory, not binding** (**Section 18**); the Commission **cannot itself punish or award compensation as of right** (it only *recommends* prosecution/relief or moves the SC/HC); the **one-year bar** (**Section 36(2)**); the **armed-forces restriction** (**Section 19** — only a report from the Centre plus recommendation); SHRC jurisdiction confined to **State List and Concurrent List** subjects. (3) **Practical limitations** — vacancies and delayed appointments, heavy pendency, dependence on the executive for funds and for **police/deputationist investigators**, a former-judge/bureaucrat-dominated membership that strains the Paris-Principles **pluralism** test, and routine state non-compliance. (4) **Balance (what works)** — suo-motu cognisance, jail visits, custodial-death reporting norms and intervention in court keep the body relevant. (5) **Remedies** — a **time-bound reasoned-rejection duty** on governments or enforceable recommendations; an **independent investigative wing**; relax the one-year bar; widen the armed-forces mandate; **transparent, pluralist appointments** including civil society; guaranteed budget; fill vacancies. (6) **Verdict** — convert a moral-suasion body into an enforcement body by amendment and GANHRI-grade independence.

### 5.2 Qualified thesis options

- *The NHRC is a constitutionally-anchored but statutorily-limited watchdog: strong in documentation and moral authority, weak in enforcement because the Protection of Human Rights Act deliberately made it recommendatory.*
- *A single umbrella Human Rights Commission is neither feasible — the SC/ST/BC commissions are constitutional bodies — nor desirable, because specialised mandates would be diluted; coordination under an apex NHRC is the answerable position.*
- *The gap between the NHRC's promise and its performance is a Paris-Principles gap — pluralist composition, independent investigation and guaranteed funding — not merely a resources gap.*
- *The NHRC–SHRC design encodes a federal safeguard: the Governor appoints the SHRC but only the President can remove it, insulating State commissions from the State executive they must scrutinise.*

### 5.4 Bank A — Statutory-body architecture on the eight comparable heads (the most-examined grid)

| Head | NHRC | SHRC |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ **Legal source** | **Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993** (statutory, *not* constitutional) | Same Act; a State government **may** constitute one (establishment is enabling, not automatic) |
| ✅ **Appointment authority** | **President** | **Governor** |
| ✅ **Selection committee** | **Six-member**: **PM (head)**, **Speaker of Lok Sabha**, **Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha**, **Leader of Opposition in both Houses**, **Union Home Minister** | **CM (head)**, **Speaker of the Assembly**, **State Home Minister**, **Leader of Opposition** (+ Council Chairman and its LoP where bicameral) |
| ✅ **Composition** | Chairperson (**retired CJI or a retired Judge of the Supreme Court**, widened by the **2019 Amendment**) + a serving/retired SC judge + a serving/retired HC Chief Justice + three HR experts (**≥1 woman**) + **seven deemed/ex-officio members** (chairs of the National Commissions for Minorities, SCs, STs, Women, **BCs**, **Protection of Child Rights** + the **Chief Commissioner for PwD**) | Chairperson (**retired HC Chief Justice or a Judge of a High Court**, widened by the 2019 Amendment) + one serving/retired HC judge or District Judge (≥7 yrs) + one HR expert |
| ✅ **Tenure** | **3 years or 70 years**, whichever earlier (**reduced from 5 years** by the 2019 Amendment); **re-appointment allowed** | **3 years or 70 years**; re-appointment allowed |
| ✅ **Removal** | By the **President**; for **proved misbehaviour/incapacity** only after a **Supreme Court inquiry** whose advice is binding | By the **President** (not the Governor) on the same grounds and the same SC-inquiry route |
| ✅ **Powers (recommendatory?)** | Powers of a **civil court** for inquiries (**Section 13**); may inquire, visit jails, intervene in court; **recommendations are advisory, not binding** (**Section 18**) — **cannot punish or award compensation as of right** | Same powers within the State; same recommendatory ceiling |
| ✅ **Appeal / enforcement limit** | No power to enforce its own recommendations; must **approach the SC/HC** for writs; government need only **respond within one month**; **one-year bar** on cognisance (**Section 36(2)**) | Same; confined to **State List / Concurrent List** subjects; barred where the NHRC/another commission is already seized (**Section 36(1)**) |
| ✅ **Independence safeguards** | Fixed statutory tenure, SC-inquiry-protected removal, own investigating staff (**Section 11**), financial provision from the Consolidated Fund | Same architecture at State level; **President-only removal** insulates it from the State executive |
| ✅ **Accountability route** | **Annual/special report → Central Government → laid before Parliament** with an action-taken memorandum | **Report → State Government → laid before the State Legislature** |

- **Analytical use:** every "toothless/limitations" and "distinguish NHRC from SHRC" answer is won or lost on this grid — the recommendatory power (row 7) plus the one-year and armed-forces limits (rows 8) are the exact points where marks are scored.

### 5.5 Bank B — Powers, mechanism and their statutory ceiling

- **Claim:** the NHRC can *investigate* effectively but cannot *deliver a remedy*. **Evidence:** **Section 13** gives it civil-court powers (summon witnesses, require documents, receive evidence) and it may inquire suo motu, on petition or on a court's reference; **Section 12** lists the functions (inquiry, jail visits, review of safeguards, HR literacy). **Significance:** it can build a credible record of a violation. **Limitation:** under **Section 18** it can only **recommend** compensation/prosecution or **approach the SC/HC**; the remedy depends on the government or the courts, not on the Commission.
- **Claim:** time and subject-matter bars shrink its reach. **Evidence:** **Section 36(2)** bars inquiry **more than one year** after the alleged act; **Section 36(1)** bars matters already pending before another duly constituted commission. **Significance:** many stale or overlapping complaints fall away. **Limitation/status:** this is a statutory ceiling — reform proposals to relax the one-year bar are **proposals, not law**.
- **Claim:** the armed forces are near-exempt from direct inquiry. **Evidence:** **Section 19** confines the NHRC, for the armed forces, to **seeking a report from the Central Government** and then making a recommendation. **Significance:** custodial and operational allegations against the forces cannot be investigated like ordinary cases. **Limitation:** widening this mandate is a recurring reform demand, not an accomplished change.
- **Claim:** enforcement machinery at district level exists but is thin. **Evidence:** **Section 30** provides for a **Human Rights Court in each district**, notified by the State Government with the **concurrence of the High Court's Chief Justice**. **Significance:** a forum for speedy trial of HR offences. **Limitation/status:** ⚠️ many States have not designated dedicated courts or special prosecutors, so the provision is under-operationalised.

### 5.6 Bank C — Paris Principles, accreditation and the independence question

- **Claim:** the benchmark for a National Human Rights Institution is the **Paris Principles** (adopted by the UN General Assembly, **1993**), not domestic satisfaction. **Evidence:** they require a **broad mandate anchored in statute/constitution, pluralist composition, independence, adequate powers of investigation and adequate funding**. **Significance:** they are the yardstick every reform answer should cite. **Limitation:** they are **international standards**, not binding Indian law.
- **Claim:** compliance is externally assessed. **Evidence:** the **Global Alliance of National Human Rights Institutions (GANHRI)** accredits NHRIs as **A/B/C** against the Paris Principles; India's NHRC holds **A-status**. **Significance:** accreditation status is the objective evidence a reform answer needs. 📰 **Status checked 21 Jul 2026:** the NHRC's official site continues to describe India as holding **A-status** while a review cycle remains unresolved; **no official downgrade has been published** — a proposed/deferred review must not be written up as a completed downgrade.
- **Claim:** India's structural gaps map onto specific Paris heads. **Evidence:** a former-judge/bureaucrat-heavy membership (pluralism gap), reliance on **police/deputationist investigators** (independence-of-investigation gap) and executive control of budget/appointments (autonomy gap). **Significance:** this turns a vague "toothless" charge into a diagnosable, remediable list. **Limitation:** ⚠️ these are analytical inferences from the design, to be argued rather than asserted as official findings.

### 5.7 Bank D — Umbrella vs specialised commissions (routed 2018 demand)

| Body | Legal status | Why it resists merger |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ **NCSC / NCST** | **Constitutional** (**Art 338 / 338A**) | Carry service-safeguard and monitoring functions a statutory NHRC cannot lawfully absorb |
| ✅ **NCBC** | **Constitutional** since the **102nd Amendment, 2018** (**Art 338B**) | Same constitutional-status bar to absorption |
| ✅ **NCW, NCM, NCPCR (CPCR Act 2005)** | **Statutory** | Group-specific expertise; NCPCR chair is *already* a **deemed NHRC member** (2019) |
| ✅ **Chief Commissioner for PwD (RPwD Act 2016)** | **Statutory** | Already a **deemed NHRC member** (2019) — integration exists without merger |

- **Argument engine:** the "merge everything" answer collapses once you note that **constitutional commissions cannot be folded into a statutory umbrella without amending Articles 338/338A/338B**; the defensible position is coordination — the NHRC as apex, **Section 36(1)** as the anti-overlap rule, and shared case-management — which honours the directive ("argue your case") with a clear, reasoned stand.

### 5.8 Institutional incentives, mechanism and consequences

- ⚠️ **Recommendatory design creates a compliance gap:** because the government is only obliged to *respond*, not to *comply*, the NHRC's leverage is publicity and litigation, so its effectiveness tracks political salience and media attention rather than legal compulsion.
- ⚠️ **Executive dependence for investigators** blunts inquiries into the police themselves — the most common category of complaint — because the investigating officers are drawn from the very forces under scrutiny.
- ⚠️ **The President-only removal of SHRC members** is a deliberate federal safeguard: it prevents a State government from disciplining the commission that scrutinises it, at the cost of a slower removal route.

### 5.9 Criticism, counter-argument, variation and implementation constraints

- **"Toothless tiger" critique:** ⚠️ advisory recommendations, the one-year bar, the armed-forces carve-out and dependence on state investigators leave the NHRC unable to *compel* redress.
- **Counter (it still matters):** ⚠️ suo-motu cognisance, jail inspections, custodial-death reporting norms, intervention in litigation and the moral weight of an A-status NHRI produce real accountability pressure even without binding power.
- **Variation:** ⚠️ SHRC performance is highly uneven — several States under-resource or under-staff their commissions, and a few have not constituted one, so "HRCs in India" is not a uniform story.
- **Implementation constraint:** ⚠️ enforceability reform runs into the separation-of-powers objection (a recommendatory body cannot be turned into a court by ordinary design), so the realistic fix is a **time-bound reasoned-rejection duty** plus independent investigation, not converting the NHRC into a tribunal.

**Remedy map (for the "suggest remedial measures" limb of the 2021 demand):**

| Limitation (named) | Remedy | Status caution |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ Recommendations advisory (**Section 18**) | Statutory **duty to comply or reject with reasons within a fixed period**; escalation to a court on default | ⚠️ Proposal; not enacted |
| ✅ **Section 36(2)** one-year bar | Relax or remove the limitation window for grave violations | ⚠️ Requires amendment |
| ✅ **Section 19** armed-forces carve-out | Permit at least a fact-finding inquiry with procedural safeguards | ⚠️ Proposal; balances operational secrecy |
| ✅ Police/deputationist investigators | A dedicated **independent investigative wing** under the Commission | ⚠️ Paris-Principles "independent investigation" head |
| ✅ Non-pluralist membership | **Transparent, pluralist appointments** including civil-society/activist members | ⚠️ Paris-Principles "pluralism" head |
| ✅ Vacancies and funding dependence | Time-bound appointments and a **guaranteed budget** from the Consolidated Fund | ⚠️ Administrative + budgetary reform |

### 5.10 Verdict scaffolds

- **Umbrella-body verdict:** "A single Human Rights Commission is blocked by the constitutional status of the SC/ST/BC commissions and would dilute specialised mandates; the answerable reform is coordination under an apex NHRC using Section 36(1), not abolition."
- **Limitations verdict:** "The NHRC's weakness is written into its statute — it can investigate but only recommend — so the remedy is legislative: enforceable recommendations, independent investigators and Paris-Principles pluralism."
- **Independence verdict:** "India's NHRC is an A-status institution on paper; closing the Paris-Principles gaps in composition, investigation and funding is what would make that status match its practice."

### 5.12 Factual-risk and current-status controls

- ✅ NHRC/SHRC are **statutory** (PHRA 1993); do not call them constitutional.
- ✅ The **2019 Amendment** *reduced* NHRC/SHRC tenure from **5 to 3 years** and *widened* eligibility (SC judge for NHRC chair; HC judge for SHRC chair) and added deemed members — **get the direction of change right**: tenure went down, eligibility widened.
- ✅ **Removal of both NHRC and SHRC members is by the President** after a Supreme Court inquiry; do not attribute SHRC removal to the Governor.
- ✅ Recommendations are **advisory**; the government must **respond within one month** but is not bound to comply.
- 📰 On **GANHRI accreditation**, use the controlling position: **A-status stands and no official downgrade has been published** (checked 21 Jul 2026) — do not report a review or recommendation as a completed downgrade.
- ⚠️ Do not fabricate case-counts, pendency figures or a national tally of functioning SHRCs; state the mechanism and flag the number as variable.
- ⚠️ Reform ideas (enforceable recommendations, removing the one-year bar, widening the armed-forces mandate) are **proposals, not enacted law** — mark them as such.
