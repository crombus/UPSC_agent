## Snapshot
| Item | CVC | CBI |
|---|---|---|
| Born | **1964** (exec resolution) | **1963** (Home Min. resolution) |
| Nature | **Statutory (CVC Act 2003)** | **NOT statutory** — runs on **DSPE Act 1946** |
| Recommended by | **Santhanam Committee** | **Santhanam Committee** |
| Head | Central Vigilance Commissioner + **≤2** VCs | **Director** (minimum 2-year tenure; annual extensions may take the total to 5 years) |

## Core idea
Both are **anti-corruption** bodies — but **neither is constitutional**. **CVC** oversees vigilance and (only for
**PC-Act 1988** cases) **superintends the CBI**. **CBI** is the Centre's main **investigating agency**, needing
**state consent** to operate in states.

> 🔑 **CVC panel:** PM + **Home Minister** + LoP(LS). **CBI Director panel:** PM + **CJI/SC judge** + LoP(LS).
> 🔑 **CBI = "caged parrot"** (SC 2013 context) — executive-created organisation using DSPE Act powers; ordinary State-area operation needs consent.

## Must-Know Facts
- ✅ CVC statutory since **2003** (after **Vineet Narain** case 1997); CBI powers from **DSPE Act 1946**.
- ✅ CVC = multi-member (CVC + ≤2 VCs), **4-yr/65**, removed by **President** (SC inquiry).
- ✅ CBI Director appointed by a **PM + LoP/largest-opposition-party leader + CJI/nominee** panel; minimum **2-year** tenure, extendable annually on committee recommendation and recorded reasons up to **5 years total**.
- ✅ **CBI vs NIA:** NIA → terror; CBI → corruption/economic/organised crime.

## 📰 Current link
📰 CBI's **"caged parrot"** autonomy debate; **withdrawal of general consent** by many states (federal friction);
**2021 amendments** allow **up to 5-year** tenures for CBI & ED chiefs via yearly extensions.

### 5.0 Direct Mains demands owned by this Core file

- **2021 · GS-II · Q11** — Demand (neutral rendering): *the CBI's jurisdiction, the requirement of State consent, and what this reveals about the federal character of India.* Directive: **Explain** (clarify the mechanism and its significance) · **15 marks · 250 words**.
  - **Core routing supersedes the older `advanced/37_CVC-and-CBI.md` pointer for this demand.**
  - **Executable route:** (1) **Thesis** — because **police and public order are State subjects** (Entries 1–2, **State List, Seventh Schedule**), the CBI's reach into a State is *conditional on consent*; the withdrawal of "general consent" is therefore a **federal safeguard**, not mere obstruction, though it carries accountability costs. (2) **Legal architecture** — the CBI has **no dedicated statute**; it derives police powers from the **Delhi Special Police Establishment (DSPE) Act, 1946**; **Section 2** confines it to Union Territories, **Section 5** extends it to a State, and **Section 6** makes that extension conditional on the **State Government's consent**. (3) **Two kinds of consent** — **case-specific** consent versus **"general consent"** (standing permission for routine operation); several States have **withdrawn general consent**, requiring case-by-case approval. (4) **Federal argument for withdrawal** — it protects State autonomy over a constitutionally State subject and answers the "caged parrot" fear of a centrally-directed agency. (5) **Costs / counter** — it can create investigative safe havens and delay corruption and inter-State cases. (6) **The judicial balance (decisive)** — in ***State of West Bengal v. Committee for Protection of Democratic Rights* (2010)** a **Constitution Bench** held that a **High Court under Art 226 (and the Supreme Court under Art 32) can direct a CBI investigation within a State even without its consent**, and that this does **not** violate federalism; so consent binds the executive route, not the constitutional-court route. (7) **Verdict** — State consent is a genuine federal feature preserved against misuse by judicial override; the real question is the CBI's political neutrality, which a **statutory charter** would address.

### 5.2 Qualified thesis options

- *The CBI's dependence on State consent is not a defect but a constitutional consequence of police being a State subject; the defect is its lack of an independent statute, which leaves it exposed to executive control.*
- *Withdrawal of general consent is a legitimate exercise of federalism whose sting is drawn by the courts' power to order a CBI probe regardless of consent — so the federal balance holds even when consent is withheld.*
- *The CVC illustrates the limit of the recommendatory model: statutory status and a fixed tenure cannot compensate for advice that binds no one and superintendence confined to corruption cases.*
- *India's investigative architecture is a patchwork of statutes and executive orders; classifying each body by its exact legal source is what separates a precise answer from a vague one.*
- *The recurring reform in this block is a single move — give the CBI its own statute — because almost every weakness, from the consent friction to the "caged parrot" charge, traces back to an agency that borrows its powers from a 1946 law.*

### 5.4 Bank A — Statutory-body architecture on the eight comparable heads

| Head | Central Vigilance Commission (CVC) | Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ **Legal source** | **CVC Act, 2003** (statutory); began as an **executive resolution, 1964** on the **Santhanam Committee**'s recommendation | **No dedicated statute**; derives police powers from the **DSPE Act, 1946** (the CBI itself was set up by a **1963 executive resolution**) |
| ✅ **Appointment authority** | **President** | **Central Government** (Director, on committee recommendation) |
| ✅ **Selection committee** | **Three-member**: **PM (head)** + **Union Home Minister** + **LoP (Lok Sabha)** | **Three-member (Lokpal Act, 2013)**: **PM (chair)** + **LoP / leader of the single-largest opposition party in the Lok Sabha** (**DSPE Amendment Act, 2014**) + **CJI or an SC judge nominated by him** |
| ✅ **Composition** | **Multi-member**: Central Vigilance Commissioner (chair) + **not more than 2** Vigilance Commissioners | Headed by a **Director**; a police organisation; SP-and-above (except Director) appointed on a **CVC-chaired** committee |
| ✅ **Tenure** | **4 years or 65**, whichever earlier | Director: **2-year minimum security of tenure**; the **DSPE Amendment Act, 2021** permits one-year extensions on committee recommendation and recorded reasons, up to **5 years total** |
| ✅ **Removal / transfer safeguard** | By the **President** (proved misbehaviour/incapacity → **Supreme Court inquiry/report**) | Director **cannot be transferred or divested of powers without prior approval of the appointing committee**; do not mislabel this as the complete removal code |
| ✅ **Powers (binding?)** | **Civil-court powers** for inquiries; **advisory — advice NOT binding** (government records reasons to differ); superintendence over the CBI **only for Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 offences** | Investigates and files charge sheets; but its **extension into a State needs consent (DSPE Section 6)**; prosecution needs **sanction** (**Section 17A/19, PC Act**) |
| ✅ **Appeal / enforcement limit** | Cannot compel or punish — only recommends | Cannot operate in a State without consent; **but constitutional courts can override consent** (*WB v. CPDR*, 2010) |
| ✅ **Independence safeguards** | Statutory status, fixed tenure, SC-inquiry removal, salary parity (**CVC = UPSC Chairman; VC = UPSC member**) | Director's fixed tenure and committee appointment (**Vineet Narain, 1997; Lokpal Act, 2013**) — **but no statutory charter of its own** |
| ✅ **Accountability route** | **Annual report → President → Parliament** | Under the **DoPT (Ministry of Personnel)**; superintendence **split — CVC for PC-Act cases, Central Government otherwise** |

- **Analytical use:** the CVC answer lives in row 7 (advisory, PC-Act-only superintendence); the CBI answer lives in rows 7–9 (consent, judicial override and the missing statute).

### 5.5 Bank B — CBI jurisdiction, State consent and federalism (routed 2021 demand)

- **Claim:** the CBI's reach is federally bounded because policing is a State function. **Evidence:** **police and public order are Entries 1–2 of the State List**; the CBI has no own statute and borrows from the **DSPE Act, 1946** — **Section 2** (Union Territories), **Section 5** (extension to States) and **Section 6** (State consent required). **Significance:** the CBI is not a national police force with automatic jurisdiction. **Limitation:** this makes central investigation dependent on State cooperation.
- **Claim:** "general consent" is the routine key, and withdrawing it is a federal act. **Evidence:** several States have **withdrawn general consent**, so the CBI needs **case-specific** consent there. **Significance:** a constitutionally grounded check on central over-reach. **Limitation/status:** ⚠️ the roster of States changes over time — **do not memorise an undated list/count**.
- **Claim:** consent does not fetter the constitutional courts. **Evidence:** ***State of West Bengal v. Committee for Protection of Democratic Rights* (2010, Constitution Bench)** held that the **HC/SC can direct a CBI probe within a State without its consent**, and this does not breach federalism. **Significance:** the federal safeguard and the fundamental-rights remedy coexist. **Limitation:** the power is the courts', not the executive's — the government cannot bypass Section 6 on its own.
- **Claim:** the CBI's legal foundation has been questioned but not undone. **Evidence:** ⚠️ in ***Navendra Kumar v. Union of India* (2013)** the **Gauhati High Court quashed the 1963 executive resolution** that constituted the CBI (it did **not** strike down the DSPE Act, 1946); the **Supreme Court stayed that judgment within days**. **Significance:** it sharpened the "no statute" critique. **Limitation/status caution:** the stay means the CBI's status is **not settled against it** — never cite this as a final ruling.

### 5.6 Bank C — CVC as the vigilance apex (origin, status and the advisory ceiling)

- **Claim:** the CVC is the statutory integrity apex, but a late one. **Evidence:** recommended by the **Santhanam Committee (1962–64)**, set up by **executive resolution in 1964**, and given **statutory status only by the CVC Act, 2003** after the Supreme Court's directions in the **Vineet Narain / Jain Hawala case (1997)**. **Significance:** its independence is a post-*Vineet Narain* judicial achievement, not an original design. **Limitation:** statutory status did not make its advice binding.
- **Claim:** the CVC's control over the CBI is partial. **Evidence:** it exercises **superintendence over the CBI only for Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 offences**; for all other CBI work superintendence rests with the **Central Government**. **Significance:** this split is a favourite Prelims/Mains distinction. **Limitation:** it fragments accountability for the CBI.
- **Claim:** the CVC recommends but cannot enforce. **Evidence:** it has **civil-court powers** for inquiries and reports to Parliament, yet its **advice is not binding** — the government need only record reasons to differ. **Significance:** it is a recommendatory body like the NHRC, unlike the binding Information Commission. **Limitation:** this is the structural cap on its effectiveness.
- **Claim:** the CVC's remit extends beyond advising on cases. **Evidence:** it is the designated agency under the **Public Interest Disclosure and Protection of Informers (PIDPI) Resolution, 2004** and participates in statutory appointment/oversight committees. **Significance:** it sits at the centre of the Union vigilance architecture. **Limitation/status:** the **Whistle Blowers Protection Act, 2014** must not be presented as an operational source of CVC authority unless commencement is verified; PIDPI is the current operative disclosure route taught here.

### 5.7 Bank D — The investigative/economic-intelligence ecosystem (exact statutory sources; routed 2026 Prelims demand)

| Body | Exact legal source | Core function |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ **CBI** | **DSPE Act, 1946** (no dedicated statute of its own) | Corruption, economic offences and special/organised crime (non-terror); needs State consent outside UTs |
| ✅ **Enforcement Directorate (ED)** | **FEMA, 1999** + **PMLA, 2002** (+ **Fugitive Economic Offenders Act, 2018**); under the Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance | Foreign-exchange violations and money-laundering — attachment, search, seizure, arrest |
| ✅ **Income-tax Department (CBDT)** | **Income-tax Act, 1961**; CBDT is statutory under the **Central Board of Revenue Act, 1963** | Direct-tax administration; the Investigation Wing conducts search/seizure ("raids") |
| ✅ **Serious Fraud Investigation Office (SFIO)** | **Statutory under the Companies Act, 2013 (Section 211)**; Ministry of Corporate Affairs | Investigates serious **corporate/company fraud** |
| ✅ **Financial Intelligence Unit–India (FIU-IND)** | **Set up by an executive order in 2004**; functions under the **PMLA, 2002**; reports to the Economic Intelligence Council (FM) | Receives and analyses **Suspicious Transaction Reports**; the national financial-intelligence hub |
| ✅ **CVC** | **CVC Act, 2003** | Vigilance apex; superintends the CBI for PC-Act cases; advisory |
| ✅ **NIA** (contrast) | **NIA Act, 2008** | **Terror** and specified scheduled offences |

- **Analytical use:** the 2026 objective demand turns on matching a body to its **exact statute** — the traps are calling the CBI "statutory," equating the ED's PMLA/FEMA base with the CBI's DSPE base, or missing that the **SFIO is statutory (Companies Act 2013)** while the **FIU is an executive creation** operating under the PMLA.

### 5.8 Institutional incentives, mechanism and consequences

- ⚠️ **The "caged parrot" (Supreme Court, 2013, Coalgate)** has institutional causes, not merely political ones: no own statute (it runs on the 1946 DSPE Act), executive control over the CBI's administration, funding and officer postings, the requirement of **government sanction** to prosecute, and superintendence by the government itself for non-corruption cases.
- ⚠️ **The consent regime** gives State governments a lever that can shield or expose officials depending on which party controls the State and the Centre — the same instrument reads as "federal safeguard" or "obstruction of justice" with the political alignment.
- ⚠️ **Multiplicity (CVC, CBI, Lokpal, ED, SFIO, FIU)** creates overlap and turf friction; a corruption case can trigger parallel CBI, ED and CVC action, which is why coordination and a clear CBI charter are recurrent reform demands.

### 5.9 Criticism, counter-argument, variation and implementation constraints

- **Autonomy critique:** ⚠️ the CBI's lack of a statute and its administrative dependence on the executive are the structural roots of the "caged parrot" charge; the CVC's non-binding advice similarly limits vigilance.
- **Counter:** ⚠️ post-*Vineet Narain* insulation (fixed Director tenure, committee appointment, *Common Cause* protection against arbitrary divestiture) and judicial oversight of CBI probes show real, if incomplete, safeguards.
- **Federal variation:** ⚠️ withdrawal of general consent is concentrated in States governed by parties opposed to the Union, so the "federalism vs central over-reach" debate is alignment-dependent, not constant.
- **Implementation constraints:** ⚠️ **Section 17A of the PC Act (2018)** now requires **prior approval** before investigating decisions taken in official functions, adding a consent-like hurdle to corruption probes; reform proposals for a dedicated **CBI Act** (2nd ARC, parliamentary panels) remain **proposals, not law**.
- **Multiplicity vs coordination:** ⚠️ a single corruption matter can trigger **parallel CVC, CBI and ED** action (plus the Lokpal's superintendence over referred cases), so the debate is "synergy or turf war?" — the coordinating design is that the **CVC superintends the CBI for PC-Act cases** and the **Lokpal directs referred investigations**, but overlapping mandates and separate statutes leave genuine friction that a unified charter would reduce.

### 5.10 Verdict scaffolds

- **Consent/federalism verdict:** "State consent under Section 6 of the DSPE Act is a real federal feature, but *WB v. CPDR* (2010) keeps the balance by letting constitutional courts order a CBI probe without consent — so withdrawal limits the executive, not the judiciary."
- **Autonomy verdict:** "The CBI is a caged parrot by construction, not accident: an agency without its own statute, dependent on the executive for administration and sanction — a dedicated CBI Act is the structural fix."
- **CVC verdict:** "Statutory status and a fixed tenure gave the CVC independence but not authority; advice that binds no one and superintendence limited to corruption cases cap what a vigilance apex can achieve."

### 5.12 Factual-risk and current-status controls

- ✅ **CVC = statutory (2003); CBI = not statutory (DSPE Act, 1946).** Get this contrast right — it is the commonest trap.
- ✅ The **DSPE Amendment Act, 2021** allows CBI/ED Director extensions **up to five years via annual extensions** — the base minimum tenure is still **2 years**; do not state a flat 5-year term.
- ✅ Superintendence over the CBI is **split**: CVC for PC-Act 1988 offences, **Central Government** otherwise.
- ✅ ***WB v. CPDR* (2010)** lets constitutional courts order a CBI probe **without State consent** — do not say State consent is absolute.
- ⚠️ ***Navendra Kumar* (2013, Gauhati HC)** quashed the **1963 resolution**, not the DSPE Act; the **SC stayed it** — never cite it as a final holding that the CBI is illegal.
- ⚠️ Do not fabricate the list/number of States that have withdrawn general consent; it changes over time.
- 📰 **PMLA / *Vijay Madanlal Choudhary* — status controls, dated 13 August 2026.** ✅ The **27 July 2022** judgment **upheld** the ED's PMLA powers of **search/seizure (s.17), survey (s.16), arrest (s.19), attachment (s.5)** and the **s.3** offence definition; held the **ECIR is an internal document** not required to be supplied (grounds of arrest suffice); **upheld the re-enacted s.45 twin conditions** for bail; and held **ED officers are not police officers** for the purpose of **s.50** statements. ⚠️ Keep the two rounds of s.45 apart — an earlier version was struck down in ***Nikesh Tarachand Shah* (2017)** and Parliament re-enacted it. 📰 A **review petition** (*Karti P. Chidambaram v. Directorate of Enforcement*) is **pending and undecided**, with the Court taking **maintainability** first; the 2022 holding therefore **remains good law while under review**. **Prohibited:** "overruled", "diluted", "stayed", "referred to a larger bench" as decided facts; predicting the review outcome; asserting the ECIR must now be supplied; quoting conviction rates, attachment values, case counts or FATF-rating language without a dated official source. Re-verify on the Supreme Court portal.
- 📰 **Section 17A of the PC Act (2018)** requires prior approval to investigate official-function decisions; ⚠️ its constitutionality is under judicial scrutiny (a Supreme Court split verdict was referred to a larger bench, 2026) — state the requirement, flag the challenge, do not pre-judge it.
- ✅ The CBI Director's selection committee (**PM + LoP/largest-opposition leader + CJI/SC judge**) is under the **Lokpal Act, 2013**; do not confuse it with the CVC's committee (**PM + Home Minister + LoP**).
