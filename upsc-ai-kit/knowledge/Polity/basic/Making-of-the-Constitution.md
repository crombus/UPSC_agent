## 2. Core idea

✅ The Constituent Assembly (CA) framed the Constitution. It was **indirectly elected** (by provincial
assemblies), **partly nominated** (princely states), and dominated by the **INC**. It took
**2 years, 11 months, 18 days** across **11 sessions**.

## 4. Objectives Resolution

✅ Moved by **Nehru (13 Dec 1946)**, adopted **22 Jan 1947**. It laid down the philosophy of the
Constitution — its **modified form became the Preamble**.

## 5. Drafting Committee (7 members)

✅ Original committee: **Ambedkar (Chair)**, N. Gopalaswamy Ayyangar, Alladi
Krishnaswamy Ayyar, K.M. Munshi, Syed Mohammad Saadullah, **B.L. Mitter** and
**D.P. Khaitan**. N. Madhava Rau replaced Mitter; T.T. Krishnamachari replaced Khaitan.
The committee sat for **141 days**.

## 6. Key dates

- ✅ **9 Dec 1946** — first meeting.
- ✅ **26 Nov 1949** — Constitution **adopted** (Preamble + 395 Articles + 8 Schedules).
- ✅ **26 Jan 1950** — Constitution **commenced** (chosen to honour **Purna Swaraj Day, 1930**).

> 🔑 Remember: **Adopted 26 Nov 1949 · Enforced 26 Jan 1950.** Don't merge the two dates!

### 9.2 Qualified thesis options

- *The Constituent Assembly was not democratically elected on universal franchise, yet it was substantively representative and, after the Independence Act 1947, fully sovereign — so its legitimacy rests on inclusive deliberation rather than on an electoral mandate it could not have had in 1946.*
- *The charge that the Assembly was a "Congress body" is true in arithmetic and misleading in substance: the Congress was itself an ideological coalition that deliberately co-opted its ablest critics, from Ambedkar to Ayyar.*
- *The Objectives Resolution is the hinge of Indian constitutionalism: moved in December 1946 and surviving, in modified form, as the Preamble, it fixed the values against which every later provision was drafted.*
- *The Assembly's "slowness" was the price of thoroughness — nearly three years, eleven sessions and the study of some sixty constitutions produced a document that has outlasted most of its contemporaries.*

### 9.4 Bank A — Composition and process (the factual base)

- **Claim:** the Assembly was constituted, not popularly elected. **Evidence:** ✅ formed under the **Cabinet Mission Plan (1946)**; total strength **389** = **296 British India** (elected by provincial assemblies via **proportional representation / single transferable vote**) + **93 princely states** (nominated by rulers); ~**1 seat per million**. **Significance:** ⚠️ it was therefore **indirectly elected and partly nominated**, on a restricted provincial franchise. **Caution:** ✅ strength fell to **299** after partition (League members from Pakistan areas withdrew).
- **Claim:** the 1946 result gave the Congress a dominant but not monolithic bloc. **Evidence:** ✅ of 296 seats — **INC 208, Muslim League 73, others 15**. **Significance:** ⚠️ Congress predominance is real, but 88 non-Congress seats and co-opted experts diluted single-party control. **Caution:** ✅ the League **boycotted** the first meeting (only 211 attended on 9 Dec 1946).
- **Claim:** the framing was done through specialised committees, not a single drafter. **Evidence (committee → chair):**

| Committee | Chair |
|---|---|
| ✅ Union Powers Committee | Nehru |
| ✅ Union Constitution Committee | Nehru |
| ✅ States Committee | Nehru |
| ✅ Provincial Constitution Committee | Sardar Patel |
| ✅ Advisory Committee on FR, Minorities & Tribal Areas | Sardar Patel |
| ✅ Drafting Committee | **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** |
| ✅ Rules of Procedure Committee | Rajendra Prasad |
| ✅ Steering Committee | Rajendra Prasad |

- ⚠️ Among the **eight major committees listed above**, the chair count is **Nehru 3, Patel 2,
  Rajendra Prasad 2 and Ambedkar 1**. Do not inflate Prasad's major-committee count by mixing in
  other committees. **Drafting Committee = 7 members**, sat **141 days**.

### 9.5 Bank B — Criticisms with replies (the core Mains device)

| Criticism | Kernel conceded | Reply (deploy this) |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ **Not a representative body** — no direct election on adult franchise | ⚠️ true in form | ⚠️ adult-franchise election was impossible in 1946 (no rolls; partition flux); the Assembly still seated Hindus, Muslims, Sikhs, Parsis, Anglo-Indians, Christians, SCs, STs and women |
| ✅ **Not a sovereign body** — a British creation | ⚠️ true at origin | ✅ the **Indian Independence Act 1947** made it **fully sovereign** — free to frame any constitution and repeal British Acts |
| ✅ **Congress-dominated** (Austin: "the Assembly was the Congress and the Congress was India") | ⚠️ arithmetically true | ⚠️ the Congress contained substantial ideological and provincial diversity; the Assembly also included non-Congress figures such as Ambedkar and Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar. Do not misclassify Congress member K.M. Munshi as a non-Congress co-option |
| ✅ **Lawyer-politician dominated** | ⚠️ true of the drafting core | ⚠️ legal precision produced a workable text; minorities, women and labour were represented on the advisory committees |
| ✅ **Time-consuming** (Naziruddin Ahmed: "Drifting Committee"; US took ~4 months) | ⚠️ longer than some | ✅ it studied ~**60 constitutions**, debated the draft **114 days** across **11 sessions** over **2 years, 11 months, 18 days** — thoroughness, not drift |
| ✅ **Hindu-dominated** (Simon: "a body of Hindus"; Churchill: "only one major community") | ⚠️ post-partition arithmetic | ⚠️ the League boycott and Partition sharply reduced Muslim participation, while the Assembly still contained minority voices; this mitigates but does not erase the representational deficit |

### 9.6 Bank C — Objectives Resolution and the value base

- **Claim:** the Resolution fixed the Constitution's philosophy before its clauses were drafted. **Evidence:** ✅ moved by **Nehru on 13 December 1946**, adopted **22 January 1947**; declared India an **independent sovereign republic**, a Union of autonomous units, with power derived from the **people**, justice/equality/freedom for all, and **safeguards for minorities and tribal/backward areas**. **Significance:** ✅ its **modified form became the Preamble**. **Caution:** ✅ it was moved by **Nehru**, not Ambedkar; the Preamble was enacted **last**.

### 9.6A Bank D — Constituent Assembly Debates as an interpretive source (the "original intent" demand family)

> This is a separate demand family from "how the Assembly worked". It is what a question on
> **originalism versus the living Constitution**, on **why the Court cites the framers**, or on
> **"the Constitution as a transformative document"** is actually testing.

| Claim | Named anchor | Mechanism / significance | Limitation / caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| The debates are an aid to construction, not a source of law | ⚠️ Settled judicial practice: courts treat the Constituent Assembly Debates as an **external aid** used when the constitutional text is **ambiguous** | Lets a court recover the purpose behind a provision — for example, why "procedure established by law" was preferred to "due process", or why the Preamble's wording was framed as it was | ⚠️ The **enacted text prevails** over anything a member said; a speech, however eminent, cannot enlarge or cut down a clause. Do **not** write that the debates are "binding" |
| Framers' intent has been used **for** and **against** expansive readings | ✅ The **due-process** history is the standard illustration: the framers consciously adopted the narrower phrase, yet ✅ ***Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India* (1978)** read Art 21 to require a procedure that is fair, just and reasonable | Demonstrates the central tension: the text was read to grow **beyond** the drafting choice | ⚠️ Present this as a **doctrinal evolution**, not as a court "overruling the framers" |
| The basic-structure doctrine sits uneasily with strict originalism | ✅ **Kesavananda Bharati (1973)** derived implied limits on amending power that Art 368 does not spell out | Shows the Indian Constitution is treated as a **living instrument** whose identity, not merely its words, is protected | ⚠️ The doctrine's critics call it judicially created; state the objection rather than concealing it |
| Ambedkar's own position bounds the argument on both sides | ⚠️ He defended the text as a **workable** framework rather than a perfect one, and stressed that a constitution's working depends on those who operate it | Gives a balanced closing line for essays on constitutional morality or on constitutional working versus constitutional text | ⚠️ Do **not** place quotation marks around any sentence attributed to a framer unless the exact wording is verified — paraphrase instead |

- ⚠️ **Answer use:** deploy this when a question asks *why* a provision reads as it does. Naming the debate as an **interpretive aid with a subordinate status** is a discriminating point that separates a good answer from a narrative one.

### 9.7 Mechanism, incentives and consequences

- ⚠️ **Deliberative mechanism:** provisions were pre-negotiated in expert committees (§9.4) and then debated clause-by-clause, which produced a technically precise text but lengthened the process to nearly three years.
- ⚠️ **Sovereignty upgrade as incentive:** the **Independence Act 1947** removed the British veto, so after August 1947 the Assembly's incentive shifted from bargaining with the Crown to internal consensus-building — which is why the League's exit did not stall the work.
- ⚠️ **Consequence:** because power was "derived from the people" yet the body was **indirectly elected**, the framers compensated by writing **universal adult franchise** into the Constitution itself — the legitimacy the Assembly lacked was built into the polity it created.

### 9.8 Counter-argument, variation and constraints

- ⚠️ **Variation within the "Congress" bloc:** the Congress majority spanned socialists, Gandhians, liberals and conservatives, so "one-party domination" understates the ideological spread that had to be reconciled.
- ⚠️ **Minority/tribal representation:** advisory committees on minorities and on tribal/excluded areas (Patel; Bardoloi; Thakkar) gave non-majority voices a structured channel, tempering the "Hindu-dominated" charge.
- ⚠️ **Implementation constraint:** the restricted colonial franchise, lack of universal electoral
  rolls, impending Partition and communal violence made a fresh adult-franchise election
  administratively and politically difficult. State this as a constraint, not as proof that direct
  election was literally impossible.

### 9.9 Verdict scaffolds

- **Representativeness verdict:** "Judged by the electoral test the Assembly fails, but judged by the test of inclusive, reasoned deliberation — the only test open to it in 1946 — it succeeds, and the Independence Act supplied the sovereignty its origin lacked."
- **Legitimacy verdict:** "A body that could not be universally elected earned its authority by universally applicable reasoning, which is why its Constitution commanded consent well beyond the Congress."
- **Process verdict:** "The framing was neither a copy nor an improvisation: sixty constitutions were ransacked, but the synthesis — universal franchise on a plural society — was India's own."

### 9.10 Prelims close-option distinctions (answers the routed objective demands)

- **Routed 2026 Prelims GS-I Q55 — title, repeal, and commencement date:** ✅ **Article 393 (Short title)** — "This Constitution may be called the Constitution of India"; ✅ **Article 394 (Commencement)** — brought certain articles into force **at once** on adoption (26 Nov 1949) and the **remaining provisions on 26 January 1950** (the "date of commencement"); ✅ **Article 395 (Repeals)** — repealed the **Indian Independence Act 1947** and the **Government of India Act 1935** (with amending Acts). *Distinction:* **26 November 1949 = adoption/enactment; 26 January 1950 = commencement.*
- **Routed 2024 Prelims GS-I Q61 — provisional (temporary) President of the CA:** ✅ **Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha** (oldest member, elected on the French practice), **not** Dr. Rajendra Prasad, who was the (permanent) President.
- **Routed 2021 Prelims GS-I Q93 — constitutional status of India on Republic Day 1950:** ✅ on **26 January 1950** the Constitution commenced and India became a **sovereign democratic republic** (an **elected** head of state, ceasing to be a Dominion); ✅ the Constituent Assembly had ratified **continued Commonwealth membership in May 1949**; ⚠️ "socialist" and "secular" were **not** yet in the Preamble (added only by the 42nd Amendment, 1976).
- **Routed 2023 Prelims GS-I Q85 — Constitution Day (26 November) and CA history:** ✅ **26 November 1949 = the date the Constitution was adopted**; 📰 the Government of India declared **26 November as Constitution Day (Samvidhan Divas) in 2015**.
- **Standard confusion pairs:** ✅ adopted **26 Nov 1949** vs commenced **26 Jan 1950**; ✅ idea of a CA first floated by **M.N. Roy (1934)**; ✅ Objectives Resolution moved by **Nehru**; ✅ **Gandhi was not** a member; ✅ Drafting Committee had **7** members; ✅ the Preamble was enacted **last**.

### 9.11 Factual-risk and current-status controls

- Do **not** merge the two dates — ✅ **adopted 26 Nov 1949, commenced 26 Jan 1950**.
- Do **not** say the whole Constitution came into force on 26 Nov 1949 — ✅ only certain articles (citizenship, elections, provisional parliament and the like) did; the **rest on 26 Jan 1950**.
- Do **not** say the Constituent Assembly was directly elected on adult franchise — ✅ it was **indirectly elected and partly nominated**.
- Do **not** state that Article 395 repealed the **Abolition of Privy Council Jurisdiction Act 1949** — ✅ that Act **continued**; Art 395 repealed the **1947 and 1935 Acts**.
- Do **not** attribute "socialist/secular" to the 1949/1950 Preamble — ✅ added by the **42nd Amendment, 1976**.
- 📰 **Constitution Day** has been observed since **2015** (Government of India declaration); state it as a 2015 designation, not a constitutional provision.
