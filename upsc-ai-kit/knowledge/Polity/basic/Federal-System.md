## Core idea
✅ Powers are **divided by the Constitution itself** between Centre & states (federal), but the balance
is deliberately **tilted toward the Union**. Hence India is a **constitutionally federal,
Union-weighted** system; “quasi-federal” is a scholarly characterisation, not a substitute for the
constitutional test or *Bommai*.

## 8. Answer architecture (10/15/20-mark support)

> Purpose: make this Core file independently able to answer directive-sensitive GS-II questions on the nature of Indian federalism, its centralising design, the cooperation/competition/confrontation dynamic and asymmetric federalism — without opening Advanced.

### 8.0 Direct Mains demands owned by this Core file

- **2020 · GS-II · Q3** — Demand (neutral rendering): *how cooperation, competition and confrontation have shaped the Indian federation.* Directive: **How far do you think** (extent/evaluation) · **10 marks · 150 words**.
  - **Core routing supersedes the older `advanced/12_Federal-System.md` pointer for this demand.**
  - **Executable route:** (1) **Thesis** — Indian federalism is *dynamic and bargaining* (Morris-Jones), oscillating between the three modes by issue and political alignment; cooperation dominates institutionally while confrontation is rising. (2) **Cooperation** — GST Council (**Art 279A**), Inter-State Council (**Art 263**), Finance Commission transfers, disaster/pandemic coordination. (3) **Competition** — NITI Aayog indices (SDG India Index, Aspirational Districts), inter-State investment competition, Ease-of-Doing-Business rankings. (4) **Confrontation** — Governors' delay in assenting to Bills, withdrawal of "general consent" to the CBI, GST-compensation and cess/surcharge disputes, southern-State grievances over the devolution formula. (5) **Frame** — *S.R. Bommai* (1994): federalism is a basic feature, so conflict is bounded by constitutionalism. (6) **Verdict** — India is cooperative/bargaining federalism punctuated by episodic confrontation; its strength is institutional channels that absorb conflict.
- **2022 · GS-II · Q13** — Demand (neutral rendering): *national parties favour centralisation while regional parties favour State autonomy.* Directive: **Comment** · **15 marks · 250 words**. Shared with `basic/Political-Parties.md`; **this file owns the federal half** (the party-system half belongs there).
  - **Core routing supersedes the older `advanced/12_Federal-System.md` pointer for this demand.**
  - **Executable route (federal half):** (1) **Thesis** — the correlation is broadly valid but tracks *incentives and position in the power structure*, not party ideology, and reverses when a party moves between office and opposition. (2) **Centralising tools a Union-holding national party leans on** — the 14 Union-bias features: residuary power, **All-India Services**, Article 249, the centrally appointed Governor (Articles 155-156) and historically Article 356. (3) **Autonomy demands of regional parties** — fairer tax devolution, neutral gubernatorial conduct, fewer conditionalities in centrally sponsored schemes, Sarkaria/Punchhi reforms and regular Inter-State Council consultation. (4) **Counter-evidence** — the coalition era (1989–2014) saw national parties *strengthen* States; regional parties centralise within their own States; national parties demand autonomy where they sit in opposition. (5) **Verdict** — preference follows the incentive of who controls the Union; qualified agreement, not a law of party behaviour.

### 8.2 Qualified thesis options

- *India is best described not as "federal" or "unitary" but as a centralised federation whose balance shifts with circumstance — federal in form, unitary in emergency, cooperative in finance and confrontational at the political margins.*
- *The cooperation–competition–confrontation triad is not a defect but the normal operating mode of a bargaining federation; Indian federalism works by channelling conflict through institutions, not by eliminating it.*
- *Party preferences on centralisation track the incentive of who holds the Union, not party ideology, which is why the same party centralises in office and demands autonomy in opposition.*
- *Since* S.R. Bommai *(1994), federalism is a basic feature, so centralisation now operates within a judicially enforced floor of State autonomy rather than at the Centre's untrammelled discretion.*
- *Asymmetric federalism is India's constitutional answer to diversity: uniform citizenship coexists with graduated autonomy, which strengthens rather than weakens the Union.*

### 8.4A Administrative and financial distribution — Core bridge

| Cluster | Provisions | Exam-safe rule |
|---|---|---|
| Compliance and directions | **Arts 256-257** | State administration must comply with applicable parliamentary law and not impede Union power; directions are constitutionally bounded, not a general takeover |
| Reciprocal delegation | **Arts 258-258A** | Union functions may be entrusted to States with consent, and State functions to the Union with consent; legislative ownership does not automatically move |
| Legal unity and consultation | **Arts 261-263** | Full faith and credit; Parliament may create water-dispute adjudication and exclude jurisdiction by law; the President may establish a recommendatory Inter-State Council |
| Union duty and emergency remedy | **Arts 355-357** | Article 355 is a duty; Article 356 requires constitutional breakdown and remains reviewable under *Bommai*; Article 357 concerns legislative power during the proclamation |
| Revenue and grants | **Arts 268-281** | Assignment, divisible-pool distribution, grants, GST apportionment and Finance Commission recommendations structure fiscal interdependence |
| State borrowing | **Art 293** | Union consent may be required where the specified Union debt condition applies |

> **Core line:** legislative autonomy needs administrative machinery and predictable fiscal
> capacity; Indian federalism distributes all three, while creating coordination routes between
> them.

### 8.6 Bank C — The three modes with named Indian instances

| Mode | What it means | Named Indian instances |
|---|---|---|
| ✅/⚠️ **Cooperative** | Centre and States act through joint institutions | **GST Council (Art 279A)**, **Inter-State Council (Art 263)**, Finance Commission transfers, disaster/pandemic coordination, Zonal Councils |
| ✅/⚠️ **Competitive** | States compete for capital and performance | NITI Aayog's **SDG India Index** and **Aspirational Districts Programme**, inter-State investment competition, ranking-driven reform |
| ✅/⚠️ **Confrontational / coercive** | Institutional friction and central pressure | Governors' delay in assenting to Bills, withdrawal of **"general consent" to the CBI** under the DSPE Act, **GST-compensation and cess/surcharge** disputes, southern-State grievances over the devolution formula, inter-State river-water disputes (e.g., Cauvery) |

- **Balance rule:** ⚠️ the three modes coexist rather than succeed one another; the strongest answers name at least one instance of each and then judge which dominates.

### 8.7 Bank D — Asymmetric federalism

- **Definition:** ✅ different States/units enjoy **different degrees of autonomy** within one Union — India's constitutional response to diversity.
- **Constitutional instances:** ✅ the **Art 371 to 371-J** special provisions (e.g., 371A Nagaland, 371G Mizoram, 371D–F Andhra/Telangana and Sikkim), the **Fifth and Sixth Schedules** for tribal areas, **Art 239AA** for Delhi, and Union-Territory vs State asymmetry.
- **Status caution:** ✅ **Art 370** (special status for J&K) was rendered inoperative in **2019** and J&K reorganised into two Union Territories — do not cite it as live. ⚠️ "**Special category status**" is an **administrative/Finance-Commission-era** concept, **not** a constitutional category.

### 8.7A Bank D2 — **Representational federalism**: the seat freeze and the 2026 delimitation attempt

> ⚠️ **Label first:** the 2026 package is a **defeated Bill** — evidence of a federal *argument*, never
> law, precedent or forecast. Full evidence unit: `Parliament.md` §7.4; the Art 368 arithmetic:
> `Amendment-and-Basic-Structure.md` §8.4.

Indian federalism is usually taught as **legislative, administrative and fiscal**. There is a fourth
dimension the syllabus rewards and most answers miss: **how many seats each State holds in the House
that makes and unmakes the Union government.**

| Claim | Named provision / event | Mechanism / significance | Limitation / caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Lok Sabha is designed to track population, and India has **no equal-representation upper house to offset it** | ✅ **Art 81(2)(a)** (seat–population ratio uniform across States, so far as practicable); ✅ **Art 80** + **Fourth Schedule** allot Rajya Sabha seats by **population, not equally** | Unlike the US Senate or the Australian Senate, India's second chamber **amplifies** rather than corrects population weight — so representation is a **single-axis** federal question | ⚠️ Do not describe the Rajya Sabha as an "equal-representation" chamber; do not import US Senate logic into India |
| Population representation has been **frozen since 1976** as an explicit federal bargain | ✅ **42nd Amendment (1976)** froze allocation on the **1971 census**; ✅ **84th Amendment (2001)** extended the freeze to after the first census taken after **2026** while allowing boundary readjustment; ✅ **87th Amendment (2003)** shifted boundary readjustment to the **2001** census | The bargain: States that reduced fertility would not lose national political weight for having done so — a rare instance of the Constitution **paying for a policy outcome in seats** | ⚠️ The freeze is on **allocation between States**, not on redrawing boundaries within a State — keep the two apart |
| The freeze was tested and the attempt failed | ✅ **Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 2026** + **Delimitation Bill, 2026** + **UT Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026**, introduced **16 April 2026**; the **131st Amendment Bill** carried the **Art 81** ceiling change — **550 → 850** (up to **815** States + **35** UTs) — with the accompanying Bill using the latest published census; ✅ **negatived 17 April 2026, 298 : 230** of **528** present and voting — below two-thirds under **Art 368(2)**; the linked Bills were withdrawn | Shows that representational change requires a broad parliamentary bargain. Article 329(a) restricts ordinary judicial challenge to delimitation laws, while constitutional amendments remain subject to the Constitution's amendment framework | ⚠️ Defeated Bill. Cite **850** only as the **131st Amendment Bill's** text, never as law; **prohibited** — State-wise projections, projected women's-seat counts, any implementation year, or treating the defeat as precedent |
| 📰 The trigger census is now **scheduled**, so the question returns on a known timetable | ✅ **Census 2027** under the **Census Act, 1948** / **Census Rules, 1990**: **Houselisting & Housing Census 1 April – 30 September 2026** (each State/UT choosing a fixed **30-day** period inside the window); **Population Enumeration February 2027**, and **September 2026** for snow-bound areas; **reference dates 00:00 hrs 1 March 2027** generally and **00:00 hrs 1 October 2026** for snow-bound areas; India's **first fully digital census** with self-enumeration, and **caste enumeration** officially announced. ✅ Because the **106th Amendment commenced on 16 April 2026** (⚠️ commencement only — the reservation is **still not operational**), this is simultaneously the **"first census taken after 2026"** for the **84th Amendment / Art 82** freeze and the **"first census taken after the commencement"** for **Art 334A(1)** | One statutory exercise now carries **two** constitutional triggers — the end of the seat freeze and the switching-on of the women's quota. That is why representational federalism is no longer a hypothetical dispute: the fact that made it postponable is expiring | ⚠️ **Notified conduct and reference dates are NOT the publication date of final figures**, and both Art 82 and Art 334A(1) turn on **publication**. **Prohibited:** any publication date, any delimitation date, any date/year/election for the women's reservation, any nationwide houselisting progress claim as on **13 August 2026** (the schedule is State/UT-specific, so no exact nationwide wording is safe), and any State-wise seat projection |
| The objection is structural, not merely regional politics | ⚠️ States with strong demographic performance face a **compounding** loss: fewer relative Lok Sabha seats, a population-weighted Rajya Sabha, and **population as a criterion in the Finance Commission's horizontal formula** as well | Makes the point that representation and devolution are **the same argument in two currencies** — the strongest available 20-mark framing | ⚠️ For the Finance Commission's criteria and their weights use `Finance-Commission.md`; do not quote weights from memory here |
| The counter-argument must be stated for balance | ⚠️ Permanent malapportionment means a citizen of a populous State has **systematically less voting weight** than a citizen of a less populous one — a democratic-equality objection of the same constitutional rank as the federal one | Converts a one-sided "South vs North" narrative into a genuine **two-value conflict** (federal equity vs democratic equality), which is what "Critically examine" rewards | ⚠️ Do not assign numbers to the disparity; argue the principle |

- **Verdict scaffold (representational federalism):** ⚠️ *"India's unresolved federal question is no longer only fiscal — it is representational. The 1976 freeze bought fifty years of peace by suspending the population rule; the 2026 attempt to end it was defeated on the Article 368 arithmetic, not on the merits, which means the question is postponed rather than settled. Any durable answer must pair reapportionment with a compensating federal safeguard, because India has no equal-representation second chamber to absorb the shock."*

### 8.9 Criticism, counter-argument and variation

- **Over-centralisation critique:** ⚠️ the 14 unitary features, the Centre's financial dominance (Santhanam) and the Governor's office let the Union crowd the State sphere; the **Sarkaria and Punchhi Commissions** recommended sparing use of Art 356 and Art 356/consultative federalism (developed in `Centre-State-Relations.md`).
- **Counter (federalism is resilient):** ⚠️ *Bommai*'s basic-feature holding, the rise of coalition and regional politics, GST's shared decision-making, and Finance Commission devolution show a real bargaining space, not a facade.
- **State/regional variation:** ⚠️ southern and eastern States press hardest on fiscal devolution and the Governor's role, while the direction of central pressure shifts with which party holds the Union — federal stress is **political-alignment-dependent**, not constant.
- **Implementation constraint:** ⚠️ cooperative institutions under-perform when under-convened — the **Inter-State Council has met rarely**, so cooperative federalism is stronger on paper than in frequency of use.

### 8.10 Verdict scaffolds

- **Nature verdict:** "India is a centralised federation — quasi-federal in Wheare's form, cooperative in Austin's spirit, and *sui generis* in Alexandrowicz's judgement — best captured as bargaining federalism operating under a *Bommai* floor of State autonomy."
- **Three-modes verdict:** "Cooperation, competition and confrontation are not phases but simultaneous registers; the health of Indian federalism lies in institutions strong enough to keep confrontation constitutional."
- **Centralisation verdict:** "Party preferences on centralisation are a function of who holds the Union, not of ideology, so the real safeguard of autonomy is institutional — the Finance Commission, the Inter-State Council and judicial review — not partisan intent."
