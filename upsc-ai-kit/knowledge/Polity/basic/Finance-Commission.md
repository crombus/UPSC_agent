## Core idea
The Finance Commission is the **"balancing wheel of fiscal federalism"** — it recommends how central taxes are
shared between the **Centre and states (vertical)** and **among states (horizontal)**, plus grants-in-aid. Its
advice is advisory but rarely rejected.

> 🔑 **Composition = 1 + 4** (chairman + 4 members) — **reappointment allowed** (unlike UPSC/EC).
> 🔑 **Vertical** = Centre↔states split · **Horizontal** = state↔state split.

## Must-Know Facts
- ✅ Chairman = experience in **public affairs**; members from **HC judge / finance-accounts / administration /
  economics**.
- ✅ Recommends **tax devolution, grants-in-aid, and measures for panchayat/municipality funds** (via SFC).
- ✅ **"Net proceeds"** = tax minus cost of collection, certified by the **CAG**.
- ✅ Report → President → both Houses with **action-taken memo**.
- ✅ **Planning Commission** (which undermined the FC) replaced by **NITI Aayog in 2015**.

## 📰 Current link
📰 **16th FC (Arvind Panagariya)** — award period **2026–31**, effective **1 April 2026**; **states' share
retained at 41%**; new **"Contribution to GDP" (10%)** criterion added to horizontal devolution. Cess/surcharge
shrinking the divisible pool remains a live states'-grievance debate.

### 5.0 Direct Mains demands owned by this Core file

- **2018 · GS-II · Q14** — Demand (neutral rendering): *the constitution and terms of reference of the Finance Commission.* Directive: **Discuss** · **15 marks · 250 words**.
  - **Core routing supersedes the older `advanced/29_Finance-Commission.md` pointer for this demand.**
  - **Executable route:** (1) **Frame** — the FC is the Constitution's periodic, expert arbiter of fiscal federalism, the **"balancing wheel"**. (2) **Constitution** — **Art 280**: the **President** constitutes it every **fifth year or earlier**; **Chairman + 4 members**; **qualifications are prescribed by Parliament** (Chairman — public affairs; the four members from a High Court judge/qualified, specialised knowledge of government finance and accounts, wide experience in financial matters and administration, and special knowledge of economics); members are **eligible for reappointment**; Laxmikant calls the role **quasi-judicial**. (3) **Terms of reference (Art 280(3))** — the **vertical** distribution of the net proceeds of taxes (**Art 270**) between the Union and the States; the **horizontal** allocation among the States; the principles governing **grants-in-aid** (**Art 275**); measures to augment a State's Consolidated Fund to supplement **panchayat and municipality** resources on the **State Finance Commission's** advice (**Arts 243-I/243-Y**); and any other matter referred **in the interests of sound finance**. (4) **Report and effect** — **Art 281**: the President lays every recommendation, with an **explanatory action-taken memorandum**, before both Houses; the advice is **advisory, not binding**, but "should not be turned down without very compelling reasons" (P. V. Rajamannar). (5) **Analytical edge** — the ToR themselves have become contested where the Union inserts population-base or fiscal-discipline conditions. (6) **Verdict** — the FC's constitution guarantees an independent, expert, periodic settlement; the live question is Union influence over its **terms of reference**.
- **2021 · GS-II · Q3** — Demand (neutral rendering): *how the Fourteenth Finance Commission's recommendations enabled the States' fiscal position.* Directive: **How have they enabled** (extent/evaluation) · **10 marks · 150 words**.
  - **Core routing supersedes the older `advanced/29_Finance-Commission.md` pointer for this demand.**
  - **Executable route:** (1) **Thesis** — the 14th FC marked the largest shift toward **untied, formula-based** devolution, enlarging the States' fiscal autonomy. (2) **Enabling changes** — a **substantial increase in the States' share of the divisible pool** (⚠️ the exact percentage is **not stated in the local files** — assert the direction, not a figure); transfers routed as **tax devolution** rather than tied grants; the **plan/non-plan distinction ended**; State spending priorities respected by avoiding sector-specific conditions. (3) **Institutional context** — it coincided with the replacement of the **Planning Commission by NITI Aayog (2015)**, which cut discretionary central plan transfers and restored the FC's primacy. (4) **Limits on the gain** — rising **cess and surcharge outside the divisible pool** eroded it, and centrally sponsored scheme conditionalities under **Art 282** persisted. (5) **Verdict** — the 14th FC enabled **autonomy and predictability**, but divisible-pool erosion and Art 282 conditionalities capped the real fiscal-space gain.

### 5.2 Qualified thesis options

- *The Finance Commission is the "balancing wheel" of fiscal federalism, but a wheel the Union increasingly steers through the terms of reference it writes and the cesses it keeps outside the divisible pool.*
- *Devolution is where equity and efficiency collide: income-distance and population criteria reward need, while demographic-performance and GDP-contribution criteria reward outcomes — and no weighting satisfies both the poorer and the richer States.*
- *The FC's recommendations are only advisory, yet their near-invariable acceptance makes them the most consequential non-binding advice in Indian governance.*
- *The real erosion of States' fiscal autonomy comes less from the devolution percentage than from the rising share of cess and surcharge, which shrinks the pool the percentage is applied to.*
- *The Finance Commission's advisory status is a feature, not a flaw: an expert, quasi-judicial body that recommends rather than commands preserves elected governments' final fiscal responsibility while making departures publicly accountable under Art 281.*

### 5.5 Bank B — Terms of reference and the grant architecture (Art 280(3), 275, 281, 282)

- **Claim → the FC's mandate is defined by its terms of reference, and grants come in two constitutionally distinct forms.** ✅ **Art 280(3)** lists the ToR (vertical and horizontal devolution, grants-in-aid principles, local-body augmentation, and referred matters). → **Mechanism:** ✅ **Art 275** grants are **statutory/charged** grants-in-aid to States in need, made on the **FC's recommendation** and charged on the Consolidated Fund of India; ✅ **Art 282** grants are **discretionary** — the Union or a State may grant for **any public purpose** even outside its legislative competence. → **Limit / contest:** ⚠️ **Art 282 is contested** because the Union uses it to fund **centrally sponsored schemes** in State-List subjects **bypassing the FC**, expanding central influence over State priorities.
- ✅ **Art 281** closes the loop: the President must lay **every** FC recommendation, with an explanatory action-taken memorandum, before **both Houses** — the transparency mechanism that makes advisory recommendations consequential.

| Grant type | Constitutional source | Trigger / use |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ Statutory grants-in-aid | **Art 275** (charged on the CFI) | On the **FC's recommendation**, to States in need of assistance |
| ✅ Discretionary grants | **Art 282** | Union or State, for **any public purpose** — the route for centrally sponsored schemes |
| ✅ Local-body augmentation | **Art 280(3)(bb)/(c)** | Measures to supplement panchayat/municipality funds via the **SFC** |

### 5.6 Bank C — Vertical and horizontal devolution and contested criteria (Art 270/271)

- **Claim → devolution has two axes, and each is politically contested.** ✅ **Vertical** devolution splits the **net proceeds** of the divisible pool (**Art 270**) between the Union and the States; ✅ **horizontal** devolution then allocates the States' portion **among States** by a formula. → **Mechanism:** the horizontal formula draws on three **criteria families** — **need/equity** (income distance, population, area), **performance/efficiency** (demographic performance, tax effort, contribution to GDP), and **cost-disability** (forest and ecology, area). → **Limit / contest:** ⚠️ the choices are zero-sum between States — **income distance** favours poorer States, **population (2011 vs 1971)** is resented by southern States that curbed growth, **area and forest** reward large/green States, and **GDP-contribution** rewards richer States; equity and efficiency cannot both be maximised.
- **Divisible-pool grievance:** ✅ **cess and surcharge** (levied under **Art 270/271**) are **excluded from the divisible pool** and retained wholly by the Union; ⚠️ their rising share **shrinks the pool** the devolution percentage is applied to — the single most cited States' grievance, independent of the headline share.

| Horizontal criterion | Criterion family | Rationale | Why it is contested |
|---|---|---|---|
| ✅ Income distance | Need / equity | Bridges the gap from the highest-income State | Rewards **poorer** States; richer States gain little |
| ✅ Population (2011) | Need / equity | Larger populations need more resources | ⚠️ Southern States say it **penalises** those who curbed growth |
| ✅ Area | Need / cost-disability | Larger area raises administrative cost | Favours **geographically large** States |
| ✅ Forest & ecology | Cost-disability | Compensates the opportunity cost of green cover | Favours **forested** States |
| ⚠️ Demographic performance | Performance | Rewards States that lowered fertility | Introduced to **offset** the 2011-census shift |
| ⚠️ Tax effort / GDP contribution | Efficiency | Rewards revenue mobilisation / output | Favours **richer, industrialised** States |

**📰 Horizontal devolution criteria — 15th FC vs 16th FC, side by side**

| Criterion | 15th FC (2021–26) | 16th FC (2026–31) | What the change signals |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Income distance** | **45%** | **42.5%** | Still the dominant equity criterion, marginally trimmed |
| **Population (2011)** | **15%** | **17.5%** | Raised — the 2011-census base is now weighted more heavily, sharpening the southern-States objection |
| **Demographic performance** | **12.5%** | **10%** | Reduced — the compensating device for the 2011 shift is **weakened** even as population's weight rises |
| **Area** | **15%** | **10%** | Cut sharply; cost-disability weight falls |
| **Forest and ecology** | **10%** | **10%** | Unchanged in weight (the 16th FC's measure was recast to reflect forest-cover change) |
| **Tax and fiscal effort** | **2.5%** | **dropped (0%)** | The old efficiency criterion is **removed** |
| **Contribution to GDP** | — | **10%** | **New** efficiency criterion rewarding output — the first time a productivity measure of this kind carries this weight |
| **Total** | **100%** | **100%** | — |

- **The analytical point, not just the numbers:** ⚠️ the 16th FC did **two things at once** — it **raised** population weight and **lowered** demographic-performance weight, while **replacing** tax effort with **contribution to GDP**. That combination is why the award is read as **shifting the formula's centre of gravity from need-with-compensation towards need-plus-output**, and it is the strongest evidence-led framing available for "evaluate the horizontal criteria" demands. The counter-argument is that income distance still carries **more than two-fifths** of the formula, so the redistributive core is intact.

### 5.7 Bank D — The third tier and the State Finance Commission link (Art 243-I, 243-Y, 280(3)(bb)/(c))

- **Claim → the national FC reaches local government only indirectly, through the States.** ✅ **Art 243-I** requires the **Governor** to constitute a **State Finance Commission** every five years to review panchayat finances; ✅ **Art 243-Y** extends the same SFC's review to **municipalities**. → **Mechanism:** ✅ under **Art 280(3)(bb) and (c)** the national FC recommends measures to **augment a State's Consolidated Fund** to supplement panchayat and municipality resources **on the basis of the SFC's recommendations**. → **Limit:** ⚠️ many States constitute their SFCs late or ignore their reports, so the constitutional link to the third tier under-performs in practice.

### 5.8 Mechanism, institutional incentives and consequences

- **Why the FC is periodic and advisory rather than permanent and binding:** ⚠️ a five-yearly, expert, quasi-judicial body can recalibrate the fiscal balance to changing needs while leaving final responsibility with elected governments; the cost is that its authority rests on the convention of acceptance, not on compulsion.
- **Why the Planning Commission weakened it and NITI Aayog restored it:** ✅ until 2014 the **Planning Commission** (a non-constitutional, non-statutory body) made large plan transfers and Art 282 grants that **bypassed** the FC; ✅ its replacement by **NITI Aayog (2015)** — a think tank with **no fund-allocation role** — returned formula-based transfers to the FC's centre.
- **Consequence of cess/surcharge growth:** ⚠️ because the devolution share applies only to the **divisible pool**, expanding cess/surcharge lets the Union raise revenue that **never enters** the sharable base — a structural incentive the FC alone cannot cure.
- **Why the pool is now shaped by GST:** ⚠️ since GST subsumed most indirect taxes into a jointly administered levy set by the **GST Council**, the base the FC devolves is increasingly co-determined with the Council — so vertical devolution and GST design have become linked questions rather than a purely Union-set tax base.
- **Why the terms of reference matter so much:** ⚠️ the Union **frames the ToR** and can insert conditions (the population base, fiscal-discipline targets, or the financing of its own schemes), so the FC's independence is real in **deliberation** but bounded by the **questions it is asked** — the contested frontier of its autonomy.

### 5.9 Criticism, counter-argument, variation and implementation constraints

- **Union-tilt critique:** ⚠️ the Union writes the FC's **terms of reference**, keeps cess/surcharge outside the pool, and funds CSS through **Art 282**, so States argue the "balancing wheel" is weighted toward the Centre.
- **Counter (the FC is a genuine arbiter):** ⚠️ its recommendations are near-invariably accepted, the **Art 281** memorandum forces public justification of any departure, and successive commissions have expanded untied devolution.
- **Inter-State variation:** ⚠️ **southern and industrialised** States press against the population base and for GDP/efficiency weight; **poorer and larger** States defend income-distance and area weight — the criteria debate is a standing federal fault line.
- **A recurring reform demand:** ⚠️ several States seek a **constitutional floor** for vertical devolution (and the inclusion of cess/surcharge in the divisible pool) so that the share cannot be hollowed out; the counter-view is that a rigid floor would erode the FC's flexibility to respond to changing needs.
- **Implementation constraint:** ⚠️ the third-tier link (SFCs) and performance conditions depend on **State compliance**, which is uneven, so the FC's local-body and reform intentions are only partly realised.

### 5.10 Verdict scaffolds

- **Balancing-wheel verdict:** "The Finance Commission remains the constitutional balancing wheel of fiscal federalism, but its balance is set as much by the terms of reference it is given and the cesses kept outside the pool as by the formula it writes."
- **Criteria verdict:** "No devolution formula can be neutral: the choice of weights is a choice between rewarding need and rewarding performance, which is why every award reopens the same North–South, rich–poor argument."
- **Autonomy verdict:** "Genuine fiscal autonomy for the States depends less on raising the devolution percentage than on widening the divisible pool it is applied to."
