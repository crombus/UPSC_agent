## Snapshot
| Item | Rajya Sabha | Lok Sabha |
|---|---|---|
| **Nature** | ✅ Permanent (never dissolved) | ✅ 5-yr term, dissolvable |
| **Max strength** | ✅ 250 | ✅ 550 |
| **Stable sanctioned/elected strength** | ✅ 245 sanctioned | ✅ 543 elected seats |
| **Elected by** | State MLAs (indirect, **STV-PR**) | People (direct, **FPTP**, 18 yrs) |
| **Retirement** | ✅ 1/3 every 2 yrs; term 6 yrs | Whole House at dissolution |
| **Nominated** | 12 (by President) | *Anglo-Indian abolished, 104th Amdt* |

## Core idea
✅ **Parliament = President + Rajya Sabha + Lok Sabha (Art 79).** The President isn't a member but is
essential (summons, prorogues, assents). Lok Sabha is the **popular, more powerful** House; Rajya Sabha
is the **permanent, revising** chamber representing states.

> 🔑 **Quorum = 1/10** → **55 LS / 25 RS.**
> 🔑 **Sessions:** Budget · Monsoon · Winter are conventional labels; Article 85 only fixes a maximum
> **six-month interval** between the last sitting of one session and the first sitting of the next.
> 🔑 **Zero Hour** = Indian innovation (since 1962), NOT in the Rules; **Question Hour** IS.

## Money Bill — must remember
✅ **Only in Lok Sabha**, on President's recommendation. Rajya Sabha can **only recommend** (not reject/amend),
must return in **14 days**. Art 110 calls the **Speaker's certificate final**, but
constitutional courts retain narrow review for illegality/constitutional misuse. No joint
sitting exists for money bills.

## Key committees
✅ **Public Accounts Committee (PAC)** — 22 members, chaired by **Opposition**, examines **CAG** reports.
✅ **Estimates Committee** — 30, **Lok Sabha only**. ✅ **Department-related Standing Committees (DRSCs)** —
**17 constituted in 1993**, restructured to **24 with effect from 2004**; **31 members each — 21 Lok Sabha +
10 Rajya Sabha** (executive accountability).

## Must-Know Facts
- ✅ RS Chairman = **Vice-President** (ex-officio); LS Speaker elected by LS.
- ✅ Speaker doesn't vacate office on LS dissolution.
- ✅ Only **3 joint sittings** ever (Dowry 1961, Banking 1978, POTA 2002).
- ✅ **Indian Parliamentary Group (IPG)** = the Indian Parliament's group for inter-parliamentary
  contact; it is India's **National Group of the IPU** and **main branch of the CPA**.
- ✅ Readjustment remains deferred until the relevant figures of the **first census taken after 2026
  have been published** (84th Amdt); it does not restart automatically in 2026. 📰 **Census 2027** is that
  census (Population Enumeration **February 2027**, reference date **1 March 2027**) — but ⚠️ the
  **publication date of its figures is not notified**, so no delimitation date may be stated.

**Exact text of the duration clause, because the derived end-year is not safe.** ✅ **Art 334A(1)** ends:
*"…and shall cease to have effect on the expiration of a period of fifteen years from such commencement."*
⚠️ **Quote the words; do not compute a year.** The referent of *"such commencement"* is contested in the
commentary: read one way it is the **commencement of the 106th Amendment Act** (which would put the
outer limit at 2041 counting from 16 April 2026); read another way it is the point at which the
**reservation provisions come into effect** after delimitation, which is the reading several
commentators prefer because Art 334A(1) is drafted around the coming-into-effect of the reservation.
✅ **334A(2)** — continuance beyond that period is as **Parliament may by law determine**; ✅ **334A(3)** —
**rotation** of reserved seats after each subsequent delimitation. ⚠️ Safe answer form: *"fifteen years
from such commencement, with continuance thereafter as Parliament may by law determine"* — **not**
"until 2041", and **not** "fifteen years from 2026", unless the Act's wording is in front of you and the
question expressly asks you to compute.

### 7.0 Direct Mains demands owned by this Core file

- **2018 · GS-II · Q4** — Demand (neutral rendering): *the usefulness of parliamentary committees, with particular reference to the Estimates Committee.* Directive: **Discuss** · **10 marks · 150 words**.
  - **Core routing supersedes the older `advanced/17_Parliament.md` pointer for this demand.**
  - **Executable route:** (1) **Thesis** — committees are the "mini-Parliaments" that keep scrutiny alive where the crowded, adjournment-hit floor cannot. (2) **Estimates Committee** — 30 members, **Lok Sabha only** (no Rajya Sabha), ruling-party chair; the "continuous economy committee" that examines whether estimates are well framed and suggests economies without waiting for the audit. (3) **The financial trio it sits in** — **PAC** (post-mortem of **CAG** reports, Opposition chair) and **CoPU** (public undertakings); ministers are barred from all three. (4) **DRSCs** — 24 committees examining demands for grants, bills and annual reports away from partisan glare. (5) **Why useful** — time, technical expertise, continuity, non-partisan working, and better-informed floor debate. (6) **Limitation** — recommendations are **advisory**, attendance is thin, and a falling share of bills is referred. (7) **Verdict** — indispensable but under-empowered; strengthen, don't bypass.
- **2019 · GS-II · Q15** — Demand (neutral rendering): *the individual parliamentarian's decline as a law-maker and the fall in the quality of debate.* Directive: **Discuss** · **15 marks · 250 words**.
  - **Core routing supersedes the older `advanced/17_Parliament.md` pointer for this demand.**
  - **Executable route:** (1) **Thesis** — the individual member has been squeezed between the party whip above and executive control of the calendar below, so law-making has become an act of the party, not the member. (2) **Anti-defection whip** — the **Tenth Schedule (52nd Amendment, 1985)** disqualifies a member who votes against the party direction, collapsing the member's independent vote. (3) **Executive dominance of time** — the government controls the legislative agenda; the **guillotine** clubs and passes un-debated demands for grants. (4) **Declining scrutiny** — falling sitting days, disruptions eating Question Hour, fewer bills sent to DRSCs, and passage without discussion. (5) **Bypass devices** — the **money-bill route (Art 110)** and the **ordinance route (Art 123)** shift law-making off the floor. (6) **Counter-point** — Private Members' Bills, committee work and Zero Hour still give members voice. (7) **Verdict** — decline is real but reversible through minimum sitting days, mandatory committee referral and protected dissent.
- **2020 · GS-II · Q5** — Demand (neutral rendering): *whether the norm "once a Speaker, always a Speaker" holds in India and what its implications are for the objectivity of the office.* Directive: **Do you think… and what implications** · **10 marks · 150 words**.
  - **Core routing supersedes the older `advanced/17_Parliament.md` pointer for this demand.**
  - **Executable route:** (1) **Thesis** — the British convention (the Speaker severs party ties and is returned unopposed) is **not** followed in India, which structurally exposes the office to partisanship. (2) **What the office demands** — under **Arts 93–97** the Speaker is the final interpreter of rules within the House, casts the deciding vote (**Art 100**), certifies a **Money Bill (Art 110, final)** and decides **anti-defection** cases. (3) **The Indian reality** — the Speaker retains party membership and contests on a party ticket; there is no "always a Speaker" security. (4) **Implication** — perceptions of bias in money-bill certification and delayed defection rulings (*Kihoto Hollohan* 1992; *Keisham Meghachandra* 2020). (5) **Reform** — resign from party, or shift defection adjudication to an independent tribunal. (6) **Verdict** — the norm is aspirational in India; institutional insulation, not personal restraint, is the fix.
- **2020 · GS-II · Q14** — Demand (neutral rendering): *the factors and the areas in which the Rajya Sabha has been transformed into a supporting/secondary organ of the Union legislature.* Directive: **Highlight the factors and areas** · **15 marks · 250 words**.
  - **Core routing supersedes the older `advanced/17_Parliament.md` pointer for this demand.**
  - **Executable route:** (1) **Thesis** — the Rajya Sabha is *secondary on finance and confidence but co-equal on federation and law*, so "supporting organ" is half true. (2) **Areas of subordination** — no power over a **Money Bill (Art 110)** beyond 14-day recommendation (**Art 109**); no confidence function (**Art 75** ties the Council of Ministers to the Lok Sabha only); on an ordinary-bill deadlock the **joint sitting (Art 108)** lets the numerically larger Lok Sabha prevail. (3) **Factors** — the money-bill route, declining productivity, and the Lok Sabha's numerical dominance in joint sittings. (4) **Areas it remains primary** — its **two exclusive powers: Art 249** (legislate on a State-List subject in the national interest) and **Art 312** (create All-India Services), plus federal representation of States and continuity as a permanent House. (5) **Frame** — it "supports" the Lok Sabha as a revising/federal chamber, not as its subordinate. (6) **Verdict** — a supporting *second* chamber, not a diminished one.
- **2021 · GS-II · Q4** — Demand (neutral rendering): *the extent to which Parliament is able to ensure accountability of the executive.* Directive: **To what extent** · **10 marks · 150 words**.
  - **Core routing supersedes the older `advanced/17_Parliament.md` pointer for this demand.**
  - **Executable route:** (1) **Thesis** — accountability is *strong in instruments, weak in use.* (2) **Deliberative tools** — Question Hour (starred/unstarred/short-notice), Zero Hour, Calling-Attention, Half-an-Hour Discussion. (3) **Motions** — **No-Confidence** and Censure/Adjournment Motions (only in the Lok Sabha; **Art 75** collective responsibility). (4) **Financial control** — the budget cycle (**Arts 112–114**), cut motions and **PAC/CAG** post-audit. (5) **Committees** — DRSCs examine demands and bills away from the floor. (6) **Limits** — the **anti-defection whip** keeps a majority government safe, plus disruptions, declining sittings, the guillotine and the ordinance route. (7) **Verdict** — accountability is real but blunted; the constraint is political-behavioural, not textual.
- **2021 · GS-II · Q15** — Demand (neutral rendering): *the working of Department-Related Parliamentary Standing Committees in enforcing parliamentary control over the executive.* Directive: **Evaluate the working** · **15 marks · 250 words**.
  - **Core routing supersedes the older `advanced/17_Parliament.md` pointer for this demand.**
  - **Executable route:** (1) **Thesis** — DRSCs are the Constitution-silent but decisive layer of everyday oversight; they work well but are structurally weak. (2) **Structure** — **17** committees when the system was introduced in **1993**, restructured to **24 from 2004**; **31 members each (21 Lok Sabha + 10 Rajya Sabha)**, 16 serviced by the LS Secretariat and 8 by the RS Secretariat; ministers are barred. (3) **Mandate** — they examine demands for grants, scrutinise Bills clause-by-clause, and review annual reports and long-term policy. (4) **Strengths** — continuity, expertise, cross-party consensus and depth away from public glare. (5) **Weaknesses** — recommendations are **not binding**, attendance is low, a shrinking share of Bills is referred, the one-year term limits specialisation, and there is little research staff. (6) **Evidence of erosion** — major Bills passed without referral. (7) **Verdict** — effective where used, but referral must be made the default to restore control.
- **2023 · GS-II · Q16** — Demand (neutral rendering): *the structure of the parliamentary committee system, and the role of financial committees within it.* Directive: **Explain the structure** · **15 marks · 250 words**.
  - **Core routing supersedes the older `advanced/17_Parliament.md` pointer for this demand.**
  - **Executable route:** (1) **Frame** — committees split into **Standing** (permanent, reconstituted yearly) and **Ad hoc** (task-specific). (2) **Financial committees** — **PAC** (22: 15 LS + 7 RS; Opposition chair; audits **CAG** reports), **Estimates** (30, Lok Sabha only; examines estimates/economies), **CoPU** (22: 15 LS + 7 RS; public undertakings); **ministers barred from all three**. (3) **DRSC layer** — 24 subject committees on ministries. (4) **Other standing committees** — Business Advisory, Privileges, Ethics, **Subordinate Legislation**, Government Assurances, **Welfare of SCs and STs**. (5) **Ad hoc** — Select/Joint committees on Bills and JPCs. (6) **Role** — pre-legislative scrutiny plus post-expenditure audit closes the accountability loop. (7) **Verdict** — a two-tier architecture that substitutes for floor scrutiny; its financial arm is the backbone of the power of the purse.

### 7.2 Qualified thesis options

- *Indian parliamentary supremacy is procedural, not sovereign: the House is supreme within a written Constitution, so its control over the executive is real but bounded by the whip, the calendar and judicial review.*
- *The Lok Sabha's dominance is confined to finance and confidence; on federation, ordinary legislation and constitutional amendment the two Houses are near-equal, which is why "Rajya Sabha as a secondary chamber" is true of money and false of federal representation.*
- *Accountability in Parliament is strong in instruments and weak in use — question hour, motions, financial control and committees exist, but the anti-defection whip, disruptions and declining sittings blunt them.*
- *The committee system, not the floor, is where legislative scrutiny now actually happens; the realistic reform is to make committee referral the default, not to revive the plenary.*
- *The money-bill route is the sharpest live test of bicameralism: certification under Art 110 is textually "final" but not immune, and its misuse converts a two-House Parliament into one — a question the courts have not yet finally settled.*

### 7.4 Bank A — The two Houses: composition, strength and the division of powers

| Claim | Named provision / anchor | Mechanism / significance | Limitation / status caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parliament is a tripartite body | ✅ **Art 79** — President + Rajya Sabha + Lok Sabha | The President summons, prorogues and assents though not a "member" | Do not describe the President as a *member* of either House |
| Strength is capped, not fixed | ✅ **Art 80** RS **max 250** (238 elected + **12 nominated**); ✅ **Art 81** LS **max 550** (530 States + 20 UTs) | Present working strength: **245** sanctioned RS, **543** elected LS | The Anglo-Indian nomination (**104th Amendment**, effective 2020) is **abolished** — LS has no nominated members now |
| The RS candidate's State tie and secret vote were changed by **statute, not the Constitution** | ✅ **Representation of the People (Amendment) Act, 2003** — dropped the domicile/"ordinarily resident" requirement and replaced secret ballot with **open ballot** | Reduced parochial residence bars and curbed cross-voting; upheld in ✅ ***Kuldip Nayar v. Union of India (2006)*** | This is a **statutory** change to the RP Act 1951, **not** a constitutional amendment — do not attribute it to the 91st/other Amendment |
| The Rajya Sabha has two **exclusive** powers no other House shares | ✅ **Art 249** — resolution by **2/3 present and voting** lets Parliament legislate on a **State-List** subject in the national interest (1 year, renewable); ✅ **Art 312** — resolution to create a new **All-India Service** | These make the RS the constitutional gatekeeper of federal legislative encroachment and of the AIS | These are RS-**initiated** powers; they do **not** make the RS superior on money or confidence |

- **Exclusive powers of the Lok Sabha (the mirror image):** ✅ a **Money Bill originates only in the Lok Sabha**; the **Speaker certifies** it; the **Council of Ministers is responsible only to the Lok Sabha** (**Art 75** → No-Confidence only here); on a joint sitting the larger House prevails. *Significance:* finance and survival of government are Lok-Sabha monopolies. *Limitation:* on ordinary Bills, constitutional amendments and elections to the offices of President/Vice-President the Houses are **equal**.

**Representation, delimitation and the population freeze — a demand family of its own:**

| Claim | Named provision / amendment | Mechanism / significance | Limitation / status caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seats are allotted to States in proportion to population, so representation was designed to track demography | ✅ **Art 81(2)(a)** — the ratio between the seats allotted to a State and its population shall, so far as practicable, be the same for all States; ✅ **Art 170** applies the parallel rule to Legislative Assemblies | Makes the Lok Sabha a **population-representative** chamber, with the Rajya Sabha carrying the (unequal, population-weighted) federal voice | ⚠️ India's Rajya Sabha, unlike the US Senate, is **not** equal-per-State, so the Lok Sabha's population rule is not offset by an equal-representation upper house |
| That population rule has been **frozen** since 1976 | ✅ **42nd Amendment (1976)** froze the allocation of seats on the **1971 census** until after the first census taken after 2000; ✅ **84th Amendment (2001)** extended the freeze until after the first census taken after **2026**, while permitting readjustment of constituency **boundaries** on 1991/2001 figures; ✅ **87th Amendment (2003)** shifted that boundary readjustment to the **2001** census | The freeze was a **federal bargain**: States that succeeded in reducing population growth would not lose seats to States that did not | ⚠️ Do **not** state a projected post-freeze seat count for any State — no such allocation exists in law, and any number is speculation |
| Delimitation is done by a statutory commission whose orders are insulated from courts | ✅ **Art 82** (readjustment after each census, by an authority Parliament determines) with the **Delimitation Acts**; ✅ **Art 329(a)** bars courts from questioning the validity of a delimitation law or allotment of seats | Creates a technocratic, judicially unreviewable process so that boundary drawing cannot be litigated into paralysis | ✅ **Four** Delimitation Commissions have been constituted (Acts of **1952, 1962, 1972 and 2002**); ⚠️ a separate commission carried out the **Jammu and Kashmir** exercise after the 2019 reorganisation — treat that as a distinct statutory exercise |
| The women's reservation is drafted to switch on **only after** delimitation | ✅ **106th Amendment (2023)** inserting **Arts 330A, 332A and 334A** — one-third reservation for women in the Lok Sabha, State Legislative Assemblies and the Delhi Assembly, including within SC/ST-reserved seats. **Art 334A(1)**: the reservation "shall come into effect after an exercise of delimitation is undertaken for this purpose after the relevant figures for the first census taken after the commencement of the Constitution (One Hundred and Sixth Amendment) Act, 2023 have been published", and "shall cease to have effect on the expiration of a period of **fifteen years** from such commencement"; **334A(2)** continuance as Parliament may by law determine; **334A(3)** rotation of reserved seats after each subsequent delimitation | Links **three** separate constitutional processes — commencement, census, delimitation — so the reservation's start date is **contingent, not fixed**; it is the clearest textual example in the Constitution of a right that is *enacted but dormant* | ✅ The Act **commenced on 16 April 2026** by notification under **s.1(2)** — write the date. ⚠️ **Commenced ≠ operational:** the reservation is **still not in effect**, and the commencement date is **not** an implementation year. ⚠️ Do **not** merge the three registers (enacted / commenced / operational — see the Current-link table), do **not** state an implementation year or election, and do **not** compute a fifteen-year end-date — quote *"fifteen years from such commencement"* instead |

- **Claim → evidence → mechanism → limitation (the federal argument in one unit):** ⚠️ *Ending the freeze would restore literal population proportionality and simultaneously redistribute national political weight between States.* ✅ **Evidence:** Art 81(2)(a) read with the 42nd, 84th and 87th Amendments and Art 82. **Mechanism:** because Lok Sabha seats determine both legislative strength and the Electoral College value of MPs under Art 54–55, any reallocation changes federal bargaining power. **Limitation:** ⚠️ the constitutional text supplies the *rule*, not the *remedy* — options such as raising the total House strength, or a differently designed upper chamber, are **proposals**, not law, and must be labelled as such.
- ⚠️ **Answer use:** this bank is the correct place to argue that India's federal problem after the freeze is **representational**, not merely fiscal — a distinction most answers miss.

**📰 Census 2027 — the dated evidence that supplies the Art 334A trigger (and the Art 82 trigger):**

> ✅ **Why this belongs in a Parliament answer.** Art 334A(1) hangs the women's reservation on *"the first
> census taken after the commencement of the 106th Amendment"*. Because the Amendment **commenced on
> 16 April 2026**, **Census 2027 is that first census**. The same census is also the "first census taken
> after 2026" that releases the **84th Amendment** freeze under **Art 82**. One exercise therefore carries
> two constitutional triggers — this is the highest-value linkage in the whole delimitation family.

| Element | What the official record shows (✅) | Significance | Limit / status caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Legal frame** | ✅ Conducted under the **Census Act, 1948** and the **Census Rules, 1990**; the **Union Cabinet approved the scheme for the Census of India 2027** (PIB release on the Cabinet decision), with the phases and reference dates notified in the Gazette and stated by the **MHA/Registrar General of India** | Census-taking is a **statutory executive exercise**, not a constitutional one — which is exactly why Art 334A had to borrow it as a trigger | ⚠️ Do not describe the census as a constitutional requirement in itself; Arts 82/334A only *refer* to it |
| **Phase 1 — Houselisting & Housing Census** | ✅ Window **1 April – 30 September 2026**, each **State/UT** conducting it over a **fixed 30-day period of its own choosing** inside that window | Explains why there is no single nationwide "census day" for phase 1 | ⚠️ **Do not** assert a nationwide completion or progress figure as on 13 August 2026 — the schedule is **State/UT-specific**, so no exact nationwide status wording is safe. State the **notified window** only |
| **Phase 2 — Population Enumeration** | ✅ **February 2027** for most of India; **snow-bound areas** (with **Ladakh, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu & Kashmir and Uttarakhand** identified in the schedule) enumerated earlier, in **September 2026** | The population count — not the houselisting — produces the figures that feed delimitation | ⚠️ Enumeration month ≠ publication of figures |
| **Reference dates** | ✅ **00:00 hours of 1 March 2027** for the country generally; **00:00 hours of 1 October 2026** for the snow-bound areas | The reference date fixes *who is counted where*; two reference dates in one census is the standard Prelims discriminator | ⚠️ Keep the two apart; do not give 1 March 2027 as universal |
| **Design features** | ✅ India's **first fully digital census** — mobile-app based collection with a **self-enumeration** facility and a central monitoring portal; ✅ **caste enumeration** officially announced for inclusion | Both are examinable "firsts"; caste enumeration is the live GS-I/GS-II social-justice hook | ⚠️ Do **not** state what the caste data will show, how it will be used, or that it settles any reservation question |

- **The constitutional chain, in the exact order an answer must state it:**
  ✅ **16 April 2026** — the 106th Amendment **commences** (s.1(2) notification) → ✅ **Census 2027** is
  therefore the **first census taken after that commencement** for **Art 334A(1)** → ⚠️ **publication of
  the relevant figures of that census — date unknown** → ⚠️ **delimitation undertaken for this purpose**
  → ⚠️ **the women's reservation takes effect**, and only then.
- ⚠️ **The single most important caution in this unit:** *notified conduct dates and reference dates are
  **not** the date of publication of final figures.* The Government has notified **when the census will be
  taken**; it has **not** notified when the relevant figures will be **published**, and Art 334A(1) turns
  on **publication**, not on enumeration. Therefore: **no** projected date for the reservation taking
  effect, **no** implementation election or year, **no** seat projection for any State, and **no** projected
  count of women's seats. The honest sentence is: *"the trigger census is now scheduled; the publication
  date, the delimitation and hence the reservation's effective date remain undetermined."*

- **The federal controversy in one unit:** ⚠️ *Claim* — an unfrozen, population-proportional Lok Sabha redistributes national political weight from States that curbed population growth to States that did not. ✅ *Evidence* — **Art 81(2)(a)** (seats proportional to population), the **42nd (1976) / 84th (2001) / 87th (2003)** freeze chain whose stated bargain was that demographic performance would not be punished, and the 2026 package's proposal to reopen that bargain on **2011** figures. **Mechanism** — because Lok Sabha seats also fix the value of MPs' votes in the Electoral College (**Arts 54–55**) and the arithmetic of every confidence vote, reallocation changes federal bargaining power, not merely constituency maps; and because the **Rajya Sabha is population-weighted rather than equal-per-State**, India has no upper-house counterweight of the US Senate type. **Why smaller and southern States object** — they carry a compounding loss: fewer relative seats, no equal-representation chamber, and a devolution formula that already uses population. **Limitation** — ⚠️ this is an argument about *incentives and design*, not a prediction; **no State's post-freeze seat count may be projected**, and the counter-argument (that permanent under-representation of populous States is itself a democratic deficit, and that malapportionment cannot be frozen forever) must be stated for balance.
- **Procedural significance (use in any Art 368 / bicameralism answer):** ✅ the episode shows (i) the **special-majority threshold is a real veto point**, not a formality; (ii) an amendment can be **defeated on the floor of the first House** without ever reaching the Rajya Sabha or, for entrenched entries, State ratification; (iii) linked ordinary legislation is **hostage** to the amendment; and (iv) **representation is the one federal question the Constitution left with a deadline rather than a settlement** — the freeze must eventually end, so the question returns.
- ⚠️ **Answer discipline:** the correct sentence form is *"A 2026 attempt to raise the ceiling to 850 — the **Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 2026** — and to advance the women's quota was **introduced on 16 April 2026 and defeated on 17 April 2026** for want of the Article 368 special majority."* Never *"India has raised the Lok Sabha to 850"*, never *"delimitation will use the 2011 census"*, and never *"women's reservation will apply from 2029."*

| Claim | Named provision / case | Mechanism / significance | Limitation / status caution |

| The Speaker cannot preside over the motion for his own removal | ✅ **Art 96** (LS) / **Art 181** (Assembly) | May speak and vote in the first instance but not exercise the casting vote while that resolution is considered | Do not say the Speaker is "barred from the House" — only from *presiding* |
| The Speaker holds decisive, near-final powers | ✅ **Art 110** — certifies a **Money Bill (final)**; ✅ **Art 100** — **casting vote** in a tie; ✅ **Art 108** — presides over the **joint sitting**; ✅ Tenth Schedule — decides **anti-defection** | Concentrates interpretive authority in one office → the "objectivity" debate | ✅ Money-Bill certification is judicially reviewable only on narrow constitutional grounds (see §7.7); the casting vote is used only to break a tie |

Union of India (2024)*** | A **seven-judge Bench (4 March 2024) unanimously overruled *P.V. Narasimha Rao* (1998)**: a legislator who takes a bribe for a vote/speech is **not immune**; the offence is complete **on acceptance** | Privilege still protects *bona fide* speech and votes; the case removed the *bribery* shield, not the core immunity for debate |

### 7.6 Bank C — Sessions, devices and the machinery of executive accountability

| Claim | Named provision / device | Mechanism / significance | Limitation / status caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sitting frequency is loosely governed | ✅ **Art 85** — the President summons each House so the gap between two sessions is **≤ 6 months** | Guarantees **at least two sessions** a year; three by convention (Budget, Monsoon, Winter) | ✅ There is **no constitutional minimum number of sitting days** — the six-month rule is the only hard floor |
| Prorogation and dissolution are the President's acts; adjournment is the presiding officer's | ✅ **Art 85(2)** — President **prorogues** a session and **dissolves** the Lok Sabha; adjournment/adjournment *sine die* by the presiding officer | Prorogation ends a *session* (pending business survives, pending notices lapse); dissolution ends the *life* of the House | Do not attribute prorogation/dissolution to the Speaker; the Rajya Sabha is **never dissolved** |
| Quorum is a fixed fraction | ✅ **Art 100(3)** — **one-tenth** of the total → **55 LS / 25 RS** | Below quorum the presiding officer adjourns or suspends the sitting | — |
| Question devices force disclosure | ✅ **Question Hour** — *starred* (oral + **supplementary** questions), *unstarred* (written, no supplementaries), *short-notice* (<10 days); ✅ **Zero Hour** (Indian innovation, since 1962, **not** in the Rules) | The daily accountability engine; supplementaries turn a written reply into cross-examination | Zero Hour has no procedural sanctity; Question Hour is the one grounded in the Rules |

### 7.7 Bank D — Bills, the money-bill route and the ordinance bypass

- **Claim — bills fall into four constitutionally distinct classes.** ✅ *Provision:* **Ordinary** (any non-financial matter), **Money (Art 110)**, **Financial (Art 117)** and **Constitution Amendment (Art 368)**. *Mechanism:* each class has a different origination rule and a different Rajya Sabha power. *Limitation:* only the money class strips the RS of amendment power.
- **Claim — the money-bill definition is exhaustive and the Speaker's certificate is textually final.** ✅ *Provision:* **Art 110(1)(a)–(g)** lists the only matters; **Art 109** gives the special procedure (RS may only *recommend*, must return in **14 days**); **Art 110(3)–(4)** makes the **Speaker's certificate final**. *Mechanism:* a Money Bill cannot be introduced in the RS or amended by it, and **no joint sitting** applies. *Limitation:* "final" is not absolute — see the controversy below.
- **Claim — the money-bill *route* is the live bicameralism controversy, and it is unsettled.** ✅ *Provision/cases:* the **Aadhaar Act** and Finance Act amendments were certified as Money Bills; ✅ ***K.S. Puttaswamy (Aadhaar), 2018*** upheld the Aadhaar certification **4:1** (Chandrachud J. dissenting); ✅ ***Rojer Mathew v. South Indian Bank (2019)*** doubted that reasoning and **referred the money-bill question to a larger (seven-judge) Bench**. *Significance:* if certification is misused, a bicameral Parliament is reduced to one House. 📰 *Status discipline:* no visible final resolution was located on the official Supreme Court judgment portals on **28 August 2026**. Do **not** state that it has been finally decided.
- **Claim — the ordinary-bill deadlock is broken by a rare joint sitting.** ✅ *Provision:* **Art 108** — the President may summon a **joint sitting**, presided over by the **Speaker of the Lok Sabha**. *Mechanism:* the numerically larger House effectively prevails. *Limitation:* used only **three times** — ✅ **Dowry Prohibition Bill (1961)**, **Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill (1978)**, **POTA (2002)**; **no joint sitting** for a Money Bill or a constitutional amendment.
- **Claim — bills lapse selectively on dissolution.** ✅ *Rule:* **lapses** — a Bill pending in the LS, or passed by the LS but pending in the RS; **does not lapse** — a Bill pending in the RS (not passed by LS), one passed by both awaiting assent, one returned by the President, or one for which a joint sitting was already notified. *Significance:* the permanence of the RS protects legislation it still holds.
- **Claim — the ordinance is law-making that bypasses Parliament.** ✅ *Provision:* **Art 123** — the President promulgates an ordinance when a House is not in session; it must be laid before both Houses and **ceases six weeks** after they reassemble. *Cases:* ✅ ***D.C. Wadhwa (1987)*** and ***Krishna Kumar Singh v. State of Bihar (2017)*** — re-promulgation is a "fraud on the Constitution" and ordinances are subject to judicial review. *Limitation:* an ordinance has the **same force** as an Act but is temporary and cannot be a substitute for legislative deliberation.

### 7.8 Bank E — Budget and the power of the purse

| Claim | Named provision / device | Mechanism / significance | Limitation / status caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| The budget is a constitutional statement, not just a policy document | ✅ **Art 112** — the **Annual Financial Statement**; expenditure is split into **charged** (non-votable) and **made** (votable) | Only the votable part goes to demands for grants; charged expenditure (judges', CAG's, President's salaries, debt charges) is discussed but **not voted** | Do not say Parliament "votes" charged expenditure |
| Grants are voted only on the executive's motion | ✅ **Art 113** — **demands for grants** are voted by the Lok Sabha alone, only on the **President's recommendation** | The Rajya Sabha cannot vote demands; this is a Lok-Sabha monopoly | RS can only discuss the budget |
| Members prune the executive through cut motions | ✅ **Policy Cut** (grant reduced to **Re 1**), **Economy Cut**, **Token Cut** (₹100) | Each cut is a graded instrument of disapproval and debate | Cut motions rarely pass under a majority government |
| Spending is authorised by two specific Bills | ✅ **Art 114** — the **Appropriation Bill** legalises withdrawal from the **Consolidated Fund**; the **Finance Bill** enacts taxation | No money leaves the Fund without an Appropriation Act | The Appropriation Bill's amounts cannot be varied to alter a charged sum |
| Time pressure and interim needs are handled by set devices | ✅ **Guillotine** (un-discussed demands are clubbed and voted at the deadline); ✅ **Vote on Account (Art 116)** advances funds pending full passage; Vote of Credit and Exceptional Grant | Keep government funded and the calendar moving | ✅ The guillotine means large sums pass **without discussion** — a core accountability criticism |
| Mid-year and over-spending are corrected by further grants | ✅ **Art 115** — **supplementary, additional and excess grants** (also vote of credit/exceptional grant under Art 116) | An **excess grant** is voted *after* the money is spent and is regularised only on the **PAC's** recommendation — closing the audit loop | Do not equate a supplementary grant (in-year top-up) with an excess grant (post-facto regularisation) |
| Every rupee sits in a constitutionally named fund | ✅ **Consolidated Fund (Art 266(1))** — all revenues, withdrawals need appropriation; **Contingency Fund (Art 267)** — at the President's disposal for unforeseen spending; **Public Account (Art 266(2))** — no appropriation needed | Appropriation and the charged/voted split operate on the **Consolidated Fund** | Contingency Fund advances are later recouped from the Consolidated Fund via a grant |

- **Budget as a sequence (the answer spine for a "procedure" question):** ✅ presentation of the Annual Financial Statement (**Art 112**) → general discussion → **DRSC** scrutiny of demands → voting on **demands for grants** (**Art 113**) with cut motions and the **guillotine** → **Appropriation Bill** (**Art 114**) → **Finance Bill** (taxation). *Significance:* the Rajya Sabha discusses but never votes demands, which is why finance is the sharpest illustration of Lok-Sabha primacy.

### 7.9A Indian Parliamentary Group: IPG, IPU and CPA

✅ The **Indian Parliamentary Group (IPG)** was formed in **1949** as an autonomous association of
members of the Indian Parliament. All members of Parliament may become members. The Speaker of the
Lok Sabha is its ex-officio President; the Deputy Speaker of the Lok Sabha and Deputy Chairman of
the Rajya Sabha are ex-officio Vice-Presidents.

| Institution | What it is | India's link |
|---|---|---|
| **IPG** | Indian parliamentary association for contacts, delegations, conferences and exchange with foreign parliaments | Domestic coordinating group |
| **IPU** | Worldwide organisation of **national parliaments**, founded in 1889 | IPG acts as India's **National Group** |
| **CPA** | Commonwealth network promoting parliamentary democracy and exchange | IPG is the **main India Branch**; State legislatures have branches within the Indian CPA structure |

> **Trap:** IPG is not an organ of the Ministry of External Affairs; IPU is not an association of
> governments; and CPA is Commonwealth-specific whereas IPU is global.

### 7.10 Mechanism, institutional incentives and consequences

- **Why the whip protects the executive:** the **Tenth Schedule** disqualifies a member who defies the party direction, so a majority government is structurally insulated from its own backbenchers — the No-Confidence Motion (**Art 75**) rarely bites when the whip holds the ranks.
- **Why the money-bill route is tempting:** certifying a Bill under **Art 110** removes the Rajya Sabha's amendment power and the possibility of a hostile Upper House, converting a bicameral obstacle into a 14-day formality (**Art 109**) — the incentive that makes *Rojer Mathew* consequential.
- **Why committees outperform the floor:** they sit in private, without cameras and without whipped voting, which lowers the incentive to grandstand and raises the incentive to specialise — the reason DRSC reports are more substantive than floor debate.
- **Consequence of declining sittings and the guillotine:** when time is short, scrutiny is the first casualty — demands for grants pass un-discussed and Bills pass unreferred, shifting real law-making to the executive drafting table and the ordinance.

### 7.11 Criticism, counter-argument, variation and implementation constraints

- **Over-executive critique:** ⚠️ cabinet control of the agenda, the whip, the guillotine and the ordinance route mean the executive largely *manages* Parliament rather than being controlled by it.
- **Counter (Parliament still bites):** ⚠️ Question Hour disclosures, PAC/CAG audits, DRSC referrals, and the *Sita Soren* and *Kihoto Hollohan* limits show the institution retains teeth where it uses them.
- **Bicameralism critique and counter:** ⚠️ the money-bill route hollows the Rajya Sabha, but the RS's exclusive powers (**Arts 249, 312**), its federal composition and its permanence keep it a genuine second chamber, not an appendage.
- **Implementation constraint:** ⚠️ committees are advisory and under-staffed, the Deputy Speaker's post was left vacant for a full term, and there is no minimum-sitting-days rule — so reform depends on political will, not fresh text.

### 7.12 Verdict scaffolds

- **Accountability verdict:** "Parliament's control of the executive is textually strong and behaviourally weak: the instruments — questions, motions, financial control and committees — are intact, but the whip, the calendar and disruption decide how much of that control is actually exercised."
- **Rajya Sabha verdict:** "The Rajya Sabha is a supporting chamber on money and confidence and a co-equal one on federation and law; to call it 'secondary' is accurate only in the two areas where the Constitution made the Lok Sabha supreme."
- **Committee verdict:** "The committee system is where scrutiny survived the decline of the floor; making referral the default, not the exception, is the single highest-yield parliamentary reform available without a constitutional amendment."
- **Speaker verdict:** "'Once a Speaker, always a Speaker' is a British insulation India never adopted; until the office is separated from party or defection adjudication is moved out of it, the objectivity of the chair rests on personal restraint rather than institutional design."

### 7.14 Factual-risk and current-status controls

- ✅ Strength ceilings: RS **250** (12 nominated), LS **550**; stable sanctioned/elected figures are
  **245 RS / 543 elected LS seats**, while working strength varies with vacancies. Anglo-Indian
  nomination was abolished by the 104th Amendment.
- ✅ Quorum **1/10 (Art 100)** = 55 LS / 25 RS; **no** constitutional minimum sitting-days rule.
- ✅ Only **three** joint sittings (1961 Dowry, 1978 Banking Service, 2002 POTA); **no** joint sitting for Money Bills or constitutional amendments.
