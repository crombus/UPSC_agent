## President — core idea
✅ **Nominal executive** (Ambedkar: "head of the State, not of the Executive"). Elected by **PR-STV,
secret ballot**; MLA vote value uses **1971 census** (84th Amdt). ✅ **42nd Amdt** made CoM advice
**binding**; **44th Amdt** lets him **return once**, then it's binding.

## Impeachment (Art 61)
> 🔑 Only ground = **"violation of the Constitution."** Charge by **1/4 members**, passed by **2/3 of
> total membership of BOTH Houses**. **Nominated MPs can join impeachment** (but not the election).

## Powers — 7 heads
> 🔑 **Mnemonic:** **"E-L-F-J-D-M-E"** = **E**xecutive · **L**egislative · **F**inancial · **J**udicial ·
> **D**iplomatic · **M**ilitary · **E**mergency. Nominates **12 Rajya Sabha** members; **Supreme
> Commander** of armed forces.

## Veto (Art 111)
✅ President has **Absolute, Suspensive, Pocket** vetoes — **NO qualified veto** (that's the US President).
✅ **Pocket veto example: Zail Singh — Indian Post Office Bill (1986).** ✅ **Must assent** to a
constitutional amendment bill (24th Amdt).

## Ordinance (Art 123) & Pardon (Art 72)
✅ Ordinance only when a House is **not in session**; lapses **6 weeks** after reassembly; **can't amend
Constitution**. ✅ Only the **President** (not Governor) can pardon a **death sentence** or a
**court-martial** sentence.

## Vice-President
✅ **Ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha**; **acts as President for max 6 months** on a vacancy. ✅ Unlike
the **US VP**, does **not** take over for the full unexpired term. Removal resolution starts **only in
Rajya Sabha**; **no ground needed**.

## Must-Know Facts
- ✅ President's oath → by **CJI**; VP's oath → by **President**.
- ✅ Only **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** served two full presidential terms.
- ✅ *D.C. Wadhwa (1987):* re-promulgating ordinances = "fraud on the Constitution."

## 📰 Current link
📰 ✅ **Current Art 200/201 position (20 Nov 2025 Article 143 opinion):** courts
cannot create rigid timelines or automatic deemed assent, but prolonged, unexplained and
indefinite gubernatorial inaction remains open to limited judicial review and mandamus.
📰 ✅ **Vice-Presidential transition (2025):** after Jagdeep Dhankhar's resignation,
C. P. Radhakrishnan was elected on 9 September 2025 and entered office on 12 September
2025. This illustrates Article 68's distinct rule: a casual Vice-Presidential vacancy is
filled **as soon as possible**, not within the President's six-month deadline, and the
successor receives a fresh five-year term.

### 10.0 Direct Mains demands owned by this Core file

- **2022 · GS-II · Q4** — Demand (neutral rendering): *the role of the Vice-President as Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.* Directive: **Discuss** · **10 marks · 150 words**.
  - **Core routing supersedes the older `advanced/15_President-and-Vice-President.md` pointer for this demand.**
  - **Executable route:** (1) **Thesis** — the VP's substantive day-to-day constitutional role is as **ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha (Art 64)**, a *non-member presiding officer* who guards the House's order; the office matters more as Chairman than as President-in-waiting. (2) **Constitutional basis** — Art 64 (ex-officio Chairman); Art 89 (Deputy Chairman presides in his absence); **Art 100** (no vote in the first instance but a **casting vote** in a tie); **Art 92** (must **not preside** while a resolution for his own removal is under consideration — may speak, not vote). (3) **Powers** — presides, maintains order, decides points of order and admissibility, interprets rules, refers matters to committees, and acts as the **Tenth Schedule disqualification tribunal for Rajya Sabha members** (para 6). (4) **Distinction from the Speaker** — the Chairman is **not a member** of the House he presides over (the Speaker is), and he does **not certify Money Bills** (that is the Lok Sabha Speaker's power, Art 110). (5) **Analytical tension** — because the RS represents the States, the Chairman anchors federal deliberation; but being an elected political figure his **neutrality is contested** (disputed rulings, disruptions, anti-defection references reviewable after *Kihoto Hollohan* 1992). (6) **Verdict** — the Chairmanship is the VP's real constitutional workload, and its authority rests on *perceived impartiality*, not on formal powers alone.
- **2025 · GS-II · Q3** — Demand (neutral rendering): *compare and contrast the President's power to pardon in India and the USA, the limits in both, and the meaning of 'preemptive pardons'.* Directive: **Compare and contrast** · **10 marks · 150 words**. This file **owns the Indian half in full**; the comparative frame and the US Article II side are completed in `basic/Comparative-Constitutional-Schemes.md` §10.
  - **Core routing supersedes the older `advanced/15_President-and-Vice-President.md` pointer for this demand.**
  - **Executable route (Indian half):** (1) **Scope** — **Art 72** covers pardon, reprieve, respite, remission and commutation, and uniquely reaches **court-martial sentences and death sentences** (the Governor's Art 161 reaches neither). (2) **Who really decides** — exercised on the **binding advice of the Council of Ministers**, not the President's personal will (*Maru Ram* 1980). (3) **Independent application of mind** — the President may examine the case afresh and take a view different from the courts (*Kehar Singh* 1989). (4) **Limits/review** — clemency is subject to **limited judicial review** for mala fides, non-application of mind, or extraneous/withheld material (*Epuru Sudhakar* 2006). (5) **Comparative hinge** — hand off to the comparative file for US Art II (a *personal* executive power over federal offences, barred for impeachment and state offences) and for **pre-emptive pardons** (pardon of *completed* conduct before charge/conviction, never of future acts). (6) **Verdict** — both are executive graces bounded by law, but India's is a *ministerially-advised, judicially-reviewable* power while the US President's is a *personal* one.
- **2022 · GS-II · Q14** (Presidents of India and France, election procedures) is routed to the comparative owner; use §10.4 here only for the **Indian PR-STV mechanism** and cross-link `basic/Comparative-Constitutional-Schemes.md` for the French half.

### 10.2 Qualified thesis options

- *The President is a nominal executive by design but not a nullity: the 42nd and 44th Amendments made ministerial advice binding, yet left a residue of situational discretion that becomes decisive precisely when the party system fails to deliver a clear majority.*
- *India's ordinance power is a legislative convenience that repeatedly tempts executive excess; the judiciary has responded not by abolishing it but by fencing it with the laying requirement and the anti-re-promulgation rule, so the real question is enforcement, not existence.*
- *The pardon power shows the difference between the Indian and American presidencies in miniature — the same clemency word conceals a ministerially-advised, reviewable Indian power and a personal, near-unreviewable American one.*
- *The Vice-Presidency is constitutionally a standby office but functionally a presiding office; its significance is measured on the floor of the Rajya Sabha, where the test is impartiality, not in the rare contingency of acting as President.*
- *Every discretionary space the President or Governor occupies is now shadowed by judicial review, so the modern constitutional question is no longer "can he refuse?" but "on what reviewable grounds, and within what time?".*

### 10.4 Bank A — Election and constitutional position

| Claim | Named anchor | Mechanism / significance | Limitation / caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| The President is indirectly elected to keep the office symbolic | ✅ **Art 54–55** | Electoral college of **elected MPs + elected MLAs + elected Delhi & Puducherry MLAs** (70th Amdt 1992); **PR-STV, secret ballot**; winner needs the quota | **Nominated members and MLCs do not vote**; direct election was rejected as costly for a nominal head |
| Vote values are weighted for Centre–State parity | ✅ **Art 55** | **MLA value = (State population ÷ elected MLAs) ÷ 1000** on the **1971 census**; **MP value = total MLA votes ÷ elected MPs of both Houses** | Frozen to the 1971 census until the first census after 2026 is published (**84th Amdt 2001**); disputes go to the **Supreme Court** (Art 71) |
| The President heads the State, not the executive | ✅ **Art 53 / Ambedkar** | All Union executive action is in his name; he is the constitutional symbol of the nation | He acts through the CoM; the PM is the real executive |
| Ministerial advice binds, with one pause | ✅ **42nd Amdt (1976), 44th Amdt (1978)** | 42nd made **advice binding**; 44th lets him **return advice once**, after which it binds | Used by **K.R. Narayanan** (UP 1997, Bihar 1998 President's-Rule reconsideration) |
| Discretion survives at the political margins | ⚠️ **situational (not constitutional) discretion** | Appointing a PM in a **hung Lok Sabha**; dismissing a CoM that cannot prove confidence; dissolving the LS when the CoM has lost majority | These are *situational*; the President has **no enumerated constitutional discretion** (unlike the Governor) |

- **Impeachment (Art 61):** ✅ the only ground is **"violation of the Constitution"** (undefined); charge by **1/4 of the members** of either House, **14 days' notice**, passed by **2/3 of the total membership** of each House; **nominated MPs may participate** (they cannot vote in the election), **State MLAs cannot** (they vote in the election). No President has been impeached.

### 10.5 Bank B — Veto and ordinance (the executive–legislative interface)

| Instrument | Named anchor | Mechanism | Limitation / instance |
|---|---|---|---|
| ✅ **Absolute veto** | Art 111 | Withholds assent; the bill dies | Rajendra Prasad — **PEPSU Appropriation Bill (1954)**; Venkataraman — **MP Salary Bill (1991)** |
| ✅ **Suspensive veto** | Art 111 | Returns a bill; Parliament overrides by an **ordinary** majority | Does **not** apply to Money Bills |
| ✅ **Pocket veto** | Art 111 (no time-limit) | Sits on the bill indefinitely — the Constitution sets **no time-limit** for assent | Zail Singh — **Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill, 1986** |
| ✅ **No veto on a constitutional amendment** | **24th Amdt (1971)** | Assent to a constitutional amendment bill is **obligatory** | — |
| ✅ **State bills reserved (Art 201)** | Art 201 | On a bill reserved by a Governor: assent / withhold / direct return | **Not bound to assent even if the State legislature re-passes it**; may also pocket veto |
| ✅ **Ordinance (Art 123)** | Art 123 | Temporary law with the force of an Act, only when **a House is not in session**; **laid before Parliament** and **ceases six weeks** after reassembly (max ≈ 6 months + 6 weeks) | **Cannot amend the Constitution** and **cannot abridge Fundamental Rights**; can be retrospective and can amend/repeal a Central Act |

- **Ordinance case-law spine:** ✅ *R.C. Cooper (1970)* — the President's satisfaction is **justiciable on mala fide** grounds; ✅ *D.C. Wadhwa (1987)* — **re-promulgation** of ordinances is a **"fraud on the Constitution"**; ✅ *Krishna Kumar Singh v. State of Bihar (2017)* (**seven-judge Bench, 5:2**) reaffirmed *Wadhwa*, held that **laying the ordinance before the legislature is mandatory** (Art 123(2)/213(2)) and that the ordinance-making satisfaction is **judicially reviewable**; ordinances do not automatically create enduring rights.
- **Analytical use:** the veto and ordinance banks together answer any "executive legislation / executive overreach" demand — the powers exist, but the *laying* requirement and the *anti-re-promulgation* rule are the constitutional brakes.

### 10.6 Bank C — Pardon power (Art 72) and the Art 161 contrast

- **Claim:** the pardon power is an executive grace bounded by advice and review. **Provision:** ✅ **Art 72** — pardon, reprieve, respite, remission, commutation. **Mechanism:** ✅ uniquely reaches **court-martial** and **death** sentences; exercised on the **Cabinet's binding advice** (*Maru Ram* 1980); the President may reappraise the case (*Kehar Singh* 1989). **Limitation:** ✅ subject to **limited judicial review** for mala fides, non-application of mind, or irrelevant/withheld material (*Epuru Sudhakar* 2006).

| Point | President (Art 72) | Governor (Art 161) |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ Pardon a **death** sentence | **Yes** | **No** (may only suspend/remit/commute) |
| ✅ **Court-martial** sentences | **Yes** | **No** |
| ✅ Advice | Binding Cabinet advice | Binding State-CoM advice |

### 10.7 Bank D — Vice-President and the Chairmanship of the Rajya Sabha

| Claim | Named anchor | Mechanism / significance | Limitation / caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| The VP is elected on a narrower base than the President | ✅ **Art 66** | Electoral college = **both Houses of Parliament only** (elected **and** nominated members), **excludes State MLAs**; PR-STV, secret ballot | The reverse of the President, whose college **includes** MLAs and **excludes** nominated members |
| The VP's substantive role is presiding, not succession | ✅ **Art 64** | **Ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha**; Art 89 Deputy Chairman presides in absence | He is **not a member** of the RS (unlike the Speaker, who is a member of the LS) |
| The Chairman shapes outcomes only in a tie | ✅ **Art 100** | No vote in the first instance; a **casting vote** when votes are equal | — |
| The Chairman is insulated when his own seat is at issue | ✅ **Art 92** | While a resolution for his removal is under consideration he **shall not preside**; may speak and take part but **not vote** | — |
| The Chairman is a quasi-judicial tribunal for defection | ✅ **Tenth Schedule, para 6** | Decides disqualification of **RS members** under the anti-defection law | ✅ his decision is **subject to judicial review** (*Kihoto Hollohan* 1992) |
| The VP acts as President only briefly | ✅ **Art 65** | Acts as President on a vacancy for a **maximum of six months** | Unlike the **US VP**, he does **not** serve the unexpired term |
| Removal is a Council-of-States-initiated process | ✅ **Art 67(b)** | Resolution by an **effective majority of the Rajya Sabha**, agreed by the **Lok Sabha**; **can be initiated only in the RS**; **no ground required** | Not the presidential "impeachment" procedure |

- **Oaths:** ✅ the President's oath is by the **CJI (Art 60)**; the VP's oath is by the **President (Art 69)**.

### 10.8 Mechanism, institutional incentives and consequences

- ⚠️ **Why the office is nominal yet contested:** the binding-advice rule (42nd/44th Amdts) reduces the President to a constitutional formality *in normal politics*, but the **situational-discretion residue** gives the office real leverage exactly when coalitions fragment — the incentive to invoke discretion rises as majorities shrink.
- ⚠️ **Why ordinances recur:** the ordinance route lets the executive legislate without immediate legislative scrutiny; the institutional incentive to **re-promulgate** is strongest where a government lacks an upper-House majority, which is precisely why *Wadhwa* and *Krishna Kumar Singh* had to make **laying-before-the-legislature mandatory**.
- ⚠️ **Why the Chairmanship is politically sensitive:** the Chairman is an elected political figure presiding over the federal House; his rulings on admissibility, disruption and defection references convert a ceremonial chair into a site of majority–opposition conflict.

### 10.9 Criticism, counter-argument and variation

- **"Rubber-stamp" critique:** ⚠️ post-42nd Amdt the President cannot resist a determined Cabinet; the return-once power (44th Amdt) is a speed-bump, not a veto.
- **Counter (the office bites at the margins):** ⚠️ hung-house PM selection, President's-Rule reconsideration (Narayanan) and mercy-petition delays show the office is consequential in abnormal politics.
- **Federal variation:** ⚠️ the President–Governor asymmetry is deliberate — the President has **no enumerated discretion** while the Governor does (Art 163), which is why Centre–State friction attaches to Raj Bhavan, not Rashtrapati Bhavan.
- **Implementation constraint:** ⚠️ judicial limits on ordinances and mercy delays depend on litigation reaching the Court; between promulgation and petition, executive advantage persists.

### 10.10 Verdict scaffolds

- **Nominal-executive verdict:** "The President is a nominal executive whose discretion is dormant in normal politics and decisive in abnormal politics; the 44th Amendment tamed the office without emptying it."
- **Ordinance verdict:** "The ordinance power survives because it is useful and is disciplined because it is abused; *Krishna Kumar Singh* completed what *Wadhwa* began by making legislative laying non-negotiable."
- **Pardon verdict:** "Clemency in India is neither royal mercy nor personal fiat: it is ministerial advice exercised under the shadow of *Epuru Sudhakar* review."
- **Vice-President verdict:** "The Vice-Presidency earns its constitutional keep in the Chair of the Rajya Sabha, where impartiality — not the six-month acting contingency — is the true measure of the office."

### 10.12 Factual-risk and current-status controls

- ✅ **Qualified veto = US President only**; the Indian President has **absolute/suspensive/pocket** vetoes.
- ✅ **Art 72 (President)** reaches death and court-martial sentences; **Art 161 (Governor)** reaches neither — never merge them.
- ✅ The pocket veto works because **no time-limit** is prescribed for assent; do not invent one.
- ⚠️ Do not overstate bench strengths; where certain, state them (*Krishna Kumar Singh* = seven judges, 5:2).
- ⚠️ Case law is a **proposition, not a name-drop**: *Maru Ram* = advice; *Kehar Singh* = independent reappraisal + reviewability; *Epuru Sudhakar* = grounds of review; *Wadhwa*/*Krishna Kumar Singh* = anti-re-promulgation and mandatory laying.
