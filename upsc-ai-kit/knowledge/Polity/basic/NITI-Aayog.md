## Core idea
NITI Aayog is the government's **premier policy think tank** — it replaced the top-down, fund-allocating Planning
Commission with an **advisory, bottom-up, cooperative-federalism** model. It **recommends** but does **not allocate
funds** (that's the Finance Ministry).

> 🔑 **Planning Commission = allocated funds (top-down); NITI Aayog = advises (bottom-up).**
> 🔑 **4 functions:** policy design · cooperative federalism · monitoring & evaluation · think-tank.

## Must-Know Facts
- ✅ Chair = **PM**; **Vice-Chair** (PM-appointed, Cabinet-Minister rank); **CEO** (Secretary rank).
- ✅ **Governing Council** = all state CMs + UT (legislature) CMs + LGs of other UTs.
- ✅ Wings: **Research, Consultancy, Team India**.
- ✅ **No fund-allocation power**; only recommends.
- ✅ Five-Year Plans ended (**12th Plan 2012–17**) → replaced by 3-yr Action Agenda / 7-yr Strategy / 15-yr Vision.

### 5.0 Direct Mains demands owned by this Core file

- **2018 · GS-III · Q11** — Demand (neutral rendering): *how the principles followed by NITI Aayog differ from those followed by the erstwhile Planning Commission.* Directive: **Discuss** (lay out and weigh the differences) · **15 marks · 250 words**.
  - **Core routing supersedes the older `advanced/34_NITI-Aayog.md` pointer for this demand.**
  - **Executable route:** (1) **Thesis** — NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission's **centralised, directive, fund-allocating** model with an **advisory, cooperative-and-competitive-federalism, bottom-up think-tank** model; the change is one of **principle and function, not legal status**, and critics say it traded fiscal leverage for consultation. (2) **Same legal footing** — **both are non-constitutional and non-statutory**, created by **Union Cabinet resolutions** (Planning Commission, **15 March 1950**; NITI Aayog, **1 January 2015**), so the shift is of *principle*, not of legal form. (3) **Principle 1 — federalism** — the Planning Commission imposed **one-size-fits-all** plans top-down; NITI works through **cooperative federalism**, with States as participants in the **Governing Council**, and **competitive federalism** through ranking indices. (4) **Principle 2 — funds** — the Planning Commission **allocated funds/grants** to States (real leverage); NITI has **no fund-allocation power** — that moved to the **Finance Ministry** — and only **recommends**. (5) **Principle 3 — planning method** — **Five-Year Plans** (imperative; ended with the **12th Plan, 2012–17**) gave way to a **3-year Action Agenda, 7-year Strategy and 15-year Vision** (indicative). (6) **Principle 4 — role** — from plan-formulation-plus-allocation to **think-tank, monitoring and evaluation, and knowledge/innovation hub**. (7) **Institutional consequence** — the **National Development Council's** plan-approval role effectively lapsed; the Governing Council is now the apex forum. (8) **Counter (the "Discuss" balance)** — without fund power NITI lacks leverage to compel reform, and **Finance Commission (Art 280) transfers and centrally sponsored schemes** now carry the fiscal weight; but **competitive federalism** (indices, Aspirational Districts) is a new, softer instrument of influence. (9) **Verdict** — a shift from directive resource-planning to advisory, cooperative-and-competitive coordination; empowerment or emasculation depends on whether soft influence can substitute for fiscal leverage.

### 5.2 Qualified thesis options

- *NITI Aayog and the Planning Commission share a legal status — both are creatures of a Cabinet resolution — so the real difference between them is one of principle: command planning versus indicative coordination.*
- *NITI's founding principle is cooperative federalism, but its operative instrument is competitive federalism — the ranking index — because, having lost the power to allocate funds, influence is the only leverage it retains.*
- *Removing the Planning Commission's fund-allocation role clarified the fiscal system but hollowed the planning body's leverage; the fiscal weight now sits with the Finance Commission and centrally sponsored schemes, not with NITI.*
- *NITI Aayog is best judged not as a planner but as a think-tank and evaluator; measured against that redefined role it succeeds, and measured against the old planning role it was never meant to occupy it appears toothless.*

### 5.5 Bank B — Composition and institutional design (this file's owned half)

- **Claim:** NITI is a PM-led federal forum, not a bureaucratic planning cell. **Evidence:** **Chairperson (PM)**; a **Vice-Chairperson** (PM-appointed, **Cabinet-Minister rank**); a **Governing Council** of **all State CMs + CMs of UTs with legislatures + LGs of other UTs**; **Regional Councils** for multi-State issues (fixed tenure, PM-convened). **Significance:** the Governing Council institutionalises the cooperative-federalism principle. **Limitation:** ⚠️ the Council's influence depends on how often it meets and whether States attend.
- **Claim:** the professional core is expert and executive, not elected. **Evidence:** **Full-time members** (Minister-of-State rank), **max 2 part-time members** (from universities/research bodies), **max 4 ex-officio** Union Ministers (PM-nominated), **special invitees** (domain experts), and a **CEO** (PM-appointed, **Secretary rank**). **Significance:** blends domain expertise with administrative reach. **Limitation:** all key appointments flow from the PM, so autonomy is executive-dependent.
- **Claim:** NITI works through hubs/wings, not plan directorates. **Evidence:** a **Team India Hub** (cooperative federalism + policy design) and a **Knowledge & Innovation Hub**, with Research, Consultancy and Team India wings. **Significance:** structure follows the think-tank + coordination role. **Limitation:** ⚠️ these are administrative arrangements, revisable by the executive.

### 5.6 Bank C — Instruments and flagship initiatives (covers the routed 2019 AIM demand)

- **Claim:** NITI's chief instrument of influence is measurement, not money. **Evidence:** the **SDG India Index**, the **Aspirational Districts Programme (2018)** and **Aspirational Blocks Programme (2023)**, the **National Multidimensional Poverty Index**, and sectoral indices (Health, Water, Innovation, Export Preparedness). **Significance:** ranking States operationalises **competitive federalism**. **Limitation:** indices nudge, they cannot compel.
- **Claim:** the **Atal Innovation Mission (AIM)** is an initiative **housed within NITI Aayog**, not a separate statutory body. **Evidence:** AIM is NITI's flagship innovation-and-entrepreneurship umbrella, running **Atal Tinkering Labs** and **Atal Incubation Centres**. **Significance:** ✅ its institutional setup — **an initiative under NITI Aayog** — is exactly the routed 2019 Prelims point. **Limitation/status:** ⚠️ do not describe AIM as an autonomous or statutory agency; it is a NITI-anchored mission.
- **Claim:** the four functions define what NITI is for. **Evidence:** **(1) design policy and programme framework; (2) foster cooperative federalism; (3) monitoring and evaluation; (4) think-tank / knowledge and innovation hub**. **Significance:** every "what does NITI do" answer maps to these four. **Limitation:** none is a power to allocate or compel.

### 5.7 Bank D — The fund-leverage critique and the competitive-federalism counter (boundary with Economy)

- **Claim:** NITI lacks the Planning Commission's leverage. **Evidence:** it **cannot allocate funds**; **Finance Commission (Art 280)** tax devolution and **centrally sponsored schemes** now carry the fiscal weight. **Significance:** the "toothless think-tank" critique rests here. **Limitation/boundary:** ⚠️ the *mechanics* of devolution and CSS financing are owned by the **Economy folder** — cite them here only to make the institutional-design point, not to work the fiscal maths.
- **Claim:** soft influence can still move States. **Evidence:** **competitive federalism** — index rankings, Aspirational Districts, best-practice diffusion — creates reputational incentives to reform. **Significance:** a new instrument the Planning Commission never used. **Limitation:** ⚠️ its effect is uneven and contested.
- **Claim:** the change reshaped the planning architecture, not just one body. **Evidence:** **Five-Year Plans ended (12th Plan, 2012–17)** and the **NDC's** role lapsed. **Significance:** a systemic shift from imperative to indicative planning. **Limitation:** ⚠️ some argue this left a **strategic-planning vacuum**.

### 5.8 Institutional incentives, mechanism and consequences

- ⚠️ **Losing fund-allocation power** changed NITI's incentive structure: unable to reward compliance with grants, it competes for influence through ideas, evaluation and rankings — persuasion replaces purse-strings.
- ⚠️ **The Governing Council** gives States voice but not a veto; its practical weight rises and falls with attendance and the political alignment between the Centre and the States, so cooperative federalism is realised episodically.
- ⚠️ **Competitive federalism via indices** can improve outcomes where States value the ranking, but it can also incentivise data-management over real reform — the classic measurement-distortion risk.

### 5.9 Criticism, counter-argument, variation and implementation constraints

- **"Toothless think-tank" critique:** ⚠️ without fund power NITI cannot enforce reform; the fiscal levers sit with the Finance Commission and CSS, so its recommendations depend on political acceptance.
- **Counter:** ⚠️ NITI was **redefined**, not weakened — as an evaluator and cooperative-federalism forum it does work the Planning Commission never did (SDG Index, Aspirational Districts), and removing allocation from a planning body arguably rationalised fiscal federalism.
- **Variation:** ⚠️ engagement varies — some States have **boycotted Governing Council meetings** over devolution grievances, so cooperative federalism is not uniform.
- **Implementation constraints:** ⚠️ infrequent Council meetings, executive-dependent appointments, the absence of any binding instrument, and the unresolved question of who does **long-horizon strategic planning** all limit NITI's reach.

### 5.10 Verdict scaffolds

- **Principles verdict:** "NITI Aayog and the Planning Commission share a legal status but not a philosophy — the Commission commanded and allocated, NITI advises and coordinates — so the difference is of principle, from imperative planning to indicative, cooperative-and-competitive federalism."
- **Federalism verdict:** "NITI institutionalised cooperative federalism in the Governing Council and pioneered competitive federalism through indices, but with no fiscal leverage its influence is persuasive, not directive."
- **Leverage verdict:** "Judged as a think-tank and evaluator NITI succeeds; judged as a planner with the Commission's clout it cannot, because the fund power it would need now rests with the Finance Ministry."
