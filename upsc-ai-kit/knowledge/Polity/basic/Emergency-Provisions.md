## Snapshot — 3 emergencies
| Type | Article | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ **National** | **352** | War / external aggression / **armed rebellion** |
| ✅ **President's Rule** (State) | **356** | Failure of constitutional machinery in a state |
| ✅ **Financial** | **360** | Threat to India's financial stability/credit |

## Core idea
✅ Emergency provisions can **temporarily expand Union executive, legislative or fiscal control** without abolishing States or amending the federal Constitution. Under Article 352, State governments and legislatures continue; Article 356 has sharper State-specific consequences.
The **44th Amendment (1978)** added most safeguards after the misuse of the **1975 Emergency**.

## National Emergency (352) — key numbers
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| ✅ Recommendation | **Cabinet in writing** (not PM alone) |
| ✅ Approval | **1 month**, **special majority** |
| ✅ Duration | 6 months, **indefinite** renewal |
| ✅ Revocation | LS simple-majority disapproval → must revoke |
| ✅ Declared | **1962, 1971, 1975** (3 times) |

✅ **FR effect:** During a war/external-aggression Emergency, **Article 358** automatically removes Article 19 restrictions only for emergency-related laws carrying the constitutional recital and action under them. **Article 359** requires a presidential order suspending the right to move specified courts for enforcement of named rights; **Articles 20 and 21 remain enforceable**.

## President's Rule (356)
✅ Approval within **2 months, simple majority**; max **3 years**. Beyond 1 year needs a National
Emergency + **ECI certification**. CM/CoM dismissed; assembly suspended/dissolved; **HC untouched**.
> 🔑 **Bommai (1994):** Art 356 is **judicially reviewable**; majority tested on the **floor**;
> **secularism = basic feature**; court can **restore** a dismissed government.

## Financial Emergency (360)
✅ Approval **2 months, simple majority**; then **indefinite** (no renewal). Centre can cut salaries of
state staff **and SC/HC judges**. ✅ **Never declared.**

## Quick contrast
> 🔑 **352 = Special majority + indefinite + affects FRs + LS can revoke.**
> 🔑 **356 = Simple majority + max 3 years + no FR effect + President revokes.**

## Must-Know Facts
- ✅ "Internal disturbance" → replaced by "**armed rebellion**" (44th Amdt).
- ✅ **Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar:** emergency provisions = "**life-breath of the Constitution**."
- ✅ Ambedkar hoped **Art 356** would stay a "**dead letter**" (last resort).
- ✅ Financial Emergency modelled on the US **National Recovery Act 1933**.

## 📰 Current link
📰 ✅ **Current control, 28 August 2026:** no National Emergency or Financial Emergency is in force. President's Rule imposed in Manipur on 13 February 2025 was revoked on **4 February 2026** when an elected government was restored. The 50th anniversary of the 1975 Emergency revived safeguard debates; this changed no constitutional rule.

### 9.0 Direct Mains demands owned by this Core file

- **2018 · GS-II · Q3** — Demand (neutral rendering): *the circumstances in which a Financial Emergency can be proclaimed and the consequences that follow.* Directive: **Under what circumstances** (+ consequences) · **10 marks · 150 words**.
  - **Core routing supersedes the older `advanced/14_Emergency-Provisions.md` pointer for this demand.**
  - **Executable route:** (1) **Provision** — **Art 360**. (2) **Circumstance** — the President's satisfaction that the **financial stability or credit of India or any part** is threatened. (3) **Procedure** — proclamation approved by both Houses within **two months** by **simple majority**; then continues **indefinitely** with **no maximum and no renewal**; revocable anytime; reviewable post-44th. (4) **Consequences** — Centre may direct States to observe **canons of financial propriety**; **reduce salaries** of persons serving a State; require all State **Money Bills** to be reserved for the President; and reduce the salaries of **Union employees and even Supreme Court and High Court judges**. (5) **Effect** — converts the federal financial order into a **unitary** one. (6) **Verdict** — a drastic centralising last-resort power; ✅ **never proclaimed** (closest: the 1991 balance-of-payments crisis), which itself signals its severity.
- **2023 · GS-II · Q13** — Demand (neutral rendering): *the legal and political factors behind the reduced frequency of Article 356 since the mid-1990s.* Directive: **Account for** · **15 marks · 250 words**.
  - **Core routing supersedes the older `advanced/14_Emergency-Provisions.md` pointer for this demand.**
  - **Executable route:** (1) **Thesis** — the decline is explained by a **legal watershed** (*Bommai*) and a **political shift** (the coalition era and a federalised party system), reinforced by commission norms. (2) **Legal factor** — *S.R. Bommai* (1994): Art 356 is **judicially reviewable**; majority must be tested on the **floor of the House**, not by the Governor's subjective report; the President's satisfaction must rest on **relevant material** with the burden on the Centre; the Assembly may be dissolved **only after Parliament approves**; courts can **restore** a wrongly dismissed government; Art 356 is an **exceptional, last-resort** power. (3) **Legal reinforcement** — *Rameshwar Prasad* (2006) struck down the 2005 Bihar Assembly dissolution as mala fide. (4) **Political factor** — post-1989/1996 coalition governments and powerful regional parties raised the political cost of dismissing State governments. (5) **Institutional factor** — the **Sarkaria** and **Punchhi** Commissions urged sparing, last-resort use with a prior warning and a floor test. (6) **Verdict** — Art 356 has moved from a routine partisan instrument (invoked **over 125 times**, mostly before the mid-1990s) to a genuinely exceptional remedy — judicial review and political federalism disciplining a centralising power together.

### 9.2 Qualified thesis options

- *The emergency provisions make India "unitary in an emergency without a formal amendment", but the 44th Amendment and* Bommai *have converted them from a standing threat to democracy into an exceptional, reviewable and largely self-limiting power.*
- *The decline of Article 356 since the mid-1990s is not a change in the text but in its politics and its case law: coalition federalism raised the cost and* Bommai *raised the risk.*
- *The non-suspendability of Articles 20 and 21 and the overruling of* ADM Jabalpur *mean the darkest possibility of the 1975 Emergency — detention beyond the reach of the courts — is now constitutionally foreclosed.*
- *A Financial Emergency has never been proclaimed not because Article 360 is weak but because it is so drastic that its very existence is a deterrent and a signal of last resort.*
- *Emergency provisions are simultaneously the Constitution's "safety-valve" and its most dangerous instrument; their legitimacy rests entirely on the post-1978 safeguards that make their misuse justiciable.*

### 9.4 Bank A — National Emergency (Art 352) and the amendment history

| Element | Anchor | Mechanism | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| ✅ Trigger | **Art 352** — war, external aggression or **armed rebellion** (even imminent danger) | War/external aggression = **External** Emergency; armed rebellion = **Internal** | "Armed rebellion" replaced "internal disturbance" via the **44th Amendment (1978)** |
| ✅ Recommendation | 44th Amendment | Only on the **written recommendation of the Cabinet**, not the PM alone | A direct fix for the 1975 misuse |
| ✅ Approval & duration | Art 352 | Approved within **one month** by **special majority**; continues **six months** at a time, renewable **indefinitely** | Earlier it was two months/simple majority (pre-44th) |
| ✅ Revocation | 44th Amendment | President may revoke anytime; **must** revoke if the **Lok Sabha** disapproves by simple majority; **one-tenth** of LS members can requisition a special sitting within 14 days | The Rajya Sabha has no revocation role |
| ✅ Declarations | Historical fact | **1962** (China), **1971** (Pakistan), **1975** (internal disturbance) | The 1975 proclamation is the contested one; **Shah Commission** faulted it |

- **Amendment-history capsule:** ✅ **38th Amendment (1975)** made the President's satisfaction **immune from judicial review**; ✅ **42nd Amendment (1976)** allowed an Emergency confined to **part of India** and extended legislature terms; ✅ **44th Amendment (1978)** added the safeguards above and **restored judicial review**.

### 9.5 Bank B — Fundamental rights in emergency (Art 358, 359) and the *ADM Jabalpur* line

| Element | Anchor | Precise proposition | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| ✅ Art 358 | Art 358 | **Automatically suspends Art 19**; post-44th only during a **War/External** Emergency and only for laws related to it | Does **not** operate in an armed-rebellion (internal) emergency |
| ✅ Art 359 | Art 359 | President may **suspend the enforcement** (not the rights themselves) of **specified** Fundamental Rights | Post-44th, **Articles 20 and 21 can NEVER be suspended** |
| ✅ *ADM Jabalpur v. Shivkant Shukla* (1976) | Supreme Court | During the 1975 Emergency the majority held that a detainee had **no locus to move any court for enforcement of Art 21**; **Justice H.R. Khanna dissented** | The Constitution's low point; the direct trigger for the 44th Amendment's Art 20/21 protection |
| ✅ *K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India* (2017) | Nine-judge Bench | Expressly held *ADM Jabalpur* to have been **wrongly decided** and **overruled** it | Detention beyond judicial reach is now constitutionally foreclosed |

### 9.6 Bank C — President's Rule (Art 356): mechanism, *Bommai* and decline

| Element | Anchor | Mechanism / proposition | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| ✅ Ground | **Art 356** (with **Art 355** duty and **Art 365** trigger) | President satisfied that a State's government "**cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution**", on the Governor's report **or otherwise** | Art 365 (failure to comply with Central directions) is a distinct trigger |
| ✅ Approval & duration | Art 356 | Approved within **two months** by **simple majority**; continues six months at a time up to a **maximum of three years** | Beyond **one year**, extension needs a **National Emergency in force** + **ECI certification** (44th Amendment) |
| ✅ Consequences | Art 356 | President assumes the State executive's functions, **dismisses the CoM/CM**, Parliament exercises State legislative power, Assembly suspended or dissolved | The **State High Court's** powers **cannot** be taken over |
| ✅ *S.R. Bommai* (1994) | Nine-judge Bench | Proclamation is **judicially reviewable**; majority tested on the **floor of the House**; satisfaction on **relevant material** (burden on the Centre); Assembly dissolved **only after Parliament approves**; courts can **restore** a dismissed government; **secularism is a basic feature**; Art 356 is a **last resort** | The single most important limiting authority |
| ✅ *Rameshwar Prasad* (2006) | Supreme Court | The **2005 dissolution of the Bihar Assembly** on the Governor's report was **unconstitutional and mala fide** | Shows continued post-*Bommai* judicial vigilance |
| ✅ Chronology | Historical fact | Imposed **over 125 times**, the great majority **before the mid-1990s** | Ambedkar hoped it would remain a "**dead letter**" |

### 9.7 Bank D — Financial Emergency (Art 360)

- ✅ **Trigger → Art 360:** the President's satisfaction that the **financial stability or credit of India** or any part is threatened.
- ✅ **Procedure:** approval within **two months** by **simple majority**; once approved it continues **indefinitely** with **no renewal**.
- ✅ **Consequences:** Centre directs States on **canons of financial propriety**; may **reduce the salaries** of State and Union employees **and of Supreme Court and High Court judges**; State Money Bills reserved for the President.
- ✅ **Status:** **never proclaimed** (closest: the **1991** crisis); ⚠️ modelled on the US **National Recovery/Industrial Recovery Act, 1933** (per Ambedkar); H.N. Kunzru warned it threatens States' financial autonomy.

### 9.8 Criticism, commission recommendations, counter-argument and variation

- **Constituent Assembly criticism:** ✅ **H.V. Kamath** ("totalitarian/police state"), **K.T. Shah** ("chapter of reaction and retrogression"), **T.T. Krishnamachari** ("constitutional dictatorship"), **H.N. Kunzru** (threat to fiscal autonomy); defenders **Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar** ("very **life-breath** of the Constitution") and **Mahabir Tyagi** ("**safety-valve**").
- **Commission norms on Art 356:** ✅ the **Sarkaria Commission (1988)** and **Punchhi Commission (2007–10)** recommended Art 356 be used **only as a last resort**, after a **warning** to the State and, wherever possible, a **floor test**; the Governor's report should be a "speaking document". ⚠️ These are recommendations, not binding law, but they shaped the post-1994 restraint.
- **Counter-argument (safeguards work):** ⚠️ the 44th Amendment, *Bommai*, *Rameshwar Prasad* and the overruling of *ADM Jabalpur* show the provisions are now **reviewable and self-limiting** rather than a standing dictatorship.
- **Federal/State variation:** ⚠️ Art 356 was historically used disproportionately against **opposition-ruled States**; its decline tracks the rise of regional parties and coalition dependence.
- **Implementation constraint:** ⚠️ the Governor's discretion and the "or otherwise" satisfaction remain the weak points, which is why floor-test controversies still reach the courts.

### 9.9 Verdict scaffolds

- **Financial Emergency verdict:** "Article 360's drastic, unitary consequences explain both why it exists and why no government has dared to use it — its deterrent value lies precisely in its severity."
- **Article 356 verdict:** "The decline of President's Rule is the clearest proof that a centralising provision can be disciplined without amending it, once judicial review and coalition politics raise the cost of its misuse."
- **Federal-balance verdict:** "Emergency provisions still make India unitary in a crisis, but after 1978 and *Bommai* they do so under justiciable limits, so the safety-valve no longer doubles as a loaded weapon."

### 9.11 Factual-risk and current-status controls

- ✅ Emergency provisions are **Part XVIII, Articles 352–360**; do not misplace them.
- ✅ National Emergency has been declared **three times (1962, 1971, 1975)**; a Financial Emergency **never**.
- ✅ The **44th Amendment (1978)**, not the 42nd, added the pro-liberty safeguards and made Articles 20 and 21 non-suspendable; the **38th Amendment (1975)** created the judicial-review immunity that the 44th later removed.
- ✅ *ADM Jabalpur* (1976) was **overruled** by *K.S. Puttaswamy* (2017) — never cite *ADM Jabalpur* as good law.
- ✅ *Bommai* requires a **floor test**; a Governor's subjective report is not a valid substitute — do not attribute a "deemed" loss of majority to the Governor.
- ⚠️ Cite the President's-Rule count as "**over 125 times**" (the commonly stated figure) rather than a false-precise number; note most predate the mid-1990s.
- 📰 ⚠️ The **50th anniversary of the 1975 Emergency (25 June 2025)** and the notified observance revived safeguard debates — frame it as commemoration/analysis, not as any change in law.
