## Legislative relations
✅ **7th Schedule:** standard examination counts are Union **(100)**, State **(61)**, Concurrent **(52)**; **residuary → Centre**. Omitted and inserted entry numbers can produce different literal counts.
✅ **42nd Amdt** moved **5 subjects State→Concurrent**: education, forests, weights & measures, wildlife
protection, administration of justice.
> 🔑 **Parliament can legislate on State List in 5 cases:** **"R-N-S-I-P"** = **R**ajya Sabha resolution
> (Art 249) · **N**ational Emergency (250) · **S**tates' request (252) · **I**nternational agreement (253) ·
> **P**resident's Rule.

## Administrative relations
✅ **Art 365:** failure to comply with Union directions may support a presidential conclusion under Article 356; it does **not** trigger President's Rule automatically. Articles 355–357 separately govern Union duty, proclamation and exercise of State legislative power, while Article 360 permits financial-emergency directions and reservation of State Money Bills.
✅ **All-India Services (Art 312):** IAS, IPS, **Indian Forest Service (IFoS,
1966)** — not the Indian Foreign Service; a new AIS needs a Rajya Sabha resolution.
✅ **Art 355:** Centre must protect states from external aggression/internal disturbance.

## Financial relations
✅ Taxing power must be expressly conferred; the Concurrent List contains **no general tax entry**.
GST is governed by the **special concurrent legislative power in Art 246-A**, not by a
Concurrent-List entry. ✅ **80th Amdt (2000):** implemented the alternative tax-devolution scheme
recommended by the Tenth Finance Commission. ✅ **Grants:** statutory (Art 275,
FC-based) + discretionary (Art 282).
> 🔑 **Tax-distribution articles:** 268 (Centre levies, state keeps) · 269 (assigned) · 269-A (inter-state
> GST) · **270 (shared via Finance Commission)** · 271 (surcharge, Centre only).

## Inter-State relations
✅ **Water disputes (Art 262):** Parliament may provide tribunal adjudication and exclude ordinary court jurisdiction over the defined dispute. The 2019 Amendment Bill lapsed with the Seventeenth Lok Sabha; a revised proposal is **not current law**. Cauvery implementation now also operates through the 2018 statutory scheme and management institutions.
✅ **Inter-State Council (Art 263):** set up **1990** (Sarkaria Commission), **chaired by PM**, advisory.
✅ **Art 301:** trade & commerce "**shall be free**." ✅ **Zonal Councils:** **statutory (SRA 1956)**, 5
zones, chaired by **Union Home Minister**; **NEC = 1971 Act**.

## Must-Know Facts
- ✅ **Finance Commission (Art 280):** constitutional and periodic (every fifth year or
  earlier); Laxmikanth describes it as quasi-judicial and the "balancing wheel of fiscal federalism."
- ✅ **GST Council (Art 279-A):** joint Centre-state forum on rates/exemptions.
- ✅ States can borrow **only within India** & need Centre's consent if a Central loan is outstanding.
- ✅ Concurrent conflict → **Central law prevails** (unless state law got President's assent).

### 8.0 Direct Mains demands owned by this Core file

- **2019 · GS-II · Q4** — Demand (neutral rendering): *the principle of Federal Supremacy and Harmonious Construction.* Directive: **Explain** · **10 marks · 150 words**.
  - **Core routing supersedes the older `advanced/13_Centre-State-and-Inter-State-Relations.md` pointer for this demand.**
  - **Executable route:** (1) **Define both** — federal supremacy: where Union and State laws conflict on a Concurrent subject, the Union law prevails; harmonious construction: courts first interpret the entries so **both** operate, invoking supremacy only when reconciliation fails. (2) **Anchor** — **Art 246** (Union List predominance, "notwithstanding"); **Art 254(1)** repugnancy → Union prevails; **Art 254(2)** a President-assented State law prevails in that State, subject to Parliament's override. (3) **Doctrines** — **pith and substance** (true nature; incidental encroachment survives), **colourable legislation** (cannot do indirectly what is barred directly), then **harmonious construction**. (4) **Order of predominance** — Union > Concurrent > State. (5) **Sarkaria** — federal supremacy is "a technique to avoid absurdity, to resolve conflict". (6) **Verdict** — harmonious construction is the first rule that preserves both jurisdictions; federal supremacy is the tie-breaker of last resort.
- **2020 · GS-II · Q11** — Demand (neutral rendering): *centralising tendencies, emergency legislation and the Farm Acts.* Directive: **Elucidate** · **15 marks · 250 words**.
  - **Core routing supersedes the older `advanced/13_Centre-State-and-Inter-State-Relations.md` pointer for this demand.**
  - **Executable route:** (1) **Thesis** — recent practice shows a centralising drift that uses Concurrent-List and disaster powers to enter the State field, stressing cooperative federalism. (2) **Emergency legislation** — the **Disaster Management Act, 2005** and **Epidemic Diseases Act, 1897** used for nationwide COVID-19 directions. (3) **Farm Acts (2020)** — enacted citing **Entry 33, Concurrent List** (trade/commerce in foodstuffs) but touching **agriculture and markets (State List Entries 14 and 28)** — the core federal grievance; **repealed in 2021**. (4) **Other centralising tools** — Art 249/250/252/253, All-India Services, rising **cesses/surcharges** shrinking the divisible pool, centrally sponsored scheme conditionalities, GST subsuming State taxes (Art 246A/279A). (5) **Counter-balance** — *S.R. Bommai* (federalism a basic feature), the GST Council's shared decisions, Finance Commission transfers, and the Farm Acts' repeal showing political federalism at work. (6) **Verdict** — centralisation via emergency/Concurrent legislation is real but bounded; the corrective is genuine consultation (Inter-State Council, Art 263) and respect for the List boundaries.
- **2024 · GS-II · Q13** — Demand (neutral rendering): *recent changes in Centre–State relations; suggest measures to strengthen federalism and build trust.* Directive: **Suggest** · **15 marks · 250 words**.
  - **Core routing supersedes the older `advanced/13_Centre-State-and-Inter-State-Relations.md` pointer for this demand.**
  - **Executable route:** (1) **Recent changes** — GST reshaping fiscal autonomy (Art 246A/279A), Planning Commission → **NITI Aayog**, Governor-assent frictions, rising cesses/surcharges, CSS conditionalities. (2) **Diagnose the trust deficit** across fiscal, administrative (Governor, CBI "general consent") and legislative dimensions. (3) **Suggest (directive demands solutions)** — predictable, rules-based transfers and fewer cesses; **regular convening of the Inter-State Council (Art 263)**; bounded Governor discretion per **Sarkaria/Punchhi/Bommai**; GST Council by consensus; transparent assent procedures consistent with the 20 November 2025 Article 143 opinion (**no court-created rigid deadline or deemed assent**); implement Punchhi. (4) **Verdict** — trust is built through institutionalised consultation and rules-based finance, not ad hoc goodwill.
- **2025 · GS-II · Q14** — Demand (neutral rendering): *the evolving pattern of Centre–State financial relations and fiscal federalism.* Directive: **Examine** · **15 marks · 250 words**.
  - **Core routing supersedes the older `advanced/13_Centre-State-and-Inter-State-Relations.md` pointer for this demand.**
  - **Executable route:** (1) **Thesis** — fiscal federalism has moved from discretionary plan-based transfers to a rules-based Finance Commission/GST architecture, but vertical imbalance and the cess/surcharge leakage persist. (2) **Evolution** — discretionary grants (Art 282) and the Planning Commission/NDC era → **14th FC** raising devolution to **42%** → 15th FC → **16th FC (Panagariya, 41% retained, effective 1 Apr 2026)**; GST (101st Amendment) pooling State taxes; NITI Aayog replacing the Planning Commission (2015). (3) **Instruments** — Art 268–271 distribution, **Art 275** statutory grants, **Art 282** discretionary grants, **Art 280** Finance Commission, **Art 293** borrowing, **Art 279A** GST Council. (4) **Live grievances** — cesses/surcharges (Art 271) outside the divisible pool, the horizontal-formula debate, ending GST compensation, conditional CSS. (5) **Verdict** — reforms have strengthened rules-based sharing, but genuine fiscal federalism needs shrinking cesses, a fair horizontal formula and honoured GST commitments.

### 8.2 Qualified thesis options

- *Centre–State relations are not fixed by the text but negotiated in practice: the Constitution tilts to the Centre, yet* Bommai*, the Finance Commission and GST bargaining give the States a real, if uneven, countervailing weight.*
- *Harmonious construction, not federal supremacy, is the everyday rule; supremacy is the exceptional tie-breaker, so a federalism that appears centralised on paper is far more accommodative in operation.*
- *India's fiscal federalism has become more rules-based since the 14th Finance Commission, but the growth of cesses and surcharges outside the divisible pool has quietly re-centralised what devolution formally decentralised.*
- *Emergency and Concurrent-List legislation are legitimate central powers, but their use to enter core State subjects — as with the 2020 Farm Acts — converts cooperative federalism into contested federalism.*
- *The weakness of Indian federalism is institutional under-use, not constitutional design: the Inter-State Council meets rarely and the Punchhi reforms remain unimplemented.*

### 8.4 Bank A — Legislative relations (Art 245–255)

Every unit: **claim → named Article/doctrine → mechanism → limitation.**

| Element | Anchor | Mechanism | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| ✅ Distribution of subjects | **Seventh Schedule** — standard counts: Union (100), State (61), Concurrent (52); **Art 246** predominance | Union > Concurrent > State; residuary → **Centre** | Omitted/inserted numbering explains alternate literal counts; 42nd Amendment moved 5 subjects **State → Concurrent** |
| ✅ Parliament in the State field | **Art 249** (RS resolution), **250** (Emergency), **252** (States' request), **253** (treaties), **356** | Centre may legislate on State subjects in five extraordinary situations | Art 249 needs only a **2/3 RS resolution**, not an emergency |
| ✅ Repugnancy | **Art 254(1)** Union prevails; **Art 254(2)** President-assented State law prevails in that State | Resolves Concurrent-List conflict | Parliament can still override a 254(2) State law |
| ✅ Pith and substance | Judicial doctrine | Tests the law's **true nature**; incidental encroachment survives | A competence test **across** Lists |
| ✅ Colourable legislation | Judicial doctrine | Invalidates doing **indirectly** what is barred directly | Looks at substance, not form |
| ✅ Harmonious construction & federal supremacy | Judicial doctrine + **Art 254** | Reconcile overlapping entries; only if impossible, Union prevails | Sarkaria: supremacy is "a technique to avoid absurdity" |
| ✅ Residuary power | **Art 248** read with **Entry 97, List I** | Any matter, **including any tax**, not enumerated in List II or III belongs to Parliament | The **opposite of the US and Australian** design, where the residue stays with the States — this is the single sharpest textual proof of centralised federalism |
| ✅ Territorial nexus | Judicial doctrine on **Art 245(1)–(2)** | A State law may reach an extra-territorial object if there is a **real and not illusory connection** with the State, and the liability is **pertinent to that connection**; a Union law is never invalid merely for extra-territorial operation (**Art 245(2)**) | It is a **competence** doctrine about *reach*, not about *subject-matter* — do not confuse it with pith and substance, which is about which List a law falls in |

➡️ **Doctrine route:** `Constitutional-Interpretation-Doctrines.md` consolidates pith and substance,
colourable legislation, territorial nexus, repugnancy, harmonious construction/federal supremacy
and incidental/implied powers. This file retains their full Centre–State application and Article
245–254 evidence.

**Why the residuary entry matters analytically (a demand family in its own right):**

- **Claim:** the residuary clause converts every constitutionally unforeseen subject into a Union subject by default. **Evidence:** ✅ **Art 248** + **Entry 97, List I**; ⚠️ the classic modern illustrations are new-technology and new-service fields that no List anticipated in 1950. **Mechanism:** the Constituent Assembly deliberately reversed the American rule because the Union had to be able to act on unforeseen national problems without a constitutional amendment. **Limitation:** ⚠️ the residue is genuinely **residual** — a subject that fairly falls in List II cannot be pulled into Entry 97 merely because the Union wants it, and the Supreme Court tests a law first against Lists II and III before reaching Entry 97.
- ⚠️ **Answer use:** deploy this against any "is Indian federalism truly federal?" demand; it is stronger evidence than a rhetorical Wheare quotation because it is textual and non-contestable.

### 8.8A Bank F — Freedom of trade, commerce and intercourse (Art 301–307): the "economic federalism" family

> This is a distinct demand family from legislative and financial relations. It is what a question on
> **"India as a common market"**, on **entry tax/octroi**, on **GST as a single market** or on
> **inter-State barriers** is actually testing.

| Element | Anchor | Mechanism | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| ✅ The guarantee | **Art 301** | Trade, commerce and intercourse **throughout the territory of India** shall be free — a limitation on **both** the Union and the States | It restrains **legislative and executive** power; it is **not** a Fundamental Right and is not enforceable under Art 32 as such |
| ✅ Parliament's power | **Art 302** | Parliament may restrict freedom **in the public interest** | ✅ **Art 303(1)** bars **both** Parliament and State legislatures from making a law giving **preference between States**; ✅ **Art 303(2)** lets **Parliament alone** do so to deal with **scarcity of goods** |
| ✅ States' power | **Art 304(a)** | A State may tax imported goods **only if** like domestic goods are taxed equally — a non-discrimination rule | ✅ **Art 304(b)** allows reasonable public-interest restrictions **but only with the President's previous sanction** |
| ✅ Institutional backstop | **Art 307** | Parliament may appoint an authority to carry out Art 301–304 | ⚠️ **No such authority has been constituted**, so the enforcement has been entirely judicial — state this as a design gap, not as a scandal |
| ✅ Judicial construction | **Atiabari Tea Co.** (1961) and **Automobile Transport (Rajasthan)** (1962) | Only laws that **directly and immediately** restrict the movement of trade attract Art 301; **regulatory and compensatory** measures do not | ⚠️ In **Jindal Stainless v. State of Haryana (2016)** a nine-judge bench **rejected the "compensatory tax" doctrine** as a judicial creation with no textual basis, and held that a tax is tested against **Art 304(a) non-discrimination**, not against a compensation theory |

- **Claim → evidence → mechanism → limitation (ready-made unit):** *A single national market in India is a constitutional command that was, for six decades, enforced through a judge-made compensatory-tax exception.* ✅ **Evidence:** Art 301 with the *Atiabari*/*Automobile Transport* line, and its correction in ✅ **Jindal Stainless (2016)**. **Mechanism:** removing the compensatory-tax defence made **Art 304(a) non-discrimination** the operative test for State entry taxes. **Limitation:** ⚠️ the constitutional guarantee never abolished State fiscal borders by itself — that required the **101st Amendment and GST**, which is a *fiscal* solution to a problem Part XIII could only litigate.
- ⚠️ **Answer use:** this bank converts a generic "cooperative federalism" answer into an evidenced one, and it is the correct place to argue that **GST did constitutionally what Art 301 could not do administratively**.

### 8.8B Bank G — **Representation as the fourth dimension of Centre–State relations**

> ⚠️ **Label first:** the 2026 package below is a **defeated Bill** — never law, precedent or forecast.
> Full evidence unit: `Parliament.md` §7.4; Art 368 arithmetic: `Amendment-and-Basic-Structure.md` §8.4;
> the federal-theory framing: `Federal-System.md` §8.7A.

This file's three dimensions (legislative, administrative, financial) do not exhaust the relationship.
A fourth — **how many seats each State holds in the House that makes and unmakes the Union government** —
is where the sharpest live federal conflict now sits, and it converts a routine "Centre–State" answer
into a distinctive one.

| Claim | Named provision / dated event | Mechanism / significance | Limitation / caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Representation is a **Centre–State** variable, not merely an electoral-law detail | ✅ **Art 81(2)(a)** (uniform seat–population ratio across States); ✅ **Art 80** + **Fourth Schedule** (Rajya Sabha seats allotted by population, not equally); ✅ **Arts 54–55** (MPs' votes weight the Presidential Electoral College) | Seat share determines a State's leverage over Union law-making, government formation and even the Presidency — the currency in which every other federal bargain is finally settled | ⚠️ Do **not** treat the Rajya Sabha as an equal-representation federal chamber; it magnifies rather than offsets population weight |
| The current allocation rests on a **deliberate 50-year suspension** of that rule | ✅ **42nd Amendment (1976)** (freeze on the **1971** census) → ✅ **84th Amendment (2001)** (freeze extended to after the first census taken after **2026**; boundaries readjustable) → ✅ **87th Amendment (2003)** (boundary readjustment on the **2001** census) | The freeze is the clearest example in the Constitution of the Union **buying federal peace with seats**, insulating demographically successful States from losing weight | ⚠️ Distinguish **allocation between States** (frozen) from **boundary readjustment within a State** (permitted) |
| The freeze was tested in 2026 and the attempt **failed on the amending arithmetic** | ✅ **Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 2026**, **Delimitation Bill, 2026** and **Union Territories Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026** introduced **16 Apr 2026**; the **131st Amendment Bill** carried the **Art 81** ceiling change — **550 → 850** (up to **815** States + **35** UTs) — on a **2011-census** basis, decoupling the 106th Amendment's women's quota from the post-2026 census; ✅ **defeated 17 Apr 2026, 298 : 230** of **528** present and voting — short of two-thirds under **Art 368(2)**; the linked Bills did not pass | Shows that the operative federal safeguard here is **political arithmetic**, because **Art 329(a)** bars courts from questioning a delimitation law or an allotment of seats — there is no judicial route for an aggrieved State | ⚠️ Defeated Bill. **850** may be cited **only** as the **131st Amendment Bill's** text, never as law. **Prohibited:** State-wise projections, projected women's-seat counts, implementation years, or any claim that the defeat binds a future Parliament |
| 📰 The suspension now has a **scheduled** end-condition, which is why the dispute has become live | ✅ **Census 2027** under the **Census Act, 1948** / **Census Rules, 1990** (scheme approved by the Union Cabinet): **Houselisting & Housing Census 1 April – 30 September 2026**, each State/UT taking a fixed **30-day** period inside that window; **Population Enumeration February 2027**, and **September 2026** in snow-bound areas; **reference dates 00:00 hrs 1 March 2027** generally, **00:00 hrs 1 October 2026** for snow-bound areas; India's **first fully digital census** with self-enumeration; **caste enumeration** officially announced. ✅ Since the **106th Amendment commenced on 16 April 2026** (⚠️ commencement only — the women's reservation is **still not operational**), Census 2027 is at once the **"first census taken after 2026"** (84th Amendment / **Art 82**) and the **"first census taken after the commencement"** (**Art 334A(1)**) | Converts representational federalism from an abstract grievance into a scheduled Centre–State negotiation: the same enumeration supplies the figures that can end the seat freeze **and** switch on the women's quota, and the **caste** dimension adds a social-justice axis to what was a purely regional argument | ⚠️ **Notified conduct/reference dates ≠ publication of final figures**; Arts 82 and 334A(1) both turn on **publication**, whose date is **not notified**. **Prohibited:** any publication date, delimitation date, or effective date/year/election for the women's reservation; any nationwide houselisting progress or completion claim as on **13 August 2026** (the 30-day periods are State/UT-specific, so no exact nationwide wording is safe); any State-wise seat projection; any claim about what the caste data will show or how it will be used |

### 8.9 Criticism, counter-argument, State variation and implementation constraints

- **Fiscal grievance:** ⚠️ cesses and surcharges (Art 271) outside the divisible pool erode devolution even when the FC share rises; centrally sponsored schemes impose central priorities through conditional funding.
- **Administrative grievance:** ⚠️ Governor's assent delays and withdrawal of **CBI "general consent"** (DSPE Act) are the sharpest recent flashpoints; both are constitutionally arguable but politically contested.
- **Legislative grievance:** ⚠️ using Concurrent-List entries and disaster law to enter State subjects (Farm Acts, 2020) triggers the strongest federal pushback.
- **Counter-argument:** ⚠️ *Bommai*'s basic-feature holding, GST Council bargaining, rules-based FC devolution and the Farm Acts' repeal show that federal safeguards do operate.
- **Variation:** ⚠️ southern and eastern States press hardest on devolution and the Governor; the direction of friction shifts with which party holds the Union.
- **Implementation constraint:** ⚠️ the Inter-State Council's infrequency and unimplemented Punchhi reforms make the problem one of institutional under-use, not missing provisions.

### 8.10 Verdict scaffolds

- **Legislative verdict:** "Harmonious construction preserves both jurisdictions and federal supremacy resolves only the residue, so India's legislative federalism is far more accommodative in practice than the Union's textual dominance suggests."
- **Fiscal verdict:** "Rules-based devolution since the 14th Finance Commission has strengthened fiscal federalism, but the growth of cesses and surcharges outside the divisible pool means the reform is incomplete until the divisible pool is protected."
- **Trust-building verdict:** "Trust between the Centre and the States is an institutional output — of a regularly convened Inter-State Council, bounded gubernatorial discretion and predictable transfers — not a matter of political goodwill."
