## Core idea
The 73rd Amendment gave **constitutional status** to **Panchayati Raj Institutions (PRIs)** — a **3-tier**
(village–intermediate–district) system of local self-government, with mandatory elections, reservations, and a
5-year term, so panchayats no longer depend on the state government's will.

> 🔑 **Mnemonic "GET-RD-FE"** — **G**ram Sabha · **E**lections (direct seats at all 3 tiers) · **T**hree-tier ·
> **R**eservation (SC/ST + 1/3 women) · **D**uration 5 yrs · **F**inance Commission (SFC) · **E**lection
> Commission (SEC).

## Must-Know Facts
- ✅ **3-tier** system; a state with population **not exceeding 20 lakh** may skip the **intermediate** level.
- ✅ **All territorial seats** at all three levels are directly elected; chairpersons at
  intermediate/district levels are elected indirectly, the village-chair method is left to
  state law, and legislatures may provide representation for MPs/MLAs and others under Art 243-C.
- ✅ Reservation: SC/ST by population + **not less than 1/3 seats for women** (many states now **50%**).
- ✅ **5-year term**; re-election within **6 months** of dissolution.
- ✅ Candidate minimum age = **21** (not 25).
- ✅ **State Election Commission** conducts polls; **State Finance Commission** every 5 years.
- ✅ **PESA Act 1996** extends Part IX to **Fifth Schedule (tribal) areas**.
- ✅ Article 243M excludes **Nagaland, Meghalaya and Mizoram**, Fifth Schedule Scheduled Areas, Sixth Schedule tribal areas, specified Manipur hill areas and the constitutionally described Darjeeling hill arrangement; Parliament may extend Part IX only through the prescribed constitutional route.

### 5.0 Direct Mains demands owned by this Core file

- **2018 · GS-II · Q15** — Demand (neutral rendering): *examine the working of the panchayat system and the scope for financing panchayat projects from non-grant (own-source) revenue.* Directive: **Assess** · **15 marks · 250 words**.

  - **Executable route:** (1) **Thesis** — the **73rd Amendment 1992 (Part IX)** gave PRIs constitutional status but not fiscal autonomy; genuine self-government under **Art 243G** needs own-source ("non-grant") revenue built on **Art 243H**, not dependence on Finance-Commission transfers. (2) **Fiscal architecture** — **Art 243H** lets the *State legislature* (a) authorise a panchayat to levy taxes, duties, tolls and fees, (b) assign State-collected taxes to it, (c) give **grants-in-aid** from the Consolidated Fund of the State, and (d) constitute Panchayat Funds — so grants are only one of four channels. (3) **Non-grant instruments** — own taxes (property/house tax, tax on land and buildings, professional and entertainment taxes, taxes on markets and fairs); **user charges and fees** (water, sanitation, licensing, registration); **tolls** on ferries/roads; **rents and income from panchayat property and common property resources** — ponds, fisheries, community land, markets and **haats**; assigned taxes/cesses; and limited **borrowings**. (4) **Why they matter** — own revenue links payment to accountability and reduces dependence on tied grants. (5) **The gap** — own-tax effort is low, **State Finance Commissions (Art 243-I)** are delayed or ignored, States withhold buoyant taxes, and **15th/16th Finance Commission** local-body grants are largely tied. (6) **What is possible** — Kerala's **People's Plan Campaign** and Karnataka's early devolution show own-fund mobilisation is achievable. (7) **Verdict** — the tools for non-grant financing already exist in **Art 243H**; the deficit is political-administrative, so *financial devolution*, not merely more grants, is the reform.
- **2019 · GS-II · Q13** — Demand (neutral rendering): *comment on how far reservation of seats for women in local bodies has helped overcome patriarchy.* Directive: **Comment** · **15 marks · 250 words**. Cross-cutting with Social Justice; **this file owns the local-government half**.

(2) **The provision** — **Art 243D(3)** reserves *not less than one-third* of seats (including for SC/ST women) and **243D(4)** one-third of chairperson offices; many States have raised this to **50 per cent**. (3) **Positive evidence** — agenda shifts toward drinking water, sanitation and child nutrition; role-model effects; second-term women more assertive. (4) **Persistence of patriarchy** — the **"sarpanch-pati / pradhan-pati"** proxy phenomenon, women signing while male relatives govern, mobility and literacy constraints. (6) **Verdict** — reservation is a necessary structural intervention that has begun to erode patriarchy but cannot alone deliver empowerment without support systems.

(2) **Functions** — **Art 243G + Eleventh Schedule (29 subjects)** is *enabling, not mandatory*; States have not completed "activity mapping"; line departments and parallel bodies retain the work. (3) **Functionaries** — no dedicated PRI cadre; dependence on deputed State staff answerable upward to the State, not to the panchayat. (4) **Funds** — **Art 243H** under-used, **SFC (Art 243-I)** delayed, dependence on tied grants, weak own revenue. (6) **Verdict** — India has achieved *political* decentralisation (elections via **SEC, Art 243K**) but not *administrative and fiscal* decentralisation; functionality needs the three Fs devolved together.

### 5.2 Qualified thesis options

- *The 73rd Amendment settled the question of political decentralisation but left administrative and fiscal decentralisation to State discretion, so PRIs are constitutionally strong and functionally weak.*
- *Panchayat finance fails not for want of constitutional instruments — Art 243H already permits taxes, tolls, fees and assigned revenues — but because States withhold buoyant taxes and own-revenue effort remains low.*
- *Women's reservation under Art 243D is a necessary structural shock to patriarchy whose substantive effect is real but partial, mediated by proxy rule and capacity deficits.*
- *The three Fs are indivisible: devolving functions (Art 243G) without functionaries and funds (Art 243H) produces recognition without functionality.*
- *PESA and the Eleventh Schedule show the Constitution's intent was genuine self-government, but the "voluntary" drafting of devolution let the States convert a mandate into an option.*

### 5.4 Bank A — Constitutional architecture on the exact Articles

- **Claim:** PRIs are a constitutional, not a statutory-at-State-will, institution. → **Evidence:** **73rd Amendment 1992**, **Part IX (Art 243–243-O)**, **Eleventh Schedule** for **Art 243G**, giving shape to **Art 40 (DPSP)**; in force **24 April 1993**. → **Significance:** panchayats no longer depend on a State's political will. → **Caution:** ✅ passed 1992 but **in force 24 April 1993** — do not date the commencement to 1992.
- **Claim:** The Gram Sabha is the participatory foundation. → **Evidence:** **Art 243A** — the assembly of all registered voters of a village. → **Significance:** the only direct-democracy body in Part IX and the unit PESA later empowers. → **Caution:** ⚠️ its powers are what State law confers; the Constitution leaves its functions to the State legislature.
- **Claim:** The system is three-tier but conditionally so. → **Evidence:** **Art 243B** — Panchayats at village, intermediate and district levels; a State with population **below 20 lakh may skip the intermediate level**. → **Significance:** structural uniformity with a demographic exemption. → **Caution:** ✅ the exemption is *only* for the intermediate tier, not the village or district tier.
- **Claim:** Direct election is guaranteed at the base, indirect at the top. → **Evidence:** **Art 243C** — all **territorial seats at all three levels are directly elected**; chairpersons of the intermediate and district panchayats are **indirectly** elected; the village-chair method is left to State law. → **Significance:** popular mandate at constituency level with collegial leadership above. → **Caution:** ⚠️ "directly elected at all levels" is true only of *territorial members*, not of every chairperson.
- **Claim:** Reservation is mandatory and gendered. → **Evidence:** **Art 243D** — SC/ST seats in proportion to population; **not less than one-third for women** (243D(3)) and one-third of chairperson offices (243D(4)). → **Significance:** built-in social-inclusion floor. → **Caution:** ✅ "not less than one-third" is a floor; many States legislate **50 per cent** — do not state a fixed one-third as the ceiling.
- **Claim:** Tenure is fixed and elections are mandatory. → **Evidence:** **Art 243E** — five-year term; fresh elections **before expiry** or **within six months** of dissolution; a reconstituted panchayat serves only the remainder. → **Significance:** ends the pre-1993 practice of indefinite supersession. → **Caution:** ✅ minimum candidate age is **21**, not the 25 of the State legislature (**Art 243F**).
- **Claim:** Independent electoral and fiscal watchdogs are constitutionally required. → **Evidence:** **State Election Commission (Art 243K)** conducts panchayat polls; **State Finance Commission (Art 243-I)** is constituted every five years to review PRI finances. → **Significance:** insulates elections and fiscal review from the State executive. → **Caution:** ✅ the **ECI does not** conduct panchayat polls — that is the SEC; ⚠️ SFC constitution and action-taken reports are frequently **delayed**.
- **Claim:** Courts are barred from panchayat electoral matters. → **Evidence:** **Art 243-O** — no interference with delimitation/seat-allotment; election disputes only by election petition as State law provides. → **Significance:** protects the election calendar from litigation delay. → **Caution:** ✅ this mirrors Art 329 for Parliament/State legislatures.

### 5.5 Bank B — Fiscal / non-grant financing (owns 2018 Q15)

- **Claim:** The Constitution itself empowers own-source taxation. → **Evidence:** **Art 243H** authorises the State legislature to let panchayats **levy and collect taxes, duties, tolls and fees** and to **assign** State taxes to them. → **Significance:** own revenue is a constitutional entitlement route, not a favour. → **Caution:** ✅ it is *enabling* — the panchayat can tax only what the **State legislature** authorises.
- **Claim:** Non-grant revenue has several distinct heads. → **Evidence:** own taxes (property/house tax, tax on land and buildings, professional and entertainment taxes, taxes on markets and fairs); **user charges/fees** (water, sanitation, licences); **tolls**; **rents and income from panchayat property and common property resources** — ponds, fisheries, community land, **haats** and markets; **assigned** State taxes; limited **borrowings**. → **Significance:** a diversified base can fund local projects without waiting for transfers. → **Caution:** ⚠️ **Finance-Commission grants (CFC and SFC) are grants, not own-source revenue** — the 2018 question specifically excludes them.
- **Claim:** Grants dominate because own effort is weak. → **Evidence:** dependence on tied **15th/16th Finance Commission** local-body grants; **SFC (Art 243-I)** delays; State reluctance to assign buoyant taxes. → **Significance:** explains why constitutional powers under Art 243H remain under-exploited. → **Caution:** 📰 treat any own-revenue *percentage* or devolution-index *rank* as unverified unless sourced to a named official report with a year — do not quote a figure.
- **Claim:** Own-fund mobilisation is demonstrably possible. → **Evidence:** Kerala's **People's Plan Campaign (1996)** channelled a large share of plan funds through local bodies with participatory budgeting; Karnataka's early devolution record. → **Significance:** shows the gap is political-administrative, not constitutional. → **Caution:** ⚠️ these are qualitative State examples, not a claim that all States perform alike.

### 5.6 Bank C — Women's reservation and patriarchy (owns 2019 Q13)

- **Claim:** Reservation produced an unprecedented scale of women's representation. → **Evidence:** **Art 243D(3)/(4)**; many States at **50 per cent**. → **Significance:** the largest body of elected women representatives in local government anywhere. → **Caution:** ⚠️ treat any exact count or percentage of elected women as approximate and source-dependent; state the scale qualitatively.
- **Claim:** Presence has begun to change substance. → **Evidence:** field research indicates women-led panchayats tend to prioritise public goods (drinking water, sanitation) valued by women; role-model and aspiration effects. → **Significance:** representation is translating into a shifted agenda. → **Caution:** ⚠️ this is analytical/empirical inference, not constitutional text — mark it accordingly and do not name unverified studies.
- **Claim:** Patriarchy persists inside the institution. → **Evidence:** the **"sarpanch-pati / pradhan-pati"** proxy phenomenon; mobility, literacy and social constraints. → **Significance:** numerical reservation does not automatically transfer decision-making power. → **Caution:** ⚠️ describe proxy rule as a documented tendency, not as universal.

### 5.7 Bank D — The three Fs and functionality (owns 2020 Q13)

- **Claim:** Functions were listed but not compulsorily devolved. → **Evidence:** **Art 243G + Eleventh Schedule (29 subjects)** is *may-devolve*, not *shall-devolve*; "activity mapping" incomplete in most States. → **Significance:** the Constitution recognised functions without guaranteeing their transfer. → **Caution:** ✅ the Eleventh Schedule = **29** panchayat subjects (not the Twelfth Schedule's 18 for municipalities).
- **Claim:** Functionaries remain State employees. → **Evidence:** absence of a dedicated PRI cadre; reliance on deputed line-department staff. → **Significance:** administrative accountability runs upward to the State, not to the elected panchayat. → **Caution:** ⚠️ variation by State — a few have created panchayat cadres.
- **Claim:** Funds stay grant-dependent. → **Evidence:** under-used **Art 243H**, delayed **Art 243-I** SFCs, tied CFC grants. → **Significance:** without untied money, functionality cannot follow function. → **Caution:** 📰 avoid quoting a devolution-index figure; use the qualitative SFC-delay evidence.
- **Claim:** Parallel bodies crowd out panchayats. → **Evidence:** DRDA-type agencies and para-statals historically ran rural schemes alongside PRIs. → **Significance:** duplicated authority hollows the constitutional tier. → **Caution:** ⚠️ institutional arrangements have shifted over time; state this as a structural tendency.

### 5.8 Bank E — Committee lineage and PESA (evolution and tribal extension)

- **Claim:** PRIs grew from a settled committee lineage. → **Evidence:** **Balwant Rai Mehta (1957)** — "democratic decentralisation", the 3-tier scheme (Gram Panchayat–Panchayat Samiti–Zila Parishad), first launched in **Rajasthan (Nagaur, 2 Oct 1959)**; **Ashok Mehta (1977–78)** — a 2-tier scheme and open party participation; **G.V.K. Rao (1985)** — the district as the basic planning unit, warning against bureaucratisation; **L.M. Singhvi (1986)** — **constitutional status** for PRIs and Nyaya Panchayats. → **Significance:** the 73rd Amendment enacted Singhvi's core recommendation. → **Caution:** ✅ do not swap the recommendations — Ashok Mehta = 2-tier, Balwant Rai Mehta = 3-tier.
- **Claim:** Tribal areas get a bespoke self-governance statute. → **Evidence:** **Art 243M** excludes Fifth/Sixth Schedule areas from Part IX; **PESA 1996** extends the panchayat framework to **Fifth Schedule areas**, vesting the **Gram Sabha** with ownership of minor forest produce and specified control over local resources/land alienation; consultation before land acquisition and mandatory prior recommendation for minor-mineral licences, leases and concessions; the **Forest Rights Act 2006** is the companion rights statute. → **Significance:** the constitutional-plus-PESA design makes the tribal Gram Sabha the strongest in law. → **Caution:** ✅ PESA covers **Fifth**, not Sixth, Schedule areas; ⚠️ implementation lags the statute.

### 5.9 Mechanism, institutional incentives and consequences

- ⚠️ **Why devolution stalls:** States control the three Fs and face an incentive to retain patronage, staff and buoyant taxes; the Constitution's *enabling* language (Art 243G "may", Art 243H "may authorise") lets them comply formally while withholding substance.
- ⚠️ **Elections vs empowerment:** the SEC (Art 243K) guarantees regular polls, so *political* decentralisation is secure; but without functionaries and funds, elected bodies cannot deliver, producing the "functions without functionality" gap.
- ⚠️ **Fiscal accountability loop:** own-source revenue (Art 243H) makes citizens who pay demand performance; grant-dependence breaks that loop and orients panchayats upward to the grantor rather than downward to voters.
- ⚠️ **Proxy dynamics:** reservation changes who holds the seat faster than it changes who holds power, so consequences depend on complementary capacity-building and tenure security.

### 5.10 Criticism, counter-argument, State variation and implementation constraints

- **Over-formal devolution:** ⚠️ critics argue the 73rd Amendment devolved status but left the three Fs optional, so many States created panchayats without empowering them.
- **Counter (real gains):** ⚠️ regular SEC-run elections, the reservation cohort, and States like Kerala and Karnataka show the framework can deliver where political will exists.
- **State variation:** ⚠️ Kerala (People's Plan Campaign) and Karnataka devolved early and comparatively deeply; several States delayed local elections, under-convened SFCs, or ran parallel para-statals — devolution is **uneven and State-dependent**.
- **Implementation constraints:** ⚠️ delayed/ignored **SFC (Art 243-I)** reports, incomplete activity mapping, absence of panchayat cadres, low own-tax effort, proxy rule, and PESA/FRA under-enforcement in Scheduled Areas.
- **Fact-safety on numbers:** 📰 do **not** state a devolution-index rank, an own-revenue percentage, a share of GDP or a count of subjects devolved by a named State unless attributable to a named official source with a year.

### 5.11 Verdict scaffolds

- **Design verdict:** "The 73rd Amendment guaranteed panchayats their existence, elections and reservations, but left their powers, staff and money to the States — a constitution-strong, function-weak settlement."
- **Finance verdict:** "The instruments of non-grant financing already sit in Art 243H; India's panchayats are under-financed less by constitutional limitation than by low own-tax effort and States' reluctance to assign buoyant revenue."
- **Women's-reservation verdict:** "Reservation under Art 243D is a necessary and partly effective structural intervention against patriarchy whose promise is completed only by capacity-building, tenure security and the end of proxy rule."
- **Functionality verdict:** "India has decentralised politics but not administration or finance; the three Fs are indivisible, and functionality begins only when functions, functionaries and funds are devolved together."

## Constitutional precision and judicial controls

- ✅ **Article 243D(4):** chairperson offices are reserved for SCs/STs in the State-wide population proportion prescribed by State law, and **not less than one-third** of all chairperson offices are reserved for women. Backward-class reservation is enabling under Article 243D(6).
- ✅ **Article 243E:** elections follow before expiry or within six months after dissolution; no election is required for a remainder shorter than six months, and an early reconstituted body serves only the unexpired term.
- ✅ **Article 243I:** the SFC recommends State-local tax distribution, assigned taxes, grants-in-aid and measures to improve finances; the Governor lays recommendations and an action memorandum before the State legislature.
- ✅ **Article 243K:** the SEC controls rolls and elections; its Commissioner has High-Court-judge removal protection, service conditions cannot be worsened after appointment, and the Governor must provide requested staff.
- ✅ **Articles 243L and 243N:** the President may apply Part IX to a Union Territory with exceptions; inconsistent pre-existing Panchayat laws could continue only up to one year from commencement unless earlier changed.
- ✅ **Article 243M:** the Nagaland/Meghalaya/Mizoram extension route requires a State Assembly resolution supported by a majority of total membership and at least two-thirds present and voting; the parliamentary extension law is not treated as an Article 368 amendment. PESA uses the separate Scheduled-Area route for Fifth Schedule areas.
- ⚖️ **Kishansing Tomar (2006):** timely local elections are a constitutional mandate. **K. Krishna Murthy (2010)** and **Vikas Kishanrao Gawali (2021)** require local-body-specific empirical justification and the triple test for OBC political reservation. **State of Goa v. Fouziya Imtiaz Shaikh (2021)** protects SEC independence.
- 📰 **Dated current controls (5 September 2026):** use the Ministry's **Devolution Index Report 2024**, the Sixteenth Finance Commission's **2026–31** report/action memorandum, and the Ministry's live State-wise PESA-rules directory. Do not freeze changing portal totals or infer implementation from rule notification alone.

### Semantic-completeness ownership and PYQ control

- **Constitutional birth:** the Constitution (Seventy-third Amendment) Act, 1992
  inserted Part IX and the Eleventh Schedule and commenced on 24 April 1993.
  Articles 243-243O and the twenty-nine Schedule matters are the exact core.
- **Democratic structure:** Article 243A leaves Gram Sabha powers to State law;
  Article 243B creates village, intermediate and district tiers, with the
  population-not-exceeding-twenty-lakh intermediate-tier exception; Articles
  243C-243F govern composition, reservation, duration and disqualification.
- **Election precision:** Article 243E requires election before expiry or within
  six months of dissolution, unless the remainder is under six months; a
  reconstituted Panchayat serves only the unexpired term. Article 243K vests
  electoral control in the State Election Commission; Article 243O channels
  challenges through election petitions.
- **Devolution limit:** Article 243G and the Eleventh Schedule enable State-law
  endowment of powers; they do not automatically transfer all twenty-nine matters.
  Article 243H supplies taxes, assignments, grants and funds; Articles 243I/J
  provide the State Finance Commission and audit routes.
- **PESA scope:** Article 243M excludes specified areas and States. The Provisions
  of the Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996 extends a
  modified framework only to Fifth Schedule Scheduled Areas, not Sixth Schedule areas.
- **PESA verb control:** Section 4 requires consultation before land acquisition
  and resettlement/rehabilitation; mandatory prior recommendation before minor-
  mineral prospecting licences, mining leases and concessions; ownership of minor
  forest produce; and specified powers over alienation, markets, money-lending,
  social sectors and local plans. These verbs are not one universal Gram Sabha veto.
- **Inclusion:** Article 243D makes SC/ST seat reservation population-linked,
  reserves not less than one-third of all seats and chairperson offices for women,
  and permits backward-class reservation. State laws providing fifty per cent
