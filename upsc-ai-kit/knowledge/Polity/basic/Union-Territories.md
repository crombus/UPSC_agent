## Core idea
A UT is centrally administered by the **President** through an **Administrator (LG/Administrator)** — his **agent**,
not a head of state. Some UTs have their own assembly + CoM, but **Parliament can still legislate on any subject
(incl. State List)** for them.

> 🔑 **8 UTs:** A&N · Chandigarh · **DNH-DD** · Delhi · J&K · Ladakh · Lakshadweep · Puducherry.
> 🔑 DNH + Daman & Diu **merged 26 Jan 2020**; **J&K + Ladakh** carved out **2019** (Ladakh = no legislature).

## Must-Know Facts
- ✅ Administrator is an **agent of the President**; designation LG / Chief Commissioner / Administrator.
- ✅ Assemblies with legislature: **Puducherry, Delhi, J&K**.
- ✅ **Delhi (Art 239-AA, 69th Amdt 1991):** NCT, 70-member assembly; **CM appointed by President**; assembly
  excludes **public order, police, land**.
- ✅ Only **Delhi** has its own High Court.
- ✅ **President's Regulations** govern A&N, Lakshadweep, DNH-DD, Ladakh.

## 📰 Current link
📰 **Delhi Services tussle** — SC (2023) gave the elected govt control over services; the **GNCTD Amendment Act
2023** restored Centre/LG primacy. **J&K statehood** restoration still pending (2026).

Union of India* resolved the LG-vs-elected-government conflict in favour of representative government, holding the LG **bound by the aid and advice** of the Council of Ministers except on the reserved entries. (2) **Constitutional frame** — **Art 239AA (69th Amendment 1991)**: NCT with a **70-member** assembly and a CoM (≤ 10% = 7); the assembly is competent over the State and Concurrent Lists **except public order, police and land** (Entries 1, 2, 18). (3) **The 2018 propositions** — (a) the LG is **bound by aid and advice** except on the three reserved subjects; (b) the LG has **no independent decision-making power** and is **not a Governor**; (c) the **proviso to Art 239AA(4)** (reference of a "difference of opinion" to the President) is an **exception for exceptional matters**, not a routine device; (d) constitutional morality, collaborative federalism and constitutional trust. (4) **What 2018 left open** — control over **"services"** (State-List Entry 41), which triggered the next round. (5) **The 2023 sequel** — the services Constitution Bench gave NCTD control over services (except the three entries); the **Government of NCT of Delhi (Amendment) Act 2023** and the **National Capital Civil Services Authority** shifted primacy back toward the LG/Centre — 📰 validity under challenge, verify status. (6) **Examine verdict** — 2018 settled the *principle* (the elected government is supreme within its sphere) but the services battle shows the Delhi model remains an unresolved asymmetric arrangement.
- **2025 · GS-II · Q4** — Demand (neutral rendering): *discuss the nature, powers and functions of the Jammu & Kashmir Legislative Assembly created by the Reorganisation Act, 2019.* Directive: **Discuss** · **10 marks · 150 words**.

(2) **Origin** — the Act (effective **31 October 2019**) split the former State into two UTs: **J&K with a legislature** and **Ladakh without one**. (3) **Powers** — the assembly may legislate on the State and Concurrent Lists **except public order and police** (which stay with the Centre/LG); unlike Delhi, **"land" is not excluded** for J&K. (4) **Central overrides** — **Parliament can still legislate on any subject** for the UT; the **LG** holds substantial powers; the assembly is **statutory**, not a constitutional (Art 239AA-type) body. (5) **State vs UT distinction** — a State assembly has a Governor bound by aid and advice and exclusive State-List competence; a UT-with-legislature has an Administrator/LG as the **President's agent** and reserved subjects. (6) **Current status** — 📰 *In re Article 370* (2023) upheld the reorganisation but directed **restoration of statehood and elections "as soon as possible"**; statehood restoration pending — verify. (7) **Verdict** — the J&K assembly is a Puducherry-type UT legislature: real but curtailed self-government pending the promised return to statehood.

### 5.2 Qualified thesis options

- *A Union Territory with a legislature is a form of curtailed self-government: it elects a government but shares its executive sphere with an Administrator who is the President's agent, and its assembly can be overridden by Parliament.*
- *The 2018 Delhi judgment settled the principle of representative supremacy within the NCT's sphere, but the post-2023 services dispute shows the Delhi model is a still-unresolved asymmetric federal experiment.*
- *The J&K assembly created in 2019 is closer to Puducherry than to a State: a statutory legislature with reserved subjects, not a constitutional State legislature — a downgrade whose promised reversal to statehood remains pending.*
- *India's UT design deliberately privileges the Union's administrative reach over local democracy, which is defensible for strategic or capital territories but sits uneasily with elected assemblies in Delhi, Puducherry and J&K.*

### 5.4 Bank A — Three categories on the exact Articles

- **Claim:** Every UT is administered by the President through an Administrator, not by a head of state. → **Evidence:** **Art 239** — the President acts through an **Administrator (LG / Chief Commissioner / Administrator)**, his **agent**; **Art 241** provides for High Courts. → **Significance:** the constitutional default for UTs is central administration. → **Caution:** ✅ the Administrator is **not** a Governor and has no independent constitutional office of head of state.
- **Claim:** Some UTs get a local legislature by law; Delhi gets one by the Constitution. → **Evidence:** **Art 239A** enabled local legislatures/CoM for certain UTs, exercised for **Puducherry** through the **Government of Union Territories Act 1963**; **Art 239AA** created the Delhi scheme by the **69th Amendment 1991**. → **Significance:** Puducherry's assembly is *statutory*, Delhi's is *constitutional* — a decisive distinction. → **Caution:** ✅ do not credit Puducherry's assembly to the 69th Amendment — that was Delhi.
- **Claim:** UTs without a legislature are governed by President's Regulations. → **Evidence:** **Art 240** — the President may make regulations for **A&N, Lakshadweep, DNH-DD, Ladakh** (and Puducherry when its assembly is suspended), with the **force of an Act of Parliament**. → **Significance:** direct central law-making for these territories. → **Caution:** ✅ **Ladakh has no legislature**; do not assume every UT elects an assembly.
- **Claim:** Parliament's power over UTs is plenary even where an assembly exists. → **Evidence:** **Art 246(4)** and the UT scheme — Parliament may legislate on **any subject, including the State List**, for any UT. → **Significance:** a UT assembly never enjoys a State's exclusive State-List competence. → **Caution:** ✅ this is the core reason a UT-with-legislature ≠ a State.

### 5.5 Bank B — The Delhi scheme and the two judgments (owns 2018 Q11)

- **Claim:** Delhi's NCT status and its limits are constitutional. → **Evidence:** **Art 239AA (69th Amendment 1991)** — a **70-member** assembly, CoM ≤ **10%** (7, incl. CM), competent over the State and Concurrent Lists **except public order, police and land**; **Art 239AB** provides a President's-Rule-type mechanism. → **Significance:** self-government with three reserved entries and a President's-rule safety valve. → **Caution:** ✅ the CM is **appointed by the President** (per the scheme), not by the LG.
- **Claim:** The 2018 Constitution Bench subordinated the LG to the elected government within the NCT's sphere. → **Evidence:** *Government of NCT of Delhi v. Union of India* (2018) — the LG is **bound by the aid and advice** of the CoM except on the reserved subjects, has **no independent decision-making power**, and the **proviso to Art 239AA(4)** reference to the President is for **exceptional** matters, not routine use. → **Significance:** representative government prevails within its allotted field. → **Caution:** ✅ state these as the Court's propositions; do not convert *obiter* on constitutional morality into the operative *ratio*.
- **Claim:** The 2023 services dispute reopened executive control. → **Evidence:** the 2023 services Constitution Bench gave NCTD control over **services (State-List Entry 41)** except the three entries; the **Government of NCT of Delhi (Amendment) Act 2023** created the **National Capital Civil Services Authority**, restoring LG/Centre primacy over services. → **Significance:** the executive-control question outran the 2018 principle. → **Caution:** 📰 the **2023 Act is law** (enacted August 2023); its constitutional validity has been **challenged before the Supreme Court** — verify the current status before dating it. An Act under challenge is still law until struck down.

### 5.6 Bank C — J&K after the 2019 Reorganisation Act (owns 2025 Q4)

- **Claim:** The 2019 Act converted a State into two UTs with unequal designs. → **Evidence:** **Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act 2019** (effective **31 October 2019**) — **J&K a UT with a legislature**, **Ladakh a UT without one**. → **Significance:** a rare downgrade of a State into UTs. → **Caution:** ✅ Ladakh has **no** assembly; do not attribute one to it.
- **Claim:** The J&K assembly is a Puducherry-type UT legislature. → **Evidence:** it legislates on the State and Concurrent Lists **except public order and police**; **land is not excluded** (unlike Delhi); **Parliament** retains overriding power and the **LG** substantial authority. → **Significance:** self-government narrower than a State's but broader than Delhi's on land. → **Caution:** ✅ contrast the exclusions — Delhi excludes **three** entries (public order, police, land), J&K **two** (public order, police).
- **Claim:** The reorganisation was judicially upheld with a statehood direction. → **Evidence:** 📰 *In re Article 370* (2023) upheld the reorganisation while directing **restoration of statehood and Assembly elections "as soon as possible."** → **Significance:** the UT status is treated as transitional. → **Caution:** 📰 statehood restoration status is dynamic — verify before asserting it is complete or pending.

### 5.7 Mechanism, institutional incentives and consequences

- ⚠️ **Why the Administrator design persists:** capital security, strategic frontiers and small populations give the Union a rationale to retain administrative control, which is why even elected UTs have reserved subjects.
- ⚠️ **The reference mechanism as friction:** the Art 239AA(4) proviso lets an LG escalate disputes to the President; used routinely it can paralyse an elected government — hence the 2018 Court confined it to exceptional matters.
- ⚠️ **Services as the real prize:** control over the civil service (Entry 41) determines who governs day-to-day, which is why the 2023 judgment and the GNCTD Act 2023 fought over it rather than over legislative lists.
- ⚠️ **Downgrade as precedent:** converting J&K into UTs shows a State's status is not constitutionally entrenched against Parliament, raising federal-stability questions addressed in `Special-Provisions.md`.

### 5.8 Criticism, counter-argument, State variation and implementation constraints

- **Democratic-deficit critique:** ⚠️ elected UT governments operate under an Administrator who is the Centre's agent, so accountability is split and can stall.
- **Counter (functional rationale):** ⚠️ for a national capital and strategic frontiers, a strong Union hand is defensible; the 2018 judgment shows courts can still protect the elected sphere.
- **Variation across UTs:** ⚠️ Puducherry (broadest assembly competence), Delhi (three reserved entries, contested services), J&K (two reserved entries, statehood promised), and the regulation-governed UTs differ sharply — do not treat "UT" as a single model.
- **Implementation constraints:** ⚠️ recurring LG-vs-government friction, litigation over services, pending J&K statehood, and Ladakh's Sixth-Schedule/statehood demands.
- **Fact-safety:** 📰 verify the current status of the GNCTD Act 2023 challenge and J&K statehood before dating either; do not assert a resolution not yet reached.

## Deep-review precision controls (28 August 2026)

- **Article 239AA field:** Delhi excludes State List Entries **1, 2 and 18** and Entries **64, 65 and 66 insofar as they relate** to those entries; executive power follows the allotted field subject to the Constitution and parliamentary law.
- **Distinct routes:** Article **239AB** is Delhi's failure-of-machinery route; Article **239B** is Puducherry's ordinance route and applies to Delhi through Article 239AA(8); Article **240** regulations cover the constitutionally/statutorily specified territories, with Puducherry's power conditional on suspension/dissolution.
- **High Courts:** Delhi has an exclusive UT High Court; J&K and Ladakh share the High Court of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh; A&N/Chandigarh/DNHDD/Lakshadweep/Puducherry are assigned respectively to Calcutta/Punjab & Haryana/Bombay/Kerala/Madras High Courts.
- **Current control:** India has eight UTs and three functioning legislatures. J&K has an elected Assembly and Council of Ministers after the 2024 election, but Statehood was not legally restored by 28 August 2026. The GNCTD Amendment Act 2023 remains operative while its constitutional challenge is unresolved.
