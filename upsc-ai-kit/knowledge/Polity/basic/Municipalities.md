## Core idea
The 74th Amendment gave **constitutional status** to **urban local bodies (ULBs)** — mirroring the 73rd for
villages. Three types of municipality by area size, with directly elected territorial seats, reservations, a 5-year term,
and district/metropolitan planning committees.

> 🔑 **"Nagar–Council–Corporation = Transitional–Smaller–Larger"** (increasing size).
> 🔑 **Schedules:** 11th → panchayats (**29**) · 12th → municipalities (**18**).

## Must-Know Facts
- ✅ **3 types**: Nagar Panchayat (transitional), Municipal Council (small town), Municipal Corporation (big city).
- ✅ All **territorial seats** are directly elected from wards; state law may also
  provide representation for experts (without voting rights) and specified MPs/MLAs/
  committee chairpersons. **Wards Committees** are required where population ≥3 lakh.
- ✅ Reservation: SC/ST by population + **≥ 1/3 for women**.
- ✅ **5-year term**; election within **6 months** of dissolution; min candidate age **21**.
- ✅ **State Election Commission** conducts polls; **State Finance Commission** reviews finances.
- ✅ **DPC** (4/5 elected) at district level; **MPC** (2/3 elected) for metropolitan areas.
- ✅ **8 types of urban bodies** exist (incl. Cantonment Board — run by the Centre/Defence Ministry).

## 📰 Current link
📰 ⚠️ **Municipal finance crisis** & weak devolution (Smart Cities/AMRUT run partly by parastatals, not elected
ULBs); **16th Finance Commission** grants to urban bodies keep the debate live.

### 5.0 Direct Mains demands owned by this Core file

- **2023 · GS-II · Q3** — Demand (neutral rendering): *comment on the extent to which States have empowered urban local bodies functionally and financially since the 74th Amendment.* Directive: **Comment** · **10 marks · 150 words**.
  - **Core routing supersedes the older `advanced/24_Municipalities.md` pointer for this demand.**
  - **Executable route:** (1) **Thesis** — the **74th Amendment 1992 (Part IX-A)** mandated urban self-government, but functional and financial empowerment was left to State discretion (**Art 243W** and **Art 243X** are *enabling*), so Indian cities are *governed but not self-governed*. (2) **Functional gap** — **Art 243W + Twelfth Schedule (18 subjects)** is may-devolve; core functions (spatial planning, water, transport) are retained by **State parastatals and development authorities**; the **District Planning Committee (Art 243ZD)** and **Metropolitan Planning Committee (Art 243ZE)** — the constitutional planning tier — are the **weakest link**, often un-constituted or powerless. (3) **Financial gap** — **Art 243X** permits own taxes, tolls and fees, but **property tax** is under-exploited, **State Finance Commissions (Art 243Y/243-I)** are delayed, and municipal bonds remain nascent, leaving dependence on tied grants. (4) **Leadership gap** — short/rotational, mostly indirectly elected **mayors** with executive power resting in the State-appointed **Municipal Commissioner**; hence the directly-elected-mayor debate. (5) **Evidence** — **Smart Cities Mission** SPVs and **AMRUT** are run partly through parastatals, bypassing elected councils. (6) **Verdict** — the constitutional design for empowerment exists; States under-deliver, so reform means completing activity mapping of the 18 subjects, empowering DPC/MPC, buoyant own-revenue and an empowered mayor.

### 5.2 Qualified thesis options

- *The 74th Amendment constitutionalised the existence of urban local bodies but left their functional and financial empowerment to the States, so Indian cities are administered by the State and its agencies more than governed by their elected councils.*
- *The weakest link in the 74th Amendment is spatial planning: without empowered District and Metropolitan Planning Committees, elected cities cannot plan themselves, and development authorities fill the vacuum.*
- *Municipal finance fails less for want of powers — Art 243X permits property tax, tolls, fees and funds — than for low property-tax effort, delayed State Finance Commissions and untapped municipal-bond markets.*
- *A city with a one-year rotational mayor and a State-appointed commissioner has representation without executive self-government; the directly-elected-mayor debate is really a debate about where urban executive power should sit.*
- *Centrally designed urban missions delivering through SPVs and parastatals can build infrastructure while weakening the constitutional claim of elected municipalities to govern.*

### 5.4 Bank A — Constitutional architecture on the exact Articles

- **Claim:** ULBs are a constitutional institution mirroring the 73rd Amendment. → **Evidence:** **74th Amendment 1992**, **Part IX-A (Art 243-P to 243-ZG)**, **Twelfth Schedule** for **Art 243W**; in force **1 June 1993**. → **Significance:** States are constitutionally obliged to adopt the system. → **Caution:** ✅ in force **1 June 1993**, not 1992; Twelfth Schedule = **18** subjects (not 29).
- **Claim:** Three types of municipality track the degree of urbanisation. → **Evidence:** **Art 243Q** — **Nagar Panchayat** (transitional area), **Municipal Council** (smaller urban area), **Municipal Corporation** (larger urban area); the Governor classifies areas by population, density, revenue, non-agricultural employment and economic importance, and may specify an **industrial township** where no municipality need be constituted. → **Significance:** a graduated urban-governance ladder. → **Caution:** ✅ Corporation = **largest**, Nagar Panchayat = transitional — do not invert.
- **Claim:** Territorial seats are directly elected with mandatory ward-level bodies in big cities. → **Evidence:** **Art 243R** composition (all territorial seats directly elected from wards; specified additional representation permitted); **Art 243S** — **Wards Committees mandatory where population ≥ 3 lakh**. → **Significance:** intra-city decentralisation for large municipalities. → **Caution:** ✅ Wards Committees are required only at **≥ 3 lakh**, not in every municipality.
- **Claim:** Reservation, tenure and disqualification mirror panchayats. → **Evidence:** **Art 243T** (SC/ST by population, **≥ 1/3 for women**, BC optional); **Art 243U** (five-year term, election within six months of dissolution); **Art 243V** (candidate age **21**). → **Significance:** uniform inclusion and tenure floor. → **Caution:** ✅ age **21**, not 25.
- **Claim:** Independent electoral and fiscal machinery is constitutionally required. → **Evidence:** **State Election Commission (Art 243ZA)** conducts municipal polls; **State Finance Commission (Art 243Y, the body constituted under Art 243-I)** reviews ULB finances every five years; **Art 243ZG** bars court interference in electoral matters. → **Significance:** insulates urban elections and fiscal review from the executive. → **Caution:** ✅ the ECI does not run municipal polls — it is the SEC; the *same* SFC serves panchayats and municipalities.

### 5.5 Bank B — Functional empowerment and the planning weak link (owns 2023 Q3)

- **Claim:** Functional devolution is enabling, not mandatory. → **Evidence:** **Art 243W + Twelfth Schedule (18 subjects)** — urban planning, regulation of land use, water supply, public health, urban poverty alleviation, etc. → **Significance:** States decide how much to actually transfer. → **Caution:** ⚠️ most States have not completed "activity mapping", so functions overlap with line departments.
- **Claim:** The planning committees are the constitutional heart — and the weakest link. → **Evidence:** **District Planning Committee (Art 243ZD, four-fifths elected)** consolidates panchayat + municipal plans; **Metropolitan Planning Committee (Art 243ZE, two-thirds elected)** for metropolitan areas. → **Significance:** they are meant to make elected bodies, not bureaucracies, plan the city. → **Caution:** ⚠️ many States have **not constituted MPCs even for major metros**, or left them powerless; do not assume they function.
- **Claim:** Development authorities and parastatals capture core urban functions. → **Evidence:** State urban development authorities, water and sewerage boards, and Smart Cities SPVs perform planning and service delivery instead of elected councils. → **Significance:** produces "cities governed but not self-governed". → **Caution:** ⚠️ arrangements vary by State; state this as a structural tendency, not a uniform fact.

### 5.6 Bank C — Financial empowerment (owns 2023 Q3)

- **Claim:** The Constitution enables own-source municipal revenue. → **Evidence:** **Art 243X** — the State legislature may authorise a municipality to levy taxes, duties, tolls and fees, assign State taxes, give grants-in-aid, and constitute municipal funds. → **Significance:** own revenue is a route to fiscal autonomy. → **Caution:** ✅ it is *enabling* — the ULB taxes only what the State authorises.
- **Claim:** Property tax is the mainstay but under-exploited. → **Evidence:** property/house tax, user charges (water, sanitation), tolls, licensing and market fees; **municipal bonds** as a capital-market route. → **Significance:** buoyant own-revenue reduces grant dependence and improves accountability. → **Caution:** 📰 do **not** quote a municipal own-revenue share of GDP or a collection-efficiency percentage without a named, dated official source.
- **Claim:** Grant dependence persists due to weak fiscal machinery. → **Evidence:** delayed **SFCs (Art 243Y/243-I)**; tied **15th/16th Finance Commission** urban grants. → **Significance:** explains why Art 243X powers stay under-used. → **Caution:** ⚠️ qualitative; avoid inventing figures.

### 5.7 Bank D — Municipal leadership and personnel

- **Claim:** The elected mayor is usually weak; executive power sits with the appointed commissioner. → **Evidence:** in many States mayors serve short/rotational terms and are indirectly elected, while the State-appointed **Municipal Commissioner (an IAS/State-service officer)** wields executive authority. → **Significance:** representation without an empowered executive head. → **Caution:** ⚠️ tenure and election mode vary by State — do not assert a single national rule.
- **Claim:** The directly-elected-mayor model is contested. → **Evidence:** several States have experimented with directly elected mayors and some have reverted to indirect election. → **Significance:** the debate is about locating urban executive power in an accountable elected office. → **Caution:** ⚠️ do not attribute a current model to a named State without verifying its present status.

### 5.8 Mechanism, institutional incentives and consequences

- ⚠️ **Why States under-devolve:** urban land, water and planning are lucrative and politically salient, so States retain them through development authorities and boards while formally constituting municipalities.
- ⚠️ **Planning vacuum:** where MPC/DPC (Art 243ZE/243ZD) are unformed, statutory planning defaults to parastatals, so the elected city cannot control its own spatial future.
- ⚠️ **Fiscal accountability:** property tax (Art 243X) ties citizens' payments to service demands; grant dependence orients municipalities upward to the grantor.
- ⚠️ **Executive-representative split:** a strong appointed commissioner plus a weak mayor concentrates accountability in the State cadre, not the ballot.

### 5.9 Criticism, counter-argument, State variation and implementation constraints

- **Governed-not-self-governed critique:** ⚠️ the 74th Amendment gave form without power; parastatals and weak planning committees hollow out elected councils.
- **Counter (real structure exists):** ⚠️ regular SEC-run elections, reservation, and the Twelfth Schedule provide a genuine framework that empowered States can use.
- **State variation:** ⚠️ some States constitute and fund DPC/MPC and devolve more subjects; others delay elections, leave MPCs unformed, or route missions through SPVs — empowerment is **uneven and State-dependent**.
- **Implementation constraints:** ⚠️ incomplete activity mapping, absent MPCs, delayed SFCs, low property-tax effort, dependence on tied grants, and parastatal capture of core functions.
- **Fact-safety on numbers:** 📰 do **not** state a municipal own-revenue percentage, a share of GDP or a devolution rank without a named official source and year.

### 5.10 Verdict scaffolds

- **Empowerment verdict:** "The 74th Amendment constitutionalised urban local bodies but left their functional and financial empowerment optional, so India's cities have representation without full self-government."
- **Planning verdict:** "The Metropolitan and District Planning Committees are the amendment's decisive institutions and its greatest failure; until they are constituted and empowered, development authorities will plan cities that elect their own councils."
- **Finance verdict:** "Municipal fiscal weakness is a problem of effort and machinery, not of constitutional power — Art 243X already permits the taxes and funds that under-collection and SFC delay leave unused."

## Constitutional precision and current controls

- ✅ **Article 243R:** experts may be represented but cannot vote in municipal meetings; State law may represent specified MPs/MLAs, registered Rajya Sabha/Legislative Council members and committee chairpersons, while State law determines the chairperson's election.
- ✅ **Article 243T:** SC/ST seat reservation is population-linked; at least one-third of total seats, including SC/ST seats, are for women. State law prescribes SC/ST and women chairperson reservations; backward-class reservation is enabling.
- ✅ **Article 243U:** a municipality gets a reasonable opportunity before dissolution; election follows before expiry or within six months after dissolution, except when the remainder is below six months; an early reconstituted body serves only the balance.
- ✅ **Articles 243ZB/ZC/ZF:** the President may adapt Part IXA for Union Territories; Scheduled and tribal areas are excluded and Parliament may extend it to Scheduled Areas; inconsistent old municipal laws continued for at most one year, while existing municipalities continued to term unless lawfully dissolved.
- ✅ **DPC/MPC:** DPC has at least four-fifths elected from district-Panchayat and municipal elected members in the rural:urban population ratio; MPC has at least two-thirds elected from municipal elected members and Panchayat chairpersons in the municipality:Panchayat population ratio. Both prepare a draft plan which the chair forwards to the State Government.
- ⚖️ **Judgments:** *Kishansing Tomar* (2006) protects timely local elections; *K. Krishna Murthy* (2010) and *Vikas Kishanrao Gawali* (2021) control backward-class political reservation; *Fouziya Imtiaz Shaikh* (2021) protects SEC independence.
- 📰 **Dated controls, checked 5 September 2026:** RBI's *Report on Municipal Finances* (2022), Sixteenth Finance Commission report/action memorandum for 2026–31, AMRUT 2.0 guidelines/toolkit, and SEBI's municipal-debt framework/current statistics. Treat dashboard and issuance totals as dated, not permanent.

### Semantic-completeness ownership and PYQ control

- **Constitutional birth:** the Constitution (Seventy-fourth Amendment) Act, 1992
  inserted Part IXA and the Twelfth Schedule and commenced on 1 June 1993.
  Articles 243P-243ZG and the eighteen Schedule matters are the exact core.
- **Municipal types:** Article 243Q distinguishes Nagar Panchayat, Municipal
  Council and Municipal Corporation and contains the industrial-township proviso.
  State notification and State law determine the actual institutional form.
- **Democratic design:** Articles 243R-243V govern composition, Ward Committees,
  reservation, five-year duration and disqualification. Article 243S requires
  Ward Committees at population three lakh or more; Article 243T fixes the
  constitutional one-third women's floor and population-linked SC/ST reservation.
- **Election and continuity:** Article 243U uses the same before-expiry/within-six-
  months rule and unexpired-term principle; Article 243ZA assigns elections to the
  State Election Commission and Article 243ZG bars ordinary electoral interference.
- **Functional and fiscal limit:** Article 243W and the Twelfth Schedule are
  enabling rather than self-executing. Article 243X permits State-law taxes,
  assignments, grants and funds; Article 243Y uses the Article 243I State Finance
  Commission. Constitutional existence is not automatic three-F devolution.
- **Planning precision:** Article 243ZD requires at least four-fifths elected
  representation on a District Planning Committee; Article 243ZE requires at
  least two-thirds on a Metropolitan Planning Committee. A metropolitan area is
  ten lakh or more under Article 243P(c); planning committees do not themselves
  control every parastatal, budget or land power.
- **Scope controls:** Articles 243ZB/ZC apply/adapt Part IXA to Union Territories
  and exclude Scheduled/tribal areas subject to the constitutional extension
  route; Article 243ZF governed transition and Article 243ZG the election bar.
- **Current finance:** the Sixteenth Finance Commission covers 2026-27 to
  2030-31 and recommends a 60:40 rural-urban aggregate split, with basic and
  performance components; these are dated grant recommendations, not permanent
  constitutional ratios.
- **Current regulation:** AMRUT 2.0 remains a scheme/reform framework rather than
  constitutional devolution. The SEBI (Issue and Listing of Municipal Debt
  Securities) Regulations, 2015 were amended on 8 July 2026, followed by the
  11 August 2026 implementation circular; municipal bonds remain debt, not own tax.
- **Four-ledger/PYQ control:** direct 2023 functional-financial empowerment and
  routed 2024 rural-urban merger demands were retained. Topic 23 owns rural
  institutions; Topic 26 owns excluded Scheduled/tribal-area architecture.
