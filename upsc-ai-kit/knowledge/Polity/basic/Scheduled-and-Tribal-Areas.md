## Core idea
Art 244 gives a **special administration** for tribal regions. The **Fifth Schedule** (protective, President/
Governor-driven, advisory TAC) covers scheduled areas in most states; the **Sixth Schedule** gives **greater
autonomy** via elected **Autonomous District Councils** in the four north-eastern states.

> 🔑 **Sixth Schedule = "A-M-T-M"** — **A**ssam, **M**eghalaya, **T**ripura, **M**izoram.
> 🔑 **5th = advisory (TAC)** · **6th = self-governing district/regional councils**.
> The ordinary Sixth-Schedule model is 26 elected + 4 nominated, but special constitutional
> amendments create exceptions such as the Bodoland Territorial Council.

## Must-Know Facts
- ✅ **Fifth Schedule:** President declares scheduled areas; Governor sends **annual report**; **Tribes Advisory
  Council** (20 members, 3/4 ST MLAs) advises.
- ✅ **Sixth Schedule:** ordinarily an Autonomous District Council has **up to 30
  members (26 elected + 4 nominated)** and elected members serve 5 years; the Bodoland
  Territorial Council has a special **46-member** design under the 2003 amendment. Councils
  legislate on specified land, forest and customary matters, subject to gubernatorial assent.
- ✅ Commissions on scheduled areas: **Dhebar (1960)**, **Bhuria (2002)**.
- ✅ **Art 244A** — autonomous state within Assam.

### 5.2 Qualified thesis options

- *The Fifth and Sixth Schedules are two different answers to the same problem: the Fifth protects tribes through the Governor and the Centre, while the Sixth empowers them through elected councils — protection versus self-government.*

### 5.4 Bank A — Fifth Schedule architecture (Scheduled Areas)

- **Claim:** Scheduled Areas are declared centrally, not by the State. → **Evidence:** **Art 244 + Fifth Schedule** — the **President** declares/alters/rescinds a Scheduled Area in consultation with the Governor; **10 States** have them (AP, Telangana, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, HP, Jharkhand, MP, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan). → **Significance:** the Union retains a supervisory stake in tribal administration. → **Caution:** ✅ Scheduled Areas exist only outside the four Sixth-Schedule States.
- **Claim:** The State administers, but under a Union check. → **Evidence:** the State's executive power extends to Scheduled Areas, **but the Union's executive power extends to giving directions to the State** on their administration, and the **Governor sends an annual report** to the President. → **Significance:** a built-in Union-takeover-by-direction mechanism short of emergency. → **Caution:** ✅ this is the exact point on "executive power and Union takeover" — the Union directs; it does not automatically administer.
- **Claim:** The Governor holds special legislative-modification and regulation powers. → **Evidence:** the Governor may direct that a Central or State Act **does not apply, or applies with modifications**, to a Scheduled Area, and may make **regulations** prohibiting/restricting **transfer of tribal land** and regulating **money-lending** — all needing the **President's assent**. → **Significance:** a protective shield over tribal land and credit. → **Caution:** ✅ these regulations need the **President's** assent, not the Governor's own.
- **Claim:** Consultation and finance are institutionalised. → **Evidence:** the **Tribes Advisory Council (20 members, 3/4 ST MLAs)** in every Fifth-Schedule State; **Art 275(1)** grants-in-aid from the Consolidated Fund of India for tribal welfare and raising Scheduled-Area administration; commissions under the Schedule (**Dhebar 1960**, **Bhuria 2002**). → **Significance:** advisory voice plus a dedicated funding route. → **Caution:** ✅ the TAC is **advisory only** — it does not legislate.

### 5.5 Bank B — Sixth Schedule architecture (Tribal Areas)

- **Claim:** Tribal areas in the north-east get elected self-government. → **Evidence:** **Art 244 + Sixth Schedule** — tribal areas of **Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram** constituted as **autonomous districts** (and **autonomous regions** where tribes differ), the **Governor** organising/reorganising them. → **Significance:** genuine devolved government, not mere protection. → **Caution:** ✅ the **Governor** organises districts here; the **President** declares Scheduled Areas under the Fifth Schedule — do not swap.
- **Claim:** District Councils legislate, adjudicate and tax. → **Evidence:** an **Autonomous District Council** (ordinarily up to **30** — 26 elected + 4 nominated, five-year term; **Bodoland Territorial Council** a special **46-member** design) legislates on **land, forests, canal water, shifting cultivation, inheritance, marriage/divorce and social customs** (Governor's assent), constitutes **village councils/courts**, and levies **land revenue and specified taxes** (professions, trades, animals, vehicles, markets, tolls). → **Significance:** legislative + judicial + fiscal autonomy in one body. → **Caution:** ✅ Central/State Acts apply to autonomous districts **only with modifications**.
- **Claim:** Assam has an additional autonomy route. → **Evidence:** **Art 244A** permits an **autonomous State within Assam** with its own legislature/Council of Ministers. → **Significance:** the deepest constitutional autonomy short of statehood. → **Caution:** ✅ Art 244A is specific to **Assam**.

### 5.6 Bank C — The rights layer: PESA, FRA and *Samatha*

- **Claim:** PESA extends self-rule to Fifth-Schedule Gram Sabhas. → **Evidence:** **PESA 1996** empowers the **Gram Sabha** to approve local development plans, identify beneficiaries, **recommend (mandatorily) grant of minor-mineral prospecting/mining leases**, be **consulted before land acquisition and resettlement**, manage **minor forest produce**, prevent and restore **tribal land alienation**, control **money-lending** and village markets. → **Significance:** converts protection into participatory power. → **Caution:** ✅ PESA covers **Fifth**, not Sixth, Schedule areas; ⚠️ implementation lags the statute.
- **Claim:** The Forest Rights Act is the companion rights statute. → **Evidence:** the **Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act 2006** recognises individual and community forest rights and vests the **Gram Sabha** with authority to initiate and verify claims. → **Significance:** statutory tenure security over forest land and produce. → **Caution:** ⚠️ recognition of community rights remains uneven in practice.
- **Claim:** Courts have barred alienation of Scheduled-Area land to non-tribals. → **Evidence:** ✅ *Samatha v. State of Andhra Pradesh* (1997) held that transfer of land in Scheduled Areas, **including the grant of mining leases to non-tribals and private companies, is barred**; such leases may go to the State, its instrumentalities or **cooperatives of tribals**. → **Significance:** a judicial reinforcement of the land-transfer prohibition. → **Caution:** ⚠️ *Samatha*'s reach beyond Andhra Pradesh has been debated — state it as the Scheduled-Areas mining-lease proposition, not as settled uniformly nationwide.

### 5.7 Mechanism, institutional incentives and consequences

- ⚠️ **Protection vs empowerment logic:** the Fifth Schedule keeps ultimate control with the Governor and Centre (annual report, directions, President's-assent regulations), while the Sixth Schedule vests power in elected councils — different theories of safeguarding tribes; the Governor's Fifth-Schedule powers depend on his own initiative and State cooperation, so without a duty to act the land-transfer and law-modification powers are rarely used.
- ⚠️ **PESA's consent as leverage:** the mandatory Gram-Sabha recommendation for minor-mineral leases and consultation before acquisition give tribes a veto-like lever — where enforced, it changes project outcomes; where bypassed, it fuels conflict and displacement disputes.

### 5.8 Criticism, counter-argument, State variation and implementation constraints

- **Dormancy critique:** ⚠️ the Governor's Fifth-Schedule powers and the TAC are widely described as under-used; PESA rules were delayed for years in several States.
- **Counter (real safeguards):** ⚠️ *Samatha*, PESA's Gram-Sabha consent and FRA community rights show the framework can deliver where courts and Gram Sabhas are active.
- **Fifth vs Sixth variation:** ⚠️ Sixth-Schedule councils genuinely legislate and tax, whereas Fifth-Schedule bodies only advise — autonomy is unequal by design; within the Sixth Schedule, Bodoland's 46-member council is a special case.
- **Implementation constraints:** ⚠️ delayed PESA State rules, weak TACs, gubernatorial inaction, contested land-transfer enforcement, and pending demands (Ladakh's Sixth-Schedule agitation, new autonomous-council demands in Assam).

### 5.9 Verdict scaffolds

- **Comparison verdict:** "The Sixth Schedule gives tribes a government; the Fifth Schedule gives them a guardian — which is why regions seeking real self-rule press for the Sixth."
- **Implementation verdict:** "India's tribal-governance failure is not one of design but of use: the Governor's powers, the Tribes Advisory Council and PESA's Gram Sabha are strong instruments left largely idle."
- **Rights verdict:** "*Samatha*, PESA and the Forest Rights Act together turned the Fifth Schedule's protective promise into enforceable tribal rights, but only where a Gram Sabha or a court insists on them."

- ✅ TAC = **20 members, 3/4 ST MLAs**; Sixth-Schedule District Council = up to **30** (26 elected + 4 nominated); **Bodoland = 46** (special); commissions **Dhebar 1960**, **Bhuria 2002**.
