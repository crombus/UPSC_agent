## Core idea
The GST Council is a **joint Centre-state forum** that recommends GST rates, exemptions,
model laws and related design through a special weighted vote. Legislatures/executives give
legal effect to those recommendations; they are **not binding** (*Mohit Minerals*, 2022).

> 🔑 **Voting maths:** decisions need **3/4 weighted votes**; **Centre = 1/3**, **all states = 2/3**; **quorum = ½**.
> 🔑 The Centre alone can **block** (3/4 needed) but **cannot pass** without states.

## Must-Know Facts
- ✅ Chair = **Union FM**; members = Union MoS Finance + one minister per state; **Vice-Chair chosen by states**.
- ✅ **Revenue Secretary** = ex-officio Secretary; **CBIC chair** = permanent non-voting invitee.
- ✅ Petrol, diesel, ATF, natural gas, crude are **outside GST** until the Council decides a date.
- ✅ Recommended **5-year compensation** to states (ended June 2022).

## 📰 Current link
📰 **56th Council recommendation (3 Sep 2025):** proposed broad **5% and 18%** rates plus a special **40%** de-merit rate, with most changes intended from **22 Sep 2025**; individual life and health insurance exemptions were recommended. Verify the applicable Gazette notification before stating any supply’s legal rate. **Mohit Minerals (2022):** Council recommendations are **persuasive, not binding**.

## 5. Answer architecture (10/15/20-mark support)

### 5.0 Direct Mains demand owned by this Core file

- **2023 · GS-II · Q15** — Demand (neutral rendering): *the significance of the 101st Constitutional Amendment Act and how far it reflects the accommodative spirit of federalism.* Directive: **Explain the significance** · **15 marks · 250 words**.
- **Core routing supersedes the older `advanced/30_GST-Council.md` pointer for this demand.**
- **Directive reading:** "Explain the significance" + "to what extent" = state the constitutional importance **and** deliver a graded judgement on the accommodativeness claim (not uncritical praise).
- **Executable route (15-mark, 250 words):**
  1. **Thesis:** the 101st Amendment (2016) created a genuinely **shared taxing space** — a rare pooling of sovereignty — that embodies accommodative/cooperative federalism, though its accommodativeness is qualified by asymmetric bargaining power.
  2. **Constitutional significance:** **Art 246A** gives **both** Parliament and State legislatures *simultaneous* power to tax goods and services, breaking the mutually exclusive Union/State-List design; **Art 269A** handles inter-State supply through **IGST**; **Art 279A** institutionalises the **GST Council**; the reform removed cascading and created one national market.
  3. **Accommodative design:** the voting formula (Centre **1/3**, States **2/3**, **3/4** of weighted votes to decide) means **neither side can act unilaterally**; the **GST (Compensation to States) Act, 2017** guaranteed States revenue protection for **five years**; States surrendered VAT, entry tax, octroi and others for a pooled share.
  4. **Judicial reinforcement:** *Union of India v. Mohit Minerals* (2022) held the Council's recommendations are **persuasive, not binding**, that both levels retain legislative power, and called for "**collaborative dialogue**".
  5. **Qualification:** loss of independent State rate-setting, dependence on transfers, disputes over rate rationalisation and compensation-cess extension, and petroleum/alcohol/electricity kept outside GST temper the "accommodative" claim.
  6. **Verdict:** the Amendment institutionalised **pooled sovereignty** — India's most striking accommodative-federalism experiment — but its durability depends on honouring compensation, deciding by consensus and preserving the States' voice.

### 5.2 Qualified thesis options

- *The 101st Amendment is best understood as a pooling of sovereignty rather than a transfer of it: both the Union and the States gained a shared taxing power and simultaneously lost the freedom to set rates alone.*
- *The GST Council is a genuine instrument of accommodative federalism in its voting design, but its accommodativeness is asymmetric because the Centre alone can block while no group of States can pass without the Centre.*
- Mohit Minerals *converted a potential centralising instrument into a collaborative one by ruling the Council merely recommendatory, thereby protecting rather than diluting fiscal federalism.*
- *GST traded the States' rate autonomy for a wider tax base and cascading-free market; whether that bargain remains "accommodative" depends on whether compensation commitments and consensus decision-making are honoured.*

### 5.4 Bank A — Constitutional architecture

Every unit: **claim → named Article → mechanism → limitation.**

| Provision | What it establishes | Mechanism / significance | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| ✅ **Art 246A** | Special power to tax goods and services | **Both** Parliament and State legislatures have **simultaneous** power — a break from the exclusive Union/State Lists | Parliament has **exclusive** power over **inter-State** supply |
| ✅ **Art 269A** | **IGST** on inter-State supply | Levied and collected by the **Centre**, then **apportioned** between the Centre and the States | Prevents each State taxing the same inter-State transaction |
| ✅ **Art 279A** | The **GST Council** | Constitutional recommendatory forum on rates, exemptions, model laws, thresholds | Recommendations are **not binding** (*Mohit Minerals*) |
| ✅ **Art 366(12A)** | Defines "goods and services tax" | A tax on supply of goods or services, except taxes on the supply of alcoholic liquor for human consumption | Petroleum products are within the definition but **kept out until the Council fixes a date** |
| ✅ **101st Amendment Act, 2016** | The enabling amendment | Subsumed most Central and State indirect taxes; created one national market | Presidential order constituted the Council on **15 Sep 2016** |

### 5.5 Bank B — Composition and the voting formula (mechanism and incentives)

| Element | Value | Institutional incentive |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ Chairperson | **Union Finance Minister** | Central convening role |
| ✅ Members | Union MoS (Revenue/Finance) + one minister per State | Every State has a seat and a vote |
| ✅ Vice-Chairperson | Chosen **by the State members** among themselves | A federal counterweight in the chair structure |
| ✅ Quorum | **½** of total members | Guards against thin-house decisions |
| ✅ Decision majority | **≥ 3/4 of the weighted votes** of members present and voting | Forces a broad coalition |
| ✅ Centre's weight | **1/3** of votes cast | ⚠️ The Centre alone can **block** (3/4 needed) but **cannot pass** alone |
| ✅ States' weight | **2/3** of votes cast (shared equally among States present and voting) | No single State dominates; States must combine |

- **Design insight:** ⚠️ the formula is engineered so that **neither the Centre alone nor the States alone can carry a decision** — the structural expression of "accommodative federalism". Acts are not invalidated by a vacancy or a non-material procedural defect.

### 5.6 Bank C — Compensation architecture and its statutory basis

- ✅ **Claim → basis:** States were guaranteed compensation for revenue loss for **five years** under the **GST (Compensation to States) Act, 2017**, funded by a **compensation cess** on demerit/luxury goods.
- ✅ **Mechanism:** the cess pooled resources to top up any State whose GST revenue grew below a protected rate.
- 📰 ✅ **Status caution:** the five-year window **ended in June 2022**; the compensation cess has since been **extended to service back-to-back loans** raised during the pandemic — cite the June 2022 end and re-verify the cess's current end-date before quoting.
- ⚠️ **Significance:** the compensation guarantee was the political price that made States accept the loss of independent taxing power; its lapse is the sharpest test of the scheme's "accommodative" character.

### 5.7 Accommodative federalism and *Mohit Minerals* — mechanism and consequences

- **Claim:** GST embodies accommodative/cooperative federalism. **Provision:** the Art 279A voting design + Art 246A's shared power. **Mechanism:** a permanent institutional forum forces the Union and the States to negotiate rates and design continuously rather than legislate unilaterally. **Consequence:** ⚠️ it converts indirect taxation into a **standing, collaborative bargain**.
- ✅ **Case as an exact proposition:** in **Union of India v. Mohit Minerals (2022)** the Supreme Court held that the **recommendations of the GST Council are not binding** on the Union and the States and have only **persuasive value**, because **both** Parliament and the State legislatures possess **simultaneous power** to legislate on GST under **Art 246A**; the Court urged the Council to function through "**collaborative dialogue**". (The dispute concerned IGST on ocean freight on reverse charge, which the Court struck down.)
- ⚠️ **Analytical significance:** the ruling **protected** fiscal federalism — a "binding" reading would have subordinated State legislatures to the Council; the "persuasive" reading keeps the States' constitutional power intact.

### 5.8 Criticism, counter-argument and variation

- **Loss of fiscal autonomy:** ⚠️ States surrendered independent rate-setting over most indirect taxes; they can no longer tailor rates to local revenue needs.
- **Dependence on transfers:** ⚠️ States rely on IGST apportionment, the divisible pool and (formerly) compensation, deepening vertical fiscal imbalance.
- **Asymmetric bargaining:** ⚠️ the Centre's 1/3 weight plus a few aligned States can block any change; smaller States have little individual leverage.
- **Rate-setting and exclusions disputes:** ⚠️ friction over rate rationalisation (GST 2.0), and over keeping **petroleum, natural gas and electricity** outside GST, recur.
- **Compensation dispute:** 📰 ⚠️ the end of the five-year guarantee (June 2022) and the demand for extension are the leading "coercive vs cooperative federalism" grievances.
- **Counter-argument:** ⚠️ *Mohit Minerals*, the near-unanimous consensus practice of the Council, and the removal of cascading and inter-State barriers show the accommodative design working in substance, not just form.

### 5.9 Verdict scaffolds

- **Significance verdict:** "The 101st Amendment is India's boldest pooling of taxing sovereignty; its accommodative character is real in design and, after *Mohit Minerals*, secure in law, but conditional in practice on honoured compensation and consensus."
- **Autonomy verdict:** "GST did not centralise indirect taxation so much as collectivise it; the States lost rate autonomy but gained a permanent seat at a table where the Centre cannot act alone."
- **Reform verdict:** "The unfinished agenda — petroleum inclusion, a durable compensation settlement and genuine consensus — will decide whether the Council remains a laboratory of cooperative federalism or slides toward coercive federalism."

### 5.11 Factual-risk and current-status controls

- ✅ GST is the **101st** Constitutional Amendment Act (2016); the enabling **Bill** was numbered the 122nd — do not present the Bill number as the Act number.
- ✅ The GST Council is **constitutional** (Art 279A); its recommendations are **not binding** (*Mohit Minerals*, 2022) — never state them as binding commands.
- ✅ Voting: Centre **1/3**, States **2/3**, decision **≥ 3/4** of weighted votes; quorum **½**.
- 📰 ✅ The **56th Council meeting release (3 Sep 2025)** recommended broad **5% and 18%** rates plus a special **40%** de-merit rate, mostly intended from **22 Sep 2025**. A recommendation is not self-executing: verify the relevant Gazette notification and effective date.
- 📰 ✅ GST compensation's statutory five-year window ended in **June 2022**; the official 56th-meeting material retained cess on specified tobacco-related goods until the related loan and interest liabilities are discharged; do not state one universal end-date.
- ⚠️ Do not claim petroleum, alcohol for human consumption or electricity are under GST; alcoholic liquor for human consumption is **constitutionally excluded**, the others are **kept out until the Council decides**.

### Semantic-completeness ownership and PYQ control

- **Constitutional map:** the 101st Amendment inserted Articles 246A, 269A and
  279A and amended related provisions. Article 246A creates simultaneous Union-
  State GST competence subject to Parliament's exclusive inter-State field;
  Article 269A governs inter-State levy/collection and apportionment; Article
  279A creates the recommendatory Council.
- **Composition:** the Union Finance Minister chairs; the Union Minister of State
  in charge of Revenue or Finance and one finance/taxation or nominated Minister
  from each State are members. State members may choose a Vice-Chairperson.
  Nirmala Sitharaman remains the official Union Finance Minister/current Chair
  on the 5 September 2026 control date.
- **Voting:** one-half of total membership is quorum. At a meeting the Union has
  one-third of total votes cast, States together two-thirds, and a proposal
  requires at least three-fourths of weighted votes of members present and voting.
  Quorum, vote weight and decision threshold must never be conflated.
- **Procedure rules:** the official Conduct of Business Rules provide ordinarily
  seven days' meeting notice, agenda notes at least three days before the meeting
  and a two-day emergency-meeting route with Chair approval. The Vice-Chairperson
  has a two-year term or until ceasing to be a member/resigning, whichever is earlier.
- **Secretariat boundary:** the Revenue Secretary is ex-officio Secretary and
  the CBIC Chair is a permanent non-voting invitee by the 2016 Cabinet decision;
  neither becomes a voting constitutional member. GSTN and GSTAT are different institutions.
- **Recommendation-to-law chain:** a Council recommendation is not a tax rate
  by itself. Parliament/State law and valid delegated notifications give legal
  effect subject to the governing statute and constitutional competence.
- **Judicial rule:** Union of India v Mohit Minerals (2022) holds Council
  recommendations persuasive rather than binding because Union and States hold
  simultaneous Article 246A legislative power. The ocean-freight levy failed
  on the statutory/constitutional analysis; the case does not erase the Council.
- **Current meeting:** the official 56th GST Council release dated 3 September
  2025 is the latest meeting release located by 5 September 2026. It recommended
  broad 5 and 18 per cent rates plus a special 40 per cent demerit rate, with
  most changes intended from 22 September 2025. Every supply-specific legal rate
  still requires the applicable CBIC notification.
- **Compensation status:** the statutory five-year State-compensation entitlement
  ended in June 2022. Under the 56th-meeting official material, compensation
  cess continued on specified tobacco-related goods until discharge of the
  related loan and interest liabilities; no universal post-2022 entitlement is implied.
- **Institution/PYQ firewall:** the GST Council does not distribute the divisible
  pool (Finance Commission), administer GST (tax administrations), operate the
  network (GSTN) or adjudicate appeals (GSTAT). The direct 2023 accommodative-
  federalism Mains demand and routed GST/fiscal-federalism demands retain their
  verified ownership.
