## Core idea
✅ The President is the *nominal* head; the **PM + Council of Ministers is the real executive**. **Art 74** —
CoM aids & advises the President (advice **binding**, can't be questioned in court). **Art 75** — collective
responsibility to the Lok Sabha: one no-confidence motion sinks the **whole** ministry.

> 🔑 **PM's role tags:** Morley = *"primus inter pares"* + *"keystone of the cabinet arch"*; Jennings = *"the
> sun around which planets revolve"*; Ambedkar = *"if any office matches the US President, it's the PM."*

> 🔑 **CoM vs Cabinet:** CoM = **60-70** (all ranks, never meets together) ⊃ **Cabinet** = **15-20** (senior
> ministers who actually decide, meet weekly).

## Three ranks of ministers
✅ **Cabinet Ministers** (top, in Cabinet) → **Ministers of State** (independent charge / attached) →
**Deputy Ministers** (assist).

## Must-Know Facts
- ✅ **91st Amdt 2003:** ministers ≤ **15% of LS**; defector can't be a minister.
- ✅ **Art 88:** ministers **speak in either House, vote only in their own**.
- ✅ PM's resignation/death = resignation of the **entire** CoM.
- ✅ **Cabinet Committees** = extra-constitutional; **Political Affairs Committee** = "Super-Cabinet";
  **Parliamentary Affairs Committee** chaired by the **Home Minister**, the rest usually by the **PM**.
- ✅ **Kitchen Cabinet** = informal inner circle of the PM (very powerful under Indira Gandhi).

### 6.2 Qualified thesis options

- *Collective responsibility is the load-bearing wall of Indian parliamentary government — Article 75(3) makes the ministry a single body that stands or falls together — yet the coalition era has turned a doctrine of solidarity into a daily negotiation.*
- *The Prime Minister is "primus inter pares" only on paper; control of portfolios, the dissolution recommendation and the channel to the President make him the keystone of the arch, but allies, the Cabinet and the courts keep the arch from becoming a pillar.*
- *The 91st Amendment tried to convert the ministry from an instrument of patronage into a disciplined executive by capping its size and barring defectors, but the same anti-defection logic that stabilises governments also hollows out the backbench.*
- *India has moved toward prime-ministerial government in fact without amending the cabinet form in law; whether that is dominance or leadership depends on whether the majority is single-party or coalitional.*

### 6.4 Bank A — Collective and individual responsibility (this file's owned demand)

| Claim | Named anchor | Mechanism / significance | Limitation / caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| The ministry is collectively answerable to the popular House | ✅ **Art 75(3)** | The CoM is **collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha**; a passed **no-confidence motion forces the entire ministry** (including RS ministers) to resign | The **Rajya Sabha cannot remove** the ministry; only the LS can |
| Cabinet decisions bind every minister | ✅ **Cabinet-solidarity convention** | A minister who cannot defend a Cabinet decision must resign | ✅ **Ambedkar resigned (1953)** over the Hindu Code Bill — dissent means exit, not public opposition |
| Ministers hold office individually at pleasure | ✅ **Art 75(2)** | Ministers hold office **during the pleasure of the President**; the President removes a minister **on the PM's advice** | Individual responsibility is weaker in practice — the PM, not Parliament, is the effective enforcer |
| The ministry's advice is legally insulated | ✅ **Art 74(2)** | Whether and what advice the CoM gave the President **cannot be inquired into by courts** | Protects collective deliberation; unlike Britain, no counter-signature requirement |
| The PM is the hinge of communication | ✅ **Art 78** | The PM communicates CoM decisions to the President and furnishes information he seeks | Makes the PM the sole authoritative channel between the ministry and the head of State |
| Ministers may speak widely but vote narrowly | ✅ **Art 88** | A minister may **speak and take part in either House** and its committees | **Votes only in the House of which he is a member** |

- **Answer rule:** a "collective responsibility" question is best answered by pairing **Art 75(3)** (the rule) against the **coalition-era caveat** (the practice), then judging whether solidarity survives.

### 6.5 Bank B — Appointment of the PM and the hung-house convention

- **Claim:** the Constitution leaves the PM's appointment to convention, which is why it matters most when convention is silent. **Provision:** ✅ **Art 75(1)** — the PM is "appointed by the President"; **no procedure or qualification** is prescribed. **Mechanism:** by convention the President appoints the **leader of the majority party** in the Lok Sabha. **Discretion arises** in two situations: ⚠️ (a) **no clear majority** — the President invites the leader of the largest party/likely coalition to **prove confidence** (first used with **Charan Singh, 1979**); (b) **sudden death of an incumbent PM** with no obvious successor (**Rajiv Gandhi, 1984**). **Limitation/status:** ✅ the **Delhi HC (1980)** held the PM need not prove majority *before* appointment; ✅ the **SC (1997)** held a **non-MP can be PM for six months** (Art 75(5)); the PM may be from **either House** — ✅ **Indira Gandhi (1966), Deve Gowda (1996), Manmohan Singh (2004)** were **Rajya Sabha** members.
- **Floor-test discipline:** ⚠️ the constitutional test of majority is a **vote on the floor**, not the President's subjective assessment — the same rule the courts apply to Governors and Chief Ministers.

### 6.6 Bank C — Council, Cabinet, committees and the PMO

| Body | Named anchor | Mechanism | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| ✅ **Council of Ministers** | Art 74, 75 | **60–70** members of all three ranks; **never meets as a body**; holds power "in theory" | The full CoM is not the decision forum |
| ✅ **Cabinet** | Convention (not in the original text) | **15–20** senior ministers; **meets, deliberates and decides**; directs the CoM | The word **"Cabinet" entered the Constitution only via the 44th Amdt (1978) in Art 352** (Emergency on the Cabinet's written recommendation) |
| ✅ **Three ranks** | Convention | **Cabinet Ministers** → **Ministers of State** (independent charge/attached) → **Deputy Ministers** | A "Deputy PM" is **descriptive only** (SC upheld Devi Lal's 1990 oath) |
| ✅ **Cabinet Committees** | **Rules of Business (extra-constitutional)** | Set up by the **PM**, 3–8 members; **Political Affairs** = "Super-Cabinet"; **CCS/CCEA/Appointments** chaired by the PM; **Parliamentary Affairs** chaired by the **Home Minister** | Names/number change; learn the **mechanism**, not a frozen count |
| ✅ **Kitchen Cabinet** | Convention (extra-constitutional) | Informal inner circle of the PM + a few trusted colleagues; powerful under **Indira Gandhi** | Not a decision body known to law |
| ⚠️ **Prime Minister's Office (PMO)** | **Non-constitutional / non-statutory** staff agency | Headed by the **Principal Secretary to the PM**; processes matters needing the PM's attention | **Not** a constitutional decision-maker and **not** the Cabinet Secretariat |

- **91st Amdt (2003):** ✅ total ministers **incl. the PM ≤ 15% of the Lok Sabha's** strength (**Art 75(1A)**); a member **disqualified for defection** is barred from ministership (**Art 75(1B)**).
- **Art 77 (business of government):** ✅ all executive action of the Government of India is expressed to be taken **in the President's name**, and the President makes rules for the **convenient transaction and allocation of business** among ministers (the Allocation/Transaction of Business Rules) — the constitutional bridge from the Council to organised departments (owner: `Ministries-and-Departments-of-Government.md`).

### 6.8 Mechanism, institutional incentives and consequences

- ⚠️ **Why collective responsibility disciplines:** the "resign together" rule (Art 75(3)) plus the **party whip** and **Cabinet secrecy** create a unified executive front that Parliament can hold to account as one — the incentive is solidarity, enforced by the fear of collapse.
- ⚠️ **Why the PM accumulates power:** control of portfolios, the dissolution threat, the Art 78 channel and party leadership give the PM structural leverage the Constitution never enumerates — office, not text, concentrates power.
- ⚠️ **Consequence of the 91st Amdt:** capping ministry size curbed "jumbo councils" and patronage, but the defection bar tied ministership to party discipline, strengthening the leadership against the backbench.

### 6.9 Criticism, counter-argument and variation

- **Dilution critique:** ⚠️ in coalitions allies dissent publicly and bargain portfolios, so collective responsibility is honoured more in form than in substance.
- **Counter:** ⚠️ even coalitions fall as a body on a lost confidence vote, so the **legal** rule of collective responsibility is intact even where the **political** convention frays.
- **Federal/coalition variation:** ⚠️ prime-ministerial dominance is strongest under a single-party majority and weakest under a fractured mandate; the same office looks presidential in one Parliament and brokered in the next.
- **Implementation constraint:** ⚠️ individual ministerial responsibility is weakly enforced — resignations for departmental failure are rare, and the PM, not Parliament, is the real disciplinary authority.

### 6.10 Verdict scaffolds

- **Collective-responsibility verdict:** "Article 75(3) still makes the ministry one body in law; the coalition era has made it many voices in practice, but the confidence of the Lok Sabha remains the single thread on which the whole ministry hangs."
- **Appointment verdict:** "The President's discretion in appointing a PM is dormant under a decisive verdict and decisive under a hung one, but it always ends at the same place — a floor test, not a Raj Bhavan opinion."
- **Prime-ministerial-government verdict:** "India has prime-ministerial government when the PM has a majority and cabinet government when he does not; the form is constant, the reality is arithmetic."

### 6.12 Factual-risk and current-status controls

- ✅ The **15% cap is on the Lok Sabha's** strength (91st Amdt), never on both Houses combined.
- ✅ **"Cabinet" is not in the original Constitution**; it entered via the **44th Amdt** in **Art 352** — do not claim it was there in 1950.
- ✅ Cabinet Committees and the **Kitchen Cabinet** are **extra-constitutional**; the **PMO** is **non-constitutional/non-statutory** — none is a body "created by the Constitution".
- ✅ A **non-MP may be PM/minister for six months only** (Art 75(5)); do not say "never".
- ⚠️ Scholarly tags (Morley, Jennings, Laski, Ambedkar) are **characterisations**, not constitutional text — attribute them, don't assert them as law.
- ⚠️ "Deputy Prime Minister" is **descriptive**, with no distinct constitutional powers (SC on Devi Lal's 1990 oath).
- ✅ Collective responsibility is **Art 75(3)** (to the Lok Sabha); individual responsibility is **Art 75(2)** (pleasure) — keep the clause numbers distinct.
