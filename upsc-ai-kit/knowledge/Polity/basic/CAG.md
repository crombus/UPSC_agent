## Core idea
The CAG is the **"guardian of the public purse"** and an **agent of Parliament** — he audits all government
spending and reports to the legislature, securing executive financial accountability. Ambedkar called him **"the
most important officer under the Constitution."**

> 🔑 **Comptroller in name only** — India's CAG audits **after** spending (no control over money issue, unlike
> Britain).
> 🔑 **Reports:** Centre → **President → Parliament**; State → **Governor → State Legislature**.

## Must-Know Facts
- ✅ Salary = **Supreme Court judge**; expenses **charged on the CFI**.
- ✅ **Not eligible for any further govt office** after retirement.
- ✅ Three reports: **Appropriation Accounts, Finance Accounts, Public Undertakings**.
- ✅ **Guide, friend & philosopher of the PAC** (Public Accounts Committee).
- ✅ **Legal/regulatory audit** = obligatory; **propriety audit** = discretionary.
- ✅ **RBI, LIC, SBI, FCI** are exclusively **privately audited** (CAG not involved).

### 5.0 Direct Mains demands owned by this Core file

- **2018 · GS-II · Q5** — Demand (neutral rendering): *the method of appointment, the terms of office and the range of powers of the CAG.* Directive: **Explain** · **10 marks · 150 words**.
  - **Core routing supersedes the older `advanced/32_CAG.md` pointer for this demand.**
  - **Executable route:** (1) **Appointment** — **Art 148**: appointed by the **President** by warrant under hand and seal; the executive appoints, with no prescribed collegium. (2) **Terms** — **6 years or 65 years**, whichever earlier; salary and service conditions equal to a **Supreme Court judge**, determined by Parliament and **not variable to disadvantage**; salary and administrative expenses **charged on the Consolidated Fund of India**; **removable only like a Supreme Court judge** (address of both Houses by special majority on proved misbehaviour/incapacity), and **barred from any further office** under the Union or a State. (3) **Powers** — under **Art 149** and the **CAG's (DPC) Act, 1971** the CAG audits the **Consolidated Fund, Contingency Fund and Public Account** of the Union/States, all receipts and expenditure, **government companies**, corporations and **bodies substantially financed** from public funds; **Art 150** — advises on the **form of accounts**; **Art 151** — submits reports to the **President → Parliament** (and Governor → State legislature); **Art 279** — certifies the **net proceeds** of a tax, finally. (4) **Limits** — "**Comptroller in name only**" (no control over the *issue* of money), secret-service expenditure, and propriety audit only discretionary. (5) **Verdict** — a constitutionally insulated auditor whose weakness lies at the *appointment* end, not the tenure end.
- **2024 · GS-II · Q4** — Demand (neutral rendering): *the CAG's duty is not merely to ensure the legality of expenditure but also its propriety.* Directive: **Comment** · **10 marks · 150 words**.
  - **Core routing supersedes the older `advanced/32_CAG.md` pointer for this demand.**
  - **Executable route:** (1) **Position** — the CAG's mandate runs beyond legality to propriety and outcomes, but must stop short of substituting its judgement for policy. (2) **Legality/regulatory audit** — **obligatory**: was the money **legally available** and **spent on the sanctioned purpose** (appropriation and compliance)? (3) **Propriety audit** — **discretionary**: tests the **wisdom, faithfulness and economy** of spending, exposing waste, extravagance and conflict of interest beyond mere legality. (4) **Performance audit** — extends propriety to whether a programme achieved its objectives economically and effectively. (5) **Tension** — propriety edges toward questioning **policy**; the executive resists, but without it legality becomes a formality that cannot catch waste. (6) **Verdict** — the CAG should audit the **propriety of implementation** while preserving the elected government's space to choose the **policy** — legality and propriety are complementary, not rival, duties.

### 5.2 Qualified thesis options

- *The CAG is powerfully protected where it works and weakly chosen where it begins: removal is as hard as for a Supreme Court judge, yet appointment is a sole executive act — the reform frontier is the appointment, not the tenure.*
- *India's CAG is a "Comptroller in name only": unlike Britain's, it cannot stop money leaving the Consolidated Fund and audits only after the fact, which makes Parliament — through the PAC and COPU — the real point at which its findings bite.*
- *The legality–propriety distinction defines the CAG's reach: legality audit is obligatory and uncontroversial, propriety audit is discretionary and contested precisely because it approaches the wisdom of policy.*
- *The CAG's authority is post-facto and advisory, so its effectiveness depends less on its own powers than on the legislative committees that act on its reports.*
- *The CAG audits both accounts and outcomes but cannot enforce either; it is the Constitution's most authoritative reporter of financial fact and its least powerful corrector of financial wrong.*

### 5.5 Bank B — Duties and powers (Art 149–151, 279 + DPC Act 1971)

- **Claim → the CAG's powers are constitutional in frame but statutory in detail.** ✅ **Art 149** makes the CAG's duties "as prescribed by Parliament", exercised through the **Comptroller and Auditor General's (Duties, Powers and Conditions of Service) Act, 1971** (amended 1976 to **separate accounts from audit**). → **Mechanism:** the CAG audits the **Consolidated Fund, Contingency Fund and Public Account** of the Union, States and UTs with a legislature; all **receipts and expenditure**; stores and stock; **government companies**; and **bodies/authorities substantially financed** from public funds. → **Limit:** the audit is **ex post** — it reports, it cannot disallow.
- ✅ **Art 150** — the accounts of the Union and States are kept in the form the **President prescribes on the CAG's advice**.
- ✅ **Art 151** — the CAG's reports on Union accounts go to the **President**, who lays them before **Parliament**; State reports go to the **Governor** and the State legislature.
- ✅ **Art 279** — the CAG **ascertains and certifies** the **net proceeds** of a tax, and the certificate is **final** (the link to the Finance Commission's divisible pool).
- ✅ **Three reports** to the President: **Appropriation Accounts**, **Finance Accounts**, and **Public Undertakings**.

| Fund / entity | Within the CAG's audit? |
|---|---|
| ✅ Consolidated Fund (Union/State/UT with a legislature) | Yes |
| ✅ Contingency Fund and Public Account | Yes |
| ✅ Receipts and expenditure; stores and stock | Yes |
| ✅ Government companies and corporations | Yes, under the DPC Act read with the Companies Act |
| ✅ Bodies substantially financed from public funds | Yes, under the DPC Act (threshold statutory — do not quote a figure) |
| ✅ Secret-service expenditure | Only on a **certificate**, not on particulars |

### 5.6 Bank C — The three kinds of audit

| Audit | Question it asks | Status / caution |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ **Legal / regulatory (compliance)** | Was the money **legally available** and spent on the **sanctioned purpose**? | **Obligatory** |
| ✅ **Propriety** | Was the spending **wise, faithful and economical** — free of waste/extravagance? | **Discretionary**; ⚠️ contested because it edges toward **policy wisdom** |
| ⚠️ **Performance / efficiency** | Did the programme achieve its **objectives economically and effectively**? | The modern expansion; drives "CAG overreach vs accountability" debates |

- **Analytical use:** the legality/propriety pair is the exact hinge of the 2024 demand — legality is a floor the CAG *must* check; propriety is the discretionary reach that makes audit meaningful, provided it audits *implementation* rather than substituting for *policy*.

### 5.7 Bank D — Audit reach, exclusions and the accountability route

- **Claim → the CAG's reach over the wider public sector is graded, not total.** ✅ Public corporations fall into **three categories**: (i) **audited fully by the CAG**; (ii) **private auditors plus CAG supplementary audit**; and (iii) **exclusively private audit** — the CAG is **not** involved (per the local files, **RBI, LIC, SBI, FCI**). → **Mechanism:** ✅ **bodies substantially financed** from government funds fall within audit under the **DPC Act** (the financing threshold is statutory — ⚠️ do not quote a figure not in these files). → **Limit:** ✅ **secret-service expenditure** is audited only on a certificate from the competent authority, not on particulars.
- **Accountability route:** ✅ the CAG's reports are examined by the **Public Accounts Committee (PAC)** and the **Committee on Public Undertakings (COPU)**; the CAG is the **"guide, friend and philosopher" of the PAC**. → **Significance:** the CAG **reports**, but **Parliament acts** — the committees are where the findings gain consequence.

### 5.8 Mechanism, institutional incentives and consequences

- **Why "Comptroller in name only":** ⚠️ unlike the British CAG, whose approval is needed **before** money leaves the Consolidated Fund, India's CAG has **no control over the issue of money** and audits **after** expenditure — it is an **Auditor-General**, not a Comptroller, so its leverage is disclosure, not prevention.

| Feature | British CAG | Indian CAG |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ Control over the **issue** of money | Approval needed **before** withdrawal from the Consolidated Fund | **None** — audits **after** expenditure |
| ✅ Role | **Comptroller and** Auditor-General | **Auditor-General only** |
| ✅ Accounts and audit | Combined | **Separated** (1976 amendment to the DPC Act) |
- **Why the CAG is an agent of Parliament:** ✅ it is responsible **to Parliament**, not the executive, which is why reports route through the legislature and why no minister may answer for it — the incentive structure ties accountability to the House, not the government.
- **Consequence of ex-post, advisory audit:** ⚠️ the CAG can **expose** but not **reverse**; effectiveness therefore depends on PAC/COPU follow-up and on media/judicial attention, which is uneven.

### 5.9 Criticism, counter-argument, variation and implementation constraints

- **Overreach critique:** ⚠️ propriety and performance audits that estimate "presumptive" losses are accused of straying into **policy** and of arithmetic that assumes a counterfactual; the executive argues audit should test process, not second-guess choices. **(Cite the debate, not any specific loss figure.)**
- **Counter (accountability):** ⚠️ without propriety and performance audit, legality becomes a formality unable to catch waste, favouritism or conflict of interest — the propriety reach is what makes the "guardian of the public purse" meaningful.
- **Appointment critique:** 📰 the **sole-executive appointment** is criticised; there is a demand for a **collegium-style** process to strengthen independence.
- **Jurisdiction gaps:** 📰 debate on bringing **PPP projects, off-budget borrowings and private-fund entities (e.g. PM-CARES)** within audit — the **substantially-financed** test is the contested boundary. ⚠️ *Appleby* went the other way, wanting the office abolished as a "negative", colonial inheritance.

### 5.10 Verdict scaffolds

- **Appointment verdict:** "The CAG is insulated at removal but exposed at appointment; strengthening the guardian of the public purse means reforming how it is chosen, not how it is protected."
- **Legality-propriety verdict:** "Legality is the CAG's floor and propriety its reach; an audit confined to legality would be lawful and useless, while one that audited policy would be powerful and improper — the balance is the propriety of implementation."
- **Comptroller verdict:** "Because India's CAG audits after the money is gone, its real force is not its own pen but the parliamentary committees that turn its reports into accountability."
