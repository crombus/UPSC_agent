## S1 - Constitutional form

### Feature versus provision

- Feature: a system-level constitutional characteristic.
- Provision: an individual textual rule.
- Institution: an office or body.
- Value: a normative constitutional commitment.
- Salient features and basic-structure elements overlap but are not identical.

### Written and detailed Constitution

Original form:

- Preamble;
- 395 Articles;
- 22 Parts;
- 8 Schedules.

Main reasons for detail:

1. geographical and social diversity;
2. bulky inheritance from the Government of India Act 1935;
3. one general constitutional framework for Union and states;
4. influence of legal specialists.

Detail supplies clarity, accommodation and enforceability, but can also produce complexity, litigation and amendment pressure. A written Constitution still operates with conventions.

### Borrowed but adapted

| Source | Association | Indian transformation |
|---|---|---|
| Government of India Act 1935 | Federal and administrative machinery | Republican accountability and rights |
| United Kingdom | Parliamentary government, rule of law, single citizenship | No monarchy or unlimited parliamentary sovereignty |
| United States | Rights, judicial review and judicial independence | No presidential executive or dual citizenship |
| Ireland | Directive Principles | Combined with enforceable Fundamental Rights |
| Canada | Strong-centre federation and residuary Union power | Combined with parliamentary and asymmetric federalism |
| Australia | Concurrent List and joint sitting | Used within India's stronger Union framework |
| USSR | Fundamental Duties | No single-party state model |
| Japan | Procedure established by law | Later interpreted through fair, just and reasonable procedure |

Originality lies in selection, rejection, modification and combination.

### Rigid and flexible

| Route | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Simple majority outside Article 368 | Selected structural or technical changes |
| Special majority under Article 368 | Most amendments |
| Special majority plus ratification by at least half the states | Specified federal matters |

- No mandatory referendum.
- No joint sitting for constitutional amendments.
- Not every amendment requires state ratification.
- Basic structure prevents constitutional destruction, not all constitutional change.

## S2 - Federal and parliamentary architecture

### Federal structure

Federal features:

- Union and state governments;
- constitutional distribution of powers;
- written supremacy;
- partial rigidity;
- judicial review;
- bicameralism.

Centralising features:

- strong Union List;
- Union residuary powers;
- Article 3 reorganisation power;
- single citizenship;
- integrated judiciary;
- centrally appointed Governors;
- All-India Services;
- emergency provisions.

Article 1's "Union of States" means the Union was not formed through an agreement among sovereign states and states possess no right to secede. It does not deny federalism.

### Federal modes

| Mode | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Cooperative | Governments jointly deliberate, finance or implement |
| Competitive | States compare and compete in policy and development |
| Asymmetric | Different territorial arrangements accommodate distinct histories or communities |

### Parliamentary government

```text
Citizens -> Lok Sabha -> Council of Ministers -> Administration
```

- President or Governor: formal constitutional executive.
- Prime Minister or Chief Minister and Council: real political executive.
- Article 75(3): Union Council collectively responsible to Lok Sabha.
- Article 164(2): state Council collectively responsible to Legislative Assembly.

### Collective responsibility

It has three dimensions:

1. confidence: the ministry survives while it retains lower-House confidence;
2. policy: all ministers share responsibility for government decisions;
3. solidarity: dissent may occur internally, but ministers publicly support the decision or resign.

The whole ministry falls after loss of confidence. An individual minister's resignation does not ordinarily bring down the ministry, while the Prime Minister's resignation does.

Collective responsibility applies to the entire Council of Ministers, although the Cabinet is the smaller core decision-making body.

### Collective versus individual responsibility

| Collective | Individual |
|---|---|
| Article 75(3) | Article 75(2) |
| Entire Council answerable to Lok Sabha | Individual minister holds office during President's pleasure |
| Whole ministry falls on loss of confidence | One minister may resign or be removed |

## S3 - Limited government and judiciary

### Separation of powers

India uses functional differentiation with authorised overlap and reciprocal checks, not strict institutional isolation.

| Organ | Core function |
|---|---|
| Legislature | Law-making and executive accountability |
| Executive | Policy and administration |
| Judiciary | Adjudication and constitutional interpretation |

Authorised overlaps include:

- Ordinances under Articles 123 and 213;
- ministers participating in legislation;
- Parliament's constituent power under Article 368;
- judicial procedural rule-making.

### Constitutional markers

| Article | Principle |
|---|---|
| 50 | Separation of judiciary from executive; DPSP |
| 121 and 211 | Limits discussion of judicial conduct except removal |
| 122 and 212 | Legislative procedural autonomy |
| 13 | Laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights void to that extent |
| 32 | Supreme Court remedies for Fundamental Rights |
| 226 | High Court writ jurisdiction |

### Constitutional supremacy

The Constitution creates and limits Parliament, the executive and courts. Judicial review enforces constitutional supremacy; it does not make courts sovereign.

### Rule of law

Rule of law requires legally authorised, non-arbitrary, equal and reviewable power. It differs from rule by law, where enacted law is used merely as an instrument of governmental control.

An elected majority remains subject to:

- competence;
- procedure;
- Fundamental Rights;
- federal limits;
- judicial review.

### Limited government

Limited government means adequate governing capacity bounded by jurisdiction, lawful purpose, fair procedure, rights and remedies. It does not mean weak government.

### Judiciary

India has an integrated hierarchy:

```text
Supreme Court -> High Courts -> subordinate courts
```

Judicial independence includes tenure and service protections, difficult removal, charged expenditure, institutional autonomy and constitutional jurisdiction. Independence does not mean immunity from criticism, absence of accountability or unlimited policy-making authority.

### Key cases used

| Case | Principle |
|---|---|
| Ram Jawaya Kapur, 1955 | No rigid separation; functions remain differentiated |
| Kesavananda Bharati, 1973 | Basic structure limits amendment power |
| Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain, 1975 | Review and separation within constitutional structure |
| Maneka Gandhi, 1978 | Article 21 procedure must be fair, just and reasonable |
| Pooja Ramesh Singh v. Jammu and Kashmir Bank, 2 July 2026 | Adjudication cannot legitimately rely on fabricated AI-generated precedent |

## S4 - Democratic and normative architecture

### Parts III, IV and IV-A

| Component | Legal status | Primary purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Fundamental Rights | Justiciable, subject to constitutional restrictions | Liberty, equality and remedies |
| Directive Principles | Non-justiciable but fundamental in governance | Social and economic democracy |
| Fundamental Duties | Non-justiciable citizen obligations | Civic responsibility |

The three answer different questions:

- Rights: what may the State not unjustifiably do?
- DPSP: what social order should the State pursue?
- Duties: what civic commitments should citizens cultivate?

Minerva Mills (1980) treats harmony between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles as central to constitutional balance.

### Fundamental Rights

- Located in Part III.
- Six present categories: equality, freedom, protection against exploitation, religious freedom, cultural and educational rights, and constitutional remedies.
- They are not absolute.
- Some belong only to citizens; others protect all persons.
- Articles 20 and 21 cannot be suspended even during an Article 359 order.

### Directive Principles

- Located in Part IV.
- Article 37 makes them non-justiciable but fundamental in governance.
- They guide legislation toward welfare, social justice and social-economic democracy.
- They are not legally irrelevant merely because courts cannot directly enforce them as DPSP.

### Fundamental Duties

- Located in Part IV-A, Article 51A.
- Ten were added by the 42nd Amendment, 1976.
- The eleventh was added by the 86th Amendment, 2002.
- They apply to citizens and are non-justiciable.
- Legislation may give a duty concrete legal effect, but Article 51A is not by itself a free-standing penal code.

### Constitutional identity

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Secular | No official religion; freedom, equality and reform-oriented engagement |
| Socialist | Democratic welfare and distributive-justice orientation |
| Democratic | Political equality, representation, rights and accountable government |
| Republic | Non-hereditary constitutional head |
| Dignity | Equal moral worth of each person |
| Fraternity | Social solidarity joining liberty and equality |

The words socialist, secular and integrity were inserted into the Preamble by the 42nd Amendment, but related constitutional commitments pre-dated 1976.

### Franchise and bicameralism

- Universal adult franchise established equal political membership from the Constitution's commencement.
- Voting age was originally 21 and reduced to 18 by the 61st Amendment Act, 1988.
- Lok Sabha and state assemblies provide direct territorial representation.
- Rajya Sabha supplies continuity, revision and a territorial voice, but states are not equally represented.
- The Houses do not possess identical powers, especially concerning confidence and Money Bills.

## S5 - Integration, accountability, resilience and pluralism

### Single citizenship and administrative unity

India combines a federal distribution of government with single Indian citizenship. Integrating devices include:

- one common citizenship;
- an integrated judicial hierarchy;
- All-India Services connecting Union standards with state administration;
- common constitutional institutions for elections, audit, recruitment and fiscal distribution.

Single citizenship strengthens national membership and mobility but does not erase state, linguistic or cultural identities. It is an integrating feature within a federal Constitution, not proof of a unitary system.

### Independent constitutional bodies

| Body | Core function |
|---|---|
| Election Commission, Article 324 | Electoral superintendence, direction and control |
| CAG, Articles 148-151 | Independent public audit |
| UPSC and State PSCs, Articles 315-323 | Recruitment and service advice |
| Finance Commission, Article 280 | Recommendations on Union-State fiscal distribution |

Independence exists to protect institutional function from improper executive control. It remains compatible with reporting, transparency, reasons, legislative scrutiny and judicial review. Not every commission is constitutional, and constitutional bodies do not share identical safeguards.

### Emergency provisions

| Emergency | Article | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| National Emergency | 352 | War, external aggression or armed rebellion |
| President's Rule | 356 | Failure of state constitutional machinery |
| Financial Emergency | 360 | Threat to financial stability or credit |

Emergency powers temporarily alter normal federal, rights or fiscal operation within the Constitution. They do not create an extra-constitutional government or permanently convert India into a unitary state.

The 44th Amendment replaced "internal disturbance" with "armed rebellion" for Article 352 and protected Articles 20 and 21 from suspension through Article 359.

### Local self-government

| Amendment | Structure |
|---|---|
| 73rd Amendment, 1992 | Part IX, Panchayats and Eleventh Schedule |
| 74th Amendment, 1992 | Part IX-A, Municipalities and Twelfth Schedule |

Constitutional status secures regular structures and participation but does not guarantee effective autonomy. Real decentralisation depends on functions, funds and functionaries.

### Cooperatives

The 97th Amendment:

- connected the right to form cooperative societies with Article 19;
- inserted Article 43B;
- inserted Part IX-B.

In Union of India v. Rajendra N. Shah (2021), the Supreme Court invalidated Part IX-B insofar as it governed state cooperative societies for want of required state ratification; the multi-state cooperative framework survived.

### Pluralism and asymmetry

The Constitution combines common citizenship with differentiated arrangements:

- Fifth and Sixth Schedule protections;
- Articles 371-371J;
- linguistic-minority and cultural rights;
- official-language arrangements;
- affirmative action;
- different Union Territory structures.

Asymmetry can strengthen unity by accommodating historically and culturally distinct groups. The Constitution does not declare Hindi the national language; it establishes an official-language framework.

### Constitutionalism

- Constitution: creates institutions, distributes power and prescribes change.
- Constitutional law: includes the text, binding judgments and valid implementing law.
- Constitutionalism: makes public power effectively limited, accountable and reviewable.

Text alone does not guarantee constitutionalism. Conventions, institutional independence, access to remedies, public reason and compliance determine whether limits operate.
