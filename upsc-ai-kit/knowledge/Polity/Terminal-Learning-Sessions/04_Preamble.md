## P1 - Origin, architecture and popular sovereignty

### Nature of the Preamble

The Preamble is the Constitution's introductory declaration. It states:

1. the source of constitutional authority;
2. the nature of the Indian State;
3. the objectives to be secured and promoted;
4. the forum and date of adoption.

It is a declaratory and interpretive instrument. It is part of the Constitution, but it is not independently enforceable and does not itself confer or restrict legislative power.

### Drafting chain

| Event | Date |
|---|---|
| Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution | 13 December 1946 |
| Assembly adopted the Resolution | 22 January 1947 |
| Modified values enacted as Preamble | 26 November 1949 |

The Preamble was enacted after the rest of the Constitution so it would conform to the text already accepted. This makes it a retrospective summary of the enacted constitutional design rather than an unrelated aspirational preface.

### Four-ingredient architecture

| Ingredient | Textual anchor |
|---|---|
| Source | We, the People of India |
| Nature of State | Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic |
| Objectives | Justice, Liberty, Equality and Fraternity |
| Adoption | Constituent Assembly, 26 November 1949; adopt, enact and give to ourselves |

The 42nd Amendment, 1976 inserted Socialist, Secular and Integrity. It is the only amendment made to the Preamble.

### Popular sovereignty

"We, the People" identifies the people as the source of authority, rather than the British Crown or Parliament. It is a claim about constitutional legitimacy, not a claim that the Constitution was approved through referendum or that the Constituent Assembly was directly elected through universal franchise.

The popular-sovereignty claim is operationalised through universal adult franchise, periodic elections, republican government and continuing constitutional acceptance.
