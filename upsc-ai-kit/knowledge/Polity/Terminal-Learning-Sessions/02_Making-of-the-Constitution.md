## M1 - Demand and composition

### Constitutional demand sequence

```text
M.N. Roy proposal, 1934
        ->
Congress demand, 1935
        ->
Nehru adult-franchise formulation, 1938
        ->
August Offer accepts the principle, 1940
        ->
Cripps proposal, 1942
        ->
Cabinet Mission supplies the operative scheme, 1946
```

### Composition lock

```text
296 British India + 93 Princely States = 389 planned
229 Indian provinces + 70 Princely States = 299 after Partition
```

- Provincial representatives were indirectly elected by provincial assemblies through proportional representation by single transferable vote.
- Princely-state representatives were nominated.
- The Constituent Assembly was therefore indirectly elected and partly nominated.

### M1 remediation lock

- August Offer: accepted the principle of constitution-making by Indians.
- Cabinet Mission: created the actual Constituent Assembly scheme.
- Never confuse 389 planned membership with 299 post-Partition membership.

## M3 - Philosophy and drafting process

### Objectives Resolution

| Event | Date |
|---|---|
| Moved by Jawaharlal Nehru | 13 December 1946 |
| Adopted by Assembly | 22 January 1947 |
| Modified values enacted in Preamble | 26 November 1949 |

It affirmed:

- an independent sovereign republic;
- authority derived from the people;
- justice, equality and freedoms;
- a Union of territories;
- safeguards for minorities and backward and tribal areas;
- territorial integrity and world peace.

It became the Preamble in modified form. It did not contain the later words "socialist" and "secular", added by the 42nd Amendment in 1976.

### Drafting and enactment

| Stage | Date or period |
|---|---|
| Draft introduced by Ambedkar | 4 November 1948 |
| Second reading and clause-by-clause consideration | 15 November 1948-17 October 1949 |
| Third reading | 14-26 November 1949 |
| Adopted | 26 November 1949 |
| Signed by 284 members | 24 January 1950 |
| General commencement | 26 January 1950 |

```text
393 = short title
394 = commencement
395 = repeals
```

The original Constitution contained a Preamble, 395 Articles, 22 Parts and 8 Schedules.

### Major debate logic

| Debate | Settlement |
|---|---|
| Provincial autonomy versus unity | Federal distribution with a strong Union |
| Stability versus accountability | Parliamentary executive responsible to legislature |
| Liberty versus order and reform | Fundamental Rights with permitted restrictions |
| Transformation versus enforceability | Non-justiciable Directive Principles |
| Group safeguards versus common citizenship | Cultural and religious protection without communal electorates |
| Linguistic unity versus plurality | Official-language settlement with accommodation |
| Rights protection versus policy space | Independent judiciary and judicial review |

### Borrowing verdict

The Constitution selected, modified and integrated comparative devices:

- British parliamentary government under written constitutional supremacy;
- American rights and judicial review without presidential government or dual citizenship;
- Irish Directive Principles combined with enforceable rights;
- Canadian strong-centre federalism adapted to Partition and integration;
- Government of India Act 1935 machinery democratised through popular sovereignty.

### Criticism and balanced reply

| Criticism | Balanced evaluation |
|---|---|
| Not directly representative | True electorally; partially answered by social diversity, deliberation and the universal franchise it created |
| Not sovereign | Plausible at origin; Indian Independence Act 1947 made it fully sovereign |
| Congress-dominated | Numerically true; Congress was ideologically broad and outside experts participated |
| Lawyer-heavy | Narrow professional profile, but legal expertise improved technical quality |
| Time-consuming | Nearly three years, but the Assembly addressed Partition, federalism, rights, minorities and thousands of amendments |
| Hindu-dominated | Post-Partition imbalance was real, shaped substantially by League boycott and territorial division |
| Borrowed | Foreign and colonial continuities were significant, but devices were adapted and placed under popular sovereignty |

### Final legitimacy thesis

The Assembly had an electoral-origin deficit, but developed qualified founding legitimacy through:

- sovereignty after 1947;
- committee deliberation;
- plural participation;
- three readings and amendments;
- the Objectives Resolution;
- universal adult franchise in the constitutional order it created.

## Doubts clarified during the session

### Government of India Act 1935, Independence Act 1947 and Constitution

```text
Government of India Act 1935
= colonial administrative machinery

Indian Independence Act 1947
= transfer of authority and transitional sovereignty

Constitution of India
= supreme republican framework created by We, the People
```

The Constitution retained elements such as legislative lists, Governors, Public Service Commissions, emergency machinery and administrative relations, but transformed them through rights, universal franchise, judicial review and popular sovereignty.

### Act and Constitution

- The Constitution creates and limits Parliament.
- Parliament enacts Acts under constitutional authority.
- Courts may invalidate Acts that violate the Constitution.

### Bills and ordinances

| Article | Subject |
|---|---|
| 107 | Introduction and passage of ordinary Bills |
| 108 | Joint sitting |
| 109-110 | Money Bills |
| 111 | Presidential assent |
| 117 | Financial Bills |
| 123 | Presidential Ordinances |
| 213 | Governor's Ordinances |
| 368 | Constitutional amendments |

```text
Bill + passage by required Houses + presidential assent = Act
```

An Ordinance is temporary legislation promulgated when both Houses are not simultaneously in session and immediate action is necessary. It ceases six weeks after Parliament reassembles unless replaced by an Act, and may be withdrawn formally by the President on ministerial advice.
