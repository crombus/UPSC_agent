# Indian Polity: Constitutional Architecture and Distinctive Features - Last-Minute Revision Chart

> **Purpose:** One-place revision immediately before Prelims or GS-II.
> **Coverage:** Constitutional evolution, rights, federalism, executive, legislatures, judiciary, local government, elections, constitutional/statutory bodies, political process, tribunals, ministries and the regulatory state.
> **Two-pass design:** Core is independently exam-complete. Advanced adds case law, comparative design, institutional critique and reform depth; skipping it must not affect the paper outcome.
> **Status rule:** Always distinguish constitutional text, statute, rule, convention, judicial interpretation, executive resolution, current practice, proposal and Bill. They are not interchangeable.

---

## 1. The 60-second constitutional map

```text
PEOPLE
  ->
CONSTITUTION: Preamble + Rights + DPSP + Duties
  ->
PARLIAMENTARY FEDERAL STATE
  |
  +-> UNION EXECUTIVE: President -> PM and Council -> ministries/administration
  +-> LEGISLATURE: Parliament -> law, finance, scrutiny and representation
  +-> JUDICIARY: Supreme Court/High Courts -> review, rights and disputes
  +-> STATES: Governor -> CM/Council -> legislature -> administration
  +-> LOCAL GOVERNMENT: Panchayats and Municipalities
  +-> INDEPENDENT AUTHORITIES: ECI, CAG, UPSC, Finance Commission...
  +-> STATUTORY/REGULATORY BODIES: rights, accountability and sector regulation
  ->
FEDERAL + DEMOCRATIC + ACCOUNTABLE GOVERNANCE
```

> **Memory line:** Identify the legal source first; then appointment, tenure, removal, powers, accountability and appeal. Most Polity errors arise from mixing these layers.

## 2. Universal institution-comparison template

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Legal source | Constitution, parliamentary statute, executive resolution, rule or judgment? |
| Composition | Single member, multi-member commission, board, court or tribunal? |
| Appointment | Who formally appoints, and who recommends/selects? |
| Tenure | Fixed term, age limit, pleasure or service rules? |
| Removal | Simple executive action, specified statutory grounds or legislative/judicial process? |
| Function | Advisory, executive, regulatory, audit, investigative, adjudicatory or recommendatory? |

## 3. Constitutional evolution ladder

| Measure | Signature change | Fastest trap correction |
|---|---|---|
| Regulating Act 1773 | Governor-General of Bengal; parliamentary control begins; Supreme Court framework | Not Governor-General of India |
| Pitt's India Act 1784 | Board of Control and dual control | Company trade and British political control coexist |
| Charter Act 1813 | Company monopoly ends except tea/China trade; education grant | Missionary entry and Rs 1 lakh provision |
| Charter Act 1833 | Governor-General of India; Company commercial role ends; Law Member | Bentinck becomes first GG of India |
| Charter Act 1853 | Legislative-executive separation in council; civil-service competition principle | Last Charter Act |
| Government of India Act 1858 | Crown rule, Secretary of State and Viceroy | Company rule ends |
| Indian Councils Act 1861 | Legislative councils, portfolio system and decentralising turn | Nominated Indians, not democratic government |
| Indian Councils Act 1892 | Budget discussion and indirect-election principle | Election word avoided formally |
| Indian Councils Act 1909 | Separate electorates for Muslims | Morley-Minto |
| Government of India Act 1919 | Provincial dyarchy and expanded legislatures | Montagu-Chelmsford |
| Government of India Act 1935 | Provincial autonomy, proposed federation and central dyarchy | Federation never operated |
| Indian Independence Act 1947 | Two dominions and lapse of paramountcy | Constituent Assembly becomes sovereign |

## 5. Salient features and constitutional sources

| Feature | Indian design |
|---|---|
| Written and detailed Constitution | Combines institutional structure, rights and administration |
| Parliamentary government | Real executive responsible to elected lower house |
| Federal system with unitary tilt | Dual polity with strong Union and emergency centralisation |
| Constitutional supremacy | All organs derive limited authority from Constitution |
| Judicial review | Courts test state action against Constitution |
| Fundamental Rights | Justiciable limitations and guarantees |
| DPSP | Non-justiciable governance directives |
| Fundamental Duties | Citizen duties, non-justiciable |
| Single citizenship | Union citizenship despite federal polity |
| Universal adult franchise | Political equality from commencement |
| Independent bodies | ECI, CAG, UPSC and others constitutionally insulated |
| Secular-social-transformative design | Liberty, equality, reform and social justice pursued together |

| Source | Borrowed/adapted element |
|---|---|
| Britain | Parliamentary system, rule of law, cabinet, legislative procedure and single citizenship |
| United States | Fundamental Rights, judicial review, impeachment and Vice-President office |
| Ireland | DPSP and presidential nomination ideas |
| Canada | Federation with strong Centre and residuary Union power |
| Australia | Concurrent List, trade provisions and joint sitting |
| Germany/Weimar | Emergency suspension influence |
| USSR/Russia | Fundamental Duties and social-economic justice inspiration |
| South Africa | Amendment procedure and Rajya Sabha election method |
| Government of India Act 1935 | Administrative/federal framework, governor and public-service architecture |

> **Rule:** Borrowed provision does not mean copied constitutional scheme. India adapted foreign mechanisms to its own political and social design.

## 6. Preamble decoding

| Ingredient | Content |
|---|---|
| Source of authority | We, the People of India |
| Nature of State | Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic |
| Objectives | Justice, Liberty, Equality and Fraternity |
| Date | 26 November 1949 |

| Judicial position | Core rule |
|---|---|
| Berubari reference | Earlier view: Preamble not part of Constitution |
| Kesavananda Bharati | Preamble is part; basic-structure relevance |
| Amendability | Can be amended under Article 368, subject to basic structure |

> **Trap:** The Preamble is not an independent source of legislative power or enforceable right, but it guides constitutional interpretation.

## 7. Territory and citizenship

### Union and territory

| Rule | Recall |
|---|---|
| Article 1 | India, that is Bharat, is a Union of States |
| Article 2 | Admission or establishment of new states |
| Article 3 | Formation, alteration of area/boundary/name of states |
| Article 4 | Laws under Articles 2-3 are not Article 368 amendments |
| State-view requirement | President refers relevant Bill to state legislature for views |
| Binding effect | State legislature's view is not binding on Parliament |

### Citizenship

| Acquisition under Citizenship Act | Loss |
|---|---|
| Birth | Renunciation |
| Descent | Termination |
| Registration | Deprivation |
| Naturalisation | — |
| Incorporation of territory | — |

> **Trap:** The Constitution dealt with citizenship at commencement in Articles 5-11; Parliament regulates later acquisition and loss by law. OCI is not dual citizenship.

## 8. Fundamental Rights master table

| Right | Articles | High-yield identity |
|---|---|---|
| Equality | 14-18 | Equality before law, non-discrimination, public employment, abolition of untouchability/titles |
| Freedom | 19-22 | Six freedoms, criminal safeguards, life/liberty and arrest/detention protections |
| Against exploitation | 23-24 | Trafficking, forced labour and child labour restrictions |
| Freedom of religion | 25-28 | Conscience, profession/practice, denomination and religious instruction rules |
| Cultural and educational rights | 29-30 | Culture and minority educational institutions |
| Constitutional remedies | 32 | Supreme Court enforcement of FR |

### Person versus citizen

| Mainly available to citizens | Available to persons generally |
|---|---|
| Articles 15, 16, 19 and 29 protections as text specifies | Articles 14, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24 and 25 protections as text specifies |

### Writs

| Writ | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Habeas corpus | Produce detained person; test legality of detention |
| Mandamus | Command public authority to perform public duty |
| Prohibition | Superior court stops lower court/tribunal before excess action is completed |
| Certiorari | Quashes completed/order-stage jurisdictional or legal error |
| Quo warranto | Tests legal authority to hold public office |

> **Article 32 versus 226:** Supreme Court issues writs for Fundamental Rights; High Courts issue them for Fundamental Rights **and other purposes**, making Article 226 jurisdiction wider in subject scope.

## 9. Rights restrictions and emergency effects

| Rule | Correct distinction |
|---|---|
| Article 19 | Available only to citizens; freedoms subject to specified reasonable restrictions |
| Article 20 | Protection in criminal conviction: ex post facto, double jeopardy and self-incrimination |
| Article 21 | Life and personal liberty except procedure established by law, judicially expanded |
| Article 21A | Free and compulsory education for specified child age group |
| Article 22 | Arrest/detention safeguards plus preventive-detention framework |
| Article 33 | Parliament may modify FR application to armed forces and specified services |
| Article 358 | Article 19 effect only during emergency on war/external aggression, subject to 44th Amendment safeguards |
| Article 359 | President may suspend right to move court for specified FR, but Articles 20 and 21 cannot be suspended |

## 10. DPSP and Fundamental Duties

### DPSP

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Socialist | Welfare, livelihood, equal pay, legal aid and humane work |
| Gandhian | Village panchayats, cottage industries, weaker sections and animal husbandry |
| Liberal-intellectual | Uniform civil code, early childhood care, environment, monuments, separation of judiciary and international peace |

| Relationship with FR | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Early phase | FR priority in conflict |
| Golaknath-era tension | Amendment power over FR restricted |
| Kesavananda | Limited amendment power; harmony matters |
| Minerva Mills | Balance between FR and DPSP is basic structure |

### Fundamental Duties

- Article 51A contains citizen duties; originally ten, with an additional education-related duty later.
- Apply to citizens, are non-justiciable and may support legislation/judicial interpretation.

> **Trap:** DPSP and Duties are non-justiciable, not legally irrelevant. Courts cannot directly enforce them like FR, but they influence law and interpretation.

## 11. Amendment and basic structure

| Amendment route | Examples |
|---|---|
| Simple majority outside Article 368 | State reorganisation, citizenship and certain legislative matters |
| Special majority of Parliament | Most constitutional provisions |
| Special majority + ratification by at least half of states | Federal provisions such as presidential election, Union-state power distribution and judiciary representation |

### Case sequence

`Shankari Prasad -> Sajjan Singh -> Golaknath -> Kesavananda Bharati -> Indira Gandhi case -> Minerva Mills -> later applications`

| Basic-structure elements commonly recognised | |
|---|---|
| Supremacy of Constitution | Republican-democratic government |
| Secular character | Federal character |
| Separation of powers | Judicial review |
| Rule of law | Free and fair elections |
| Dignity/liberty | Balance between FR and DPSP |
| Limited amending power | Independence of judiciary |

> **Trap:** Parliament's amendment power is wide but not unlimited. Courts do not compare an amendment with every ordinary constitutional provision; they test damage to basic structure.

## 12. Parliamentary versus presidential systems

| Dimension | Parliamentary | Presidential |
|---|---|---|
| Executive origin | Drawn from legislature | Separately elected |
| Responsibility | Collective responsibility to lower house | Fixed tenure; not responsible in same manner |
| Executive duality | Nominal and real executive | Usually single real executive |
| Tenure | Depends on legislative confidence | Fixed |
| Separation | Fusion of executive-legislature | Stronger separation |
| Stability/accountability | More daily accountability, potentially less stability | More tenure stability, potential deadlock |

> **India:** President is constitutional head; PM-led Council is real executive and collectively responsible to Lok Sabha.

## 13. Federal architecture

| Federal feature | Unitary/centralising feature |
|---|---|
| Dual polity | Strong Union and indestructible Union |
| Written Constitution | Single Constitution for Union/states, with limited exceptions historically |
| Division of powers | Residuary power with Parliament |
| Constitutional supremacy | Single citizenship |
| Independent judiciary | Integrated judiciary |
| Bicameral Parliament | Unequal state representation in Rajya Sabha |
| Rigidity in federal provisions | Emergency centralisation and governors |

### Legislative lists

| List | General rule |
|---|---|
| Union List | Parliament legislates |
| State List | State legislatures normally legislate |
| Concurrent List | Both legislate; Union law generally prevails in conflict subject to constitutional rules |
| Residuary | Parliament |

### Parliament legislating on State List

| Route | Article / circumstance |
|---|---|
| Rajya Sabha national-interest resolution | Article 249 |
| National Emergency | Article 250 |
| Two or more states request | Article 252 |
| International obligation | Article 253 |
| President's Rule | Article 356 framework |

## 14. Centre-state and inter-state relations

| Dimension | Instruments |
|---|---|
| Legislative | Lists, repugnancy, parliamentary State-List routes |
| Administrative | Union directions, All India Services and inter-governmental coordination |
| Financial | Tax assignment, grants, Finance Commission and GST |
| Inter-state | Inter-State Council, zonal councils, river-water dispute mechanisms and full faith/credit |

| Body/mechanism | Identity |
|---|---|
| Inter-State Council | Constitutional possibility under Article 263; coordination/advice |
| Zonal Councils | Statutory bodies under States Reorganisation Act framework |
| GST Council | Constitutional federal fiscal forum under Article 279A |
| Finance Commission | Constitutional devolution body under Article 280 |

> **Mains verdict:** Indian federalism is negotiated through constitutional rules, political practice, fiscal dependence and inter-governmental institutions—not list division alone.

## 15. Emergency comparison

| Emergency | Article | Ground | Parliamentary approval / effect identity |
|---|---|---|---|
| National Emergency | 352 | War, external aggression or armed rebellion | Alters federal/rights environment; special-majority safeguards |
| President's Rule | 356 | Failure of constitutional machinery in state | Union assumes state executive/legislative functions subject to approval and judicial review |
| Financial Emergency | 360 | Threat to financial stability/credit | Financial directions and salary implications; never used so far |

> **Key safeguards:** Written Cabinet advice for Article 352; periodic parliamentary approval; Lok Sabha revocation route; Articles 20 and 21 protected from Article 359 suspension.

> **Trap:** President's Rule is not a national emergency and does not automatically suspend Fundamental Rights.

## 16. President and Vice-President

| Dimension | President | Vice-President |
|---|---|---|
| Electoral college | Elected MPs + elected MLAs of states and eligible UT legislatures | Members of both Houses, elected and nominated |
| Voting | Proportional representation, single transferable vote, secret ballot | Same broad voting method |
| Role | Constitutional head of Union | Ex officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha |
| Removal | Impeachment for violation of Constitution | Rajya Sabha resolution agreed by Lok Sabha |
| Vacancy role | — | Acts as President |

### Presidential powers

`Executive + legislative + financial + judicial + diplomatic + military + emergency`

| Device | Rule |
|---|---|
| Assent | May assent, withhold or return ordinary Bill once; cannot return Money Bill |
| Pocket veto | No constitutional time limit for assent decision |
| Ordinance | Article 123 when both Houses are not in session together as required; temporary law subject to Parliament |
| Pardon | Article 72, including Union-law, court-martial and death-sentence contexts |

> **Trap:** The President ordinarily acts on ministerial advice; discretion is narrow and constitutionally structured.

## 17. Prime Minister and Council of Ministers

| Concept | Core rule |
|---|---|
| PM appointment | President appoints person commanding Lok Sabha confidence |
| Other ministers | Appointed by President on PM advice |
| Collective responsibility | Council collectively responsible to Lok Sabha |
| Individual responsibility | Ministers hold office during President's pleasure in constitutional practice through PM |
| Size cap | Total Union ministers cannot exceed 15% of Lok Sabha strength |
| Non-member minister | Must become member of either House within six months |
| Cabinet | Inner decision-making core |
| Council of Ministers | Wider constitutional body |

### Minister ranks

- Cabinet Minister
- Minister of State, with or without independent charge
- Deputy Minister category in conventional classification, though current practice may vary

> **Trap:** Cabinet and Council of Ministers are not synonymous in size or function.

## 18. Ministries, departments and Central Secretariat

```text
President
  -> Allocation of Business Rules: distributes business among ministries/departments
  -> Transaction of Business Rules: governs disposal, consultation and Cabinet routing
  -> PM / Cabinet / Cabinet Committees
  -> Minister
  -> Department and Secretary
  -> Attached/subordinate offices, agencies and field implementation
```

| Institution | Primary role |
|---|---|
| Ministry | Political-administrative portfolio grouping |
| Department | Major policy/business division within ministry |
| Central Secretariat | Policy formulation, coordination, parliamentary work and control |
| Executive agency/field office | Implementation, regulation or service delivery |
| Cabinet Secretariat | Cabinet support and inter-ministerial coordination |
| PMO | Direct staff support and policy/coordination assistance to PM |

> **Trap:** Cabinet Secretariat and PMO are distinct. A ministry formulates/oversees policy; an attached or field agency may execute it.

## 19. Parliament: structure and sessions

| Component | Identity |
|---|---|
| President | Part of Parliament, though not a member of either House |
| Lok Sabha | Directly elected lower House; controls executive confidence and Money Bills |
| Rajya Sabha | Permanent upper House; represents states and has special federal powers |

| Device | Core distinction |
|---|---|
| Adjournment | Suspends sitting for time |
| Adjournment sine die | Ends sitting without next date |
| Prorogation | President ends session |
| Dissolution | Ends Lok Sabha itself |
| Quorum | One-tenth of total membership |
| Joint sitting | Deadlock mechanism for ordinary/most financial Bills; not Money or Constitution Amendment Bills |

## 20. Bill comparison

| Bill | Introduction | Rajya Sabha power | Joint sitting | President |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary Bill | Either House | Equal legislative power | Possible | May return once |
| Money Bill | Lok Sabha only, presidential recommendation | Recommendations within 14 days | No | Cannot return for reconsideration |
| Financial Bill category I | Lok Sabha only with recommendation | Can amend/reject | Possible | Ordinary assent framework |
| Financial Bill category II | Either House; recommendation needed before consideration for expenditure provision | Equal | Possible | Ordinary assent framework |
| Constitution Amendment Bill | Either House; no prior presidential recommendation | Equal; each must separately pass | No | Must give assent after due passage |

> **Money Bill:** Article 110 content test; Speaker certifies. Rajya Sabha cannot amend or reject, only recommend.

## 22. Privileges, motions and anti-defection

| Device | Identity |
|---|---|
| Question Hour | Ministerial accountability through questions |
| Zero Hour | Indian parliamentary innovation, not formally constitutional |
| No-confidence motion | Tests Lok Sabha confidence in Council |
| Censure motion | Criticises policy/action; reasons specified |
| Adjournment motion | Interrupts business for urgent definite public matter |
| Privilege | Rights/immunities necessary for legislative functioning |

### Anti-defection

| Ground | Rule |
|---|---|
| Voluntary giving up membership | Wider than formal resignation |
| Voting/abstaining against direction | Subject to permission/condonation rules |
| Independent member joins party | Disqualification |
| Nominated member joins after permitted initial period | Disqualification |
| Merger exception | Requires two-thirds of legislature-party members |
| Decision | Presiding officer, subject to judicial review |

> **Trap:** The one-third split exception was removed. Anti-defection improves stability but can weaken deliberation and concentrate party leadership power.

## 23. Supreme Court and High Courts

| Dimension | Supreme Court | High Court |
|---|---|---|
| Constitutional position | Apex court | Highest state-level constitutional court |
| Original jurisdiction | Centre-state/inter-state constitutional disputes | Limited original areas vary |
| Writ jurisdiction | Article 32 for FR | Article 226 for FR and other purposes |
| Appellate jurisdiction | Constitutional, civil, criminal and special leave | Appeals from subordinate courts/tribunals |
| Advisory | President may refer under Article 143 | No equivalent general advisory jurisdiction |
| Court of record | Yes | Yes |
| Superintendence | — | Over subordinate courts/tribunals within territory, subject to Constitution |

### Judicial review and independence

- Security of tenure and difficult removal.
- Salaries/service safeguards.
- Contempt and court-of-record powers.
- Separation from executive.
- Judicial review and constitutional remedies.

> **Removal:** Judges are formally removed by President after each House passes the required special-majority address on proved misbehaviour or incapacity.

> **Trap:** Judicial review, judicial activism, public-interest litigation and judicial overreach are related but not synonymous.

## 25. Governor, Chief Minister and State Council

| Office | Core rule |
|---|---|
| Governor | Constitutional head appointed by President; normally acts on state ministerial advice |
| CM | Head of real state executive; must command Assembly confidence |
| State Council | Collectively responsible to Legislative Assembly |
| Governor's ordinance | Article 213 |
| Bill options | Assent, withholding, return where permitted, or reservation for President |

### Discretion-sensitive zones

- Choosing CM when no clear majority.
- Requiring floor test in constitutional circumstances.
- Reserving Bills for President.
- Reporting constitutional breakdown.
- Sixth Schedule and special responsibilities where Constitution provides.

> **Trap:** Governor is neither an elected state President nor a free-standing Union administrator. Discretion is exceptional and judicially reviewable.

## 26. State Legislature

| Dimension | Legislative Assembly | Legislative Council |
|---|---|---|
| Status | Lower House, directly elected | Upper House, permanent |
| Executive responsibility | State Council responsible to Assembly | Cannot remove ministry |

## 33. Accountability and rights bodies

| Body | Legal source | Main function | Decision character |
|---|---|---|---|
| NHRC/SHRC | Protection of Human Rights Act | Inquiry, review and recommendations | Primarily recommendatory |
| CIC/SIC | Right to Information Act | RTI appeals/complaints | Statutory adjudicatory orders within Act |

## 34. Statutory, regulatory and quasi-judicial bodies

### Three labels, three questions

| Label | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Statutory | Source: created by legislation |
| Regulatory | Function: sets/enforces sector rules |
| Quasi-judicial | Procedure/power: adjudicates disputes or determines rights with hearing and reasoned order |

> A body can be statutory and regulatory and quasi-judicial simultaneously.

### Functional powers

| Power | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Quasi-legislative | Makes regulations, standards or codes under delegated law |
| Executive/administrative | Licences, inspects, investigates and enforces |
| Quasi-judicial | Hears parties and decides legal/factual disputes |
| Advisory | Recommends without binding determination |

### High-yield body families

| Family | Examples |
|---|---|
| Economic/market regulators | RBI, SEBI, CCI, IRDAI and PFRDA |
| Infrastructure regulators | TRAI, electricity regulators and airport-sector bodies |
| Rights/accountability bodies | NHRC, information commissions and Lokpal |
| Environmental adjudication | National Green Tribunal |
| Service/administrative adjudication | Central Administrative Tribunal |
| Consumer adjudication | Consumer commissions |

> **Regulatory capture:** Regulator begins serving regulated interests because of information dependence, revolving doors, lobbying, funding or weak accountability.

## 35. Administrative tribunals

| Dimension | Core rule |
|---|---|
| Constitutional basis | Articles 323A and 323B |
| Article 323A | Administrative-service matters; Parliament |
| Article 323B | Specified other matters; appropriate legislature |
| CAT | Central service matters under Administrative Tribunals Act |
| Purpose | Expertise, speed and reduced court burden |
| Judicial review | Tribunal decisions remain subject to constitutional High Court/Supreme Court review framework |

> **Trap:** Tribunals supplement specialised adjudication; they cannot wholly exclude the constitutional judicial-review role of High Courts and Supreme Court.

## 39. Comparative constitutional design

| Dimension | India | Comparison clue |
|---|---|---|
| Government | Parliamentary republic | US presidential; France semi-presidential |
| Constitution | Supreme written Constitution | UK parliamentary sovereignty with uncodified constitutional order |
| Federalism | Strong-centre federation | US dual federal features; Canada strong-centre influence |
| Upper House | Unequal state representation, indirectly elected | US equal state representation and direct election |
| Rights | Justiciable FR plus DPSP | South Africa integrates socio-economic-rights design differently |
| Judicial review | Supreme/High Courts and basic structure | Models vary from strong-form review to parliamentary systems |
| Amendment | Mixed rigidity with basic-structure limit | US highly rigid; UK flexible |
| Secularism | Principled engagement/equal citizenship | US non-establishment model; French laicite |
| Emergency | Detailed constitutional provisions and safeguards | Comparative systems distribute powers differently |

> **Comparison rule:** Compare the same design variable, not whole countries through slogans.

## 40. Optional Advanced enrichment

| Topic | Core identification | Optional Advanced correction |
|---|---|---|
| Constitutional history | Act-to-feature sequence | Colonial institutions combined centralisation, limited representation and administrative continuity. |
| Constituent Assembly | Composition and process | Legitimacy came from deliberation, social vision and later democratic ratification through practice, despite indirect election. |
| Preamble | Values and interpretive role | Use as structural guide, not free-standing power or right. |
| Fundamental Rights | Justiciable guarantees | Rights adjudication balances text, proportionality, procedure and institutional competence. |
| DPSP | Welfare directives | Transformative constitutionalism depends on harmony, not simple hierarchy between Parts III and IV. |
| Basic structure | Limit on amendment | Judicial protection prevents constitutional self-destruction but raises democratic-legitimacy debates. |
| Parliament | Law, finance and accountability | Executive dominance, party discipline and limited sitting time affect deliberation. |
| Federalism | Division plus negotiation | Political party alignment, fiscal design and administrative capacity shape actual federal balance. |
| Governor | Constitutional head | Reform must reconcile Union appointment with impartial state constitutional functioning. |
| Judiciary | Review and rights | Independence must coexist with appointment transparency, accountability and access to justice. |
| Local government | Constitutional devolution | State control over money, staff and functions creates the central implementation gap. |
| Election Commission | Electoral administration | Independence is a package of appointment, tenure, staff, finance, transparency and review. |
| CAG | Legislative audit | Audit effectiveness depends on timely reports, committee examination and corrective action. |
| Rights bodies | Inquiry/recommendation | Weak enforcement may coexist with agenda-setting, documentation and institutional pressure. |
| Regulators | Expertise and credible commitment | Independence without accountability risks capture; ministry control risks politicisation. |
| Tribunals | Specialisation and speed | Executive influence, vacancies, tenure and appellate design determine genuine independence. |
| Anti-defection | Stability | Restrict whip to confidence/financial survival or redesign adjudication to restore deliberation. |
| Political parties | Representation | Internal democracy and finance are constitutional-democratic concerns despite parties' associational character. |
| Comparative design | Foreign models | Institutions cannot be transplanted without party system, federal, judicial and political context. |

## 41. Evidence and legal-status rules

| Source | What it proves | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| Constitutional text | Formal allocation, right or procedure | Complete present practice |
| Statute | Legal mandate and powers | Effective implementation |
| Rule/regulation | Delegated procedure | Constitutional validity by itself |
| Judgment | Court's holding and interpretation | That every observation is binding ratio |
| Convention | Repeated political practice | Legally enforceable rule in every case |
| Commission report | Recommendation and analysis | Adopted law |
| Bill | Proposed law | Current legal position |
| Ordinance | Temporary law with constitutional force | Permanent enactment without legislative process |
| Executive resolution | Administrative creation/mandate | Constitutional or statutory status |
| Annual report | Institution's recorded activity | Independent proof of impact |
| Parliamentary answer | Official statement on date | Permanent or judicially settled law |

> **Answer discipline:** Write `the Constitution provides`, `the Act authorises`, `the Court held`, `convention expects`, `the commission recommended` and `the Bill proposes` separately.
