# Legal and Latin Expressions - UPSC Glossary

> **Subject:** Indian Polity and Governance | **GS Paper:** GS-II  
> **Use:** Rapid decoding of judgments, constitutional questions, editorials and legal passages.

---

## 1. Good faith, authority and validity

| Expression | Plain meaning | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **Mala fide** | In bad faith; with a dishonest or improper purpose | A power used to punish political opponents rather than achieve its lawful purpose |
| **Mala fides** | Bad faith itself; the noun form of *mala fide* | "The petitioner alleged mala fides." |
| **Bona fide** | In good faith; genuine and honest | A bona fide administrative decision |
| **De jure** | Existing according to law | A de jure government has lawful authority |
| **De facto** | Existing in fact or practice | A de facto authority exercises actual control |
| **Ultra vires** | Beyond the power granted by law | A rule exceeding the parent Act is ultra vires |
| **Intra vires** | Within the power granted by law | An order issued within statutory authority |
| **Colourable exercise of power** | Doing indirectly what cannot legally be done directly | Legislation disguised as belonging to a permitted field |
| **Fraud on the Constitution** | Using constitutional form to defeat constitutional purpose | Formally valid power exercised to achieve an unconstitutional object |
| **Non est** | Legally nonexistent or having no legal effect | A void order may be treated as non est |
| **Void ab initio** | Invalid from the very beginning | An act made without jurisdiction |
| **Ipso facto** | By the fact itself; automatically | Disqualification does not arise ipso facto unless the law says so |

## 2. Evidence, intention and criminal law

| Expression | Plain meaning | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **Prima facie** | Sufficient or apparently true at first sight | A prima facie case permits proceedings to continue |
| **Mens rea** | Guilty mind or criminal intention | Intention or knowledge required for many offences |
| **Actus reus** | The prohibited act or omission | Physical element of an offence |
| **Actus non facit reum nisi mens sit rea** | An act ordinarily does not make a person guilty unless the mind is also guilty | General relationship between criminal act and intention |
| **Corpus delicti** | Essential facts proving that an offence occurred | Proof that the alleged crime actually happened |
| **Alibi** | Claim that the accused was elsewhere when the offence occurred | Defence based on impossibility of presence |
| **Modus operandi** | A person's characteristic method of operating | Repeated method used to commit offences |
| **Burden of proof** | Responsibility to establish a disputed fact | Normally rests on the prosecution in a criminal trial |
| **Beyond reasonable doubt** | High standard of proof used for criminal conviction | Guilt must be established beyond reasonable doubt |
| **Preponderance of probabilities** | A fact is more likely than not | Usual civil-law standard of proof |

## 3. Court proceedings and judgments

| Expression | Plain meaning | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **Suo motu** | Action initiated by a court or authority on its own | A court takes suo motu cognisance of an issue |
| **Locus standi** | Legal standing or capacity to approach a court | PIL relaxed the traditional rule of locus standi |
| **Sub judice** | Under consideration by a court | A matter awaiting judicial determination |
| **Res judicata** | A finally decided dispute ordinarily cannot be litigated again | Prevents repetitive litigation between the same parties |
| **Ratio decidendi** | Legal principle necessary for deciding a case | Binding part of a precedent |
| **Obiter dicta** | Incidental judicial observations not essential to the decision | Persuasive but generally not binding |
| **Stare decisis** | Follow previously decided binding precedents | Promotes stability and consistency in law |
| **Per incuriam** | A decision made without considering binding law or precedent | May weaken the decision's precedential value |
| **Sub silentio** | A legal point passed over without conscious determination | The point may not operate as a binding precedent |
| **Ex parte** | Proceeding or order made in the absence of the other side | Temporary ex parte injunction |
| **In camera** | Proceeding conducted privately rather than in open court | Used to protect privacy or sensitive information |
| **Amicus curiae** | Friend of the court appointed to assist it | Provides neutral assistance on difficult questions |
| **Caveat** | Formal notice requesting a hearing before an adverse order is passed | Prevents an ex parte order without hearing the caveator |
| **Affidavit** | Written statement confirmed by oath or affirmation | Evidence submitted in writ proceedings |
| **Interim order** | Temporary order operating until further hearing or final decision | Stay on implementation of a disputed measure |
| **Injunction** | Judicial order requiring or preventing an act | Prevents continuing unlawful conduct |

## 4. Natural justice

| Expression | Plain meaning | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **Audi alteram partem** | Hear the other side | No adverse decision without a fair opportunity to respond |
| **Nemo judex in causa sua** | No one should judge their own cause | Rule against bias |
| **Speaking order** | A reasoned order explaining the basis of the decision | Enables accountability and judicial review |
| **Bias** | Predisposition that compromises impartiality | Personal, pecuniary, subject-matter or institutional bias |
| **Post-decisional hearing** | Hearing supplied after an urgent decision | Exceptional substitute where a prior hearing was impracticable |

## 5. Constitutional writs

| Writ | Literal idea | Constitutional function |
|---|---|---|
| **Habeas corpus** | "Produce the body" | Requires production of a detained person and tests the legality of detention |
| **Mandamus** | "We command" | Directs a public authority to perform a legal duty |
| **Certiorari** | "To be certified/informed" | Quashes an unlawful order of a lower court, tribunal or qualifying authority |
| **Prohibition** | "To forbid" | Prevents a lower court or tribunal from continuing beyond its jurisdiction |
| **Quo warranto** | "By what authority?" | Tests a person's legal authority to hold a substantive public office |

> **Close-option trap:** Prohibition is mainly preventive, while certiorari is mainly corrective.

## 6. Interpretation and legal reasoning

| Expression | Plain meaning | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **Literal rule** | Give statutory words their ordinary meaning | Starting point of interpretation |
| **Golden rule** | Modify the literal meaning to avoid absurdity | Limited departure from literal wording |
| **Mischief rule** | Interpret the law to remedy the defect it targeted | Focuses on the problem Parliament intended to cure |
| **Purposive interpretation** | Interpret a provision according to its objective | Common in constitutional and rights adjudication |
| **Ejusdem generis** | General words following specific words are limited to the same class | "Cars, buses, trucks and other vehicles" |
| **Noscitur a sociis** | A word is understood from surrounding words | Context determines meaning |
| **Expressio unius est exclusio alterius** | Express mention of one thing may imply exclusion of another | Used cautiously when a list appears exhaustive |
| **Generalia specialibus non derogant** | General law does not override a special law | Special provision normally prevails over a conflicting general provision |
| **Harmonious construction** | Interpret provisions so both can operate | Avoids unnecessary conflict between constitutional provisions |
| **Reading down** | Give a narrow interpretation to preserve validity | Saves a provision from being struck down |
| **Severability** | Remove the invalid portion while retaining the valid remainder | Applied when valid and invalid parts can operate separately |
| **Pith and substance** | Identify the true nature of legislation | Resolves overlap between legislative fields |
| **Prospective overruling** | A new legal rule operates mainly for the future | Protects settled past transactions |

## 7. Frequently used general expressions

| Expression | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| **Quid pro quo** | Something given in exchange for something else |
| **Status quo** | Existing state of affairs |
| **Status quo ante** | State of affairs that existed before the disputed action |
| **Inter alia** | Among other things |
| **Mutatis mutandis** | With necessary changes being made |
| **Per se** | By itself |
| **Ad hoc** | Created for a particular purpose |
| **Pro bono** | Professional work undertaken for public good without ordinary payment |
| **Sine die** | Without fixing a future date |
| **In toto** | Completely or as a whole |
| **Vis-a-vis** | In relation to or compared with |
| **A priori** | Based on reasoning before examining particular facts |
| **A posteriori** | Based on observation or experience |

## 8. Emergency-provision application

If an Article 352 proclamation is challenged as **mala fide**, the allegation is that the
constitutional power was used for an improper purpose rather than because of a genuine threat
from war, external aggression or armed rebellion.

The court may examine the legality of the proclamation and its supporting material without
inquiring into the confidential ministerial advice barred by Article 74(2). Article 361 protects
the President personally from being answerable to a court for official acts, but it does not make
the resulting Union action immune from judicial review.

> **UPSC distinction:** Judicial review primarily tests and corrects the validity of state action.
> Criminal punishment requires a separate offence, evidence, procedure and trial.
