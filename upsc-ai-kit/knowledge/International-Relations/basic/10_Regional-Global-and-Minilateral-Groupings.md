**Core proposition:** ⚠️ India participates in a deliberately overlapping set of
regional, trans-regional, continental, global-governance and minilateral
groupings, each serving a distinct purpose, rather than anchoring its foreign
policy in membership of any single bloc.

## 2. Essential definitions

| Concept | Exam-ready meaning |
|---|---|
| ⚠️ **SAARC** | The South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation, India's oldest South Asia-specific regional grouping, whose momentum has been constrained by India-Pakistan tensions in recent years. |
| ✅ **BIMSTEC** | ✅ Originally the Bangladesh-India-Myanmar-Sri Lanka-Thailand Economic Cooperation (BIMST-EC), "since renamed" the Bay of Bengal Initiative for Multi-Sectoral Technical and Economic Cooperation (BIMSTEC) — a Bay-of-Bengal-focused grouping that has gained salience as SAARC's momentum has slowed. Its Charter was adopted at Colombo on 30 March 2022 and entered into force 20 May 2024. 📰 The 6th Summit (Bangkok, 4 April 2025) adopted the Summit Declaration, **BIMSTEC Bangkok Vision 2030**, Rules of Procedure for BIMSTEC Mechanisms and the Eminent Persons Group report; Bangladesh took the chair. |
| ✅ **IORA / IOR-ARC** | ✅ The Indian Ocean Rim Association (earlier IOR-ARC), a trans-regional forum for Indian Ocean littoral-state economic and security cooperation, explicitly cited by Tharoor as a platform India engages alongside larger bodies under its multi-alignment approach. 📰 23 members and 12 dialogue partners; Secretariat at Ebène, Mauritius; India chairs for 2025-27. |
| ✅ **IBSA** | ✅ The India-Brazil-South Africa Initiative, a "potentially significant" trilateral grouping (Sikri) linking three major Southern democracies. 📰 A Leaders' Meeting was held at Johannesburg on 23 November 2025. |
| ✅ **RIC** | ✅ The Russia-India-China trilateral grouping, cited by Sikri as one of the trilateral platforms India participates in alongside IBSA. ⚠️ The most recent official Indian record located is the 18th Foreign Ministers' Meeting convened virtually on 26 November 2021; no 2025-26 resumption is officially verified. |
| ✅ **SCO** | The Shanghai Cooperation Organisation, a Eurasian security-and-economic body with 10 full members (including China, Russia, Pakistan, India, Iran and Belarus), 2 observers and 15 dialogue partners; 📰 its 2025 summit at Tianjin (31 August-1 September 2025) adopted the Tianjin Declaration, and Kyrgyzstan chairs for 2025-26 (full connectivity/energy treatment in topic 05). |
| ✅ **BRICS** | Originally BRIC (Brazil-Russia-India-China), later expanded with South Africa and additional members; 📰 the 17th Summit (Rio de Janeiro, 6-7 July 2025) issued the **Rio de Janeiro Declaration**. A separate **partner-country** category was created through the Kazan process on 24 October 2024. |
| ⚠️ **Quad** | A non-treaty minilateral coalition (US, Japan, India, Australia) with a maritime-security and Indo-Pacific cooperation focus, marked by asymmetric treaty relationships among its members (full maritime-security treatment in topic 04). 📰 The most recent Leaders' Summit was at Wilmington, Delaware, on 21 September 2024; the most recent Foreign Ministers' Meeting was in New Delhi on 26 May 2026. |
| ✅ **G20** | The premier global economic-governance forum; ✅ Tharoor's multi-alignment framing explicitly pairs India's G20 role alongside its UN role as parallel, non-exclusive engagements. 📰 The African Union became a permanent member on 9 September 2023; the 2025 Summit met at Johannesburg on 22-23 November 2025; the United States holds the 2026 presidency. |

**Current membership, participation tiers and institutional nature (verified 3 August 2026):**

| Grouping | Full membership | Other participation tiers | Institutional nature |
|---|---|---|---|
| SAARC | 8 members | Observers | Charter-based South Asian regional organisation; last Summit 18th, Kathmandu, 26-27 November 2014 |
| BIMSTEC | 7 members | — | Charter-based Bay of Bengal regional organisation (Charter in force 20 May 2024) |
| ASEAN | 11 members, including Timor-Leste (admitted at the 47th ASEAN Summit, 26 October 2025) | Dialogue partners (India since 1995 as a full dialogue partner) | Treaty/charter-based Southeast Asian regional organisation |
| IORA | 23 member states | 12 dialogue partners | Indian Ocean intergovernmental regional organisation; India chairs 2025-27 |
| SCO | 10 members, including Iran and Belarus | 2 observers (Afghanistan, Mongolia); 15 dialogue partners | Charter-based Eurasian intergovernmental organisation |
| BRICS | 11 full members | 10 partner countries (category created 24 October 2024) | Intergovernmental political-economic grouping, not a treaty alliance |
| G20 | 19 states plus the European Union and African Union | Guest countries and invited international organisations | Informal premier forum for international economic cooperation |
| Quad | India, Australia, Japan and the United States | — | Non-treaty consultative minilateral |
| Mekong-Ganga Cooperation | 6 members (Cambodia, India, Lao PDR, Myanmar, Thailand, Viet Nam) | — | Ministerial cooperation mechanism under Act East |

⚠️ **Read this table as a status list, not a power ranking.** Partner, observer,
dialogue-partner and guest categories confer participation, not the rights and
obligations of membership — a distinction UPSC tests directly.

## 3. How the grouping-participation mechanism works

1. **Regional layer (SAARC, BIMSTEC):** ✅ SAARC represents the historical South
   Asia-wide framework, while BIMSTEC (renamed from BIMST-EC) has gained
   relative prominence as SAARC's functioning has been constrained; India
   invests in both, but increasingly channels South Asia-plus-Southeast Asia
   connectivity ambitions through BIMSTEC.
2. **Trans-regional layer (IORA, IBSA):** these platforms link India with
   littoral states across the Indian Ocean (IORA) or with major Southern
   democracies on other continents (IBSA), reflecting a "beyond the immediate
   neighbourhood" engagement logic.
3. **Continental/Eurasian layer (SCO):** provides India a seat in a body
   otherwise dominated by China, Russia and Pakistan, useful for continental
   dialogue despite mixed membership (topic 05 for connectivity detail).
4. **Global-governance layer (G20, BRICS):** gives India a voice in shaping
   international economic-governance norms, pursued in parallel with, not
   instead of, UN engagement.
5. **Minilateral/issue-based layer (Quad, RIC, I2U2):** addresses specific
   functional interests (maritime security, major-power trilateral dialogue,
   food/water/energy/technology cooperation) without binding treaty commitments.
6. **Overarching multi-alignment logic:** ✅ Tharoor's own listing —
   "both the United Nations and the G20; both the... NAM... and the Community of
   Democracies; both the G77... and... IOR-ARC; both SAARC and [ASEAN-linked
   engagement]" — is the explicit organising principle tying all these layers
   together.

## 4. Institutions and agreements

- 📰 **BIMSTEC Charter (adopted Colombo, 30 March 2022; entered into force 20 May
  2024):** the anchor instrument formalising BIMSTEC's institutional/legal
  status. 📰 The **6th BIMSTEC Summit (Bangkok, 4 April 2025)** adopted the
  Summit Declaration, **BIMSTEC Bangkok Vision 2030**, Rules of Procedure for
  BIMSTEC Mechanisms and the Eminent Persons Group report, and endorsed the
  Leaders' Joint Statement on the 28 March 2025 Myanmar-Thailand earthquake.
  📰 The **BIMSTEC Agreement on Maritime Transport Cooperation was signed on
  3 April 2025** and **entered into force on 16 May 2026**, initially among
  Bhutan, India, Myanmar and Thailand. ⚠️ A treaty in force among four of seven
  members — not among the whole grouping. ⚠️ The BIMSTEC FTA framework agreement
  dates from 8 February 2004; no concluded FTA is verified.
- 📰 **BRICS Rio de Janeiro Declaration (17th Summit, 6-7 July 2025):** the
  anchor current BRICS summit outcome document. 📰 A **partner-country**
  category was created through the Kazan process on 24 October 2024, and India
  launched its 2026 chairship theme, logo and website on 13 January 2026.
  ⚠️ Distinguish full members from partner countries, and a chairship launch
  from a summit outcome.
- 📰 **New Development Bank:** ten actual members — the five founders plus
  Bangladesh (2021), the UAE (2021), Egypt (2023), Algeria (2025) and Uzbekistan
  (2026); several other states are listed as prospective, not actual, members.
- 📰 **ASEAN-India architecture:** the **22nd ASEAN-India Summit** met at Kuala
  Lumpur on 26 October 2025 — the same summit at which Timor-Leste became
  ASEAN's 11th member — and the current framework is the **ASEAN-India Plan of
  Action to implement the ASEAN-India Comprehensive Strategic Partnership
  (2026-2030)**. ⚠️ The AITIGA review remained ongoing, not concluded.
- 📰 **IORA:** India assumed the two-year chair in November 2025; the 24th
  Council of Ministers met virtually on 21 May 2025 and adopted the Colombo
  Communiqué.
- 📰 **G20:** the 2025 Summit met at Johannesburg on 22-23 November 2025 and
  adopted a Leaders' Declaration; the United States holds the 2026 presidency.
- 📰 **Quad:** most recent Leaders' Summit at Wilmington, Delaware, 21 September
  2024; most recent Foreign Ministers' Meeting in New Delhi, 26 May 2026, which
  launched the Indo-Pacific Maritime Security Cooperation initiative and a Quad
  Initiative on Indo-Pacific Energy Security and announced a Quad Critical
  Minerals Framework; the Quad Critical Minerals Initiative was launched on
  1 July 2025. ⚠️ India was announced as intended host of a Leaders' Summit, but
  no India-hosted Leaders' Summit — or a formal postponement — is officially
  verified.
- ✅ **IBSA and RIC:** trilateral groupings explicitly named by Sikri as part of
  India's overlapping-membership strategy. 📰 An IBSA Leaders' Meeting was held
  at Johannesburg on 23 November 2025; ⚠️ RIC has no officially verified
  meeting since the virtual 18th Foreign Ministers' Meeting of 26 November 2021.
- ⚠️ **Mekong-Ganga Cooperation:** a six-member grouping (Cambodia, India, Lao
  PDR, Myanmar, Thailand, Viet Nam) linking India with mainland Southeast Asian
  states under the broader Act East policy; ⚠️ the most recent ministerial
  meeting located officially is the 10th, at Bangkok on 1 August 2019.
- ✅ **Book-period membership gap:** Sikri noted that India had not secured APEC
  or ASEM membership in the period he discussed. India joined ASEM in 2007;
  therefore only APEC remains a current non-membership example, and its status
  should be dated whenever used.

## 5. Indian applications and examples

- ⚠️ **PYQ mapping:** no GS-II Mains question in the audited 2024-2025 papers
  directly names SAARC, BIMSTEC, ASEAN, IORA, SCO, BRICS, G20 or Quad as the
  sole framing device. State this honestly; use the layered-grouping framework
  here as the structuring device for any regionalism/minilateralism-themed
  question.
- 📰 The BIMSTEC Charter's entry into force (20 May 2024) and the 6th BIMSTEC
  Summit's adoption of Bangkok Vision 2030 (4 April 2025) are concrete,
  dateable institutional milestones worth citing for any "regionalism in South
  Asia" question, given SAARC's relatively constrained momentum (no Summit since
  the 18th at Kathmandu, 26-27 November 2014).
- 📰 The BRICS Rio de Janeiro Declaration (6-7 July 2025) is the relevant dated
  reference for any current BRICS-agenda question; India holds the 2026
  chairship.

## 6. Must-Know Facts for Prelims

- ✅ BIMSTEC was originally BIMST-EC (Bangladesh-India-Myanmar-Sri Lanka-
  Thailand Economic Cooperation) before being renamed.
- 📰 The BIMSTEC Charter was adopted at Colombo on 30 March 2022 and entered into
  force on 20 May 2024; the 6th BIMSTEC Summit (Bangkok, 4 April 2025) adopted
  BIMSTEC Bangkok Vision 2030.
- 📰 The BIMSTEC Agreement on Maritime Transport Cooperation was signed on
  3 April 2025 and entered into force on 16 May 2026 among Bhutan, India,
  Myanmar and Thailand.
- 📰 The BRICS Rio de Janeiro Declaration was issued at the 17th Summit,
  6-7 July 2025; BRICS has 11 full members and a separate partner-country
  category of 10 countries created on 24 October 2024.
- 📰 Timor-Leste became ASEAN's 11th member at the 47th ASEAN Summit on
  26 October 2025; the 22nd ASEAN-India Summit met the same day.
- 📰 The African Union became a permanent G20 member on 9 September 2023; the
  2025 G20 Summit met at Johannesburg on 22-23 November 2025, and the United
  States holds the 2026 presidency.
- 📰 India chairs IORA for 2025-27 and holds the BRICS chairship for 2026.
- 📰 The most recent Quad Leaders' Summit was at Wilmington on 21 September 2024
  and the most recent Quad Foreign Ministers' Meeting in New Delhi on
  26 May 2026.
- ✅ IBSA (India-Brazil-South Africa) and RIC (Russia-India-China) are
  trilateral groupings India participates in; an IBSA Leaders' Meeting was held
  at Johannesburg on 23 November 2025.
- ✅ The last SAARC Summit held was the 18th, at Kathmandu on 26-27 November 2014.
- ✅ Sikri's APEC/ASEM statement is period-specific: India joined ASEM in 2007,
  while it is not an APEC member as of 3 August 2026.
- ⚠️ Quad membership comprises the US, Japan, India and Australia, with a
  maritime-security/Indo-Pacific cooperation focus.

## 7. UPSC traps

- ❌ SAARC has been formally dissolved. -> SAARC continues to exist as an
  institution even though summit-level momentum has been constrained by
  India-Pakistan tensions; do not assert dissolution without a specific,
  verified source.
- ❌ BIMSTEC and BIMST-EC are two different organisations. -> BIMSTEC is the
  renamed continuation of BIMST-EC; they are the same institutional lineage.
- ❌ Quad is a treaty alliance comparable to NATO. -> It is a non-treaty
  minilateral coalition with acknowledged asymmetry among members (full
  treatment in topic 04).
- ❌ BRICS and the Global South are synonymous. -> BRICS has a specific,
  distinct (though expanding) membership; the Global South is a broader
  category (cross-link to topic 08).
- ❌ BRICS partner countries are BRICS members. -> The partner category, created
  through the Kazan process on 24 October 2024, confers participation without
  the rights and obligations of full membership; BRICS had 11 full members and
  10 partner countries as of 3 August 2026.
- ❌ India's BRICS 2026 chairship means India hosted a 2026 BRICS Summit. -> The
  13 January 2026 launch of theme, logo and website is a chairship-process
  anchor; no 2026 Leaders' Summit outcome was officially verified as of
  3 August 2026.
- ❌ The BIMSTEC maritime transport agreement binds all seven members. -> It
  entered into force on 16 May 2026 among the four states that had deposited
  instruments — Bhutan, India, Myanmar and Thailand.
- ❌ India holds membership in every major regional/global forum it has sought.
  -> APEC remains a current non-membership example; ASEM does not, because
  India joined it in 2007.

## 8. 📰 Current anchor

- 📰 The **BIMSTEC Charter (in force 20 May 2024)**, the **6th BIMSTEC Summit
  and Bangkok Vision 2030 (4 April 2025)** and the **BRICS Rio de Janeiro
  Declaration (6-7 July 2025)** remain key instruments. India launched its
  **BRICS 2026 chairship** logo, theme and website on **13 January 2026**;
  "Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability" is
  the current chairship anchor, not a new treaty or completed summit outcome.
- 📰 Two further dated markers carry the current cycle: the **entry into force
  of the BIMSTEC Agreement on Maritime Transport Cooperation (16 May 2026,
  among four members)** and the **Quad Foreign Ministers' Meeting (New Delhi,
  26 May 2026)**, which launched the Indo-Pacific Maritime Security Cooperation
  initiative and an Indo-Pacific energy-security initiative.

## 9. PYQ application

- ✅ **2020 GS-II:** "'Quadrilateral Security Dialogue (QUAD)' is
  transforming itself into a trade bloc from a military alliance, in present
  times — Discuss." Correct the premise: Quad is neither a treaty military
  alliance nor a trade bloc; evaluate its widening functional agenda.
- ✅ **2021 GS-II:** "Critically examine the aims and objectives of SCO. What
  importance does it hold for India?" — mandate, interests, constraints.
- ✅ **2022 GS-II:** "How will I2U2 (India, Israel, UAE and USA) grouping
  transform India's position in global politics?" — use West Asia, investment,
  technology and strategic-autonomy dimensions.
- ⚠️ No 2024-2025 question names one grouping as the sole subject; use the
  layered-grouping framework and dated BIMSTEC/BRICS anchors.

## 10. Mains angles

- ⚠️ Always specify which layer (regional, trans-regional, continental,
  global-governance, minilateral) a grouping belongs to, rather than listing
  groupings without categorisation.
- ⚠️ Cite the dated BIMSTEC Charter/Bangkok Vision 2030/BRICS Rio de Janeiro
  Declaration rather than an undated "regional cooperation" claim.
- ⚠️ Distinguish treaty-bound institutions (BIMSTEC, SCO, SAARC) from non-treaty
  coalitions (Quad, RIC, IBSA, I2U2), and full membership from partner,
  observer, dialogue-partner and guest status.
- ⚠️ Use Tharoor's explicit multi-alignment pairing list as the organising
  citation for any "why does India join so many groupings" question.

> **Answer thesis:** India's participation across regional (SAARC, BIMSTEC),
> trans-regional (IORA, IBSA), continental (SCO), global-governance (G20,
> BRICS) and minilateral (Quad, RIC) groupings reflects a deliberate,
> multi-alignment strategy of engaging every relevant platform on its own
> terms, rather than anchoring foreign policy in a single exclusive bloc.
