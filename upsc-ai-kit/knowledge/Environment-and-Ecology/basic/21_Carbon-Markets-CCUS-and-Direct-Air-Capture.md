## 1. Visual foundation

```text
CARBON-MITIGATION TOOLKIT (complementary, not substitute, instruments)

CARBON MARKETS (price signal)       POINT-SOURCE CCUS             DIRECT AIR CAPTURE (DAC)
Compliance market: law sets    Separate CO2 at an emitting    Separate CO2 from AMBIENT
  an obligation; eligible       facility before release;       AIR, where it is dilute
  units meet that obligation    then transport for use/store   Separation alone is not CDR
Voluntary market: buyer        Usually avoids/reduces a new    DAC + durable storage = DACCS
  chooses an outside credit     emission; use may re-release   Net CDR also deducts lifecycle emissions

CDR = intentional atmospheric CO2 removal + durable storage + lifecycle accounting
```

**Core proposition:** Carbon markets create economic incentives and accounting units;
point-source CCUS separates CO₂ before atmospheric release and therefore usually avoids or
reduces a new emission; Direct Air Capture (DAC) separates CO₂ from ambient air. DAC becomes
a carbon-dioxide-removal pathway only when the captured CO₂ is durably stored and full
lifecycle emissions are deducted. These instruments are complementary but physically and
legally distinct.

## 2. Essential definitions

| Concept | Exam-ready meaning |
|---|---|
| ✅ **Carbon market** | A trading system where emission allowances or offset credits are bought and sold, putting a price on carbon emissions/reductions. |
| ✅ **Compliance carbon market** | A market created by law in which covered entities must surrender eligible units according to a quantified obligation. In cap-and-trade, surrender normally follows covered emissions; in baseline-and-credit, certificates address performance above or below a target rather than the entity's entire emissions. |
| ✅ **Voluntary carbon market** | A market where entities voluntarily purchase carbon credits (often from projects like afforestation or renewable energy) to offset their own emissions, without a regulatory mandate. |
| ✅ **CCUS** | Carbon Capture, Utilisation and Storage — a chain that separates CO₂ at a point source, conditions and transports it, then uses it or places it in storage. Standard fossil/process-source CCUS usually avoids or reduces emissions; utilisation is not automatically durable storage. |
| ✅ **Direct Air Capture (DAC)** | A separation process that captures CO₂ directly from dilute ambient air. DAC alone does not establish durable carbon removal: the destination of the CO₂, energy source and full capture-to-storage lifecycle decide the net outcome. |
| ✅ **DACCS** | Direct Air Carbon Capture and Storage — DAC followed by transport and durable storage, normally geological. It can qualify as CDR only after lifecycle emissions, leakage, monitoring and storage durability are accounted for. |
| ✅ **Carbon dioxide removal (CDR)** | Human activity that intentionally removes CO₂ from the atmosphere and durably stores it in geological, terrestrial, oceanic or product reservoirs. Methods include carefully governed biological, technological and geochemical routes; avoiding an emission or issuing a market credit is not itself CDR. |
| ✅ **Indian Carbon Market (ICM) / Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS)** | India's domestic carbon market framework, enabled by the Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022 and given effect by the **Carbon Credit Trading Scheme notified in June 2023**. It has a **dual structure**: a **compliance mechanism** for obligated energy-intensive entities and an **offset mechanism** for voluntary registration by non-obligated entities (Economic Survey 2025-26, Ch. 10). |
| ✅ **Baseline-and-credit vs cap-and-trade** | India's compliance mechanism is an **emission-intensity baseline-and-credit** system: obligated entities receive a **greenhouse gas emission intensity (GEI) target** (tCO₂e per unit of output). Those who beat their target become eligible for **Carbon Credit Certificates (CCCs)**, denominated in **tonnes of CO₂ equivalent**; those who miss it must acquire and surrender certificates. The Scheme and CERC regulations provide for exchange trading. ⚠️ This is **not** cap-and-trade — there is no absolute economy-wide emissions cap. |
| ✅ **Carbon Credit Certificate (CCC)** | The tradable instrument under the CCTS, denominated in tCO₂e. Distinguish it from a **green credit** (Green Credit Rules, 2023 — rewards specified environmental actions, not tCO₂e) and from an **Energy Saving Certificate/ESCert** under PAT. BEE material defines one ESCert as the energy-saving attribute of one metric tonne of oil equivalent; it is not a carbon-denominated certificate. |

## 3. Topic mechanism

1. Carbon markets work by assigning a price (direct, via allowance trading, or indirect, via
   credit purchase) to carbon emissions, creating a financial incentive for entities to
   reduce emissions where doing so is cheaper than buying additional allowances/credits —
   theoretically achieving emission reductions at the lowest overall economic cost.
2. India's Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022 provided the legal basis for the Indian
   Carbon Market, building on the existing Perform, Achieve and Trade (PAT) scheme (which
   traded Energy Saving Certificates among energy-intensive industries) toward a broader
   carbon-credit trading mechanism.
   Institutional inheritance does not make the instruments identical: PAT regulates
   Specific Energy Consumption and ESCerts, whereas CCTS compliance uses greenhouse-gas-
   emissions-intensity targets and Carbon Credit Certificates in tCO2e.
3. Point-source CCUS separates CO₂ from power or industrial streams. Higher-concentration
   streams are generally easier to separate than ambient air, but the climate result still
   depends on capture coverage, extra energy, transport, utilisation or storage, leakage and
   the counterfactual technology.
4. DAC addresses a different physical problem: separating CO₂ already dispersed in ambient
   air. The low concentration means large air handling and significant heat/electricity
   requirements, so the carbon intensity of the energy supply materially affects net removal.
5. DAC is not synonymous with DACCS. CO₂ used in fuels or short-lived products may soon return
   to the atmosphere. DAC followed by durable geological storage, or a demonstrably durable
   product/mineral pathway, can qualify as CDR only with full lifecycle MRV.
6. CDR is a portfolio, not one machine. It includes afforestation/reforestation and ecosystem
   restoration, carefully qualified soil-carbon and biochar practices, BECCS, DACCS, enhanced
   weathering/mineralisation and proposed ocean-based approaches. Their storage media,
   measurement uncertainty, reversal risk, permanence time horizons and governance needs differ.
7. Point-source CCUS is most relevant to residual process emissions in sectors such as cement,
   steel and fertiliser. DACCS and other CDR methods are economy-wide balancing tools for
   genuinely residual emissions after feasible direct mitigation, not substitutes for broader
   decarbonisation (cross-refer Topic 20's LT-LEDS industry pathway).

## 4. Institutions and policy tools

- ✅ **Ministry of Power:** notified the Carbon Credit Trading Scheme, 2023 under the Energy
  Conservation Act framework and chairs the National Steering Committee for the Indian
  Carbon Market.
- ✅ **Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE):** is the statutory **Administrator** for the Indian
  Carbon Market and also administers PAT. Under CCTS it develops procedures/methodologies,
  identifies sectors and obligated entities, supports target setting, accredits verification
  agencies and performs the functions assigned by the Scheme.
- ✅ **MoEFCC:** co-chairs the National Steering Committee and, under the compliance
  procedure, notifies obligated-entity greenhouse-gas-emissions-intensity targets after the
  prescribed Ministry of Power/BEE/Steering Committee process. It also leads India's
  international Article 6 engagement; it is not the CCTS registry or trading regulator.
- ✅ **Grid Controller of India Limited:** is the **Registry** for the Indian Carbon Market,
  maintaining entity accounts and transaction/holding records under the Scheme and detailed
  procedures.
- ✅ **Central Electricity Regulatory Commission (CERC):** is the regulator for CCC trading
  activities. Its 2026 purchase-and-sale regulations govern exchange trading and related
  market arrangements; notification of trading rules does not by itself prove market
  liquidity or verified mitigation.
- ⚠️ International carbon-market mechanisms (Article 6.2 bilateral cooperation, Article 6.4
  centralised crediting) provide the global architecture within which India's domestic
  market may eventually interlink (cross-refer Topic 19).

## 5. Indian applications and examples

- ⚠️ India's PAT scheme, predating the formal Indian Carbon Market, demonstrated the
  feasibility of a market-based energy-efficiency trading mechanism among energy-intensive
  industries (e.g., cement, steel, thermal power), providing an institutional foundation for
  the broader carbon market.
- ⚠️ CCUS pilot projects in India (e.g., in cement and other industrial sectors) are at an
  early stage, reflecting the technology's still-developing cost and deployment-scale
  profile domestically.
- ⚠️ Afforestation and renewable-energy projects have historically been common sources of
  voluntary carbon credits from India in international voluntary carbon markets.

## 6. Must-Know Facts for Prelims

- ✅ The Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022 provides the legal basis for the Indian
  Carbon Market.
- ✅ CCUS captures CO₂ at concentrated point sources (e.g., power plants and industrial
  facilities); DAC separates CO₂ from ambient, dilute air.
- ✅ Direct Air Capture is generally more energy-intensive per tonne of CO₂ captured than
  CCUS, due to the much lower concentration of CO₂ in ambient air compared to industrial
  flue gas.
- ✅ DAC alone is not a complete removal claim. DACCS requires transport and durable storage;
  DAC-to-fuel or other short-lived use normally recycles CO₂ rather than storing it durably.
- ✅ CDR includes biological routes (afforestation/reforestation, ecosystem restoration,
  carefully qualified soil carbon and biochar), BECCS, DACCS, enhanced weathering/
  mineralisation and proposed ocean-based routes. Their permanence and MRV are not equivalent.
- ✅ A removal claim must identify the atmospheric source, lifecycle emissions, storage medium,
  expected storage duration, reversal controls, monitoring and liability.
- ✅ India's PAT (Perform, Achieve and Trade) scheme, administered by BEE, traded Energy
  Saving Certificates and served as an institutional precursor to the broader carbon market.
  One ESCert represents the energy-saving attribute of one metric tonne of oil equivalent;
  it is not a CCC.
- ✅ Compliance carbon markets are government-mandated; voluntary carbon markets involve
  non-mandated, voluntary credit purchases.
- ✅ The **Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS) was adopted in June 2023** with a **dual
  compliance-plus-offset** design; the compliance leg is an **emission-intensity
  baseline-and-credit** system (not cap-and-trade), and **Carbon Credit Certificates (CCCs)**
  are denominated in **tCO₂e**; the Scheme and CERC's 2026 regulations provide for trading
  through power exchanges.
- ✅ In **2025** the Government notified pro-rata **Greenhouse Gas Emission Intensity (GEI)
  targets for four sectors — Aluminium, Cement, Chlor-Alkali, and Pulp and Paper**.
- ✅ The **offset mechanism** covers an approved list of **10 sectors**: energy, industries,
  agriculture, waste handling and disposal, forestry, transport, fugitive emissions,
  construction, solvent use, and **carbon capture, utilisation and storage (CCUS)**.
- ✅ Under the CCTS institutional split, **BEE is Administrator**, **Grid Controller of India
  is Registry**, and **CERC regulates trading**; MoEFCC notifies compliance GEI targets under
  the prescribed recommendation process.
- ✅ CERC notified the **Terms and Conditions for Purchase and Sale of Carbon Credit
  Certificates Regulations, 2026**. This establishes trading rules but is not evidence that
  a liquid market, a discovered price or a verified volume of mitigation already exists.
- ✅ Under the EU ETS (launched 2005, now in its **fourth phase**), up to **57%** of
  allowances are auctioned and the rest allocated free to limit carbon leakage; the **Carbon
  Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) gradually replaces free allocation from 2026**, and a
  second system, **ETS2**, covering buildings, road transport and additional small-industry
  fuel combustion, is to be implemented **by 2027** (Economic Survey 2025-26, Ch. 10, Box X.5).
- ✅ **Korea's ETS has been operational since 2015**; China operates a **national ETS** —
  together with the EU these are the three major reference systems India's design is compared
  against.

## 7. UPSC traps

- ❌ CCUS and Direct Air Capture are the same technology. -> CCUS captures CO₂ at a
  concentrated point source; DAC captures CO₂ directly from dilute ambient air, a
  technologically distinct and generally more energy-intensive process.
- ❌ Every tonne separated by DAC is automatically permanent carbon removal. -> DAC is the
  separation step; durable CDR requires low-emission operation, transport and verified storage.
  Fuel and short-lived-product uses can re-release the CO₂.
- ❌ All CDR routes are interchangeable and permanent. -> Biological, geological, product,
  mineral and ocean reservoirs have different measurement, saturation, reversal, ecological,
  governance and time-horizon risks.
- ❌ Carbon markets alone can fully decarbonise hard-to-abate sectors without any
  technological intervention. -> Carbon markets provide a price incentive; actual
  decarbonisation of hard-to-abate sectors also requires technologies like CCUS, green
  hydrogen and process innovation.
- ❌ India's carbon market is purely voluntary with no legal/regulatory basis. -> The Indian
  Carbon Market has a specific legal basis under the Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act,
  2022.
- ❌ PAT and the Indian Carbon Market are entirely unrelated schemes. -> PAT is an
  institutional precursor/foundation that has informed the evolving Indian Carbon Market
  framework, but ESCerts and CCCs retain different metrics, legal procedures and compliance
  purposes.
- ❌ CCUS eliminates the need for renewable-energy transition. -> CCUS is a complementary
  tool primarily for hard-to-abate sectors and existing infrastructure, not a substitute for
  the broader renewable-energy transition.
- ❌ India's compliance carbon market is a cap-and-trade system like the EU ETS. -> It is an
  **emission-intensity baseline-and-credit** system with no absolute economy-wide cap.
- ❌ A carbon credit certificate and a green credit are the same instrument. -> CCCs are
  denominated in **tCO₂e** under the CCTS; **green credits** (Green Credit Rules, 2023) reward
  specified environmental actions and are not emission-denominated.
- ❌ BEE, Grid Controller of India and CERC are interchangeable carbon-market bodies. -> BEE
  is Administrator, Grid Controller is Registry, and CERC regulates trading.
- ❌ MoEFCC administers the registry and exchange. -> Its domestic compliance role includes
  GEI-target notification under the prescribed process; registry and trading regulation
  belong to Grid Controller and CERC respectively.
- ❌ CBAM is an EU carbon tax on all imports. -> It is a border adjustment that **gradually
  replaces free allocation** for carbon-leakage-exposed sectors within the EU ETS from 2026,
  applying to specified goods rather than to imports generally.

## 8. 📰 Current anchor

- 📰 **CCTS status (Economic Survey 2025-26, Ch. 10):** adopted June 2023; compliance
  mechanism being operationalised on PAT institutional experience; **GEI targets notified
  in 2025 for Aluminium, Cement, Chlor-Alkali and Pulp
  and Paper**; offset mechanism approved for **10 sectors** including **CCUS**. GEI target
  setting proceeds by estimating each obligated entity's baseline GEI, comparing entities
  within the same sub-sectoral group, and assigning the **least demanding target to the most
  efficient unit and the most demanding to the least efficient** (Bureau of Energy
  Efficiency methodology).
- 📰 **Trading-rule status:** CERC notified its Terms and Conditions for Purchase and Sale
  of Carbon Credit Certificates Regulations on 27 February 2026, with Gazette publication
  on 4 May 2026. This adds the exchange-trading rule layer; verify actual issuance, trade,
  surrender, price and liquidity data before claiming an operating market outcome.
- 📰 **International reference points (Economic Survey 2025-26, Box X.5):** EU ETS (2005,
  fourth phase; up to 57% of the cap auctioned; **CBAM replacing free allocation from 2026**;
  **ETS2** for buildings/road transport by **2027**; offset credits discontinued in Phase 4),
  Korea ETS (2015) and China's national ETS. The EU's early-phase experience — oversupply of
  offset credits diluting the carbon price — is the specific cautionary lesson the Survey
  draws for India's offset mechanism.
- 📰 **IPCC AR7 cycle:** the scientific content of a **2027 Methodology Report on Carbon
  Dioxide Removal Technologies, Carbon Capture, Utilization and Storage for National
  Greenhouse Gas Inventories** was agreed at **IPCC-63 (Lima, Peru, October 2025)** — meaning
  internationally agreed *accounting rules* for CCUS and removals are being written now
  (ipcc.ch, verified 2 August 2026).

⚠️ **Interpretation caution:** global CCUS and DAC deployment scale remains small relative
to IPCC-modelled mitigation pathway requirements — cite specific deployment figures with
their source and date rather than assuming large-scale current deployment. Equally,
distinguish a **notified scheme** (CCTS, 2023), **notified sectoral targets** (2025),
**notified trading regulations** (2026), and an **operating, liquid market with issued,
traded and surrendered certificates**. The first three do not prove the fourth.

## 10. Mains angles

- ⚠️ Argue that carbon markets, point-source CCUS and CDR are complementary instruments
  serving different functions: price/accounting incentive, avoided or reduced point-source
  emissions, and atmosphere-to-reservoir removal. DAC is the separation step; DACCS is the
  corresponding durable-storage pathway.
- ⚠️ Use the hard-to-abate-sector framing (steel, cement, fertiliser) for point-source CCUS,
  while treating DACCS and other CDR routes as potential balancing tools for residual emissions
  after renewable-energy substitution, efficiency and process change.
- ⚠️ Conclude with a market-integrity thesis: carbon markets deliver genuine mitigation only
  with robust monitoring, reporting and verification (MRV) systems preventing double-
  counting or low-quality credits.

> **Answer thesis:** Treat carbon markets, point-source CCUS and carbon dioxide removal as
> complementary but non-interchangeable instruments: a price/accounting system, an
> emissions-avoidance chain, and an atmosphere-to-reservoir service. DAC is only the separation
> stage; judge DACCS and every other removal route by lifecycle emissions, storage medium,
> permanence, reversal risk, MRV and mitigation-first use.

## 13. Core answer architecture (10/15/20-mark support)

### 13.1 Direct demand — CCUS and climate role (2025 GS-III, 10 marks)

**Thesis:** CCUS captures CO₂ at a concentrated source, separates it, then uses or stores it; it is most defensible as an emissions-avoidance complement for hard-to-abate processes, not a licence to defer direct mitigation or a synonym for atmospheric removal.

| Claim | Named evidence/example → significance | Qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Capture has a sectoral role. | **Cement, steel, fertiliser and other hard-to-abate point sources** → process emissions may remain after efficiency/electrification. | Standard fossil-source CCUS avoids new emissions; it is not automatically negative emissions. |
| Storage/accounting determine climate value. | **Geological storage, monitoring and permanence/MRV** → captured CO₂ only helps if leakage and lifecycle emissions are controlled. | Utilisation, especially where carbon is later re-released, must not be claimed as permanent storage. |
| India is creating an enabling framework. | **CCTS offset mechanism includes CCUS among approved sectors; 2027 IPCC methodology work is scheduled** → policy/accounting are evolving. | A notified mechanism or methodology work is not proof of large-scale operating capture in India. |

**150-word spine:** define capture–transport–use/store → role in hard-to-abate sectors → two limits (energy/cost and permanence/MRV/lock-in) → conclusion: prioritise direct reduction, reserve capture for residual/process emissions and verify every tonne.

### 13.2 Market/removal comparison for 15/20 marks

| Instrument | Function | Core integrity test |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon market | Price incentive for reductions/removals | additionality, double-counting and MRV |
| Standard CCUS | Point-source emissions avoidance | capture rate, energy penalty, storage permanence |
| DACCS/BECCS | Potential atmospheric removal with durable storage | energy/feedstock, lifecycle accounting, transport, MRV and permanence |
| Biological/geochemical CDR | Atmospheric uptake into terrestrial, product, mineral or ocean reservoirs | additionality, measurement, reversal, ecology, rights and storage time horizon |

Use the CCTS intensity-baseline-and-credit design only as a dated Indian example; it is not an economy-wide absolute cap-and-trade system.
