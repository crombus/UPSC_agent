# Environment and Ecology: Processes, Laws and Distinctive Features - Last-Minute Revision Chart

> **Purpose:** One-place revision immediately before Prelims or GS-III Mains.
> **Coverage:** Ecology, biodiversity, protected areas, wildlife and forest law, pollution, waste, EIA, climate science and governance, environmental conventions, land and marine ecology, clean energy, disasters, institutions and species current affairs.
> **Two-pass design:** Core is independently exam-complete. Advanced adds legal nuance, ecological trade-offs and implementation depth; skipping it must not affect the paper outcome.

## 2. Universal environment-answer framework

| Step | Question |
|---|---|
| System | Which ecosystem, species, resource or pollutant is involved? |
| Process | How does energy, matter, water, habitat or climate mechanism operate? |
| Pressure | Is the driver land-use change, extraction, pollution, invasive species or climate change? |
| Scale | Local, landscape, river-basin, airshed, national or global? |
| Law/status | Which statute, rule, treaty or designation applies, and at what current stage? |
| Institution | Who regulates, monitors, adjudicates and implements? |
| Equity | Who bears ecological cost and who depends on the resource? |
| Outcome | Is success measured by spending/area or by ecological quality and resilience? |

### E-C-O-L-O-G-Y test

`Ecosystem -> Causal pressure -> Outcome risk -> Law -> Organisation -> Governance gap -> Yardstick`

## 3. Ecosystem structure and function

| Concept | Exam-ready meaning |
|---|---|
| Ecosystem | Functional unit of organisms interacting with physical environment |
| Habitat | Place/environment where organism lives |
| Niche | Functional role and resource-use position of a species |
| Population | Individuals of same species in an area |
| Community | Interacting populations of different species |
| Ecotone | Transition zone between ecosystems |
| Edge effect | Changed diversity/conditions near ecosystem boundary |
| Carrying capacity | Population supportable under prevailing resources and conditions |
| Resilience | Ability to absorb disturbance and retain/recover function |
| Resistance | Ability to remain unchanged during disturbance |

### Trophic architecture

`sun -> producers -> primary consumers -> secondary/tertiary consumers -> decomposers`

- energy flow is unidirectional and declines between trophic levels;
- matter is recycled through biogeochemical cycles;
- decomposers act across trophic levels;
- food webs generally provide alternative pathways compared with a single chain;
- keystone impact is disproportionately large relative to abundance.

### Ecosystem services

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Provisioning | Food, water, fibre and genetic resources |
| Regulating | Climate, flood, pollination and water purification |
| Cultural | Recreation, spiritual and educational value |
| Supporting/ecological processes | Soil formation and nutrient cycling |

## 4. Cycles, productivity and ecological pyramids

| Concept | Core rule |
|---|---|
| Gross primary productivity | Total producer energy fixed |
| Net primary productivity | GPP minus producer respiration |
| Secondary productivity | Biomass production by consumers |
| Standing crop | Biomass/number present at a time |
| Standing state | Quantity of nutrient present at a time |

### Cycle fingerprints

| Cycle | Main reservoir/process clue |
|---|---|
| Water | Evaporation, condensation, precipitation, runoff and infiltration |
| Carbon | Photosynthesis, respiration, decomposition, combustion and ocean exchange |
| Nitrogen | Fixation, nitrification, assimilation, ammonification and denitrification |
| Phosphorus | Mainly sedimentary; no major atmospheric phase |
| Sulphur | Rock, ocean and atmospheric emissions; acid-deposition linkage |

### Pyramid rules

| Pyramid | Can it invert? |
|---|---|
| Energy | No |
| Biomass | Yes, notably some aquatic systems |
| Numbers | Yes |

> **Trap:** The ten-percent law is an approximate trophic-transfer heuristic, not a universal fixed efficiency for every ecosystem.

## 5. Succession, biomes and restoration

| Pair | Correct distinction |
|---|---|
| Primary succession | Begins without developed soil |
| Secondary succession | Begins after disturbance where soil/propagules remain |
| Autogenic succession | Driven by community-induced environmental change |
| Allogenic succession | Driven by external physical forces |
| Pioneer community | Early coloniser |
| Climax concept | Relatively stable state under a model, not immutable endpoint |

### Succession chain

`nudation -> invasion -> establishment -> competition/reaction -> changing community`

### Biome controls

`temperature + precipitation + seasonality + soil + disturbance -> dominant vegetation and fauna`

> **Restoration rule:** Plantation area is not equivalent to ecological restoration. Test native composition, structure, connectivity, soil-water function and long-term survival.

## 6. Biodiversity, hotspots and endemism

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Genetic diversity | Variation within species |
| Species diversity | Richness plus relative abundance |
| Ecosystem diversity | Variety of habitats and ecological systems |
| Alpha diversity | Within-site/community diversity |
| Beta diversity | Change between sites |
| Gamma diversity | Regional/landscape diversity |

| Concept | Distinctive feature |
|---|---|
| Endemic | Naturally restricted to a defined area |
| Native | Occurs naturally, not necessarily restricted |
| Exotic/alien | Introduced outside natural range |
| Invasive alien | Introduced species causing or likely to cause harm under relevant usage |
| Flagship | Used to mobilise conservation support |
| Umbrella | Protection benefits many co-occurring species |
| Keystone | Disproportionate ecosystem effect |
| Indicator | Reflects environmental condition |

### Hotspot rule

A biodiversity hotspot is a scientific global prioritisation category based on high plant endemism and severe habitat loss under the recognised framework. It is **not** a statutory protected-area category.

> **Trap:** Species richness, endemism, rarity and threat are different concepts.

## 7. IUCN Red List

### Category sequence

`NE -> DD -> LC -> NT -> VU -> EN -> CR -> EW -> EX`

| Category group | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Threatened | Vulnerable, Endangered and Critically Endangered |
| Data Deficient | Evidence insufficient for assessment; not “safe” |
| Not Evaluated | Assessment not yet made |
| Extinct in the Wild | Survives only in cultivation, captivity or naturalised outside former range under definition |

### Assessment signals

`population decline + geographic range + small population/decline + very small/restricted population + quantitative extinction analysis`

> **Core rule:** IUCN status is a scientific global assessment. It does not itself determine Indian statutory protection, CITES appendix or local population condition.

## 8. Protected-area network

| Category | Core identity |
|---|---|
| National Park | Strong statutory habitat/wildlife protection with rights and activity governed under law |
| Wildlife Sanctuary | Statutory protected area with legal treatment distinct from National Park |
| Conservation Reserve | Government-owned land, often landscape/linkage role |
| Community Reserve | Community/private land conservation participation under statute |
| Tiger Reserve | Core/buffer conservation framework under wildlife law and NTCA architecture |
| Eco-Sensitive Zone | Regulated transition around protected area under environmental law; not itself a protected-area category |

### Landscape conservation

`core habitat + buffer + corridor + surrounding land use + community coexistence`

> **Trap:** Biosphere Reserve, Ramsar Site, UNESCO designation, Tiger Reserve and National Park arise from different legal/institutional frameworks and may overlap geographically.

## 9. Biosphere reserves and wetlands

### Biosphere zonation

| Zone | Function |
|---|---|
| Core | Long-term conservation |
| Buffer | Compatible research, education and limited activity |
| Transition | Sustainable human activity and cooperation |

### Ramsar logic

`international designation -> wise-use commitment and visibility -> domestic wetland governance -> ecological monitoring`

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Wise use | Maintaining ecological character through ecosystem approaches within sustainable-development context |
| Ecological character | Combination of ecosystem components, processes and benefits/services |
| Montreux Record | Register of listed sites where ecological character has changed, is changing or is likely to change |

> **Trap:** Ramsar designation does not automatically convert a wetland into a National Park or prohibit all human use.

## 10. Wildlife law, CITES and CMS

### Domestic wildlife-law map

| Layer | Function |
|---|---|
| Species schedules | Statutory protection/trade framework under current amended law |
| Protected areas | Habitat-based conservation |
| Hunting prohibitions/exceptions | Species protection subject to statutory provisions |
| CITES implementation | Management/scientific authority and scheduled-specimen controls |
| Enforcement | Forest/wildlife authorities, WCCB and other agencies within mandate |

### CITES appendices

| Appendix | Trade logic |
|---|---|
| I | Species threatened with extinction; commercial trade generally highly restricted |
| II | Trade controlled to avoid incompatible utilisation |
| III | Species protected in at least one country seeking international assistance |

### CMS appendices

| Appendix | Core logic |
|---|---|
| I | Endangered migratory species needing strict protection/cooperation |
| II | Migratory species benefiting from international agreements/cooperation |

> **Comparison:** CITES regulates international trade in listed specimens; CMS coordinates conservation across migratory ranges. Neither is the IUCN Red List.

## 11. Forests, rights and governance

### Forest distinctions

| Term | Correct meaning |
|---|---|
| Recorded forest area | Land recorded as forest in government records |
| Forest cover | Canopy-based remote-sensing category under stated methodology |
| Tree cover | Tree patches outside forest-cover definition under stated methodology |
| Legal forest | Land governed as forest under applicable statute/record/judicial interpretation |
| Natural forest | Ecological category based on composition and processes |
| Plantation | Planted stand; may count in canopy statistics but differs ecologically |

### Forest Rights Act

| Right/process | Core identity |
|---|---|
| Individual forest rights | Eligible individual/household rights under statute |
| Community rights | Community use/access rights |
| Community forest resource right | Protect, regenerate, conserve or manage community forest resource under law |
| Gram Sabha | Initiates claims and performs statutory functions |

### CAMPA/restoration chain

`forest diversion -> compensatory levy/fund -> site and plantation/restoration activity -> survival and ecological-quality monitoring`

> **Core verdict:** Conservation and rights need not be opposites; secure community tenure and accountable stewardship can strengthen conservation, but legal processes and ecological limits must both be respected.

## 12. Air pollution

### Pollution pathway

`source -> emission -> atmospheric transport/chemistry -> concentration/exposure -> health/ecosystem effect`

| Pollutant | High-yield source/effect clue |
|---|---|
| PM2.5/PM10 | Combustion, dust and secondary formation; respiratory/cardiovascular harm |
| SO2 | Sulphur-bearing fuel; acid deposition and secondary particles |
| NOx | Combustion; ozone and secondary-particle formation |
| Ground-level ozone | Secondary pollutant from precursors in sunlight |
| CO | Incomplete combustion; impairs oxygen transport |
| Lead | Toxic metal with neurological effects |

| Tool | Function |
|---|---|
| Emission standard | Controls source release |
| Ambient standard | Defines desired outdoor concentration |
| AQI | Communicates combined air-quality condition |
| Source apportionment | Estimates source contributions |
| Airshed management | Coordinates beyond administrative boundaries |

> **Trap:** Smog is an atmospheric outcome; it is not a single pollutant. Emergency restrictions cannot substitute for year-round source reduction.

## 13. Water pollution and river restoration

| Indicator | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Dissolved oxygen | Oxygen available in water; higher is generally favourable |
| BOD | Oxygen required by microbes to decompose biodegradable organic matter |
| COD | Oxygen equivalent needed to chemically oxidise pollutants |
| Eutrophication | Nutrient enrichment causing excessive productivity and oxygen stress |

### River-pollution chain

`sewage + industrial effluent + agricultural runoff + solid waste -> altered chemistry/pathogens -> ecological and health damage`

### Restoration framework

`source reduction + sewerage/faecal-sludge management + industrial compliance + environmental flows + wetland/floodplain protection + basin governance + monitoring`

> **Trap:** Treatment capacity sanctioned or installed is not equal to sewage actually intercepted, treated to standard and sustainably operated.

## 14. Solid, plastic and e-waste

### Waste hierarchy

`prevent -> reduce -> reuse/repair -> recycle/compost -> recover -> safe disposal`

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Extended Producer Responsibility | Producer responsibility for post-consumer management under applicable rules |
| Material recovery | Sorting/recovering useful material |
| Refuse-derived fuel | Processed combustible waste fraction |
| Sanitary landfill | Engineered disposal for residual waste |
| Legacy waste | Old accumulated dumpsite waste |
| Circular economy | Retain product/material value and design out waste |

### EPR chain

`producer registration/obligation -> traceable collection/recycling -> certificate/verification where applicable -> audit and enforcement`

> **Trap:** Incineration is not automatically recycling, and source segregation is the foundation of effective downstream processing.

## 15. EIA and NGT

### EIA sequence

`screening -> scoping/terms -> baseline and impact prediction -> alternatives -> mitigation/EMP -> public consultation where applicable -> appraisal/decision -> post-clearance monitoring`

| Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Precautionary principle | Scientific uncertainty does not justify postponing proportionate prevention of serious harm |
| Polluter pays | Polluter bears prevention/remediation cost |
| Sustainable development | Integrates environment and development |
| Public-trust doctrine | State holds certain natural resources in trust |

### EIA-quality test

`independent baseline + cumulative impact + alternatives + seasonal data + meaningful participation + enforceable conditions + monitoring`

> **Trap:** Environmental clearance is conditional regulatory approval, not certification that a project has no environmental impact. NGT is a specialised statutory tribunal, not the regulator that grants every clearance.

## 16. Climate science

### Greenhouse mechanism

`solar shortwave enters -> Earth emits longwave infrared -> greenhouse gases absorb/re-emit -> lower atmosphere/surface warms`

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Weather | Short-term atmospheric condition |
| Climate | Long-term statistical pattern |
| Greenhouse effect | Natural warming process intensified by anthropogenic emissions |
| Radiative forcing | Change in energy balance from a driver |
| Feedback | Response that amplifies or dampens initial change |
| Carbon sink | Reservoir/process removing more carbon than it emits over period |
| Mitigation | Reduce emissions or enhance sinks |
| Adaptation | Reduce vulnerability/exposure or build resilience |
| Loss and damage | Adverse impacts beyond avoided/reduced adaptation framing |

### Main anthropogenic gases

`CO2 + CH4 + N2O + fluorinated gases`

> **Trap:** Water vapour is a major greenhouse gas and feedback, but anthropogenic warming is driven primarily by long-lived forcing emissions rather than direct water-vapour emissions.

## 17. IPCC architecture

| Body | Function |
|---|---|
| Working Group I | Physical science basis |
| Working Group II | Impacts, adaptation and vulnerability |
| Working Group III | Mitigation |
| Task Force on inventories | Greenhouse-gas inventory methodologies |
| Synthesis Report | Integrates assessment-cycle findings |

### Evidence rule

- IPCC assesses published knowledge; it does not conduct all underlying research itself;
- scenarios are conditional pathways, not predictions;
- confidence and likelihood language have defined assessment meanings;
- global findings should not be converted into India-specific numbers without Indian evidence.

## 18. UNFCCC, Kyoto and Paris

| Instrument | Distinctive architecture |
|---|---|
| UNFCCC | Framework convention with equity and CBDR-RC principles |
| Kyoto Protocol | Binding quantified commitments for specified developed parties under its design |
| Paris Agreement | Universal nationally determined contributions, transparency and progression cycle |

### Paris cycle

`NDC -> domestic implementation -> transparency reporting/review -> Global Stocktake -> stronger subsequent NDC`

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| CBDR-RC | Common but differentiated responsibilities and respective capabilities |
| NDC | Nationally determined climate contribution |
| Global Stocktake | Periodic collective assessment of progress |
| Climate finance | Finance supporting mitigation/adaptation under relevant architecture |
| Article 6 cooperation | Paris mechanisms for cooperative approaches and non-market cooperation |

> **Trap:** NDCs are nationally determined, but reporting, review and progression occur within an international framework.

## 20. Carbon markets, CCUS and removals

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Carbon tax | Explicit tax on emissions/carbon content |
| Emissions trading | Cap/obligation with tradable allowances/credits under scheme |
| Offset | Reduction/removal used to compensate emissions elsewhere under rules |
| Additionality | Outcome would not occur without credited intervention |
| Permanence | Stored carbon remains out of atmosphere for required duration |
| Leakage | Emissions shift outside project/system boundary |
| Double counting | Same reduction claimed more than once |

### CCUS chain

`capture -> compression -> transport -> utilisation or geological storage -> monitoring and liability`

### DAC distinction

Direct air capture removes CO2 from ambient air; point-source capture intercepts concentrated emissions before release.

> **Core verdict:** Carbon markets and CCUS may complement deep direct emission cuts; weak baselines, verification or permanence can undermine integrity.

## 22. Desertification and land degradation

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Land degradation | Decline in biological/economic productivity and ecosystem function |
| Desertification | Land degradation in arid, semi-arid and dry sub-humid areas |
| Drought | Prolonged deficient water availability under defined context |
| Land degradation neutrality | Balance intended between expected degradation and restoration/avoidance gains |

### Degradation chain

`vegetation removal + erosion + salinisation + overgrazing + unsustainable irrigation/mining + climate stress -> soil and productivity decline`

### Restoration hierarchy

`avoid degradation -> reduce ongoing degradation -> restore degraded land`

> **Trap:** Desertification does not mean literal expansion of an existing desert into every affected area.

## 25. Disaster risk and Sendai

### Risk relationship

`disaster risk = hazard x exposure x vulnerability`, moderated by capacity.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hazard | Potentially damaging event/process |
| Exposure | People/assets present in hazard zone |
| Vulnerability | Susceptibility to harm |
| Capacity | Strength/resources to manage risk |
| Resilience | Ability to resist, absorb, adapt and recover |
| Mitigation | Measures reducing long-term disaster risk |
| Preparedness | Readiness for response |

### Disaster cycle

`prevention/mitigation -> preparedness -> response -> recovery -> build back better`

### Sendai priorities

1. Understand disaster risk.
2. Strengthen disaster-risk governance.
3. Invest in resilience.
4. Enhance preparedness and build back better.

> **Core rule:** A natural hazard becomes a disaster through exposure and vulnerability; climate change can alter hazard probability/intensity but should not be casually attributed without evidence.

## 29. UPSC traps and safe corrections

| Trap | Safe correction |
|---|---|
| “All pyramids are upright.” | Energy always; biomass and numbers may invert. |
| “DD means low risk.” | It means insufficient evidence. |
| “Hotspot is a protected area.” | It is a scientific prioritisation category. |
| “Ramsar status prohibits all use.” | Wise use and domestic rules govern activity. |
| “IUCN category decides Indian schedule.” | Scientific and legal systems are separate. |
| “More tree cover proves natural-forest recovery.” | Check composition, quality and legal/ecological category. |
| “EIA prevents every impact.” | It predicts, compares, mitigates and conditions decisions. |
| “Installed treatment capacity means clean river.” | Check interception, utilisation, compliance and ecological outcome. |
| “Waste-to-energy means recycling.” | Recovery and recycling are distinct hierarchy stages. |
| “Every extreme event is caused by climate change.” | Use attribution evidence and vulnerability analysis. |
| “Paris imposes identical targets.” | NDCs are nationally determined within common framework. |
| “Carbon offset equals emission elimination.” | Test additionality, leakage, permanence and double counting. |
| “Renewable capacity equals renewable generation.” | Capacity, generation and grid integration differ. |
| “Designation/count equals conservation success.” | Measure ecological character, populations, connectivity and enforcement. |

## 30. Reusable Mains answer skeletons

### A. Ecosystem degradation

1. Define ecosystem and service.
2. Explain causal pressures and feedback.
3. Identify law/institution and governance gap.
4. Address community dependence and equity.
5. Recommend source reduction, landscape restoration and ecological indicators.

### B. Pollution

1. Map source-pathway-exposure-effect.
2. Separate point/non-point and emergency/structural measures.
3. Assess standards, monitoring, enforcement and local capacity.
4. Apply polluter-pays, technology and market tools with safeguards.
5. End with health/ecological outcome monitoring.

### C. Climate policy

1. State scientific mechanism.
2. Separate mitigation, adaptation and loss/damage.
3. Place India within equity/CBDR-RC and development needs.
4. Evaluate energy, finance, technology and just-transition constraints.
5. Recommend measurable sectoral implementation.

### D. Conservation law

1. Identify species/ecosystem and threat.
2. State exact domestic/international category.
3. Explain institution and enforcement chain.
4. Address rights, livelihoods and landscape connectivity.
5. Measure ecological outcome rather than designation.

### E. Technology/market instrument

1. Explain mechanism.
2. State suitable sector/use case.
3. Test measurement, additionality and lifecycle effects.
4. Identify cost, equity and lock-in risk.
5. Keep it complementary to direct prevention/reduction.

### F. Direct-demand checks before writing

| Demand family | Non-negotiable Core check |
|---|---|
| Industrial river pollution | Source → pathway → ETP/CETP/consent-monitoring → NMCG/Water Act; do not substitute riverfront work for effluent control. |
| Freshwater technologies | Give **three** technologies, each with mechanism, one merit and one demerit; distinguish water supply from aquifer restoration. |
| EIA NGOs/activists | Name four cases with the participation route and bounded legal/project outcome; do not label a forest-rights case as an ordinary EIA hearing. |
| Resilience/Sendai | Give the resilience elements and **seven targets A–G**, not only four Sendai priorities. |
| Urban floods | Causes + **two named cases with causal features** + NDMA/ULB/CWC–IMD frameworks. |
| Paris/COP26/NDC | Separate original NDC, Panchamrit announcement, 2022 updated NDC and dated installed-capacity evidence. |
| CCUS | Point-source avoidance versus DAC/BECCS removal; storage permanence and MRV are part of the answer. |
