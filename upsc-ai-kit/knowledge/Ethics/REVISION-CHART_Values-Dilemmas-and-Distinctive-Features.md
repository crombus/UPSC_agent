## 2. Universal theory-answer framework

### D-E-F-I-N-E

| Step | Use |
|---|---|
| **D**efine | Give a precise, non-circular definition |
| **E**xplain distinction | Separate it from the nearest related concept |
| **F**unction | Show how it changes conduct or institutions |
| **I**llustrate | Add a verified Indian administrative example |
| **N**uance | State a limit, conflict or misuse risk |
| **E**nd | Connect to constitutional morality, public interest or trust |

### Minimum scoring unit

`definition + distinction + mechanism + example + application`

## 3. Ethics and the human interface

| Concept | Exam-ready meaning |
|---|---|
| Ethics | Reasoned standards of right conduct |
| Morality | Social or personal beliefs about right and wrong |
| Values | Enduring standards guiding choices across situations |
| Norms | Shared expectations governing group conduct |
| Virtue | Stable excellence of character formed through practice |
| Ethical dilemma | Conflict between legitimate moral duties or values |

### Branches of ethics

| Branch | Question |
|---|---|
| Descriptive | What do people believe or practise? |
| Normative | What ought one to do? |
| Meta-ethics | What do moral claims mean and can they be true? |
| Applied | How do principles govern a concrete domain? |

### Determinants of ethical conduct

`individual character + family/socialisation + incentives + institutional culture + law + leadership + situation`

> **Trap:** Unethical conduct cannot be explained only as personal moral decline; incentives and institutions also shape behaviour.

Do not write biography. Use:

`person -> specific principle -> conduct mechanism -> administrative application -> limitation`

## 5. Attitude, behaviour and persuasion

### Attitude structure

```text
COGNITIVE: what I believe
      +
AFFECTIVE: what I feel
      +
BEHAVIOURAL: how I tend to act
      =
ATTITUDE TOWARD A SPECIFIC OBJECT
```

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| Value vs attitude | General standard vs object-specific evaluation |
| Attitude vs behaviour | Disposition vs actual act shaped also by context |
| Persuasion vs coercion | Voluntary attitude change vs compliance through force/threat |
| Compliance vs internalisation | External conformity vs genuine acceptance |

### Ethical persuasion

`credible source + reasoned message + audience fit + transparency + freedom to choose`

> **Core rule:** Behavioural change is durable when the desired norm is understood, trusted, socially reinforced and feasible.

## 6. Aptitude and foundational civil-service values

| Value | Administrative test |
|---|---|
| Integrity | Same ethical standard under pressure and in private |
| Impartiality | No improper favour based on identity or relationship |
| Non-partisanship | Professional service independent of party allegiance |
| Objectivity | Decision based on relevant evidence and criteria |
| Dedication | Sustained commitment to public service |
| Empathy | Understand another from their frame |
| Tolerance | Respect difference within legal limits |
| Compassion | Empathy translated into action relieving suffering |

### Distinctions

`sympathy = feeling for`  
`empathy = understanding from within`  
`compassion = understanding + commitment to help`

> **Trap:** Objectivity does not mean value-neutral indifference; evidence must operate within constitutional values and humane administration.

### EI cycle

`self-awareness -> self-regulation -> motivation -> empathy -> relationship management`

| Administrative setting | EI contribution |
|---|---|

## 10. Moral theories as decision tools

| Theory | Central question | Strength | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deontology | Is the act consistent with duty/right? | Protects dignity and constraints | Can become rigid under conflicting duties |
| Consequentialism | Which option produces best outcomes? | Attends to real-world effects | May sacrifice rights/minorities |
| Virtue ethics | What would a good/prudent person do? | Develops character and judgment | May give less precise action rule |
| Justice/fairness | Are benefits and burdens fairly distributed? | Corrects structural inequity | Competing standards of fairness |
| Care ethics | Are relationships and vulnerabilities respected? | Humanises abstract rules | Can become partial without safeguards |

### Triangulation

`legal threshold -> duty/right -> consequences -> virtue -> justice/care -> transparent justification`

> **Core rule:** Use theories as lenses, not labels. State how each changes the evaluation of the actual option.

## 11. Public-service ethics and dilemmas

### Public-office test

`legality + constitutional values + public interest + impartiality + accountability + compassion`

| Concept | Distinction |
|---|---|
| Ethical dilemma | Two legitimate moral pulls conflict |
| Legal violation | Settled law/rule is breached |
| Conflict of interest | Private interest could compromise duty |
| Corruption | Actual abuse of entrusted power for undue benefit |
| Propriety | Conduct appropriate to office and circumstances |

### Conflict-of-interest response

`identify -> disclose -> recuse where required -> record -> independent review`

> **Trap:** A conflict of interest is a condition to manage; concealment or biased action converts risk into misconduct.

## 12. Sources of ethical guidance

```text
CONSTITUTION
    |
LAW AND VALID RULES
    |
CODES / PROFESSIONAL NORMS
    |
REASONED CONSCIENCE
    |
ETHICAL THEORIES + PUBLIC INTEREST
```

| Source | Strength | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Law | Democratic authority and enforceability | May be incomplete or unjust |
| Rules | Predictability and consistency | Rigidity or rule worship |
| Conscience | Personal moral responsibility | Bias or self-serving intuition |
| Codes | Role-specific standards | Symbolism without enforcement |
| Constitutional morality | Rights, dignity and institutional restraint | Must not become subjective rhetoric |

> **Core rule:** Conscience can question an instruction, but a public servant must use lawful channels, reasoned dissent and documented escalation—not private arbitrariness.

## 13. Accountability and ethical governance

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Responsibility | Duty to perform a role or task |
| Answerability | Obligation to explain and justify |
| Enforcement | Capacity to impose consequences/correction |
| Accountability | Answerability plus an appropriate forum and consequences |

### Accountability ecosystem

`legislature + judiciary + audit + vigilance + information commissions + media + civil society + social audit + internal control`

### Ethical-governance loop

`clear standards -> transparent decision -> record -> scrutiny -> correction/sanction -> institutional learning`

> **Trap:** Transparency supplies information; it does not automatically create accountability unless someone can question, correct and enforce.

## 15. Emerging ethics: AI, data, biotechnology and environment

### Technology-ethics test

`purpose -> data/source -> consent -> bias -> explainability -> accountability -> human oversight -> remedy`

| Risk | Safeguard |
|---|---|
| Algorithmic bias | Representative data, testing and audit |
| Opacity | Explainability and reasoned decision |
| Surveillance | Legality, necessity and proportionality |
| Automation bias | Meaningful human review |
| Privacy loss | Purpose limitation and data minimisation |
| Responsibility gap | Named institutional owner and appeal mechanism |

### Environmental ethics

`intrinsic value + stewardship + intergenerational justice + polluter responsibility + precaution`

> **Core rule:** “Human in the loop” is meaningful only if the human has authority, competence, information and time to override the system.

## 16. Probity in governance

| Concept | Distinctive meaning |
|---|---|
| Honesty | Truthfulness in communication/disclosure |
| Integrity | Consistency between values and conduct |
| Probity | Demonstrable, procedurally verifiable rectitude in public office |

### Probity instruments

`asset disclosure + conflict rules + audit trail + open criteria + reasoned orders + RTI + independent review`

### Good-governance characteristics

`participatory + consensus-oriented + accountable + transparent + responsive + effective/efficient + equitable/inclusive + rule of law`

> **Trap:** Private moral confidence is not public probity. Public power must leave a record capable of external scrutiny.

## 17. Transparency, RTI and information sharing

### Disclosure spectrum

`proactive publication -> request-based access -> reasoned exemption -> independent appeal`

### Balance test

`public interest + privacy + security + fiduciary duty + ongoing process + proportionality`

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Transparency | Availability and intelligibility of information |
| Openness | Willingness to expose reasoning and receive scrutiny |
| Confidentiality | Legitimate restricted access for a defined purpose |
| Secrecy | Withholding; may be lawful or abusive depending on basis |

> **Dynamic-status caution:** RTI exemptions and their interaction with data-protection law can change; verify the operative text, commencement and judicial position.

## 18. Codes of ethics and conduct

| Code of Ethics | Code of Conduct |
|---|---|
| Broad values and aspirations | Specific dos, don'ts and procedures |
| Guides judgment | Regulates behaviour |
| Usually principle-oriented | Usually enforceable through discipline |
| Answers “what should guide us?” | Answers “what is permitted/prohibited?” |

### Effective-code chain

`consultation -> clarity -> training -> advice -> disclosure -> monitoring -> fair enforcement -> review`

> **Trap:** A code displayed on a wall does not create ethical culture; leadership, incentives, advice and consistent enforcement must support it.

| Citizen's Charter | Public statement of service commitments |
| Grievance redress | Mechanism for complaint and correction |

### Service-delivery ethic

`accessibility + timeliness + dignity + reliability + responsiveness + remedy`

> **Core rule:** Digitisation reduces discretion only when processes are redesigned, records are reliable and exclusion is addressed.

## 20. Public funds and corruption mechanisms

### Public-money test

`economy + efficiency + effectiveness + equity + ethics`

| Failure | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Misappropriation | Public money diverted for private use |
| Procurement corruption | Inflated price, collusion, kickback or substandard delivery |
| Squandering | Wasteful spending without identifiable private gain |
| Underutilisation | Allocated funds remain unspent or delayed |
| Diversion | Funds used outside authorised purpose |
| Discretion abuse | Allocation serves political/private interest |

### Procurement safeguards

`need specification -> open criteria -> competition -> conflict disclosure -> documented evaluation -> contract monitoring -> audit/remedy`

> **Trap:** Corruption is not only bribery; waste, favouritism, regulatory capture and deliberate underperformance can also violate public trust.

## 21. Corruption: legal and ethical architecture

### ARC diagnostic

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Coercive corruption | Citizen unwillingly pays for an entitlement |
| Collusive corruption | Giver and taker both benefit while public interest loses |

### Four-process distinction

| Process | Function |
|---|---|
| Criminal | Prosecution and punishment under penal law |
| Departmental | Service discipline and administrative penalty |
| Civil | Recovery/compensation and related liability |
| Vigilance | Screening, prevention and routing of misconduct concerns |

### Status discipline

`recommendation != enactment != rules != commencement != operational enforcement`

> **Core rule:** Do not quote a historical ARC recommendation as present law. Verify current provisions, amendments, judgments and commencement.

## 22. Anti-corruption institutions and honest-official protection

| Institution/process | Ethics-specific role |
|---|---|
| CVC | Vigilance advice/superintendence within statutory remit |
| CBI | Investigation under its legal jurisdiction |
| Lokpal/Lokayukta | High-level ombudsman architecture within applicable law |
| Departmental vigilance | Prevention, detection and disciplinary routing |
| Audit | Financial/procedural scrutiny |
| Whistleblowing channel | Protected disclosure and follow-up |

### Honest-decision protection

`record reasons + disclose conflict + consult law/rules + obtain written instruction + document dissent + proportionate decision + independent review`

### Balance

`protect bona fide judgment` **without** `creating impunity for mala fide conduct`

> **Trap:** Prior approval or sanction safeguards are not declarations of innocence; nor should vigilance fear paralyse legitimate discretion.

## 23. Section-B case-study architecture

### Six steps

```text
1. FACTS AND CENTRAL DILEMMA
2. STAKEHOLDERS + ETHICAL ISSUES + HARD CONSTRAINTS
3. REALISTIC OPTIONS, INCLUDING HYBRID/SEQUENCED OPTIONS
4. EVALUATE EACH OPTION
5. DECIDE AND JUSTIFY
6. IMPLEMENT, COMMUNICATE, MITIGATE AND REVIEW
```

### Six checks

| Check | Question |
|---|---|
| Conflict of interest | Is private interest influencing duty? |
| Legality | Is the option within law, authority and procedure? |
| Public interest | Who benefits and who bears the burden? |
| Proportionality | Is response calibrated to the harm? |
| Transparency | Can the decision and reasons survive scrutiny? |
| Compassion + long term | Are dignity and future consequences protected? |

### Option table

| Option | Merit | Risk | Ethical lens | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Immediate gain | Duty/right/institutional cost | Name theory | Safeguard |
| B | Rule certainty | Human/consequence cost | Name theory | Safeguard |
| C/hybrid | Balances legitimate claims | Execution complexity | Practical wisdom | Written plan |

> **Core rule:** Never jump directly to “I will do X.” Compare options, state constraints and finish with an implementable administrative plan.

## 26. UPSC traps and safe corrections

| Trap | Safe correction |
|---|---|
| “Ethics equals legality.” | Law is the minimum enforceable floor; ethics also tests justice, dignity and public interest. |
| “Conscience overrides rules.” | Use reasoned dissent and lawful escalation, not private arbitrariness. |
| “Impartiality means ignoring disadvantage.” | Apply fair criteria while recognising relevant vulnerability. |
| “Empathy requires agreeing.” | Understand another's frame without surrendering judgment. |
| “Good intention makes an action ethical.” | Examine means, rights, consequences and institutional precedent. |
| “Public interest justifies any method.” | Means must remain lawful, proportionate and rights-respecting. |
| “Conflict of interest proves corruption.” | It requires disclosure/management; abuse creates misconduct. |
| “Transparency automatically ensures accountability.” | Enforcement and remedy are also necessary. |
| “Technology removes corruption.” | It may relocate discretion or create opacity/exclusion. |
| “More severe punishment eliminates corruption.” | Detection certainty, systems and culture also matter. |
| “ARC recommendation is current law.” | Verify enactment, commencement and operation separately. |
| “Case studies reward heroic sacrifice.” | Prefer sustainable, lawful and institutionally responsible action. |

2. Identify the value/theory.
3. Give personal, social and administrative dimensions.

5. Conclude with practical application.

### C. Thinker

1. State principle without biography.
2. Explain moral logic.
3. Translate into public administration.
4. Give example.

2. Diagnose individual + institutional causes.
