### Q1 — 10 + 10

**Q1(a) [10] ✅** — *"Owing to paucity of time, a university professor generates a Ph.D. evaluation
report using Artificial Intelligence and submits it with some modifications. Discuss this from the
perspective of accountability and integrity."*

- **Primary owner:** [`basic/13_Emerging-Ethics-Technology-AI-and-Environment.md`](basic/13_Emerging-Ethics-Technology-AI-and-Environment.md)
  — professional and academic integrity in AI-assisted work.
- **Mandatory cross-link:** [`basic/11_Accountability-and-Ethical-Governance.md`](basic/11_Accountability-and-Ethical-Governance.md)
  **§2A — "Accountability under AI assistance: the six-element test applied to non-human agents."**
  `11` supplies the accountability grammar the answer is built on: a named actor, forum, standard,
  explanation, judgment and possible correction — **none of which a generative model can supply**,
  so accountability is never transferred, only retained, diffused or concealed.
- **Demand decode:** the directive is *Discuss*, and the paper names **two** evaluative registers,
  so the answer must separate **two distinct wrongs**: (i) delegation of a **non-delegable evaluative
  judgment** (accountability), and (ii) **misrepresentation** of machine output as the examiner's own
  scholarly assessment (integrity). The second stands even if the report is substantively correct.
- **Must contain:** the responsibility/accountability distinction; the delegable-task vs
  non-delegable-duty test; automation bias defeating the "I modified it" defence; ICMJE's fourth
  authorship criterion — **agreement to be accountable for all aspects of the work**; UNESCO's
  *Guidance for Generative AI in Education and Research* (2023) on non-cession of human
  accountability; the institution's academic-integrity machinery; a concession that the time-scarcity
  pressure is real, with the honest responses (decline, extension, workload reform).
- **Do not:** ⚠️ answer this as a **bias/algorithmic-fairness** question — that is a different failure
  mode. ❌ Do not assert a binding national UGC instrument on generative-AI use; none is verified in
  this repository. ⚠️ Cite the UGC (Academic Integrity and Prevention of Plagiarism) Regulations,
  **2018** for the DAIP/IAIP institutional machinery only, and note that they address
  plagiarism/similarity rather than undisclosed generative-AI use.
- **Current affairs:** ⚠️ illustrative only. The concept-essential core is the non-delegability and
  authorship doctrine; AI-governance news is decoration.

**Q1(b) [10] ✅** — *"A business house is working on a project that could displace a forest community
from their habitat. As the administrative officer of that district, what ethical challenges are you
likely to face?"*

- **Primary owner:** [`basic/13_Emerging-Ethics-Technology-AI-and-Environment.md`](basic/13_Emerging-Ethics-Technology-AI-and-Environment.md)
  — environmental ethics, extended for displacement, consent and forest-community rights.
- **Supporting:** [`basic/09_Public-Service-Values-Status-and-Ethical-Dilemmas.md`](basic/09_Public-Service-Values-Status-and-Ethical-Dilemmas.md)
  §3 (dilemma-diagnosis anatomy); [`basic/22_Case-Study-Method-and-Answer-Architecture.md`](basic/22_Case-Study-Method-and-Answer-Architecture.md)
  for the weighted-checks vocabulary.
- **Demand decode:** the directive asks for **challenges**, not a decision. The answer is a
  **taxonomy of conflicts**, closed by the officer's own deliverable — *process integrity*, not the
  project's outcome.
- **Must contain:** developmental utilitarianism vs rights-as-trumps; **procedural** justice

### Q3 — 10 + 10 + 10 ⚠️ *three sub-parts*

**Q3(a) [10] ✅** — *"Dr. B. R. Ambedkar had cautioned that employing civil disobedience within a
constitutional democracy equates to endorsing 'anarchy'. How should modern democracies navigate the
ethical dilemmas posed by civil disobedience movements that aim to promote social justice against
policies that may raise genuine concerns?"*

- **Primary owner:** [`basic/06_Indian-Moral-Thinkers-and-Philosophers.md`](basic/06_Indian-Moral-Thinkers-and-Philosophers.md)
  **§10A — "Constitutional methods vs civil disobedience: Ambedkar's conditional test"** — *added in
  this remediation pass.*
- **Supporting:** §10 (constitutional morality, equality, fraternity) in the same file;
  [`basic/07_Western-Moral-Philosophers-and-Thinkers.md`](basic/07_Western-Moral-Philosophers-and-Thinkers.md)
  §3(7) for Rawls; [`basic/10_Sources-of-Ethical-Guidance-Laws-Rules-Conscience.md`](basic/10_Sources-of-Ethical-Guidance-Laws-Rules-Conscience.md)
  and `advanced/10` for the distinction from a public servant's institutional dissent.
- **Demand decode:** the highest-scoring move is to **correct the premise**. Ambedkar's claim is
  **conditional** — he expressly concedes "a great deal of justification" for unconstitutional methods
  where constitutional methods are **closed**, and condemns them only where those methods are
  **open**. The ethical question therefore becomes: *are the constitutional channels genuinely open,
  effective and accessible for this grievance and these people?*
- **Must contain:** the 25 November 1949 Constituent Assembly speech and the phrase "Grammar of
  Anarchy" with its conditional structure; the definitional elements of civil disobedience
  (lawbreaking, direct vs indirect, publicity, non-violence, conscientiousness, communicative appeal);
  **Rawls's three conditions** (clear substantial injustice; good-faith exhaustion of normal political
  appeals; not so widespread as to impair constitutional efficacy) and acceptance of legal
  consequences as fidelity to law; **King's four steps** and the acceptance of the penalty; the
  democracy's **dual duty** — the state's burden to keep channels genuinely open, and the movement's
  burden of good faith, non-violence and proportionate disruption; and the administrative posture
  (facilitate lawful protest, least-restrictive proportionate policing, a live negotiation channel,
  recorded reasons, prosecute violence not dissent, decide the policy on merits).
- **Do not:** ❌ present the caution as an unconditional ban; ❌ equate civil disobedience with a
  serving official's dissent, which must run through recorded internal channels; ❌ write a pure
  Article 19 answer. ⚠️ **Provenance caution:** verify long verbatim stretches of the speech against
  CAD Vol. XI (25 Nov 1949) or *Writings and Speeches* before reproducing them at length.
- **Current affairs:** ⚠️ illustrative only.

**Q3(c) [10] ✅** — *"Shakespeare in Henry IV says, 'The arms are fair when the intent of bearing them
is just'. What ethical implications does this have in governance?"*

- **Primary owner:** [`basic/12_Corporate-Governance-and-International-Ethics.md`](basic/12_Corporate-Governance-and-International-Ethics.md)
  **§11A — "Right intention, legitimate authority, last resort and double effect"**, extending the
  existing §11 just-war register.
- **Supporting:** [`basic/08_Moral-Theories-Deontology-Consequentialism-Virtue-Ethics.md`](basic/08_Moral-Theories-Deontology-Consequentialism-Virtue-Ethics.md)
  (intention vs consequence; the three-theory triangulation);
  [`basic/07_Western-Moral-Philosophers-and-Thinkers.md`](basic/07_Western-Moral-Philosophers-and-Thinkers.md)
  §3(4) (Kant on the law/ethics gap — intention alone cannot legalise an act);
  [`basic/16_Codes-of-Ethics-and-Codes-of-Conduct.md`](basic/16_Codes-of-Ethics-and-Codes-of-Conduct.md)
  (proportionality factors).
- **Demand decode:** the claim is that just intention makes the resort to force *fair*. The
  answer must show intention is a **necessary but not sufficient** condition, and supply the
  externally testable criteria that must accompany it: legitimate authority, just cause, last resort,
  reasonable prospect of success, proportionality, discrimination, and accountability for the use of
  force.
- **⚠️ Attribution caution — record this in the owner file.** The line is from ***Henry IV, Part 1*,
  Act 5, Scene 2**, and it is spoken by **Hotspur, a rebel**, immediately before the Battle of
  Shrewsbury, which he loses. The paper says only "Henry IV". ⚠️ The dramatic context **inverts** the
  apparent moral: a self-certifying insurgent is exactly the case that shows *self-declared* just
  intent cannot validate the resort to force. A strong answer uses this as its decisive
  counter-example — **intention must be externally testable, or "just intent" becomes a licence**.
  ⚠️ Attribute as "Henry IV Part 1, V.ii (spoken by Hotspur)" and note that the paper's citation is
  unqualified.
- **Must contain:** the governance transposition, which is what the question actually wants — force
  in policing and regulation must pass necessity, minimum force, recorded reasons, reporting and
  independent review; the doctrine of double effect as the bridge between intent and effect; and the
  point that good intent without institutional verification is the oldest alibi of power.
- **Current affairs:** ⚠️ illustrative only.

### Q4 — 10 + 10

**Q4(a) [10] ✅** — *"Citing the serious adverse effects of long-term medication, a doctor convinces
the family of a female patient for surgery. This led the patient to reluctantly consent for the
surgery, though her original choice was to opt for medication. Explain the concepts of paternalism
and beneficence by analysing the doctor's action."*

- **Primary owner:** [`basic/08_Moral-Theories-Deontology-Consequentialism-Virtue-Ethics.md`](basic/08_Moral-Theories-Deontology-Consequentialism-Virtue-Ethics.md)
  **§12B — "Applied ethics: autonomy, beneficence and paternalism"** — *added in this remediation
  pass; this was previously a total doctrinal gap across the Ethics corpus.*
- **Supporting:** [`basic/07_Western-Moral-Philosophers-and-Thinkers.md`](basic/07_Western-Moral-Philosophers-and-Thinkers.md)
  §2 (autonomy, beneficence, paternalism, harm principle definitions) and §3(5) (Mill's harm
  principle promoted into the Basic owner); [`basic/09_Public-Service-Values-Status-and-Ethical-Dilemmas.md`](basic/09_Public-Service-Values-Status-and-Ethical-Dilemmas.md)
  §11 (coercion vs undue influence — the voluntariness analysis).
- **Demand decode:** the directive is **"Explain … by analysing"**, so the two concepts must be
  defined *and* then applied to the doctor's specific act. The pivot is that beneficence and
  paternalism **share the same motive** and differ at exactly one point — **whether autonomy is
  respected or overridden**.
- **Must contain:** the four principles (autonomy, non-maleficence, beneficence, justice) as
  *prima facie* principles requiring specification and balancing; **Dworkin's three conditions** for
  paternalism, applied to the facts; the **soft/hard** distinction, with the classification turning on
  whether the patient's original preference was competent, informed and voluntary; the persuasion /
  manipulation / coercion / **undue influence** ladder; why **family-mediated** persuasion converts
  support into substitution for a competent adult; "reluctant consent" as a diagnostic flag; the
  Indian anchor ***Samira Kohli v. Dr. Prabha Manchanda* (2008) 2 SCC 1**; and the ethical alternative
  (direct disclosure of alternatives, decision aid, time, second opinion, documented consent, family
  in a supporting role).
- **⚠️ Cross-link boundary — *Samira Kohli*.** That case concerned consent **never given** by the
  patient (the mother signed while the patient was under anaesthesia). Here the patient **does**
  consent, though reluctantly. Use the case for **two propositions only**: (i) the competent adult,
  not the family, is the locus of real consent, and (ii) consent must be voluntary and informed about
  **alternatives**. ❌ It is *not* authority that reluctant consent is automatically invalid;
  voluntariness must be argued separately on the facts.
- **Do not:** ⚠️ treat all paternalism as wrong (soft paternalism is generally defensible);
  ⚠️ condemn persuasion as such (persuading the *patient* is part of the duty); ⚠️ recite the four
  principles without balancing them.
- **Current affairs:** ⚠️ not required; purely static doctrine.
