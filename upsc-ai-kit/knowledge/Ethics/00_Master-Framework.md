# GS-IV Ethics - Master Conceptual Framework

> **Purpose:** Connect every Ethics topic before studying the detailed files.
> **Core question:** How does a public servant convert personal values, institutional rules and
> constitutional morality into decisions that are lawful, impartial, transparent and in the
> public interest — especially when these pulls conflict?

**Core proposition:** ✅ ARC 1.5 — "values need to be sustained by institutions to be durable" and
"[v]alues without institutional support will soon be weakened and dissipated"; institutions
"provide the container, which gives shape and content to values." ⚠️ GS-IV therefore always tests
the *combination*: a value/attitude/thinker's idea (the 2024 and 2025 Mains papers each set three
quotation questions — Vivekananda, Sardar Patel and Kant in 2024; Thiruvalluvar, William James and
Vivekananda in 2025 — see `02`, `07`) applied through an institutional/legal lens (Nolan principles,
codes, RTI, PC Act — see `09`, `15`, `16`, `19`).

## 3. The ARC's central diagnostic (dated 2007, still the exam's reasoning spine)

- ✅ ARC 1.4: two contrary approaches to corruption — an "overemphasis on values and character"
  that laments the "decline in values", versus the belief that most people are "fundamentally
  decent" and only a small deviant proportion needs consistent reward-and-punishment. ARC's own
  position (1.5): **both values and institutions matter.**
- ✅ ARC 1.13: **coercive corruption** (citizen is an "unwilling victim" of extortion for a service
  to which he is entitled) vs **collusive corruption** (bribe-giver and bribe-taker "both parties
  benefit at immense cost to society") — the single most tested distinction in the corruption-law
  space (see `19`). ARC adds the trend claim: as the economy is freed from state controls,
  extortionary corruption declines and collusive corruption tends to increase.
- ✅ ARC 1.9: over-centralisation means "the more remotely power is exercised from the people, the
  greater is the distance between authority and accountability."
- ✅ ARC 1.12: quality of politics is "perhaps the most important determinant" of societal
  integrity — corruption migrates to sectors where "competition cannot be introduced and the state
  exercises a natural monopoly."
- ⚠️ Exam application: almost every GS-IV theory question (why do good people act corruptly?
  why do systems fail honest officials?) can be answered by combining this values-vs-incentives
  diagnostic with a concrete Indian institutional example.

## 4. Recurring conceptual distinctions (exam traps live here)

| Distinction | Core difference | File |
|---|---|---|
| Descriptive vs normative vs meta- vs applied ethics | Descriptive = what people *do* believe (empirical); normative = what people *ought* to do (deontology/consequentialism/virtue); meta-ethics = what moral words *mean* and whether moral claims can be true; applied = the normative theory worked out for a concrete domain (bio-, environmental, AI, administrative ethics) | 01, 08, 13 |
| Legality vs morality vs propriety | Legality = conformity to enacted law; morality = conformity to a standard of right conduct; propriety = conformity to what the office and occasion make *appropriate* — an act can be legal and moral yet improper (a collector accepting a lavish private dinner from a bidder) | 09, 10, 14 |
| Code of Ethics vs Code of Conduct | Ethics = broad guiding principles; Conduct = precise list of dos/don'ts with sanctions | 09, 16 |
| Ethical dilemma vs legal violation | Dilemma = conflict between two legitimate duties/values; violation = breach of a settled rule/law | 09, 22 |
| Empathy vs sympathy vs compassion | Empathy = accurately grasping another's state from *their* frame; sympathy = feeling *for* them from outside; compassion = empathy plus a motivated commitment to relieve the suffering | 04, 05 |
| Attitude vs value vs aptitude | Attitude = evaluative stance toward a *specific* object; value = general, cross-situational standard; aptitude = capacity/disposition to perform the role well | 03, 04 |
| Conflict of interest vs corruption | Conflict of interest is a *situation* (a private interest that could compromise duty) and is not itself wrongdoing — failure to disclose/recuse is; corruption requires an actual abuse of office, in the PC Act sense an undue advantage | 09, 14, 19 |
| Means vs end; motive vs intention vs consequence | Gandhi/Kant judge the means and the maxim; utilitarians judge the end/consequence. Motive = *why* the agent wanted the act; intention = *what* the agent chose to bring about; consequence = what actually followed (and may diverge from both — see "moral luck") | 06, 07, 08 |
| Coercive vs collusive bribery | Coercive = citizen-victim under extortion; collusive = both parties gain, public/state loses | 19 |
| Accountability vs responsibility | Responsibility = duty owed going forward; accountability = answerability after the fact, to a specific forum | 11 |
| Corporate ethics vs CSR | Ethics = how business is done (means); CSR = discretionary/statutory giving-back (often ends-focused spend) | 12 |
| Probity vs integrity vs honesty | Honesty = truthfulness; integrity = consistency of values in action; probity = demonstrable, procedurally verifiable rectitude in public office | 14 |
| Civil vs criminal vs departmental vs vigilance process | Civil = recovery/compensation on the balance of probabilities; criminal = PC Act/BNS prosecution before a Special Judge, proof beyond reasonable doubt, sanction under s.19 and prior approval under s.17A; departmental = disciplinary proceedings under service/conduct rules, penalty up to dismissal, standard = preponderance of probability; vigilance = the *pre-decisional* screening (is there a "vigilance angle"?) that routes a case into one of the other three. All four can run on the same facts, and acquittal in one does not automatically end the others | 19, 20, 21 |
| CVC/CBI/Lokpal jurisdiction | CVC advises/superintends; CBI investigates; Lokpal has overarching jurisdiction over Ministers, MPs and specified officials | 11, 20 |
| ARC recommendation vs enacted law | ARC 2007 recommended a Lokpal, whistleblower law, collusive-bribery offence; each has a distinct, separately dated legislative history — and enactment, rules, commencement and operation are four separate facts | throughout |

## 5. Answer-writing spine used across every Mains-angle section

1. **Define/anchor** the core concept precisely (definition-first, not story-first).
2. **Locate the tension** — which two legitimate values, duties or institutions are pulling apart.
3. **Apply a named framework** — a thinker, moral theory or Nolan-type principle, explicitly.
4. **Ground in Indian administration** — a real, source-grounded example, case or provision.
5. **Resolve/recommend** — a decision or reform that is legal, proportionate, transparent and
   protects long-term public interest, not just the immediate optics.
6. **State the safeguard** — how misuse of your own recommendation would itself be checked.
