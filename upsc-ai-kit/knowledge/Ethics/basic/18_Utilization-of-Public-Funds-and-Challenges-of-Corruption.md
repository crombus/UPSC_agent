## 1. Public money as fiduciary trust: concepts and classifications

```text
CITIZENS / PUBLIC REVENUE
          |
          v
LEGISLATIVE AUTHORISATION -> PURPOSE + AMOUNT + CONDITIONS
          |
          v
EXECUTIVE SANCTION -> LIMITED DELEGATED POWER
          |
          v
IMPLEMENTATION -> PROCUREMENT -> DELIVERY -> PAYMENT
          |
          v
VERIFIED OUTPUT / OUTCOME -> AUDIT -> REMEDY -> PUBLIC TRUST
```

The ethical starting point is **fiduciary stewardship**. A public official does not own the money
controlled by a department. Authority is received for a defined public purpose and must be
exercised for intended beneficiaries under law, reason, fairness and review.

### Five connected duties

1. **Fidelity to purpose:** do not substitute personal, partisan or organisational convenience.
2. **Prudence:** avoid foreseeable waste and whole-life cost.
3. **Impartiality:** do not prefer a connected person or group.
4. **Accountability:** preserve records, reasons and a responsible decision-maker.
5. **Restoration:** correct the loss or denied service, not merely punish an offender.

### Allocation is not utilisation

| Stage | What it proves | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| Allocation | legislature/budget has authorised an amount | that money is available or needed immediately |
| Release | funds have become available to spend | that procurement or delivery occurred |
| Expenditure | a recorded payment or liability arose | that goods, works or services were genuine |
| Output | an immediate deliverable exists | that the social objective improved |
| Outcome | intended condition changed | that the change was economical or equitable |

> **Exam line:** expenditure is an accounting event; ethical utilisation is a public-value chain.

### Classify before assigning blame

| Category | Precise meaning | Example | Primary response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Error | inadvertent mistake | wrong budget code | correction and training |
| Irregularity | departure from an applicable rule | missing required approval | regularise if lawful; fix control |
| Waste/squandering | avoidable public loss; private gain unnecessary | ostentatious furnishing | value-for-money review |
| Negligence | failure of due care | no receipt verification | assess gravity and capacity |
| Fraud | intentional deception | fictitious invoice or beneficiary | preserve evidence; investigate |
| Abuse of discretion | entrusted choice used for irrelevant purpose | arbitrary vendor preference | reasons, conflict and review |
| Corruption | public power used for improper connected advantage | kickback or partisan diversion | competent disciplinary/criminal route |

⚠️ Categories overlap. A false measurement used to pay a connected contractor may be irregular,
fraudulent, wasteful and corrupt. Conversely, every irregularity is not corruption.

✅ The Second ARC separately identified squandering of public money as harmful even without
misappropriation to an individual: the exchequer, public interest and trust can suffer without a
personal cash gain.

## 2. Budget to outcome: economy, efficiency, effectiveness and equity

```text
NEED -> PLAN -> BUDGET -> RELEASE -> INPUT -> OUTPUT -> ACCESS -> OUTCOME
  |       |        |         |         |        |        |         |
data   realism  priority   timing   economy efficiency equity effectiveness
```

✅ CAG defines performance audit around **economy, efficiency and effectiveness** and links it to
value for money, accountability and transparency. ⚠️ **Equity** is the ethical fourth test: it
asks who receives benefit, who bears cost and who remains excluded.

| Test | Question | Common trap |
|---|---|---|
| Economy | Were suitable inputs obtained at the lowest defensible whole-life cost? | equating cheapest price with economy |
| Efficiency | Did resources produce the greatest suitable output, or the output with fewer resources? | increasing disposal by sacrificing quality or access |
| Effectiveness | Did the programme achieve its intended objective or outcome? | treating outputs as outcomes |
| Equity | Were benefits and burdens distributed fairly, considering unequal need and access? | treating identical expenditure as equal justice |

### Worked illustration

A district buys medical devices and spends its full grant:

- it fails **economy** if maintenance and consumables make the devices unaffordable;
- it fails **efficiency** if trained staff or power are unavailable and devices remain idle;
- it fails **effectiveness** if the intended health outcome does not improve;
- it fails **equity** if remote facilities receive no usable access.

⚠️ The four dimensions can conflict. Additional outreach to a tribal region may raise cost per
beneficiary but improve equity and long-term effectiveness. A strong answer names the trade-off
and demands recorded reasons rather than asserting one universal metric.

### Under-utilisation

Common causes:

- unrealistic or inflated budgeting;
- late release or short working season;
- weak project design, land readiness or approvals;
- shortage of procurement, engineering or financial capacity;
- fragmented authority and unclear ownership;
- litigation or genuine change in need;
- fear of audit or punishment causing decision paralysis;
- failure to resolve grievances or local implementation barriers.

### Mis-utilisation

Common causes:

- expenditure outside the authorised purpose;
- procurement tailored to a connected vendor;
- false beneficiaries, attendance, measurement or completion;
- collusion, kickback or inflated variation;
- politically timed or partisan diversion;
- weak receipt, asset and utilisation verification;
- rush-of-expenditure behaviour that sacrifices value.

⚠️ Do not recommend “spend faster” as the cure for every low utilisation ratio. First diagnose
whether the bottleneck is planning, authority, capacity, legality or changed public need.

## 3. Financial propriety, delegated authority and the control chain

📰 The Department of Expenditure hosts the General Financial Rules, 2017 updated up to
**31 January 2026**. The GFR is the Union framework; States, public-sector undertakings and
autonomous bodies may have their own applicable rules.

### Public-propriety tests

An officer dealing with public money should ask:

1. Is the purpose authorised?
2. Is the expenditure necessary and proportionate?
3. Would a prudent person spend similarly from their own money while remembering that public
   distribution and rights impose stricter duties?
4. Does the decision directly or indirectly benefit the decision-maker or a connected person?
5. Has avoidable extravagance been prevented?
6. Is the competent authority approving the total genuine requirement?
7. Can an auditor reconstruct need, alternatives, sanction, receipt and payment?

### Delegated financial power

```text
TOTAL GENUINE REQUIREMENT
          |
          v
APPLICABLE CATEGORY + VALUE
          |
          v
COMPETENT AUTHORITY
          |
          v
PROCUREMENT METHOD + REVIEW + RECORD
```

Delegation is an allocation of responsibility, not an inconvenience threshold. An officer cannot
create competence by renaming, fragmenting or informally shifting one genuine requirement.

### GFR Rule 157 precision

✅ Rule 157, “Avoiding Piecemeal Purchases,” states the anti-splitting rule for **goods**: a demand
for goods should not be divided into small quantities to avoid the higher sanction required with
reference to the estimated value of the total demand.

⚠️ Splitting a works or services requirement to evade sanction or competition is the same
propriety failure, but the answer must cite the applicable works/services and delegation framework.
Do not falsely present Rule 157's goods wording as a verbatim all-procurement rule.

### Segregation and reconciliation

| Function | Why separation matters |
|---|---|
| identify need/specification | prevents buyer from inventing demand for a chosen seller |
| create/approve vendor | reduces fictitious or connected vendors |
| evaluate bid | protects fair competition |
| certify receipt/measurement | tests actual delivery |
| authorise payment | prevents self-certification |
| reconcile bank, ledger and asset | exposes duplicate, missing or diverted entries |

Small offices may not achieve perfect separation. Use proportionate compensating controls:
supervisory review, rotation, sample verification, direct confirmation, immutable logs and
post-payment reconciliation.

### Re-appropriation

Re-appropriation is a budget transfer authorised within the governing framework. Ethical review
still asks:

- Who loses or waits?
- Is the original purpose still urgent?
- Are there less harmful financing options?
- Is the transfer transparent to the legislature?
- Are contractual and opportunity costs compared?
- Is the decision being driven by an election or connected interest?

Formal legality is a floor, not a complete distributive justification.

## 4. Procurement and contract integrity across the lifecycle

```text
NEED -> ESTIMATE -> SPECIFICATION -> TENDER -> EVALUATION -> AWARD
  -> DELIVERY -> VARIATION -> PAYMENT -> USE / WARRANTY -> DISPOSAL
```

Procurement corruption is not confined to opening bids. A fair award can be reversed by false
measurement, inferior supply, unjustified extension or inflated variation.

### Risk-and-control matrix

| Lifecycle stage | Corruption/waste risk | Integrity control |
|---|---|---|
| Need | invented or oversized requirement | demand plan, affordability and outcome case |
| Estimate | inflated benchmark | independent market/technical estimate |
| Specification | brand or vendor tailoring | functional, neutral, reviewed specification |
| Tender | artificial urgency or restricted access | proportionate publicity and adequate time |
| Evaluation | changed criteria, leakage, conflict | criteria fixed beforehand, declarations, committee reasons |
| Award | arbitrary negotiation or favour | speaking record and challenge route |
| Delivery | inferior quantity/quality | independent inspection, testing and measurement |
| Variation | contract value expanded after competition | predefined authority and reasoned change control |
| Payment | duplicate, premature or false payment | three-way match and milestone verification |
| Use | idle or unusable asset | utilisation, maintenance and outcome review |
| Disposal | connected undervaluation | transparent valuation and approval |

### Conflict of interest

A conflict exists when a private or connected interest could improperly influence, or reasonably
appear to influence, public duty. It is a risk condition, not automatic proof of corruption.

```text
IDENTIFY -> DISCLOSE -> INDEPENDENT MATERIALITY TEST
        -> RESTRICT / RECUSE / REASSIGN / DIVEST
        -> RECORD -> REVIEW
```

Disclosure alone does not cure a material conflict. A procurement chair whose sibling controls a
bidder should not cast the deciding vote merely after announcing the relationship.

### Collusion and cartel red flags

- regular bid rotation among the same firms;
- losing “cover bids” predictably above the winner;
- identical unusual wording, errors, metadata or addresses;
- coordinated withdrawals or territorial allocation;
- common beneficial ownership or connected directors;
- repeated single bids after restrictive conditions;
- unexplained concentration of awards;
- subcontracting back to an apparent competitor.

Red flags justify review, not automatic guilt. Use market analysis, ownership checks, secure
evidence and a fair competent process.

### Lowest price trap

The lowest quoted price can be a relevant criterion, but value for money includes fitness,
quality, reliability, delivery, maintenance, disposal and competition. Conversely, “quality”
cannot become a slogan permitting arbitrary preference.

## 5. Leakage, diversion, bribery and abuse of discretion

### Leakage map

```text
ALLOCATED FUND
   |
   +--> idle balance / delay ----------------------> UNDER-UTILISATION
   +--> unauthorised head / partisan redirection --> DIVERSION
   +--> duplicate / ghost / false measurement ----> FRAUD
   +--> inflated input / idle asset --------------> WASTE
   +--> bribe / favour / connected vendor --------> CORRUPTION
```

### Coercive and collusive bribery

| Dimension | Coercive bribery | Collusive bribery |
|---|---|---|
| Relationship | official extorts a person seeking lawful service | giver and taker cooperate for improper advantage |
| Example | payment demanded for a due certificate | contractor pays for false quality certification |
| Immediate victim | service-seeker | State, competitors, users and public |
| Response emphasis | protect complainant, deliver service, preserve evidence | investigate both sides and underlying transaction |

✅ The coercive/collusive distinction is an analytical distinction used by the Second ARC. Topic 19
owns the exact Prevention of Corruption Act treatment and current statutory ingredients.

### Abuse by omission

Wilful non-performance can produce corrupt advantage:

- deliberately delaying entitlement to extract payment;
- ignoring known overbilling by a connected contractor;
- failing to enforce penalties after political instruction;
- keeping inspection dormant so illegal activity continues.

⚠️ Every poor outcome is not corruption. Test clear duty, knowledge, capacity, wilfulness or gross
negligence, foreseeable harm, improper advantage and concealed reasons. Protect good-faith judgment
and distinguish capacity failure from corrupt omission.

### Squandering

Wasteful public expenditure can be ethically serious without bribery:

- prestige construction unrelated to functional need;
- avoidable luxury travel or furnishing;
- equipment bought without staff, maintenance or demand;
- hurried year-end purchasing merely to exhaust allocation.

The harm includes opportunity cost: every avoidable rupee can displace maintenance, welfare,
safety or debt reduction.

## 6. Conflicts, collusion, capture and the political-administrative-business nexus

Corruption may rise through levels:

```text
ONE CONFLICTED DECISION
        |
REPEATED FAVOUR / DEPENDENCE
        |
REGULATORY CAPTURE
        |
POLITICAL-ADMINISTRATIVE-BUSINESS NEXUS
        |
STATE CAPTURE: RULES + APPOINTMENTS + ENFORCEMENT RESHAPED
```

### Regulatory capture

Capture occurs when a regulator persistently serves the regulated interest instead of statutory
public purpose. Mechanisms include:

- dependence on industry for information and expertise;
- exclusive access for dominant firms;
- revolving-door expectations;
- budget or appointment dependence;
- cultural identification with the regulated sector;
- selective consultation and weak independent research.

No envelope of cash is required. The evidence lies in patterns of access, reasons, enforcement and
institutional dependence.

### State capture

State capture is deeper than petty corruption. Powerful interests influence:

- legislation and subordinate rules;
- public appointments and transfers;
- budget priorities and eligibility criteria;
- enforcement intensity and prosecution;
- media or information channels;
- complaint and audit institutions.

The 2022 GS-IV stone-mining case is a useful neutral illustration of a nexus involving illegal
business, officials, politics and media ownership. Individual punishment alone fails if the network
continues to control appointments, permits, data and complaints.

### Institutional response

- publish appointments, criteria and consultation records where lawful;
- build independent technical capacity;
- use conflict, gift and post-employment controls;
- disclose beneficial ownership in high-risk procurement as applicable;
- distribute approval and review across institutions;
- trace money, decisions, appointments and enforcement patterns;
- protect complainants, journalists and honest officials;
- preserve fair hearing and avoid guilt by association.

Industry consultation is not automatically capture. The test is whether lawful public purpose and
reasoned independence have been persistently displaced.

## 7. Audit and legislative accountability: CAG, PAC and social audit

### Complementary oversight

| Mechanism | Primary role | What it cannot alone do |
|---|---|---|
| management control | prevent and correct during implementation | supply fully independent external assurance |
| internal audit | test controls for management | replace constitutional external audit |
| CAG audit | independent external scrutiny of public finances/performance | itself impose every disciplinary or criminal sanction |
| legislative committee | examine audit findings and executive explanation | conduct every technical field verification |
| social audit | compare records with lived delivery | replace professional audit, investigation or courts |
| citizen complaint/RTI | supply local evidence and trigger review | establish guilt without fair process |

✅ CAG's official performance-audit formulation asks whether programmes and organisations operate
with economy, efficiency and effectiveness, whether decisions were efficiently and effectively
implemented and whether citizens received value for money.

### Audit-to-remedy chain

```text
AUDIT EVIDENCE
    -> ENTITY RESPONSE
    -> CAG REPORT / COMPETENT FINDING
    -> LEGISLATIVE OR MANAGEMENT SCRUTINY
    -> CORRECTION / RECOVERY / REFERRAL
    -> ACTION-TAKEN FOLLOW-UP
    -> SYSTEM LEARNING
```

An audit paragraph is not a conviction. It can identify loss, weak control or suspicious pattern
and trigger competent investigation or disciplinary review. The affected person must receive the
process required by the applicable forum.

### Social audit

Social audit is strongest where citizens can verify:

- who was eligible and selected;
- attendance or service receipt;
- sanctioned work and physical existence;
- measured quantity and payment;
- exclusion, delay or intimidation;
- action taken on findings.

It requires accessible records, independent facilitation, local-language explanation, public
hearing, safety and time-bound response. Privacy-sensitive data should not be disclosed merely
because a public hearing occurs.

> **Boundary:** detailed transparency and RTI law is Topic 15-owned. Detailed Citizens' Charters,
> work culture and routine service-delivery standards remain Topic 17-owned.

## 8. Anti-corruption system design: five separate stages

```text
PREVENT -> DETECT -> TRIAGE -> INVESTIGATE -> ADJUDICATE -> SANCTION
    \___________________________________________________________/
                              |
                              v
                   REMEDY + SYSTEM REDESIGN
```

### 1. Prevention

Reduce opportunity before loss:

- simplify and publish rules and criteria;
- identify high-value/high-discretion processes;
- use competent sanction and role separation;
- manage conflicts and connected interests;
- improve planning and standard documents;
- rotate sensitive responsibilities where risk justifies it;
- create safe advice and leadership accountability.

### 2. Detection

Find possible deviation:

- bank-ledger-asset reconciliation;
- internal audit and surprise verification;
- complaints and whistleblowing;
- duplicate, split-order and concentration analytics;
- field and social audit;
- contract variation and override review.

### 3. Investigation

Preserve and test evidence:

- secure physical and digital records;
- trace decision, benefit and money flows;
- obtain technical expertise;
- distinguish error, negligence, fraud and corrupt motive;
- protect confidentiality and reputation;
- hear relevant persons and corroborate allegation.

### 4. Adjudication

A competent disciplinary, administrative or judicial forum determines responsibility. Essential
values are notice, access to relevant material, impartiality, appropriate proof, reasoned decision
and review.

### 5. Sanction

Possible consequences depend on the applicable system:

- record correction and service restoration;
- recovery or contract remedy where legally established;
- disciplinary consequence;
- exclusion/debarment under applicable procurement rules;
- prosecution and judicial sentence;
- institutional responsibility for supervisory or control failure.

Sanction should reflect intent, benefit, harm, role, obstruction and repetition. Punishing a junior
operator while leaving a captured approval chain intact is not systemic accountability.

### Institutional boundaries

- Topic 19: precise corruption statutes and offences.
- Topic 20: exact CVC, CBI, Lokpal and related jurisdictions.
- Topic 21: detailed vigilance administration, sanctions/approvals and protection of honest officials.
- Topic 22: full case-study option architecture.

Topic 18 uses these as a system map without inventing powers.

## 9. Transparency, technology, citizen oversight and their limits

📰 The official PFMS/GIFMIS description states that centralised treasury payments use electronic
instruments for vendors, employees, States and implementing agencies. It describes the e-bill
system, launched on **3 March 2022**, as permitting trackable online claims and supporting reduced
payment cycles and effective audit trails.

### What technology can improve

- timestamped sanction, invoice and payment trails;
- reduced cash handling and manual re-entry;
- role-based access and maker-checker approval;
- duplicate beneficiary or bank-account alerts;
- split-order, repeated override and vendor-concentration analysis;
- easier reconciliation and remote review;
- publication of safe scheme-level progress.

### What technology cannot prove

```text
DIGITAL RECORD says "PAID"
        |
        +--> Was beneficiary genuine?
        +--> Was consent/identity valid?
        +--> Did asset or work exist?
        +--> Was quantity and quality correct?
        +--> Was specification biased?
        +--> Were bidders colluding?
        +--> Did intended outcome occur?
```

Risks include garbage data, ghost beneficiaries, shared credentials, collusive vendors, biased
specifications, algorithmic exclusion, privacy harm, cyber manipulation and dashboard gaming.

### Balanced digital design

1. clear legal authority and purpose;
2. minimum necessary data;
3. accurate master data and provenance;
4. least-privilege access and immutable logs;
5. human review of adverse flags;
6. field verification and citizen confirmation;
7. accessible correction and appeal;
8. independent system, algorithm and override audit.

Transparency should make allocation, release, criteria, physical progress, outcomes and action
taken intelligible. A mass data dump without context or remedy can create visibility without
accountability.

## 10. Whistleblowing, reforms, PYQ routes and answer architecture

### Responsible whistleblowing

```text
OBSERVE -> VERIFY -> PRESERVE EVIDENCE -> USE SAFE AUTHORISED CHANNEL
       -> MINIMISE UNRELATED DISCLOSURE -> PROTECT SOURCE / CITIZEN
       -> ESCALATE IF CAPTURED -> RECEIVE FEEDBACK / PROTECTION
```

A trustworthy system needs:

- confidential and accessible reporting;
- protection against retaliation;
- independent triage and investigation;
- acknowledgment and appropriate feedback;
- sanctions for malicious retaliation;
- protection of the accused from publicity based only on vague allegations;
- an escalation path when the ordinary chain is implicated.

Whistleblowing is not a licence to publish an entire beneficiary or commercial database. Public
interest, evidence quality, necessity and proportionality remain.

### Reform matrix

| Problem | Specific reform | Limitation/safeguard |
|---|---|---|
| unrealistic budget | readiness and capacity appraisal | avoid systematic under-allocation to difficult regions |
| split or unauthorised spending | total-requirement and competent-sanction check | emergency route should be narrow and recorded |
| tailored procurement | functional specification and independent estimate | preserve justified technical standards |
| cartel | cross-tender analytics and ownership checks | red flag is not guilt |
| weak delivery | independent measurement and milestone payment | avoid duplicative inspection burden |
| capture | plural consultation, technical independence, conflict/cooling-off controls | consultation itself is not capture |
| unsafe reporting | confidentiality and anti-retaliation | protect due process and unrelated privacy |
| dashboard gaming | field verification and outcome metrics | do not abandon useful transaction data |
| audit without action | named responsibility and action-taken tracking | preserve reasoned disagreement with audit |

### PYQ routes

- **2019 Q2(a):** define under-/mis-utilisation -> causes by stage -> fiscal, developmental,
  distributive and trust implications -> specific controls.
- **2019 Q2(b):** agree conditionally -> wilful omission can create corrupt advantage -> protect
  honest error and capacity-constrained officials.
- **2020 Rajesh Kumar:** legal competence + welfare-development trade-off + alternatives + recorded
  advice; resignation only after remedies fail.
- **2021 social audit:** usable records + citizen verification + hearing + action taken; adapt for
  institutional independence.
- **2022 mining nexus:** identify state-capture features, not merely one bribe.
- **2023 coercion:** explicit threat versus domination through dependency; different evidence.
- **2025 Q6(b):** accountability from allocation to outcome; technology plus independent verification.
- **2025 procurement case:** consolidate genuine goods requirement; Rule 157 precision; higher
  sanction; neutral tender and written escalation.
- **2025 MGNREGA case:** continue genuine entitlement while securing evidence, reconciling records,
  paying dues and repairing controls.

### Answer-writing framework: TRUST TO REMEDY

1. **Define** the precise failure: under-utilisation, irregularity, waste, fraud, diversion,
   coercive bribery, collusion or capture.
2. **Thesis:** public money is fiduciary power tied to authorised purpose and citizens.
3. **Locate** the break in need, allocation, release, sanction, procurement, delivery, payment,
   access or outcome.
4. **Evaluate** with economy, efficiency, effectiveness, equity, legality and public trust.
5. **Name** the relevant control or institution without inventing jurisdiction.
6. **Separate** prevention, detection, investigation, adjudication, sanction and remedy.
7. **Add** one India-centric illustration and one technology/institutional limitation.
8. **Conclude** with timely delivery, protection of honest judgment, fair consequence and system learning.

> **Final thesis:** ethical utilisation turns authorised money into lawful, economical, efficient,
> effective and equitable public value. Corruption control is a chain of competent authority,
> contestable decisions, independent verification, protected voice, fair adjudication,
> proportionate consequence and restoration—not a single heroic agency or digital portal.
