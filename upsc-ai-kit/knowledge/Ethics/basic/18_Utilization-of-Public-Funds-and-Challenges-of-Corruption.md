**Core proposition:** ✅ ARC 3.2.1.7 — "squandering public money, including ostentatious official

## 2. Essential definitions

| Concept | Exam-ready meaning |
|---|---|
| ✅ **Squandering public money** (ARC 3.2.1.7) | Wasteful, ostentatious or unnecessary expenditure of public funds without individual misappropriation or specific pecuniary gain to anyone, yet still harmful to the exchequer and public trust. |
| ✅ **Procurement corruption** | Inflated pricing, substandard delivery, collusive bidding or kickback-driven contract awards in government purchase of goods/services/works — historically the largest single category of public-expenditure corruption (ARC Annexure-VII(1)). |
| ✅ **MPLADS/discretionary-fund risk** (ARC 2.6.7-2.6.10) | Local Area Development Schemes empower legislators to sanction public works and authorise expenditure — the ARC argues this constitutes an executive function exercised by legislators, blurring separation of powers and creating a distinctive discretionary-fund corruption risk. |
| ⚠️ **Utilisation vs allocation** | Allocation (deciding how much money goes where) is a policy/budgetary decision; utilisation (whether allocated money is actually, efficiently and honestly spent for its intended purpose) is where most of the ethical failures examined in this topic occur. |
| ✅ **Delegated financial power / sanction** (General Financial Rules, 2017) | Every expenditure requires a competent authority's *sanction*, with the level of authority (Head of Office, Head of Department, Ministry, Cabinet Committee, etc.) fixed by the *value* of the total requirement under the government's delegation-of-financial-powers schedule — not by how the requirement happens to be described or divided. |
| ✅ **Splitting of expenditure (GFR Rule 157, "Avoiding Piecemeal Purchases")** | Rule 157's verbatim text covers only the piecemeal purchase of **goods**: "A demand for goods should not be divided into small quantities to make piecemeal purchases to avoid the necessity of obtaining the sanction of higher authority required with reference to the estimated value of the total demand." ⚠️ Splitting a **works or services** contract, or dividing any procurement specifically to duck a **competitive-tender threshold**, is the same underlying financial-propriety violation by analogy, but is governed by the applicable works/services-procurement rule and the delegation-of-financial-powers framework for that category of expenditure (the department's own works manual/GFR works chapter and its financial-powers schedule) — not by Rule 157's text itself, which does not extend verbatim to works/services. |
| ✅ **Re-appropriation** | Transfer of budgeted funds from one sanctioned purpose/head to another within the same grant, permissible only within the limits and approval levels the GFR/budget manual prescribe — an unauthorised re-appropriation is itself a distinct financial-propriety breach from splitting or squandering. |
| ✅ **Value-for-money procurement/audit trail** | The GFR's core procurement propriety tests — competition (open/limited tender as the value threshold requires), transparency of evaluation criteria fixed before bidding, and value for money — paired with a mandatory record/file-noting trail so that the CAG and internal audit can reconstruct exactly who sanctioned what, on what stated justification, and when. |

## 3. Mechanism: how public funds are diverted or wasted

1. **Procurement stage:** ✅ ARC's Annexure-VII(1) cites a 1999 CII survey finding 60% of 210
   surveyed private firms confirmed 2-25% of contract value as the "price payable to secure a
   government contract"; a World Bank 2003 assessment found officials estimated up to 5% and
   contractors up to 15% of contract value as the informal cost "built into the price."
2. **Discretionary-scheme stage:** ✅ ARC 2.6.7-2.6.9 argues Local Area Development Schemes
   (MPLADS/MLALADS) let legislators "sanction public works and authorize expenditure" — a
   textbook executive function — while the "District Magistrate" nominally controlling funds does
   not change the underlying separation-of-powers concern, per the ARC's own reasoning (citing Era
   Sezhiyan's critique, "MPLADS: Concept, Confusion and Contradictions").
3. **Expenditure/lifestyle stage:** ✅ ARC 3.2.1.7 identifies ostentatious official spending as a
   distinct harm category — no bribery or misappropriation occurs, yet public resources are wasted
   and citizen trust erodes.
4. ✅ 2025 GS-IV Q6(b) directly tests this topic: India's economic rise "as per IMF projection" (4th
   largest economy) alongside sectors where "allocated funds" are "either underutilised or misused,"
   asking for specific measures to "ensure accountability" and "stop leakages."
5. **General Financial Rules (GFR), 2017 — the propriety architecture governing utilisation itself:**
   ✅ every item of expenditure requires *sanction* from the authority competent for that value under
   the delegation-of-financial-powers schedule; GFR Rule 157 explicitly prohibits dividing "a demand
   for goods... into small quantities to make piecemeal purchases to avoid the necessity of
   obtaining the sanction of higher authority required with reference to the estimated value of the
   total demand" — ⚠️ that text governs **goods** specifically; splitting a **works or services**
   contract, or dividing any procurement to duck a competitive-tender threshold, is a **financial-
   propriety analogy of the same evasion logic**, governed by the applicable works/services-
   procurement rule and delegation-of-financial-powers framework for that category, not by Rule 157
   verbatim; re-appropriation of funds between heads is permitted only within
   prescribed limits and approval levels; procurement must proceed through competition (open/limited
   tender as value requires) with evaluation criteria fixed and published *before* bidding, to secure
   value for money; and every sanction, re-appropriation and procurement decision must leave a
   file-noting/record trail sufficient for internal audit and the CAG to reconstruct who decided
   what, on what stated justification.
6. **Worked logic — 2025 GS-IV Q10 (Rajesh, expenditure splitting to duck a GFR sanction limit):**
   see `22` for the full eight-element case architecture; applied here to make this topic
   self-sufficient for a Section-A theory answer on public-funds propriety —
   (a) **facts:** a single, genuine departmental requirement is split into multiple smaller purchase
   orders/work orders so each stays under Rajesh's own sanction limit, avoiding reference to the
   next competent authority (and possibly the tender threshold too);
   (b) **stakeholders:** Rajesh (the sanctioning officer), the higher sanctioning authority being
   bypassed, the exchequer, the vendor(s) potentially favoured by avoided competition, and the
   public whose service delivery depends on honest fund use;
   (c) **legality/propriety check:** ⚠️ where the split requirement is for **goods**, this is a
   direct GFR Rule 157 violation; where it is a **works/services** requirement (as in `22`'s Sec.
   14.1 culvert-repair case), it is the equivalent evasion under the applicable works/services-
   procurement rule and delegation-of-financial-powers framework, not Rule 157 verbatim — either
   way it is a *systemic* propriety breach regardless of whether any individual bribe changed hands,
   not merely a documentation lapse;
   (d) **options:** (i) continue splitting (indefensible); (ii) consolidate the requirement, refer it
   to the competent higher authority/tender process as the GFR requires, and accept the resulting
   delay; (iii) report the pattern (if directed or pressured by a superior) through file noting and,
   if necessary, the vigilance channel;
   (e) **justification:** option (ii) is the only propriety-compliant path — sanction limits and
   tender thresholds exist precisely to secure competent-authority scrutiny and competitive pricing,
   and self-sanctioned convenience cannot override them;
   (f) **implementation and safeguards:** consolidate the requirement into a single sanction file,
   record the full justification and total value, route it to the correct competent authority/
   tender committee, and flag the earlier splitting pattern (if discovered) for departmental review
   — with escalation to the vigilance machinery only if a superior insists on continued splitting
   after being shown the rule position in writing (see `21` and the whistleblowing ladder in `19`-`21`).
7. **Resignation is not the first response to a public-funds-propriety demand.** ⚠️ The correct
   sequence is: record the objection in writing/file noting -> cite the specific GFR provision
   violated -> escalate through the departmental/vigilance channel if the superior insists ->
   consider resignation only if every institutional remedy has been exhausted and continued service
   would make the officer complicit in a clearly illegal act — resigning first forecloses the very
   accountability mechanisms (audit trail, vigilance referral) the GFR architecture depends on.

## 4. Indian applications and examples

- ✅ ARC Annexure-VII(1)'s "Weaknesses in the Procurement Regime": absence of a dedicated policy-
  making agency, absence of a central procurement law, absence of a credible complaint/grievance
  mechanism, absence of standard tender documents, preferential treatment, opaque negotiations, and
  delayed tender processing.
- ✅ ARC's own recommended correctives: a "Transparency in Procurement Act" (citing Tamil Nadu's
  1998 and Karnataka's 1999 pioneering state legislation), published evaluation criteria fixed before
  bidding, committee-based evaluation for large contracts, and increased use of IT for transparency.
- ⚠️ A Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) mechanism reduces the "utilisation" leakage risk by removing
  intermediary discretion between allocation and the intended beneficiary (see `11` for the
  accountability-mechanism framing).

## 5. Must-Know Facts for Prelims

- ✅ ARC's 4th Report classifies "squandering public money" as a distinct category of official
  misconduct that the Prevention of Corruption Act does not reach (see `19` for full legal
  treatment); ARC 3.2.1.10 recommends criminalising it, and that recommendation is still
  unimplemented as at 14 August 2026.
- ✅ Tamil Nadu's Transparency in Tenders Act (1998) and Karnataka's Transparency in Public
  Procurement Act (1999) were the first state-level procurement-transparency laws in India, per the
  ARC's citation.
- ✅ GFR Rule 157 ("Avoiding Piecemeal Purchases") is the specific, current provision that makes
  splitting a **goods** purchase to duck a sanction/tender threshold a propriety violation; splitting
  a **works or services** requirement for the same reason is the identical propriety failure by
  analogy, cited through the applicable works/services-procurement rule and delegation-of-financial-
  powers framework rather than Rule 157 itself (see mechanism step 5-6 above) — name the correct
  category before citing the rule in an answer.
- 📰 MPLADS's constitutional validity was **upheld by a five-judge Constitution Bench in *Bhim Singh
  v. Union of India*, (2010) 5 SCC 538** (6 May 2010): the Court held Article 282 permits such
  grants, that an MP's recommendation of works is not itself an executive function (implementation
  rests with the district administration), and that MPLADS does not constitute an "office of
  profit." As at 14 August 2026, MPLADS continues to operate (₹5 crore annual entitlement per MP,
  administered through the digital eSAKSHI portal since the 2023 guideline revision) — the ARC's
  separation-of-powers *concern* (2.6.7-2.6.10) remains a valid analytical critique to raise in an
  answer, but it is not an unresolved or unconstitutional status.

## 6. UPSC traps

- ❌ Public-fund misuse always involves an individual pocketing a bribe. -> ARC 3.2.1.7 explicitly
  identifies "squandering" (ostentatious, wasteful spending) as harmful even without any
  identifiable personal pecuniary gain.
- ❌ MPLADS-type schemes are unconstitutional and have been struck down. -> A five-judge Constitution
  Bench upheld MPLADS's constitutional validity in *Bhim Singh v. Union of India* (2010); the ARC's
  separation-of-powers point remains a legitimate analytical *critique* to raise, but is not a
  claim that the scheme has been or is likely to be struck down.
- ❌ Procurement corruption is mainly a problem of individual dishonesty. -> ARC's own diagnosis
  emphasises *systemic weaknesses* (absence of standard documents, absence of a complaint mechanism,
  absence of a central law) as much as individual venality.
- ❌ Splitting a purchase into smaller parts is a harmless administrative shortcut if no bribe is
  involved. -> GFR Rule 157 makes piecemeal splitting of a **goods** purchase to duck a sanction/
  tender threshold a propriety violation in itself, and the same evasion applied to a works/services
  requirement is an equally culpable propriety breach under the applicable works/services rule and
  delegation framework — regardless of whether any individual pecuniary gain is proved —
  structurally identical to ARC 3.2.1.7's "squandering" category in requiring no bribe to be
  culpable.
- ❌ GFR Rule 157 itself governs splitting of works or services contracts, or tender-threshold
  evasion generally. -> Rule 157's text is confined to piecemeal *goods* purchases; splitting works/
  services contracts or dividing any procurement to duck a tender threshold is a broader financial-
  propriety/procurement analogy of the same evasion logic, answered by citing the applicable
  works/services-procurement rule and delegation-of-financial-powers framework, not Rule 157
  verbatim — conflating the two is a precise citation error worth avoiding in a Mains answer.

## 8. Mains angles

- ⚠️ Distinguish squandering, procurement corruption and discretionary-scheme risk as three separate
  mechanisms when a question asks broadly about "misuse of public funds" — naming the specific
  mechanism earns more credit than a generic "corruption in spending" answer.
- ⚠️ Recommend specific, source-grounded correctives: standard tender documents, pre-fixed evaluation
  criteria, committee-based evaluation, IT-enabled transparency, and a dedicated procurement law.
- ⚠️ For a splitting/sanction-limit scenario, name the correct category first (goods vs works/
  services) and cite GFR Rule 157 only for a goods split — for works/services, cite the applicable
  works/services-procurement rule and delegation-of-financial-powers framework by name — then walk
  through the implementation sequence (consolidate -> route to competent authority -> record
  justification -> escalate only if overridden) rather than a generic "follow the rules" answer.

> **Answer thesis:** Public-fund misuse in India operates through at least three distinct
> mechanisms — procurement corruption, ostentatious squandering without individual gain, and
> discretionary-fund schemes (constitutionally upheld but carrying a genuine separation-of-powers
> critique) — each requiring a distinct, named GFR-grounded corrective rather than a single generic
> anti-corruption gesture.
