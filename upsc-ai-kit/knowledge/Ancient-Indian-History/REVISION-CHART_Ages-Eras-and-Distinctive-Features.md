# Ancient India: Ages, Eras and Distinctive Features - Last-Minute Revision Chart

> **Purpose:** One-place revision immediately before Prelims or GS-I.
> **Coverage:** Prehistory to the Imperial Cholas, with the feature that most clearly distinguishes each age.
> **Source base:** Every relevant Core and Advanced Ancient Indian History file, the Master Chronology, R.S. Sharma and Upinder Singh.
> **Two-pass design:** The master chart gives the non-negotiable, exam-complete Core identity of each era. The optional Advanced layer adds source criticism, debates and regional variation; skipping it must not affect the ability to answer Prelims or Mains.
> **Rule:** Dates are approximate and cultures overlap regionally. Identify an age from a combination of evidence, not from one isolated object.

---

## 1. The 60-second chronology ladder

```text
Large stone tools
    -> flakes
    -> blades
    -> microliths
    -> polished tools + farming
    -> copper-stone villages
    -> Bronze Age Harappan cities
    -> pastoral Rig Vedic tribes
    -> iron-using Later Vedic janapadas
    -> NBPW cities + mahajanapadas + new religions
    -> Mauryan subcontinental empire
    -> post-Mauryan trade, coins and regional states
    -> Gupta imperial culture
    -> post-Gupta land grants and regionalisation
    -> Pallava-Chalukya temple-state formation
    -> Chola agrarian, temple and maritime power
```

> **Memory line:** Hunt -> tame -> farm -> copper village -> bronze city -> cattle tribe -> iron territory -> city-state -> empire -> trade kingdoms -> classical court -> regional temple states.

## 2. Master age-identification chart

| Age / era | Approximate date | Economy and settlement | Technology / material marker | Polity and society | Religion / culture | Fastest identification clue |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lower Palaeolithic** | c. 600,000-150,000 BC in R.S. Sharma's chronology | Mobile hunter-gatherers; river-valley and open-air occupation | **Hand-axes, cleavers, pebble/core tools** | No state, agriculture or permanent village economy | Earliest human occupation; cultural reconstruction mainly from tools | A large bifacial hand-axe or cleaver |
| **Middle Palaeolithic** | c. 150,000-35,000 BC in the textbook chronology | Hunting and gathering | **Flake tools**, prepared-core techniques | Small mobile groups | Evidence remains mainly lithic | Flakes replace the dominance of large core tools |
| **Upper Palaeolithic** | c. 35,000-10,000 BC | Advanced foraging | **Blades and burins** | Mobile groups | Greater symbolic/art evidence | Blade-burin technology before microlithic culture |
| **Mesolithic** | c. 9000-4000 BC | Hunting, gathering, fishing; beginnings of domestication; some semi-settlement | **Microliths** | Small communities; no full agrarian state | **Rock paintings**, burials and early domestication evidence | Microlith + Bhimbetka/Bagor/Adamgarh |
| **Neolithic** | c. 7000-1000 BC, regionally varied | **Food production**, settled villages, farming and cattle-keeping | **Polished stone tools**, pottery; pit dwellings or ash mounds in specific regions | Kin-based village communities | Burial and ritual practices vary by region | Polished axe + farming; Burzahom pit dwelling; southern ash mound |
| **Chalcolithic** | mainly c. 2000-700 BC, regionally varied | Mostly **rural farming villages**; storage and regional craft traditions | **Copper + stone**, painted pottery; no iron definition | Chiefly or village societies, not Harappan-scale cities | Regional cultures such as Ahar, Malwa and Jorwe | Copper-stone village economy, not planned Bronze Age urbanism |
| **Mature Harappan** | c. 2600-1900 BC | **First urbanisation**; agriculture, specialised crafts and long-distance trade | Bronze; standardised bricks, weights and measures; seals; undeciphered script; drains | Urban civic organisation, but exact rulers/property system unknown | Great Bath, figurines, animal/pipal motifs; religious labels remain debated | Planned city + drainage + standardisation + seals |
| **Late Harappan** | c. 1900-1300 BC | Deurbanisation and regional rural cultures | Decline of mature urban markers; continuity in some pottery/crafts | Localised settlement patterns | Selective cultural continuities | Harappan traits survive, but large integrated cities decline |
| **Rig Vedic / Early Vedic** | c. 1500-1000 BC | **Pastoral economy** with agriculture known; cattle as principal wealth | Chariots and copper/bronze; **iron and PGW are not defining markers** | Tribal *jana*; *rajan* as chief; sabha, samiti and vidatha | Indra, Agni and Varuna; sacrificial hymns; Rig Veda | Sapta-Sindhu + cattle + tribal assemblies |
| **Later Vedic** | c. 1000-600 BC | Settled agriculture expands; rice and wheat; regular tribute becomes stronger | **Iron + Painted Grey Ware (PGW)** in the upper Ganga region | *Jana* moves towards *janapada*; stronger kingship; varna hierarchy hardens | Prajapati, elaborate sacrifice, rajasuya/ashvamedha/vajapeya; Upanishadic speculation | Kuru-Panchala + PGW + iron + territorial kingship |
| **Mahajanapada / Second Urbanisation** | c. 600-321 BC | Towns, trade, surplus, punch-marked coins and **NBPW** context | Iron agriculture; fortified cities; coin use | **Sixteen mahajanapadas**; monarchies and gana-sanghas; Magadha rises | Jainism, Buddhism and other shramana movements | NBPW + cities + coins + heterodox religions |
| **Mauryan Empire** | c. 324-187 BC | Large agrarian-revenue base, roads, cities and state-regulated activity | Stone pillars, inscriptions and expanding use of polished stone art | First virtually subcontinental empire; provinces, officers, taxation and espionage | Ashokan **Dhamma**, edicts and Buddhist patronage without abolition of the state | Ashokan inscription + Pataliputra + named bureaucracy |
| **Post-Mauryan north-west** | c. 200 BC-AD 300 | Silk Route and Indo-Roman trade; monetisation | **Bilingual/portrait coins**, extensive Kushana gold coinage; cavalry innovations | Indo-Greeks, Shakas, Parthians and Kushanas | Gandhara and Mathura art; Mahayana patronage; cultural interaction | Foreign dynasty + coins + Gandhara/Mathura + transregional trade |
| **Satavahana Deccan** | c. 1st century BC-mid-3rd century AD | Deccan agriculture, craft production and inland/coastal trade | Prakrit-Brahmi inscriptions; lead and other coins; rock-cut caves | Deccan state; *ahara*, amatyas; early land grants | Brahmanical claims alongside strong Buddhist patronage | Gautamiputra + Nahapana overstrikes + Prakrit + Deccan |
| **Sangam / Early Historic Tamilakam** | c. 300 BC-AD 300 | Agro-pastoral zones, ports, pepper/pearl trade and Roman contact | Megalithic burials, iron, black-and-red ware, coins and port archaeology | Chera, Chola and Pandya chiefdoms/early states | Tamil Sangam corpus; **akam, puram and tinai** | Tamil poems + megaliths + Yavana trade + three crowned kings |
| **Gupta age** | c. AD 319-550 | Agrarian expansion, trade and growing land-grant practice | Gold coins, inscriptions, early structural temples and scientific-literary production | Large north Indian empire, but less centralised than Mauryas; feudatories gain importance | Sanskrit prashastis; Brahmanical-Puranic growth; major literature, mathematics and astronomy | Prayag Prashasti + gold coins + Sanskrit court culture |
| **Post-Gupta / Harsha** | c. AD 550-750; Harsha AD 606-647 | Rural expansion and increasing land grants | Copper-plate grants and regional inscriptions | Political fragmentation; Harsha's north Indian hegemony centred on Kannauj | Nalanda, Xuanzang, Banabhatta; regional religious patronage | Kannauj + Xuanzang + land grants + regionalisation |
| **Pallava-Chalukya age** | c. 6th-8th centuries AD | Agrarian expansion through grants; temple-centred settlements | Rock-cut, monolithic and structural temples; bilingual epigraphy | Pallavas of Kanchi versus Chalukyas of Badami; locality integration | Early Bhakti; Sanskrit and Tamil/Kannada regional cultures | Mahabalipuram/Kanchi versus Aihole/Badami/Pattadakal |
| **Early-medieval regional kingdoms** | c. AD 750 onward | Land grants, rural networks, temples and merchant bodies | Regional inscriptions and mature temple styles | Palas, Pratiharas and Rashtrakutas; regional state systems | Regional languages, monasteries, temples and sectarian traditions | Tripartite struggle + land-grant/temple networks |
| **Imperial Cholas** | c. AD 850-1279 | Kaveri wet-rice core, irrigation, taxation, temples and Indian Ocean commerce | Monumental temples, bronzes and dense inscriptions | Mandalam-valanadu-nadu; ur, sabha and nagaram; strong kingship plus locality bodies | Shaiva temple culture; Tamil and Sanskrit learning | Rajaraja/Rajendra + Brihadisvara + Uttaramerur + Srivijaya |

## 3. Optional Advanced enrichment: deeper interpretation

| Age / era | Core identification | Advanced correction / deeper discriminator |
|---|---|---|
| Palaeolithic-Mesolithic | Tool sequence and subsistence | Tool traditions and dates vary regionally; Attirampakkam gives a much earlier Middle Palaeolithic transition than one all-India textbook band. Rock art is cultural evidence, not a written narrative. |
| Neolithic-Chalcolithic | Food production; polished tools; copper-stone villages | The transition was a **mosaic**, not a simultaneous revolution. Microliths can continue into Neolithic contexts; copper need not instantly replace a Neolithic material package; rice remains do not automatically prove domesticated rice. |
| Harappan | Urbanism, standardisation, seals, script and drainage | Because the script is undeciphered, polity, religion and property remain debated. Mature Harappan is an **integration era** and Late Harappan a **localisation/deurbanisation process**, not abrupt extinction. "Granary", "dockyard", "Mother Goddess" and "Pashupati" are interpretations. |
| Rig Vedic | Pastoral, tribal and north-western | The Rig Veda is a layered ritual-poetic source, not a chronicle. Agriculture existed; the distinction is pastoral predominance. Arya/Dasa should not be reduced to fixed biological races. |
| Later Vedic | Iron, PGW, agriculture and territorialisation | Text, PGW and polity cannot be matched one-to-one. Iron did not create states automatically; agrarian surplus, ritual, warfare and social differentiation interacted gradually. |
| Mahajanapadas | Cities, states, coins, NBPW and shramana religions | The sixteen-state lists vary across texts. Magadha's rise cannot be reduced to an iron monopoly; geography, resources, political strategy, armies and administration worked together. |
| Jainism-Buddhism | Renunciation, ethical paths and monastic institutions | They were products of the world of **cities, kings and renunciants**, not purely anti-Brahmanical revolts. Sangha, lay support, merchants and patronage networks explain institutional spread. |
| Mauryan | Large empire, bureaucracy, edicts and Dhamma | Do not treat the Arthashastra as an exact Mauryan constitution or assume uniform centralisation. Ashokan edicts primarily reveal Dhamma and royal communication; control varied regionally and Dhamma did not mean total pacifism. |
| Post-Mauryan north-west | Foreign-origin dynasties, coins, trade and art | Treat the period as **interaction and innovation**, not foreign rule alone. Kushana coins were religiously plural; Gandhara was hybrid rather than simply Greek. AD 78 is the Shaka era, while Kanishka's accession is commonly placed around AD 127 in the Advanced source. |
| Satavahana | Deccan polity, Prakrit, grants and Buddhist caves | Origins and dates are debated because reconstruction depends on Puranic lists, inscriptions and coins. The polity worked through regional chiefs and donation networks; matronymics do not prove matriarchy. |
| Sangam | Tamil corpus, megaliths, ports and three dynasties | Poems are not direct chronicles. Arikamedu was not automatically a Roman colony, Roman objects do not prove resident Romans, and Pattanam's identification with Muziris remains debated. |
| Gupta | Sanskrit prashastis, coins, empire and cultural production | A prashasti is evidence plus ideology. Samudragupta used graded sovereignty rather than direct annexation everywhere. "Golden Age" and one-event Huna-collapse claims are unsafe. |
| Post-Gupta-Harsha | Kannauj, Xuanzang, Bana and regionalisation | Bana and Xuanzang have courtly and Buddhist perspectives. The Aihole inscription checks Harsha's southern-imperial claim. Land grants were political and agrarian instruments, not only religious charity. |
| Pallava-Chalukya | Regional rivalry, temples, grants and Bhakti | Aihole is a royal eulogy; temple architecture is evidence for power, craft and regional identity. Brahmanisation includes land, caste and agrarian change; Nagara-Dravida-Vesara labels are not rigid boxes. |
| Early medieval transition | Regional states, land grants and temple networks | Avoid a one-model "dark age" or total feudal-decline story. Balance feudal tendencies with regional state formation, agrarian expansion, new languages, cults and uneven urban change. |
| Imperial Chola | Kaveri state, local bodies, temples and maritime activity | Neither a fully centralised state nor autonomous village republics alone explains it. Uttaramerur rules were local and exclusionary; Rajendra's Srivijaya action was a major raid, not durable conquest of Southeast Asia. |

> **Firewall rule:** Core alone must support a complete Prelims decision and a complete Mains answer. Advanced may improve sophistication, but no indispensable fact, distinction or answer mechanism may exist only in Advanced.

## 4. The distinctions UPSC most often tests

### A. Stone Age and early farming

| If the question mentions... | Think... | Do not confuse it with... |
|---|---|---|
| Hand-axes and cleavers | Lower Palaeolithic | Mesolithic microliths |
| Flake tools | Middle Palaeolithic | Upper Palaeolithic blades |
| Blades and burins | Upper Palaeolithic | Neolithic polished axes |
| Microliths and rock art | Mesolithic | Full settled farming |
| Polished tools and food production | Neolithic | Chalcolithic copper-stone villages |
| Copper plus stone, painted pottery and rural settlements | Chalcolithic | Harappan planned cities |

### B. Harappan versus Vedic

| Dimension | Harappan | Rig Vedic | Later Vedic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core setting | Urban Indus/Ghaggar-Hakra world | Pastoral Sapta-Sindhu | Agrarian upper Ganga-Yamuna doab |
| Defining material marker | Seals, script, standardised bricks/weights, drains | Rig Veda, cattle wealth, tribal assemblies | PGW, iron, settled agriculture |
| Political inference | Exact polity uncertain | Tribal chiefship | Stronger territorial kingship |
| Social clue | Archaeology cannot securely prove ownership or caste system | Varna ideas are emerging, less rigid | Varna and patriarchal hierarchy become more pronounced |
| Religion | Archaeological motifs; labels debated | Indra, Agni, Varuna | Prajapati, elaborate sacrifice, Upanishads |
| Biggest trap | Do not call it Vedic merely from a fire installation | Do not insert iron, cities or tax bureaucracy | Do not describe it as still mainly pastoral |

### C. First versus second urbanisation

| Feature | First urbanisation: Harappan | Second urbanisation: c. 6th century BC |
|---|---|---|
| Region | North-west and western India | Middle Gangetic basin and north India |
| Material package | Planned cities, seals, script, standardised bricks and weights | NBPW, coins, fortified towns, iron-supported agrarian surplus |
| Political context | Exact form remains debated | Mahajanapadas, monarchies and gana-sanghas |
| Religious context | Reconstructed archaeologically | Jainism, Buddhism and other shramana traditions |
| Key warning | Script is undeciphered | Literary and archaeological evidence can be combined, but not mechanically |

### D. Maurya versus Gupta

| Dimension | Maurya | Gupta |
|---|---|---|
| Dates | c. 324-187 BC | c. AD 319-550 |
| State form | More elaborate imperial bureaucracy and direct officer vocabulary | Less centralised; feudatories and land grants more visible |
| Signature source | Ashokan edicts, Arthashastra, Megasthenes | Prayag Prashasti, coins, inscriptions, travellers |
| Ruler clue | Chandragupta Maurya, Ashoka | Samudragupta, Chandragupta II |
| Ideological clue | Dhamma communicated through edicts | Sanskrit prashasti and Brahmanical-Puranic court culture |
| Traveller | Megasthenes at Chandragupta Maurya's court | Fa-hsien during Chandragupta II |
| Biggest trap | Ashoka did not abolish army/state punishment | "Golden Age" does not mean equal prosperity for every group or Mauryan-style centralisation |

### E. Simultaneous post-Mauryan worlds

```text
NORTH-WEST: Indo-Greeks -> Shakas -> Kushanas
              coins, Silk Routes, Gandhara/Mathura, Mahayana

DECCAN:      Satavahanas
              Prakrit, land grants, Buddhist caves, Deccan trade

DEEP SOUTH:  Chera-Chola-Pandya / Sangam
              Tamil corpus, megaliths, ports, pepper and Roman trade
```

> **Trap:** These are substantially overlapping regional histories, not three consecutive all-India empires.

### F. Gupta to early medieval transition

| Earlier imperial tendency | Early-medieval tendency |
|---|---|
| Large imperial claims | Multiplication of regional kingdoms |
| Court-centred political narrative | Greater visibility of locality bodies and intermediaries |
| Land grants present | Land grants become more extensive and structurally important |
| Early structural temples | Temple complexes become major economic and political institutions |
| Sanskrit cosmopolitan culture | Sanskrit continues while regional languages and cultures expand |

## 5. Evidence-to-inference rules for statement questions

| Archaeological / textual evidence | Safe inference | Unsafe overreach |
|---|---|---|
| Spindle whorls in houses | Household spinning activity | Exact gender, status or ownership of every spinner |
| Graduated scales and standardised weights | Measurement knowledge and technical/economic standardisation | A fully known modern scientific system |
| Courtyard houses with private wells and bathing floors | Planned household architecture and water access | A common-property system |
| Fortification | Concern with defence, control or separation | Proof of a particular king or permanent warfare |
| Uniform bricks, seals or weights | Shared standards and coordination | Proof of one centralised empire without corroboration |
| Grave goods | Beliefs, identity or social differentiation may be investigated | Exact religion or rigid class system from one grave |
| Coins | Authority, exchange, iconography and chronology | Universal monetisation of every transaction |
| Land-grant inscription | Transfer of rights/revenue and agrarian expansion | Automatic proof of complete state collapse |
| Religious image or motif | Presence or patronage of a tradition | Exclusive religion of the entire population |
| Prashasti | Royal claims, ideals and political geography | Literal acceptance of every conquest claim |

> **Elimination rule:** Material evidence is strongest for **technology, production, trade and construction**. It is weaker by itself for **property systems, exact political institutions, caste structure or personal beliefs**.

## 6. Pottery, metals and urban markers

| Marker | Strongest association | Important caution |
|---|---|---|
| Hand-axe / cleaver | Lower Palaeolithic | A stone tool alone needs typological context |
| Microlith | Mesolithic | Microlith use can continue into later cultures |
| Polished stone axe | Neolithic | Neolithic dates vary by region |
| Copper + stone | Chalcolithic | Does not mean an iron-using society |
| Bronze + seals + planned cities | Mature Harappan | Bronze alone is not uniquely Harappan |
| Painted Grey Ware | Later Vedic / early Iron Age upper Ganga region | It does not represent all later Vedic settlements |
| Northern Black Polished Ware | Second urbanisation / early historic north India | Not a Harappan ceramic |
| Black-and-red ware | Several regional cultures, including megalithic contexts | Never identify an age from this ware alone |
| Iron megalithic grave goods | Deep-south megalithic culture | Megaliths are often burials, not cities |
| Gold coins | Especially Kushanas and Guptas | Coin-metal questions require dynasty-specific evidence |
