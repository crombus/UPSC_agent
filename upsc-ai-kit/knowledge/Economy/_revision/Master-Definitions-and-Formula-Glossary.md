# MASTER ECONOMY DEFINITIONS AND FORMULA GLOSSARY

> **Scope:** exam-safe definitions, formulas, interpretation rules and close-option
> distinctions for the complete UPSC Economy syllabus. Symbols and accounting conventions
> are stated explicitly because similar-looking formulas often measure different things.
>
> **Data discipline:** formulas remain stable; rates, weights, base years, regulatory
> thresholds and scheme parameters may change. Verify changing facts from RBI, MoSPI,
> Budget, Economic Survey or the relevant ministry.
>
> **Companion index:** use
> [`Major-Committees-Commissions-and-Reports.md`](Major-Committees-Commissions-and-Reports.md)
> for major Economy panels, their assigned tasks and reform contributions.

## 1. Formula conventions

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `C` | Private final consumption expenditure |
| `I` | Gross investment / gross capital formation in the expenditure identity |
| `G` | Government final consumption expenditure |
| `X` | Exports |
| `M` | Imports |
| `P` | Price level or price index |
| `Y` | Real output/income |
| `PY` | Nominal output/income |
| `i` | Nominal interest rate |
| `r` | Real interest rate |
| `pi` | Inflation rate |
| `H` or `M0` | Monetary base / reserve money |
| `C/D` or `c` | Currency-deposit ratio |
| `RR/D` or `rr` | Required-reserve-deposit ratio |
| `ER/D` or `er` | Excess-reserve-deposit ratio |

> **Percentage rule:** a ratio multiplied by 100 becomes a percentage. A percentage-point
> change is not the same as a percentage change. Moving from 5% to 6% is **+1 percentage
> point** or **+20% relative increase**.

## 2. National income and output

| Term | Exam-safe definition | Formula / interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Gross Domestic Product (GDP) | Market value of final goods and services produced within a country's domestic territory during a period | Territory-based, not nationality-based |
| Nominal GDP | GDP valued at current-period prices | Changes because of both quantities and prices |
| Real GDP | GDP valued at constant/base-period prices or through the official volume-measure method | Intended to isolate volume/quantity change |
| GDP at market prices | Value of domestic output at purchasers' prices | `GDPmp = GVA at basic prices + product taxes - product subsidies` |
| Gross Value Added (GVA) | Value of output minus intermediate consumption generated by producers | `GVA = value of output - intermediate consumption` |
| Gross National Income (GNI) / GNP | Income accruing to residents, including net primary/factor income from abroad | `GNI = GDP + NFIA` |
| Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA) | Factor/primary income received from abroad minus paid abroad | Positive NFIA makes GNI exceed GDP |
| Net Domestic Product (NDP) | GDP after consumption of fixed capital | `NDP = GDP - depreciation` |
| Net National Product (NNP) | GNI/GNP after depreciation | `NNP = GNI - depreciation` |
| National income | Income accruing to resident factors; older texts often identify it with NNP at factor cost | Do not mix obsolete factor-cost terminology with current accounts without explanation |
| Depreciation / consumption of fixed capital | Estimated value of fixed capital used up through wear, obsolescence and normal damage | Gross minus depreciation equals net |
| Domestic territory | Economic territory within which production is attributed to the economy | Includes specified resident territorial enclaves; not identical to political citizenship |
| Resident institutional unit | Unit with a centre of predominant economic interest in the economy | Residency is economic, not nationality-based |
| Final good/service | Output purchased for final use rather than further production in the accounting period | Included to avoid double counting |
| Intermediate consumption | Goods/services used up in producing other output | Deducted when calculating GVA |
| Double counting | Counting the same value at multiple production stages | Avoided through final-output or value-added method |
| Transfer payment | Payment without a corresponding current good/service, such as a pension or subsidy transfer | Excluded from GDP as payment; resulting consumption may enter GDP |
| Imputed value | Estimated value assigned where no explicit market transaction occurs | Used for specified items such as owner-occupied housing services |
| Mixed income | Income of unincorporated enterprises combining labour and capital returns | Common where wages and profit cannot be separated |
| Operating surplus | Return to capital and entrepreneurship after labour compensation and production taxes/subsidies | Broadly profit/rent-type income in accounts |
| Compensation of employees | Wages, salaries and employers' social contributions | Income-method component |
| Gross capital formation | Addition to produced assets plus inventory changes and valuables under national accounts | Includes gross fixed capital formation and change in stocks |
| Gross Fixed Capital Formation (GFCF) | Acquisition less disposal of fixed assets during a period | Common proxy for investment in productive capacity |
| Change in stocks | Change in inventories held by producers | Part of gross capital formation |
| Expenditure-method GDP | Domestic final expenditure plus net exports | `GDP = C + I + G + (X - M)` |
| Production method | Sum of value added across producing units plus net product taxes | Avoids adding gross turnover repeatedly |
| Income method | Sum of incomes generated in production | Compensation + operating surplus/mixed income + relevant taxes less subsidies |
| Per-capita GDP | Average domestic output per person | `GDP / population` |
| Per-capita income | Average national/resident income per person | `GNI or NNI / population`, depending stated measure |
| GDP growth rate | Percentage change in GDP between periods | `[(GDPt - GDPt-1) / GDPt-1] x 100` |
| Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) | Constant annual rate linking beginning and ending value | `[(Ending / Beginning)^(1/n) - 1] x 100` |
| GDP deflator | Broad price index for domestically produced final output | `(Nominal GDP / Real GDP) x 100` |
| Implicit price deflator | Price measure derived by dividing a current-price aggregate by its volume measure | Basket composition changes with output |
| Base year | Reference year used for constant-price comparison and index weights | Revision can change weights, sources and methodology |
| Current prices | Valuation using prices prevailing in the same period as output | Produces nominal estimates |
| Constant prices | Valuation using prices of a chosen reference/base period | Produces real estimates |
| Chain-volume measure | Real measure linking successive-period volume changes rather than using one distant fixed basket | Reduces substitution bias but complicates additivity |
| Output gap | Difference between actual and potential output | `Actual output - potential output`; often expressed as % of potential |
| Potential output | Sustainable level of output consistent with available capacity without persistent inflation pressure | Not directly observed |
| Recession | Significant, broad decline in economic activity; a technical two-quarter rule is only a shorthand | Use official national context rather than assume one universal legal definition |
| Depression | Exceptionally deep and prolonged contraction | No single universally binding numerical threshold |
| Base effect | Distortion in year-on-year growth/inflation because the comparison-period level was unusually high or low | Does not by itself describe current momentum |

### Core national-income formulas

| Formula | Use |
|---|---|
| `GDP = C + I + G + (X - M)` | Expenditure identity |
| `GVA = output - intermediate consumption` | Producer value addition |
| `GDPmp = GVA basic + product taxes - product subsidies` | GVA-to-GDP bridge |
| `GNI = GDP + NFIA` | Domestic-to-national bridge |
| `NDP = GDP - depreciation` | Gross-to-net domestic |
| `NNP = GNI - depreciation` | Gross-to-net national |
| `GDP deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) x 100` | Broad output-price level |
| `Real GDP approximately Nominal GDP adjusted for GDP deflator` | Never subtract a CPI rate mechanically when official deflated series exists |
| `Per-capita GDP = GDP / population` | Average output, not distribution |
| `Growth rate = [(New - Old) / Old] x 100` | Period change |
| `CAGR = [(End / Start)^(1/n) - 1] x 100` | Multi-period annualised growth |

## 3. Growth, investment and productivity

| Term | Exam-safe definition | Formula / interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Economic growth | Sustained increase in real output or real income | Distinguish aggregate from per-capita growth |
| Economic development | Structural expansion of capabilities, productivity, institutions and welfare | Broader than GDP growth |
| Inclusive growth | Growth with broad participation in production and distribution of benefits | Requires jobs, access and opportunity |
| Sustainable development | Development meeting present needs without undermining future capability | Integrates economic, social and ecological dimensions |
| Productivity | Output produced per unit of input | Labour productivity = output/labour input |
| Labour productivity | Output per worker or per hour worked | `Real output / labour input` |
| Total Factor Productivity (TFP) | Output growth not explained by measured growth of labour and capital inputs | Captures technology, efficiency and measurement residuals |
| Capital-output ratio | Capital required per unit of output | `Capital stock / output` |
| Incremental Capital-Output Ratio (ICOR) | Additional capital/investment required for an additional unit of output | Approx. `investment rate / growth rate`; lower ICOR implies higher capital efficiency |
| Savings rate | Saving as a share of income | `(Saving / income) x 100` |
| Investment rate | Investment/gross capital formation as a share of GDP | `(Investment / GDP) x 100` |
| Capital deepening | Increase in capital available per worker | Can raise labour productivity |
| Crowding in | Public action stimulates private investment | Often through infrastructure, demand or risk reduction |
| Crowding out | Government borrowing/spending displaces private activity | May occur through interest rates, scarce finance or resources |
| Multiplier | Change in output resulting from an initial autonomous spending change | `k = change in income / initial spending change` |
| Simple Keynesian multiplier | Textbook multiplier under restrictive assumptions | `k = 1 / (1 - MPC) = 1 / MPS` |
| Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC) | Additional consumption from an additional unit of disposable income | `MPC = change in C / change in Yd` |
| Marginal Propensity to Save (MPS) | Additional saving from additional disposable income | `MPS = change in S / change in Yd`; `MPC + MPS = 1` in simple model |
| Accelerator principle | Investment responds to changes in expected demand/output | Stronger demand growth can induce capacity investment |

## 4. Money, monetary aggregates and money multiplier

| Term | Exam-safe definition | Formula / composition |
|---|---|---|
| Money | Asset generally accepted as medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value and standard of deferred payment | Degree of liquidity varies |
| Currency in circulation | Notes and coins issued for circulation, including cash held by banks | Broader than currency with public |
| Currency with public | Currency in circulation minus cash held by banks | Component of M1 and M3 |
| Demand deposit | Deposit withdrawable on demand without a fixed maturity | Highly liquid |
| Time deposit | Deposit repayable after a specified period or subject to maturity conditions | Included in broad money, not M1 |
| Monetary base / high-powered money | Central-bank money supporting currency and bank reserves | Simplified `H = C + R` |
| Reserve money (M0) | RBI monetary base | `M0 = currency in circulation + bankers' deposits with RBI + other deposits with RBI` |
| Narrow money (M1) | Most liquid commonly used monetary aggregate | `M1 = currency with public + demand deposits with banking system + other deposits with RBI` |
| M2 | M1 plus savings deposits with the Post Office Savings Bank | Traditional Indian aggregate extended by specified postal savings |
| Broad money (M3) | M1 plus time deposits with banking system | `M3 = M1 + time deposits with banking system` |
| M4 | M3 plus all deposits with the Post Office Savings Organisation, excluding National Savings Certificates | Broadest traditional Indian aggregate |
| Money supply | Stock of monetary assets included in a stated aggregate at a point in time | Always specify M1/M3 etc. |
| Liquidity | Ease of converting an asset into means of payment without material loss | Money is most liquid; liquidity is broader than money |
| Money multiplier | Ratio of a broader money aggregate to monetary base | Empirical broad multiplier: `m = M3 / M0` |
| Simple deposit multiplier | Maximum deposit expansion from reserves under no currency leakage and no excess reserves | `1 / required reserve ratio` |
| General money multiplier | Money expansion allowing currency leakage and excess reserves | `m = (1 + c) / (c + rr + er)` |
| Currency-deposit ratio (`c`) | Public's currency holding relative to deposits | `c = C / D`; higher `c` lowers multiplier, other things equal |
| Required-reserve ratio (`rr`) | Required bank reserves relative to deposits | Higher ratio lowers multiplier, other things equal |
| Excess-reserve ratio (`er`) | Bank reserves above requirement relative to deposits | Higher excess reserves lower multiplier |
| Reserve-deposit ratio | Total reserves relative to deposits | If total reserve ratio is used, do not add excess reserves again |
| Credit creation | Banking-system expansion of deposits through lending subject to reserves, capital, liquidity, demand and risk | Not an unlimited mechanical process |
| Velocity of money | Average frequency with which a unit of money supports nominal transactions/output | Quantity identity: `V = PY / M` |
| Quantity equation | Accounting identity relating money, velocity, prices and output | `MV = PY` |
| Cambridge cash-balance equation | Desired money balances as fraction of nominal income | `M = kPY`, with `k = 1/V` under matching definitions |
| Real money balance | Purchasing power of nominal money stock | `M / P` |
| Monetisation | Increase in use of money and formal financial transactions | Not identical with deficit monetisation |
| Seigniorage | Real resources obtained by issuing base money | Approximate inflation-tax linkage, but not identical to inflation tax |
| Inflation tax | Loss of purchasing power imposed on holders of money by inflation | Approx. `inflation rate x real money balances` under simple assumptions |

### Money-multiplier interpretation

```text
M0 / reserve money rises
        |
        v
banks and public choose currency, deposits and reserves
        |
        v
credit/deposit expansion, constrained by capital, liquidity, risk and demand
        |
        v
M3
```

- A lower CRR can support expansion but does not guarantee lending.
- Greater public preference for currency raises `c` and usually reduces the multiplier.
- Banks holding extra reserves raise `er` and reduce the multiplier.
- Weak credit demand, capital stress or risk aversion can prevent textbook expansion.
- `M3/M0` is an observed ratio; `1/CRR` is a simplified theoretical multiplier.

## 5. Interest rates and monetary policy

| Term | Exam-safe definition | Formula / distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Nominal interest rate | Stated money rate paid or earned | Does not remove inflation |
| Real interest rate | Inflation-adjusted purchasing-power return/cost | Approx. `r = i - expected inflation` |
| Fisher equation | Exact relation between nominal rate, real rate and expected inflation | `(1 + i) = (1 + r)(1 + pi^e)` |
| Policy repo rate | Monetary Policy Committee's policy rate for collateralised liquidity under RBI's framework | Signal rate, not every lending rate |
| Repo transaction | Sale of securities with agreement to repurchase; economically collateralised borrowing | Direction depends on viewpoint |
| Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) | RBI facility absorbing bank liquidity without collateral | Floor-side standing facility under current framework |
| Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) | Overnight backstop borrowing facility for eligible banks against approved securities at a penal spread | Safety valve, not routine cheap funding |
| Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF) | RBI framework for liquidity injection/absorption through specified operations | Helps align overnight rates with policy stance |
| Bank rate | RBI rate used for specified statutory/penal and long-term signalling purposes | Not identical to policy repo |
| Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) | Share of specified bank liabilities maintained as cash balance with RBI | Cash reserve; earns treatment as prescribed |
| Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR) | Share of specified liabilities maintained in approved liquid assets | Held by banks in eligible assets |
| Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL) | Net specified deposit and liability base used for reserve requirements | CRR/SLR are applied to NDTL under rules |
| Open Market Operation (OMO) | Outright RBI purchase/sale of government securities | Purchase injects durable liquidity; sale absorbs it, other things equal |
| Variable Rate Repo (VRR) | Auction-based liquidity injection at market-determined cut-off rate | Tenor and amount are operation-specific |
| Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRRR) | Auction-based liquidity absorption | Distinct from fixed standing facilities |
| Sterilisation | Offset of domestic liquidity effect of foreign-exchange intervention | Can use OMOs or other liquidity tools |
| Monetary transmission | Process from policy/liquidity changes to market rates, bank rates, credit, demand, output and inflation | Occurs with lags and frictions |
| Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) | Statutory committee deciding policy rate to pursue mandated objectives | Distinguish rate decision from RBI's other regulatory powers |
| Inflation targeting | Framework orienting monetary policy around a publicly specified inflation target | Does not mean ignoring growth/financial stability |
| Accommodative stance | Policy orientation supporting liquidity/financial conditions and growth | Exact RBI wording and operational meaning must be date-checked |
| Tightening | Raising policy restraint or reducing monetary accommodation | Can occur through rates, liquidity or guidance |
| Neutral stance | No preset directional commitment beyond incoming data/objectives | Not equivalent to no policy action |
| Quantitative easing | Large-scale central-bank asset purchase when conventional easing is constrained | Not every OMO is QE |
| Yield curve | Relationship between yields and maturities of comparable debt | Shape reflects expectations, term premium and risk |

## 6. Inflation and price indices

| Term | Exam-safe definition | Formula / interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Inflation | Sustained increase in the general price level | Reduces purchasing power of money |
| Inflation rate | Percentage change in a price index | `[(Indext - Indext-1) / Indext-1] x 100` |
| Headline inflation | Inflation in the complete stated consumer/index basket | Includes volatile items |
| Core inflation | Headline inflation excluding specified volatile components under stated methodology | Always state exclusion convention |
| Food inflation | Price change in food component of stated index | Weight differs across indices |
| Fuel inflation | Price change in fuel/energy component | Pass-through may be regulated or taxed |
| Demand-pull inflation | General price pressure from aggregate demand exceeding available supply | Demand management is relevant |
| Cost-push inflation | Price pressure from higher input costs or adverse supply shocks | Tight money cannot directly create supply |
| Imported inflation | Domestic inflation caused by higher import prices or currency depreciation | Depends on pass-through |
| Wage-price spiral | Wages and prices reinforce each other through expectations and bargaining | Not automatic |
| Inflation expectations | Beliefs about future inflation influencing wages, prices, saving and rates | Can make inflation persistent |
| Disinflation | Positive inflation rate falls | Prices still rise, but more slowly |
| Deflation | General price level falls | Can raise real debt burden |
| Reflation | Policy-supported increase in demand/prices from deflationary weakness | Different from uncontrolled inflation |
| Stagflation | High inflation with weak/stagnant output and high unemployment | Difficult policy trade-off |
| Hyperinflation | Extremely rapid, self-reinforcing loss of money's value | No single UPSC-safe universal threshold should be assumed without source |
| Consumer Price Index (CPI) | Weighted index of retail prices faced by a specified consumer population | Used for household inflation |
| Wholesale Price Index (WPI) | Index of wholesale prices of covered goods | Does not represent services like CPI |
| GDP deflator | Price measure for all domestically produced final output in GDP | Basket changes with current production |
| Laspeyres index | Price index using base-period quantity weights | Tends to overstate cost if substitution is strong |
| Paasche index | Price index using current-period quantity weights | Often understates cost relative to fixed basket |
| Index number | Relative measure comparing a variable with a reference period usually set to 100 | Index points differ from percentage change |
| Purchasing power | Quantity of goods/services a unit of money can buy | Inversely related to price level, other things equal |
| Real wage | Nominal wage adjusted for prices | Approx. nominal wage growth minus inflation |

## 7. Banking and financial stability

| Term | Exam-safe definition | Formula / distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial bank | Regulated institution accepting deposits and extending credit/payment services | Creates deposits through lending within constraints |
| Scheduled bank | Bank included in the Second Schedule to the RBI Act subject to specified conditions | Scheduled status is legal, not a size label |
| Universal bank | Bank providing a broad range of commercial and financial services | Differentiated banks face narrower mandates |
| Regional Rural Bank (RRB) | Statutory rural-focused bank with specified ownership and mandate | Distinct from cooperative banks |
| Cooperative bank | Member/cooperative-owned banking institution under applicable banking and cooperative laws | Governance structure differs from commercial bank |
| Small Finance Bank | Differentiated bank focused on inclusion and underserved borrowers | Can accept deposits and lend under licence conditions |
| Payments Bank | Differentiated bank providing deposits/payments within restrictions and generally not undertaking ordinary lending | Not a universal bank |
| NBFC | Company conducting specified financial business without being a bank | Generally cannot accept demand deposits or issue bank cheques |
| Development Finance Institution | Institution focused on long-term development/project finance | Addresses maturity and infrastructure-finance gaps |
| Bank asset | Claim or use of funds, such as loan or investment | Deposits are liabilities to the bank |
| Bank liability | Obligation owed by bank, such as customer deposit or borrowing | Capital absorbs losses but is not ordinary debt |
| Capital adequacy ratio / CRAR | Regulatory capital relative to risk-weighted assets | `(Eligible capital / risk-weighted assets) x 100` |
| Risk-weighted asset | Exposure weighted by regulatory risk category | Not equal to gross asset value |
| Tier 1 capital | Core going-concern capital able to absorb losses | Includes specified common equity and additional components |
| Tier 2 capital | Supplementary gone-concern capital under regulatory rules | Lower loss-absorption quality than core capital |
| Leverage ratio | Tier 1 capital relative to total exposure without risk weighting | `Tier 1 capital / exposure measure x 100` |
| Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) | High-quality liquid assets relative to projected net cash outflows over 30 days | `HQLA / 30-day net cash outflows x 100` |
| Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR) | Available stable funding relative to required stable funding over one-year horizon | Structural liquidity measure |
| Credit risk | Risk borrower/counterparty fails to pay | Major source of bank loss |
| Liquidity risk | Risk institution cannot meet obligations when due without unacceptable loss | Solvent institution can still be illiquid |
| Market risk | Loss from movements in market prices/rates | Includes interest-rate, equity and FX risk |
| Operational risk | Loss from failed processes, people, systems or external events | Includes fraud and cyber incidents |
| Systemic risk | Risk distress spreads and disrupts the financial system/economy | Macroprudential policy addresses system-wide risk |
| Non-Performing Asset (NPA) | Loan/advance no longer generating income under RBI's applicable overdue/IRAC norms | Many non-agricultural advances use a 90-day norm; crop loans have crop-season rules |
| Gross NPA ratio | Gross NPAs relative to gross advances | `(Gross NPAs / gross advances) x 100` |
| Net NPA | Gross NPAs net of specified provisions/adjustments | Ratio uses net advances under regulatory definition |
| Provision | Expense/reserve recognised against expected or identified loan loss | Reduces reported profit and cushions loss |
| Provision Coverage Ratio | Provisions held against gross NPAs | `(Provisions / gross NPAs) x 100`, subject to stated adjustments |
| Write-off | Accounting removal of loan from active balance sheet | Does not automatically extinguish recovery claim |
| Loan waiver | Policy/legal cancellation of borrower's repayment obligation as specified | Different from write-off |
| Restructuring | Change in loan terms to address stress or restore viability | May involve tenor, rate or repayment schedule |
| Insolvency | Inability to pay debts or liabilities exceeding realisable value under applicable test | Distinguish cash-flow and balance-sheet insolvency |
| Insolvency resolution | Collective legal process to reorganise or liquidate a distressed debtor | Distinct from ordinary recovery action |
| Deposit insurance | Protection of eligible deposits up to the statutory limit through designated insurer | Coverage limit and categories are current-law facts |
| Moral hazard | Protection encourages greater risk because costs are partly shifted to others | Regulation/pricing can mitigate it |
| Adverse selection | Higher-risk participants are more likely to seek a contract when information is asymmetric | Screening reduces it |
| Credit-deposit ratio | Bank credit relative to deposits | `(Bank credit / aggregate deposits) x 100` |
| Net interest margin | Net interest income relative to average earning assets | `(Interest income - interest expense) / average earning assets x 100` |
| Return on Assets (ROA) | Profit relative to average total assets | Indicator of bank profitability |
| Priority Sector Lending (PSL) | Directed lending obligations to specified sectors/categories | Targets and categories require current RBI verification |

## 8. Financial markets and instruments

| Term | Exam-safe definition | Formula / distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Financial market | Mechanism connecting savers, borrowers and risk-transfer participants | Includes money and capital markets |
| Money market | Market for short-term funds/instruments | Liquidity management focus |
| Capital market | Market for medium/long-term debt and equity | Capital formation focus |
| Primary market | Market where new securities are issued | Funds flow to issuer |
| Secondary market | Trading of existing securities | Provides liquidity and price discovery |
| Equity share | Residual ownership claim in a company | Return through dividends/capital gain, not fixed |
| Debt security | Contractual claim to interest/principal | Has priority over equity in insolvency subject to law |
| Bond | Tradable debt instrument promising specified cash flows | Price and yield generally move inversely |
| Face value | Principal amount on which coupon is stated | Market price can differ |
| Coupon rate | Annual coupon relative to face value | `Annual coupon / face value x 100` |
| Current yield | Annual coupon relative to current market price | `Annual coupon / market price x 100` |
| Yield to maturity (YTM) | Discount rate equating bond price with present value of promised cash flows if held to maturity and assumptions hold | Includes coupon and capital gain/loss |
| Bond duration | Measure of sensitivity of bond price to yield changes and weighted timing of cash flows | Higher duration generally means greater interest-rate sensitivity |
| Treasury bill | Short-term government discount instrument | Issued below face value and redeemed at par |
| Government security (G-sec) | Sovereign debt instrument issued by government | Carries interest-rate risk even if default risk is low |
| Corporate bond | Debt issued by company | Yield includes credit/liquidity risk premium |
| Debenture | Company debt instrument whose security status depends on issue terms | Not automatically unsecured in every jurisdiction/use |
| Commercial Paper (CP) | Unsecured short-term money-market instrument issued by eligible corporates/entities | Credit-quality dependent |
| Certificate of Deposit (CD) | Negotiable time deposit issued by eligible banks/financial institutions | Money-market instrument |
| Call money | Overnight unsecured interbank funds | Notice/term money have longer short maturities |
| Repo market | Collateralised short-term borrowing/lending through securities sale-repurchase | Distinct from unsecured call money |
| Derivative | Contract whose value derives from an underlying asset, rate or index | Used for hedging, speculation or arbitrage |
| Forward | Customised OTC agreement for future transaction at agreed price | Counterparty risk is bilateral |
| Futures | Standardised exchange-traded forward-like contract with margining | Marked to market |
| Option | Right, not obligation, to buy or sell at strike price | Buyer pays premium |
| Swap | Agreement to exchange cash-flow streams | Interest-rate and currency swaps are common |
| Hedging | Reducing exposure to adverse price movement | Can limit upside as cost of risk reduction |
| Speculation | Taking risk to profit from expected price movement | Adds risk rather than offsets existing exposure |
| Arbitrage | Exploiting price differences for near-riskless profit | Tends to align prices |
| Mutual fund | Pooled investment vehicle investing according to stated mandate | Investor holds units, not direct ownership of each security |
| Net Asset Value (NAV) | Per-unit value of fund assets net of liabilities | `(Assets - liabilities) / units outstanding` |
| Exchange-Traded Fund (ETF) | Pooled fund traded on exchange, often tracking an index | Market price may differ slightly from NAV |
| Price-Earnings ratio | Market price per share relative to earnings per share | `Market price / EPS` |
| Earnings per share (EPS) | Profit attributable to equity holders per weighted average share | Stated accounting convention matters |
| Market capitalisation | Market value of company's outstanding equity | `Share price x outstanding shares` |
| Initial Public Offering (IPO) | First public issue of a company's shares | Primary-market event |
| Rights issue | New shares offered to existing shareholders in proportion to holdings | Can raise fresh equity |
| Buyback | Company repurchases its own shares | Reduces outstanding shares subject to law |
| Short selling | Selling borrowed security expecting to repurchase at lower price | Subject to market rules |
| Insider trading | Trading while possessing material non-public information in breach of law/duty | Distinct from informed public analysis |

## 9. Public finance, Budget and deficits

| Term | Exam-safe definition | Formula / distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Fiscal policy | Government use of taxation, expenditure and borrowing to affect economy and public objectives | Operates through allocation, distribution and stabilisation |
| Union Budget | Annual statement and associated proposals concerning government receipts and expenditure | Constitutional/statutory terminology should be used precisely |
| Revenue receipt | Receipt not creating liability or reducing government assets | Includes tax and non-tax revenue |
| Capital receipt | Receipt creating liability or reducing assets | Borrowing and disinvestment are common examples |
| Revenue expenditure | Expenditure not ordinarily creating assets or reducing liabilities under budget classification | Economic effect may still be productive |
| Capital expenditure | Expenditure creating assets or reducing liabilities under classification | Includes capital formation and specified loans/equity |
| Revenue deficit | Excess of revenue expenditure over revenue receipts | `Revenue expenditure - revenue receipts` |
| Effective revenue deficit | Revenue deficit minus grants for creation of capital assets | `Revenue deficit - grants for capital asset creation` |
| Fiscal deficit | Total expenditure minus revenue receipts and non-debt capital receipts | Government's gross borrowing requirement |
| Primary deficit | Fiscal deficit minus interest payments | `Fiscal deficit - interest payments` |
| Gross fiscal deficit | Fiscal deficit before netting specified recoveries under stated convention | Check document convention |
| Budget Estimate (BE) | Amount projected in Budget for coming fiscal year | Not actual expenditure |
| Revised Estimate (RE) | Updated estimate during fiscal year | Still not final actual |
| Actual / provisional actual | Recorded outcome, provisional until finalised | Do not compare BE with actual without label |
| Public debt | Outstanding liabilities arising from government borrowing under stated coverage | Stock, unlike deficit flow |
| Debt-GDP ratio | Public debt relative to nominal GDP | `(Debt / nominal GDP) x 100` |
| Tax-GDP ratio | Tax revenue relative to nominal GDP | `(Tax revenue / GDP) x 100` |
| Tax buoyancy | Responsiveness of tax revenue to GDP | `% change in tax revenue / % change in nominal GDP` |
| Tax elasticity | Responsiveness of tax revenue to income after removing discretionary tax changes | Differs from observed buoyancy |
| Direct tax | Tax whose legal burden is intended to fall directly on taxpayer | Economic incidence can still shift |
| Indirect tax | Tax imposed on transactions/goods/services and potentially passed through prices | Legal payer and economic bearer can differ |
| Progressive tax | Average tax rate rises with taxable base/income | Regressive tax burdens lower incomes proportionately more |
| Proportional tax | Constant tax rate across base | Average and marginal rate remain same in simple form |
| Tax incidence | Final economic distribution of tax burden | Statutory incidence identifies legal remitter |
| Tax expenditure | Revenue foregone through exemptions, deductions or preferential treatment | Functions like off-budget policy support |

### HDI calculation

| Dimension | Indicator | Calculation |
|---|---|---|
| Health | Life expectancy at birth | `Ihealth = (actual life expectancy - minimum) / (maximum - minimum)` |
| Education | Mean years of schooling (MYS) and expected years of schooling (EYS) | `IMYS = (actual MYS - minimum) / (maximum - minimum)`; similarly for `IEYS` |
| Education index | Equal arithmetic mean of the two education indicator indices | `Ieducation = (IMYS + IEYS) / 2` |
| Standard of living | GNI per capita in purchasing-power-parity terms | `Iincome = [ln(actual GNIpc) - ln(minimum)] / [ln(maximum) - ln(minimum)]` |
| HDI | Geometric mean of the three dimension indices | `HDI = (Ihealth x Ieducation x Iincome)^(1/3)` |

- The logarithm in the income index represents diminishing human-development returns from
  additional income.
- Goalposts, PPP base and source data belong to the relevant UNDP Human Development Report
  technical note and should be checked for the edition being quoted.
- HDI measures average achievement; it does not directly show inequality, poverty, gender
  disparity, human security or environmental sustainability.

### IHDI calculation

For each HDI dimension `d`, UNDP estimates inequality in the distribution of that
dimension using an Atkinson-type measure:

```text
Ad = 1 - (geometric mean of individual achievements /
          arithmetic mean of individual achievements)

I*d = Id x (1 - Ad)

IHDI = (I*health x I*education x I*income)^(1/3)

Loss due to inequality = [1 - (IHDI / HDI)] x 100
```

| Expression | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `Ad` | Inequality measure for dimension `d`; greater inequality produces a larger discount |
| `Id` | Unadjusted HDI dimension index |
| `I*d` | Distribution-adjusted dimension index |
| `IHDI / HDI` | Share of potential human development retained after accounting for inequality |
| `1 - IHDI/HDI` | Percentage loss in potential human development due to inequality |

**Interpretation:** HDI is the potential level of human development under equal distribution
of achievements; IHDI represents the distribution-sensitive level. A country can therefore
have a relatively high HDI but a substantial inequality loss.

### MPI calculation: Alkire-Foster method

For person or household `i`, assign deprivation status `gij = 1` when deprived in indicator
`j`, otherwise `0`. Multiply each status by indicator weight `wj`:

```text
Weighted deprivation score: ci = sum(wj x gij)
Multidimensionally poor: ci >= poverty cutoff k
Headcount ratio: H = q / n
Intensity among the poor: A = sum(ci for poor persons) / q
MPI = H x A
```

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `wj` | Weight of indicator `j`; all indicator weights sum to 1 |
| `ci` | Weighted deprivation score of person or household `i` |
| `k` | Multidimensional-poverty cutoff; global and Indian national MPI use `k = 1/3` |
| `q` | Number of multidimensionally poor persons |
| `n` | Total population |
| `H` | Incidence: proportion of people who are multidimensionally poor |
| `A` | Intensity: average weighted deprivation score among the poor |
| `MPI` | Adjusted headcount ratio combining how many are poor and how poor they are |

#### Global MPI: 3 dimensions and 10 indicators

| Dimension | Indicator | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Health | Nutrition | `1/6` |
| Health | Child mortality | `1/6` |
| Education | Years of schooling | `1/6` |
| Education | School attendance | `1/6` |
| Standard of living | Cooking fuel | `1/18` |
| Standard of living | Sanitation | `1/18` |
| Standard of living | Drinking water | `1/18` |
| Standard of living | Electricity | `1/18` |
| Standard of living | Housing | `1/18` |
| Standard of living | Assets | `1/18` |

- Each of the three dimensions has weight `1/3`; indicators divide their dimension's weight.
- A weighted deprivation score of at least `1/3` identifies multidimensional poverty.
- In the global MPI, a score from `1/5` to below `1/3` denotes vulnerability, while a score
  of at least `1/2` denotes severe multidimensional poverty.
- Exact deprivation cutoffs—for example, what counts as inadequate schooling, nutrition,
  sanitation or assets—must be taken from the applicable UNDP-OPHI methodology and survey.

#### India's National MPI: 3 dimensions and 12 indicators

| Dimension | Indicator | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Health | Nutrition | `1/6` |
| Health | Child and adolescent mortality | `1/12` |
| Health | Maternal health | `1/12` |
| Education | Years of schooling | `1/6` |
| Education | School attendance | `1/6` |
| Standard of living | Cooking fuel | `1/21` |
| Standard of living | Sanitation | `1/21` |

- A person is nationally MPI-poor when the household's weighted deprivation score is at
  least `33.33%`.
- The national index uses NFHS household microdata and supports national, State/UT and
  district-level diagnosis. Quote the survey period separately from the report publication year.

## 19. Common traps

| Trap | Correct rule |
|---|---|
| Nominal GDP growth equals real growth | Nominal growth combines real-volume and price change |
| GDP measures welfare | GDP measures production; distribution, unpaid work, leisure and environmental damage require separate indicators |
| GDP and GVA are interchangeable | GDP adds net product taxes to GVA at basic prices |
| GNP/GNI is territory-based | GNI is resident-based; GDP is domestic-territory-based |
| HDI and IHDI are interchangeable | HDI shows average potential achievement; IHDI discounts each dimension for unequal distribution |
| MPI is only the percentage of poor people | `H` measures incidence; MPI also includes intensity through `MPI = H x A` |
| Global MPI and India's national MPI use identical indicators | Global MPI has 10 indicators; India's national MPI has 12, adding maternal health and bank account |
| Every indicator in India's Health dimension has equal weight | Nutrition is `1/6`; child/adolescent mortality and maternal health are `1/12` each |
| M0 is currency with public only | M0 also includes bankers' and other deposits with RBI |
| M1 includes time deposits | Time deposits enter M3, not M1 |
| Money multiplier is always `1/CRR` | That is a restrictive textbook case; currency/excess reserves, capital, risk and demand matter |
| RBI creates all M3 directly | RBI creates base money; banking/public behaviour expands or contracts broader money |
| Repo-rate cut automatically raises loans | Transmission depends on funding, risk, capital and credit demand |
| CRR and SLR are identical | CRR is cash balance with RBI; SLR is approved liquid assets held by banks |
| Write-off means waiver | Write-off is accounting; waiver extinguishes obligation as specified |
| Capital receipt means government income | Capital receipts often create debt or reduce assets |
| Fiscal deficit equals total debt | Deficit is annual flow; debt is accumulated stock |
| Revenue expenditure is always wasteful | Classification does not determine social/economic productivity |
| Currency depreciation always improves trade balance | Elasticities, import dependence, contracts and J-curve matter |
| FDI is always stable and beneficial | Sector, terms, profit repatriation, technology and competition determine effects |
| Account ownership equals financial inclusion | Usage, suitable products, protection and resilience are required |
| Installed power capacity equals generation | Generation depends on utilisation, fuel/resource availability and grid conditions |
| MSP means every farmer can sell every quantity at MSP | Procurement is crop-, region-, quality- and operation-dependent |
| Disinvestment always means privatisation | Minority sale may not transfer management control |
| Inflation falling means prices falling | Falling inflation is disinflation; prices still rise unless inflation becomes negative |

## 20. Answer-writing rule

For every Economy term:

```text
Definition -> formula/composition -> transmission mechanism
-> distributional effect -> limitation -> dated Indian evidence -> reform
```

Always write the **unit, period, coverage, source and vintage** beside a number. Never compare
stocks with flows, current prices with constant prices, Budget Estimates with actuals, or
different base-year series without an official bridge.
