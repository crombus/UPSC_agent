## 3. Core identities and formula sheet

| Identity | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `GDP = C + I + G + (X - M)` | Expenditure on domestic final output |
| `NDP = GDP - depreciation` | Net domestic output |
| `GNP/GNI = GDP + NFIA` | Resident income after net factor income from abroad |
| `NNP = GNP - depreciation` | Net national product |
| `GDP at market prices = GVA at basic prices + product taxes - product subsidies` | Producer value added converted to purchaser-price output |
| `Nominal GDP = current prices` | Includes price and quantity change |
| `Real GDP = constant prices` | Attempts to isolate quantity change |
| `GDP deflator = nominal GDP / real GDP x 100` | Broad domestic-output price measure |
| `Fiscal deficit = total expenditure - non-borrowing receipts` | Government borrowing requirement |
| `Revenue deficit = revenue expenditure - revenue receipts` | Revenue-account gap |
| `Primary deficit = fiscal deficit - interest payments` | Current borrowing excluding inherited interest burden |
| `Effective revenue deficit = revenue deficit - grants for creation of capital assets` | Adjusted revenue gap |
| `Current account = goods + services + primary income + transfers` | External current-flow balance |
| `BoP = current account + capital/financial account + errors/omissions + reserve change convention` | Complete external transactions accounting |
| `Real interest rate approximately nominal rate - inflation` | Purchasing-power return/cost |

> **Prelims rule:** A flow is measured over a period; a stock is measured at a point in time. GDP, deficit and exports are flows; debt, reserves and capital stock are stocks.

## 4. National income and measurement

| Pair | Correct distinction |
|---|---|
| Domestic vs national | Territory-based production vs resident income |
| Gross vs net | Before vs after depreciation |
| Market price vs basic price | Purchaser taxes/subsidies included vs producer-oriented valuation |
| Nominal vs real | Current prices vs constant prices |
| GDP vs GVA | Final domestic output vs value added by producers |
| GDP vs welfare | Production measure vs broader well-being |

### Three measurement methods

| Method | Adds |
|---|---|
| Production/value-added | Value added across producers |
| Income | Wages, rent, interest, profits/mixed income within accounting framework |
| Expenditure | Final consumption, investment, government spending and net exports |

### Included/excluded logic

- include final market production and imputed items recognised by national accounts;
- exclude intermediate goods to avoid double counting;
- exclude pure transfers because no current production is received in exchange;
- include second-hand transaction only to the extent of current brokerage/service;
- unpaid household services are generally outside conventional GDP, though own-account production treatment varies by accounting rules;
- environmental depletion, inequality and leisure are not adequately captured by GDP.

> **Series trap:** A base-year revision can change weights, sources, coverage and methods. Do not splice old and new series without an official comparable back-series.

## 5. Growth, development and human capability

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Economic growth | Increase in real output/income |
| Economic development | Structural and welfare transformation |
| Inclusive growth | Broad participation in and benefit from growth |
| Sustainable development | Present welfare without undermining future capability |
| Human development | Expansion of health, education and living-standard capabilities |
| HDI | Composite of health, education and income dimensions |
| IHDI | HDI adjusted for inequality in dimensions |
| MPI | Overlapping household deprivation across specified indicators |
| Productivity | Output per unit of input |
| Potential output | Sustainable output consistent with available capacity |

### Growth-to-development chain

`productivity and output -> income and revenue -> jobs/services/investment -> capability`

This chain fails with:

`jobless growth + inequality + weak public services + environmental damage + regional exclusion`

> **Core verdict:** Growth is necessary for durable development finance, but its employment intensity, distribution, public-service conversion and ecological cost determine development quality.

## 6. Inflation and business cycles

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Inflation | Sustained rise in general price level |
| Disinflation | Inflation remains positive but slows |
| Deflation | General price level falls |
| Reflation | Policy-induced recovery from weak demand/deflation |
| Stagflation | High inflation with stagnant growth/high unemployment |
| Core inflation | Headline measure excluding selected volatile items under stated methodology |
| Demand-pull | Aggregate demand exceeds available supply |
| Cost-push | Input/supply shock raises production cost |
| Imported inflation | Foreign price/currency movement raises domestic cost |

### Inflation transmission

`shock -> wholesale/input cost or demand -> producer pricing -> retail prices -> expectations/wages -> persistence`

| Index | Main use/distinction |
|---|---|
| CPI | Retail consumer basket; household inflation |
| WPI | Wholesale-goods price movement; services generally not represented like CPI |
| GDP deflator | All domestically produced final output in GDP, with changing composition |

### Distribution

- borrowers may gain and fixed-income savers may lose from unexpected inflation;
- food inflation disproportionately affects poorer households;
- moderate demand inflation and supply-shock inflation require different responses;
- monetary tightening cannot directly produce food or energy, though it can contain second-round demand and expectations.

## 7. RBI, monetary policy and liquidity

### Transmission chain

`policy rate/liquidity -> money-market rates -> bank funding/deposit/lending rates -> credit, demand, investment and exchange rate -> output and inflation`

| Tool | Core effect |
|---|---|
| Repo/policy rate | Price of short-term central-bank liquidity under framework |
| Standing deposit facility | Absorbs liquidity without collateral under applicable framework |
| Marginal standing facility | Overnight bank borrowing backstop at penal rate |
| CRR | Share of bank liabilities maintained as cash balance with RBI |
| SLR | Prescribed liquid-asset holding by banks |
| Open-market operations | Outright purchase/sale of government securities affects durable liquidity |
| Variable-rate repo/reverse repo | Auction-based liquidity injection/absorption |
| Moral suasion/regulatory action | Influences conduct and credit conditions |

### Policy stance logic

| Action | Usual direction, other things equal |
|---|---|
| Rate increase/liquidity tightening | Credit/demand cools; currency may receive support; transmission has lags |
| Rate reduction/liquidity easing | Credit/demand supported, subject to risk and banking transmission |

> **Trap:** Monetary policy operates through transmission and expectations; a repo-rate change does not mechanically or immediately alter every loan, deposit, price or exchange rate.

## 8. Banking, NBFCs and financial regulation

| Institution | Distinctive feature |
|---|---|
| Commercial bank | Accepts deposits and creates credit within regulation |
| Cooperative bank | Cooperative ownership with banking regulation and relevant dual institutional context |
| Regional Rural Bank | Rural-focused scheduled banking institution under its legal framework |
| Small finance bank | Inclusion-focused differentiated bank |
| Payments bank | Restricted banking model; cannot undertake full lending like universal bank |
| NBFC | Financial intermediation without being a bank; cannot generally accept demand deposits/issue cheques like banks |
| Development finance institution | Long-term/project finance orientation |

### Bank balance-sheet logic

`deposits/borrowings + capital -> loans/investments/reserves`

| Risk | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Credit risk | Borrower fails to repay |
| Liquidity risk | Institution cannot meet near-term obligations |
| Interest-rate risk | Value/margin changes with rates |
| Market risk | Loss from traded price movement |
| Operational risk | Process, system, fraud or external-event failure |
| Systemic risk | Failure spreads across financial system |

> **Core rule:** Regulation must balance stability, competition, innovation and inclusion; identical rules for unequal risk profiles can be inefficient, but regulatory arbitrage is dangerous.

## 9. NPAs, resolution and inclusion

### Stress chain

`poor appraisal / shock / diversion / project delay -> repayment stress -> NPA -> provisioning and capital erosion -> weaker lending -> growth effect`

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| NPA | Loan ceases to generate income under regulatory overdue criteria |
| Provisioning | Recognising expected loss against profit/capital |
| Write-off | Accounting removal from active balance sheet; does not automatically waive recovery |
| Waiver | Borrower's repayment obligation legally/policy-wise extinguished as specified |
| Restructuring | Terms modified for viable stressed exposure |
| Insolvency resolution | Time-bound creditor/debtor process under applicable law |
| Capital adequacy | Capital cushion relative to risk-weighted assets |

### Financial inclusion ladder

`account/access -> regular use -> suitable credit/savings/insurance/pension -> consumer protection -> resilience`

> **Trap:** Account opening is not complete inclusion; dormant accounts, unsuitable credit, fraud, low literacy and weak grievance systems matter.

## 12. Budget, fiscal policy and deficits

### Budget architecture

| Account/item | Core identity |
|---|---|
| Revenue receipt | Does not create liability or reduce asset |
| Capital receipt | Creates liability or reduces financial asset |
| Revenue expenditure | Does not directly create government asset/reduce liability under classification |
| Capital expenditure | Creates asset or reduces liability |
| Charged expenditure | Not voted, though discussable |
| Voted expenditure | Submitted through demands for grants |

### Fiscal transmission

`tax/spending/borrowing -> disposable income and demand + public investment and supply capacity + debt/interest/crowding effects`

| Deficit interpretation | Safe rule |
|---|---|
| Large fiscal deficit | Not automatically bad; inspect cycle, financing and expenditure quality |
| Revenue deficit | Signals dissaving/current expenditure exceeding revenue receipts |
| Primary deficit | Shows current fiscal gap net of interest burden |
| Capital expenditure | Often raises capacity, but project quality and completion matter |

### Fiscal multiplier

Impact depends on:

`economic slack + import leakage + monetary response + spending type + confidence + implementation speed`

> **Vintage trap:** BE, RE, provisional actual and final actual are different. Never compare unlike stages as if realised outcomes.

## 13. Taxation, GST and fiscal federalism

| Pair | Correct distinction |
|---|---|
| Direct vs indirect tax | Legal/economic incidence intended directly on person vs tax on transaction shifted through price |
| Progressive vs proportional vs regressive | Rate burden rises, remains constant or falls with income |
| Tax avoidance vs evasion | Lawful arrangement within rules vs illegal concealment |
| Tax buoyancy vs elasticity | Revenue response with policy changes vs response excluding discretionary changes |
| Vertical imbalance | Revenue/expenditure mismatch across government levels |
| Horizontal imbalance | Unequal capacity/need among states |

### GST chain

`destination-based value-added tax -> input-tax credit -> reduced cascading -> integrated market`

Constraints:

`rate complexity + compliance burden + classification disputes + refunds + state-revenue concerns + informal transition`

### Finance Commission

- constitutional periodic body recommending specified Union-state fiscal transfers;
- tax devolution and grants are distinct;
- Finance Commission, GST Council and centrally sponsored schemes perform different fiscal-federal roles.

> **Trap:** A cess/surcharge and shareable divisible-pool tax treatment are not identical; apply current constitutional/statutory rules rather than intuition.

MSP ANNOUNCEMENT

 -> actual procurement in selected crops/regions

 -> allocation and PDS/other disposal
 -> food access and price stabilisation

|---|---|
| MSP | Announced support price; not universal legal procurement guarantee by itself |
| Procurement | Actual government purchase |
| Buffer stock | Stock for food security/price/operational purposes |

`farm-income support + food security` versus `fiscal/storage cost + crop distortion + regional concentration + ecological stress`

## 19. Industrial policy, reforms and disinvestment

### 1991 reform triad

`liberalisation + privatisation/disinvestment + globalisation`

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Liberalisation | Reduce licensing/control and enable competition |
| Privatisation | Transfer ownership/control toward private sector |
| Disinvestment | Government sells part/all equity; need not always transfer control |
| Strategic sale | Substantial stake with management control under transaction |
| Asset monetisation | Unlock value from existing public assets without necessarily selling ownership permanently |

### Industrial-policy objectives

`scale + productivity + technology + jobs + exports + regional development + strategic capability`

> **Balanced verdict:** Markets improve allocation and innovation, but competition policy, infrastructure, skills, finance, environmental rules and social transition determine outcomes.

## 22. Balance of payments and exchange rates

| Component | Includes |
|---|---|
| Current account | Goods, services, primary income and transfers |
| Capital/financial account | Capital transfers and financial flows under accounting presentation |

### Exchange-rate vocabulary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|

## 32. Animal rearing and allied sectors

| Sector | Economic significance | Core constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Dairy | Regular cash flow, nutrition and women’s work | Low productivity, feed, health and market power |
| Livestock | Asset, manure, draught/meat/fibre and risk buffer | Disease, breed/feed and extension |
| Poultry | Short production cycle and protein | Disease, feed-price and contract concentration |
| Fisheries/aquaculture | Nutrition, exports and livelihoods | Stock sustainability, disease and cold chain |

### Value chain

`breed/seed + feed + health + production + aggregation + chilling/cold chain + processing + market`

### Integrated farming

`crop residues -> livestock feed -> manure/biogas -> soil/fish/energy -> diversified income and risk`

> **Trap:** Allied-sector growth can raise rural income, but ownership, unpaid labour, veterinary access and producer bargaining determine who benefits.

## 33. Energy infrastructure and security

### Energy-security quadrilateral

`availability + affordability + accessibility/reliability + environmental acceptability`

| Segment | Core issue |
|---|---|
| Generation/supply | Capacity and fuel/resource mix |
| Transmission | Grid evacuation and interstate transfer |
| Distribution | Utility finance, losses, metering and service quality |
| Storage/flexibility | Integrating variable renewables and peak demand |
| Fuel logistics | Import dependence, pipelines, ports and strategic stocks |

### Electricity distinctions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Installed capacity | Maximum rated capacity |
| Generation | Actual electricity produced |
| Plant load/capacity factor | Utilisation over time under relevant measure |
| Renewable intermittency | Output varies with resource |
| Grid balancing | Matching supply and demand continuously |

### Transition test

`clean capacity + grid + storage + domestic manufacturing + finance + critical minerals + worker/region transition + reliable affordable supply`

> **Core verdict:** Energy transition is an infrastructure and political-economy transformation, not merely addition of renewable capacity.

## 34. Economic Survey and current-dashboard discipline

Before using any current number, record:

| Field | Required entry |
|---|---|
| Indicator | Exact concept |
| Period | Month, quarter, fiscal/calendar year |
| Unit | %, rupees, index, tonnes, persons etc. |
| Coverage | Centre/state/general government; sector/population |
| Vintage | Estimate, projection, BE, RE, provisional or final |
| Source | Named first-party institution |
| Series/base | Applicable base year/method |
| Inference | What the number does and does not establish |

> **Rule:** The Economic Survey is an analytical annual document and dated data source—not a live dashboard and not proof that every recommendation is government policy.

## 35. High-frequency Prelims eliminators

| Statement pattern | Elimination rule |
|---|---|
| “Always/only/necessarily” | Reject unless definition or law truly makes it exclusive |
| Rate rises, bond price rises | Usually inverse for existing fixed-coupon bond |
| RBI instrument changes government expenditure directly | Monetary and fiscal tools are distinct |
| Capital receipt means asset creation | Receipt classification concerns liability/reduction of asset |
| Disinvestment always means privatisation | Control may remain with government |
| MSP means all crop output procured | Announcement and procurement differ |
| Currency depreciation always improves trade balance | Elasticity, imports and time lags matter |
| Account opened means financial inclusion | Usage, product, protection and resilience remain |
| Higher GDP means higher welfare for everyone | Distribution, externalities and non-market welfare matter |
| Digital platform is currency | Payment rail and money/liability differ |
| Installed renewable capacity equals generation | Capacity and output differ |
| Subsidy is automatically WTO-prohibited | Classification and commitments matter |

## 36. Reusable Mains answer skeletons

### A. Policy transmission

1. Define instrument and objective.
2. Draw price/credit/income/supply channel.
3. State short-run and long-run effects.
4. Identify distribution and federal/sector effects.
5. Add leakage, lag and external constraint.
6. Recommend sequenced calibration and outcome indicator.

### B. Sector reform

1. Establish sector role in output, jobs or security.
2. Map full value chain.
3. Diagnose productivity, finance, market, technology and regulation gaps.
4. Protect small producers/workers and competition.
5. Build infrastructure, skills and measurable value addition.

### C. Growth versus inclusion

1. Explain growth-capability link.
2. Identify job, service and asset channels.
3. Show unequal outcomes/structural barriers.
4. Combine productive investment with universal services and protection.
5. End with mobility, resilience and sustainability.

### D. External shock

1. Identify commodity, trade, finance or exchange-rate channel.
2. Trace inflation/current-account/growth effect.
3. Separate temporary management from structural correction.
4. Diversify source/market and build domestic capability.

### E. Data-based question

1. Define indicator.
2. State period, coverage and vintage.
3. Explain trend without mixing series.
4. Triangulate with related indicator.
5. Avoid converting correlation, allocation or projection into outcome.

7. Calculate revenue, fiscal and primary deficits.
8. Explain MSP-to-PDS chain.
9. Draw farm and infrastructure value chains.
10. Distinguish current account, BoP and reserves.
11. Explain depreciation with one benefit and two caveats.
12. State LFPR, WPR and unemployment-rate denominators.
13. Distinguish UPI, bank money and CBDC.
14. Apply green-finance additionality and just-transition tests.
15. Check period, coverage and vintage before quoting any number.
