## F. Word formation, confusables and exact vocabulary

1. decision  2. alleviate  3. discreet  4. affect  5. stationery
6. principle  7. *abundant*; *plentiful* is accepted in a suitable sense
8. *wise* or *judicious*; another exact formal near-synonym is accepted

## G. Correction and accepted variants

1. **Acceptable.** Singular `their` is accepted modern formal English; `his or
her` or plural recasting is also correct. [VAR]
2. **Acceptable in modern English, but avoid in a conservative correction
test.** Preferred safe rewrite: *The reason is that funding was delayed.* Do
not call the original objectively ungrammatical. [VAR]
3. **Acceptable.** `Angry at the contractor` and `angry with the contractor`
are both standard. [VAR]
4. **Acceptable**, especially in North American usage. Conservative Indian
formal alternatives: *provided the workers with meals* / *provided meals for
the workers*. [VAR]
5. **Acceptable.** The collective noun refers to members acting separately;
the plural pronoun matches. [VAR]

## H. Transformations

1. *No sooner had the meeting ended than the members left.*
2. *Unless you revise, you will forget the rules.*
3. *The box is too heavy for me to lift.*
4. *In spite of being tired, he continued working.* / *In spite of the fact
   that he was tired, he continued working.*
5. *The accused was arrested by the police.* Agent omission is accepted.
6. *Riya was given a prize by the board.* / *A prize was given to Riya by the
   board.*
7. *He said (that) he was ready.* `is ready` may be accepted if the report is
   immediate and the state continues; the context should normally decide.
8. *The teacher asked/told Riya not to leave early.*
9. *The guide said that the Earth moves around the Sun.*
10. *Ravi solved only the problem.* Award only if the revised sentence clearly
    limits Ravi's action/object rather than the solver.

## I. Meaning, use and tags

For 1–5, award one mark only for both a correct meaning and natural use:

1. *at sea* = confused/uncertain.
2. *a thorn in the flesh* = persistent source of irritation.
3. *move heaven and earth* = make every possible effort.
4. *A stitch in time saves nine* = prompt action prevents a larger problem.
5. *All that glitters is not gold* = appearances can mislead.

6. *won't they?*  7. *shall we?*  8. *is she?*  9. *will you?*
10. *isn't there?*

## Scoring and diagnosis

Score **85**. For B–H, withhold credit only for a changed meaning, a
grammatical error, or a form outside the acceptance guidance above. Record the
question number and error code. The retry thresholds in the test apply to the
revised 85-mark total as follows: **77–85** proceed; **68–76** targeted retry;
**below 68** or a critical-section zero requires Core review and a fresh timed
attempt. Two consecutive 77+ attempts are required before the full mock.

### Prompt-specific planning anchors

These are planning routes, not memorisable essays. No Core model answers any Mock 1 prompt; score the script by the band descriptors rather than phrase matching.

| Prompt | Qualified thesis | Four distinct moves |
|---|---|---|
| Convenience without responsibility… | Convenience can improve access, but citizenship weakens when people enjoy public benefits while disregarding their shared costs. | access; hidden costs; habits of responsibility; fair institutional design |
| Efficiency is not the same as effectiveness. | Efficiency conserves time and resources, but effectiveness asks whether the intended public or human outcome has actually been achieved. | distinction; useful speed; proxy/measurement risk; outcome-based correction |

**Model-quality paragraph, not a prewritten essay:** *An office may process a large number of applications quickly and still fail the people it serves. If applicants receive automatic messages but cannot correct an error, the system has become efficient at movement rather than effective at service. Speed remains valuable because delay has costs; however, the relevant test is whether the application reaches a fair and usable outcome. Measures should therefore track completion and correction, not merely disposal.* This models claim → mechanism → illustration → qualification. It must be adapted, not copied.

## Q2. Model points and practice splits

1. Construction produces a visible asset **(5)** and an occasion for inauguration **(4)**, whereas maintenance is repetitive and dispersed **(3)** and therefore less publicly celebrated **(3)**.
2. Small defects first reduce a system’s resilience **(5)**. Under heavy rain, high demand or emergency, weaknesses interact **(5)**; the apparent sudden failure is thus accumulated ordinary neglect, not a single event **(5)**.
3. **Financial (7):** capital and maintenance are planned separately **(3)**; funds for staff, parts, supervision and renewal are unpredictable **(4)**. **Institutional (8):** responsibility is fragmented **(3)**, agencies assume another will act **(2)**, and no official has both authority and incentive to repair the defect **(3)**.
4. It means a system records acknowledgement or a registration number while no repair occurs **(7)**. User information becomes effective only with a simple channel **(2)**, acknowledgement **(2)** and published or visible corrective action **(4)**.
5. **Title:** *“Maintenance Is Public Service”*, *“The Ethics of Maintenance”*, or another title covering the central claim **(4)**. The attitude is strongly approving and corrective **(2)**: maintenance protects past expenditure and prevents harm **(4)**, extends scarce assets **(2)** and is equitable for people without private alternatives **(3)**.

## Q3. Model précis

**Count record:** source **458** words; one-third target **about 153** words; model **155** words. The count excludes the candidate instruction to record rough-work counts. This is a practice calculation, not a published tolerance.

Modern life often equates speed with competence because rapid communication and delivery can save lives, cut costs and connect people. Yet valuable decisions require attention, verification and judgement. In medicine, justice and journalism, action without adequate evidence can cause harm that later correction cannot undo. Digital systems intensify this risk by making approval, sharing and acceptance effortless while leaving responsibility unchanged. Institutions must distinguish idle delay, caused by neglect or pointless procedure, from deliberation, which gathers evidence, compares claims and records reasons. They should remove waiting but protect stages where thought improves decisions. They must also ensure that difficult cases are not discarded merely to improve figures. Measures based only on closures, examinations or turnover can reward superficial completion instead of real outcomes. Individuals also need pauses before forwarding claims, reacting in anger or making irreversible choices. The alternative to haste is not procrastination but disciplined timeliness: acting when knowledge, authority and safeguards are sufficient.

Accept accurate, coherent versions close to the instructed proportion. Deduct diagnostically for omitted concessions, unsupported additions, copied wording, disconnected notes, wrong title instruction or material count failure.

## PART 03 — 03 Full-Length-Mock-2-Key

## Q1. Essay rubric

Use the Mock 1 anchored bands. A high-band answer decodes the exact proposition, states a qualified thesis in the introduction, develops distinct mechanisms, tests a real limitation, uses safe examples analytically and finishes near 600 words with a reasoned verdict.

### Planning anchors

| Prompt | Defensible qualified thesis |
|---|---|
| Rules acquire legitimacy… | Enforcement is necessary, but a rule gains durable legitimacy when people can understand its purpose, application and route of challenge. |
| The ability to listen… | Listening is public responsibility because decisions affect people unequally; it requires conditions for informed dissent, not passive silence. |
| Progress should enlarge… | Progress is incomplete if more options are offered without the education, time and institutions needed for considered judgement. |
| A city is made… | Buildings enable urban life, but shared habits of care, rule-following and coexistence determine whether the city remains liveable. |

## Q2. Model points and practice splits

1. The data must be accurate **(3)**, carefully interpreted **(4)**, connected to a decision **(4)**, and owned by someone responsible for making that decision **(4)**.
2. A record can be factually accurate yet measure an inadequate proxy rather than the relevant outcome **(8)**. For example, attendance does not establish learning, or clinic visits do not establish completed treatment **(7)**.
3. Numbers are simplified representations and should not be mistaken for the fuller lived condition they measure **(8)**. In public services this can reward an attractive number while excluding unmeasured harm, especially for people unable to complain or switch services **(7)**.
4. The tone is **qualified and cautionary**, not anti-data **(3)**. Targets can clarify purpose and improve performance **(6)**, but can distort behaviour when the number is rewarded instead of the purpose **(6)**.
5. Suitable titles include *“Measure Without Losing Meaning”* or *“Data Needs Judgement”* **(4)**. The examples show, across education, health and transport, that a single number can miss the service outcome it is meant to represent **(6)**; they make the abstract warning concrete **(3)** and demonstrate that the problem applies across public institutions **(2)**.

## Q3. Model précis

**Count record:** source **246** words; one-third target **about 82** words; model **83** words.

Silence in public life cannot safely be treated as consent because it may reflect agreement, fear, confusion or exclusion. A formal invitation to comment does not ensure that participants can influence a decision: they may lack confidence, technical language or belief that dissent matters. Listening therefore requires checking understanding, representation, candid formats and minority reasons. Although action cannot await unanimity and may sometimes be urgent, institutions should distinguish informed acceptance from silence caused by exclusion so that firm decisions remain open to correction.
