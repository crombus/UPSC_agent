## A. Function in context

1. noun  2. pronoun  3. adjective  4. adverb  5. verb  6. determiner

## B. Nouns and pronouns

1. *One of the applicants has withdrawn.* [AGR/CNT]
2. *The police are investigating the complaint.* [AGR]
3. *She gave me two useful pieces of advice.* [CNT]
4. *We bought three pieces of furniture for the office.* [CNT]
5. *Between you and me, the proposal is unsafe.* [PRO]
6. *Every applicant must bring their identity card.* [PRO/CNT] The singular
   possessive must match the one card required from each applicant. `his or
   her` and a clear plural recast also receive credit.
7. *The report that identified the error was published yesterday.* [REL]
8. No change: the clause identifies which candidates. [PRO/REL]
9. *The committee has submitted its report as one body.* [AGR]
10. *The scissors are on the table.* [AGR]

## C. Agreement, tense, conditionals, articles and determiners

1. *A number of students are absent.* [AGR]
2. *Neither the manager nor the clerks are ready.* [AGR]
3. *The news is encouraging.* [AGR]
4. *She finished the report yesterday.* [TNS]
5. *By the time we arrived, the meeting had already ended.* [TNS]
6. *I know the answer now.* [TNS]
7. *If I had known, I would have called.* [COND]
8. *If she had accepted the post, she would live in Mumbai now.* [COND]
9. *When I arrive, I will call you.* [TNS]
10. *He went to the hospital to visit his friend.* [ART]
11. *She is a teacher in a village school.* [ART]
12. *Honesty is the best policy.* [ART]
13. No change. `Few` means scarcely any; `a few` means some. [ART]
14. No change: article choice follows sound. [ART]

## D. Prepositions, verb patterns, phrasal verbs, conjunctions and modifiers

1. *She agreed to the proposal.* [PREP]
2. *The officer discussed the proposal.* [PREP]
3. *The unit comprises five sections.* / *The unit consists of five sections.*
   [PREP]
4. *I have been waiting for three hours.* [PREP]
5. *We must comply with the order.* [PREP]
6. *The office provided meals for/to the workers.* [PREP]
7. *She turned it down without explanation.* [PHR]
8. *Although it was late, they continued.* [CONJ]
9. *The job requires planning, coordination and monitoring.* [PAR]
10. *While I was walking to the office, it began to rain.* [MOD] Equivalent
    repairs that give the walker as the modifier’s subject receive credit.

## E. Punctuation, capitals and spelling

1. *After the interview, we returned home.*
2. *The proposal was ambitious; however, it lacked funding.* A full stop before
   `However` is also accepted.
3. *The reports that were incomplete were returned.* No commas: the clause is
   restrictive.
4. *Riya has only one brother, who lives in Pune.*
5. *The causes of the crisis are unemployment, inflation and weak demand.*
   A consistent serial comma is also accepted.
6. *The officer's report was delayed.*
7. *No, the answer is not correct.*
8. *The inquiry had one conclusion: postpone the launch.*
9. *It's important to check every candidate's identity card.*
10. *The accommodation was definitely insufficient.*

## F. Word formation, confusables and exact vocabulary

1. decision  2. alleviate  3. discreet  4. affect  5. stationery
6. principle  7. *abundant*; *plentiful* is accepted in a suitable sense
8. *wise* or *judicious*; another exact formal near-synonym is accepted
9. Award one mark only where the sentence shows “practicable/capable of being
done”: e.g., *The engineer proved that the bridge was feasible within the
budget.*
10. Award one mark only where it means improperly reducing standards to save
time/money: e.g., *The contractor cut corners on safety.*

## G. Correction and accepted variants

1. **Acceptable.** Singular `their` is accepted modern formal English; `his or
her` or plural recasting is also correct. [VAR]
2. **Acceptable in modern English, but avoid in a conservative correction
test.** Preferred safe rewrite: *The reason is that funding was delayed.* Do
not call the original objectively ungrammatical. [VAR]
3. **Acceptable.** `Angry at the contractor` and `angry with the contractor`
are both standard. [VAR]
4. **Acceptable**, especially in North American usage. Conservative Indian
formal alternatives: *provided the workers with meals* / *provided meals for
the workers*. [VAR]
5. **Acceptable.** The collective noun refers to members acting separately;
the plural pronoun matches. [VAR]

## H. Transformations

1. *No sooner had the meeting ended than the members left.*
2. *Unless you revise, you will forget the rules.*
3. *The box is too heavy for me to lift.*
4. *In spite of being tired, he continued working.* / *In spite of the fact
   that he was tired, he continued working.*
5. *The accused was arrested by the police.* Agent omission is accepted.
6. *Riya was given a prize by the board.* / *A prize was given to Riya by the
   board.*
7. *He said (that) he was ready.* `is ready` may be accepted if the report is
   immediate and the state continues; the context should normally decide.
8. *The teacher asked/told Riya not to leave early.*
9. *The guide said that the Earth moves around the Sun.*
10. *Ravi solved only the problem.* Award only if the revised sentence clearly
    limits Ravi's action/object rather than the solver.

## I. Meaning, use and tags

For 1–5, award one mark only for both a correct meaning and natural use:

1. *at sea* = confused/uncertain.
2. *a thorn in the flesh* = persistent source of irritation.
3. *move heaven and earth* = make every possible effort.
4. *A stitch in time saves nine* = prompt action prevents a larger problem.
5. *All that glitters is not gold* = appearances can mislead.

6. *won't they?*  7. *shall we?*  8. *is she?*  9. *will you?*
10. *isn't there?*

## Scoring and diagnosis

Score **85**. For B–H, withhold credit only for a changed meaning, a
grammatical error, or a form outside the acceptance guidance above. Record the
question number and error code. The retry thresholds in the test apply to the
revised 85-mark total as follows: **77–85** proceed; **68–76** targeted retry;
**below 68** or a critical-section zero requires Core review and a fresh timed
attempt. Two consecutive 77+ attempts are required before the full mock.
