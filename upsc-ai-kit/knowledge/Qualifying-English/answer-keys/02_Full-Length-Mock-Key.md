## Q2 Model points

1. Construction creates a visible object and inauguration, whereas maintenance is repetitive, dispersed and less publicly attractive.
2. Small defects reduce resilience; under stress they interact, turning accumulated neglect into apparent sudden failure.
3. Financial causes include separation of capital and maintenance budgets and uncertain funding for staff/parts. Institutional causes include fragmented responsibility and weak authority/incentives.
4. Users supply early, local evidence. It becomes effective when reporting is simple, acknowledged and followed by visible corrective action.
5. Maintenance protects past public expenditure, prevents avoidable harm, extends asset life and protects poorer users who lack private alternatives.

## Q3 Model précis

Modern society often equates speed with competence, although sound decisions require enough time for evidence and judgment. Medicine, justice and journalism show that quick action without care can cause lasting harm. Digital systems further shorten the gap between impulse and action without reducing responsibility. Institutions must therefore separate wasteful delay from useful deliberation: the former reflects neglect or pointless procedure, while the latter gathers evidence, compares claims and records reasons. Performance measures should assess outcomes rather than reward disposal counts that encourage superficial closure. Individuals likewise need deliberate pauses before sharing information, reacting emotionally or making irreversible choices. The answer is neither haste nor indefinite postponement, but disciplined timeliness—acting once necessary knowledge, authority and safeguards are available.

Accept equivalent accurate versions near one-third of the source length.
