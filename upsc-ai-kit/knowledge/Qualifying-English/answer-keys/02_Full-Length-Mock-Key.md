**Model-quality paragraph, not a prewritten essay:** *An office may process a large number of applications quickly and still fail the people it serves. If applicants receive automatic messages but cannot correct an error, the system has become efficient at movement rather than effective at service. Speed remains valuable because delay has costs; however, the relevant test is whether the application reaches a fair and usable outcome. Measures should therefore track completion and correction, not merely disposal.* This models claim → mechanism → illustration → qualification. It must be adapted, not copied.

2. Small defects first reduce a system’s resilience **(5)**. Under heavy rain, high demand or emergency, weaknesses interact **(5)**; the apparent sudden failure is thus accumulated ordinary neglect, not a single event **(5)**.
3. **Financial (7):** capital and maintenance are planned separately **(3)**; funds for staff, parts, supervision and renewal are unpredictable **(4)**. **Institutional (8):** responsibility is fragmented **(3)**, agencies assume another will act **(2)**, and no official has both authority and incentive to repair the defect **(3)**.

## Q3. Model précis

**Count record:** source **458** words; one-third target **about 153** words; model **155** words. The count excludes the candidate instruction to record rough-work counts. This is a practice calculation, not a published tolerance.

Modern life often equates speed with competence because rapid communication and delivery can save lives, cut costs and connect people. Yet valuable decisions require attention, verification and judgement. In medicine, justice and journalism, action without adequate evidence can cause harm that later correction cannot undo. Digital systems intensify this risk by making approval, sharing and acceptance effortless while leaving responsibility unchanged. Institutions must distinguish idle delay, caused by neglect or pointless procedure, from deliberation, which gathers evidence, compares claims and records reasons. They should remove waiting but protect stages where thought improves decisions. They must also ensure that difficult cases are not discarded merely to improve figures. Measures based only on closures, examinations or turnover can reward superficial completion instead of real outcomes. Individuals also need pauses before forwarding claims, reacting in anger or making irreversible choices. The alternative to haste is not procrastination but disciplined timeliness: acting when knowledge, authority and safeguards are sufficient.

Accept accurate, coherent versions close to the instructed proportion. Deduct diagnostically for omitted concessions, unsupported additions, copied wording, disconnected notes, wrong title instruction or material count failure.

### B. Meaning, form and self-marking boundary

| Word | Required form and meaning | Natural model sentence | Accept / reject |
|---|---|---|---|
| prudent | adjective: wisely cautious; showing good judgement | *A prudent applicant kept copies of every document.* | Accept a clear adjective use meaning cautious/judicious; reject “careful” if context does not show judgement. |
| alleviate | transitive verb: make pain, hardship or severity less | *The extra water supply helped alleviate the shortage.* | Accept correct inflections; reject “eliminate completely” or noun use where a verb is required. |
| reluctant | adjective, commonly *reluctant to + verb*: unwilling or hesitant | *Reluctant to sign without evidence, she asked for time.* | Accept unwilling/hesitant; reject “eager” or a sentence where hesitation is absent. |
| impartial | adjective: fair; not favouring either side | *An impartial officer heard both complaints before deciding.* | Accept neutral/fair if the sentence demonstrates equal treatment; reject “uninterested” when it means careless. |
| feasible | adjective: practicable or capable of being done | *The engineer showed that the repair was feasible within the available time.* | Accept practicable/viable; reject merely “desirable” or an adverb/noun substitution. |

### D. Meaning, form and self-marking boundary

| Expression | Meaning and form | Natural model sentence | Accept / reject |
|---|---|---|---|
| at sea | idiomatic adjective phrase: confused or uncertain | *Without the map, I was at sea.* | Accept uncertainty/confusion; reject a literal sea voyage unless the figurative meaning is also clear. |
| cut corners | verb phrase: save time/money improperly by lowering standards | *The contractor cut corners on safety checks.* | Reject efficient economy that does not imply improper reduced standards. |
