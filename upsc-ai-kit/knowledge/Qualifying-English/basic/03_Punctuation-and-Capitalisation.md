# Punctuation and Capitalisation - MUST-DO

## 1. The comma: where to put it

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Items in a series | We need data, staff, finance, and time. |
| After an introductory word/phrase/clause | After the meeting, we left. |
| Before a coordinating conjunction joining two complete sentences | The proposal was sound, but funding was absent. |
| Around non-essential information | The chairperson, who arrived late, apologised. |
| Direct address | Riya, please read the passage. |
| Contrasting phrase | The decision was legal, not wise. |
| Dates/places where required | On 15 August 1947, India became independent. |

### Do not use a comma

- Between subject and verb: ❌ *The causes of pollution, are complex.*
- Between verb and object: ❌ *The committee submitted, its report.*
- To join two complete sentences alone: ❌ *The plan failed, we revised it.* This is a **comma splice**.
- Around essential information: *Students who practise regularly improve.*

## 2. Semicolon and colon

- **Semicolon (;)** joins closely related complete sentences or separates complicated list items.
  - *The evidence was incomplete; the inquiry continued.*
- **Colon (:)** introduces a list, explanation or quotation after a complete clause.
  - *The paper tests four abilities: comprehension, précis, usage, and essay writing.*
- Do not place a colon immediately after a verb/preposition: ❌ *The abilities are: grammar and usage.*

## 3. Apostrophe

- Possession: *the candidate's answer; the candidates' answers; children's books*.
- Omission: *don't, it's*.
- **its** = possessive; **it's** = it is/it has.
- Never use an apostrophe for an ordinary plural: ❌ *NGO's work here* if merely plural -> ✅ *NGOs work here*.

## 4. Hyphen, dash, brackets and quotation marks

- Hyphen in a compound modifier before a noun: *a well-written answer*; but *the answer is well written*.
- Dash marks interruption or emphasis; use sparingly.
- Brackets add secondary information.
- Quotation marks enclose exact speech or a quoted expression.
- In indirect speech, remove quotation marks and adjust person/tense where required.

## 5. Full stop, question mark and exclamation mark

- End statements with a full stop.
- Use a question mark only for a direct question: *He asked whether I was ready.* (no question mark)
- Avoid exclamation marks in formal essays.

## 6. Capitalisation

Capitalise:

- first word of a sentence;
- proper nouns and official names;
- languages and nationalities: Hindi, English, Indian;
- Acts, institutions and formal titles when used as part of a name.

Do not capitalise ordinary common nouns merely for importance.

## 7. Comma decision test

```text
Is it a list? -> comma
Introductory element? -> comma
Two complete clauses joined by and/but/or/so/yet? -> comma before conjunction
Extra removable information? -> commas around it
Subject-verb or verb-object boundary? -> NO comma
Two complete clauses with no conjunction? -> full stop / semicolon / add conjunction
```
