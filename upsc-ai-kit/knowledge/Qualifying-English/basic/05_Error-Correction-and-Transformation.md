# Error Correction, Voice, Narration and Transformation — Core

## 1. Correction method and error codes

Read for meaning first, then scan in this order:

```text
finite verb/agreement → tense/conditional → noun/determiner → pronoun
→ verb pattern/preposition → word form/spelling → modifier/parallelism → punctuation
```

Make the smallest change that restores standard formal English. Do not mark an
accepted modern variant wrong merely because a traditional alternative exists.

| Code | Check |
|---|---|
| AGR | agreement/number |
| CNT | countability/determiner |
| PRO | pronoun case, reference or agreement |
| TNS | tense/aspect |
| COND | conditional/polarity |
| ART | article/determiner |
| PREP | preposition or verb pattern |
| PHR | phrasal-verb position/meaning |
| MOD | misplaced/dangling modifier |
| PAR | parallel structure |
| PUNC | punctuation/capital |
| SPL | spelling/confusable |
| WF | word form |
| VAR | acceptable-variant issue |

## 2. Frequent correction families

| Fault | Safe correction |
|---|---|
| `One of the applicant have…` | *One of the applicants has…* [AGR/CNT] |
| `furnitures/informations` | *furniture/information* or a measure phrase [CNT] |
| `She is senior than me` | *senior to me* [PREP] |
| `He discussed about the proposal` | *discussed the proposal* [PREP] |
| `I have been waiting since three hours` | *for three hours* [PREP] |
| `When I will arrive…` | *When I arrive…* [TNS] |
| `comprises of` | *comprises* / *consists of* [PREP] |
| `look at children` (care for) | *look after children* [PHR] |
| `more wiser` | *wiser* [WF] |
| `The woodcutter was falling trees` | *was felling trees* [WF] |

`The reason is because funding was delayed` occurs in modern English, but
some conservative examination keys reject it as redundant. To avoid a
marking dispute, write *The reason is that funding was delayed* or *Funding
was delayed because…*. A key must not present the original as objectively
ungrammatical without stating its house style [VAR].

## 3. Voice: preserve tense, object and meaning

Only a verb that has an object normally makes a passive. Move the object to
subject position; keep tense, aspect, modal and relevant adverbials.

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| *They write reports.* | *Reports are written.* |
| *They are writing a report.* | *A report is being written.* |
| *They wrote a report.* | *A report was written.* |
| *They had written a report.* | *A report had been written.* |
| *They have written a report.* | *A report has been written.* |
| *They will write a report.* | *A report will be written.* |
| *They can/must write a report.* | *A report can/must be written.* |

### Double objects, questions and imperatives

| Active | Accepted passive(s) |
|---|---|
| *The board gave Riya a prize.* | *Riya was given a prize.* / *A prize was given to Riya.* |
| *Someone has sent me the file.* | *I have been sent the file.* / *The file has been sent to me.* |
| *Did the officer sign the order?* | *Was the order signed by the officer?* |
| *Who wrote the letter?* | *By whom was the letter written?* (formal) |
| *Open the door.* | *Let the door be opened.* (formal, often unnatural) |
| *Do not disturb the patient.* | *Let the patient not be disturbed.* |

Omit `by + agent` when unknown, obvious or irrelevant: *The accused was
arrested.* Do not force passive with intransitives (*arrive, occur, sleep*) or
verbs that do not take the intended object. Prepositional passives are possible
where the preposition belongs to the verb: *The proposal was approved of* is
grammatical but often awkward; *The children were looked after* is natural.

## 4. Direct and indirect speech (narration)

First identify speaker, listener, reporting time and exact communicative
purpose. Pronouns, time/place words and tense change only when the new context
requires it.

| Direct form | Indirect pattern |
|---|---|
| statement: *He said, “I am tired.”* | *He said (that) he was tired.* |
| yes/no question: *She asked, “Are you ready?”* | *She asked whether/if I was ready.* |
| wh-question: *He asked, “Where do you live?”* | *He asked where I lived.* |
| command: *The officer said, “Close the door.”* | *The officer ordered me to close the door.* |
| request: *She said to me, “Please wait.”* | *She asked me to wait.* |
| negative command: *He said, “Do not move.”* | *He told us not to move.* |

In formal reported speech, a past reporting verb commonly backshifts:
`am/is → was; are → were; have/has done → had done; did → had done; will →
would; can → could`. Backshift is not automatic when the fact remains true,
the report is immediate, or the writer deliberately keeps present relevance:
*The teacher said that the Earth moves around the Sun.* Universal truths
normally remain present. Do not change a question into question word order:
`He asked where did I live` → *He asked where I lived.* `Said to` is usually
replaced by a purpose verb (*asked, told, advised, ordered*) in commands.

## 5. Meaning-preserving transformations: conditions first

A transformation is correct only if time, polarity, emphasis, agent and scope
remain the same. These are patterns, not automatic substitutions.

| Original and condition | Safe transformation |
|---|---|
| *As soon as the bell rang, students left* (past) | *No sooner had the bell rung than the students left.* |
| past event immediately followed by another | *Hardly/Scarcely had the rain stopped when/before work resumed.* |
| `if ... not` with equivalent positive condition | *Unless you practise, you will not improve.* |
| *so + adjective + that + subject cannot/could not + verb* | *too + adjective + for + object + to + verb*: *The box is too heavy for me to lift.* |
| *although + clause* | *In spite of/despite + noun phrase* or *in spite of/despite the fact that + clause*: *Despite being tired, he worked.* |
| two positive parallel facts | *He is not only honest but also efficient.* |
| two negative qualities of same subject | *She is neither careless nor dishonest.* |
| purpose | *He worked hard so that he could qualify* / *in order to qualify* (same subject only) |
| comparative degree | *No other metal is as useful as iron* → *Iron is the most useful metal* (only when the comparison set is unchanged) |
| `only` scope | *Only Meera checked the accounts* → *Meera checked only the accounts* changes focus from actor to object; use only when that exact changed focus is requested. |

Do not use an alleged `although → however + adjective/adverb` formula.
`However` is a conjunctive adverb and normally joins independent clauses:
*The task was difficult; however, she completed it.* It cannot simply replace
`although` at the start of a dependent clause.

`Being + adjective/past participle` must modify the grammatical subject:
*Being flooded, the road was closed* is valid. `Being a holiday, the office
was closed` dangles because an office is not a holiday. Use *As it was a
holiday, the office was closed* or the formal absolute *It being a holiday,
the office was closed.*

## 6. Question tags

| Statement | Tag |
|---|---|
| *They will come* | *won't they?* |
| *She is not ready* | *is she?* |
| *I am late* | *aren't I?* |
| *Let's begin* | *shall we?* |
| *Open the door* | *will you?* |
| *Don't be late* | *will you?* |
| *There is a problem* | *isn't there?* |

Use the auxiliary already present; use `do/does/did` when none appears.

## 7. Timed mixed diagnostic and retry gate

Attempt without notes in **15 minutes**:

1. Convert: *Someone has offered the students scholarships.*
2. Report: *The teacher said to Riya, “Please do not leave early.”*
3. Report: *The guide said, “The Earth moves around the Sun.”*
4. Transform without changing meaning: *Although the roads were flooded, the
   buses continued.*
5. Begin with *Unless*: *If you do not submit the form, you cannot sit the test.*
6. Correct: *Being a holiday, the office was closed.*
7. Correct: *No sooner the train arrived than we boarded it.*
8. Add a tag: *Let us revise the answer, ___?*
9. Correct: *The manager gave to Riya the file.*
10. Correct only if necessary: *Each candidate must bring their admit card.*

**Key:** 1 *The students have been offered scholarships* / *Scholarships have
been offered to the students* [VOICE]; 2 *The teacher asked/told Riya not to
leave early* [NAR]; 3 *The guide said that the Earth moves around the Sun*
[NAR]; 4 *Despite/In spite of the flooded roads, the buses continued* or
*Despite/In spite of the fact that the roads were flooded…* [TRN]; 5 *Unless
you submit the form, you cannot sit the test* [COND]; 6 *As it was a holiday…*
[MOD]; 7 *No sooner had the train arrived than…* [TNS]; 8 *shall we?* [TAG];
9 *The manager gave Riya the file* / *gave the file to Riya* [VAR]; 10 no
change required; singular *their* is accepted [VAR].

**Retry gate:** score at least **9/10**, with no `COND`, `VOICE`, `NAR` or
`VAR` error. If not, log item number, code, wrong rule and corrected sentence;
wait one day, then complete a fresh mixed set from the Foundation test. Do not
move to full-paper practice until two consecutive timed attempts meet this
gate.
