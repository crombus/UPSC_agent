# Sentence Grammar — Core

## 1. Complete sentences and agreement

A complete sentence needs a finite verb and a complete thought. Repair a
fragment (*Because the policy failed*) by attaching it to a main clause. Do not
join two complete clauses with a comma alone: use a full stop, semicolon, or
coordinating conjunction.

| Subject pattern | Verb |
|---|---|
| singular noun/pronoun | *The officer is ready.* |
| plural noun/pronoun | *The officers are ready.* |
| `each/every/either/neither` + singular noun | *Each proposal has merit.* |
| `each of/neither of` + plural noun | formal default singular: *Neither of the alternatives is acceptable.* Plural is also common with `neither of` in modern use; use singular in a correction test unless context requires otherwise. |
| `one of` + plural noun | singular: *One of the answers is wrong.* |
| `a number of` / `the number of` | plural / singular: *A number are absent; the number is rising.* |
| amount, period, distance as one unit | singular: *Ten kilometres is far to walk.* |
| `along with/as well as/together with` | agrees with first subject: *The minister, along with officials, is attending.* |
| `either...or/neither...nor` | normally agree with nearer subject: *Neither the officers nor the minister is ready.* Recast awkward mixed-person subjects: *Neither the minister nor the officers are ready.* |
| collective noun | singular unit or plural members; keep pronoun consistent: *The team has its plan / are arguing among themselves.* |
| `none of` + plural/count noun | singular is traditional when “not one”; plural is common when “not any.” Choose one form and keep the reference clear: *None of the evidence is…; none of the applicants are…* |
| fraction/percentage + `of` | agree with the noun after `of`: *Half of the work is done; half of the files are missing.* |
| `there is/are` | agree with the following real subject: *There are two errors; there is one error.* |
| gerund/infinitive clause | usually singular: *Reading carefully is essential.* |

`More than one + singular noun` normally takes singular (*More than one
candidate has applied*). `Many a + singular noun` also takes singular.

## 2. Tense and aspect: choose the time relation

| Meaning | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| fact, habit, timetable | simple present | *Water freezes at 0°C; the train leaves at six.* |
| action happening now/temporary arrangement | present continuous | *She is reading; they are staying in Delhi.* |
| completed action with present result; unfinished time | present perfect | *She has submitted the form; I have seen her today.* |
| duration continuing to now | present perfect continuous | *He has been working for five days.* |
| completed past at stated/finished time | simple past | *She submitted it yesterday; I saw her in 2024.* |
| ongoing past interrupted by another event | past continuous | *She was reading when I entered.* |
| earlier of two past points | past perfect | *The meeting had ended before we arrived.* Use it for a needed sequence, not automatically for every past sentence. |
| duration before a past point | past perfect continuous | *They had been waiting for an hour before the bus came.* |
| future prediction/decision | will + base verb | *I will call later.* |
| arranged future | present continuous | *We are meeting tomorrow.* |
| action in progress at future time | future continuous | *This time tomorrow, she will be travelling.* |
| future before a stated future point | future perfect | *By June, she will have finished.* |
| duration up to a future point | future perfect continuous | *By June, she will have been working here for a year.* |

State verbs normally avoid continuous forms in their ordinary meanings:
*know, believe, understand, own, belong, prefer, need*. Write *I know the
rule*, not usually `I am knowing`. Some change meaning: *I think it is right*
(opinion) / *I am thinking about it* (considering).

### Time clauses and conditionals

Do not ordinarily put `will` after *if, when, until, as soon as, before* for a
future condition: *If/when she arrives, I will call.* `Will` is possible when
it means willingness: *If you will wait, I will check.*

| Type | Form | Meaning/example |
|---|---|---|
| Zero | if + present, present | general result: *If water freezes, it expands.* |
| First | if + present, will/may/can/imperative | real future: *If you revise, you will improve.* |
| Second | if + past form, would/could/might + base | unreal present/future: *If I knew, I would tell you.* `were` is safest formal form: *If I were you…* |
| Third | if + past perfect, would/could/might have + past participle | unreal past: *If she had left earlier, she would have caught the train.* |
| Mixed: past condition, present result | if + past perfect, would + base | *If I had taken the job, I would live in Mumbai now.* |
| Mixed: present condition, past result | if + past form, would have + participle | *If he were more careful, he would not have made that error.* |

Do not use `would` in the ordinary if-clause: `If I would know` → *If I knew*.
With *unless*, use an affirmative condition where it means *if ... not*:
*Unless you practise, you will not improve.* Do **not** replace *if* with
*unless* if it changes polarity, emphasis, or a negative main-clause meaning.

## 3. Articles and determiners

### Article choice

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| `a/an` = one, non-specific singular count noun | *a university; an hour; an MBA graduate* — choose by sound, not spelling |
| `the` = specific, previously identified, unique in context, superlative/ordinal | *the file you sent; the sun; the first answer; the best option* |
| zero article = plural/uncountable in general | *Books are useful; Honesty matters; Water is scarce.* |
| institution purpose | *go to school/prison/hospital* as pupil/prisoner/patient; *go to the school/hospital* as a building/visitor |
| geographical names | *India, Delhi, Mount Everest*; but *the Himalayas, the Ganga, the Indian Ocean, the United States* |

A singular count noun normally cannot stand bare: `She is teacher` → *She is a
teacher.* General abstract nouns can take `the` when qualified: *Freedom is
valuable; the freedom guaranteed by law matters.*

### Determiners and quantity

| Count nouns | Uncountable nouns | Either/other |
|---|---|---|
| many, few/a few, fewer, several | much, little/a little, less | some, any, enough, more, most, all, no |

`few/little` means hardly any; `a few/a little` means some and is positive.
Use *fewer candidates* but *less water*; `less than ten candidates` is common
in measurement-style usage but *fewer* is safest in formal correction.
`much` is common in negatives/questions; *a great deal of* is natural in
positive statements. Use `some` in offers/requests expecting yes: *Would you
like some tea?*

| Determiner contrast | Safe use |
|---|---|
| each/every | both take singular noun and verb; *each* views individuals, *every* the group: *Every room; each of the rooms* |
| either/neither | normally one of/not one of two: *either answer is; neither answer is* |
| both | plural: *both answers are* |
| another/other/the other | one more; additional/different; the remaining one of two: *another book; other books; the other book* |
| fewer/less | number of countable items; amount/quantity |
| amount/number | uncountable quantity; countable items: *amount of water; number of books* |

## 4. Prepositions, verb patterns and phrasal verbs

Memorise the whole construction. A preposition may vary with meaning; do not
invent a universal rule from one example.

| Meaning | Safe construction |
|---|---|
| agree with a person / agree to a plan / agree on a point | *I agree with her; they agreed to the proposal.* |
| angry with/at a person; angry at/about an act | Both *angry with the contractor* and *angry at the contractor* are standard. |
| approve of; comply with; consist of; depend on; insist on | *comply with the order; insist on verification* |
| abstain from; distinguish X from Y; responsible for | *abstain from voting* |
| discuss/enter/approach/reach | no `about/into/to` in ordinary transitive use: *discuss the proposal; enter a room; approach the officer; reach Delhi* |
| comprise / consist | *The unit comprises five sections*; *The unit consists of five sections.* Avoid `comprises of` in formal correction. |
| wait for; listen to; belong to; suffer from | *wait for a bus; listen to advice* |

### Double objects and `provide`

Many verbs allow two patterns:

| Verb | Pattern 1 | Pattern 2 |
|---|---|---|
| give/send/show/teach | *give her the file* | *give the file to her* |
| buy/make/find | *buy her a book* | *buy a book for her* |
| provide | *provide students with meals* | *provide meals for/to students* |

`Provide someone something` is established, especially in North American
usage, but is less preferred in conservative British formal prose. It is not a
grammar error. In an Indian formal correction answer, prefer one of the two
standard `provide ... with/for/to` patterns unless the question tests a stated
house style.

### Phrasal/prepositional verbs

| Verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| look after | care for | *look after children* |
| look for | seek | *look for evidence* |
| look into | investigate | *look into the complaint* |
| look up to | admire | *look up to her teacher* |
| put off | postpone | *put off the meeting* |
| put up with | tolerate | *put up with noise* |
| carry out | perform | *carry out an inquiry* |
| bring up | mention/raise | *bring up the issue* |
| turn down | reject/reduce | *turn down the offer* |
| take over | assume control | *take over the office* |

Separable verb + pronoun: *turn the offer down / turn it down*, not `turn down
it`. Do not split inseparable *look after*: `look the child after` is wrong.

## 5. Conjunctions, parallelism and modifiers

| Rule | Correct form |
|---|---|
| No double conjunction | *Although it rained, we left*; not `Although… but…` |
| `because` gives reason; `because of` takes noun phrase | *because it rained / because of rain* |
| `despite/in spite of` takes noun phrase or *the fact that* clause | *despite the rain; in spite of the fact that it rained* |
| `not only...but also` needs matched units | *not only honest but also efficient; not only reads but also writes* |
| `either...or` / `neither...nor` / `both...and` | match grammar on both sides: *either to revise or to withdraw; both accurate and concise* |
| paired list stays parallel | *planning, coordinating and monitoring* |
| comparison compares like with like | *the population of Delhi is larger than that of Pune* |

Place a modifier beside its target:

| Fault | Repair |
|---|---|
| Dangling: `Walking to office, the rain began.` | *While I was walking to office, it began to rain.* |
| Misplaced: `She served sandwiches to the children on paper plates.` | *She served the children sandwiches on paper plates.* |
| Squinting: `Students who regularly revise improve.` | Place *regularly* beside the intended verb: *Students who revise regularly improve.* |

## 6. Timed diagnostic

In **10 minutes**, correct and label:
1. A number of applicant is absent.
2. If I would have known, I would have called.
3. She has finished the report yesterday.
4. He went to hospital to visit his friend.
5. Neither the manager nor the clerks is ready.
6. We discussed about how to put up with the delay.
7. The office provided the workers meals.
8. Although he was tired but he worked.
9. Having read the report, the errors were obvious.
10. She turned down it.

**Key:** 1 *applicants are* [AGR]; 2 *had known* [COND]; 3 *finished* [TNS];
4 *the hospital* [ART]; 5 *are* [AGR]; 6 *discussed how to put up with…*
[PREP]; 7 accepted, though *provided the workers with meals* is safest
[VAR]; 8 remove *but* [CONJ]; 9 *Having read the report, I found the errors
obvious* [MOD]; 10 *turned it down* [PHR].

## Semantic-completeness closure — 2026-09-06

### Literal syllabus, ownership and boundary

- **Syllabus demand:** Usage and Vocabulary; sentence completeness, agreement, tense/aspect, articles/determiners, prepositions, conjunctions, parallelism and modifiers.
- **Canonical ownership:** Owns clause completeness, subject-verb agreement, tense/aspect/conditionals, article systems, determiner quantity, verb-preposition patterns, phrasal verbs, conjunctions, parallelism and modifier placement.
- **Cross-topic boundary:** Word-class foundations belong to Topic 01; punctuation choices to Topic 03; correction workflow, voice, narration and transformation to Topic 05.

### Complete learner and answer route

1. **Clause completeness:** Distinguish complete clauses, fragments, comma splices and subordinated units.
2. **Agreement controller:** Locate the grammatical subject, ignoring interrupting phrases.
3. **Special agreement:** Handle each/every, number expressions, fractions, there constructions and coordinated subjects.
4. **Tense timeline:** Place events on a timeline before choosing tense and aspect.
5. **Conditionals:** Preserve real/unreal time, polarity and result.
6. **Articles:** Choose a/an by sound, the by identifiability and zero article for general plural/mass reference.
7. **Determiners:** Control few/a few, little/a little, fewer/less, each/every and other/another.
8. **Preposition patterns:** Learn verb/adjective/noun plus preposition as complete constructions.
9. **Phrasal verbs:** Preserve idiomatic meaning and separability.
10. **Conjunction logic:** Match clause type and avoid double conjunctions.
11. **Parallelism and comparison:** Coordinate grammatically equivalent units and compare like with like.
12. **Modifier placement:** Attach opening and internal modifiers to their intended heads.

### Authority, variation and hostile-query gate

Every correction must preserve the intended time relation, polarity and meaning. Where usage varies, label the conservative examination form without calling a standard alternative ungrammatical.

The review checked the local official UPSC compulsory-English papers listed in `README.md`,
the repository's verbatim syllabus, the subject-wide solved package, and standard public usage
references recorded in the learner successor. It explicitly stress-tested:
**fragment; subject-verb agreement; aspect; conditionals; article; determiner; phrasal verb; dangling**.

Prefer plain contemporary formal English. Where more than one standard form is legitimate,
state the variation and select a conservative examination form only as a risk-control choice;
do not falsely label the alternative ungrammatical.

### Progressive practice and timed-paper transfer

1. Foundation: identify the demand and state the governing rule in plain language.
2. Controlled application: correct or construct one sentence and explain the change.
3. Passage/paragraph transfer: preserve meaning, reference, register and logical relation.
4. Hostile test: reject a close option that is grammatical in another meaning or variety.
5. Timed execution: answer, verify, classify the error, and retry a fresh item.
