These axes are independent: a doctrine may combine transcendence and immanence rather than choosing only one.

### 1. Personal and impersonal

✅ **Personal God:** possesses intellect, will and purposive agency; can know, command, love and respond. Classical theism and Rāmānuja’s Brahman are central examples.

✅ **Impersonal God/Absolute:** ultimate reality is not a person among persons and does not exercise human-like volition. Spinoza’s one substance and Śaṅkara’s Nirguṇa Brahman are major examples.

⚠️ **Important qualification:** “Personal” does not mean bodily or finite. “Impersonal” does not mean unreal, unconscious matter or morally indifferent by definition.

### 2. Transcendent and immanent

✅ **Transcendent:** God exceeds the world and is not reducible to it. This denotes ontological independence—not spatial remoteness.

✅ **Immanent:** God is present and operative within the world as its ground, sustainer or inner controller. This need not mean that God is physically located inside objects.

✅ Classical theism generally affirms both: God is distinct from creation yet continuously sustains it. Viśiṣṭādvaita similarly presents Brahman as the indwelling controller while exceeding the world.

### 3. Four conceptual combinations

| Combination | Meaning | Illustrative model |
|---|---|---|
| Personal + transcendent | Creator distinct from creation | Classical theism |
| Personal + immanent | Providence or inner governance | Rāmānuja’s *antaryāmin* |
| Impersonal + immanent | Ultimate reality expressed in everything | Spinoza |
| Impersonal + transcendent | Absolute beyond finite predicates | Nirguṇa Brahman |

⚠️ These are analytical placements, not complete descriptions. Classical theism, for example, combines personal transcendence with immanent sustaining activity.

### 4. Why UPSC cares

The syllabus is not asking merely, “Does God exist?” It asks what the word **God** means and how each conception explains three relations:

```text
       │
       └────────→ How is God related to human beings?
```

- A notion of God is a metaphysical profile, not simply a name.
- Personal God has intellect, will and relational agency.
- Impersonal Absolute transcends person-like predicates.
- Transcendence means ontological independence from the world.
- Immanence means presence or sustaining activity within the world.

### MCQ 1

A. Transcendence denotes God’s ontological independence, while immanence denotes divine presence or sustaining activity within the world.
B. Transcendence means God is spatially distant, while immanence means God has a physical location in every object.
C. Transcendence belongs only to personal theism, while immanence belongs only to impersonal systems.

---

❌ **Incorrect.** Transcendence is not spatial distance, and immanence is not physical location. They describe an **ontological relation**: God may be independent of the world while remaining present as its ground or sustainer.

The four positions differ principally over whether God is **distinct from**, **active in**, **inclusive of**, or **identical with** the world.

## 1. Theism

✅ God is a **personal creator**, distinct from the world but continuously sustaining and governing it.

✅ It combines **transcendence**—God exceeds creation—with **immanence**—God remains present and active within it.

✅ Classical attributes include necessity, omnipotence, omniscience, perfect goodness and eternity.

**Example:** Aquinas’s God sustains secondary natural causes without competing with them as another worldly cause.

## 2. Deism

✅ God creates the universe and its rational laws but does not subsequently intervene through miracles or special revelation.

```text
Divine creation → Natural laws begin → Universe operates autonomously
```

> 🔑 Memory hook: **Deism = Designer who becomes Distant.**

⚠️ Deism is not atheism: it affirms a creator but rejects continuing providential intervention.

## 3. Pantheism

✅ **God is identical with nature or reality as a whole.** There is no independent creator standing outside creation.

✅ Spinoza’s formula is *Deus sive Natura*—“God, or Nature.” God is the one infinite substance; finite things are its modes.

⚠️ Spinoza’s God is not merely physical matter. Nature includes the infinite substance expressed through attributes, of which human beings know Thought and Extension.

> 🔑 Memory hook: **PAN = all; pantheism = all is divine.**

## 4. Panentheism

✅ The world exists **in God**, but God possesses a reality exceeding the world.

```text
Pantheism    : God = World
Panentheism  : World ⊂ God
Theism       : God ≠ World, though God sustains World
```

✅ It attempts to preserve divine immanence without eliminating transcendence. Process theology frequently uses a panentheistic model.

> 🔑 Memory hook: **PAN-EN-THEISM = all “in” God.**

A God-concept is a **package of mutually connected attributes**. UPSC expects you to test whether the package is coherent, not merely list its contents.

## 1. Foundational attributes

| Attribute | Meaning | Necessary qualification |
|---|---|---|
| **Aseity** | God depends on nothing else for existence | God is self-existent, not self-caused |
| **Necessity** | God cannot fail to exist | Stronger than merely existing forever |
| **Eternity** | God is not subject to temporal decay | Interpreted as timelessness or everlasting duration |
| **Immutability** | God’s essential nature does not change | Need not exclude every relational difference |
| **Simplicity** | God is not composed of separable parts | Attributes are not independent components added to God |

## 2. The three “omni” attributes

### Omnipotence

✅ God can actualize every **logically possible** state of affairs consistent with divine nature.

```text
Power extends to genuine possibilities
                 ≠
Producing contradictions such as a square circle
```

⚠️ Creating a stone that an omnipotent being cannot lift describes an internally contradictory task, not a genuine object of power.

> 🔑 Omnipotence means **maximal coherent power**, not the ability to make contradictions true.

### Omniscience

✅ God knows every truth—past, present, future and counterfactual, under the classical account.

**Problem of foreknowledge:**

```text
God infallibly knows that X will choose A
                    ↓
Can X genuinely choose B?
```

**Classical reply:** knowledge does not cause the act. God knows the free act because the agent performs it; divine timelessness removes chronological “prediction.”

⚠️ Objection: even non-causal, infallible prior knowledge appears to make alternatives impossible. Compatibility remains disputed.

### Perfect goodness

✅ God is morally perfect and cannot will evil as evil.

⚠️ This generates the problem of evil when combined with omnipotence and omniscience:

```text
God knows evil + can prevent evil + is perfectly good
                          yet
                       evil exists
```

Responses include free-will defence, soul-making and the claim that some goods require permitted risk. These responses must be evaluated rather than merely named.

## 3. Attribute conflicts

| Tension | Philosophical problem | Standard reply | Remaining pressure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Power vs logic | Can God do contradictions? | Contradictions are not possible tasks | Does this redefine “all-powerful”? |
| Knowledge vs freedom | Does foreknowledge fix human action? | Knowing does not cause choosing | Alternative possibilities still appear closed |
| Goodness vs evil | Why permit preventable suffering? | Freedom or soul-making | Natural and excessive evil remain difficult |
| Immutability vs personality | Can an unchanging God respond? | Essential nature remains fixed while relations differ | Real responsiveness seems to involve change |
| Simplicity vs attributes | How can God have many properties? | Attributes are conceptually distinct but identical in God | The doctrine becomes difficult to understand |

## 4. Freedom of will and an omnipotent God

✅ **Compatibility model:** God voluntarily permits created agents to exercise limited causal power. Self-restraint is not inability.

```text
GOD’S OMNIPOTENCE
        │ voluntarily grants
        ▼
CREATURELY FREEDOM → accountable human action
```

⚠️ **Analogy:** A sovereign choosing not to exercise every legal power has not thereby lost that power. However, this analogy does not by itself solve divine foreknowledge.

✅ **PYQ:** UPSC 2020 asked whether freedom of will is compatible with an omnipotent God.

### Answer spine

```text
→ distinguish power from constant control
```

⚠️ **Analytical use:** Voluntary non-intervention can be presented as an exercise of power rather than absence of power, allowing a qualified reconciliation with human agency. The paper is a contemporary anchor, not a substitute for canonical arguments.

```text
                       GOD OR NATURE
                    ONE INFINITE SUBSTANCE
                  exists in and through itself
                              │
              ┌───────────────┴───────────────┐
              │                               │
```

✅ **Central thesis:** There are not two realities—God and creation. There is only **one infinite substance**, and everything else exists as its mode.

> 🔑 Formula: **One substance → infinite attributes → finite modes.**

## 1. Substance

✅ In Spinoza, a substance is **“in itself and conceived through itself.”** Its existence and conception do not depend upon another thing.

### Argument for one substance

```text
1. Substance is self-conceived and ontologically independent.
2. Distinct substances cannot share the same attribute.
3. God possesses infinite attributes and excludes every rival substance.
4. Therefore, only one substance exists: God or Nature.
```

⚠️ The argument assumes that conceptual independence accurately reveals ontological independence. Critics may reject this movement from definition to reality.

## 2. God: *Deus sive Natura*

✅ *Deus sive Natura* means **“God, or Nature.”** It expresses identity, not comparison.

| Classical theism | Spinoza |
|---|---|
| God creates the world freely | The world follows necessarily from divine nature |
| God is distinct from creation | Everything exists in God as a mode |
| God possesses intellect and will like supreme personality | God is impersonal, not a deliberating person |
| Providence involves choice and purpose | Nature operates through necessary order |
| Creator–creature dualism | Substance monism |

⚠️ Spinoza’s God is not simply physical nature. The one substance possesses infinitely many attributes; human beings know only **Thought** and **Extension**.

## 3. Attributes

✅ An attribute is what the intellect perceives as constituting the essence of substance.

```text
Same infinite reality
       ├── understood under Thought → minds and ideas
       └── understood under Extension → bodies and motion
```

✅ Thought and Extension are not two substances. They are two ways in which the same substance is intelligible.

## 4. Modes

✅ Modes are dependent modifications of substance—particular minds, bodies and events.

> **Analogy:** A wave is not a second substance beside the ocean; it is a determinate expression of the ocean. Likewise, finite things are expressions of the one substance.

⚠️ The analogy is limited: Spinoza’s modes follow with metaphysical necessity, whereas ordinary waves are contingent physical events.

## 5. *Natura naturans* and *Natura naturata*

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Natura naturans**—nature naturing | God/Nature considered as active, infinite and self-caused substance |
| **Natura naturata**—nature natured | The complete order of modes necessarily following from divine nature |

```text
NATURA NATURANS ──necessary expression──→ NATURA NATURATA
 infinite source                              modal order
```

✅ This is not creation at a first moment. It is an eternal relation of ontological dependence.

## 6. Necessity and freedom

✅ Everything follows necessarily from divine nature. God could not have chosen a fundamentally different world through arbitrary will.

✅ Spinoza redefines freedom:

```text
Freedom ≠ uncaused choice between alternatives
Freedom = acting from the necessity of one’s own nature
```

God is perfectly free because nothing external constrains God. Human freedom increases as rational understanding replaces passive determination by inadequate ideas and emotions.

⚠️ Critics argue that this turns freedom into understood necessity and abandons genuine alternative choice.

## 7. Relation to human beings

✅ A human mind is a finite mode under Thought; its body is the corresponding finite mode under Extension.

✅ Humans are **in God**, not independently created substances outside God.

⚠️ God does not love, punish or respond as a personal deity. Spinoza’s “intellectual love of God” is rational participation in and understanding of the necessary whole.

## 8. Is Spinoza a pantheist?

✅ The standard reading is pantheistic because God and Nature are identical and finite things exist in God.

⚠️ A qualification is necessary: God is not merely the collection of finite things. God is infinite substance; finite things are modes. Some interpreters therefore emphasize Spinoza’s complex substance monism rather than the slogan “everything is God.”

## Criticisms and replies

| Criticism | Spinozistic reply | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| God loses personality | Personality would impose finite human categories on infinity | Saves infinity but abandons prayerful relationship |
| The doctrine is atheism in religious language | God is the only independent and infinite reality | It affirms ultimacy but rejects classical theism |
| Necessity destroys freedom | Freedom is self-determination through one’s nature | Coherent redefinition, but not libertarian freedom |
| Evil becomes part of God | Good and evil reflect finite human perspectives | Weakens objective moral evil and providence |
| Modes are difficult to derive from substance | They follow necessarily from divine attributes | “Following from” remains insufficiently transparent |

> 🔑 Memory hook: **Absolute Brahman; relational Īśvara.**

## 1. Nirguṇa Brahman

✅ **Nirguṇa** means Brahman is free from limiting, finite and relational qualities. It does not mean a blank nothing.

✅ Brahman is non-dual, self-revealing consciousness and the ultimate ground of experience.

### *Sat–Cit–Ānanda*

| Term | Meaning | Advaitic significance |
|---|---|---|
| **Sat** | Being/reality | Brahman cannot be negated without presupposing reality |
| **Cit** | Consciousness | The witnessing condition of every known object |
| **Ānanda** | Fullness/bliss | Freedom from limitation, not an episodic pleasure |

⚠️ These are not three separable properties composing Brahman. They indicate the same non-dual reality from different conceptual directions.

### *Neti neti*

✅ “Not this, not this” removes inadequate finite predicates.

```text
Brahman is not body
        not mind
        not a finite object
        not one being among others
        ↓
not nothing—but the presupposed reality of all experience
```

## 2. Saguṇa Īśvara

✅ When Brahman is understood in relation to **māyā**, it is Īśvara—the omniscient and omnipotent creator, sustainer and dissolver of the empirical world.

✅ Īśvara is the legitimate object of devotion, meditation and moral-religious life at the **vyāvahārika** level.

⚠️ Īśvara is not false in the sense of non-existent. Īśvara remains valid wherever the world and individual selves are experienced as real.

## 3. Māyā and *adhyāsa*

✅ **Māyā** explains how non-dual Brahman appears as a world of plurality without undergoing real transformation.

✅ **Adhyāsa** is superimposition: characteristics of one thing are wrongly attributed to another.

### Rope–snake model

```text
ROPE + ignorance → SNAKE APPEARANCE → fear
       knowledge → appearance sublated → ROPE recognized

BRAHMAN + avidyā → WORLD OF SEPARATION → bondage
          knowledge → difference sublated → BRAHMAN recognized
```

✅ The snake is experienced but later sublated. Similarly, the world is **mithyā**—empirically experienced and dependent, but not absolutely real.

⚠️ *Mithyā* does not mean sheer non-existence like a square circle.

## 4. *Vivarta* rather than *pariṇāma*

| Theory | Meaning | Illustration | School |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vivarta** | Apparent transformation; source remains unchanged | Rope appearing as snake | Advaita |
| **Pariṇāma** | Real transformation | Milk becoming curd | Often used to contrast Rāmānuja’s real-world account |

✅ Brahman does not literally transform into the world. Otherwise the immutable Absolute would become divided and changing.

## 5. Three levels of reality

```text
PĀRAMĀRTHIKA  → Brahman alone is absolutely real
      ↑ sublates
VYĀVAHĀRIKA   → shared world, selves, causation and Īśvara
      ↑ sublates
PRĀTIBHĀSIKA  → private illusion, dream or rope–snake error
```

✅ A lower level is not sheer nothing; it is valid until knowledge from a higher level sublates it.

## 6. God–world–human relation

| Relation | Advaita account |
|---|---|
| Brahman–world | World is a dependent appearance through *vivarta* |
| Īśvara–world | Īśvara is empirical creator and governor |
| Ātman–Brahman | Identity: *tat tvam asi*—“That thou art” |
| Jīva–Īśvara | Distinction valid under ignorance at the empirical level |
| Liberation | Knowledge removes ignorance; no new unity is produced |

✅ Liberation is recognition of an identity that always existed, not the finite self physically merging into another entity.

## 7. Does Śaṅkara leave room for theism?

### Case for “yes”

- ✅ Īśvara is omniscient, creator and moral governor at the empirical level.
- ✅ Devotion and meditation purify the mind and prepare it for liberating knowledge.
- ✅ Religious language and worship have genuine practical validity.

### Case for “only provisionally”

- ✅ Ultimate reality is Nirguṇa Brahman, beyond creator–creature duality.
- ✅ Īśvara, jīva and world belong to the standpoint eventually sublated by non-dual knowledge.
- ⚠️ Therefore Advaita accommodates **empirical theism**, but its ultimate metaphysics is non-dual rather than personalistic theism.

> 🔑 Verdict: **Advaita preserves religion at the path level but transcends theism at the goal level.**

## 1. Three real principles

| Principle | Nature | Relation to Brahman |
|---|---|---|
| **Īśvara** | Supreme personal God, omniscient and omnipotent | Independent controller and whole |
| **Cit** | Conscious selves or *jīvas* | Real, dependent and inseparable from God |
| **Acit** | Non-conscious matter or *prakṛti* | Real, dependent and governed by God |

✅ Rāmānuja accepts all three as real but denies that they are three independent substances. Only Brahman is completely independent.

## 2. Why “qualified” non-dualism?

```text
ADVaita:          plurality is ultimately sublated
VIŚIṢṬĀDVAITA:    plurality is real but belongs within divine unity
DVAITA:           God, selves and matter remain categorically distinct
```

✅ Brahman is one, but not an undifferentiated unity. *Cit* and *acit* qualify Brahman as real internal differentiations.

⚠️ “Qualified” does not mean that finite things limit God externally. They exist dependently within the divine whole.

## 3. *Śarīra–śarīrī* relation

✅ The world and selves constitute God’s **body** (*śarīra*); God is their indwelling Self (*śarīrī*).

A body, in Rāmānuja’s technical sense, is that which:

1. is controlled by a conscious self,
2. exists for that self’s purposes, and
3. is completely dependent upon that self.

```text
Human body : human self
      ::
World + souls : Brahman
```

⚠️ This is a metaphysical relation, not the claim that God has a giant physical organism or anthropomorphic form.

## 4. *Apṛthak-siddhi*: inseparability without identity

✅ *Apṛthak-siddhi* means that a qualifier cannot exist separately from the substantive it qualifies.

```text
Jīva ≠ Brahman
Jīva cannot exist independently of Brahman
Therefore: distinction + inseparability
```

✅ The self belongs to God and depends completely upon God, but it never becomes numerically identical with the whole of Brahman.

> 🔑 Memory hook: **Different, dependent, inseparable.**

## 5. God as material and efficient cause

✅ God is the **efficient cause** because divine intelligence directs cosmic manifestation.

✅ God is also the **material cause** because the world arises from the *acit* that exists inseparably as God’s body.

### Real manifestation

```text
Subtle cit–acit in Brahman
            ↓ manifestation
Gross plurality of souls and world
            ↓ dissolution
Subtle condition in Brahman
```

✅ Unlike Advaita’s *vivarta*, the world is a genuine manifestation or transformation of the dependent material principle.

⚠️ Rāmānuja does not hold that God’s essential perfection transforms into defective matter. Change occurs in the divine body or dependent modes, not in God’s essential nature.

| Advaita | Viśiṣṭādvaita |
|---|---|
| Ātman is Brahman | Jīva is distinct but inseparable from Brahman |
| Difference is due to ignorance | Difference is real and eternal |
| Liberation removes all ultimate duality | Liberation preserves individuality |
| Knowledge is decisive | Bhakti, knowledge, action, grace and surrender matter |

✅ Each self is conscious, active and morally responsible. Its consciousness is obscured in bondage and expands in liberation.

✅ Liberation is communion and service to God—not absorption that destroys personal identity.

## 7. Bhakti and *prapatti*

### Bhakti

✅ Bhakti is sustained loving contemplation of God, grounded in knowledge and disciplined action.

### Prapatti

✅ *Prapatti* is complete surrender to divine grace, especially important where the demanding discipline of bhakti cannot be fully performed.

```text
Knowledge of God
      ↓
Loving devotion
      ↓

      ↓
Liberation as communion and service
```

⚠️ Grace does not erase moral agency. Surrender reorganizes agency around dependence upon God.

## 8. Rāmānuja’s criticism of Advaita

1. ✅ A wholly quality-less Brahman is religiously and conceptually unintelligible.
2. ✅ Consciousness must belong to a conscious subject; it cannot be an impersonal absolute alone.
3. ✅ Perception and scripture affirm genuine plurality rather than a merely apparent world.
4. ✅ If ignorance conceals Brahman, its locus and power become difficult to explain.
5. ✅ The great sayings can express organic unity and dependence, not numerical identity.

### Advaitic reply

⚠️ Finite predicates limit Brahman; non-duality is disclosed only when superimposed distinctions are removed. Rāmānuja answers that infinite auspicious qualities perfect rather than limit God.

## 9. Main philosophical pressure

### Objection: Does evil contaminate God?

**Rāmānuja-style reply:**

```text

                    ≠
```

# Part I — Nyāya’s notion of God

## 🖼️ Visual: God orders rather than materially becomes the world

```text
ETERNAL REALITIES

Atoms + selves + space + time + karma
                   │
                   │ ordered and coordinated by
                   ▼
            ĪŚVARA — omniscient agent
                   │
                   ▼
          COSMOS + KARMIC DISTRIBUTION

Material cause: atoms     Efficient cause: God
```

✅ Nyāya is a **pluralist realist** system. God, selves, atoms, space and time are real; they are not appearances of one undifferentiated Absolute.

> 🔑 Formula: **Nyāya God designs and directs; God does not become the material world.**

## 1. Nature of Īśvara

✅ Īśvara is eternal, omniscient, omnipotent and a special self never subject to bondage or karma.

✅ God is the **nimitta-kāraṇa**—intelligent efficient cause—of cosmic order.

✅ Eternal atoms are the **material cause**. Therefore Nyāya does not teach creation *ex nihilo* or a real transformation of God into the world.

✅ God initiates atomic combination, sustains order, governs dissolution and allocates karmic fruits.

✅ Later Nyāya treats God as the omniscient author of the Veda.

## 2. God and karma

```text
Human action → merit/demerit (*adṛṣṭa*)
                       │
                       ▼
           Īśvara coordinates consequences
                       │
                       ▼
              appropriate pleasure/pain
```

✅ Karma supplies the moral basis of consequences; God intelligently administers their distribution.

⚠️ God is not supposed to reward and punish arbitrarily. Nyāya uses karma to defend divine impartiality.

⚠️ Philosophical pressure remains: if karma already determines results, why is God required? Nyāya answers that unconscious merit, demerit and atoms cannot administer themselves intelligently.

## 3. Major Nyāya arguments for God

| Argument | Reasoning |
|---|---|
| **Kāryāt**—from effect | The composite world is an effect; an effect requires an intelligent maker |
| **Āyojanāt**—from arrangement | Inert atoms cannot initiate purposive combinations unaided |
| **Dhṛtyādeḥ**—from sustenance | Cosmic maintenance and dissolution require governing intelligence |
| **Śruteḥ**—from scripture | Authoritative Vedic testimony implies an omniscient source |
| **Padāt/Vākyāt**—from words/sentences | Linguistic convention and meaningful discourse point to intelligence |

⚠️ These are proofs **for God’s existence**, but a question on the **nature of God** should prioritize attributes, causal role, karma, atoms and souls rather than merely listing proofs.

## 4. Objections and replies

| Objection | Nyāya reply | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Effects need makers only within the world; the analogy cannot prove a world-maker | A composite and ordered whole still calls for intelligent explanation | The inference may overextend ordinary causation |
| Sāṃkhya’s *prakṛti* can explain evolution | Unconscious nature cannot explain purposive order | Purpose itself must first be established |
| Mīmāṃsā says the Veda is authorless | Coherent authoritative sentences imply an omniscient author | Depends upon rejecting authorless revelation |
| Karma makes God redundant | *Adṛṣṭa* is unconscious and needs administration | Risks dividing explanatory work between karma and God |
| Evil challenges divine goodness | Suffering follows agents’ karma, impartially dispensed | Does not fully explain the origin of suffering or cosmic arrangement |

# Part II — Is Hinduism polytheistic?

## 🖼️ Visual: Classifications that must not be conflated

```text
 MANY DIVINE FORMS
        │
        ├── POLYTHEISM: many gods possess distinct divine status
        │
        ├── HENOTHEISM: many gods accepted; one receives exclusive devotion
        │
        ├── KATHENOTHEISM: different gods are treated as supreme in turn
        │
        └── THEISTIC PLURALISM: multiple forms relate to one supreme divine reality

 ONE ULTIMATE
        │
        ├── MONOTHEISM: one supreme personal God
        ├── MONISM: one ultimate reality
        └── PANTHEISM: God and the whole of reality are identical
```

## 5. Essential distinctions

| Position | Exact claim |
|---|---|
| **Polytheism** | More than one god is genuinely divine |
| **Henotheism** | Several gods are acknowledged, but one is worshipped as supreme |
| **Kathenotheism** | Each deity may be elevated as supreme in a particular hymn or devotional context |
| **Monotheism** | Only one supreme God is affirmed |
| **Monism** | Reality is ultimately one; this need not imply a personal God |
| **Pantheism** | God is identical with the totality of reality |

## 6. Why Hinduism resists a single label

✅ Hindu traditions include Vedic plurality, devotional worship of a supreme personal deity, Advaitic non-dualism, Viśiṣṭādvaitic qualified unity and Nyāya’s intelligent creator.

✅ Vaiṣṇava, Śaiva and Śākta traditions may treat Viṣṇu, Śiva or Devī respectively as supreme while accommodating other deities within a wider sacred order.

✅ The Vedic statement **“Reality is one; sages speak of it variously”** is frequently used to interpret plurality as diverse naming or manifestation rather than independent rival gods.

⚠️ This verse does not automatically prove that every Hindu school is monotheistic or monistic. Later traditions interpret divine plurality differently.

### Balanced verdict

```text
Ritual/iconographic surface → plurality of deities
Devotional standpoint       → often one chosen supreme deity (*iṣṭa-devatā*)
Metaphysical standpoint     → one God, one Brahman, or pluralist realism depending on school
```

⚠️ Therefore, “Hinduism is polytheistic” is descriptively partial but philosophically inadequate. Classification must specify the tradition and level under examination.

## 7. Comparative synthesis

| School/model | Ultimate reality | World relation | Divine plurality |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nyāya | Personal Īśvara plus other eternal realities | God orders eternal atoms | One cosmic governor |
| Advaita | Nirguṇa Brahman | World is dependent appearance | Deities valid empirically |
| Viśiṣṭādvaita | Personal qualified Brahman | World and selves are God’s real body | Other deities subordinate within unity |
| Vedic henotheistic reading | One deity praised as supreme at a time | Varies | Many acknowledged |
| Straight polytheism | Multiple gods | Distinct divine agencies | Many genuinely divine beings |

## 📰 Contemporary linkage

⚠️ Modern academic discussion increasingly avoids treating “Hinduism” as if it had one uniform God-concept. This supports a tradition-specific, conceptually differentiated UPSC answer.

⚠️ **Inference:** The coexistence of unity and plurality can aid interfaith analysis, but it should not be converted into a simplistic claim that all deities or religions are philosophically identical.

## 2. Four relations that must never be confused

```text

QUALIFIED UNITY   : World and selves form God’s body    [Rāmānuja]
DEPENDENT APPEARANCE: World is *mithyā* upon Brahman     [Śaṅkara]
CAUSAL DISTINCTION: God orders material distinct from God [Nyāya/theism]
```

> 🔑 These are four different metaphysical grammars: **identity, embodiment, appearance and causation**.

## 3. Must God have physical manifestation to be the ultimate cause?

### Argument supporting manifestation

⚠️ Every familiar cause is encountered through some effect or embodied activity. An entirely unmanifest cause may seem indistinguishable from no cause.

⚠️ Religious traditions also use incarnation, symbol and sacred embodiment to make divine agency intelligible.

### Arguments against necessity

1. ✅ A physical form is spatially limited, divisible and dependent; these features conflict with metaphysical ultimacy.
2. ✅ Classical theism treats God as the non-physical ground sustaining physical reality.
3. ✅ Nyāya’s Īśvara orders atoms without being an atom or material body.
4. ✅ Advaita’s Brahman is the presupposition of manifestation, not one manifested object.
5. ✅ Rāmānuja calls the cosmos God’s body technically, but this is dependence and control—not a finite anthropomorphic body.
6. ⚠️ A cause may be known through effects without being physically similar to its effects.

### Balanced verdict

```text
Physical embodiment may aid religious accessibility
                         ≠
Physical embodiment is necessary for metaphysical causality
```

⚠️ The stronger conclusion is that ultimacy requires **explanatory non-dependence**, not bodily manifestation. Some God-concepts involve embodiment, but it is not conceptually necessary for every ultimate cause.

## 4. Personal versus impersonal God

| Personalistic strength | Impersonalistic strength |
|---|---|
| Explains devotion, prayer, morality and providence | Avoids anthropomorphism and finite predicates |
| Grounds relational religious experience | Preserves infinity and metaphysical absoluteness |
| Supports purpose and grace | Explains unity beneath plurality |

| Personalistic pressure | Impersonalistic pressure |
|---|---|
| How can an infinite being possess person-like will? | Can an impersonal Absolute be worshipped or morally authoritative? |
| Why does a good and powerful person permit evil? | How can it explain purpose, grace and religious response? |

⚠️ Advaita creates a hierarchy between the two; Viśiṣṭādvaita makes personality ultimate; Spinoza rejects volitional personality; classical theism extends personal predicates analogically rather than univocally.

## 5. Immanence versus transcendence

```text
ONLY TRANSCENDENCE → remote God, weak world-presence
ONLY IMMANENCE     → risk of collapsing God into world
BOTH               → God exceeds yet sustains/indwells world
```

✅ Classical theism and Viśiṣṭādvaita combine both, though differently.

- Classical theism: God remains ontologically distinct while sustaining creation.
- Viśiṣṭādvaita: God exceeds the world while indwelling it as the soul of a body.
- Spinoza: radical immanence without a transcendent creator.
- Advaita: Brahman transcends every finite predicate yet is the reality of all appearances.
