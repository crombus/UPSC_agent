### SESSION 1 — Yoga as Theistic Samkhya (seśvara-sāṃkhya) and the Structure of Mind-field (citta)

#### Plain-language visual - the mind is the instrument, not the self

```text
OBJECTS -> SENSES -> MIND-FIELD (citta)
                     |
        sensory mind (manas) + ego-maker (ahaṃkāra) + determinative intellect (buddhi)
                     |
          reflects CONSCIOUS WITNESS (puruṣa)'s light
                     |
        "I am thinking and suffering"

Yoga disciplines mind-field (citta) so witness and instrument are no longer confused.
```

**In simple words:** Yoga accepts Samkhya's Purusa-Prakriti dualism but develops a detailed practical psychology. Mind-field (citta) is the subtle material mind-complex; Conscious witness (puruṣa) is the conscious witness.

| Mind-field (citta) condition | Easy meaning |
|---|---|
| Restless mind (kṣipta) | Restless |
| Dull mind (mūḍha) | Dull |
| Distracted mind (vikṣipta) | Sometimes focused |
| One-pointed mind (ekāgra) | One-pointed |
| Fully restrained mind (niruddha) | Fully restrained |

> **Memory line:** Samkhya gives the map; Yoga trains the mind that misreads the map.

> ⚠️ **Exam key:** Yoga is not merely posture-discipline. It is a full theory of mind, bondage, meditative cognition and liberation built on Sāṃkhya ontology but modified by the admission of **Lord (Īśvara)** as **puruṣa-viśeṣa**.

#### INTRODUCTION — YOGA AS THEISTIC SAMKHYA (seśvara-sāṃkhya)

- ✅ Classical Yoga is often described as **sāṃkhya-pravacana** or **theistic Samkhya (seśvara-sāṃkhya)**: it shares the metaphysical structure of [Sāṃkhya](./Samkhya.md) but adds a practical path and a theistic element.
- ✅ It accepts the basic dualism of **conscious witness (puruṣa)** and **primordial material nature (prakṛti)**, and the standard **25 principles (tattvas)** of Sāṃkhya.
- ✅ What Yoga adds is a carefully graded **soteriology**: control of mind-field (citta), attenuation of afflictions (kleśas), meditative absorption, and final isolation (kaivalya).
- ✅ It also admits **Lord (Īśvara)**, a **special conscious witness (puruṣa)** untouched by affliction and karmic residue, thereby distinguishing itself from nirīśvara Sāṃkhya.
- ⚠️ Thus the school may be summed up as: **Sāṃkhya gives the metaphysical map; Yoga gives the psychology and method of release.**
- ⚠️ In UPSC answers, this introductory framing immediately prevents two confusions: Yoga is neither merely physical culture nor an Advaitic path of union with Brahman.

#### 1. MIND-FIELD (citta)

#### 1.1 Statement

- ✅ **Mind-field (citta)** (citta) is the internal organ or mind-complex through which cognition, affect, volition and meditative discipline become possible.
- ✅ In Yoga, mind-field (citta) is not merely **sensory mind (manas)** in isolation; it functions as the unified internal apparatus comprising **determinative intellect (buddhi)**, **ego-maker (ahaṃkāra)** and **sensory mind (manas)**.
- ✅ Its material basis is primordial nature: determinative intellect evolves first, ego-maker from intellect, and coordinating mind from the sattva-predominant side of ego-maker. Therefore only one component of mind-field (citta)—mind in the narrow sense—arises directly from sattva-predominant ego-maker.
- ✅ Mind-field (citta) as a functioning whole is **sattva-predominant**, which explains its transparency, but it should not be said that intellect, ego and mind all arise directly from sattva-predominant ego-maker.
- ⚠️ Therefore mind-field (citta) is best understood as **the psychical instrument of experience**, not as the true self.

#### 1.2 Constitution of mind-field (citta)

| Component within the functioning unity of mind-field (citta) | Primary role | Why Yoga includes it under mind-field (citta) |
|---|---|---|
| **Determinative intellect (buddhi)** | determinative intellect, ascertainment | decisive cognition, discrimination, resolve occur here |
| **Ego-maker (ahaṃkāra)** | ego-principle, appropriation as "I" | ownership of states, doership and identification arise here |
| **Sensory mind (manas)** | sensory co-ordination, doubt, oscillation | receives and arranges sense-data, moves between options |

- ✅ Though Sāṃkhya analytically distinguishes these, Yoga often treats them as one operative continuum called **mind-field (citta)**.
- ⚠️ This broad usage is philosophically useful because bondage and liberation both concern the **whole mental field**, not one isolated faculty.

#### 1.3 Mind-field (citta) as reflective, not self-luminous in its own right

- ✅ Mind-field (citta) is a product of primordial material nature (prakṛti) and therefore **insentient** in itself.
- ✅ Yet because of its subtle, sāttvika transparency, it can **reflect** consciousness.
- ✅ The standard analogy is that of a **clear crystal placed near a flower**: the crystal appears coloured by the flower though it is itself colourless.
- ✅ Similarly, mind-field (citta) takes on the form and colour of objects placed before it, while the consciousness of **conscious witness (puruṣa)** is reflected in it.
- ⚠️ The empirical self arises when the reflected consciousness in mind-field (citta) is mistaken for the intrinsic nature of mind-field (citta) itself.
- ⚠️ Hence Yoga's theory of mind is neither crude materialism nor idealism: mind-field (citta) is material-subtle, but consciousness belongs originally to conscious witness (puruṣa).

#### 1.4 Misidentification of self with mind-field (citta)

- ✅ So long as mind-field (citta) is modified by objects, memories, desires, fears and conceptual constructions, the self appears entangled in them.
- ✅ The reflective relation causes bondage when **discriminative knowledge** is absent.
- ✅ A standard exam-worthy formulation is: **so long as there are changes and modifications in mind-field (citta), the self is reflected therein and, lacking discriminative discernment, identifies itself with those modifications.**
- ⚠️ This is the psychological core of bondage in Yoga: the seer is confused with the seen because reflection masquerades as identity.

#### 1.4A Reflection (pratibimba/chāyā) presuppositions

**Statement.** ✅ Yoga's reflection theory says that **mind-field (citta)** appears conscious because it stands near **conscious witness (puruṣa)**, while conscious witness (puruṣa) appears bound because its light is reflected in mind-field (citta); this is a **pratibimba** (pratibimba; reflection) or **chāyā** (chaya; shadow-image) model, not a literal alteration of conscious witness (puruṣa).

**Argument.**
1. ✅ **Conscious witness (puruṣa)** is **apariṇāmin** (aparinamin; unchanging) and therefore cannot literally act, suffer, acquire qualities or undergo modification.
2. ✅ **Mind-field (citta)** is a product of **primordial material nature (prakṛti)** and is therefore **jaḍa** (jada; insentient), yet its sattva-predominance makes it transparent, subtle and capable of mirroring.
3. ✅ Since an inactive consciousness cannot enter causal contact like one physical object striking another, knowledge requires a reflection/proximity model rather than a crude causal-contact model.
4. ✅ Bondage is therefore the confusion of **ego-identification (asmitā)** and **sva-svāmi-śakti** (sva-svami-shakti; the power of the owned and the owner): mind-field (citta)'s borrowed consciousness is misread as selfhood, and conscious witness (puruṣa)'s witnesshood is misread as agency.
5. ⚠️ The soteriological task is not to change conscious witness (puruṣa) but to end the cognitive misattribution by purifying and restraining mind-field (citta).

**Presupposition.** ✅ The model presupposes the Sāṃkhya-Yoga dualism of two irreducible orders: conscious but inactive conscious witness (puruṣa), and active but insentient primordial material nature (prakṛti)/mind-field (citta).

**Example.** ✅ The crystal (**sphaṭika**) near a red flower seems red without itself becoming the flower; ⚠️ similarly, a magnet and iron image helps exam writing: mere presence (**sannidhi**) can explain movement on the iron/mind-field (citta) side without action on the magnet/conscious witness (puruṣa) side.

**Objection.** ⚠️ A reflection normally requires some real relation between mirror and original. If conscious witness (puruṣa) and primordial material nature (prakṛti) are ontologically disjoint, how can inactive conscious witness (puruṣa) be "reflected" in mind-field (citta) at all?

**Reply.** ✅ Yoga replies through **sannidhi-mātra** (sannidhi-matra; mere proximity): the transformation is entirely on the mind-field (citta) side; conscious witness (puruṣa) neither acts nor changes. ⚠️ Yet the objection retains residual force, because Advaita and Nyāya can press the classic problem of how two radically different principles can explain one experience.

#### 1.5 Why mind-field (citta) matters for soteriology

- ✅ Since bondage occurs through mind-field (citta), liberation must also be worked out through mind-field (citta).
- ✅ One does not destroy conscious witness (puruṣa)'s bondage by changing conscious witness (puruṣa) itself, because conscious witness (puruṣa) is eternally pure.
- ✅ Rather, one disciplines mind-field (citta) so completely that the false identification ceases.
- ⚠️ Yoga is therefore fundamentally a **therapy of attention, habit, impression and misidentification**.

#### 1.6 Citta-bhūmis — the five levels or conditions of mind-field (citta)

- ✅ Yoga tradition classifies mind-field (citta) into **five bhūmis** or operative levels.
- ✅ These levels indicate the dominance of the qualities (guṇas) and the fitness of the mind for yoga.

| Citta-bhūmi | Dominant guṇa-pattern | Basic character | Exam use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Restless mind (kṣipta)** (ksipta) | **rajas** dominant | restless, scattered, thrown outward | ordinary agitated mind |
| **Dull mind (mūḍha)** (mudha) | **tamas** dominant | dull, torpid, stupefied | lethargic, clouded mind |
| **Distracted mind (vikṣipta)** (viksipta) | mixed, with occasional **sattva** | intermittently focused but unstable | capable in bursts, still worldly |
| **One-pointed mind (ekāgra)** (ekagra) | **sattva** dominant | one-pointed, concentrated | fit for true yogic practice |
| **Fully restrained mind (niruddha)** (niruddha) | vṛttis restrained | all modifications arrested | culmination of yogic discipline |

#### 1.7 Explanation of the five bhūmis

##### (a) Restless mind (kṣipta)
- ✅ The mind runs from object to object under rajasic impulsion.
- ✅ There is activity, but not integration.
- ⚠️ Desire, worry, ambition and sensory dispersion dominate.

##### (b) Dull mind (mūḍha)
- ✅ The mind is overpowered by tamas.
- ✅ Torpor, delusion, inertia and confusion prevail.
- ⚠️ This state is not calmness but obscuration.

##### (c) Distracted mind (vikṣipta)
- ✅ The mind can attend, infer, analyse and even sustain brief concentration, but cannot remain inwardly collected.
- ✅ Sattva flashes forth occasionally, yet rajas and tamas are not overcome.
- ⚠️ This is the condition of many intelligent worldly persons: capable of focus, but not yet spiritually integrated.

##### (d) One-pointed mind (ekāgra)
- ✅ The mind becomes one-pointed on a chosen support.
- ✅ Sattva predominates sufficiently for meditative absorption to deepen.
- ✅ This is one of the two genuinely yoga-fit states.

##### (e) Fully restrained mind (niruddha)
- ✅ The modifications are fully restrained.
- ✅ This is not unconscious blankness but the consummation of disciplined inwardness.
- ✅ It is the citta-state correlated with the highest meditative absorption (samādhi) and the approach to isolation (kaivalya).

#### 1.8 Which citta-bhūmis are fit for yoga?

- ✅ Only **one-pointed mind (ekāgra)** and **fully restrained mind (niruddha)** are fully fit for authentic yoga.
- ✅ **Restless mind (kṣipta)**, **dull mind (mūḍha)** and **distracted mind (vikṣipta)** remain pre-yogic or only partially prepared stages.
- ⚠️ The transition from distracted mind (vikṣipta) to one-pointed mind (ekāgra) is decisive: the mind moves from occasional concentration to sustained one-pointedness.

#### 1.9 PYQ application: scientist, devotee, self-realized yogi

- ✅ UPSC in 2019 used the citta-bhūmi classification in applied form.
- ✅ A **scientist** intensely focused on an empirical problem yet still directed outward and attached to worldly ends is best classified as **distracted mind (vikṣipta)**.
- ✅ A **God-realized devotee** whose mind is one-pointedly centred upon Lord (Īśvara) is **one-pointed mind (ekāgra)**.
- ✅ A **self-realized yogi** in whom all modifications are fully stilled is **fully restrained mind (niruddha)**.
- ⚠️ The subtlety is important: high intellectual focus does not itself amount to yogic restraint.

#### 1.10 Mind-field (citta) in inter-school perspective

| School | What is the internal organ? | Key contrast with Yoga |
|---|---|---|
| [Sāṃkhya](./Samkhya.md) | determinative intellect (buddhi), ego-maker (ahaṃkāra), sensory mind (manas) analytically distinct | Yoga gathers them functionally as mind-field (citta) |
| [Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika](./Nyaya-Vaisesika.md) | **sensory mind (manas)** as atomic internal sense | Yoga's mind-field (citta) is far broader than atomic sensory mind (manas) |
| [Vedānta](./Vedanta.md) | antaḥkaraṇa under ignorance (avidyā), with Brahman framework | Yoga retains plural conscious witness (puruṣas) and no Brahman-doctrine |

#### 1.11 Mini-conclusion on mind-field (citta)

- ⚠️ For UPSC, define mind-field (citta) as the **sāttvika psychical instrument composed of buddhi-ahaṃkāra-manas, reflecting conscious witness (puruṣa) and becoming modified by objects**.
- ⚠️ Then show why Yoga insists on discipline: without citta-niyama, conscious witness (puruṣa) continues to identify with borrowed mental states.

### SESSION 2 — Restraint of mental modifications (citta-vṛtti-nirodha): Five Modifications and Real Transformation of Mind

#### Plain-language visual - why even true thoughts are waves

```text
MIND-FIELD (citta) takes the form of:
true knowledge | error | word-construction | sleep | memory
                         |
                 CONSCIOUS WITNESS (puruṣa) identifies
                         |
                     bondage

SUSTAINED PRACTICE (abhyāsa) + DISPASSION (vairāgya) -> increasingly stable restraint
```

**In simple words:** Yoga does not say every thought is false. It says every thought is a changing form of mind-field (citta). True cognition can help liberation, but final freedom requires ending identification with all mental forms.

| Vrtti | Simple meaning |
|---|---|
| Means of valid knowledge (pramāṇa) | Valid cognition |
| Viparyaya | Error |
| Vikalpa | Words without corresponding object |
| Nidra | Sleep-mode |
| Smrti | Memory |

> **Memory line:** truth can be a ladder, but the witness is not the ladder.

#### 2. CITTAVṚTTI

#### 2.1 Statement

- ✅ **Cittavṛtti** (cittavrtti) means a modification, fluctuation or operative mode of mind-field (citta).
- ✅ Yoga holds that mind-field (citta) constantly assumes forms corresponding to objects, memories, errors, absences and verbal constructions.
- ✅ These modifications are the immediate field of bondage because the reflected self falsely identifies with them.

#### 2.2 Foundational sūtra

- ✅ The defining statement of the system is: **"Yogaś-citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ"** — Yoga is the restraint of the modifications of mind-field (citta).
- ✅ The immediately following implication is: **"tadā draṣṭuḥ svarūpe'vasthānam"** — then the seer abides in its own nature.
- ⚠️ These two aphorisms together explain the entire logic of Yoga: control is not an end in itself; it is required because only when vṛttis cease does conscious witness (puruṣa) stand free of misidentification.
#### 2.3 Why is cessation prescribed?

1. ✅ Mind-field (citta), by its nature, takes the form of whatever it attends to.
2. ✅ Conscious witness (puruṣa)'s consciousness is reflected in mind-field (citta).
3. ✅ In ignorance, the self appropriates these changing forms as "I know," "I suffer," "I desire," "I remember."
4. ✅ Therefore the stream of vṛttis sustains bondage.
5. ✅ Their restraint uncovers the already-free nature of the seer.
- ⚠️ A safe image is the clear pool: when ripples settle, the water reflects without distortion; when mental modifications (citta-vṛttis) settle, conscious witness (puruṣa) is no longer misread through mental turbulence.
- ⚠️ Thus **nirodha** is revelatory, not destructive: it removes concealment, not consciousness.

#### 2.4 The five vṛttis

- ✅ Patañjali classifies all modifications into **five kinds**.
- ✅ Each may be either **kliṣṭa** or **akliṣṭa** depending on whether it sustains affliction or aids release.

| Vṛtti | Definition | Example | Philosophical note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Means of valid knowledge (pramāṇa)** | valid cognition | seeing smoke and correctly inferring fire | even true knowledge is still a modification |
| **Viparyaya** | error or misconception | taking nacre for silver | false cognition distorts conduct |
| **Vikalpa** | verbal or conceptual construction without real object | "the horn of a hare" | language can move without ontic referent |
| **Nidrā** | sleep as a positive mental mode grounded in absence-content | awareness later reported as "I slept" | even sleep is not non-mental blankness |
| **Smṛti** | memory | recollection of past pain or pleasure | retained traces re-present prior experience |

#### 2.5 Means of valid knowledge (pramāṇa) as vṛtti

- ✅ Yoga does not deny valid cognition; it includes it as one kind of vṛtti.
- ✅ The accepted forms of means of valid knowledge (pramāṇa) are perception (pratyakṣa), inference (anumāna) and **āgama/verbal testimony (śabda)** (authoritative testimony).
- ✅ This aligns Yoga with the three-pramāṇa scheme of Sāṃkhya.
- ⚠️ Exam point: because even valid cognition is still a citta-modification, Yoga seeks not endless conceptual accumulation but eventual stilling.
- ⚠️ Cross-reference: see [Means of valid knowledge (pramāṇa) across schools](../_themes/Pramana-across-schools.md).

#### 2.6 Viparyaya

- ✅ **Viparyaya** is false knowledge not corresponding to the object as it is.
- ✅ It includes perceptual and conceptual misapprehension.
- ⚠️ Since error reinforces wrong attachment and aversion, it is typically **kliṣṭa**.

#### 2.7 Vikalpa

- ✅ **Vikalpa** is cognition produced by words alone where no corresponding object exists.
- ✅ It is not sheer nonsense; it has linguistic intelligibility without objective referent.
- ⚠️ Yoga is sensitive to the autonomous power of language to create mental worlds, anxieties, dogmas and imagined identities.

#### 2.8 Nidrā

- ✅ Sleep is treated as a positive vṛtti rather than a mere absence.
- ✅ Later recollection — "I slept and knew nothing" — shows that sleep leaves a determinate mental trace.
- ⚠️ Philosophically, Yoga refuses to equate quiescence with liberation: mere sleep suppresses activity, but does not produce discriminative freedom.

#### 2.9 Smṛti

- ✅ Memory is the re-presentation of past experience without complete loss of its prior content.
- ✅ Smṛti links past impressions to present identity and reaction.
- ⚠️ In practical psychology, memory feeds attachment, trauma, fear and habitual self-narration.

#### 2.10 Kliṣṭa and akliṣṭa vṛttis

- ✅ Each of the five vṛttis can be either **kliṣṭa** (afflicted) or **akliṣṭa** (non-afflicted).
- ✅ **Kliṣṭa vṛttis** are those coloured by ignorance (avidyā) and the other afflictions (kleśas) and hence productive of bondage.
- ✅ **Akliṣṭa vṛttis** are those purified and redirected toward discrimination, detachment and liberation.
- ⚠️ For example, valid cognition of impermanence, disciplined scriptural reflection or recollection aiding practice may be akliṣṭa, while the same broad vṛtti-types in service of vanity or desire remain kliṣṭa.

#### 2.11 Why this distinction matters

- ✅ Yoga does not begin by abolishing all cognition at once.
- ✅ It first transforms the quality of mental life, replacing afflicted modifications by non-afflicted ones.
- ⚠️ Akliṣṭa vṛttis are therapeutic instruments that ultimately subside once their work is done.

#### 2.12 Means of restraint of mental modifications (citta-vṛtti-nirodha)

- ✅ The basic means are sustained practice (abhyāsa) and **dispassion (vairāgya)** (detachment).
- ✅ Preparatory discipline (kriyā-yoga) weakens afflictive forces.
- ✅ Eight-limbed discipline (aṣṭāṅga-yoga) progressively disciplines the body-sense-mind complex.
- ✅ Dedication to the Lord (īśvara-praṇidhāna) offers a theistic aid to concentration and obstacle-removal.
- ⚠️ Thus nirodha is methodical, not abrupt.

#### 2.13 Cittavṛtti and the 2023 demand

- ✅ The 2023 question asked both **what mind-field (citta) and its modifications are** and **why cessation is prescribed**.
- ⚠️ The ideal answer must therefore move in three steps: define mind-field (citta), list the five vṛttis, and explain nirodha through the misidentification of conscious witness (puruṣa) with mind-field (citta).

#### 2.14 Cittavṛtti and the 2024 demand

- ✅ The 2024 wording emphasized that as long as mind-field (citta) changes, the self is reflected therein and identifies itself with those changes in the absence of discriminative knowledge.
- ⚠️ This directly joins §1 and §2: the question is not about mental mechanics alone but about Yoga's full **soteriology of reflection, confusion and restraint**.

#### 2.15 Mini-conclusion on cittavṛtti

- ⚠️ In exam language: **mental modifications (citta-vṛttis) are the dynamic forms assumed by the mind-complex; they must be restrained because their ongoing operation sustains the false identification of the seer with the seen.**

#### 2.16 Citta-pariṇāma — transformation of the mental substrate

**Statement.** ✅ **Citta-pariṇāma** (citta-parinama; transformation of citta) is Yoga's account of how the mind-complex really changes across practice without destroying its continuity as a prakṛtic substrate.

**Argument.**
1. ✅ The Vibhūti-pāda tradition recognises three specifically yogic transformations of mind-field (citta): **nirodha-pariṇāma**, **samādhi-pariṇāma** and **ekāgratā-pariṇāma**.
2. ✅ **Nirodha-pariṇāma** is the transformation in which impressions of restraint increasingly overpower impressions of emergence; the mind becomes habituated to cessation rather than dispersion.
3. ✅ **Samādhi-pariṇāma** is the transformation from many-pointedness to unified absorption; the distracting multiplicity of contents weakens while one object becomes dominant.
4. ✅ **Ekāgratā-pariṇāma** is the transformation of one-pointed continuity, where similar cognitive moments arise and subside in an unbroken stream toward the same object.
5. ✅ Yoga also accepts a general threefold transformation of any substrate: **dharma-pariṇāma** (dharma-parinama; change of property), **lakṣaṇa-pariṇāma** (laksana-parinama; change of temporal character) and **avasthā-pariṇāma** (avastha-parinama; change of condition or state).
6. ✅ These transformations presuppose a **dharmin** (dharmin; persisting substrate) that undergoes changes of property, temporal phase and condition without becoming sheer momentary flashes.
7. ⚠️ This is philosophically necessary because Yoga inherits Sāṃkhya **real transformation (pariṇāmavāda)** (parinamavada; real transformation): mind-field (citta) really changes, and the path's graded progress would be unintelligible if there were no continuing mental substrate.

**Presupposition.** ✅ The doctrine presupposes that primordial material nature (prakṛti) and its evolutes are real and transformable, while conscious witness (puruṣa) remains unmodified witness-consciousness.

**Example.** ⚠️ The same lake can be stormy, gradually calming, and finally still; the states differ, yet the lake-substrate persists. Likewise mind-field (citta) can pass from restless mind (kṣipta) to one-pointed mind (ekāgra) to fully restrained mind (niruddha) without becoming a new self.

**Objection.** ⚠️ A Buddhist **kṣaṇikavādin** (ksanikavadin; momentarist) may argue that only a stream of momentary events is needed; a persisting citta-substrate is an unnecessary fiction.

**Reply.** ✅ Yoga answers that practice, memory, saṃskāra-continuity and stable meditative acquisition require more than disconnected flashes. ⚠️ Both Yoga and Buddhism admit ceaseless change, but Yoga retains a persisting prakṛtic substrate, whereas Buddhist **kṣaṇikavāda** (ksanikavada; momentariness) denies enduring mental substance.

| Comparison | Yoga's position | Exam contrast |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ Yoga's **real transformation (pariṇāmavāda)** vs Buddhist **kṣaṇikavāda** | ✅ Real change occurs in a continuing mind-field (citta)/dharmin. | ⚠️ Buddhism can explain flow without a substrate; Yoga needs substrate for saṃskāra and graded discipline. |
| ✅ Yoga's **real transformation (pariṇāmavāda)** vs Advaita **vivartavāda** (vivartavada; apparent transformation) | ✅ Change in mind-field (citta)/primordial material nature (prakṛti) is real, not merely apparent. | ⚠️ Advaita makes ultimate reality non-transforming Brahman; Yoga keeps real prakṛtic change and many conscious witness (puruṣas). |

### SESSION 3 — Afflictions (kleśas), Action and moral consequence (karma) and Astanga Yoga: The Causal Therapy of Bondage

#### Plain-language visual - diagnosis and treatment

```text
IGNORANCE (avidyā)
  |
EGO-IDENTIFICATION (asmitā) -> ATTACHMENT (rāga) / AVERSION (dveṣa) -> CLINGING TO LIFE
  (abhiniveśa)
  |
ACTION AND MORAL CONSEQUENCE (karma) -> DEPOSIT -> FRUITION -> REBIRTH

ETHICAL RESTRAINT (yama) + OBSERVANCE (niyama) + POSTURE (āsana) + BREATH REGULATION (prāṇāyāma)
   + SENSE WITHDRAWAL (pratyāhāra) + CONCENTRATION (dhāraṇā) + MEDITATION (dhyāna) +
     MEDITATIVE ABSORPTION (samādhi)
                    |
             weaken and burn the causes
```

**In simple words:** Yoga treats bondage as a causal illness. Ignorance produces ego-identification, attachment, aversion and fear; these colour action and continue the cycle. The eight limbs transform conduct, embodiment and attention at progressively deeper levels.

> **Memory line:** ethics calms the causes that meditation must finally uproot.

#### 3. AFFLICTIONS (kleśas)

#### 3.1 Statement

- ✅ **Afflictions (kleśas)** (klesas) are the afflictions that poison mind-field (citta), generate action and moral consequence (karma) and sustain saṃsāric bondage.
- ✅ They are deeper than surface thoughts: they are the causal colouring of mental life.
- ✅ Yoga lists **five afflictions (kleśas)**, with **ignorance (avidyā)** as the root.

#### 3.2 The five afflictions (kleśas) at a glance

| Affliction (kleśa) | Core meaning | Why it binds |
|---|---|---|
| **Ignorance (avidyā)** | ignorance, misapprehension of reality | root of all other afflictions |
| **Ego-identification (asmitā)** | egoity, I-am-ness | confuses conscious witness (puruṣa) with determinative intellect (buddhi) |
| **Attachment (rāga)** | attachment | pursues pleasure and repetition |
| **Aversion (dveṣa)** | aversion | flees pain and breeds hostility |
| **Clinging to life (abhiniveśa)** | clinging to life / fear of death | instinctive self-preservation binds the stream |

#### 3.3 Ignorance (avidyā) as the root

- ✅ Ignorance (avidyā) is specifically defined as mistaking:
  - the **non-eternal** for the eternal,
  - the **impure** for the pure,
  - the **painful** for the pleasant,
  - the **non-self** for the self.
- ✅ It is the matrix from which all other afflictions (kleśas) arise.
- ⚠️ Ignorance (avidyā) in Yoga is not merely lack of information; it is a pervasive existential misorientation.

#### 3.4 Ego-identification (asmitā)

- ✅ **Ego-identification (asmitā)** is the confusion of the seer with the instrument of seeing.
- ✅ More precisely, it is the identification of **conscious witness (puruṣa)** with **determinative intellect (buddhi)** or the internal organ.
- ⚠️ The ego says, "I think, I act, I suffer," because it cannot distinguish consciousness from its reflected vehicle.

#### 3.5 Attachment (rāga)

- ✅ **Attachment (rāga)** is attachment arising from remembered pleasure.
- ✅ It pushes mind-field (citta) toward repetition and possession.
- ⚠️ In practical life, attachment (rāga) keeps the mind outward-turned and dependent on conditions.

#### 3.6 Aversion (dveṣa)

- ✅ **Aversion (dveṣa)** is aversion arising from remembered pain.
- ✅ It is the negative counterpart of attachment (rāga).
- ⚠️ Together attachment (rāga) and aversion (dveṣa) structure most reactive life: chasing the agreeable, fleeing the disagreeable.

#### 3.7 Clinging to life (abhiniveśa)

- ✅ **Clinging to life (abhiniveśa)** is clinging to life, often expressed as fear of death.
- ✅ Yoga stresses that it is present **even in the learned**.
- ⚠️ This is a subtle and frequently tested point: clinging to life (abhiniveśa) is not merely an intellectual error, but a deeply rooted instinctive attachment carried by latent impressions.
#### 3.8 The four states of afflictions (kleśas)

- ✅ Afflictions (kleśas) do not always function at the same intensity.
- ✅ Tradition describes **four states**.

| State | Meaning | Illustration |
|---|---|---|
| **Prasupta** | dormant | seed-like, not presently manifest |
| **Tanu** | attenuated | weakened by discipline and insight |
| **Vicchinna** | interrupted | temporarily suppressed by opposing forces |
| **Udāra** | active, expanded | fully operative in conduct and reaction |

#### 3.9 Explanation of the four states

##### (a) Prasupta
- ✅ The affliction (kleśa) remains in latency.
- ⚠️ Dormancy is not destruction; favourable conditions can reactivate it.

##### (b) Tanu
- ✅ Through practice, austerity, meditation and discernment, afflictions are thinned out.
- ⚠️ This is the desirable middle state in disciplined progress.

##### (c) Vicchinna
- ✅ An affliction can be temporarily interrupted by the operation of another tendency.
- ⚠️ For example, attachment may seem absent only because fear is presently stronger.

##### (d) Udāra
- ✅ The affliction (kleśa) becomes active and behaviour-shaping.
- ⚠️ Here it visibly governs speech, action and affect.

#### 3.10 Preparatory discipline (kriyā-yoga) as first therapy of afflictions (kleśas)

- ✅ Patañjali prescribes **preparatory discipline (kriyā-yoga)** as consisting of:
  1. **tapas** — disciplined austerity or heat-generating effort,
  2. **svādhyāya** — self-study and/or recitation-study of sacred teaching,
  3. **Dedication to the Lord (īśvara-praṇidhāna)** — surrender or devotion to Lord (Īśvara).
- ✅ Its explicit purpose is to **attenuate afflictions (kleśas)** and prepare for meditative absorption (samādhi).
- ⚠️ Thus Yoga begins not from abstract metaphysics alone but from deliberate re-education of habit and motive.

#### 3.11 Meditation (dhyāna) as deeper burning of afflictions (kleśas)

- ✅ Gross kleśa-expressions are weakened by ethical and practical discipline.
- ✅ Their subtle forms (**sūkṣma**) are burned through **meditation (dhyāna)** and higher meditative absorption.
- ⚠️ This explains why mere moral restraint is insufficient: latent affliction survives unless its causal seed is scorched.

#### 3.12 Causal chain of bondage

- ✅ The afflictive mechanism may be summarised as:

```text
Afflictions (kleśas) → Action and moral consequence (karma) → Vipāka → Āśaya → Punarjanma
```

- ✅ **Afflictions (kleśas)** motivate action.
- ✅ Action deposits karmic force.
- ✅ Action and moral consequence (karma) ripens as **vipāka** (fruition).
- ✅ The residual store becomes **āśaya** or karmic deposit.
- ✅ This fuels further birth and suffering.
- ⚠️ The chain shows why Yoga is not satisfied with external behaviour-management; it aims at the root-affliction itself.

#### 3.13 Action and moral consequence (karma) and vipāka — kleśa-rooted moral causation

**Statement.** ✅ **Karmāśaya** (karmashaya; karmic deposit or stock) is the subtle store of action-traces rooted in afflictions (kleśas), and **vipāka** (vipaka; fruition) is its ripening as concrete embodied experience.

**Argument.**
1. ✅ The karmic deposit is **kleśa-mūla** (klesha-mula; rooted in the afflictions): action binds because it is coloured by ignorance (avidyā), ego-identification (asmitā), attachment (rāga), aversion (dveṣa) and clinging to life (abhiniveśa).
2. ✅ Vipāka is classically threefold: **jāti** (jati; birth or species-condition), **āyus** (ayus; life-span) and **bhoga** (bhoga; experience/enjoyment-suffering).
3. ✅ **Vāsanā** (vasana; latent tendency) and **saṃskāra** (samskara; impression) explain continuity: repeated cognition and action leave traces, traces dispose future cognition and action, and the cycle becomes morally structured.
4. ✅ Yoga classifies action and moral consequence (karma) as **śukla** (shukla; white), **kṛṣṇa** (krishna; black), **śukla-kṛṣṇa** (mixed white-black) and **aśukla-akṛṣṇa** (ashukla-akrishna; neither white nor black), the last being associated with the yogin whose action no longer produces ordinary bondage.
5. ⚠️ This matters philosophically because Yoga is not merely therapeutic psychology; it explains how psychological purification can have soteriological effect by interrupting the moral-causal engine of rebirth.
6. ⚠️ Therefore kleśa-attenuation (**tanū-karaṇa**, tanu-karana; thinning) and final **reverse evolution (pratiprasava)** (reabsorption into the cause) are the real targets, not temporary emotional calm.

**Presupposition.** ✅ Yoga presupposes a morally ordered cosmos in which subtle mental-affective causes can ripen beyond a single visible episode of experience.

**Example.** ✅ The **dagdha-bīja** (dagdha-bija; burnt seed) image is exam-safe: a burnt seed may retain seed-shape, but it cannot germinate; similarly attenuated and burnt afflictions (kleśas) lose karmic fertility.

**Objection.** ⚠️ If liberation depends on exhausting an unsurveyable stock of karmāśaya, release seems indefinitely postponed.

**Reply.** ✅ Yoga replies that liberating knowledge and meditative absorption (samādhi) burn the seeds of future bondage. ⚠️ Using later comparative vocabulary, only **prārabdha-type** (prarabdha-type; already-fructifying) embodiment continues until its momentum is spent, like a potter's wheel turning briefly after the potter stops pushing.

#### 3.14 Why removal of afflictions (kleśas) leads to isolation (kaivalya)

1. ✅ Afflictions (kleśas) are the roots of karmically binding activity.
2. ✅ Without afflictions (kleśas), karmic production and karmic reactivation cease.
3. ✅ Without karmic deposit, the machinery of repeated birth collapses.
4. ✅ As mind-field (citta) is purified and discrimination matures, meditative absorption (samādhi) becomes stable.
5. ✅ This culminates in isolation (kaivalya).
- ⚠️ The 2021 20-marker demanded precisely this transition from **diagnosis of affliction** to **logic of liberation**.

#### 3.15 Affliction (kleśa) and error across schools

| School | Root problem | Yoga's distinctive feature |
|---|---|---|
| Buddhism | ignorance (avidyā) + tṛṣṇā | Yoga keeps enduring conscious witness (puruṣa) and citta-psychology |
| [Sāṃkhya](./Samkhya.md) | aviveka between conscious witness (puruṣa) and primordial material nature (prakṛti) | Yoga adds meditative psychology and Lord (Īśvara) |
| [Advaita Vedānta](./Vedanta.md) | ignorance (avidyā)/adhyāsa | Yoga has no Brahman-identity thesis |
| [Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika](./Nyaya-Vaisesika.md) | mithyājñāna | Yoga stresses affective affliction and meditative remedy |

#### 3.16 Mini-conclusion on afflictions (kleśas)

- ⚠️ A compact answer-line: **afflictions (kleśas) are the afflictive roots of cycle of rebirth (saṃsāra), with ignorance (avidyā) as the source and preparatory discipline (kriyā-yoga) plus meditation as the means of attenuation and destruction.**

#### 4. AṢṬĀṄGA YOGA

#### 4.1 Why include eight-limbed discipline (aṣṭāṅga-yoga) here?

- ✅ Though the syllabus clause names mind-field (citta), cittavṛtti, afflictions (kleśas), meditative absorption (samādhi) and isolation (kaivalya), the path linking them is the **aṣṭāṅga** discipline.
- ✅ UPSC often expects the mediating practical architecture in any fuller answer on Yoga.
- ⚠️ The point is philosophical significance, not physical drill.

#### 4.2 The eight limbs

| Limb | Basic meaning | Group | Philosophical significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ethical restraint (yama)** | restraints | **bahiraṅga** | social-ethical purification of conduct |
| **Observance (niyama)** | observances | **bahiraṅga** | positive self-cultivation and inner orientation |
| **Posture (āsana)** | posture | **bahiraṅga** | embodied steadiness enabling meditative work |
| **Breath regulation (prāṇāyāma)** | regulation of breath-life-force | **bahiraṅga** | calms psycho-physical disturbance |
| **Sense withdrawal (pratyāhāra)** | withdrawal of senses | **bahiraṅga** / bridge | de-links mind-field (citta) from compulsive object-pursuit |
| **Concentration (dhāraṇā)** | concentration | **antaraṅga** | fixing attention on one locus |
| **Meditation (dhyāna)** | meditation | **antaraṅga** | continuous flow toward the object |
| **Meditative absorption (samādhi)** | absorption | **antaraṅga** | consummated identity of attention with support |

#### 4.3 Bahiraṅga and antaraṅga

- ✅ The first five are called **bahiraṅga** — external limbs.
- ✅ The last three are **antaraṅga** — internal limbs.
- ⚠️ "External" does not mean unimportant; it means preparatory with respect to the subtler operations of meditative consciousness.

#### 4.4 Ethical restraint (yama)

- ✅ The **ethical restraint (yamas)** are the great restraints governing relation to others and to desire.
- ✅ The classical list is:
  1. **ahiṃsā** — non-violence,
  2. **satya** — truthfulness,
  3. **asteya** — non-stealing,
  4. **brahmacarya** — chastity / disciplined energy,
  5. **aparigraha** — non-possessiveness.
- ⚠️ Philosophically, ethical restraint (yama) reduces the rajasic-tāmasic turbulence that continually destabilizes mind-field (citta).

#### 4.5 Observance (niyama)

- ✅ The **observance (niyamas)** are positive observances:
  1. **śauca** — purity,
  2. **santoṣa** — contentment,
  3. **tapas** — disciplined austerity,
  4. **svādhyāya** — self-study/scriptural recitation,
  5. **Dedication to the Lord (īśvara-praṇidhāna)** — surrender to Lord (Īśvara).
- ⚠️ Observance (niyama) gives inward form to ethical discipline and opens the mind-field (citta) toward reflective and devotional stabilization.

#### 4.6 Posture (āsana)

- ✅ Posture (āsana) in classical Yoga is primarily the establishment of a **steady and comfortable posture**.
- ⚠️ Its significance is not gymnastic display but freedom from bodily distraction.
- ⚠️ A body at war with itself becomes an obstacle to meditative one-pointedness.

#### 4.7 Breath regulation (prāṇāyāma)

- ✅ Breath regulation (prāṇāyāma) regulates breathing and, through it, the psycho-physical economy.
- ⚠️ Breath functions as a hinge between body and mind-field (citta); its refinement steadies attention and weakens agitation.

#### 4.8 Sense withdrawal (pratyāhāra)

- ✅ Sense withdrawal (pratyāhāra) is withdrawal of the senses from their objects.
- ⚠️ It is not mutilation of sensory life but release from compulsive extroversion.
- ⚠️ This is the transition from ethical-embodied preparation to explicitly internal yogic work.

#### 4.9 Concentration (dhāraṇā)

- ✅ Concentration (dhāraṇā) is concentration: binding mind-field (citta) to a chosen locus.
- ⚠️ It is the initial stabilization of attention and the first clear sign of **ekāgratā**.

#### 4.10 Meditation (dhyāna)

- ✅ Meditation (dhyāna) is uninterrupted flow of cognition toward that same object.
- ⚠️ Unlike concentration (dhāraṇā)'s effortful fixing, meditation (dhyāna) is sustained continuity.
- ✅ It is also a direct means for thinning and burning subtle afflictions (kleśas).

#### 4.11 Meditative absorption (samādhi)

- ✅ Meditative absorption (samādhi) is the culmination of meditative integration wherein the object alone shines with minimal interference from discursiveness.
- ⚠️ Because meditative absorption (samādhi) is itself a syllabus-term, it receives separate detailed treatment in §5 below.

#### 4.12 Integrated concentration (saṃyama)

- ✅ **Integrated concentration (saṃyama)** is the integrated operation of **concentration (dhāraṇā) + meditation (dhyāna) + meditative absorption (samādhi)** on one object.
- ✅ It represents the inner technical core of Yoga's contemplative method.
- ⚠️ Philosophically, integrated concentration (saṃyama) is the progressive refinement of intentionality until cognition becomes translucent to the structure of its object.

#### 4.13 Eight-limbed discipline (aṣṭāṅga-yoga) as psychology of liberation

- ⚠️ The eight limbs are best read as a graded transformation:
  - ethics cures gross disturbance,
  - posture and breath calm embodiment,
  - withdrawal cuts sensory dispersion,
  - concentration and meditation unify the mental stream,
  - meditative absorption (samādhi) prepares discriminative liberation.
- ⚠️ Thus eight-limbed discipline (aṣṭāṅga-yoga) operationalizes the abstract definition of Yoga as restraint of mental modifications (citta-vṛtti-nirodha).

### SESSION 4 — Meditative absorption (samādhi): Object-Supported Insight, Seedless Restraint and Classification Control

#### Plain-language visual - increasingly transparent attention

```text
ordinary mind -> one-pointed attention -> object-supported absorption
                                      -> beyond objectification
                                      -> even residual seed ceases

SAMPRAJNATA: cognitive support remains
ASAMPRAJNATA: vrttis cease, samskara may remain
NIRBIJA: even residual seed is restrained
```

**In simple words:** Meditative absorption (samādhi) is not sleep. It is the disciplined refinement of attention until mind-field (citta) becomes transparent and finally ceases producing binding object-forms.

```text
YS 1.17 factors: vitarka, vicara, ananda, ego-identification (asmitā)
YS 1.42-1.44 samapattis: savitarka/nirvitarka,
                         savicara/nirvicara
These are related grids, not the same list.
```

> **Memory line:** preserve the classification before explaining the ascent.

#### 5. MEDITATIVE ABSORPTION (samādhi)

#### 5.1 Statement

- ✅ **Meditative absorption (samādhi)** (samadhi) is meditative absorption in which mind-field (citta) becomes radically unified and transparent.
- ✅ It is not mere passivity; it is the highest refinement of attention and the threshold of liberating discernment.
- ✅ Classical Yoga distinguishes two main forms: **samprajñāta** and **asamprajñāta** meditative absorption (samādhi).

#### 5.2 Object-supported absorption (samprajñāta-samādhi) — with object / with seed

- ✅ **Object-supported absorption (samprajñāta-samādhi)** is cognitive or object-oriented absorption.
- ✅ A support remains; there is still a subtle seed of cognition.
- ✅ It is called **sabīja** (YS 1.46) because an object-support ("seed") is still present.
- ⚠️ **Two senses of "seed" must be kept apart:** in YS 1.46 *sabīja* means "with object-support", whereas the *bīja* denied in **nirbīja** at YS 1.51 is the residual **saṃskāra**. Losing the object-support therefore does not by itself make a meditative absorption (samādhi) *nirbīja*.
- ⚠️ The mind is profoundly stilled, but not utterly free of object-reference.

#### 5.3 Two canonical schemes that must not be conflated

- ✅ Pātañjala Yoga uses two related but distinct classifications in the Samādhi-pāda.
- ✅ **Yogasūtra 1.17** defines **samprajñāta** meditative absorption (samādhi) through four accompanying factors: **vitarka, vicāra, ānanda, ego-identification (asmitā)** — *vitarka-vicārānandāsmitā-rūpānugamāt saṃprajñātaḥ*.
- ✅ **Yogasūtra 1.42–1.44** classifies **samāpatti** (samapatti; meditative coalescence with an object) as **savitarkā/nirvitarkā** and **savicārā/nirvicārā**.
- ⚠️ The first scheme answers: "What characterises object-supported absorption (samprajñāta-samādhi)?" The second answers: "How does object-absorption differ according to conceptual-verbal overlay and object-level?"

#### 5.4 YS 1.17: the fourfold character of object-supported absorption (samprajñāta-samādhi)

| Factor in samprajñāta | Object-level / cognitive tone | Safe explanation |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ **Vitarka** | ✅ gross object-level engagement | ✅ absorption still accompanied by gross deliberative grasp. |
| ✅ **Vicāra** | ✅ subtle object-level reflection | ✅ attention moves from gross entities toward subtle principles. |
| ✅ **Ānanda** | ✅ refined sattvic joy/bliss-aspect | ⚠️ not ordinary pleasure; it is a purified meditative affect attached to sattva. |
| ✅ **Ego-identification (asmitā)** | ✅ bare "I-am-ness" | ⚠️ not the ordinary kleśa-asmitā in its binding form, but the extremely subtle objectification of the I-sense. |

- ✅ Therefore **sānanda** and **sāsmita** are not a contested addition to Patañjali's list; **ānanda** and **ego-identification (asmitā)** are explicitly present in the sūtra's own fourfold characterisation.
- ⚠️ What becomes interpretively delicate is not whether ānanda and ego-identification (asmitā) belong to samprajñāta, but how later commentators map them onto the samāpatti sequence.

#### 5.5 YS 1.42–1.46: samāpatti-bhedas, not the YS 1.17 fourfold list

| Samāpatti-bheda | Object-range | Conceptual-verbal overlay | Doctrinal point |
|---|---|---|---|
| ✅ **Savitarkā samāpatti** | ✅ gross (**sthūla**) object | ✅ mixed with **śabda-artha-jñāna-vikalpa** (word-object-knowledge construction) | ✅ absorption still carries name, meaning and concept together. |
| ✅ **Nirvitarkā samāpatti** | ✅ gross object | ✅ memory and verbal construction are purified | ✅ the object shines more immediately without gross conceptual overlay. |
| ✅ **Savicārā samāpatti** | ✅ subtle (**sūkṣma**) object | ✅ subtle reflective consideration remains | ✅ absorption turns from gross objects to subtle principles. |
| ✅ **Nirvicārā samāpatti** | ✅ subtle object | ✅ even subtle reflective overlay is stilled | ✅ refined subtle-object absorption becomes luminous and clear. |

- ✅ Under YS 1.44, the **savicārā/nirvicārā** pair is stated with reference to subtle objects.
- ✅ YS 1.45 extends the subtle domain up to **aliṅga** (alinga; the undifferentiated prakṛtic condition).
- ✅ YS 1.46 states that these samāpattis remain **sabīja** (sabija; with seed) meditative absorption (samādhi).
- ✅ YS 1.47 says the clarity (**vaiśāradya**, vaisaradya; lucidity) of **nirvicārā** leads to **adhyātma-prasāda** (adhyatma-prasada; inner clarity of the self-field).
- ✅ YS 1.48 says that therein the prajñā is **ṛtambharā** (rtambhara; truth-bearing).
- ✅ **Example.** The crystal (**sphaṭika**) taking the colour of a nearby flower captures samāpatti: mind-field (citta) becomes so object-coloured that the object seems to shine through it.

#### 5.6 Reconciliation: how commentators relate the two schemes

**Statement.** ✅ The safe reconciliation is: **vitarka-vicāra-ānanda-asmitā** are the canonical four factors of samprajñāta, while **savitarkā/nirvitarkā/savicārā/nirvicārā** are samāpatti varieties within object-supported absorption.

**Argument.**
1. ✅ The sa/nir- distinction concerns whether conceptual-verbal overlay (**vikalpa**, especially **śabda-artha-jñāna-vikalpa**) is present or absent in absorption.
2. ✅ The vitarka/vicāra/ānanda/ego-identification (asmitā) series concerns the level or refinement of the object/support on which samprajñāta rests: gross → subtle → sattva-bliss aspect → bare I-am-ness.
3. ⚠️ Vyāsa's **Yogabhāṣya** and later Vācaspati Miśra and Vijñānabhikṣu attempt to map these two analytic grids onto one graded meditative path.
4. ❓ Commentarial differences remain over whether **ānanda** and **ego-identification (asmitā)** should be counted as independent samāpattis, as supervening aspects of the subtle stage, or as higher refinements beyond the four named sa/nir samāpattis.

**Presupposition.** ✅ The reconciliation presupposes that Patañjali can classify the same meditative ascent by different criteria: object-level, cognitive factor, and degree of conceptual purification.

**Example.** ⚠️ A student may compare it to classifying the same university course by year-level and by assessment-mode: the grids overlap, but they are not the same list.

**Objection.** ⚠️ If both schemes operate within sabīja absorption, why not simply call savitarkā, nirvitarkā, savicārā and nirvicārā the four samprajñāta stages?

**Reply.** ✅ Because YS 1.17 itself names the four factors of samprajñāta as vitarka, vicāra, ānanda and ego-identification (asmitā); the sa/nir list belongs to the later samāpatti analysis of gross and subtle object-absorption.

**Safe examination formula.** ⚠️ Write: "Patañjali characterises samprajñāta by vitarka, vicāra, ānanda and ego-identification (asmitā). He then analyses samāpatti as savitarkā/nirvitarkā for gross objects and savicārā/nirvicārā for subtle objects. Commentators map these schemes, but the lists should not be conflated."

#### 5.7 Trap box: samprajñāta vs samāpatti lists

```text
DO NOT WRITE:
  "The four types of object-supported absorption (samprajñāta-samādhi) listed by Patañjali are
   savitarka, nirvitarka, savicāra and nirvicāra."

WRITE INSTEAD:
  YS 1.17 fourfold samprajñāta = vitarka + vicāra + ānanda + ego-identification (asmitā).
  YS 1.42–1.44 samāpatti-bhedas = savitarkā/nirvitarkā and savicārā/nirvicārā.
```

#### 5.8 Ṛtambharā prajñā — truth-bearing insight

**Statement.** ✅ **Ṛtambharā prajñā** (rtambhara prajna; truth-bearing insight) is the special lucidity arising with **nirvicāra-vaiśāradya** (nirvicara-vaisaradya; clarity of subtle absorption), where mind-field (citta) grasps the object with exceptional purity.

**Argument.**
1. ✅ It is linked to **adhyātma-prasāda**, the inward clarity that follows the perfection of nirvicārā samāpatti.
2. ✅ It differs from **śruta** (sruta; scriptural/testimonial knowledge) and **inference (anumāna)** (anumana; inference), because those normally reach universals or general relations, while ṛtambharā has a **viśeṣa-artha** (visesa-artha; particular object) as its field.
3. ✅ Thus yogic insight reaches the individual/particular in a way ordinary testimony and inference do not.
4. ✅ Its saṃskāra obstructs other saṃskāras: the impression born of truth-bearing insight counteracts ordinary afflicted and dispersive impressions.
5. ✅ When even the saṃskāra of ṛtambharā ceases, seedless absorption (**nirbīja samādhi**, YS 1.51) becomes possible.
6. ✅ Yoga accepts three means of valid knowledge (pramāṇas) — **perception (pratyakṣa)**, **inference (anumāna)** and **āgama/verbal testimony (śabda)** — so ṛtambharā is not a fourth means of valid knowledge (pramāṇa).
7. ⚠️ It is best classified as perfected **yogi-pratyakṣa** (yogi-pratyaksa; extraordinary yogic perception), an extraordinary refinement of perception rather than a new independent means of knowledge.

**Presupposition.** ✅ Yoga presupposes that mind-field (citta) can become so sattva-pure that perception is no longer distorted by ordinary conceptual, affective and karmic deposits.

**Example.** ⚠️ Ordinary inference knows "fire is present because smoke is present" as a general relation; ṛtambharā is like direct yogic disclosure of the concrete object in its particularity, without dependence on discursive mediation.

**Objection.** ⚠️ If ṛtambharā is still a mental modification (citta-vṛtti), how can it help liberation rather than bind?

**Reply.** ✅ Yoga replies that akliṣṭa vṛttis can cancel kliṣṭa vṛttis; the truth-bearing impression blocks lower impressions and then itself ceases. ⚠️ It is a ladder that must finally be left behind.

**Caution.** ⚠️ Do not simply equate ṛtambharā with Aurobindo's "knowledge by identity" or "supermind". The resemblance is thematic only: in Yoga, ṛtambharā is a purified mental modification (citta-vṛtti) that must finally cease; in Aurobindo, supramental knowledge by identity is reinterpreted as a permanent, world-affirming gnosis.

#### 5.9 Objectless absorption (asamprajñāta-samādhi) — beyond cognitive objectification

- ✅ **Objectless absorption (asamprajñāta-samādhi)** is non-cognitive or supra-cognitive absorption in which the vṛttis cease and no object stands forth as support in the ordinary sense.
- ✅ **YS 1.18** defines it as *virāma-pratyaya-abhyāsa-pūrvaḥ saṃskāra-śeṣo 'nyaḥ* — "the other [meditative absorption (samādhi)] is preceded by the practice of the idea of cessation, and **has saṃskāra as its residue**."
- ✅ So on the sūtra's own wording asamprajñāta is characterised by **saṃskāra-śeṣa**: latent impressions may still remain even though cognitive absorption has stopped.
- ⚠️ It is crucial not to confuse this with sleep or blank unconsciousness; it is the fruition of radical meditative restraint.

#### 5.9A Seedless absorption (nirbīja-samādhi) — when even the residual seed ceases

- ✅ **YS 1.51** states *tasyāpi nirodhe sarva-nirodhān nirbījaḥ samādhiḥ* — "on the restraint of **that too**, everything being restrained, meditative absorption (samādhi) is **seedless**."
- ✅ The "that too" is the residual impression left by the preceding stage (the saṃskāra born of ṛtambharā prajñā, YS 1.50); when even it is restrained, absorption becomes **nirbīja**.
- ⚠️ **Do not equate the two terms.** Asamprajñāta (YS 1.18) is defined by *residual saṃskāra*; nirbīja (YS 1.51) is defined by *the cessation of even that residue*. Nirbīja is best treated as the culminating condition reached within the asamprajñāta phase, not as a synonym for it.
- ⚠️ ✅ Safe answer formula: *asamprajñāta = vṛttis stopped, saṃskāra-śeṣa remains; nirbīja = even that residual saṃskāra restrained.*

#### 5.10 Relation between saṃskāra and nirodha

- ✅ Even when active vṛttis cease, latent impressions can remain as residual potency — this is exactly the *saṃskāra-śeṣa* of YS 1.18.
- ✅ Hence the path must proceed beyond surface quietness to the restraint of even the subtle seed, which is what YS 1.51 marks as nirbīja.
- ⚠️ This explains why temporary calm or trance is not yet liberation.

#### 5.11 Cloud-of-virtue absorption (dharmamegha-samādhi)

- ✅ **Cloud-of-virtue absorption (dharmamegha-samādhi)** is the highest meditative absorption (samādhi) immediately preceding isolation (kaivalya).
- ✅ The image is that of a **cloud of dharma** raining down the fullness of discriminative knowledge.
- ✅ At this level, even fascination with extraordinary knowledge or powers is relinquished.
- ⚠️ It marks the consummation of non-attachment: the yogin no longer seeks omniscience, attainment or display.

#### 5.12 Meditative absorption (samādhi) and siddhis

- ✅ Higher concentrations may yield extraordinary capacities or **siddhis**.
- ⚠️ But Yoga warns that these are obstacles if appropriated by egoity or fascination.
- ⚠️ They belong to the side-effects of concentration, not to the essence of isolation (kaivalya).

#### 5.13 Role of Lord (Īśvara) in meditative absorption (samādhi)

- ✅ **Dedication to the Lord (īśvara-praṇidhāna)** is both a **observance (niyama)** and an independent means conducive to meditative absorption (samādhi).
- ✅ Devotion or surrender to Lord (Īśvara) stabilizes mind-field (citta) and helps overcome obstacles.
- ✅ Lord (Īśvara) is also linked to the meditative use of **praṇava (OṂ)**.
- ⚠️ In Yoga, theism is practical: Lord (Īśvara) functions as an unsurpassed focus, model and aid to concentration.

#### 5.14 Obstacles and their removal

- ✅ Classical Yoga lists obstacles (**antarāyas**) such as disease, mental dullness, doubt, carelessness, laziness, sensuality, false perception, failure to attain stages and instability in attained stages.
- ✅ Dedication to the Lord (īśvara-praṇidhāna) is one of the important means of overcoming these.
- ⚠️ This is why questions on meditative absorption (samādhi) often open into the role of God: Yoga's God is not a creator-first doctrine but a **practice-enabling doctrine**.

#### 5.15 Meditative absorption (samādhi) in the 2022 PYQ

- ✅ The 2022 question required the **nature and stages of meditative absorption (samādhi)** along with the **role of Lord (Īśvara)**.
- ⚠️ Therefore a complete answer must cover the distinction between samprajñāta and asamprajñāta (including YS 1.18's *saṃskāra-śeṣa* and its further culmination in YS 1.51's nirbīja), the YS 1.17 fourfold characterisation, the samāpatti-bhedas, dharmamegha as culmination, and devotional concentration on Lord (Īśvara) as an aid.

#### 5.16 Mini-conclusion on meditative absorption (samādhi)

- ⚠️ A sharp answer line: **meditative absorption (samādhi) is the progressive refinement of concentration from object-supported cognitive absorption (sabīja, YS 1.46) through non-cognitive absorption with residual impressions (asamprajñāta, YS 1.18: saṃskāra-śeṣa) to the restraint of even that residue (nirbīja, YS 1.51), with YS 1.17's samprajñāta factors, the samāpatti refinements, and Dedication to the Lord (īśvara-praṇidhāna) as a powerful aid but not a substitute for discriminative realization.**

### SESSION 5 — Isolation (kaivalya) and Lord (Īśvara): Isolation, Discernment and the Special Conscious witness (puruṣa)

#### Plain-language visual - liberation removes confusion, not consciousness

```text
practice + detachment
        |
afflictions (kleśas) weaken, meditative absorption (samādhi) stabilizes
        |
DISCRIMINATIVE INSIGHT (viveka-khyāti): Conscious witness (puruṣa) is not mind-field
  (citta)/Primordial material nature (prakṛti)
        |
supreme detachment + dharmamegha
        |
ISOLATION (kaivalya): Conscious witness (puruṣa) abides in its own nature

LORD (Īśvara) helps the path but does not replace discernment.
```

**In simple words:** Isolation (kaivalya) is isolation of consciousness from material nature, not union with God or Brahman. Lord (Īśvara) is a perfect, eternally free Conscious witness (puruṣa) who supports concentration, surrender and teaching.

| Lord (Īśvara) is | Lord (Īśvara) is not |
|---|---|
| Purusa-visesa | Material cause |
| Untouched by affliction (kleśa) and action and moral consequence (karma) | Ordinary karmic self |
| Timeless teacher | Nyaya-style creator by default |
| Privileged meditative support | External giver of isolation (kaivalya) |

> **Memory line:** God accelerates the path; viveka completes it.

#### 6. ISOLATION (kaivalya)

#### 6.1 Exact meaning

- ✅ **Isolation (kaivalya)** means aloneness, isolation or absolute distinctness.
- ✅ In Yoga it is the isolation of **conscious witness (puruṣa)** from **primordial material nature (prakṛti)** and its evolutes.
- ✅ It is therefore **not** union with God, **not** merger into Brahman, and **not** mere relief from pain considered negatively.
- ⚠️ The precise meaning must always be guarded because UPSC frequently tests it through contrasts.

#### 6.2 Why isolation (kaivalya) is needed if conscious witness (puruṣa) is already pure

- ✅ Conscious witness (puruṣa) is indeed eternally pure, inactive and free.
- ✅ Yet through ignorance, mind-field (citta)'s modifications are appropriated as self.
- ✅ Isolation (kaivalya) is the cessation of this false relation at the level of experience and bondage.
- ⚠️ So liberation is not a production of a new essence; it is the ending of misidentification.

#### 6.3 The process leading to isolation (kaivalya)

```text
Sustained practice (abhyāsa) + Dispassion (vairāgya)
        ↓
Preparatory discipline (kriyā-yoga) and Eight-limbed discipline (aṣṭāṅga-yoga)
        ↓
Attenuation and destruction of afflictions (kleśas)
        ↓
Stable meditative absorption (samādhi)
        ↓
Discriminative insight (viveka-khyāti) (discriminative discernment)
        ↓
Para-vairāgya (supreme detachment)
        ↓
Cloud-of-virtue absorption (dharmamegha-samādhi)
        ↓
Isolation (kaivalya)
```

#### 6.4 Discriminative insight (viveka-khyāti)

- ✅ **Discriminative insight (viveka-khyāti)** is discriminative knowledge discerning the absolute difference between conscious witness (puruṣa) and primordial material nature (prakṛti).
- ✅ It is the decisive knowledge that ends confusion.
- ⚠️ Yoga thus remains deeply cognitivist at the summit: meditative absorption (samādhi) is indispensable, but its soteriological function is to mature discriminative discernment.

#### 6.5 Para-vairāgya

- ✅ **Para-vairāgya** is supreme detachment even from the highest subtle attainments.
- ✅ The yogin ceases to crave powers, experiences, pleasures, even exalted cognitive states.
- ⚠️ Without this, subtle egoity can survive advanced practice.

#### 6.6 Dharmamegha and final transition

- ✅ In **cloud-of-virtue absorption (dharmamegha-samādhi)**, the cloud of dharma pours down the fullness of liberating discernment.
- ✅ The final barriers to isolation of conscious witness (puruṣa) are removed.
- ⚠️ This is not a divine grace replacing effort; it is the ripened summit of perfected discipline and discernment.

#### 6.7 Reverse evolution (pratiprasava) of the qualities (guṇas)

- ✅ With liberation, the **qualities (guṇas)** cease to function for the liberated conscious witness (puruṣa) and resolve back toward their source.
- ✅ This return or involution is described as **reverse evolution (pratiprasava)** — reabsorption back into primordial material nature (prakṛti).
- ✅ Mind-field (citta), as a product of primordial material nature (prakṛti), no longer binds; its function is exhausted.
- ⚠️ The metaphor is one of reversal of the evolutionary process for soteriological purposes.

#### 6.8 Isolation (kaivalya) transition: farmer, burnt seed and potter's wheel

- ✅ The Kaivalya-pāda uses the **kṣetrika** (ksetrika; farmer) image: the farmer does not push water into the field by force; he breaches the embankment, and water flows by its own nature.
- ⚠️ Exam use: yogic discipline removes obstructions; it does not manufacture conscious witness (puruṣa)'s freedom, because conscious witness (puruṣa) is already free and primordial material nature (prakṛti)'s processes complete themselves when obstacles are removed.
- ✅ The burnt seed (**dagdha-bīja**) image explains why destroyed afflictions (kleśas) cannot germinate into future bondage.
- ⚠️ The potter's wheel image explains residual embodiment: even after liberating knowledge removes the push of fresh action and moral consequence (karma), the already-moving psycho-physical complex may continue briefly until its momentum is exhausted.
- ⚠️ This allows a careful account of **jīvanmukti** (jivanmukti; liberation while living) language in Yoga: the conscious witness (puruṣa) is no longer bound, though bodily-citta processes may continue until their residual cause is spent.

#### 6.9 Establishment in one's own nature (svarūpa-pratiṣṭhā)

- ✅ Isolation (kaivalya) may also be expressed as the abiding of consciousness in its own nature — **establishment in one's own nature (svarūpa-pratiṣṭhā)**.
- ⚠️ This continues the logic of the foundational sūtra: when vṛttis cease, the seer stands in itself.
#### 6.10 How Yoga isolation (kaivalya) differs from Advaita liberation (mokṣa)

| Yoga | Advaita Vedānta |
|---|---|
| many conscious witness (puruṣas) | one Brahman as ultimate reality |
| liberation = isolation of conscious witness (puruṣa) from primordial material nature (prakṛti) | liberation = realization of identity of enduring self (ātman) and Brahman |
| no Brahman-doctrine | Brahman central |
| no merger or non-dual identity | liberation (mokṣa) is non-dual realization |

- ⚠️ Therefore isolation (kaivalya) is not identity but **disentanglement**.
- ⚠️ Cross-reference: [Vedānta](./Vedanta.md).

#### 6.11 How Yoga isolation (kaivalya) differs from Nyāya release (apavarga)

| Yoga isolation (kaivalya) | Nyāya release (apavarga) |
|---|---|
| positive isolation of pure consciousness in its own nature | often described as cessation of pain and bondage |
| rooted in puruṣa-prakṛti discrimination | rooted in true knowledge of categories and self |
| mind-field (citta) resolves, conscious witness (puruṣa) shines | self remains but liberation is characterized more negatively |

- ⚠️ UPSC-safe line: **Nyāya emphasizes cessation of suffering; Yoga emphasizes the isolation and self-abidance of conscious witness (puruṣa).**
- ⚠️ Cross-reference: [Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika](./Nyaya-Vaisesika.md).

#### 6.12 Lord (Īśvara) and isolation (kaivalya)

- ✅ Lord (Īśvara) assists the aspirant but does **not grant** isolation (kaivalya) as an external gift.
- ✅ Liberation finally depends on the yogin's own **discriminative insight (viveka-khyāti)**.
- ✅ Lord (Īśvara) is the perfect model, object of devotion and remover of obstacles.
- ⚠️ This is central to the 2025 demand on the nature of God and his role in isolation (kaivalya).

#### 6.13 What isolation (kaivalya) is not

- ⚠️ Not **union with God** in the bhakti-school sense.
- ⚠️ Not **identity with Brahman** in the Advaitic sense.
- ⚠️ Not **annihilation of consciousness**.
- ⚠️ Not **mere painlessness** abstracted from the self's own luminosity.
- ⚠️ Not **destruction of mind as substance**; rather, cessation of its binding modifications and reabsorption of its function into primordial material nature (prakṛti).

#### 6.14 Mini-conclusion on isolation (kaivalya)

- ⚠️ Best exam sentence: **isolation (kaivalya) is the isolation of conscious witness (puruṣa) from primordial material nature (prakṛti), reached through discriminative discernment matured in meditative absorption (samādhi), with qualities (guṇas) returning to quiescence and consciousness abiding in its own form.**

#### 7. LORD (Īśvara) IN YOGA

#### 7.1 Why a separate section is necessary

- ✅ Though the syllabus clause names mind-field (citta) through isolation (kaivalya), UPSC repeatedly asks the role of **God** in Yoga.
- ✅ Yoga's distinctiveness from Sāṃkhya cannot be fully explained without Lord (Īśvara).
- ⚠️ Hence any mature note on Yoga must include a separate theistic section.

#### 7.2 Definition: puruṣa-viśeṣa

- ✅ Yoga defines **Lord (Īśvara)** as a **puruṣa-viśeṣa** — a special conscious witness (puruṣa).
- ✅ He is untouched by **affliction (kleśa)**, **action and moral consequence (karma)**, **vipāka** and **āśaya**.
- ✅ This is the standard doctrinal core of the Yoga-sūtra account.

#### 7.3 Omniscience and timeless teacherhood

- ✅ In Lord (Īśvara) lies the unsurpassed seed of omniscience.
- ✅ He is the teacher of the ancients and is not limited by time.
- ✅ He is denoted by **praṇava (OṂ)**.
- ⚠️ These predicates make Lord (Īśvara) the ideal meditative and pedagogic principle in Yoga.

#### 7.4 Lord (Īśvara) is not creator in the Nyāya sense

- ✅ Yoga does **not** primarily present Lord (Īśvara) as creator-God.
- ✅ **Primordial material nature (prakṛti)** remains the material basis of worldly evolution.
- ✅ Evolution proceeds through the puruṣa-prakṛti framework, not through divine creation ex nihilo.
- ⚠️ Therefore Yoga's God differs sharply from the Nyāya God as **nimitta-kāraṇa** or efficient cause of the world-order.

#### 7.5 Lord (Īśvara) is not a Vaiṣṇava-style personal saviour

- ✅ Though devotion to Lord (Īśvara) is accepted, Yoga does not make him a saviour who arbitrarily grants liberation.
- ✅ The structure of liberation remains impersonal in one crucial sense: it depends on the yogin's own discriminative realization.
- ⚠️ This must be clearly said to avoid importing later devotional theologies into classical Yoga.

#### 7.6 Positive role of Lord (Īśvara)

| Role of Lord (Īśvara) in Yoga | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Object of meditation** | one-pointed devotion stabilizes mind-field (citta) |
| **Aid through Dedication to the Lord (īśvara-praṇidhāna)** | surrender reduces egoity and attachment |
| **Remover of obstacles** | helps counter antarāyas in practice |
| **Perfect model** | exemplifies untouched, unsurpassed spiritual status |
| **Teacher principle** | anchor of timeless transmission and authority |

#### 7.7 Why Yoga is called theistic Samkhya (seśvara-sāṃkhya)

- ✅ Because it preserves the Sāṃkhyan dualistic ontology while adding Lord (Īśvara).
- ✅ Hence the expression **theistic Samkhya (seśvara-sāṃkhya)** — Sāṃkhya with God.
- ⚠️ This is not a small decorative addition; it changes the psychological and devotional resources of the path while leaving the basic metaphysical dualism intact.

#### 7.8 Lord (Īśvara) and praṇava

- ✅ Meditation on **OṂ** as the designation of Lord (Īśvara) is a canonical Yogic practice.
- ⚠️ This links theology, language, sound and contemplative concentration in a single discipline.

#### 7.9 Lord (Īśvara), meditative absorption (samādhi) and isolation (kaivalya) together

- ✅ Lord (Īśvara) aids meditative absorption (samādhi) by offering a privileged support for concentration.
- ✅ Through obstacle-removal and ego-surrender, the aspirant's path to discriminative knowledge is accelerated.
- ✅ But isolation (kaivalya) still follows from **discriminative insight (viveka-khyāti)**, not divine fiat.
- ⚠️ Thus the most accurate exam formula is: **Lord (Īśvara) is instrumental, not constitutive, in liberation.**

#### 7.10 The 2025 demand

- ✅ The 2025 question asked for the **nature of God and his role in isolation (kaivalya)**.
- ⚠️ A strong answer should therefore establish four points:
  1. Lord (Īśvara) is a **special conscious witness (puruṣa)**.
  2. He is untouched by kleśa-karma-vipāka-āśaya.
  3. He aids concentration, removes obstacles and is the object of praṇava-mediated devotion.
  4. He does not create the world in the Nyāya sense and does not bestow isolation (kaivalya) independently of discriminative insight (viveka-khyāti).

#### 7.11 Mini-conclusion on Lord (Īśvara)

- ⚠️ In Yoga, God is best understood as **the supreme contemplative support within a Sāṃkhyan metaphysical frame**.

#### Plain-language visual - four major comparison axes

**In simple words:** Yoga must be located through exact contrasts. It is not merely Samkhya plus a decorative God, not Advaitic union, not Buddhist no-self and not Nyaya creator-theism.

> **Memory line:** compare self, God, causation, mind and liberation one axis at a time.

| Issue | Yoga | Advaita Vedānta |
|---|---|---|
| Ultimate reality | many conscious witness (puruṣas) + primordial material nature (prakṛti) | non-dual Brahman |
| Bondage | confusion of conscious witness (puruṣa) with mind-field (citta)/primordial material nature (prakṛti) | ignorance (avidyā)/adhyāsa causing Brahman-self ignorance |
| Liberation | isolation | identity-realization |
| God | meditative aid, special conscious witness (puruṣa) | saguṇa Brahman at vyāvahārika level |

|---|---|---|

#### 8.5 Kaivalya-pāda realism against Vijñānavāda/Yogācāra

**Statement.** ✅ The Kaivalya-pāda contains a genuine realist polemic: Yoga is not a mind-only idealism but a Sāṃkhya-realist system in which objects, mind-field (citta) and conscious witness (puruṣa) are distinct.

**Opponent's prima-facie position (pūrvapakṣa).** ⚠️ **Vijñānavāda/Yogācāra** argues that what is taken as an external object can be explained as cognition-only; the object need not be admitted as independently real.

**Established conclusion (siddhānta).** ✅ Yoga's reply is Sāṃkhya-realism: objects are not reducible to a single cognition, mind-field (citta) is itself knowable, and conscious witness (puruṣa) is required as the witness of the mind.

**Argument.**
1. ✅ **Vastu-sāmya / ekacitta-tantra argument.** An object is not dependent on one mind; if it were **ekacitta-tantra** (ekacitta-tantra; dependent on a single mind), it would vanish when that mind ceased to cognise it. ⚠️ The persistence and common availability of objects support object-realism.
2. ✅ **Mind's knownness argument.** Mind-field (citta) is not self-illuminating (**na tat svābhāsam**, na tat svabhasam), because it is itself **dṛśya** (drsya; an object seen) for conscious witness (puruṣa). ⚠️ A purely mind-only theory struggles to terminate the regress of cognitions cognising cognitions.
3. ✅ **Against simultaneous self-cognition.** The mind cannot fully apprehend itself and its object in the same act as though it were both lamp and illuminated object. ⚠️ Yoga therefore distinguishes the object, the mental modification and the witnessing conscious witness (puruṣa).
4. ✅ **Uparāga argument.** An object is known when mind-field (citta) is **uparakta** (uparakta; coloured/affected) by it; whether the object is known or unknown depends on mind-field (citta)'s colouring, which implies that the object is not identical with the cognition itself.

**Presupposition.** ✅ The argument presupposes Yoga's three-term realism: object (**viṣaya**), mental modification (**citta-vṛtti**) and witness (**puruṣa**) are analytically distinct.

**Example.** ✅ The sphaṭika crystal near a flower again helps: the crystal becomes coloured by the flower, but the flower is not created by the crystal's colouration; similarly, mind-field (citta) is coloured by an object that stands outside the cognition.

**Objection.** ⚠️ The Yogācāra can reply that shared karmic traces explain intersubjective object-appearance without positing external objects.

**Reply.** ⚠️ Yoga reconstructs the answer by insisting that shared appearance itself is better explained by a common object plus multiple cittas than by private cognitions alone; the residual force is that Yoga must still explain precisely how external objects colour an insentient but subtle mind-field (citta).

**Exam payoff.** ⚠️ This material shows that Yoga is not only a praxis manual; it defends a metaphysically committed realism against Buddhist idealism.

#### 8.6 Yoga and philosophy of religion

- ⚠️ Yoga's Lord (Īśvara) is useful for cross-paper deployment with [Notions of God](../../paper-2/philosophy-of-religion/Notions-of-God.md) and [Religious Experience](../../paper-2/philosophy-of-religion/Religious-Experience.md).

#### 9. CRITICISMS AND REPLIES

#### 9.1 Criticism: If conscious witness (puruṣa) is inactive and pure, how can it ever be bound?

- ⚠️ The objection is that an inactive witness cannot genuinely enter bondage.
- ✅ Yoga's reply parallels Sāṃkhya: bondage belongs not to conscious witness (puruṣa) intrinsically but to misidentification through reflection in mind-field (citta).
- ⚠️ Thus bondage is phenomenological and epistemic, not an actual alteration of conscious witness (puruṣa).

#### 9.2 Criticism: If mind-field (citta) is insentient, how can cognition occur at all?

- ⚠️ The challenge targets the possibility of experience in an unconscious psychical instrument.
- ✅ Yoga replies that mind-field (citta) becomes luminous by reflecting conscious witness (puruṣa), just as a crystal appears lit by what is near it.
- ⚠️ The school thus explains cognition through a relation of reflected consciousness rather than by attributing intrinsic consciousness to matter.

#### 9.3 Criticism: Why count valid cognition itself as a vṛtti if it helps liberation?

- ⚠️ If means of valid knowledge (pramāṇa) is true, why restrain it?
- ✅ Yoga replies that even true cognition is still a modification.
- ✅ It is useful in its **akliṣṭa** form as a ladder toward discriminative insight (viveka-khyāti).
- ⚠️ But what serves as means at one stage must also finally be transcended.

#### 9.4 Criticism: Is objectless absorption (asamprajñāta-samādhi) just unconsciousness or sleep?

- ✅ No.
- ✅ Sleep is itself one vṛtti, while objectless absorption (asamprajñāta-samādhi) is cessation of vṛttis through discipline.
- ⚠️ The difference lies in both genesis and value: sleep is natural obscuration; meditative absorption (samādhi) is luminous restraint.

#### 9.5 Criticism: Why add Lord (Īśvara) if Sāṃkhya ontology already explains liberation?

- ⚠️ This is the classic challenge from nirīśvara Sāṃkhya.
- ✅ Yoga's answer is pragmatic and soteriological: Lord (Īśvara) offers a unique focus of devotion, removes obstacles and supports meditative absorption (samādhi).
- ⚠️ The addition is justified by practical efficacy rather than by an overhaul of metaphysical fundamentals.

#### 9.6 Criticism: If liberation depends on discriminative insight (viveka-khyāti), does God become redundant?

- ✅ Not redundant, but non-final.
- ✅ Lord (Īśvara) is a privileged aid, not the substitute for discernment.
- ⚠️ This balanced answer is exactly what UPSC expects.

#### 9.7 Criticism: Is Yoga's liberation merely negative isolation?

- ⚠️ Some critics see isolation (kaivalya) as a stark separation lacking fullness.
- ✅ Yoga replies that the positive side of liberation is **establishment in one's own nature (svarūpa-pratiṣṭhā)** — abiding in pure consciousness free from all alien admixture.
- ⚠️ Its positivity is not ecstatic union but unconfused self-abidance.

#### 9.8 Criticism: Is meditation (dhyāna) sufficient without ethics?

- ✅ Yoga's answer is no.
- ✅ Without ethical restraint (yama) and observance (niyama), meditative power can coexist with affliction and egoity.
- ⚠️ This is why the system places ethics at the foundation of contemplative success.

#### 10. COMMON UPSC TRAPS

1. ⚠️ **Isolation (kaivalya) ≠ Advaita liberation (mokṣa) ≠ Nyāya release (apavarga).** Yoga's isolation (kaivalya) is isolation of conscious witness (puruṣa), not identity with Brahman and not merely cessation of pain.
2. ⚠️ **Yoga's Lord (Īśvara) is not a creator-God in the Nyāya sense.** Do not import nimitta-kāraṇa language uncritically.
3. ⚠️ **Mind-field (citta) is not identical with sensory mind (manas) alone.** It includes determinative intellect (buddhi), ego-maker (ahaṃkāra) and sensory mind (manas) functioning together.
4. ⚠️ **Samprajñāta and asamprajñāta must not be reversed, and asamprajñāta must not be flatly equated with nirbīja.** Samprajñāta still has object-support (*sabīja*, YS 1.46); asamprajñāta (YS 1.18) is defined as *saṃskāra-śeṣa*, i.e. residual impressions may still remain; nirbīja (YS 1.51) is the further condition in which even that residual saṃskāra is restrained.
5. ⚠️ **Do not conflate YS 1.17 with YS 1.42–1.44.** Samprajñāta's four factors are vitarka, vicāra, ānanda and ego-identification (asmitā); savitarkā/nirvitarkā/savicārā/nirvicārā are samāpatti-bhedas.
6. ⚠️ **Kliṣṭa/akliṣṭa qualifies vṛttis, not afflictions (kleśas).** Afflictions (kleśas) are inherently afflictive.
7. ⚠️ **Restraint of mental modifications (citta-vṛtti-nirodha) does not mean violent destruction of mind.** It means cessation of modifications so the seer abides in itself.
8. ⚠️ **Clinging to life (abhiniveśa) is present even in the wise.** Do not reduce it to mere doctrinal ignorance.
9. ⚠️ **Sleep is a vṛtti, not liberation.** Nidrā is not meditative absorption (samādhi).
10. ⚠️ **Yoga shares Sāṃkhya's constitutive principle (tattva) framework.** Do not write as if Yoga has a wholly separate cosmology.
11. ⚠️ **Lord (Īśvara) helps but does not grant isolation (kaivalya) from outside.** Liberation finally follows discriminative insight (viveka-khyāti).

#### 11A. PRESUPPOSITION LEDGER

| Doctrine | Presupposition it rests on | What collapses if the presupposition is denied |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ **Restraint of mental modifications (citta-vṛtti-nirodha)** | ✅ Mind-field (citta) really takes forms, can be restrained, and is distinct from conscious witness (puruṣa). | ⚠️ Nirodha becomes mere mental quietism, not liberation. |
| ✅ **Affliction (kleśa) theory** | ✅ Ignorance (avidyā) and its derivatives causally colour cognition, action and rebirth. | ⚠️ Yoga loses its diagnosis of bondage and becomes moral advice only. |
| ✅ **Reflection/ego-identification (asmitā)** | ✅ Conscious witness (puruṣa) is inactive consciousness; mind-field (citta) is transparent but insentient. | ⚠️ Bondage of a pure conscious witness (puruṣa) becomes inexplicable. |
| ✅ **Eight-limbed discipline (aṣṭāṅga-yoga)** | ✅ Body, conduct, breath, senses and attention condition mind-field (citta)'s purity. | ⚠️ Meditative absorption (samādhi) becomes disconnected from ethics and discipline. |
| ✅ **Meditative absorption (samādhi)** | ✅ Attention can become progressively object-transparent and finally seedless. | ⚠️ Meditation reduces to trance or concentration without soteriological force. |
| ✅ **Karma-vipāka** | ✅ Actions leave subtle deposits rooted in afflictions (kleśas) and ripen across experience. | ⚠️ Yoga becomes therapeutic psychology rather than a theory of cycle of rebirth (saṃsāra) and release. |
| ✅ **Lord (Īśvara)** | ✅ A special conscious witness (puruṣa) untouched by kleśa-karma-vipāka-āśaya is possible and useful for practice. | ⚠️ Yoga collapses toward nirīśvara Sāṃkhya and loses its distinctive devotional aid. |
| ✅ **Isolation (kaivalya)** | ✅ Conscious witness (puruṣa) is ultimately distinct from primordial material nature (prakṛti), and primordial material nature (prakṛti) functions for conscious witness (puruṣa)'s experience and release. | ⚠️ Isolation becomes unintelligible; Advaitic identity or Buddhist no-self becomes more plausible. |

|---|---|---|---|
| ✅ **Buddhist kṣaṇikavāda** | ⚠️ No persisting mind-field (citta) is needed; a stream of momentary cognitions explains experience. | ✅ Yoga invokes mind-field (citta) as a continuing prakṛtic substrate bearing saṃskāras and undergoing real transformation (pariṇāmavāda). | ⚠️ Momentariness can still explain continuity through causal series without a substance. |
| ✅ **Yogācāra/Vijñānavāda** | ⚠️ External objects are unnecessary; cognition-only explains appearance. | ✅ Kaivalya-pāda realism: objects are not dependent on a single mind, and mind-field (citta) is known by conscious witness (puruṣa). | ⚠️ Shared karmic appearance remains a sophisticated idealist counter. |
| ✅ **Advaita Vedānta** | ⚠️ Why many conscious witness (puruṣas), and why make Lord (Īśvara) only a special conscious witness (puruṣa) rather than the sole reality? | ⚠️ Yoga replies from experience-plurality, Sāṃkhyan realism and the practical need for discriminating each conscious witness (puruṣa) from primordial material nature (prakṛti). | ⚠️ Advaita presses the unity of consciousness more radically. |
| ✅ **Nyāya** | ⚠️ The self is a substance with adventitious consciousness; why call conscious witness (puruṣa) consciousness itself, and why desire cessation of all vṛttis? | ⚠️ Yoga replies that consciousness cannot be an accidental property of the true seer, and nirodha reveals rather than destroys awareness. | ⚠️ Nyāya's commonsense realism about persons and cognition remains attractive. |
| ✅ **Cārvāka** | ⚠️ Conscious witness (puruṣa), action and moral consequence (karma), rebirth and isolation (kaivalya) are unverifiable beyond perception. | ⚠️ Yoga replies through disciplined yogi-pratyakṣa, pragmatic transformation and the inadequacy of material pleasure to end suffering. | ⚠️ Public verification remains difficult. |
| ✅ **Mīmāṃsā** | ⚠️ A non-creator Lord (Īśvara) is otiose; ritual action, not yoga, secures dharma. | ⚠️ Yoga replies that ritual cannot by itself remove ignorance (avidyā)/ego-identification (asmitā), while Dedication to the Lord (īśvara-praṇidhāna) directly stabilizes mind-field (citta). | ⚠️ The traditional Yoga proof of Lord (Īśvara)'s necessity is weaker than Nyāya's creator-God argument. |

|---|---|---|
| ⚠️ **Husserl's epoché/reduction vs nirodha** | ⚠️ Both suspend the natural attitude toward ordinary experience. | ⚠️ Epoché is methodological and retains the constituting ego; nirodha is soteriological and aims at cessation of the mental stream's binding modifications. |

| ⚠️ **Aristotle's habituation vs abhyāsa-vairāgya** | ⚠️ Both transform character through repeated discipline. | ⚠️ Aristotle aims at virtuous flourishing in the polis; Yoga aims at disentangling conscious witness (puruṣa) from primordial material nature (prakṛti). |
| ⚠️ **Freud's unconscious vs saṃskāra/vāsanā** | ⚠️ Both posit latent dispositions shaping conduct. | ⚠️ Saṃskāras are karmically indexed and metaphysically real deposits in a rebirth framework, not merely a psychodynamic hypothesis. |

#### 11F. GRADED VERDICT ON YOGA

**Philosophically strong ✅**
- ✅ Yoga gives a rigorous phenomenology of mental process: vṛtti, smṛti, nidrā, saṃskāra, affliction (kleśa) and meditative absorption (samādhi) form a coherent psychology.
- ✅ It integrates psychology, ethics and metaphysics: yama-niyama, karma-vipāka and citta-nirodha are not separate compartments.
- ✅ It offers an operational path: sustained practice (abhyāsa), dispassion (vairāgya), preparatory discipline (kriyā-yoga), aṣṭāṅga and meditative absorption (samādhi) supply a disciplined method rather than mere speculation.
- ✅ Its Kaivalya-pāda realist arguments show that Yoga is metaphysically committed, not merely a praxis manual.

**Philosophically weak ⚠️**
- ⚠️ It inherits the puruṣa-prakṛti interaction problem: mere proximity explains much, but not all critics will accept it.
- ⚠️ Lord (Īśvara) has limited explanatory role: he aids practice but is not creator, material cause or final giver of isolation (kaivalya).
- ⚠️ The value of contentless pure consciousness can be questioned by schools that define fulfilment through knowledge, relation, bliss or activity.

**Genuinely contested ❓**
- ❓ The precise commentarial status of ānanda/ego-identification (asmitā) as separate samāpattis or higher aspects of samprajñāta remains debated.
- ❓ Whether isolation (kaivalya) should be described as blissful self-abidance or as austere isolation is interpreted differently by expositors.
- ❓ The historical unity and layering of the Yogasūtra tradition remain scholarly questions; do not build a UPSC answer on uncertain dating claims.

**Ready verdicts.**
- ⚠️ **10 marks:** "Yoga is best read as a disciplined psychology of citta-nirodha grounded in Sāṃkhya dualism and completed in isolation (kaivalya)."
- ⚠️ **15 marks:** "Its strength lies in linking mental discipline, moral causation and liberation; its vulnerability lies in the unexplained proximity of inactive conscious witness (puruṣa) and active primordial material nature (prakṛti)."
- ⚠️ **20 marks:** "Yoga is philosophically richer than a praxis manual: it offers a realist metaphysics, a subtle moral psychology and a graded meditative epistemology, though its dualism and limited Lord (Īśvara) leave live objections for Advaita, Nyāya and Buddhist critics."

### SESSION 9 — REVIEW-PROMOTED TEXT, PRACTICE AND THEISM COMPLETENESS

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** Pātañjala Yoga is a four-chapter discipline of mental restraint in which sustained practice, dispassion, ethics, absorption and a limited theism mature discriminative isolation.

**Technical definition:** Source-controlled Yoga distinguishes Patañjali's sūtra claims from Vyāsa's mind-level taxonomy and later theistic proofs while integrating practice/dispassion, supra-cognitive routes, obstacles and powers.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Yoga operationalizes Sāṃkhya dualism through a precise psychology of restraint, but its distinctiveness lies in disciplined practice and a real yet non-creator Lord (Īśvara) rather than in a new material cosmology.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **four chapters (pādas)**
- **practice and dispassion**
- **universal great vow**
- **condition-based and means-based absorption**
- **nine obstacles**
- **combined discipline and powers**
- **special conscious witness**
- **limited theism**

**How to use them:** Establish textual ownership, define restraint, show the graded practice-to-absorption mechanism, and qualify both extraordinary powers and the role of Lord (Īśvara) before concluding with isolation.

#### 0A. PĀTAÑJALA YOGA: TEXTUAL IDENTITY, FOUR CHAPTERS AND OWNERSHIP

- ✅ Patañjali is the traditional systematizer of classical Yoga through the **Yoga Sūtra**, read in the classical tradition with Vyāsa's **Yoga Bhāṣya** and later commentators such as Vācaspati Miśra and Vijñānabhikṣu.
- ⚠️ The historical layering and dating of the text/commentary tradition remain debated. For UPSC, identify the received Pātañjala system without building an answer on uncertain chronology.
- ✅ The Yoga Sūtra has four chapters (**pādas**):

| Chapter | Safe orientation | Owner emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| absorption chapter (**Samādhi-pāda**) | definition of Yoga, mental modifications, practice/dispassion, Lord (Īśvara) and object-supported/seedless absorption | mind and absorption |
| practice chapter (**Sādhana-pāda**) | afflictions, action and moral consequence (karma), eight-limbed discipline and discriminative removal of conjunction | causal therapy and practice |
| powers chapter (**Vibhūti-pāda**) | concentration–meditation–absorption as combined discipline, transformations and extraordinary powers | advanced contemplative application |
| isolation chapter (**Kaivalya-pāda**) | mind/object realism, latent tendencies, discriminative knowledge, cloud-of-dharma absorption and isolation | final metaphysics and liberation |

- ✅ In Pātañjala usage, Yoga is not defined as union with a universal God; it is **restraint of mental modifications (citta-vṛtti-nirodha)** so that the seer abides in its own nature.
- ⚠️ **Source caution:** the five mind-levels (**citta-bhūmis**) are standard in Vyāsa's commentarial exposition, not a five-item sūtra list stated by Patañjali himself.

#### 0A.1 Canonical ownership and answer-worthiness

- **Marks-essential Yoga owner content:** mind-field, five modifications, afflicted/non-afflicted status, practice and dispassion, five afflictions, action and moral consequence (karma), eight limbs, absorption taxonomies, combined discipline, Lord (Īśvara), discriminative knowledge, cloud-of-dharma absorption, isolation and all seven routed PYQs.
- **Bridge material owned by Sāṃkhya:** primordial nature, conscious witness, qualities, twenty-five principles and real transformation. Yoga restates only enough metaphysics to explain its psychology and path.
- **Optional enrichment:** exhaustive power catalogues, specialist commentarial disputes, uncertain textual chronology and modern wellness claims.

#### 2.12A Practice and lower/higher dispassion

| Discipline | Textual meaning | Exam use |
|---|---|---|
| sustained practice (**abhyāsa**) | effort to remain stable in restraint; it becomes firmly grounded when cultivated for a long time, without interruption and with devoted seriousness | supplies continuity against old impressions |
| lower dispassion (**apara-vairāgya / vaśīkāra-vairāgya**) | mastery in which thirst for seen and scripturally promised objects has ceased | weakens ordinary and heavenly attachment |
| higher dispassion (**para-vairāgya**) | non-thirst even for the qualities of nature, arising from direct discernment of conscious witness | releases attachment to subtle states, knowledge and powers |

- ✅ Yoga Sūtra 1.12 makes practice and dispassion jointly responsible for restraint; neither repetition without detachment nor detachment without stabilized practice is sufficient.
- ✅ Commentarial tradition analyses lower dispassion through effort toward control (**yatamāna**), separation of subdued and unsubdued tendencies (**vyatireka**), withdrawal to a single residual mental desire (**ekendriya**) and mastery (**vaśīkāra**).
- ⚠️ The four lower stages are commentarial elaboration; Patañjali's own concise definition in Yoga Sūtra 1.15 is mastery over thirst for seen and heard-of objects.
- ✅ Higher dispassion in Yoga Sūtra 1.16 follows knowledge of conscious witness and extends renunciation even to sattvic insight and the qualities themselves.

#### 4.4 Ethical restraint (yama)

- ✅ The **ethical restraint (yamas)** are the great restraints governing relation to others and to desire.
- ✅ The classical list is:
  1. **ahiṃsā** — non-violence,
  2. **satya** — truthfulness,
  3. **asteya** — non-stealing,
  4. **brahmacarya** — chastity / disciplined energy,
  5. **aparigraha** — non-possessiveness.
- ⚠️ Philosophically, ethical restraint (yama) reduces the rajasic-tāmasic turbulence that continually destabilizes mind-field (citta).
- ✅ When unrestricted by class/birth, place, time or circumstance, the restraints constitute the **universal great vow (sārvabhauma-mahāvrata)** under Yoga Sūtra 2.31.
- ⚠️ This prevents ethics from becoming an optional preliminary: no exceptional context licenses violence, falsehood or possessiveness for the yogin.

#### 5.9B Two routes associated with supra-cognitive absorption

- ✅ Yoga Sūtra 1.19 identifies a condition-based route (**bhava-pratyaya**) associated with disembodied beings and those merged in primordial nature (**videha / prakṛti-laya**).
- ✅ Yoga Sūtra 1.20 gives the means-based route (**upāya-pratyaya**) for other yogins through faith, energy, mindfulness, absorption and wisdom (**śraddhā-vīrya-smṛti-samādhi-prajñā**).
- ⚠️ Commentators warn that mere condition-based absorption or nature-mergence need not equal final discriminative isolation; the explicitly cultivated route better displays Yoga's soteriological method.
- ⚠️ Do not turn these into a third main meditative absorption (samādhi) classification. They qualify routes or bases within the movement beyond ordinary cognitive absorption.

#### 5.12 Meditative absorption (samādhi) and siddhis

- ✅ Higher concentrations may yield extraordinary capacities or **siddhis**.
- ✅ Yoga Sūtra 3.37 states that such capacities are attainments in the outward-turned state but **obstacles to absorption** (**samādhāv upasargāḥ**) when they attract appropriation.
- ⚠️ Powers are not intrinsically liberation and can reactivate egoity, attachment and pride.
- ⚠️ They belong to side-effects of combined discipline, not to the essence of isolation.

#### 5.14 Obstacles and their removal

- ✅ Classical Yoga lists nine obstacles (**antarāyas**):
  disease (**vyādhi**), mental stagnation (**styāna**), doubt (**saṃśaya**), carelessness (**pramāda**), laziness (**ālasya**), sensual non-restraint (**avirati**), false vision (**bhrānti-darśana**), failure to attain a stage (**alabdha-bhūmikatva**) and instability in an attained stage (**anavasthitatva**).
- ✅ Their accompanying symptoms are suffering (**duḥkha**), dejection (**daurmanasya**), bodily tremor (**aṅgamejayatva**) and disturbed inhalation/exhalation (**śvāsa-praśvāsa**).
- ✅ Dedication to the Lord (īśvara-praṇidhāna) is one of the important means of overcoming these.
- ✅ Other sūtra-level remedies include practice of one principle, friendliness/compassion/joy/equanimity, breath regulation and suitable meditative supports.
- ⚠️ This is why questions on meditative absorption (samādhi) often open into the role of God: Yoga's God is not a creator-first doctrine but a **practice-enabling doctrine**.

#### 7.3A Grounds for Lord (Īśvara) and historical control

- ✅ Patañjali's own compact grounds are internal to practice and definition: a special conscious witness untouched by affliction/action/fruition/deposit; unsurpassed seed of omniscience; teacher of the ancients beyond temporal limitation; and praṇava-mediated contemplation.
- ✅ Later Yoga commentators add more theoretical arguments from scripture, maximal degrees of knowledge/power and the need for intelligent coordination of witness and nature.
- ⚠️ Do not attribute the full later creator/administrator proof-set to Patañjali. The Yoga Sūtra primarily presents Lord (Īśvara) as a uniquely free teacher and optional yet powerful meditative support.

#### 7.7 Why Yoga is called theistic Samkhya (seśvara-sāṃkhya)

- ✅ Because it preserves the Sāṃkhyan dualistic ontology while adding Lord (Īśvara).
- ✅ Hence the expression **theistic Samkhya (seśvara-sāṃkhya)** — Sāṃkhya with God.
- ⚠️ This is not a small decorative addition; it changes the psychological and devotional resources of the path while leaving the basic metaphysical dualism intact.
- ⚠️ Yoga is genuinely theistic because it affirms a real Lord (Īśvara), but its theism is limited: Lord (Īśvara) is not the material cause, not clearly the Patañjala creator of the cosmos, and not the external giver of isolation.

### Solved PYQ 1 - 2019 Q7(b), 15 marks

**Question:** How would Yoga comprehend the citta-levels of a scientist, a God-realized devotee and a self-realized yogi?

#### Model solution

Yoga classifies mind-field (citta) into five bhumis: **restless mind (kṣipta)** (restless), **dull mind (mūḍha)** (dull), **distracted mind (vikṣipta)** (intermittently focused), **one-pointed mind (ekāgra)** (one-pointed), and **fully restrained mind (niruddha)** (fully restrained).

| Person | Citta-level | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| Scientist | distracted mind (vikṣipta) | capable of sustained empirical focus, but attention remains outward and fluctuates beyond the chosen task |
| God-realized devotee | one-pointed mind (ekāgra) | mind is one-pointedly established in Lord (Īśvara) and fit for deep meditation |
| Self-realized yogi | fully restrained mind (niruddha) | all vrttis are restrained and the seer abides in its own nature |

The scientist should not automatically be called one-pointed mind (ekāgra) merely because of intellectual concentration. Yogic one-pointedness includes freedom from ordinary attachment and a stable inward orientation. The devotee's concentration is supported by Dedication to the Lord (īśvara-praṇidhāna). The self-realized yogi has crossed from concentration on an object to complete restraint.

**Verdict:** The hierarchy measures not intelligence but the degree of guna-purification and freedom from mental dispersion.

**Why this earns marks:** It applies all three cases precisely, avoids equating technical focus with yogic realization, and explains the governing criterion.

1. **Ignorance (avidyā):** mistaking impermanent for permanent, impure for pure, painful for pleasurable and not-self for self.
2. **Ego-identification (asmitā):** identification of seer with the seeing instrument.
3. **Attachment (rāga):** attachment based on remembered pleasure.
4. **Aversion (dveṣa):** aversion based on remembered pain.
5. **Clinging to life (abhiniveśa):** deep clinging to life, present even in the learned.

Ignorance (avidyā) is the field of the other four. Afflictions (kleśas) may be prasupta, tanu, vicchinna or udara—dormant, attenuated, interrupted or fully active.

```text
ignorance (avidyā) → ego-identification (asmitā) → attachment (rāga)/aversion (dveṣa) → action
→ karmasaya → vipaka → rebirth and suffering
```

Their removal is graded. **Preparatory discipline (kriyā-yoga)**—tapas, svadhyaya and Dedication to the Lord (īśvara-praṇidhāna)—attenuates them. Eight-limbed discipline purifies conduct, body, breath, senses and attention. Meditation (dhyāna) burns subtle afflictions; discriminative knowledge destroys ignorance (avidyā)'s root. When afflictions (kleśas) cease, fresh karmic deposits stop, old seeds are exhausted or burnt, and mind-field (citta) no longer produces identification.

Meditative absorption (samādhi) alone is instrumental unless it matures into **discriminative insight (viveka-khyāti)**, uninterrupted discrimination between Conscious witness (puruṣa) and mind-field (citta). Para-vairagya prevents attachment even to insight and powers. Cloud-of-virtue absorption (dharmamegha-samādhi) completes the cessation of binding tendencies; the qualities (guṇas) undergo reverse evolution (pratiprasava) and Conscious witness (puruṣa) stands in isolation (kaivalya).

An objection says Conscious witness (puruṣa) was always free, so removal is unnecessary. Yoga replies that liberation removes misidentification in mind-field (citta), not an actual impurity in Conscious witness (puruṣa).

**Verdict:** Klesa-removal leads to isolation (kaivalya) by breaking both the cognitive root and karmic reproduction of bondage.

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

### OPTIONAL DEPTH 1 — Yoga as Theistic Samkhya (seśvara-sāṃkhya) and the Structure of Mind-field (citta)

#### Advanced reflection problem

Mind-field (citta) unifies determinative intellect (buddhi), ego-maker (ahaṃkāra) and sensory mind (manas) as one operative field. It is materially subtle and sattva-dominant, so it can reflect consciousness while remaining insentient in itself. Bondage occurs when borrowed luminosity is mistaken for intrinsic selfhood.

The reflection model protects an unchanging Conscious witness (puruṣa): every transformation occurs on the mind-field (citta) side. Yet it presupposes an intelligible relation between radically different orders. Mere proximity avoids physical interaction but does not completely explain how one experience contains both object-form and witnesshood.

The citta-bhumi scheme is soteriological rather than a generic intelligence scale. A scientist may display powerful but outward and intermittent focus, while only one-pointed mind (ekāgra) and fully restrained mind (niruddha) are fully suited to liberating Yoga.

### OPTIONAL DEPTH 2 — Restraint of mental modifications (citta-vṛtti-nirodha): Five Modifications and Real Transformation of Mind

#### Advanced transformation and continuity

Vrttis may be afflicted or non-afflicted. Yoga therefore proceeds therapeutically: aklista cognition and disciplined memory replace afflicted patterns before all object-identification subsides. Nirodha is revelatory restraint, not violent destruction or unconscious blankness.

Citta-parinama supplies a realist account of progress. Nirodha-parinama strengthens impressions of restraint; samadhi-parinama shifts from many-pointedness to absorption; ekagrata-parinama stabilizes one-pointed continuity. These changes presuppose a persisting prakritic substrate rather than momentary flashes.

Buddhist momentariness can explain habits through causal series, but Yoga argues that a continuing mind-field (citta) better bears samskaras and graded transformation. The dispute concerns whether continuity requires a substrate or only a stream.

### OPTIONAL DEPTH 3 — Afflictions (kleśas), Action and moral consequence (karma) and Astanga Yoga: The Causal Therapy of Bondage

#### Advanced moral psychology

Ignorance (avidyā) is not missing information but systematic inversion: impermanent as permanent, impure as pure, painful as pleasant and non-self as self. The other afflictions (kleśas) can be dormant, thinned, interrupted or active; temporary suppression is not destruction.

Karmasaya and vipaka turn psychology into a theory of cycle of rebirth (saṃsāra). Afflicted action leaves deposits that ripen as birth, lifespan and experience. The burnt-seed model explains why meditation can remove future fertility even while already-fructifying embodiment retains momentum.

Astanga is an ethical psychology rather than an exercise sequence. Without ethical restraint (yama) and observance (niyama), concentration can magnify ego and affliction. Without the inner limbs, ethical conduct remains incomplete as a route to direct discrimination.

### OPTIONAL DEPTH 4 — Meditative absorption (samādhi): Object-Supported Insight, Seedless Restraint and Classification Control

#### Advanced interpretive control

Samprajnata retains cognition and support. Its canonical factors are vitarka, vicara, ananda and ego-identification (asmitā). The separate samapatti analysis distinguishes gross and subtle object-absorption according to whether conceptual-verbal overlay remains. Conflating these schemes is a major doctrinal error.

Mature nirvicara yields truth-bearing insight, but even exalted cognition remains an event in mind-field (citta). Asamprajnata arises through disciplined restraint and may retain residual samskara; nirbija marks the further cessation of that seed. Therefore neither state should be equated casually with sleep or blankness.

Siddhis can accompany integrated concentration (saṃyama) but become obstacles when appropriated. Dharmamegha meditative absorption (samādhi) and supreme dispassion turn the path away from powers toward final discrimination.

### OPTIONAL DEPTH 5 — Isolation (kaivalya) and Lord (Īśvara): Isolation, Discernment and the Special Conscious witness (puruṣa)

#### Advanced necessity and redundancy

Isolation (kaivalya) is needed at the empirical level even though Conscious witness (puruṣa) is eternally pure because misidentification operates through mind-field (citta). Discipline removes obstructions like a farmer opening a channel; it does not manufacture freedom. Burnt seed and potter's wheel images explain the end of future bondage and residual embodiment.

Lord (Īśvara) is metaphysically limited but practically significant. He neither becomes the world nor grants liberation by fiat. As timeless teacher, perfect exemplar and pranava-supported meditative object, he de-centres ego and removes obstacles.

The redundancy objection therefore has force: Samkhya ontology and viveka can formally explain liberation without God. Yoga's strongest reply is soteriological rather than cosmological—practical indispensability for some aspirants need not imply creatorhood.

### OPTIONAL DEPTH 6 — Inter-School Debates, Realism, Criticisms and UPSC Traps

#### Advanced realist and interaction debates

Against Yogacara, Yoga defends three-term realism: external object, citta-modification and witnessing Conscious witness (puruṣa). Objects are not dependent on one mind, and mind-field (citta) cannot be self-luminous because it is itself seen. Shared karmic appearance remains a sophisticated idealist counter.

The recurring internal objection concerns dualist interaction. Reflection and proximity explain why Conscious witness (puruṣa) does not change, but critics may still ask how ontologically distinct principles jointly constitute experience.

Yoga is strongest as operational moral psychology: it connects habit, attention, memory, latent impression, affliction and meditative transformation. Its metaphysical claims about Conscious witness (puruṣa), rebirth and yogic perception face a greater public verification burden.

### OPTIONAL DEPTH 7 — Advanced Ledgers, Comparative Positioning and the Graded Verdict

#### Advanced system assessment

Yoga's architecture is unusually integrated. Mind-field (citta) and vrtti give phenomenology; affliction (kleśa) and action and moral consequence (karma) give moral causation; astanga supplies method; meditative absorption (samādhi) gives meditative epistemology; viveka and isolation (kaivalya) complete liberation.

Its costs are inherited and added. The system must defend plural inactive witnesses, a continuing subtle mind, karmic deposits, extraordinary cognition and a special Conscious witness (puruṣa) whose practical role exceeds his cosmological role.

Controlled Western comparisons may clarify suspension, witness-consciousness or habit, but must remain secondary. Husserlian reduction does not seek isolation (kaivalya), Cartesian consciousness acts, and psychoanalytic dispositions lack Yoga's rebirth framework.

This doctrine boundary is exam-useful: public Yoga shows contemporary social relevance, while philosophical Yoga asks a different question—how disciplined transformation of mind-field (citta) ends misidentification and karmic bondage.
