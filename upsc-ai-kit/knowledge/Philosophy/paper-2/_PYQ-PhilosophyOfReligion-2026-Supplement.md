## 1. THE FOURTEEN 2026 SECTION B PARTS, WITH ROUTES ✅

### Q5 — compulsory, 10 marks × 5, "about 150 words each"

- **Q5(a) · 10 marks · [Religious Experience](./philosophy-of-religion/Religious-Experience.md):** On what grounds does William James characterize mystical states as a genuine expression of religious experience? Elaborate.
  - ⚠️ **Route:** James's four marks of mysticism (ineffability, noetic quality, transiency, passivity) and his three conclusions about their authority, then the "genuine expression" test. Keep the printed word **grounds** — the answer is a list of criteria plus their warrant, not a narrative of *Varieties*.
  - ⚠️ **Boundary:** the *veridicality* debate (Swinburne's principles of credulity/testimony, Katz's constructivism) is admissible as evaluation; the argument **from** religious experience as a proof of God belongs to [Proofs for God](./philosophy-of-religion/Proofs-for-God.md) and should be a single bounded line only.

- **Q5(b) · 10 marks · [Proofs for God](./philosophy-of-religion/Proofs-for-God.md) → §1(b) + §8.2:** Discuss Aquinas' argument from the contingency of the world to prove the existence of God.
  - ⚠️ **Route:** the **Third Way** specifically. Possibility-and-necessity premises, the "if everything can fail to be, at some time nothing was" step, the necessary being *per se*, then the standard objections (quantifier shift, fallacy of composition, Hume on necessary existence, the gap to the God of religion).
  - ❌ **Trap:** do **not** answer with all Five Ways; do not convert the Third Way into a temporal first-cause argument.

- **Q5(c) · 10 marks · [Reason, Revelation and Faith](./philosophy-of-religion/Reason-Revelation-Faith.md) → §9.10:** How does Paul Tillich's idea of faith as ultimate concern address the relation between faith, doubt and reason? Evaluate.
  - ✅ **Gap closed by this remediation.** §9.10 is a new dedicated dossier: twelve numbered steps, presuppositions, objections and replies, three comparison charts, traps, and 10/15/20-mark spines.
  - ⚠️ **Route:** ultimate concern as the state of the whole personality → the unconditional/conditional criterion and idolatrous faith → *fides qua* / *fides quae* → the three doubts (methodological, sceptical, **existential**) → "doubt is a necessary element in it" and courage → technical vs ontological reason, ecstasy as fulfilled rationality → evaluation.
  - ⚠️ **Boundary:** Tillich's **symbol** theory ("symbols participate in that to which they point") stays with [Religious Language](./philosophy-of-religion/Religious-Language.md); bring it in only as the one line needed to explain why the content of faith must be symbolic.

- **Q5(d) · 10 marks · [Religious Language](./philosophy-of-religion/Religious-Language.md):** Critically discuss the views of R. B. Braithwaite on the nature of religious language.
  - ⚠️ **Route:** the empiricist starting point, religious assertions as **conative/moral** declarations of intention to follow a policy of life, stories entertained without belief, the resulting non-cognitivism, then the standard criticisms (loss of truth-claim, why *these* stories, the believer's own self-understanding, falsification debate).
  - 📝 Already an established owner: the same author was set in 2018 Q7(b) and 2024 Q8(a); the 2026 stem is broader, so cover *nature* of religious language on Braithwaite's account, not only his non-cognitivism.

- **Q5(e) · 10 marks · [Reason, Revelation and Faith](./philosophy-of-religion/Reason-Revelation-Faith.md) → §1 + §9.1 + §9.6:** "If religion were a subject of reason alone, it would have lacked an experiential account." Do you agree with this statement? Give reasons in support of your answer.
  - ⚠️ **Routing justification (a judgement call, stated openly).** The stem's governing term is **reason alone** and its conditional form is about the *sufficiency of reason for religion* — the classic reason/faith/revelation question. It is therefore owned here, not by [Religious Experience](./philosophy-of-religion/Religious-Experience.md), whose clause covers the *nature and object of religious experience* rather than the limits of rational religion. The experiential half of the answer is imported as a bounded cross-link.
  - ⚠️ **Route:** rationalism and natural religion (Deism, Kant's religion within the bounds of reason alone) → what a purely rational religion retains and loses → Schleiermacher's feeling of absolute dependence, Otto's *numinous*, James's noetic quality → Tillich's "reason is the precondition of faith" as the *both/and* verdict → agree with qualification, not flat assent.
  - ⚠️ **Cross-link:** [Religious Experience](./philosophy-of-religion/Religious-Experience.md) for the phenomenological content; [Religious Pluralism](./philosophy-of-religion/Religious-Pluralism.md) only if the answer needs the diversity-of-experience point.

### Q6 — 20 + 15 + 15

- **Q6(a) · 20 marks · [Problem of Evil](./philosophy-of-religion/Problem-of-Evil.md):** In what sense does St. Augustine's identification of all evil as either sin or as the punishment of sin succeed or fail in providing a coherent account of both moral and natural evil? Evaluate.
  - ⚠️ **Route:** *privatio boni*, evil as lack not substance → free will and the Fall → the sin/punishment dichotomy printed in the stem → **moral** evil handled well, **natural** evil handled only derivatively (penal consequence of the Fall, angelic sin) → Schleiermacher's and Hick's objection that a flawless creature falling is self-contradictory → Irenaean/soul-making contrast → verdict split by evil-type, which is exactly what "both moral and natural evil" demands.
  - ❌ **Trap:** an answer that treats "theodicy in general" loses the printed dichotomy. Structure the evaluation as *succeeds for moral evil / strains for natural evil*.

- **Q6(b) · 15 marks · [Religion and Morality](./philosophy-of-religion/Religion-and-Morality.md) → §2 + §9.2 + §9.11:** How does the issue of moral autonomy conflict with conception of religion as a source of morality? Critically discuss.
  - ⚠️ **Route:** Kantian autonomy and heteronomy → the Euthyphro dilemma as the sharpened form of the conflict → divine command theory's two horns → Adams's **modified** DCT (God's loving nature) and Quinn's reply → the residual concession that "loving" presupposes an independent standard of goodness → the reconciliation options (natural law's mediation; God as source of *motivation* rather than *obligation*).
  - ⚠️ **Indian parity:** Mīmāṃsā's authorless injunction and the karma/*dharma* order supply a non-commander grounding — use the parity bench in §6A.

- **Q6(c) · 15 marks · [Religious Pluralism](./philosophy-of-religion/Religious-Pluralism.md):** Does the idea of Religious Pluralism necessarily entail moral relativism? Critically discuss.
  - ⚠️ **Route:** the printed word is **necessarily** — so the answer must attack an *entailment* claim, not merely describe pluralism. Distinguish *truth*-pluralism from *value*-relativism; Hick's Real *an sich* with a **common soteriological criterion** (the transformation from self-centredness to Reality-centredness) is itself a cross-tradition moral standard, which blocks the entailment.
  - ⚠️ **Cross-link:** [Religion and Morality](./philosophy-of-religion/Religion-and-Morality.md) for the relativism half only; do not relocate ownership.

### Q7 — 20 + 15 + 15

- **Q7(a) · 20 marks · [Religious Language](./philosophy-of-religion/Religious-Language.md):** Analyse R. M. Hare's concept of 'blik' and assess whether it successfully defends the meaningfulness of religious language.
  - ⚠️ **Route:** the University Discussion — Flew's falsification challenge and the parable of the invisible gardener → Hare's lunatic-and-the-dons parable → a *blik* as an unfalsifiable but not therefore meaningless framework attitude, with the sane/insane distinction → assessment: it saves meaningfulness by **surrendering cognitive assertion**, which Mitchell's partisan parable tries to avoid; believers do take themselves to assert something.
  - ⚠️ **Pair with Q5(d):** Braithwaite and Hare are the two non-cognitivist routes; the 2026 paper sets both, so keep the *conative* (Braithwaite) vs *attitudinal-framework* (Hare) distinction crisp.

- **Q7(b) · 15 marks · [Notions of God](./philosophy-of-religion/Notions-of-God.md):** Discuss the difference between personalistic and impersonalistic notions of God in the context of devotion.
  - ⚠️ **Route:** *saguṇa*/*nirguṇa*, personal theism vs Absolute/*nirguṇa* Brahman, and the parallel Western pair (classical personal theism vs Tillich's being-itself and Spinoza's *Deus sive Natura*) → the printed qualifier **in the context of devotion** requires the *bhakti* consequence: an impersonal Absolute strains the I–Thou relation, which is why Rāmānuja and the *bhakti* traditions insist on a personal God, and why Advaita retains *saguṇa* Brahman at the *vyāvahārika* level as a legitimate object of worship.
  - ⚠️ **Cross-link:** [Soul, Immortality, Rebirth and Liberation](./philosophy-of-religion/Soul-Immortality-Rebirth.md) for *bhakti* as a path; keep it to a bounded paragraph.

- **Q7(c) · 15 marks · [Religious Pluralism](./philosophy-of-religion/Religious-Pluralism.md):** Can conflicting truth claims among religions be reconciled within the framework of religious pluralism? Critically discuss.
  - ⚠️ **Route:** exclusivism / inclusivism / pluralism → Hick's Kantian *Real an sich* and the phenomena/noumenon distinction → the "different responses to one Reality" reconciliation → the standard objections (it revises rather than reconciles the claims; it is itself a first-order metaphysical thesis; traditions do not recognise themselves in it) → Indian resources: *ekaṃ sad viprā bahudhā vadanti*, *anekāntavāda*/*syādvāda*, Vivekananda's universal religion.
  - 📝 **Pairing note:** 2026 sets **two** Pluralism parts (Q6(c) and Q7(c)). Do not write the same answer twice: Q6(c) is about a claimed *entailment to relativism*; Q7(c) is about the *reconciliation of conflicting truth claims*.

### Q8 — 20 + 15 + 15

- **Q8(a) · 20 marks · [Religion and Morality](./philosophy-of-religion/Religion-and-Morality.md) → §9.12:** Critically evaluate the shift in Gandhi's view from 'God is Truth' to 'Truth is God', in the context of interrelation between religion and morality.
  - ✅ **Gap closed by this remediation.** §9.12 is a new dedicated dossier: the dated textual base, Gandhi's own five reasons for the shift, the predication-vs-identification table, the atheist-inclusiveness argument, Absolute vs relative truth and fallibilism, objections and replies, traps, and 10/15/20-mark spines.
  - ⚠️ **Route:** state the shift with its source (*Young India*, 31 December 1931, pp. 427–28, where Gandhi says he reached it "two years ago", i.e. ≈1929) → the five reasons (inclusiveness of atheists; universality; Truth as the one undeniable; *sat* as being; God as name vs Truth as essence) → the religion–morality core: "the essence of religion is morality", "no religion overriding morality", means–end unity → the inversion of divine command theory (Euthyphro's second horn) → relative truth, fallibilism and the argument to *ahiṃsā* → objections (thin metaphysics, conscience as judge, relativism charge) with replies.
  - ❌ **Traps:** not atheism; not a 1931 Lausanne invention; relative truth ≠ relativism; *satya* ≠ mere honesty.
  - ⚠️ **Boundary:** *sarva-dharma-samabhāva* as an equal-respect thesis is [Religious Pluralism](./philosophy-of-religion/Religious-Pluralism.md); Truth-as-*sat* metaphysics is [Notions of God](./philosophy-of-religion/Notions-of-God.md).

- **Q8(b) · 15 marks · [Proofs for God](./philosophy-of-religion/Proofs-for-God.md) → §8.9:** Explain and examine Madhvacharya's arguments for the existence of God.
  - ✅ **Gap closed by this remediation.** §8.9 is a new dedicated dossier, built around the distinction the question makes it easy to blur.
  - ⚠️ **Route:** *svatantra*/*paratantra* ontology and *pañca-bheda* → the three *pramāṇas* and the transcendental argument that perception and inference must be intrinsically valid (otherwise scripture itself is unreadable) → the four strands **graded**: (A) scripture, decisive; (B) metaphysical dependence, structurally central; (C) design, deliberately demoted by Madhva himself; (D) perfection, about the concept and **not** an Anselmian proof → objections and replies → the historiographic caution that the polished dialectic is **Jayatīrtha** (1356–1388) and **Vyāsatīrtha/Vyāsarāya** (1478–1589).
  - ❌ **Traps:** do not transplant Nyāya's *kāryāt* proof onto Madhva; do not call *pañca-bheda* five proofs; do not read dependence as temporal creation *ex nihilo*.

- **Q8(c) · 15 marks · [Notions of God](./philosophy-of-religion/Notions-of-God.md):** Present an account of criticisms against omniscience and omnipotence as attributes of God.
  - ⚠️ **Route:** the stem asks for **criticisms**, so organise by objection, not by attribute-definition. **Omnipotence:** the paradox of the stone, whether omnipotence covers the logically impossible (Aquinas's restriction vs Descartes's universal possibilism), and the omnipotence–goodness tension in the problem of evil. **Omniscience:** foreknowledge vs human free will (Boethian eternity, Ockhamist hard/soft facts, Molinist middle knowledge), the essentially indexical "knowledge *de se*" problem, and whether an immutable knower can know changing tensed facts.
  - ⚠️ **Cross-link:** the evil-based objection is developed in [Problem of Evil](./philosophy-of-religion/Problem-of-Evil.md); use one bounded paragraph here.

### What 2026 changes in the eight-year trend ⚠️

1. **The flattest distribution in the corpus.** Six owners take exactly two parts each and two take one. The 2018–2025 pattern of a dominant owner (Soul–Immortality at 17 parts) does not repeat.
2. **Two absences at once.** ✅ 2026 is the **first paper in the local corpus with no primary-owned part** for *Soul, Immortality, Rebirth and Liberation* — which had appeared in every year from 2018 to 2025 — and *Religion without God* is absent for only the second time (previously 2022). Treat both as due, not as deprioritised.
3. **Religious Experience holds its one-part-per-year record**, now nine years running (2018–2026).
4. **Named-thinker density rises sharply.** Eight of fourteen parts name an individual: James, Aquinas, Tillich, Braithwaite, Augustine, Hare, Gandhi, Madhva. Thinker-specific dossiers, not theme surveys, are what the paper rewards.
5. **Two Indian named thinkers in one paper** (Gandhi, Madhva) against six Western. The examiner is testing named-Indian-thinker depth in philosophy of religion, not only school-level positions (Nyāya, Advaita) as in earlier years.
6. **Doubled owners must be differentiated.** Pluralism (Q6(c) vs Q7(c)) and Religious Language (Q5(d) Braithwaite vs Q7(a) Hare) each appear twice with *different* demands; so do Proofs (Aquinas contingency vs Madhva) and Religion and Morality (autonomy vs Gandhi).
