## 1. SECTION A — ROUTING, PART BY PART

### Q1 — compulsory (10 × 5 = 50; about 150 words each)

**Q1(a) [10] ✅** — *"Why does Rawls consider it necessary that the two principles of justice be
applied in lexical order ? Explain."*
- **Primary owner:** [Social and Political Ideals](./socio-political/Social-Political-Ideals.md)
- **Core anchor:** §3.5.3A *Why the lexical order is necessary* — **added in this remediation pass**
- **Supporting:** §3.5.1 original position and veil; §3.5.2 the two principles; §3.5.3 priority
  rules
- **Demand decode:** the directive is *Explain*, and the object is the **necessity** of the
  ordering, not a description of the principles. Marks lie in the anti-aggregation argument, the
  original-position choice argument, the anti-intuitionism/determinacy argument and self-respect as
  a primary good; the internal priority of fair equality of opportunity over the difference
  principle must appear.
- **Do not:** ⚠️ present the priority as unconditional — it holds under favourable conditions.

**Q1(b) [10] ✅** — *"Is Plato's vision of an ideal state compatible with the rights and liberties
of individual citizens ? Discuss."*
- **Primary owner:** [Individual and State](./socio-political/Individual-and-State.md)
- **Core anchor:** §4.4B *Plato's ideal state and the rights and liberties of individual citizens* —
  **added in this remediation pass**
- **Supporting:** §1 rights; §2 duties; §4.0 organic and mechanistic views; §4.2 the idealist view;
  §4.4A Plato and Aristotle between statism and individualism
- **Bridges (cite, do not re-teach):** [Plato and Aristotle](../paper-1/western/Plato-Aristotle.md)
  for the metaphysics and the tripartite soul;
  [Social and Political Ideals](./socio-political/Social-Political-Ideals.md) §2 for liberty
- **Demand decode:** a compatibility question. The answer must fix the **anachronism** —
  subjective claim-rights are not Plato's category — then give the functional-justice argument, the
  censorship/property/family restrictions, and a graded verdict distinguishing *welfare* of
  citizens (which the kallipolis intends) from *rights against the state* (which it lacks).

**Q1(c) [10] ✅** — *"Anarchism is not an independent political ideology in itself, rather it is
inspired by liberalism on the one hand and socialism on the other. Analyze."*
- **Primary owner:** [Political Ideologies](./socio-political/Political-Ideologies.md)
- **Core anchor:** §1.8 *Is anarchism an independent ideology, or a confluence of liberalism and
  socialism?* — **added in this remediation pass**
- **Supporting:** §1.1–§1.7 anarchism's types and arguments; §3 liberalism; §2 Marxism and socialism
- **Demand decode:** *Analyze* on an embedded thesis. The answer must state the dependence thesis
  at full strength (liberal roots: individual liberty, anti-coercion, voluntarism; socialist roots:
  property critique, class analysis, mutualism/communism) and then the independence reply — the
  **anti-authority premise is not derivable from either parent**, and anarchism splits from
  organised socialism over the state as a transitional instrument.
- **Anchor fact ✅:** the First International's split at the **Hague Congress, 1872**, marks the
  organisational break between the Marxist and anarchist wings.

**Q1(d) [10] ✅** — *"On what grounds does Marxism criticize pure democracy ?"*
- **Primary owner:** [Political Ideologies](./socio-political/Political-Ideologies.md)
- **Core anchor:** §2.10 *The Marxist critique of "pure" democracy* — **added in this remediation
  pass** (coverage was previously nil; without it this part had no route)
- **Supporting:** §2.1–§2.9 the materialist conception, class and ideology
- **Bridges:** [Forms of Government](./socio-political/Forms-of-Government.md) §4.3 procedural vs
  substantive democracy; [Individual and State](./socio-political/Individual-and-State.md) §4.6 the
  Marxist challenge to the liberal state
- **Demand decode:** the stem asks for **grounds**, so the answer is a numbered list: the class
  character of the state; political vs human emancipation; the abstraction of the citizen from the
  *bourgeois*; ruling ideas and manufactured consent; periodic authorisation as delegated
  alienation; formal equality masking material inequality; and the Commune-type institutional
  alternative.
- **Terminological ✅:** "pure democracy" is attacked by name in the **1918** polemic
  *The Proletarian Revolution and the Renegade Kautsky*; the class-state thesis is already in the
  *Communist Manifesto* (**1848**) and the emancipation distinction in *On the Jewish Question*
  (**1843**).

**Q1(e) [10] ✅** — *"How does value based education address the problem of corruption ?"*
- **Primary owner:** [Crime and Punishment](./socio-political/Crime-and-Punishment.md) — ⚠️ this is
  the most defensible canonical owner because **corruption** is its owned syllabus concept (§2), and
  §2.4 already frames the ethical/systemic distinction on which the answer turns
- **Core anchor:** §2.8 *Value-based education as an answer to corruption* — **added in this
  remediation pass**
- **Supporting:** §2.1 definition; §2.2 why corruption is wrong; §2.4 systemic or ethical;
  §2.7 Indian institutional illustrations
- **Cross-links (deliberate, one-way):**
  [Development and Social Progress](./socio-political/Development-Social-Progress.md) §3.2 for
  education and capability; [Individual and State](./socio-political/Individual-and-State.md) §2.5
  for duties and civic formation;
  [Forms of Government](./socio-political/Forms-of-Government.md) §4A.5 for civic education against
  manipulation
- **Demand decode:** *How* — a mechanism question. Marks lie in naming the mechanisms (moral
  perception, motivation, practical competence, norm correction, civic agency, professional
  identity), relating each to a component of corruption, and closing on complementarity: education
  builds **moral demand**; institutional reform alters the **opportunity structure**.

### Q2 [20 + 15 + 15]

**Q2(a) [20] ✅** — *"Evaluate the importance of Kautilya's theory of Rajamandala in the context of
sovereignty."*
- **Primary owner:** [Sovereignty](./socio-political/Sovereignty.md)
- **Core anchor:** §5.5 the circle-of-states (*maṇḍala*) theory and the six-fold policy
  (*ṣāḍguṇya*) — **pre-existing coverage; no new section required**
- **Supporting:** §5.1–§5.4 Kautilya's seven limbs (*saptāṅga*) and the conception of rule;
  §1 the concept of sovereignty
- **Demand decode:** *Evaluate* — state the doctrine, then assess it **against the modern concept
  of sovereignty**: internal supremacy versus external independence, the relational and graded
  character of *maṇḍala* sovereignty versus the absolute, indivisible sovereignty of Bodin and
  Austin (§2–§3), and the realist reading's limits.

**Q2(b) [15] ✅** — *"Does providing land and property rights to women constitute a necessary
condition or a sufficient condition or both for ensuring social equality among the genders ?
Discuss."*
- **Primary owner:** [Gender Discrimination](./socio-political/Gender-Discrimination.md)
- **Core anchor:** §3.1–§3.6 — property and its relation to gender equality — **pre-existing
  coverage**
- **Supporting:** §3.2 why property matters; §3.3 ownership, access and control; §3.4 formal law
  and social practice; §3.5 personal law
- **Demand decode:** a **conditions** stem — the answer must use the necessary/sufficient
  apparatus explicitly, conclude *necessary but not sufficient*, and say what else is required:
  enforcement, control as against nominal title, education and employment, freedom from violence,
  and the reform of social norms.
- ⚠️ Legal-status caution: statutes and judgments are dated illustrations, not proof of social
  outcomes.

**Q2(c) [15] ✅** — *"Examine the challenges faced by a multicultural society in creating a
harmonious balance between group rights and individual rights."*
- **Primary owner:**
  [Humanism, Secularism and Multiculturalism](./socio-political/Humanism-Secularism-Multiculturalism.md)
- **Core anchors:** §3.4 Kymlicka's group-differentiated rights; §3.10 objections and replies;
  §3A.2 parity of participation — **pre-existing coverage**
- **Demand decode:** *Examine* the **challenges**, so the structure is problem-led: the internal
  minorities problem, exit costs, the boundary between external protections and internal
  restrictions, the risk of essentialising cultures, and the adjudication principle the answer
  adopts.

### Q3 [20 + 15 + 15]

**Q3(a) [20] ✅** — *"Retributivism presents a deontological justification for capital punishment,
whereas deterrence presents a consequential justification for it. Explain."*
- **Primary owner:** [Crime and Punishment](./socio-political/Crime-and-Punishment.md)
- **Core anchors:** §1.3A deontological and consequentialist justificatory logics; §1.4
  retributivism; §1.5 deterrence; §5.1–§5.7 the capital-punishment cluster — **pre-existing
  coverage**
- **Demand decode:** *Explain* a stated contrast. The answer must keep the two logics separate —
  desert and proportionality versus aggregate prevention — show how each applies to the death
  penalty, and then state each one's characteristic difficulty (Kantian dignity tension for
  retributivism; evidential and use-as-means problems for deterrence).

**Q3(b) [15] ✅** — *"The two antagonistic state structures — monarchy and democracy, both share
some common features with dictatorship". Do you agree with this view ? Give reasons and
justifications for your answer."*
- **Primary owner:** [Forms of Government](./socio-political/Forms-of-Government.md)
- **Core anchor:** §5.6 *Monarchy, democracy and dictatorship: shared features and decisive
  differences* — **added in this remediation pass**
- **Supporting:** §2 monarchy; §4 democracy; §4A.4 populism and illiberal democracy; §4A.6
  democratic pathologies
- **Demand decode:** an **agree/disagree with reasons** stem, so a graded verdict is mandatory.
  Concede the shared features — concentration of decision, emergency powers, personalisation of
  authority, symbolic legitimation, bureaucratic apparatus — then state the decisive differences:
  the source and revocability of authority, legal limitation, institutionalised opposition, and
  accountability. Verdict: **partial agreement — resemblance in the exercise of power, difference
  in its authorisation and termination**.
- ❌ No present-day country, leader or party may be named.

**Q3(c) [15] ✅** — *"Negative liberty seems to embody the idea of ultimate personal freedom,
whereas positive liberty enables individuals to achieve their full potential. Discuss."*
- **Primary owner:** [Social and Political Ideals](./socio-political/Social-Political-Ideals.md)
- **Core anchors:** §2.2 Berlin on negative and positive liberty; §2.5 Green and positive freedom;
  §2.8 republican liberty as non-domination — **pre-existing coverage**
- **Demand decode:** *Discuss* an embedded contrast. Marks lie in Berlin's warning about the
  "higher self" and its authoritarian misuse, the reply that formal non-interference without
  capability is empty, and the non-domination position as the third option that dissolves the
  binary.

### Q4 [20 + 15 + 15]

**Q4(a) [20] ✅** — *"How has Ambedkar applied the concept of 'Social Endosmosis' for annihilation
of castes. Evaluate its relevance for contemporary debates on identity and the politics of
difference."*
- **Primary owner:**
  [Caste Discrimination: Gandhi and Ambedkar](./socio-political/Caste-Gandhi-Ambedkar.md)
- **Core anchor:** §3.3A *Social endosmosis, annihilation, and contemporary identity and
  difference* — **added in this remediation pass** (the term appeared nowhere in the socio-political
  owners before this pass)
- **Supporting:** §3.2 endogamy (*Castes in India*, 1916); §3.3 *Annihilation of Caste* (1936);
  §3.4 caste as anti-social; §3.5 liberty, equality, fraternity; §5.3 and §5.3A caste–class–gender
  and the recognition/redistribution frame; §5A.1 and §5A.5 affirmative action and its limits
- **Demand decode:** ⚠️ a **two-demand** stem — application **and** contemporary evaluation. Both
  are separately marked; a caste-only answer is capped. The spine is: endosmosis defined in
  Ambedkar's own markers → endosmosis = fraternity = democracy as associated living → the four
  channels caste closes → the remedies that follow → the identity-politics test (does the
  mobilisation open channels or harden membranes?) → intersection caution → verdict.

**Q4(b) [15] ✅** — *"Explain the contrast between the monistic and pluralistic approaches to
sovereignty."*
- **Primary owner:** [Sovereignty](./socio-political/Sovereignty.md)
- **Core anchor:** §6.1 monist vs pluralist table — **pre-existing coverage**
- **Supporting:** §2 Bodin; §3 Austin; §3A Kelsen and Hart; §4.1–§4.7 Laski
- **Demand decode:** *Explain* a contrast — an axis-by-axis comparison is the efficient form:
  locus, divisibility, source of obligation, treatment of associations, and the account of law.

**Q4(c) [15] ✅** — *"Examine the relationship between democracy and development. Does democracy
promote or hinder development ?"*
- **Primary owner:**
  [Development and Social Progress](./socio-political/Development-Social-Progress.md)
- **Core anchor:** §2.7 *Democracy and development* — **added in this remediation pass**
- **Supporting:** §1.2 the growth/development distinction; §1.4–§1.5 human development and Sen;
  §2.3 criteria of progress; §2.6 social progress and democracy; §2.6A participation and
  empowerment
- **Demand decode:** ⚠️ two demands again — the **relationship** (two-way and conditional) and the
  **verdict** (promote or hinder). The conception of development must be fixed first, because the
  answer changes with it; the authoritarian-efficiency case must be stated at full strength before
  it is answered; the closing formula is *accountability + state capacity + rule of law → inclusive
  development*.
- ❌ No country may be named as an instance of democratic or authoritarian development.

## 4. 2026 PATTERN INSIGHTS ⚠️

1. **Two-demand stems are now standard.** Q4(a) and Q4(c) each contain two separately markable
   demands, and Q3(b) requires both agreement-assessment and justification. Answering only the
   first demand caps the score regardless of quality.
2. **Embedded-thesis stems dominate.** Q1(c), Q3(b) and Q3(c) place a proposition in the stem and
   ask for analysis or assent. The examiner is testing whether the candidate can state the thesis
   at full strength before qualifying it.
3. **Conditions-language is being tested directly.** Q2(b) uses the necessary/sufficient vocabulary
   explicitly — the same apparatus as 2024 Q3(c). This is now a recurring examiner device.
4. **Applied-ethics entry into the socio-political section.** Q1(e) is the first part in the local
   record to route an education-and-integrity question into the corruption cluster.
5. **Cross-owner triangulation.** Q3(b) requires three regime concepts in one answer and Q1(c)
   requires three ideologies; single-concept preparation is no longer sufficient.
6. **Comparison is the dominant form.** Nine of fourteen parts are comparative or contrastive, which
   is why every new section in this pass carries a detailed verbal comparison chart.
7. **Indian-tradition presence is stable.** Kautilya in Q2(a) and Ambedkar in Q4(a) together carry
   40 of Section A's 200 marks.
