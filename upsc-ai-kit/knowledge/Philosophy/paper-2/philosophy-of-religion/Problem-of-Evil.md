## 1. THE PROBLEM ✅
- **Logical problem (Mackie):** the propositions *God is omnipotent*, *God is perfectly good*, and *evil exists* are **not formally contradictory by themselves**. The contradiction requires added principles: a perfectly good being opposes evil as far as it can, and an omnipotent being has no relevant limit on preventing it. ✅
- **Evidential problem (Rowe):** even if not a strict contradiction, the **amount and distribution** of pointless suffering makes God's existence **improbable**. ✅
- **Kinds of evil:** **moral evil** (from human wrongdoing — cruelty, injustice) vs **natural evil** (earthquakes, disease, suffering not caused by will). ✅
- **"Problem is an offshoot of how God is conceptualised" (2024 PYQ):** the problem bites *only* on a God who is **simultaneously omnipotent + wholly good + creator**. Weaken any attribute (finite God, impersonal Absolute, no God) and the problem dissolves — hence it is **concept-dependent**. ⚠️

### 1.1 Divine profile, additional premises and three burdens

The full classical profile adds **omniscience**: God knows every evil and every
available prevention. Omniscience is not a separate member of Mackie's familiar
three-proposition set, but it blocks the reply that evil escaped divine notice.

```text
G is omnipotent + G is omniscient + G is perfectly good + evil exists
        + good prevents every evil it can unless a morally sufficient reason exists
        + unlimited power can prevent any evil whose prevention is logically possible
        -> alleged contradiction
```

| Burden | What must be shown |
|---|---|
| Logical | The set is inconsistent, or a possible morally sufficient reason restores compatibility |
| Evidential | The observed amount, kinds or distribution of suffering lowers or does not lower the probability of God |
| Existential/moral | A response respects lament, agency and the sufferer rather than merely balancing a world-total |

⚠️ Plantinga's defence addresses the first burden. It does not by itself explain
the actual quantity of suffering or make the divine reason probable.

## 2. THEODICIES (Western) ✅
- **(a) Free-will defence (Augustine, Plantinga):** God gave humans **free will** (a great good); **moral evil** is the misuse of that freedom, not God's doing. A world with free agents who sometimes choose evil is better than automata. ✅ *Limit:* explains moral, not obviously natural, evil.
- **(b) Augustinian "privation" (2023 PYQ):** evil is **not a positive substance** but a **privation/absence of good** (*privatio boni*); God created all good; evil entered through the **Fall** (misused free will). *"All evil is either sin or the punishment for sin"* — evil is **parasitic**, not created by God. ✅
- **(c) Soul-making theodicy (Irenaeus, revived by John Hick):** the world is a **"vale of soul-making"** — a good God creates humans *immature*, at an "epistemic distance", in a world with real hardship, so they can **freely grow** into virtue and relationship with God. Evil/suffering is the **necessary environment for moral & spiritual development**. ✅
  - ✅ **Attribution discipline:** the phrase **"vale of Soul-making" is John Keats's**, from his journal-letter to George and Georgiana Keats (spring 1819), where he writes that we should call the world "The vale of Soul-making" rather than "a vale of tears". **Hick borrowed it** as the title-idea of *Evil and the God of Love* (1966). Do **not** attribute the phrase to Irenaeus, and do not attribute it to Hick as his own coinage. Irenaeus supplies the *theology* (the image/likeness distinction: humans are made in God's *image* and must grow into his *likeness*); Keats supplies the *phrase*; Hick supplies the modern *theodicy*.
- **(d) Process theodicy (Whitehead, Hartshorne, Griffin, Cobb — 2025 PYQ):** God is **NOT omnipotent** in the classical sense — God works by **persuasion, not coercion**, luring the world toward good within a partly self-creative, evolving reality. God suffers with the world. Evil is real but **God is not culpable** (couldn't unilaterally prevent it). ✅ *Cost:* gives up classical omnipotence. Named sources at §9.5.

## 3. THE INDIAN RESPONSE — Karma ✅
- **Karma-saṃsāra:** suffering is the **fruit of one's own past deeds** across lives; the moral law is **self-operating**. So evil raises **no problem for a creator's goodness** — it is **cosmic justice**, not divine cruelty. ✅
- **Advantage:** explains the *distribution* of suffering (why the apparently innocent suffer — past-life karma) that stumps Western theodicy.
- **In theistic Indian systems (Nyāya, Rāmānuja):** God dispenses karmic fruit *justly* (karmādhyakṣa) — God is a judge, not an arbitrary cause of evil. ✅
- **Critique:** risks blaming victims; infinite regress of karma; a-morality of an impersonal law.

### 7.1 — "'The problem of evil is an offshoot of how God is conceptualised.' Discuss." (2024, 15m)
```

        no God (karma/atheism).
```

### 7.2 — "'All evil is either sin or punishment for sin.' (Augustine) Critically discuss." (2023, 20m)
```
Intro : Augustine's free-will + privation theodicy.
Body  : evil = privatio boni (not created by God); moral evil from misused free will;
        suffering as just punishment for sin (original + actual).
Crit  : natural evil & innocent/animal suffering fit poorly; "privation" is strained;
        contrast Hick's soul-making & Indian karma (better on distribution).
Concl : preserves God's goodness for moral evil, but incomplete for natural/innocent suffering.
```

### 7.3 — "What role do the concepts of evil and profane play to provide a firm foundation to religion?" (2019, 15m)
```
Intro : the question is FUNCTIONAL, not theological — how do negative categories constitute religion?
B1    : Durkheim — every known religion partitions the world into sacred and profane;
        religion = beliefs and practices relative to sacred things, uniting a moral community.
        [Warn explicitly: profane = ordinary, NOT evil.]
B2    : Otto — the holy as numinous (object-side); Eliade — hierophany, sacred space (axis mundi)
        and sacred time (recoverable) constituted by contrast with profane homogeneity.
B3    : The founding sequence — predicament (sin/impurity/dukkha/avidyā/bandha) → boundary
        (pure–impure) → institution (priesthood, initiation, penance) → soteriology (the way out).
Indian: Buddhism's First Noble Truth is the clearest case of evil AS foundation;
        Jain āsrava/bandha; Advaita's avidyā; śuddhi/aśuddhi and pāpa/puṇya codes.
        Rider: India's founding defect is more often IGNORANCE/BONDAGE than moral evil.
Crit  : the binary is an ideal type, not a law; and religion also grows from wonder and gratitude,
        not only from the negative pole → route the sacred/holy question to Religious Experience.
Concl : evil and the profane found religion STRUCTURALLY — they supply the predicament and the
        boundary — but they found religion, not theism.
```

### 7.4 — "Explain the main tenets of Process Theodicy." (2025, 15m)
```
Intro : process theodicy answers evil by REVISING omnipotence, not by justifying permission.
Tenets: (1) creativity, not God, is the ultimate; actual entities are self-creative occasions.
        (2) God is dipolar — primordial nature (envisages possibilities, supplies the INITIAL AIM)
            and consequent nature (receives and preserves what the world becomes).
        (3) Power is intrinsically distributed: to be actual is to have some self-determination.
        (4) Therefore divine power is PERSUASIVE by metaphysical necessity (Griffin) — not by
            self-limitation, which is what separates process from the free-will defence.
        (5) Evil = finite occasions deviating from the divine lure; God is not culpable.
        (6) God is "the fellow-sufferer who understands" (Whitehead) — solidarity, not detachment.
Sources: Whitehead (Process and Reality), Hartshorne (omnipotence incoherent), Griffin
        (God, Power, and Evil), Cobb. Name them separately.
Crit  : a God who cannot guarantee victory over evil may be unworthy of worship; the metaphysics
        is contested; classical theists deny that actuality entails self-determination.
Concl : it purchases God's innocence at the price of God's sovereignty — the trade must be stated.
```

## 9. ADVANCED DOCTRINE DOSSIERS

### 9.1 Logical and evidential problems
- **Doctrine statement.** ✅ The logical problem alleges inconsistency among divine omnipotence, perfect goodness and evil; the evidential problem argues that apparently gratuitous evil lowers the probability of such a God.
- **Argument.** ✅ Logical form adds bridging principles: a good being eliminates every evil it can, and an omnipotent being can eliminate every evil. Evidential form begins from intense suffering for which no justifying good is visible.
- **Presupposition.** ⚠️ The bridging principles admit no morally sufficient reason, or the observed distribution of suffering is evidentially representative.
- **Distinction.** ✅ A defence seeks logical compatibility; a theodicy claims to identify God's actual reasons.
- **Canonical example.** ✅ Rowe's suffering fawn functions as an evidential case, not as a deductive contradiction.
- **Objection → reply.** ⚠️ Sceptical theism denies that inability to see a reason warrants belief that none exists; critics reply that excessive scepticism also undermines ordinary moral reasoning.

### 9.2 Free-will defence
- **Doctrine statement.** ✅ Significant creaturely freedom is a great good whose possibility includes morally wrong choice; God can create free persons without causally determining only good choices.
- **Argument.** ✅ (1) Determined goodness lacks significant freedom; (2) freedom includes alternatives; (3) some misuse is possible; (4) moral evil can therefore coexist with divine goodness. Plantinga's defence needs only logical possibility, not a full history of evil.
- **Presupposition.** ⚠️ Libertarian freedom is coherent and sufficiently valuable.
- **Distinction.** ✅ Defence is strongest for moral evil; natural evil requires an additional account.
- **Canonical example.** ✅ A world of programmed beneficence lacks the moral authorship of a world containing genuinely responsible agents.
- **Objection → reply.** ⚠️ Why not free agents who always freely choose good? The reply invokes possible transworld depravity, though its actuality is not demonstrated.

### 9.3 Augustinian privation
- **Doctrine statement.** ✅ Evil is not a created substance but privation or disorder of a due good; culpable evil originates in defective will.
- **Argument.** ✅ (1) Being insofar as it is being is good; (2) God creates beings, not evil substances; (3) corruption depends parasitically on a good nature; (4) moral fault is disordered willing.
- **Presupposition.** ⚠️ Goodness and being are convertible, and privation can explain experienced evil without trivialising it.
- **Distinction.** ✅ Calling blindness a privation does not call it unreal; it is a real lack in a subject that ought to possess sight.
- **Canonical example.** ✅ Rot is not a rival substance to wood but the loss of the wood's proper integrity.
- **Inherited-guilt control.** ⚠️ A claim that later persons are personally
  guilty for an ancestor's act is morally harder to defend than the claim that
  they inherit a damaged condition or disordered social-natural order. The
  second interpretation reduces the inherited-guilt objection but still owes
  an account of why a good creator permits the condition and its unequal effects.
- **Objection → reply.** ⚠️ Innocent and animal suffering resist punishment-for-sin explanations. Augustinian accounts appeal to fallen order; critics find the historical and moral burden unresolved. Privation explains evil's metaphysical dependence on good, but does not by itself justify why this deprivation was permitted.

### 9.4 Hick's Irenaean soul-making
- **Doctrine statement.** ✅ Humans are created immature and develop into morally responsible likeness through freedom, epistemic distance and a stable world containing danger.
- **Argument.** ✅ (1) Ready-made virtue is not achieved virtue; (2) courage, compassion and trust require real difficulty; (3) a law-governed world must permit harmful consequences; (4) suffering can be the environment of soul-making.
- **Presupposition.** ⚠️ Moral development can justify risk and suffering, and eventual fulfilment prevents lives from being finally wasted.
- **Distinction.** ✅ Soul-making is not retributive punishment; its rationale is developmental.
- **Canonical example.** ✅ Courage requires danger and compassion requires another's need; a "hedonistic paradise" would block these virtues.
- **Objection → reply.** ⚠️ Horrendous and animal suffering seems disproportionate. Hick's universal eschatological fulfilment is the reply, but it depends on contested afterlife commitments.

### 9.5 Process and karmic approaches
- **Doctrine statement.** ✅ Process thought limits unilateral divine control: God persuades finite actualities and shares their suffering. ✅ Karma approaches explain suffering through morally structured causation across lives, with or without a divine dispenser.
- **Named process sources (use these, not a generic "process thinkers").** ✅
  - **Alfred North Whitehead**, *Process and Reality* (1929) — creativity is the ultimate; God is **dipolar**, with a *primordial nature* (the ordered envisagement of possibilities, supplying each occasion its **initial aim**) and a *consequent nature* (God's reception and preservation of what the world becomes). Whitehead's closing description of God as the "fellow-sufferer who understands" is the canonical anchor for divine solidarity. ✅
  - **Charles Hartshorne**, *The Divine Relativity* (1948) and *Omnipotence and Other Theological Mistakes* (1984) — the systematic argument that classical omnipotence is **incoherent**: to have all the power is to leave no power to anything else, and a world of genuinely actual entities entails distributed power. Hartshorne's "surrelativism"/panentheism makes God supremely *related*, not supremely *unaffected*.
  - **David Ray Griffin**, *God, Power, and Evil: A Process Theodicy* (1976) and *Evil Revisited* (1991) — the fullest process treatment of evil; Griffin's key move is that divine persuasive power is a **metaphysical necessity, not a self-limitation**: God *cannot* coerce, because to be actual just is to have some self-determining power. This is what distinguishes process theodicy from the free-will defence, where God *chooses* not to intervene.
  - **John B. Cobb Jr.**, *A Christian Natural Theology* — the theological systematisation.
- **Argument.** ✅ Process: (1) to be actual is to be a self-creative occasion with some power of self-determination; (2) power is therefore intrinsically distributed, not concentrated; (3) so no being can unilaterally determine another's becoming; (4) God's power is the maximal *persuasive* power — the provision of the initial aim; (5) evil arises from finite occasions' deviation from the divine lure; (6) God is therefore not culpable and is the fellow-sufferer. ✅ Karma: deeds generate consequences; rebirth widens the temporal field of justice.
- **Presupposition.** ⚠️ Classical omnipotence is dispensable or incoherent, or trans-life moral continuity is intelligible.
- **Distinction.** ✅ Process theodicy is a **theodicy**, not a defence: it claims to identify God's actual situation. ✅ It differs from the free-will defence in *modality* — Griffin's God cannot coerce, Plantinga's God chooses not to. ✅ Karma is not one uniform doctrine: Mīmāṃsā/Buddhist accounts need no creator, while Nyāya admits an intelligent dispenser.
- **Canonical examples.** ✅ The initial aim as God's persuasive "lure"; Buddhist dependent continuity; Nyāya's *karmādhyakṣa*.
- **Objection → reply.** ⚠️ Process may yield a God unworthy of worship and unable to guarantee any final victory over evil; karma may rationalise victim-blaming. Replies stress divine solidarity, the objective immortality of achieved value in the consequent nature, and non-retrospective compassion — but neither objection disappears.

#### Karma, beginninglessness and moral caution

- ✅ Karma traditions commonly treat the action–consequence series as
  **beginningless**, so they do not need to identify a first wrongful act.
  Beginninglessness blocks one regress question but does not verify any claimed
  past-life cause.
- ✅ Non-theistic Buddhist, Jain and Mīmāṃsā accounts do not require a divine
  distributor; Nyāya and theistic Vedānta assign God a just administrative role
  without making God the arbitrary author of deserts.
- ⚠️ The inference from “this person suffers” to “this person deserves it for a
  specific past act” is unavailable to ordinary observers. Using karma that way
  is epistemically unsupported and can convert explanation into victim-blaming.
- ⚠️ Karma addresses distribution more directly than free-will defence, but
  natural suffering, collective harms and unequal starting conditions still
  press the fairness and verification of the mechanism.
- ✅ Whatever its metaphysics, karma does not cancel present duties of
  compassion, protection and relief; causal explanation is not moral permission
  to withhold aid.

### 9.5A Greater-good, best-world, mystery, eschatology and anti-theodicy

| Response | Precise claim | Main cost |
|---|---|---|
| Greater-good / stable-order | Some goods—freedom, reliable agency, courage or compassion—require risk, regular law and the possibility of loss | May explain the kind of world without explaining the amount or distribution of suffering |
| Leibnizian best possible world | A perfect God chooses the best total compossible order, not a world containing every local good | There may be no unique best world; world-total optimisation can neglect goodness to particular persons |
| Eschatological fulfilment | No finite suffering is final; later fulfilment, restoration or union can prevent a life from being finally wasted | Compensation after suffering is not automatically justification for permitting it |
| Divine mystery | Finite minds may fail to grasp providential reasons | Without an access principle this becomes an unfalsifiable appeal; skeptical theism is the disciplined evidential version |
| Protest / anti-theodicy | Refusing to justify suffering can protect truthfulness, lament and the moral priority of resistance | Protest diagnoses the ethics of explanation but does not by itself settle the logical or evidential argument |

⚠️ **Compensation is not defeat.** A later good can compensate for loss while
leaving the evil externally balanced. Adams' stronger requirement is that a
horrendous evil be defeated within the person's life as a whole. Pastoral
responsibility therefore requires listening, protection and action before
speculative explanation; a theodicy never licenses passivity toward preventable harm.

### 9.6 Evil, the profane and the sacred as religion-forming categories (2019 Q6(b) owner-module)
> ⚠️ **Ownership rule for this folder.** The **evil / profane** pole is owned *here* (2019 Q6(b): "What role do the concepts of evil and profane play to provide a firm foundation to religion?"). The **sacred / holy as the object of religion** pole is owned by [Religious Experience §9.6](./Religious-Experience.md) (2018 Q7(a)). Each file carries the full conceptual pair so that either question can be answered from one file, and each routes the other half by cross-link. Do not treat this as duplication: the *questions* differ — here the category is **religion-founding**, there it is **object-identifying**.

- **Doctrine statement.** ✅ On the sociological-phenomenological analysis, religion is constituted not by belief in God but by a **binary partition of the world into the sacred and the profane**, and by a practical response to **evil/suffering/impurity** as that which must be diagnosed, bounded and overcome.
- **The three canonical theorists.** ✅
  - **Émile Durkheim**, *The Elementary Forms of Religious Life* (1912): "all known religious beliefs... presuppose a classification of all things... into two classes or opposed groups, designated by the terms **profane** and **sacred**." Religion is then defined as a unified system of beliefs and practices *relative to sacred things*, uniting adherents into a single moral community, a **church**. The definition is deliberately **God-free** — which is why it is the standard resource for the "religion without God" clause too.
  - **Rudolf Otto**, *The Idea of the Holy* (1917): the sacred is not a social classification but an *a priori* category of the mind's response — the **numinous**, *mysterium tremendum et fascinans*. Otto's "holy" is the object-side; Durkheim's "sacred" is the social-side; they are not interchangeable.
  - **Mircea Eliade**, *The Sacred and the Profane* (1957): the sacred irrupts into ordinary reality as **hierophany**, generating sacred **space** (a fixed centre, *axis mundi*, against homogeneous profane extension) and sacred **time** (recoverable, repeatable, against irreversible profane duration).
- **Argument — how evil and the profane found religion.** ⚠️ (1) A religion must first identify a **predicament**: something is wrong (sin, impurity, *dukkha*, *avidyā*, karmic bondage, alienation). (2) The predicament is marked by a **boundary** — pure/impure, sacred/profane, saved/lost — which generates ritual, prohibition and moral code. (3) The boundary in turn generates **institutions**: priesthood, initiation, purification, penance, community discipline. (4) The predicament generates a **soteriology** — the ordered path out. (5) Hence evil and the profane are not embarrassments *to* religion but part of its **structural foundation**: without a diagnosis of what is wrong, salvation-language has no work to do.
- **Indian parity (mandatory).** ✅ *Śuddhi/aśuddhi* (purity/impurity) and *śauca* organise ritual practice; *maṅgala/amaṅgala*; *pāpa/puṇya*; the Buddhist **First Noble Truth** makes *dukkha* the explicit foundation of the whole religion — the clearest world case of "evil as religion-founding"; Jain *āsrava*/*bandha* names the predicament and *saṃvara*/*nirjarā* the exit; Mīmāṃsā's ritual purity codes; Advaita's *avidyā* as the founding defect. ⚠️ Note the critical rider: in Indian traditions the founding category is more often **ignorance or bondage** than **moral evil**, which changes the shape of the resulting religion — diagnosis-and-insight rather than sin-and-forgiveness.
- **Presupposition.** ⚠️ That religion can be defined functionally by structure rather than substantively by its object; that the sacred/profane binary is genuinely cross-cultural.
- **Distinction.** ✅ *Evil* (moral/natural harm) ≠ *profane* (the ordinary, the non-consecrated) ≠ *impure* (ritually polluting) ≠ *demonic* (actively anti-sacred). Durkheim's profane is **not** evil — it is merely the everyday; conflating them is the commonest error in this question.
- **Objection → reply.** ⚠️ **Objection:** the binary is a Durkheimian imposition; many traditions grade rather than dichotomise, and Eliade's universal "archaic ontology" is unfalsifiable. **Reply:** treat the pair as an *ideal type* that organises comparison, not a law. ⚠️ **Second objection:** founding religion on evil makes religion parasitic on suffering, so a world without suffering would be a world without religion. **Reply:** worship, wonder and gratitude are independent religious roots; the sacred is not exhausted by the negative pole — which is exactly why the sacred/holy question is treated separately in [Religious Experience](./Religious-Experience.md).
- **Verdict formula.** ⚠️ "Evil and the profane found religion **structurally rather than doctrinally**: they supply the predicament, the boundary and the soteriological task without which religious practice would be unintelligible. But they found *religion*, not *theism* — which is why the same analysis covers Buddhism and Jainism, and why it leaves the theistic problem of evil entirely untouched."

### 9.7 Horrendous evils (Marilyn McCord Adams)
- **Doctrine statement.** ✅ **Marilyn McCord Adams** (*Horrendous Evils and the Goodness of God*, 1999) defines a **horrendous evil** as one whose participation — as perpetrator or victim — gives the participant *prima facie* reason to doubt whether their life could be **a great good to them on the whole**. Her cases involve grave betrayal, coerced participation in severe wrongdoing and devastating violence against intimate relations; graphic detail is unnecessary for the philosophical distinction.
- **Argument.** ✅ (1) Standard theodicies work at the **global** level: they seek a reason why God permits *evil in general*, balancing it off within the world's total value. (2) But horrendous evils threaten the individual's life as a whole — a global balance can be struck while a particular person's life remains, for them, a net disaster. (3) A God who is good *to persons* (not merely a good *producer of worlds*) must therefore **defeat** the horrendous evil **within the frame of the individual's life**, not merely outweigh it globally. (4) Adams argues that only resources internal to a particular religion — divine identification with human suffering, beatific intimacy with God as an incommensurable good — can accomplish that defeat.
- **Presupposition.** ⚠️ That goodness-to-persons is a distinct and non-negotiable requirement; that there are incommensurable goods (Adams: relationship with God) that can defeat rather than merely counterbalance; and — controversially — that the theodicist is entitled to use religion-specific resources rather than only "generic theism".
- **Distinction.** ✅ **Balancing off** (the good outweighs the evil in a sum) vs **defeat** (Chisholm's term: the evil becomes an organic part of a whole whose value it contributes to). ✅ **Global** defeat (world-level) vs **local/personal** defeat (life-level). Adams' whole point is that the second is what matters.
- **Canonical example.** ✅ A person coerced into grave harm against someone they love: no aggregate of unrelated goods automatically makes that life a great good to that person from within.
- **Objection → reply.** ⚠️ **Objection:** the reply is purely eschatological and unverifiable, and it also seems to make the horrendous evil instrumentally necessary. **Reply:** Adams denies instrumentality — the evil need not be *required*; it must be *defeated*. ⚠️ **Second objection:** using Christian-specific resources abandons the neutral philosophical arena. **Reply:** the atheological argument targets a *particular* theism, so a particular theism may answer.
- **Indian parity.** ⚠️ Karma theory answers a *different* question: it explains the **distribution** of horrendous suffering (why this person) but does not by itself show that the sufferer's life is a great good **to them**; Advaita's ultimate-level sublation and Buddhist liberation both address the predicament by dissolving the standpoint from which the life is evaluated — an answer that critics call evasive and defenders call therapeutic. This contrast is the highest-value paragraph available in a 20-mark answer.

### 9.8 Animal suffering and the pre-human problem
- **Doctrine statement.** ✅ Animal pain from predation, disease and pre-human natural processes is a hard form of natural evil because **no free-will defence and no soul-making rationale plainly applies** to a creature that neither chooses nor grows morally.
- **Argument.** ✅ (1) Animal suffering predates human moral agency; (2) it cannot be the misuse of human free will; (3) it cannot straightforwardly be the environment of *moral* growth for the animal sufferer; (4) it cannot simply be called retributive; (5) therefore standard theodicies leave a major remainder. **Rowe's fawn** — severe apparently unobserved suffering after a natural fire — is an evidential case, not a deductive contradiction.
- **Replies, with their costs.** ⚠️
  - **Neo-Cartesian:** animals lack the higher-order awareness that makes pain *matter* morally (Michael Murray canvasses this in *Nature Red in Tooth and Claw*, 2008). *Cost:* the premise is philosophically and empirically contested.
  - **Nomic-regularity:** a stable, law-governed world capable of supporting any life at all entails predation and pain; the alternative is constant miracle. *Cost:* explains the *type*, not the *quantity*.
  - **Soul-making extended:** a world of real danger is the only kind in which courage, compassion and stewardship are possible — including *toward animals*. *Cost:* uses animals instrumentally.
  - **Eschatological:** animal fulfilment or redemption in some final state (a minority theological line). *Cost:* unverifiable, and undeveloped.
  - **Fallen-order:** a cosmic fall distorts nature prior to human history. *Cost:* ❓ historically and scientifically strained.
- **Indian parity.** ✅ Karma-saṃsāra offers a distinctive distribution-account by extending moral causation to **animal births** (*tiryak-yoni*); **Jainism** ascribes living selves graded sense-capacity and grounds non-injury (*ahiṃsā*) in that recognition; **Buddhism** places animals within the same field of suffering (*dukkha*) and extends compassion (*karuṇā*) to sentient beings. ⚠️ The cost is symmetrical to karma's general cost: the explanation is unverifiable and must not be used to infer that present suffering is deserved.
- **Objection → reply.** ⚠️ **Objection:** karma explains distribution but is itself the thing in question; and an animal cannot learn from a fruit it cannot understand. **Reply:** karmic maturation is not pedagogical but causal; the moral response it demands (compassion, non-injury) is unaffected by whether the animal grasps it.
- **Verdict formula.** ⚠️ "Animal suffering is the point at which free-will and soul-making theodicies both run out. Only nomic-regularity survives on the Western side, and it explains the kind of world rather than the amount of pain in it; Indian karma answers the distribution question the West cannot, at the price of an unverifiable trans-life mechanism."

### 9.9 Skeptical theism
- **Doctrine statement.** ✅ **Skeptical theism** does not offer a theodicy. It attacks the *inference* in the evidential argument: from "I can see no justifying reason for this evil" to "there is no justifying reason". The proponents are **Stephen Wykstra**, **William Alston**, **Michael Bergmann** and **Daniel Howard-Snyder**.
- **Argument.** ✅ (1) **Wykstra's CORNEA** (Condition of ReasoNable Epistemic Access, 1984): one may claim "it appears that p is absent" only if it is reasonable to believe that, given one's cognitive situation, p **would likely be discernible** if it were there. (2) Our grasp of the space of possible goods, of possible evils, and of the entailment relations between them is (Bergmann's three theses) **not representative** of the total space. (3) Therefore the *noseeum* inference — "no seeum, so none there" — is unwarranted for goods that would justify God's permission. (4) Alston's parallel: a novice examining a chess grandmaster's move and seeing no point in it has no warrant to conclude there is none.
- **Presupposition.** ⚠️ That the relevant goods would plausibly exceed human cognitive reach, and that the theist may appeal to that gap without special pleading.
- **Distinction.** ✅ Skeptical theism is a **defence**, not a theodicy; it claims that the atheist's evidence fails, not that God's reasons are known. ✅ It targets the **evidential**, not the **logical**, problem. ✅ It is compatible with holding *also* a positive theodicy, though the two sit awkwardly together.
- **Canonical example.** ✅ Wykstra's parent-and-infant analogy: an infant's inability to see why a painful procedure is being performed is no evidence that there is no reason.
- **Objection → reply.** ⚠️ (a) **Moral-scepticism objection (Michael Almeida, Bruce Russell, Erik Wielenberg):** if we cannot judge that an evil is gratuitous, we equally cannot judge that we should *prevent* an evil — perhaps God is permitting it for an unseen good. This threatens ordinary moral reasoning and the duty to rescue. **Reply:** obligation is fixed by what we *know*, not by unknown all-things-considered value; critics find this unstable. (b) **Divine-deception/parity objection:** the same scepticism would undercut confidence in divine promises, revelation and the goodness of God. **Reply:** restrict the scepticism to the value-entailment domain only — but the restriction looks *ad hoc*. (c) **Bracketing objection:** if extended, no empirical evidence could ever count against theism, which makes the claim unfalsifiable.
- **Indian parity.** ⚠️ Structurally analogous moves exist: the Nyāya/Vedāntic appeal that the workings of *adṛṣṭa* are *atīndriya* (beyond the senses) and known only through *śabda*; and the Buddhist treatment of the precise ripening of karma as one of the **acinteyya** (imponderable) matters. The parallel is genuine, and it inherits the same objection — a mechanism placed permanently beyond checking cannot be evidence either for or against.
- **Verdict formula.** ⚠️ "Skeptical theism is the strongest available *defensive* reply to the evidential argument and the most corrosive one: it neutralises the atheist's inference only by conceding that the moral structure of the universe is opaque to us, and that concession costs the theist much of the moral confidence religion is usually taken to supply."

## 11. CRITICISMS AND REPLIES

1. **Excess evil.** ⚠️ Even if some hardship is needed, the scale seems unnecessary. **Reply:** epistemic limits and long-range goods; **rejoinder:** unknown reasons cannot become a blanket licence.
2. **Natural evil.** ⚠️ Free will does not explain earthquakes or disease. **Reply:** stable natural law enables agency; **rejoinder:** stability does not explain every extreme distribution.
3. **Divine responsibility.** ⚠️ Creating foreknown abusers seems culpable. **Reply:** foreknowledge does not cause free action; **rejoinder:** knowingly choosing the whole system remains morally relevant.
4. **Karma and compassion.** ⚠️ Desert explanations can blame sufferers. **Reply:** classical ethical practice can require compassion regardless of past causes; explanatory karma does not authorise social judgement.

## 12. COMMON UPSC TRAPS

1. ⚠️ Do not treat the inconsistent triad as self-contradictory without stating bridging premises.
2. ⚠️ Do not call Plantinga's defence a proof of God's actual reason.
3. ⚠️ Do not say privation makes pain imaginary.
4. ⚠️ Do not reduce Hick to "suffering makes us stronger"; include epistemic distance, freedom and law-governed order.
5. ⚠️ Do not write that process God is simply powerless; divine power is persuasive and relational.
6. ⚠️ Do not present karma as a single theistic doctrine across Indian schools.
7. ⚠️ **"Vale of soul-making" is Keats's phrase** (letter to George and Georgiana Keats, spring 1819), adopted by Hick. Do not attribute it to Irenaeus or present it as Hick's coinage.
8. ⚠️ Do not write "process theodicy (Whitehead)" and stop. Name the layer you are using: Whitehead (dipolar God, initial aim, consequent nature), Hartshorne (omnipotence is incoherent), Griffin (persuasion is metaphysically necessary, not chosen).
9. ⚠️ **Do not equate profane with evil.** Durkheim's profane is the *ordinary*, not the wicked; evil, impurity and the demonic are three further, distinct categories.
10. ⚠️ Do not use "horrendous evil" loosely for "very bad evil". Adams' definition is technical: it gives the participant *prima facie* reason to doubt that their life could be a great good **to them on the whole** — and the required reply is *defeat within the individual life*, not global balancing off.
11. ⚠️ Do not answer animal suffering with the free-will defence. It does not apply, and saying so is itself a mark-earning move.
12. ⚠️ Do not present skeptical theism as a theodicy. It denies the atheist's *inference*; and its standing cost is that the same scepticism threatens ordinary moral judgement.
13. ⚠️ Do not let "God's ways are mysterious" stand in for skeptical theism. State CORNEA or Bergmann's representativeness theses, or do not use the position.

## 15. ANSWER ARCHITECTURE (10 / 15 / 20 MARKS)

### 10 marks
```text
State exact problem → identify God-concept → one response with presupposition →
one objection → verdict on compatibility, not a devotional conclusion.
```

### 15 marks
```text
Logical/evidential frame → doctrine's argument → canonical example →
objection and reply → Indian comparator → assess explanatory cost.
```

### 20 marks
```text
Define evil and inconsistent set → strongest theodicy in full → rival theodicy/Indian view →

```

## 16. DIRECTIVE DECODER

| Directive | Examiner is buying | Structural consequence | Live example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What is the logical problem of evil? Discuss** | The *set* and its **bridging premises**, not a lament | Write the propositions as a numbered set; show why they are not *formally* contradictory without the bridges | 2020 Q6(b) |
| **Discuss the paradox of…** | A paradox stated as a dilemma with both horns | Horn 1 → cost; Horn 2 → cost; then the restriction that dissolves it | 2021 Q5(e) omnipotence |
| **Critically examine [a quoted thesis]** | Fidelity to the quotation's *scope* before evaluation | Ask first: does the claim cover moral evil only, or natural too? | 2022 Q6(c) absolute creator |
| **"Quotation." — Critically discuss** | Reconstruct the author's own system, then test it | Give Augustine's privation metaphysics before objecting | 2023 Q8(a) "All evil is either sin or punishment for sin" |
| **"Direct offshoot of how God is conceptualised." Critically discuss** | Show the *dependence* by varying the God-concept | Run the triad against 4 God-concepts and watch it dissolve | 2024 Q6(c) |
| **Explain the main tenets of X** | Enumerated doctrine, correctly attributed | Number the tenets; name Whitehead/Hartshorne/Griffin separately | 2025 Q6(b) process theodicy |
| **What role do A and B play to provide a firm foundation to…** | A *functional* account, not a definition | Predicament → boundary → institution → soteriology | 2019 Q6(b) evil and profane → §9.6 |

> 🔑 **Rule:** always state *which version* of the problem you are answering (logical / evidential / horrendous / animal / distribution) in the first two lines. Theodicies are version-specific, and a mismatched theodicy scores as an error.

## 17. GRADED VERDICT LADDER

| Band | What the script contains | Typical fault |
|---|---|---|
| **Weak (≈40%)** | "God is good but evil exists"; lists free-will and karma | Triad asserted without bridging premises; no version identified |
| **Average (≈50%)** | Logical/evidential distinction; two theodicies | Defence and theodicy used interchangeably |
| **Good (≈60%)** | Bridging premises stated; one theodicy fully reconstructed; Indian comparator | Natural/animal evil unaddressed |
| **Strong (≈65–70%)** | Version-matched theodicy; objection with reply; distribution question raised; cost of each move stated | Horrendous/skeptical-theist layer absent |
| **Top (≈70%+)** | All the above + defeat-vs-balancing-off, or CORNEA, or the animal-suffering gap + an explicit statement of what the theist has *given up* to survive | — |

**Graded-verdict templates:**
- ⚠️ *Logical problem:* "The set is inconsistent only with the bridging premises; deny either and the contradiction dissolves — but the theist then owes an account of what a good omnipotent being *would* tolerate, which is where the evidential problem restarts."
- ⚠️ *Theodicy stem:* "The theodicy answers the version of the problem it was designed for and leaves ___ untouched; its cost is ___, and a theodicy is not refuted by its cost but is measured by it."
- ⚠️ *Concept-dependence stem:* "The problem is a stress-test of one God-concept, not of religion. Weaken omnipotence (process), weaken personality (Advaita), or remove the creator (Buddhism/Jainism/Mīmāṃsā) and it dissolves — but each dissolution purchases relief with a religiously significant loss."
