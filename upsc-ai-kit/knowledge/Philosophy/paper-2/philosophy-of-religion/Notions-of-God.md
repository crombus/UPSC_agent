## 1. WESTERN NOTIONS ✅
- **Classical theism:** God = a **personal, transcendent Creator**, distinct from the world, with the "omni" attributes: **omnipotent, omniscient, omnibenevolent, eternal, immutable, simple, necessary**. Immanent (present/active in world) *and* transcendent (beyond it). ✅
- **Deism:** God creates the world + natural laws, then **withdraws** — no miracles/revelation (the "cosmic clockmaker"). ✅
- **Pantheism (Spinoza — 2022, 2024 PYQ):** **God = Nature (Deus sive Natura)** — the one infinite substance; Thought & Extension are attributes; things are modes. **Impersonal, immanent, necessary**; no transcendent creator. ✅
- **Panentheism:** the world is **in** God but God exceeds the world (Process theism — God dipolar).
- **Immanent & Transcendent (2023 PYQ):** theism holds **both** — God is *transcendent* (beyond, self-sufficient, creator) yet *immanent* (sustaining, present in creation). ⚠️

## 2. INDIAN NOTIONS ✅
- **Spectrum first (do not flatten it):** ⚠️ *polytheism* (many independent deities) → *henotheism/kathenotheism* (the invoked deity addressed as supreme, one at a time) → *polymorphic/sectarian monotheism* (Vaiṣṇava, Śaiva, Śākta — one ultimate, many forms) → *monism* (one non-dual reality) → *qualified theism* (Rāmānuja) → *dualistic theism* (Madhva). Full module at §8.5.
- **Saguṇa vs Nirguṇa Brahman:** ✅
  - **Nirguṇa** — Brahman **without qualities**, beyond all predication, impersonal, and **beyond the reach of objectifying speech** (*avācya*; *yato vāco nivartante* — "whence words turn back", Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.9; method: *neti neti*, Bṛhadāraṇyaka 2.3.6); the highest reality for **Advaita (Śaṃkara)**.
  - ⚠️ **Terminological correction (examiner-visible):** do **not** gloss nirguṇa Brahman as *anirvacanīya*. In Advaita's technical vocabulary *anirvacanīya* ("indeterminable") is the status of **māyā / avidyā and the world-appearance** — *sad-asad-vilakṣaṇa*, neither absolutely real nor absolutely unreal, hence not classifiable either way. Brahman is not indeterminable in that sense: it is *sat*, the one reality, whose ineffability is the failure of **predication**, not an ontological in-between. Spelling standard for this folder: ***anirvacanīya*** (adjective), ***anirvacanīyatā*** (abstract noun) — see [Religious Language §9.5](./Religious-Language.md).
  - **Saguṇa (Īśvara)** — Brahman **with qualities**, personal God, object of devotion (**Rāmānuja, bhakti** traditions).
  - Advaita: Saguṇa Īśvara is Brahman-as-related-to-māyā, a lower (*vyāvahārika*) standpoint; Nirguṇa is the absolute (*pāramārthika*).
- **Attributes:** **Sat-Cit-Ānanda** (Being-Consciousness-Bliss); **antaryāmin** (inner controller — immanent) yet transcendent.
- **Rāmānuja's God (Viśiṣṭādvaita — 2022 PYQ):** a **personal, saguṇa God (Brahman)** whose **body** is the world (cit + acit); souls (*jīvas*) are real **modes/attributes** of God — related as **aprthak-siddhi** (inseparable). God is both the material & efficient cause (real transformation — pariṇāma). ✅
- **Personalistic vs impersonalistic (2023 PYQ):** the Saguṇa/Nirguṇa distinction *is* the Indian form of this debate. ⚠️

## 3. RELATION OF GOD TO WORLD & MAN ⚠️
| System | God–World relation | God–Man relation |
|---|---|---|
| Classical theism | creator, transcendent+immanent | personal, covenantal; man in God's image |
| Deism | creator then absent | remote, no intervention |
| Spinoza (pantheism) | God = Nature (identity) | man is a mode of God |
| Advaita (Nirguṇa) | world = vivarta (appearance) of Brahman | ātman **IS** Brahman (identity) |
| Rāmānuja (Saguṇa) | world = God's real body (pariṇāma) | jīva = real mode of God (aprthak-siddhi), devotion |

> 🔑 **"Must God have physical manifestation to be ultimate cause?" (2024):** classical theism & Advaita say **NO** (God/Brahman is non-physical, spiritual); pantheism identifies God with nature; Rāmānuja gives God a "body" (the world) but not a *physical form* in the anthropomorphic sense. Argue: ultimacy requires **non-dependence**, which a physical form would compromise. ⚠️

## 8. ADVANCED DOCTRINE DOSSIERS

### 8.1 Classical theism
- **Doctrine statement.** ✅ God is a necessary, personal creator, distinct from yet continuously sustaining the world, traditionally described as omnipotent, omniscient, perfectly good, eternal, immutable and simple.
- **Argument.** ✅ (1) Contingent reality requires a non-contingent ground; (2) ordered dependence requires an intelligent source; (3) the ultimate ground cannot derive its actuality from another; (4) the source is therefore construed as necessary and perfect. ⚠️ This is a cumulative metaphysical profile, not one self-contained proof.
- **Presupposition.** ⚠️ Explanatory dependence cannot terminate in an unexplained contingent item, and maximal perfection is coherent.
- **Distinction.** ✅ Creation is not identical with manufacture: classical creation is dependence of the whole being of creatures upon God, not rearrangement of prior material.
- **Canonical examples.** ✅ Aquinas' primary/secondary causation permits God to sustain natural causes without competing with them; Nyāya similarly treats Īśvara as efficient cause while atoms remain material causes.
- **Objection → reply.** ⚠️ The omni-attributes generate paradoxes and evil. A classical reply restricts omnipotence to what is logically possible and treats goodness as identical with, not external to, divine nature.

### 8.2 Spinoza's God or Nature
- **Doctrine statement.** ✅ God is the one infinite substance, *Deus sive Natura*; finite minds and bodies are modes, not created substances outside God.
- **Argument.** ✅ (1) Substance is conceived through itself; (2) two substances cannot share an attribute; (3) an absolutely infinite substance excludes any rival substance; (4) therefore only God/Nature is substance, expressed through infinitely many attributes.
- **Presupposition.** ⚠️ Conceptual self-sufficiency tracks ontological self-sufficiency, and substance monism follows from the definition of attribute.
- **Distinction.** ✅ Spinozistic pantheism is not deism: God neither withdraws from nature nor chooses to create it; nature follows necessarily from divine essence.
- **Canonical example.** ✅ A human mind is a finite mode under Thought and its body the corresponding mode under Extension.
- **Objection → reply.** ⚠️ The view seems to erase personality, providence and freedom. Spinoza replies by redefining divine freedom as action from the necessity of one's own nature, not arbitrary choice.

### 8.3 Advaita: nirguṇa Brahman and saguṇa Īśvara
- **Doctrine statement.** ✅ Nirguṇa Brahman is non-dual reality beyond limiting predicates; saguṇa Īśvara is Brahman understood through *māyā* at the empirical level.
- **Argument.** ✅ (1) Changing objects are dependent and cannot be ultimate; (2) consciousness is the presupposition of every known object; (3) Upaniṣadic instruction removes superimposed difference; (4) ātman is recognised as Brahman.
- **Presupposition.** ⚠️ Self-revealing consciousness is not reducible to an object, and *śruti* is a valid means for what perception and inference cannot disclose.
- **Distinction.** ✅ Nirguṇa does not mean a bare non-entity; it denies limiting empirical qualities while affirming non-dual reality as *sat-cit-ānanda*.
- **Canonical example.** ✅ Rope–snake: the world is not sheer nothing but dependently experienced through superimposition, as a snake appears on a rope.
- **Objection → reply.** ⚠️ Rāmānuja argues that a quality-less absolute is unintelligible. Advaita replies that negation removes finite predicates rather than cancelling reality.

### 8.4 Viśiṣṭādvaita: God, selves and world
- **Doctrine statement.** ✅ Brahman is a personal, qualified unity whose body is the real plurality of conscious selves (*cit*) and matter (*acit*).
- **Argument.** ✅ (1) Scripture affirms unity and plurality; (2) the body–self relation preserves both; (3) body is controlled by and exists for its indwelling self; (4) the cosmos is therefore inseparably dependent on Brahman without being illusory.
- **Presupposition.** ⚠️ Unity need not be undifferentiated identity; internal differentiation can qualify one substantive whole.
- **Distinction.** ✅ *Apṛthaksiddhi* is inseparability, not numerical identity: the jīva belongs to God yet never becomes the whole of God.
- **Canonical example.** ✅ The soul governs its body while remaining distinct from it; analogously Brahman is the inner ruler of world and selves.
- **Objection → reply.** ⚠️ If the world is God's body, worldly defect appears to infect God. Rāmānuja's reply distinguishes the controller's perfection from the changing condition of the controlled body.

### 8.5 Polytheism, henotheism and the Vedic case (2018 Q5(e) owner-module)
- **Doctrine statement.** ✅ *Polytheism* posits many independent personal deities with divided jurisdiction. *Henotheism* (and its finer variant *kathenotheism*, "one-god-at-a-time") names the pattern in which the deity currently invoked is addressed with the full language of supremacy without the others being denied. ✅ Both terms were introduced into the study of the Veda by **F. Max Müller**, who read the Ṛgvedic hymns to Agni, Indra, Varuṇa and Uṣas as successively supreme.
- **Argument that "Hinduism is polytheistic" is inadequate.** ✅ (1) **Ṛgveda 1.164.46** — *ekaṃ sad viprā bahudhā vadanti*: the same one existent is spoken of under the names Indra, Mitra, Varuṇa, Agni, Garutmān — a doctrine of **plural names for one referent**, not plural ultimates. (2) **Ṛgveda 10.129 (Nāsadīya)** — the cosmogonic hymn resolves the origin into "the One" (*tad ekam*) breathing without breath, and closes by doubting whether even the highest overseer knows; a naive pantheon cannot generate this. (3) The **Upaniṣadic** turn makes Brahman the single reality of which devas are dependent expressions (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 3.9 reduces "three and three thousand" gods to one). (4) **Iṣṭadevatā**: the chosen deity functions as the whole of divinity for that devotee — structurally monotheistic devotion inside a plural pantheon. (5) **Avatāra and Trimūrti** subordinate plurality to one ultimate agency. (6) The living **sectarian theisms** (Vaiṣṇava, Śaiva, Śākta) are each *monotheistic in form*, treating rival deities as subordinate powers.
- **Argument on the other side (do not suppress it).** ✅ The Ṛgvedic pantheon is genuinely plural and function-divided; ritual, temple and village practice addresses many deities with distinct competencies; iconography and vrata-calendars are irreducibly plural.
- **Presupposition.** ⚠️ That "polytheism/monotheism" — categories built from the Abrahamic dispute — can be applied without distortion to a non-credal tradition; the safest answer states this reservation explicitly.
- **Distinction.** ✅ polytheism ≠ henotheism ≠ kathenotheism ≠ monolatry ≠ monism ≠ monotheism ≠ panentheism. Monism denies plurality of *reality*; monotheism denies plurality of *deity*; henotheism denies neither but ranks contextually.
- **Canonical example.** ✅ A Ṛgvedic Agni hymn ascribes to Agni what an adjacent Indra hymn ascribes to Indra — the textbook henotheistic signature.
- **Objection → reply.** ⚠️ Objection: "one being, many names" is a modern apologetic overlay. Reply: the verse is genuinely Ṛgvedic and Nāsadīya independently resists plural ultimacy; but its extension into a **theory of inter-religious pluralism** is a nineteenth–twentieth-century development and must be argued, not assumed — routed to [Religious Pluralism §8/§5](./Religious-Pluralism.md).
- **Verdict formula.** ⚠️ "Hinduism is not adequately described as polytheistic. It is better described as a tradition in which polytheistic *practice*, henotheistic *invocation*, sectarian *monotheism* and Upaniṣadic *monism* coexist at different levels; plurality lies in names, forms and access-points, not in ultimate principles — while conceding that at the level of lived worship the many deities are genuine objects of devotion."

### 8.6 Divine simplicity, aseity and the coherence of the attribute-package
- **Doctrine statement.** ✅ The doctrine of **divine simplicity (DDS)** denies every kind of composition in God — no matter/form, no substance/accident, no genus/difference, and no real distinction between essence and existence; the divine attributes are not distinct properties inhering in God but one reality diversely signified (Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I, qq.3–13; anticipated in Augustine and paralleled in Maimonides). **Aseity** (*a se*) is the closely related claim that God exists from himself, deriving nothing.
- **Argument.** ✅ (1) Whatever is composite depends on its parts and on whatever unites them; (2) the first, underived ground cannot be dependent; (3) therefore the ultimate is non-composite; (4) therefore "wise", "good", "powerful" signify one identical divine reality under different creaturely modes of conception.
- **Presupposition.** ⚠️ Composition entails ontological dependence, and plurality in our *concepts* need not mirror plurality in the *thing*.
- **Distinction.** ✅ Simplicity, aseity, immutability, impassibility, eternity and necessity are **distinct theses** that support one another; conflating them is a common answer-fault. Simplicity is about composition, aseity about derivation, immutability about change, impassibility about being acted upon.
- **Canonical example.** ✅ In creatures, "existence" is received and "wisdom" is a quality possessed; in God, on this view, there is no possessor-plus-possessed — hence Aquinas' formula that in God essence and existence are the same.
- **Objection → reply.** ⚠️ (a) **Modal collapse:** if God is identical with his act of will and God exists necessarily, everything willed is necessary, so creation is not free. *Reply:* the divine act is intrinsically necessary but its *terminus* is contingent; critics answer that this reintroduces a distinction simplicity had denied. (b) **Plantinga's objection** (*Does God Have a Nature?*): if God is identical with his properties, God is a property, and no property is a person or an agent. *Reply:* read the identity analogically or as a truthmaker claim rather than as literal property-identity. (c) **Religious adequacy:** a simple, impassible God appears unable to respond, love or grieve. *Reply:* reinterpret impassibility as unwavering constancy of love rather than emotional inertness — a revision many find too costly.
- **Indian parity (required for a full-marks 15/20).** ✅ Advaita reaches the same problem by a different route: *sat-cit-ānanda* are **svarūpa-lakṣaṇa** — the essential nature-defining marks, not qualities inhering in a substrate — as against **taṭastha-lakṣaṇa**, the accidental/relational definition of Brahman as world-cause (*janmādy asya yataḥ*). This is the Indian functional analogue of divine simplicity. ✅ **Rāmānuja** attacks it head-on: an entity with no determinations whatever is neither knowable, nameable nor worshipable, so Brahman must be *saviśeṣa* (qualified). ✅ **Madhva** takes a third route: his device of **viśeṣa** — a real "distinguisher" within a partless substance — lets Viṣṇu possess genuine attributes without composition. ⚠️ Compressed presentation; expand only the school the question names.

### 8.7 Omnipotence, foreknowledge and free will (2020 Q5(d) owner-module)
- **Doctrine statement.** ✅ Omnipotence is maximal power; omniscience is knowledge of all truths; the classical package asserts both of a God who nevertheless creates genuinely free agents. The examinable problem is whether these are jointly coherent.
- **Argument (the two paradoxes, stated in premises).**
  - ✅ **Paradox of the stone / Mackie's paradox of omnipotence:** (1) An omnipotent being can create any object it chooses; (2) it can therefore create a being (or a rule, or a stone) it cannot subsequently control; (3) either it cannot create such a thing, or it cannot control it; (4) either way there is something it cannot do. Mackie's own framing distinguishes **first-order omnipotence** (power over things) from **second-order omnipotence** (power to determine what powers exist), arguing that both cannot be held unrestrictedly at once.
  - ✅ **Foreknowledge dilemma:** (1) God infallibly believed at t₁ that S will do A at t₂; (2) the past is now fixed (accidental necessity); (3) an infallible belief cannot be false; (4) so at t₂, S cannot refrain from A; (5) so S does not act freely in the libertarian sense.
- **Replies to the omnipotence paradox.** ✅ **Aquinas' standard restriction:** omnipotence extends to everything *absolutely possible*; what implies a contradiction is not a thing at all, so it is no restriction on power that God cannot make it (*ST* I q.25 a.3). ✅ **Descartes' universal possibilism** is the deliberate outlier: even the eternal truths depend on God's will — coherent but at the price of making logic contingent. ⚠️ **"God cannot lie/sin":** perfect-being theology treats moral impeccability as a *perfection* of power, not a deficit — inability to defect is not weakness.
- **Replies to the foreknowledge dilemma (know all four).**
  - ✅ **Boethian/Thomist eternity:** God is timeless; all times are present to the *nunc stans*; therefore there is strictly no *fore*knowledge and premise (2)'s past-fixity does not apply. *Objection:* timeless knowledge still entails the act; and a timeless God's responsiveness is hard to state.
  - ✅ **Ockhamism:** "God believed at t₁ that S will do A" is a **soft fact** about the past — partly about the future — and soft facts are not accidentally necessary. *Objection:* the hard/soft distinction is difficult to specify non-circularly.
  - ✅ **Molinism (Luis de Molina, *Concordia*):** God has **middle knowledge** (*scientia media*) of counterfactuals of creaturely freedom logically prior to the creative decree, and so can foreknow free acts without causing them. *Objection:* the **grounding objection** — what makes such counterfactuals true before any decree or act?
  - ✅ **Open theism:** future free acts are not yet truths; omniscience is knowledge of all *truths*, so no failure follows. *Objection:* this revises the classical attribute the question presupposes.
- **Indian parity.** ✅ **Nyāya:** Īśvara is omniscient and *karmādhyakṣa*, but allots fruits *in accordance with* each self's adṛṣṭa; he is the administering, not the choosing, cause — so human effort (*puruṣakāra*) is preserved against fate (*daiva*). ✅ **Bhagavad Gītā 18.61** places the Lord "in the heart-region of all beings, causing them to revolve as though mounted on a machine (*yantra*)", and yet **18.63** ends the entire teaching with *yathecchasi tathā kuru* — "act as you wish": the text deliberately holds sovereignty and genuine deliberative address together, and is best used as the canonical Indian **crux**, not as a solved proof. ⚠️ **Advaita** dissolves rather than answers the problem: agency belongs to the jīva under *upādhis*; freedom is not an exception inside the causal order but the ending of the ignorance that produced the sense of bondage. ✅ **Rāmānuja** uses divine *anumati* (permission/consent): God sustains the capacity to act and consents to its exercise, so the moral authorship remains the jīva's.
- **Verdict formula.** ⚠️ "The attributes survive the paradoxes only if each is *specified* — omnipotence as power over the absolutely possible, omniscience as knowledge of all truths, freedom as either libertarian or compatibilist. The costs are unequally distributed: eternity and Ockhamism preserve the classical package at the price of contested metaphysics; Molinism preserves it at the price of the grounding objection; open theism preserves freedom by amending omniscience."

### 8.8 Śaiva, Śākta and Mādhva God-concepts — the Indian parity bench
> Required whenever the question says "**any one of the religions in India**" (2019 Q8(c)) or asks for the Hindu God-concept beyond Advaita/Viśiṣṭādvaita. Pick **one** and complete it; do not sample all four.

- **Śaiva Siddhānta (dualistic-theistic Śaivism).** ✅ Triad **pati–paśu–pāśa**: Śiva (the Lord), the bound soul, and the three bonds — *āṇava* (primal finitude), *karma*, *māyā*. Śiva is the **efficient** cause, māyā the material cause; his activity is the **pañca-kṛtya** (five acts): creation, maintenance, dissolution, concealment (*tirodhāna*) and grace (*anugraha*). Liberation is *śiva-sāyujya*: the soul becomes **like** Śiva (*śiva-tulya*), sharing his nature but never becoming the Lord. *God–man relation:* master–servant transformed by grace (*śakti-nipāta*).
- **Kashmir (Pratyabhijñā/Trika) Śaivism — Utpaladeva, Abhinavagupta.** ✅ Non-dual but **realist about the world**: Śiva is *prakāśa* (self-luminous consciousness) inseparably united with *vimarśa* (self-reflective awareness = Śakti). The universe is Śiva's free self-manifestation (*ābhāsa*) through *svātantrya-śakti* — an act of sovereign freedom, not an illusory superimposition. ⚠️ **This is the sharpest available contrast with Advaita:** both are non-dual, but Advaita's world is *vivarta* (apparent), Kashmir Śaivism's world is a real self-expression. Liberation is *pratyabhijñā* — **recognition** of the identity one never actually lost.
- **Śākta.** ✅ The ultimate is **Devī / Mahādevī**; the *Devī-Māhātmya* (in the Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa) presents the Goddess as the supreme, self-sufficient reality from whom the gods derive power. The philosophical point that earns marks: the divine **power (Śakti) is not an attribute added to God but the divine itself in its dynamic aspect** — which is why Śiva without Śakti is described as inert. Contrast: Advaita's māyā is dependent and finally sublatable; Sāṃkhya's prakṛti is unconscious; Śākta Śakti is conscious, ultimate and identical with the Goddess. ⚠️ Tantric ritual context is broad; keep the answer at the metaphysical level.
- **Madhva's Dvaita.** ✅ One organising axiom: **svatantra–paratantra** — Viṣṇu alone is independent, everything else is dependent. ✅ **Pañca-bheda**, five eternally real differences: God–soul, God–matter, soul–soul, soul–matter, matter–matter. ✅ **Bimba–pratibimba**: the soul is the *reflection* of God — dependence without identity. ✅ **Taratamya**: an intrinsic gradation among souls, with the notorious threefold classification (*mukti-yogya*, *nitya-saṃsārin*, *tamo-yogya*) — the one Vedāntic system asserting eternal damnation for a class of souls. Liberation is *ānanda* proportioned to the soul's intrinsic capacity, expressed as service (*dāsya*); the soul never attains equality with God.
- **Parity grid.**

| System | Ultimate | World's status | Soul in liberation | Distinctive device |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Advaita | ✅ Nirguṇa Brahman | *Vivarta*, sublatable | Individuality sublated | *Adhyāsa*, two levels of truth |
| Viśiṣṭādvaita | ✅ Saguṇa Brahman (Viṣṇu) | Real body of God | Eternal jīva in communion | *Apṛthaksiddhi*, śarīra–śarīrī |
| Śaiva Siddhānta | ✅ Śiva as pati | Real, from māyā | *Śiva-tulya*, never Śiva | *Pañca-kṛtya*, *āṇava-mala* |
| Kashmir Śaivism | ✅ Śiva = prakāśa+vimarśa | Real self-manifestation | Recognition of identity | *Ābhāsa*, *svātantrya* |
| Śākta | ✅ Devī/Śakti | Real emanation of Śakti | Union with the Goddess | Śakti as the dynamic divine |
| Dvaita | ✅ Viṣṇu, *svatantra* | Real and eternally distinct | Graded bliss, service | *Pañca-bheda*, *bimba-pratibimba* |

- **Objection → reply.** ⚠️ Objection: these are sectarian theologies, not philosophy. Reply: each states an explicit **causal doctrine, a bondage-analysis and a liberation-criterion**, and each can be assessed for coherence — which is exactly what the examiner's "critically examine" requires.

## 11. COMMON UPSC TRAPS

1. ⚠️ Do not equate **pantheism** with **panentheism**: identity differs from inclusion-with-excess.
2. ⚠️ Do not call Advaita's world absolute non-existence; it is empirically available but sublatable.
3. ⚠️ Do not write that Rāmānuja identifies jīva and Brahman; his relation is difference-with-inseparability.
4. ⚠️ Do not treat divine transcendence as spatial distance or immanence as physical embodiment.
5. ⚠️ Do not list attributes without testing their mutual coherence.
6. ⚠️ A question on "physical manifestation" requires distinguishing incarnation, embodiment, material causation and metaphysical dependence.
7. ⚠️ Do not gloss **nirguṇa Brahman as *anirvacanīya***. *Anirvacanīya* is the technical status of **māyā/avidyā and the world-appearance** (*sad-asad-vilakṣaṇa*); Brahman's ineffability is the failure of objectifying **predication** (*avācya*, *neti neti*). Spell it ***anirvacanīya*** throughout.
8. ⚠️ Do not answer "Is Hinduism polytheistic?" with a yes/no. The marks lie in separating polytheism, henotheism/kathenotheism, monolatry, sectarian monotheism and Upaniṣadic monism, and in noting that the category itself is imported.
9. ⚠️ Do not treat divine simplicity as a stylistic remark about God being "uncomplicated"; it is a denial of composition with hard consequences (modal collapse, property-identity).
10. ⚠️ Do not "solve" the foreknowledge problem by asserting that God is outside time and stopping there; state which premise — past-fixity, infallibility, or the entailment — the eternity move actually targets.
11. ⚠️ Do not merge Śaiva Siddhānta (dualistic, *pati–paśu–pāśa*) with Kashmir Śaivism (non-dual, *ābhāsa*); and do not describe Madhva's *bimba–pratibimba* as identity — it is dependence.

## 14. ANSWER ARCHITECTURE (10 / 15 / 20 MARKS)

### 10 marks
```text
Define the contrast → give one Western and one Indian formulation → one discriminating
distinction → one objection → verdict answering the exact term in the question.
```

### 15 marks
```text
Frame the attribute/relation problem → reconstruct the doctrine and presupposition →
use the canonical example → contrast the nearest rival → objection and reply → graded verdict.
```

### 20 marks
```text
Classify the God-concept → present full metaphysical argument → explain God–world and
God–human relations → Indian–Western comparison → two objections with replies →
assess what notion of ultimacy survives.
```

## 15. DIRECTIVE DECODER (what the verb obliges you to produce)

| Directive (as printed in Paper II) | Examiner is buying | Structural consequence | Live example from this clause |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Elucidate** | Clarification of a *distinction*, not narration | Two-column contrast + one reconciling device | 2023 Q5(a) personalistic vs impersonalistic → §2 + Advaita's two levels |
| **Discuss** | Balanced exposition **plus** a stated position | Thesis → both sides → verdict | 2019 Q8(c) man–God relation → pick ONE school (§8.8) and finish it |
| **Critically examine** | Reconstruction **and** evaluation of validity | Doctrine → presupposition → objection → reply → graded verdict | 2022 Q8(c) Brahman in Advaita, room for theism? |
| **Is there any compatibility…?** | A yes/no thesis defended by naming the disputed premise | State the argument in numbered premises, deny one, pay the cost | 2020 Q5(d) free will vs omnipotence → §8.7 |
| **Do you agree… give reasons and justifications** | An explicit first-person verdict with reasons ranked | Position in the intro, reasons in descending strength | 2024 Q8(b) physical manifestation → §3 🔑 |
| **Write an essay on** | Coverage with architecture, not exhaustiveness | Classify → expound → contrast → assess | 2022 Q5(a) Spinoza's God |
| **How does X embedded in…? Critically discuss** | Show the *systemic* dependence, then test it | Locate the concept inside the system's machinery first | 2024 Q6(b) Spinoza's God in substance-metaphysics |
| **Give reasons for your answer** | A named criterion, then application | Announce the criterion before using it | 2018 Q5(e) polytheism → criterion: plurality of *ultimates* vs of *names/forms* |

> 🔑 **Rule:** the verdict sentence must reuse the question's own operative term ("compatible", "ultimate cause", "personalistic", "poly-theistic"). Examiners mark for the answered *term*, not for the topic.

## 16. GRADED VERDICT LADDER (self-scoring before you leave the page)

| Band | What the script contains | Typical fault |
|---|---|---|
| **Weak (≈40%)** | Lists attributes and schools; narrates Advaita and Rāmānuja | No premises, no objection, no verdict |
| **Average (≈50%)** | One tradition done properly; correct terminology | Indian **or** Western only; verdict is a summary, not a judgement |
| **Good (≈60%)** | Both traditions; one reconstructed argument; one objection with reply | Objection not answered; parity is decorative |
| **Strong (≈65–70%)** | Numbered reconstruction + presupposition named + objection/reply + a **graded** verdict that concedes a cost | Occasionally over-claims a quotation |
| **Top (≈70%+)** | All the above + a *discriminating distinction* the average script misses (henotheism vs monism; soft vs hard fact; svarūpa- vs taṭastha-lakṣaṇa; *apṛthaksiddhi* ≠ identity) + explicit statement of what would refute the position | — |

**Graded-verdict templates (adapt, do not copy blindly):**
- ⚠️ *Compatibility question:* "The two are compatible only on reading X of omnipotence and reading Y of freedom; on the libertarian-plus-temporal reading they are not, and the theist must pay for the reconciliation with either timeless eternity, soft facts, middle knowledge or an amended omniscience."
- ⚠️ *Classification question:* "Neither label fits without remainder; the tradition is best described as ___, provided we distinguish plurality of ultimates from plurality of names and forms."
- ⚠️ *Attribute question:* "The attribute is coherent once specified, but its specification narrows what the religious believer originally wanted to assert — that narrowing is the real cost."
