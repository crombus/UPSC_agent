## 7. School-wise Sanskrit/Pali-to-English map

> This section is the rapid-revision list. The **English keyword** is the short expression
> usable in an answer; the **precise meaning** prevents misleading one-word translations.

### 7.1 Common Indian-philosophy vocabulary

| Sanskrit / Pali term | English keyword | Precise exam meaning | Main use |
|---|---|---|---|
| darśana | philosophical viewpoint | A disciplined way of seeing or investigating reality; conventionally used for an Indian philosophical system | All schools |
| āstika | Veda-affirming | A school that accepts Vedic authority; it does **not** necessarily mean theistic | Nyāya, Vaiśeṣika, Sāṃkhya, Yoga, Mīmāṃsā, Vedānta |
| nāstika | Veda-denying | A school that does not accept Vedic authority; it does **not** simply mean atheist | Cārvāka, Jainism, Buddhism |
| śruti | revelation / “that which is heard” | The Vedic corpus regarded as authorless revelation in orthodox traditions | Mīmāṃsā, Vedānta |
| smṛti | remembered tradition | Authoritative but derivative remembered texts and traditions; in Yoga, *smṛti* also means memory | Orthodox traditions / Yoga |
| puruṣārtha | human end | A recognised aim of life: dharma, artha, kāma and mokṣa | General |
| dharma | duty / sustaining norm | Moral, ritual, social or cosmic norm; its exact meaning changes by school | General |
| artha | material welfare | Wealth, means, interest or practical success | General / Cārvāka |
| kāma | desire / enjoyment | Sensuous or aesthetic enjoyment as a human aim | General / Cārvāka |
| karma | action and moral efficacy | Action, and often the causal moral consequence generated by intentional action | General |
| saṃsāra | cycle of rebirth | Conditioned existence marked by repeated birth, death and suffering/bondage | Most Indian systems |
| mokṣa | liberation | Release from bondage or rebirth; its positive content differs across schools | Most Indian systems |
| duḥkha | suffering / unsatisfactoriness | Pain, frustration or conditioned dissatisfaction that liberation overcomes | Buddhism, Sāṃkhya-Yoga, Nyāya |

### 7.2 Cārvāka

| Sanskrit term | English keyword | Precise exam meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Cārvāka / Lokāyata | materialism | The materialist tradition associated with perception-centred epistemology and rejection of transcendent entities |
| pratyakṣaika-pramāṇavāda | perception-only epistemology | The strict claim that perception alone is an independent means of valid knowledge |
| bhūtacaitanyavāda | emergent consciousness | Consciousness arises from a particular organisation of material elements rather than from an immaterial self |
| dehātmavāda | body-self identity | The living body, not a separable eternal soul, is the self/person |
| svabhāvavāda | naturalism | Events and regularities are explained through the observable natures of things rather than unseen karma or divine purpose |
| paraloka | afterlife | A post-mortem world rejected because it is not perceptually established |
| adṛṣṭa | unseen force | An imperceptible causal or moral potency rejected by Cārvāka |
