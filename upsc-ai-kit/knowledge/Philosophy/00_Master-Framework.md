# 00 · MASTER FRAMEWORK — Philosophy Optional

> **Purpose.** This file is the *constitution* of the Philosophy knowledge base. It fixes (a) the exact syllabus text that controls every other file, (b) the file-ownership matrix, (c) the house style contract every note must obey, and (d) the evidence and honesty rules.
> **Controlling authority:** the UPSC Civil Services (Main) Philosophy syllabus copied from the official UPSC website and preserved verbatim in [`OFFICIAL-UPSC-SYLLABUS-VERBATIM.md`](OFFICIAL-UPSC-SYLLABUS-VERBATIM.md). It is reproduced in structured form in [`README.md`](README.md); the original planning prompt remains archived in [`_PROMPT-VERBATIM.md`](_PROMPT-VERBATIM.md). **Coaching expansions are not authority.** If a topic is not traceable to a syllabus clause, it does not get a section of its own.

---

## 1. THE TWO GOVERNING PRINCIPLES

**P1 — Clause supremacy.** Every heading in every content file must be traceable to a named syllabus term. Where a clause names sub-terms (e.g. Yoga: *Citta; Cittavṛtti; Kleśas; Samādhi; Kaivalya*), **each sub-term gets its own explicitly labelled treatment.** No clause term may be silently folded into another.

**P2 — Examinable depth.** The UPSC Philosophy paper does not reward recall of school-summaries; it rewards (i) precise doctrinal statement, (ii) the *argument structure* behind a doctrine, (iii) the presuppositions the doctrine needs, (iv) the standard objection, (v) the standard reply, and (vi) a defensible evaluative position. Every core file must deliver all six for its main doctrines.

**P3 — Core sufficiency.** The forty syllabus-owner files must, by themselves, support a
serious attempt at every syllabus term and every available PYQ. Nothing required for a
10-, 15- or 20-mark answer may be displaced into `_advanced`. Advanced material is optional
enrichment only; if its omission could affect paper attemptability, it is misclassified and
must be promoted to Core.

## 4. HOUSE STYLE CONTRACT (binding on every content file)

### 4.1 File skeleton
```
# TITLE — Syllabus Item N (Paper X · Section Y)

> **Syllabus (verbatim):** <the clause terms exactly as printed>
> **Evidence key:** ✅ canonical doctrine · ⚠️ analytical synthesis (mine, for exam use) · ❓ contested/uncertain
> **Placement:** where this sits in the paper and why it matters.

## 0. ONE-SCREEN MAP
## 1..n  <one section per syllabus term, in syllabus order>
## n+1  INTER-THINKER / INTER-SCHOOL DEBATES
## n+2  CRITICISMS AND REPLIES
## n+3  COMMON UPSC TRAPS
## n+4  KEYWORD & STATEMENT BANK
## n+5  PYQ ROUTING (state the exact years actually routed)
## n+6  ANSWER ARCHITECTURE (10 / 15 / 20 marks)
## n+7  LINK-OUTS
## SOURCES
```

### 4.2 Evidence discipline — non-negotiable
1. **Never fabricate** a quotation, a page number, a date, a Sanskrit term, a doctrine name, a PYQ number, or an attribution.
2. If the exact wording of a quotation is not certain, **paraphrase and say so** ("in substance", "the standard formulation runs…"). Do not invent quotation marks around an unverified sentence.
3. Mark every claim: **✅** = doctrine as standardly presented in the core literature; **⚠️** = my analytical framing for exam use; **❓** = genuinely disputed or where sources differ.
   The `❓` marker never substitutes for an unreadable Sanskrit character. Use standard IAST,
   and consult the [IAST–Devanāgarī glossary](_revision/Sanskrit-and-Pali-Terms.md).
4. **No verification theatre.** Retrieval from a book or paper does not by itself authenticate every transcription or interpretation. The `SOURCES` section names works a reader should consult — nothing more.
5. **No tooling leakage.** Never mention retrieval systems, indexes, extraction pipelines, PDF handling, or the mechanics of how the file was produced.
6. Where a PYQ's printed wording is itself odd or garbled in the available scan, quote it as printed and flag the discrepancy — do not silently "correct" UPSC.

### 4.3 Depth requirements per doctrine
Every major doctrine gets, at minimum:
- **Statement** — one crisp sentence a examiner would accept as the definition.
- **Argument** — the steps by which the philosopher gets there (numbered where possible).
- **Presupposition** — what must already be granted for the argument to work.
- **Distinction** — the term it is most often confused with, and the discriminating test.
- **Example** — canonical to the tradition (rope–snake, wax, bundle, pineal gland, seed–tree, mustard-seed atoms), Indian wherever the tradition is Indian.
- **Objection → Reply** — at least one live objection with the school's own answer.

### 4.4 Formatting
- Tables for any 3-way or 4-way comparison; never bury a comparison in prose.
- Fenced code blocks (` ``` `) for maps, ladders and answer skeletons only.
- Diacritics used consistently for Sanskrit (ā ī ū ṛ ṃ ḥ ś ṣ ṭ ḍ ṇ ñ). Give the plain-ASCII spelling once in parentheses when a term first appears in a file, so a search for "Pratityasamutpada" also succeeds.
- Bold the term being defined; italics for foreign terms and titles.
- Internal cross-references must be **real relative Markdown links** that resolve from the file they sit in — not bare backticked paths.

## 6. ANSWER ARCHITECTURE STANDARDS

### 10-mark (~150 words, ~7 minutes)
```
1 line : definition/thesis, using the technical term the examiner used.
3–4 lines : the doctrine's own argument or its structural parts.
1–2 lines : the standard objection OR the discriminating contrast.
1 line : evaluative close that answers the directive word.
```
No introduction paragraph. No headings. Begin with the concept.

### 15-mark (~250 words)
```
Frame  : the problem the doctrine is a solution to (2 lines).
Body A : exposition — statement, argument, presupposition.
Body B : the contrast or the critique demanded by the question.
Assess : take a position and defend it in one reason.
Close  : one sentence linking back to the directive word.
```

### 20-mark (~350 words, usually the compulsory anchor of a question)
```
Frame   : locate the issue in the tradition; state what turns on it.
Body A  : full exposition with the internal steps.
Body B  : the rival position, stated at its strongest.
Body C  : objections and the school's replies.
Assess  : a graded verdict — what survives, what does not, and why.
Close   : one sentence of philosophical (not rhetorical) closure.
```

**Directive-word discipline.** *Elucidate* = make clear, expand the given statement. *Examine* = test the claim against evidence/objection. *Critically examine/discuss* = examine **plus** deliver a verdict. *Evaluate* = weigh worth, must end in a judgement. *Comment* = brief pointed assessment. *Discuss* = present the debate in the round. *Compare / Distinguish* = must be organised by **axes**, never by consecutive block-summaries. *Justify your answer* = the marks sit in the reasons, not in the position.

## 7. HONESTY LEDGER (source limitations, stated once, here)

- The local question-paper set now covers **2018–2025 continuously for both Paper I and
  Paper II**.
- The 2018 (`EGT-P-PHLY`) and 2020 (`URC-U-PHLY`) Paper I sources were image-only; their
  OCR-repaired searchable layers were checked against page images before bank insertion.
- Machine-readable text is imperfect in some scans. English question text was rechecked against
  the held pages; visible printed anomalies are retained and marked **📝 printed wording**, rather than silently
  regularised.
- Duplicate files exist for 2022 and 2023 (an official release and a separate scan of the same paper). These are **the same papers**; questions are counted once. The official releases are treated as the reference text.
- Every question-part has **one primary owner file**. Cross-links explain comparisons but do not
  create a second owner. Frequency statements count primary-owned parts over the stated corpus;
  they are not predictions and carry no probability claim.

## 8. HOW TO USE THIS BASE

1. **First pass (concept build).** Owner file → sections 1..n only. Ignore PYQ and answer-architecture sections.
2. **Second pass (exam build).** Owner file → traps, keyword bank, PYQ routing, answer architecture. Then the relevant `_themes` file.
3. **Third pass (revision).** [`_revision/Master-Sheet-Paper-1.md`](_revision/Master-Sheet-Paper-1.md) / [`_revision/Master-Sheet-Paper-2.md`](_revision/Master-Sheet-Paper-2.md) + [`_revision/Comparison-Matrices.md`](_revision/Comparison-Matrices.md), then [`_playcards/Playcards-All-Decks.md`](_playcards/Playcards-All-Decks.md) for recall testing.
4. **Answer practice.** Pick a PYQ from the year-wise banks; write cold; compare against the owner file's answer architecture and [`_application/`](_application).
5. **Optional depth.** Only after Core can answer the PYQs, use
   [`_advanced/Depth-Map.md`](_advanced/Depth-Map.md). Never repair a Core weakness by expecting
   the candidate to study Advanced.
