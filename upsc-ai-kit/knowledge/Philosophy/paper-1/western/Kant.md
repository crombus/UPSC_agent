## 1. THE CENTRAL PROBLEM — POSSIBILITY OF SYNTHETIC A PRIORI JUDGMENTS ✅

### 1.1 The Two Distinctions (Cross-Classification)

| | **A priori** (independent of experience; necessary, universal) | **A posteriori** (dependent on experience; contingent, particular) |
|---|---|---|
| **Analytic** (predicate contained in subject; denial is self-contradictory) | "All bodies are extended." — trivially true, adds no new information | (empty — does not exist for Kant) |
| **Synthetic** (predicate adds new information to subject; denial is not self-contradictory) | **⭐ THE KEY CLASS:** "7 + 5 = 12"; "Every event has a cause"; "The straight line between two points is the shortest" | "This table is brown." — informative but contingent |

### 1.2 Why Synthetic A Priori is THE Problem ✅

- **Rationalists** claimed a priori knowledge (necessary truths about God, soul, world) — but could not explain how such knowledge could be *informative* (synthetic), not merely definitional.
- **Empiricists** (Hume) could explain synthetic (informative) knowledge — through experience — but could not explain how it could be *necessary* (a priori). Hume showed that *experience alone* can never yield necessity (the causation argument).
- **Kant's question:** if synthetic a priori knowledge *exists* (and it does — mathematics and the basic principles of physics are both informative and necessary), **how is it possible?** ✅

### 1.3 The Answer in Outline (the Copernican Revolution) ✅

- The mind does not passively mirror reality; it actively **structures** experience by imposing its own a priori forms (space, time, categories) on the raw data of sensation.
- Because *we* impose these forms, they are necessarily valid for all objects of *our* experience — hence synthetic a priori truths are possible and legitimate, but **only within the bounds of possible experience (phenomena)**. ✅
- Beyond experience (noumena — things-in-themselves), these forms do not apply; metaphysical claims about God, soul, and the world-as-a-whole exceed the legitimate scope of knowledge.

### 1.4 "Precepts without concepts are blind, and concepts without precepts are empty" — the UPSC misquotation ⚠️

> PYQ 2023 Q1(a) prints: *"Precepts without concepts are blind and concepts without precepts are empty."*

**The accurate Kantian formulation** is: *"Thoughts without content [Inhalt] are empty; intuitions [Anschauungen] without concepts are blind"* (*CPR* A51/B75). ✅

- **Meaning:** knowledge requires *both* sensory intuitions (content from experience) and conceptual organisation (form from the understanding). Neither alone suffices. This IS Kant's synthesis of rationalism and empiricism:
  - Rationalism (concepts without intuitions) → "empty" metaphysics.
  - Empiricism (intuitions without concepts) → "blind" unstructured sensation.
  - Kant: knowledge = intuitions + concepts = experience. ✅

> **Note for exam:** Quote the paper's printed text exactly, then give the correct Kantian original, flagging the substitution of "precepts" for "intuitions." Do not silently correct UPSC.

## 2. SPACE AND TIME — Transcendental Aesthetic ✅

### 2.1 The Doctrine

- **Space** = the **a priori form of outer sense** (the form under which we intuit external objects). ✅
- **Time** = the **a priori form of inner sense** (and thereby of all sense whatsoever — all representations, even inner ones, are in time). ✅
- They are **not**:
  - (a) things existing independently of the mind (Newton's absolute space/time — rejected);
  - (b) properties of things-in-themselves (Leibniz's relational view — rejected for the wrong reasons in Kant's view);
  - (c) empirical concepts abstracted from experience (since we need space/time *already in place* to have any experience at all). ✅
- They are: **pure forms of sensible intuition** — the subjective conditions under which any sensory content can be given to us. ✅

### 2.2 Metaphysical Exposition of Space (the arguments that space is a priori and intuitive)

| Argument | Content |
|---|---|
| **1. Space is not derived from experience** | To perceive things as "outside me" or "beside each other," I must already represent space; so space is presupposed by outer experience, not abstracted from it. ✅ |
| **2. Space is necessary** | We can represent space without objects in it, but cannot represent objects without space. Space is a necessary a priori condition of outer experience. ✅ |
| **3. Space is not a discursive concept but a pure intuition** | There is only ONE space (the various "spaces" are limitations within it); a concept applies to many instances, but space is a singular representation. ✅ |
| **4. Space is given as infinite** | An infinite space is *given* (not constructed from finite parts); a concept can contain infinite instances *under* it but not *in* it. ✅ |

(Similar arguments apply, *mutatis mutandis*, to Time.)

### 2.3 Transcendental Exposition of Space and Time (PYQ 2022 Q1e — direct demand) ✅

- A **transcendental exposition** explains how a given a priori representation makes synthetic a priori knowledge possible. ✅
- **Space:** the a priori intuition of space explains how **geometry** (synthetic a priori) is possible. Geometrical truths (e.g. "the angles of a triangle sum to 180°") are necessarily true of all objects of experience because we *construct* spatial figures in pure intuition. ✅
- **Time:** the a priori intuition of time explains how **arithmetic** and the pure science of motion (mechanics) are possible — succession, duration, simultaneity are forms we impose on phenomena. ✅

> PYQ 2019 Q4(a) 20m: "How does Kant argue for the transcendence [= the a priori character] of Space and Time?" — the metaphysical and transcendental expositions above ARE the answer.

### 2.4 Consequence: Transcendental Ideality / Empirical Reality ✅

- **Transcendentally ideal:** space and time are not features of things-in-themselves; they belong to our subjective cognitive constitution.
- **Empirically real:** within experience, space and time are objectively valid — all phenomena must conform to them.
- This is **transcendental idealism** — NOT Berkeley's subjective idealism (Kant explicitly rejects the identification in the *Refutation of Idealism*). ✅

## 3. CATEGORIES — Transcendental Analytic ✅

### 3.1 The Table of Categories

Kant derives the twelve categories from the twelve logical forms of judgment:

| Group | Categories |
|---|---|
| **Quantity** | Unity, Plurality, Totality |
| **Quality** | Reality, Negation, Limitation |
| **Relation** | Inherence & Subsistence (substance/accident), Causality & Dependence (cause/effect), Community (reciprocity) |
| **Modality** | Possibility/Impossibility, Existence/Non-existence, Necessity/Contingency |

> The star category for the syllabus is **Causality** (under Relation) — it answers Hume. ✅

### 3.2 The Transcendental Deduction ✅

**Problem:** how can a priori concepts (which originate in the mind) be legitimately applied to objects of experience (which are given by the senses)?

**Kant's answer (the "Transcendental Deduction of the Categories"):**
1. All experience requires a **unified consciousness** — a single "I think" that accompanies all my representations (the **transcendental unity of apperception**). ✅
2. This unity is possible only if the manifold of intuition is **synthesised** (combined) according to rules.
3. These rules ARE the categories — they are the conditions under which a unified self-consciousness (and therefore *any experience whatsoever*) is possible.
4. ∴ The categories are necessarily valid for all objects of experience — not because they correspond to things-in-themselves, but because *experience cannot occur without them*. ✅

### 3.3 Transcendental Unity of Apperception (PYQ 2022 Q1e) ✅

- The **"I think"** (*Ich denke*) must be able to accompany all my representations — otherwise something would be represented in me that could not be thought, which is impossible (or at least, would not be *my* representation). ✅
- This is not an empirical, introspectable self (not Hume's bundle); it is a **logical/formal condition** — the unity that makes *any* experience mine. It is *transcendental* (a condition of possibility), not *transcendent* (a knowable substance). ✅
- **Against Hume:** Hume found no impression of a self because he looked for an *empirical* self; Kant's "I think" is not an impression but the *form of all thinking* — it cannot appear IN experience because it is the condition OF experience. ⚠️
- **Against rational psychology:** we cannot infer that this "I think" is a *substance* or *soul* (that would be the Paralogism — §4.2). The transcendental ego is a *function*, not a thing.

### 3.4 Causality as an A Priori Category — Kant's Response to Hume ✅

| Hume's position | Kant's response |
|---|---|
| Causation = constant conjunction + custom | Causation = an a priori *category* the mind necessarily applies to organise phenomena |
| No impression of necessary connection → causation is subjective (psychological) | "Every event has a cause" is *synthetic a priori* — necessarily true of all possible experience |
| Induction cannot be justified | The causal principle is not *inductive* (from repeated experience) but *constitutive* (a condition of experience itself) |
| Science rests on mere habit | Science is objectively valid — its principles are the mind's own contribution, legislating to nature |

- **But:** causality applies **only to phenomena** — applying it *beyond* experience (e.g. to infer God as "first cause") is illegitimate. This limits Hume's scepticism and rationalist dogmatism equally. ✅

### 3.5 Phenomena and Noumena ✅

| Phenomena (Erscheinungen) | Noumena (Dinge an sich) |
|---|---|
| Things as they *appear* to us (structured by space, time, categories) | Things as they are *in themselves* (apart from our cognitive forms) |
| Objects of possible experience | NOT objects of knowledge |
| Knowable (science applies here) | Thinkable (we can form the concept) but not knowable |
| Realm of nature, determinism | Realm of freedom, morality (practical postulates) |

- **Noumenon in the negative sense:** the concept of a thing insofar as it is *not* an object of sensible intuition — a *limiting concept* that marks the boundary of knowledge. ✅
- **Hegel's challenge** (PYQ 2025 Q1e): Hegel objects that to *posit* the noumenon is already to *think* beyond the limit Kant draws — the very assertion "there are things we cannot know" is self-refuting, since it claims to *know* something about the unknowable. For Hegel, there is no wall between phenomena and reality; the Absolute is fully knowable through dialectical reason. ⚠️ (For the Hegelian side, see [`Hegel.md`](Hegel.md).)

## 4. IDEAS OF REASON — Transcendental Dialectic ✅

### 4.1 The Three Transcendental Ideas

Reason (*Vernunft*) seeks the *unconditioned* — the totality of conditions for any given conditioned thing. This drive generates three Ideas:

| Idea | Object | Totality sought | Faculty misapplied |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Soul** (*Psychologische Idee*) | the absolute unity of the thinking subject | totality of *inner* conditions | categories applied to the "I think" |
| **World** (*Kosmologische Idee*) | the absolute totality of the series of conditions in appearance | totality of *outer* conditions | categories applied to the world-as-a-whole |
| **God** (*Theologische Idee*) | the absolute unity of the condition of all objects of thought in general | totality of *all* conditions | categories applied to the ens realissimum |

### 4.2 Regulative vs Constitutive Use ✅

- These Ideas are **regulative** — useful heuristic guides that direct enquiry toward systematic unity ("seek the soul's unity," "explain nature as if it were a complete system," "think of all existence as grounded in a necessary being"). ✅
- They are NOT **constitutive** — they do not give theoretical knowledge of actually existing objects (soul-substance, world-totality, God).
- **Error (transcendental illusion):** reason mistakes regulative Ideas for constitutive knowledge — and then falls into Paralogisms, Antinomies, and failed God-proofs. ✅

### 4.3 The Paralogisms (Fallacious Proofs of the Soul) ✅

Rational psychology tries to infer from the bare "I think" (the logical subject) that the soul is: (1) a *substance*, (2) *simple* (immaterial), (3) *numerically identical* (personal identity), (4) in relation to possible objects in space (existence distinct from body).

**Kant's reply:** all four inferences commit the fallacy of **equivocation** (*Paralogismus*) — they confuse the logical unity of apperception (a formal condition) with a real, knowable *thing*. The "I think" is not a substance but a *function*; we cannot derive metaphysical conclusions about an immaterial soul from a merely logical subject. ✅

## 5. THE ANTINOMIES ✅ (Star sub-topic — PYQ 2022 Q3a, 20m)

### 5.1 What is an Antinomy?

An antinomy is a **pair of contradictory propositions (thesis and antithesis), each of which can be "proved" by apparently valid argument** — demonstrating that pure reason, when applied beyond experience, generates irresolvable contradictions. ✅

### 5.2 The Four Antinomies — Stated

| # | THESIS | ANTITHESIS |
|---|---|---|
| **1st (Time & Space)** | The world has a *beginning* in time and is *limited* in space. | The world has *no beginning* and *no limits* in space — it is infinite. |
| **2nd (Composition)** | Every composite substance consists of *simple* (indivisible) parts. | No composite thing consists of simple parts; nothing simple exists. |
| **3rd (Freedom)** | Causality according to laws of nature is *not* the only causality; there is also *freedom* (spontaneous causality). | There is *no freedom*; everything happens solely according to laws of nature. |
| **4th (Necessary Being)** | There belongs to the world a being that is *absolutely necessary* (either as part or cause). | There exists *no absolutely necessary being*, either in the world or outside it as its cause. |

### 5.3 The Mathematical/Dynamical Distinction ⚠️

| | Mathematical Antinomies (1st & 2nd) | Dynamical Antinomies (3rd & 4th) |
|---|---|---|
| Subject matter | the *magnitude* or *composition* of the world-series | the *causality* or *existence* grounding the world |
| Resolution | **Both thesis and antithesis are FALSE** | **Both thesis and antithesis can be TRUE** (in different domains) |
| Why | The "world as a whole" (an absolute totality of appearances) is not a legitimate object — neither finite nor infinite, because it is never given as a completed whole | Freedom and necessity, or a necessary being and contingent nature, can coexist if they apply to *different realms* (noumena vs phenomena) |

### 5.4 Kant's Resolution ✅

**Mathematical antinomies (1 & 2):** The error is treating the empirical series (which is always *in progress*, never *complete*) as if it were a finished totality (a thing-in-itself). Since the "world as a whole" is never an object of possible experience, calling it finite or infinite is senseless. Both sides err by treating phenomena as noumena. ✅

**Dynamical antinomies (3 & 4):**
- **3rd antinomy (Freedom):** In the phenomenal world, every event is determined by prior causes (antithesis holds for phenomena). But the same agent, considered as a *noumenon* (a thing-in-itself), may possess *freedom* — a spontaneous causality not subject to temporal determination. ✅ Kant thus makes room for **moral freedom** (a causality "from freedom") alongside natural determinism. This is crucial for his entire ethics.
- **4th antinomy (Necessary Being):** Within the phenomenal series, there need be no necessary member (antithesis holds for phenomena). But a necessary being may exist *outside* the series as its transcendent ground (thesis holds for noumena) — though this cannot be *proved*, only posited as a regulative Idea. ✅

### 5.5 Significance of the Antinomies ⚠️

- They demonstrate that **metaphysics-as-science is impossible** — pure reason, operating beyond experience, generates irresolvable contradictions.
- They motivate the critical turn: knowledge must be confined to phenomena; but **practical reason** (morality) can legitimately *postulate* freedom, God, and immortality as conditions of the moral life.
- The antinomies are Kant's most dramatic argument against pre-critical metaphysics (both rationalist and empiricist). ✅

## 6. CRITIQUE OF PROOFS FOR THE EXISTENCE OF GOD ✅ (High-yield, cross-paper)

### 6.1 General Framework

- God (the *ens realissimum*) lies beyond possible experience → the categories (existence, causality) cannot legitimately be applied to God → **all theoretical proofs fail**. ✅
- There are only THREE possible types of speculative proof:
  1. **Ontological** (from the mere *concept* of God).
  2. **Cosmological** (from the *existence of anything at all*).
  3. **Physico-theological / Design** (from the *particular order* of nature). ✅

### 6.2 Critique of the Ontological Argument ✅

**The argument (Descartes/Anselm form):**
1. God = the most perfect / most real being.
2. Existence is a perfection/reality.
3. A being lacking existence would lack a perfection → would not be the most perfect being.
4. ∴ God necessarily exists.

**Kant's refutation — "Existence is not a real predicate" (*kein reales Prädikat*):**
- A "real predicate" (*Bestimmung*) is one that *adds to* (determines) the concept of a thing. "Existence" does NOT enlarge the concept — it merely *posits* the thing. ✅
- "A hundred real thalers do not contain the least coin more than a hundred possible thalers" — the *concept* is identical whether the object exists or not. Existence is the *positing* of a thing, not a *property* of it. ✅
- Therefore one cannot include "existence" among the perfections in the concept of God and then argue that God must exist because the concept includes existence.
- PYQ 2021 Q2(c) 15m directly: "Explain how Cartesian formulation of ontological argument is criticised by Kant." The above IS the answer.

### 6.3 Critique of the Cosmological Argument ✅

**The argument:**
1. Something exists (at least, I exist).
2. The existence of anything contingent requires a necessary being as its ground.
3. ∴ A necessary being exists.
4. The only candidate for a necessary being is the *ens realissimum* (God).

**Kant's critique:**
- Step 2 misuses the category of causality *beyond* possible experience (applying it to the totality of all existents → illegitimate). ✅
- Step 4 **covertly depends on the ontological argument:** to identify the "necessary being" with God (the most perfect being), one must assume that the concept of a most-perfect being includes necessary existence — which is the ontological argument in disguise. ✅
- Therefore: the cosmological argument *presupposes* the very argument it was supposed to replace.

### 6.4 Critique of the Physico-Theological (Design) Argument ✅

**The argument:**
1. The world exhibits order, purpose, design.
2. This order could not arise from blind matter alone.
3. ∴ An intelligent designer exists.

**Kant's critique:**
- At best, this proves a very powerful *architect* who orders pre-existing material — NOT an omnipotent *creator ex nihilo*. ✅
- To get from "architect" to "infinite, all-powerful God," the argument must smuggle in the cosmological argument (an infinite cause) — which in turn depends on the ontological argument. ✅
- Therefore: **all three proofs ultimately rest on the ontological argument**, and since that fails (existence ≠ predicate), they all fail together. ✅

### 6.5 Kant's Positive Doctrine — God as a Postulate of Practical Reason ✅

- Theoretical reason cannot prove or disprove God's existence.
- **Practical reason** (morality) requires God as a *postulate*: the highest good (virtue + happiness proportioned to virtue) is morally necessary; its realisation requires a being with the power and goodness to ensure that proportion → God must be postulated as a condition of the moral world. ✅
- *"I had to deny knowledge in order to make room for faith"* (*CPR* Bxxx). ✅

## 7. INTER-THINKER / INTER-SCHOOL DEBATES ⚠️

| Axis | Rationalism | Empiricism (Hume) | **Kant** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Source of knowledge** | reason alone (a priori) | experience alone (a posteriori) | **both**: pure intuitions + categories (a priori) applied to sensory content (a posteriori) |
| **Causality** | rational, necessary truth | mere habit/custom | **a priori category** — necessary for experience |
| **Synthetic a priori** | asserted (dogmatically) | denied (Hume's Fork) | **explained** (mind imposes form on experience) |
| **Metaphysics** | possible and necessary | impossible (anti-metaphysics) | impossible as *science* of noumena; possible as *critique* of reason |
| **God** | provable (ontological, cosmological) | unknowable (scepticism) | **thinkable but not knowable** (postulate of practical reason) |
| **Things-in-themselves** | knowable by reason | — (Hume doesn't raise the question) | **unknowable** (noumena) |
| **Self** | substance (Descartes' cogito) | bundle (Hume) | **transcendental unity of apperception** (logical condition, not a substance) |

## 8. CRITICISMS AND REPLIES

| Criticism | Source | Kant's possible reply |
|---|---|---|
| The noumenon is incoherent — to assert "there are things we cannot know" is self-contradictory | Hegel | Kant: the noumenon is a *limiting concept*, not a positive claim; "I cannot know X" ≠ "I know about X" |
| Why exactly *twelve* categories? The derivation from the logical forms of judgment is artificial | Critics (Fichte, Hegel, later Kantians) | The table of judgments is (Kant claims) exhaustive and systematic; no alternative is offered that is more complete ❓ |
| Transcendental idealism collapses into Berkeley's subjective idealism | Critics (Garve, Feder) | Kant: Berkeley denies the existence of external objects; I affirm it — objects are empirically real, only *transcendentally* ideal. The Refutation of Idealism (B274) is Kant's explicit answer. ✅ |
| If we cannot know things-in-themselves, how do we know they *exist* or *cause* our sensations? | Critics (Jacobi: "without the assumption of things-in-themselves I cannot enter the system; with it I cannot remain") | This is a genuine tension in Kant's system ❓ — he needs noumena to "affect" sensibility, but cannot use causation (a phenomenal category) to describe that relation. |
| The phenomena/noumena distinction opens a two-world dualism as problematic as Descartes' | Hegel, post-Kantians | Kant insists it is one world under two *aspects* (how things appear to us vs how they are in themselves), not two *worlds*. ⚠️ |

## 9. COMMON UPSC TRAPS ⚠️

| Trap | Discrimination |
|---|---|
| Confusing Kant's *transcendental* with *transcendent* | **Transcendental** = concerning the a priori *conditions* of possible experience (legitimate). **Transcendent** = going *beyond* all possible experience (illegitimate). Kant's philosophy is transcendental; the errors he attacks are transcendent. |
| "Kant proves God does not exist" | **Wrong.** He proves God's existence cannot be *demonstrated* by theoretical reason; he then *postulates* God's existence via practical reason. He is agnostic theoretically, not atheistic. |
| Confusing Kant's idealism with Berkeley's | Kant: objects are empirically real; Berkeley: objects are ideas (immaterial). Kant's idealism is about the *form* of experience (space, time, categories are mind-contributed); Berkeley's is about the *existence* of objects. |
| "The synthetic a priori = analytic" (conflating the two because both are a priori) | Analytic a priori = trivially true by definition; synthetic a priori = *informative* yet necessary — a different and far more interesting class. |
| "Noumena = Plato's Forms" | **Superficial.** Plato's Forms are *knowable* by reason; Kant's noumena are precisely *un*knowable. The resemblance is only that both are "beyond sense." |
| "The antinomies show that reason is unreliable" | **Not quite.** They show that reason, *when applied beyond experience*, generates contradictions — within its proper domain (phenomena), reason is perfectly reliable. |
| Confusing the *transcendental deduction* with the *metaphysical deduction* | Metaphysical deduction: identifies the categories (from the table of judgments). Transcendental deduction: justifies their application to experience (via apperception). |

## RESTORED 2018/2020 DOCTRINE DOSSIER

### “Understanding makes Nature”
- ✅ **Statement:** understanding “makes” nature as the law-governed unity of appearances because categories supply the a priori rules under which a manifold can count as objective experience.
- ✅ **Argument:** sensations alone are unordered; synthesis under categories such as substance and causality yields objects and universal laws; therefore experienced nature conforms to the conditions of possible experience.
- ⚠️ **Presupposition/distinction:** empirical objects are not created by private imagination. Transcendental constitution concerns form and lawful objectivity, not the material existence of things-in-themselves.
- ❓ **Objection → reply:** this appears to collapse nature into mind. Kant replies that transcendental idealism is empirical realism: appearances are publicly real even though known only under our forms of cognition.

### Kantian dualism and Hegelian culmination
- ✅ Hegel accepts the constitutive activity of thought but rejects an unknowable noumenal remainder; reason must comprehend the apparent opposition between subject and object as moments within the Absolute.
- ⚠️ A strong answer calls Hegel a radicalisation and attempted overcoming of Kant, not a straightforward deduction from Kantian dualism.

## 12. ANSWER ARCHITECTURE (10 / 15 / 20 marks) ⚠️

### 10-mark: "What is apperception? Discuss with reference to transcendental exposition of space and time." (2022)
```
Line 1: Kant's "transcendental unity of apperception" is the formal condition that the
         "I think" must be able to accompany all my representations — without it, nothing
         could count as MY experience.
Body  : (i) It is not an empirical self (Hume's bundle) but a logical/formal condition.
        (ii) It requires synthesis of the manifold according to categories.
        (iii) Connection to space and time: the transcendental exposition shows that the a
             priori intuitions of space and time make synthetic a priori knowledge (geometry,
             arithmetic) possible BECAUSE the same apperceptive unity synthesises the
             manifold of pure intuition according to rules (categories).
Close : "Discuss" = state the connection clearly; apperception unifies what space/time
        supply, making science possible.
```

### 15-mark: "Kant's objections against the ontological argument" (2024)
```
Frame : The ontological argument (Descartes/Anselm): God = most perfect being; existence =
        a perfection; ∴ God exists.
Body A: Kant's central objection: "Existence is not a real predicate." To say "God exists"
        does not add a property to God's concept — it posits the whole concept as actual.
        "100 real thalers = 100 possible thalers" in concept.
Body B: Consequence: one cannot define a thing into existence. The concept of God, however
        rich, does not entail God's actuality outside thought.
Body C: The cosmological and design arguments depend on this same move (identifying
        "necessary being" with "most perfect being" requires the ontological step).
Assess: Kant's argument is widely regarded as devastating. Critics (Plantinga, Malcolm)
        have tried to rehabilitate the argument; contested whether Kant fully refutes
        modal versions. ❓
Close : "Critically examine" = assess the strength of the objection; deliver a verdict.
```

### 20-mark: "How does Kant construct antinomies to illustrate the illusory tendencies of pure reason?" (2022)
```
Frame : The Transcendental Dialectic diagnoses reason's natural illusion — seeking the
        unconditioned beyond experience and generating contradictions.
Body A: What is an antinomy? Thesis + antithesis both "provable" → demonstrates that reason,
        unchecked, refutes itself.
Body B: State all four antinomies (table: thesis/antithesis).
        Distinction: mathematical (1-2) vs dynamical (3-4).
Body C: Resolution — mathematical: both sides false (the "world as a whole" is not a
        legitimate object). Dynamical: both sides can be true in different domains
        (phenomena/noumena). The 3rd antinomy = room for moral freedom alongside natural law.
Assess: The antinomies are Kant's strongest argument that speculative metaphysics is
        impossible — reason contradicts itself when it oversteps experience. The critical
        philosophy (confine knowledge to phenomena) is the remedy.
Close : The antinomies do not show reason is *flawed* — they show it must respect its own
        boundaries.
```
