# THEME NOTE — Causation Across Indian Schools

> **Type:** Cross-school theme file (Paper-I, Section B). Spans items 14–19.
> **Why this file exists:** "Theory of causation" is asked school-wise (Nyāya, Sāṃkhya, Vedānta) AND as comparison. The *whole* metaphysics of a school follows from its stand on **"does the effect pre-exist in the cause?"** Master this axis once.
> **Grounding:** ✅ = canonical (Chatterjee & Datta; C.D. Sharma) · ⚠️ = exam synthesis

---

## 0. THE MASTER QUESTION ⚠️

> **Does the effect (kārya) already exist in its material cause (upādāna) before production?**

```
                 CAUSATION THEORIES
     ┌───────────────────────┼───────────────────────┐
 ASATKĀRYAVĀDA        SADASATKĀRYAVĀDA          SATKĀRYAVĀDA
 (effect is NEW,      (effect is in one         (effect PRE-EXISTS
  did not pre-exist)   respect existent, in      in cause)
     │                 another non-existent)          │
 Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika          │                ┌──────────┴──────────┐
   = ĀRAMBHAVĀDA       JAINISM         PARIṆĀMAVĀDA         VIVARTAVĀDA
 (new whole from     (dravya persists,  (REAL transformation) (UNREAL appearance)
   parts)             paryāya is new)   Sāṃkhya, Rāmānuja     Advaita (Śaṃkara)
 Buddhism (in a         Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā
   sense: momentary     (usually placed here ❓)
   arising)
```
> 🔑 **Mnemonic:** *"**A**sat = **A**rambha (new); **Sat** splits into **P**ariṇāma (real change) vs **V**ivarta (mere appearance); **Jaina sits in the middle** and says *syāt* to both."*

## 1. THE THREE POSITIONS ✅

### A. ASATKĀRYAVĀDA — "the effect is non-existent (before production)"
- **Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika (Ārambhavāda / "the theory of the new beginning"):** the effect is a **fresh entity**, numerically distinct from its cause. Threads (cause) and cloth (effect) are two different things; the cloth is *begun* (ārambha) anew by the conjunction of parts. ✅
- **Grounds:** effect and cause are perceived as distinct; the effect has properties the cause lacks; we say the effect is "produced."
- **Buddhism** is sometimes grouped here (asat-kārya in spirit): under pratītyasamutpāda a *new* momentary entity arises dependent on the prior — nothing persists to "contain" the effect.

### B. SATKĀRYAVĀDA — "the effect pre-exists (in latent form)"
Effect is already present in the cause and is only *manifested*. Splits by **whether the transformation is real or only apparent**:

- **B1. PARIṆĀMAVĀDA (real transformation):**
  - **Sāṃkhya:** the effect is a **real modification** of prakṛti (curd really from milk; the world really evolves from prakṛti). 5 arguments (asadakaraṇāt, upādāna-grahaṇāt, sarvasambhavābhāvāt, śaktasya-śakyakaraṇāt, kāraṇabhāvāt). ✅
  - **Rāmānuja (Viśiṣṭādvaita):** the world is a real transformation of Brahman's dependent
    modes/body (*cit* and *acit*), not a mutation that destroys Brahman's essential perfection.
    Māyā denotes God's real creative power rather than an indeterminable illusion.

- **B2. VIVARTAVĀDA (apparent/unreal transformation):**
  - **Advaita (Śaṃkara):** the effect is only an **apparent, unreal appearance** of the cause — Brahman does not really change; the world is *vivarta*, like the **snake superimposed on the rope** or silver on shell. The cause alone is real; the "effect" is a name-and-form (nāma-rūpa) illusion. ✅
  - Special sub-cases: *ajātivāda* (Gauḍapāda — "no real origination at all") and *dṛṣṭi-sṛṣṭi-vāda*.

### C. SADASATKĀRYAVĀDA — "the effect is in one respect existent and in another non-existent"

- **Jainism** — the direct application of **anekāntavāda** to causation. ✅
  - **Statement.** Every existent is **utpāda-vyaya-dhrauvya**: origination, cessation and permanence together. Hence in the effect, the **dravya** (substance) is *pre-existent* and persists, while the **paryāya** (mode) is *newly originated* and the prior mode perishes.
  - **Argument.** (1) Sat is defined as that which originates, perishes and endures. (2) A cause and its effect share one substance-continuum. (3) So relative to the dravya, the effect *is* in the cause (satkārya); relative to the paryāya, it is *not* (asatkārya). (4) Since both are true from their own standpoints, an unqualified answer to "does the effect pre-exist?" is **ekāntavāda** — one-sidedness.
  - **Example.** Gold and the ornament: the gold (dravya) persists through the melting of a bangle into a necklace; bangle-ness ceases and necklace-ness originates. Also the standard clay/pot and milk/curd cases, re-read on two axes.
  - **The exam formula:** *syād asti, syād nāsti* — from the substance-standpoint the effect exists in the cause; from the mode-standpoint it does not. Jainism is therefore not a compromise but a **principled middle position** derived from its own metaphysics.
  - **Objection (Śaṃkara; Rāmānuja):** to say the effect both exists and does not exist is to violate non-contradiction. **Reply:** the two predicates are indexed to *different respects* (dravya vs paryāya), so there is no contradiction in the same respect. ⚠️ Residual force: critics reply that this only pushes the question back to whether "the same thing" really bears both aspects.
  - → Owner file: [`indian/Jainism.md`](../indian/Jainism.md).

- **Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā** — usually placed here in the standard surveys. ❓
  - Kumārila's position is presented as a qualified satkāryavāda: the effect pre-exists **as substance** but not **as the particular form/mode**, and production is a real **pariṇāma** of the material. Doxographies therefore align the Bhāṭṭas with sadasatkāryavāda, alongside Jainism. ⚠️
  - The **Prābhākaras** are usually aligned instead with Nyāya's **asatkāryavāda**. ❓
  - ⚠️ **Handling rule:** surveys differ on the exact Mīmāṃsā classification. Write it as "Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā is standardly presented as a qualified satkāryavāda close to the Jaina position, while Prābhākara leans asatkārya" — and do not claim more precision than the sources allow.

### D. THE MĪMĀṂSĀ CONTRIBUTION — APŪRVA, the causal doctrine no other school has ✅

- **The problem.** A Vedic sacrifice is performed now; the promised fruit (svarga) accrues much later, after the act has perished. A perished act cannot produce a later effect. So how is the scripturally guaranteed connection between act and fruit possible?
- **The answer.** The act generates **apūrva** ("the not-before", an unprecedented potency) — an imperceptible, persisting causal capacity that links the perishing act to its distant fruit. It is established by **arthāpatti** (postulation): the connection is otherwise inexplicable, so apūrva must be assumed. ✅
- **Why it matters for a causation answer.** Mīmāṃsā's real innovation is not its stand on satkārya/asatkārya but its insistence that **moral-ritual causation needs a mediating potency**, and that this potency is *impersonal* — it does not require a God to store or dispense the fruit. Compare: Nyāya's **adṛṣṭa** does the same work but is administered by **Īśvara**; Jainism's karma is **literally material**; Sāṃkhya-Yoga uses **saṃskāra / karmāśaya**; Buddhism uses **vāsanā / bīja** in the santāna. ⚠️ This four-way comparison is a high-yield paragraph.
- **Objection (Cārvāka; and Nyāya):** apūrva is an unverifiable posit invented to save a doctrine. **Reply:** it is no more occult than adṛṣṭa, and unlike adṛṣṭa it needs no further theistic apparatus — a parsimony argument. ⚠️ Residual force: the objection stands, since the whole scheme is warranted only by the scripture whose authority is in dispute.
- → Owner file: [`indian/Mimamsa.md`](../indian/Mimamsa.md).

## 3. WHY IT MATTERS — causation determines the whole system ⚠️

The causation stance is the *root* from which each metaphysics grows:
- **Asatkāryavāda → pluralistic realism** (Nyāya): many really-new things, atoms, a God to combine them.
- **Pariṇāmavāda → evolutionary realism** (Sāṃkhya): one real prakṛti unfolding; (Rāmānuja): one real Brahman with real parts.
- **Vivartavāda → non-dual idealism** (Advaita): only Brahman real, world illusory → māyā, avidyā, jīvanmukti all follow.
- **Sadasatkāryavāda → standpoint-relative realism** (Jainism): a real substance bearing real successive modes → anekāntavāda, nayavāda, syādvāda, and a pluralist universe with no creator.
- **Apūrva → impersonal moral order** (Mīmāṃsā): if a ritual act generates its own persisting potency, no God is needed to dispense fruits → **nirīśvaravāda**. The causation doctrine *is* the atheism argument.

> 🔑 In any "compare/critically examine" answer, **trace the school's larger doctrine back to its causation theory** — this shows synthesis and scores.

## 4. INTER-SCHOOL DEBATE POINTS ⚠️
- **Sāṃkhya vs Nyāya:** "nothing comes from nothing" (satkārya) vs "the effect is a genuinely new whole" (asatkārya). Nyāya replies: if effect = cause, why the effort to produce it, and why the perceived difference?
- **Nyāya's disambiguation:** the Nyāya charge is that satkāryavāda equivocates between *absolutely* non-existent (a hare's horn) and *not-yet* existent (the pot before the potter acts). Nyāya asserts only the latter. Sāṃkhya replies that "not-yet existent" must still be *something determinate* in the material cause, or the causal operation would have no fixed terminus (why milk and not water for curd?).
- **Jaina vs both:** the Jaina says the Sāṃkhya–Nyāya dispute is a textbook case of **ekāntavāda** — each side absolutises one true standpoint. Sadasatkāryavāda dissolves the dispute by indexing "exists" to dravya and "does not exist" to paryāya. **Counter (Śaṃkara):** dissolving a contradiction by indexing is a verbal manoeuvre, not a solution. **Jaina reply:** the indexing is grounded in a real two-aspect structure of every existent, not invented for the occasion.
- **Advaita vs Sāṃkhya (both satkārya):** the crucial split — is the transformation **real (pariṇāma)** or **apparent (vivarta)**? Advaita: prakṛti-pariṇāma still leaves you in an unexplained real-plurality; only vivarta preserves non-duality.
- **Advaita vs Rāmānuja:** vivarta (world unreal) vs pariṇāma (world real, God's body). The 2022/2025 liberation questions trace to this.
- **Mīmāṃsā vs Nyāya on the moral link:** both need an unseen mediating potency, but **apūrva is impersonal and self-executing** while **adṛṣṭa requires Īśvara to administer it**. This is the causation-side of the Mīmāṃsā atheism question, and it is the sharpest way to show that a theory of causation carries theological consequences.
- **Four-way comparison on karmic causation** (high-yield): Jaina material karma-pudgala · Nyāya adṛṣṭa + Īśvara · Mīmāṃsā apūrva without God · Sāṃkhya-Yoga saṃskāra/karmāśaya · Buddhist vāsanā/bīja in the santāna.

## 5. STATEMENT / QUOTE BANK ⚠️
1. ✅ *"nāsato vidyate bhāvo nābhāvo vidyate sataḥ"* — "of the non-existent there is no coming-to-be; of the existent there is no ceasing-to-be." **Attribution: Bhagavad-gītā 2.16.** ⚠️ **Attribute it correctly.** It is a *scriptural echo* commonly quoted in support of satkāryavāda; it is **not** a verse of the *Sāṃkhyakārikā* and it is **not itself an argument** for satkāryavāda. Cite it as a supporting formula only, and let the argumentative weight rest on the five Sāṃkhya proofs (asadakaraṇāt, upādāna-grahaṇāt, sarvasambhavābhāvāt, śaktasya śakya-karaṇāt, kāraṇa-bhāvāt).
2. ✅ *"The effect is a new beginning, distinct from its cause."* — Nyāya ārambhavāda.
3. ✅ *"Brahman does not really change; the world is a vivarta (apparent modification)."* — Advaita.
4. ⚠️ *"Milk into curd is pariṇāma (real); rope into snake is vivarta (apparent)."* — the one-line discriminator.
5. ✅ *"utpāda-vyaya-dhrauvya-yuktaṃ sat"* — "that is real which is endowed with origination, cessation and permanence." — the Jaina formula, and the ground of sadasatkāryavāda. (Standardly attributed to the *Tattvārthasūtra* of Umāsvāti; quote the formula, not a sūtra number.)
6. ⚠️ *"The act perishes, but its apūrva persists."* — the Mīmāṃsā link between ritual act and distant fruit.

## 6A. CONTROLLED WESTERN COMPARISON ⚠️

Deploy only **after** the Indian case is complete, and always with the disanalogy attached.

| Indian position | Western point of contact | The disanalogy that must be stated |
|---|---|---|
| Satkāryavāda | **Aristotle's** potentiality/actuality: change is the actualisation of what is potentially present; nothing comes from nothing | Aristotelian matter is *indeterminate* potentiality requiring an external form and a final cause; prakṛti already contains determinate effects and unfolds *by itself*, with no God and no external agent. |
| Asatkāryavāda / ārambhavāda | Ordinary **event-causation** and the idea of genuine novelty / emergence | Nyāya's novelty is the production of a *new substance* (the avayavin) by inherence in its parts — an ontological commitment no Western causal theory makes. |
| Vivartavāda | **Bradley's** Appearance and Reality; degrees of reality | Bradley's Absolute *includes and harmonises* appearance; Brahman *sublates* it. And Advaita is **not** Berkeleyan idealism — the vyāvahārika world is intersubjective, not "my idea". |
| Sadasatkāryavāda (Jaina) | **Hegelian** dialectic: opposites belong to the real | Hegel's opposites are *sublated* into a higher synthesis; Jaina aspects simply coexist, indexed to standpoints, with no synthesis and no movement. |
| Apūrva / adṛṣṭa | Dispositional **causal powers**; "storage" of causal capacity | Apūrva is a *moral-ritual* potency warranted by scripture and postulated by arthāpatti, not an empirically grounded disposition. |
| Buddhist pratītyasamutpāda | **Humean** regularity; Whiteheadian process | Hume denies necessary connexion but keeps enduring objects; Buddhism keeps a *strict causal conditioning* while denying any enduring substance at all. |

> **Rubric.** A causation question in Paper I is answered from the Indian grid. Western parallels are illustrative garnish — one or two closing lines at most, each carrying its disanalogy. A bare equation such as "satkāryavāda is Aristotle's potentiality" is a marked weakness.

## 7A. ONE-LINE RECALL CARD ⚠️
- **Asatkāryavāda:** the effect was not there and now is. (Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika)
- **Satkāryavāda:** the effect was always there and is now manifest. (Sāṃkhya — really; Advaita — only apparently)
- **Sadasatkāryavāda:** the effect was there *as substance* and was not there *as mode*. (Jainism; and, as standardly presented, Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā ❓)
