# SCHOOLS OF VEDĀNTA

> **Syllabus (verbatim):** Schools of Vedanta : Brahman; Isvara; Atman; Jiva; Jagat; Maya; Avida; Adhyasa; Moksa; Aprthaksiddhi; Pancavidhabheda.

**Evidence key:** ✅ canonical · ⚠️ analytical synthesis · ❓ contested

**Placement:** This file covers Syllabus Clause 19, the densest and highest-scoring Vedānta cluster in Paper I Indian Philosophy. It must always be read comparatively across **Advaita (Śaṃkara)**, **Viśiṣṭādvaita (Rāmānuja)**, and **Dvaita (Madhva)**, while linking out to cross-school themes in causation, self, pramāṇa, liberation and critiques of metaphysics. See [00_Master-Framework](../../00_Master-Framework.md).

## 1. BRAHMAN

### 1.1 Svarūpa-lakṣaṇa and taṭastha-lakṣaṇa

- ✅ Vedānta gives two standard definitions of Brahman.
- ✅ **Svarūpa-lakṣaṇa** states Brahman's intrinsic nature as **sat-cit-ānanda** — pure being, consciousness and bliss.
- ✅ **Taṭastha-lakṣaṇa** indicates Brahman through its relation to the world: **janmādyasya yataḥ** — that from which the world originates, in which it is sustained, and into which it dissolves.
- ⚠️ In answers, use **svarūpa** to define what Brahman is in itself, and **taṭastha** to explain why Brahman functions as the metaphysical ground of cosmology.

### 1.2 Advaita: nirguṇa Brahman

- ✅ For Śaṃkara, **nirguṇa Brahman** is the sole pāramārthika reality.
- ✅ **Nirguṇa** does **not** mean sheer blankness or absence of nature.
- ✅ It means Brahman is free from limiting, relational, empirical or objectifiable attributes.
- ✅ Sat-cit-ānanda is not a list of accidental predicates added to Brahman; it expresses Brahman's very essence.
- ✅ All determinate qualities such as creatorhood, omniscience, lordship and moral governance belong to **Īśvara**, that is, Brahman as conditioned by māyā at the vyāvahārika level.
- ⚠️ Thus Advaita maintains a strict distinction between **nirguṇa Brahman** as ultimate and **saguṇa Brahman/Īśvara** as empirical-absolutist theology.

### 1.3 Viśiṣṭādvaita: Brahman as saguṇa only

- ✅ Rāmānuja rejects any level of Brahman beyond the personal Lord.
- ✅ Brahman is **saguṇa** in the highest sense: **ananta-kalyāṇa-guṇa-viśiṣṭa** — endowed with infinite auspicious qualities.
- ✅ These qualities are not limiting adjuncts but perfections constitutive of Brahman's fullness.
- ✅ Brahman is identified with **Viṣṇu/Nārāyaṇa**, the supreme personal reality who includes within himself all selves and matter as inseparable modes.
- ⚠️ Hence Rāmānuja's Brahman is not less absolute because qualified; rather, qualification is what makes absoluteness intelligible.

### 1.4 Dvaita: Brahman as supreme independent reality

- ✅ Madhva also rejects nirguṇa Brahman.
- ✅ Brahman is the personal Viṣṇu possessing perfect and infinite attributes.
- ✅ Brahman alone is **svatantra** (independent), while jīvas and jagat are **paratantra** (dependent).
- ✅ Unlike Rāmānuja, Madhva does not subsume difference within qualified unity; he makes difference itself metaphysically ultimate.

### 1.5 Brahman as nimitta-kāraṇa and upādāna-kāraṇa

- ✅ Vedānta frequently defines Brahman as both **nimitta-kāraṇa** (efficient cause) and **upādāna-kāraṇa** (material cause) of the world.
- ✅ This issue was asked directly in **2025**.

#### (a) Advaita

- ✅ Since nothing exists independently of Brahman, there can be no second material out of which the world is made.
- ✅ Therefore Brahman is both the intelligence behind the world and the material basis of its appearance.
- ✅ The classic image is the **spider** that spins the web out of itself.
- ⚠️ But Śaṃkara qualifies the material-cause claim through **vivarta**: Brahman does not really transform into the world; the world is an appearance dependent on Brahman.

#### (b) Viśiṣṭādvaita

- ✅ Rāmānuja also makes Brahman both efficient and material cause.
- ✅ But here causation is **real**: cit and acit are inseparable from Brahman, and the world-process is a real manifestation of Brahman's body through prakṛti.

#### (c) Dvaita

- ✅ Madhva preserves God as supreme cause, but without collapsing God, souls and matter into identity.
- ✅ Dependence on God is universal, yet difference remains real and eternal.

### 1.6 Contrast with Sāṃkhya and Nyāya

- ✅ This Vedāntic doctrine differs sharply from [Sāṃkhya](Samkhya.md) and [Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika](Nyaya-Vaisesika.md).
- ✅ In Sāṃkhya, **prakṛti** is material cause and there is no efficient creator-God.
- ✅ In Nyāya, God is primarily the **efficient** cause, while eternal atoms are the **material** cause.
- ⚠️ A compact contrast line for answers: **Sāṃkhya splits material causality from intelligence, Nyāya splits efficient from material causality, Vedānta reunites both in Brahman.**

### 1.7 Rāmānuja's critique of nirguṇa Brahman

- ✅ Asked in **2023**, this is one of the core anti-Advaita arguments.
- ✅ Rāmānuja argues that an entity without attributes cannot be known.
- ✅ If utterly indeterminate, nirguṇa Brahman cannot be the referent of scriptural description.
- ✅ If it has no internal intelligibility, it cannot be the cause of a richly qualified world.
- ✅ Scriptural passages that negate defects do not negate all qualities; they deny limiting or undesirable predicates while affirming perfection.
- ⚠️ Therefore, for Rāmānuja, Śaṃkara's Brahman becomes either unknowable emptiness or an abstraction too thin to ground religion, devotion, causality or ethics.

### 1.8 Why Brahman is the highest-yield topic

- ⚠️ Most Vedānta questions reduce to a hidden dispute over the nature of Brahman.
- ⚠️ Once Brahman is interpreted as **nirguṇa**, the world becomes appearance, Īśvara becomes lower truth, mokṣa becomes identity, and scripture becomes ultimately sublatable.
- ⚠️ Once Brahman is interpreted as **saguṇa**, world and devotion become philosophically central.

## 2. ĪŚVARA

### 2.1 Advaita: māyā-upahita Brahman

- ✅ In Advaita, Īśvara is **Brahman conditioned by māyā**.
- ✅ The standard expressions are **māyā-upahita Brahman** or **māyā-viśiṣṭa Brahman**.
- ✅ Īśvara is the omniscient, omnipotent, moral governor and creator of the empirical world.
- ✅ Īśvara is fully real at the **vyāvahārika** level.
- ✅ Yet at the **pāramārthika** level, Īśvara too is sublated into nirguṇa Brahman.
- ⚠️ Hence theism in Advaita is neither false in a crude sense nor ultimate in the final sense.

### 2.2 Viśiṣṭādvaita: Īśvara is Brahman

- ✅ For Rāmānuja, Īśvara is not a lower appearance of Brahman.
- ✅ Īśvara **is** Brahman — the supreme personal Viṣṇu/Nārāyaṇa.
- ✅ There is no nirguṇa level above him.
- ✅ His infinite auspicious qualities are real and eternal.
- ✅ He is immanent because the world and souls are his body, and transcendent because he exceeds them.

### 2.3 Dvaita: supreme personal theism

- ✅ In Madhva, Viṣṇu is the absolutely supreme Lord.
- ✅ He alone is independent and self-sufficient.
- ✅ All jīvas and the entire jagat depend upon him ontologically, morally and soteriologically.
- ✅ Difference between Īśvara and all else is never erased by liberation.

### 2.4 Bimba-pratibimba-vāda

- ✅ Asked in **2025** as a 20-marker.
- ✅ In one important Advaita line, Īśvara is described as the **bimba** (original), while the jīva is the **pratibimba** (reflection).
- ✅ Īśvara is the original consciousness reflected in **māyā**.
- ✅ Jīva is consciousness reflected in **avidyā** or the internal limiting medium.
- ✅ The original remains unaffected by the defects of the reflecting medium, just as a face is not altered by defects in a mirror.

### 2.5 Soteriological significance of bimba-pratibimba-vāda

- ⚠️ The theory is not a mere ontological curiosity; it serves liberation.
- ⚠️ The reflected image has only derivative reality.
- ⚠️ When the reflecting medium of avidyā is removed, the finite self recognises that it was never an autonomous substance over against Brahman.
- ⚠️ Mokṣa is thus the discovery that one was always the **bimba** in truth and never truly an independent **pratibimba**.
- ⚠️ The reflection does not travel somewhere new; ignorance is removed and original identity is disclosed.

### 2.6 Contrast with avacchedavāda and ābhāsavāda

- ✅ The Advaita tradition uses multiple explanatory models for jīva-Īśvara relation.

#### (a) Avacchedavāda

- ✅ The finite self is Brahman as though limited by adjuncts, like one space appearing as pot-space (**ghaṭākāśa**) through limitation.
- ⚠️ Here emphasis falls on delimitation rather than reflection.

#### (b) Pratibimbavāda

- ✅ The finite self is the reflection of consciousness in ignorance.
- ⚠️ Here emphasis falls on derivative appearance through a reflecting medium.

#### (c) Ābhāsavāda

- ✅ The jīva is a mere semblance or appearance (**ābhāsa**) of Brahman.
- ⚠️ Here emphasis falls on ontological thinness of individuality.

### 2.7 Ontological status of Īśvara, jīva and sākṣī in Advaita

- ✅ This was asked in **2022**.
- ✅ **Īśvara** = Brahman reflected/conditioned by māyā; empirically real, cosmically authoritative.
- ✅ **Jīva** = Brahman reflected/limited by avidyā; empirically finite, agentive and bound.
- ✅ **Sākṣī** = pure witnessing consciousness, unaffected, immediate and non-objectifiable.
- ⚠️ The hierarchy is crucial: sākṣī is closest to the pāramārthika standpoint, jīva belongs to empirical bondage, and Īśvara belongs to cosmic-ordering empirical reality.

### 2.8 Rāmānuja's critique of Advaita's Īśvara

- ✅ If Īśvara belongs only to the vyāvahārika level, devotion becomes merely preparatory.
- ✅ Scriptural descriptions of God become lower truth instead of ultimate truth.
- ✅ Religious life is thereby structurally subordinated to non-personal absolutism.
- ⚠️ Rāmānuja objects that this hollows out bhakti and makes scriptural theism provisional rather than final.

## 3. ĀTMAN

### 3.1 Advaita: Ātman = Brahman

- ✅ In Advaita, the true self is identical with Brahman.
- ✅ The great Upaniṣadic sayings (**mahāvākyas**) such as **tat tvam asi** and **ahaṃ brahmāsmi** express this identity.
- ✅ The empirical self appears individual because of ignorance, but its essence is non-dual consciousness.
- ✅ Ātman is self-luminous, not an object among objects.

### 3.2 Sākṣī as witness-consciousness

- ✅ Advaita characterises Ātman as **sākṣī** — witness-consciousness.
- ✅ It illuminates all mental states but is never altered by them.
- ✅ Pleasure, pain, doubt, memory and desire are witnessed, not essential to the self.
- ✅ The sākṣī is not a passive blank but the condition for all experience.
- ⚠️ This gives Advaita its elegant distinction between changing psychology and changeless consciousness.

### 3.3 Viśiṣṭādvaita: real but non-identical self

- ✅ Rāmānuja accepts the reality, eternity and consciousness of the self.
- ✅ The self is **aṇu** (atomic in size), though capable of pervasive knowledge through dharma-bhūta-jñāna.
- ✅ It is a **prakāra** or mode of Brahman, not identical with Brahman.
- ✅ It is dependent, but not illusory.
- ⚠️ Thus selfhood is preserved without making the self an independent absolute.

### 3.4 Dvaita: eternal difference of self and God

- ✅ In Madhva, the self is real, eternal and dependent upon God.
- ✅ Difference from God is fundamental, not produced by ignorance.
- ✅ The self never becomes God, even in liberation.
- ⚠️ This provides the metaphysical basis of devotion as permanent relation rather than provisional means.

### 3.5 Comparative formula

| School | Status of Ātman | Relation to Brahman/Īśvara |
|---|---|---|
| Advaita | pure consciousness | identical in essence |
| Viśiṣṭādvaita | real eternal conscious self, aṇu | inseparable mode, not identical |
| Dvaita | real eternal dependent self | eternally different |

### 3.6 Debate-point

- ⚠️ The self is the pressure-point where each school reveals its ethical and religious consequences.
- ⚠️ If self is Brahman, liberation is recognition.
- ⚠️ If self is a mode of Brahman, liberation is communion.
- ⚠️ If self is permanently different, liberation is eternal service.

## 4. JĪVA

### 4.1 General note

- ✅ Ātman often designates the self in its essential metaphysical nature.
- ✅ **Jīva** usually denotes the embodied, empirical individual caught in bondage, agency and transmigration.
- ⚠️ Many UPSC answers improve immediately if this distinction is explicitly made.

### 4.2 Advaita account (I): avacchedavāda

- ✅ Associated especially with **Vācaspati Miśra**.
- ✅ Brahman appears as jīva when delimited by avidyā or internal adjuncts.
- ✅ The classic analogy is **space in a pot**: one undivided space seems many because of limiting containers.
- ⚠️ Jīva is not a real fragment of Brahman; apparent limitation alone produces plurality.

### 4.3 Advaita account (II): pratibimbavāda

- ✅ Associated with **Padmapāda** and the **Vivaraṇa** line.
- ✅ Jīva is the reflection of consciousness in avidyā.
- ✅ Like a face reflected in a mirror, the reflected self appears individual while the original remains unaffected.
- ⚠️ This model explains both dependence and phenomenological individuality more vividly than sheer delimitation.

### 4.4 Advaita account (III): ābhāsavāda

- ✅ Jīva is a mere semblance (**ābhāsa**) of Brahman.
- ⚠️ This pushes the derivative status of individuality even further than reflection theory.
- ⚠️ It is useful in answers to show internal plurality within Advaita without losing the common conclusion: individuality lacks ultimate reality.

### 4.5 Viśiṣṭādvaita: jīva as real mode of Brahman

- ✅ For Rāmānuja, the jīva is a real, conscious **prakāra** of Brahman.
- ✅ It is neither an illusion nor a detached independent substance.
- ✅ Its relation to God is often explained through the **śarīra-śarīrī** analogy.
- ✅ The jīva is God's body in the sense that it depends entirely on God for its existence, support and activity.
- ⚠️ Body here does not mean gross physical body only; it means that which is controlled, sustained and used by an indwelling self.

### 4.6 Dvaita: jīva as distinct dependent substance

- ✅ Madhva treats the jīva as a real, distinct, dependent entity.
- ✅ It is never reducible to God, never a mere mode of God, and never illusory.
- ✅ Difference among jīvas is also real.
- ❓ Madhva is often presented as teaching a hierarchy among souls — **sāttvikas, rājasas, tāmasas** — but the rigidity and exact doctrinal framing of this classification is debated in scholarship.
- ⚠️ In UPSC, mention the hierarchy but tag its most rigid formulations as contested.

### 4.7 Jīva in the 2021 and 2022 questions

- ✅ **2021** asked Madhva's account of jīva and jagat.
- ✅ **2022** asked the ontological status of Īśvara, jīva and sākṣī in Advaita.
- ⚠️ Therefore the jīva topic must always be prepared in both **intra-Advaita** detail and **inter-school** comparison.

### 4.8 Compact comparative statement bank

- ⚠️ Advaita: **jīva is Brahman appearing finite due to avidyā**.
- ⚠️ Viśiṣṭādvaita: **jīva is a real conscious mode of Brahman**.
- ⚠️ Dvaita: **jīva is a real dependent self eternally different from God**.

## 5. JAGAT

### 5.1 Advaita: vivartavāda

- ✅ For Advaita, the world is an **apparent transformation** (**vivarta**) of Brahman.
- ✅ Brahman does not undergo real change.
- ✅ The classic example is the **rope-snake** illusion: the substrate is real, the appearance is dependent and sublatable.
- ✅ Therefore jagat is **mithyā** — neither absolutely real nor absolutely unreal.
- ✅ It is not **tuccha** like a hare's horn.
- ⚠️ This is one of the most important trap-points in Vedānta answers.

### 5.2 Three levels of reality

- ✅ **Pāramārthika**: Brahman alone.
- ✅ **Vyāvahārika**: the world, Īśvara and jīvas as empirically real.
- ✅ **Prātibhāsika**: dream and illusion, such as rope-snake or shell-silver.
- ⚠️ The world is denied absolute reality, not empirical functioning.

### 5.3 Vivartavāda as logical development of pariṇāmavāda

- ✅ Asked in **2022**.
- ✅ Sāṃkhya's **pariṇāmavāda** says the effect is a real transformation of the cause.
- ✅ Advaita radicalises the logic of causality.
- ✅ If the cause is the absolute Brahman, and Brahman admits no real modification, then the effect cannot be equally real as transformation.
- ✅ Therefore transformation must be only **apparent** — not pariṇāma but **vivarta**.
- ⚠️ This is why one may say vivartavāda is the logical development of pariṇāmavāda: it preserves causal dependence while preventing real change in the absolute.

### 5.4 Viśiṣṭādvaita: the world is real

- ✅ Rāmānuja firmly rejects world-appearance doctrine.
- ✅ The world is real and forms the **acit** dimension of Brahman's body.
- ✅ Creation is a real manifestation or transformation involving Brahman's attribute **prakṛti**.
- ✅ Since effects are real, the universe is a meaningful field of duty, devotion and divine manifestation.

### 5.5 Dvaita: world as real and eternally distinct

- ✅ Madhva accepts the world as real.
- ✅ Its dependence on God does not cancel its distinctness.
- ✅ Matter is not absorbed into identity by knowledge.
- ⚠️ This gives Dvaita the strongest realist posture among the three schools.

### 5.6 Śaṃkara vs Rāmānuja on the world's status

- ✅ Asked in **2021**.

| Issue | Śaṃkara | Rāmānuja |
|---|---|---|
| Reality of world | mithyā, dependent appearance | real manifestation |
| Change in absolute | denied | accepted in qualified sense through modes |
| Soteriology | world sublated in knowledge | world meaningful in devotion |
| God-world relation | empirical | intrinsic body-soul relation |

- ⚠️ A useful evaluative line: Śaṃkara protects absoluteness by reducing world-status; Rāmānuja protects world-status by qualifying absoluteness.

### 5.7 Madhva on jīva and jagat

- ✅ Also asked in **2021**.
- ✅ Both jīva and jagat are real and distinct from God.
- ✅ Difference is not merely practical but metaphysical.
- ✅ This prepares directly for **pañcavidhabheda**.

### 5.8 Comparative placement

- ⚠️ On jagat, Vedānta spans three positions:
  - Advaita = ontological dependence with empirical validity.
  - Viśiṣṭādvaita = real world as divine body.
  - Dvaita = real world as distinct dependent order.

## 6. MĀYĀ

### 6.1 Basic Advaita definition

- ✅ Māyā is the cosmic power through which plurality appears.
- ✅ It is beginningless and dependent on Brahman.
- ✅ It is neither **sat** nor **asat**, but **anirvacanīya** — indescribable.
- ⚠️ Māyā is not pure nothingness; if it were, no appearance could occur.
- ⚠️ Yet it cannot be fully real, because knowledge sublates its products.

### 6.2 Two powers of māyā

- ✅ **Āvaraṇa-śakti** — the concealing power that hides Brahman's true nature.
- ✅ **Vikṣepa-śakti** — the projecting power that throws up the world of names and forms.
- ⚠️ These two powers explain how ignorance can both cover and fabricate at the empirical level.

### 6.3 Māyā and avidyā: identity or distinction?

- ❓ Advaita sources are not fully uniform.
- ❓ In some formulations, **māyā** and **avidyā** are treated as broadly interchangeable.
- ❓ In others, **māyā** is cosmic/objective and associated with Īśvara, while **avidyā** is individual/subjective and associated with the jīva.
- ✅ Both are usually regarded as **anirvacanīya**.
- ⚠️ For UPSC, state the distinction carefully and note that exact usage varies.

### 6.4 The locus-and-object problem

- ✅ A classical problem asks: whose is avidyā?
- ✅ If avidyā belongs to the jīva, the account seems circular because the jīva itself is supposed to arise within ignorance.
- ✅ If avidyā belongs to Brahman, Brahman appears compromised by ignorance.
- ⚠️ This is the **āśraya/viṣaya** problem and forms a major polemical opening for Viśiṣṭādvaita critiques.

### 6.5 Rāmānuja's seven objections against māyāvāda

- ✅ Asked in **2025**.
- ✅ This list is conventionally called the **sapta-vidhā-anupapatti**.
- ❓ Exact names and sequence may vary slightly across commentators on the *Śrī-bhāṣya*; the following is the standard exam list.

#### 1. Āśrayānupapatti

- ✅ The locus of avidyā is inexplicable.
- ✅ It cannot coherently belong either to jīva or to Brahman.

#### 2. Tirodhānānupapatti

- ✅ Concealment of self-luminous Brahman is unintelligible.
- ✅ What is pure light cannot be covered by ignorance in any literal sense.

#### 3. Svarūpānupapatti

- ✅ The very nature of avidyā is self-contradictory if it is said to be neither real nor unreal.

#### 4. Anirvacanīyatānupapatti

- ✅ To call something indescribable is itself to describe it.
- ⚠️ Therefore the category appears self-refuting.

#### 5. Pramāṇānupapatti

- ✅ No valid means of knowledge can establish an entity that is neither real nor unreal.
- ⚠️ A pramāṇa must terminate in something intelligibly cognisable.

#### 6. Nivartakānupapatti

- ✅ What exactly removes avidyā?
- ✅ If knowledge removes it, then one must explain how knowledge arises in a knower already compromised by ignorance.

#### 7. Nivṛttyānupapatti

- ✅ The cessation of avidyā is equally unintelligible.
- ✅ If avidyā is neither real nor unreal, its disappearance too becomes conceptually unstable.

### 6.6 Philosophical force of the seven objections

- ⚠️ Collectively, the seven objections target Advaita at three levels:
  1. the **status** of avidyā,
  2. the **possibility** of concealment and projection,
  3. the **coherence** of liberation as removal of ignorance.
- ⚠️ They are among the most fertile 10-marker and 15-marker materials in the entire Indian philosophy syllabus.

### 6.7 Dvaita's position

- ✅ Dvaita does not accept māyā in the Advaita sense of ontological illusion.
- ✅ The world is real, and ignorance is real ignorance, not cosmic appearance masking non-dual identity.

## 7. AVIDYĀ

### 7.1 Advaita: beginningless but terminable

- ✅ Avidyā is **anādi** (beginningless).
- ✅ Yet it is **sānta** or terminable through knowledge.
- ✅ It is not eternal in the sense that Brahman is eternal.
- ✅ Avidyā explains bondage, individuality, agency and misidentification.
- ⚠️ This formula — beginningless but endable — is essential in mokṣa answers.

### 7.2 Relation to bondage

- ✅ Through avidyā, the self identifies with body, mind, senses, doership and enjoyership.
- ✅ Thus empirical existence is structured by ignorance, not by a real transformation of the self.

### 7.3 Viśiṣṭādvaita

- ✅ Rāmānuja does not admit avidyā as anirvacanīya cosmic principle.
- ✅ Ignorance is real ignorance of God's nature and of the soul's dependence on God.
- ✅ It is tied to karma and bondage.
- ✅ It is removed through right knowledge integrated with devotion, bhakti and especially **prapatti**.

### 7.4 Dvaita

- ✅ In Dvaita too ignorance is real.
- ✅ It is not a metaphysical veil covering non-dual identity.
- ✅ It is overcome through correct knowledge, devotion and divine grace.
- ⚠️ Madhva's realism preserves stronger continuity between ignorance, ethics, devotion and salvation than Advaita's appearance theory.

### 7.5 Māyā–avidyā distinction as an exam trap

- ⚠️ Do not flatten the two into a crude synonym-pair.
- ⚠️ Safer formula: **in many Advaita formulations, māyā is the cosmic power associated with Īśvara, while avidyā is the individual ignorance associated with jīva; both are anirvacanīya, though some texts use them interchangeably.**

## 8. ADHYĀSA

### 8.1 Śaṃkara's classical definition

- ✅ In the opening of the *Brahma-sūtra-bhāṣya*, Śaṃkara defines **adhyāsa** as mutual superimposition between subject and object.
- ✅ The shorthand phrase is **atasmin tad-buddhiḥ** — cognition of one thing in what is not that thing.
- ✅ More fully: the superimposition of the attributes of the object on the subject, and vice versa.

### 8.2 Natural and beginningless

- ✅ Śaṃkara characterises adhyāsa as **naisargika** (natural) and **anādi** (beginningless).
- ⚠️ This means ordinary empirical life is already structured by misidentification prior to philosophical reflection.

### 8.3 Two components

- ✅ Adhyāsa includes:
  1. **āvaraṇa** — concealment of the true nature,
  2. **vikṣepa** — projection of the false appearance.
- ⚠️ This mirrors the two powers of māyā and connects epistemology to metaphysics.

### 8.4 Root of all vyavahāra

- ✅ Śaṃkara treats adhyāsa as the root of all empirical dealing.
- ✅ Statements such as “I am tall,” “I am happy,” “I act,” and “I know” presuppose the mixing of self and not-self.
- ✅ Bodily and mental predicates are falsely attributed to the self, while consciousness is attributed to body and mind.
- ⚠️ Thus worldly experience is not merely occasionally mistaken; it is structurally pervaded by superimposition.

### 8.5 Relation to anirvacanīya-khyāti

- ✅ Adhyāsa is the dynamic process of superimposition.
- ✅ **Anirvacanīya-khyāti** is the theory explaining the ontological status of the erroneous object thereby presented.
- ⚠️ The two should be connected, not confused.

### 8.6 Standard examples

- ✅ Rope-snake.
- ✅ Shell-silver.
- ✅ Desert-mirage.
- ⚠️ Use at least one of these examples in every adhyāsa answer; they make the abstraction examinable.

### 8.7 Comparative significance

- ⚠️ Adhyāsa gives Advaita a deeper diagnosis of bondage than schools that treat error as isolated episodic misjudgment.
- ⚠️ For Nyāya, error concerns wrong cognition of objects; for Advaita, the very egoic standpoint is already infected by primordial superimposition.

## 9. MOKṢA

### 9.1 Advaita: prāptasya prāptiḥ

- ✅ Asked in **2023**.
- ✅ Mokṣa in Advaita is not production of a new state.
- ✅ It is **prāptasya prāptiḥ** — attainment of what is already attained.
- ✅ The self is always Brahman; bondage is ignorance alone.
- ✅ Therefore liberation is simply removal of avidyā through knowledge.
- ⚠️ The metaphor is often like finding a necklace already hanging around one's own neck.

### 9.2 Jñāna alone liberates

- ✅ Śaṃkara insists that **jñāna alone** is the direct means to mokṣa.
- ✅ Karma and upāsanā purify the mind but do not directly produce liberation.
- ✅ This is his rejection of **jñāna-karma-samuccayavāda**, the doctrine that liberation comes from a combination of knowledge and ritual action.
- ✅ Preparatory qualifications are summarised in **sādhana-catuṣṭaya**:
  1. **viveka** — discrimination between eternal and non-eternal,
  2. **vairāgya** — detachment,
  3. **ṣaṭ-sampatti** — sixfold discipline,
  4. **mumukṣutva** — desire for liberation.

### 9.3 Jīvanmukti and videhamukti

- ✅ A distinctive strength of Advaita is **jīvanmukti** — liberation while still embodied.
- ✅ Once avidyā is destroyed, the person knows the identity of Ātman and Brahman.
- ✅ Yet the body continues due to **prārabdha karma**, just as an arrow already released continues its course.
- ✅ At the fall of the body comes **videhamukti**, but no new liberation is produced then.
- ⚠️ This is crucial for both Paper I and cross-over Paper II answers on liberation and religious experience.

### 9.4 Prārabdha karma explained

- ✅ **Prārabdha** is that part of past karma already set in motion.
- ✅ It sustains the body even after knowledge dawns.
- ✅ No new binding karma accrues to the jīvanmukta because doership has been dissolved.

### 9.5 Viśiṣṭādvaita: communion, not identity

- ✅ Mokṣa for Rāmānuja is the jīva's attainment of likeness to God and eternal loving communion with him.
- ✅ The liberated self remains real and distinct.
- ✅ The path is **bhakti** culminating in **prapatti** (self-surrender).
- ✅ There is no Advaitic jīvanmukti in the strict sense, because embodiment remains a condition of bondage and liberation is not mere epistemic sublation of individuality.
- ⚠️ This makes liberation relational and devotional rather than absorptive.

### 9.6 Dvaita: blissful service and tāratamya

- ✅ For Madhva, mokṣa is eternal blissful service of Viṣṇu in Vaikuṇṭha.
- ✅ Liberation requires God's grace.
- ✅ Difference from God is never abolished.
- ✅ A distinctive doctrine is **tāratamya** — gradation among liberated souls.
- ✅ Not all released souls enjoy identical bliss or rank.
- ❓ Some accounts go further and suggest that some souls remain eternally bound or destined to darkness; the exact interpretation is contested and should be marked carefully.

### 9.7 Madhva's mokṣa versus Śaṃkara's

- ✅ Asked in **2022**.

| Issue | Śaṃkara | Madhva |
|---|---|---|
| Nature of liberation | identity-realisation | eternal difference and service |
| Means | jñāna | devotion plus grace |
| World-status | ultimately sublated | remains real |
| Selfhood | ultimately non-different | permanently distinct |
| Equality of liberated souls | no real plurality ultimately | gradation (tāratamya) |

### 9.8 Comparative insight

- ⚠️ Mokṣa expresses each school's ontology in soteriological form:
  - non-dual ontology → liberation as recognition,
  - qualified non-dual ontology → liberation as communion,
  - dualist ontology → liberation as service.

## 10. APṚTHAKSIDDHI

### 10.1 Definition

- ✅ **Apṛthaksiddhi** is Rāmānuja's doctrine of inseparable existence.
- ✅ Cit (selves) and acit (matter) exist in an inseparable relation with Brahman.
- ✅ They cannot exist or be conceived independently of Brahman.
- ✅ Yet they are not identical with Brahman.
- ⚠️ This is the central conceptual instrument by which Viśiṣṭādvaita combines unity and plurality.

### 10.2 Aprthaksiddha-viśeṣaṇa

- ✅ An inseparable qualifier (**apṛthaksiddha-viśeṣaṇa**) is one that cannot stand apart from the substance it qualifies.
- ✅ The attribute is real and distinct, but inseparable.
- ⚠️ This is stronger than mere conceptual relation and weaker than identity.

### 10.3 Śarīra-śarīrī-bhāva

- ✅ Rāmānuja explains the God-world relation through the **body-soul** analogy.
- ✅ Jīvas and jagat are Brahman's **body**.
- ✅ Brahman is their indwelling **soul**.
- ✅ This means:
  1. they are entirely supported by Brahman,
  2. entirely controlled by Brahman,
  3. exist for Brahman's purposes.
- ⚠️ This was asked directly in **2024**.

### 10.4 Why apṛthaksiddhi matters

- ✅ It preserves non-duality because there is only one ultimate substantive whole: Brahman.
- ✅ It preserves real difference because cit and acit are genuine modes, not illusory appearances.
- ✅ It preserves devotion because the relation between God and soul remains meaningful.
- ⚠️ In one stroke, it answers Advaita's monism and Dvaita's pluralism.

### 10.5 Not identity

- ⚠️ The major UPSC trap is to treat apṛthaksiddhi as identity.
- ⚠️ Rāmānuja does **not** say soul and world are numerically identical with God.
- ⚠️ He says they are inseparably related as real modes of God.

### 10.6 Formula for quick recall

- ⚠️ **Advaita:** identity without real plurality.
- ⚠️ **Viśiṣṭādvaita:** inseparable plurality within unity.
- ⚠️ **Dvaita:** eternal plurality with dependence.

## 11. PAÑCAVIDHABHEDA

### 11.1 Definition

- ✅ Madhva's hallmark doctrine is **pañcavidhabheda** — the five real and eternal differences.
- ✅ These are not practical distinctions generated by ignorance.
- ✅ They are metaphysically ultimate.

### 11.2 The five differences

1. ✅ **jīva-Īśvara-bheda** — difference between soul and God.
2. ✅ **jīva-jīva-bheda** — difference among souls.
3. ✅ **jīva-jaḍa-bheda** — difference between soul and matter.
4. ✅ **Īśvara-jaḍa-bheda** — difference between God and matter.
5. ✅ **jaḍa-jaḍa-bheda** — difference among material things.

### 11.3 Philosophical significance

- ✅ This doctrine directly rejects Advaita's claim of identity.
- ✅ It also rejects Rāmānuja's attempt to retain unity through qualified inseparability.
- ✅ Difference is neither epistemic nor merely empirical; it is real and eternal.

### 11.4 Consequences

- ⚠️ Since jīva remains eternally different from God, devotion is never transcended.
- ⚠️ Since jīva-jīva difference is real, hierarchy among souls is possible.
- ⚠️ Since jaḍa-jaḍa difference is real, the plural world is ontologically robust.

### 11.5 UPSC trap

- ⚠️ Do not reduce pañcavidhabheda to only “difference between God and world.”
- ⚠️ The examiner expects all five, especially **jīva-jīva** and **jaḍa-jaḍa**.

## 12. ŚAṂKARA AS PRADHĀNA-MALLA AGAINST SĀṂKHYA

### 12.1 Why Sāṃkhya is the chief rival

- ✅ Asked in **2025**.
- ✅ Śaṃkara's chief metaphysical opponent is often identified as Sāṃkhya, the **pradhāna-malla** debate.
- ✅ This is because Sāṃkhya is not a distant enemy but a close rival.
- ✅ It accepts **satkāryavāda**, as Advaita also broadly does in transformed fashion.
- ✅ It posits a single material principle (**prakṛti/pradhāna**) as explanatory ground of the world.
- ⚠️ Therefore Sāṃkhya is the nearest alternative grand metaphysics competing for explanatory supremacy.

### 12.2 Śaṃkara's first argument: cause must be conscious

- ✅ Upaniṣads describe the cause of the world in conscious terms — as **sat**, as knowing, willing and purposive.
- ✅ An unconscious **pradhāna** cannot satisfy scriptural descriptions of the world-cause.
- ⚠️ Hence Sāṃkhya's material principle falls short of Vedāntic cosmology.

### 12.3 Second argument: teleology requires intelligence

- ✅ The world exhibits purposive order.
- ✅ An unconscious cause cannot produce teleological arrangement without direction.
- ✅ Mere guṇa-equilibrium and disturbance do not explain meaningful ordered creation.
- ⚠️ Therefore intelligent ground is needed.

### 12.4 Third argument: puruṣa-prakṛti relation is unintelligible

- ✅ If puruṣa is inactive witness and prakṛti is unconscious activity, their contact itself becomes obscure.
- ✅ How does the mere proximity of pure witness set prakṛti into purposive motion?
- ⚠️ Śaṃkara presses this as an unresolved dualistic tension.

### 12.5 Fourth argument: Upaniṣadic cause is one, not dual

- ✅ The Upaniṣads present the cause as one absolute reality.
- ✅ Sāṃkhya divides the ultimate into puruṣa and prakṛti.
- ✅ Therefore it cannot claim full scriptural support for its dual ontology.

### 12.6 Why the debate matters

- ⚠️ This controversy clarifies Advaita's self-understanding.
- ⚠️ Śaṃkara is not merely anti-realistic; he is defending a conscious absolute against an unconscious metaphysical source.
- ⚠️ Use this section whenever asked to compare Vedānta causation with [Sāṃkhya](Samkhya.md).

## 13. MAHĀVĀKYA INTERPRETATION — “TAT TVAM ASI”

### 13.1 Central problem

- ✅ Asked in **2023**.
- ✅ If “tat” means omniscient Īśvara and “tvam” means finite individual self, literal identity seems impossible.
- ✅ Advaita solves this by **bhāga-tyāga-lakṣaṇā** (also called **jahad-ajahal-lakṣaṇā** in this context).

### 13.2 Direct and implied meanings

- ✅ **Tat** has:
  - **vācyārtha** = Īśvara associated with māyā,
  - **lakṣyārtha** = pure consciousness.
- ✅ **Tvam** has:
  - **vācyārtha** = jīva associated with avidyā and limitation,
  - **lakṣyārtha** = pure consciousness.

### 13.3 Bhāga-tyāga-lakṣaṇā explained

- ✅ The incompatible parts of the direct meanings are abandoned.
- ✅ Omniscience, lordship and cosmic causality on the one hand, and finitude, agency and ignorance on the other hand, are dropped.
- ✅ What is retained is the common implied meaning: pure consciousness.
- ✅ Thus “tat tvam asi” teaches identity at the level of essence, not crude literal sameness of empirical conditions.

### 13.4 Devadatta illustration

- ✅ The classic analogy is: **“This is that Devadatta.”**
- ✅ The “this” and “that” involve differing temporal and spatial contexts — here/there, now/then, youth/age.
- ✅ These accidental differences are dropped, and the person is identified.
- ⚠️ Likewise, the jīva-Īśvara contradiction is resolved by dropping adjuncts and retaining underlying consciousness.

### 13.5 Comparative note

- ⚠️ Viśiṣṭādvaita and Dvaita reject this identity-reading.
- ⚠️ For them, mahāvākyas indicate dependence, participation or special relation, not absolute identity.
- ⚠️ So the interpretation of one sentence becomes a battleground for the whole architecture of Vedānta.

## 14. ANIRVACANĪYA-KHYĀTI

### 14.1 Definition

- ✅ Asked in **2021**.
- ✅ Advaita's theory of error is **anirvacanīya-khyāti**.
- ✅ In illusion such as shell-silver, the presented silver is neither fully real nor absolutely unreal.
- ✅ It is **anirvacanīya** — indescribable.

### 14.2 Why neither real nor unreal?

- ✅ If the silver were real, it should survive sublation by correct knowledge.
- ✅ But it disappears once the shell is correctly known.
- ✅ If it were unreal like a hare's horn, it could never appear at all.
- ✅ Therefore it has a dependent, provisional, indefinable status.

### 14.3 Refutation of Nyāya's anyathākhyāti

- ✅ Nyāya says error involves apprehending something real elsewhere in the wrong locus.
- ✅ Advaita objects: if the silver were a real elsewhere-object somehow presented here, sublation would not cancel its apparent objecthood so radically.
- ✅ The illusion is dissolved not by travel to another place but by recognition of the substrate.
- ⚠️ Therefore the silver cannot simply be real silver misplaced in cognition.

### 14.4 Relation to avidyā

- ✅ The erroneous object is a product of avidyā.
- ✅ It has empirical momentariness but no ultimate standing.
- ⚠️ This theory supports the broader Advaita claim that the whole world can be experienced yet ultimately sublated.

### 14.5 Why this matters philosophically

- ⚠️ Anirvacanīya-khyāti is not only about minor perceptual mistakes.
- ⚠️ It is the epistemological miniature of Advaita metaphysics.
- ⚠️ What shell-silver is to shell, the world is to Brahman.

## 15. LOGICAL CONSEQUENCES OF ADVAITA'S METAPHYSICAL ABSOLUTISM

### 15.1 The 2019 demand

- ✅ Asked in **2019** as a 20-marker.
- ✅ The question tests whether one can derive consequences from the doctrine that **Brahman alone is ultimately real**.

### 15.2 Consequence 1: world reduced to appearance

- ✅ If Brahman alone is real, then the world cannot possess independent absolute reality.
- ✅ It becomes **mithyā**, dependent, sublatable appearance.

### 15.3 Consequence 2: individuality and agency become provisional

- ✅ Jīva as finite doer-enjoyer cannot be ultimately real.
- ✅ Individuality belongs to ignorance-conditioned existence.
- ⚠️ Hence moral agency is affirmed at the empirical level but denied ultimacy.

### 15.4 Consequence 3: ethics and karma become vyāvahārika

- ✅ Ethical life, duty, merit, sin and karmic causality remain valid within worldly existence.
- ✅ Yet they do not belong to the final truth.
- ⚠️ This gives Advaita great flexibility, but also creates the charge that it downgrades ethics.

### 15.5 Consequence 4: devotion becomes preparatory

- ✅ Religious devotion to a personal God is accepted.
- ✅ But if Īśvara belongs to the vyāvahārika order, devotion is not ultimate.
- ⚠️ Bhakti becomes pedagogic or preparatory rather than final consummation.

### 15.6 Consequence 5: pramāṇas are empirically valid only

- ✅ Perception, inference and even scripture function in the empirical order.
- ✅ Once Brahman is realised, the duality presupposed by all pramāṇas is transcended.
- ⚠️ Therefore Advaita appears to use logic and scripture to establish a truth that finally relativises logic and scripture.

### 15.7 The self-referential tension

- ⚠️ This generates a famous philosophical tension.
- ⚠️ Is Advaita self-defeating because it relies on the authority of reason and scripture only to subordinate them?
- ⚠️ Or is this not self-defeat but a ladder-model in which means are valid until the highest insight dawns?
- ⚠️ This tension is excellent material for critical conclusions in 20-mark answers.

### 15.8 Balanced assessment

- ⚠️ Advaita's absolutism purchases metaphysical purity at the cost of reclassifying ordinary life as lower truth.
- ⚠️ Its admirers call this the highest wisdom.
- ⚠️ Its critics call it ontological austerity purchased by practical instability.

## 16. INTER-THINKER DEBATES

### 16.1 Śaṃkara vs Rāmānuja on Brahman

- ⚠️ Śaṃkara: the absolute must be nirguṇa, else it becomes finite, relational and conditioned.
- ⚠️ Rāmānuja: an attribute-less absolute is unintelligible, religiously sterile and causally ineffective.

### 16.2 Śaṃkara vs Rāmānuja on world-status

- ⚠️ Śaṃkara: the world must be mithyā to preserve Brahman's changelessness.
- ⚠️ Rāmānuja: the world must be real to preserve scripture, devotion and causality.

### 16.3 Śaṃkara vs Madhva on selfhood

- ⚠️ Śaṃkara: difference is ignorance-born appearance.
- ⚠️ Madhva: difference is eternal structure of reality.

### 16.4 Rāmānuja vs Madhva

- ⚠️ Rāmānuja tries to combine unity and plurality via inseparability.
- ⚠️ Madhva rejects qualified identity and insists on uncompromising real difference.

### 16.5 Vedānta vs Sāṃkhya

- ⚠️ Vedānta insists that ultimate cause is conscious.
- ⚠️ Sāṃkhya places explanatory weight on unconscious prakṛti and multiple puruṣas.
- See [Sāṃkhya](Samkhya.md) and [Causation across schools](../_themes/Causation-across-schools.md).

### 16.6 Vedānta vs Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika

- ⚠️ Nyāya holds God to be efficient cause and atoms material cause.
- ⚠️ Advaita collapses both into Brahman while relativising world-status.
- ⚠️ Nyāya's realism is therefore an important foil to Advaita's anirvacanīya ontology.
- See [Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika](Nyaya-Vaisesika.md).

### 16.7 Vedānta and Mīmāṃsā

- ⚠️ Mīmāṃsā's defence of Vedic authority and pramāṇa theory provides part of the hermeneutic background from which Vedānta emerges.
- ⚠️ But Vedānta redirects scriptural interpretation from ritual duty to metaphysical knowledge and liberation.
- See [Mīmāṃsā](Mimamsa.md) and [Pramāṇa across schools](../_themes/Pramana-across-schools.md).

## 17. CRITICISMS AND REPLIES

### 17.1 Criticism: nirguṇa Brahman is unknowable

- ✅ Raised centrally by Rāmānuja.
- ⚠️ Reply from Advaita: Brahman is not known as an object with predicates; it is self-revealed as the very ground of all knowing.

### 17.2 Criticism: māyā is self-contradictory

- ✅ Raised through the seven objections.
- ⚠️ Reply from Advaita: empirical contradiction belongs to the level generated within avidyā; māyā is posited precisely because ordinary binary categories fail to explain illusion.

### 17.3 Criticism: if world is mithyā, ethics collapses

- ⚠️ Reply from Advaita: ethics is fully binding at the vyāvahārika level; purification is indispensable to liberation.
- ⚠️ Yet critics remain unconvinced because ultimate sublation still seems to weaken moral realism.

### 17.4 Criticism: if self is identical with Brahman, bondage is inexplicable

- ⚠️ Reply from Advaita: bondage is not real transformation but ignorance-born superimposition.
- ⚠️ Critics respond that this simply relocates, rather than solves, the problem into the concept of avidyā.

### 17.5 Criticism of Viśiṣṭādvaita: if Brahman has real modes, does this compromise absoluteness?

- ⚠️ Rāmānuja replies that qualifications do not limit Brahman; they express plenitude.
- ⚠️ A qualified whole can still be absolute if nothing lies outside it.

### 17.6 Criticism of Dvaita: eternal difference risks ultimate pluralism and hierarchy

- ⚠️ Madhva replies that plurality is not a defect; it is reality as created and sustained by God.
- ⚠️ Devotion is strongest when ontological distinction is preserved.

## 18. COMMON UPSC TRAPS

1. ⚠️ **Māyā and avidyā are not simply identical in every formulation.** Safer answer: māyā is cosmic and linked to Īśvara, avidyā individual and linked to jīva, though some Advaita texts overlap them.
2. ⚠️ **Apṛthaksiddhi is not identity.** It means inseparable relation of a real attribute/mode to its substance.
3. ⚠️ **Pañcavidhabheda is fivefold.** Do not stop at God-world difference.
4. ⚠️ **Vivartavāda does not mean total non-existence of the world.** Jagat is mithyā, not tuccha.
5. ⚠️ **Nirguṇa does not mean blank nothingness.** Sat-cit-ānanda remains Brahman's svarūpa.
6. ⚠️ **In Advaita, Īśvara is not the final pāramārthika reality.** Nirguṇa Brahman alone is ultimate.
7. ⚠️ **Madhva's mokṣa includes gradation (tāratamya).** Avoid writing as though all muktas are equal.
8. ⚠️ **“Tat tvam asi” is not interpreted by crude literal identity.** The standard Advaitic device is **bhāga-tyāga-lakṣaṇā/jahad-ajahal-lakṣaṇā**.
9. ⚠️ **Jīvanmukti is central to Advaita, not to Viśiṣṭādvaita or Dvaita.**
10. ⚠️ **Rāmānuja rejects a nirguṇa level above personal God.** Do not write “Rāmānuja accepts both nirguṇa and saguṇa Brahman.”

- ⚠️ **Every Vedāntic dispute turns on whether difference is appearance, qualification or ultimate fact.**

## RESTORED 2018/2020 DOCTRINE DOSSIER

### Indescribability of Brahman and Māyā
- ✅ Brahman is beyond limiting predication because it is non-objective, non-dual reality; Māyā is *anirvacanīya* because it is neither ultimately real nor sheer non-being and is sublated by knowledge.
- ❌ The two indescribabilities are not identical: Brahman's transcendence of predicates is not the same as Māyā's dependent, indefinable ontological status.

### Wider Vedānta comparison
| School | Self/world relation | Matter/world | Mokṣa |
|---|---|---|---|
| Viśiṣṭādvaita | souls and matter are real modes/body of Brahman | real and inseparable | communion and service through knowledge, devotion and grace |
| Dvaita | eternal difference | real, dependent on Viṣṇu | blissful God-directed existence without identity |
| Śuddhādvaita | world is a real manifestation of Brahman, not Māyā-produced illusion | pure real transformation/manifestation | participation in Kṛṣṇa through grace/devotion |
| Acintyabhedābheda | inconceivable difference-and-non-difference | real divine energy | loving devotion without erasing distinction |

### Nimbārka and Advaitic Jīva–sākṣī
- ✅ Nimbārka's *dvaitādvaita* accepts Brahman, conscious selves (*cit*) and non-conscious reality (*acit*) as real; *cit* and *acit* are different from Brahman yet cannot exist independently of Brahman.
- ✅ *Acit* is commonly classified as **prākṛta** (primordial material nature and its products), **aprākṛta** (non-prakritic substance associated with the divine realm/body) and **kāla** (time). ❓ Terminology and the placement of time vary across expositions, so an answer should state the adopted classification rather than invent further subtypes.
- ⚠️ Matter is therefore neither illusion nor an independent second absolute: it is real, transformable and dependent, related to Brahman as power/attribute to its possessor.
- ❓ **Objection → reply:** real difference seems to threaten non-duality, while dependence seems to erase difference. Nimbārka treats both difference and non-difference as equally real aspects of one relation rather than reducing either side.
- ✅ In Advaita, **jīva** is consciousness reflected/conditioned by the internal organ and avidyā; **sākṣī** is the unaffected witnessing consciousness that illumines mental states. Their apparent relation is due to limiting adjuncts, not a relation between two ultimately separate selves.
- ❓ The 2018 paper's claim that Māyā is “needed” by Rāmānuja is philosophically unusual; answer by flagging Rāmānuja's rejection of Advaitic Māyā and interpreting the demand as a comparison of each system's explanatory treatment of plurality.

## 21. ANSWER ARCHITECTURE

### 21.1 Ten-marker (10 marks)

**Use when asked one term or one objection-list.**

```text
1. Define the concept in one crisp sentence.
2. Give school placement (Advaita / Viśiṣṭādvaita / Dvaita).
3. State 3–5 doctrinal points with one Sanskrit keyword.
4. Add one contrast line with rival school.
5. End with one evaluative conclusion.
```

**Example openings**
- “For Śaṃkara, māyā is the anirvacanīya power of projection and concealment through which the non-dual Brahman appears as plural world.”
- “Rāmānuja's apṛthaksiddhi means inseparable existence of cit and acit in Brahman without reducing them to identity.”

### 21.2 Fifteen-marker (15 marks)

**Use when asked comparative or explanatory-analytical questions.**

```text
Intro      : define the issue and name the school(s).
Body I     : doctrine in the primary thinker.
Body II    : conceptual mechanism (e.g., bhāga-tyāga-lakṣaṇā, vivarta, apṛthaksiddhi).
Body III   : contrast with one rival school.
Assessment : philosophical strength / criticism.
Conclusion : return to syllabus term.
```

**Recommended pattern**
- Paragraph 1 = doctrinal statement.
- Paragraph 2 = mechanism and example.
- Paragraph 3 = objection and reply.
- Paragraph 4 = comparative conclusion.


### 21.3 Twenty-marker (20 marks)

**Use when the demand is inter-school, problem-based or critical.**

```text
Intro        : frame the central metaphysical issue.
Section I    : primary doctrine in conceptual detail.
Section II   : internal distinctions/models.
Section III  : criticism from rival school.
Section IV   : reply / evaluative defence.
Section V    : broader implications for world, self, God, liberation.
Conclusion   : balanced judgement with one comparative line.
```

### 21.4 Stock comparative endings

- ⚠️ **Advaita resolves plurality by sublation, Viśiṣṭādvaita by inseparability, and Dvaita by eternal difference.**
- ⚠️ **The Vedānta debate is finally a debate over whether relation to the absolute is identity, qualification or difference.**

### 21.5 How to score extra

- ⚠️ Always insert one Sanskrit technical term in transliteration.
- ⚠️ Always give one example: rope-snake, shell-silver, pot-space, mirror-reflection, self-body.
- ⚠️ Always contrast with at least one rival school even in single-school answers.
- ⚠️ In critical answers, use one tension-line rather than one-sided praise.
