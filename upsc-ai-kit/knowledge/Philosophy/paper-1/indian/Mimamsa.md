## 1. INTRODUCTION — PŪRVA-MĪMĀṂSĀ AS DHARMA-JIJÑĀSĀ

### 1.1 The governing orientation

- ✅ **Pūrva-Mīmāṃsā** begins from **dharma-jijñāsā**: the inquiry into dharma.
- ✅ The school is centred on action-guidance rather than speculative theology.
- ✅ The opening sūtras of Jaimini make clear that the concern is with what ought to be done, not merely with what is metaphysically the case.
- ✅ The key formula is that **dharma is known through Vedic injunction**.
- ✅ Hence epistemology here is inseparable from hermeneutics.
- ✅ A pramāṇa is not discussed in the abstract only; it is discussed in relation to the kinds of objects it can disclose.
- ✅ Perception can reveal visible things.
- ✅ Inference can extend beyond present perception where a reliable relation is established.
- ✅ But dharma, ritual efficacy and future fruit are not open to ordinary perception.
- ✅ Therefore **śabda**, especially Vedic śabda, becomes central.
- ✅ The defence of Veda then requires a defence of cognition itself.
- ✅ That defence takes the form of **svataḥ-prāmāṇyavāda**.
- ✅ If cognition were suspect until certified from outside, Vedic cognition too would hang upon some further authority.
- ✅ Mīmāṃsā refuses such dependence.
- ✅ It therefore develops one of the most sophisticated theories of intrinsic validity in Indian philosophy.
- ⚠️ This makes Mīmāṃsā appear ritualistic if read narrowly, but philosophically it is a general epistemic realism.
- ✅ The school assumes that knowledge normally discloses a real world.
- ✅ Error is exceptional, not constitutive.
- ✅ Language is action-guiding, not merely descriptive.
- ✅ Normative content is objective enough to be known.

### 1.2 Two main sub-schools

| Sub-school | Principal thinker | Doctrinal profile | Recurrent UPSC focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bhāṭṭa** | **Kumārila Bhaṭṭa** | accepts 6 pramāṇas, including **anupalabdhi**; develops **viparīta-khyāti**; defends **jñātatā** | anupalabdhi, abhāva, arthāpatti, jñātatā |
| **Prābhākara** | **Prabhākara Miśra** | accepts 5 pramāṇas; rejects separate anupalabdhi; develops **akhyāti / vivekākhyāti**; defends **tripuṭī-saṃvit** | error, self-luminosity, sentence meaning |

### 1.3 Why the sub-school split matters

- ✅ UPSC repeatedly asks not for generic Mīmāṃsā but for **Bhāṭṭa vs Prābhākara**.
- ✅ The split is not superficial.
- ✅ It affects the accepted list of pramāṇas.
- ✅ It affects the explanation of absence.
- ✅ It affects the explanation of illusion.
- ✅ It affects whether cognition is self-luminous.
- ✅ It affects the theory of sentence meaning.
- ✅ Yet both sub-schools remain recognisably Mīmāṃsā because both share the larger commitments: Vedic authority, realism, ritual efficacy, intrinsic validity, and the irreducibility of certain forms of cognition.
- ⚠️ A good answer must therefore show unity-in-difference.
- ✅ Bhāṭṭa is often more willing to multiply entities and epistemic instruments where irreducible phenomena demand them.
- ✅ Prābhākara is often more economical and tries to avoid positing extra faculties.
- ✅ Bhāṭṭa accepts independent **anupalabdhi**.
- ✅ Prābhākara rejects it.
- ✅ Bhāṭṭa allows a genuinely false cognition in error.
- ✅ Prābhākara interprets error as non-discrimination between two valid cognitions.
- ✅ Bhāṭṭa denies self-luminosity of cognition.
- ✅ Prābhākara affirms it.
- ✅ These are not isolated theses; each belongs to a larger architecture.

### 1.4 Minimum metaphysical commitments behind the epistemology

- ✅ Mīmāṃsā is broadly **realist** about the external world.
- ✅ It accepts enduring objects sufficiently stable to be known, named and acted upon.
- ✅ It accepts a plurality of selves.
- ✅ The self is an agent, knower and enjoyer.
- ✅ Moral responsibility and ritual responsibility therefore make sense.
- ✅ The school does not require a creator God to secure truth or distribute fruits.
- ✅ Instead it relies on **apūrva** and the self-valid authority of the Veda.
- ✅ This means Mīmāṃsā can defend objectivity without theological voluntarism.
- ✅ Knowledge is possible because objects are real.
- ✅ Ritual obligation is possible because injunction is meaningful.
- ✅ Future fruit is possible because action leaves an unseen potency.
- ⚠️ The metaphysics stays sparse because the school's focus is not ontological inventory for its own sake.
- ✅ Yet the realism is strong enough to support default trust in cognition.
- ✅ The school's anti-sceptical thrust depends on this realism.
- ✅ If cognition did not ordinarily reach real objects, ritual instruction would collapse into uncertainty.
- ✅ Therefore epistemology is the service-foundation of ethics and ritual theory.

## 2. SVATAḤ-PRĀMĀṆYAVĀDA — INTRINSIC VALIDITY OF KNOWLEDGE

### 2.1 Statement

- ✅ **Svataḥ-prāmāṇyavāda** means that a cognition is valid by its own very occurrence under normal causal conditions.
- ✅ The cognition does not first appear neutral and then wait for an external certificate of truth.
- ✅ Validity belongs to cognition intrinsically, whereas invalidity is due to some defeating factor.
- ✅ In short: knowledge begins with default authority and loses it only when subsequently sublated.
- ✅ The doctrine is about the epistemic status with which cognition presents itself.
- ✅ It is not about psychological confidence alone.
- ✅ It is not about dogmatic refusal to revise belief.
- ✅ It is a thesis about how pramā initially functions.

### 2.2 The fourfold formulation

| Aspect | Mīmāṃsā thesis | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Utpatti of prāmāṇya** | **Intrinsic** | the same conditions that generate cognition generate its validity |
| **Jñapti of prāmāṇya** | **Intrinsic** | cognition presents itself as trustworthy without a further validating cognition |
| **Utpatti of aprāmāṇya** | **Extrinsic** | falsity arises from defect, obstruction, mislocation or other abnormal factor |
| **Jñapti of aprāmāṇya** | **Extrinsic** | invalidity becomes known through contradiction, failure, correction, or later defect-awareness |

### 2.3 Why this doctrine is central to Mīmāṃsā

- ✅ Mīmāṃsā must secure the authority of Vedic cognition without invoking God.
- ✅ If all validity depended on something external, the Veda too would depend on an external certifier.
- ✅ That would force us to prove the certifier first.
- ✅ But the certifier would itself need justification.
- ✅ Therefore the chain would either become circular or regress indefinitely.
- ✅ By treating validity as intrinsic, Mīmāṃsā blocks scepticism at the starting point.
- ✅ This is not a special privilege only for scripture.
- ✅ It is a general theory of cognition.
- ✅ Ordinary perceptual knowledge, inferential knowledge and verbal knowledge all participate in the same structure.
- ✅ The Veda is then secure because cognition as such is secure.
- ✅ Hence epistemology and scriptural authority rise or fall together.
- ✅ The doctrine also explains how practical life can begin at all.
- ✅ We do not pause before every cognition waiting for certification.
- ✅ We act on it unless defeated.

### 2.4 Argument for the doctrine

#### 2.4.1 Statement

- ✅ Every cognition carries prima facie validity unless and until defeated.

#### 2.4.2 Argument

1. ✅ Cognition is meant to disclose an object.
2. ✅ If every cognition needed another cognition to validate it, the validating cognition would also need validation.
3. ✅ This generates **anavasthā** (infinite regress).
4. ✅ To avoid regress, some cognition must be accepted as valid without external validation.
5. ✅ The most economical view is that each cognition is prima facie valid on its own.
6. ✅ We in fact live by trusting cognitions unless some defeating condition appears.
7. ✅ Practical life therefore confirms default trust rather than default doubt.
8. ✅ Hence validity is intrinsic and invalidity is adventitious.
9. ✅ If later confirmation were required for every act of cognition, no first action could ever be rationally launched.
10. ✅ Therefore epistemic economy favours intrinsic validity.
11. ✅ The burden of proof lies on the challenger, not on every fresh cognition.

#### 2.4.3 Presupposition

- ✅ The argument presupposes **realism**: cognitions are aimed at real objects and are normally successful disclosures of them.
- ✅ It also presupposes that normal causes tend toward truth rather than toward arbitrary fabrication.
- ✅ Without this background realism, the doctrine would collapse into mere subjectivist self-certification.
- ✅ Mīmāṃsā does not want that.
- ✅ It wants object-directed cognition with default warrant.

#### 2.4.4 Distinction

- ✅ **Svataḥ-prāmāṇya is not infallibilism**.
- ✅ It does not say every cognition is indefeasible.
- ✅ It says every cognition begins with prima facie authority.
- ✅ A cognition can later be corrected.
- ✅ What is intrinsic is default validity, not guaranteed incorrigibility.
- ✅ This distinction is one of the most common exam traps.

#### 2.4.5 Example

- ✅ I see a pot on the floor.
- ✅ I do not first suspend judgment pending a further certificate.
- ✅ I immediately move around the pot, pick it up, or refer to it.
- ✅ This ordinary structure shows that cognition functions as already authoritative.
- ✅ Only if later I discover poor light, hallucination, or contradiction do I revoke it.

#### 2.4.6 Objection → Reply

- ❌ **Objection:** If cognition is intrinsically valid, error should be impossible.
- ✅ **Reply:** No. Error occurs when some **extrinsic defect** intervenes. Intrinsic validity is only prima facie validity under normal conditions.

### 2.5 Nyāya's parataḥ-prāmāṇyavāda

#### Statement

- ✅ Nyāya is the chief rival here.
- ✅ It is classically described as defending **parataḥ-prāmāṇyavāda**.
- ✅ In the contrast as taught for UPSC, Nyāya treats both the origin and ascertainment of validity as extrinsic relative to the mere occurrence of cognition.
- ✅ The cognition becomes accepted as valid by appeal to something outside itself.

#### Nyāya method of verification

- ✅ One route is **pravṛtti-sāmarthya**—successful practical activity based on the cognition.
- ✅ If acting on a cognition succeeds, the cognition is certified as valid.
- ✅ A second route is **saṃvādi-pratyakṣa**—a later confirming perception that agrees with the earlier cognition.
- ✅ Example: I see water in the distance; later I approach it, touch it, or drink it; success confirms the earlier cognition.
- ✅ Thus Nyāya ties validation to later confirmation.

#### Mīmāṃsā criticism

1. ✅ If action-success is needed before validity is granted, one must act before one is entitled to trust the cognition.
2. ✅ But that action itself presupposes some trust already.
3. ✅ So Nyāya secretly relies on what it officially postpones.
4. ✅ If a later cognition validates the earlier one, a further cognition must validate that validating cognition.
5. ✅ Regress returns.
6. ✅ Therefore later success can at most confirm or disclose validity, not generate it.
7. ✅ Mīmāṃsā distinguishes **validity itself** from our reflective **ascertainment** of it.
- ✅ This distinction weakens Nyāya's challenge.

### 2.6 Comparison with Sāṃkhya and Buddhism

#### Sāṃkhya

- ✅ Sāṃkhya is commonly represented as taking both validity and invalidity to be intrinsic.
- ✅ Both truth and falsity are disclosed from within cognition itself.
- ✅ Mīmāṃsā agrees with Sāṃkhya against Nyāya on the intrinsic side of validity.
- ✅ But it refuses to make invalidity equally intrinsic.
- ✅ For Mīmāṃsā, falsity does not self-announce at birth.
- ✅ It is discovered through later defeat.

#### Buddhist position

- ✅ Buddhist epistemologists are often presented in survey texts as taking validity to be extrinsic and invalidity intrinsic.
- ✅ Truth is linked to causal efficacy or successful function, while falsity reveals itself in failure.
- ✅ Mīmāṃsā rejects this asymmetry.
- ✅ If falsity were intrinsic, illusion should reveal itself as illusion immediately.
- ✅ But illusion deceives precisely because it does not do so.

### 2.7 Dream and mirage objection

#### Nyāya objection

- ❌ Dream-cognitions and mirage-cognitions arise spontaneously and feel normal to the subject, yet they are false.
- ❌ Therefore spontaneous occurrence cannot be enough for truth.
- ❌ If normal-looking cognitions can be false, why privilege intrinsic validity?

#### Mīmāṃsā reply

- ✅ The appearance of normalcy is not actual normalcy.
- ✅ In dreams there is an extrinsic defect such as exhaustion, sleep-state or internal disturbance.
- ✅ In mirages there is heated air and optical distortion.
- ✅ Hence those cases do not arise from fully normal truth-producing conditions.
- ✅ They are abnormal cases.
- ✅ Therefore the principle still stands: cognition produced under genuinely normal conditions is valid.

### 2.8 Default trust and practical life

- ✅ Human life would stop if every cognition had to wait for prior certification.
- ✅ Crossing a road, reading a page, recognising a friend, or obeying an instruction all presuppose immediate trust.
- ✅ Mīmāṃsā turns this practical inevitability into an epistemic principle.
- ✅ Default trust is not laziness; it is the structure of embodied cognition.
- ✅ Doubt is parasitic upon a more basic trust.

### 2.9 Presuppositions of the doctrine

- ✅ There is a real world.
- ✅ Objects are normally available to cognition.
- ✅ Defects are exceptional, not the norm.
- ✅ Later defeat is intelligible because first-order cognition has determinate content.
- ✅ Cognition is world-directed, not self-enclosed.

### 2.10 Distinctions to remember

- ✅ **Validity** is not the same as **certainty**.
- ✅ **Intrinsic validity** is not the same as **incorrigibility**.
- ✅ **Extrinsic invalidity** means falsity arises from some external defect, not that error is impossible.
- ✅ **Later confirmation** is not the source of truth but one way of detecting correction or stability.

### 2.11 Objection → Reply set

- ❌ **Objection:** Intrinsic validity licenses gullibility.
- ✅ **Reply:** No. The doctrine is defeasible and welcomes correction.

- ❌ **Objection:** Later correction proves original invalidity was already intrinsic.
- ✅ **Reply:** It proves only that a previously unnoticed defect operated.

- ❌ **Objection:** Why call a cognition valid before checking correspondence?
- ✅ **Reply:** Because correspondence-checking itself must begin from some trusted cognition.

### 2.12 Exam-ready conclusion

- ✅ Mīmāṃsā uses svataḥ-prāmāṇya to secure both ordinary knowledge and Vedic authority.
- ✅ Its core motives are anti-regress, epistemic economy and the lived structure of default trust.
- ✅ Always contrast it with Nyāya's parataḥ-prāmāṇya, and explicitly say: **default validity is not infallibility**.

## 3. THE SIX PRAMĀṆAS — BHĀṬṬA AND PRĀBHĀKARA

### 3.1 Why pramāṇa enumeration matters

- ✅ A pramāṇa list is a map of irreducible knowledge-sources.
- ✅ Schools differ because they disagree on whether a type of cognition can be reduced to another source.
- ✅ Mīmāṃsā accepts more pramāṇas than Nyāya or Sāṃkhya because it treats some forms of knowing as structurally unique.
- ✅ It is not counting casually; it is defending irreducibility.

### 3.2 Comparative count

| School | Accepted pramāṇas |
|---|---|
| **Sāṃkhya** | **pratyakṣa, anumāna, śabda** |
| **Nyāya** | **pratyakṣa, anumāna, upamāna, śabda** |
| **Prābhākara Mīmāṃsā** | **pratyakṣa, anumāna, upamāna, śabda, arthāpatti** |
| **Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā** | **pratyakṣa, anumāna, upamāna, śabda, arthāpatti, anupalabdhi** |

### 3.3 One-line definitions of the six

- ✅ **Pratyakṣa:** immediate knowledge produced by sense-object contact.
- ✅ **Anumāna:** mediate knowledge arising from a sign through known concomitance.
- ✅ **Upamāna:** knowledge of an unknown through similarity to a known.
- ✅ **Śabda:** valid verbal testimony, especially Vedic testimony regarding dharma.
- ✅ **Arthāpatti:** necessary postulation of an unseen fact to reconcile incompatible known facts.
- ✅ **Anupalabdhi:** knowledge of absence through non-apprehension of what would be apprehended if present.

### 3.4 Why Mīmāṃsā accepts more pramāṇas than Nyāya or Sāṃkhya

#### Statement

- ✅ A new pramāṇa is admitted only where a type of knowledge cannot be reduced without remainder to another pramāṇa.

#### Argument

1. ✅ Perception handles directly given objects.
2. ✅ Inference handles sign-based mediated knowledge.
3. ✅ Testimony handles verbally transmitted content.
4. ✅ Comparison handles resemblance-based identification.
5. ✅ Yet some cognitions arise neither through signhood nor positive direct perception.
6. ✅ Postulation resolves contradiction-like tension among known facts.
7. ✅ Absence-cognition, for Bhāṭṭa, discloses what is not there under proper conditions.
8. ✅ If these cannot be reduced without circularity, they deserve independent status.

#### Distinction

- ✅ Mīmāṃsā is not multiplying faculties psychologically.
- ✅ It is distinguishing logical structures of knowing.
- ✅ One pramāṇa is not identified by topic alone but by the way the cognition is generated.

### 3.5 Pratyakṣa in Mīmāṃsā

- ✅ Both Bhāṭṭa and Prābhākara accept perception.
- ✅ Both regard it as immediate and realist.
- ✅ It is the most basic disclosure of sensible objects.
- ✅ Yet they disagree over the self-disclosure of cognition within perception.
- ✅ **Prābhākara:** perception is self-luminous as part of tripuṭī-saṃvit.
- ✅ The object, subject and cognition are simultaneously revealed.
- ✅ **Bhāṭṭa:** the object is perceived, but the cognition itself is not self-luminous.
- ✅ The occurrence of cognition is inferred from the object's jñātatā.
- ✅ Thus the pramāṇa is shared, but its reflexive structure is disputed.

### 3.6 Anumāna in Mīmāṃsā

- ✅ Mīmāṃsā accepts inference in its standard sign-based form.
- ✅ Where smoke is seen and fire-smoke concomitance is known, fire is inferred.
- ✅ The school has no general hostility to inference.
- ✅ Its distinctive move is only to deny that all mediate cognition is inferential.
- ✅ Therefore one must not write as if Mīmāṃsā opposes anumāna.
- ✅ It opposes **reductionism about anumāna**.

### 3.7 Upamāna

- ✅ Upamāna is comparison-based knowledge.
- ✅ I am told that a gavaya resembles a cow.
- ✅ Later I see such an animal in the forest.
- ✅ Recognising “this is gavaya” occurs through comparison.
- ✅ Testimony alone did not identify the presently seen animal.
- ✅ Perception alone did not supply the classificatory bridge.
- ✅ So upamāna retains its place.

### 3.8 Śabda

- ✅ Śabda is verbal testimony.
- ✅ In ordinary matters it depends on a competent source.
- ✅ In matters of dharma the decisive verbal source is the Veda.
- ✅ Mīmāṃsā therefore gives śabda a special philosophical dignity.
- ✅ It is not a fallback after perception fails.
- ✅ It is the only route to supersensible duty.

### 3.9 Arthāpatti and anupalabdhi in the list

- ✅ Prābhākara includes arthāpatti because explanatory postulation is irreducible.
- ✅ Bhāṭṭa includes both arthāpatti and anupalabdhi.
- ✅ The former handles explanatory supplementation.
- ✅ The latter handles determinate knowledge of absence.
- ✅ These are the two pramāṇas by which Mīmāṃsā exceeds Nyāya's fourfold list.

### 3.10 Why memory is not a pramāṇa

#### Statement

- ✅ Memory is not a pramāṇa because it does not produce fresh object-knowledge.

#### Prābhākara argument

1. ✅ A pramāṇa must generate new cognition of an object.
2. ✅ Memory depends on an earlier cognition and latent impression.
3. ✅ It reproduces old content.
4. ✅ It does not reveal a new object as presently known.
5. ✅ Therefore it is not pramāṇa.

#### Nyāya agreement

- ✅ Nyāya agrees with this assessment.
- ✅ Memory presents what was already known.
- ✅ It is not a fresh disclosure but a re-presentation.
- ✅ Hence the 2024 PYQ appropriately pairs Prābhākara and Nyāya here.

#### Distinction

- ✅ Memory may be true.
- ✅ It may be useful.
- ✅ It may guide action.
- ✅ But usefulness is not enough for pramāṇa-status.
- ✅ Novel valid disclosure is required.

#### Example

- ✅ Seeing a temple now is perception.
- ✅ Recalling yesterday's visit is memory.
- ✅ The latter is not new knowledge of the temple.

#### Objection → Reply

- ❌ **Objection:** Memory can be the basis of successful conduct; why deny it?
- ✅ **Reply:** Because pramāṇa is defined by production of fresh knowledge, not by mere practical utility.

### 3.11 Summary formula

- ✅ Bhāṭṭa = six pramāṇas.
- ✅ Prābhākara = five pramāṇas.
- ✅ The difference turns on **anupalabdhi**.
- ✅ The whole list is governed by **irreducibility**, not by inflation.

## 4. ARTHĀPATTI — POSTULATION / PRESUMPTION

### 4.1 Statement

- ✅ **Arthāpatti** is the necessary postulation of an unperceived fact to remove incompatibility between already known facts.
- ✅ It is compelled explanatory supplementation.
- ✅ It is neither arbitrary conjecture nor standard inference.

### 4.2 Core structure

- ✅ Fact A is known.
- ✅ Fact B is also known.
- ✅ A and B cannot stand together unless C is admitted.
- ✅ Therefore C is postulated.
- ✅ The necessity lies in explanatory reconciliation.

### 4.3 Classical Devadatta case — worked in full

#### Statement

- ✅ “Fat Devadatta does not eat by day” yields “Devadatta eats at night.”

#### Argument

1. ✅ I know Devadatta is fat.
2. ✅ This may be known through perception.
3. ✅ I know Devadatta does not eat during the day.
4. ✅ This may be known through testimony or repeated observation.
5. ✅ Fatness is incompatible with complete absence of nourishment.
6. ✅ Therefore the two known facts produce explanatory tension.
7. ✅ To resolve the tension, I must posit that Devadatta eats at another time.
8. ✅ The natural and sufficient postulate is that he eats at night.
9. ✅ This night-eating is not directly perceived in the present case.
10. ✅ It is not supplied by memory either.
11. ✅ It is postulated because otherwise the known data remain inexplicable.

#### Presupposition

- ✅ Bodily conditions have intelligible causes.
- ✅ Fatness normally requires nourishment.
- ✅ Rational cognition seeks coherence among accepted facts.

#### Distinction

- ✅ The reasoning does not begin from a sign like smoke.
- ✅ It begins from tension between facts.
- ✅ The output is not simply “he eats,” but the minimally reconciling postulate.

#### Example

- ✅ This is the canonical Indian illustration and should be used in almost every answer.

#### Objection → Reply

- ❌ **Objection:** Why not simply suspend judgment instead of postulating?
- ✅ **Reply:** Because the facts as accepted press toward reconciliation; arthāpatti is the cognition produced by that necessity.

### 4.4 2024-style candidate example

#### Statement

- ✅ “A candidate never seen studying during the day tops the exam” can be explained by Bhāṭṭa through arthāpatti.

#### Bhāṭṭa analysis

1. ✅ The candidate tops the exam.
2. ✅ The candidate is never seen studying by day.
3. ✅ Exam success is incompatible with no preparation at all.
4. ✅ Therefore some unobserved preparation must be posited.
5. ✅ The natural postulate is study at night or otherwise outside observed daytime.

#### Nyāya reduction attempt

- ✅ Nyāya may frame this as inference:
  - wherever there is exam success, there is preparation;
  - this candidate has exam success;
  - therefore this candidate had preparation.
- ✅ But this misses the explanatory tension central to the original case.
- ✅ It also slides in a vyāpti too quickly.

### 4.5 Why arthāpatti is irreducible to anumāna

#### First argument: no independently established vyāpti

- ✅ In inference the relation between hetu and sādhya must be known beforehand.
- ✅ In arthāpatti, no independently established universal concomitance drives the transition.
- ✅ We do not already possess a universal rule “all fat non-day-eaters eat at night.”
- ✅ The cognition arises because facts would otherwise conflict.
- ✅ Therefore its engine is not vyāpti.

#### Second argument: direction of reasoning differs

- ✅ In inference the movement is from observed sign to unobserved signified.
- ✅ In arthāpatti the movement is from explanatory tension to reconciling postulate.
- ✅ The logic is not “this sign indicates that fact.”
- ✅ It is “without that postulate, these facts are inexplicable.”

#### Third argument: no pakṣa/hetu/sādhya structure

- ✅ Standard inference can be parsed into subject, reason and probandum.
- ✅ Arthāpatti lacks this form.
- ✅ There is no identified hetu present in a pakṣa from which sādhya is derived.
- ✅ There is a clash of accepted facts and a demanded supplementation.

### 4.6 Nyāya reduction answered as circular

- ❌ Nyāya says “fatness” is the hetu and “eating” the sādhya.
- ✅ Mīmāṃsā replies that the proposed vyāpti “whoever is fat eats” is not independently known.
- ✅ If one says it is known, one is already relying on arthāpatti-like reasoning from lived cases.
- ✅ Hence the reduction uses as premise what arthāpatti alone supplies.
- ✅ The reduction is circular.

### 4.7 Arthāpatti and explanatory necessity

- ✅ Arthāpatti resembles what modern philosophy might call inference to the best explanation, but the classical formulation is stronger.
- ✅ It is not merely “best among options.”
- ✅ It is the postulate without which known facts remain untenable.
- ✅ This necessity is why Mīmāṃsā grants it pramāṇa-status.

### 4.8 Distinctions to remember

- ✅ Arthāpatti ≠ mere guess.
- ✅ Arthāpatti ≠ probabilistic hunch.
- ✅ Arthāpatti ≠ standard anumāna.
- ✅ Arthāpatti = explanatory postulation compelled by otherwise unresolved incompatibility.

### 4.9 Presuppositions

- ✅ The world is intelligible.
- ✅ Contradictory accepted facts demand rational adjustment.
- ✅ Postulation can be a source of knowledge when compelled.
- ✅ Not all mediate knowledge is sign-based.

### 4.10 Example bank

- ✅ Fat Devadatta does not eat by day → eats at night.
- ✅ Candidate not studying by day yet topping → studies at another time.
- ✅ Person never seen leaving home yet present at office daily → must leave at some unobserved hour.
- ⚠️ Use canonical example first; modern examples only for illustration.

### 4.11 Objection → Reply set

- ❌ **Objection:** Any explanation could be re-labelled arthāpatti.
- ✅ **Reply:** No. Only those postulations that are necessary to resolve established incompatibility qualify.

- ❌ **Objection:** Why not reduce the whole process to hidden universal laws?
- ✅ **Reply:** Because those universals are often themselves abstracted from the very explanatory move in question.

### 4.12 Exam-ready conclusion

- ✅ Arthāpatti is accepted because explanatory compulsion is epistemically basic.
- ✅ The best answer always states the **Devadatta steps**, the **three arguments against reduction to anumāna**, and one **Nyāya reduction with circularity reply**.

## 5. ANUPALABDHI — NON-COGNITION / NON-APPREHENSION

### 5.1 Statement

- ✅ **Anupalabdhi** is the independent means of knowing absence through non-apprehension of what would be apprehended if present.
- ✅ This doctrine is characteristic of **Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā**.
- ✅ Prābhākara and Nyāya reject it as a separate pramāṇa.

### 5.2 Kumārila's argument structure

#### Statement

- ✅ We undeniably know absences, and that knowledge cannot be reduced without distortion.

#### Argument

1. ✅ We do have knowledge of absence, such as “there is no pot on the table.”
2. ✅ This cognition is definite and action-guiding.
3. ✅ It is not ordinary perception, because perception gives positive content.
4. ✅ It is not inference, because inference requires a positive sign.
5. ✅ It is not memory, comparison or testimony in the typical case.
6. ✅ Therefore an independent source is needed.
7. ✅ That source is **anupalabdhi**.

#### Presupposition

- ✅ Absence is a genuine object of cognition.
- ✅ Negative knowledge is not a mere linguistic shadow of positive perception.

#### Distinction

- ✅ Anupalabdhi is not simple ignorance.
- ✅ It is not “I failed to notice.”
- ✅ It is determinate cognition that something is absent.

#### Example

- ✅ Looking at a well-lit table and knowing “the book is not here.”

#### Objection → Reply

- ❌ **Objection:** How can a non-occurrence generate knowledge?
- ✅ **Reply:** Because under appropriate conditions, non-apprehension of the perceptible functions as a positive disclosure of absence.

### 5.3 Yogyānupalabdhi — the fitness condition

- ✅ The key limiting notion is **yogyatā**.
- ✅ The absent object must be the kind of thing that would be perceived if present.
- ✅ The locus must be suitable.
- ✅ The sense organ must be functioning.
- ✅ Conditions like light, distance and attention must be proper.
- ✅ Only then does non-perception justify absence-cognition.
- ✅ Thus “no pot on the table” is knowable if the table is clearly before me.
- ✅ But I cannot infer “there are no atoms here” from non-perception, because atoms are not perceptible objects.
- ✅ So the doctrine is carefully limited.
- ✅ This prevents reckless use of non-perception.

### 5.4 Why Bhāṭṭa denies reduction to perception

- ✅ Perception reveals what is positively present.
- ✅ A table, colour, shape and spatial spread are perceptually given.
- ✅ But “absence of pot” is not itself a positive sensible datum of that kind.
- ✅ Therefore absence-knowledge cannot simply be collapsed into perception of the locus.
- ✅ The perception of the empty table is one thing.
- ✅ The cognition that the pot is absent is another.

### 5.5 Why Bhāṭṭa denies reduction to inference

- ✅ Inference requires a positive sign and known concomitance.
- ✅ In absence-cognition, no independent positive sign is typically identified.
- ✅ If one says “not-seeing the pot” is the sign, one must explain why not-seeing has evidentiary force.
- ✅ That force already presupposes the relevance of non-apprehension under fit conditions.
- ✅ Thus inferential reduction is either strained or circular.

### 5.6 Prābhākara rejection

#### Statement

- ✅ Prābhākara rejects a separate pramāṇa of non-apprehension.

#### Argument

1. ✅ What we directly know is the locus.
2. ✅ We perceive the table as bare, not occupied by the pot.
3. ✅ No special faculty for “what is not there” need be posited.
4. ✅ Therefore ordinary perception is enough.

#### Distinction

- ✅ Prābhākara's move is an economy-thesis.
- ✅ He prefers not to multiply pramāṇas where a simpler explanation is available.

#### Objection → Reply

- ❌ **Objection:** Perception of a bare locus does not yet explain a determinate negative judgment.
- ✅ **Reply:** Prābhākara holds that the bare-locus cognition already carries the negative differentiation needed.

### 5.7 Nyāya rejection

- ✅ Nyāya also denies an independent anupalabdhi.
- ✅ It accepts **abhāva** but says absence is directly perceived.
- ✅ Looking at the floor, I perceive “pot-absence-on-the-floor.”
- ✅ Thus Nyāya and Prābhākara converge against Bhāṭṭa on independence, though their broader theories differ.

### 5.8 Ontological status of abhāva

- ✅ Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā accepts **abhāva** as real.
- ✅ It is not merely a feature of language.
- ✅ It has a **pratiyogin**, the counterpositive whose absence is involved.
- ✅ Thus “absence of pot” is a structured ontological content.
- ✅ Negative cognition therefore has a real object.
- ✅ This is crucial for the 2021-style question on the ontological status of abhāva.

### 5.9 Bhāṭṭa vs Prābhākara debate on abhāva

- ✅ **Bhāṭṭa:** abhāva is real and known by an independent pramāṇa, anupalabdhi.
- ✅ **Prābhākara:** what is known is simply the locus as unqualified by the absent object; no separate pramāṇa is needed.
- ✅ The 2025 PYQ turns on precisely this divergence.

### 5.10 Presuppositions and examples

- ✅ Suitable perceptual conditions matter.
- ✅ The absent item must be perceptible in principle.
- ✅ Negative cognition is woven into daily life: no chalk on the tray, no key in the drawer, no bus at the stop.
- ✅ Philosophy must explain what everyday life constantly presupposes.

### 5.11 Objection → Reply set

- ❌ **Objection:** If absence is real, reality becomes overcrowded with negatives.
- ✅ **Reply:** Bhāṭṭa accepts this ontological cost to preserve the reality of negative cognition.

- ❌ **Objection:** Non-perception is too subjective.
- ✅ **Reply:** Only **yogyānupalabdhi** under fit conditions counts, not careless overlooking.

### 5.12 Exam-ready conclusion

- ✅ Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā defends anupalabdhi because absence-cognition is irreducible.
- ✅ The decisive phrase is: **non-perception of what would be perceived if present**.
- ✅ Always contrast with Prābhākara and Nyāya.

## 6. THEORIES OF ERROR

### 6.1 Why error matters

- ✅ Any theory of intrinsic validity must explain illusion.
- ✅ If cognition is prima facie valid, what exactly goes wrong in false experience?
- ✅ This is why the Mīmāṃsā theory of error is central, not peripheral.

### 6.2 Prābhākara's Akhyāti / Vivekākhyāti

#### Statement

- ✅ Error is not a single false cognition but failure to discriminate between two valid cognitions.

#### Argument

1. ✅ In shell-silver illusion, the subject validly perceives **this shining object here**.
2. ✅ That perception is of the shell's present appearance.
3. ✅ The subject also recalls previously experienced silver.
4. ✅ That recollection is valid as memory.
5. ✅ The mistake lies in failure to distinguish these two cognitions.
6. ✅ Because of this non-discrimination, one behaves as if the present object were silver.
7. ✅ There is no need to posit a separate false cognition over and above these two.

#### Presupposition

- ✅ Perception and memory can operate together in confused synthesis.
- ✅ Practical action can arise from such non-discrimination.

#### Distinction

- ✅ Akhyāti does not mean total non-apprehension.
- ✅ It means non-apprehension of the **difference** between what is perceived and what is remembered.
- ✅ Hence the label **vivekākhyāti** is often more precise.

#### Example

- ✅ A shining shell on the beach evokes silver-memory, and the two are not discriminated.

#### Objection → Reply

- ❌ **Objection:** But the person says “this is silver,” which sounds like one positive false judgment.
- ✅ **Reply:** Prābhākara answers that the action and utterance can be explained by the fusion of two valid cognitions; a further false cognition is unnecessary.

### 6.3 How Akhyāti preserves svataḥ-prāmāṇya

- ✅ Every constituent cognition remains valid.
- ✅ The presentational cognition of “this” is valid.
- ✅ The memory of silver is valid.
- ✅ Error lies only in their conflation.
- ✅ Therefore no individual cognition needs to be labelled false.
- ✅ This is Prābhākara's distinctive way of protecting intrinsic validity.

### 6.4 Bhāṭṭa's Viparīta-khyāti

#### Statement

- ✅ Error is a genuine positive misapprehension in which one object is wrongly cognised as another.

#### Argument

1. ✅ In shell-silver illusion the shell is before the subject.
2. ✅ Due to bad light, distance, similarity of shine or sensory defect, the shell is miscognised.
3. ✅ A single erroneous cognition arises: “this is silver.”
4. ✅ The cognition is false.
5. ✅ Its falsity, however, is due to extrinsic defect, not to cognition's intrinsic nature.
6. ✅ Later correction occurs when the subject approaches and sees the shell clearly.
7. ✅ Invalidity is then known extrinsically.

#### Presupposition

- ✅ False cognition is possible.
- ✅ But its cause lies in defect, not in the normal operation of cognition.

#### Distinction

- ✅ Bhāṭṭa permits false cognition.
- ✅ He does not therefore abandon intrinsic validity.
- ✅ He merely says that validity belongs to cognition under normal conditions, whereas falsity belongs to defect-ridden conditions.

#### Example

- ✅ Mistaking nacre for silver.

#### Objection → Reply

- ❌ **Objection:** If false cognition exists, why call validity intrinsic at all?
- ✅ **Reply:** Because falsity is not basic. It arises through abnormal interference and is recognised through later defeat.

### 6.5 The 2022 puzzle clarified

- ✅ Both Bhāṭṭa and Prābhākara accept svataḥ-prāmāṇya.
- ✅ They differ because they locate error differently.
- ✅ **Prābhākara:** no false cognition, only failure of discrimination.
- ✅ **Bhāṭṭa:** yes, a false cognition can occur, but only due to defect and only be known false extrinsically.
- ✅ Hence one protects all constituent cognitions; the other protects the principle governing normal cognition.

### 6.6 Contrast table

| Theory | School | Shell-silver explained | Status of the "silver" |
|--------|--------|----------------------|----------------------|
| **Akhyāti / Vivekākhyāti** | **Prābhākara** | Two valid cognitions not discriminated | No false silver at all |
| **Viparīta-khyāti** | **Bhāṭṭa** | Shell positively miscognised as silver | Really wrong identification |
| **Anyathākhyāti** | **Nyāya** | Real silver elsewhere presented wrongly here | Real but displaced |
| **Anirvacanīya-khyāti** | **Advaita** | Silver is neither real nor unreal | Indescribable |

### 6.7 Contrast with Nyāya and Advaita

- ✅ **Nyāya:** error involves misplacement of a real object known elsewhere.
- ✅ **Advaita:** the illusory object is neither fully real nor unreal.
- ✅ Mīmāṃsā remains more epistemologically anchored and realist.
- ✅ It neither disperses the object elsewhere like Nyāya nor renders it metaphysically indeterminate like Advaita.

### 6.8 Action in illusion

- ✅ The behavioural fact of reaching for silver is central.
- ✅ Bhāṭṭa thinks this is best explained by a positive false judgment.
- ✅ Prābhākara thinks non-discrimination suffices.
- ✅ The debate therefore concerns both epistemology and philosophical psychology.

### 6.9 Presuppositions

- ✅ Error must be explainable without collapsing trust in cognition.
- ✅ Memory and perception can interact.
- ✅ Defect and correction are intelligible features of cognitive life.

### 6.10 Objection → Reply set

- ❌ **Objection to Akhyāti:** It underestimates the assertive force of illusion.
- ✅ **Reply:** Non-discrimination can explain assertive behaviour without adding a false cognition.

- ❌ **Objection to Viparīta-khyāti:** It grants too much to falsity.
- ✅ **Reply:** It grants falsehood only under abnormal conditions, preserving the default truth of cognition.

### 6.11 Exam-ready conclusion

- ✅ State shell-silver, then say exactly how each school preserves svataḥ-prāmāṇya.
- ✅ That is the core of a 20-marker.

## 7. TRIPUṬĪ-SAṂVIT AND JÑĀTATĀ

### 7.1 Why the issue arises

- ✅ Once object-knowledge is admitted, a second question appears: how is the cognition itself known?
- ✅ Is knowing self-revealing, inferred, or grasped in a second-order act?
- ✅ Mīmāṃsā and Nyāya diverge sharply here.

### 7.2 Prābhākara's tripuṭī-pratyakṣa

#### Statement

- ✅ Every cognition reveals simultaneously the knower, the known object and the cognition itself.

#### Argument

1. ✅ Cognition illuminates its object.
2. ✅ If cognition were not itself manifest, another cognition would be required to reveal it.
3. ✅ That second cognition would need a third, leading to regress.
4. ✅ Therefore cognition must reveal itself in the very act of revealing the object.
5. ✅ The subject is also disclosed as the one who knows.
6. ✅ Thus one act yields a threefold revelation: **jñātṛ, jñeya, jñāna**.

#### Presupposition

- ✅ Cognition is self-luminous (**svaprakāśa**).
- ✅ Awareness need not wait for a reflective duplicate to be present to itself.

#### Distinction

- ✅ This is not the claim that the self alone shines and the object is projected.
- ✅ It is the claim that cognition is immediately present in revealing the object.

#### Example

- ✅ When I know the pot, I know the pot, myself-as-knower and the very occurring knowledge in one integral act.

#### Objection → Reply

- ❌ **Objection:** Then why do we ever need reflective statements like “I know that I know”?
- ✅ **Reply:** Reflection is a later conceptual articulation of what was already manifest pre-reflectively.

### 7.3 Lamp analogy

- ✅ Prābhākara compares cognition to a lamp.
- ✅ A lamp reveals itself and other things at once.
- ✅ Similarly, cognition discloses itself and its object simultaneously.
- ✅ This analogy supports epistemic economy.
- ✅ It also blocks regress.

### 7.4 Bhāṭṭa's jñātatā theory

#### Statement

- ✅ Cognition is not self-luminous; it is known through the object's acquired property of **jñātatā**—being known.

#### Argument

1. ✅ An object after being cognised stands in a new relation: it is now known.
2. ✅ This new status is **jñātatā**.
3. ✅ Observing the object's knownness, we infer that a cognition has occurred.
4. ✅ Therefore awareness of cognition is inferential, not immediate.

#### Presupposition

- ✅ Objects can bear relational properties grounded in cognition.
- ✅ Such properties can serve as inferential bases.

#### Distinction

- ✅ Cognition here is like a painter who paints objects but does not paint himself.
- ✅ It reveals the world but not itself directly.
- ✅ Its occurrence is established through what it leaves behind in the object.

#### Example

- ✅ The pot is now known.
- ✅ From that knownness I infer that a pot-cognition must have occurred.

#### Objection → Reply

- ❌ **Objection:** Jñātatā seems like an unnecessary mysterious property.
- ✅ **Reply:** Bhāṭṭa accepts this cost to avoid the stronger thesis of self-luminosity.

### 7.5 Consequence of jñātatā view

- ✅ There is a temporal and logical gap between first-order knowing and second-order awareness of that knowing.
- ✅ I know first.
- ✅ I know-that-I-know afterward.
- ✅ This gives Bhāṭṭa a mediated theory of self-awareness.

### 7.6 Nyāya's anuvyavasāya

#### Statement

- ✅ Nyāya says cognition is known by a distinct second-order cognition, **anuvyavasāya**.

#### Argument

1. ✅ First-order cognition reveals the object.
2. ✅ A later inner awareness takes that cognition as object.
3. ✅ It has the form “I know the pot” or “I knew the pot.”
4. ✅ Therefore knowing-that-I-know is neither self-luminosity nor inference from jñātatā but second-order apprehension.

### 7.7 Comparison table

| School | How cognition is known | Self-luminous? | Mechanism |
|--------|----------------------|----------------|-----------|
| **Prābhākara** | Simultaneously revealed in every cognition | Yes | **Tripuṭī-saṃvit** |
| **Bhāṭṭa** | Inferred after the fact | No | **Jñātatā** → inference |
| **Nyāya** | By a second-order cognition | No | **Anuvyavasāya** |

### 7.8 Distinctions worth remembering

- ✅ Prābhākara = no metacognitive gap.
- ✅ Bhāṭṭa = inferential gap.
- ✅ Nyāya = reflective second-order gap.
- ✅ These are three distinct solutions to the same problem.

### 7.9 Objection → Reply set

- ❌ **Objection to Prābhākara:** Simultaneous revelation confuses subject, object and cognition.
- ✅ **Reply:** They are simultaneously disclosed, not ontologically collapsed.

- ❌ **Objection to Bhāṭṭa:** Ordinary self-awareness feels too immediate for inference.
- ✅ **Reply:** Immediacy in experience need not equal non-inferential structure.

- ❌ **Objection to Nyāya:** Second-order awareness threatens regress.
- ✅ **Reply:** Nyāya treats the higher-order awareness as sufficient without endless repetition.

### 7.10 Exam-ready conclusion

- ✅ A perfect short answer states the problem, names the three theories, and shows how each avoids infinite regress.

## 8. WORD-MEANING AND SENTENCE-MEANING

### 8.1 Why language is central

- ✅ Mīmāṃsā is a philosophy of Vedic interpretation.
- ✅ Therefore the problem of how words yield sentence meaning is foundational.
- ✅ The school cannot defend injunction unless it can explain linguistic comprehension.

### 8.2 Bhāṭṭa's Abhihitānvayavāda

#### Statement

- ✅ Words first express their own meanings; sentence-meaning results from the subsequent connection of those meanings.

#### Argument

1. ✅ Each word has denotative force.
2. ✅ On hearing the sentence, the hearer first grasps the isolated word-meanings.
3. ✅ These meanings are then linked through expectancy (**ākāṅkṣā**), compatibility (**yogyatā**) and contiguity (**sannidhi**).
4. ✅ The sentence-meaning is therefore a second-stage synthesis.

#### Presupposition

- ✅ Words can possess determinate lexical meaning independently enough to be first grasped.
- ✅ Sentence meaning is compositional.

#### Distinction

- ✅ Word-meaning is logically prior; sentence-meaning is derivative from its combination.
- ✅ Bricks first, wall later: this is the simplest analogy.

#### Example

- ✅ In “Bring the cow,” “bring” and “cow” are first separately understood, then related as action and object.

#### Objection → Reply

- ❌ **Objection:** Real understanding is too immediate for this two-step story.
- ✅ **Reply:** Bhāṭṭa can say the distinction is logical, not necessarily a slow temporal sequence.

### 8.3 Prābhākara's Anvitābhidhānavāda

#### Statement

- ✅ Words directly express meanings already connected in context; they do not first express isolated fragments.

#### Argument

1. ✅ In actual speech, words occur in sentential contexts.
2. ✅ Hearers understand them as related.
3. ✅ Therefore denotation is itself relational.
4. ✅ Sentence-meaning is directly conveyed in and through connected word-usage.

#### Presupposition

- ✅ Context is constitutive of meaning.
- ✅ Purely isolated lexical meaning is an abstraction from actual linguistic use.

#### Distinction

- ✅ Sentence-meaning is not built after independent denotation.
- ✅ It is what words express in connected use.
- ✅ Melody, not separately handled notes, is the better analogy.

#### Example

- ✅ “Cow” in the injunction is grasped as what is to be brought, not as an inert lexical item awaiting later attachment.

#### Objection → Reply

- ❌ **Objection:** Then word-meaning becomes parasitic and dictionaries become unintelligible.
- ✅ **Reply:** Dictionaries abstract from recurring contexts; primary understanding remains relational.

### 8.4 The debate sharpened

- ✅ Bhāṭṭa says Prābhākara makes the word too dependent on the sentence.
- ✅ Prābhākara says Bhāṭṭa artificially atomises living language.
- ✅ The dispute is about the order of semantic priority.
- ✅ It is not about whether syntax matters; both agree that it does.

### 8.5 Universals or particulars?

#### Statement

- ✅ Mīmāṃsā generally holds that words primarily denote the **universal (jāti)**.

#### Mīmāṃsā explanation

- ✅ The word “cow” applies to many individual cows because it primarily conveys **cow-ness** instantiated through a recognisable form.
- ✅ Particulars are reached through the universal.
- ✅ This explains repeatability of language.

#### Nyāya contrast

- ✅ Nyāya says words primarily denote the **individual (vyakti)** qualified by universal and form.
- ✅ Hence both jāti and vyakti are involved, but primacy differs.

#### Exam point

- ✅ Do not state that one school reaches only universals and the other only particulars.
- ✅ Both reach both; they differ on what is primary.

### 8.6 Shared semantic conditions

- ✅ Both schools accept **ākāṅkṣā**, **yogyatā** and **sannidhi** as central to sentence comprehension.
- ✅ The dispute is whether these connect prior meanings or belong to the very denotation-process itself.

### 8.7 Exam-ready conclusion

- ✅ Bhāṭṭa = **Abhihitānvayavāda**.
- ✅ Prābhākara = **Anvitābhidhānavāda**.
- ✅ Add the jāti-vyakti contrast with Nyāya for high-scoring answers.

## 9. AUTHORITY OF THE VEDA

### 9.1 Statement

- ✅ The Veda is authoritative because it is **apauruṣeya**, eternal and self-valid.

### 9.2 Apauruṣeyatva

- ✅ The Veda is not authored by any human.
- ✅ Nor is a divine author needed.
- ✅ Authorlessness protects it from personal defects.

### 9.3 Śabda-nityatva

- ✅ Words are eternal in the relevant Mīmāṃsā sense.
- ✅ Utterance manifests them rather than creates them.
- ✅ Vedic revelation is therefore not dependent on temporal composition.

### 9.4 Why authorlessness matters

#### Argument

1. ✅ A personal author may err.
2. ✅ A personal author may deceive.
3. ✅ A personal author may lack complete knowledge.
4. ✅ If Veda had an author, its authority would depend on proving that author's competence and sincerity.
5. ✅ By being authorless, the Veda is free from these defects.
6. ✅ Hence its validity is intrinsic.

### 9.5 Against Nyāya's God-guarantee

- ✅ Mīmāṃsā objects that Nyāya's route is cumbersome:
  - ✅ first prove God exists,
  - ✅ then prove God is omniscient,
  - ✅ then prove God intends truth.
- ✅ Each stage needs independent support.
- ✅ Authorlessness avoids this dependence.

### 9.6 Vidhi as the heart of Veda

- ✅ Veda primarily enjoins.
- ✅ Its core function is **vidhi/codanā**.
- ✅ Descriptive portions serve the injunctional purpose.
- ✅ Therefore the famous formula applies: **Codanā-lakṣaṇo'rtho dharmaḥ**.
- ✅ Dharma is what Vedic injunction reveals.

### 9.7 Presupposition, distinction, example

- ✅ Presupposition: dharma is supersensible.
- ✅ Distinction: Vedic authority rests on authorlessness, not speaker intention.
- ✅ Example: ritual obligations and unseen fruits are beyond perception.

### 9.8 Objection → Reply

- ❌ **Objection:** Communication requires a speaker's intention.
- ✅ **Reply:** Mīmāṃsā grounds meaning in the eternal relation of word and meaning, not in a historical author's psychology.

### 9.9 Exam-ready conclusion

- ✅ The Veda is self-valid because it is eternal, authorless and uniquely revelatory of dharma.

## 10. MĪMĀṂSĀ ETHICS — MINIMUM CONTEXT

### 10.1 Ethical frame

- ✅ Mīmāṃsā epistemology culminates in duty.
- ✅ The point of knowing is to know what ought to be done.

### 10.2 Vidhi and niṣedha

- ✅ **Vidhi** gives positive injunction.
- ✅ **Niṣedha** gives prohibition.
- ✅ Together they define the normative shape of dharma.

### 10.3 Apūrva

- ✅ **Apūrva** is the unseen potency produced by correctly performed ritual.
- ✅ It persists after the act.
- ✅ It matures into future fruit.
- ✅ This allows Mīmāṃsā to explain ritual efficacy without God as fruit-dispenser.

### 10.4 Bhāṭṭa and Prābhākara on motivation

- ✅ **Bhāṭṭa:** ritual is performed for the desired fruit such as heaven.
- ✅ The phala motivates the act.
- ✅ **Prābhākara:** the injunction itself creates obligation (**niyoga**).
- ✅ One ought to perform the act because the Veda commands it, not merely because one desires the fruit.
- ✅ This is a genuine Indian anticipation of the deontology/consequentialism contrast.

### 10.5 Objection → Reply

- ❌ **Objection:** Without God there is no moral authority.
- ✅ **Reply:** For Mīmāṃsā, Vedic injunction and apūrva are enough to secure binding duty and future fruit.

## 11. INTER-THINKER / INTER-SCHOOL DEBATES

### 11.1 Bhāṭṭa vs Prābhākara

- ✅ On the list of pramāṇas, Bhāṭṭa accepts six while Prābhākara accepts five.
- ✅ On absence, Bhāṭṭa posits anupalabdhi while Prābhākara reduces absence-cognition to ordinary perception.
- ✅ On error, Bhāṭṭa defends viparīta-khyāti while Prābhākara defends akhyāti/vivekākhyāti.
- ✅ On cognition of cognition, Bhāṭṭa uses jñātatā while Prābhākara uses tripuṭī-saṃvit.
- ✅ On language, Bhāṭṭa supports abhihitānvayavāda while Prābhākara supports anvitābhidhānavāda.
- ✅ On ethics, Bhāṭṭa foregrounds result-oriented motivation while Prābhākara stresses niyoga.
- ✅ These debates show that internal plurality does not break Mīmāṃsā's shared realism and Vedic orientation.

### 11.2 Mīmāṃsā vs Nyāya

- ✅ Intrinsic vs extrinsic validity is the largest epistemic contrast.
- ✅ Mīmāṃsā grants independent arthāpatti; Nyāya seeks to reduce it to anumāna.
- ✅ Bhāṭṭa grants independent anupalabdhi; Nyāya denies that need.
- ✅ Mīmāṃsā makes the Veda authorless; Nyāya often secures it through God's omniscience.
- ✅ In semantics Mīmāṃsā gives primacy to universal, Nyāya to qualified particular.
- ✅ On knowing cognition, Nyāya prefers anuvyavasāya.

### 11.3 Mīmāṃsā vs Advaita

- ✅ Mīmāṃsā is primarily injunction-centred.
- ✅ Advaita is liberation-centred and metaphysically non-dualist.
- ✅ In error theory Mīmāṃsā stays realist, while Advaita gives anirvacanīya status to the illusory object.
- ✅ Thus they use some common vocabulary but not the same ultimate philosophical horizon.

### 11.4 Mīmāṃsā vs Buddhism and Sāṃkhya

- ✅ Against Buddhism, Mīmāṃsā resists making validity derivative from pragmatic success.
- ✅ Against the Buddhist tendency to make falsity self-intimating, it says illusion does not present itself as false.
- ✅ With Sāṃkhya, it shares sympathy for intrinsic authority of cognition.
- ✅ But it differs by making invalidity extrinsic and by embedding epistemology in ritual hermeneutics.

## 12. CRITICISMS AND REPLIES

### 12.1 On svataḥ-prāmāṇya

- ❌ **Criticism:** intrinsic validity breeds gullibility.
- ✅ **Reply:** The doctrine concerns defeasible default warrant, not dogmatic finality.
- ✅ Error remains possible through extrinsic defect.
- ✅ Correction remains possible through later defeat.

### 12.2 On arthāpatti

- ❌ **Criticism:** postulation is just disguised inference.
- ✅ **Reply:** Arthāpatti lacks independently established vyāpti and standard hetu-sādhya form.
- ✅ It is driven by explanatory incompatibility, which Mīmāṃsā treats as epistemically irreducible.

### 12.3 On anupalabdhi

- ❌ **Criticism:** a non-event cannot reveal anything.
- ✅ **Reply:** Under conditions of yogyatā, non-apprehension of the perceptible is cognitively informative.
- ✅ Ordinary life constantly relies on such negative cognition.

### 12.4 On akhyāti and viparīta-khyāti

- ❌ **Criticism:** both theories seem strained compared with common sense.
- ✅ **Reply:** They are sophisticated attempts to save default trust in cognition while accounting for illusion.
- ✅ Prābhākara minimises false cognition; Bhāṭṭa localises it in extrinsic defect.

### 12.5 On Vedic authority

- ❌ **Criticism:** meaning without authorial intention is impossible.
- ✅ **Reply:** Mīmāṃsā locates semantic authority in the eternal relation of word and meaning, not in historical authorship.

## 13. COMMON UPSC TRAPS

1. ⚠️ Prābhākara accepts **five** pramāṇas, not six.
2. ⚠️ Svataḥ-prāmāṇya means default validity, not infallibility.
3. ⚠️ Nyāya is the chief opponent on prāmāṇya.
4. ⚠️ Arthāpatti is not mere anumāna.
5. ⚠️ Anupalabdhi is Bhāṭṭa's independent pramāṇa.
6. ⚠️ Akhyāti is failure of discrimination, not total ignorance.
7. ⚠️ Viparīta-khyāti allows false cognition but ties falsity to defect.
8. ⚠️ Tripuṭī-saṃvit belongs to Prābhākara.
9. ⚠️ Jñātatā belongs to Bhāṭṭa.
10. ⚠️ Mīmāṃsā privileges jāti primarily, not vyakti primarily.
11. ⚠️ The Veda is apauruṣeya, not God-authored.
12. ⚠️ Apūrva is the unseen link between ritual and future fruit.

## RESTORED 2018/2020 DOCTRINE DOSSIER

### Svayaṃprakāśavāda versus svataḥprāmāṇyavāda
- ✅ Self-luminosity concerns how cognition manifests itself and/or its object; intrinsic validity concerns cognition's prima facie truth and action-guiding authority before a defeater appears.
- ✅ Mīmāṃsā connects the two but they are not logically identical: one could hold cognition immediately manifest yet require external certification of truth, as Nyāya does in a different form.
- ⚠️ Advaita extends self-luminosity to consciousness as such, whereas Mīmāṃsā debates cognition episodes and validity within pramāṇa theory.
- ❓ **Objection → reply:** intrinsic validity seems unable to explain error. The reply is that falsity is extrinsically discovered through sublation or causal defect; cognition presents itself as valid until defeated.

### Why memory is not pramāṇa
- ✅ Memory depends on a prior cognition and reproduces an already known object; it lacks the novelty (*anadhigatatva*) required of valid knowledge-generating means.
