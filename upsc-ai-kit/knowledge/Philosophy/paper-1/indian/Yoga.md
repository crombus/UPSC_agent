# YOGA — Syllabus Item 17 (Paper I · Section B)

> **Syllabus (verbatim):** Yoga; Citta; Cittavrtti; Klesas; Samadhi; Kaivalya.
> **Evidence key:** ✅ canonical doctrine · ⚠️ analytical synthesis · ❓ contested
> **Placement:** Yoga is the practical-soteriological counterpart of Sāṃkhya in Paper I Indian philosophy. UPSC repeatedly asks citta, citta-vṛtti-nirodha, kleśa-removal, samādhi, kaivalya and the distinct place of Īśvara in **seśvara-sāṃkhya**.

## 0. ONE-SCREEN MAP

```text
YOGA = sāṃkhya-pravacana + Īśvara + disciplined soteriology
        │
Metaphysical base: 25 tattvas shared with Sāṃkhya
        │
Practical field: citta (mind-complex) becomes coloured by objects and reflects puruṣa
        │
Problem: cittavṛttis + kleśas → karma → vipāka → āśaya → punarjanma
        │
Method: abhyāsa + vairāgya; kriyā-yoga; aṣṭāṅga-yoga; Īśvara-praṇidhāna
        │
Culmination: samādhi → viveka-khyāti → para-vairāgya → dharmamegha-samādhi
        │
End: kaivalya = isolation/aloneness of puruṣa from prakṛti
```

> ⚠️ **Exam key:** Yoga is not merely posture-discipline. It is a full theory of mind, bondage, meditative cognition and liberation built on Sāṃkhya ontology but modified by the admission of **Īśvara** as **puruṣa-viśeṣa**.

## INTRODUCTION — YOGA AS SEŚVARA-SĀṂKHYA

- ✅ Classical Yoga is often described as **sāṃkhya-pravacana** or **seśvara-sāṃkhya**: it shares the metaphysical structure of [Sāṃkhya](./Samkhya.md) but adds a practical path and a theistic element.
- ✅ It accepts the basic dualism of **puruṣa** and **prakṛti**, and the standard **25 tattvas** of Sāṃkhya.
- ✅ What Yoga adds is a carefully graded **soteriology**: control of citta, attenuation of kleśas, meditative absorption, and final kaivalya.
- ✅ It also admits **Īśvara**, a **special puruṣa** untouched by affliction and karmic residue, thereby distinguishing itself from nirīśvara Sāṃkhya.
- ⚠️ Thus the school may be summed up as: **Sāṃkhya gives the metaphysical map; Yoga gives the psychology and method of release.**
- ⚠️ In UPSC answers, this introductory framing immediately prevents two confusions: Yoga is neither merely physical culture nor an Advaitic path of union with Brahman.

## 1. CITTA

### 1.1 Statement

- ✅ **Citta** (citta) is the internal organ or mind-complex through which cognition, affect, volition and meditative discipline become possible.
- ✅ In Yoga, citta is not merely **manas** in isolation; it functions as the unified internal apparatus comprising **buddhi**, **ahaṃkāra** and **manas**.
- ✅ Its material basis is from prakṛti and, more specifically, from the **sāttvika** aspect of **ahaṃkāra** in the Sāṃkhyan evolutionary scheme.
- ⚠️ Therefore citta is best understood as **the psychical instrument of experience**, not as the true self.

### 1.2 Constitution of citta

| Component within the functioning unity of citta | Primary role | Why Yoga includes it under citta |
|---|---|---|
| **Buddhi** | determinative intellect, ascertainment | decisive cognition, discrimination, resolve occur here |
| **Ahaṃkāra** | ego-principle, appropriation as "I" | ownership of states, doership and identification arise here |
| **Manas** | sensory co-ordination, doubt, oscillation | receives and arranges sense-data, moves between options |

- ✅ Though Sāṃkhya analytically distinguishes these, Yoga often treats them as one operative continuum called **citta**.
- ⚠️ This broad usage is philosophically useful because bondage and liberation both concern the **whole mental field**, not one isolated faculty.

### 1.3 Citta as reflective, not self-luminous in its own right

- ✅ Citta is a product of prakṛti and therefore **insentient** in itself.
- ✅ Yet because of its subtle, sāttvika transparency, it can **reflect** consciousness.
- ✅ The standard analogy is that of a **clear crystal placed near a flower**: the crystal appears coloured by the flower though it is itself colourless.
- ✅ Similarly, citta takes on the form and colour of objects placed before it, while the consciousness of **puruṣa** is reflected in it.
- ⚠️ The empirical self arises when the reflected consciousness in citta is mistaken for the intrinsic nature of citta itself.
- ⚠️ Hence Yoga's theory of mind is neither crude materialism nor idealism: citta is material-subtle, but consciousness belongs originally to puruṣa.

### 1.4 Misidentification of self with citta

- ✅ So long as citta is modified by objects, memories, desires, fears and conceptual constructions, the self appears entangled in them.
- ✅ The reflective relation causes bondage when **discriminative knowledge** is absent.
- ✅ A standard exam-worthy formulation is: **so long as there are changes and modifications in citta, the self is reflected therein and, lacking discriminative discernment, identifies itself with those modifications.**
- ⚠️ This is the psychological core of bondage in Yoga: the seer is confused with the seen because reflection masquerades as identity.

### 1.5 Why citta matters for soteriology

- ✅ Since bondage occurs through citta, liberation must also be worked out through citta.
- ✅ One does not destroy puruṣa's bondage by changing puruṣa itself, because puruṣa is eternally pure.
- ✅ Rather, one disciplines citta so completely that the false identification ceases.
- ⚠️ Yoga is therefore fundamentally a **therapy of attention, habit, impression and misidentification**.

### 1.6 Citta-bhūmis — the five levels or conditions of citta

- ✅ Yoga tradition classifies citta into **five bhūmis** or operative levels.
- ✅ These levels indicate the dominance of the guṇas and the fitness of the mind for yoga.

| Citta-bhūmi | Dominant guṇa-pattern | Basic character | Exam use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kṣipta** (ksipta) | **rajas** dominant | restless, scattered, thrown outward | ordinary agitated mind |
| **Mūḍha** (mudha) | **tamas** dominant | dull, torpid, stupefied | lethargic, clouded mind |
| **Vikṣipta** (viksipta) | mixed, with occasional **sattva** | intermittently focused but unstable | capable in bursts, still worldly |
| **Ekāgra** (ekagra) | **sattva** dominant | one-pointed, concentrated | fit for true yogic practice |
| **Niruddha** (niruddha) | vṛttis restrained | all modifications arrested | culmination of yogic discipline |

### 1.7 Explanation of the five bhūmis

#### (a) Kṣipta
- ✅ The mind runs from object to object under rajasic impulsion.
- ✅ There is activity, but not integration.
- ⚠️ Desire, worry, ambition and sensory dispersion dominate.

#### (b) Mūḍha
- ✅ The mind is overpowered by tamas.
- ✅ Torpor, delusion, inertia and confusion prevail.
- ⚠️ This state is not calmness but obscuration.

#### (c) Vikṣipta
- ✅ The mind can attend, infer, analyse and even sustain brief concentration, but cannot remain inwardly collected.
- ✅ Sattva flashes forth occasionally, yet rajas and tamas are not overcome.
- ⚠️ This is the condition of many intelligent worldly persons: capable of focus, but not yet spiritually integrated.

#### (d) Ekāgra
- ✅ The mind becomes one-pointed on a chosen support.
- ✅ Sattva predominates sufficiently for meditative absorption to deepen.
- ✅ This is one of the two genuinely yoga-fit states.

#### (e) Niruddha
- ✅ The modifications are fully restrained.
- ✅ This is not unconscious blankness but the consummation of disciplined inwardness.
- ✅ It is the citta-state correlated with the highest samādhi and the approach to kaivalya.

### 1.8 Which citta-bhūmis are fit for yoga?

- ✅ Only **ekāgra** and **niruddha** are fully fit for authentic yoga.
- ✅ **Kṣipta**, **mūḍha** and **vikṣipta** remain pre-yogic or only partially prepared stages.
- ⚠️ The transition from vikṣipta to ekāgra is decisive: the mind moves from occasional concentration to sustained one-pointedness.

### 1.9 PYQ application: scientist, devotee, self-realized yogi

- ✅ UPSC in 2019 used the citta-bhūmi classification in applied form.
- ✅ A **scientist** intensely focused on an empirical problem yet still directed outward and attached to worldly ends is best classified as **vikṣipta**.
- ✅ A **God-realized devotee** whose mind is one-pointedly centred upon Īśvara is **ekāgra**.
- ✅ A **self-realized yogi** in whom all modifications are fully stilled is **niruddha**.
- ⚠️ The subtlety is important: high intellectual focus does not itself amount to yogic restraint.

### 1.10 Citta in inter-school perspective

| School | What is the internal organ? | Key contrast with Yoga |
|---|---|---|
| [Sāṃkhya](./Samkhya.md) | buddhi, ahaṃkāra, manas analytically distinct | Yoga gathers them functionally as citta |
| [Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika](./Nyaya-Vaisesika.md) | **manas** as atomic internal sense | Yoga's citta is far broader than atomic manas |
| [Vedānta](./Vedanta.md) | antaḥkaraṇa under avidyā, with Brahman framework | Yoga retains plural puruṣas and no Brahman-doctrine |

### 1.11 Mini-conclusion on citta

- ⚠️ For UPSC, define citta as the **sāttvika psychical instrument composed of buddhi-ahaṃkāra-manas, reflecting puruṣa and becoming modified by objects**.
- ⚠️ Then show why Yoga insists on discipline: without citta-niyama, puruṣa continues to identify with borrowed mental states.

## 2. CITTAVṚTTI

### 2.1 Statement

- ✅ **Cittavṛtti** (cittavrtti) means a modification, fluctuation or operative mode of citta.
- ✅ Yoga holds that citta constantly assumes forms corresponding to objects, memories, errors, absences and verbal constructions.
- ✅ These modifications are the immediate field of bondage because the reflected self falsely identifies with them.

### 2.2 Foundational sūtra

- ✅ The defining statement of the system is: **"Yogaś-citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ"** — Yoga is the restraint of the modifications of citta.
- ✅ The immediately following implication is: **"tadā draṣṭuḥ svarūpe'vasthānam"** — then the seer abides in its own nature.
- ⚠️ These two aphorisms together explain the entire logic of Yoga: control is not an end in itself; it is required because only when vṛttis cease does puruṣa stand free of misidentification.
### 2.3 Why is cessation prescribed?

1. ✅ Citta, by its nature, takes the form of whatever it attends to.
2. ✅ Puruṣa's consciousness is reflected in citta.
3. ✅ In ignorance, the self appropriates these changing forms as "I know," "I suffer," "I desire," "I remember."
4. ✅ Therefore the stream of vṛttis sustains bondage.
5. ✅ Their restraint uncovers the already-free nature of the seer.
- ⚠️ Thus **nirodha** is revelatory, not destructive: it removes concealment, not consciousness.

### 2.4 The five vṛttis

- ✅ Patañjali classifies all modifications into **five kinds**.
- ✅ Each may be either **kliṣṭa** or **akliṣṭa** depending on whether it sustains affliction or aids release.

| Vṛtti | Definition | Example | Philosophical note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pramāṇa** | valid cognition | seeing smoke and correctly inferring fire | even true knowledge is still a modification |
| **Viparyaya** | error or misconception | taking nacre for silver | false cognition distorts conduct |
| **Vikalpa** | verbal or conceptual construction without real object | "the horn of a hare" | language can move without ontic referent |
| **Nidrā** | sleep as a positive mental mode grounded in absence-content | awareness later reported as "I slept" | even sleep is not non-mental blankness |
| **Smṛti** | memory | recollection of past pain or pleasure | retained traces re-present prior experience |

### 2.5 Pramāṇa as vṛtti

- ✅ Yoga does not deny valid cognition; it includes it as one kind of vṛtti.
- ✅ The accepted forms of pramāṇa are **pratyakṣa** (perception), **anumāna** (inference) and **āgama/śabda** (authoritative testimony).
- ✅ This aligns Yoga with the three-pramāṇa scheme of Sāṃkhya.
- ⚠️ Exam point: because even valid cognition is still a citta-modification, Yoga seeks not endless conceptual accumulation but eventual stilling.
- ⚠️ Cross-reference: see [Pramāṇa across schools](../_themes/Pramana-across-schools.md).

### 2.6 Viparyaya

- ✅ **Viparyaya** is false knowledge not corresponding to the object as it is.
- ✅ It includes perceptual and conceptual misapprehension.
- ⚠️ Since error reinforces wrong attachment and aversion, it is typically **kliṣṭa**.

### 2.7 Vikalpa

- ✅ **Vikalpa** is cognition produced by words alone where no corresponding object exists.
- ✅ It is not sheer nonsense; it has linguistic intelligibility without objective referent.
- ⚠️ Yoga is sensitive to the autonomous power of language to create mental worlds, anxieties, dogmas and imagined identities.

### 2.8 Nidrā

- ✅ Sleep is treated as a positive vṛtti rather than a mere absence.
- ✅ Later recollection — "I slept and knew nothing" — shows that sleep leaves a determinate mental trace.
- ⚠️ Philosophically, Yoga refuses to equate quiescence with liberation: mere sleep suppresses activity, but does not produce discriminative freedom.

### 2.9 Smṛti

- ✅ Memory is the re-presentation of past experience without complete loss of its prior content.
- ✅ Smṛti links past impressions to present identity and reaction.
- ⚠️ In practical psychology, memory feeds attachment, trauma, fear and habitual self-narration.

### 2.10 Kliṣṭa and akliṣṭa vṛttis

- ✅ Each of the five vṛttis can be either **kliṣṭa** (afflicted) or **akliṣṭa** (non-afflicted).
- ✅ **Kliṣṭa vṛttis** are those coloured by avidyā and the other kleśas and hence productive of bondage.
- ✅ **Akliṣṭa vṛttis** are those purified and redirected toward discrimination, detachment and liberation.
- ⚠️ For example, valid cognition of impermanence, disciplined scriptural reflection or recollection aiding practice may be akliṣṭa, while the same broad vṛtti-types in service of vanity or desire remain kliṣṭa.

### 2.11 Why this distinction matters

- ✅ Yoga does not begin by abolishing all cognition at once.
- ✅ It first transforms the quality of mental life, replacing afflicted modifications by non-afflicted ones.
- ⚠️ Akliṣṭa vṛttis are therapeutic instruments that ultimately subside once their work is done.

### 2.12 Means of citta-vṛtti-nirodha

- ✅ The basic means are **abhyāsa** (sustained practice) and **vairāgya** (detachment).
- ✅ Kriyā-yoga weakens afflictive forces.
- ✅ Aṣṭāṅga-yoga progressively disciplines the body-sense-mind complex.
- ✅ Īśvara-praṇidhāna offers a theistic aid to concentration and obstacle-removal.
- ⚠️ Thus nirodha is methodical, not abrupt.

### 2.13 Cittavṛtti and the 2023 demand

- ✅ The 2023 question asked both **what citta and its modifications are** and **why cessation is prescribed**.
- ⚠️ The ideal answer must therefore move in three steps: define citta, list the five vṛttis, and explain nirodha through the misidentification of puruṣa with citta.

### 2.14 Cittavṛtti and the 2024 demand

- ✅ The 2024 wording emphasized that as long as citta changes, the self is reflected therein and identifies itself with those changes in the absence of discriminative knowledge.
- ⚠️ This directly joins §1 and §2: the question is not about mental mechanics alone but about Yoga's full **soteriology of reflection, confusion and restraint**.

### 2.15 Mini-conclusion on cittavṛtti

- ⚠️ In exam language: **cittavṛttis are the dynamic forms assumed by the mind-complex; they must be restrained because their ongoing operation sustains the false identification of the seer with the seen.**

## 3. KLEŚAS

### 3.1 Statement

- ✅ **Kleśas** (klesas) are the afflictions that poison citta, generate karma and sustain saṃsāric bondage.
- ✅ They are deeper than surface thoughts: they are the causal colouring of mental life.
- ✅ Yoga lists **five kleśas**, with **avidyā** as the root.

### 3.2 The five kleśas at a glance

| Kleśa | Core meaning | Why it binds |
|---|---|---|
| **Avidyā** | ignorance, misapprehension of reality | root of all other afflictions |
| **Asmitā** | egoity, I-am-ness | confuses puruṣa with buddhi |
| **Rāga** | attachment | pursues pleasure and repetition |
| **Dveṣa** | aversion | flees pain and breeds hostility |
| **Abhiniveśa** | clinging to life / fear of death | instinctive self-preservation binds the stream |

### 3.3 Avidyā as the root

- ✅ Avidyā is specifically defined as mistaking:
  - the **non-eternal** for the eternal,
  - the **impure** for the pure,
  - the **painful** for the pleasant,
  - the **non-self** for the self.
- ✅ It is the matrix from which all other kleśas arise.
- ⚠️ Avidyā in Yoga is not merely lack of information; it is a pervasive existential misorientation.

### 3.4 Asmitā

- ✅ **Asmitā** is the confusion of the seer with the instrument of seeing.
- ✅ More precisely, it is the identification of **puruṣa** with **buddhi** or the internal organ.
- ⚠️ The ego says, "I think, I act, I suffer," because it cannot distinguish consciousness from its reflected vehicle.

### 3.5 Rāga

- ✅ **Rāga** is attachment arising from remembered pleasure.
- ✅ It pushes citta toward repetition and possession.
- ⚠️ In practical life, rāga keeps the mind outward-turned and dependent on conditions.

### 3.6 Dveṣa

- ✅ **Dveṣa** is aversion arising from remembered pain.
- ✅ It is the negative counterpart of rāga.
- ⚠️ Together rāga and dveṣa structure most reactive life: chasing the agreeable, fleeing the disagreeable.

### 3.7 Abhiniveśa

- ✅ **Abhiniveśa** is clinging to life, often expressed as fear of death.
- ✅ Yoga stresses that it is present **even in the learned**.
- ⚠️ This is a subtle and frequently tested point: abhiniveśa is not merely an intellectual error, but a deeply rooted instinctive attachment carried by latent impressions.
### 3.8 The four states of kleśas

- ✅ Kleśas do not always function at the same intensity.
- ✅ Tradition describes **four states**.

| State | Meaning | Illustration |
|---|---|---|
| **Prasupta** | dormant | seed-like, not presently manifest |
| **Tanu** | attenuated | weakened by discipline and insight |
| **Vicchinna** | interrupted | temporarily suppressed by opposing forces |
| **Udāra** | active, expanded | fully operative in conduct and reaction |

### 3.9 Explanation of the four states

#### (a) Prasupta
- ✅ The kleśa remains in latency.
- ⚠️ Dormancy is not destruction; favourable conditions can reactivate it.

#### (b) Tanu
- ✅ Through practice, austerity, meditation and discernment, afflictions are thinned out.
- ⚠️ This is the desirable middle state in disciplined progress.

#### (c) Vicchinna
- ✅ An affliction can be temporarily interrupted by the operation of another tendency.
- ⚠️ For example, attachment may seem absent only because fear is presently stronger.

#### (d) Udāra
- ✅ The kleśa becomes active and behaviour-shaping.
- ⚠️ Here it visibly governs speech, action and affect.

### 3.10 Kriyā-yoga as first therapy of kleśas

- ✅ Patañjali prescribes **kriyā-yoga** as consisting of:
  1. **tapas** — disciplined austerity or heat-generating effort,
  2. **svādhyāya** — self-study and/or recitation-study of sacred teaching,
  3. **Īśvara-praṇidhāna** — surrender or devotion to Īśvara.
- ✅ Its explicit purpose is to **attenuate kleśas** and prepare for samādhi.
- ⚠️ Thus Yoga begins not from abstract metaphysics alone but from deliberate re-education of habit and motive.

### 3.11 Dhyāna as deeper burning of kleśas

- ✅ Gross kleśa-expressions are weakened by ethical and practical discipline.
- ✅ Their subtle forms (**sūkṣma**) are burned through **dhyāna** and higher meditative absorption.
- ⚠️ This explains why mere moral restraint is insufficient: latent affliction survives unless its causal seed is scorched.

### 3.12 Causal chain of bondage

- ✅ The afflictive mechanism may be summarised as:

```text
Kleśas → Karma → Vipāka → Āśaya → Punarjanma
```

- ✅ **Kleśas** motivate action.
- ✅ Action deposits karmic force.
- ✅ Karma ripens as **vipāka** (fruition).
- ✅ The residual store becomes **āśaya** or karmic deposit.
- ✅ This fuels further birth and suffering.
- ⚠️ The chain shows why Yoga is not satisfied with external behaviour-management; it aims at the root-affliction itself.

### 3.13 Why removal of kleśas leads to kaivalya

1. ✅ Kleśas are the roots of karmically binding activity.
2. ✅ Without kleśas, karmic production and karmic reactivation cease.
3. ✅ Without karmic deposit, the machinery of repeated birth collapses.
4. ✅ As citta is purified and discrimination matures, samādhi becomes stable.
5. ✅ This culminates in kaivalya.
- ⚠️ The 2021 20-marker demanded precisely this transition from **diagnosis of affliction** to **logic of liberation**.

### 3.14 Kleśa and error across schools

| School | Root problem | Yoga's distinctive feature |
|---|---|---|
| Buddhism | avidyā + tṛṣṇā | Yoga keeps enduring puruṣa and citta-psychology |
| [Sāṃkhya](./Samkhya.md) | aviveka between puruṣa and prakṛti | Yoga adds meditative psychology and Īśvara |
| [Advaita Vedānta](./Vedanta.md) | avidyā/adhyāsa | Yoga has no Brahman-identity thesis |
| [Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika](./Nyaya-Vaisesika.md) | mithyājñāna | Yoga stresses affective affliction and meditative remedy |

### 3.15 Mini-conclusion on kleśas

- ⚠️ A compact answer-line: **kleśas are the afflictive roots of saṃsāra, with avidyā as the source and kriyā-yoga plus meditation as the means of attenuation and destruction.**

## 4. AṢṬĀṄGA YOGA

### 4.1 Why include aṣṭāṅga-yoga here?

- ✅ Though the syllabus clause names citta, cittavṛtti, kleśas, samādhi and kaivalya, the path linking them is the **aṣṭāṅga** discipline.
- ✅ UPSC often expects the mediating practical architecture in any fuller answer on Yoga.
- ⚠️ The point is philosophical significance, not physical drill.

### 4.2 The eight limbs

| Limb | Basic meaning | Group | Philosophical significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Yama** | restraints | **bahiraṅga** | social-ethical purification of conduct |
| **Niyama** | observances | **bahiraṅga** | positive self-cultivation and inner orientation |
| **Āsana** | posture | **bahiraṅga** | embodied steadiness enabling meditative work |
| **Prāṇāyāma** | regulation of breath-life-force | **bahiraṅga** | calms psycho-physical disturbance |
| **Pratyāhāra** | withdrawal of senses | **bahiraṅga** / bridge | de-links citta from compulsive object-pursuit |
| **Dhāraṇā** | concentration | **antaraṅga** | fixing attention on one locus |
| **Dhyāna** | meditation | **antaraṅga** | continuous flow toward the object |
| **Samādhi** | absorption | **antaraṅga** | consummated identity of attention with support |

### 4.3 Bahiraṅga and antaraṅga

- ✅ The first five are called **bahiraṅga** — external limbs.
- ✅ The last three are **antaraṅga** — internal limbs.
- ⚠️ "External" does not mean unimportant; it means preparatory with respect to the subtler operations of meditative consciousness.

### 4.4 Yama

- ✅ The **yamas** are the great restraints governing relation to others and to desire.
- ✅ The classical list is:
  1. **ahiṃsā** — non-violence,
  2. **satya** — truthfulness,
  3. **asteya** — non-stealing,
  4. **brahmacarya** — chastity / disciplined energy,
  5. **aparigraha** — non-possessiveness.
- ⚠️ Philosophically, yama reduces the rajasic-tāmasic turbulence that continually destabilizes citta.

### 4.5 Niyama

- ✅ The **niyamas** are positive observances:
  1. **śauca** — purity,
  2. **santoṣa** — contentment,
  3. **tapas** — disciplined austerity,
  4. **svādhyāya** — self-study/scriptural recitation,
  5. **Īśvara-praṇidhāna** — surrender to Īśvara.
- ⚠️ Niyama gives inward form to ethical discipline and opens the citta toward reflective and devotional stabilization.

### 4.6 Āsana

- ✅ Āsana in classical Yoga is primarily the establishment of a **steady and comfortable posture**.
- ⚠️ Its significance is not gymnastic display but freedom from bodily distraction.
- ⚠️ A body at war with itself becomes an obstacle to meditative one-pointedness.

### 4.7 Prāṇāyāma

- ✅ Prāṇāyāma regulates breathing and, through it, the psycho-physical economy.
- ⚠️ Breath functions as a hinge between body and citta; its refinement steadies attention and weakens agitation.

### 4.8 Pratyāhāra

- ✅ Pratyāhāra is withdrawal of the senses from their objects.
- ⚠️ It is not mutilation of sensory life but release from compulsive extroversion.
- ⚠️ This is the transition from ethical-embodied preparation to explicitly internal yogic work.

### 4.9 Dhāraṇā

- ✅ Dhāraṇā is concentration: binding citta to a chosen locus.
- ⚠️ It is the initial stabilization of attention and the first clear sign of **ekāgratā**.

### 4.10 Dhyāna

- ✅ Dhyāna is uninterrupted flow of cognition toward that same object.
- ⚠️ Unlike dhāraṇā's effortful fixing, dhyāna is sustained continuity.
- ✅ It is also a direct means for thinning and burning subtle kleśas.

### 4.11 Samādhi

- ✅ Samādhi is the culmination of meditative integration wherein the object alone shines with minimal interference from discursiveness.
- ⚠️ Because samādhi is itself a syllabus-term, it receives separate detailed treatment in §5 below.

### 4.12 Saṃyama

- ✅ **Saṃyama** is the integrated operation of **dhāraṇā + dhyāna + samādhi** on one object.
- ✅ It represents the inner technical core of Yoga's contemplative method.
- ⚠️ Philosophically, saṃyama is the progressive refinement of intentionality until cognition becomes translucent to the structure of its object.

### 4.13 Aṣṭāṅga-yoga as psychology of liberation

- ⚠️ The eight limbs are best read as a graded transformation:
  - ethics cures gross disturbance,
  - posture and breath calm embodiment,
  - withdrawal cuts sensory dispersion,
  - concentration and meditation unify the mental stream,
  - samādhi prepares discriminative liberation.
- ⚠️ Thus aṣṭāṅga-yoga operationalizes the abstract definition of Yoga as citta-vṛtti-nirodha.
## 5. SAMĀDHI

### 5.1 Statement

- ✅ **Samādhi** (samadhi) is meditative absorption in which citta becomes radically unified and transparent.
- ✅ It is not mere passivity; it is the highest refinement of attention and the threshold of liberating discernment.
- ✅ Classical Yoga distinguishes two main forms: **samprajñāta** and **asamprajñāta** samādhi.

### 5.2 Samprajñāta samādhi — with object / with seed

- ✅ **Samprajñāta samādhi** is cognitive or object-oriented absorption.
- ✅ A support remains; there is still a subtle seed of cognition.
- ✅ It is often called **sabīja** in contrast with the seedless culmination.
- ⚠️ The mind is profoundly stilled, but not utterly free of object-reference.

### 5.3 Four principal stages of samprajñāta samādhi

| Stage | Object-level | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Savitarka** | gross object | absorption with deliberative association, name-form-concept still present |
| **Nirvitarka** | gross object | gross object remains, but discursiveness around it falls away |
| **Savicāra** | subtle object | reflective absorption on subtle entities/principles |
| **Nirvicāra** | subtle object | even subtle discursiveness ceases; luminous subtle absorption |

### 5.4 Savitarka and nirvitarka

- ✅ In **savitarka**, concentration on a gross object retains traces of conceptuality, verbalization or deliberative support.
- ✅ In **nirvitarka**, the same gross-object level is held without gross conceptual superimposition.
- ⚠️ The movement is from meditative cognition still mixed with linguistic-associative content to greater immediacy.

### 5.5 Savicāra and nirvicāra

- ✅ **Savicāra** turns to subtle objects or principles and still retains a reflective mode.
- ✅ **Nirvicāra** transcends even that subtle reflective discursiveness.
- ⚠️ This marks the deepening interiorization of citta from gross phenomenal supports to subtle ontological supports.

### 5.6 Contested extension: sānanda and sāsmita

- ❓ Some commentators enumerate **sānanda** and **sāsmita** as additional stages or sub-levels of samprajñāta samādhi.
- ❓ Others treat them not as wholly separate stages but as dimensions or aspects within the broader progression.
- ⚠️ Safe UPSC handling: mention the canonical four-stage sequence, then note the commentator-variation regarding **sānanda** and **sāsmita**.

### 5.7 Asamprajñāta samādhi — beyond cognitive objectification

- ✅ **Asamprajñāta samādhi** is non-cognitive or supra-cognitive absorption in which all vṛttis cease.
- ✅ No object stands forth as support in the ordinary sense.
- ✅ At first only latent **saṃskāras** remain.
- ✅ Ultimately even these are burnt away.
- ✅ This is **nirbīja samādhi**, the seedless consummation.
- ⚠️ It is crucial not to confuse this with sleep or blank unconsciousness; it is the fruition of radical meditative restraint.

### 5.8 Relation between saṃskāra and nirodha

- ✅ Even when active vṛttis cease, latent impressions can remain as residual potency.
- ✅ Hence the path must proceed beyond surface quietness to the burning of even the subtle seeds.
- ⚠️ This explains why temporary calm or trance is not yet liberation.

### 5.9 Dharmamegha-samādhi

- ✅ **Dharmamegha-samādhi** is the highest samādhi immediately preceding kaivalya.
- ✅ The image is that of a **cloud of dharma** raining down the fullness of discriminative knowledge.
- ✅ At this level, even fascination with extraordinary knowledge or powers is relinquished.
- ⚠️ It marks the consummation of non-attachment: the yogin no longer seeks omniscience, attainment or display.

### 5.10 Samādhi and siddhis

- ✅ Higher concentrations may yield extraordinary capacities or **siddhis**.
- ⚠️ But Yoga warns that these are obstacles if appropriated by egoity or fascination.
- ⚠️ They belong to the side-effects of concentration, not to the essence of kaivalya.

### 5.11 Role of Īśvara in samādhi

- ✅ **Īśvara-praṇidhāna** is both a **niyama** and an independent means conducive to samādhi.
- ✅ Devotion or surrender to Īśvara stabilizes citta and helps overcome obstacles.
- ✅ Īśvara is also linked to the meditative use of **praṇava (OṂ)**.
- ⚠️ In Yoga, theism is practical: Īśvara functions as an unsurpassed focus, model and aid to concentration.

### 5.12 Obstacles and their removal

- ✅ Classical Yoga lists obstacles (**antarāyas**) such as disease, mental dullness, doubt, carelessness, laziness, sensuality, false perception, failure to attain stages and instability in attained stages.
- ✅ Īśvara-praṇidhāna is one of the important means of overcoming these.
- ⚠️ This is why questions on samādhi often open into the role of God: Yoga's God is not a creator-first doctrine but a **practice-enabling doctrine**.

### 5.13 Samādhi in the 2022 PYQ

- ✅ The 2022 question required the **nature and stages of samādhi** along with the **role of Īśvara**.
- ⚠️ Therefore a complete answer must cover the distinction between samprajñāta and asamprajñāta, the internal stages, dharmamegha as culmination, and devotional concentration on Īśvara as an aid.

### 5.14 Mini-conclusion on samādhi

- ⚠️ A sharp answer line: **samādhi is the progressive refinement of concentration from object-supported cognitive absorption to seedless cessation, with Īśvara-praṇidhāna as a powerful aid but not a substitute for discriminative realization.**

## 6. KAIVALYA

### 6.1 Exact meaning

- ✅ **Kaivalya** means aloneness, isolation or absolute distinctness.
- ✅ In Yoga it is the isolation of **puruṣa** from **prakṛti** and its evolutes.
- ✅ It is therefore **not** union with God, **not** merger into Brahman, and **not** mere relief from pain considered negatively.
- ⚠️ The precise meaning must always be guarded because UPSC frequently tests it through contrasts.

### 6.2 Why kaivalya is needed if puruṣa is already pure

- ✅ Puruṣa is indeed eternally pure, inactive and free.
- ✅ Yet through ignorance, citta's modifications are appropriated as self.
- ✅ Kaivalya is the cessation of this false relation at the level of experience and bondage.
- ⚠️ So liberation is not a production of a new essence; it is the ending of misidentification.

### 6.3 The process leading to kaivalya

```text
Abhyāsa + Vairāgya
        ↓
Kriyā-yoga and Aṣṭāṅga-yoga
        ↓
Attenuation and destruction of kleśas
        ↓
Stable samādhi
        ↓
Viveka-khyāti (discriminative discernment)
        ↓
Para-vairāgya (supreme detachment)
        ↓
Dharmamegha-samādhi
        ↓
Kaivalya
```

### 6.4 Viveka-khyāti

- ✅ **Viveka-khyāti** is discriminative knowledge discerning the absolute difference between puruṣa and prakṛti.
- ✅ It is the decisive knowledge that ends confusion.
- ⚠️ Yoga thus remains deeply cognitivist at the summit: samādhi is indispensable, but its soteriological function is to mature discriminative discernment.

### 6.5 Para-vairāgya

- ✅ **Para-vairāgya** is supreme detachment even from the highest subtle attainments.
- ✅ The yogin ceases to crave powers, experiences, pleasures, even exalted cognitive states.
- ⚠️ Without this, subtle egoity can survive advanced practice.

### 6.6 Dharmamegha and final transition

- ✅ In **dharmamegha-samādhi**, the cloud of dharma pours down the fullness of liberating discernment.
- ✅ The final barriers to isolation of puruṣa are removed.
- ⚠️ This is not a divine grace replacing effort; it is the ripened summit of perfected discipline and discernment.

### 6.7 Pratiprasava of the guṇas

- ✅ With liberation, the **guṇas** cease to function for the liberated puruṣa and resolve back toward their source.
- ✅ This return or involution is described as **pratiprasava** — reabsorption back into prakṛti.
- ✅ Citta, as a product of prakṛti, no longer binds; its function is exhausted.
- ⚠️ The metaphor is one of reversal of the evolutionary process for soteriological purposes.

### 6.8 Svarūpa-pratiṣṭhā

- ✅ Kaivalya may also be expressed as the abiding of consciousness in its own nature — **svarūpa-pratiṣṭhā**.
- ⚠️ This continues the logic of the foundational sūtra: when vṛttis cease, the seer stands in itself.
### 6.9 How Yoga kaivalya differs from Advaita mokṣa

| Yoga | Advaita Vedānta |
|---|---|
| many puruṣas | one Brahman as ultimate reality |
| liberation = isolation of puruṣa from prakṛti | liberation = realization of identity of ātman and Brahman |
| no Brahman-doctrine | Brahman central |
| no merger or non-dual identity | mokṣa is non-dual realization |

- ⚠️ Therefore kaivalya is not identity but **disentanglement**.
- ⚠️ Cross-reference: [Vedānta](./Vedanta.md).

### 6.10 How Yoga kaivalya differs from Nyāya apavarga

| Yoga kaivalya | Nyāya apavarga |
|---|---|
| positive isolation of pure consciousness in its own nature | often described as cessation of pain and bondage |
| rooted in puruṣa-prakṛti discrimination | rooted in true knowledge of categories and self |
| citta resolves, puruṣa shines | self remains but liberation is characterized more negatively |

- ⚠️ UPSC-safe line: **Nyāya emphasizes cessation of suffering; Yoga emphasizes the isolation and self-abidance of puruṣa.**
- ⚠️ Cross-reference: [Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika](./Nyaya-Vaisesika.md).

### 6.11 Īśvara and kaivalya

- ✅ Īśvara assists the aspirant but does **not grant** kaivalya as an external gift.
- ✅ Liberation finally depends on the yogin's own **viveka-khyāti**.
- ✅ Īśvara is the perfect model, object of devotion and remover of obstacles.
- ⚠️ This is central to the 2025 demand on the nature of God and his role in kaivalya.

### 6.12 What kaivalya is not

- ⚠️ Not **union with God** in the bhakti-school sense.
- ⚠️ Not **identity with Brahman** in the Advaitic sense.
- ⚠️ Not **annihilation of consciousness**.
- ⚠️ Not **mere painlessness** abstracted from the self's own luminosity.
- ⚠️ Not **destruction of mind as substance**; rather, cessation of its binding modifications and reabsorption of its function into prakṛti.

### 6.13 Mini-conclusion on kaivalya

- ⚠️ Best exam sentence: **kaivalya is the isolation of puruṣa from prakṛti, reached through discriminative discernment matured in samādhi, with guṇas returning to quiescence and consciousness abiding in its own form.**

## 7. ĪŚVARA IN YOGA

### 7.1 Why a separate section is necessary

- ✅ Though the syllabus clause names citta through kaivalya, UPSC repeatedly asks the role of **God** in Yoga.
- ✅ Yoga's distinctiveness from Sāṃkhya cannot be fully explained without Īśvara.
- ⚠️ Hence any mature note on Yoga must include a separate theistic section.

### 7.2 Definition: puruṣa-viśeṣa

- ✅ Yoga defines **Īśvara** as a **puruṣa-viśeṣa** — a special puruṣa.
- ✅ He is untouched by **kleśa**, **karma**, **vipāka** and **āśaya**.
- ✅ This is the standard doctrinal core of the Yoga-sūtra account.

### 7.3 Omniscience and timeless teacherhood

- ✅ In Īśvara lies the unsurpassed seed of omniscience.
- ✅ He is the teacher of the ancients and is not limited by time.
- ✅ He is denoted by **praṇava (OṂ)**.
- ⚠️ These predicates make Īśvara the ideal meditative and pedagogic principle in Yoga.

### 7.4 Īśvara is not creator in the Nyāya sense

- ✅ Yoga does **not** primarily present Īśvara as creator-God.
- ✅ **Prakṛti** remains the material basis of worldly evolution.
- ✅ Evolution proceeds through the puruṣa-prakṛti framework, not through divine creation ex nihilo.
- ⚠️ Therefore Yoga's God differs sharply from the Nyāya God as **nimitta-kāraṇa** or efficient cause of the world-order.

### 7.5 Īśvara is not a Vaiṣṇava-style personal saviour

- ✅ Though devotion to Īśvara is accepted, Yoga does not make him a saviour who arbitrarily grants liberation.
- ✅ The structure of liberation remains impersonal in one crucial sense: it depends on the yogin's own discriminative realization.
- ⚠️ This must be clearly said to avoid importing later devotional theologies into classical Yoga.

### 7.6 Positive role of Īśvara

| Role of Īśvara in Yoga | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Object of meditation** | one-pointed devotion stabilizes citta |
| **Aid through Īśvara-praṇidhāna** | surrender reduces egoity and attachment |
| **Remover of obstacles** | helps counter antarāyas in practice |
| **Perfect model** | exemplifies untouched, unsurpassed spiritual status |
| **Teacher principle** | anchor of timeless transmission and authority |

### 7.7 Why Yoga is called seśvara-sāṃkhya

- ✅ Because it preserves the Sāṃkhyan dualistic ontology while adding Īśvara.
- ✅ Hence the expression **seśvara-sāṃkhya** — Sāṃkhya with God.
- ⚠️ This is not a small decorative addition; it changes the psychological and devotional resources of the path while leaving the basic metaphysical dualism intact.

### 7.8 Īśvara and praṇava

- ✅ Meditation on **OṂ** as the designation of Īśvara is a canonical Yogic practice.
- ⚠️ This links theology, language, sound and contemplative concentration in a single discipline.

### 7.9 Īśvara, samādhi and kaivalya together

- ✅ Īśvara aids samādhi by offering a privileged support for concentration.
- ✅ Through obstacle-removal and ego-surrender, the aspirant's path to discriminative knowledge is accelerated.
- ✅ But kaivalya still follows from **viveka-khyāti**, not divine fiat.
- ⚠️ Thus the most accurate exam formula is: **Īśvara is instrumental, not constitutive, in liberation.**

### 7.10 The 2025 demand

- ✅ The 2025 question asked for the **nature of God and his role in kaivalya**.
- ⚠️ A strong answer should therefore establish four points:
  1. Īśvara is a **special puruṣa**.
  2. He is untouched by kleśa-karma-vipāka-āśaya.
  3. He aids concentration, removes obstacles and is the object of praṇava-mediated devotion.
  4. He does not create the world in the Nyāya sense and does not bestow kaivalya independently of viveka-khyāti.

### 7.11 Mini-conclusion on Īśvara

- ⚠️ In Yoga, God is best understood as **the supreme contemplative support within a Sāṃkhyan metaphysical frame**.

## 8. INTER-THINKER / INTER-SCHOOL DEBATES

### 8.1 Yoga and Sāṃkhya

| Issue | Sāṃkhya | Yoga |
|---|---|---|
| Metaphysical base | puruṣa-prakṛti dualism; 25 tattvas | same basic ontology |
| God | nirīśvara | **seśvara** — admits Īśvara |
| Main stress | explanatory metaphysics, viveka-jñāna | practical psychology, meditative discipline, samādhi |
| Liberation | kaivalya through discrimination | kaivalya through discrimination matured by yoga-practice |

- ⚠️ Yoga does not reject Sāṃkhya; it operationalizes it.

### 8.2 Yoga and Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika

| Issue | Yoga | Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika |
|---|---|---|
| Internal organ | citta as broad mind-complex | atomic manas plus self-substance |
| God | special puruṣa, aid to meditation | creator/efficient cause and moral governor |
| Liberation | kaivalya as isolation of puruṣa | apavarga as cessation of suffering |
| Valid knowledge | 3 pramāṇas | 4 pramāṇas |

### 8.3 Yoga and Vedānta

| Issue | Yoga | Advaita Vedānta |
|---|---|---|
| Ultimate reality | many puruṣas + prakṛti | non-dual Brahman |
| Bondage | confusion of puruṣa with citta/prakṛti | avidyā/adhyāsa causing Brahman-self ignorance |
| Liberation | isolation | identity-realization |
| God | meditative aid, special puruṣa | saguṇa Brahman at vyāvahārika level |

### 8.4 Yoga and Buddhism

| Issue | Yoga | Buddhism |
|---|---|---|
| Self | enduring puruṣa | nairātmyavāda/no permanent self |
| Affliction | kleśas rooted in avidyā | kleśa/tṛṣṇā/avidyā nexus |
| Meditation | samādhi aimed at puruṣa-prakṛti discrimination | meditation aimed at insight into impermanence, non-self, dependent origination |
| Liberation | kaivalya | nirvāṇa |

### 8.5 Yoga and philosophy of religion

- ⚠️ Yoga's Īśvara is useful for cross-paper deployment with [Notions of God](../../paper-2/philosophy-of-religion/Notions-of-God.md) and [Religious Experience](../../paper-2/philosophy-of-religion/Religious-Experience.md).
- ⚠️ It represents a distinctive Indian model of God as contemplative principle rather than creator-first metaphysical absolute.
## 9. CRITICISMS AND REPLIES

### 9.1 Criticism: If puruṣa is inactive and pure, how can it ever be bound?

- ⚠️ The objection is that an inactive witness cannot genuinely enter bondage.
- ✅ Yoga's reply parallels Sāṃkhya: bondage belongs not to puruṣa intrinsically but to misidentification through reflection in citta.
- ⚠️ Thus bondage is phenomenological and epistemic, not an actual alteration of puruṣa.

### 9.2 Criticism: If citta is insentient, how can cognition occur at all?

- ⚠️ The challenge targets the possibility of experience in an unconscious psychical instrument.
- ✅ Yoga replies that citta becomes luminous by reflecting puruṣa, just as a crystal appears lit by what is near it.
- ⚠️ The school thus explains cognition through a relation of reflected consciousness rather than by attributing intrinsic consciousness to matter.

### 9.3 Criticism: Why count valid cognition itself as a vṛtti if it helps liberation?

- ⚠️ If pramāṇa is true, why restrain it?
- ✅ Yoga replies that even true cognition is still a modification.
- ✅ It is useful in its **akliṣṭa** form as a ladder toward viveka-khyāti.
- ⚠️ But what serves as means at one stage must also finally be transcended.

### 9.4 Criticism: Is asamprajñāta samādhi just unconsciousness or sleep?

- ✅ No.
- ✅ Sleep is itself one vṛtti, while asamprajñāta samādhi is cessation of vṛttis through discipline.
- ⚠️ The difference lies in both genesis and value: sleep is natural obscuration; samādhi is luminous restraint.

### 9.5 Criticism: Why add Īśvara if Sāṃkhya ontology already explains liberation?

- ⚠️ This is the classic challenge from nirīśvara Sāṃkhya.
- ✅ Yoga's answer is pragmatic and soteriological: Īśvara offers a unique focus of devotion, removes obstacles and supports samādhi.
- ⚠️ The addition is justified by practical efficacy rather than by an overhaul of metaphysical fundamentals.

### 9.6 Criticism: If liberation depends on viveka-khyāti, does God become redundant?

- ✅ Not redundant, but non-final.
- ✅ Īśvara is a privileged aid, not the substitute for discernment.
- ⚠️ This balanced answer is exactly what UPSC expects.

### 9.7 Criticism: Is Yoga's liberation merely negative isolation?

- ⚠️ Some critics see kaivalya as a stark separation lacking fullness.
- ✅ Yoga replies that the positive side of liberation is **svarūpa-pratiṣṭhā** — abiding in pure consciousness free from all alien admixture.
- ⚠️ Its positivity is not ecstatic union but unconfused self-abidance.

### 9.8 Criticism: Is dhyāna sufficient without ethics?

- ✅ Yoga's answer is no.
- ✅ Without yama and niyama, meditative power can coexist with affliction and egoity.
- ⚠️ This is why the system places ethics at the foundation of contemplative success.

## 10. COMMON UPSC TRAPS

1. ⚠️ **Kaivalya ≠ Advaita mokṣa ≠ Nyāya apavarga.** Yoga's kaivalya is isolation of puruṣa, not identity with Brahman and not merely cessation of pain.
2. ⚠️ **Yoga's Īśvara is not a creator-God in the Nyāya sense.** Do not import nimitta-kāraṇa language uncritically.
3. ⚠️ **Citta is not identical with manas alone.** It includes buddhi, ahaṃkāra and manas functioning together.
4. ⚠️ **Samprajñāta and asamprajñāta must not be reversed.** Samprajñāta still has object/seed; asamprajñāta is seedless.
5. ⚠️ **Kliṣṭa/akliṣṭa qualifies vṛttis, not kleśas.** Kleśas are inherently afflictive.
6. ⚠️ **Citta-vṛtti-nirodha does not mean violent destruction of mind.** It means cessation of modifications so the seer abides in itself.
7. ⚠️ **Abhiniveśa is present even in the wise.** Do not reduce it to mere doctrinal ignorance.
8. ⚠️ **Sleep is a vṛtti, not liberation.** Nidrā is not samādhi.
9. ⚠️ **Yoga shares Sāṃkhya's tattva framework.** Do not write as if Yoga has a wholly separate cosmology.
10. ⚠️ **Īśvara helps but does not grant kaivalya from outside.** Liberation finally follows viveka-khyāti.

## 13. ANSWER ARCHITECTURE

### 13.1 Ten-marker (10 marks)

```text
Open with the exact doctrinal definition.
Name the canonical list/stages.
Give one causal or soteriological explanation.
Add one inter-school distinction or one trap-avoidance line.
Close by answering the directive directly.
```

**Best use-cases:** samādhi stages; citta-bhūmis; role of Īśvara; meaning of kaivalya.

### 13.2 Fifteen-marker (15 marks)

```text
Frame the issue: Yoga as psychology of liberation within Sāṃkhya metaphysics.
Define the central term.
List and explain the canonical sub-types or stages.
Show why the doctrine matters for bondage/liberation.
Contrast with at least one rival school or one common confusion.
Conclude with one evaluative sentence.
```

**Best use-cases:** citta-vṛtti-nirodha; nature of God and role in kaivalya; citta and self-reflection.

### 13.3 Twenty-marker (20 marks)

```text
Intro: locate Yoga as seśvara-sāṃkhya.
Exposition: define and classify the demanded concept fully.
Mechanism: show the causal chain from ignorance/kleśa to bondage.
Therapy: explain kriyā-yoga / aṣṭāṅga / samādhi / viveka-khyāti as needed.
Comparison: differentiate from Sāṃkhya, Advaita or Nyāya.
Critique and reply: add at least one live objection.
Conclusion: kaivalya as the school's final soteriological meaning.
```

**Best use-case:** kleśas and their removal leading to kaivalya.

### 13.4 Directive-word cues for Yoga answers

| Directive | What examiner wants in Yoga topic |
|---|---|
| **Explain** | definition + canonical classification + brief mechanism |
| **Discuss** | doctrine plus significance and interpretive spread |
| **Critically examine** | doctrine + objection + reply + guarded verdict |
| **Comment** | brief thesis-focused evaluative note |
| **Distinguish** | axis-wise comparison: Yoga vs Sāṃkhya / Advaita / Nyāya |
