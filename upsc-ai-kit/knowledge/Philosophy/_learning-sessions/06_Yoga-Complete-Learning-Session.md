# YOGA - Syllabus Item 17 (Paper I - Section B)

> **Syllabus (verbatim):** Yoga; Citta; Cittavrtti; Klesas; Samadhi; Kaivalya.
> **Evidence key:** ✅ canonical doctrine · ⚠️ analytical synthesis · ❓ contested
> **Placement:** Yoga is the practical-soteriological counterpart of Sāṃkhya in Paper I Indian philosophy. UPSC repeatedly asks citta, citta-vṛtti-nirodha, kleśa-removal, samādhi, kaivalya and the distinct place of Īśvara in **seśvara-sāṃkhya**.

## 0. ONE-SCREEN MAP

```text
YOGA = sāṃkhya-pravacana + Īśvara + disciplined soteriology
        │
Metaphysical base: 25 tattvas shared with Sāṃkhya
        │
Practical field: citta (mind-complex) becomes coloured by objects and reflects puruṣa
        │
Problem: cittavṛttis + kleśas → karma → vipāka → āśaya → punarjanma
        │
Method: abhyāsa + vairāgya; kriyā-yoga; aṣṭāṅga-yoga; Īśvara-praṇidhāna
        │
Culmination: samādhi → viveka-khyāti → para-vairāgya → dharmamegha-samādhi
        │
End: kaivalya = isolation/aloneness of puruṣa from prakṛti
```

> ⚠️ **Exam key:** Yoga is not merely posture-discipline. It is a full theory of mind, bondage, meditative cognition and liberation built on Sāṃkhya ontology but modified by the admission of **Īśvara** as **puruṣa-viśeṣa**.

## INTRODUCTION — YOGA AS SEŚVARA-SĀṂKHYA

- ✅ Classical Yoga is often described as **sāṃkhya-pravacana** or **seśvara-sāṃkhya**: it shares the metaphysical structure of [Sāṃkhya](./Samkhya.md) but adds a practical path and a theistic element.
- ✅ It accepts the basic dualism of **puruṣa** and **prakṛti**, and the standard **25 tattvas** of Sāṃkhya.
- ✅ What Yoga adds is a carefully graded **soteriology**: control of citta, attenuation of kleśas, meditative absorption, and final kaivalya.
- ✅ It also admits **Īśvara**, a **special puruṣa** untouched by affliction and karmic residue, thereby distinguishing itself from nirīśvara Sāṃkhya.
- ⚠️ Thus the school may be summed up as: **Sāṃkhya gives the metaphysical map; Yoga gives the psychology and method of release.**
- ⚠️ In UPSC answers, this introductory framing immediately prevents two confusions: Yoga is neither merely physical culture nor an Advaitic path of union with Brahman.

## 1. CITTA

### 1.1 Statement

- ✅ **Citta** (citta) is the internal organ or mind-complex through which cognition, affect, volition and meditative discipline become possible.
- ✅ In Yoga, citta is not merely **manas** in isolation; it functions as the unified internal apparatus comprising **buddhi**, **ahaṃkāra** and **manas**.
- ✅ Its material basis is from prakṛti and, more specifically, from the **sāttvika** aspect of **ahaṃkāra** in the Sāṃkhyan evolutionary scheme.
- ⚠️ Therefore citta is best understood as **the psychical instrument of experience**, not as the true self.

### 1.2 Constitution of citta

| Component within the functioning unity of citta | Primary role | Why Yoga includes it under citta |
|---|---|---|
| **Buddhi** | determinative intellect, ascertainment | decisive cognition, discrimination, resolve occur here |
| **Ahaṃkāra** | ego-principle, appropriation as "I" | ownership of states, doership and identification arise here |
| **Manas** | sensory co-ordination, doubt, oscillation | receives and arranges sense-data, moves between options |

- ✅ Though Sāṃkhya analytically distinguishes these, Yoga often treats them as one operative continuum called **citta**.
- ⚠️ This broad usage is philosophically useful because bondage and liberation both concern the **whole mental field**, not one isolated faculty.

### 1.3 Citta as reflective, not self-luminous in its own right

- ✅ Citta is a product of prakṛti and therefore **insentient** in itself.
- ✅ Yet because of its subtle, sāttvika transparency, it can **reflect** consciousness.
- ✅ The standard analogy is that of a **clear crystal placed near a flower**: the crystal appears coloured by the flower though it is itself colourless.
- ✅ Similarly, citta takes on the form and colour of objects placed before it, while the consciousness of **puruṣa** is reflected in it.
- ⚠️ The empirical self arises when the reflected consciousness in citta is mistaken for the intrinsic nature of citta itself.
- ⚠️ Hence Yoga's theory of mind is neither crude materialism nor idealism: citta is material-subtle, but consciousness belongs originally to puruṣa.

### 1.4 Misidentification of self with citta

- ✅ So long as citta is modified by objects, memories, desires, fears and conceptual constructions, the self appears entangled in them.
- ✅ The reflective relation causes bondage when **discriminative knowledge** is absent.
- ✅ A standard exam-worthy formulation is: **so long as there are changes and modifications in citta, the self is reflected therein and, lacking discriminative discernment, identifies itself with those modifications.**
- ⚠️ This is the psychological core of bondage in Yoga: the seer is confused with the seen because reflection masquerades as identity.


### 1.4A Reflection (pratibimba/chāyā) presuppositions

**Statement.** ✅ Yoga's reflection theory says that **citta** appears conscious because it stands near **puruṣa**, while puruṣa appears bound because its light is reflected in citta; this is a **pratibimba** (pratibimba; reflection) or **chāyā** (chaya; shadow-image) model, not a literal alteration of puruṣa.

**Argument.**
1. ✅ **Puruṣa** is **apariṇāmin** (aparinamin; unchanging) and therefore cannot literally act, suffer, acquire qualities or undergo modification.
2. ✅ **Citta** is a product of **prakṛti** and is therefore **jaḍa** (jada; insentient), yet its sattva-predominance makes it transparent, subtle and capable of mirroring.
3. ✅ Since an inactive consciousness cannot enter causal contact like one physical object striking another, knowledge requires a reflection/proximity model rather than a crude causal-contact model.
4. ✅ Bondage is therefore the confusion of **asmitā** and **sva-svāmi-śakti** (sva-svami-shakti; the power of the owned and the owner): citta's borrowed consciousness is misread as selfhood, and puruṣa's witnesshood is misread as agency.
5. ⚠️ The soteriological task is not to change puruṣa but to end the cognitive misattribution by purifying and restraining citta.

**Presupposition.** ✅ The model presupposes the Sāṃkhya-Yoga dualism of two irreducible orders: conscious but inactive puruṣa, and active but insentient prakṛti/citta.

**Example.** ✅ The crystal (**sphaṭika**) near a red flower seems red without itself becoming the flower; ⚠️ similarly, a magnet and iron image helps exam writing: mere presence (**sannidhi**) can explain movement on the iron/citta side without action on the magnet/puruṣa side.

**Objection.** ⚠️ A reflection normally requires some real relation between mirror and original. If puruṣa and prakṛti are ontologically disjoint, how can inactive puruṣa be "reflected" in citta at all?

**Reply.** ✅ Yoga replies through **sannidhi-mātra** (sannidhi-matra; mere proximity): the transformation is entirely on the citta side; puruṣa neither acts nor changes. ⚠️ Yet the objection retains residual force, because Advaita and Nyāya can press the classic problem of how two radically different principles can explain one experience.

### 1.5 Why citta matters for soteriology

- ✅ Since bondage occurs through citta, liberation must also be worked out through citta.
- ✅ One does not destroy puruṣa's bondage by changing puruṣa itself, because puruṣa is eternally pure.
- ✅ Rather, one disciplines citta so completely that the false identification ceases.
- ⚠️ Yoga is therefore fundamentally a **therapy of attention, habit, impression and misidentification**.

### 1.6 Citta-bhūmis — the five levels or conditions of citta

- ✅ Yoga tradition classifies citta into **five bhūmis** or operative levels.
- ✅ These levels indicate the dominance of the guṇas and the fitness of the mind for yoga.

| Citta-bhūmi | Dominant guṇa-pattern | Basic character | Exam use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kṣipta** (ksipta) | **rajas** dominant | restless, scattered, thrown outward | ordinary agitated mind |
| **Mūḍha** (mudha) | **tamas** dominant | dull, torpid, stupefied | lethargic, clouded mind |
| **Vikṣipta** (viksipta) | mixed, with occasional **sattva** | intermittently focused but unstable | capable in bursts, still worldly |
| **Ekāgra** (ekagra) | **sattva** dominant | one-pointed, concentrated | fit for true yogic practice |
| **Niruddha** (niruddha) | vṛttis restrained | all modifications arrested | culmination of yogic discipline |

### 1.7 Explanation of the five bhūmis

#### (a) Kṣipta
- ✅ The mind runs from object to object under rajasic impulsion.
- ✅ There is activity, but not integration.
- ⚠️ Desire, worry, ambition and sensory dispersion dominate.

#### (b) Mūḍha
- ✅ The mind is overpowered by tamas.
- ✅ Torpor, delusion, inertia and confusion prevail.
- ⚠️ This state is not calmness but obscuration.

#### (c) Vikṣipta
- ✅ The mind can attend, infer, analyse and even sustain brief concentration, but cannot remain inwardly collected.
- ✅ Sattva flashes forth occasionally, yet rajas and tamas are not overcome.
- ⚠️ This is the condition of many intelligent worldly persons: capable of focus, but not yet spiritually integrated.

#### (d) Ekāgra
- ✅ The mind becomes one-pointed on a chosen support.
- ✅ Sattva predominates sufficiently for meditative absorption to deepen.
- ✅ This is one of the two genuinely yoga-fit states.

#### (e) Niruddha
- ✅ The modifications are fully restrained.
- ✅ This is not unconscious blankness but the consummation of disciplined inwardness.
- ✅ It is the citta-state correlated with the highest samādhi and the approach to kaivalya.

### 1.8 Which citta-bhūmis are fit for yoga?

- ✅ Only **ekāgra** and **niruddha** are fully fit for authentic yoga.
- ✅ **Kṣipta**, **mūḍha** and **vikṣipta** remain pre-yogic or only partially prepared stages.
- ⚠️ The transition from vikṣipta to ekāgra is decisive: the mind moves from occasional concentration to sustained one-pointedness.

### 1.9 PYQ application: scientist, devotee, self-realized yogi

- ✅ UPSC in 2019 used the citta-bhūmi classification in applied form.
- ✅ A **scientist** intensely focused on an empirical problem yet still directed outward and attached to worldly ends is best classified as **vikṣipta**.
- ✅ A **God-realized devotee** whose mind is one-pointedly centred upon Īśvara is **ekāgra**.
- ✅ A **self-realized yogi** in whom all modifications are fully stilled is **niruddha**.
- ⚠️ The subtlety is important: high intellectual focus does not itself amount to yogic restraint.

### 1.10 Citta in inter-school perspective

| School | What is the internal organ? | Key contrast with Yoga |
|---|---|---|
| [Sāṃkhya](./Samkhya.md) | buddhi, ahaṃkāra, manas analytically distinct | Yoga gathers them functionally as citta |
| [Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika](./Nyaya-Vaisesika.md) | **manas** as atomic internal sense | Yoga's citta is far broader than atomic manas |
| [Vedānta](./Vedanta.md) | antaḥkaraṇa under avidyā, with Brahman framework | Yoga retains plural puruṣas and no Brahman-doctrine |

### 1.11 Mini-conclusion on citta

- ⚠️ For UPSC, define citta as the **sāttvika psychical instrument composed of buddhi-ahaṃkāra-manas, reflecting puruṣa and becoming modified by objects**.
- ⚠️ Then show why Yoga insists on discipline: without citta-niyama, puruṣa continues to identify with borrowed mental states.

## 2. CITTAVṚTTI

### 2.1 Statement

- ✅ **Cittavṛtti** (cittavrtti) means a modification, fluctuation or operative mode of citta.
- ✅ Yoga holds that citta constantly assumes forms corresponding to objects, memories, errors, absences and verbal constructions.
- ✅ These modifications are the immediate field of bondage because the reflected self falsely identifies with them.

### 2.2 Foundational sūtra

- ✅ The defining statement of the system is: **"Yogaś-citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ"** — Yoga is the restraint of the modifications of citta.
- ✅ The immediately following implication is: **"tadā draṣṭuḥ svarūpe'vasthānam"** — then the seer abides in its own nature.
- ⚠️ These two aphorisms together explain the entire logic of Yoga: control is not an end in itself; it is required because only when vṛttis cease does puruṣa stand free of misidentification.
### 2.3 Why is cessation prescribed?

1. ✅ Citta, by its nature, takes the form of whatever it attends to.
2. ✅ Puruṣa's consciousness is reflected in citta.
3. ✅ In ignorance, the self appropriates these changing forms as "I know," "I suffer," "I desire," "I remember."
4. ✅ Therefore the stream of vṛttis sustains bondage.
5. ✅ Their restraint uncovers the already-free nature of the seer.
- ⚠️ A safe image is the clear pool: when ripples settle, the water reflects without distortion; when cittavṛttis settle, puruṣa is no longer misread through mental turbulence.
- ⚠️ Thus **nirodha** is revelatory, not destructive: it removes concealment, not consciousness.

### 2.4 The five vṛttis

- ✅ Patañjali classifies all modifications into **five kinds**.
- ✅ Each may be either **kliṣṭa** or **akliṣṭa** depending on whether it sustains affliction or aids release.

| Vṛtti | Definition | Example | Philosophical note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pramāṇa** | valid cognition | seeing smoke and correctly inferring fire | even true knowledge is still a modification |
| **Viparyaya** | error or misconception | taking nacre for silver | false cognition distorts conduct |
| **Vikalpa** | verbal or conceptual construction without real object | "the horn of a hare" | language can move without ontic referent |
| **Nidrā** | sleep as a positive mental mode grounded in absence-content | awareness later reported as "I slept" | even sleep is not non-mental blankness |
| **Smṛti** | memory | recollection of past pain or pleasure | retained traces re-present prior experience |

### 2.5 Pramāṇa as vṛtti

- ✅ Yoga does not deny valid cognition; it includes it as one kind of vṛtti.
- ✅ The accepted forms of pramāṇa are **pratyakṣa** (perception), **anumāna** (inference) and **āgama/śabda** (authoritative testimony).
- ✅ This aligns Yoga with the three-pramāṇa scheme of Sāṃkhya.
- ⚠️ Exam point: because even valid cognition is still a citta-modification, Yoga seeks not endless conceptual accumulation but eventual stilling.
- ⚠️ Cross-reference: see [Pramāṇa across schools](../_themes/Pramana-across-schools.md).

### 2.6 Viparyaya

- ✅ **Viparyaya** is false knowledge not corresponding to the object as it is.
- ✅ It includes perceptual and conceptual misapprehension.
- ⚠️ Since error reinforces wrong attachment and aversion, it is typically **kliṣṭa**.

### 2.7 Vikalpa

- ✅ **Vikalpa** is cognition produced by words alone where no corresponding object exists.
- ✅ It is not sheer nonsense; it has linguistic intelligibility without objective referent.
- ⚠️ Yoga is sensitive to the autonomous power of language to create mental worlds, anxieties, dogmas and imagined identities.

### 2.8 Nidrā

- ✅ Sleep is treated as a positive vṛtti rather than a mere absence.
- ✅ Later recollection — "I slept and knew nothing" — shows that sleep leaves a determinate mental trace.
- ⚠️ Philosophically, Yoga refuses to equate quiescence with liberation: mere sleep suppresses activity, but does not produce discriminative freedom.

### 2.9 Smṛti

- ✅ Memory is the re-presentation of past experience without complete loss of its prior content.
- ✅ Smṛti links past impressions to present identity and reaction.
- ⚠️ In practical psychology, memory feeds attachment, trauma, fear and habitual self-narration.

### 2.10 Kliṣṭa and akliṣṭa vṛttis

- ✅ Each of the five vṛttis can be either **kliṣṭa** (afflicted) or **akliṣṭa** (non-afflicted).
- ✅ **Kliṣṭa vṛttis** are those coloured by avidyā and the other kleśas and hence productive of bondage.
- ✅ **Akliṣṭa vṛttis** are those purified and redirected toward discrimination, detachment and liberation.
- ⚠️ For example, valid cognition of impermanence, disciplined scriptural reflection or recollection aiding practice may be akliṣṭa, while the same broad vṛtti-types in service of vanity or desire remain kliṣṭa.

### 2.11 Why this distinction matters

- ✅ Yoga does not begin by abolishing all cognition at once.
- ✅ It first transforms the quality of mental life, replacing afflicted modifications by non-afflicted ones.
- ⚠️ Akliṣṭa vṛttis are therapeutic instruments that ultimately subside once their work is done.

### 2.12 Means of citta-vṛtti-nirodha

- ✅ The basic means are **abhyāsa** (sustained practice) and **vairāgya** (detachment).
- ✅ Kriyā-yoga weakens afflictive forces.
- ✅ Aṣṭāṅga-yoga progressively disciplines the body-sense-mind complex.
- ✅ Īśvara-praṇidhāna offers a theistic aid to concentration and obstacle-removal.
- ⚠️ Thus nirodha is methodical, not abrupt.

### 2.13 Cittavṛtti and the 2023 demand

- ✅ The 2023 question asked both **what citta and its modifications are** and **why cessation is prescribed**.
- ⚠️ The ideal answer must therefore move in three steps: define citta, list the five vṛttis, and explain nirodha through the misidentification of puruṣa with citta.

### 2.14 Cittavṛtti and the 2024 demand

- ✅ The 2024 wording emphasized that as long as citta changes, the self is reflected therein and identifies itself with those changes in the absence of discriminative knowledge.
- ⚠️ This directly joins §1 and §2: the question is not about mental mechanics alone but about Yoga's full **soteriology of reflection, confusion and restraint**.

### 2.15 Mini-conclusion on cittavṛtti

- ⚠️ In exam language: **cittavṛttis are the dynamic forms assumed by the mind-complex; they must be restrained because their ongoing operation sustains the false identification of the seer with the seen.**


### 2.16 Citta-pariṇāma — transformation of the mental substrate

**Statement.** ✅ **Citta-pariṇāma** (citta-parinama; transformation of citta) is Yoga's account of how the mind-complex really changes across practice without destroying its continuity as a prakṛtic substrate.

**Argument.**
1. ✅ The Vibhūti-pāda tradition recognises three specifically yogic transformations of citta: **nirodha-pariṇāma**, **samādhi-pariṇāma** and **ekāgratā-pariṇāma**.
2. ✅ **Nirodha-pariṇāma** is the transformation in which impressions of restraint increasingly overpower impressions of emergence; the mind becomes habituated to cessation rather than dispersion.
3. ✅ **Samādhi-pariṇāma** is the transformation from many-pointedness to unified absorption; the distracting multiplicity of contents weakens while one object becomes dominant.
4. ✅ **Ekāgratā-pariṇāma** is the transformation of one-pointed continuity, where similar cognitive moments arise and subside in an unbroken stream toward the same object.
5. ✅ Yoga also accepts a general threefold transformation of any substrate: **dharma-pariṇāma** (dharma-parinama; change of property), **lakṣaṇa-pariṇāma** (laksana-parinama; change of temporal character) and **avasthā-pariṇāma** (avastha-parinama; change of condition or state).
6. ✅ These transformations presuppose a **dharmin** (dharmin; persisting substrate) that undergoes changes of property, temporal phase and condition without becoming sheer momentary flashes.
7. ⚠️ This is philosophically necessary because Yoga inherits Sāṃkhya **pariṇāmavāda** (parinamavada; real transformation): citta really changes, and the path's graded progress would be unintelligible if there were no continuing mental substrate.

**Presupposition.** ✅ The doctrine presupposes that prakṛti and its evolutes are real and transformable, while puruṣa remains unmodified witness-consciousness.

**Example.** ⚠️ The same lake can be stormy, gradually calming, and finally still; the states differ, yet the lake-substrate persists. Likewise citta can pass from kṣipta to ekāgra to niruddha without becoming a new self.

**Objection.** ⚠️ A Buddhist **kṣaṇikavādin** (ksanikavadin; momentarist) may argue that only a stream of momentary events is needed; a persisting citta-substrate is an unnecessary fiction.

**Reply.** ✅ Yoga answers that practice, memory, saṃskāra-continuity and stable meditative acquisition require more than disconnected flashes. ⚠️ Both Yoga and Buddhism admit ceaseless change, but Yoga retains a persisting prakṛtic substrate, whereas Buddhist **kṣaṇikavāda** (ksanikavada; momentariness) denies enduring mental substance.

| Comparison | Yoga's position | Exam contrast |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ Yoga's **pariṇāma** vs Buddhist **kṣaṇikavāda** | ✅ Real change occurs in a continuing citta/dharmin. | ⚠️ Buddhism can explain flow without a substrate; Yoga needs substrate for saṃskāra and graded discipline. |
| ✅ Yoga's **pariṇāma** vs Advaita **vivartavāda** (vivartavada; apparent transformation) | ✅ Change in citta/prakṛti is real, not merely apparent. | ⚠️ Advaita makes ultimate reality non-transforming Brahman; Yoga keeps real prakṛtic change and many puruṣas. |

## 3. KLEŚAS

### 3.1 Statement

- ✅ **Kleśas** (klesas) are the afflictions that poison citta, generate karma and sustain saṃsāric bondage.
- ✅ They are deeper than surface thoughts: they are the causal colouring of mental life.
- ✅ Yoga lists **five kleśas**, with **avidyā** as the root.

### 3.2 The five kleśas at a glance

| Kleśa | Core meaning | Why it binds |
|---|---|---|
| **Avidyā** | ignorance, misapprehension of reality | root of all other afflictions |
| **Asmitā** | egoity, I-am-ness | confuses puruṣa with buddhi |
| **Rāga** | attachment | pursues pleasure and repetition |
| **Dveṣa** | aversion | flees pain and breeds hostility |
| **Abhiniveśa** | clinging to life / fear of death | instinctive self-preservation binds the stream |

### 3.3 Avidyā as the root

- ✅ Avidyā is specifically defined as mistaking:
  - the **non-eternal** for the eternal,
  - the **impure** for the pure,
  - the **painful** for the pleasant,
  - the **non-self** for the self.
- ✅ It is the matrix from which all other kleśas arise.
- ⚠️ Avidyā in Yoga is not merely lack of information; it is a pervasive existential misorientation.

### 3.4 Asmitā

- ✅ **Asmitā** is the confusion of the seer with the instrument of seeing.
- ✅ More precisely, it is the identification of **puruṣa** with **buddhi** or the internal organ.
- ⚠️ The ego says, "I think, I act, I suffer," because it cannot distinguish consciousness from its reflected vehicle.

### 3.5 Rāga

- ✅ **Rāga** is attachment arising from remembered pleasure.
- ✅ It pushes citta toward repetition and possession.
- ⚠️ In practical life, rāga keeps the mind outward-turned and dependent on conditions.

### 3.6 Dveṣa

- ✅ **Dveṣa** is aversion arising from remembered pain.
- ✅ It is the negative counterpart of rāga.
- ⚠️ Together rāga and dveṣa structure most reactive life: chasing the agreeable, fleeing the disagreeable.

### 3.7 Abhiniveśa

- ✅ **Abhiniveśa** is clinging to life, often expressed as fear of death.
- ✅ Yoga stresses that it is present **even in the learned**.
- ⚠️ This is a subtle and frequently tested point: abhiniveśa is not merely an intellectual error, but a deeply rooted instinctive attachment carried by latent impressions.
### 3.8 The four states of kleśas

- ✅ Kleśas do not always function at the same intensity.
- ✅ Tradition describes **four states**.

| State | Meaning | Illustration |
|---|---|---|
| **Prasupta** | dormant | seed-like, not presently manifest |
| **Tanu** | attenuated | weakened by discipline and insight |
| **Vicchinna** | interrupted | temporarily suppressed by opposing forces |
| **Udāra** | active, expanded | fully operative in conduct and reaction |

### 3.9 Explanation of the four states

#### (a) Prasupta
- ✅ The kleśa remains in latency.
- ⚠️ Dormancy is not destruction; favourable conditions can reactivate it.

#### (b) Tanu
- ✅ Through practice, austerity, meditation and discernment, afflictions are thinned out.
- ⚠️ This is the desirable middle state in disciplined progress.

#### (c) Vicchinna
- ✅ An affliction can be temporarily interrupted by the operation of another tendency.
- ⚠️ For example, attachment may seem absent only because fear is presently stronger.

#### (d) Udāra
- ✅ The kleśa becomes active and behaviour-shaping.
- ⚠️ Here it visibly governs speech, action and affect.

### 3.10 Kriyā-yoga as first therapy of kleśas

- ✅ Patañjali prescribes **kriyā-yoga** as consisting of:
  1. **tapas** — disciplined austerity or heat-generating effort,
  2. **svādhyāya** — self-study and/or recitation-study of sacred teaching,
  3. **Īśvara-praṇidhāna** — surrender or devotion to Īśvara.
- ✅ Its explicit purpose is to **attenuate kleśas** and prepare for samādhi.
- ⚠️ Thus Yoga begins not from abstract metaphysics alone but from deliberate re-education of habit and motive.

### 3.11 Dhyāna as deeper burning of kleśas

- ✅ Gross kleśa-expressions are weakened by ethical and practical discipline.
- ✅ Their subtle forms (**sūkṣma**) are burned through **dhyāna** and higher meditative absorption.
- ⚠️ This explains why mere moral restraint is insufficient: latent affliction survives unless its causal seed is scorched.

### 3.12 Causal chain of bondage

- ✅ The afflictive mechanism may be summarised as:

```text
Kleśas → Karma → Vipāka → Āśaya → Punarjanma
```

- ✅ **Kleśas** motivate action.
- ✅ Action deposits karmic force.
- ✅ Karma ripens as **vipāka** (fruition).
- ✅ The residual store becomes **āśaya** or karmic deposit.
- ✅ This fuels further birth and suffering.
- ⚠️ The chain shows why Yoga is not satisfied with external behaviour-management; it aims at the root-affliction itself.


### 3.13 Karma and vipāka — kleśa-rooted moral causation

**Statement.** ✅ **Karmāśaya** (karmashaya; karmic deposit or stock) is the subtle store of action-traces rooted in kleśas, and **vipāka** (vipaka; fruition) is its ripening as concrete embodied experience.

**Argument.**
1. ✅ The karmic deposit is **kleśa-mūla** (klesha-mula; rooted in the afflictions): action binds because it is coloured by avidyā, asmitā, rāga, dveṣa and abhiniveśa.
2. ✅ Vipāka is classically threefold: **jāti** (jati; birth or species-condition), **āyus** (ayus; life-span) and **bhoga** (bhoga; experience/enjoyment-suffering).
3. ✅ **Vāsanā** (vasana; latent tendency) and **saṃskāra** (samskara; impression) explain continuity: repeated cognition and action leave traces, traces dispose future cognition and action, and the cycle becomes morally structured.
4. ✅ Yoga classifies karma as **śukla** (shukla; white), **kṛṣṇa** (krishna; black), **śukla-kṛṣṇa** (mixed white-black) and **aśukla-akṛṣṇa** (ashukla-akrishna; neither white nor black), the last being associated with the yogin whose action no longer produces ordinary bondage.
5. ⚠️ This matters philosophically because Yoga is not merely therapeutic psychology; it explains how psychological purification can have soteriological effect by interrupting the moral-causal engine of rebirth.
6. ⚠️ Therefore kleśa-attenuation (**tanū-karaṇa**, tanu-karana; thinning) and final **pratiprasava** (reabsorption into the cause) are the real targets, not temporary emotional calm.

**Presupposition.** ✅ Yoga presupposes a morally ordered cosmos in which subtle mental-affective causes can ripen beyond a single visible episode of experience.

**Example.** ✅ The **dagdha-bīja** (dagdha-bija; burnt seed) image is exam-safe: a burnt seed may retain seed-shape, but it cannot germinate; similarly attenuated and burnt kleśas lose karmic fertility.

**Objection.** ⚠️ If liberation depends on exhausting an unsurveyable stock of karmāśaya, release seems indefinitely postponed.

**Reply.** ✅ Yoga replies that liberating knowledge and samādhi burn the seeds of future bondage. ⚠️ Using later comparative vocabulary, only **prārabdha-type** (prarabdha-type; already-fructifying) embodiment continues until its momentum is spent, like a potter's wheel turning briefly after the potter stops pushing.

### 3.14 Why removal of kleśas leads to kaivalya

1. ✅ Kleśas are the roots of karmically binding activity.
2. ✅ Without kleśas, karmic production and karmic reactivation cease.
3. ✅ Without karmic deposit, the machinery of repeated birth collapses.
4. ✅ As citta is purified and discrimination matures, samādhi becomes stable.
5. ✅ This culminates in kaivalya.
- ⚠️ The 2021 20-marker demanded precisely this transition from **diagnosis of affliction** to **logic of liberation**.

### 3.15 Kleśa and error across schools

| School | Root problem | Yoga's distinctive feature |
|---|---|---|
| Buddhism | avidyā + tṛṣṇā | Yoga keeps enduring puruṣa and citta-psychology |
| [Sāṃkhya](./Samkhya.md) | aviveka between puruṣa and prakṛti | Yoga adds meditative psychology and Īśvara |
| [Advaita Vedānta](./Vedanta.md) | avidyā/adhyāsa | Yoga has no Brahman-identity thesis |
| [Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika](./Nyaya-Vaisesika.md) | mithyājñāna | Yoga stresses affective affliction and meditative remedy |

### 3.16 Mini-conclusion on kleśas

- ⚠️ A compact answer-line: **kleśas are the afflictive roots of saṃsāra, with avidyā as the source and kriyā-yoga plus meditation as the means of attenuation and destruction.**

## 4. AṢṬĀṄGA YOGA

### 4.1 Why include aṣṭāṅga-yoga here?

- ✅ Though the syllabus clause names citta, cittavṛtti, kleśas, samādhi and kaivalya, the path linking them is the **aṣṭāṅga** discipline.
- ✅ UPSC often expects the mediating practical architecture in any fuller answer on Yoga.
- ⚠️ The point is philosophical significance, not physical drill.

### 4.2 The eight limbs

| Limb | Basic meaning | Group | Philosophical significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Yama** | restraints | **bahiraṅga** | social-ethical purification of conduct |
| **Niyama** | observances | **bahiraṅga** | positive self-cultivation and inner orientation |
| **Āsana** | posture | **bahiraṅga** | embodied steadiness enabling meditative work |
| **Prāṇāyāma** | regulation of breath-life-force | **bahiraṅga** | calms psycho-physical disturbance |
| **Pratyāhāra** | withdrawal of senses | **bahiraṅga** / bridge | de-links citta from compulsive object-pursuit |
| **Dhāraṇā** | concentration | **antaraṅga** | fixing attention on one locus |
| **Dhyāna** | meditation | **antaraṅga** | continuous flow toward the object |
| **Samādhi** | absorption | **antaraṅga** | consummated identity of attention with support |

### 4.3 Bahiraṅga and antaraṅga

- ✅ The first five are called **bahiraṅga** — external limbs.
- ✅ The last three are **antaraṅga** — internal limbs.
- ⚠️ "External" does not mean unimportant; it means preparatory with respect to the subtler operations of meditative consciousness.

### 4.4 Yama

- ✅ The **yamas** are the great restraints governing relation to others and to desire.
- ✅ The classical list is:
  1. **ahiṃsā** — non-violence,
  2. **satya** — truthfulness,
  3. **asteya** — non-stealing,
  4. **brahmacarya** — chastity / disciplined energy,
  5. **aparigraha** — non-possessiveness.
- ⚠️ Philosophically, yama reduces the rajasic-tāmasic turbulence that continually destabilizes citta.

### 4.5 Niyama

- ✅ The **niyamas** are positive observances:
  1. **śauca** — purity,
  2. **santoṣa** — contentment,
  3. **tapas** — disciplined austerity,
  4. **svādhyāya** — self-study/scriptural recitation,
  5. **Īśvara-praṇidhāna** — surrender to Īśvara.
- ⚠️ Niyama gives inward form to ethical discipline and opens the citta toward reflective and devotional stabilization.

### 4.6 Āsana

- ✅ Āsana in classical Yoga is primarily the establishment of a **steady and comfortable posture**.
- ⚠️ Its significance is not gymnastic display but freedom from bodily distraction.
- ⚠️ A body at war with itself becomes an obstacle to meditative one-pointedness.

### 4.7 Prāṇāyāma

- ✅ Prāṇāyāma regulates breathing and, through it, the psycho-physical economy.
- ⚠️ Breath functions as a hinge between body and citta; its refinement steadies attention and weakens agitation.

### 4.8 Pratyāhāra

- ✅ Pratyāhāra is withdrawal of the senses from their objects.
- ⚠️ It is not mutilation of sensory life but release from compulsive extroversion.
- ⚠️ This is the transition from ethical-embodied preparation to explicitly internal yogic work.

### 4.9 Dhāraṇā

- ✅ Dhāraṇā is concentration: binding citta to a chosen locus.
- ⚠️ It is the initial stabilization of attention and the first clear sign of **ekāgratā**.

### 4.10 Dhyāna

- ✅ Dhyāna is uninterrupted flow of cognition toward that same object.
- ⚠️ Unlike dhāraṇā's effortful fixing, dhyāna is sustained continuity.
- ✅ It is also a direct means for thinning and burning subtle kleśas.

### 4.11 Samādhi

- ✅ Samādhi is the culmination of meditative integration wherein the object alone shines with minimal interference from discursiveness.
- ⚠️ Because samādhi is itself a syllabus-term, it receives separate detailed treatment in §5 below.

### 4.12 Saṃyama

- ✅ **Saṃyama** is the integrated operation of **dhāraṇā + dhyāna + samādhi** on one object.
- ✅ It represents the inner technical core of Yoga's contemplative method.
- ⚠️ Philosophically, saṃyama is the progressive refinement of intentionality until cognition becomes translucent to the structure of its object.

### 4.13 Aṣṭāṅga-yoga as psychology of liberation

- ⚠️ The eight limbs are best read as a graded transformation:
  - ethics cures gross disturbance,
  - posture and breath calm embodiment,
  - withdrawal cuts sensory dispersion,
  - concentration and meditation unify the mental stream,
  - samādhi prepares discriminative liberation.
- ⚠️ Thus aṣṭāṅga-yoga operationalizes the abstract definition of Yoga as citta-vṛtti-nirodha.
## 5. SAMĀDHI

### 5.1 Statement

- ✅ **Samādhi** (samadhi) is meditative absorption in which citta becomes radically unified and transparent.
- ✅ It is not mere passivity; it is the highest refinement of attention and the threshold of liberating discernment.
- ✅ Classical Yoga distinguishes two main forms: **samprajñāta** and **asamprajñāta** samādhi.

### 5.2 Samprajñāta samādhi — with object / with seed

- ✅ **Samprajñāta samādhi** is cognitive or object-oriented absorption.
- ✅ A support remains; there is still a subtle seed of cognition.
- ✅ It is called **sabīja** (YS 1.46) because an object-support ("seed") is still present.
- ⚠️ **Two senses of "seed" must be kept apart:** in YS 1.46 *sabīja* means "with object-support", whereas the *bīja* denied in **nirbīja** at YS 1.51 is the residual **saṃskāra**. Losing the object-support therefore does not by itself make a samādhi *nirbīja*.
- ⚠️ The mind is profoundly stilled, but not utterly free of object-reference.

### 5.3 Two canonical schemes that must not be conflated

- ✅ Pātañjala Yoga uses two related but distinct classifications in the Samādhi-pāda.
- ✅ **Yogasūtra 1.17** defines **samprajñāta** samādhi through four accompanying factors: **vitarka, vicāra, ānanda, asmitā** — *vitarka-vicārānandāsmitā-rūpānugamāt saṃprajñātaḥ*.
- ✅ **Yogasūtra 1.42–1.44** classifies **samāpatti** (samapatti; meditative coalescence with an object) as **savitarkā/nirvitarkā** and **savicārā/nirvicārā**.
- ⚠️ The first scheme answers: "What characterises samprajñāta samādhi?" The second answers: "How does object-absorption differ according to conceptual-verbal overlay and object-level?"

### 5.4 YS 1.17: the fourfold character of samprajñāta samādhi

| Factor in samprajñāta | Object-level / cognitive tone | Safe explanation |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ **Vitarka** | ✅ gross object-level engagement | ✅ absorption still accompanied by gross deliberative grasp. |
| ✅ **Vicāra** | ✅ subtle object-level reflection | ✅ attention moves from gross entities toward subtle principles. |
| ✅ **Ānanda** | ✅ refined sattvic joy/bliss-aspect | ⚠️ not ordinary pleasure; it is a purified meditative affect attached to sattva. |
| ✅ **Asmitā** | ✅ bare "I-am-ness" | ⚠️ not the ordinary kleśa-asmitā in its binding form, but the extremely subtle objectification of the I-sense. |

- ✅ Therefore **sānanda** and **sāsmita** are not a contested addition to Patañjali's list; **ānanda** and **asmitā** are explicitly present in the sūtra's own fourfold characterisation.
- ⚠️ What becomes interpretively delicate is not whether ānanda and asmitā belong to samprajñāta, but how later commentators map them onto the samāpatti sequence.

### 5.5 YS 1.42–1.46: samāpatti-bhedas, not the YS 1.17 fourfold list

| Samāpatti-bheda | Object-range | Conceptual-verbal overlay | Doctrinal point |
|---|---|---|---|
| ✅ **Savitarkā samāpatti** | ✅ gross (**sthūla**) object | ✅ mixed with **śabda-artha-jñāna-vikalpa** (word-object-knowledge construction) | ✅ absorption still carries name, meaning and concept together. |
| ✅ **Nirvitarkā samāpatti** | ✅ gross object | ✅ memory and verbal construction are purified | ✅ the object shines more immediately without gross conceptual overlay. |
| ✅ **Savicārā samāpatti** | ✅ subtle (**sūkṣma**) object | ✅ subtle reflective consideration remains | ✅ absorption turns from gross objects to subtle principles. |
| ✅ **Nirvicārā samāpatti** | ✅ subtle object | ✅ even subtle reflective overlay is stilled | ✅ refined subtle-object absorption becomes luminous and clear. |

- ✅ Under YS 1.44, the **savicārā/nirvicārā** pair is stated with reference to subtle objects.
- ✅ YS 1.45 extends the subtle domain up to **aliṅga** (alinga; the undifferentiated prakṛtic condition).
- ✅ YS 1.46 states that these samāpattis remain **sabīja** (sabija; with seed) samādhi.
- ✅ YS 1.47 says the clarity (**vaiśāradya**, vaisaradya; lucidity) of **nirvicārā** leads to **adhyātma-prasāda** (adhyatma-prasada; inner clarity of the self-field).
- ✅ YS 1.48 says that therein the prajñā is **ṛtambharā** (rtambhara; truth-bearing).
- ✅ **Example.** The crystal (**sphaṭika**) taking the colour of a nearby flower captures samāpatti: citta becomes so object-coloured that the object seems to shine through it.

### 5.6 Reconciliation: how commentators relate the two schemes

**Statement.** ✅ The safe reconciliation is: **vitarka-vicāra-ānanda-asmitā** are the canonical four factors of samprajñāta, while **savitarkā/nirvitarkā/savicārā/nirvicārā** are samāpatti varieties within object-supported absorption.

**Argument.**
1. ✅ The sa/nir- distinction concerns whether conceptual-verbal overlay (**vikalpa**, especially **śabda-artha-jñāna-vikalpa**) is present or absent in absorption.
2. ✅ The vitarka/vicāra/ānanda/asmitā series concerns the level or refinement of the object/support on which samprajñāta rests: gross → subtle → sattva-bliss aspect → bare I-am-ness.
3. ⚠️ Vyāsa's **Yogabhāṣya** and later Vācaspati Miśra and Vijñānabhikṣu attempt to map these two analytic grids onto one graded meditative path.
4. ❓ Commentarial differences remain over whether **ānanda** and **asmitā** should be counted as independent samāpattis, as supervening aspects of the subtle stage, or as higher refinements beyond the four named sa/nir samāpattis.

**Presupposition.** ✅ The reconciliation presupposes that Patañjali can classify the same meditative ascent by different criteria: object-level, cognitive factor, and degree of conceptual purification.

**Example.** ⚠️ A student may compare it to classifying the same university course by year-level and by assessment-mode: the grids overlap, but they are not the same list.

**Objection.** ⚠️ If both schemes operate within sabīja absorption, why not simply call savitarkā, nirvitarkā, savicārā and nirvicārā the four samprajñāta stages?

**Reply.** ✅ Because YS 1.17 itself names the four factors of samprajñāta as vitarka, vicāra, ānanda and asmitā; the sa/nir list belongs to the later samāpatti analysis of gross and subtle object-absorption.

**Safe examination formula.** ⚠️ Write: "Patañjali characterises samprajñāta by vitarka, vicāra, ānanda and asmitā. He then analyses samāpatti as savitarkā/nirvitarkā for gross objects and savicārā/nirvicārā for subtle objects. Commentators map these schemes, but the lists should not be conflated."

### 5.7 Trap box: samprajñāta vs samāpatti lists

```text
DO NOT WRITE:
  "The four types of samprajñāta samādhi listed by Patañjali are
   savitarka, nirvitarka, savicāra and nirvicāra."

WRITE INSTEAD:
  YS 1.17 fourfold samprajñāta = vitarka + vicāra + ānanda + asmitā.
  YS 1.42–1.44 samāpatti-bhedas = savitarkā/nirvitarkā and savicārā/nirvicārā.
```

### 5.8 Ṛtambharā prajñā — truth-bearing insight

**Statement.** ✅ **Ṛtambharā prajñā** (rtambhara prajna; truth-bearing insight) is the special lucidity arising with **nirvicāra-vaiśāradya** (nirvicara-vaisaradya; clarity of subtle absorption), where citta grasps the object with exceptional purity.

**Argument.**
1. ✅ It is linked to **adhyātma-prasāda**, the inward clarity that follows the perfection of nirvicārā samāpatti.
2. ✅ It differs from **śruta** (sruta; scriptural/testimonial knowledge) and **anumāna** (anumana; inference), because those normally reach universals or general relations, while ṛtambharā has a **viśeṣa-artha** (visesa-artha; particular object) as its field.
3. ✅ Thus yogic insight reaches the individual/particular in a way ordinary testimony and inference do not.
4. ✅ Its saṃskāra obstructs other saṃskāras: the impression born of truth-bearing insight counteracts ordinary afflicted and dispersive impressions.
5. ✅ When even the saṃskāra of ṛtambharā ceases, seedless absorption (**nirbīja samādhi**, YS 1.51) becomes possible.
6. ✅ Yoga accepts three pramāṇas — **pratyakṣa**, **anumāna** and **āgama/śabda** — so ṛtambharā is not a fourth pramāṇa.
7. ⚠️ It is best classified as perfected **yogi-pratyakṣa** (yogi-pratyaksa; extraordinary yogic perception), an extraordinary refinement of perception rather than a new independent means of knowledge.

**Presupposition.** ✅ Yoga presupposes that citta can become so sattva-pure that perception is no longer distorted by ordinary conceptual, affective and karmic deposits.

**Example.** ⚠️ Ordinary inference knows "fire is present because smoke is present" as a general relation; ṛtambharā is like direct yogic disclosure of the concrete object in its particularity, without dependence on discursive mediation.

**Objection.** ⚠️ If ṛtambharā is still a citta-vṛtti, how can it help liberation rather than bind?

**Reply.** ✅ Yoga replies that akliṣṭa vṛttis can cancel kliṣṭa vṛttis; the truth-bearing impression blocks lower impressions and then itself ceases. ⚠️ It is a ladder that must finally be left behind.

**Caution.** ⚠️ Do not simply equate ṛtambharā with Aurobindo's "knowledge by identity" or "supermind". The resemblance is thematic only: in Yoga, ṛtambharā is a purified citta-vṛtti that must finally cease; in Aurobindo, supramental knowledge by identity is reinterpreted as a permanent, world-affirming gnosis.

### 5.9 Asamprajñāta samādhi — beyond cognitive objectification

- ✅ **Asamprajñāta samādhi** is non-cognitive or supra-cognitive absorption in which the vṛttis cease and no object stands forth as support in the ordinary sense.
- ✅ **YS 1.18** defines it as *virāma-pratyaya-abhyāsa-pūrvaḥ saṃskāra-śeṣo 'nyaḥ* — "the other [samādhi] is preceded by the practice of the idea of cessation, and **has saṃskāra as its residue**."
- ✅ So on the sūtra's own wording asamprajñāta is characterised by **saṃskāra-śeṣa**: latent impressions may still remain even though cognitive absorption has stopped.
- ⚠️ It is crucial not to confuse this with sleep or blank unconsciousness; it is the fruition of radical meditative restraint.

### 5.9A Nirbīja samādhi — when even the residual seed ceases

- ✅ **YS 1.51** states *tasyāpi nirodhe sarva-nirodhān nirbījaḥ samādhiḥ* — "on the restraint of **that too**, everything being restrained, samādhi is **seedless**."
- ✅ The "that too" is the residual impression left by the preceding stage (the saṃskāra born of ṛtambharā prajñā, YS 1.50); when even it is restrained, absorption becomes **nirbīja**.
- ⚠️ **Do not equate the two terms.** Asamprajñāta (YS 1.18) is defined by *residual saṃskāra*; nirbīja (YS 1.51) is defined by *the cessation of even that residue*. Nirbīja is best treated as the culminating condition reached within the asamprajñāta phase, not as a synonym for it.
- ⚠️ ✅ Safe answer formula: *asamprajñāta = vṛttis stopped, saṃskāra-śeṣa remains; nirbīja = even that residual saṃskāra restrained.*

### 5.10 Relation between saṃskāra and nirodha

- ✅ Even when active vṛttis cease, latent impressions can remain as residual potency — this is exactly the *saṃskāra-śeṣa* of YS 1.18.
- ✅ Hence the path must proceed beyond surface quietness to the restraint of even the subtle seed, which is what YS 1.51 marks as nirbīja.
- ⚠️ This explains why temporary calm or trance is not yet liberation.

### 5.11 Dharmamegha-samādhi

- ✅ **Dharmamegha-samādhi** is the highest samādhi immediately preceding kaivalya.
- ✅ The image is that of a **cloud of dharma** raining down the fullness of discriminative knowledge.
- ✅ At this level, even fascination with extraordinary knowledge or powers is relinquished.
- ⚠️ It marks the consummation of non-attachment: the yogin no longer seeks omniscience, attainment or display.

### 5.12 Samādhi and siddhis

- ✅ Higher concentrations may yield extraordinary capacities or **siddhis**.
- ⚠️ But Yoga warns that these are obstacles if appropriated by egoity or fascination.
- ⚠️ They belong to the side-effects of concentration, not to the essence of kaivalya.

### 5.13 Role of Īśvara in samādhi

- ✅ **Īśvara-praṇidhāna** is both a **niyama** and an independent means conducive to samādhi.
- ✅ Devotion or surrender to Īśvara stabilizes citta and helps overcome obstacles.
- ✅ Īśvara is also linked to the meditative use of **praṇava (OṂ)**.
- ⚠️ In Yoga, theism is practical: Īśvara functions as an unsurpassed focus, model and aid to concentration.

### 5.14 Obstacles and their removal

- ✅ Classical Yoga lists obstacles (**antarāyas**) such as disease, mental dullness, doubt, carelessness, laziness, sensuality, false perception, failure to attain stages and instability in attained stages.
- ✅ Īśvara-praṇidhāna is one of the important means of overcoming these.
- ⚠️ This is why questions on samādhi often open into the role of God: Yoga's God is not a creator-first doctrine but a **practice-enabling doctrine**.

### 5.15 Samādhi in the 2022 PYQ

- ✅ The 2022 question required the **nature and stages of samādhi** along with the **role of Īśvara**.
- ⚠️ Therefore a complete answer must cover the distinction between samprajñāta and asamprajñāta (including YS 1.18's *saṃskāra-śeṣa* and its further culmination in YS 1.51's nirbīja), the YS 1.17 fourfold characterisation, the samāpatti-bhedas, dharmamegha as culmination, and devotional concentration on Īśvara as an aid.

### 5.16 Mini-conclusion on samādhi

- ⚠️ A sharp answer line: **samādhi is the progressive refinement of concentration from object-supported cognitive absorption (sabīja, YS 1.46) through non-cognitive absorption with residual impressions (asamprajñāta, YS 1.18: saṃskāra-śeṣa) to the restraint of even that residue (nirbīja, YS 1.51), with YS 1.17's samprajñāta factors, the samāpatti refinements, and Īśvara-praṇidhāna as a powerful aid but not a substitute for discriminative realization.**

## 6. KAIVALYA

### 6.1 Exact meaning

- ✅ **Kaivalya** means aloneness, isolation or absolute distinctness.
- ✅ In Yoga it is the isolation of **puruṣa** from **prakṛti** and its evolutes.
- ✅ It is therefore **not** union with God, **not** merger into Brahman, and **not** mere relief from pain considered negatively.
- ⚠️ The precise meaning must always be guarded because UPSC frequently tests it through contrasts.

### 6.2 Why kaivalya is needed if puruṣa is already pure

- ✅ Puruṣa is indeed eternally pure, inactive and free.
- ✅ Yet through ignorance, citta's modifications are appropriated as self.
- ✅ Kaivalya is the cessation of this false relation at the level of experience and bondage.
- ⚠️ So liberation is not a production of a new essence; it is the ending of misidentification.

### 6.3 The process leading to kaivalya

```text
Abhyāsa + Vairāgya
        ↓
Kriyā-yoga and Aṣṭāṅga-yoga
        ↓
Attenuation and destruction of kleśas
        ↓
Stable samādhi
        ↓
Viveka-khyāti (discriminative discernment)
        ↓
Para-vairāgya (supreme detachment)
        ↓
Dharmamegha-samādhi
        ↓
Kaivalya
```

### 6.4 Viveka-khyāti

- ✅ **Viveka-khyāti** is discriminative knowledge discerning the absolute difference between puruṣa and prakṛti.
- ✅ It is the decisive knowledge that ends confusion.
- ⚠️ Yoga thus remains deeply cognitivist at the summit: samādhi is indispensable, but its soteriological function is to mature discriminative discernment.

### 6.5 Para-vairāgya

- ✅ **Para-vairāgya** is supreme detachment even from the highest subtle attainments.
- ✅ The yogin ceases to crave powers, experiences, pleasures, even exalted cognitive states.
- ⚠️ Without this, subtle egoity can survive advanced practice.

### 6.6 Dharmamegha and final transition

- ✅ In **dharmamegha-samādhi**, the cloud of dharma pours down the fullness of liberating discernment.
- ✅ The final barriers to isolation of puruṣa are removed.
- ⚠️ This is not a divine grace replacing effort; it is the ripened summit of perfected discipline and discernment.

### 6.7 Pratiprasava of the guṇas

- ✅ With liberation, the **guṇas** cease to function for the liberated puruṣa and resolve back toward their source.
- ✅ This return or involution is described as **pratiprasava** — reabsorption back into prakṛti.
- ✅ Citta, as a product of prakṛti, no longer binds; its function is exhausted.
- ⚠️ The metaphor is one of reversal of the evolutionary process for soteriological purposes.


### 6.8 Kaivalya transition: farmer, burnt seed and potter's wheel

- ✅ The Kaivalya-pāda uses the **kṣetrika** (ksetrika; farmer) image: the farmer does not push water into the field by force; he breaches the embankment, and water flows by its own nature.
- ⚠️ Exam use: yogic discipline removes obstructions; it does not manufacture puruṣa's freedom, because puruṣa is already free and prakṛti's processes complete themselves when obstacles are removed.
- ✅ The burnt seed (**dagdha-bīja**) image explains why destroyed kleśas cannot germinate into future bondage.
- ⚠️ The potter's wheel image explains residual embodiment: even after liberating knowledge removes the push of fresh karma, the already-moving psycho-physical complex may continue briefly until its momentum is exhausted.
- ⚠️ This allows a careful account of **jīvanmukti** (jivanmukti; liberation while living) language in Yoga: the puruṣa is no longer bound, though bodily-citta processes may continue until their residual cause is spent.

### 6.9 Svarūpa-pratiṣṭhā

- ✅ Kaivalya may also be expressed as the abiding of consciousness in its own nature — **svarūpa-pratiṣṭhā**.
- ⚠️ This continues the logic of the foundational sūtra: when vṛttis cease, the seer stands in itself.
### 6.10 How Yoga kaivalya differs from Advaita mokṣa

| Yoga | Advaita Vedānta |
|---|---|
| many puruṣas | one Brahman as ultimate reality |
| liberation = isolation of puruṣa from prakṛti | liberation = realization of identity of ātman and Brahman |
| no Brahman-doctrine | Brahman central |
| no merger or non-dual identity | mokṣa is non-dual realization |

- ⚠️ Therefore kaivalya is not identity but **disentanglement**.
- ⚠️ Cross-reference: [Vedānta](./Vedanta.md).

### 6.11 How Yoga kaivalya differs from Nyāya apavarga

| Yoga kaivalya | Nyāya apavarga |
|---|---|
| positive isolation of pure consciousness in its own nature | often described as cessation of pain and bondage |
| rooted in puruṣa-prakṛti discrimination | rooted in true knowledge of categories and self |
| citta resolves, puruṣa shines | self remains but liberation is characterized more negatively |

- ⚠️ UPSC-safe line: **Nyāya emphasizes cessation of suffering; Yoga emphasizes the isolation and self-abidance of puruṣa.**
- ⚠️ Cross-reference: [Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika](./Nyaya-Vaisesika.md).

### 6.12 Īśvara and kaivalya

- ✅ Īśvara assists the aspirant but does **not grant** kaivalya as an external gift.
- ✅ Liberation finally depends on the yogin's own **viveka-khyāti**.
- ✅ Īśvara is the perfect model, object of devotion and remover of obstacles.
- ⚠️ This is central to the 2025 demand on the nature of God and his role in kaivalya.

### 6.13 What kaivalya is not

- ⚠️ Not **union with God** in the bhakti-school sense.
- ⚠️ Not **identity with Brahman** in the Advaitic sense.
- ⚠️ Not **annihilation of consciousness**.
- ⚠️ Not **mere painlessness** abstracted from the self's own luminosity.
- ⚠️ Not **destruction of mind as substance**; rather, cessation of its binding modifications and reabsorption of its function into prakṛti.

### 6.14 Mini-conclusion on kaivalya

- ⚠️ Best exam sentence: **kaivalya is the isolation of puruṣa from prakṛti, reached through discriminative discernment matured in samādhi, with guṇas returning to quiescence and consciousness abiding in its own form.**

## 7. ĪŚVARA IN YOGA

### 7.1 Why a separate section is necessary

- ✅ Though the syllabus clause names citta through kaivalya, UPSC repeatedly asks the role of **God** in Yoga.
- ✅ Yoga's distinctiveness from Sāṃkhya cannot be fully explained without Īśvara.
- ⚠️ Hence any mature note on Yoga must include a separate theistic section.

### 7.2 Definition: puruṣa-viśeṣa

- ✅ Yoga defines **Īśvara** as a **puruṣa-viśeṣa** — a special puruṣa.
- ✅ He is untouched by **kleśa**, **karma**, **vipāka** and **āśaya**.
- ✅ This is the standard doctrinal core of the Yoga-sūtra account.

### 7.3 Omniscience and timeless teacherhood

- ✅ In Īśvara lies the unsurpassed seed of omniscience.
- ✅ He is the teacher of the ancients and is not limited by time.
- ✅ He is denoted by **praṇava (OṂ)**.
- ⚠️ These predicates make Īśvara the ideal meditative and pedagogic principle in Yoga.

### 7.4 Īśvara is not creator in the Nyāya sense

- ✅ Yoga does **not** primarily present Īśvara as creator-God.
- ✅ **Prakṛti** remains the material basis of worldly evolution.
- ✅ Evolution proceeds through the puruṣa-prakṛti framework, not through divine creation ex nihilo.
- ⚠️ Therefore Yoga's God differs sharply from the Nyāya God as **nimitta-kāraṇa** or efficient cause of the world-order.

### 7.5 Īśvara is not a Vaiṣṇava-style personal saviour

- ✅ Though devotion to Īśvara is accepted, Yoga does not make him a saviour who arbitrarily grants liberation.
- ✅ The structure of liberation remains impersonal in one crucial sense: it depends on the yogin's own discriminative realization.
- ⚠️ This must be clearly said to avoid importing later devotional theologies into classical Yoga.

### 7.6 Positive role of Īśvara

| Role of Īśvara in Yoga | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Object of meditation** | one-pointed devotion stabilizes citta |
| **Aid through Īśvara-praṇidhāna** | surrender reduces egoity and attachment |
| **Remover of obstacles** | helps counter antarāyas in practice |
| **Perfect model** | exemplifies untouched, unsurpassed spiritual status |
| **Teacher principle** | anchor of timeless transmission and authority |

### 7.7 Why Yoga is called seśvara-sāṃkhya

- ✅ Because it preserves the Sāṃkhyan dualistic ontology while adding Īśvara.
- ✅ Hence the expression **seśvara-sāṃkhya** — Sāṃkhya with God.
- ⚠️ This is not a small decorative addition; it changes the psychological and devotional resources of the path while leaving the basic metaphysical dualism intact.

### 7.8 Īśvara and praṇava

- ✅ Meditation on **OṂ** as the designation of Īśvara is a canonical Yogic practice.
- ⚠️ This links theology, language, sound and contemplative concentration in a single discipline.

### 7.9 Īśvara, samādhi and kaivalya together

- ✅ Īśvara aids samādhi by offering a privileged support for concentration.
- ✅ Through obstacle-removal and ego-surrender, the aspirant's path to discriminative knowledge is accelerated.
- ✅ But kaivalya still follows from **viveka-khyāti**, not divine fiat.
- ⚠️ Thus the most accurate exam formula is: **Īśvara is instrumental, not constitutive, in liberation.**

### 7.10 The 2025 demand

- ✅ The 2025 question asked for the **nature of God and his role in kaivalya**.
- ⚠️ A strong answer should therefore establish four points:
  1. Īśvara is a **special puruṣa**.
  2. He is untouched by kleśa-karma-vipāka-āśaya.
  3. He aids concentration, removes obstacles and is the object of praṇava-mediated devotion.
  4. He does not create the world in the Nyāya sense and does not bestow kaivalya independently of viveka-khyāti.

### 7.11 Mini-conclusion on Īśvara

- ⚠️ In Yoga, God is best understood as **the supreme contemplative support within a Sāṃkhyan metaphysical frame**.

### 8.5 Kaivalya-pāda realism against Vijñānavāda/Yogācāra

**Statement.** ✅ The Kaivalya-pāda contains a genuine realist polemic: Yoga is not a mind-only idealism but a Sāṃkhya-realist system in which objects, citta and puruṣa are distinct.

**Pūrvapakṣa.** ⚠️ **Vijñānavāda/Yogācāra** argues that what is taken as an external object can be explained as cognition-only; the object need not be admitted as independently real.

**Siddhānta.** ✅ Yoga's reply is Sāṃkhya-realism: objects are not reducible to a single cognition, citta is itself knowable, and puruṣa is required as the witness of the mind.

**Argument.**
1. ✅ **Vastu-sāmya / ekacitta-tantra argument.** An object is not dependent on one mind; if it were **ekacitta-tantra** (ekacitta-tantra; dependent on a single mind), it would vanish when that mind ceased to cognise it. ⚠️ The persistence and common availability of objects support object-realism.
2. ✅ **Mind's knownness argument.** Citta is not self-illuminating (**na tat svābhāsam**, na tat svabhasam), because it is itself **dṛśya** (drsya; an object seen) for puruṣa. ⚠️ A purely mind-only theory struggles to terminate the regress of cognitions cognising cognitions.
3. ✅ **Against simultaneous self-cognition.** The mind cannot fully apprehend itself and its object in the same act as though it were both lamp and illuminated object. ⚠️ Yoga therefore distinguishes the object, the mental modification and the witnessing puruṣa.
4. ✅ **Uparāga argument.** An object is known when citta is **uparakta** (uparakta; coloured/affected) by it; whether the object is known or unknown depends on citta's colouring, which implies that the object is not identical with the cognition itself.

**Presupposition.** ✅ The argument presupposes Yoga's three-term realism: object (**viṣaya**), mental modification (**citta-vṛtti**) and witness (**puruṣa**) are analytically distinct.

**Example.** ✅ The sphaṭika crystal near a flower again helps: the crystal becomes coloured by the flower, but the flower is not created by the crystal's colouration; similarly, citta is coloured by an object that stands outside the cognition.

**Objection.** ⚠️ The Yogācāra can reply that shared karmic traces explain intersubjective object-appearance without positing external objects.

**Reply.** ⚠️ Yoga reconstructs the answer by insisting that shared appearance itself is better explained by a common object plus multiple cittas than by private cognitions alone; the residual force is that Yoga must still explain precisely how external objects colour an insentient but subtle citta.

**Exam payoff.** ⚠️ This material shows that Yoga is not only a praxis manual; it defends a metaphysically committed realism against Buddhist idealism.

### 8.6 Yoga and philosophy of religion

- ⚠️ Yoga's Īśvara is useful for cross-paper deployment with [Notions of God](../../paper-2/philosophy-of-religion/Notions-of-God.md) and [Religious Experience](../../paper-2/philosophy-of-religion/Religious-Experience.md).
- ⚠️ It represents a distinctive Indian model of God as contemplative principle rather than creator-first metaphysical absolute.
## 9. CRITICISMS AND REPLIES

### 9.1 Criticism: If puruṣa is inactive and pure, how can it ever be bound?

- ⚠️ The objection is that an inactive witness cannot genuinely enter bondage.
- ✅ Yoga's reply parallels Sāṃkhya: bondage belongs not to puruṣa intrinsically but to misidentification through reflection in citta.
- ⚠️ Thus bondage is phenomenological and epistemic, not an actual alteration of puruṣa.

### 9.2 Criticism: If citta is insentient, how can cognition occur at all?

- ⚠️ The challenge targets the possibility of experience in an unconscious psychical instrument.
- ✅ Yoga replies that citta becomes luminous by reflecting puruṣa, just as a crystal appears lit by what is near it.
- ⚠️ The school thus explains cognition through a relation of reflected consciousness rather than by attributing intrinsic consciousness to matter.

### 9.3 Criticism: Why count valid cognition itself as a vṛtti if it helps liberation?

- ⚠️ If pramāṇa is true, why restrain it?
- ✅ Yoga replies that even true cognition is still a modification.
- ✅ It is useful in its **akliṣṭa** form as a ladder toward viveka-khyāti.
- ⚠️ But what serves as means at one stage must also finally be transcended.

### 9.4 Criticism: Is asamprajñāta samādhi just unconsciousness or sleep?

- ✅ No.
- ✅ Sleep is itself one vṛtti, while asamprajñāta samādhi is cessation of vṛttis through discipline.
- ⚠️ The difference lies in both genesis and value: sleep is natural obscuration; samādhi is luminous restraint.

### 9.5 Criticism: Why add Īśvara if Sāṃkhya ontology already explains liberation?

- ⚠️ This is the classic challenge from nirīśvara Sāṃkhya.
- ✅ Yoga's answer is pragmatic and soteriological: Īśvara offers a unique focus of devotion, removes obstacles and supports samādhi.
- ⚠️ The addition is justified by practical efficacy rather than by an overhaul of metaphysical fundamentals.

### 9.6 Criticism: If liberation depends on viveka-khyāti, does God become redundant?

- ✅ Not redundant, but non-final.
- ✅ Īśvara is a privileged aid, not the substitute for discernment.
- ⚠️ This balanced answer is exactly what UPSC expects.

### 9.7 Criticism: Is Yoga's liberation merely negative isolation?

- ⚠️ Some critics see kaivalya as a stark separation lacking fullness.
- ✅ Yoga replies that the positive side of liberation is **svarūpa-pratiṣṭhā** — abiding in pure consciousness free from all alien admixture.
- ⚠️ Its positivity is not ecstatic union but unconfused self-abidance.

### 9.8 Criticism: Is dhyāna sufficient without ethics?

- ✅ Yoga's answer is no.
- ✅ Without yama and niyama, meditative power can coexist with affliction and egoity.
- ⚠️ This is why the system places ethics at the foundation of contemplative success.

## 10. COMMON UPSC TRAPS

1. ⚠️ **Kaivalya ≠ Advaita mokṣa ≠ Nyāya apavarga.** Yoga's kaivalya is isolation of puruṣa, not identity with Brahman and not merely cessation of pain.
2. ⚠️ **Yoga's Īśvara is not a creator-God in the Nyāya sense.** Do not import nimitta-kāraṇa language uncritically.
3. ⚠️ **Citta is not identical with manas alone.** It includes buddhi, ahaṃkāra and manas functioning together.
4. ⚠️ **Samprajñāta and asamprajñāta must not be reversed, and asamprajñāta must not be flatly equated with nirbīja.** Samprajñāta still has object-support (*sabīja*, YS 1.46); asamprajñāta (YS 1.18) is defined as *saṃskāra-śeṣa*, i.e. residual impressions may still remain; nirbīja (YS 1.51) is the further condition in which even that residual saṃskāra is restrained.
5. ⚠️ **Do not conflate YS 1.17 with YS 1.42–1.44.** Samprajñāta's four factors are vitarka, vicāra, ānanda and asmitā; savitarkā/nirvitarkā/savicārā/nirvicārā are samāpatti-bhedas.
6. ⚠️ **Kliṣṭa/akliṣṭa qualifies vṛttis, not kleśas.** Kleśas are inherently afflictive.
7. ⚠️ **Citta-vṛtti-nirodha does not mean violent destruction of mind.** It means cessation of modifications so the seer abides in itself.
8. ⚠️ **Abhiniveśa is present even in the wise.** Do not reduce it to mere doctrinal ignorance.
9. ⚠️ **Sleep is a vṛtti, not liberation.** Nidrā is not samādhi.
10. ⚠️ **Yoga shares Sāṃkhya's tattva framework.** Do not write as if Yoga has a wholly separate cosmology.
11. ⚠️ **Īśvara helps but does not grant kaivalya from outside.** Liberation finally follows viveka-khyāti.

## 11A. PRESUPPOSITION LEDGER

| Doctrine | Presupposition it rests on | What collapses if the presupposition is denied |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ **Citta-vṛtti-nirodha** | ✅ Citta really takes forms, can be restrained, and is distinct from puruṣa. | ⚠️ Nirodha becomes mere mental quietism, not liberation. |
| ✅ **Kleśa theory** | ✅ Avidyā and its derivatives causally colour cognition, action and rebirth. | ⚠️ Yoga loses its diagnosis of bondage and becomes moral advice only. |
| ✅ **Reflection/asmitā** | ✅ Puruṣa is inactive consciousness; citta is transparent but insentient. | ⚠️ Bondage of a pure puruṣa becomes inexplicable. |
| ✅ **Aṣṭāṅga-yoga** | ✅ Body, conduct, breath, senses and attention condition citta's purity. | ⚠️ Samādhi becomes disconnected from ethics and discipline. |
| ✅ **Samādhi** | ✅ Attention can become progressively object-transparent and finally seedless. | ⚠️ Meditation reduces to trance or concentration without soteriological force. |
| ✅ **Karma-vipāka** | ✅ Actions leave subtle deposits rooted in kleśas and ripen across experience. | ⚠️ Yoga becomes therapeutic psychology rather than a theory of saṃsāra and release. |
| ✅ **Īśvara** | ✅ A special puruṣa untouched by kleśa-karma-vipāka-āśaya is possible and useful for practice. | ⚠️ Yoga collapses toward nirīśvara Sāṃkhya and loses its distinctive devotional aid. |
| ✅ **Kaivalya** | ✅ Puruṣa is ultimately distinct from prakṛti, and prakṛti functions for puruṣa's experience and release. | ⚠️ Isolation becomes unintelligible; Advaitic identity or Buddhist no-self becomes more plausible. |

## 11B. PŪRVAPAKṢA–SIDDHĀNTA LEDGER

| Objector | Objection | Yoga's reply | Residual force ⚠️ |
|---|---|---|---|
| ✅ **Buddhist kṣaṇikavāda** | ⚠️ No persisting citta is needed; a stream of momentary cognitions explains experience. | ✅ Yoga invokes citta as a continuing prakṛtic substrate bearing saṃskāras and undergoing pariṇāma. | ⚠️ Momentariness can still explain continuity through causal series without a substance. |
| ✅ **Yogācāra/Vijñānavāda** | ⚠️ External objects are unnecessary; cognition-only explains appearance. | ✅ Kaivalya-pāda realism: objects are not dependent on a single mind, and citta is known by puruṣa. | ⚠️ Shared karmic appearance remains a sophisticated idealist counter. |
| ✅ **Advaita Vedānta** | ⚠️ Why many puruṣas, and why make Īśvara only a special puruṣa rather than the sole reality? | ⚠️ Yoga replies from experience-plurality, Sāṃkhyan realism and the practical need for discriminating each puruṣa from prakṛti. | ⚠️ Advaita presses the unity of consciousness more radically. |
| ✅ **Nyāya** | ⚠️ The self is a substance with adventitious consciousness; why call puruṣa consciousness itself, and why desire cessation of all vṛttis? | ⚠️ Yoga replies that consciousness cannot be an accidental property of the true seer, and nirodha reveals rather than destroys awareness. | ⚠️ Nyāya's commonsense realism about persons and cognition remains attractive. |
| ✅ **Cārvāka** | ⚠️ Puruṣa, karma, rebirth and kaivalya are unverifiable beyond perception. | ⚠️ Yoga replies through disciplined yogi-pratyakṣa, pragmatic transformation and the inadequacy of material pleasure to end suffering. | ⚠️ Public verification remains difficult. |
| ✅ **Mīmāṃsā** | ⚠️ A non-creator Īśvara is otiose; ritual action, not yoga, secures dharma. | ⚠️ Yoga replies that ritual cannot by itself remove avidyā/asmitā, while Īśvara-praṇidhāna directly stabilizes citta. | ⚠️ The traditional Yoga proof of Īśvara's necessity is weaker than Nyāya's creator-God argument. |

| ⚠️ **Mīmāṃsā** | ❓ sub-school variation, commonly 5 or 6 | ✅ enduring self | ✅ creator God not necessary in classical ritualism | ✅ ritual action produces apūrva | ✅ cessation of bondage / attainment linked to dharma | ⚠️ not a central doctrine |
| ✅ **Advaita Vedānta** | ✅ commonly 6 | ✅ one non-dual ātman-Brahman | ✅ Īśvara as saguṇa Brahman at empirical level | ✅ vivartavāda at the highest level | ✅ mokṣa as Brahman-realization | ✅ strongly admitted |

**Precise Sāṃkhya–Yoga difference.** ✅ Yoga is not merely "Sāṃkhya plus God" in a decorative sense. ⚠️ It retains Sāṃkhya's puruṣa-prakṛti ontology but adds (i) **Īśvara-praṇidhāna** as a practical means, (ii) a more elaborate psychology of citta-vṛtti-nirodha, (iii) samādhi-methodology and yogi-pratyakṣa, and (iv) a systematic path from kleśa-attenuation to kaivalya. ⚠️ In a discuss-answer, say: **Sāṃkhya supplies the metaphysical grammar; Yoga turns it into a disciplined technology of liberation with Īśvara as a privileged contemplative aid.**


## 11D. CONTROLLED WESTERN COMPARISON

| Western parallel | Point of contact | Disanalogy / limit |
|---|---|---|
| ⚠️ **Husserl's epoché/reduction vs nirodha** | ⚠️ Both suspend the natural attitude toward ordinary experience. | ⚠️ Epoché is methodological and retains the constituting ego; nirodha is soteriological and aims at cessation of the mental stream's binding modifications. |
| ⚠️ **Descartes' cogito vs puruṣa** | ⚠️ Both make consciousness indubitable. | ⚠️ The cogito yields a thinking substance that acts; puruṣa is inactive, non-agentive witness-consciousness. |
| ⚠️ **Kant's transcendental unity of apperception vs asmitā** | ⚠️ Both notice an "I think" accompanying representations. | ⚠️ Kant makes it a condition of objectivity; Yoga treats egoic I-am-appropriation as an affliction to be dissolved. |
| ⚠️ **Spinoza's third kind of knowledge vs ṛtambharā** | ⚠️ Both are non-discursive and higher than ordinary inference. | ⚠️ Spinoza's is knowledge of God/Nature under an attribute; Yoga's ṛtambharā is object-specific insight that must itself cease. |
| ⚠️ **Aristotle's habituation vs abhyāsa-vairāgya** | ⚠️ Both transform character through repeated discipline. | ⚠️ Aristotle aims at virtuous flourishing in the polis; Yoga aims at disentangling puruṣa from prakṛti. |
| ⚠️ **Freud's unconscious vs saṃskāra/vāsanā** | ⚠️ Both posit latent dispositions shaping conduct. | ⚠️ Saṃskāras are karmically indexed and metaphysically real deposits in a rebirth framework, not merely a psychodynamic hypothesis. |

**Rubric.** ⚠️ Western parallels are illustrative garnish only; they never substitute for the Indian argument, and in an answer they must appear as at most one or two lines after the Yoga case is complete.

## 11F. GRADED VERDICT ON YOGA

**Philosophically strong ✅**
- ✅ Yoga gives a rigorous phenomenology of mental process: vṛtti, smṛti, nidrā, saṃskāra, kleśa and samādhi form a coherent psychology.
- ✅ It integrates psychology, ethics and metaphysics: yama-niyama, karma-vipāka and citta-nirodha are not separate compartments.
- ✅ It offers an operational path: abhyāsa, vairāgya, kriyā-yoga, aṣṭāṅga and samādhi supply a disciplined method rather than mere speculation.
- ✅ Its Kaivalya-pāda realist arguments show that Yoga is metaphysically committed, not merely a praxis manual.

**Philosophically weak ⚠️**
- ⚠️ It inherits the puruṣa-prakṛti interaction problem: mere proximity explains much, but not all critics will accept it.
- ⚠️ Īśvara has limited explanatory role: he aids practice but is not creator, material cause or final giver of kaivalya.
- ⚠️ The value of contentless pure consciousness can be questioned by schools that define fulfilment through knowledge, relation, bliss or activity.

**Genuinely contested ❓**
- ❓ The precise commentarial status of ānanda/asmitā as separate samāpattis or higher aspects of samprajñāta remains debated.
- ❓ Whether kaivalya should be described as blissful self-abidance or as austere isolation is interpreted differently by expositors.
- ❓ The historical unity and layering of the Yogasūtra tradition remain scholarly questions; do not build a UPSC answer on uncertain dating claims.

**Ready verdicts.**
- ⚠️ **10 marks:** "Yoga is best read as a disciplined psychology of citta-nirodha grounded in Sāṃkhya dualism and completed in kaivalya."
- ⚠️ **15 marks:** "Its strength lies in linking mental discipline, moral causation and liberation; its vulnerability lies in the unexplained proximity of inactive puruṣa and active prakṛti."
- ⚠️ **20 marks:** "Yoga is philosophically richer than a praxis manual: it offers a realist metaphysics, a subtle moral psychology and a graded meditative epistemology, though its dualism and limited Īśvara leave live objections for Advaita, Nyāya and Buddhist critics."

# PART II - SOLVED PYQs, PRACTICE AND ANSWER TRAINING

## Solved PYQ 1 - 2019 Q7(b), 15 marks

**Question:** How would Yoga comprehend the citta-levels of a scientist, a God-realized devotee and a self-realized yogi?

### Model solution

Yoga classifies citta into five bhumis: **ksipta** (restless), **mudha** (dull), **viksipta** (intermittently focused), **ekagra** (one-pointed), and **niruddha** (fully restrained).

| Person | Citta-level | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| Scientist | viksipta | capable of sustained empirical focus, but attention remains outward and fluctuates beyond the chosen task |
| God-realized devotee | ekagra | mind is one-pointedly established in Isvara and fit for deep meditation |
| Self-realized yogi | niruddha | all vrttis are restrained and the seer abides in its own nature |

The scientist should not automatically be called ekagra merely because of intellectual concentration. Yogic one-pointedness includes freedom from ordinary attachment and a stable inward orientation. The devotee's concentration is supported by Isvara-pranidhana. The self-realized yogi has crossed from concentration on an object to complete restraint.

**Verdict:** The hierarchy measures not intelligence but the degree of guna-purification and freedom from mental dispersion.

**Why this earns marks:** It applies all three cases precisely, avoids equating technical focus with yogic realization, and explains the governing criterion.

## Solved PYQ 2 - 2020 Q5(b), 10 marks

**Question:** Distinguish Samprajnata Samadhi and Asamprajnata Samadhi.

### Model solution

| Samprajnata | Asamprajnata |
|---|---|
| cognitive absorption with an object | cessation beyond objectifying cognition |
| supported by prajna and a seed | only residual samskaras may remain |
| includes vitarka, vicara, ananda and asmita dimensions | reached through supreme dispassion and nirodha |
| yields truth-bearing insight at higher levels | prepares for nirbija when even seed is exhausted |

Samprajnata is not ordinary thought. Citta becomes transparent and takes the object's form with extraordinary clarity. Its detailed samapatti distinctions—savitarka/nirvitarka and savicara/nirvicara—must not be confused mechanically with the fourfold Yoga Sutra 1.17 scheme.

Asamprajnata is not sleep or unconsciousness. Sleep is itself a vrtti supported by absence-cognition, whereas asamprajnata results from disciplined restraint and leaves a transformative nirodha-samskara.

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes object, seed, cognition and residual impressions and directly removes the common sleep-confusion.

## Solved PYQ 3 - 2021 Q6(a), 20 marks

**Question:** Explain the nature of klesas and how their removal leads to kaivalya.

### Model solution

Klesas are afflictive structures colouring cognition and generating karma. The five are:

1. **Avidya:** mistaking impermanent for permanent, impure for pure, painful for pleasurable and not-self for self.
2. **Asmita:** identification of seer with the seeing instrument.
3. **Raga:** attachment based on remembered pleasure.
4. **Dvesa:** aversion based on remembered pain.
5. **Abhinivesa:** deep clinging to life, present even in the learned.

Avidya is the field of the other four. Klesas may be prasupta, tanu, vicchinna or udara—dormant, attenuated, interrupted or fully active.

```text
avidya → asmita → raga/dvesa → action
→ karmasaya → vipaka → rebirth and suffering
```

Their removal is graded. **Kriya-yoga**—tapas, svadhyaya and Isvara-pranidhana—attenuates them. Eight-limbed discipline purifies conduct, body, breath, senses and attention. Dhyana burns subtle afflictions; discriminative knowledge destroys avidya's root. When klesas cease, fresh karmic deposits stop, old seeds are exhausted or burnt, and citta no longer produces identification.

Samadhi alone is instrumental unless it matures into **viveka-khyati**, uninterrupted discrimination between Purusa and citta. Para-vairagya prevents attachment even to insight and powers. Dharmamegha-samadhi completes the cessation of binding tendencies; the gunas undergo pratiprasava and Purusa stands in kaivalya.

An objection says Purusa was always free, so removal is unnecessary. Yoga replies that liberation removes misidentification in citta, not an actual impurity in Purusa.

**Verdict:** Klesa-removal leads to kaivalya by breaking both the cognitive root and karmic reproduction of bondage.

**Why this earns marks:** It defines all five, gives their states and causal chain, integrates kriya-yoga, astanga, samadhi and viveka, and answers the already-free objection.

## Solved PYQ 4 - 2022 Q5(c), 10 marks

**Question:** Discuss stages of Samadhi and examine the role of Isvara.

### Model solution

Patanjala Yoga broadly distinguishes **samprajnata** and **asamprajnata** samadhi. Samprajnata retains cognitive support and is associated with vitarka, vicara, ananda and asmita. The samapatti analysis further distinguishes gross and subtle absorptions with and without conceptual admixture. Nirvicara maturity yields **rtambhara prajna**, truth-bearing insight.

Asamprajnata suspends objectifying cognition while residual samskaras may remain. **Nirbija samadhi** occurs when even the seed sustaining further mental emergence is exhausted. Dharmamegha marks the culminating cloud of virtue and complete dispassion.

Isvara is **purusa-visesa**, eternally untouched by klesa, karma, fruition and latent deposits. Devotion to Isvara, repetition and contemplation of pranava, and surrender reduce obstacles and accelerate samadhi.

**Verdict:** Isvara is a privileged practical aid and timeless teacher, not the material cause or automatic giver of kaivalya; discriminative knowledge remains decisive.

**Why this earns marks:** It gives the stage architecture without conflating lists and evaluates Isvara's exact, limited function.

## Solved PYQ 5 - 2023 Q7(c), 15 marks

**Question:** Explain citta and its modifications. Why prescribe cessation of cittavrttis?

### Model solution

Citta is the material-subtle internal instrument comprising buddhi, ahamkara and manas. It reflects Purusa and assumes forms corresponding to objects. Its five vrttis are:

| Vrtti | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pramana | valid cognition |
| Viparyaya | error |
| Vikalpa | verbal construction without corresponding object |
| Nidra | sleep supported by cognition of absence |
| Smrti | retention and recollection |

Each may be klista or aklista. Even valid cognition is a vrtti because it modifies citta and can sustain identification, though aklista cognition helps generate discrimination.

Yoga prescribes nirodha because the seer mistakes reflected mental states for itself. Modification creates samskara; samskara generates further vrtti and karmic continuity. Through abhyasa and vairagya this cycle is restrained:

```text
vrtti → identification → klesa → karma → samskara → further vrtti
nirodha → viveka → non-identification → kaivalya
```

Nirodha is not destruction of mind or blankness. It is disciplined cessation allowing Purusa to abide in its own nature.

**Verdict:** Cessation is prescribed not because cognition is intrinsically evil, but because unrestrained modification obscures the distinction between seer and seen.

**Why this earns marks:** It defines citta, lists all vrttis, explains the samskara loop and gives a non-nihilistic account of nirodha.

## Solved PYQ 6 - 2024 Q7(b), 15 marks

**Question:** Appraise Yoga soteriology in light of citta-reflection and self-identification.

### Model solution

Yoga accepts the Samkhya dualism of inactive consciousness and active but unconscious citta. Citta is sattva-predominant and transparent; it reflects Purusa like a crystal reflecting a flower. It thereby appears conscious, while Purusa appears to know, act and suffer.

Bondage begins when **asmita** identifies drk-sakti, the seer's power, with darsana-sakti, the seeing instrument. Vrttis become appropriated as "I think," "I desire" and "I suffer." Klesas generate karma, deposits and rebirth.

Yoga's soteriology transforms the reflecting medium:

1. yama-niyama reduce moral disturbance;
2. asana-pranayama stabilize body and vitality;
3. pratyahara withdraws sensory dispersion;
4. dharana-dhyana-samadhi produce one-pointed transparency;
5. viveka-khyati permanently distinguishes Purusa from citta.

Kaivalya does not alter Purusa; it ends misattribution. The gunas cease functioning for that Purusa and return toward their causal ground.

The objection is that reflection presupposes a relation between ontologically separate principles. Mere proximity names rather than explains it. Yoga replies that all change occurs on the citta side; Purusa remains untouched.

**Verdict:** Yoga offers a coherent therapy of misidentification, though its reflection model inherits Samkhya's unresolved interaction problem.

**Why this earns marks:** It translates the quotation, traces the complete therapeutic sequence and gives the strongest metaphysical criticism.

## Solved PYQ 7 - 2025 Q7(b), 15 marks

**Question:** Explain the nature of God and its role in Kaivalya in Yoga.

### Model solution

Isvara is a **special Purusa** untouched by klesas, karma, karmic fruition and latent impressions. He is eternally free, omniscient in the unsurpassed sense and the timeless teacher of earlier teachers. Pranava is his verbal symbol.

Isvara is not Prakriti, not one liberated Purusa among others, and not straightforwardly the creator-God of Nyaya. Patanjali's primary interest is practical. **Isvara-pranidhana** weakens egoism, creates one-pointed devotion, removes obstacles and offers a direct aid to samadhi.

Kaivalya nevertheless consists in discriminative knowledge between Purusa and Prakriti. God does not absorb the individual, grant union with himself, or replace citta-nirodha. His role is privileged but instrumental:

```text
Isvara-pranidhana → reduced obstacles and ego
→ deeper samadhi → viveka-khyati
→ cessation of guna-purpose → kaivalya
```

Critics ask why Isvara is needed if the Samkhya ontology and viveka already suffice. Yoga may concede that other routes exist while insisting that devotion is uniquely efficient and that timeless teacherhood grounds the transmission of liberating knowledge.

**Verdict:** Isvara distinguishes Yoga from nirisvara Samkhya, but kaivalya remains isolation of Purusa through discrimination, not salvation by divine fusion.

**Why this earns marks:** It defines Isvara precisely, states positive and negative functions, connects devotion to kaivalya and evaluates the redundancy objection.

## Original Mains Practice 1 - 10 marks

**Question:** Why does Yoga count valid cognition among the vrttis to be restrained?

### Model solution

Pramana presents objects truly, but it still modifies citta and can sustain identification: "I know." Yoga distinguishes epistemic truth from soteriological freedom. Valid cognition may be aklista and assist viveka, yet any object-form is still part of the seen.

The path therefore uses true cognition to remove error and finally relinquishes even the instrument when stable discrimination is achieved—like a thorn removing a thorn.

**Verdict:** Pramana is not condemned as false; it is transcended because kaivalya requires freedom from all citta-identification.

**Why this earns marks:** It resolves the apparent paradox and distinguishes truth-value from liberating finality.

## Original Mains Practice 2 - 15 marks

**Question:** Is Astanga Yoga an ethical psychology rather than a sequence of exercises?

### Model solution

The eight limbs form a causal architecture. Yama and niyama reduce interpersonal and intrapsychic disturbance. Asana stabilizes embodiment; pranayama regulates vitality and attention; pratyahara breaks sensory compulsion. Dharana, dhyana and samadhi progressively unify cognition.

The limbs therefore transform habits, affects, attention and identity. Without non-violence, truthfulness and non-grasping, concentration may strengthen ego and klesa rather than dissolve them. Without the inner limbs, ethics remains good conduct but does not mature into direct discrimination.

**Verdict:** Astanga is an ethical psychology completed in meditative epistemology; treating it as bodily exercise severs the very mechanism by which it leads to kaivalya.

**Why this earns marks:** It explains the functional relation among all limbs and states why ethical preparation is philosophically necessary.

## Original Mains Practice 3 - 20 marks

**Question:** "Isvara is indispensable to Yoga practice but dispensable to Yoga metaphysics." Critically examine.

### Model solution

Yoga's metaphysical framework largely follows Samkhya: Purusa, Prakriti, gunas, citta, karma and viveka explain bondage and liberation. Kaivalya occurs through discriminative knowledge, not union with or creation by God. This supports the claim of metaphysical dispensability.

Yet Isvara is more than ornament. As purusa-visesa he represents eternally unobstructed consciousness, grounds timeless teacherhood, supplies pranava as meditative support and makes surrender a powerful antidote to egoism. Patanjali explicitly presents Isvara-pranidhana as a route to samadhi.

The strongest instrumental reading says other means—abhyasa, vairagya and astanga—can also lead to realization, so Isvara is a uniquely efficient optional aid. A stronger theistic reading argues that transmission of liberating knowledge and coordination of Purusa-Prakriti require him; later commentators expand this role.

The redundancy objection remains: if Isvara neither creates Prakriti nor grants liberation, Yoga's distinct theism seems thin. The reply is that practical indispensability need not mean cosmological authorship. A perfect intentional object can reorganize citta without replacing its own effort and insight.

**Verdict:** The statement is broadly correct but too sharp. Isvara is not constitutive of the material ontology or formal definition of kaivalya, yet he is philosophically significant as exemplar, teacher and privileged technology of de-centering the ego.

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes metaphysical, epistemic and practical roles, presents competing readings and reaches a qualified verdict.

## FINAL CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES - YOGA

### System Spine

```text
CITTA-VRTTI + KLESA
→ KARMA + SAMSKARA + REBIRTH
→ ABHYASA + VAIRAGYA + KRIYA/ASTANGA
→ SAMADHI + VIVEKA-KHYATI
→ PRATIPRASAVA
→ KAIVALYA
```

### Citta

- Material-subtle inner instrument: buddhi, ahamkara, manas.
- Reflects Purusa and assumes object-forms.
- Five bhumis: ksipta, mudha, viksipta, ekagra, niruddha.
- Ekagra and niruddha are Yoga-fit.

### Five Vrttis

- Pramana: valid cognition.
- Viparyaya: error.
- Vikalpa: word-construction without object.
- Nidra: sleep based on absence-cognition.
- Smrti: recollection.
- Each can be klista or aklista.
- Nirodha is restraint, not unconscious destruction.

### Five Klesas

- Avidya: root misapprehension.
- Asmita: seer-seen identification.
- Raga: attachment.
- Dvesa: aversion.
- Abhinivesa: clinging to life.
- States: prasupta, tanu, vicchinna, udara.

### Eight Limbs

```text
YAMA → NIYAMA → ASANA → PRANAYAMA
→ PRATYAHARA → DHARANA → DHYANA → SAMADHI
```

- Yama: ahimsa, satya, asteya, brahmacarya, aparigraha.
- Niyama: sauca, santosa, tapas, svadhyaya, Isvara-pranidhana.
- Final three combine as samyama.

### Samadhi

- Samprajnata: object-supported cognitive absorption.
- Fourfold character: vitarka, vicara, ananda, asmita.
- Samapattis: savitarka/nirvitarka; savicara/nirvicara.
- Rtambhara prajna: truth-bearing insight.
- Asamprajnata: beyond cognitive objectification, residual seed may remain.
- Nirbija: even seed ceases.
- Dharmamegha: supreme dispassion and final transition.

### Isvara

- Purusa-visesa untouched by klesa, karma, vipaka and asaya.
- Omniscient and timeless teacher.
- Pranava is symbol.
- Removes obstacles and accelerates samadhi.
- Not straightforward Nyaya creator or Vedantic absolute.
- Does not replace viveka-khyati.

### Kaivalya

- Isolation/self-establishment of Purusa.
- Citta completes its purpose; gunas undergo pratiprasava.
- Purusa was never intrinsically modified.
- Not union with Brahman or God.
- Not blank unconsciousness.
