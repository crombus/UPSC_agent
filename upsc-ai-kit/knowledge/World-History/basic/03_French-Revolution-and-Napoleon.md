## 3. Crisis of the Old Regime

| Estate / force | ✅ Source-grounded fact | Historical role |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ First Estate | Clergy enjoyed privilege and tax exemptions | Linked Church to old order |
| ✅ Second Estate | Nobility held privilege, office and much land | Beneficiary of feudal hierarchy |
| ✅ Third Estate | Common people formed about 95% of population in old NCERT | Carried tax burden and political exclusion |
| ✅ Peasantry | Rent, dues, taxes and bad harvests made life insecure | Anti-feudal anger |
| ✅ Bourgeoisie / middle class | Wealth without matching status or power | Leadership in institutional rupture |
| ✅ Urban workers / artisans | Long hours, poverty, secret societies and unrest | Mass pressure from below |

- ✅ Old NCERT calls the French monarchy financially troubled and ultimately pushed toward bankruptcy.
- ✅ Both NCERT and old NCERT link the crisis to **social inequality + fiscal disorder + intellectual challenge**.

## 4. Ideas and phases of the Revolution

| Phase | What happened | What to remember |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ 1789–91 | National Assembly, Bastille, Rights of Man, constitutional monarchy | End of legal privilege begins |
| ✅ 1792–94 | Republic, war, Jacobins, Terror | Revolution radicalises under pressure |
| ✅ 1795–99 | Directory | Instability and fatigue weaken civilian rule |
| ✅ 1799–1815 | Napoleon | Revolutionary gains mixed with authoritarian empire |

```text
fiscal crisis + unequal estates + bread stress
                   +
         Enlightenment attack on privilege
                   ->
         1789 political rupture
                   ->
    war + counter-revolutionary fear + radicalisation
                   ->
         Jacobin republic -> Directory -> Napoleon
```

## 5. Napoleon: continuity and contradiction

- ✅ Old NCERT says feudal laws were annulled and many revolutionary gains survived even after Napoleon seized power.
- ✅ Old NCERT and Britannica both connect Napoleon with the **Napoleonic Code**, which preserved important elements of legal equality and modern civil order.
- ⚠️ Napoleon should be written as both **heir to the Revolution** (legal codification, merit, anti-feudal reform) and **its limiter** (empire, censorship, conquest, concentration of power).

## 6. Must-Know Facts (Prelims) and UPSC traps

- ✅ **14 July 1789** = storming of the Bastille.
- ✅ **Third Estate** represented the overwhelming majority but lacked privilege.
- ✅ **Declaration of the Rights of Man and Citizen** proclaimed equality before law and rights of citizenship.
- ✅ **Jacobins** are associated with radical republicanism and the Reign of Terror.
- ✅ **Napoleonic Code** is a major legal legacy of the revolutionary era.
- ✅ The Revolution gave the term **nation** a modern political meaning in old NCERT.

> 🔑 Trap: Do not present the French Revolution as a single-stage event of 1789 only; UPSC expects phases.

- ❌ Bastille was important because many prisoners were inside. → ✅ Its symbolic political value mattered more than prisoner count.
- ❌ Declaration of Rights immediately ended all inequality. → ✅ It transformed law and legitimacy, but practice remained conflict-ridden.
- ❌ Napoleon simply restored the old order. → ✅ He preserved important anti-feudal legal changes even while ending the republic.

## 7. Exam linkage and Mains angles

- ✅ UPSC GS-I syllabus explicitly covers 18th-century world-history events and their effects on society.
- ⚠️ The strongest answer themes are **destruction of feudal privilege, citizenship, popular sovereignty, nationalism, radicalisation under war, and the Napoleonic legacy**.

### 8.1 Directive and demand map

| If the question says | It is really testing | Do NOT write |
|---|---|---|
| *Analyse the causes* | Weighting of fiscal, social, ideological and conjunctural causes | A month-by-month 1789 narrative |
| *Trace the phases* | Periodisation logic and why each phase produced the next | A flat list of dates |
| *How far did it transform society?* | Measured verdict: legal transformation vs surviving inequality | An unqualified "it changed everything" |
| *Was Napoleon heir or betrayer?* | Split verdict across social vs political inheritance | Choosing one side without evidence |
| *Enduring relevance / contemporary significance* | Transmission of **principles and vocabulary**, plus exclusions and non-linear afterlife | A claim that 1789's institutions survive intact |
| *Compare with the American Revolution* | Difference in social depth, violence and property/feudal question | Treating the two as one "Atlantic revolution" |
| *Revolution and women / slavery* | Rights-universalism vs its limits in practice | Presenting 1789 as immediately universal |

### 8.2 Qualified thesis templates (pick, then prove)

- ⚠️ **Causal:** "The Revolution was not caused by ideas alone; Enlightenment vocabulary converted a fiscal-agrarian crisis into a challenge to the very *legitimacy* of privilege."
- ⚠️ **Transformation:** "The Revolution transformed the basis of legal and political authority far more completely than it transformed the distribution of wealth and power."
- ⚠️ **Napoleon:** "Napoleon consolidated the Revolution's social settlement while dismantling its republican politics."
- ⚠️ **Relevance:** "The Revolution's enduring relevance lies in the categories it made unavoidable — citizen, rights, popular sovereignty, nation — not in the survival of its institutions, and its exclusions are as instructive as its proclamations."

### 8.4 Evidence bank A — causes and the crisis of the old regime

| Claim | Named evidence | Analytical significance | Limitation / caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Privilege was legally, not merely economically, entrenched | ✅ First and Second Estates held tax exemptions, office and land; Third Estate carried the burden | Makes the target of 1789 *legal privilege*, not wealth as such | ⚠️ The Third Estate was internally divided; bourgeois and peasant aims were not identical |
| Fiscal breakdown opened the political door | ✅ Old NCERT: the monarchy was financially troubled and pushed toward bankruptcy; Estates-General summoned May 1789 | Explains *why in 1789* and not earlier | ⚠️ Fiscal crisis alone had been survived before; it became fatal only when combined with ideology and subsistence stress |
| Subsistence stress mobilised the crowd | ✅ NCERT Class IX "The struggle to survive": tax burden and bread crisis | Supplies the mass force behind elite constitutionalism | ⚠️ Bread riots are not by themselves revolutionary programmes |
| Ideas supplied legitimacy, not the grievance | ✅ Locke, Montesquieu, Rousseau (see `basic/01`) | Converts grievance into a claim of right | ⚠️ Most peasants did not read the philosophes; transmission was mediated through pamphlets, clubs and lawyers |

### 8.5 Evidence bank B — enduring relevance to the contemporary world (2025 GS-I owner)

> ⚠️ **Frame the answer as transmission of principles, not survival of institutions.** The claim
> to prove is that modern political argument still uses categories the Revolution forced into
> use — and that the Revolution's own failures are part of that inheritance.

| Enduring principle | What 1789–94 actually established (✅) | Why it still structures politics (⚠️) | Built-in qualification (⚠️) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rights as a public standard** | ✅ Declaration of the Rights of Man and Citizen (Aug 1789) proclaimed equality before law and rights of citizenship | Rights-declarations became the normal form in which political legitimacy is claimed and criticised | The Declaration did not end inequality; it created a standard against which exclusion could be *measured* |
| **Citizenship replacing subjecthood** | ✅ NCERT: new language, symbols and forms of address remade everyday life; ✅ old NCERT dates equal political rights for non-propertied men to the 1792–93 phase | Shifts the political unit from subject-of-a-ruler to citizen-with-claims | Citizenship was gendered and property-conditioned by turns; it was contested, not conferred once |
| **Popular sovereignty** | ✅ Third Estate declares itself the National Assembly (17 June 1789); Tennis Court Oath; monarchy abolished 1792 | Makes constituent power — "who may make the constitution" — a permanent question | Sovereignty of the people was invoked by the Terror as well as by liberals; the principle does not by itself guarantee liberty |
| **The modern nation** | ✅ Old NCERT: the Revolution gave the term **nation** its modern political meaning | Supplies the vocabulary later used by 19th-century unifications and 20th-century anti-colonial movements | Nationalism derived from it was also exclusionary and war-making; see `basic/06`, `basic/09` |
| **Secular public authority** | ✅ The Revolution attacked the ideological authority of the Church and the First Estate's privileges | Establishes the argument that public office and law need no religious sanction | Revolutionary anti-clericalism was itself coercive; secularism has plural later forms |
| **Equality before law as a durable transplant** | ✅ Napoleonic Code (1804) preserved legal equality and anti-feudal reform; ✅ old NCERT: feudal laws annulled and gains survived Napoleon's seizure of power | Shows the *mechanism* of transmission: codification carried the settlement beyond France | The same Code narrowed married women's legal position; transmission was selective |

**Exclusions that must appear in the same answer (this is what converts description into "Explain"):**

- ✅ NCERT devotes a section to "Did women have a revolution?" — women were central to revolutionary action yet were denied equal political citizenship.
- ✅ NCERT covers "The abolition of slavery": universal rights collided with a colonial slave economy; abolition in the revolutionary period was contested and later reversed under Napoleon before final abolition came in 1848.
- ✅ Old NCERT: workers and artisans obtained political voice but **not** economic equality.
- ⚠️ Therefore the honest formulation is: **the Revolution's relevance is double — it supplied the universal claim and simultaneously demonstrated how such claims get bounded in practice.**

**Non-linear legacy (mandatory qualification):**

```text
1789 rights claim
     -> 1793-94 Terror (rights invoked against rights)
     -> 1799-1815 Napoleon (social settlement kept, republic ended)
     -> 1815 Vienna restoration (see basic/05)
     -> 1830 / 1848 renewed revolutions
     -> 19th-c. nationalism and constitutionalism (see basic/06)
```

⚠️ Use this ladder to refuse a straight-line "1789 → modern democracy" story. The Revolution's
principles survived **through repeated contestation, defeat and re-assertion**, not by
uninterrupted institutional continuity.

### 8.6 Evidence bank C — phases, coalitions and the Terror

| Phase | Dominant social coalition (✅/⚠️) | What it achieved | Why it gave way |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1789–91 | ⚠️ Bourgeois-led constitutionalism with peasant and crowd pressure | ✅ End of legal privilege; Declaration; constitutional monarchy | Fiscal, religious and war crises unresolved |
| 1792–94 | ⚠️ Jacobin republicanism resting on sans-culotte Paris | ✅ Republic; wider male political rights; national defence | Emergency coercion outlived the emergency's justification |
| 1795–99 | ⚠️ Propertied republicans | ✅ Retreat from Terror | ✅ Instability shifted power toward the army |
| 1799–1815 | ⚠️ Army, notables and codifying state | ✅ Napoleonic Code; careers open to talent; anti-feudal reform exported | ✅ Empire, censorship and conquest ended republican liberty |

⚠️ **Terror handling rule:** explain it as war emergency **plus** ideological logic **plus**
factional struggle — three causes, not one. Refuse both "necessary" and "insane" as complete
verdicts, and state the cost explicitly.

### 8.7 Verdict scaffolds

- **"How far did it transform society?"** → "Completely at the level of legal status and political legitimacy; substantially for the peasantry through the destruction of feudal dues; only partially for urban workers; and least of all for women and colonial subjects."
- **"Heir or betrayer?"** → "Heir to the social revolution, betrayer of the political revolution — which is precisely why the Restoration could not simply undo 1789."
- **"Enduring relevance?"** → "Relevant not as an institutional model but as the origin of the standard by which modern states are judged — and, through its own exclusions, as the origin of the critique of that standard."

### 8.8 Factual-risk controls

- ❌ Do not give a casualty figure for the Terror in a Core answer unless quoting the sourced figure that ⚠️ old NCERT reports (about **17,000** tried and executed in some 14 months, recorded in `advanced/03`); do not invent totals for the Revolution as a whole.
- ❌ Do not claim the Declaration granted universal suffrage in 1789. It did not.
- ❌ Do not say the Revolution "abolished slavery permanently". Abolition was reversed under Napoleon; final French abolition is 1848.
- ❌ Do not date the Napoleonic Code to the 1790s. ✅ 1804.
- ❌ Do not assert that the French Revolution caused any specific later revolution. Say **influenced / supplied vocabulary**, and keep the causal arrow from `00_Master-Chronology.md` §1 in mind (Enlightenment → both American and French Revolutions in parallel).
- ❌ Do not quote Rousseau, Robespierre or the Declaration verbatim unless the wording is verified; paraphrase instead.
