# Modern World History: Ages, Revolutions and Distinctive Features - Last-Minute Revision Chart

> **Purpose:** One-place revision immediately before GS-I Mains, with factual distinctions that prevent chronology and concept errors.
> **Coverage:** Enlightenment to the post-Cold-War order, including revolutions, industrialisation, nationalism, imperialism, world wars, political ideologies, decolonisation and redrawing of boundaries.
> **Syllabus priority:** World History is primarily a GS-I Mains area. Static factual recall supports analysis, but it should not displace dedicated Prelims subjects.
> **Two-pass design:** The Core chart is independently exam-complete. The optional Advanced layer adds historiography, debate and nuance; skipping it must not damage the paper outcome.

---

## 1. The 60-second chronology ladder

```text
Enlightenment
    -> American Revolution
    -> French Revolution
    -> Napoleon
    -> Vienna settlement
    -> Industrial capitalism and social change
    -> liberalism, nationalism and socialism
    -> Latin American independence
    -> Italian and German unification
    -> New Imperialism and Scramble for Africa
    -> alliance blocs and Balkan crises
    -> First World War
    -> Versailles and League of Nations
    -> Russian Revolution and Stalinism
    -> Fascism, Nazism and Japanese militarism
    -> Second World War and Holocaust
    -> United Nations
    -> Cold War bipolarity
    -> Chinese communist revolution
    -> Asian-African decolonisation
    -> detente and renewed Cold War
    -> collapse of Soviet bloc
    -> post-1991 globalisation, regionalism and new conflicts
```

> **Memory line:** Rights challenged monarchy; industry transformed society; nationalism redrew Europe; imperialism globalised rivalry; world wars destroyed the old order; decolonisation and the Cold War remade it; 1991 ended bipolarity but not conflict.

## 2. Master era-identification chart

| Era / phase | Approximate period | Political signature | Social-economic signature | Fastest identification clue |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| **Enlightenment** | c. 1650s-1780s | Reason challenges divine-right and inherited authority | Individual rights, toleration and rational reform | Locke-rights; Montesquieu-separation; Rousseau-general will |
| **Atlantic revolutions** | 1775-1815 | Colonial independence, popular sovereignty, republic and constitutionalism | Legal equality expands but slavery, gender and property exclusions remain | America = constitutional independence; France = social-political rupture |
| **Industrial Revolution** | c. 1760s-1840s onward | State increasingly manages an industrial society | Factory production, capitalism, urbanisation and working class | Britain first; textiles, coal, iron and steam |
| **Vienna-conservative order** | 1814-1848 | Legitimacy, restoration and balance of power | Liberal and nationalist demands suppressed but survive | Metternich and Concert of Europe |
| **Latin American independence** | c. 1808-1826 | Spanish and Portuguese empires retreat from mainland Americas | Creole-led states retain major social inequalities | Bolivar and San Martin; independence without complete social revolution |
| **National unifications** | 1859-1871 | Italy and Germany consolidated under Piedmont and Prussia | Nationalism becomes an instrument of state power | Cavour-Garibaldi versus Bismarck-three wars |
| **New Imperialism** | c. 1870s-1914 | Formal conquest and partition of Africa and Asia | Industrial markets, raw materials, investment and racial ideology | Berlin Conference regulated partition, not African consent |
| **Pre-war armed peace** | 1871-1914 | Alliance blocs, nationalism, militarism and balance-of-power competition | Naval arms race and imperial rivalry | Sarajevo triggered an already unstable system |
| **First World War** | 1914-1918 | Total war and mass mobilisation | State direction, conscription, civilian suffering and women's expanded work | Trench warfare in West; empires collapse |
| **Versailles-interwar order** | 1919-1939 | National self-determination selectively applied; League collective security | Reparations, instability and Depression weaken liberalism | League lacked power and major-power unity |
| **Russian Revolution and Stalinism** | 1917-1953 | Tsarism falls; Bolshevik one-party state; Stalinist dictatorship | Land revolution, NEP, forced industrialisation, collectivisation and terror | February removed Tsar; October brought Bolsheviks |
| **Fascist-authoritarian challenge** | 1922-1945 | Leader state, ultra-nationalism, anti-liberalism and anti-communism | Corporate control, mobilisation, repression and racial policy | Italy first; Nazism most racial-totalitarian; Japan military-led |
| **Second World War** | 1939-1945 | Axis expansion defeated by Allied coalition | Genocide, total mobilisation, mass destruction and atomic warfare | Poland begins European war; Holocaust central, not incidental |
| **Post-war institutional order** | from 1945 | UN, Bretton Woods and rules-based multilateralism | Reconstruction, welfare-state growth and development institutions | Stronger than League but limited by great-power veto |
| **Cold War bipolarity** | c. 1947-1991 | US-USSR rivalry through blocs, deterrence and proxy wars | Competing capitalist and communist models | No direct general superpower war |
| **Chinese and Asian communisms** | 1949 onward | Peasant-based Chinese revolution and diverse communist regimes | Maoist campaigns followed by Deng's market reform under party rule | Economic opening without political democratisation |
| **Decolonisation** | c. 1945-1975 | European empires retreat; new sovereign states emerge | Colonial borders, weak institutions and dependent economies persist | Political freedom did not guarantee stable development |
| **Detente and renewed tension** | c. 1969-1985 | Arms control and dialogue, then Afghanistan-era confrontation | Strategic restraint without ending ideological rivalry | SALT/Helsinki, then Soviet Afghanistan |
| **End of Cold War** | 1985-1991 | Soviet reform, Eastern European revolutions and USSR collapse | Command-system stagnation and nationalist pressures | Gorbachev sought reform, not dissolution |
| **New world order** | 1991-present | US predominance, EU enlargement, terrorism and contested multipolarity | Globalisation deepens but inequality and crises persist | Cold War ended; history and conflict did not |

## 3. Political ideas: do not interchange them

| Idea / ideology | Core principle | Historical expression | Essential qualification |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Liberalism** | Individual liberty, law, consent and constitutional government | Enlightenment, Atlantic revolutions and 19th-century reform | Early liberalism often restricted voting and property rights |
| **Nationalism** | Political community should govern itself | Italy, Germany, anti-imperial movements | Can liberate subject peoples or justify exclusion and expansion |
| **Conservatism** | Order, tradition, authority and gradual change | Vienna settlement and restoration regimes | Could adapt through controlled reform |
| **Capitalism** | Private ownership, markets and profit-directed production | Industrial Revolution and global trade | Historical capitalism developed with states, empires and regulation |
| **Socialism** | Social ownership/equality and critique of capitalist exploitation | Utopian socialism, Marxism and social democracy | Includes revolutionary and parliamentary forms |
| **Communism** | Classless society through common ownership; historically party-led revolution | Bolshevik USSR, Maoist China and other regimes | Ideal doctrine and actual one-party states must be distinguished |
| **Fascism** | Ultra-nationalist, anti-liberal, anti-communist authoritarian mobilisation | Mussolini's Italy | Not identical to every conservative dictatorship |
| **Nazism** | Fascist authoritarianism plus central racial-biological hierarchy | Hitler's Germany | Anti-Semitism and racial conquest are constitutive, not secondary |
| **Imperialism** | Political-economic domination beyond the metropolitan state | European empires and informal spheres | Motives combined economics, strategy, prestige and ideology |

## 4. American versus French Revolution

| Dimension | American Revolution | French Revolution |
|---|---|---|
| Core period | 1775-1783 | 1789-1799; Napoleonic phase to 1815 |
| Immediate conflict | Colonies versus British imperial authority | Society and nation versus Bourbon Old Regime |
| Main claim | No taxation without representation; independence | End privilege; citizenship, popular sovereignty and equality before law |
| Social transformation | More limited within settler society | Directly attacked feudal privilege and estate order |
| Constitutional result | Durable federal republic and written Constitution | Monarchy, republic, Terror, Directory and Napoleonic empire |
| Key contradiction | Liberty coexisted with slavery and exclusion | Universal rights coexisted with gender, colonial and political exclusions |
| World-historical effect | Model of anti-colonial constitutional revolution | Wider transformation of citizenship, nationalism and social hierarchy |

> **Trap:** Both drew from Enlightenment ideas, but the French Revolution was not simply a copy of the American Revolution.

## 5. French Revolution phase ladder

| Phase | Core event | Distinctive identity |
|---|---|---|
| 1789-1791 | National Assembly, Bastille, Rights Declaration and constitutional monarchy | Legal privilege dismantled |
| 1792-1794 | Republic, war, Jacobins and Terror | Revolution radicalised under internal-external pressure |
| 1795-1799 | Directory | Weak civilian order and political fatigue |
| 1799-1815 | Napoleon | Legal-administrative consolidation plus authoritarian empire |

> **Mains verdict:** Napoleon was both heir and limiter of the Revolution: he preserved legal equality and anti-feudal reform while curbing liberty through empire, censorship and concentrated power.

## 6. Industrial Revolution diagnostic chart

| Question | Exam-ready answer |
|---|---|
| Why Britain first? | Capital, coal and iron, labour released by agrarian change, markets and colonies, shipping, institutions and relative political stability |
| Leading sector | Cotton textiles |
| Transforming energy | Steam power |
| Production shift | Domestic/putting-out system to factory system |
| Social shift | Rural-to-urban migration; industrial bourgeoisie and wage proletariat |
| Political response | Trade unions, Factory Acts, Chartism and later welfare regulation |
| Global effect | Uneven industrialisation, imperial demand for markets/raw materials and international division of labour |
| India linkage | English mechanisation mattered through colonial tariff, market and power structures; it was not a technology-only explanation of Indian deindustrialisation |

```text
capital + resources + labour + markets + innovation
                         ->
            mechanisation and factory production
                         ->
       urbanisation + class formation + productivity
                         ->
 labour conflict + reform + imperial economic expansion
```

## 7. Vienna, Italy and Germany

| Feature | Congress of Vienna | Italian unification | German unification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Date spine | 1814-1815 | 1859-1870/71 | 1864-1871 |
| Political logic | Restoration, legitimacy and balance of power | Nationalism through ideas, diplomacy, popular action and monarchy | Nationalism through Prussian state power and war |
| Central strategist | Metternich | Cavour | Bismarck |
| Popular-national figure | Revolutionary nationalism suppressed | Mazzini and Garibaldi | No direct Garibaldi equivalent |
| Core state | Great-power concert | Piedmont-Sardinia | Prussia |
| Decisive endpoint | Conservative European settlement | Rome incorporated, 1870 | Empire proclaimed at Versailles, 1871 |

> **Trap:** Mazzini inspired republican nationalism, but Cavour, Garibaldi and Victor Emmanuel II completed unification. Bismarck excluded Austria and unified Germany under Prussia.

## 8. Imperialism and decolonisation: opposite movements

| Dimension | New Imperialism | Decolonisation |
|---|---|---|
| Main period | c. 1870s-1914 | Mainly 1945-1975 |
| Direction | Expansion of European rule | Retreat of European empires |
| Drivers | Industry, strategy, prestige, nationalism, technology and racial ideology | Nationalism, European exhaustion, anti-imperial legitimacy, international pressure and cost of repression |
| Boundary effect | Borders often imposed externally | New states usually inherited colonial borders |
| Key African marker | Berlin Conference, 1884-85 | Ghana 1957; Congo 1960; Algeria 1962; Portuguese Africa 1975 |
| Social consequence | Extraction, coercion and restructuring of local economies | Sovereignty alongside institutional and economic inheritance |
| Essential qualification | Europeans faced sustained African and Asian resistance | Independence did not automatically create democracy or development |

> **Trap:** The Berlin Conference did not grant Europeans a legitimate African mandate; it regulated competition among imperial powers.

## 9. First World War: cause-to-consequence chain

```text
nationalism + alliance blocs + arms race + imperial tension
                         +
            Balkan instability and Austro-Serb rivalry
                         ->
                 Sarajevo assassination
                         ->
 ultimatum + mobilisation plans + German support + Belgian invasion
                         ->
                     world war
```

| Layer | Core content |
|---|---|
| Immediate trigger | Assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand at Sarajevo, 28 June 1914 |
| Regional cause | Serbian nationalism versus survival of multinational Austria-Hungary |
| Structural causes | Alliances, militarism, nationalism, imperial rivalry and balance-of-power anxiety |
| Escalation mechanism | Russian mobilisation, German support for Austria and attack through Belgium |
| Why total war? | Industrial weapons, mass armies, state mobilisation and global empires |
| Major result | German, Austro-Hungarian, Ottoman and Russian empires collapse |
| Political effect | New states and mandates; Bolshevik Revolution; enlarged US role |
| Social effect | Casualties, displacement, state control and changed gender/work patterns |

> **PYQ-safe verdict:** Balance-of-power preservation was important but insufficient alone; Balkan nationalism, alliance obligations, mobilisation, imperial rivalry and contingent July 1914 decisions converted tension into war.

## 10. Versailles and League of Nations

| Instrument / issue | Core feature | Why the system weakened |
|---|---|---|
| Treaty of Versailles | German territorial losses, military restrictions, reparations and war-guilt clause | Resentment without stable reconciliation |
| National self-determination | New states in Europe | Selective and incomplete; minorities remained |
| Mandate system | Former German/Ottoman territories administered under League authority | Often continued imperial control in a new form |
| League Assembly/Council | Collective-security consultation | Decisions depended on member cooperation |
| US absence | Senate rejected membership | Major power missing |
| Manchuria | Japanese aggression, 1931 | Sanctions and enforcement failed |
| Abyssinia | Italian aggression, 1935 | Great powers prioritised interests over collective security |
| Appeasement | Concessions intended to avoid war | Encouraged further aggression when unsupported by credible deterrence |

> **Trap:** The League had some humanitarian and dispute-settlement successes, but failed on great-power aggression.

## 11. Russian Revolution and Stalinism

| Phase | Event / policy | Diagnostic identity |
|---|---|---|
| 1905 | Revolution, October Manifesto and Duma | Autocracy survives without solving structural crisis |
| February/March 1917 | Tsar abdicates | Broad collapse of tsarism, not Bolshevik seizure |
| October/November 1917 | Bolsheviks overthrow Provisional Government | Peace, land and soviet-power programme |
| 1918 | Brest-Litovsk | Exit from WWI at huge territorial cost |
| 1918-1920 | Civil War | Red victory through leadership, central geography and divided Whites |
| 1921 | NEP | Limited markets under communist political monopoly |
| From 1928 | Five Year Plans | Rapid heavy industrialisation |
| From 1929 | Collectivisation | State control of agriculture, coercion and famine |
| 1930s | Purges | Personal dictatorship and terror |

> **Trap:** Lenin did not create a liberal democracy later destroyed only by Stalin. Party monopoly, coercion and dissolution of the Constituent Assembly began under Lenin; Stalin radically intensified and systematised repression.

## 12. Fascism, Nazism and Japanese militarism

| Dimension | Italian Fascism | German Nazism | Japanese militarism | Franco's Spain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rise marker | Mussolini appointed PM after March on Rome, 1922 | Hitler Chancellor, 1933; Enabling Act | Manchuria 1931; army dominance | Civil War, 1936-39 |
| Political form | Fascist leader-party state | Racial-totalitarian party state | Military-authoritarian imperial state | Conservative-authoritarian dictatorship |
| Main mobilisation | National rebirth and anti-communism | National rebirth, race, anti-Semitism and Lebensraum | Emperor-centred nationalism and expansion | Army, Church, order and anti-left coalition |
| Economic form | Corporate state claims; private property controlled | Rearmament and directed economy | Military-industrial expansion | More traditional authoritarian structure |
| Distinctive warning | First fascist regime | Most radical and genocidal form | Not a copy of European mass-party fascism | Related to, but not identical with, fascism |

> **Common rise mechanism:** post-war grievance + economic crisis + weak parliamentary order + fear of socialism + elite accommodation + propaganda and coercion.

## 13. Second World War identification chart

| Question | Exam-ready answer |
|---|---|
| Long-term causes | Versailles weaknesses, fascist expansion, Depression, failed collective security and appeasement |
| Immediate European trigger | German invasion of Poland, 1 September 1939 |
| Globalisation of war | European, African, Atlantic, Soviet and Asia-Pacific theatres converge |
| Decisive turning zone | 1942-43: Midway, El Alamein and Stalingrad reverse Axis momentum |
| Genocide | Holocaust: systematic Nazi persecution and murder of European Jews, alongside other victim groups |
| End in Europe | Germany surrenders, May 1945 |
| End in Asia | Atomic bombs and Soviet entry against Japan precede Japanese surrender, August/September 1945 |
| Structural consequences | Axis defeat, UN, US-USSR superpowers, European decline, decolonisation and Cold War |

> **Trap:** The Second World War was not merely a continuation of the First. Versailles mattered, but fascist ideology, Depression-era breakdown, expansionist choices and failures of deterrence were independent causes.

## 14. League versus United Nations

| Dimension | League of Nations | United Nations |
|---|---|---|
| Founded | 1920 | 1945 |
| Great-power design | US absent; unstable participation | Major powers embedded through Security Council |
| Enforcement | Weak sanctions and dependence on unanimity/cooperation | Security Council can authorise binding measures |
| Great-power limitation | Aggressors ignored it | P5 veto can block action |
| Security practice | Limited collective security | Peacekeeping evolved though not explicit in Charter |
| Core verdict | Institution could not compensate for absent political will | Stronger design, but still dependent on power politics |

## 15. Cold War phase ladder

| Phase | Markers | Identity |
|---|---|---|
| Origins, 1945-1949 | Eastern Europe, Truman Doctrine, Marshall Plan, Berlin blockade and NATO | Wartime alliance becomes bipolar confrontation |
| Militarisation, 1950-1962 | Korea, Warsaw Pact, Berlin Wall and Cuba | Proxy war and nuclear brinkmanship |
| Managed rivalry, 1963-1979 | Arms control, Sino-Soviet split, Vietnam, SALT and Helsinki | Detente reduces risk, not competition |
| Renewed Cold War, 1979-1985 | Soviet Afghanistan, missile tensions and US pressure | Confrontation returns |
| Ending, 1985-1991 | Gorbachev, INF, Eastern Europe and Soviet collapse | Reform and non-intervention undermine bloc control |

| Theatre | Local cause plus Cold War layer |
|---|---|
| Korea | Korean division and civil conflict plus US-China-USSR rivalry |
| Vietnam | Anti-colonial nationalism and communist revolution plus containment |
| Cuba | Cuban revolution and US hostility plus Soviet strategic deployment |
| Middle East | Territory, nationalism, refugees, oil and security plus superpower involvement |
| Afghanistan | Internal revolution and regional conflict plus Soviet intervention and US-backed resistance |

> **Trap:** Proxy conflicts had autonomous local causes. The Cold War internationalised them but did not create every grievance.

## 16. Chinese Revolution and Mao-Deng distinction

| Phase | Core feature | Main outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1911 Revolution | Qing/Manchu dynasty falls | Republic without stable state authority |
| Warlord/KMT-CCP struggle | Fragmentation, nationalism and civil war | Competing state-building projects |
| Long March, 1934-35 | Communist strategic retreat | Mao's leadership consolidated |
| Japanese invasion | National crisis from 1937 | KMT weakened; CCP expands legitimacy |
| Communist victory, 1949 | Mao proclaims PRC | KMT retreats to Taiwan |
| Great Leap Forward, 1958-61 | Communes and crash industrialisation | Severe disruption and famine |
| Cultural Revolution, 1966-76 | Mass ideological mobilisation | Institutional, educational and social damage |
| Deng reforms, from 1978 | Markets, decollectivisation and foreign investment | Rapid growth under continued one-party rule |
| Tiananmen, 1989 | Protest suppressed | Political liberalisation blocked |

> **Trap:** Mao adapted communism to a peasant-majority society. Deng reformed the economy without ending Communist Party political monopoly.

## 17. Decolonisation case-pattern chart

| Case | Pattern | Diagnostic lesson |
|---|---|---|
| India/Pakistan | Negotiated British withdrawal with Partition | Freedom and mass displacement coexist |
| Ghana | Relatively constitutional transfer | Smooth independence does not ensure later stability |
| Algeria | Violent settler-colonial war | Settler interests made withdrawal exceptionally bitter |
| Congo | Hurried transfer, weak institutions and external intervention | Sovereignty can precede viable state capacity |
| Vietnam | Anti-colonial and revolutionary war | Nationalism and communism reinforce each other |
| Angola/Mozambique | Late Portuguese withdrawal amid armed struggle | Decolonisation overlaps Cold War civil conflict |

```text
colonial nationalism + imperial exhaustion + global anti-colonial legitimacy
                                  ->
                              independence
                                  +
colonial borders + narrow economies + weak institutions + Cold War intervention
                                  ->
             uneven post-colonial stability and development
```

## 18. World economy since 1900

| Phase | Defining mechanism | Social-political consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-1914 global economy | Trade, migration, finance and empire | Integration under European dominance |
| World wars/interwar crisis | State control, debt, reparations and Depression | Protectionism, unemployment and extremist politics |
| Bretton Woods era | IMF, World Bank, fixed-rate framework and US leadership | Reconstruction and managed capitalism |
| Post-war boom | Welfare states, mass production and consumer growth | Rising living standards in industrial economies |
| Decolonised development | Import substitution, planning and aid | Mixed results under structural dependence |
| 1970s crisis | Oil shocks, inflation and slowdown | Neoliberal turn gains influence |
| Globalisation from 1980s/90s | Trade, finance, supply chains and technology | Growth and integration alongside inequality and vulnerability |
| Demographic transition | Mortality decline precedes fertility decline | Rapid population growth followed by ageing at different speeds |

## 19. Post-1991 world order

| Development | Core significance | Essential qualification |
|---|---|---|
| USSR collapse | Bipolar order ends | Gorbachev intended reform, not state dissolution |
| US unipolar moment | One superpower possesses unmatched reach | Predominance did not create uncontested legitimacy |
| Yugoslav wars | Nationalism and state breakup return to Europe | Not explained by "ancient hatred" alone |
| European Union | Integration deepens through Maastricht and widens eastward | Tension persists between sovereignty, widening and deepening |
| 9/11 and War on Terror | Transnational terrorism reshapes security policy | Afghanistan and Iraq reveal limits and costs of intervention |
| Arab Spring | Mass challenge to authoritarian regimes | Outcomes differ sharply across states |
| Multipolarity debate | China and other powers alter global balance | Treat post-2013 developments through dated Current Affairs, not undated history claims |

## 20. Redrawing of boundaries: high-yield sequence

| Settlement / event | Boundary effect |
|---|---|
| Congress of Vienna, 1815 | Restored dynastic balance after Napoleon |
| Italian unification, 1859-70 | Consolidated fragmented peninsula |
| German unification, 1864-71 | Created Prussian-led German Empire |
| Berlin Conference, 1884-85 | Regulated European partition claims in Africa |
| Paris Peace Settlement, 1919-20 | Dissolved empires and created/reconstituted European states and mandates |
| WWII settlements, 1945 onward | Germany divided; Soviet influence expands in Eastern Europe |
| Decolonisation, 1945-75 | Colonial territories become sovereign states, usually within inherited borders |
| German reunification, 1990 | Cold War division ends |
| USSR breakup, 1991 | Fifteen union republics become independent states |
| Yugoslav breakup, 1991 onward | New Balkan states emerge through conflict |

> **Mains rule:** Boundary change is never only cartographic. Link it to legitimacy, minorities, migration, violence, state capacity and international power.

## 21. Optional Advanced enrichment: deeper interpretation

| Topic | Core identification | Optional Advanced correction |
|---|---|---|
| Enlightenment | Reason and rights challenge inherited authority | Ideas travelled through print, salons, institutions and political conflict; they did not mechanically cause revolutions. |
| American Revolution | Anti-colonial constitutional revolution | Its universal language coexisted with slavery, Indigenous dispossession and restricted citizenship. |
| French Revolution | End of privilege and rise of citizenship | Class, fiscal crisis, political contingency, popular action and war all mattered; no single-cause theory is sufficient. |
| Industrial Revolution | Technology, factories and class society | Industrialisation was a long, uneven process; empire contributed markets and resources but did not alone explain Britain's take-off. |
| Vienna order | Conservative restoration and balance | It produced relative great-power stability while postponing, not eliminating, liberal-national questions. |
| National unification | Nation-states built through leadership and war | "Nationalism from below" interacted with monarchical and military state-building from above. |
| Imperialism | Economic, strategic and ideological expansion | Avoid one-cause economic determinism; metropolitan rivalry and colonial agency shaped outcomes. |
| WWI origins | Sarajevo plus structural tensions | Distinguish long-term conditions from choices during the July Crisis; war was possible without being mechanically inevitable. |
| Versailles | Punitive and unstable peace | The settlement also attempted collective security and nationality principles; its failure arose from design and implementation. |
| Russian Revolution | War accelerates autocratic collapse | Separate structural weakness, contingency, popular mobilisation and Bolshevik organisation. |
| Stalinism | Industrialisation plus terror | Modernising achievement cannot erase coercion, famine and repression; debate concerns causation and alternatives, not whether suffering occurred. |
| Fascism | Crisis of liberal order | Elite collaboration, mass appeal, coercion and institutional weakness interacted; fascism was not merely capitalist conspiracy or popular revolution. |
| WWII | Expansionist Axis war | Treat Holocaust as a central ideological project within Nazi rule and war, not a side effect. |
| Cold War | Ideology, security dilemma and power rivalry | Orthodox, revisionist and post-revisionist interpretations assign responsibility differently; reciprocal insecurity is not moral equivalence. |
| Chinese Revolution | Peasant-rooted communist victory | KMT weakness, Japanese invasion, CCP organisation and land politics mattered together. |
| Decolonisation | Nationalism plus imperial exhaustion | Colonised peoples were agents of change; European withdrawal was not a voluntary gift. |
| African state crises | Colonial inheritance and weak capacity | Avoid deterministic claims that ethnic diversity automatically causes failure; institutions, leadership and external intervention matter. |
| Soviet collapse | Stagnation, reform and nationalism | Reform weakened coercion faster than it built a legitimate alternative; collapse was contingent, not pre-programmed. |
| Globalisation | Intensified cross-border integration | It redistributes power and risk unevenly; neither inevitable convergence nor simple Westernisation explains it. |

> **Firewall rule:** Core alone must supply every necessary cause, mechanism, comparison, consequence and balanced verdict. Advanced improves sophistication but must remain optional.

## 22. Evidence-to-inference rules

| Evidence | Safe inference | Unsafe overreach |
|---|---|---|
| Declaration / constitution | Formal political ideals and institutional claims | Equal enjoyment of rights by every social group |
| Treaty text | Agreed legal settlement | Durable reconciliation or faithful implementation |
| Election result | Support under a specific electoral system and moment | Unanimous national consent |
| Propaganda poster | Regime message, target audience and mobilisation method | Actual belief of the whole population |
| Production statistics | Direction and scale of economic change | Human welfare without wages, consumption and coercion data |
| Colonial map | Formal territorial claim | Effective control or legitimate consent |
| Diplomatic memoir | Participant perception and justification | Neutral reconstruction of responsibility |
| Wartime speech | Declared objective and mobilisation language | Complete motive for entering or continuing war |
| Institutional charter | Structure and legal mandate | Automatic capacity to overcome member-state interests |
| Demographic data | Population trend | Cause without social, economic and policy context |

> **Answer rule:** Separate **idea**, **institution**, **implementation**, **social effect** and **historical legacy**. UPSC questions often reward precisely this movement from promise to practice.
