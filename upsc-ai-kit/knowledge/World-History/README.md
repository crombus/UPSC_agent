## How this folder works

- **Two tiers per topic:** `basic/NN` is the **exam-complete Core** — the standard global narrative
  (what happened, why, core actors and concepts) **plus** a topic-specific `Answer architecture
  (10/15/20-mark support)` section containing the directive/demand map, qualified thesis
  templates, mark-scaled structures, named evidence banks for each distinct demand family,
  verdict scaffolds and factual-risk controls. `advanced/NN` is **optional enrichment**:
  historiographical debate, named-historian positions and interpretive nuance. Skipping the
  advanced tier should not change the paper outcome.
- **Revise by comparison:** Before a test, use
  `REVISION-CHART_Ages-Eras-and-Distinctive-Features.md` for chronology, ideologies, causal
  chains, boundary changes, high-frequency comparisons and balanced verdicts.
- **Same number = same topic** across both tiers (e.g., `basic/13_...md` pairs with
  `advanced/13_...md`).
- **Tagging:** ✅ = directly grounded in a cited source (Lowe's text, NCERT/NIOS, Britannica,
  archival material); ⚠️ = analytical inference, historiographical synthesis, or an
  interpretive verdict (including where a source author's own argued opinion is being
  reported — it is still marked ⚠️ as an interpretation rather than a bare fact); 📰 = a
  verified, dated current-affairs anchor (used sparingly here, since most topics are historical;
  present mainly in Topics 16, 18, 20, 21 where live institutional/geopolitical continuity
  exists).
- **Answer discipline:** no invented dates, statistics, quotes, or PYQ question numbers appear
  in this folder. Where a specific UPSC PYQ could not be verified, the file cites the general,
  real UPSC GS-I/GS-II syllabus line and flags the theme as "⚠️ recurring UPSC theme" instead of
  fabricating a question number.
- **Cross-links, not duplication:** Indian-side depth (colonial economic drain and
  deindustrialisation, the Indian freedom movement's own periodisation, Non-Aligned Movement
  positioning, post-1991 liberalisation) is already covered by the `Modern-Indian-History` and
  `Economy` folders; this folder cross-links to them rather than re-explaining that material.

## Recent exact PYQ support route

> **Core-sufficiency rule:** for every exact PYQ below, the **Core (`basic/`) owner is
> sufficient**. Each Core owner carries a topic-specific `Answer architecture
> (10/15/20-mark support)` section with directive maps, thesis templates, mark-scaled
> structures, demand-family evidence banks, verdict scaffolds and factual-risk controls.
> `advanced/` files are optional historiographical enrichment; skipping them should not change
> the mark.
>
> **How this relates to the older ledgers.** The authoritative ledgers
> `_PYQ-ROUTING-MAINS-GS1-GS2-ESSAY-2024-2025.md` and `_PYQ-ROUTING-MAINS-GS1-GS2-ESSAY-2018-2023.md`
> remain the record of which question exists and which subject owns it. **Where this folder's
> architecture has been promoted from the Advanced tier into Core, the Core routing below
> supersedes any older ledger entry that points at an `advanced/` file.** This README does **not**
> claim to reproduce every owner recorded in those ledgers, and it does not claim that every older
> ledger entry already names the Core file: several older entries name `advanced/` owners that are
> now enrichment only. Where the two disagree, **use the Core owner named here** and treat the
> ledger entry as a superseded pointer, not as a contradiction to be resolved in the ledger's
> favour.
>
> **Coverage limit, stated honestly.** The ledgers held in this repository begin at **2018**. No
> verbatim text for any pre-2018 paper is held locally. Pre-2018 demands that this folder
> nonetheless supports are listed separately below as **neutral renderings**, with no invented
> question number or wording.

- **2024 GS-I:** “How far was the Industrial Revolution in England responsible for the decline of handicrafts and cottage industries in India? (Answer in 250 words)” → the ledger routes this question to **`Modern-Indian-History/basic/07_Economic-Impact-of-British-Rule.md`**, which is the primary owner and holds colonial policy, Indian deindustrialisation and the balanced “how far” verdict. The **world-side English mechanism** required by the prompt is supplied by **`basic/04_Industrial-Revolution.md` §8.5** (mechanisation → steam → factory system → transport → structural pressure outward), together with its explicit boundary rule and its caution against overstating an automatic outcome. `advanced/04` is not required.
- **2024 GS-I:** “How far is it correct to say that the First World War was fought essentially for the preservation of balance of power? (Answer in 250 words)” → **`basic/09_World-in-1914-and-Outbreak-of-WWI.md` §8**, which concedes what balance of power explains (§8.4), sets out what it fails to explain — Habsburg imperial survival, South Slav nationalism, French revanchism, German preventive timing, mobilisation rigidity and the contingent July decisions (§8.5) — supplies the escalation chain (§8.6) and a graded “how far” verdict (§8.7). `advanced/09` adds named historiography as optional enrichment.
- **2025 GS-I:** “The French Revolution has enduring relevance to the contemporary world. Explain. (Answer in 250 words)” → **`basic/03_French-Revolution-and-Napoleon.md` §8.5**, which supplies the rights, citizenship, popular sovereignty, nationhood, secular-authority and legal-equality banks, the exclusions (women, enslaved people and colonial subjects, workers) and the non-linear legacy ladder that prevents any claim of unchanged institutions. `advanced/03` is optional.
- **2023 GS-I (neutral rendering — no verbatim text is held locally):** socio-economic effects of the introduction of railways in different countries → **`basic/04_Industrial-Revolution.md` §8.6**, which differentiates effects by country type (early industrialiser, continental catch-up, settler frontier, late state-assisted, colonial) rather than offering one generic paragraph.
- **2021 GS-I (neutral rendering):** challenge to the democratic state system between the two World Wars → **`basic/12_Rise-of-Fascism-Italy-Germany-Japan.md` §10.4**, which sets out the eight mechanisms by which interwar parliamentary democracy lost legitimacy, including elite complicity.
- **2019 GS-I (neutral rendering):** the American and French Revolutions and the foundations of the modern world → **`basic/02_American-Revolution.md` §8.6** (exportable constitutional models) with **`basic/01` §8.6** (the disciplined Atlantic comparison) and **`basic/03` §8.5** (the French contribution). Give each revolution its own distinct contribution before stating the shared inheritance.
- **Boundary:** this folder must not duplicate India's drain, tariff, patronage-loss or artisan-displacement analysis merely because the prompt begins with the English Industrial Revolution. `Modern-Indian-History` remains the Indian-side owner.
