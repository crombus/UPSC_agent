#### 1. Snapshot and syllabus fit

✅ This topic supplies the vocabulary every later architecture file in this
folder reuses (material, plan, elevation, function) and answers the direct
2025 Mains question on Harappan architecture. ⚠️ Its exam value is
twofold: as a stand-alone Prelims/Mains topic on the Indus Valley
Civilisation (IVC), and as the foundation for tracing how Indian
architecture evolved from Harappan urban planning through Mauryan,
temple, Indo-Islamic and colonial phases (topics 02-05).

#### 2. Essential vocabulary

| Term | Exam-ready meaning |
|---|---|
| ✅ **Citadel** | The upraised, western part of a Harappan town, used for large-dimension buildings such as granaries, administrative buildings and pillared halls (*Nitin…pdf*, PDF p. 33). |
| ✅ **Lower town** | The larger, lower-lying residential part of a Harappan town, containing small one-roomed houses for working-class residents alongside larger private houses (*Nitin…pdf*, PDF p. 34). |
| ✅ **Great Bath** | The most famous public bath of the Harappan civilisation, excavated at Mohenjo-daro, surrounded by galleries and rooms (*Nitin…pdf*, PDF p. 34). |
| ✅ **Standardised burnt brick** | Harappan builders used burnt mud bricks of standardised dimensions, laid in many layers and joined with gypsum mortar (*Nitin…pdf*, PDF p. 33). |
| ✅ **Drainage system** | Small house drains connected to larger covered street drains, with cesspits at regular intervals, allowing regular cleaning (*Nitin…pdf*, PDF p. 34). |
| ⚠️ **"Meluha"** | Many scholars argue Mesopotamians of the Tigris-Euphrates valley called the Indus Valley Civilisation "Meluha"; Indus seals have been found in Mesopotamia, evidencing trade contact (*Nitin…pdf*, PDF p. 35). |

#### 3. Core classification

✅ The book classifies the evolution of Indian architecture, by time, into
five broad periods that this folder's architecture topics (01-05) trace in
sequence: Harappan; Mauryan and post-Mauryan (rock-cut and stupa
architecture); temple architecture (Nagara/Dravida/Vesara); Indo-Islamic
architecture (Sultanate, provincial, Mughal, regional styles); and modern/
colonial and post-Independence architecture (*Nitin…pdf*, PDF p. 32).

✅ Harappan town planning itself shows a threefold building classification:
dwelling houses, public buildings, and public baths (*Nitin…pdf*, PDF p.
33).

#### 4. Foundation narrative

✅ Harappa and Mohenjo-daro are the two major Harappan sites and "among the
earliest and finest examples of urban civic planning," with a "planned
network of roads, houses, and drainage systems" (*Nitin…pdf*, PDF p. 32).
✅ Towns were laid out in a rectangular grid, with roads running north-south
and east-west, cutting each other at right angles (*Nitin…pdf*, PDF p. 33).
❌ "Indus Valley Civilisation sites do not have large monumental structures
such as temples or palaces for rulers unlike the Egyptian and Mesopotamian
Civilisation" (*Nitin…pdf*, PDF p. 33) — a direct correction to any answer
that assumes Harappan cities had palace or temple complexes as settled
fact. ⚠️ Structures conventionally labelled "granaries" used raised platforms
and ventilation, but their exact function is an archaeological
interpretation rather than an inscriptionally confirmed name. ✅ Most
buildings had private wells and ventilated bathrooms, and
some lower-town houses had stairs suggesting double storeys (*Nitin…pdf*,
PDF p. 34).

✅ **Regional variation matters:** Mohenjo-daro's Great Bath and drainage,
Harappa's large brick platforms, Dholavira's fortified multi-part plan and
extensive water-storage works, Lothal's basin (conventionally called a
dockyard, with function debated), and Kalibangan's distinctive planning
show a shared urban vocabulary without identical city plans. R.S. Sharma
confirms grid planning, household bathing/drainage and Dholavira's
fortification, while also stressing that the undeciphered script sharply
limits claims about polity and belief (*India's Ancient Past*, PDF pp.
94-101).

#### 9. ❌ Traps and corrections

- ❌ Harappan buildings can be confidently labelled "palaces" or "temples."
  -> The book explicitly states no such large monumental religious/royal

- ❌ Harappan town planning was irregular or organic. -> It followed a
  strict rectangular grid with roads at right angles.
- ❌ The drainage system was a minor detail. -> It is repeatedly
  highlighted as the civilisation's most advanced and distinctive
  engineering feature, alongside standardised bricks.
- ❌ All Harappan houses were uniform in size and status. -> The citadel/
  lower-town division and variation in house size (including some
  double-storeyed houses) show social and functional differentiation.
- ❌ "Granary," "dockyard" and "ritual bath" are ancient Harappan labels.

- ❌ The horned figure on one seal proves a settled proto-Shiva cult. ->
  R.S. Sharma explicitly calls the Pashupati identification doubtful

##### 13.2 Qualified thesis options

- ⚠️ **Civic-engineering thesis (default for 2025-type wording):** "Harappan
  architecture is best described not as monumental but as *civic*: its
  achievement lies in a repeated grid plan, standardised burnt brick,
  zoned settlement and a networked drainage system, which together imply
  coordinated norms rather than displays of individual royal power."
- ⚠️ **Standardisation-through-adaptation thesis (comparison/variation
  demands):** "Harappan urbanism shows shared planning norms executed
  through site-specific solutions — the same brick module and drainage
  logic appear at Mohenjo-daro, Harappa, Dholavira, Lothal and
  Kalibangan, but no two plans are identical."
- ⚠️ **Evidence-limit thesis (critical/'to what extent' demands):** "The
  built form supports strong claims about technology, hygiene and
  planning, weaker claims about social differentiation, and no settled
  claim at all about religion or kingship — because the script is
  undeciphered and no structure is inscriptionally named."
- ⚠️ **Comparative thesis:** "The absence of confirmed temples and palaces
  is not a deficiency but a difference: Harappan surplus and coordination
  went into shared civic infrastructure, where Egyptian and Mesopotamian
  surplus went into monumental religious and royal architecture."

##### 13.4 Evidence bank A — salient features (the description/'discuss' family)

| Feature | ✅ Named evidence | Analytical significance | Limitation / caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grid plan | ✅ Roads running north–south and east–west, cutting at right angles (*Nitin…pdf*, PDF p. 33) | Right-angled streets are the clearest single proof of pre-planning rather than accretion | ⚠️ Grid regularity varies between sites and between phases |
| Two-part settlement | ✅ Upraised western citadel with large-dimension buildings; larger, lower residential town (*Nitin…pdf*, PDF pp. 33-34) | Functional zoning — administration/storage/ritual separated from dwelling | ⚠️ Zoning is functional evidence; it does not by itself prove a rigid social hierarchy |
| Public architecture | ✅ Structures conventionally identified as granaries (raised platforms, air ducts), pillared halls, and the Great Bath at Mohenjo-daro with surrounding galleries and rooms (*Nitin…pdf*, PDF pp. 33-34) | Investment in *shared* facilities rather than in royal display | ❌ "Granary," "dockyard" and "ritual bath" are modern interpretive labels — say "conventionally identified as" |
| Drainage network | ✅ House drains → covered street drains → cesspits at regular intervals, allowing cleaning (*Nitin…pdf*, PDF p. 34) | The system is *networked and maintainable*, which is what distinguishes it from isolated soakage pits elsewhere | ⚠️ Coverage was not uniform across every quarter of every site |
| Material and module | ✅ Burnt mud bricks of standardised dimensions, laid in many layers, joined with gypsum mortar (*Nitin…pdf*, PDF p. 33) | A shared brick module across distant sites implies a common technical or administrative norm | ❌ Do not say stone was the primary Harappan building material |
| Domestic architecture | ✅ Private wells, ventilated bathrooms, stairs implying double storeys in some lower-town houses (*Nitin…pdf*, PDF p. 34) | Hygiene and water access were designed into ordinary houses, not reserved for elites | ⚠️ House size varies; read differentiation, not equality or rigid stratification |
| Regional adaptation | ✅ Dholavira's fortified multi-part plan with extensive water storage; Lothal's basin; Kalibangan's distinctive planning; Harappa's large brick platforms (R.S. | Dimension | ✅ Harappan | ✅ Egypt / Mesopotamia | Use in an answer |

##### 13.6 Evidence bank C — the evidence-to-inference chain (critical / 'to what extent' family)

```text
DIRECT EVIDENCE (form)                 SUPPORTED INFERENCE (function)
grid roads at right angles      -->    coordinated planning norms
standardised brick + mortar     -->    shared technical/administrative standard
raised platforms + air ducts    -->    organised storage (label debated)
Great Bath + galleries          -->    collective/ceremonial use (⚠️ inferred)
citadel vs. lower town          -->    functional, possibly social, zoning
covered drains + cesspits       -->    sustained maintenance, not one-off build
NO confirmed temple/palace      -->    a different, not absent, expression of
                                        religion and authority
```

- ⚠️ **Strong claims the form supports:** planning, standardisation,
  hydraulic engineering, hygiene, storage capacity, labour organisation.
- ⚠️ **Weak claims:** the precise nature of social stratification, craft
  specialisation hierarchies, the identity of decision-makers.
- ❌ **Claims the form cannot support:** named rulers, a state religion, a
  varna-type order, or the Pashupati identification — R.S. Sharma
  explicitly calls the proto-Shiva reading doubtful because horned deities
  occur elsewhere and the expected bull is absent (*India's Ancient Past*,
  PDF p. 100). Safe phrasing: "a horned seated figure, often interpreted
  as proto-Shiva/Pashupati."

##### 13.7 Continuity, change and the decline caution

- ⚠️ **Defensible continuity:** burnt-brick construction, water structures
  (wells, tanks, bathing platforms) and the idea of an ordered street
  layout recur in later South Asian urbanism.
- ⚠️ **Defensible discontinuity:** monumental stone architecture, temple
  form and inscriptional patronage belong to much later periods (topics
  02-03); the Harappan repertoire does not evolve smoothly into them.
- ❌ **Prohibited:** attributing Harappan decline to a single named cause
  (invasion, one river shift, one flood) in an architecture answer. The
  decline debate belongs to Ancient History; here, note only that urban
  planning features fade in the late/post-urban phase and say so as a
  bounded observation.
- ⚠️ **External corroboration available:** many scholars hold that
  Mesopotamians called the civilisation "Meluha," and Indus seals found in
  Mesopotamia evidence trade contact (*Nitin…pdf*, PDF p. 35) — usable to
  show that Harappan cities were nodes in a wider network, not isolated.
