# Social Justice: Rights, Capabilities and Distinctive Features - Last-Minute Revision Chart

> **Purpose:** One-place revision immediately before GS-II Mains, with selective Prelims utility.
> **Coverage:** Social justice, poverty, nutrition, health, education, women, children, Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, OBC/EWS, minorities, disability, elderly persons, transgender and nomadic communities, sanitation labour, informal workers, migrants, homelessness and welfare-scheme performance.

## 2. Universal social-justice answer framework

| Step | Question |
|---|---|
| Identify | Which group and disadvantage are involved? |
| Explain | Is the barrier economic, social, legal, spatial, occupational, digital or intersectional? |
| Ground | Which constitutional principle, protective law, entitlement or policy applies? |
| Route | Which ministry, state/local institution and frontline system deliver it? |
| Test access | Is the service available, affordable, accessible, acceptable and good quality? |
| Diagnose | Is the gap identification, enforcement, delivery, quality or learning? |
| Protect | What grievance, remedy, rehabilitation or accountability mechanism exists? |
| Measure | Which capability or outcome—not merely coverage—must improve? |

### R-I-G-H-T-S test

`Recognition -> Inclusion -> Guarantee/status -> Human capability -> Targeting/portability -> Safeguard/remedy`

Use it for every vulnerable group, welfare scheme, protective law or social-sector service.

## 3. Justice, equality and inclusion

| Concept | Exam-ready meaning | Distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Formal equality | Same legal rule applies to similarly situated persons | May preserve unequal starting conditions |
| Substantive equality | Differential support removes structural disadvantage | Not arbitrary preference |
| Equity | Fair allocation according to need and disadvantage | Equality often stresses equal status/opportunity |
| Social justice | Fair distribution, recognition, protection and participation | Broader than poverty relief |
| Inclusion | Ability to participate in institutions and access opportunities | Presence alone is not meaningful participation |
| Empowerment | Expansion of agency, control, voice and capability | Benefit receipt without agency is welfare, not necessarily empowerment |
| Affirmative action | Targeted measures correcting historical/structural exclusion | One instrument within social justice |
| Reasonable accommodation | Necessary appropriate modification for equal participation unless disproportionate burden applies | Distinct from generic accessibility |
| Intersectionality | Multiple identities combine to create a distinct disadvantage | Not a simple addition of categories |

### Three dimensions of justice

| Dimension | Central question | Instruments |
|---|---|---|
| Distributive | Who receives resources and opportunity? | Services, transfers, reservation and social security |
| Corrective | How is a wrong repaired? | Penalty, compensation, rehabilitation and restoration |
| Recognitive | Whose identity, dignity and voice are denied? | Anti-discrimination, representation and recognition |

> **Core verdict:** Social justice combines redistribution, protection from harm, recognition of dignity and participation in decision-making.

## 4. Welfare-state architecture

```text
CONSTITUTIONAL VALUES
        ->
PROTECTIVE LAW / ENTITLEMENT
        ->
SCHEME OR PUBLIC SYSTEM
        ->
INSTITUTION + BUDGET + FRONTLINE STAFF
        ->
ACCESS AND QUALITY
        ->
OUTCOME AND CAPABILITY
        ->
GRIEVANCE + EVALUATION + REDESIGN
```

| Instrument | What it does | What it cannot substitute |
|---|---|---|
| Protective law | Prohibits discrimination/harm and creates duties/remedies | Adequate service delivery |
| Statutory entitlement | Establishes legally claimable benefit/service | Frontline capacity and awareness |
| Welfare scheme | Operationalises a policy objective | Fundamental or statutory rights |
| Commission/body | Advises, monitors, investigates or hears complaints within mandate | Department responsible for delivery |
| Public system | Maintains continuing health, education or care capacity | Short-duration scheme |
| Cash transfer | Supports purchasing power | Availability and quality of public services |
| Reservation | Expands representation/opportunity | Schooling, assets, safety and anti-discrimination enforcement |

### Universalism and targeting

| Universal approach | Targeted approach |
|---|---|
| Lower stigma and exclusion error | Concentrates resources on higher need |
| Simpler eligibility | Requires identification and updating |
| Builds common public institutions | Risks documentation and threshold exclusion |
| Higher fiscal requirement | Risks fragmentation and political labelling |

> **Balanced approach:** Universal basic services can coexist with targeted transfers, affirmative action and group-specific rehabilitation.

## 5. Five recurring welfare gaps

| Gap | Identification marker | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Identification gap | Eligible person absent from list or lacks documents | Dynamic registry, outreach, self-declaration where lawful and assisted enrolment |
| Legal-enforcement gap | Protection exists but violation continues | Trained enforcement, accessible complaint, investigation, trial and remedy |
| Delivery gap | Benefit sanctioned but not received | Portability, convergence, local capacity and grievance tracking |
| Access-quality gap | Facility exists but is unsafe, unaffordable or poor | Standards, staffing, accessibility and quality assurance |
| Outcome-learning gap | Coverage rises but capability does not | Survey triangulation, disaggregated outcomes and redesign |

### Access test: 5A + quality

`Availability + Affordability + Accessibility + Acceptability + Awareness + Quality`

For migrants add **portability**; for disability add **reasonable accommodation**; for identity-based harm add **safety and non-discrimination**.

## 6. Poverty, hunger and nutrition

### Core distinctions

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Absolute poverty | Inability to meet a specified minimum standard |
| Relative poverty | Deprivation compared with prevailing social standard |
| Multidimensional poverty | Overlapping deprivation across health, education and living standards |
| Chronic poverty | Persistent long-duration deprivation |
| Transient poverty | Temporary fall due to shock or seasonality |
| Food availability | Sufficient food exists in system/market |
| Food access | Household can obtain it physically and economically |
| Food utilisation | Body can use nutrients, shaped by diet, health, water and care |
| Food stability | Availability/access persist over time |
| Malnutrition | Deficiency, excess or imbalance of energy/nutrients |
| Hidden hunger | Micronutrient deficiency |

### Intergenerational deprivation chain

```text
maternal undernutrition
 -> low birth weight / weak early development
 -> illness + poor learning
 -> low productivity and income
 -> food insecurity and inadequate care
 -> next generation deprivation
```

### Food-security architecture

`procurement/buffer -> allocation -> identification -> portability/distribution -> household use -> nutrition outcome`

| Policy layer | Function |
|---|---|
| Food entitlement/distribution | Protects household cereal access |
| Maternal-child nutrition | Targets life-cycle nutrition and care |
| School/child meals | Nutrition plus attendance/socialisation |
| Public health, water and sanitation | Prevents disease-driven malnutrition |
| Livelihood/social protection | Stabilises purchasing power |
| Dietary diversification | Improves protein and micronutrient intake |

> **Trap:** Hunger is not identical to foodgrain shortage. Nutrition depends on diet diversity, health, sanitation, care practices, gender relations and income stability.

## 7. Health systems and universal health coverage

### Health-system building blocks

`governance + financing + workforce + facilities + medicines/technology + information + community/public-health action`

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Public health | Population-level prevention, promotion and protection |
| Primary care | First-contact, continuous and comprehensive essential care |
| Universal Health Coverage | Needed quality services without financial hardship |
| Out-of-pocket expenditure | Direct household health spending |
| Financial protection | Prevention of catastrophic or impoverishing expenditure |
| Preventive care | Reduces disease risk before illness |
| Curative care | Diagnoses and treats illness |
| Continuum of care | Linked services across prevention, primary, secondary, tertiary and rehabilitation |

### UHC cube

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Population | Who is covered? |
| Services | Which services and quality are covered? |
| Cost | How much financial protection exists? |

### Public versus private role

| Public system strength | Private-sector contribution/risk |
|---|---|
| Equity, prevention, surveillance and remote reach | Additional capacity, innovation and choice |
| Cross-subsidisation and public-health functions | Information asymmetry, over-treatment and cost escalation |
| Continuity during epidemics/disasters | Requires standards, contracting and accountability |

> **Mains line:** Insurance can improve financial access to hospital care, but UHC also requires strong primary care, public health, workforce, medicines, referral and quality regulation.

## 8. Education and human-resource development

### Education capability chain

`early childhood care -> foundational literacy/numeracy -> school retention -> learning -> secondary/higher/skill pathway -> employment, citizenship and mobility`

| Problem | Why enrolment alone misses it |
|---|---|
| Foundational learning deficit | Child attends but cannot progress |
| Dropout/irregular attendance | Household, safety, work, migration or language barriers |
| Digital divide | Device, connectivity, language and support inequality |
| Teacher shortage/deployment | School exists without effective instruction |
| Social discrimination | Formal admission without belonging or safety |
| Disability barrier | Infrastructure/content/pedagogy not accessible |
| Skill mismatch | Certification without employability or labour demand |
| Higher-education inequality | Cost, location, language and preparatory gaps |

### Education indicators

| Indicator | What it measures |
|---|---|
| Enrolment ratio | Participation, not learning |
| Attendance | Presence, not mastery |
| Retention/completion | Continuity through level |
| Learning outcome | Acquired competency |
| Transition rate | Movement to next level |
| Pupil-teacher ratio | Aggregate staffing relation, not deployment quality |

> **Core verdict:** Education is simultaneously a right, public good, capability, mobility mechanism and foundation of democratic citizenship.

## 9. Women and gender justice

### Protection and empowerment are complementary

```text
ANTI-DISCRIMINATION + SAFETY + JUSTICE
                    +
HEALTH + EDUCATION + ASSETS + WORK + CARE SUPPORT
                    +
VOICE + REPRESENTATION + BODILY AUTONOMY
                    ->
SUBSTANTIVE GENDER EQUALITY
```

| Dimension | Structural barrier | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Health | Nutrition and reproductive burden | Quality health, autonomy and information |
| Education | Safety, care work and social norms | Retention, transport and support |
| Work | Unpaid care, segregation and unsafe mobility | Childcare, assets, skills, safe work and equal opportunity |
| Property | Unequal ownership/control | Legal awareness, titles and credit |
| Violence | Household, workplace and public-space power | Prevention, support services and effective justice |
| Politics | Entry without effective voice | Representation plus capacity and autonomy |
| Digital sphere | Harassment, access gap and surveillance | Safety, literacy and rights-preserving platforms |

### Essential distinctions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sex | Biological attributes |
| Gender | Social roles, expectations and power relations |
| Gender equality | Equal rights, opportunity and status |
| Gender equity | Measures accounting for unequal starting conditions |
| Empowerment | Agency and control over choices/resources |
| Unpaid care work | Unremunerated household and care activity |

> **Trap:** Women's welfare is not synonymous with gender justice. The latter requires redistribution of power, resources, care and decision-making.

## 10. Children and child protection

### Child-rights framework

| Pillar | Examples |
|---|---|
| Survival | Nutrition, health, identity and basic care |
| Development | Education, play and cognitive/emotional growth |
| Protection | Protection from abuse, exploitation, labour, trafficking and harmful content |
| Participation | Age-appropriate voice in matters affecting the child |

### Protection system

`prevention -> early identification -> reporting -> child-sensitive rescue/inquiry -> care/rehabilitation -> family/community reintegration where appropriate -> follow-up`

### Digital-era risks

- grooming, sexual exploitation and abuse material;
- cyberbullying and harmful contact;
- addictive/manipulative design;
- privacy loss and commercial profiling;
- misinformation and age-inappropriate content;
- mental-health stress and social comparison;
- unequal parental, school and platform capability.

| Safeguard | Design principle |
|---|---|
| Age-appropriate design | Safety and privacy by default |
| Reporting/takedown | Fast, child-sensitive and reviewable |
| Digital literacy | Child, parent and teacher capability |
| Institutional coordination | School, police, platform, child-protection body and health services |
| Rehabilitation | Counselling, legal aid and reintegration |

> **Core rule:** A child in conflict with law and a child in need of care and protection are legally and institutionally distinct categories.

## 11. Scheduled Castes: rights, atrocities and mobility

### Protection-to-mobility chain

```text
ABOLITION OF UNTOUCHABILITY + ANTI-DISCRIMINATION
        ->
PREVENTION/PROSECUTION OF ATROCITIES
        ->
REPRESENTATION + EDUCATION + LIVELIHOOD + ASSETS
        ->
REAL SOCIAL AND ECONOMIC MOBILITY
```

| Layer | Core focus |
|---|---|
| Constitutional | Equality, non-discrimination, abolition of untouchability, representation and special provisions |
| Protective law | Defined offences, special procedures, victim/witness protection and relief |
| Welfare | Education, skill, livelihood, housing and development support |
| Institution | Monitoring, investigation, courts, commissions and departments within mandate |
| Social transformation | End stigma, segregation, occupational trapping and violence |

### Why legal protection may not produce justice

`under-reporting -> registration/investigation weakness -> delay -> intimidation/compromise -> low effective remedy -> continued impunity`

> **Trap:** Reservation addresses representation and opportunity; it does not by itself eliminate untouchability, violence, landlessness or occupational exclusion.

## 12. Scheduled Tribes and PVTGs

### Tribal-justice triangle

| Axis | Core requirement |
|---|---|
| Rights over land/forest/resources | Recognition, tenure security and lawful process |
| Self-governance and participation | Gram Sabha/community voice and culturally appropriate administration |
| Human development | Health, education, nutrition, connectivity and livelihoods |

### Development-displacement chain

```text
project / extraction / conservation restriction
 -> loss of land, forest access and commons
 -> livelihood + culture + community network disruption
 -> displacement / impoverishment / conflict
```

### Rehabilitation test

`prior information + lawful consent/consultation where required + fair compensation + land/livelihood restoration + community relocation + cultural continuity + services + long-term monitoring`

| Category | Distinction |
|---|---|
| Scheduled Tribe | Constitutionally notified category |
| PVTG | Administrative sub-category among STs with distinct vulnerability criteria/history |
| Forest-dwelling community | Ecological/residential description; legal status depends on applicable framework |

> **Core verdict:** Tribal development must avoid both forced assimilation and romantic isolation; equal citizenship, self-governance, cultural protection and capability expansion must coexist.

## 13. OBC, EWS and social mobility

| Concept | Core distinction |
|---|---|
| Social and educational backwardness | Structural disadvantage relevant to OBC framework |
| Economic weakness | Income/asset-based disadvantage relevant to EWS framework |
| Creamy layer | Exclusion principle within applicable backward-class reservation framework |
| Reservation | Representation/opportunity instrument |
| Welfare support | Scholarships, skilling, credit, housing or other capability support |
| Social mobility | Movement in education, occupation, income, status or power |

### Mobility chain

`accurate identification -> school quality -> higher-education access -> skill/credit/network -> non-discriminatory labour market -> assets and representation`

### Analytical cautions

- category and list status are legal questions, not interchangeable social labels;
- economic deprivation and social backwardness are analytically distinct;
- reservation ceilings, sub-classification and creamy-layer questions require current constitutional/judicial verification;
- representation data, educational outcomes and occupational mobility should be separated.

> **Trap:** Reservation can open institutional entry, but mobility also depends on preparatory capability, discrimination-free participation, networks and assets.

## 14. Minorities: rights and welfare

### Dual framework

| Rights/protection | Development/welfare |
|---|---|
| Equality and non-discrimination | Education, skills, credit and livelihood |
| Freedom of religion | Area-based infrastructure and service access |
| Cultural and educational rights | Scholarships and capability support |
| Protection from violence/hate | Representation and trust in institutions |

### Minority-justice chain

`security + equal citizenship + cultural freedom + education/economic capability + institutional trust -> substantive inclusion`

| Institution/tool | Function |
|---|---|
| Protective commission | Monitor safeguards, inquire within mandate and advise |
| Ministry/department | Design and implement welfare policy |
| Courts | Enforce justiciable rights |
| Police/administration | Prevent and respond to violence/discrimination |
| Educational institution safeguards | Protect constitutionally recognised cultural/educational space |

> **Core rule:** Minority rights are not exemptions from equal citizenship; they enable equality through freedom, cultural security and participation.

## 15. Persons with disabilities

### Models of disability

| Model | Focus |
|---|---|
| Medical | Individual impairment and treatment |
| Charity | Dependence and benevolence |
| Social | Barriers created by environment and institutions |
| Rights-based | Equality, autonomy, accessibility and participation |

### Disability inclusion chain

`legal capacity and non-discrimination -> accessibility -> reasonable accommodation -> inclusive education/work -> support services -> independent living and participation`

### Accessibility dimensions

| Dimension | Examples |
|---|---|
| Built environment | Ramps, lifts, tactile routes and accessible toilets |
| Transport | Boarding, information and last-mile access |
| Digital | Standards, screen-reader compatibility, captions and keyboard use |
| Communication | Sign language, easy-read and accessible formats |
| Education | Inclusive pedagogy, aids and accommodations |
| Employment | Job redesign, assistive technology and non-discrimination |

> **Trap:** Accessibility is anticipatory system design for users generally; reasonable accommodation is an individualised adjustment in a particular case.

## 16. Elderly persons and senior citizens

### Ageing challenge map

`longer life + smaller households + migration + feminisation of ageing + chronic disease -> income, health, care, housing and social-isolation needs`

| Need | Policy response |
|---|---|
| Income security | Pension, savings protection and social assistance |
| Health | Geriatric, primary, palliative and long-term care |
| Maintenance/care | Family responsibility plus public/community support |
| Housing/mobility | Age-friendly design and transport |
| Protection | Prevention of abuse, neglect and financial exploitation |
| Participation | Work, volunteering, digital inclusion and community life |

### Care architecture

`self/family care -> community/home-based support -> primary health -> assisted living/day care -> institutional/long-term and palliative care`

> **Core verdict:** Active ageing requires autonomy, health, security and participation—not merely maintenance or institutional care.

## 17. Transgender persons and denotified/nomadic communities

### Transgender justice

| Dimension | Barrier | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Recognition | Identity/document mismatch | Respectful, accessible legal recognition |
| Education | Bullying and dropout | Safe inclusive institutions |
| Health | Stigma and unsuitable services | Trained, accessible and rights-based care |
| Work | Discrimination and informalisation | Skills, equal opportunity and workplace protection |
| Housing/public space | Exclusion and violence | Shelter, rental access and policing accountability |
| Voice | Policy made without community participation | Representation and consultation |

### DNT/nomadic vulnerability chain

`historical stigma + mobility + weak documentation + uncertain enumeration -> exclusion from residence-linked services, education, housing and livelihoods`

| Needed correction | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Accurate enumeration | Visibility without stigmatising surveillance |
| Portable identity/service access | Account for mobility |
| Residential/educational support | Reduce continuity barriers |
| Livelihood recognition | Avoid criminalisation of traditional occupations |
| Anti-stigma enforcement | Repair historical suspicion |

> **Trap:** Transgender persons, intersex persons and persons with diverse sexual orientation are distinct concepts; legal categories and protections must not be casually merged.

## 18. Sanitation, manual scavenging and Safai Karamcharis

### Complete justice chain

```text
PROHIBITION AND IDENTIFICATION
        +
MACHINE-BASED SAFE SANITATION
        +
ENFORCEMENT AND ACCOUNTABILITY
        +
LIVELIHOOD, CREDIT, SKILL AND SOCIAL REHABILITATION
        +
END TO CASTE-BASED OCCUPATIONAL STIGMA
```

| Problem | Required response |
|---|---|
| Hazardous sewer/septic work | Mechanisation, standard operating procedure and emergency safeguards |
| Under-identification | Transparent survey, self-identification route and local accountability |
| Contractor chain | Principal-agency responsibility and traceable contracts |
| Death/injury | Immediate rescue protocol, investigation, compensation and accountability |
| Occupational trapping | Alternative livelihood, assets, education and long-term rehabilitation |
| Social stigma | Anti-discrimination and community transformation |

> **Core rule:** Personal protective equipment is not a substitute for eliminating hazardous human entry wherever mechanised alternatives and legal prohibitions apply.

## 19. Labour social security, unorganised and gig workers

### Worker classification

| Category | Core feature |
|---|---|
| Organised worker | Employment in formally covered/registered establishment or arrangement |
| Unorganised worker | Work outside stable formal protection, often dispersed or self-employed |
| Gig worker | Earns through task-based work outside traditional long-term employment |
| Platform worker | Gig work mediated through an online platform |
| Migrant worker | Works away from usual place of residence; may be formal or informal |

### Social-security chain

`legal recognition -> registration -> contribution/financing -> portable account -> benefit eligibility -> claim/service -> grievance and appeal`

### Platform-work concerns

| Opportunity | Risk |
|---|---|
| Flexible entry and matching | Income volatility and unpaid waiting |
| Digital scale | Algorithmic control and opacity |
| Consumer reach | Rating-based discipline and account deactivation |
| Work autonomy claim | Weak bargaining and disputed employment status |
| Data trail | Surveillance and information asymmetry |

### Social-security design choices

- tax-financed universal floor;
- contributory insurance;
- employer/aggregator contribution;
- worker contribution calibrated to capacity;
- portability across occupation and state;
- maternity, accident, disability, health, pension and unemployment-related protection.

> **Status discipline:** Enacted labour-code provisions, commencement, rules and actual benefit operation are separate claims.

## 20. Urban poor, homeless persons and migrant workers

### Urban vulnerability chain

`low/volatile income + high rent/transport cost + informal work + insecure tenure + weak documents -> overcrowding, homelessness, service exclusion and shock vulnerability`

| Group/problem | Distinctive need |
|---|---|
| Homeless person | Safe all-season shelter plus identity, health, care and livelihood linkage |
| Slum/informal-settlement resident | Tenure-sensitive upgrading, water, sanitation and services |
| Migrant worker | Portable food, health, education, housing and social security |
| Street vendor | Livelihood space, recognition and fair regulation |
| Domestic/construction worker | Labour protection, safety and portable benefits |
| Circular migrant | Services not tied permanently to origin or destination |

### Portability chain

`portable identity -> interoperable eligibility -> destination enrolment/access -> benefit continuity -> grievance across jurisdictions`

### Smart-city justice test

`Who participates? Who is displaced? Which neighbourhood receives investment? Are informal livelihoods retained? Are services affordable and universally accessible?`

> **Core verdict:** Urban poverty is not merely low income; it is intensified by high living costs, insecure housing, informal work and residence-linked exclusion.

## 21. Scheme performance, convergence and data

### Scheme-performance chain

```text
ELIGIBILITY RULE
   ->
IDENTIFICATION AND ENROLMENT
   ->
SANCTION / FUND FLOW
   ->
SERVICE OR TRANSFER RECEIVED
   ->
QUALITY AND CONTINUITY
   ->
OUTCOME
   ->
GRIEVANCE + EVALUATION + REDESIGN
```

| Failure | Example logic |
|---|---|
| Exclusion error | Eligible person omitted |
| Inclusion error | Ineligible person included |
| Under-coverage | Programme reaches only part of eligible population |
| Leakage/diversion | Resource does not reach intended use/person |
| Fragmentation | Multiple schemes do not combine around household need |
| Portability failure | Entitlement stops after migration |
| Data invisibility | Non-enrolled person absent from dashboard |
| Metric gaming | Target achieved on paper without quality |

### Convergence

`common beneficiary/household plan + clear departmental role + interoperable but protected data + coordinated frontline delivery + joint outcome review`

### Evidence hierarchy

| Evidence | What it proves |
|---|---|
| Budget allocation | Intended resource provision |
| Release/expenditure | Money moved/spent |
| Registration | Administrative enrolment |
| Benefit transaction | Recorded delivery event |
| Facility/output | Immediate service capacity |
| Household survey | Population-level access/outcome estimate |
| Independent evaluation | Implementation or causal insight |

> **Trap:** Dashboard counts exclude people not registered and may measure transactions rather than capability outcomes.

## 24. UPSC traps and safe corrections

| Trap | Safe correction |
|---|---|
| "Formal equality is sufficient." | Test structural barriers and substantive capability. |
| "Every vulnerable group only needs a scheme." | Combine law, enforcement, service, representation, remedy and outcome. |
| "Reservation eliminates social discrimination." | It addresses representation/opportunity, not all structural exclusion. |
| "Foodgrain delivery equals nutrition security." | Add diet, health, sanitation, care and gender. |
| "Insurance equals universal health coverage." | Add primary care, public health, quality, workforce and financial protection. |
| "School enrolment proves education success." | Check attendance, retention, learning and transition. |
| "Digital registration proves inclusion." | Non-registered people may be invisible; check access and benefit receipt. |
| "Targeting always saves resources efficiently." | Consider exclusion, stale lists, stigma and administrative burden. |
| "Protective commission directly delivers welfare." | Separate monitoring/inquiry mandate from departmental delivery. |
| "An Act is automatically operational." | Check commencement, rules, institutions, funding and implementation. |
| "Disability lies only in the person." | Apply social and rights-based models to institutional barriers. |
| "PPE solves hazardous sanitation work." | Prioritise prohibition, mechanisation and accountability. |
| "Gig work flexibility removes worker vulnerability." | Examine algorithmic control, income risk and social-security access. |
| "Urban poverty is rural poverty in a city." | Add rent, transport, tenure, informality and residence-linked services. |

## 25. Reusable Mains answer skeletons

### A. Vulnerable-group question

1. Define the group and structural disadvantage.
2. State constitutional/legal protection.
3. Explain current service/welfare architecture.
4. Diagnose enforcement, access, quality and intersectional gaps.
5. Recommend representation, convergence, remedy and measurable capability.

### B. Social-sector question

1. Establish health/education/nutrition as a right/capability/public good.
2. Map financing, workforce, infrastructure and frontline delivery.
3. Separate coverage from quality and outcome.
4. Address federal, regional, gender and income inequality.
5. Recommend stronger public systems plus accountable partnerships.

### C. Scheme-performance question

1. State intended beneficiary and outcome.
2. Trace eligibility-to-outcome chain.
3. Test exclusion, portability, convergence and data quality.
4. Triangulate administrative and survey evidence.
5. Add grievance, social accountability and redesign trigger.

### D. Protection-versus-empowerment question

1. Explain why protection from harm is necessary.
2. Show why it does not create agency alone.
3. Add health, education, assets, livelihood and representation.
4. Address norms and institutional discrimination.
5. End with autonomy, dignity and voice.

### E. Law-on-paper question

1. Identify the law and exact legal status.
2. Explain enforcement route and responsible institutions.
3. Diagnose reporting, investigation, capacity, delay and remedy gaps.
4. Add victim-sensitive access and accountability.
5. Measure reduced harm and restored capability.

## 26. Five-minute active recall

1. Recite the three dimensions of justice.
2. Explain formal, substantive and capability equality.
3. Name the five welfare gaps.
4. Apply 5A + quality to health or education.
5. Distinguish entitlement, scheme, commission and public system.
6. Draw the food-and-nutrition chain.
7. Explain why insurance is not UHC.
8. Give protection and empowerment measures for women and children.
9. State the central barrier and policy combination for SCs, STs and PwDs.
10. Explain accessibility versus reasonable accommodation.
11. Draw registration-to-benefit for a gig or migrant worker.
12. Separate allocation, expenditure, coverage, output and outcome.
13. Give one intersectional example.
14. End one answer with a capability indicator, not a scheme slogan.

## 27. Optional Advanced enrichment

Use Advanced only after Core is secure:

- capability approach: real freedoms rather than resources alone;
- recognition theory: stigma and status injury alongside redistribution;
- intersectionality: interacting structures produce distinct disadvantage;
- social determinants of health: living and working conditions shape outcomes;
- life-cycle approach: early disadvantage compounds over time;
- care economy: unpaid and paid care as social infrastructure;
- administrative burden: learning, compliance and psychological costs of claiming welfare;
- universalism-targeting design: fiscal, stigma and exclusion trade-offs;
- portability and dynamic registries under migration;
- counterfactual evaluation, attribution and data justice.

> **Firewall:** Advanced theory may sharpen diagnosis, but every answer must still cover legal protection, delivery institution, effective access, remedy and measurable capability.
