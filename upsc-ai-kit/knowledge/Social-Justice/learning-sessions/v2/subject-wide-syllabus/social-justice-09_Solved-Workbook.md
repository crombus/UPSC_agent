### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Distinguish reservation for the other backward classes from reservation for the economically weaker sections with reference to their constitutional basis and identification criteria. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model thesis:** The two routes rest on different enabling provisions and different tests, so the distinction must name Article 15(4) and Article 16(4) against Article 15(6) and Article 16(6), then show that social and educational backwardness and a notified income-and-asset test are not interchangeable evidence.

**Claim → named evidence → analysis → qualification:**

- The owners record the law bank precisely: Article 15(4) and Article 16(4) permit special provision and reservation for socially and educationally backward classes, Article 342A governs the Central List of socially and educationally backward classes, Article 338B carries the national commission for those classes after the Constitution (One Hundred and Second Amendment) Act, 2018, and Article 15(6) and Article 16(6), inserted by the Constitution (One Hundred and Third Amendment) Act, 2019, enable reservation of up to ten per cent for economically weaker sections; the statutory layer is the National Commission for Backward Classes Act, 1993, which was repealed once the commission was given constitutional status, and the answer-writing discipline is that the backward-class route and the economic-criterion route are two constitutionally distinct routes and must never be merged into one reservation story.
- The Constitution (One Hundred and Third Amendment) Act, 2019 inserted Article 15(6) and Article 16(6) and enabled reservation of up to ten per cent in education and public employment for economically weaker citizens who do not fall within the Scheduled Castes, the Scheduled Tribes or the other backward classes, and the owners insist on the exclusion as part of the definition rather than as a side condition; the consequence for an answer is that the category is defined negatively as well as economically, so an economically poor member of a reserved category is outside it by design, and the criticism that economic backwardness inside the reserved categories is ignored is a criticism of that deliberate design and not evidence of an administrative failure.
- The owners record that the criteria were defined by a Department of Personnel and Training notification dated 31 January 2019, under which the gross annual family income must be below eight lakh rupees and the family must not own five acres or more of agricultural land, a residential flat of 1,000 sq ft or more, a residential plot of 100 sq yards or more in a notified municipality, or 200 sq yards or more elsewhere; the owners add that these figures have remained unchanged since 2019 and were before the Supreme Court when the amendment was upheld in November 2022, and the discipline is to quote the asset limits together with the income limit, because an answer that cites only the income figure has described half of a two-limbed test.
- The owners flag the single most productive close-option trap in this topic: the creamy-layer income and wealth ceiling for the other backward classes stands at eight lakh rupees and has done so since a Department of Personnel and Training notification of September 2017, and the economically weaker sections income ceiling also stands at eight lakh rupees since January 2019, yet the two are legally different tests, because the first sits on top of notified parental service and status categories and carries income exclusions for salary and agricultural land, while the second is a gross annual family income test paired with notified asset limits; identical arithmetic therefore proves nothing about identical eligibility, and an answer must say which test it is applying.

**Qualified conclusion:** The two routes rest on different enabling provisions and different tests, so the distinction must name Article 15(4) and Article 16(4) against Article 15(6) and Article 16(6), then show that social and educational backwardness and a notified income-and-asset test are not interchangeable evidence.
