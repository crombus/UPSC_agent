# Disaster Management: Hazards, Risk and Distinctive Features - Last-Minute Revision Chart

> **Purpose:** One-place revision immediately before GS-III Mains, with selective Prelims utility.
> **Coverage:** Risk and resilience, Indian institutions, community protection, early warning, natural and anthropogenic hazards, public-health emergencies, urban and climate resilience, finance, humanitarian logistics and international cooperation.
> **Two-pass design:** Core is independently answer-complete. Advanced adds systems analysis, equity, financing and governance depth; skipping it must not affect the paper outcome.
> **Status rule:** Distinguish statutory enabling power from a body actually constituted; warning from prediction; relief compensation from ex-ante risk finance; and infrastructure sanctioned from resilience achieved.

---

## 1. The 60-second disaster map

```text
HAZARD
natural / technological / biological
        +
EXPOSURE
people + assets + infrastructure
        +
VULNERABILITY
physical + social + economic + environmental
        -
CAPACITY
institutions + knowledge + resources + networks
        =
DISASTER RISK
        |
        v
PREVENT -> MITIGATE -> PREPARE -> WARN -> RESPOND
        -> RELIEF -> RECOVER -> BUILD BACK BETTER
        |
        v
RISK-INFORMED DEVELOPMENT AND RESILIENCE
```

> **Memory line:** Hazards may be unavoidable; disasters are shaped by exposure, vulnerability and capacity.

## 2. Universal disaster-answer framework

| Step | Question |
|---|---|
| Hazard | Which physical, biological or technological event/process? |
| Exposure | Who and what lie in the affected zone? |
| Vulnerability | Why are they susceptible—construction, poverty, age, disability, ecology or governance? |
| Capacity | Which warning, institutions, infrastructure and community networks exist? |
| Cycle | Which prevention, preparedness, response and recovery measures apply? |
| Scale | Union, state, district, city, village, basin or transboundary? |
| Inclusion | Who may not receive warning, evacuation, relief or recovery support? |
| Outcome | Lower mortality/loss, continuity of services and safer reconstruction? |

### R-E-S-I-L-I-E-N-C-E test

`Risk -> Exposure -> Safeguard -> Institution -> Last mile -> Inclusion -> Early action -> Needs -> Continuity -> Evaluation`

---

## 3. Essential definitions

| Concept | Exam-ready meaning |
|---|---|
| Hazard | Potentially damaging event, process or condition |
| Disaster | Serious disruption exceeding affected community’s coping capacity |
| Exposure | People, assets and systems located in hazard-prone area |
| Vulnerability | Conditions increasing susceptibility to harm |
| Capacity | Strengths and resources available to manage risk |
| Risk | Potential loss arising from hazard, exposure and vulnerability |
| Resilience | Ability to resist, absorb, adapt, recover and transform where needed |
| Prevention | Avoid creation of disaster risk |
| Mitigation | Reduce severity or likelihood of harm |
| Preparedness | Develop readiness for anticipated events |
| Response | Immediate action during/after event |
| Recovery | Restore and improve livelihoods, systems and assets |

### Vulnerability dimensions

`physical + social + economic + environmental + institutional`

> **Trap:** A hazard is not automatically a disaster, and vulnerability is not synonymous with poverty alone.

---

## 4. Disaster-management cycle

| Phase | Core measures |
|---|---|
| Prevention | Avoid unsafe siting, risky land use and new exposure |
| Mitigation | Codes, retrofitting, embankments where suitable, ecosystems and insurance |
| Preparedness | Plans, inventories, training, drills and evacuation routes |
| Early warning | Detection, forecast, communication and actionable response |
| Response | Search, rescue, emergency health, shelter and coordination |
| Relief | Immediate essential assistance |
| Rehabilitation | Restore livelihoods, services and social functioning |
| Reconstruction | Rebuild physical systems |
| Build Back Better | Reduce pre-existing vulnerability during recovery |

### Four measure categories

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Structural | Resistant buildings, shelters and drainage |
| Non-structural | Codes, zoning, insurance, awareness and plans |
| Ecosystem-based | Mangroves, wetlands, slopes and watershed restoration |
| Technological | Sensors, satellites, models and communication systems |

## 5. Sendai Framework

### Four priorities

1. Understand disaster risk.
2. Strengthen disaster-risk governance.
3. Invest in disaster-risk reduction for resilience.
4. Enhance preparedness and Build Back Better.

### Seven targets

| Group | Target substance |
|---|---|
| A | Reduce mortality |
| B | Reduce affected people |
| C | Reduce direct economic loss relative to GDP |
| D | Reduce critical-infrastructure damage and basic-service disruption |
| E | Increase national and local DRR strategies |
| F | Enhance international cooperation to developing countries |
| G | Increase access to multi-hazard early warning and risk information |

> **Trap:** Sendai is a voluntary, non-binding framework. Target E carried the earlier 2020 deadline; the others are framed toward 2030.

## 6. Indian legal and institutional architecture

```text
UNION: NDMA + MHA/NEC + NCMC/HLC + NIDM + NDRF
                    |
STATE: SDMA + SEC + state departments
                    |
DISTRICT: DDMA + line departments
                    |
ULB / PRI / community / volunteers
```

| Institution | Primary role |
|---|---|
| NDMA | Apex national policy/guideline body under DM Act |
| NEC | Assists national coordination and planning under Act |
| NCMC | Statutory crisis-coordination body under current amended law |
| HLC | Statutory financial-assistance approval role under current amended law |
| NIDM | Training, research and capacity building |
| NDRF | Specialised disaster response force |
| SDMA | State policy/planning |
| DDMA | District planning, coordination and implementation |

### Enabling-power caution

Current law may enable State-level or urban authorities/forces, but existence requires an actual State notification and operational capacity.

> **Core rule:** Plans must align vertically and horizontally; an apex authority cannot replace district, municipal and community capability.

## 7. Community-based and inclusive DRR

### Community chain

`local risk knowledge -> participatory mapping -> warning volunteers -> evacuation and first response -> relief monitoring -> recovery ownership`

| Group | Distinct vulnerability/need |
|---|---|
| Women | Safety, care burden, sanitation and livelihood loss |
| Children | Separation, nutrition, education and protection |
| Elderly | Mobility, medicines and communication |
| Persons with disabilities | Accessible alerts, transport, shelters and assistance |
| Migrants/homeless | Documentation, language, shelter and exclusion from lists |
| Tribal/coastal communities | Culture, customary livelihoods and ecological dependence |

### Inclusive warning test

`multilingual + multi-channel + accessible + trusted messenger + clear action + transport/support`

> **Trap:** Calling communities “first responders” cannot justify shifting State responsibility without training, equipment and institutional support.

## 8. Early warning and disaster technology

### End-to-end warning chain

`risk knowledge -> detection/monitoring -> forecast -> authoritative warning -> dissemination -> comprehension -> early action -> feedback`

| Term | Distinction |
|---|---|
| Prediction | Precise claim of event occurrence; often impossible for earthquakes |
| Forecast | Probabilistic estimate over time/area |
| Early warning | Timely actionable information before impact |
| Nowcasting | Very short-range forecast |
| Impact-based forecast | Translates hazard into likely consequences |

### Technology toolkit

`satellite + radar + gauges/buoys + GIS + remote sensing + models + cell broadcast/sirens/apps`

> **Core verdict:** A warning succeeds only when the last person understands, trusts and can act on it.

## 9. Earthquakes

### Risk chain

`tectonic hazard + dense exposure + unsafe construction + weak code enforcement -> collapse, fire and lifeline failure`

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Focus/hypocentre | Point of rupture within Earth |
| Epicentre | Surface point above focus |
| Magnitude | Energy-size measure |
| Intensity | Observed shaking/damage at a location |
| Liquefaction | Saturated loose soil loses strength during shaking |
| Retrofitting | Strengthening existing structure |

### Priority measures

`microzonation + code-compliant design + audit/retrofit + land-use planning + drills + lifeline redundancy`

> **Trap:** Earthquakes cannot presently be predicted precisely; risk reduction relies on construction, planning and preparedness.

---

## 10. Tsunami and coastal hazards

### Generation chain

`major undersea displacement -> long ocean waves -> shoaling near coast -> inundation`

| Fact | Correct rule |
|---|---|
| Deep ocean | Wave height may be low and speed high |
| Shallow water | Wave slows, height grows and inundation risk rises |
| Trigger | Usually undersea earthquake; also landslide/volcanic sources possible |

### Preparedness

`seismic detection + ocean observations + modelling + warning centre + coastal dissemination + evacuation routes + natural buffers`

> **Trap:** Every undersea earthquake does not generate a destructive tsunami; displacement mechanism, depth and magnitude/location matter.

---

## 11. Cyclones and storm surge

### Cyclone risk

`strong winds + storm surge + heavy rain + river/urban flooding + infrastructure failure`

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Storm surge | Abnormal sea-level rise driven mainly by storm winds and pressure |
| Landfall | Centre crosses coast |
| Eye | Relatively calm central region |
| Eyewall | Strongest winds/convection surrounding eye |

### Mortality-reduction chain

`forecast -> graded warning -> evacuation -> cyclone shelter -> resilient housing/power/telecom -> rapid restoration`

> **Core rule:** Improved cyclone warning may reduce deaths while economic losses rise because coastal assets and infrastructure exposure increase.

---

## 12. Riverine and urban floods

| Riverine flood | Urban flood |
|---|---|
| Basin rainfall, snowmelt, river capacity and floodplain processes | Intense rain plus impermeable surfaces, blocked drainage, wetland loss and encroachment |
| Often broader lead time | Can develop rapidly at neighbourhood scale |
| Basin and floodplain governance | Drainage, land use and municipal governance |

### Flood-risk chain

`extreme rain/release + floodplain occupation + drainage/river constriction + vulnerable housing -> inundation and service failure`

### Measures

`basin forecasting + floodplain zoning + wetland restoration + drainage audits + reservoir coordination + permeable surfaces + resilient utilities`

> **Trap:** Embankments may protect locally but can transfer risk, fail catastrophically or encourage exposure behind them.

---

## 13. Drought, heat waves and slow-onset risk

### Drought types

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Meteorological | Rainfall deficit |
| Agricultural | Soil moisture insufficient for crops |
| Hydrological | Low surface/groundwater availability |
| Socio-economic | Water shortage affects supply of goods/services |

### Heat-risk chain

`high temperature + humidity/night-time heat + urban heat island + outdoor work + weak housing/health -> illness and mortality`

### Measures

`seasonal forecast + Heat Action Plan + water/cooling access + work-hour changes + cool roofs/trees + health surveillance`

> **Core rule:** Slow-onset disasters require anticipatory social protection and livelihood planning, not only emergency relief after threshold declaration.

---

## 14. Landslides, avalanches and GLOFs

| Hazard | Trigger/vulnerability |
|---|---|
| Landslide | Slope, geology, saturation, earthquake and unsafe cutting/loading |
| Avalanche | Unstable snowpack, slope and trigger |
| GLOF | Sudden release from glacial lake due to moraine/ice-dam failure or displacement |

### Himalayan risk chain

`young geology + steep slopes + extreme precipitation + seismicity + road/construction alteration + weak drainage -> cascading risk`

### Measures

`hazard zonation + slope drainage/stabilisation + construction regulation + lake monitoring + downstream warning + evacuation planning`

> **Trap:** Engineering protection cannot make every unstable slope safe; avoidance and land-use control may be preferable.

---

## 15. Forest fires

### Fire triangle

`fuel + heat + oxygen`

| Fire type | Identity |
|---|---|
| Surface fire | Burns litter/low vegetation |
| Ground fire | Burns organic matter below surface |
| Crown fire | Spreads through tree canopy |

### Risk reduction

`fuel management + fire lines where appropriate + early detection + trained crews + community incentives + controlled-use safeguards + restoration`

> **Trap:** Total fire exclusion can sometimes increase fuel load in fire-adapted systems; ecological context and controlled management matter.

---

## 16. Industrial, chemical, nuclear and CBRN emergencies

### Industrial-risk chain

`hazardous material/process + design/maintenance failure + weak safety culture + dense surrounding exposure -> release, fire, explosion or contamination`

| Stage | Measure |
|---|---|
| Prevention | Process safety, siting, maintenance and audit |
| Preparedness | On-site/off-site plans, inventory and drills |
| Response | Detection, isolation, evacuation/shelter, decontamination and medical triage |
| Recovery | Remediation, health monitoring, liability and livelihood restoration |

### CBRN

`chemical + biological + radiological + nuclear`

> **Core rule:** General disaster coordination and specialised technical command must operate together; responders require hazard-specific protection and decontamination.

---

## 17. Epidemics and public-health emergencies

### Epidemic chain

`pathogen + susceptible population + transmission route + mobility/contact + health-system vulnerability`

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Endemic | Expected persistent presence in area/population |
| Epidemic | Occurrence above expected level |
| Pandemic | Epidemic spreading across countries/continents |
| Surveillance | Continuous collection, analysis and action on health data |
| One Health | Human, animal and environmental health interconnection |

### Response

`surveillance -> testing/diagnosis -> risk communication -> isolation/treatment -> vaccination/medical countermeasure where available -> surge capacity -> continuity of essential care`

> **Trap:** Quarantine applies to potentially exposed persons; isolation separates infected cases under applicable public-health practice.

---

## 18. Urban and critical-infrastructure resilience

### Urban systemic risk

`density + interdependent networks + informal settlements + ageing infrastructure + climate extremes -> cascading failure`

| Lifeline | Cascading consequence |
|---|---|
| Electricity | Telecom, water, health and transport disruption |
| Telecom/data | Coordination and finance failure |
| Water/sanitation | Disease and service disruption |
| Transport | Rescue and supply-chain blockage |
| Health facilities | Mortality and prolonged crisis |

### Resilience principles

`redundancy + robustness + diversity + modularity + safe failure + rapid recovery`

> **Core verdict:** Critical infrastructure must be designed as an interdependent system, not protected facility by facility.

---

## 19. Climate risk, adaptation and loss and damage

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Climate hazard | Climate-related event/trend |
| Adaptation | Adjustment reducing harm or using opportunities |
| Maladaptation | Action unintentionally increases vulnerability or emissions |
| Residual risk | Risk remaining after adaptation |
| Loss and damage | Adverse impacts not avoided through mitigation/adaptation |
| Slow-onset event | Gradual process such as sea-level rise or desertification |

### Climate-resilient development

`risk assessment + adaptation + low-emission development + equity + local knowledge + flexible pathways`

> **Boundary:** Environment owns climate negotiation/science; Disaster Management owns warning, preparedness, response and recovery from climate-related hazards.

---

## 20. Disaster finance and risk transfer

### Financing layers

| Layer | Instrument |
|---|---|
| Routine/high-frequency loss | Budget reserve and local contingency |
| Medium loss | Disaster funds and contingent credit |
| Severe low-frequency loss | Insurance/reinsurance, catastrophe instruments and sovereign support |

| Pair | Correct distinction |
|---|---|
| Relief finance | Post-event assistance |
| Mitigation finance | Ex-ante risk reduction |
| Risk transfer | Shifts defined financial loss contractually |
| Risk reduction | Lowers hazard, exposure or vulnerability |

### Build Back Better test

`safer location + stronger standard + restored livelihood + inclusive services + ecological safeguards + maintenance finance`

> **Trap:** Insurance transfers financial risk; it does not physically reduce hazard or vulnerability unless paired with incentives and standards.

---

## 21. Humanitarian logistics and recovery

### Logistics chain

`needs assessment -> procurement/pre-positioning -> transport -> warehousing -> last-mile distribution -> tracking/accountability`

### Response priorities

`search and rescue + triage/health + water/sanitation + food + shelter + protection + information + family reunification`

| Phase | Distinctive objective |
|---|---|
| Relief | Immediate survival |
| Rehabilitation | Restore functioning and livelihoods |
| Reconstruction | Rebuild assets |
| Recovery | Broad social, economic and institutional restoration |

> **Core rule:** Supply-driven dumping of relief can create waste and inequality; needs assessment, local procurement and transparent distribution are essential.

---

## 22. Governance and international cooperation

### Capacity chain

`clear mandate + trained staff + plans + drills + interoperable communication + finance + audit + learning`

| Cooperation route | Utility |
|---|---|
| Bilateral/regional warning | Shared hazard information |
| Humanitarian assistance and disaster relief | Surge capability |
| UNDRR/Sendai | Global DRR framework |
| WHO/WMO and technical bodies | Health/weather standards and coordination |
| CDRI | Infrastructure-resilience cooperation |
| SAARC/BIMSTEC/G20 platforms | Regional/political coordination |

> **Trap:** International assistance supplements national/local responsibility and must fit domestic command, needs and accountability systems.

## 23. High-frequency comparisons

| Pair | Correct distinction |
|---|---|
| Hazard vs disaster | Potential event vs serious disruption |
| Exposure vs vulnerability | Presence in danger zone vs susceptibility |
| Capacity vs resilience | Available strength vs ability to absorb/recover |
| Prevention vs mitigation | Avoid risk creation vs reduce severity |
| Preparedness vs response | Readiness before event vs immediate action |
| Prediction vs forecast vs warning | Precise occurrence claim vs probability vs actionable alert |
| Relief vs rehabilitation | Immediate assistance vs restored functioning |
| Reconstruction vs Build Back Better | Rebuild vs rebuild safer and more inclusive |
| Structural vs non-structural | Physical works vs rules/plans/incentives |
| Riverine vs urban flood | Basin overflow vs city drainage/land-use system |
| Drought vs aridity | Temporary/episodic deficit vs long-term climatic condition |
| Quarantine vs isolation | Exposed persons vs infected persons |
| Relief fund vs mitigation fund | Post-event assistance vs ex-ante risk reduction |

## 24. UPSC traps and safe corrections

| Trap | Safe correction |
|---|---|
| “Natural hazards cause natural disasters.” | Vulnerability and exposure produce disaster risk. |
| “Disaster management means relief.” | Cover the complete cycle. |
| “Earthquakes can be predicted.” | Use probabilistic hazard assessment and resilient construction. |
| “Warning issued means warning succeeded.” | Test reach, comprehension and ability to act. |
| “Every State has every body enabled by law.” | Verify notification and operation. |
| “More embankments always reduce floods.” | Consider transferred risk and floodplain exposure. |
| “Every forest fire is ecologically abnormal.” | Context, intensity and fire regime matter. |
| “Climate change caused a specific disaster.” | Use attribution evidence and vulnerability analysis. |
| “Insurance is mitigation.” | It transfers financial loss; physical risk remains. |
| “Rebuilding restores resilience.” | Build Back Better must reduce previous vulnerability. |
| “Technology replaces communities.” | Last-mile action depends on trust and local capacity. |
| “International aid can bypass local command.” | Integrate with domestic coordination and accountability. |

## 25. Reusable Mains answer skeletons

### A. Hazard-specific question

1. Define hazard and mechanism briefly.
2. Map exposure, vulnerability and capacity.
3. Apply complete DM cycle.
4. Assign institutions and scales.
5. Add inclusion, technology and ecosystem measures.
6. End with resilience and Build Back Better.

### B. Early-warning question

1. Distinguish detection, forecast and warning.
2. Draw end-to-end chain.
3. Identify technical and last-mile gaps.
4. Add accessible multi-channel communication and drills.
5. Measure early action, not message transmission alone.

### C. Urban/critical-infrastructure question

1. Identify cascading interdependencies.
2. Map informal-settlement and service vulnerability.
3. Combine land use, codes, redundancy and continuity planning.
4. Assign ULB, utility, district and community roles.
5. Finance maintenance and resilient recovery.

### D. Finance/recovery question

1. Separate relief, mitigation and risk transfer.
2. Layer finance by frequency/severity.
3. Protect poor households and local governments.
4. Tie funds to standards, transparency and audits.
5. Apply Build Back Better to livelihoods and infrastructure.
