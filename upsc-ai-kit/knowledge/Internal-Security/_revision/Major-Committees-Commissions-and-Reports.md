# MAJOR INTERNAL SECURITY COMMITTEES AND TASK FORCES

> **Use:** identify the security failure, cite the panel that diagnosed it and distinguish
> recommendation from implementation. Police-administration panels are canonically indexed
> in Governance and cross-linked here.

## National-security and defence-management reform

| Priority | Panel and chair | Year | Assigned task | Major contribution |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| A | **Kargil Review Committee - K. Subrahmanyam** | 1999-2000 | Examine events leading to the Kargil intrusion and systemic deficiencies | Identified intelligence, surveillance, coordination and higher defence-management weaknesses |
| A | **Group of Ministers on Reforming the National Security System - L. K. Advani** | 2000-01 | Convert Kargil Review findings into institutional reform | Umbrella blueprint for intelligence, internal security, border management and defence-management reform |
| A | **Task Force on Intelligence Apparatus - G. C. Saxena** | 2000 | Review intelligence collection, assessment and inter-agency coordination | Stronger multi-agency information sharing, technological intelligence and assessment capability |
| A | **Task Force on Internal Security - N. N. Vohra** | 2000 | Review terrorism, insurgency, organised crime and Centre-State response | Integrated planning, police modernisation and better crisis/terror coordination |
| A | **Task Force on Border Management - Madhav Godbole** | 2000 | Review land, coastal and island-border administration | One-border-one-force logic, fencing/surveillance, infrastructure and coordinated coastal management |
| A | **Task Force on Management of Defence - Arun Singh** | 2000 | Examine higher defence organisation, jointness and procurement | Integrated Defence Staff, joint planning and the reform route toward CDS-type integration |
| A | **Task Force on National Security - Naresh Chandra** | 2011-12 | Revisit post-Kargil reforms and emerging security domains | Higher-defence, intelligence, cyber, aerospace and special-operations reform proposals; use only officially confirmed details |
| A | **Shekatkar Committee - Lt Gen D. B. Shekatkar (Retd.)** | 2016 | Improve combat capability while rebalancing defence expenditure | Manpower redeployment, logistics, training and organisational optimisation |

## Organised crime, terrorism and response failures

| Priority | Panel and chair | Year | Assigned task | Major contribution |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| A | **Vohra Committee - N. N. Vohra** | 1993 | Examine links among crime syndicates, politicians, officials and enforcement agencies | Documented the criminal-political-bureaucratic nexus and proposed coordinated intelligence records |
| A | **Ram Pradhan High-Level Enquiry Committee** | 2008-09 | Examine Maharashtra police, intelligence and response failures during 26/11 | Identified command, information-flow, training, equipment and emergency-response weaknesses |
| B | **Review Committee on the Armed Forces Special Powers Act - Justice B. P. Jeevan Reddy** | 2004-05 | Review AFSPA and recommend a rights-security framework | Recommended repeal of AFSPA as a separate law while placing necessary provisions in a revised UAPA framework |
| B | **Santosh Hegde Commission** | 2013 | Supreme Court-appointed inquiry into selected alleged extra-judicial killings in Manipur | Strengthened accountability and independent scrutiny in counter-insurgency operations |

## High-yield distinctions

| Do not confuse | Correct distinction |
|---|---|
| Kargil Review Committee and Group of Ministers | KRC diagnosed failures; the GoM converted findings into four reform tracks |
| Vohra Committee and Vohra Internal Security Task Force | 1993 panel examined the crime-state nexus; 2000 task force addressed the wider internal-security system |
| Godbole and Ram Pradhan | Godbole reviewed border/coastal architecture; Pradhan investigated the 26/11 Maharashtra response |
| Naresh Chandra and Shekatkar | Naresh Chandra revisited national-security architecture; Shekatkar focused combat capability and expenditure |
| Police reform and counter-terror law | Police reform improves ordinary capacity/accountability; exceptional security laws require separate necessity and rights scrutiny |

## Answer-use chain

```text
threat -> institutional gap -> committee diagnosis -> reform adopted/pending
-> federal or rights constraint -> current capability
```
