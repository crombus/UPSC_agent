✅ Gauba defines political theory as systematic knowledge of political phenomena and insists that it cannot be confined to supposedly value-free scientific knowledge alone (Gauba, PDF pp.16-17).

## 2. Why this topic matters

- ✅ This chapter gives the module's basic vocabulary: what counts as political, what a theory is, and why political theory includes both factual explanation and value-judgment (Gauba, PDF pp.15-18).
- ✅ Gauba treats political science and political philosophy as complementary, not rival, components of political theory; this is the base for later files on ideology, approaches, power, rights and democracy (Gauba, PDF pp.17-18, 23, 27).
- ⚠️ In UPSC and PSIR answers, this topic prevents two common errors: reducing theory to moral sermon, or reducing it to data without a judgment on ends.
- ⚠️ This file is foundational for questions on methods, values, legitimacy, liberty, equality, justice and public policy because all of them require a facts-plus-values frame.

## 3. Essential definitions

| Term | Exam-ready meaning |
|---|---|
| Political | ✅ The political concerns what is public or common to the whole community, especially authoritative rule, collective decision and regulation (Gauba, PDF pp.15-16). |
| Political theory | ✅ Systematic knowledge of political phenomena (Gauba, PDF p.16). |
| Empirical statement | ✅ A statement based on observation through sense-experience alone (Gauba, PDF p.16). |
| Logical statement | ✅ A statement based on reasoning, such as formal or inferential relations (Gauba, PDF p.16). |
| Evaluative statement | ✅ A statement based on value-judgment about what is right, good or desirable (Gauba, PDF p.16). |
| Political science | ✅ The component of theory concerned with facts, observation, explanation and generalized propositions about political behaviour (Gauba, PDF pp.17-18). |
| Political philosophy | ✅ The component of theory concerned with justification, criticism, concept-clarification and the ends of political life (Gauba, PDF pp.17-20). |
| Ideology | ✅ A set of ideas used to defend an existing or proposed distribution of power, accepted by adherents without testing validity (Gauba, PDF p.20). |

## 4. Core argument / chapter spine

1. ✅ Gauba begins by defining the political as the public sphere of rule-making, authoritative decision and regulation for the whole community (Gauba, PDF pp.15-16).
2. ✅ He then defines political theory as systematic knowledge and distinguishes empirical, logical and evaluative statements within that knowledge (Gauba, PDF p.16).
3. ✅ Positivists and behaviouralists try to restrict valid knowledge to empirical and logical statements, but Gauba rejects the move to expel evaluative inquiry from theory (Gauba, PDF pp.16-17, 23).
4. ✅ Political theory must therefore include both political science and political philosophy: science explains political reality, while philosophy asks what goals and principles should guide collective life (Gauba, PDF pp.17-18).
5. ✅ Political science works through observation, generalization, explanation, prediction and prescription; behaviouralism focuses on actual behaviour rather than merely formal institutions (Gauba, PDF pp.18-19).
6. ✅ Political philosophy is not only about ideal blueprints; it performs critical evaluation and concept-clarification, and seeks justification rather than explanation alone (Gauba, PDF pp.19-20).
7. ✅ The significance of political theory lies in control of social life, social criticism and reconstruction, clarification of concepts, and encouragement to mutual respect and toleration (Gauba, PDF pp.21-23).
8. ⚠️ The chapter's basic synthesis is simple: political theory becomes exam-usable only when facts are interpreted through concepts and judged against defensible values.

## 5. Essential distinctions

| Distinction | What to say precisely |
|---|---|
| Public vs private | ✅ Politics concerns the public sphere; not every social or cultural activity is political unless regulation for public order or safety becomes necessary (Gauba, PDF p.16). |
| Theory vs ideology | ✅ Theory is dispassionate and disinterested; ideology is interested and justificatory toward a particular distribution of power (Gauba, PDF p.20). |
| Political science vs political philosophy | ✅ Science asks what the real situation is and which regularities govern behaviour; philosophy asks what is good, right or justified in political life (Gauba, PDF pp.17-20). |
| Explanation vs justification | ✅ Raphael's distinction, endorsed by Gauba, is that science seeks explanation whereas philosophy seeks justification (Gauba, PDF p.19). |
| Empirical/logical vs evaluative | ✅ Empirical and logical claims are presented as verifiable; evaluative claims concern value-judgment, but Gauba still treats them as rationally discussable rather than mere emotion (Gauba, PDF pp.16-17, 20). |
| Behavioural vs post-behavioural | ✅ Behaviouralism privileges scientific study of actual behaviour; post-behaviouralism restores concern for values without discarding scientific work on facts (Gauba, PDF pp.18-19, 23). |

## 6. Thinkers and positions

| Thinker | Position in this chapter |
|---|---|
| Sheldon S. Wolin | ✅ The political is uniquely concerned with what is common to the whole community (Gauba, PDF p.15). |
| George Catlin | ✅ Political theory is divided into political science and political philosophy; science studies control and facts, philosophy studies ends and values (Gauba, PDF p.17). |

| D.D. Raphael | ✅ Political philosophy should be recognized by its critical and justificatory function, plus concept-clarification (Gauba, PDF pp.19-20). |
| David Held | ✅ If political theory is not systematically pursued, politics may be left to ignorance, self-interest or mere will to power (Gauba, PDF p.23). |
| David Easton | ✅ His 1953 behavioural critique and his 1969 post-behavioural correction are both reconstructed in full in §12 of this package; post-behaviouralism reopens the value-question rather than abolishing science (Gauba, PDF pp.24-25). |

## 7. Illustrative examples

- ✅ A question such as "why do riots occur?" belongs to political science when it seeks causal explanation through observation and generalization (Gauba, PDF pp.18-19, 21).
- ✅ A question such as "what is a good society?" belongs to political philosophy because it asks about ends, justification and value (Gauba, PDF p.17).
- ✅ Clarifying whether "freedom" means non-interference, self-development or something else is part of political philosophy's concept-clarifying role (Gauba, PDF pp.19, 22).
- ✅ Gauba warns that vague political language allows demagogues and autocrats to exploit words like liberty, equality and democracy for emotional manipulation or legitimation (Gauba, PDF p.22).
- ⚠️ A strong answer can therefore use data to explain unemployment, but it still needs a philosophical yardstick to judge whether a given policy is fair, free or humane.

## 9. UPSC traps and corrections

- ❌ Political theory is merely normative. -> ✅ Gauba explicitly includes political science as a part of theory, so theory includes factual explanation as well as value-judgment (Gauba, PDF pp.17-18).
- ❌ Political theory is identical with ideology. -> ✅ Theory critically examines claims; ideology justifies or denounces a power arrangement from an interested standpoint (Gauba, PDF p.20).
- ❌ Political philosophy only designs ideal states. -> ✅ Raphael's role, adopted here, is criticism, justification and concept-clarification, not fantasy alone (Gauba, PDF pp.19-20).
- ❌ Behaviouralism made political theory unnecessary. -> ✅ Even Gauba's summary of post-behaviouralism restores values while preserving scientific method for facts (Gauba, PDF pp.18-19, 23).
- ❌ Evaluative statements are automatically irrational. -> ✅ Gauba rejects that dismissal and argues that values can be debated through dialogue and reasoning (Gauba, PDF pp.16-17, 20).

## 12. The decline/revival debate in full (self-contained)

### 12.1 The problem posed
- ✅ Alfred Cobban described a decline of political theory under both capitalist and communist systems in the mid-twentieth century, yet still insisted that politics required criteria of judgment that narrow social-science methodology alone could not supply (Gauba, PDF p.25).
- ✅ Seymour Martin Lipset went further: in the mid-twentieth-century United States he argued the long search for the good society had substantially ended because liberal democracy itself approximated it in operation — a claim tied to the wider end-of-ideology context of `Political-Theory/basic/02_Ideology-and-End-of-Ideology.md`, not a free-standing timeless verdict (Gauba, PDF p.25).
- ✅ David Easton's 1953 complaint held that traditional political theory was speculative, tied to classical texts and the history of ideas, had produced no outstanding political philosopher after Marx and Mill, and lacked the causal research tools needed to explain fascism, communism or wartime policy relevance; his remedy was a behavioural political science that would suspend the quest for values in favour of explaining political behaviour causally (Gauba, PDF p.24).

### 12.2 Decline of a mode, not disappearance of theory
- ⚠️ The decisive move for an unfamiliar question is this distinction: Cobban, Lipset and Easton(1953) describe the decline of one particular MODE of political theorising — classical, speculative, historicist and explicitly normative — not the disappearance of political theory as an enterprise. Conflating the two is the single most common trap on this topic.
- ✅ Easton's own 1969 post-behavioural turn makes the distinction explicit from inside the behavioural camp itself: he called on political science to become practically relevant to a crisis-ridden society and to restore concern with values without discarding scientific method for the study of facts (Gauba, PDF pp.24-25).

### 12.3 The revival
- ✅ Leo Strauss argued that a positivist political science modelled on natural science ignored normative questions altogether and flattened qualitative distinctions among values, so that a "value-neutral" politics could not even rank tyranny below justice (Gauba, PDF pp.25-26).
- ✅ Dante Germino identified revival with the recovery of political philosophy itself — critical inquiry into right political order, distinct both from reductionist behaviouralism and from ideology; on his reading, detachment is not the same thing as ethical neutrality (Gauba, PDF pp.26-27).
- ✅ Herbert Marcuse warned that a natural-science-style social science, by converting critique into measurable administrative categories, could itself become an instrument of social control rather than of emancipation (Gauba, PDF p.27).

### 12.4 Critical verdict (answer-ready)
- ⚠️ The debate operates on two levels at once: a methodological dispute over what counts as valid evidence (Easton 1953 vs Strauss/Germino), and a civilizational dispute over whether politics can ever avoid argument about ends (Strauss/Germino/Marcuse vs Lipset's "good society already found" thesis). Gauba's closing formula — science strengthens means, philosophy judges ends (Gauba, PDF p.27) — settles the first dispute in favour of integration without conceding the second, i.e. theory can be scientifically disciplined and normatively serious at the same time.
- ⚠️ Defensible one-line verdict for an unfamiliar question: political theory did not die; a narrow, ahistorical, facts-only variant of it lost credibility, and the discipline recovered by re-admitting values without discarding evidence.
- ❌ Do not write "political theory ended in the 1950s and was revived in the 1970s" as flat chronology. Cobban, Lipset and Easton(1953) are three distinct claims with different scope, and Easton's own 1969 correction already begins the revival from inside behaviouralism itself, well before the 1970s consolidation Gauba records (Gauba, PDF pp.24-25, 27).

## 13. Qualified answer thesis and directive-specific decoding

- ⚠️ **Qualified thesis formula for this topic:** "Political theory is neither pure science nor pure philosophy; it is the systematic study of political life that uses science to explain political reality and philosophy to judge its ends — a balance whose exact weighting has itself been a historically contested question, as the decline/revival debate shows." Adapt this sentence to the directive word and use it as the opening thesis for any unfamiliar question on this topic.
- ⚠️ **Directive decoding table** (subject-wide decoder: `Political-Theory/00_Master-Framework.md` §5A):

| Directive | What it demands here | Trap to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| "Define" | Genus + differentia in one line — systematic knowledge of political phenomena — then one boundary contrast (theory vs ideology, or science vs philosophy). | Substituting a descriptive paragraph for a bounded definition. |
| "Explain" | Run premises → mechanism → conclusion (§14) with the riot-causation / good-society pairing as the worked illustration. | Listing the three statement-types with no causal chain. |
| "Discuss" | Lay out both the science and philosophy components and their relationship; end with a verdict on integration. | Describing only one component. |
| "Examine" | Test the claim that theory needs both components against decline/revival counter-claims (Easton 1953, Lipset). | Narrating the definition without testing it. |
| "Critically examine" | Examine, plus attack the internal logic — e.g. that value-neutrality is itself a value-choice, and that Easton's own 1969 turn undercuts his 1953 remedy. | Treating "critically" as licence to attack only the behavioural side. |
| "Critically analyse/evaluate" | Weigh the behavioural critique against the post-behavioural/Straussian revival, naming the criterion of worth (explanatory power vs normative adequacy), and give a graded verdict. | Presenting only one side, or refusing to conclude. |
| "Analyse" | Decompose theory into empirical, logical and evaluative statements and show how the three interact in one policy question. | Describing the parts without showing interaction. |
| "Comment" | Give a compact, judgment-bearing paragraph, not a neutral summary. | Merely restating the question. |
| "Compare" | Run political science and political philosophy on shared axes — basic concern, method, typical question, key danger (§8) — then name the decisive divergence (explanation vs justification). | Sequential description of one, then the other. |

## 14. Doctrine architecture: premises, mechanism, conclusion

- ✅ **Premises:** (a) the political is the public/common sphere of authoritative rule (Gauba, PDF pp.15-16); (b) knowledge divides into empirical, logical and evaluative statements (Gauba, PDF p.16); (c) positivists/behaviouralists wish to restrict valid knowledge to the first two categories alone (Gauba, PDF pp.16-17).
- ✅ **Mechanism:** political life generates factual questions ("why do riots occur?") and value questions ("what is a good society?") simultaneously; a theory that excludes evaluative statements cannot fully account for political phenomena, so political science supplies explanation while political philosophy supplies justification and concept-clarification — two angles on the same subject-matter (Gauba, PDF pp.17-20).
- ✅ **Conclusion:** political theory as a whole must integrate both components; its significance lies in control of social life, social criticism/reconstruction, concept-clarification and cultivation of mutual respect and toleration (Gauba, PDF pp.21-23).
- ⚠️ This premises → mechanism → conclusion spine is the fastest route to an unfamiliar question such as "Can political theory be value-free? Discuss," without needing to memorise the chapter verbatim.

## 15. Reusable named evidence units

Each unit runs the subject-specific pattern: **claim → reconstructed doctrine/argument → named
thinker/text/application → significance → objection/limit → reply/verdict**.

- ✅ **Unit 1 — Claim:** politics is defined by what is common to the whole community → **Named:** Sheldon Wolin → **Significance:** distinguishes political theory's subject matter from private/social life in general, letting an answer justify why not every dispute is "political" (Gauba, PDF p.15) → **Limitation:** risks under-counting informal power (e.g. within family or community) that later feminist and critical theorists (see `Political-Theory/basic/06_Feminism-Sex-and-Gender.md`) treat as political → **Reply/verdict:** retain Wolin's public-sphere criterion as a starting boundary, but treat "public" as contestable rather than fixed.
- ✅ **Unit 2 — Claim:** every political scientist is part scientist, part philosopher → **Named:** Andrew Hacker → **Significance:** grounds the integration thesis and is directly usable to reject a one-sided answer (Gauba, PDF pp.17-18) → **Limitation:** does not itself specify how much weight science versus philosophy should get in any one concrete policy dispute → **Reply/verdict:** the weighting is settled question-by-question by the directive, not by a standing rule.
- ✅ **Unit 3 — Claim:** science seeks explanation, philosophy seeks justification → **Named:** D.D. Raphael → **Significance:** a crisp criterion for classifying any given political question, useful for fast directive decoding (Gauba, PDF p.19) → **Limitation:** many real questions ("does democracy increase legitimacy?") straddle both and resist a clean split → **Reply/verdict:** treat the pair as two *questions* asked of one subject-matter, not as two separate subject-matters.
- ✅ **Unit 4 — Claim:** without systematic theory, politics is left to ignorance, self-interest or naked will to power → **Named:** David Held → **Significance:** supplies a normative reason the topic matters, useful for "why this matters" openings (Gauba, PDF p.23) → **Limitation:** the claim is itself a value-judgment and cannot serve as a neutral, empirical justification → **Reply/verdict:** that is precisely the chapter's point — the defence of theory is itself a normative argument, and openly so.
- ✅ **Unit 5 — Claim:** traditional theory declined because it was speculative and lacked causal tools → **Named/Event:** David Easton's 1953 behavioural critique → **Significance:** explains the mid-century turn to behaviouralism, a frequently tested comparison point (Gauba, PDF p.24) → **Limitation:** Easton's own 1969 post-behavioural correction shows the critique's proposed remedy was itself incomplete (Gauba, PDF pp.24-25) → **Reply/verdict:** the critique lands on *method*, not on the enterprise; the remedy over-corrected and was withdrawn by its own author.
- ✅ **Unit 6 — Claim:** positivist political science cannot even rank tyranny below justice → **Named:** Leo Strauss → **Significance:** the sharpest revival argument against a purely value-free political science (Gauba, PDF pp.25-26) → **Limitation:** risks over-claiming that all empirical/behavioural work is worthless, a conclusion Gauba does not endorse → **Reply/verdict:** accept Strauss against value-neutral *closure*, reject him if read as anti-empirical.

## 16. Objection → reply chains

- ⚠️ **Chain 1 — Objection:** "Evaluative statements cannot be empirically verified, so they are not real knowledge; political theory should be confined to political science." **Reply:** Gauba treats evaluative statements as rationally discussable through dialogue and reasoned justification, not mere emotive noise; excluding them leaves public policy without any standard by which to judge outcomes as fair or unjust (Gauba, PDF pp.16-17, 20).
- ⚠️ **Chain 2 — Objection:** "The decline/revival debate proves political theory is now settled science, with no further room for philosophy." **Reply:** Easton's own 1969 turn and Gauba's closing statement show political science and political philosophy as continuing, complementary, mutually testing enterprises since the 1970s, not a final victory for either side (Gauba, PDF p.27; §12.4 above).
- ⚠️ **Chain 3 — Objection:** "Ideology and political theory are the same thing because both discuss liberty, equality and the state." **Reply:** Theory is disinterested and critically tests claims for validity; ideology is interested and justificatory towards a particular distribution of power (Gauba, PDF p.20) — see `Political-Theory/basic/02_Ideology-and-End-of-Ideology.md` for the full distinction.

## 2. Scope, assumptions and conceptual boundary

- ✅ This debate emerges inside Gauba's broader definition of political theory as including both political science and political philosophy; it is not a separate problem from the opening definition (Gauba, PDF pp.17-20, 23-27).
- ✅ The behavioural challenge is directed chiefly against traditional speculation, historical classicism and explicit normativity, not against all theory whatsoever (Gauba, PDF pp.24-25).
- ⚠️ The key exam boundary is this: post-behaviouralism criticizes value-neutral closure, but it does not reject empirical inquiry or scientific discipline.
- ⚠️ The decline/revival debate is best read as a dispute over the proper balance between explanation and justification, not as a simple fight between "modern" and "old" writers.

## 3. Direct-book argument reconstruction

1. ✅ Gauba reports David Easton's 1953 complaint that traditional political theory relied on speculation, lacked acute observation of political reality, stayed tied to classics and history of ideas, and had little relevance for contemporary politics (Gauba, PDF p.24).
2. ✅ Easton argued that there had been no outstanding political philosopher after Marx and J.S. Mill and that political scientists lacked research tools capable of explaining fascism, communism and policy relevance in wartime decision-making (Gauba, PDF p.24).
3. ✅ His remedy was behavioural political science: causal theory, closer integration with other social sciences, and suspension of the quest for values in favour of explaining political behaviour (Gauba, PDF p.24).
4. ✅ Gauba then notes Easton's own 1969 shift: the post-behavioural revolution asked political science to address contemporary crisis and restored concern with values while retaining scientific investigation (Gauba, PDF pp.24-25).
5. ✅ Alongside Easton, Cobban described decline under both capitalist and communist systems and still insisted that political science must evolve criteria of judgment beyond social-science methodology alone (Gauba, PDF p.25).
6. ✅ Lipset went further by claiming that in the United States the long search for the good society had effectively ended because liberal democracy itself approximated it in operation (Gauba, PDF p.25).
7. ✅ Against this, Strauss argued that positivist political science ignored normative issues and flattened qualitative distinctions among values; Germino identified revival with political philosophy; Marcuse warned that a natural-science style social science could become an instrument of social control (Gauba, PDF pp.25-27).
8. ✅ Gauba closes by saying that, since the 1970s, political science and political philosophy increasingly test and inform each other rather than remaining sealed apart (Gauba, PDF p.27).

## 4. Definitions and distinctions

| Concept | Advanced clarification |
|---|---|
| Causal theory | ✅ A theory explaining relations of cause and effect, central to Easton's behavioural ambition (Gauba, PDF p.24). |
| Behaviouralism | ✅ A scientific approach centred on actual political behaviour, causal explanation and methodological affinity with other social sciences (Gauba, PDF pp.18-19, 24-25). |
| Post-behaviouralism | ✅ Easton's 1969 turn toward applying science to crisis and reconnecting inquiry with values and public relevance (Gauba, PDF pp.24-25). |
| Positivism | ✅ The view that scientific method is the only source of true knowledge and that knowledge rests on sense-experience (Gauba, PDF p.26). |
| Political philosophy | ✅ Critical inquiry into right and wrong, right order, justification and perennial social questions, not reducible to behavioural metrics (Gauba, PDF pp.19-20, 26-27). |
| Detachment | ✅ Germino's point is that detachment is not the same as ethical neutrality; one may critically engage without becoming partisan ideology (Gauba, PDF p.26). |

| Perspective | Strongest claim | Gauba's treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Behaviouralist | ✅ Valid theory should explain political behaviour causally and scientifically (Gauba, PDF p.24). | ✅ Gauba takes the scientific demand seriously but does not let it exhaust theory (Gauba, PDF pp.18-19, 27). |

## 7. Internal debates and criticisms

- ✅ Easton's early criticism bites hardest where traditional theory merely repeats classic texts without explaining contemporary political behaviour; Gauba does not deny that problem (Gauba, PDF p.24).
- ✅ Yet Easton's own post-behavioural turn is itself evidence that pure behavioural closure was insufficient for political inquiry in times of crisis (Gauba, PDF pp.24-25).
- ✅ Cobban and Lipset represent different decline narratives: one says modern systems marginalize theory; the other says liberal democracy has already solved the central normative search, at least in affluent Western context (Gauba, PDF p.25).
- ✅ Strauss rejects behavioural flattening of value-rankings; Germino rejects any reunion that strips theory of perennial moral concerns; Marcuse rejects metrics that convert critique into social control (Gauba, PDF pp.25-27).
- ⚠️ The debate therefore has two levels: a methodological dispute over evidence, and a civilizational dispute over whether politics can ever avoid argument about ends.
- ⚠️ Gauba's own synthesis is closer to "critical empiricism" than either crude positivism or free-floating moralism.

## 9. Application without current-affairs dependence

- ⚠️ If asked to analyse a policy on preventive detention, a behavioural approach can study compliance, opposition and institutional effects, but a philosophical approach must still ask whether liberty is being unjustifiably restricted.
- ⚠️ If asked whether elections deepen democracy, science can measure turnout and participation, while philosophy must judge whether those patterns amount to meaningful freedom, equality and legitimacy.
- ⚠️ If a regime claims "efficiency" as its justification, behavioural data may show administrative output, but political philosophy asks whether efficiency without justice or consent is politically defensible.

### Q1 (10 marks, ~150 words). Explain the distinction between political science and political philosophy, and why Gauba treats both as components of political theory.

**Demand decode.** Directive is **"Explain"** — it wants the *mechanism*, not a list. Two tasks are
set: (i) draw the distinction precisely, (ii) show *why* the two nevertheless belong together. A
verdict is not strictly demanded, but a closing integration line is. Evidence load at this weight:
one to two named units, one worked illustration.

**Model answer.**

Political theory is systematic knowledge of political phenomena, and Gauba divides the statements
within it into empirical, logical and evaluative kinds (Gauba, PDF p.16). Political **science**
works on the first two: it observes actual political behaviour, generalises, explains and
predicts. Political **philosophy** works on the third: it justifies, criticises and clarifies the
concepts through which political life is described. D.D. Raphael's criterion, which Gauba adopts,
fixes the line sharply — science seeks *explanation*, philosophy seeks *justification*.

The two are inseparable because political life poses both questions at once. "Why do riots occur?"
is answerable only by observation and causal generalisation; "what is a good society?" is
answerable only by reasoned evaluation. Andrew Hacker's formulation — every political scientist is
part scientist and part philosopher — captures the consequence.

**Conclusion.** Theory that keeps only facts becomes value-blind; theory that keeps only values
becomes ungrounded. Gauba's integration is therefore not a compromise but the condition of the
subject's competence: science strengthens the means, philosophy judges the ends.

---

### Q2 (15 marks, ~250 words). Examine the claim that political theory declined in the mid-twentieth century.

**Demand decode.** Directive is **"Examine"** — restate the claim as a testable proposition, marshal
the evidence for it, then the strongest evidence against, and deliver a finding on whether it holds,
holds partly, or fails. A verdict is mandatory. Evidence load: two to three named units, at least
one carried through objection → reply.

**Model answer.**

**Thesis.** The decline claim holds against one *mode* of political theorising, but fails as a claim
about political theory as an enterprise — and its most decisive refutation comes from inside the
camp that advanced it.

**The case for decline.** Three distinct claims are usually merged and should be separated. Alfred
Cobban diagnosed a decline under both capitalist and communist systems. Seymour Martin Lipset
argued that in the mid-twentieth-century United States the long search for the good society had
substantially ended, liberal democracy approximating it in operation. David Easton's 1953 critique
was methodological: traditional theory was speculative, tied to classical texts and the history of
ideas, had produced no outstanding political philosopher after Marx and Mill, and lacked causal
research tools capable of explaining fascism, communism or wartime policy. His remedy was a
behavioural science that suspended the quest for values (Gauba, PDF pp.24-25).

**Objection.** Easton's own 1969 post-behavioural turn withdrew the remedy. He called on political
science to become relevant to a crisis-ridden society and to restore concern with values *without*
discarding scientific method — an internal admission that behavioural closure was insufficient
(Gauba, PDF pp.24-25). Leo Strauss pressed further: a value-neutral science that refuses
qualitative rankings cannot even place tyranny below justice. Dante Germino located the revival in
the recovery of political philosophy itself, insisting that detachment is not ethical neutrality.
Herbert Marcuse warned that measurement can convert critique into administrative categories and so
serve social control.

**Reply available to the decline thesis.** Easton's 1953 complaint still bites wherever theory
merely recites classics without explaining political behaviour; Gauba does not deny that failure.
Lipset's thesis, however, is contextual — an affluent mid-century American claim tied to the wider
end-of-ideology debate — and cannot be generalised.

**Graded conclusion.** The claim **holds partly**: a classical, speculative, historicist and
explicitly normative mode did lose credibility, and that loss was deserved. It **fails** as a
claim about the discipline, which recovered by re-admitting values without discarding evidence.
Since the 1970s science and philosophy increasingly test and inform each other (Gauba, PDF p.27).

---

### Q3 (20 marks, ~300 words). "Political theory can and should be value-free." Critically examine.

**Demand decode.** A **proposition stem** with the directive **"Critically examine"**. Two moves are
therefore compulsory: test the proposition against evidence *and* attack its internal logic —
here, the hidden premise that value-neutrality is itself value-free. Decode the proposition before
answering (Master Framework §5D); a graded verdict stating the conditions under which each side
wins is mandatory. Evidence load: three to four named units spanning founding definition and the
decline/revival debate; two objection → reply chains.

**Decoding the proposition.** It asserts two separable things: a *possibility* claim (theory can be
value-free) and a *normative* claim (it ought to be). The concept invoked is valid political
knowledge; the rival concept is justification. The hidden premise is that only empirically
verifiable statements count as knowledge.

**Thesis.** The proposition is defensible for one *component* of political theory and self-defeating
as a claim about the whole: political science can and should discipline its factual claims against
evidence, but a political theory that expels evaluation cannot perform the tasks — criticism,
reconstruction, concept-clarification — that make it political theory at all.

**The case for the proposition.** Positivism holds that scientific method is the only source of
true knowledge and that knowledge rests on sense-experience (Gauba, PDF p.26). Easton's 1953
programme drew the practical conclusion: causal theory, integration with the other social sciences,
and suspension of the quest for values. The gain is real — observation of actual behaviour rather
than formal institutions alone corrected a genuine weakness in traditional theory.

**Objection 1 — internal logic.** Gauba separates empirical, logical and evaluative statements and
refuses to treat the third as irrational; values are debatable through dialogue and reasoning
(Gauba, PDF pp.16-17, 20). Choosing what to study, and declaring evaluation out of bounds, is
itself a value-choice — so the proposition cannot satisfy its own standard. **Reply:** the
positivist may retreat to a weaker claim — that *within* explanation, evidence alone should decide.
That weaker claim survives; the strong claim does not.

**Objection 2 — consequences.** Strauss argued that a value-neutral science flattens qualitative
distinctions and cannot rank tyranny below justice; Marcuse warned that critique converted into
measurable administrative categories becomes an instrument of social control; Germino insisted
detachment is not ethical neutrality. **Reply:** these are correctives to scientism, not licences
for evidence-free assertion — Gauba does not endorse abandoning empirical discipline.

**The decisive evidence.** Easton's own 1969 turn restored relevance and values while retaining
scientific investigation of facts — the proposition's leading advocate revised it (Gauba,
PDF pp.24-25). David Held's warning states the stake: without systematic theory, politics is left
to ignorance, self-interest or naked will to power (Gauba, PDF p.23).

**Graded conclusion.** The proposition **succeeds** as a discipline on the explanatory component —
factual claims should not be bent to preference. It **fails** as a description of political theory
as a whole, and fails **necessarily**, because value-neutrality is itself a normative commitment.
The defensible position is Gauba's: science strengthens the means, philosophy judges the ends
(Gauba, PDF p.27) — and what declined in the mid-twentieth century was a mode of theorising, not
the enterprise itself.

### Boundary of "the political"
- ✅ The political = what is **public or common to the whole community**, especially authoritative rule, collective decision and regulation (Gauba, PDF pp.15-16); Sheldon Wolin supplies the common-to-the-community criterion (PDF p.15).
- ✅ Public vs private: not every social or cultural activity is political unless regulation for public order or safety becomes necessary (PDF p.16).
- ⚠️ Limit of Wolin's criterion: under-counts informal power inside family/community that feminist and critical theorists treat as political.

### Definition and the three statement-types
- ✅ **Political theory = systematic knowledge of political phenomena** (PDF p.16).
- ✅ **Empirical** = based on observation through sense-experience · **Logical** = based on reasoning/inference · **Evaluative** = based on value-judgment about what is right, good or desirable (PDF p.16).
- ✅ Positivists/behaviouralists restrict valid knowledge to the first two; Gauba refuses to expel evaluative inquiry (PDF pp.16-17, 23).

### Science, philosophy and their integration
- ✅ **Political science** — facts, observation, generalisation, explanation, prediction, prescription (PDF pp.17-18).
- ✅ **Political philosophy** — justification, criticism, concept-clarification, the ends of political life (PDF pp.17-20); not ideal blueprints alone.
- ✅ **Catlin** — theory divides into science (control, facts) and philosophy (ends, values), PDF p.17. **Hacker** — every political scientist is part scientist, part philosopher, PDF pp.17-18. **Raphael** — science seeks explanation, philosophy seeks justification, PDF p.19. **Held** — without systematic theory, politics is left to ignorance, self-interest or naked will to power, PDF p.23.
- ✅ Significance of theory: control of social life · social criticism and reconstruction · clarification of concepts · encouragement of mutual respect and toleration (PDF pp.21-23).
- ✅ Gauba's closing formula: **science strengthens means; philosophy judges ends** (PDF p.27).

### Theory vs ideology
- ✅ **Ideology** = ideas used to defend an existing or proposed distribution of power, accepted by adherents without testing validity (PDF p.20).
- ✅ Theory is dispassionate and tests validity; ideology is interested and justificatory. Both discuss liberty, equality and institutions — the difference is **standpoint**, not subject-matter.

### Decline and revival — the three decline claims
- ✅ **Cobban** — decline under both capitalist and communist systems, yet politics still needs criteria of judgment beyond social-science methodology (PDF p.25).
- ✅ **Lipset** — in the mid-twentieth-century **United States** the long search for the good society had substantially ended; a contextual thesis tied to the end-of-ideology debate, not a timeless verdict (PDF p.25).
- ✅ **Easton (1953)** — traditional theory was speculative, tied to classics and history of ideas, no outstanding political philosopher after Marx and Mill, no causal tools for fascism, communism or wartime policy; remedy = behavioural science suspending the quest for values (PDF p.24).

### Decline and revival — the correction and the three revival claims
- ✅ **Easton (1969)** — post-behavioural turn: relevance to a crisis-ridden society and restored concern with values, **without** discarding scientific method (PDF pp.24-25). The remedy was withdrawn by its own author.
- ✅ **Strauss** — value-free positivism flattens qualitative distinctions and cannot rank tyranny below justice (PDF pp.25-26). ⚠️ Not an anti-empirical claim.
- ✅ **Germino** — revival = recovery of political philosophy as critical inquiry into right order; **detachment ≠ ethical neutrality** (PDF pp.26-27).
- ✅ **Marcuse** — measurement can convert critique into administrative categories and become an instrument of social control (PDF p.27).
- ✅ Since the 1970s science and philosophy increasingly test and inform each other (PDF p.27).

### Decline/revival — verdict and traps
- ⚠️ **Decisive distinction:** decline of a **mode** (classical, speculative, historicist, explicitly normative) ≠ disappearance of the **enterprise**.
- ⚠️ Two levels of dispute: methodological (what counts as valid evidence) and civilizational (can politics avoid argument about ends).
- ⚠️ One-line verdict: political theory did not die; a narrow, ahistorical, facts-only variant lost credibility and the discipline recovered by re-admitting values without discarding evidence.
- ❌ Flat chronology "ended in the 1950s, revived in the 1970s" — the 1969 correction begins the revival from inside behaviouralism.
- ❌ Merging Easton 1953 with Easton 1969 · ❌ treating Cobban, Lipset and Easton as one claim · ❌ reading Lipset's thesis as universal.

### Standing traps and corrections
- ❌ Theory is merely normative → ✅ political science is a part of theory (PDF pp.17-18).
- ❌ Theory = ideology → ✅ theory tests claims; ideology justifies power (PDF p.20).
- ❌ Philosophy only designs ideal states → ✅ criticism, justification, concept-clarification (PDF pp.19-20).
- ❌ Behaviouralism made theory unnecessary → ✅ post-behaviouralism restores values while preserving method (PDF pp.18-19, 23).
- ❌ Evaluative statements are irrational → ✅ they are debatable through dialogue and reasoning (PDF pp.16-17, 20).
- ❌ Post-behaviouralism rejects science → ✅ it adds relevance and action to verification, never substituting for it (PDF p.19).

### Doctrine architecture (premises → mechanism → conclusion)
- ✅ **P1** the political is the public/common sphere of authoritative rule · **P2** knowledge divides into empirical, logical and evaluative statements · **P3** positivists restrict valid knowledge to the first two.
- ✅ **Mechanism:** political life poses factual and value questions simultaneously; excluding evaluation leaves political phenomena unaccounted for, so science explains while philosophy justifies and clarifies.
- ✅ **Conclusion:** theory must integrate both; its significance lies in control, criticism/reconstruction, concept-clarification and toleration (PDF pp.21-23).

### Named evidence units (claim → doctrine → name → significance → limit → reply)
- ✅ Wolin — politics is what is common to the community · limit: informal power.
- ✅ Hacker — part scientist, part philosopher · limit: no weighting rule for a concrete dispute.
- ✅ Raphael — explanation vs justification · limit: many real questions straddle both.
- ✅ Held — without theory, politics falls to ignorance or will to power · limit: itself a value-judgment (and openly so).
- ✅ Easton 1953 — decline from speculation and missing causal tools · limit: withdrawn by Easton 1969.
- ✅ Strauss — value-free science cannot rank tyranny below justice · limit: over-claims if read as anti-empirical.

### Objection → reply chains
- ⚠️ **Evaluation is not knowledge** → values are rationally discussable; excluding them leaves policy with no standard of fairness (PDF pp.16-17, 20).
- ⚠️ **Theory is now settled science** → Easton 1969 and Gauba's closing statement keep science and philosophy mutually testing (PDF p.27).
- ⚠️ **Ideology = theory** → disinterested validity-testing vs interested justification of power (PDF p.20).

### Illustrations and application lens
- ✅ "Why do riots occur?" → political science (causal explanation, PDF pp.18-19, 21). "What is a good society?" → political philosophy (ends, justification, PDF p.17).
- ✅ Clarifying whether "freedom" means non-interference or self-development is philosophy's concept-clarifying role (PDF pp.19, 22).
- ✅ Vague political language lets demagogues exploit liberty, equality and democracy for manipulation or legitimation (PDF p.22).
- ⚠️ Preventive detention · turnout and democratic depth · "efficiency" as justification — in each case data explains, philosophy judges.
- ⚠️ **Indian anchor (no Polity/Ethics fact-ownership):** where a debate is framed only in output/efficiency terms, the explanation/justification split supplies the missing question — is the distribution of burdens and benefits fair, and is dignity and voice respected? Pair the lens with the candidate's own verified Indian facts.

### Directive decoder (topic-specific)
- **Define** boundary + one contrast · **Explain** premises→mechanism + worked pairing · **Discuss** both components + verdict · **Examine** test the claim against Easton 1953/Lipset · **Critically examine** add the internal-logic attack (value-neutrality is itself a value-choice) · **Evaluate** name the criterion, then grade · **Analyse** decompose the three statement-types and show interaction · **Comment** compact judgment-bearing position · **Compare** shared axes then decisive divergence · **Elucidate** the riot/good-society pairing.
