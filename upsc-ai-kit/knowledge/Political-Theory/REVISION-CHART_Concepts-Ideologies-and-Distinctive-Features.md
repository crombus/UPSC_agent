## 2. Five-question decode engine

Use these before writing any answer:

| Question | Spectrum |
|---|---|
| Unit of analysis | Individual → class → community → gendered subject → association → state → humanity |
| Source of legitimacy | Consent → law → common good → emancipation → participation → justice → moral self-rule |
| Meaning of freedom | Non-interference → self-development → non-domination → emancipation → swaraj |
| Criterion of justice | Procedure → entitlement → fairness → need → recognition → common good |
| Role of state | Minimal → enabling/welfare → coordinating → transformative → decentralised → eventual disappearance |

### Necessary-versus-sufficient test

- Elections are necessary but insufficient for liberal democracy.
- Legal validity is necessary for an enforceable legal right but insufficient for moral legitimacy.
- Inequality benefiting the least advantaged is insufficient under Rawls unless equal liberties and fair opportunity are secured.
- Voluntary transfer is insufficient for Nozick if original acquisition or title is unjust.

## 3. Universal answer framework

### D-E-B-A-T-E

1. **Define** the central concept precisely.
2. **Expose** the conceptual question hidden in the prompt.
3. **Bring** two or more rival traditions.
4. **Analyse** each tradition's reasoning, not merely its label.
5. **Test** strengths, limitations and a neutral illustration.
6. **End** with a qualified synthesis.

> **Minimum scoring unit:** `definition + rival views + mechanism + criticism + synthesis`

## 4. Nature and significance of political theory

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Political theory | Systematic explanation and evaluation of political concepts, institutions and practices |
| Political philosophy | Normative inquiry into the good political order |
| Political science | Empirical and analytical study of political phenomena |
| Political thought | Ideas of an individual, age or tradition |

### Functions

`concept clarification + explanation + evaluation + criticism + prescription + ideological disclosure`

### Decline and revival

```text
Behavioural fact-focus + alleged ideological consensus
             -> "decline" thesis
Normative conflicts + injustice + new movements
             -> revival of political theory
```

> **Trap:** Political theory is neither pure speculation nor value-free data collection; it joins empirical understanding with normative judgment.

## 5. Ideology and the “end of ideology”

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ideology | Action-oriented system of political ideas interpreting society and prescribing change/order |
| False consciousness | Marxist account of beliefs obscuring material domination |
| Hegemony | Consent organised through culture and ideas |
| End-of-ideology thesis | Claim that grand ideological conflict had exhausted itself in advanced industrial societies |

### Why ideology persists

`facts require interpretation + interests differ + values conflict + power shapes knowledge + new exclusions generate movements`

> **Trap:** “End of ideology” is a historical thesis, not an established permanent fact or the end of political disagreement.

## 6. Ideology comparison

| Ideology | Unit | Freedom | State | Property/economy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Classical liberalism | Individual | Negative liberty | Minimal, rights-protecting | Strong private property |
| Welfare liberalism | Socially situated individual | Positive freedom | Enabling and redistributive | Regulated private property |
| Neoliberalism | Market actor | Competition/non-interference | Limited but market-framing | Strong property/market order |
| Libertarianism | Self-owning individual | Non-coercion | Minimal protective state | Entitlement subject to just title |
| Marxism | Class | Emancipation from class domination | Class instrument, ultimately withers | Social ownership of means of production |
| Democratic socialism | Individual in society | Substantive freedom | Welfare/regulatory state | Mixed/socially regulated |
| Fascism | Nation/state | Individual subordinated | Totalitarian, leader-centred | Instrumental, not a coherent universal doctrine |
| Anarchism | Individual/voluntary association | Freedom from coercive authority | Abolished | Varies by tradition |
| Gandhism | Moral person in community | Swaraj | Decentralised/minimal | Trusteeship |
| Feminism | Gendered subject | Freedom from patriarchy | Reform or transformation | Varies by school |
| Communitarianism | Embedded self/community | Socially constituted freedom | Common-good orientation | Subordinate to legitimate community purposes |

## 7. Liberalism and neoliberalism

### Liberal family

`individual moral worth + rights + constitutional limits + rule of law + consent`

| Variant | Distinctive position |
|---|---|
| Classical | Negative liberty, limited government, property |
| Welfare | State creates capabilities/conditions for meaningful freedom |
| Neoliberal | Markets, competition and spontaneous order; scepticism of expansive welfare |
| Libertarian | Strong self-ownership and entitlement, minimal state |

> **Trap:** Liberalism is not uniformly anti-state. Welfare liberalism justifies substantial enabling and redistributive action.

---

## 8. Marxism and neo-Marxism

### Core mechanism

`productive forces + relations of production -> class structure -> conflict -> historical transformation`

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Historical materialism | Material production and class relations structure historical change |
| Alienation | Separation from product, process, fellow humans and human potential |
| Surplus value | Value appropriated from labour under capitalist production |
| Class state | State reflects/reproduces dominant class power |
| Ideology | Ideas can legitimise material domination |
| Neo-Marxism | Greater attention to culture, ideology, relative autonomy and advanced capitalism |

> **Trap:** Avoid mechanical economic determinism; later Marxist analysis recognises interaction and relative autonomy in politics/culture.

---

## 9. Socialism, fascism, anarchism and Gandhism

| Tradition | Goal | Method/state |
|---|---|---|
| Democratic socialism | Equality and social control with democracy | Constitutional welfare and redistribution |
| Fascism | Organic national unity under leader/state | Authoritarian mobilisation and suppression |
| Anarchism | Voluntary cooperation without coercive hierarchy | Abolition of state; decentralised associations |
| Gandhism | Swaraj, sarvodaya and non-violent social order | Satyagraha, trusteeship and village decentralisation |

### Gandhian chain

`truth -> ahimsa -> satyagraha -> swaraj -> trusteeship -> sarvodaya`

> **Trap:** Gandhian non-violence is active disciplined resistance, not passive submission.

---

## 10. Feminism: sex, gender and patriarchy

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sex | Biological classification, itself interpreted socially |
| Gender | Socially constructed roles and expectations |
| Patriarchy | Institutionalised male dominance across public and private spheres |
| Intersectionality | Interacting structures such as gender, caste, class, race and disability |

| School | Diagnosis | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Liberal feminism | Unequal rights/opportunity | Legal equality and access |
| Radical feminism | Patriarchy as autonomous power system | Transform gender relations/private sphere |
| Socialist feminism | Capitalism and patriarchy interact | Transform production and reproduction |

> **Trap:** Feminism is not one doctrine, and formal legal equality may coexist with substantive disadvantage.

## 11. Nature of politics and communitarianism

| View | Politics is... |
|---|---|
| Liberal | Reconciliation of interests through rules |
| Marxist | Expression of class conflict and domination |
| Communitarian | Deliberation over shared purposes/common good |
| Feminist | Also embedded in allegedly “private” relations |

### Communitarian critique

`liberal abstract self -> neglects social membership, identity, obligation and shared goods`

> **Balance:** Community shapes persons, but community claims cannot automatically override individual dignity, dissent or internal minorities.

## 12. Approaches to political analysis

| Approach | Focus |
|---|---|
| Philosophical | Normative concepts and good order |
| Historical | Development through context and time |
| Institutional | Formal structures, law and organisation |
| Behavioural | Observable behaviour, regularities and empirical methods |
| Post-behavioural | Relevance and action alongside rigour |
| Systems | Inputs, conversion, outputs and feedback |
| Structural-functional | Functions performed by political structures |

### Behaviouralism vs post-behaviouralism

| Behaviouralism | Post-behaviouralism |
|---|---|
| Empirical, measurable, value-neutral aspiration | Relevance, value-awareness and action |
| “Science of politics” | Rigour in service of urgent political problems |

> **Trap:** Post-behaviouralism did not reject empirical research; it rejected irrelevance and false value-neutrality.

## 13. Interdisciplinary political analysis

| Discipline | Contribution |
|---|---|
| History | Institutional path and context |
| Economics | Allocation, class, incentives and distribution |
| Sociology | Groups, stratification and institutions |
| Psychology | Attitudes, identity and leadership |
| Anthropology | Culture and local political organisation |
| Philosophy | Meaning, justification and normative standards |
| Law | Valid authority, rights and procedures |

> **Core rule:** Borrowed methods deepen analysis but cannot reduce politics wholly to economics, psychology or any single discipline.

## 14. State, government, society, nation and civil society

| Concept | Distinctive meaning |
|---|---|
| State | Sovereign political association over territory/population |
| Government | Temporary institutional agency exercising state authority |
| Society | Wider network of human relationships |
| Nation | Community claiming shared political identity/destiny |
| Civil society | Associations between household, market and state, defined differently across traditions |
| Internationalism | Cooperation/solidarity beyond national boundaries |

### Civil-society variants

| Liberal | Marxist | Gramscian |
|---|---|---|
| Autonomous sphere checking state | Part of bourgeois order | Terrain of hegemony and counter-hegemony |

### Secularism and multiculturalism

`plurality is a fact; secularism/multiculturalism are normative arrangements`

> **Trap:** State ≠ government; nation ≠ state; non-theocracy alone is insufficient for substantive secular equality.

## 15. Sovereignty and pluralism

| Dimension | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Legal | Determinate law-making authority |
| Political | Actual forces shaping legal authority |
| Popular | Ultimate authority of the people |
| Internal | Supremacy within territory |
| External | Independence from outside control |
| De jure | Authority in law |
| De facto | Effective control in practice |

### Pluralist challenge

`state is not the only real association; social groups possess independent authority and loyalty`

> **Trap:** Legal sovereignty may be formally undivided while political power is socially dispersed and institutionally constrained.

## 16. Globalisation and sovereignty

### Pressure channels

`capital + technology + trade rules + transnational firms + international institutions + global norms + cross-border problems`

### Correct conclusion

Globalisation rarely “abolishes” the state. It:

- constrains some choices;
- creates new regulatory tasks;
- redistributes capacity unevenly;
- encourages pooled or negotiated authority;
- sometimes strengthens states able to shape global flows.

> **Source caution:** Treat examples from the 2009 source as historical unless independently updated.

## 17. Perspectives on the state

| Theory | State as | Core purpose/problem |
|---|---|---|
| Organic/idealist | Ethical whole | Common good and moral development |
| Social-contract liberal | Consent-created instrument | Protect rights/security |
| Laissez-faire | Night-watchman | Prevent coercion and protect property |
| Welfare liberal | Enabling agency | Positive freedom and welfare |
| Marxist | Class instrument | Reproduce dominant relations |
| Pluralist | Coordinator among groups | Mediate associational interests |
| Feminist | Historically gendered structure | Reproduce or reform patriarchy |
| Post-colonial | Colonial inheritance/developmental structure | Domination and transformation |
| Gandhian | Concentrated coercive power | Decentralised self-rule |

> **Trap:** The state is neither inherently neutral nor necessarily reducible to one ruling interest across all theories.

## 18. Political obligation, resistance and law

### Why obey?

`consent + gratitude/fair play + common good + justice + associative duty + necessity/order`

### Resistance ladder

`petition -> protest -> non-cooperation -> civil disobedience -> resistance/revolution`

### Civil disobedience tests

`public + conscientious + non-violent + targeted + last-resort orientation + acceptance of legal consequence (contested)`

### Punishment theories

| Theory | Logic | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Retributive | Deserved response to wrongdoing | Proportionality |
| Deterrent | Prevent future offending | Empirical effectiveness |
| Reformative | Rehabilitate offender | Public safety and feasibility |

> **Trap:** Legal validity does not automatically establish moral legitimacy, but subjective disagreement does not automatically justify disobedience.

## 19. Power, authority and legitimacy

| Concept | Distinguishing test |
|---|---|
| Power | Capacity to secure outcomes/compliance |
| Influence | Persuasion without authoritative sanction |
| Authority | Power accepted as rightful |
| Legitimacy | Quality producing justified/willing acceptance |
| Coercion | Compliance through threat or force |
| Hegemony | Consent shaped through ideology and culture |

### Weberian authority

`traditional + charismatic + legal-rational`

### Power lenses

`elite concentration | pluralist competition | Marxist class domination | feminist structural power | Gramscian consent`

> **Memory line:** `authority = power + legitimacy`.

## 20. Citizenship and its critiques

| Dimension | Distinction |
|---|---|
| Formal vs substantive | Legal membership vs actual capacity to exercise rights |
| Universal vs differentiated | Equal status vs group-specific recognition/accommodation |
| Active vs passive | Participation/duties vs status/entitlements |

### Marshall's rights

`civil -> political -> social`

### Critiques

| Tradition | Critique |
|---|---|
| Marxist | Equal legal status masks class inequality |
| Feminist | Universal citizen historically coded male |
| Communitarian | Rights language neglects membership/duties |
| Subaltern/post-colonial | Formal inclusion coexists with exclusion |

> **Trap:** Equal citizenship need not mean identical treatment where relevant structural disadvantage requires accommodation.

## 21. Rights and civil liberties

| Theory | Basis | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Natural | Human nature/reason | Disagreement over content |
| Legal | State recognition/enforcement | Unjust law may create deficient rights |
| Historical | Custom and evolution | Longevity cannot validate oppression |
| Personality/idealist | Conditions for human development | Requires institutional guarantee |
| Social-welfare | Common good | Who defines welfare? |

| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Human rights | Universal moral/legal claims of persons |
| Civil liberties | Protected freedoms against interference |
| Democratic rights | Participation in collective political power |

> **Trap:** Rights are not simply gifts from the state; legal enforceability and moral justification are distinct questions.

## 22. Liberty, equality and property

### Liberty

| Negative | Positive |
|---|---|
| Freedom from interference | Capacity/self-mastery to act meaningfully |
| Guards private sphere | May justify enabling conditions |
| Risk: ignores structural incapacity | Risk: paternalism/coercive perfection |

### Equality

| Formal | Substantive |
|---|---|
| Same rules/status | Corrects relevant disadvantage and unequal capacity |

`equality of status + opportunity + treatment + outcome-sensitive justice`

### Property

| Position | Core idea |
|---|---|
| Locke/liberal | Labour, independence and protected title |
| Utilitarian | Justified by social utility |
| Marxist | Private ownership of productive assets enables exploitation |
| Nozick | Just acquisition, transfer and rectification |
| Proudhon/anarchist | Critique of exploitative property claims |
| Gandhian | Trusteeship and social obligation |

> **Trap:** Equality does not require sameness; liberty and equality can conflict but can also be mutually enabling.

## 23. Justice: concepts and dimensions

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Legal | Was settled law applied? |
| Political | Is power/participation fairly distributed? |
| Socio-economic | Are opportunities and resources fairly arranged? |
| Procedural | Was the decision process fair? |
| Substantive/distributive | Is the outcome/share fair? |
| Recognitive | Are identities and status injuries acknowledged? |
| Corrective | Was a wrong repaired? |

### Distribution criteria

`equality + need + desert + contribution + entitlement + welfare + priority to disadvantaged`

> **Trap:** Fair procedure can produce substantively unjust outcomes; fair outcomes pursued through arbitrary procedure also undermine justice.

## 24. Perspectives on justice

| Perspective | Criterion |
|---|---|
| Rawls | Equal basic liberties; fair opportunity; inequalities benefit least advantaged |
| Nozick | Just acquisition, transfer and rectification |
| Marxist | End exploitation/class domination; need-based horizon |
| Feminist | Gendered distribution, care, recognition and private power |
| Communitarian | Goods interpreted within social meanings/common life |
| Subaltern | Voice, recognition and structural exclusion |

### Rawls sequence

`equal basic liberties -> fair equality of opportunity -> difference principle`

### Nozick sequence

`just acquisition -> just transfer -> rectification`

> **Trap:** Rawls does not demand identical outcomes; Nozick does not justify every existing holding.

## 25. Common good and community

| View | Common good |
|---|---|
| Liberal | Conditions allowing individuals to pursue diverse ends |
| Communitarian | Shared goods embedded in common practices |
| Marxian | Non-dominating community beyond alienated class society |
| Gandhian | Sarvodaya, decentralisation and moral cooperation |

### Balance test

`shared purpose` must be checked against `pluralism + dissent + minority rights + internal domination`

> **Trap:** Common good is neither the arithmetic sum of preferences nor whatever the majority declares.

---

## 26. Democracy and representation

### Liberal-democracy minimum

`competitive elections + civil liberties + rule of law + accountable government + independent adjudication + minority protection`

| Model | Core claim |
|---|---|
| Representative | Citizens authorise decision-makers |
| Elitist | Competition among elites for votes |
| Pluralist | Group bargaining disperses power |
| Participatory | Active participation beyond elections |
| Deliberative | Legitimacy through public reasoning |
| Marxist | Formal democracy constrained by class power |

### Representation types

`delegate + trustee + mandate + descriptive + substantive`

> **Trap:** Elections are necessary but insufficient for democracy; meaningful choice, liberties, accountability and inclusion are also required.

## 27. Contemporary democracy, change and development

### Change models

| Model | Route |
|---|---|
| Liberal/incremental | Gradual constitutional reform |
| Marxist/revolutionary | Structural transformation through conflict |
| Gandhian | Non-violent decentralised reconstruction |
| Feminist/subaltern | Transform public and private power relations |

### Development models

| Model | Priority |
|---|---|
| Market | Growth, enterprise and allocation |
| Welfare | Growth plus redistribution/social protection |
| Socialist | Planned/social direction of production |
| Gandhian | Local self-reliance and need-centred development |
| Sustainable/human | Capability, institutions, equity and ecology |

> **Core rule:** Development is not GDP alone; ask who participates, who benefits, what capacities expand and which freedoms or ecologies are lost.

## 29. UPSC traps and safe corrections

| Trap | Safe correction |
|---|---|
| “Political theory is only abstract philosophy.” | It explains, evaluates and guides political practice. |
| “Liberalism means minimal state.” | Welfare liberalism supports enabling and redistributive government. |
| “Marxism is mechanical economic determinism.” | Politics and ideology possess interaction and relative autonomy. |
| “Fascism has a coherent universal property theory.” | Its central traits are totalitarian nationalism and subordination. |
| “Feminism is one school.” | Liberal, radical and socialist diagnoses differ. |
| “Communitarianism rejects all rights.” | It challenges the abstract self and stresses social membership. |
| “Sovereignty disappeared under globalisation.” | It is constrained, pooled, negotiated and transformed. |
| “Lawful authority is automatically legitimate.” | Legal validity and justified acceptance can diverge. |
| “Equal treatment always creates equality.” | Substantive equality may require relevant differentiation. |
| “Rawls permits any inequality helping the poor.” | Prior liberty and opportunity conditions must first hold. |
| “Nozick validates current holdings.” | Title must satisfy acquisition, transfer and rectification. |
| “Majority rule equals democracy.” | Rights, opposition, accountability and minority safeguards matter. |
| “Common good is majority preference.” | It requires reasoned, inclusive and rights-compatible justification. |
| “Development equals economic growth.” | Add capability, equity, participation and ecological sustainability. |

## 30. Reusable Mains answer skeletons

### A. Concept question

1. Define.
2. Distinguish nearest concepts.
3. Present two theoretical views.
4. Explain the disagreement mechanism.
5. Criticise each.
6. Synthesis.

### B. Ideology question

1. Historical/intellectual context.
2. Unit of analysis.
3. Freedom, justice and state role.
4. Internal variants.
5. Rival critique.
6. Contemporary conceptual relevance without unsupported current claims.

### C. Thinker comparison

1. Common question.
2. Thinker A's premises and conclusion.
3. Thinker B's premises and conclusion.
4. Exact point of disagreement.
5. Strength and limitation of each.
6. Qualified judgment.

### D. Democracy/development question

1. Define the model.
2. State necessary conditions.
3. Explain institutional/social mechanism.
4. Identify exclusion or domination.
5. Add participation, rights and accountability.
6. Conclude with substantive democratic capacity.
