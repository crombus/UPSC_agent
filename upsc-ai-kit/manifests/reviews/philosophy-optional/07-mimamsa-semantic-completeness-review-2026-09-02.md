## 6. Canonical and cross-owner boundaries

- Mīmāṃsā owns intrinsic validity, its sub-school pramāṇa dispute, postulation, Bhāṭṭa non-cognition, ritual hermeneutics, authorless Veda, injunction, duty and unseen ritual potency.
- Nyāya owns external validation, higher-order cognition, its word-reference comparison account and God-guaranteed scripture; these appear only as contrasts.
- Vedānta owns Brahman-centred Upaniṣadic interpretation and identity/liberation theories; it must not absorb Pūrva-Mīmāṃsā hermeneutics.
- Buddhism owns momentariness, no-self and meaning through exclusion; Mīmāṃsā answers from realism, enduring selves and universals.
- Cārvāka owns the radical perceptualist challenge; Mīmāṃsā's reply does not by itself prove every ritual proposition.
- Detailed ritual procedure, specialist sub-commentarial disputes and positive-bliss controversies remain optional enrichment.
