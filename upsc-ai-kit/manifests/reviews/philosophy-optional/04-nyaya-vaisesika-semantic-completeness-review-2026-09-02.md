## 6. Canonical and cross-owner boundaries

- Nyāya owns its sixteen inquiry topics, four means of valid knowledge, inference/debate method, theory of appearance, self arguments and developed natural theology.
- Vaiśeṣika owns its seven ontological categories, nine substances, universal, particularity, inherence, absence and atomism.
- The later Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika synthesis owns their combined realist causation, cosmic order and explicit theistic integration; this does not erase original ownership.
- Buddhism owns exclusion theory, momentariness, no-self and its means/result account; Nyāya retains only the opponent side required by routed debates.
- Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā owns non-cognition as an independent means; Nyāya contains only the contrast showing reduction to perception or inference.
- Mīmāṃsā also owns authorless Veda and its own word/sentence theories; it must not be portrayed as rejecting universals in the Buddhist manner.
- Cārvāka owns radical inference scepticism; Nyāya contains its reply.
- Sāṃkhya owns pre-existence of effect and primordial nature; Nyāya contains the causal contrast.
- Yoga owns the special conscious witness and devotion-to-God discipline; Nyāya contains only the verified 2018 comparative requirement.
- Paper II owns general philosophy-of-religion development; Udayana's Indian-school proofs remain marks-essential here because directly printed and repeatedly tested.
