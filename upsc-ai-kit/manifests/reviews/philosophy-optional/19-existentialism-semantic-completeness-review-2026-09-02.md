## 6. Canonical and cross-owner boundaries

- Kierkegaard, Sartre and Heidegger are the only printed thinker-owners.
- Sartre owns “existence precedes essence”; it is not existentialism's shared formula.
- Kierkegaard owns religious existence, the existing individual and subjective appropriation, with pseudonym controls.
- Heidegger owns fundamental ontology, Dasein, being-in-the-world, authenticity and temporality.
- Husserl owns the positive theory of reduction; Existentialism owns Heidegger's rejection in the routed 2018 question.
- Hegel owns the positive system; Kierkegaard's anti-system criticism is owned here only to answer the routed comparison.
- Nietzsche, Marcel, Jaspers, Camus and de Beauvoir are optional comparison, not substitute owner-content.

## 7. Answer architecture and factual control

- Every routed PYQ has an explicit demand-specific route.
- Existentialism is presented as a family-label, not one doctrine.
- Kierkegaardian subjectivity is appropriation, not factual relativism.
- The three spheres are not a compulsory developmental sequence.
- Johannes de Silentio's staged analysis is not a direct licence to suspend ethics privately.
- Heidegger's project is fundamental ontology, not existentialist psychology.
- Dasein is not identical with authentic existence.
- Authenticity is a non-moral modification and does not abolish being-with.
- Clock/public time is derived; it is not simply false or morally inauthentic.
- Sartrean freedom is structurally inescapable but situated, not omnipotence.
- Facticity includes body, past and social/material constraint.
- In-itself is not merely matter or a timeless substance.
- Bad faith is not automatically proved by role-performance.
- The Look is a powerful account of exposure to another perspective, not proof that every relation is necessarily conflict.
- Husserlian appresentation is not reduced to a discursive analogy-inference.
- “Leap of faith” and translated mood vocabulary carry source/translation cautions.
- English concepts precede Danish, German or French terms.
