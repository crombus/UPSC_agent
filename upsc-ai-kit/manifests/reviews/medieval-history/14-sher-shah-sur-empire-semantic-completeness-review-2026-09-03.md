## 4. State and administration

Sher Shah's realm was mapped as direct core, route/fort garrison, subordinate/
tributary ruler and temporary campaign reach. Strong personal monarchy worked
through Afghan nobles/clans, assignments, officers, zamindars and local
personnel.

Central Sultanate-derived portfolios, sarkar/shiq supervision, pargana
shiqdar-amin/munsif-qanungo-fotedar/writers and village
muqaddam-patwari-panchayat structures were separated. No Akbari suba or modern
bureaucracy was projected backward.

## 5. Revenue, circulation and money

The repaired chain is:

measurement → good/middling/bad classification → rai schedule → qualified
one-third norm → patta/qabuliyat → cash/kind collection → intermediaries and
regional exceptions.

Sarwani idealization, Multan/frontier exceptions and crop/price variation
prevent universal measurement or ryotwari claims.

The tri-metallic gold–silver rupiya–copper dam field was treated as
standardization, not invention. Earlier silver/rupee traditions and later Mughal
adaptation remained explicit.

Older corridors were repaired/extended and linked to sarais, wells, trees,
bridges/ferries, horses/runners, security and intelligence. "Grand Trunk Road,"
two-karoh spacing and modern postal/public-works analogies were bounded.

## 6. Army, society, religion and architecture

- Direct recruitment, cash/assignments, cavalry, artillery, forts and logistics
  coexisted.
- Dagh/chehra had Sultanate precedents and chronicle-mediated Sur enforcement.
- Rohtas and Rohtasgarh were distinguished.
- Justice stories were read as impartial-king rhetoric, not crime statistics.
- Written demand could regularize and still burden peasants; zamindars remained.
- Raisen/Puran Mal blocked both universal tolerance and persecution labels.
- Sasaram's octagonal lake tomb spans Sher/Islam Shah phases.
- Purana Qila/Dinpanah-Shergarh is multi-phase; Qila-i-Kuhna attribution was
  bounded.

## 7. Succession and legacy

Islam Shah's continuation and stricter noble control were restored. Firuz's
killing, Adil Shah, Sikandar/Ibrahim Sur, Hemu and civil wars explained rapid
fragmentation. The cause was personalized centralization, succession and
faction—not inherent Afghan incapacity.

Sultanate precedents → Sur systematization → later Mughal selection/adaptation
replaced the false complete-Akbar-blueprint model.
