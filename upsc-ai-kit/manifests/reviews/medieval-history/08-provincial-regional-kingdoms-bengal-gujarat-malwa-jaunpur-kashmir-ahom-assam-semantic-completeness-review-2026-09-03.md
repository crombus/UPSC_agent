## 5. State-by-state completion ledger

### Bengal

- 1338 autonomy and Ilyas Shah's wider unification from 1342;
- Ilyas Shahi and restored Ilyas Shahi continuity;
- Ghiyasuddin Azam Shah and Alauddin Husain Shah;
- Pandua/Gaur, Sonargaon/Satgaon and Chittagong contexts;
- delta agrarian surplus, textiles, maritime routes and local intermediaries;
- Persianate-Bengali-Sanskrit/local interaction and brick/terracotta adaptation.

### Gujarat

- Zafar Khan/Muzaffar Shah and formal independence around 1407;
- Ahmad Shah I, Ahmedabad and the c. 1411–13 foundation/date caution;
- Mahmud Begarha, Girnar/Junagadh and Champaner;
- Bahadur Shah as a bounded 1526–37 transition ruler;
- Khambhat/Cambay, ports, merchants, crafts, textiles and hinterlands;
- Mahmud Begarha was not conflated with Bahadur Shah's Bassein/Diu concessions.

### Malwa

- Dilawar Khan Ghuri and Hushang Shah before the Malwa Khaljis;
- Mandu's plateau, forts and north-west/Deccan routes;
- court, tribute/revenue, nobles and commercial actors;
- Gujarat, Mewar, Delhi and Bahmani relations as bounded inter-state context;
- monument phases were not collapsed into one ruler.

### Jaunpur

- Malik Sarwar/Malik-us-Sharq and Sharqi legitimacy;
- Ganga-valley revenue and political competition with Delhi/Lodis/Bengal;
- learning, Sufis, Persianate/regional traditions and the "Shiraz of the East";
- Atala Mosque and the Jaunpur regional idiom;
- Bahlul Lodi's prolonged campaigns and absorption by 1484.

### Kashmir

- Shah Mir dynasty frame;
- Sikandar claims treated through chronicle/source caution;
- Zain-ul-Abidin (1420–70), reconciliation, crafts, learning and translation;
- valley agriculture, routes and court/local factions;
- Persianate, Sanskrit, Kashmiri, Sufi and Rishi interaction;
- no one-word tolerant/intolerant classification.

### Bounded Ahom/Assam

- Sukapha's entry is presented as traditionally dated to 1228;
- state formation through wet-rice expansion, conflict, alliance and incorporation;
- paik service is historical and changing, not timeless or chattel slavery;
- Buranjis are indispensable but court-produced and unevenly transmitted;
- Moidam political context remains here; detailed art/UNESCO treatment does not.
