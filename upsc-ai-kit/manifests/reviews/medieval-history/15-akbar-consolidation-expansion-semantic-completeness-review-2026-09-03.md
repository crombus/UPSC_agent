  cavalry/elephants, Bairam leadership and Hemu's wound contingency.

- Maham Anaga/Adham/Atka were analysed through household patronage, not gossip.

- The completed chronology extends through Kashmir 1586, Sindh 1591, Odisha,
  Qandahar 1595, Ahmadnagar/Berar and Khandesh/Asirgarh.

## 5. Rajputs, Afghans and frontiers

Amber alliance combined marriage, rank, service, watan/jagir and honour;
Surjan Hada showed marriage was not universal. Chittor massacre and Mewar
resistance qualified tolerance narratives. Haldighati did not end Pratap's
resistance, and mixed coalitions rejected communal/nationalist framing.

Daud Karrani's defeat ended one state, not Afghan politics. Bengal required
riverine logistics and continuing incorporation.

Mirza Hakim, Lahore mobility, Roshanai/Pashtun networks, Kashmir/Sindh/
Baluchistan/Qandahar and late Deccan campaigns were separated by method and
control.

## 6. Bounded sovereignty, policy and succession

Timurid padshahship, fateh claims, court ritual, coins, paintings and
Fatehpur/Buland Darwaza made sovereignty visible without proving consent.

Mansab/jagir/watan/suba/revenue appeared only as political instruments.
Pilgrimage tax/jizya abolition, Ibadat Khana, mahzar, sulh-i kul and
small-circle Tauhid-i Ilahi were bounded to consolidation context—not modern
secularism or a mass religion.

Agra, Fatehpur Sikri, Lahore and the mobile camp matched different strategic
phases. Salim's Allahabad court and Abu'l Fazl's 1602 killing restored the
late-reign succession problem.
