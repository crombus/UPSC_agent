## 5. Chronology and rulers

### Vijayanagara

- Sangama: Harihara I, Bukka I, Deva Raya I and Deva Raya II.
- Late Sangama contraction and succession stress.
- Saluva Narasimha's dynastic seizure and restoration attempt.
- Tuluva: Vira Narasimha, Krishnadevaraya (1509–29), Achyuta Raya
  (1529–42), Sadashiva Raya and Aliya Rama Raya's effective power.
- Talikota/Rakshasi-Tangadi/Bannihatti in 1565.
- Aravidu continuation under Tirumala and successors from altered capitals.

### Bahmani

- Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah established independent rule in 1347.
- Gulbarga was the early capital.
- Firuz Shah (1397–1422) combined war, learning, ports and diplomacy.
- Ahmad Shah I shifted the political centre to Bidar.
- Mahmud Gawan served as minister/wakil-i-sultanat under Muhammad Shah III,
  with execution dated c. 1481–82 across repository sources.
- Breakup into Ahmadnagar, Bijapur, Golconda, Berar and Bidar was gradual.

## 6. Conflict, diplomacy and Talikota

- The Raichur/Tungabhadra doab joined fertile land, forts and frontier revenue.
- Krishna-Godavari routes and ports and the Konkan-Goa-Dabhol horse circuit
  made conflict fiscal, military and maritime.
- Political marriages, Muslim personnel in Vijayanagara service, Hindu/local
  officials under Bahmani rulers and shifting alliances disprove a permanent
  Hindu-Muslim camp model.
- Talikota's principal coalition is commonly associated with Ahmadnagar,
  Bijapur, Golconda and Bidar; Berar was not inserted automatically.
- Rama Raya's diplomacy, coalition formation, command, cavalry/artillery,
  logistics and contingency interacted.
- Hampi was devastated, but Aravidu and nayaka power continued; 1565 was a
  catastrophic transformation, not instant extinction.

## 7. Administration and state debate

| Vijayanagara | Bahmani |
|---|---|
| raya and ministerial council | sultan and court |
| rajya/mandala, nadu, sthala and grama | taraf/tarafdar and forts |
| amaram/nayaka military-revenue service | Gawan's eight tarafs, salaries and obligations |
| changing hereditary nayaka bases | khalisa, measurement and fort control |
| ayagar village-service descriptions require date/region caution | local revenue personnel and provincial bargaining |

Vijayanagara was assessed through segmentary, centralized, military-fiscal and
integrative models. The final verdict is variable, negotiated centralization.
Nayankara was not equated with European feudalism, Sultanate iqta or every later
poligar system.

## 8. Economy, society, culture and landscape

- Tanks, canals, temples, local officers and nayakas linked agrarian expansion
  with revenue and military provisioning.
- Tax incidence varied by crop, soil, irrigation and region.
- Hampi was treated as an urban region of royal/sacred centres, bazaars, craft,
  water, settlement and defence.
- Ports and merchant networks linked horses, textiles, spices, grain and luxury
  goods to the interior.
- Vijayanagara varaha/pagoda and Bahmani coinage were used as bounded evidence
  of sovereignty/exchange.
- Courts, warriors, Brahmans, Muslim soldiers/officials, merchants, artisans,
  peasants, temple/matha personnel, captives/dependants and women were covered.
- Gangadevi and Portuguese accounts supplied bounded female-agency evidence,
  not a census.
- Temples, mosques, madrasas and dargahs were treated as landed, educational,
  ritual and political institutions.
- *Amuktamalyada*, *Madhuravijayam* and Telugu/Kannada/Sanskrit/Tamil patronage
  completed the literature-language matrix.

## 9. Architecture and terminology controls

- Virupaksha is an older sacred site expanded under Vijayanagara.
- Vitthala was phase-built; Hazara Rama belongs to the royal-centre landscape.
- "Lotus Mahal" and "Elephant Stables" are conventional labels whose original
  names/functions require caution.
- Gulbarga fort/Jama Masjid, Bidar fort/court and Mahmud Gawan's madrasa were
  integrated into Bahmani political history.
- Local southern craft and Persianate/West Asian techniques interacted, but
  motifs were not treated as automatic proof of uncomplicated syncretism.
