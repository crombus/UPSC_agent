## 5. Identity and Central Asian context

- "Mughal" was treated as a later Indian umbrella, not a simple ethnic label.
- Paternal Timurid and maternal Chagatai-Chingizid genealogies were instruments
  of remembered descent and political legitimacy.
- Babur's world combined Chagatai Turkic language, Barlas/Mongol traditions and
  Persianate court culture.
- Timurid fragmentation, Shaybani Uzbek consolidation, Safavid intervention and
  the Ottoman-Safavid balance narrowed options without determining India.

## 6. Kabul, Qandahar and campaign chronology

Kabul (1504) was restored as refuge, army/court base, trade junction and
cultural-garden centre. Qandahar provided rear/flank and Iran-Central
Asia-Hindustan linkage.

| Phase | Completed significance |
|---|---|
| 1519 | Bajaur/Bhera-Bhira probes and coercive frontier entry |
| 1519–24 | repeated Punjab advances including Sialkot/Lahore-Dipalpur contexts |
| 1524 | Daulat Khan/Alam Khan intervention |
| November 1525 | final march after rear/flank preparation |
| December 1525–April 1526 | Indus crossing, Punjab control and reconnaissance |

Invitations created opportunity but did not cancel Babur's aggression.

## 8. Aftermath, Khanwa, Chanderi and Ghaghra

- Delhi/Agra occupation used khutba, treasure, grants, offices and garrisons.
- Beg homesickness, climate, food/fodder, roads, forts and eastern Afghans
  demonstrated fragility.
- Khanwa's coalition included Rajput rulers, Mahmud Lodi and Hasan Khan Mewati;
  it was not modern nationalism or a Hindu-Muslim binary.
- Jihad/ghazi, Quran oath, wine and tamgha rhetoric recorded mobilization and
  piety, not exhaustive motive.
- Chanderi (1528) was a bounded Rajput follow-through.
- Ghaghra (1529) addressed eastern Afghans and Bengal/Nusrat Shah but did not
  annex Bengal.

## 9. State, culture and religion

- Timurid padshah claims coexisted with councils and bargaining with begs.
- Gifts, assignments, khutba, coinage, local personnel and Sultanate revenue
  continuities linked victory to rule.
- "Afghan equality" and "Timurid absolutism" were not treated as fixed
  constitutions.
- Chagatai prose/poetry, Persianate court culture, landscape comparison and
  natural-history observation were included.
- Gardens joined water, memory and sovereignty; Bagh-e Babur's multiple phases
  prevented an unchanged-1530 reading.
- Sunni practice, Safavid accommodation and Khanwa religious rhetoric were kept
  separate from secular/fanatical personality labels.

## 10. Founder assessment

Babur created a durable dynastic claim and Kabul-Delhi-Agra axis, won Panipat,
Khanwa and Ghaghra, and chose to remain. Yet rule lasted only 1526–30 and left
fort, Afghan, revenue and elite-cohesion problems.

**Verdict:** conqueror and founder of a fragile imperial project, not creator of
the fully consolidated Mughal empire. Babur's death and Humayun's succession in
1530 remain only a Topic-13 bridge.
