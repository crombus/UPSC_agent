## 5. Bhakti chronology and doctrine

- Tamil Alvar/Nayanar hymn, pilgrimage and temple worlds form the earlier bridge.
- Ramanuja: *Vishishtadvaita*, *prapatti* and Sri Vaishnava institutions.
- Nimbarka: *Dvaitadvaita/Bhedabheda*, with disputed chronology.
- Madhva: *Dvaita* and Udupi-centred lineage.
- Ramananda: Rama devotion/wider access; disciple genealogies are hagiographic.
- Vallabha: *Shuddhadvaita* and *Pushtimarg*.
- Chaitanya (1486–1533): Gaudiya Krishna devotion and public *kirtan*.

Saguna/nirguna were retained as analytical tendencies, not fixed
conservative/radical organizations.

## 7. Guru Nanak and Sikh boundary

- Standard Sikh chronology, 1469–1539, was distinguished from a local OCR 1538
  printing.
- Divine-name remembrance, ethical work, sharing, guru discipline and
  householder life were retained.
- Hymns in the Guru Granth tradition were distinguished from later Janamsakhi
  itineraries.
- Sangat/langar developed historically.
- Later Gurus, Khalsa and Sikh state formation remain outside Topic 10.

## 8. Caste, gender and vernacularization

- Spiritual dignity/access did not automatically transform property, occupation,
  marriage or political power.
- Panths, sects, mathas, temples, sattras and shrines could reproduce hierarchy.
- Andal/Karaikkal Ammaiyar formed an earlier bridge; Akka Mahadevi, Lalla and
  Mirabai supplied distinct women-saint cases.
- Tamil hymns, Kannada vachanas, Marathi abhangs, Kashmiri vakhs, Braj/Awadhi
  poetry and Bengali/Assamese performance completed the regional map.
- Kirtan, bhajan, song, recitation and pilgrimage widened affective access but
  did not erase patronage, literacy or caste.

## 10. Khanqah economy, shrines and conversion

- Khanqahs required food, labour, buildings, books and hospitality.
- Resources included gifts/futuh, waqf/endowments, grants, patrons and disciples.
- Chishti refusal of fixed office/grants was an important ideal with varied
  historical practice.
- Dargahs developed through burial, succession, pilgrimage, donations,
  architecture and political patronage.
- Women/non-elites appeared as devotees, donors, workers and mystics; Bibi
  Fatima Sam was used as a bounded Chishti-memory example.
- Conversion was treated through settlement, marriage, patronage, social
  mobility, persuasion, agrarian context and local conflict—not a saint-led
  monocause.

## 11. Bhakti-Sufi encounter and limits

Possible overlaps included love, remembrance, guide-disciple language,
vernacular performance and shared spaces. Incarnation, prophecy, scripture,
law and temple/matha/panth versus khanqah/dargah/silsila structures remained
distinct.

"Syncretism" was not accepted without named evidence. Institutionalization
created panths, sampradayas, guru lineages, mathas, sattras, shrines and
manuscript canons while sometimes reproducing caste, gender and hereditary
authority.
