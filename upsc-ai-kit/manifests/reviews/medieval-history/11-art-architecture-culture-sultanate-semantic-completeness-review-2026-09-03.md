## 5. Technical and terminology decision

- Trabeate and arcuate systems coexisted.
- A true arch requires radial voussoirs; a corbelled opening does not.
- Dome-transition terms were made fabric-specific.
- Lime mortar was treated as one component of centring, cutting, bonding,
  curing and workshop skill.
- "Indo-Islamic" became a localization/process label, not a religious essence.
- No ruler/community received a single-introduction claim for arch, dome or
  mortar.
- Tughlaq batter was not called uniquely Egyptian or read automatically as
  poverty, insecurity or ideology.

## 6. Monument chronology

### Mamluk/early Sultanate

- Quwwat-ul-Islam: Aibak foundation, Iltutmish/Alauddin enlargement and
  site-specific reused fabric.
- Qutb Minar: Aibak-Iltutmish phases plus later repairs/alterations.
- Arhai Din ka Jhonpra: early mosque and enlargement over layered earlier fabric;
  popular-name tradition cautioned.
- Sultan Ghari: Iltutmish's mausoleum for Nasiruddin Mahmud.
- Iltutmish's tomb: square-to-dome/squinch experimentation.
- Balban's tomb: assigned-source first surviving true-arch marker.

### Khalji

- Siri, Alai Darwaza (1311), incomplete Alai Minar and Hauz-i-Alai.
- Alai Darwaza was described as a mature surviving arcuate/dome phase, not the
  first valid arch/dome.

### Tughlaq

- Tughlaqabad, Ghiyasuddin's tomb, Jahanpanah, Adilabad, Firoz Shah
  Kotla/Firozabad and the Hauz Khas repair-madrasa-tomb landscape.
- Alauddin's reservoir phase was separated from Firuz's institutional phase.

### Sayyid-Lodi

- Square/octagonal tendencies, platforms, kiosks/eaves and garden/enclosure.
- Muhammad Shah Sayyid, Sikandar Lodi, Bara Gumbad and Shish Gumbad were
  retained with conventional-name/function cautions.
- Double-dome/garden transition was bounded and not assigned one inventor.

## 7. Building, city, labour and region

Mosques, tombs, madrasas/maktabs, khanqahs, forts, palaces, reservoirs, baolis,
canals, bridges, sarais, markets and workshops were linked to social function.

Delhi was reconstructed as a shifting Mehrauli/Qutb–Siri–Tughlaqabad/
Adilabad–Jahanpanah–Firozabad/Kotla urban-hydraulic region.

Patrons and material supply were connected to architects, masons, stonecutters,
brick/lime workers, carpenters, calligraphers, tile/pigment workers,
transporters and other labour.

| Regional bridge | Completed distinction |
|---|---|
| Bengal | brick/terracotta and climate/material adaptation |
| Gujarat | stone, screens, pillars/brackets and workshop/merchant networks |
| Malwa/Mandu | platforms, mass, colour and plateau/lake setting |
| Jaunpur | emphatic portal and regional proportion |
| Kashmir | timber-masonry and tiered/sloping roof traditions |
| Deccan | Gulbarga/Bidar plaster/tile/madrasa, bounded to Topic 09 |

Regional architecture was not treated as derivative copying.

## 8. Wider culture ledger

- Persian court/chancery/history/poetry, Arabic scholarship, Sanskrit
  continuity and Hindavi/regional languages coexisted.
- Hasan Nizami, Minhaj, Amir Khusrau, Barani, Isami and Afif were separated by
  genre, chronology and bias.
- Maktabs, madrasas, mosques, khanqahs, libraries, pathshalas and mathas
  supported unequal knowledge circulation.
- Sama/qawwali, court/popular performance and Amir Khusrau's reputation were
  retained without tabla/sitar/qawwali invention claims.
- Manuscript painting, calligraphy, textiles, metalwork, ceramics, furnishings,
  food/dress evidence and uneven material survival were included.
- Non-elite artisans, labourers, performers, scribes and market workers were
  restored as cultural producers.
