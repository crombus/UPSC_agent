## 7. Factual and interpretive controls

- Southeast Asian societies were not called colonies or passive recipients.
- Sanskrit inscriptions were not equated with Indian political rule.
- Hindu/Buddhist monuments were not attributed automatically to Indian migrants.
- Modern national borders were not projected backward.
- No single route, ruler, mission or council explained all transmission.
- Greater India was treated as historiography, not neutral description.
- Mahavamsa tradition was separated from Ashokan inscriptional evidence.
- Translation was treated as conceptual remaking by local teams.
- Theravada, Mahayana and Vajrayana chronologies were kept distinct.
- Angkor Wat and mature Angkor were not pulled into the Ancient core.
- Borobudur and Prambanan were treated as Javanese monuments.
- Coin and ceramic finds did not prove rule or settlement.
- Rajendra I's campaign did not become a permanent Southeast Asian empire.
- Reciprocity did not imply equal influence in every region.
