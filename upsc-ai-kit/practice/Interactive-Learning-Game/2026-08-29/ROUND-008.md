## Question

> “The Preamble is an interpretive charter, but not an independently enforceable source of
> constitutional power.” Discuss.

## Challenge

Use this answer-writing engine:

```text
Claim -> named case evidence -> legal development -> qualification
```

Your paragraph must include:

1. *Berubari Union* (1960).
2. *Kesavananda Bharati* (1973).
3. *LIC of India* (1995).
4. A final qualification that constitutional status does not create independent enforceability.

Do not write an introduction or conclusion.

## How a body paragraph works

A body paragraph develops **one argument**. It should not list disconnected facts. For this
question, the argument is that the Preamble's constitutional status became stronger through case
law, but its enforceability remained limited.

### Four sentence-jobs

| Sentence | Job | What it should do here |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Claim | State the paragraph's main argument |
| 2 | Evidence | Give the earlier *Berubari* position |
| 3 | Development and analysis | Show how *Kesavananda* changed it and *LIC* reaffirmed it |
| 4 | Qualification | Explain that “part of the Constitution” does not mean independently enforceable |

### Demonstration using a different topic

```text
Claim: Indian federalism has a strong central bias.
Evidence: Article 3 allows Parliament to alter state boundaries.
Analysis: Since state consent is unnecessary, the Union possesses unusual authority over
          constituent units.
Qualification: However, constitutional consultation and political negotiation still protect
               federal interests.
```

The paragraph is coherent because every sentence supports the first claim.

### Sentence frame for this question

```text
1. Judicial interpretation progressively strengthened the constitutional status of the Preamble.
2. In Berubari...
3. Kesavananda changed this position..., and LIC...
4. Nevertheless...
```

## Guided construction - Step 1

Write only the **claim sentence** in your own words by completing:

> The Supreme Court's view of the Preamble evolved from __________________ to
> __________________.

Do not add the case details yet.

## Step 1 response

> The Supreme Court's view of the Preamble evolved from berubari1960 to kesavnanda1973

**Assessment:** Correct chronology, but the claim is incomplete.

The sentence names the beginning and end points but does not tell the examiner **what doctrinal
change occurred**. A claim must state the proposition that the later evidence will prove.

### Compare

| Type | Sentence | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Timeline only | The view evolved from *Berubari* to *Kesavananda* | Names cases but not the change |
| Analytical claim | The view evolved from treating the Preamble as outside the Constitution to recognising it as an integral constitutional part | States the change to be demonstrated |

## Step 1 refinement

Complete only this sentence:

> The Supreme Court's view evolved from treating the Preamble as __________________ to
> recognising it as __________________.

Do not use case names in this sentence; they will become the evidence in Step 2.

## Refined Step 1 response

> The Supreme Court’s view evolved from treating the Preamble as not part of constituion
> (berubari 1960) to recognising it as part of constitution kesavnanda (1973).

**Step 1 result:** Correct

### Polished claim

> The Supreme Court's view evolved from treating the Preamble as outside the Constitution to
> recognising it as an integral part of the constitutional text.

The case names are removed from the claim because they will perform the separate job of
**evidence** in the next sentences.

## Guided construction - Step 2

Now write only the *Berubari* evidence sentence:

> In *Berubari Union* (1960), the Supreme Court held that ________________________________,
> although it could _________________________________________________________________.

Include both:

1. The Court's conclusion about whether the Preamble was part of the Constitution.
2. Its limited usefulness in interpretation.

## Step 2 response

> In Berubari Union (1960), the Supreme Court held that it is not part of the consitution,
> although it could be interpreted by justice department for consituional understanding.

**Step 2 result:** Substantively correct

### Terminology correction

Constitutional interpretation is performed by **courts**, not by a “justice department.” The
Preamble itself is not “interpreted for understanding” in this context; it is used as an
**interpretive aid** to understand constitutional purpose or resolve ambiguity.

### Polished evidence sentence

> In *Berubari Union* (1960), the Supreme Court held that the Preamble was not part of the
> Constitution, although courts could use it as an interpretive key to the framers' purposes.

## Guided construction - Step 3

Write one sentence showing the later development:

> *Kesavananda Bharati* (1973) ________________________________, and *LIC of India* (1995)
> ________________________________________________.

Include:

1. *Kesavananda* recognised the Preamble as part of the Constitution and amendable subject to
   basic structure.
2. *LIC* reaffirmed its status as an integral part.

## Step 3 response

> Kesavananda Bharati (1973) case preamble be part of constituion and is amendable and part of
> consitution basic structure, and LIC of India (1995) for interpreting it by courts

**Step 3 assessment:** Partially correct

### Corrections

1. *Kesavananda Bharati* held that the Preamble is part of the Constitution and may be amended,
   but the amendment cannot destroy the Constitution's **basic structure**. Avoid saying that the
   entire Preamble is itself simply “part of basic structure.”
2. *LIC of India* (1995) **reaffirmed that the Preamble is an integral part of the Constitution**.
   Its main value here is doctrinal reaffirmation, not merely that courts may interpret it.

## Step 3 refinement

Rewrite only one sentence:

> *Kesavananda Bharati* (1973) recognised the Preamble as ________________________________,
> while limiting amendment by ________________________________; *LIC of India* (1995)
> subsequently reaffirmed ____________________________________________________________.

## Refined Step 3 response

> Kesavananda recognised the Preamble as part of constitution, while limiting amendment by its by
> basic structure; LIC subsequently reaffirmed Preamble as an integral part of consitution.

**Step 3 result:** Correct

### Polished development sentence

> *Kesavananda Bharati* (1973) recognised the Preamble as part of the Constitution and limited
> its amendment through the basic-structure doctrine; *LIC of India* (1995) subsequently
> reaffirmed its status as an integral part.

## Guided construction - Step 4

Now write the final **qualification sentence**, beginning with `Nevertheless`.

It must explain both:

1. The Preamble creates no independently enforceable right or governmental power.
2. Courts may still use it to interpret an ambiguous constitutional provision.

Write only one sentence:

> Nevertheless, _________________________________________________________________.

## Step 4 response

> Neverthless, it did not create any binding law or no enforceble right. Although, it is used as
> interpretation tool by courts.

**Step 4 result:** Substantively correct

### Precision correction

The Preamble does not itself create a **standalone enforceable right** or confer **governmental
power**. Saying that it creates “no binding law” is too broad because it remains a legally
significant part of the Constitution and informs interpretation.

### Polished qualification

> Nevertheless, it remains non-justiciable and creates no independent right or governmental
> power, although courts may use it to interpret ambiguous constitutional provisions.

## Assembled body paragraph

> Judicial interpretation progressively strengthened the Preamble's constitutional status. In
> *Berubari Union* (1960), the Supreme Court held that it was not part of the Constitution, though
> it could guide interpretation. *Kesavananda Bharati* (1973) reversed this position, recognised
> it as part and permitted amendment subject to basic structure; *LIC of India* (1995) reaffirmed
> its integral status. Nevertheless, it remains non-justiciable, creates no independent right or
> governmental power, and operates mainly as an aid when constitutional text is ambiguous.
