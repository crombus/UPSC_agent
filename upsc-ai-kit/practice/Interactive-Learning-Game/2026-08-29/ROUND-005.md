### What was done well

- Correctly recalled both *Berubari Union* and *Kesavananda Bharati*.
- Recognised that the Preamble expresses constitutional purposes and goals.
- Attempted to distinguish constitutional status from enforceability.

### Precision corrections

1. The Preamble states the **source, nature and objectives of the constitutional order**; it does
   not define the purpose of every citizen.
2. *Berubari Union* (1960) specifically held that the Preamble was **not a part of the
   Constitution**, though it could aid interpretation.
3. *Kesavananda Bharati* (1973) held that the Preamble **is part of the Constitution** and may be
   amended subject to the basic-structure limitation. It is safer not to say that the entire
   Preamble itself is simply “part of basic structure.”
4. Even after *Kesavananda*, the Preamble remains **non-justiciable**: it creates no independent
   enforceable right or governmental power.
