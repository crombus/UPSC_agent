## Challenge

Which one of the following correctly describes the constitutional position of the Preamble?

**A.** It is part of the Constitution and can be amended, but it is non-justiciable and its basic
features cannot be destroyed.

**B.** It is not part of the Constitution, although courts may enforce its objectives
independently.

**C.** It is the direct source of Parliament's legislative powers and therefore overrides express
constitutional provisions.

**D.** It is part of the Constitution but cannot be amended under any circumstances.

## Learner response and evaluation

**Learner's answer:** A  
**Correct answer:** A  
**Result:** Correct without hint  
**XP earned:** 10

### Why A is correct

- **Part of the Constitution:** *Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala* (1973) established the
  current position that the Preamble is part of the Constitution.
- **Amendable:** it may be amended under Article 368. The 42nd Amendment Act 1976 added
  `socialist`, `secular` and `integrity`.
- **Limited by basic structure:** Parliament cannot use its amending power to destroy basic
  constitutional features reflected in the Preamble.
- **Non-justiciable:** a person cannot independently ask a court to enforce a Preamble objective
  as though it were a Fundamental Right.
- **Not a source of power:** legislative and executive powers must arise from operative
  constitutional provisions, not from the Preamble alone.

### Why the other options fail

- **B:** *Berubari Union* (1960) had said the Preamble was not part, but this is no longer the
  governing position after *Kesavananda*. Non-justiciable objectives are not independently
  enforceable.
- **C:** the Preamble guides interpretation but does not grant legislative power or override
  express provisions.
- **D:** the Preamble was amended in 1976, proving that it is not completely unamendable.

> **Recall chain:** Berubari 1960 - not part; Kesavananda 1973 - part and amendable subject to
> basic structure; 42nd Amendment 1976 - amended once; LIC 1995 - integral part reaffirmed.
