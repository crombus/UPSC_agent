## Challenge

Consider the following statements:

1. In the *Berubari Union* opinion, the Supreme Court held that the Preamble was not a part of the
   Constitution.
2. In *Kesavananda Bharati*, the Supreme Court held that the Preamble is part of the Constitution
   and may be amended subject to the basic-structure limitation.
3. Since the Preamble is part of the Constitution, a court may enforce its objectives
   independently even when no operative constitutional provision is violated.
4. *LIC of India v. Consumer Education and Research Centre* reaffirmed that the Preamble is an
   integral part of the Constitution.

Which statements are correct?

**A.** 1 and 3 only  
**B.** 1, 2 and 4 only  
**C.** 2 and 4 only  
**D.** 1, 2, 3 and 4

## Learner response and evaluation

**Learner's answer:** B  
**Correct answer:** B - Statements 1, 2 and 4 only  
**Result:** Correct without hint  
**XP earned:** 10

### Statement analysis

1. **Correct.** *Berubari Union* (1960) treated the Preamble as an interpretive key but not as a
   part of the Constitution.
2. **Correct.** *Kesavananda Bharati* (1973) displaced that position by holding that the Preamble
   is part of the Constitution. It may be amended under Article 368, but its basic features cannot
   be destroyed.
3. **Incorrect.** Being part of the Constitution does not make the Preamble independently
   enforceable. A claimant must rely on an operative provision such as a Fundamental Right.
4. **Correct.** *LIC of India v. Consumer Education and Research Centre* (1995) reaffirmed the
   Preamble as an integral part of the Constitution.

### Close-option trap

The trap equates **“part of the Constitution”** with **“independently enforceable.”** These are
different:

| Constitutional status | Legal consequence |
|---|---|
| Part of the Constitution | Guides interpretation and reflects basic constitutional values |
| Non-justiciable | Creates no standalone claim enforceable by a court |
| Not a source of power | Government must identify an operative Article authorising its action |
