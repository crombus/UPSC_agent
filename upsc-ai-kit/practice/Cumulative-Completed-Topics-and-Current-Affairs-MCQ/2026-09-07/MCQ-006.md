# MCQ 006 — Conceptions of God in Indian Philosophy

**Area:** Indian Philosophy + Philosophy of Religion  
**Difficulty:** Hard  
**Status:** Completed - remediation required

## Question

With reference to conceptions of God in Indian philosophy, consider the following statements:

1. Nyāya generally treats God as the intelligent and efficient cause that orders eternal atoms,
   rather than as the material substance from which the world is constituted.
2. Classical Sāṃkhya explains cosmic evolution through the interaction of prakṛti and puruṣa
   without requiring a creator God as an indispensable explanatory principle.
3. Advaita Vedānta distinguishes empirical Īśvara associated with māyā from ultimate nirguṇa
   Brahman, while denying that this distinction is final at the highest level of truth.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. 1 and 2 only  
B. 2 and 3 only  
C. 1 and 3 only  
D. 1, 2 and 3

## Learner response

**A. 1 and 2 only**  
Learner note: "I am not able to recall."

## Evaluation and explanation

**Result:** Incorrect  
**Correct answer:** D. 1, 2 and 3

The response correctly retained the Nyāya and Sāṃkhya positions but omitted the Advaita
distinction. This is recorded as a recall gap rather than a conceptual failure.

### Statement 1 — Nyāya

**Correct.** Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika accepts eternal atoms as the material constituents of the physical
world. God (Īśvara) is the intelligent or efficient cause who orders those atoms and connects
results with the moral law of karma. God is therefore not ordinarily described as the material
stuff that transforms into the world.

**Core argument route**

```
World displays effect-like order
        ↓
Effects require an intelligent maker
        ↓
Atoms cannot organise themselves purposively
        ↓
Īśvara inferred as omniscient organiser
```

Udayana later systematised major Nyāya proofs for God in the *Nyāyakusumāñjali*.

### Statement 2 — Classical Sāṃkhya

**Correct.** Classical Sāṃkhya is commonly described as non-theistic (nirīśvara). It explains the
world through:

- **Prakṛti:** unconscious primordial material nature and the source of the evolutes.
- **Puruṣa:** plural conscious selves that are witnesses but do not create the world.
- **Three guṇas:** sattva, rajas and tamas, whose disequilibrium drives manifestation.

A creator is not logically required because prakṛti supplies the material and causal process,
while karma explains differences in embodied experience. This does not mean Sāṃkhya denies every
possible divine being in the same manner as strict materialism; its classical explanatory system
does not require a creator God.

### Statement 3 — Advaita Vedānta

**Correct.** Advaita distinguishes two levels relevant here:

| Level | Conception |
|---|---|
| Vyāvahārika, empirical truth | Īśvara is Brahman associated with māyā: omniscient, cosmic ruler and cause of the experienced world |
| Pāramārthika, ultimate truth | Nirguṇa Brahman is non-dual, without limiting attributes or a final subject-object distinction |

The empirical distinction among God, individual self and world functions within ignorance and
ordinary experience. At the highest level, Brahman alone is ultimately real and the distinction
is sublated by knowledge. It is therefore misleading either to equate Īśvara straightforwardly
with the individual illusion or to treat the empirical creator as a second ultimate reality.

### Approximate textual timeline

| Period | Development |
|---|---|
| Early centuries BCE/CE | Foundational Nyāya and Sāṃkhya sūtra traditions develop; exact dating is debated |
| Around 4th century CE | Īśvarakṛṣṇa's *Sāṃkhyakārikā* gives the classical Sāṃkhya system |
| Around 8th century CE | Śaṅkara systematises non-dual Advaita through commentaries |
| Around 10th-11th century CE | Udayana develops influential Nyāya arguments for God's existence |

### Rapid-recall table

| School | Material basis | God's position | Recall phrase |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika | Eternal atoms | Intelligent arranger and efficient cause | **Atoms are material; God orders** |
| Classical Sāṃkhya | Prakṛti | Creator not required | **Prakṛti evolves; puruṣa observes** |
| Advaita | Māyā-dependent empirical world | Īśvara empirically valid; nirguṇa Brahman ultimate | **Īśvara below; Brahman beyond** |

### UPSC trap

Do not collapse three different questions: what constitutes the world, what initiates or orders
it, and what is ultimately real. Each school distributes these roles differently.
