# MCQ 002 — Jain Epistemological Doctrines

**Area:** Indian Philosophy - Jainism  
**Difficulty:** Medium  
**Status:** Completed

## Question

With reference to Jain philosophy, consider the following statements:

1. Anekāntavāda holds that reality has multiple aspects and cannot be captured exhaustively by a
   single, unqualified judgment.
2. Syādvāda expresses judgments conditionally and is developed through a sevenfold scheme of
   predication.
3. Nayavāda explains that an ordinary judgment represents a particular partial standpoint rather
   than the whole of reality.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. 1 and 2 only  
B. 1, 2 and 3  
C. 2 and 3 only  
D. 1 and 3 only

## Learner response

**B. 1, 2 and 3**

## Evaluation and explanation

**Result:** Correct  
**Correct answer:** B. 1, 2 and 3

### Statement analysis

1. **Correct.** Anekāntavāda is the doctrine of many-sided reality. A real object possesses
   indefinitely many characteristics and relations, so an ordinary finite judgment cannot state
   the whole truth about it at once.
2. **Correct.** Syādvāda is the doctrine of conditioned predication. The qualifier `syāt` means
   "in some respect", "from a certain standpoint", or "under certain conditions"; it does not mean
   careless doubt. Its sevenfold formulation is called saptabhaṅgī.
3. **Correct.** Nayavāda analyses the limited standpoint from which a judgment is made. A `naya`
   is a partial but potentially valid perspective when it is not falsely treated as exhaustive.

### Relationship among the doctrines

```
Many-sided reality (anekāntavāda)
              ↓
Known through limited standpoints (nayavāda)
              ↓
Expressed through qualified propositions (syādvāda)
```

They are related but not interchangeable: anekāntavāda concerns the character of reality,
nayavāda concerns epistemic standpoints, and syādvāda concerns the qualified expression of
judgments.

### Sevenfold predication

The seven modes combine affirmation, negation and inexpressibility: in some respect it is; it is
not; it is and is not; it is inexpressible; it is and is inexpressible; it is not and is
inexpressible; and it is, is not, and is inexpressible. The predicates apply under different
conditions, times, relations or standpoints, so Jainism is not asserting a flat contradiction.

### Concrete example

A clay pot exists as a pot in its present form, does not exist as a cloth, and did not exist as a
pot before its production. Qualified predication prevents one context-bound assertion from being
mistaken for an absolute description.

### UPSC trap

Syādvāda is often misrepresented as scepticism or the claim that every proposition is equally
true. Jainism instead defends contextual and qualified truth while rejecting one-sided
absolutism (ekāntavāda).
