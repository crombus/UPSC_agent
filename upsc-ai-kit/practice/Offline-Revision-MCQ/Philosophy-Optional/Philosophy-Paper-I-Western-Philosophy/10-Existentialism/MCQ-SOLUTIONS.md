## MCQ 1

When Existentialism is treated as a philosophical family, which classification preserves the projects of Kierkegaard, Sartre and Heidegger?

A. Kierkegaard develops religious inwardness, Sartre develops atheistic freedom, and Heidegger pursues fundamental ontology.
B. Kierkegaard develops atheistic freedom, Sartre pursues fundamental ontology, and Heidegger defends religious inwardness.
C. All three thinkers defend a single doctrine, with differences caused merely by their different philosophical vocabularies.
D. Kierkegaard and Heidegger share Sartre's humanism, while Sartre denies freedom altogether.

**Answer: A.**

**Option explanations:**

- **A:** Correct proposition: “Kierkegaard develops religious inwardness, Sartre develops atheistic freedom, and Heidegger pursues fundamental ontology.” Rationale: The family resemblance concerns concrete existence, but Kierkegaard's God-relation, Sartre's atheistic freedom and Heidegger's Being-question remain distinct.
- **B:** False proposition: “Kierkegaard develops atheistic freedom, Sartre pursues fundamental ontology, and Heidegger defends religious inwardness.” Correct replacement: “Kierkegaard develops religious inwardness, Sartre develops atheistic freedom, and Heidegger pursues fundamental ontology.” Rationale: Atheistic freedom is Sartrean, fundamental ontology Heideggerian, and religious inwardness Kierkegaardian; this option permutes all three owners. Restore each doctrine to its distinct project.
- **C:** False proposition: “All three thinkers defend a single doctrine, with differences caused merely by their different philosophical vocabularies.” Correct replacement: “Kierkegaard develops religious inwardness, Sartre develops atheistic freedom, and Heidegger pursues fundamental ontology.” Rationale: A family resemblance does not make these projects one doctrine. Kierkegaard's God-relation, Sartre's atheistic freedom, and Heidegger's Being-question retain different aims and methods.
- **D:** False proposition: “Kierkegaard and Heidegger share Sartre's humanism, while Sartre denies freedom altogether.” Correct replacement: “Kierkegaard develops religious inwardness, Sartre develops atheistic freedom, and Heidegger pursues fundamental ontology.” Rationale: Sartrean humanism neither governs Kierkegaard's theology nor Heidegger's ontology, and Sartre makes freedom inescapable rather than denying it. The three projects must remain differentiated.

- **C:** False proposition: “Heidegger treats humanism as mistaken because it gives insufficient attention to empirical psychology and biology.” Correct replacement: “Heidegger says Sartre's reversal remains metaphysical because it keeps human subjectivity central.” Rationale: The Letter on Humanism is not a plea for more psychology or biology. Heidegger challenges the inherited essence/existence framework and its anthropocentric orientation.
- **D:** Correct proposition: “Heidegger says Sartre's reversal remains metaphysical because it keeps human subjectivity central.” Rationale: Heidegger argues that merely reversing essence and existence retains the metaphysics of subjectivity; his concern is the disclosure of Being, not human self-production.
