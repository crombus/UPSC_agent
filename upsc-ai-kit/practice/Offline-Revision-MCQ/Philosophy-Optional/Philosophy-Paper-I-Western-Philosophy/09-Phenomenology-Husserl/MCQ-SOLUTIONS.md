## MCQ 1

In Husserl's 1911 programme of philosophy as a rigorous science, what kind of discipline should phenomenology become?

A. Phenomenology should become an empirical psychology that classifies observable mental states.
B. Phenomenology should become a metaphysical proof establishing the existence of a divine guarantor.
C. Phenomenology should become a historical survey comparing successive philosophical worldviews.
D. Phenomenology should become a presuppositionless rigorous science describing structures of consciousness.

**Answer: D.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** False proposition: “Phenomenology should become an empirical psychology that classifies observable mental states.” Correct replacement: “Phenomenology should become a presuppositionless rigorous science describing structures of consciousness.” Rationale: Empirical psychology studies worldly mental events and therefore retains naturalistic presuppositions that phenomenology must examine.
- **B:** False proposition: “Phenomenology should become a metaphysical proof establishing the existence of a divine guarantor.” Correct replacement: “Phenomenology should become a presuppositionless rigorous science describing structures of consciousness.” Rationale: A divine guarantee belongs to Cartesian reconstruction, not Husserl's descriptive programme of intentional givenness.
- **C:** False proposition: “Phenomenology should become a historical survey comparing successive philosophical worldviews.” Correct replacement: “Phenomenology should become a presuppositionless rigorous science describing structures of consciousness.” Rationale: Historicism records changing outlooks but cannot provide the eidetic validity claimed by rigorous phenomenology.
- **D:** Correct proposition: “Phenomenology should become a presuppositionless rigorous science describing structures of consciousness.” Rationale: Husserl's rigorous-science programme opposes naturalism and historicism by seeking disciplined, presupposition-critical description of consciousness.

A. Cartesian Meditations first appeared in German in 1931 and was translated into French afterward.
B. Ideas II and Ideas III were published together with Ideas I as a completed trilogy in 1913.
C. Ideas I appeared in 1913, while the Crisis appeared partly in 1936 and completely in 1954.
D. Husserl prepared the time-consciousness lectures for publication without editorial intervention in 1928.

**Answer: C.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** False proposition: “Cartesian Meditations first appeared in German in 1931 and was translated into French afterward.” Correct replacement: “Ideas I appeared in 1913, while the Crisis appeared partly in 1936 and completely in 1954.” Rationale: Cartesian Meditations appeared first in French in 1931, with the German text published posthumously.
- **B:** False proposition: “Ideas II and Ideas III were published together with Ideas I as a completed trilogy in 1913.” Correct replacement: “Ideas I appeared in 1913, while the Crisis appeared partly in 1936 and completely in 1954.” Rationale: Only Ideas I appeared in 1913, while the remaining Ideas manuscripts were published later.
- **C:** Correct proposition: “Ideas I appeared in 1913, while the Crisis appeared partly in 1936 and completely in 1954.” Rationale: The chronology distinguishes Husserl's lifetime publications from posthumous editorial completion without conflating different works.
- **D:** False proposition: “Husserl prepared the time-consciousness lectures for publication without editorial intervention in 1928.” Correct replacement: “Ideas I appeared in 1913, while the Crisis appeared partly in 1936 and completely in 1954.” Rationale: Edith Stein edited the time manuscripts and Heidegger published them, so intervention was substantial.

**Examiner trap 52:** Confusing the tested referents in Husserl, major works, Ideas I, Crisis.

## MCQ 53

Why is the claim 'constitution is not fabrication' important when assessing Husserl's transcendental idealism?

A. Constitution makes validity private to the empirical experience that psychologically produces each meaning.
B. Constitution discloses sense in subjectivity without claiming that an empirical mind creates truths or objects.
C. Constitution turns transcendental idealism into Berkeley's doctrine that finite perceiving produces existing things.
D. Constitution removes intersubjectivity because one transcendental ego privately guarantees objective validity.

**Answer: B.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** False proposition: “Constitution makes validity private to the empirical experience that psychologically produces each meaning.” Correct replacement: “Constitution discloses sense in subjectivity without claiming that an empirical mind creates truths or objects.” Rationale: Empirical production would reduce validity to contingent mental causation and revive the psychologism rejected.
- **B:** Correct proposition: “Constitution discloses sense in subjectivity without claiming that an empirical mind creates truths or objects.” Rationale: The distinction blocks crude psychologism while leaving open the subtler dispute about transcendental dependence.
- **C:** False proposition: “Constitution turns transcendental idealism into Berkeley's doctrine that finite perceiving produces existing things.” Correct replacement: “Constitution discloses sense in subjectivity without claiming that an empirical mind creates truths or objects.” Rationale: Husserl analyses conditions of sense rather than making finite perception the efficient cause of objects.
- **D:** False proposition: “Constitution removes intersubjectivity because one transcendental ego privately guarantees objective validity.” Correct replacement: “Constitution discloses sense in subjectivity without claiming that an empirical mind creates truths or objects.” Rationale: Objective validity requires transcendental intersubjectivity and cannot rest on one private ego's guarantee.

**Examiner trap 53:** Confusing the tested referents in constitution is not fabrication, Husserl, transcendental idealism.

**Repair action:** Restate the operative distinction and explain why the nearest rival proposition fails.
