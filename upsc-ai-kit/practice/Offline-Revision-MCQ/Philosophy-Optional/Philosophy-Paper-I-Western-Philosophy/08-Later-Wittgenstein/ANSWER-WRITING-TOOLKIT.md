## 1. 2018 Q1(c) · 10 marks

**Question:** How does the notion of 'family resemblance' help Wittgenstein to uphold that 'Language is a game'? Discuss.

### Verified metadata and demand decoding

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Provenance | Exact wording and marks verified in the repository-controlled PYQ ledger |
| Demand decoding | Answer the directive and its internal relation, not a generic note on Later Wittgenstein |
| Ownership | Directly owned here and counted once |
| Timed band | 150–200 words |

### Timed independent model answer

Family resemblance explains how Wittgenstein can compare language with games without claiming that every linguistic activity shares one essence. When we inspect board-games, ball-games, card-games and solitary games, no single feature such as competition, amusement or skill occurs in all. Instead, there is a network of overlapping and criss-crossing similarities, like resemblances among family members.

Language displays the same pattern. Reporting, ordering, praying, joking, calculating and avowing are language-games woven into different activities. Their unity is not one referential or pictorial function. The expression “language is a game” therefore redirects inquiry from an abstract essence to trained moves, circumstances and standards within practices. “Don't think, but look” names this descriptive method.

The claim does not make classification arbitrary. Central cases, examples, correction and shared practice guide applications even where boundaries remain open. Family resemblance thus supports language-games by replacing necessary and sufficient conditions with a disciplined but non-essentialist account of conceptual unity.

**Measured model-answer words:** 152

**Explicit qualification / criticism:** A critic may argue that resemblance is unlimited, since any two things resemble in some respect. The reply is that established practice, not bare similarity, determines which resemblances matter.

## 2. 2019 Q3(b) · 15 marks

**Question:** Show how Wittgenstein’s critique of solipsism culminates in the critique of private language.

### Timed independent model answer

Later Wittgenstein does not defeat solipsism by proving an external world. He asks whether the solipsist can give sense to the claim that only private experience is real. To formulate that privileged inner world, the solipsist requires words whose meanings are fixed by experiences accessible in principle only to the individual.

Rule-following supplies the first pressure. A meaningful sign must distinguish correct application from merely seeming correct. An interpretation cannot provide the final standard because it invites another interpretation. Standards are displayed in a trained practice of correction and continuation.

The diary case at section 258 sharpens the point. A diarist associates “S” with a private sensation and later records its recurrence. Yet the present impression is both application and alleged test. If whatever seems right is right, there is no usable contrast between right and wrong. Memory cannot validate itself within the same private ceremony.

The beetle analogy at section 293 reinforces, but does not duplicate, the argument. If everyone calls an inaccessible box-content “beetle,” the hidden object has no role in the shared grammar and may vary or be absent. Public use carries meaning.

Thus a world grounded solely in private objects cannot be stated in the language it requires. The culmination is grammatical: private semantic foundations collapse, not inner experience. Solitude remains possible where a person uses stable, learnable public-type criteria.

**Explicit qualification / criticism:** The argument may not prove that actual community agreement is necessary. Its strongest conclusion is narrower: present private seeming cannot by itself institute a norm of correctness.

## 3. 2020 Q4(c) · 15 marks

**Question:** “But could we also imagine a language in which a person could write down or give vocal expression to his inner experiences—his feelings, moods and the rest—for his private use?” Critically discuss the answer offered by Wittgenstein to this question.

### Verified metadata and demand decoding

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Provenance | Exact wording and marks verified in the repository-controlled PYQ ledger |
| Demand decoding | Answer the directive and its internal relation, not a generic note on Later Wittgenstein |
| Ownership | Directly owned here and counted once |
| Timed band | 250–300 words |

### Timed independent model answer

Wittgenstein's answer is that an essentially private language cannot be established. “Private” here does not mean a secret code, solitary speech or an unshared natural language. It means signs whose meanings are fixed by sensations that only one speaker could in principle identify.

The core argument concerns correctness. In the diary example, the speaker associates “S” with a sensation and records later recurrences. A rule for “S” requires a distinction between applying it correctly and merely believing it has been applied correctly. But private ostension supplies no independent standard of sameness. If present seeming is the only court of appeal, “whatever seems right is right,” which means that talk of right has lost its function.

The beetle-in-a-box analogy shows that an inaccessible private object cannot determine a public word's grammar. Its content may differ or be absent while the word continues to work. Sensation language is instead connected with outward, defeasible criteria and learned expressive practices. First-person “I am in pain” normally functions as an avowal, not an inference from observed behaviour.

The strongest objection is behaviourism: does public criteria reduce pain to pain-behaviour? Wittgenstein's reply is that criteria are defeasible. Pretence and suppression allow pain and behaviour to diverge. He denies the private object's foundational semantic role, not sensations.

The argument convincingly blocks private ostension as a complete foundation. It is less decisive if expanded into the stronger thesis that an actual community must inspect every rule application.

The answer should therefore distinguish the doctrine's central target from stronger conclusions that its argument does not establish.

**Measured model-answer words:** 257

**Explicit qualification / criticism:** A solitary speaker using an inherited or repeatable technique remains possible; logical privacy, not physical isolation, is the target.

**Why this earns marks:** It reconstructs the named doctrine, uses exact technical distinctions, answers the directive, includes a criticism or qualification and reaches a defended judgement.

## 4. 2022 Q1(c) · 10 marks

**Question:** Establish the tenability of later Wittgenstein’s motion of language as form of life.

### Verified metadata and demand decoding

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Provenance | Exact wording and marks verified in the repository-controlled PYQ ledger |
| Demand decoding | Answer the directive and its internal relation, not a generic note on Later Wittgenstein |
| Ownership | Directly owned here and counted once |
| Timed band | 150–200 words |

### Timed independent model answer

The official wording prints “motion,” evidently for “notion.” The intended claim is defensible: language is intelligible only within a form of life, a background of activity, natural reaction, training and shared judgment.

Section 19 states that to imagine a language is to imagine a form of life. The builders' calls work because ordering, fetching and constructing form one practice. Agreement is therefore not primarily agreement in opinions; it is practical agreement that makes particular disputes, corrections and innovations possible. The speaking-lion remark adds that shared vocabulary alone may fail where activities and reactions are radically different. At justificatory bedrock, explanation ends in “This is simply what I do.”

This view explains why meaning cannot be grounded in an isolated idea or a self-interpreting rule. It also avoids pure conventionalism because bodily reactions and natural history constrain practices.

Its limitation is under-definition: “form of life” can be read biologically, culturally or as both. If made wholly cultural it risks relativism; if made wholly natural it neglects historical change. Properly qualified, it is a tenable account of the enabling background of language, not a complete social theory.

**Measured model-answer words:** 185

**Explicit qualification / criticism:** Tenability requires resisting both extremes: forms of life are neither majority opinions nor an exhaustive biological explanation.

## 5. 2023 Q4(c) · 15 marks

**Question:** What were the main reasons that led Wittgenstein to shift from picture-theory of meaning to use-theory of meaning? Critically discuss.

### Verified metadata and demand decoding

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Provenance | Exact wording and marks verified in the repository-controlled PYQ ledger |
| Demand decoding | Answer the directive and its internal relation, not a generic note on Later Wittgenstein |
| Ownership | Directly owned here and counted once |
| Timed band | 250–300 words |

### Timed independent model answer

The shift arose from internal difficulties in the Tractatus and a broader change in philosophical method. The early view explained factual propositions as pictures of possible facts sharing logical form. Analysis was expected to reach simple names and logically independent elementary propositions.

First, colour exclusion created internal pressure. “This patch is red all over” excludes “this patch is green all over,” although apparently elementary propositions were meant to be independent. Second, the Augustinian naming model was too narrow. It fits some concrete nouns but not “if,” “five,” questions, commands, jokes or avowals. Third, ostension proved unable to fix its own dimension of application; pointing to two red squares does not say whether colour, shape, number or material is taught.

The later positive turn is qualified. Section 43 says that for a large class of cases, though not all, a word's meaning is its use in language. Words operate like diverse tools; understanding is mastery of a technique. Language-games are woven into activities and sustained by forms of life, training and correction. Philosophy consequently describes grammar and offers perspicuous reminders rather than constructing one ideal calculus.

The shift gains fidelity to linguistic diversity and explains norm-sensitive practices beyond factual representation. Its cost is reduced theoretical unity. “Use” may seem circular unless rule-following and criteria explain correctness, and therapeutic description invites the charge of quietism.

The best verdict is not that picture and use are rival exhaustive theories. Later Wittgenstein rejects the demand for one essence while preserving the aim of philosophical clarification.

**Measured model-answer words:** 251

**Explicit qualification / criticism:** Colour exclusion is an important pressure rather than a single conclusive cause; the semantic and methodological criticisms complete the explanation.

## 6. 2024 Q1(d) · 10 marks

**Question:** Why does later Wittgenstein think that there cannot be a language that only one person can speak — a language that is essentially private? Discuss.

### Verified metadata and demand decoding

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Provenance | Exact wording and marks verified in the repository-controlled PYQ ledger |
| Demand decoding | Answer the directive and its internal relation, not a generic note on Later Wittgenstein |
| Ownership | Directly owned here and counted once |
| Timed band | 150–200 words |

### Timed independent model answer

An essentially private language would contain signs for sensations accessible in principle only to one speaker. It is not a secret code, private diary in an ordinary language or solitary use of an inherited technique.

Wittgenstein argues that a meaningful sign needs a rule distinguishing correct from incorrect application. In the diary case, a speaker associates “S” with a private sensation and later marks its recurrence. No standard independent of present seeming establishes that the same sensation has returned. If whatever seems right is right, the distinction between being right and thinking oneself right disappears; therefore no rule has been constituted.

The beetle-in-a-box analogy supports this result. Where the public use of “beetle” proceeds without access to anyone's box, the hidden item cannot determine the word's grammar and may even be absent. Sensation words instead acquire sense within practices of expression, response and defeasible outward criteria.

The conclusion does not deny sensations or require another person to monitor every utterance. A solitary person can use public-type techniques. What is impossible is a language whose correctness is grounded solely in logically private ostension.

**Explicit qualification / criticism:** The argument establishes a semantic limit on private grounding, not an empirical claim that isolated people cannot speak.

## 7. 2025 Q3(a) · 20 marks

**Question:** “We should look not to an ideal language which derives its meaning from facts and has a precise ‘logical structure but empirically, to the ways in which languages are actually used.” Explain the transition from early views of Wittgenstein to his later views on language and meaning with reference to this statement.

### Verified metadata and demand decoding

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Provenance | Exact wording and marks verified in the repository-controlled PYQ ledger |
| Demand decoding | Answer the directive and its internal relation, not a generic note on Later Wittgenstein |
| Ownership | Directly owned here and counted once |
| Timed band | 340–400 words |

### Timed independent model answer

The printed statement contains an unmatched quotation mark before “logical structure,” but its contrast is clear. It places the early search for one logical architecture of factual sense against the later examination of actual linguistic practices.

In the Tractatus, a proposition is meaningful by picturing a possible fact. Proposition and fact share logical form; analysis was expected to terminate in simple names and independent elementary propositions. Philosophy clarified the limits of what could be said. This was not merely a proposal to replace bad everyday sentences, since the Tractatus also regarded ordinary propositions as logically ordered. The decisive commitment was to one general form and a depth analysis beneath their surface.

Several pressures produced the transition. Colour exclusion challenged elementary independence. The Augustinian picture in which every word names an object could not explain numerals, logical particles, questions, orders, jokes or sensation avowals. Ostensive definition also required prior training to select colour, shape, number or another dimension. Wittgenstein increasingly treated the craving for crystalline logical purity as a philosophical imposition.

The Investigations therefore asks how expressions are actually used. Section 43 cautiously connects meaning with use for a large class of cases. Words are tools with diverse functions; understanding is mastery of a technique. Language-games combine signs with activities, while family resemblance replaces one common essence. Training, forms of life and public-type criteria explain rule-following.

The methodological consequence is therapy. Philosophy compares cases, gives reminders and constructs perspicuous representations so that language removed from its ordinary work returns home. It dissolves rather than theoretically solves many inherited puzzles.

The later view handles linguistic diversity and private-language problems more convincingly, but it sacrifices systematic explanation and faces questions about normativity and quietism. The transition is thus from one general logical account of factual sense to plural, practice-grounded grammatical investigation, not from logic to arbitrary usage.

The answer should therefore distinguish the doctrine's central target from stronger conclusions that its argument does not establish.

## 8. 2026 Q3(c) · 15 marks

**Question:** Can words be viewed as the ideas in the mind of the person who uses them? Answer with reference to the philosophy of later Wittgenstein.

### Verified metadata and demand decoding

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Provenance | Exact wording and marks verified in the repository-controlled PYQ ledger |
| Demand decoding | Answer the directive and its internal relation, not a generic note on Later Wittgenstein |
| Ownership | Directly owned here and counted once |
| Timed band | 250–300 words |

### Timed independent model answer

Later Wittgenstein would reject the general equation of words with ideas in a speaker's mind. An image, feeling or intention may accompany speech, but such an episode neither constitutes the word's meaning nor supplies its standard of correct use.

The Augustinian picture encourages mentalism by treating words as names and ideas as their private objects. Yet expressions such as “if,” “not,” “five,” requests and avowals have no uniform inner item that they name. Even with concrete words, different speakers may associate different images while using the expression identically, and one speaker may retain the same competence while imagery changes.

Section 43 redirects inquiry to use within language. Understanding is mastery of a technique: the ability to continue, respond and accept correction in relevant cases. Rule-following deepens the point. A private interpretation cannot fix all future applications because any interpretation can itself be interpreted differently. Normativity is exhibited in practice.

The diary argument then blocks private ideas as semantic foundations. If “S” is tied only to an inward sensation, no independent criterion distinguishes correct recurrence from present seeming. The beetle analogy similarly shows that an inaccessible object drops out of the public grammar. First-person avowals preserve inner life without making it an inner-object report.

This is not behaviourism. Outward criteria are defeasible, and Wittgenstein does not deny images or sensations. He denies that their private occurrence explains meaning. Words are best viewed as instruments in language-games, while ideas are possible accompaniments whose identity and relevance are themselves described through public grammar.

**Measured model-answer words:** 250

**Explicit qualification / criticism:** Mental episodes can causally prompt expression and enrich thought; the denial concerns their alleged role as self-authenticating semantic standards.

**Why this earns marks:** It reconstructs the named doctrine, uses exact technical distinctions, answers the directive, includes a criticism or qualification and reaches a defended judgement.

## Original 1 · 10 marks

**Question:** Explain why ostensive definition cannot by itself fix meaning, and relate this failure to meaning as use.

### Demand decoding

Build a direct thesis, reconstruct the mechanism, test the strongest objection and close with a qualified judgement.

### Timed model answer

Pointing is not a self-interpreting semantic ceremony. If a teacher indicates two red squares and says “red,” the learner may attend to colour, shape, number, material, location or the teacher's gesture. The sample does not select which dimension should govern future applications.

Ostensive definition succeeds only within prior training. Contrasting samples, correction and repeated use teach the learner how to continue. A colour chart then functions as a standard inside a method of representation rather than as an object carrying its own interpretation.

This supports the qualified claim in section 43 that, for a large class of cases, meaning is use in language. Use is not frequency or personal convenience; it is a norm-governed role in a language-game. Reference remains one legitimate role, but it cannot explain the conditions under which pointing counts as teaching reference.

The residual question is whether appeal to practice is circular. Wittgenstein treats trained practice as bedrock rather than offering another hidden mechanism. The account explains how ostension works while refusing the demand for a final self-interpreting sign.

**Measured model-answer words:** 173

**Explicit qualification / criticism:** The account does not deny ostensive teaching; it denies that pointing operates without grammar, attention and training.

## Original 2 · 15 marks

**Question:** How do language-games and forms of life support rule-following without reducing correctness to social conformity?

### Demand decoding

Build a direct thesis, reconstruct the mechanism, test the strongest objection and close with a qualified judgement.

### Timed model answer

A language-game is language interwoven with activity, including participants, purposes, training and standards. In the builders' example, “Slab!” works because an order, a trained response and a building task form one practice. A form of life is the wider background of action and natural reaction that makes such moves intelligible.

These notions answer the rule-following problem. No interpretation can determine every future application of a rule, because the interpretation requires application and generates regress. Grasp of a rule is instead exhibited in obeying, correcting and going against it in actual cases. Finite instruction succeeds because learners share techniques and judgments that extend beyond the examples.

Correctness is not thereby whatever a majority happens to do. A majority may misunderstand a calculation, and an innovator may be right. The relevant agreement is not a vote on each proposition but the deeper practice that gives “calculation,” “correction” and “mistake” their roles. Natural constraints, purposes and internal relations among moves limit acceptable continuation.

The view nevertheless faces pressure. If every standard is internal to practice, radical criticism and conceptual change may appear difficult to explain. Wittgenstein can answer that practices contain contestation and evolve through uptake, but he supplies no complete theory of legitimate reform.

Thus language-games and forms of life ground normativity in shared, teachable practices without reducing truth or correctness to numerical conformity.

## Original 3 · 20 marks

**Question:** Does the critique of private language complete Wittgenstein's transition from picture theory to the later conception of language? Critically examine.

### Demand decoding

Build a direct thesis, reconstruct the mechanism, test the strongest objection and close with a qualified judgement.

### Timed model answer

The critique of private language is not an isolated argument about diaries. It is the culminating test of the later claim that meaning is sustained by use, rule-following and criteria rather than by a word's relation to a self-authenticating object.

The Tractatus centred factual sense on picturing: names correlate with objects and propositions share logical form with possible facts. The Investigations attacks the temptation to generalize this name-object model. Words perform many jobs, ostension requires training, concepts exhibit family resemblance and language-games are woven into forms of life.

Rule-following supplies the normative bridge. No interpretation fixes all future applications, since another interpretation would be needed. A rule is grasped in trained practices of obeying, correcting and continuing. The proposed private diary then fails. A sign “S” tied solely to an inward sensation provides no standard independent of present seeming. Where seeming right is being right, the correct-incorrect distinction disappears. The beetle analogy adds that an inaccessible object does no work in the public grammar of a sensation word.

In this sense the argument completes the transition. It demonstrates, at the hardest case, why neither an external simple object nor an internal idea can carry meaning independently of use. Sensation language rests on expression, outward criteria and first-person avowal, not inner naming.

However, “completion” should not mean a deductive system. The argument depends on a contested account of normativity, and critics ask whether a solitary individual can preserve standards through memory and stable practice. The behaviourism charge also arises, though defeasible criteria allow pain and pain-behaviour to diverge.

The private-language critique therefore completes the transition architecturally: it unites use, games, rules and therapy against private foundations. It does not end debate about community, inner experience or conceptual innovation.
