## MCQ 1

In the syllabus item "Quine and Strawson", which pairing correctly matches each philosopher to his project?

A. Quine - critique of empiricism, and Strawson - theory of basic particulars and persons.
B. Quine - theory of basic particulars and persons, and Strawson - critique of empiricism.
C. Both jointly propose a single naturalized theory of persons.
D. Quine develops descriptive metaphysics, and Strawson replaces epistemology with naturalized psychology.

**Answer: A.**

**Option explanations:**

- **A:** Correct proposition: “Quine - critique of empiricism, and Strawson - theory of basic particulars and persons.” Rationale: Quine attacks empiricism from within ("Two Dogmas"); Strawson develops a descriptive metaphysics of basic particulars and persons. The two projects are kept distinct.
- **B:** False proposition: “Quine - theory of basic particulars and persons, and Strawson - critique of empiricism.” Correct replacement: “Quine - critique of empiricism, and Strawson - theory of basic particulars and persons.” Rationale: This reverses the philosophers' projects: basic particulars and persons belong to Strawson's descriptive metaphysics, while the critique of empiricism is Quine's project.
- **C:** False proposition: “Both jointly propose a single naturalized theory of persons.” Correct replacement: “Quine - critique of empiricism, and Strawson - theory of basic particulars and persons.” Rationale: This conflates two distinct programmes. Quine naturalizes inquiry and criticizes empiricism, whereas Strawson develops the primitive concept of a person within descriptive metaphysics; they offer no joint theory of persons.
- **D:** False proposition: “Quine develops descriptive metaphysics, and Strawson replaces epistemology with naturalized psychology.” Correct replacement: “Quine - critique of empiricism, and Strawson - theory of basic particulars and persons.” Rationale: The attribution is reversed. Descriptive metaphysics is Strawson's method, while replacing first philosophy with empirical psychology belongs to Quine's naturalized epistemology and wider critique of empiricism.

Neither project is principally an attack on Kant's defence of the synthetic a priori; both target empiricism, especially atomistic verification and the private-given model.

Quine challenges logical empiricism's dogmas, while Strawson rejects the private-given starting point of empiricist accounts.

Their convergence instead concerns empiricism: atomistic confirmation and the priority of private sense-data.

## MCQ 3

A. Both primarily use transcendental arguments to establish necessary conditions of objective experience.
B. Quine makes philosophy continuous with science, and Strawson practises descriptive metaphysics.
C. Both treat philosophy as continuous with empirical science and reject autonomous conceptual analysis.
D. Quine is a descriptive metaphysician, and Strawson is a naturalist.

**Answer: B.**

**Option explanations:**

Strawson preserves autonomous conceptual analysis by describing the structure of ordinary thought rather than reducing philosophy to empirical science.

Quine is the naturalist who makes philosophy continuous with science; Strawson is the descriptive metaphysician who contrasts description with revisionary reconstruction.

## MCQ 4

The claim "Quine reforms empiricism from within while Strawson rejects its starting point" means that:

A. Quine retains empirical constraint, unlike Strawson's rejection of the private given.
B. Both abandon empiricism as irredeemable and ground knowledge in rationalist first principles.
C. Both take private sense-data as the basic given.
D. Quine denies that experience is any check on belief.

**Answer: A.**

**Option explanations:**

- **A:** Correct proposition: “Quine retains empirical constraint, unlike Strawson's rejection of the private given.” Rationale: Quine's residue is "empiricism without the dogmas"; Strawson holds the given is public (bodies, persons), not private sense-data.
- **B:** False proposition: “Both abandon empiricism as irredeemable and ground knowledge in rationalist first principles.” Correct replacement: “Quine retains empirical constraint, unlike Strawson's rejection of the private given.” Rationale: The option overstates their break with empiricism and invents a rationalist foundation. Quine retains empirical constraint without the two dogmas, while Strawson replaces private sense-data with public bodies and persons.
- **C:** False proposition: “Both take private sense-data as the basic given.” Correct replacement: “Quine retains empirical constraint, unlike Strawson's rejection of the private given.” Rationale: This reverses Strawson's central objection and miscasts Quine. Strawson makes publicly identifiable bodies and persons basic, while Quine retains empiricism without granting private sense-data an atomistic foundational role.
- **D:** False proposition: “Quine denies that experience is any check on belief.” Correct replacement: “Quine retains empirical constraint, unlike Strawson's rejection of the private given.” Rationale: Quine does not remove experience from inquiry; that would abandon empiricism altogether. Experience constrains the web corporately, while Strawson's separate correction locates the given in public bodies and persons rather than private sense-data.

## MCQ 5

Quine's precise conclusion about the analytic/synthetic distinction is that:

A. There is no principled, non-circular criterion marking off a class of analytic truths.
B. No sentence has ever appeared analytic to any competent speaker.
C. All meaningful sentence is analytic once translated into canonical logical notation.
D. Analyticity is identical with logical truth and therefore needs no account of synonymy.

**Answer: A.**

**Option explanations:**

- **A:** Correct proposition: “There is no principled, non-circular criterion marking off a class of analytic truths.” Rationale: Quine denies a non-circular boundary, not the appearance of analyticity; logical truths are only one part of the alleged analytic class.
- **B:** False proposition: “No sentence has ever appeared analytic to any competent speaker.” Correct replacement: “There is no principled, non-circular criterion marking off a class of analytic truths.” Rationale: This overstates Quine's conclusion. He does not deny that competent speakers recognize familiar apparent examples; he denies a principled, non-circular boundary for the alleged analytic class.
- **C:** False proposition: “All meaningful sentence is analytic once translated into canonical logical notation.” Correct replacement: “There is no principled, non-circular criterion marking off a class of analytic truths.” Rationale: Regimentation does not turn every meaningful sentence into an analytic truth. Quine's complaint is that canonical notation supplies no non-circular criterion separating an alleged analytic class from synthetic statements.
- **D:** False proposition: “Analyticity is identical with logical truth and therefore needs no account of synonymy.” Correct replacement: “There is no principled, non-circular criterion marking off a class of analytic truths.” Rationale: This wrongly collapses analyticity into logical truth. The alleged analytic class also includes truths said to depend on synonymy, and Quine argues that synonymy itself lacks the required non-circular explanation.

## MCQ 6

Interchangeability salva veritate fails to define synonymy because:

A. It is explicitly defined in terms of definition, which is circular.
B. It cannot be expressed in any formal language.
C. It already presupposes translation indeterminacy, so synonymy cannot be compared across languages.
D. Coextensive terms interchange extensionally, and adding necessity presupposes analyticity.

**Answer: D.**

**Option explanations:**

- **A:** False proposition: “It is explicitly defined in terms of definition, which is circular.” Correct replacement: “Coextensive terms interchange extensionally, and adding necessity presupposes analyticity.” Rationale: This wrongly treats interchangeability as explicitly defined through definition. Its real problem is that coextensive terms substitute extensionally, while restricting substitution by necessity reintroduces analyticity.
- **B:** False proposition: “It cannot be expressed in any formal language.” Correct replacement: “Coextensive terms interchange extensionally, and adding necessity presupposes analyticity.” Rationale: The test can be expressed formally, so inexpressibility is not the objection. It fails because extensional interchangeability over-generates, and an intensional repair presupposes the analyticity under analysis.
- **C:** False proposition: “It already presupposes translation indeterminacy, so synonymy cannot be compared across languages.” Correct replacement: “Coextensive terms interchange extensionally, and adding necessity presupposes analyticity.” Rationale: This wrongly makes translation indeterminacy across languages the presupposition. Salva-veritate interchangeability fails within a language because coextension is too weak and necessity would presuppose analyticity.
- **D:** Correct proposition: “Coextensive terms interchange extensionally, and adding necessity presupposes analyticity.” Rationale: Extensional interchangeability over-generates; adding necessity to repair it reintroduces the very notion (analyticity) under analysis.

## MCQ 7

Quine regards the appeal to "semantic rules" in an artificial language as inadequate because such rules:

A. They generate contradictions whenever an artificial language classifies a sentence as analytic.
B. They merely stipulate which sentences count as analytic and leave analyticity itself unexplained.
C. They cannot be formulated for artificial languages without presupposing natural-language meaning.
D. They presuppose a completed radical-translation manual before analyticity can be stipulated.

**Answer: B.**

**Option explanations:**

- **A:** False proposition: “They generate contradictions whenever an artificial language classifies a sentence as analytic.” Correct replacement: “They merely stipulate which sentences count as analytic and leave analyticity itself unexplained.” Rationale: Quine does not claim that semantic rules generate contradictions. His objection is explanatory: listing analytic sentences by stipulation fixes an extension but leaves the general concept of analyticity unexplained.
- **B:** Correct proposition: “They merely stipulate which sentences count as analytic and leave analyticity itself unexplained.” Rationale: Listing analytic sentences by fiat specifies an extension, not the concept of analyticity.
- **C:** False proposition: “They cannot be formulated for artificial languages without presupposing natural-language meaning.” Correct replacement: “They merely stipulate which sentences count as analytic and leave analyticity itself unexplained.” Rationale: This confuses formulability with explanatory adequacy. Artificial languages can contain explicit semantic rules, but those stipulations do not explain what makes the listed sentences analytic.
- **D:** False proposition: “They presuppose a completed radical-translation manual before analyticity can be stipulated.” Correct replacement: “They merely stipulate which sentences count as analytic and leave analyticity itself unexplained.” Rationale: Radical translation is irrelevant to this objection and need not precede a stipulation. The failure is that semantic rules merely label sentences analytic without clarifying the concept of analyticity itself.

## MCQ 8

The Grice-Strawson reply "In Defence of a Dogma" (1956) contends that:

A. Quine conclusively proved the distinction to be unreal.
B. Synonymy can be defined without residue by unrestricted interchangeability salva veritate.
C. A distinction with agreed use and clear examples is not discredited merely by the absence of a reductive definition.
D. Analyticity is identical with empirical verifiability, so agreed usage cannot defend it.

**Answer: C.**

**Option explanations:**

- **A:** False proposition: “Quine conclusively proved the distinction to be unreal.” Correct replacement: “A distinction with agreed use and clear examples is not discredited merely by the absence of a reductive definition.” Rationale: This reverses the Grice-Strawson reply. They argue that stable ordinary use and agreed examples support the distinction despite Quine's demand for a reductive or behavioural boundary.
- **B:** False proposition: “Synonymy can be defined without residue by unrestricted interchangeability salva veritate.” Correct replacement: “A distinction with agreed use and clear examples is not discredited merely by the absence of a reductive definition.” Rationale: The reply does not claim that unrestricted substitution fully defines synonymy; coextensive expressions defeat that test. Grice and Strawson instead defend the distinction through established use and clear cases.
- **C:** Correct proposition: “A distinction with agreed use and clear examples is not discredited merely by the absence of a reductive definition.” Rationale: They defend the distinction's reality via ordinary use; Quine replies this lacks the principled behavioural boundary logical empiricism required.
- **D:** False proposition: “Analyticity is identical with empirical verifiability, so agreed usage cannot defend it.” Correct replacement: “A distinction with agreed use and clear examples is not discredited merely by the absence of a reductive definition.” Rationale: This conflates analyticity with the empiricist verification criterion. Grice and Strawson appeal to agreed linguistic practice to defend analytic-synthetic usage, while Quine still demands a principled boundary.

## MCQ 13

Given (H & A) -> O and not-O, what is the strongest conclusion licensed by modus tollens in the Duhem-Quine setting?

A. The focal hypothesis H alone is false, regardless of whether any auxiliary assumption is retained.
B. The auxiliary assumptions A alone are false, so the focal hypothesis H remains confirmed.
C. H and A cannot both be retained, and not-O does not show which conjunct must be revised.
D. Both H and A are confirmed because the failed observation leaves the theoretical package untouched.

**Answer: C.**

**Option explanations:**

- **A:** False proposition: “The focal hypothesis H alone is false, regardless of whether any auxiliary assumption is retained.” Correct replacement: “H and A cannot both be retained, and not-O does not show which conjunct must be revised.” Rationale: Inferring that the focal hypothesis alone is false commits the formal error of deriving not-H from not-(H and A). Modus tollens falsifies only the conjunction and leaves the revision underdetermined.
- **B:** False proposition: “The auxiliary assumptions A alone are false, so the focal hypothesis H remains confirmed.” Correct replacement: “H and A cannot both be retained, and not-O does not show which conjunct must be revised.” Rationale: The inference to not-A alone is no better than the inference to not-H alone. The failed prediction shows that H and A cannot both be retained, but leaves the locus of revision underdetermined.
- **C:** Correct proposition: “H and A cannot both be retained, and not-O does not show which conjunct must be revised.” Rationale: Modus tollens establishes only that the conjunction of hypothesis and auxiliaries is false. It leaves underdetermined whether H, A or some component within A should be revised.
- **D:** False proposition: “Both H and A are confirmed because the failed observation leaves the theoretical package untouched.” Correct replacement: “H and A cannot both be retained, and not-O does not show which conjunct must be revised.” Rationale: This reverses the logical consequence of the failed observation. Not-O falsifies the conjunction of H and A; it confirms neither conjunct, although it leaves underdetermined which one to abandon.

## MCQ 15

The Neptune and Vulcan episodes together illustrate that:

A. Revising an auxiliary is invariably the correct response to an anomaly.
