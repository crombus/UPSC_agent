## MCQ 1

The analytic revolt represented by Moore, Russell and the early Wittgenstein is most accurately described as:

A. a shared rejection of all metaphysics in favour of experimental psychology alone.
B. a revolt against idealist system-building through three distinct uses of analysis
C. a single doctrine that identifies common sense with logical atomism
D. a defence of British Idealism through more exact grammatical distinctions

**Answer: B.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Experimental psychology is not their common programme, and each retains philosophical commitments beyond experiment.
- **B:** Correct: the shared revolt uses analysis, but Moore protects ordinary knowledge, Russell reveals logical form, and Wittgenstein marks limits.
- **C:** Common sense is Moore's method, whereas logical atomism belongs to Russell and, differently, the early Wittgenstein.
- **D:** They oppose rather than defend the Bradleyan-Hegelian system that formed their immediate background.

**Examiner trap 1:** Flattening three programmes into one doctrine.

**Repair action:** State one distinct question and method for each thinker.

**Coverage mapping:** `C01`.

**PYQ linkage:** All; attribution control.

## MCQ 2

Which attribution is exact?

A. Moore—verification; Russell—family resemblance; Wittgenstein—common-sense certainty as their defining methods

C. Moore—logical constructions; Russell—diaphanous consciousness; Wittgenstein—ordinary names
D. Moore—negative facts; Russell—hinge propositions; Wittgenstein—proof by two hands

**Answer: B.**

**Option explanations:**

- **B:** Correct: these are the printed syllabus owners and their characteristic doctrines.
- **C:** Logical constructions are Russell's, diaphanous consciousness is Moore's, and ordinary-name analysis is Russell's.
- **D:** Negative facts are Russell's; hinge propositions are later Wittgenstein's; the hands proof is Moore's.

**Examiner trap 2:** Choosing by one familiar name while missing two misattributions.

**Repair action:** Use the owner table before recalling details.

## MCQ 3

Which chronology is correct?

A. Moore's 1903 refutation; Russell's 1905 descriptions; Russell's 1918 lectures; *Tractatus* 1921/22; Moore's 1925 defence and 1939 proof
B. Russell's 1918 lectures; Moore's 1903 refutation; *Tractatus* 1921/22; Russell's 1905 descriptions; Moore's 1939 proof
C. Moore's 1925 defence; Russell's 1905 descriptions; Moore's 1903 refutation; *Tractatus* 1921/22; Russell's 1918 lectures
D. *Tractatus* 1921/22; Russell's 1905 descriptions; Moore's 1939 proof; Moore's 1903 refutation; Russell's 1918 lectures

**Answer: A.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Correct: the sequence preserves the key publication dates and keeps Moore's defence distinct from his later proof.
- **B:** This places 1918 before 1903 and 1905, reversing the actual emergence of the analytic revolt.
- **C:** This starts with 1925 and places Moore's 1903 paper after Russell's 1905 paper.
- **D:** This begins with the *Tractatus* and badly scrambles the earlier Moore and Russell texts.

**Examiner trap 3:** Merging Moore's 1925 and 1939 papers or misdating the *Tractatus*.

**Repair action:** Memorise 1903–1905–1918–1921/22–1925–1939.

**Coverage mapping:** `C01`.

**PYQ linkage:** Provenance across all answers.

## MCQ 4

Moore's distinction between knowing and analysing permits him to claim that:

A. ordinary propositions remain uncertain until philosophers complete a final conceptual analysis of them
B. a proposition may be known with certainty even when its correct analysis remains disputed
C. correct analysis changes the truth-value of the proposition being analysed
D. common-sense knowledge is merely shorthand for majority agreement

**Answer: B.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Moore reverses this priority: knowledge of a truism need not await philosophical analysis.
- **B:** Correct: failure to analyse 'body' or 'external object' does not cancel knowledge that one has a body.
- **C:** Analysis clarifies content; it does not alter the truth-value of the proposition.
- **D:** Moorean common sense is a claim to knowledge, not an opinion poll or convention.

**Examiner trap 4:** Treating conceptual uncertainty as evidential uncertainty.

**Repair action:** Keep truth-known and analysis-known in separate columns.

**Coverage mapping:** `C02`.

**PYQ linkage:** 2019 Q3(c); 2021 Q3(a); 2024 Q1(c).

## MCQ 5

The paradox of analysis arises because a correct analysis appears:

A. either empirically verified, or metaphysically meaningless
B. either identical and uninformative, or informative and therefore not identical
C. either a logically proper name, or an incomplete symbol
D. either a tautology, or a contradiction in logical space

**Answer: B.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Verification and metaphysical meaning belong to a later positivist debate, not this paradox.
- **B:** Correct: sameness threatens informativeness, while difference threatens correctness.
- **C:** Proper names and incomplete symbols are Russellian categories, not the two horns of Moore's paradox.
- **D:** Tautology and contradiction are Tractarian limiting cases, not analyses of synonymy.

**Examiner trap 5:** Substituting a later philosophy-of-language distinction.

**Repair action:** Use analysandum/analysans and state both horns.

**Coverage mapping:** `C02`.

**PYQ linkage:** 2019 Q3(c); 2024 Q1(c).

## MCQ 6

Moore's truisms are best understood as:

A. claims certified solely by widespread public assent and established philosophical convention over time
B. analytic truths whose denial is formally self-contradictory in every possible case

**Answer: C.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Moore does not infer truth from popularity or sociological consensus.
- **B:** Many truisms are contingent world-claims, not analytic truths.
- **C:** Correct: comparative certainty, not polling, gives the truisms their dialectical role.
- **D:** Their status is not reduced to inductive scientific confirmation.

**Examiner trap 6:** Equating common sense with common belief.

## MCQ 7

Moore's central reply to scepticism is a burden reversal because:

A. he derives the external world from premises accepted by every radical sceptic
B. he shows that dreaming is logically impossible whenever hands are visible
C. he replaces perceptual knowledge with knowledge of private sense-data
D. he argues that no sceptical premise is more certain than the truism it denies

**Answer: D.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** The radical sceptic contests Moore's premises, so they are not neutral common ground.
- **B:** The hands argument does not establish the logical impossibility of dreaming.
- **C:** Moore defends ordinary external-world claims rather than retreating to private data.
- **D:** Correct: Moore compares certainties and refuses to surrender the better-known proposition.

**Examiner trap 7:** Mistaking burden reversal for a sceptic-neutral deduction.

**Repair action:** Ask which premise Moore says is better known.

## MCQ 8
