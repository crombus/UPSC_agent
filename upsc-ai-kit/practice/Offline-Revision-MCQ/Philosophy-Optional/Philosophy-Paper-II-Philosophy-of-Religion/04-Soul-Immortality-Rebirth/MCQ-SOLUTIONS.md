## MCQ 2

Which relation between substantial-soul immortality and rebirth is philosophically defensible?

A. It is neither sufficient by itself nor necessary.
B. It is sufficient by itself but not necessary.
C. It is necessary but insufficient by itself.
D. It is both necessary and sufficient.

**Answer:** A

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Correct. Immortality can identify a continuity bearer, but rebirth additionally needs a transmigration or re-embodiment doctrine; Buddhist causal continuity also shows that a substantial immortal soul is not necessary.
- **B:** Incorrect — it treats survival as if it already entailed another embodiment. The option claims “It is sufficient by itself but not necessary.” Immortality alone does not supply a rebirth mechanism.
- **C:** Incorrect — Buddhism rejects the alleged necessity, and immortality alone still does not establish re-embodiment. The option claims “It is necessary but insufficient by itself.”
- **D:** Incorrect — it overstates both directions. The option claims “It is both necessary and sufficient.” Buddhist rebirth denies necessity, while the need for an additional transmigration account denies sufficiency.

**Examiner trap:** confusing survival after death with entry into another embodiment.
**Repair action:** State both limits: an immortal soul may bear continuity only inside an additional transmigration or re-embodiment doctrine, and Buddhist continuity shows that such a soul is not necessary.
**Coverage mapping:** C03
**PYQ linkage:** Direct survival/identity transfer.
