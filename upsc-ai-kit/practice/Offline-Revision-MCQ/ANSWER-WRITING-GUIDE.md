# Offline Answer-Writing Guide

## Decode before writing

Underline the subject, directive, scope and required evaluation. `Explain/elucidate` requires derivation; `discuss` requires ordered arguments; `examine` requires testing assumptions and implications; `critically evaluate` requires objections, replies and a signed judgement; `differentiate` requires matched axes.

## Structures

| Marks | Working length | Structure |
|---:|---:|---|
| 10 | 150–200 words | definition/thesis; two core moves; example; qualification; verdict |
| 15 | 250–300 words | demand-led introduction; three arguments; objection/reply; balanced conclusion |
| 20 | 340–400 words | conceptual map; textual arguments; comparison/application; criticisms and replies; graded judgement |

Use headings only when they reveal the argument. Underline technical terms selectively. Add a small diagram when it compresses a relation, sequence or comparison. Never substitute quotations for analysis.

## Practice progression

Oral recall → introductions/conclusions/diagrams → five-minute skeletons → one complete 10-marker → one 15-marker → timed mixed block. Stop and reassess if writing causes increasing pain, numbness, tingling, swelling or weakness.

## Self-evaluation

Check demand coverage, conceptual accuracy, named evidence, causal logic, objection/reply balance, examples, and whether the conclusion actually answers the question. Compare structure before wording; model answers are learning aids, not scripts to reproduce.

## Solved-PYQ completeness rule

Every verified PYQ assigned to a topic must be included in that topic's
`ANSWER-WRITING-TOOLKIT.md` with its year, question number, marks, exact verified wording, demand
decoding, complete independent model answer, qualification or criticism, and an explanation of
why the structure earns marks. A question list, hint, or answer spine alone is not a solved PYQ.
Model answers are independent learner practice and are never presented as official UPSC keys.
