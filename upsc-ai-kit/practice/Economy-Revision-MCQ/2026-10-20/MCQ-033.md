# Economy 10 — Taxation, GST, Finance Commission and Fiscal Federalism — Revision 1, Question 1

**Learner answer:** B  
**Correct answer:** B  
**Result:** Correct

Which statement best distinguishes direct and indirect taxes?

A. The distinction depends only on whether the Union or a State collects the tax.
B. Direct taxes apply to income, profits or wealth-related bases; indirect taxes apply to transactions or consumption and may be shifted through prices.
C. Indirect taxes cannot be shifted because the statutory taxpayer always bears the final burden.
D. Direct taxes are always progressive, while indirect taxes are always regressive.

**Source:** `final-mcq/subjects/Economy/10-Taxation-GST-Finance-Commission-and-Fiscal-Federalism/revision-1/QUESTIONS.md` and `ANSWERS.md`, Q3; canonical Economy 10 Basic owner. Options reordered to vary the key position in this mixed session.

**Explanation**
- **A — Incorrect:** Union-versus-State collection is not what defines direct versus indirect taxation; both levels may administer different taxes.
- **B — Correct:** Direct taxes are imposed on income, profits or wealth-related bases. Indirect taxes apply to transactions or consumption, and their economic burden may be shifted through prices.
- **C — Incorrect:** The statutory person responsible for remitting a tax need not bear its final economic burden; sellers may pass an indirect tax to consumers, depending on market conditions.
- **D — Incorrect:** These are not absolute rules. Progressivity or regressivity depends on design and incidence; for example, exemptions and tax credits can affect a direct tax's progressivity.

**Relevant terms:** **Legal incidence** identifies who is legally liable to pay or remit the tax. **Economic incidence** identifies who ultimately bears its cost after price and behaviour adjustments.

**Examiner trap:** Do not confuse who remits a tax with who bears its economic burden, or treat progressivity as inherent in every tax category.
