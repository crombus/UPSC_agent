Which statement best describes a bond?

A. An ownership claim whose dividend is contractually fixed like interest
B. A derivative whose value depends only on an equity-market index
C. A creditor claim with contractual interest and principal obligations, generally ranking ahead of equity in repayment
D. A pooled vehicle that issues units and has no issuer-creditor relationship

**Source:** `final-mcq/subjects/Economy/08-Securities-Bonds-Equity-Derivatives-and-Investment-Funds/revision-1/QUESTIONS.md` and `ANSWERS.md`, Q1; `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/08_Securities-Bonds-Equity-Derivatives-and-Investment-Funds.md` §2. Options have been reordered for this mixed revision.

**Explanation**
- **A — Incorrect:** This describes neither equity nor a bond accurately. Equity is an ownership claim; dividends are not contractually fixed like bond interest.
- **B — Incorrect:** A derivative derives value from an underlying asset, rate or index; it is not a creditor claim promising principal repayment.
- **C — Correct:** A bond is a debt claim with contractual interest and principal obligations. Bondholders generally rank ahead of shareholders in repayment, though bonds still carry credit, interest-rate and market-price risks.
- **D — Incorrect:** This describes a pooled investment vehicle, such as a fund, not a bond issued by a borrower.

**Relevant terms:** The **issuer** borrows by issuing a bond; the **bondholder** is the creditor. Contractual cash flows distinguish debt claims from equity's residual return. “Generally ranking ahead” does not mean guaranteed repayment if the issuer defaults.

**Examiner trap:** Creditor priority over equity is not the same as risk-free repayment; also distinguish bonds from ownership claims, derivatives and pooled funds.
