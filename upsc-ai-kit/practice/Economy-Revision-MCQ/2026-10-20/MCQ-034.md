# Polity 10 — Amendment and Basic Structure — Diagnostic Question 2

**Learner answer:** B
**Correct answer:** B  
**Result:** Correct

Which statement best reflects the present constitutional position on Parliament's power to amend Fundamental Rights?

A. Parliament cannot amend any Fundamental Right under any circumstances.
B. Parliament may amend Fundamental Rights under Article 368, but it cannot damage or destroy the Constitution's basic structure.
C. Parliament may amend Fundamental Rights by an ordinary simple-majority law.
D. Parliament's amending power is unlimited and cannot be reviewed by courts.

**Source:** Canonical Polity topic 10 Basic owner; cross-check the 24th Amendment, *Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala* (1973), and *Minerva Mills v. Union of India* (1980).

**Explanation**
- **A — Incorrect:** Parliament can amend Fundamental Rights through the constitutional amendment procedure; the power is not categorically barred.
- **B — Correct:** Article 368 permits Parliament to amend any part of the Constitution, including Fundamental Rights, but the basic-structure doctrine limits that power.
- **C — Incorrect:** The ordinary simple-majority route is not the Article 368 procedure for amending Fundamental Rights.
- **D — Incorrect:** The amending power is limited and subject to judicial review; it is not unlimited.

**Relevant context:** In *Golaknath* (1967), the Supreme Court held that Parliament could not amend Fundamental Rights. The 24th Amendment (1971) responded; in *Kesavananda Bharati* (1973), the Court upheld Parliament's power to amend any part while holding that it could not damage the basic structure. *Minerva Mills* (1980) reaffirmed limited amending power and judicial review as basic features.

**Examiner trap:** Avoid both extremes: Fundamental Rights are amendable, but Article 368 does not give Parliament unlimited power.
