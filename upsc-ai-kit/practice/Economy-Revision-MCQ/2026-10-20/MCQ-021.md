# Polity 03 — Salient Features — Diagnostic Question 1

**Learner answer:** A  
**Correct answer:** A  
**Result:** Correct

Consider the following statements:

1. Constitutional supremacy means that public institutions derive their authority from the Constitution and remain subject to its limits.
2. The list of salient features is a closed constitutional schedule identical to the basic-structure doctrine.
3. A salient feature may emerge from the interaction of several provisions, institutions and values.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. 1 and 3 only  
B. 2 only  
C. 1, 2 and 3  
D. 2 and 3 only

**Source:** `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Polity/03_Salient-Features_Complete-Topic-Package.md`, Part I, §01. The same section distinguishes salient features from the basic-structure doctrine.

**Explanation**
- **Statement 1 — Correct:** Constitutional supremacy means that the Constitution is the source and limit of public authority. Parliament, the executive and other public institutions must act within their constitutional powers and constraints.
- **Statement 2 — Incorrect:** Salient features are not a closed constitutional schedule. They are a teaching classification, and lists can vary. They are also not identical to the basic-structure doctrine, which is a judicial limit on Parliament's amending power.
- **Statement 3 — Correct:** A feature can arise from the interaction of multiple provisions, institutions and values. For example, India's federal character is expressed through the distribution of powers and institutions, while also having centralising elements.

**Relevant terms:** *Constitutional supremacy* places the Constitution above ordinary exercises of public power. *Basic-structure doctrine* limits the amending power so that Parliament cannot destroy the Constitution's basic structure.

**Examiner trap:** Do not treat a broad constitutional characteristic as one discrete provision, or conflate the descriptive category “salient features” with the legal doctrine governing constitutional amendments.
