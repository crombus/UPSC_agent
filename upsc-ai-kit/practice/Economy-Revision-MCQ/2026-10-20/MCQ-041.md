Which statement correctly distinguishes a futures contract from an option contract?

A. A futures contract creates obligations for both parties; an option gives its buyer a right, not an obligation, while the writer has a contingent obligation.
B. Both futures and options give the buyer a right, not an obligation, while leaving the seller with no contractual duty.
C. A futures contract gives only its buyer a right, not an obligation; an option binds both buyer and writer symmetrically.
D. Neither contract creates a legal exposure until the underlying asset is physically delivered.

**Source:** `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/08_Securities-Bonds-Equity-Derivatives-and-Investment-Funds.md`, Essential definitions and Topic mechanism.

**Explanation**
- **A — Correct:** A futures contract places both parties under symmetric contractual obligations. An option gives the buyer a right, not an obligation; the writer must meet the option's terms if exercised.
- **B — Incorrect:** It treats both products as buyer-only rights and wrongly removes the option writer's obligation.
- **C — Incorrect:** It reverses the defining asymmetry: futures bind both sides, while an option buyer may choose whether to exercise.
- **D — Incorrect:** Derivative contracts create financial exposure without requiring physical delivery; many are cash-settled or closed out before expiry.

**Relevant terms:** A **future** is a standardised derivative with obligations for buyer and seller. An **option** gives its buyer a right against the writer's contingent obligation. Derivatives may be used to hedge an existing exposure or to speculate.

**Examiner trap:** Distinguish obligations at contract formation from whether physical delivery ultimately occurs; settlement method does not change the buyer's option right or the parties' futures obligations.
