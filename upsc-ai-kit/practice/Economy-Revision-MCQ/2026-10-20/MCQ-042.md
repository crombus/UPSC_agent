# Indian Philosophy — Buddhism — Diagnostic Question 1

**Learner answer:** A  
**Correct answer:** C  
**Result:** Incorrect

Which statement best explains the Buddhist Middle Path?

A. It is only a social compromise between the monastic and household ways of life.
B. It is the affirmation of a permanent self that avoids both ritualism and materialism.
C. It rejects both sensual indulgence and self-mortification in practice, and avoids eternalism and annihilationism at the doctrinal level.
D. It teaches that liberation is attained by extreme asceticism alone, because all bodily experience is suffering.

**Source:** `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/paper-1/indian/Buddhism.md`, One-Screen Map and Middle Path coverage.

**Explanation**
- **A — Incorrect:** The Middle Path is not a social arrangement between monastic and household life. It is a practical and doctrinal principle.
- **B — Incorrect:** Buddhism's Middle Path does not affirm a permanent self; the doctrine of no-self (nairātmyavāda) rejects a permanent ātman.
- **C — Correct:** Practically, it avoids sensual indulgence and self-mortification, with the Noble Eightfold Path as its positive discipline. Doctrinally, it avoids both eternalism and annihilationism.
- **D — Incorrect:** It recommends neither extreme self-mortification nor liberation through asceticism alone.

**Relevant terms:** The Middle Path (madhyamā pratipad; Pali: majjhimā paṭipadā) is not a vague compromise or merely moderate lifestyle. Its practical form is the Noble Eightfold Path (āryāṣṭāṅgamārga), which operationalises the Fourth Noble Truth; its doctrinal dimension is connected to dependent origination and no-self.

**Examiner trap:** Do not reduce the Middle Path to only rejecting sensual pleasure and severe asceticism. UPSC may test its second, doctrinal sense: avoiding eternalism and annihilationism.
