# Polity 08 — Directive Principles of State Policy — Diagnostic Question 1

**Learner answer:** B  
**Correct answer:** B  
**Result:** Correct

Which statement best describes the Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP)?

A. They are directly enforceable by courts and bind private individuals in the same way as Fundamental Rights.
B. They are non-justiciable, yet Article 37 declares them fundamental in governance and makes it the State's duty to apply them in law-making.
C. They are contained in Part III and primarily secure political democracy.
D. They may be set aside by the State because they are only political suggestions without constitutional status.

**Source:** `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Polity/basic/Directive-Principles.md`, §§1–2 and 7; compare Article 37.

**Explanation**
- **A — Incorrect:** DPSP are non-justiciable under Article 37; courts do not directly enforce them as individual claims in the manner of Fundamental Rights.
- **B — Correct:** Article 37 says the principles are not enforceable by any court, yet are fundamental in the governance of the country and it is the State's duty to apply them in making laws.
- **C — Incorrect:** DPSP are in Part IV (Articles 36–51), not Part III. They principally guide the State toward social and economic democracy; Fundamental Rights protect political democracy.
- **D — Incorrect:** Non-justiciable does not mean they lack constitutional status or can be ignored. Article 37 expressly gives them fundamental governance status and places a duty on the State.

**Relevant terms:** “Non-justiciable” means not directly enforceable through court proceedings. “State” in this context includes the constitutional and public authorities to which Part IV applies.

**Examiner trap:** Keep legal enforceability distinct from constitutional importance and the State's governance duty.
