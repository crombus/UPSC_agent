# Economy 01 — Question 6

**Learner answer:** D  
**Correct answer:** C  
**Result:** Incorrect

An Indian firm buys a newly imported machine for investment. Why is its value subtracted in the imports term after it appears in expenditure?

A. To adjust gross production for depreciation  
B. To remove machinery from all investment figures  
C. To exclude foreign production from domestic output  
D. To treat imported machinery as an intermediate input

**Explanation:** Expenditure totals can include spending on foreign-produced goods. Subtracting imports removes that foreign-produced value so the identity measures domestic GDP. The machine remains investment; a capital good is not thereby classified as an intermediate input.

- **A:** Incorrect; depreciation adjusts gross aggregates to net aggregates.
- **B:** Incorrect; imported machinery remains part of investment spending; imports offset its foreign origin.
- **C:** Correct; the imports term removes foreign production from domestic output.
- **D:** Incorrect; a machine purchased as investment is a capital good, not an intermediate input.
