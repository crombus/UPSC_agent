# Polity 04 — Preamble — Diagnostic Question 1

**Learner answer:** B  
**Correct answer:** B  
**Result:** Correct

Which statement best describes the constitutional Preamble?

A. It is a directly enforceable catalogue of individual rights.
B. It introduces the Constitution's authority, identity, objectives and adoption.
C. It is the source of Parliament's residual legislative power.
D. It is a schedule dividing legislative subjects between the Union and States.

**Source:** `final-mcq/subjects/Polity/04-Preamble/revision-1/QUESTIONS.md` and `ANSWERS.md`, Q1; `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Polity/basic/Preamble.md`.

**Explanation**
- **A — Incorrect:** The Preamble is non-justiciable; it does not itself provide a directly enforceable catalogue of individual rights. Fundamental Rights are set out elsewhere in Part III.
- **B — Correct:** The Preamble introduces the Constitution's authority (the people), the nature of the State, its objectives and the date of adoption.
- **C — Incorrect:** The Preamble is neither a source of nor a limit on legislative power. Residual legislative powers arise from the Constitution's distribution of legislative competence, not the Preamble.
- **D — Incorrect:** The distribution of legislative subjects is provided in the Seventh Schedule, not the Preamble.

**Relevant context:** The Preamble is part of the Constitution (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973), but is not independently enforceable in court. It may guide constitutional interpretation. It was amended once by the 42nd Amendment Act, 1976, which added “Socialist”, “Secular” and “Integrity”.

**Examiner trap:** “Part of the Constitution” does not mean “independently enforceable”; interpretive significance and justiciability are different legal questions.
