Which statement best expresses the Jain doctrine of anekāntavāda (many-sidedness)?

A. Reality has many aspects, so a complete account of an object must recognise the standpoint and limits of a particular judgment.
B. Only one permanent substance exists, and apparent differences are entirely unreal.
C. Knowledge is possible only through perception, so inference and testimony are invalid.
D. Every proposition is simply true or false in the same unqualified way, irrespective of standpoint.

**Source:** `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Philosophy/Indian-Philosophy/learning-sessions/Jainism/Jainism_Layered-Complete-Learning-Session_2026-08-17.md`, Layer 1 and Layer 2, Theory of Reality; the same session's canonical explanation of anekāntavāda.

**Explanation**
- **A — Correct:** *Anekāntavāda* is the metaphysical claim that reality is many-sided: a finite judgment captures an aspect, not the whole nature of a real. For example, a jar can be described by its material, shape, use and temporal condition; one description does not exhaust it.
- **B — Incorrect:** This is a monistic position, not Jain pluralistic realism. Jainism recognises multiple real substances and their changing modes.
- **C — Incorrect:** Jain epistemology does not reject inference and testimony. Perception and non-perceptual forms of knowledge are recognised; the doctrine warns against treating a partial standpoint as exhaustive.
- **D — Incorrect:** This ignores qualified predication. *Anekāntavāda* is connected with *nayavāda* (partial standpoints) and *syādvāda* (conditional predication), not unqualified absolutism.

**Relevant terms:** *Dravya* is substance; *guṇa* is a quality; *paryāya* is a changing mode. Jainism holds that a real persists as substance while modes originate and decay. *Anekāntavāda* is not scepticism or indecision; it is a claim about the complexity of reality and the limits of one-sided judgments.

**Examiner trap:** Do not confuse Jain many-sidedness with “anything goes.” The doctrine supports disciplined, qualified claims, not the denial of knowledge.
