Which statement best distinguishes inflation from a one-off change in a single price?

A. Inflation is any rise in a politically important item.  
B. Inflation is a sustained rise in the general price level, not merely a rise in one item.  
C. Inflation is a fall in output with any price movement.  
D. Inflation is a rise in imported goods only.

**Source:** `final-mcq/subjects/Economy/03-Inflation-Price-Indices-and-Business-Cycles/revision-1/QUESTIONS.md` and `ANSWERS.md`, Q1; `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/03_Inflation-Price-Indices-and-Business-Cycles.md` §2.

**Explanation**
- **A — Incorrect:** A rise in one politically important item is a relative-price change, not necessarily inflation. For example, a temporary onion shortage may raise onion prices while the broader price level remains stable.
- **B — Correct:** Inflation is a sustained increase in the general price level across an economy. “General” distinguishes it from an isolated price rise; “sustained” distinguishes it from a temporary movement. Price indices such as the Consumer Price Index (CPI) track changes in a representative basket.
- **C — Incorrect:** A fall in output alongside inflation may describe stagflation in the right context, but falling output is not part of the definition of inflation.
- **D — Incorrect:** Imported goods can contribute to domestic inflation through costs or prices, but a rise in imported goods alone does not establish a sustained rise in the general price level.

**Examiner trap:** Confusing a change in one price (or one cause of price pressure) with a sustained, broad-based movement in the general price level.
