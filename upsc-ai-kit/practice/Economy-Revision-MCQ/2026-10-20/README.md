## Provisional weak areas

- Distinguishing net primary income from abroad (used to bridge GDP to GNI) from personal remittances/current transfers.
- Distinguishing domestic net output (NDP) from national net output (NNP); depreciation alone gives NDP, while NNP also requires net primary income from abroad.
- Statistical discrepancy between production, income and expenditure estimates can arise from differing data sources, timing and revisions; do not confuse this with conceptual bridges between aggregates or price-index differences.
- Imports are subtracted in the expenditure identity to remove foreign-produced value from domestic GDP; imported capital goods remain investment, not intermediate inputs.
- GDP deflator calculation: nominal GDP divided by real/constant-price GDP, multiplied by 100.
- Unsold finished output is recorded as inventory investment (change in inventories), not as an intermediate input.
- Distinguishing working capital consumed or used up in production (such as yarn) from fixed capital used repeatedly (such as a loom).
- Distinguishing nominal output growth from real output growth: real output rises only when nominal output grows faster than the corresponding output-price index.
- Buddhism: the Middle Path has both a practical meaning (avoiding sensual indulgence and self-mortification through the Noble Eightfold Path) and a doctrinal meaning (avoiding eternalism and annihilationism); it is not a social compromise or an affirmation of a permanent self.
