# Polity 07 — Fundamental Rights — Diagnostic Question 1

**Learner answer:** A  
**Correct answer:** A  
**Result:** Correct

Which statement best describes Article 32 of the Constitution?

A. It is itself a Fundamental Right and empowers the Supreme Court to issue writs to enforce Fundamental Rights.
B. It is the High Courts' power to issue writs for any legal right.
C. It is an ordinary statutory remedy that Parliament may remove by ordinary law.
D. It is outside Part III and protects the right to property under Article 300A.

**Source:** `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Polity/basic/Fundamental-Rights.md`, §§3–6; cross-check with the final-mcq Polity 07 revision bank, Q1.

**Explanation**
- **A — Correct:** Article 32 is itself a Fundamental Right under Part III. It empowers a person to move the Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental Rights; the Court may issue writs for this purpose.
- **B — Incorrect:** This describes aspects of High Court jurisdiction under Article 226, not Article 32. High Courts may issue writs for Fundamental Rights and “for any other purpose,” giving Article 226 a broader subject-matter reach.
- **C — Incorrect:** Article 32 is a constitutional right, not an ordinary statutory remedy removable by ordinary legislation.
- **D — Incorrect:** Article 32 is within Part III. Article 300A protects property as a legal right outside Part III, not as a Fundamental Right.

**Relevant context:** The five traditional writs are habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari and quo warranto. Article 32 enforces Fundamental Rights; Article 226 empowers High Courts to issue writs for Fundamental Rights and other legal purposes. Article 300A was introduced after the right to property ceased to be a Fundamental Right under the 44th Amendment Act, 1978.

**Examiner trap:** Do not conflate Article 32 with Article 226: the former is itself a Fundamental Right and is confined to enforcing Fundamental Rights, while the latter has wider remedial reach.
