Which statement best defines the money market?

A. A market only for currency notes issued by the central bank
B. A market exclusively for long-term equity claims
C. A short-term market for funds and liquidity instruments
D. A market whose defining test is the issuer's public-sector status

**Source:** `final-mcq/subjects/Economy/07-Money-Market-Capital-Market-and-Financial-Instruments/revision-1/QUESTIONS.md` and `ANSWERS.md`, Q1; `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Economy/basic/07_Money-Market-Capital-Market-and-Financial-Instruments.md` §2. Options have been reordered for this mixed revision.

**Explanation**
- **A — Incorrect:** The money market is not limited to physical currency or notes issued by the central bank; it includes a range of short-term financial instruments.
- **B — Incorrect:** Long-term equity finance belongs to the capital market, not the money market.
- **C — Correct:** The money market deals in short-term funds and liquidity instruments. Examples include Treasury Bills, commercial paper and certificates of deposit.
- **D — Incorrect:** Issuer ownership is not the defining criterion. Instruments from public and private issuers can participate in money markets, depending on their type and maturity.

**Relevant terms:** “Short-term” distinguishes money-market finance from the medium- and long-term debt and equity finance of capital markets. Liquidity instruments help institutions and investors manage short-term cash needs.

**Examiner trap:** Do not equate “money market” with cash alone or classify an instrument by issuer rather than by its function and maturity.
