# Economy 01 — Question 7

**Learner answer:** D  
**Correct answer:** D  
**Result:** Correct

For one unchanged series, nominal GDP rises while real GDP is flat. Which inference is justified?

A. Foreign factor income must have risen  
B. Output volume has necessarily contracted  
C. Consumption of fixed capital has fallen  
D. Higher prices can explain the rise

**Explanation:** Nominal GDP values output at current prices, while real GDP uses constant/base-year prices to track volume. If real output is flat while nominal GDP rises, higher prices can account for the nominal increase.

- **A:** Incorrect; the statement gives no information about foreign primary income.
- **B:** Incorrect; flat real GDP indicates no measured volume growth, not necessarily contraction.
- **C:** Incorrect; depreciation is not inferable from nominal and real GDP alone.
- **D:** Correct; a higher price level can raise nominal GDP while real output is unchanged.

**Examiner trap:** A rise in nominal GDP alone does not establish output-volume growth.
