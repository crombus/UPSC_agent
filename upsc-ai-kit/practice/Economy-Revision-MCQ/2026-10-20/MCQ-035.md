# Polity 11 — Parliamentary System — Diagnostic Question 1

**Learner answer:** B  
**Correct answer:** B  
**Result:** Correct

What is the defining feature of India's parliamentary system of government?

A. The executive is separately elected for a fixed term and is not answerable to the legislature.
B. The executive is drawn from and collectively responsible to the legislature, and must retain the Lok Sabha's confidence.
C. The President is the real executive and may disregard the Council of Ministers' advice.
D. The legislature and executive are rigidly separated, with no ministerial membership of Parliament.

**Source:** `upsc-ai-kit/knowledge/Polity/basic/Parliamentary-System.md`, snapshot and core idea; compare Articles 74–75.

**Explanation**
- **A — Incorrect:** A fixed-term executive elected separately from the legislature describes a presidential model, not India's parliamentary system.
- **B — Correct:** The Prime Minister and Council of Ministers are drawn from and answerable to the legislature. The ministry remains in office only while it retains the Lok Sabha's confidence; Article 75(3) provides for collective responsibility to the House of the People.
- **C — Incorrect:** The President is the nominal constitutional head. Under Article 74, the President acts on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister.
- **D — Incorrect:** India's system involves a fusion between executive and legislature: ministers are generally members of Parliament, or must become members within six months.

**Relevant terms:** **Collective responsibility** means the Council of Ministers acts as a team and is collectively answerable to the Lok Sabha. A no-confidence vote defeats the ministry as a whole.

**Examiner trap:** Do not confuse a republican head of State with a presidential executive; India has an elected President but parliamentary government.
