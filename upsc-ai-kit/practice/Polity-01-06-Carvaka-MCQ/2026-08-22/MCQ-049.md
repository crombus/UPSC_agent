# MCQ 049

**Topic:** Philosophy - Carvaka  
**Difficulty:** Hard  
**Coverage point:** Dehatmavada, transcendent entities, svabhavavada and ethics  
**Status:** Completed

## Question

Consider the following statements:

1. Dehātmavāda identifies the self with the living conscious body rather than with an immaterial,
   independently surviving soul.
2. Cārvāka rejects karma and rebirth because their operation is not available to perception.
3. Svabhāvavāda necessarily means that every event occurs randomly without any natural
   characteristics or regularities.
4. Cārvāka ethics must be interpreted as recommending every immediate pleasure regardless of
   foreseeable pain or practical consequences.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. 1 and 2 only  
B. 1, 2 and 3 only  
C. 3 and 4 only  
D. 1, 2 and 4 only

## Response and explanation

**Your answer:** D  
**Correct answer:** A  
**Result:** Incorrect

Statements 1 and 2 are correct.

### Meaning of the terms

- **Dehātmavāda:** the doctrine that the living body itself is the self. Cārvāka rejects an
  immaterial ātman existing independently of the body.
- **Karma:** an unseen moral-causal force said to connect deeds with future consequences,
  including another birth.
- **Rebirth:** continuation into a new embodiment after death.
- **Svabhāvavāda:** explanation through the intrinsic nature or natural characteristics of things.
  Fire burns and water flows because of their natures, not because an unseen divine agent directs
  every event.
- **Yadṛcchāvāda:** a doctrine of chance or accidental occurrence. It should not automatically be
  identified with svabhāvavāda.
- **Puruṣārtha:** a recognised human goal. Cārvāka is commonly associated with artha and kāma
  rather than unseen post-mortem goals.

### Statement-by-statement analysis with examples

1. **Correct.** The conscious body is the person; no separate soul is required.  
   **Example:** When the body is destroyed, consciousness ceases, just as an emergent property
   disappears when the organised material arrangement ends.
2. **Correct.** Karma and rebirth are rejected because they transcend perceptual verification.  
   **Example:** One may observe an action and its immediate consequence, but not an invisible
   karmic deposit travelling into a future birth.
3. **Incorrect.** Natural operation is not identical to pure randomness.  
   **Example:** Saying fire burns by its own nature offers a natural explanation; it does not mean
   fire behaves without any characteristic pattern.
4. **Incorrect.** The crude “consume every pleasure immediately” picture largely comes through
   hostile descriptions and ignores practical calculation.  
   **Example:** Avoiding a pleasure that predictably causes greater bodily pain remains compatible
   with concern for embodied well-being.

### Wider philosophical context

Cārvāka replaces transcendent explanations with natural and embodied ones:

```text
Soul      → living body
Divine agency → natural properties
Future-life reward → present embodied welfare
```

> **Core lesson:** materialism and this-worldliness do not logically require irrational,
> consequence-blind indulgence.
