# MCQ 021

**Topic:** Philosophy - Carvaka  
**Coverage point:** Four-element materialism and rejection of akasa  
**Status:** Completed

## Question

Which statement correctly represents the classical Carvaka account of the elements?

A. It accepts earth, water, fire and air, but rejects akasa because it is not perceptually
available.

B. It accepts all five elements because each is established by scripture.

C. It accepts only earth because the remaining elements are inferred.

D. It considers consciousness to be an eternal fifth element.

## Response and explanation

**Your answer:** A  
**Correct answer:** A  
**Result:** Correct

Classical Cārvāka accepts four perceptible material elements: earth, water, fire and air.
It rejects **ākāśa** because its existence is not directly established through perception.

The physical body and consciousness are explained through combinations of these material
elements rather than by introducing an immaterial soul or an eternal consciousness-element.

> **Trap:** The standard five-element scheme belongs to other Indian philosophical systems;
> Cārvāka's epistemic restriction produces a four-element ontology.
