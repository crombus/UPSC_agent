# MCQ 057

**Topic:** Polity 01 - Historical Background  
**Difficulty:** Hard  
**Coverage point:** Pitt's India Act, Charter Act 1813, Councils Act 1861 and Communal Award  
**Status:** Completed

## Question

Consider the following statements:

1. Pitt's India Act, 1784 created a Board of Control and produced a system often described as
   double government.
2. The Charter Act, 1813 completely ended the Company's commercial monopoly, including trade in
   tea and with China.
3. The Indian Councils Act, 1861 recognised the portfolio system and restored legislative powers
   to Bombay and Madras.
4. The Communal Award, 1932 introduced separate electorates for Muslims for the first time.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. 1 and 3 only  
B. 1, 2 and 3 only  
C. 2 and 4 only  
D. 1, 3 and 4 only

## Response and explanation

**Your answer:** A  
**Correct answer:** A  
**Result:** Correct

Statements 1 and 3 are correct.

### Meaning of the terms

- **Board of Control:** British governmental body supervising the Company's political and
  administrative affairs.
- **Double government:** division between the Company's commercial management and governmental
  political control through the Board of Control.
- **Commercial monopoly:** exclusive legal right of the Company to conduct specified trade.
- **Portfolio system:** allocation of particular government departments to individual executive
  council members.
- **Separate electorate:** members of a community vote separately to elect representatives from
  that community.

### Timeline and statement analysis

1. **Correct - Pitt's India Act, 1784.** It distinguished commercial and political functions.  
   **Example:** The Court of Directors continued managing Company affairs, while the Board of
   Control supervised political administration.
2. **Incorrect - Charter Act, 1813.** It ended most of the Company's trade monopoly but retained
   exceptions for tea and trade with China.  
   **Example:** Private British traders gained wider access to Indian trade, but the Company kept
   the protected tea-China sphere until the Charter Act, 1833.
3. **Correct - Indian Councils Act, 1861.** It recognised the portfolio system and restored
   legislative authority to Bombay and Madras.  
   **Example:** Individual council members could manage departments, an institutional precursor
   to ministerial allocation of portfolios.
4. **Incorrect - Communal Award, 1932.** Separate Muslim electorates had already been introduced
   by the Indian Councils Act, 1909.  
   **Example:** The 1932 Award extended communal representation and proposed separate electorates
   for the Depressed Classes. Gandhi's opposition and negotiations with Ambedkar produced the
   **Poona Pact**, which substituted reserved seats within a joint electorate for the Depressed
   Classes.

### Compact timeline

```text
1784 Board of Control
1813 partial end of trade monopoly
1861 portfolio system and legislative decentralisation
1909 separate Muslim electorates
1932 Communal Award → Poona Pact
```

> **Hard trap:** “Ended the monopoly” in 1813 was not absolute; tea and China remained exceptions.
