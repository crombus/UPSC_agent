# MCQ 050

**Topic:** Polity 01 - Historical Background  
**Difficulty:** Hard  
**Coverage point:** Federal and provincial scheme under the Government of India Act, 1935  
**Status:** Completed

## Question

Consider the following statements regarding the Government of India Act, 1935:

1. It proposed an All-India Federation, but that federation never came into operation.
2. It established provincial autonomy and proposed dyarchy at the federal level.
3. It vested residuary legislative powers in the provincial legislatures.
4. It introduced bicameral legislatures in every province.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. 1, 2 and 3 only  
B. 1 and 2 only  
C. 2, 3 and 4 only  
D. 1 and 4 only

## Response and explanation

**Your answer:** A  
**Correct answer:** B  
**Result:** Incorrect

Statements 1 and 2 are correct.

### Meaning of the terms

- **All-India Federation:** the proposed union of British Indian provinces and willing princely
  states under a federal constitutional scheme.
- **Provincial autonomy:** replacement of provincial dyarchy with ministries responsible to
  provincial legislatures, subject to the Governor's special powers.
- **Federal dyarchy:** proposed division of federal subjects into reserved and transferred fields
  at the Centre. It never operated because the federation itself did not commence.
- **Residuary powers:** authority over matters not appearing in any legislative list.
- **Bicameralism:** a legislature consisting of two Houses.

### Statement-by-statement analysis with examples

1. **Correct.** The Act proposed a federation of provinces and princely states, but the necessary
   princely-state accessions did not occur.  
   **Example:** The Act's federal provisions existed on paper while British India continued
   without the proposed federation becoming operational.
2. **Correct.** Provincial dyarchy introduced in 1919 was abolished and provincial autonomy was
   introduced; dyarchy was proposed at the Centre.  
   **Example:** Provincial ministers became responsible for ordinary provincial departments,
   while Governors retained safeguards and discretionary powers.
3. **Incorrect.** Residuary powers were vested in the Governor-General, not provincial
   legislatures.  
   **Example:** If a subject appeared in none of the three lists, the Governor-General determined
   the competent legislature.
4. **Incorrect.** Bicameralism was introduced in only six provinces, not every province.  
   **Example:** Bengal and Bombay had bicameral legislatures, while several other provinces
   remained unicameral.

### Timeline

```text
1919: Provincial dyarchy
        ↓
1935: Provincial autonomy + proposed federal dyarchy
        ↓
1937: Provincial provisions and Federal Court operational
        ↓
Federal scheme itself never commenced
```

> **Hard trap:** “Provided for” and “came into operation” are different propositions.
