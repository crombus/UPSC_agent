# MCQ 070

**Topic:** Philosophy - Carvaka  
**Difficulty:** Hard  
**Coverage point:** Internal diversity, positivism comparison, Jayarasi and lost sources  
**Status:** Correct

## Question

Consider the following statements:

1. The distinction between Dhūrta and Suśikṣita Cārvākas is supported by complete surviving
   original texts and is historically uncontested.
2. Comparing Cārvāka with positivism can illuminate their shared suspicion of unverifiable
   metaphysics, but it should not erase their historical and doctrinal differences.
3. Jayarāśi's sceptical arguments may challenge even established definitions of pramāṇas rather
   than merely defending a fixed materialist system.
4. The complete Bṛhaspati Sūtra survives and provides a neutral first-person account of every
   Cārvāka doctrine.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. 1 and 2 only  
B. 2 and 3 only  
C. 1, 3 and 4 only  
D. 2, 3 and 4 only

## Response and explanation

**Learner's answer:** B  
**Correct answer:** B - Statements 2 and 3 only  
**Result:** Correct

### Statement-by-statement analysis

1. **Incorrect.** The Dhūrta-Suśikṣita classification is not established through a complete body
   of surviving first-person Cārvāka texts. It is largely reconstructed from later references,
   classifications and reports, often written by opponents. It can be used as a scholarly
   interpretive distinction, but not as an uncontested historical fact.
2. **Correct.** Cārvāka and modern positivist approaches may both distrust unverifiable
   metaphysical claims. The comparison is illuminating only if their different periods,
   arguments and intellectual purposes are preserved.
3. **Correct.** Jayarāśi Bhaṭṭa's *Tattvopaplavasimha* radically challenges the definitions and
   justification of accepted pramāṇas. Consequently, he may be read as a sceptical critic of
   knowledge-claims rather than simply as the spokesman of a fixed Cārvāka materialist system.
4. **Incorrect.** The complete *Bṛhaspati Sūtra*, traditionally associated with Cārvāka, has not
   survived. Much of what is known about the school comes from fragments, summaries and
   criticisms preserved by rival traditions.

### Technical terms

- **Dhūrta Cārvāka:** a reported "cruder" strand usually associated with strict materialism and
  denial of a surviving self.
- **Suśikṣita Cārvāka:** a reported "cultivated" strand sometimes presented as accepting a subtle
  distinction involving consciousness while still rejecting an eternal, transmigrating self.
- **Positivism:** broadly, an approach that privileges empirically testable knowledge and resists
  speculative metaphysics. It is a modern movement and is not identical to Cārvāka.
- **Pramāṇa:** a valid means or method of knowledge, such as perception, inference or testimony.
- **First-person source:** a text composed from within the school, unlike an opponent's report.

### Timeline and source context

| Period | Development |
|---|---|
| Ancient India | Materialist or Lokāyata ideas emerge; a Bṛhaspati tradition is later reported |
| c. 8th century CE | Jayarāśi Bhaṭṭa is commonly associated with the *Tattvopaplavasimha* |
| Medieval period | Rival philosophical works preserve many Cārvāka fragments and descriptions |
| Modern scholarship | Cārvāka is reconstructed cautiously from fragmentary and often hostile evidence |

### Wider context and example

Suppose a rival text says, "The Cārvākas accept only perception." This is valuable evidence, but
it does not automatically prove that every thinker labelled Cārvāka held precisely the same view.
The source may simplify the opponent to make refutation easier. Similarly, Jayarāśi's attack on
definitions of perception and inference does not by itself establish that he intended to replace
them with a conventional materialist doctrine.

The UPSC-ready conclusion is therefore methodological: **Cārvāka should be reconstructed
critically, distinguishing secure doctrines, hostile reports and later scholarly interpretations.**
