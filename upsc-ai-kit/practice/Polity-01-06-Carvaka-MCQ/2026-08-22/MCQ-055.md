# MCQ 055

**Topic:** Polity 06 - Citizenship  
**Difficulty:** Hard  
**Coverage point:** Constitutional citizenship at commencement, Articles 5-11  
**Status:** Completed

## Question

Consider the following statements:

1. Articles 5 to 8 dealt with different categories of citizenship at the Constitution's
   commencement, including domicile and migration-related categories.
2. Article 9 denied commencement citizenship to a person who had voluntarily acquired the
   citizenship of a foreign state.
3. Article 10 continued citizenship rights subject to laws made by Parliament.
4. Article 11 prohibited Parliament from changing or supplementing the constitutional
   citizenship scheme.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. 1 and 2 only  
B. 2, 3 and 4 only  
C. 1, 2 and 3 only  
D. 1, 3 and 4 only

## Response and explanation

**Your answer:** A  
**Correct answer:** C  
**Result:** Incorrect

Statements 1, 2 and 3 are correct.

### Meaning of the terms

- **Citizenship at commencement:** determination of who became a citizen when the Constitution
  commenced on 26 January 1950.
- **Domicile:** a person's permanent legal home, combining residence with an intention to remain.
  It is deeper than temporary residence.
- **Migration category:** special rules addressing movement between India and Pakistan around
  Partition.
- **Voluntary foreign citizenship:** citizenship of another country acquired by a person's own
  voluntary act.

### Articles 5-11 map

| Article | Principal subject |
|---:|---|
| 5 | Citizenship based on domicile plus birth, parentage or residence |
| 6 | Certain migrants from Pakistan to India |
| 7 | Certain persons who migrated from India to Pakistan, with a permit-related exception |
| 8 | Persons of Indian origin residing outside India and registered by diplomatic/consular authorities |
| 9 | Voluntary foreign citizenship |
| 10 | Continuance of citizenship subject to parliamentary law |
| 11 | Parliament's power to regulate citizenship |

### Statement-by-statement analysis with examples

1. **Correct.** Articles 5-8 dealt with commencement categories rather than a permanent,
   exhaustive future code.  
   **Example:** Article 6 addressed Partition-era migration from Pakistan, while Article 8
   addressed eligible persons of Indian origin abroad.
2. **Correct.** Article 9 prevented a person who voluntarily acquired foreign citizenship from
   claiming commencement citizenship under the preceding provisions.  
   **Example:** A voluntary foreign naturalisation could defeat a claim based solely on domicile
   or Indian origin.
3. **Correct.** Article 10 preserved citizenship subject to laws Parliament might enact.  
   **Example:** The later Citizenship Act, 1955 could regulate acquisition and loss.
4. **Incorrect.** Article 11 does the opposite: it expressly empowers Parliament to legislate.  
   **Example:** Parliament created statutory modes such as citizenship by birth, descent,
   registration and naturalisation.

> **Core distinction:** Articles 5-10 answered “Who is a citizen on 26 January 1950?”; Article 11
> enabled Parliament to answer future citizenship questions.
