# MCQ 056

**Topic:** Philosophy - Carvaka  
**Difficulty:** Hard  
**Coverage point:** Perception, error, Nyaya response and source problem  
**Status:** Completed

## Question

Consider the following statements:

1. The standard dogmatic Cārvāka position treats perception as the only independent pramāṇa.
2. The occurrence of perceptual illusion logically forces Cārvāka to accept scriptural testimony
   as an independent pramāṇa.
3. Nyāya defends inference through repeated positive and negative observations, examination of
   upādhis and supportive reasoning or tarka.
4. Because much Cārvāka doctrine survives through opponents' accounts, claims about the school
   require source-critical caution.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. 1 and 2 only  
B. 2, 3 and 4 only  
C. 1 and 3 only  
D. 1, 3 and 4 only

## Response and explanation

**Your answer:** D  
**Correct answer:** D  
**Result:** Correct

Statements 1, 3 and 4 are correct.

### Meaning of the terms

- **Pratyakṣa:** direct perception through an appropriate sensory or experiential channel.
- **Pramāṇa:** an independent means or source of valid cognition.
- **Perceptual illusion:** an appearance that does not correspond to the object as it actually is.
- **Tarka:** supportive reasoning used by Nyāya to test consequences and eliminate unreasonable
  alternatives; it assists knowledge rather than ordinarily functioning as an independent
  pramāṇa.
- **Source criticism:** evaluating who preserved a claim, their purpose, possible bias and the
  distance between the source and the doctrine described.

### Statement-by-statement analysis with examples

1. **Correct.** Standard Cārvāka treats perception as the only independent pramāṇa.  
   **Example:** A fire directly seen and felt is perceptually known; a supposed invisible karmic
   force is not.
2. **Incorrect.** Illusion creates a problem for perception but does not logically establish
   scriptural authority.  
   **Example:** A mirage may be corrected by approaching the location and perceiving that no water
   exists. Cārvāka can claim that further perception corrects initial appearance, though critics
   argue memory and reasoning also participate.
3. **Correct.** Nyāya builds vyāpti through disciplined observation and reasoning.  
   **Example:** It observes smoke with fire in positive cases, the absence of smoke where relevant
   fire conditions are absent, investigates hidden conditions such as wet fuel, and uses tarka to
   test whether smoke without combustion is coherent.
4. **Correct.** Much Cārvāka teaching is preserved by critics rather than intact original texts.  
   **Example:** The Cārvāka chapter of the *Sarvadarśanasaṅgraha* is useful but polemical, while
   Jayarāśi's *Tattvopaplavasimha* survives yet its precise affiliation remains disputed.

### Wider philosophical context

```text
Cārvāka: certainty must remain tied to perception
Nyāya: disciplined inference can extend knowledge beyond present perception
```

> **Core dispute:** not whether people actually reason, but whether reasoning can become an
> independently valid and certainty-producing pramāṇa.
