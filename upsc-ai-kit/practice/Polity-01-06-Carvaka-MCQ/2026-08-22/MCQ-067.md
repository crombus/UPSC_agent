# MCQ 067

**Topic:** Polity 04 - Preamble  
**Difficulty:** Hard  
**Coverage point:** Evolution of Supreme Court doctrine on the Preamble  
**Status:** Completed

## Question

Consider the following statements:

1. In the *Berubari Union* opinion, the Supreme Court did not treat the Preamble as part of the
   Constitution.
2. *Kesavananda Bharati* recognised the Preamble as part of the Constitution.
3. *LIC of India v. Consumer Education and Research Centre* reaffirmed the Preamble's integral
   constitutional status.
4. Because the Preamble is part of the Constitution, it independently creates and limits
   Parliament's legislative competence.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. 1 and 2 only  
B. 2, 3 and 4 only  
C. 1, 2 and 3 only  
D. 1, 3 and 4 only

## Response and explanation

**Your answer:** A  
**Correct answer:** C  
**Result:** Incorrect

Statements 1, 2 and 3 are correct.

### Meaning of the legal concepts

- **Part of the Constitution:** possesses constitutional status and may assist interpretation.
- **Non-justiciable:** cannot independently generate an enforceable legal claim.
- **Legislative competence:** legal authority to enact legislation, supplied by operative
  constitutional provisions and the legislative lists.
- **Integral status:** recognised as belonging to the constitutional document and expressing its
  governing philosophy.

### Case-law timeline

1. **1960 - *Berubari Union*:** treated the Preamble as not part of the Constitution, though useful
   for understanding the framers' purpose.
2. **1973 - *Kesavananda Bharati*:** recognised the Preamble as part of the Constitution and
   connected its foundational values with the basic-structure doctrine.
3. **1995 - *LIC of India v. Consumer Education and Research Centre*:** reaffirmed its integral
   constitutional place.

### Statement-by-statement analysis with examples

1. **Correct.** This was the earlier judicial position.  
   **Example:** The Court still consulted the Preamble but denied it the status of an operative
   constitutional part.
2. **Correct.** *Kesavananda Bharati* changed the doctrinal position.  
   **Example:** Parliament could amend the Preamble but could not destroy basic constitutional
   features reflected in it.
3. **Correct.** *LIC of India* reaffirmed the later understanding.
4. **Incorrect.** Constitutional status does not make the Preamble an independent source or
   limitation of legislative competence.  
   **Example:** Parliament's power over citizenship comes from Article 11 and relevant legislative
   entries, not merely from the Preamble's word “sovereign.”

> **Core distinction:** the Preamble is constitutionally authoritative for interpretation but not
> an independent law-making power or standalone cause of action.

### Learner doubt: What does LIC mean in the 1995 case?

**LIC** means the **Life Insurance Corporation of India**, the statutory public-sector life
insurance corporation.

The case commonly cited here is ***LIC of India v. Consumer Education and Research Centre*
(1995)**. In discussing the constitutional obligations of a public institution and fairness in
insurance access, the Supreme Court treated the Preamble as an integral part of the Constitution
and connected constitutional interpretation with dignity and social justice.

**Example:** LIC could not be treated exactly like an unrestricted private individual when
designing public-facing insurance conditions; as a public institution, its actions were examined
against constitutional standards of fairness.

> **Exam use:** cite *LIC (1995)* mainly as reaffirming the Preamble's integral status after
> *Kesavananda Bharati*, not as the original case establishing that status.
