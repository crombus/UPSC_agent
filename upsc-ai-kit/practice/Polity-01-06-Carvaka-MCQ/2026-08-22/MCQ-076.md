# MCQ 076

**Topic:** Polity 06 - Citizenship  
**Difficulty:** Hard  
**Coverage point:** Citizen-only rights, rights of aliens and OCI restrictions  
**Status:** Incorrect

## Question

Consider the following statements:

1. The rights under Articles 15, 16, 19, 29 and 30 are constitutionally confined to citizens.
2. Articles 14 and 21 generally protect persons and are therefore not confined only to Indian
   citizens.
3. Enemy aliens are excluded from the arrest and detention safeguards contained in clauses (1)
   and (2) of Article 22.
4. An Overseas Citizen of India cardholder cannot vote in Indian elections or become a member of
   Parliament merely by virtue of OCI status.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

A. Only one  
B. Only two  
C. Only three  
D. All four

## Response and explanation

**Learner's answer:** B  
**Correct answer:** D - All four statements  
**Result:** Incorrect

### Statement-by-statement analysis

1. **Correct.** Articles 15, 16 and 19 expressly use the term **citizen**. Articles 29 and 30
   protect the cultural and educational rights of sections of citizens and minorities within the
   constitutional citizenship framework. These are conventionally grouped among Fundamental
   Rights available only to citizens.
2. **Correct.** Article 14 guarantees equality before law and equal protection of laws to **any
   person**, while Article 21 protects the life and personal liberty of **no person** except
   according to procedure established by law. They therefore generally protect foreigners as well
   as citizens.
3. **Correct.** Article 22(3)(a) expressly excludes an **enemy alien** from the safeguards in
   Article 22(1) and 22(2), such as being informed of the grounds of arrest, consulting a lawyer
   and production before a magistrate within 24 hours.
4. **Correct.** OCI is a statutory immigration and diaspora status, not Indian citizenship. An OCI
   cardholder cannot vote, contest elections or become an MP or MLA merely because of OCI status.

### Why option B fails

Option B means that only two statements are correct, but each statement correctly identifies a
different constitutional distinction: citizen-only rights, person-based rights, the enemy-alien
exception and the limited nature of OCI.

### Technical terms

- **Citizen-only right:** a constitutional protection whose text or recognised scope is confined
  to Indian citizens.
- **Person-based right:** a protection available generally to every human or legal person within
  the Constitution's reach, irrespective of citizenship.
- **Alien:** a person who is not an Indian citizen.
- **Enemy alien:** a national of a state at war with India; Article 22 creates a specific
  constitutional exception for this category.
- **OCI:** Overseas Citizen of India, a statutory status providing specified travel and residence
  facilities without political citizenship.

### Rights comparison

| Category | Examples | Basic position |
|---|---|---|
| Citizens only | Articles 15, 16, 19, 29 and 30 | Not generally available to foreigners |
| All persons | Articles 14 and 21 | Generally available to citizens and foreigners |
| Enemy-alien exception | Article 22(1)-(2) safeguards | Expressly excluded by Article 22(3)(a) |
| OCI status | Citizenship Act 1955 | No vote, legislature membership or constitutional office |

### Wider context and examples

- A foreign tourist may invoke Article 21 if deprived of personal liberty without lawful
  procedure, but cannot claim a citizen's freedom under Article 19.
- Diplomatic immunity is different from the enemy-alien rule. It arises from international and
  diplomatic law, especially the Vienna Convention framework, rather than from Article 22.
- Long residence or an OCI card does not convert a person into a citizen or confer political
  rights.

> **Memory line:** **14 and 21 = persons; 15, 16, 19, 29 and 30 = citizens; OCI is not
> citizenship; enemy aliens lack Article 22(1)-(2) safeguards.**
