# MCQ 042

**Topic:** Philosophy - Carvaka  
**Difficulty:** Hard  
**Coverage point:** Vyapti, upadhi, practical inference and Jayarasi  
**Status:** Completed

## Question

Consider the following statements:

1. Cārvāka questions inference partly because finite perception cannot establish an exceptionless
   vyāpti covering every possible case.
2. An upādhi is an observed instance that conclusively proves a universal relation.
3. A moderate reconstruction may permit corrigible everyday inference while rejecting inference
   as proof of transcendent entities.
4. Jayarāśi's relationship to Cārvāka is uncontested, and he clearly defends the four-element
   ontology as certain.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. 1 and 2 only  
B. 1 and 3 only  
C. 2, 3 and 4 only  
D. 1, 3 and 4 only

## Response and explanation

**Your answer:** D  
**Correct answer:** B  
**Result:** Incorrect

Statements 1 and 3 are correct.

### Meaning of the terms

- **Vyāpti:** an unconditional, universal relation between the reason and what is inferred. In the
  standard example, “wherever there is smoke, there is fire” claims a vyāpti between smoke and
  fire.
- **Upādhi:** a hidden limiting condition that makes an apparent universal relation too broad.
  It does not prove vyāpti; it exposes why an alleged vyāpti may fail. For example, a particular
  kind of combustion or fuel may be the unnoticed condition behind observed smoke.
- **Practical/lokasiddha inference:** everyday, world-established expectation used for action,
  such as expecting rain from dark clouds. It may be useful and corrigible without possessing
  absolute philosophical certainty.
- **Jayarāśi Bhaṭṭa:** author of the *Tattvopaplavasimha*, often associated with a radical sceptical
  tendency. Whether he should straightforwardly be classified as a standard Cārvāka is disputed.

### Statement-by-statement analysis

1. **Correct.** Finite observation covers only some places and times. It cannot directly inspect
   every past, future, distant and possible case required for an exceptionless universal.
2. **Incorrect.** Upādhi is not confirming evidence. It is a concealed qualifier or defeating
   condition that reveals why a generalisation may be unreliable.
3. **Correct.** Moderate interpretations distinguish useful everyday inference from inference
   claiming certainty about imperceptible entities such as an eternal soul, karma or God.
4. **Incorrect.** Jayarāśi's affiliation is contested, and his scepticism attacks established
   definitions and truth claims rather than securely defending a dogmatic four-element ontology.

> **Core lesson:** Cārvāka's strongest target is not every practical expectation, but the move
> from limited experience to certain universal or transcendent conclusions.
