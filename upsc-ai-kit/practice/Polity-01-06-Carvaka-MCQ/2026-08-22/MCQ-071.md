# MCQ 071

**Topic:** Polity 01 - Historical Background  
**Difficulty:** Hard  
**Coverage point:** Charter Acts of 1833 and 1853; Indian Councils Act 1861  
**Status:** Correct

## Question

Consider the following statements:

1. The Charter Act of 1833 ended the East India Company's commercial activities and made the
   Governor-General of Bengal the Governor-General of India.
2. The Charter Act of 1853 abolished Company rule and transferred the government of India
   directly to the British Crown.
3. The Indian Councils Act of 1861 introduced direct elections and responsible government in the
   legislative councils.
4. The Charter Act of 1853 separated the legislative and executive functions of the
   Governor-General's Council and opened the way for recruitment to the civil service through
   competition.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. 1 only  
B. 2 and 3 only  
C. 1 and 4 only  
D. 1, 2 and 4 only

## Response and explanation

**Learner's answer:** C  
**Correct answer:** C - Statements 1 and 4 only  
**Result:** Correct

### Statement-by-statement analysis

1. **Correct.** The Charter Act of 1833 ended the Company's remaining commercial activities,
   making it a purely administrative body. It redesignated the Governor-General of Bengal as the
   **Governor-General of India**; Lord William Bentinck was the first to hold that office.
2. **Incorrect.** The Charter Act of 1853 did not abolish Company rule. Governance was transferred
   from the Company to the British Crown by the **Government of India Act 1858**, following the
   Revolt of 1857.
3. **Incorrect.** The Indian Councils Act of 1861 associated nominated Indian non-official members
   with legislation and recognised the portfolio system. It did not establish direct elections or
   responsible government.
4. **Correct.** The 1853 Act separated the legislative and executive functions of the
   Governor-General's Council by adding legislative members. It also opened the civil services to
   competition in principle; the Macaulay Committee of 1854 helped implement that system.

### Technical terms

- **Charter Act:** legislation periodically renewing and altering the East India Company's legal
  authority and functions.
- **Responsible government:** an executive that remains in office only while enjoying the
  confidence of the elected legislature. The 1861 councils did not possess this power.
- **Portfolio system:** allocation of particular government departments to individual executive
  council members.
- **Open competition:** recruitment through a competitive examination rather than patronage-based
  nomination.

### Timeline

| Year | Constitutional development |
|---:|---|
| 1833 | Company's commercial functions ended; Governor-General of India created |
| 1853 | Legislative and executive functions of the Governor-General's Council separated |
| 1854 | Macaulay Committee recommended the competitive civil-service examination scheme |
| 1858 | Company rule abolished and governance transferred to the Crown |
| 1861 | Indians nominated to legislative councils; portfolio system recognised |
| 1892 | Councils gained budget-discussion and questioning powers with limited indirect election |

### Wider context and example

The progression was gradual rather than democratic at once. In 1861, an Indian could be
**nominated** to assist in making laws, but could neither claim a popular electoral mandate nor
remove the executive through a no-confidence vote. Thus, Indian association with legislation
preceded both meaningful election and responsible government.

> **Memory line:** **1833 = commercial end and GG of India; 1853 = council separation and
> competition; 1858 = Crown takeover; 1861 = nomination and portfolios.**
