# MCQ 063

**Topic:** Philosophy - Carvaka  
**Difficulty:** Hard  
**Coverage point:** Rejection of God, karma, rebirth, moksa and akasa  
**Status:** Completed

## Question

Consider the following statements:

1. Cārvāka rejects a creator Īśvara because such an entity is not perceptually established and
   is unnecessary for its naturalistic account.
2. It rejects karma, rebirth and mokṣa understood as transcendent or post-mortem realities.
3. Its rejection of a permanent self makes its metaphysics identical to Buddhism in every
   important respect.
4. Its rejection of ākāśa follows from its refusal to admit an element not available to
   perception.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. 1 and 2 only  
B. 2, 3 and 4 only  
C. 1, 2 and 4 only  
D. 1, 3 and 4 only

## Response and explanation

**Your answer:** C  
**Correct answer:** C  
**Result:** Correct

Statements 1, 2 and 4 are correct.

### Meaning of the terms

- **Īśvara:** a supreme Lord or creator-God posited by some Indian systems.
- **Karma:** unseen moral causation linking actions with consequences, potentially across births.
- **Rebirth:** renewed embodiment after bodily death.
- **Mokṣa:** liberation from bondage or the cycle of rebirth.
- **Ākāśa:** ether or space accepted as an element/substance by several Indian schools.
- **Naturalism:** explanation through nature and material processes rather than transcendent
  agents.

### Statement-by-statement analysis with examples

1. **Correct.** Cārvāka finds no perceptual evidence for a creator and explains the world through
   material elements and their natures.  
   **Example:** Fire burns through its natural character, not because an invisible deity directs
   each flame.
2. **Correct.** These doctrines require entities or processes continuing beyond perceptible bodily
   life.  
   **Example:** Cārvāka accepts observable consequences of conduct but rejects an invisible karmic
   balance transferred into another birth.
3. **Incorrect.** Cārvāka and Buddhism both reject a permanent ātman, but their explanations
   differ substantially.  
   **Example:** Buddhism accepts karma, rebirth and dependent causal continuity without an eternal
   self; Cārvāka rejects rebirth and treats consciousness as body-dependent.
4. **Correct.** The four perceptible elements are admitted while ākāśa is excluded.  
   **Example:** Earth, water, fire and air are connected to sensory experience; an imperceptible
   ether is not admitted merely through another school's testimony or inference.

### Comparison

```text
Cārvāka: no permanent self + no rebirth + material body
Buddhism: no permanent self + rebirth through causal continuity
```

> **Core lesson:** agreement on one negative thesis—no eternal self—does not make two complete
> philosophical systems identical.
