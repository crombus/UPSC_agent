## BASIC MCQS / REMEDIATION

### Practice protocol

Attempt every item before reading its key. The correct position was fixed **independently for each
question** from a randomised draw, so there is no rotation, cycle or seed-derived sequence to
exploit; the four positions occur eight times each and twice in every consecutive block of eight, no
position repeats on consecutive questions, and no window of four items forms an A→B→C→D rotation or
an ABAB alternation. Option length, specificity, grammar and formatting are balanced so that the
correct answer is not visually identifiable; the key is the longest option in only a minority of
items and every distractor carries comparable doctrinal detail.

**Composition:** 24 core diagnostics (items 1–24) covering all twenty-two teaching sessions, then 8
remedial drills (items 25–32) targeting the errors this topic reliably produces.

#### CORE DIAGNOSTICS

#### MCQ 1. What Aurobindo's system is built to refuse

Which statement best identifies the structural problem that Aurobindo's philosophy is constructed to
solve?

A. That Vedānta had never produced an account of ultimate reality adequate to modern science
B. That Indian philosophy lacked a theory of society and needed a political supplement
C. That the Upaniṣads contain no doctrine of liberation and therefore require a substitute
D. That the ascetic and the materialist each affirm one pole of reality and deny the other

**Answer: D.** That the ascetic and the materialist each affirm one pole of reality and deny the
other

- **A:** Aurobindo does not present his metaphysics as a scientific correction; he supplements
  proximate description with an account of consciousness, and the adequacy of Vedānta to
  science is not the problem he sets himself.
- **B:** *The Human Cycle* and *The Ideal of Human Unity* bound a collective horizon, but the
  collective material is a consequence of the evolution and yoga doctrines and never the
  generating problem.
- **C:** The Upaniṣadic tradition supplies exactly the Absolute Aurobindo accepts; his quarrel is
  with illusionism about the world, not with the presence of a liberation doctrine.
- **D:** Correct — each position is credible on its own evidence and incredible on the other's, so
  the fault lies in the negation rather than the affirmation, and involution and evolution
  are the machinery that lets both affirmations be kept.

**Examiner trap 1:** Candidates open with biography or the freedom movement and spend the
introduction on context, when the marks are in naming the denial being refused and the doctrine that
replaces it.

#### MCQ 2. The Absolute and the world

Consider the following statements about Aurobindo's ultimate reality.

1. He accepts Existence–Consciousness–Bliss as ultimate reality in the Upaniṣadic manner.
2. He rejects illusionism as the final truth about the world and treats the world as real
   self-manifestation.
3. He denies transcendence in order to secure the reality of the world.

Which of the statements is or are correct?

A. Statements 1 and 3 only, because a genuinely real world is incompatible with a transcendent
   Absolute
B. Statements 1 and 2 only, since transcendence is retained and only its cancellation is denied
C. Statement 2 only, as Aurobindo replaces the Upaniṣadic Absolute with a purely evolutionary
   principle
D. All three statements, because world-affirmation entails the surrender of transcendence

**Answer: B.** Statements 1 and 2 only, since transcendence is retained and only its cancellation is
denied

- **A:** Reverses the exam-safe formulation; the Divine is not exhausted by the world, and that
  reservation is exactly what keeps the position non-dual rather than pantheist.
- **B:** Correct — Aurobindo accepts Brahman and rejects illusionism as final, while insisting that
  transcendence does not have to cancel immanence.
- **C:** He does not replace the Upaniṣadic Absolute at all; he redescribes its character as dynamic
  and creative while retaining Existence–Consciousness–Bliss.
- **D:** Treats world-affirmation and transcendence as competitors, which is the very assumption the
  dynamic Absolute is introduced to dissolve.

**Examiner trap 2:** The sentence "Aurobindo rejects **māyā**" is written reflexively; what he
rejects is illusionism as the *final truth about the world*, and the vocabulary of divine creative
power can itself be read sympathetically.

#### MCQ 3. Why the Absolute can manifest at all

Which statement best expresses the role of consciousness-force (**cit-śakti**) in Aurobindo's
system?

A. It is a second substance beside the Divine that carries out creation on the Divine's behalf
B. It is the mental faculty by which a yogin becomes aware of the higher planes
C. It is the Divine's own power, so consciousness is force and manifestation needs no external
   agency
D. It names the involvement of consciousness in matter and is therefore simply another name for
   involution

**Answer: C.** It is the Divine's own power, so consciousness is force and manifestation needs no
external agency

- **A:** Making it a second substance reintroduces the dualism Aurobindo refuses; it is a power of
  the Divine, not a partner of the Divine.
- **B:** It is an ontological principle, not a faculty of the practitioner; faculties belong to the
  graded planes, not to the Absolute's own dynamism.
- **C:** Correct — because consciousness (**cit**) is inseparable from force, a purely static
  Absolute could neither involve nor evolve, and this is Aurobindo's objection to a strictly
  qualityless reading of Advaita.
- **D:** Involution is the process of graded self-concealment; consciousness-force is what makes any
  such process possible, so the two are related but not identical.

**Examiner trap 3:** Answers slide from "consciousness is fundamental" to "matter is conscious",
which discards the concept of concealment on which the whole system depends.

#### MCQ 4. What the Supermind is introduced to do

The Supermind is best described as a solution to which precise problem?

A. How the One can become genuinely Many without illusion and without an unexplained leap
B. How the individual soul can survive bodily death and continue across successive births
C. How the practitioner can maintain concentration once the mental modifications have ceased
D. How a qualityless Absolute can be an object of devotion for an embodied worshipper

**Answer: A.** How the One can become genuinely Many without illusion and without an unexplained
leap

- **A:** Correct — without a mediating principle the transition from infinite unity to finite
  diversity looks either like illusion or like a brute leap, and the Supermind is the plane
  at which the One articulates itself as the Many without ceasing to be One.
- **B:** Survival across births is handled by the individual eternal self and the psychic being, not
  by the supramental principle.
- **C:** Cessation of mental modifications belongs to Patañjali's discipline, whose goal is
  isolation rather than mediation between unity and multiplicity.
- **D:** Devotion is integrated in Integral Yoga, but the Supermind is a metaphysical grade rather
  than a device for making the Absolute worshipful.

**Examiner trap 4:** The commonest error in the topic is describing the Supermind as a superior
intellect; it is a different *order* of consciousness, not a refined rational faculty.

#### MCQ 5. Three poises and where separateness begins

Which statement correctly represents the three poises of the Supermind?

A. Individuality and separateness both begin at the third poise, which is why the Absolute is
   fragmented by manifestation
B. The three poises are three successive historical epochs in the cosmic descent of the Divine
C. The many are contained in the first, faced in the second and inhabited in the third, with
   separateness only below
D. The first poise is the Lord, the second the cosmos and the third the ego of the ordinary person

**Answer: C.** The many are contained in the first, faced in the second and inhabited in the third,
with separateness only below

- **A:** Individuality is real at the third poise but separateness is not; the distinction is the
  whole point of the doctrine and its loss makes multiplicity a fracture in the Absolute.
- **B:** The poises are self-positions of one consciousness, not dated epochs, exactly as involution
  is ontological rather than chronological.
- **C:** Correct — the comprehending poise holds all in undivided unity, the apprehending or
  creative poise projects a subject–object distinction within unity as the cosmic Self or the
  Lord, and the individualising poise stands in each centre.
- **D:** The Lord belongs to the second and not the first poise, and the ordinary ego is a
  construction below the Supermind altogether.

**Examiner trap 5:** Candidates defend the labels "comprehending, apprehending, projecting" as though
they were canonical; naming and numbering vary, and the substance rather than the vocabulary should
be defended.

#### MCQ 6. Overmind and Supermind

What is the decisive difference between the Overmind and the Supermind?

A. The Overmind is unreal while the Supermind alone has ontological standing in the system
B. The Overmind distributes one truth into independent powers, while the Supermind holds indivisible
   truth
C. The Overmind belongs to involution only, while the Supermind belongs to evolution only
D. The Overmind is reached by devotion and the Supermind by knowledge, so the difference is one of
   spiritual path

**Answer: B.** The Overmind distributes one truth into independent powers, while the Supermind holds
indivisible truth

- **A:** The Overmind is a genuine plane of cosmic powers and vast spiritual attainment; calling it
  unreal imports an illusionism Aurobindo has already refused.
- **B:** Correct — the Overmind is where unity first breaks into independently self-existent powers,
  which makes it the last plane before ignorance becomes possible, whereas the Supermind
  alone is indivisible truth-consciousness.
- **C:** Both planes occur in the descent and in the ascent; the gradation is a single ladder
  traversed in two directions.
- **D:** Integral Yoga synthesises knowledge, devotion and works, so no plane is the private
  property of one path.

**Examiner trap 6:** Answers treat the Overmind as a slightly weaker Supermind, losing the structural
point that the difference is between distributed and indivisible truth rather than between degrees of
light.

#### MCQ 7. The ascent ladder

Arrange the following planes in Aurobindo's usual **ascending** order, beginning immediately above
ordinary mind.

1. Overmind
2. Illumined Mind
3. Higher Mind
4. Intuition

Which is the correct ascending sequence?

A. 2 → 3 → 1 → 4, since intuition is the final rung immediately below the Supermind
B. 1 → 4 → 2 → 3, since the overmental plane is the first attained after ordinary mentality
C. 4 → 3 → 2 → 1, since intuitive flashes precede any spiritual ideation
D. 3 → 2 → 4 → 1, since intuition stands above illumination and below the overmental plane

**Answer: D.** 3 → 2 → 4 → 1, since intuition stands above illumination and below the overmental
plane

- **A:** Places Illumined Mind before Higher Mind and makes intuition the final rung, which
  displaces the Overmind from its position as the last plane before the Supermind.
- **B:** Puts the Overmind first, which inverts the whole gradation and would make ignorance
  possible before mind has been transcended at all.
- **C:** Treats intuitive flashes as the lowest of the spiritual ranges, whereas Higher Mind is the
  large ideative consciousness immediately above ordinary thought.
- **D:** Correct — the order usually given for the ascent is Higher Mind, Illumined Mind, Intuition,
  Overmind and then Supermind.

**Examiner trap 7:** Writing the descent order when the question asks for ascent, or vice versa, is
an avoidable and highly visible error in any answer on the double movement.

#### MCQ 8. Grades of knowing

Ordinary human knowledge, in Aurobindo's graded scheme, is best classified as which of the
following?

A. Wholly separative indirect knowledge, since it represents rather than possesses the thing known
B. Knowledge by intimate direct contact, since the mind inwardly enters what it studies
C. Knowledge by identity in an attenuated form, since every act of knowing is ultimately a
   self-knowing
D. Knowledge by separative direct contact, since perception reaches the object from outside

**Answer: A.** Wholly separative indirect knowledge, since it represents rather than possesses the
thing known

- **A:** Correct — mental knowledge is partial, mediated and error-liable, which is why Aurobindo's
  remedy is a change of *mode* rather than an accumulation of content.
- **B:** Intimate direct contact bridges separation and belongs to higher spiritual or intuitive
  contact, not to discursive intellect.
- **C:** Knowledge by identity is native to Existence–Consciousness–Bliss and the Supermind, and
  treating ordinary knowing as its weak form erases the very contrast that justifies descent.
- **D:** Sense-perception is direct but external and is the third grade; ordinary knowledge is wider
  than perception and works chiefly through concepts and inference.

**Examiner trap 8:** Knowledge by identity is glossed as "intuition", which reduces a distinct
supramental mode of knowing by being to a quick mental guess.

#### MCQ 9. The involution principle

Which formulation states the involution principle most accurately?

A. Consciousness is produced by matter once matter reaches sufficient organisational complexity
B. The Divine descended into matter at a datable moment, after which evolution began
C. Nothing can evolve out of Matter unless its power was already involved in it
D. Matter and Spirit are two independent ultimates whose interaction generates the world-process

**Answer: C.** Nothing can evolve out of Matter unless its power was already involved in it

- **A:** This is precisely the emergentist position Aurobindo rejects; on his account matter is
  self-concealed consciousness, not the producer of consciousness.
- **B:** Involution is ontological and logical rather than chronological, and dating it turns the
  doctrine into a mythic fall vulnerable to irrelevant empirical objections.
- **C:** Correct — matter cannot produce life, mind and spirit if these are absolutely absent from
  it, so evolution presupposes prior involution.
- **D:** Two independent ultimates is the Sāṃkhya structure Aurobindo declines; matter for him is
  concealed consciousness within one Divine reality.

**Examiner trap 9:** The principle is presented as self-evident rather than as load-bearing and
contestable, which forfeits the marks available for identifying what actually carries the argument.

#### MCQ 10. Matter and the Inconscient

Which statement correctly describes the status of Matter and the Inconscient?

A. Matter is anti-spiritual and the Inconscient is a second principle opposed to the Divine
B. Matter is Spirit in self-oblivion and the Inconscient is consciousness hidden, not absent
C. Matter is unreal and the Inconscient is the illusion by which unreality appears real
D. Matter is real and independent, while the Inconscient is merely a name for our ignorance of it

**Answer: B.** Matter is Spirit in self-oblivion and the Inconscient is consciousness hidden, not
absent

- **A:** An anti-divine second principle would make evil ultimate and destroy the non-dualism;
  Aurobindo's Inconscient is self-oblivion, not opposition.
- **B:** Correct — matter is the densest self-concealment of Spirit and therefore holds the whole
  divine potential in latent form, which is why it can be transformed rather than merely
  escaped.
- **C:** Declaring matter unreal reinstates the illusionism the system exists to refuse and removes
  the field in which transformation is supposed to occur.
- **D:** Real and independent matter is materialism's premise; for Aurobindo matter is a derivative
  poise of concealed consciousness.

**Examiner trap 10:** The word "Inconscient" is repeated without explanation; because the phrase is
genuinely paradoxical, an answer that does not gloss it as complete self-concealment reads as
unexamined.

#### MCQ 11. What ignorance is

In Aurobindo's system, ignorance is best defined as which of the following?

A. Consciousness narrowed, self-limited and divided from its larger truth
B. The absence of information about the Divine, curable by scriptural instruction
C. A permanent anti-divine substance that resists the evolutionary process
D. The mistaken belief that the world exists, dispelled when appearance is sublated

**Answer: A.** Consciousness narrowed, self-limited and divided from its larger truth

- **A:** Correct — the ordinary ego knows itself as isolated because it loses transcendent source,
  cosmic unity, subliminal depths and temporal continuity, and what remains, taken as a
  centre, is the ego.
- **B:** If ignorance were missing information, information would cure it; Aurobindo's whole method
  presupposes that the defect is one of mode rather than of content.
- **C:** Ignorance is real in its evolutionary effects but is not an independent substance; it is a
  restricted operation of consciousness and is therefore capable of reintegration.
- **D:** This is the illusionist account of bondage; Aurobindo affirms the world and locates bondage
  in division rather than in the world's existence.

**Examiner trap 11:** Ignorance is defined negatively as "lack of knowledge", after which the answer
cannot explain why liberation must be a widening rather than an addition.

#### MCQ 12. Man as a transitional being

Which statement best captures what Aurobindo means by calling man a transitional being?

A. That human beings are biologically unstable and will eventually be replaced by a better-adapted
   species
B. That human life is a temporary illusion between two states of pure consciousness
C. That mind is intermediate, not terminal, so humanity bridges animal mind and supramental
   consciousness
D. That the human being has no fixed essence and must therefore create its own values

**Answer: C.** That mind is intermediate, not terminal, so humanity bridges animal mind and
supramental consciousness

- **A:** The claim is metaphysical rather than biological; Aurobindo supplements proximate mechanism
  with an account of consciousness and does not predict a fitter organism.
- **B:** Human life is not illusion in this system; the world is a real manifestation and the human
  stage has genuine evolutionary work to perform.
- **C:** Correct — mind is intermediate, humanity is a bridge, and therefore history is unfinished.
- **D:** Essence-creation is an existentialist thesis and does not follow from the transitional
  claim, which concerns the position of mind in a graded series.

**Examiner trap 12:** The formulation is quoted as a slogan without the supporting claim that mind is
intermediate, leaving the examiner with rhetoric rather than doctrine.

#### MCQ 13. What "already involved" does not mean

Which statement correctly states Aurobindo's position on involved possibility and evolutionary
novelty?

A. The power of consciousness is involved, while its later manifestation takes genuinely new
   organisation
B. Future species and institutions already exist in Matter as miniature preformed objects awaiting
   unfolding
C. Nothing genuinely new ever appears, since evolution merely rearranges what is already visible
D. Novelty is absolute, so each evolutionary stage is created from nothing at the moment it appears

**Answer: A.** The power of consciousness is involved, while its later manifestation takes genuinely
new organisation

- **A:** Correct — continuity of ontological potential is combined with novelty of evolutionary
  form, and the dispute with emergentism concerns whether novelty requires prior involution,
  not the observed biological sequence.
- **B:** Preformationism is explicitly rejected; involution concerns powers and principles rather
  than miniature objects.
- **C:** Aurobindo affirms real novelty of form, so reducing evolution to rearrangement removes the
  emergent side of his position.
- **D:** Creation from absolute absence is exactly what involution was introduced to avoid, since it
  would make emergence a miracle rather than a manifestation.

**Examiner trap 13:** Candidates write that "everything was already there", which collapses
Aurobindo into preformationism and hands the emergentist an easy victory.

#### MCQ 14. Aurobindo and Darwin

Which statement most accurately describes Aurobindo's relation to Darwinian evolution?

A. He refutes natural selection and replaces it with a spiritual mechanism of directed variation and
   ascent
B. He accepts Darwinism in full and adds only that God set the process in motion at the beginning
C. He treats biological evolution as an illusion and the spiritual ascent as the only real process
D. He supplements proximate biological description with a metaphysical account of emergent
   consciousness

**Answer: D.** He supplements proximate biological description with a metaphysical account of
emergent consciousness

- **A:** Refutation is neither claimed nor needed; the two accounts operate in different registers
  and answer different questions.
- **B:** Deism confines divine agency to an initial act, whereas involution and descent operate
  throughout and the telos is internal to the process.
- **C:** Treating biology as illusion would contradict the world-affirmation that defines the
  system.
- **D:** Correct — biological mechanisms may describe surface processes, while Aurobindo's claim is
  about the meaning and direction of emergence rather than a laboratory substitute for
  biology.

**Examiner trap 14:** The category-confusion objection is omitted from critical answers, when
conceding that transitions between explanatory levels require argument is exactly what "critically
examine" rewards.

#### MCQ 15. The fourfold individual

Match each term with its correct status in Aurobindo's analysis of the individual.

1. Self (**ātman**) — (i) evolving soul-personality formed around the divine spark
2. Individual eternal self (**jīvātman**) — (ii) functional construction of mind-life-body
3. Psychic being (**caitya puruṣa**) — (iii) eternal, unborn, immutable Spirit
4. Ego — (iv) individual centre presiding above manifestation without descending

Which is the correct matching?

A. 1–(iv), 2–(iii), 3–(ii), 4–(i)
B. 1–(iii), 2–(iv), 3–(i), 4–(ii)
C. 1–(iii), 2–(i), 3–(iv), 4–(ii)
D. 1–(i), 2–(ii), 3–(iii), 4–(iv)

**Answer: B.** 1–(iii), 2–(iv), 3–(i), 4–(ii)

- **A:** Swaps the Self and the individual eternal self and makes the ego the evolving soul, which
  destroys the distinction on which the 2021 question turns.
- **B:** Correct — the Self is immutable and never evolves, the individual eternal self presides
  over the whole line of births without descending into change, the psychic being is the one
  term that genuinely evolves, and the ego is a provisional construction.
- **C:** Makes the psychic being the unevolving presiding centre, which contradicts its defining
  role as the delegate of that centre *in* the evolution.
- **D:** Assigns immutability to the psychic being and evolution to the Self, inverting the entire
  scheme.

**Examiner trap 15:** The individual eternal self and the psychic being are treated as one term, after
which the candidate cannot say what "gradual prominence over all other parts of the being" means.

#### MCQ 16. The psychic being and its neighbours

The psychic being is correctly distinguished from the Sāṃkhya **puruṣa** on which ground?

A. The Sāṃkhya **puruṣa** is many while the psychic being is single and universal
B. The Sāṃkhya **puruṣa** is material while the psychic being is spiritual
C. The Sāṃkhya **puruṣa** belongs to liberation while the psychic being belongs entirely to bondage
D. The Sāṃkhya **puruṣa** is a static witness while the psychic being evolves through experience

**Answer: D.** The Sāṃkhya **puruṣa** is a static witness while the psychic being evolves through
experience

- **A:** Sāṃkhya's plurality of selves is real but is not the operative difference; Aurobindo also
  affirms real individual centres.
- **B:** The Sāṃkhya **puruṣa** is pure consciousness and emphatically not material; the contrast is
  misdescribed.
- **C:** Both terms are located within their systems' accounts of bondage and release, so this
  division does not identify a doctrinal difference.
- **D:** Correct — the psychic being is the evolving soul-personality that grows through lives and
  comes forward in psychicisation, whereas the Sāṃkhya witness does not evolve at all.

**Examiner trap 16:** Answers describe the psychic being as "the soul" without differentiating it
from the witness-self, which is the single most punished error in 2021-type questions.

#### MCQ 17. The order of the triple transformation

Why must psychic transformation precede spiritual transformation?

A. Because only a soul-centred base lets higher influx be assimilated rather than magnify the ego
B. Because the psychic being is the highest of the three principles and therefore acts first
C. Because spiritual transformation is optional and occurs only in exceptional practitioners
D. Because the descent of the Supermind cannot begin until the physical body has already been
   transformed

**Answer: A.** Because only a soul-centred base lets higher influx be assimilated rather than
magnify the ego

- **A:** Correct — the ordinary person is governed by surface mind, desire, habit and ego, which are
  unstable and divisive, so the psychic being must first supply an inner principle of
  discernment and sincerity.
- **B:** The Supermind, not the psychic being, is the highest principle involved; the psychic being
  leads because of its function, not its rank.
- **C:** Spiritual transformation is a necessary stage in the sequence, not an optional extra for
  the gifted.
- **D:** This reverses the order: bodily transformation is an outcome of the supramental descent
  rather than its precondition.

**Examiner trap 17:** The three stages are listed in the right order but the *reason* for the order is
omitted, which turns an argued sequence into an arbitrary one.

#### MCQ 18. What makes Integral Yoga integral

Which set correctly states the three senses in which Aurobindo's yoga is integral?

A. Integration of scriptures, of sects and of nations under one recognised spiritual authority
B. Integration of concentration, posture and breath into a single graded discipline
C. Integration of paths; of mind, life and body; and of individual and collective destiny
D. Integration of knowledge and devotion only, since works belong to the preparatory stage

**Answer: C.** Integration of paths; of mind, life and body; and of individual and collective
destiny

- **A:** No claim of institutional or scriptural unification is made; the integrations are
  methodological, psychological and teleological.
- **B:** These are limbs of the eight-limbed discipline, which Integral Yoga explicitly does not
  take as its model.
- **C:** Correct — knowledge, devotion and works are combined into one many-sided discipline, the
  whole being rather than the inner life alone is transformed, and the horizon is terrestrial
  and collective rather than private liberation.
- **D:** Works are integral rather than preparatory, and *Essays on the Gita* is the warrant for
  treating action as spiritual in its own right.

**Examiner trap 18:** Surrender is described as passivity or resignation, when it is conscious
offering to the Divine Force and is what distinguishes Integral Yoga from spiritual voluntarism.

#### MCQ 19. The two failure modes of the double movement

Which statement correctly explains why both ascent and descent are required?

A. Ascent alone may liberate while leaving the lower nature untransformed; descent alone is
   impossible
B. Ascent is impossible without descent, and descent alone can leave the higher planes wholly
   uncontacted
C. Ascent belongs to knowledge and descent to devotion, so both are needed for a complete
   temperament
D. Ascent transforms the body and descent transforms the mind, so the two divide the work between
   them

**Answer: A.** Ascent alone may liberate while leaving the lower nature untransformed; descent alone
is impossible

- **A:** Correct — this is the four-line argument behind the 2024 statement, and it is what
  distinguishes transformation from escape.
- **B:** Reverses both failure modes; ascent is the movement that can occur first, and descent is
  the one that presupposes contact.
- **C:** Integral Yoga synthesises the paths rather than assigning each to a movement, so this
  misdescribes both the method and the movements.
- **D:** Descent brings peace, light, force, wideness and finally supramental truth into mind, life
  **and** body; the work is not divided by faculty.

**Examiner trap 19:** The body is dropped from the list of what receives the descent, which quietly
concedes the ascetic's premise in an answer meant to refute it.

#### MCQ 20. Aurobindo and Patañjali

Which contrast between Aurobindo and Patañjali is stated correctly?

A. Patañjali aims at transformation of nature while Aurobindo aims at isolation of consciousness
B. Patañjali aims at release **from** nature while Aurobindo aims at transformation **of** nature
C. Both aim at the same goal and differ only in the sequence of practices they recommend
D. Patañjali is non-dualist in metaphysics while Aurobindo is dualist about consciousness and nature

**Answer: B.** Patañjali aims at release **from** nature while Aurobindo aims at transformation
**of** nature

- **A:** Exactly inverts the two positions; isolation (**kaivalya**) is Patañjali's goal and
  transformation is Aurobindo's.
- **B:** Correct — the single preposition carries the whole contrast, and everything about method
  and world-attitude follows from it.
- **C:** The goals are opposed, not the sequences: one system's completion is cessation and
  separation, the other's is truth-conscious participation in divine life.
- **D:** Patañjali works within a Sāṃkhya–Yoga dualism while Aurobindo's frame is a dynamic
  non-dualism, so the metaphysical labels are reversed here.

**Examiner trap 20:** Aurobindo's supramental knowledge is identified with Patañjali's truth-bearing
insight (**ṛtambharā prajñā**), although the latter is a purified mental modification that must itself
cease before isolation.

#### MCQ 21. The gnostic being and the Life Divine

Which statement correctly describes the Life Divine in Aurobindo's philosophy?

A. A post-mortem heaven attained by those who have completed the supramental transformation
B. A political order established by spiritually advanced individuals governing on the Divine's
   behalf
C. A state of private mystical absorption in which the world ceases to present itself to
   consciousness
D. An embodied earthly existence governed increasingly by supramental truth-consciousness

**Answer: D.** An embodied earthly existence governed increasingly by supramental
truth-consciousness

- **A:** Divine life is explicitly not post-mortem bliss; the whole force of the doctrine is that
  divinisation occurs here.
- **B:** Collective transformation means the spread of a new principle of consciousness, never rule
  by a self-certified spiritual elite.
- **C:** Private absorption is precisely what Aurobindo classes as insufficient, since ascent
  without descent leaves the lower nature untransformed.
- **D:** Correct — and its individual form is the gnostic being, in whom knowledge is direct and
  integral and body and life are instruments rather than obstacles.

**Examiner trap 21:** The 2025 statement is read as an attack on the ascetic's spirituality, when the
printed word is **negation** and the ascetic's affirmation of Spirit is accepted.

#### MCQ 22. Possibility and inevitability

Which statement best states the sense in which divine life is inevitable for Aurobindo?

A. Every individual alive today will attain supramental consciousness within a determinate period of
   time
B. Inevitability is merely rhetorical, since Aurobindo claims no more than bare logical possibility
C. Inevitability belongs to the inner direction of cosmic evolution, not to any timetable
D. Inevitability follows from human effort, which mechanically produces the supramental result

**Answer: C.** Inevitability belongs to the inner direction of cosmic evolution, not to any
timetable

- **A:** Universal individual attainment within a period is explicitly denied; cosmic direction does
  not fix any person's response.
- **B:** The claim is more than bare possibility, since the argument from prior emergence and the
  teleology together establish a direction.
- **C:** Correct — mind is transitional and the involved truth-consciousness presses toward fuller
  manifestation, so the tendency is stronger than mere possibility but weaker than dated
  empirical prediction.
- **D:** The scheme requires both a call from below and a sanction or descent from above, which is
  exactly what prevents effort from mechanically producing the outcome.

**Examiner trap 22:** Only one half of the 2020 demand is answered; possibility and inevitability
carry different burdens of proof and each requires its own argument.

#### MCQ 23. Evil in a world-affirming non-dualism

Why is the problem of evil sharper for Aurobindo than for an illusionist Advaitin?

A. Because Aurobindo denies that suffering is real and must therefore explain why it appears
B. Because Aurobindo makes evil an independent principle opposed to the Divine
C. Because Aurobindo has no doctrine of ignorance and therefore cannot locate the true source of
   evil
D. Because he affirms the world as real manifestation, so evil cannot be confined and sublated

**Answer: D.** Because he affirms the world as real manifestation, so evil cannot be confined and
sublated

- **A:** He does not deny that suffering is experientially real; his claim concerns what pain is
  contact with under conditions of division.
- **B:** Making evil an independent anti-divine principle would destroy the non-dualism and render
  evil ultimate, which he explicitly refuses.
- **C:** Ignorance as division is central to his account and is precisely where evil is located, so
  the premise of this option is false.
- **D:** Correct — and the free-will defence is equally unavailable, because suffering rooted in the
  Inconscient precedes human moral choice.

**Examiner trap 23:** The claim that pain is delight received by a too-divided consciousness is
quoted without its guard, so the answer reads as a denial of suffering rather than a metaphysical
thesis about distorted contact.

#### MCQ 24. The strongest objection

Which is the strongest philosophical objection to Aurobindo's system, and what is the correct
assessment of its force?

A. That the doctrine of the Divine Mother introduces theism into a non-dual system and is therefore
   internally inconsistent
B. That the involution premise is load-bearing and close to question-begging against an emergentist
C. That Aurobindo's account of the planes contradicts the Upaniṣadic texts he claims to be
   interpreting
D. That his theory of knowledge collapses into scepticism, since mental knowledge is declared
   unreliable

**Answer: B.** That the involution premise is load-bearing and close to question-begging against an
emergentist

- **A:** The Divine Mother is the Divine's own power rather than a second deity, so the charge of
  inconsistency does not get a grip.
- **B:** Correct — the verification objection can be partly answered, since a future evolutionary
  possibility need not be presently demonstrable, whereas involution is doing argumentative
  work now and a naturalist who denies directionality is committed to no checkable
  prediction.
- **C:** Whether his readings are exegesis or reconstruction is genuinely contested, but this is a
  hermeneutic dispute rather than the load-bearing philosophical weakness.
- **D:** Mental knowledge is declared partial and mediated, not worthless, and the remedy offered is
  a change of mode rather than a suspension of judgement.

**Examiner trap 24:** Criticisms are listed without replies and residues, producing a paragraph that
summarises secondary opinion instead of performing the objection–reply–residue movement that
"critically examine" requires.

#### REMEDIAL DRILLS

#### MCQ 25. A superior intellect is not a supramental principle

A candidate writes that the Supermind is "the highest development of the human rational faculty".
What is the precise error?

A. The Supermind is a plane of devotion rather than of knowledge, so reason is the wrong faculty
B. The Supermind belongs only to the descent and cannot be reached by an ascending consciousness
C. The Supermind is identical with Existence–Consciousness–Bliss, so calling it a faculty
   understates it
D. The Supermind is a distinct order of consciousness above mind altogether, not a refinement of
   mind

**Answer: D.** The Supermind is a distinct order of consciousness above mind altogether, not a
refinement of mind

- **A:** Integral Yoga integrates knowledge, devotion and works, so no plane is characterised by one
  path to the exclusion of others.
- **B:** The gradation is one ladder traversed in both directions, and the ascent reaches the
  Supermind through the Overmind.
- **C:** Identifying the two is a different error and would remove the very notion of the
  Supermind's *position* between the Absolute and the lower creation.
- **D:** Correct — mind knows indirectly, analytically and divisively and is error-liable, whereas
  the Supermind is truth-native and knows by identity, so no amount of mental refinement
  produces it.

**Examiner trap 25:** Both errors are made in one sentence — Supermind as super-intellect and
Supermind as the Absolute — and each independently destroys the answer to the 2019 demand.

#### MCQ 26. Involution is not a dated event

A candidate writes that "involution occurred first, and evolution began afterwards". Which correction
is required?

A. Evolution actually precedes involution, since matter is prior to spirit in the order of
   experience
B. Involution and evolution are two names for a single movement, so no order between them can be
   stated
C. Involution is a logical and ontological condition of manifestation rather than a dated event
D. Involution occurred repeatedly at intervals, so the singular past tense is what is mistaken

**Answer: C.** Involution is a logical and ontological condition of manifestation rather than a
dated event

- **A:** Matter's priority in experience is exactly what involution explains; reversing the
  dependence would concede the materialist premise.
- **B:** The two are distinct movements with a definite order of dependence; collapsing them removes
  the argument that evolution presupposes involution.
- **C:** Correct — involution logically precedes evolution, and describing it as a dated cosmic fall
  turns the doctrine into myth and exposes it to irrelevant empirical objections.
- **D:** Multiplying the supposed events keeps the chronological framing that is itself the mistake.

**Examiner trap 26:** "Logically prior" is replaced by "earlier in time", which converts a
metaphysical claim into a cosmological narrative that no evidence could support.

#### MCQ 27. Divine life is not another world

Which of the following most directly contradicts Aurobindo's conception of the Life Divine?

A. That transformation includes mind, life and body rather than inner awareness alone
B. That the gnostic being acts from knowledge by identity rather than from ego, conflict and
   ignorance
C. That collective transformation is the spread of a principle of consciousness
D. That the goal is a blissful existence entered after death by those who have practised the yoga

**Answer: D.** That the goal is a blissful existence entered after death by those who have practised
the yoga

- **A:** This is Aurobindo's own position; the second integration of Integral Yoga is precisely the
  transformation of mind, life and body.
- **B:** This is the standard description of the gnostic being and is consistent with the Life
  Divine.
- **C:** This is the correct bounded statement of the collective horizon, with the guard against
  rule by a self-certified elite.
- **D:** Correct as the contradiction — divine life means divinised existence here on earth, not
  post-mortem bliss, and the post-mortem reading removes the world-affirmation that defines
  the system.

**Examiner trap 27:** The phrase "life divine" is treated as a devotional synonym for salvation,
losing the specific and examinable claim that the transformation is terrestrial and embodied.

#### MCQ 28. Which term actually evolves

In the sentence "the soul evolves through many lives and finally comes forward", which term is being
correctly described?

A. The individual eternal self (**jīvātman**)
B. The psychic being (**caitya puruṣa**)
C. The Self (**ātman**)
D. The surface ego

**Answer: B.** The psychic being (**caitya puruṣa**)

- **A:** The individual eternal self presides over the whole line of births without itself
  descending into change, so it does not evolve and does not come forward.
- **B:** Correct — the psychic being is formed around the divine spark, gathers the essence of
  experience across lives, and comes forward in psychicisation as the delegate of the
  individual eternal self in the evolution.
- **C:** The Self is eternal, unborn and immutable, and is never bound or altered by evolution.
- **D:** The ego is a provisional construction of mind-life-body that changes without being the true
  being, and its displacement is what psychicisation accomplishes.

**Examiner trap 28:** The single English word "soul" is used for three different Aurobindonian terms
in the same answer, after which no claim about what evolves can be assessed.

#### MCQ 29. Echo, not identity

Why must Aurobindo's supramental knowledge not be identified with Patañjali's truth-bearing insight
(**ṛtambharā prajñā**)?

A. Because truth-bearing insight must itself cease for isolation, while supramental knowledge is
   embodied
B. Because Patañjali denies that any knowledge above discursive mind is possible
C. Because supramental knowledge is a purified mental modification while truth-bearing insight is a
   distinct plane
D. Because the two belong to different centuries and no historical contact can be established

**Answer: A.** Because truth-bearing insight must itself cease for isolation, while supramental
knowledge is embodied

- **A:** Correct — the resemblance in truth-bearing character is real, but the destinies are
  opposite: one must cease before isolation, the other must be embodied.
- **B:** Patañjali affirms such knowledge; **ṛtambharā prajñā** arises with **nirvicāra-vaiśāradya**
  and is infallible with respect to its object.
- **C:** This reverses the two descriptions and would make Aurobindo the mentalist and Patañjali the
  supramentalist.
- **D:** Chronology is irrelevant to the doctrinal point, which concerns the ontological status and
  final destiny of the two cognitions.

**Examiner trap 29:** The sentence "Aurobindo adopts Patañjali's **ṛtambharā**" is written as though
it displayed erudition, when it erases the originality the question is testing.

#### MCQ 30. Reading the printed 2025 statement

The 2025 paper prints: 'Both Ascetic and materialist are partial in their negation of each other'.
Which reading is correct?

A. Both are criticised for what they **affirm**, and Aurobindo replaces both affirmations with a
   third principle
B. Both are criticised for what they **deny**, while what each affirms is retained in the integral
   synthesis
C. The ascetic is criticised and the materialist is endorsed, since matter is the field of evolution
D. The statement recommends a moderate position between spirituality and worldliness

**Answer: B.** Both are criticised for what they **deny**, while what each affirms is retained in
the integral synthesis

- **A:** Replacing both affirmations would leave nothing for the integral position to affirm and
  would contradict the claim that Spirit is the truth of Matter.
- **B:** Correct — the printed word is *negation*, so the ascetic's affirmation of Spirit and the
  materialist's affirmation of Matter are both kept, and only the denials are refused.
- **C:** The criticism is explicitly symmetrical; endorsing one side misreads the sentence and loses
  the structure of the answer.
- **D:** A moderate midpoint is not the claim; the synthesis is generated by involution and
  evolution rather than by splitting the difference.

**Examiner trap 30:** The statement is answered with a general essay on spirituality and materialism,
without ever decoding the operative word in the printed line.

#### MCQ 31. Inevitability without automatism

Which pair of claims does Aurobindo hold together on the inevitability of divine life?

A. Cosmic direction is fixed, and therefore individual effort and discipline are entirely redundant
B. Cosmic direction is undetermined, and individual effort alone decides the outcome
C. Cosmic direction is fixed, and individual openness and surrender remain significant
D. Cosmic direction is undetermined, and no meaningful claim about the future can be made

**Answer: C.** Cosmic direction is fixed, and individual openness and surrender remain significant

- **A:** This is the determinism objection rather than Aurobindo's position; his reply is that
  cosmic direction does not fix any individual's timetable or response.
- **B:** Effort alone cannot produce the result, since the descent is mediated by the Divine Force
  rather than achieved by the practitioner.
- **C:** Correct — the scheme requires both a call from below and a sanction or descent from above,
  which is exactly what blocks the slide from tendency to automatism.
- **D:** Aurobindo does make a directional claim; the calibration is that it is stronger than
  possibility and weaker than dated prediction, not that it is empty.

**Examiner trap 31:** "Inevitable" is glossed as "guaranteed for everyone", which is the explicitly
flagged trap for the 2020 question and misstates the only sense in which the word is defensible.

#### MCQ 32. Comparison and quotation discipline

Which practice is correct when using Western parallels and quoted formulations in an Aurobindo
answer?

A. Give the parallel briefly after the Aurobindo case, always with the disanalogy
B. Name the parallel prominently in the introduction, since it establishes comparative range and
   reading
C. Assert historical influence where the periods overlap, since convergence implies contact
D. Avoid Western comparison entirely, since Paper I Section B is confined to Indian material

**Answer: A.** Give the parallel briefly after the Aurobindo case, always with the disanalogy

- **A:** Correct — parallels are illustrative only, belong in at most one or two lines, must carry
  the disanalogy, and unverified wording should be given as attributed doctrinal formulation
  without quotation marks.
- **B:** Leading with the parallel displaces Aurobindo's own argument and signals that the surface
  resemblance has been noticed but not examined.
- **C:** Roughly same-period convergence with Teilhard is best treated as parallel development, and
  influence should not be asserted for Bergson either.
- **D:** Controlled comparison is permitted and useful; the restriction is on unqualified
  identification, not on comparison as such.

**Examiner trap 32:** "Aurobindo is the Indian Bergson" is written without the disanalogy, which the
corpus treats as a marked weakness precisely because the resemblance is genuine and therefore
tempting.
