## BASIC MCQS / REMEDIATION

### CORE UPSC MCQS

### Q1. Which method is safest for identifying an unfamiliar Indian painting tradition in a UPSC answer?

A. Start from support, technique, patronage and audience before moving to theme and style
B. Begin from the most famous school and fit the painting into it
C. Identify the religion first and assume the region next
D. Treat colour preference alone as enough evidence

**Answer: A.**

**Explanation:** A is correct because the topic insists on format, material and patronage before style labels. B and C guess identity too early. D ignores support, technique and audience, which are often decisive.

### Q2. Which pair best reflects the indigenous conceptual vocabulary of painting retained in the topic?

A. Pradakshina and garbhagriha
B. Roopbheda and Varnikabhanga
C. Squinch and pendentive
D. Minbar and mihrab

**Answer: B.**

**Explanation:** B is correct because both belong to the Shadanga vocabulary. A belongs to temple architecture, C to Indo-Islamic structural vocabulary and D to mosque terminology.

### Q3. Which statement about Ajanta's technique is safest?

A. Ajanta is an unquestioned example of true wet fresco on fresh plaster.
B. Ajanta was painted directly on bare rock without prepared ground.
C. Ajanta is safer to describe as fresco secco or tempera on prepared dry plaster than as rigid true fresco.
D. Technique is irrelevant because Ajanta matters only for Buddhist themes.

**Answer: C.**

**Explanation:** C is correct because it preserves the technique caution central to this topic. A overstates certainty, B ignores the plastered surface and D removes an important exam discriminator.

### Q4. Which statement best fits prehistoric rock art in this topic?

A. It was designed as album art for elite private viewing.
B. It belongs only to the medieval period.
C. It proves named dynasties and formal doctrinal systems.
D. It preserves painted scenes of life, movement and environment on rock surfaces, but interpretation must remain cautious.

**Answer: D.**

**Explanation:** D is correct. A confuses rock art with portable painting, B destroys chronology and C claims far more than the evidence allows.

### Q5. Which statement about the Bagh murals is safest?

A. They are paired with Ajanta as a major Gupta-era mural survival, yet they retain a stronger, more earthly outline logic of their own.
B. They are identical to Mughal album painting in material and audience.
C. They are best treated as a Bengal School experiment.
D. They belong to the same category as Kalighat bazaar sheets.

**Answer: A.**

**Explanation:** A is correct because Bagh is both linked to and distinct from Ajanta. B, C and D all place Bagh in the wrong chronology, format and patronage world.

### Q6. Which statement is safest about the Hallisalasya reference in the Bagh paintings?

A. It can be confidently assigned to a modern classical dance label.
B. It should be kept to the source limit: a dance depicted in Bagh paintings, performed in royal courts, temples and ceremonial gatherings, and described as devotional or courtly in Gupta-period art and sculpture.
C. It is securely tied to Cave 4 alone.
D. It proves that all Bagh paintings are dance manuals.

**Answer: B.**

**Explanation:** B is correct because it repeats only what the source directly supports. A invents a modern identification, C over-specificies the cave and D wildly expands the evidence.

### Q7. Which statement should be preferred when writing about South Indian murals?

A. Only Ajanta deserves mural status in Indian art history.
B. South India has murals, but they are all later copies of Mughal painting.
C. Sittanavasal, Pallava sites, Brihadisvara and Lepakshi show a long southern mural trajectory, though survival gaps require caution.
D. Murals disappear from India after the Gupta period.

**Answer: C.**

**Explanation:** C is correct because it combines continuity with evidence control. A and D erase major traditions, while B wrongly reverses chronology and influence.

### Q8. Which combination is correctly matched?

A. Pala - oil on canvas public prints; Western Indian Jain - royal hunt albums
B. Pala - only temple mural painting; Western Indian Jain - no manuscript culture
C. Pala - twentieth-century nationalist wash school; Western Indian Jain - Mughal portrait album
D. Pala - Buddhist illustrated manuscripts; Western Indian / Apabhramsa - Jain-dominant portable manuscript painting

**Answer: D.**

**Explanation:** D is correct. A, B and C deliberately scramble period, medium and patronage. Both traditions belong to the pre-Mughal portable manuscript world.

### Q9. Why is the Nimatnama important in this topic?

A. It is a concrete Sultanate-period example of Persianate-indigenous manuscript synthesis.
B. It is the canonical high point of Kangra landscape painting.
C. It is a Chola mural at Brihadisvara.
D. It is a Warli ritual wall panel.

**Answer: A.**

**Explanation:** A is correct because the Nimatnama anchors the transitional Sultanate phase. B, C and D move it to unrelated regions, periods and forms.

### Q10. Which statement about early Mughal formation is safest?

A. Babur established a massive illustrated workshop in India before all others.
B. Humayun's Persian exile helped bring painters such as Mir Sayyid Ali and Abdus Samad into the Mughal orbit.
C. Jahangir, not Humayun, first imported Persian-trained painters.
D. Mughal painting developed without Persianate contact.

**Answer: B.**

**Explanation:** B is correct. A exaggerates Babur's role, C shifts the Persian bridge too late and D denies a central fact of Mughal formation.

### Q11. Which statement best captures Akbar's contribution to painting?

A. He discouraged large illustrated manuscripts and preferred only single portraits.
B. He closed the atelier and dispersed artists.
C. He developed the Tasvir Khana as a salaried collaborative workshop for major illustrated manuscripts.
D. He abolished all Persian influence from court painting.

**Answer: C.**

**Explanation:** C is correct because Akbar's key contribution was institutional scale and collaboration. A fits Jahangir poorly, B fits later dispersal and D is historically false.

### Q12. Which statement best identifies the Jahangir phase?

A. Exclusive emphasis on rock-shelter imagery
B. Strict rejection of flora and fauna studies
C. Replacement of all paper painting by temple murals
D. Greater emphasis on album pages, individual portraiture and refined observation of nature

**Answer: D.**

**Explanation:** D is correct because the Jahangir phase is tied to muraqqa, portraiture and natural history studies. A, B and C contradict the core shift of the period.

### Q13. Which statement about later Mughal painting is safest?

A. Reduced patronage under Aurangzeb matters historically because artist dispersal encouraged regional courts to absorb miniature resources.
B. Later Mughal contraction ended Indian painting altogether.
C. Shah Jahan rejected all courtly ornament.
D. Jahangir's atelier abandoned portraiture.

**Answer: A.**

**Explanation:** A is correct because dispersal is the key analytic effect. B is too absolute, C contradicts the polished Shah Jahan phase and D reverses Jahangir's known profile.

### Q14. Which set most safely signals Deccani painting?

A. Cream-coloured Gupta sandstone and thin drapery
B. Saturated colour, elongated figures, fantastic landscape and a courtly world distinct from Mughal naturalism
C. White geometric wall figures on red ochre ground for village ritual only
D. Gold-rich devotional relief on wooden panels

**Answer: B.**

**Explanation:** B is correct for the Deccani line. A belongs to sculpture, C to Warli and D to Tanjore painting.

### Q15. Which statement about Bani Thani is safest?

A. It belongs to the Kangra school because both are lyrical.
B. It belongs to the Bundi school because both use colour boldly.
C. It is associated with the Kishangarh school and works best as a precise sub-school identifier rather than a vague Rajput label.
D. It is a Mughal natural-history study.

**Answer: C.**

**Explanation:** C is correct because Bani Thani is the Kishangarh anchor. A and B are classic trap pairings, and D moves it out of the Rajput miniature field altogether.

### Q16. Which sequence best reflects the safest Pahari teaching order?

A. Kangra -> Basohli -> Guler
B. Guler -> Mughal -> Ajanta
C. Basohli -> Kishangarh -> Gond
D. Basohli -> Guler -> Kangra

**Answer: D.**

**Explanation:** D is correct because it captures the bold-to-transitional-to-lyrical arc. A reverses the internal order, while B and C mix unrelated schools and media.

### Q17. Which statement best captures Tanjore painting?

A. It is a devotional painted object marked by bold central figures and rich ornamental surface, often using gold-rich embellishment.
B. It is mainly an Akbari manuscript style.
C. It is a prehistoric rock-art tradition.
D. It is identical to Warli wall painting.

**Answer: A.**

**Explanation:** A is correct. B, C and D confuse Tanjore with entirely different media, periods and audiences.

### Q18. Which feature is most closely associated with Mysore painting in this topic?

A. White stick-like geometry on mud walls
B. Fine line and gesso-based devotional finish
C. Dynamic hunt scenes in Kota forests
D. Album portraiture of Jahangir

**Answer: B.**

**Explanation:** B is correct because Mysore painting is remembered for refined line and gesso work. A is Warli, C is Rajput and D is Mughal.

### Q19. Which statement best defines Company painting?

A. It is the same as Bengal School because both arose under colonial rule.
B. It is identical to prehistoric rock painting because both record environment.
C. It is a colonial-period hybrid where Indian artists responded to European patrons, documentary needs and changing market tastes.
D. It is a Gupta manuscript style.

**Answer: C.**

**Explanation:** C is correct. A collapses different historical agendas, B ignores medium and context, and D is chronologically impossible.

### Q20. Which statement best fits Kalighat painting?

A. It is a hill-court miniature of Kangra.
B. It is a Mughal flora-fauna album.
C. It belongs only to temple murals and never to market circulation.
D. It is an urban market-facing painted sheet tradition linked to the Kolkata-Kalighat milieu.

**Answer: D.**

**Explanation:** D is correct because Kalighat belongs to an urban bazaar-temple-market world. A and B confuse it with elite miniature traditions, while C denies its market character.

### Q21. Which statement about the Bengal School is safest?

A. It uses style as a cultural-national response and is strongly associated with Abanindranath Tagore and Bharat Mata.
B. It is merely a continuation of Akbar's Tasvir Khana.
C. It is identical to Company painting because both use paper.
D. It can be reduced to a simple rejection of all non-Indian influences.

**Answer: A.**

**Explanation:** A is correct because it captures both personnel and purpose. B is absurdly anachronistic, C ignores the ideological break and D is too crude for the nuanced modern turn.

### Q22. Which statement most safely distinguishes Santiniketan and the Progressive Artists Group?

A. Both are pre-Mughal manuscript schools.
B. Santiniketan widened experimentation through pedagogy and cultural synthesis, while the Progressive Artists Group reopened modern Indian painting through a different post-1947 modernist language.
C. Both are purely temple mural traditions.
D. Both are only folk arts of eastern India.

**Answer: B.**

**Explanation:** B is correct and keeps the distinction analytical. A, C and D shift both movements into wholly wrong periods and formats.

### Q23. Which statement about Pattachitra is safest?

A. It is mainly a Mughal court portrait genre.
B. It is a nineteenth-century oil public-print tradition.
C. It is linked to Odisha, treated cloth or palm leaf, ornate framing and Jagannath-Vaishnava thematic worlds.
D. It is the same as Gond because both use patterning.

**Answer: C.**

**Explanation:** C is correct because it identifies region, material and theme together. A and B misplace the form, while D erases the strong regional and devotional specificity.

### Q24. Which statement best identifies Kalamkari?

A. It is a Jain palm-leaf manuscript school.
B. It is a Gupta cave mural.
C. It is a Pahari court miniature.
D. It is a treated-cloth painting tradition associated with pen work or block printing in centres such as Srikalahasti and Machilipatnam.

**Answer: D.**

**Explanation:** D is correct because Kalamkari is tied to textile preparation and line work on cloth. A, B and C place it in unrelated historical traditions.

### Q25. Which statement about Warli painting is safest?

A. It is a ritual wall-painting tradition associated with geometric figures, white pigment and a red ochre ground.
B. It is a Mughal imperial manuscript style.
C. It is the same as Tanjore because both can depict deities.
D. It belongs only to Buddhist monastic caves.

**Answer: A.**

**Explanation:** A is correct because it captures surface, colour logic and ritual setting. B, C and D confuse Warli with court, devotional-panel and cave-mural traditions.

### Q26. Which statement best fits Gond painting in the safest exam language?

A. It is only a medieval Rajput manuscript school.
B. It is a tribal painting tradition that treats nature as sacred and often builds forms through dense patterning, lines and dots.
C. It is identical to Pattachitra because both use treated cloth.
D. It must always be called a Gupta mural survival.

**Answer: B.**

**Explanation:** B is correct because Gond painting is best identified through community belief, natural themes and patterned surface logic. A, C and D impose false school, material or chronology claims.

### Q27. Which statement best explains why portable formats changed Indian painting?

A. Portability eliminated all regional variation.
B. Small formats prevented narrative altogether.
C. Palm leaf, paper and album formats compressed space, sharpened line and altered audience from collective wall-viewing to more focused manuscript or folio viewing.
D. Portable painting made technique irrelevant.

**Answer: C.**

**Explanation:** C is correct because format affects composition, viewing habit and circulation. A and B are too absolute, while D ignores the importance of materials and handling.

### Q28. Which statement about mural survival should be accepted?

A. Whatever survives today represents the full original output of a period.
B. South Indian mural remains are too fragmentary to say anything useful.
C. Repainting and reuse only affect miniature painting, not murals.
D. Wall-painting survival is uneven because plaster loss, repainting and architectural reuse distort what remains visible.

**Answer: D.**

**Explanation:** D is correct because it states the methodological caution directly. A is naive, B is too sceptical and C is simply false.

### Q29. Which statement about the Ajanta-Bagh comparison is safest?

A. Both belong to the great mural tradition, but Bagh should be used to show regional variation within the Gupta-era visual field rather than treated as Ajanta's clone.
B. Bagh is a Mughal workshop and Ajanta a Bengal School experiment.
C. Ajanta is portable manuscript art while Bagh is market painting.
D. Neither site is useful for chronology.

**Answer: A.**

**Explanation:** A is correct because it preserves both comparison and distinction. B and C destroy chronology and medium, while D ignores why the pair repeatedly appears in exam routes.

### Q30. Which statement best explains the rise of regional miniature schools after Mughal contraction?

A. Regional schools arose because all earlier manuscript traditions disappeared.
B. Reduced central patronage and the movement of artists helped regional courts adapt shared miniature resources in distinctive ways.
C. Regional schools rejected all earlier Persianate or Indian influences.
D. Only one regional school survived, so comparison is unnecessary.

**Answer: B.**

**Explanation:** B is correct because it links political change with workshop mobility. A, C and D are absolutist and contradict the topic's emphasis on adaptation and plurality.

### Q31. In an objective question on Hallisalasya, which statement should be avoided as unsafe or false?

A. The Bagh source links Hallisalasya to royal courts, temples and ceremonial gatherings.
B. The source describes the dance as devotional or courtly in Gupta-period art and sculpture.
C. Hallisalasya can be securely identified with one modern named classical dance form and pinned to Cave 4 alone.
D. The safest answer stays within the source and avoids extra iconographic speculation.

**Answer: C.**

**Explanation:** C is correct because it is the unsafe statement. The source does not identify Hallisalasya with any one modern classical dance form and does not pin it specifically to Cave 4. A, B and D stay within the documented source boundary.

### Q32. Which conclusion best fits a UPSC Mains answer on painting traditions?

A. Indian painting is a single timeless style that ignores support and audience.
B. All change in Indian painting came only from foreign intervention.
C. Only mural traditions deserve serious study because miniatures are minor.
D. Indian painting shows continuity through adaptation of support, technique, patronage and audience from rock surface to mural, manuscript, album, market sheet and living ritual art.

**Answer: D.**

**Explanation:** D is correct because it synthesises the topic's central argument. A, B and C are reductive and erase the format history that structures the entire package.
