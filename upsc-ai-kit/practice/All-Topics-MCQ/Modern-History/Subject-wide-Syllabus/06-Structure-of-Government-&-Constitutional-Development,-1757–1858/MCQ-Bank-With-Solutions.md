## BASIC MCQS / REMEDIATION

### Q1. By 1765-72, the East India Company in Bengal held which combination of functions simultaneously?

A. Revenue authority (Diwani), army command, and dispensation of justice through overlapping courts, alongside its original trading-corporation identity
B. Only a trading privilege, with all governance functions remaining exclusively with the Nawab's own administration
C. Only military command, with revenue and justice remaining entirely with the Nawab
D. Full Crown-sanctioned sovereignty, formally recognised by Parliament from 1765 itself

**Answer: A.**
**Explanation:** This is this topic's own starting fact -- a chartered trading corporation had become, in practice, a territorial sovereign in Bengal, with no British constitutional framework designed for that combination; the other options each remove one of the genuinely simultaneous functions.

### Q2. What was the primary driver of Parliament's 1772-73 intervention in Company affairs?

A. A direct military defeat suffered by Company forces in Bengal
B. Company financial crisis (a bankruptcy-threatening cash crisis and a Bank of England loan request) combined with parliamentary concern over patronage and accountability
C. A wave of British public humanitarian concern over the 1770 Bengal famine
D. A formal request from the Mughal emperor for British Parliament to intervene

**Answer: B.**
**Explanation:** The famine forms background context, not the causal headline; Parliament's actual trigger was the Company's own solvency crisis and the political-patronage and accountability anxieties this crisis exposed.

### Q3. Which of the following correctly describes the Regulating Act, 1773's core executive institution?

A. A Board of Control in London directly administering Bengal
B. A Governor-General of India with an eight-member all-India council
C. A Governor-General of Fort William (Bengal) with a four-member Council, and presidency governments subordinated to Bengal in specified matters
D. A Viceroy directly appointed by the Crown, with no Council at all

**Answer: C.**
**Explanation:** The Regulating Act's institution was explicitly Bengal-specific (Fort William), with a four-member Council and only bounded presidency subordination; the all-India Governor-Generalship arrives only in 1833, and the Board of Control only in 1784.

### Q4. Why did the Regulating Act's Bengal Council prove unworkable in 1774-76?

A. Warren Hastings refused to attend Council meetings
B. The Company's Court of Directors dissolved the Council directly
C. The Supreme Court overruled every Council decision
D. A standing 3-1 majority (Clavering, Monson and Francis against Hastings and Barwell) repeatedly outvoted the Governor-General, a deadlock resolved only contingently by Monson's 1776 death, with a statutory fix arriving only in 1784

**Answer: D.**
**Explanation:** The failure was structural vote-arithmetic, not personal incompetence or judicial overreach; the correct answer names the specific 3-1 split and both the contingent 1776 resolution and the real 1784 statutory fix.

### Q5. The 1775 trial and execution of Maharaja Nand Kumar should be treated, in a rigorous answer, as which of the following?

A. A source-critical case -- Nand Kumar's separate corruption accusation against Hastings, his 1775 forgery trial and execution under English law, and the Hastings-Impey personal closeness are documented facts, while the "deliberate silencing" reading remains a contested, widely held interpretation, not settled fact
B. A case with no connection whatsoever to Warren Hastings or the Supreme Court
C. A settled instance of proven judicial murder by Hastings and Impey, established beyond dispute
D. An entirely fabricated episode with no basis in any historical record

**Answer: A.**
**Explanation:** A precise answer separates the documented facts from the contested causal interpretation, rather than either dismissing the episode or treating the conspiracy theory as proven.

### Q6. The Patna Case (1777-79) and the Cossijurah Case (1779-80) both illustrate which underlying structural problem?

A. A conflict between the Bengal Council and the Board of Control
B. The Supreme Court's undefined jurisdiction overlapping with the Company's own courts and revenue administration -- Patna in personal-law/civil-court territory, Cossijurah in revenue-administration territory
C. A dispute purely about the personal conduct of Warren Hastings
D. A rebellion by zamindars against Company rule

**Answer: B.**
**Explanation:** Both cases are best read as two angles on the same structural overlap between Supreme Court jurisdiction and Company judicial/revenue authority, which together made the 1781 Amending Act necessary.

### Q7. Which of the following was NOT a correction made by the Amending Act (Act of Settlement), 1781?

A. Excluding revenue-collection matters from the Supreme Court's jurisdiction
B. Directing that Hindu law apply to Hindus and Muslim law to Muslims in personal-law matters before the Supreme Court
C. Reducing the Bengal Council from four members to three, ending the 1774-76 style deadlock
D. Exempting the Governor-General-in-Council's own official acts from Supreme Court jurisdiction

**Answer: C.**
**Explanation:** The 1781 Act corrected Supreme Court jurisdiction only; the Council-size reduction that fixed the executive deadlock is a separate, later provision of Pitt's India Act, 1784.

### Q8. Pitt's India Act, 1784's Board of Control was empowered, in the Act's own phrasing, to do what?

A. Appoint the Governor-General without any reference to the Company's Court of Directors
B. Abolish the Company's Court of Directors outright
C. Directly govern India in place of the Company's own administration
D. "Superintend, direct and control" the Company's civil, military and revenue affairs in India, while the Company's trade monopoly remained intact

**Answer: D.**
**Explanation:** The Board of Control supervised Company administration from London through this exact statutory phrase, without displacing the Company's own commercial and administrative machinery or ending its trade monopoly, which continued until the Charter Act of 1813 began to open it.

### Q9. How did Pitt's India Act, 1784 change the size of the Bengal Council, and with what structural effect?

A. It reduced the Council from a Governor-General-plus-four to a Governor-General-plus-three, meaning the Governor-General now needed only one ally for a working majority
B. It left the Council's size unchanged but gave the Governor-General a casting vote for the first time
C. It enlarged the Council to six members, making consensus even harder to reach
D. It abolished the Council entirely, leaving the Governor-General to rule alone

**Answer: A.**
**Explanation:** This smaller council is the direct statutory response to the Regulating Act's 3-1 deadlock problem, reducing (though not eliminating) the odds of a standing hostile majority.

### Q10. Which of the following correctly distinguishes Bengal's Dual Government from Pitt's India Act's "dual control"?

A. The Dual Government continued unchanged until 1858, running parallel to dual control
B. The Dual Government (1765-72, Nawab's Nizamat vs Company's Diwani, Bengal) and dual control (from 1784, Board of Control vs Court of Directors, both based in Britain) are different institutions, in different decades, located in different places
C. They are two names for the exact same institution, just at different dates
D. Dual control refers only to Bengal's own internal administration after 1784

**Answer: B.**
**Explanation:** This is this topic's single highest-value trap -- conflating the two "dual" structures collapses two decades, two continents and four separate institutions into one confused answer.

### Q11. The Act of 1786 gave the Governor-General which specific power?

A. Unlimited dictatorial authority over all Bengal administration, overriding the Council in every matter
B. The power to appoint the Board of Control's members directly
C. A bounded override power against the Council's majority, specifically "in matters of importance affecting safety, peace, or the interests of the Empire," combined for Cornwallis with the Commander-in-Chief's office
D. The power to dissolve the Supreme Court

**Answer: C.**
**Explanation:** The 1786 override is precise and matter-specific, not a general grant of unlimited authority; Cornwallis's simultaneous holding of the Commander-in-Chief's office is a separate but related unification of civil-military command.

### Q12. What was the Charter Act of 1793's principal distinctive contribution?

A. Ending the Company's trade monopoly entirely
B. Creating the office of Governor-General of India
C. Creating the Board of Control for the first time
D. Renewing the Company's charter and trade monopoly for twenty years, and codifying Cornwallis-era Bengal regulations into a systematised, translated body of law

**Answer: D.**
**Explanation:** 1793 is a continuation-and-codification Act; it neither ended the trade monopoly (which happened only partly in 1813) nor created the Board of Control (1784) or the all-India Governor-Generalship (1833).

### Q13. Which of the following is a correct statutory provision of the Charter Act, 1813?

A. It ended the Company's monopoly of trade in India for other British subjects, while preserving the Company's monopoly over tea and China trade specifically
B. It transferred all Indian revenue collection directly to the British Parliament
C. It ended the Company's monopoly over all trade, including tea and China trade, with no exceptions
D. It abolished the Company's charter outright

**Answer: A.**
**Explanation:** The tea/China exception is the exact, frequently tested precision point of this provision; options C, C and D each misstate the Act's actual scope.

### Q14. The Charter Act, 1813 also included which further provision, distinct from the trade provision?

A. The introduction of open competitive examinations for the civil service
B. An assertion of the "undoubted sovereignty of the Crown of the United Kingdom" over the Company's Indian territories, alongside permission for missionaries to enter and preach, and a one-lakh-rupee education grant
C. The direct abolition of the Company's Court of Directors
D. The creation of a Legislative Council with additional members

**Answer: B.**
**Explanation:** These are 1813's other two verifiable provisions; the Court of Directors survived until 1858, the Legislative Council with additional members arrives in 1853, and competitive examinations are an 1853-55 development.

### Q15. Which Act designated the Governor-General of Bengal as the Governor-General of India, and who first held the resulting all-India title?

A. The Government of India Act, 1858; Lord Canning
B. Pitt's India Act, 1784; Warren Hastings
C. The Charter Act of 1833; Lord William Bentinck
D. The Charter Act of 1793; Lord Cornwallis

**Answer: C.**
**Explanation:** This is directly tested (2023 Prelims GS-I, Q50, subtopic 10); Bentinck, already Governor-General of Bengal, became the first Governor-General of India under this 1833 upgrade -- Canning's 1858 title is the separate, later Viceroy addition.

### Q16. Which two new features did the Charter Act, 1833 add to the Governor-General's Council?

A. A Secretary of State and a Council of India
B. A Viceroy and an enlarged Legislative Council with additional members
C. A Board of Control and a Secret Committee
D. A fourth, Law Member (first held by Thomas Babington Macaulay) and a Law Commission tasked with codifying Indian law

**Answer: D.**
**Explanation:** The Law Member and Law Commission are 1833's specific legal-institutional additions; the Secretary of State/Council of India belong to 1858, the Board of Control/Secret Committee to 1784, and the enlarged Legislative Council to 1853.

### Q17. The Charter Act of 1833's non-discrimination clause is best understood as which of the following?

A. A formal promise removing a legal bar to Company office by reason of religion, place of birth, descent or colour, whose practical effect remained extremely limited for decades, since it created no accessible competitive mechanism by itself
B. A clause that immediately produced large-scale Indian entry into the higher covenanted civil service
C. A clause specifically limited to only the judicial service
D. A clause that was never implemented in any form and was formally repealed soon after

**Answer: A.**
**Explanation:** This is the topic's clearest promise-versus-practice example -- a formal legal bar was removed, but no practical, accessible route existed until the 1853-55 developments, and even then only partially.

### Q18. Which of the following correctly describes a structural feature of the Charter Act, 1853?

A. It fixed a new twenty-year renewal term, like every earlier Charter Act
B. It enlarged the Governor-General's Council for legislative purposes by adding additional legislative members (including Supreme Court judges and presidency representatives), distinct from the smaller executive Council
C. It abolished the office of Governor-General
D. It created the Board of Control for the first time

**Answer: B.**
**Explanation:** 1853 uniquely continued Company rule only "until Parliament shall otherwise provide" (no fixed term) and created this first real legislative-executive institutional split; the Board of Control dates to 1784.

### Q19. What is the correct four-step sequence of civil-service reform between 1833 and 1855?

A. 1833 first competitive examination held -> 1853 Act ends the examination -> 1854 patronage restored -> 1855 non-discrimination clause added
B. 1858 introduced competitive examinations for the first time, replacing the 1833 clause
C. 1833 formal non-discrimination promise -> 1853 Act ends patronage and opens the competitive principle -> 1854 Macaulay Committee designs the mechanism -> 1855 first competitive examination held
D. 1853 alone instantly introduced full, operating competitive recruitment, with no further steps needed

**Answer: C.**
**Explanation:** Collapsing this exact four-step, four-year sequence into "1853 alone" is this topic's most common civil-service trap; the detailed service structure itself is routed to Topic 08.

### Q20. The Government of India Act, 1858 abolished which three institutions together?

A. The Regulating Act, the Amending Act, and the Charter Act of 1793
B. The Supreme Court, the Sadar Diwani Adalat, and the Legislative Council
C. The Governor-General's office, the Diwani, and the Nizamat
D. Company rule itself, the Board of Control (created 1784), and the Company's own Court of Directors -- replaced by a Secretary of State for India and a statutory Council of India

**Answer: D.**
**Explanation:** These three apex bodies are precisely what 1858 abolished; the Governor-General's office itself (now also carrying the Viceroy title) was retained, not abolished, and the Supreme Court/Sadar Diwani Adalat/Legislative Council are separate judicial and legislative institutions untouched by this specific provision.

### Q21. The 1765 Diwani grant and the Dual Government (1765-72), fully examined in Topic 04, function in Topic 06's own narrative as which of the following?

A. The trigger -- the structural anomaly (a private corporation holding sovereign revenue, war and justice powers) that made a British constitutional response necessary, without being re-derived here in detail
B. The subject of Topic 06's own most detailed analysis
C. An episode entirely unrelated to the Regulating Act, 1773
D. A development that occurred after, not before, the Regulating Act, 1773

**Answer: A.**
**Explanation:** Topic 06 deliberately treats 1765-72 only as its own starting trigger, per this topic's own boundary discipline, since the full mechanics belong to Topic 04.

### Q22. What specific financial event forced the East India Company to approach the British government in 1772?

A. The Company's own gold reserves were seized by the Nawab of Bengal
B. A cash crisis threatening the Company's solvency, leading it to request an emergency loan (understood in this topic's own terms as arranged through Bank of England channels)
C. A trade embargo imposed by France
D. A currency devaluation ordered by the Mughal emperor

**Answer: B.**
**Explanation:** This financial emergency, not a humanitarian response to the 1770 famine, is the proximate trigger that brought Company affairs before Parliament in 1772-73.

### Q23. How many members sat on the Bengal Council created by the Regulating Act, 1773, including the Governor-General?

A. Six
B. Three
C. Four
D. Five

**Answer: C.**
**Explanation:** The Regulating Act created a Governor-General plus a four-member Council (Warren Hastings alongside Barwell, Clavering, Monson and Francis) -- this specific number is precisely what Pitt's India Act reduced in 1784.

### Q24. The Regulating Act's language on presidency subordination to Bengal covered which matters?

A. All administrative matters without any exception whatsoever
B. Only commercial and trading matters
C. Only matters of religious administration
D. War and peace-related matters, subject to a narrower and more ambiguous "except in emergency situations"-type qualification later widened by Pitt's India Act, 1784

**Answer: D.**
**Explanation:** The Regulating Act's own subordination clause was narrower and more qualified than later provisions; Pitt's India Act clarified and extended presidency subordination to Bengal more broadly.

### Q25. Who was appointed the first Governor-General under the Regulating Act, 1773?

A. Warren Hastings
B. Lord Canning
C. Lord William Bentinck
D. Lord Cornwallis

**Answer: A.**
**Explanation:** Hastings became the first Governor-General of Fort William (Bengal) under the 1773 Act; Cornwallis (1786), Bentinck (1833, first Governor-General of India) and Canning (1858, first Viceroy) hold later, distinct titles.

### Q26. Which three Council members formed the standing majority that repeatedly outvoted Warren Hastings between 1774 and 1776?

A. Nand Kumar, Impey and Barwell
B. Clavering, Monson and Francis
C. Macaulay, Bentinck and Metcalfe
D. Barwell, Impey and Cornwallis

**Answer: B.**
**Explanation:** These three named Council members formed the 3-1 majority against Hastings and his sole ally Barwell -- the precise structural mechanism behind the Regulating Act's practical failure.

### Q27. What event contingently, though not statutorily, broke the Hastings-Council deadlock in 1776?

A. The Charter Act of 1793's council reforms
B. A direct Crown order dismissing Clavering and Francis
C. Monson's death, restoring Hastings's casting-vote advantage
D. The passage of the Amending Act

**Answer: C.**
**Explanation:** Monson's 1776 death was a contingent, non-statutory event; the actual statutory fix for this deadlock problem came only with Pitt's India Act's smaller council in 1784.

### Q28. Which office, established under the 1774 Charter, gave the Supreme Court at Calcutta its first Chief Justice?

A. Warren Hastings himself
B. Thomas Babington Macaulay
C. Lord Cornwallis
D. Sir Elijah Impey

**Answer: D.**
**Explanation:** Impey, a close personal associate of Hastings, was the Supreme Court's first Chief Justice under the 1774 Charter -- this personal closeness is central to the source-critical caution around the Nand Kumar episode.

### Q29. Why is forgery relevant to understanding the severity of Nand Kumar's 1775 sentence?

A. Forgery was a capital offence under the English law the Supreme Court applied, though not typically treated as a capital matter under the pre-existing Indian legal practice applied to such matters
B. Nand Kumar was actually acquitted of the forgery charge
C. Forgery carried no penalty at all under English law
D. The forgery charge was later proven to be a British fabrication with no basis in any document

**Answer: A.**
**Explanation:** This legal-severity mismatch (a capital English-law penalty applied via the new Supreme Court) is part of why the episode remains historically contested, alongside the timing relative to Nand Kumar's own corruption accusation against Hastings.

### Q30. The Patna Case (1777-79) specifically involved the Supreme Court asserting jurisdiction over which kind of matter?

A. A dispute over Company shareholding in London
B. A property/inheritance dispute already decided by a Company provincial court applying Islamic personal law
C. A criminal rebellion case
D. A revenue-collection dispute involving a zamindar's debts

**Answer: B.**
**Explanation:** Patna shows the Supreme Court reaching into personal-law/civil-court territory already settled by a Company court; Cossijurah (Q31) shows the parallel revenue-administration version of the same overlap.

### Q31. The Cossijurah Case (1779-80) specifically provoked which executive response?

A. The Supreme Court was permanently abolished
B. Parliament immediately annexed Cossijurah to the Crown
C. The Governor-General-in-Council threatened armed resistance to the Supreme Court's own process-servers over a zamindar's revenue-related debt matter
D. The Company voluntarily submitted to full Supreme Court jurisdiction over all revenue matters

**Answer: C.**
**Explanation:** This sharp executive-judicial confrontation over revenue-administration territory, alongside Patna's personal-law-territory clash, made the 1781 Amending Act's jurisdictional corrections all but inevitable.

### Q32. Which matter did the Amending Act of 1781 specifically exclude from the Supreme Court's jurisdiction, routing it instead to the Company's own revenue courts and the Governor-General-in-Council?

A. Ecclesiastical disputes
B. Commercial contract disputes between British merchants
C. Criminal cases involving Europeans
D. Revenue-collection matters

**Answer: D.**
**Explanation:** This is one of the 1781 Act's four precise corrections (alongside the official-acts exemption, the Hindu/Muslim personal-law direction, and provincial-appeal routing) -- directly responding to the Cossijurah-type conflict.

### Q33. The Amending Act of 1781 directed the Supreme Court to apply which law in personal-law matters?

A. Hindu law to Hindus and Muslim law to Muslims, specifically in matters such as marriage, inheritance, caste usage and religious practice
B. No law at all -- personal-law matters were entirely excluded from any court
C. French civil law, following the Chandernagore precedent
D. English common law uniformly to all litigants regardless of religion

**Answer: A.**
**Explanation:** This personal-law pluralism direction, examined further as a colonial governance technique in subtopic 14, is a distinct 1781 provision from the revenue-matter exclusion and official-acts exemption.

### Q34. How many members composed the Board of Control created by Pitt's India Act, 1784, including a Secretary of State and the Chancellor of the Exchequer?

A. Three
B. Six
C. Nine
D. Fifteen

**Answer: B.**
**Explanation:** The six-member Board, including these two senior ministers, gave Parliament its first standing supervisory body over Company administration -- distinct from the fifteen-member Council of India created only in 1858.

### Q35. What was the function of the Secret Committee created alongside the Board of Control in 1784?

A. To supervise missionary activity in India
B. To codify Bengal's regulations into a printed body of law
C. To provide a channel for the Board's urgent or confidential orders to reach the Company's Court of Directors without full deliberation by the whole Court
D. To try capital criminal cases in Bengal

**Answer: C.**
**Explanation:** The Secret Committee is a specific administrative mechanism within the 1784 dual-control design, distinct from the Board of Control's own general supervisory power.

### Q36. To how many members (including the Governor-General) did Pitt's India Act, 1784 reduce the Bengal Council?

A. Two
B. Five
C. Six
D. Three

**Answer: D.**
**Explanation:** This reduction from the Regulating Act's four-member Council structurally lowered the odds of a standing hostile majority, directly answering the 1774-76 deadlock.

### Q37. Lord Cornwallis, appointed Governor-General in 1786, combined that office with which other position?

A. Commander-in-Chief, unifying civil and military command in Bengal for the first time under statute
B. President of the Court of Directors
C. Chairman of the Board of Control
D. Chief Justice of the Supreme Court

**Answer: A.**
**Explanation:** This unification, alongside Cornwallis's new bounded override power under the Act of 1786, marks a significant executive-authority consolidation distinct from any judicial or legislative change.

### Q38. For how many years did the Charter Act of 1793 renew the East India Company's charter and trade monopoly?

A. Thirty years
B. Twenty years
C. Ten years
D. Indefinitely, with no fixed term

**Answer: B.**
**Explanation:** This twenty-year renewal pattern (also seen in 1813 and 1833) is broken only by the Charter Act of 1853, which abandoned a fixed term altogether.

### Q39. What was the Charter Act of 1793's specific contribution to Bengal's legal administration?

A. Creating the Supreme Court for the first time
B. Introducing competitive examinations for the civil service
C. Codifying Cornwallis-era Bengal regulations into a printed, systematised body of law, issued with translations
D. Establishing the Board of Control

**Answer: C.**
**Explanation:** This administrative codification is distinct from, and a precursor in spirit to, the more ambitious all-India Law Commission codification effort begun only in 1833.

### Q40. What was the size and purpose of the education-related grant introduced by the Charter Act, 1813?

A. Ten lakh rupees, for direct construction of English-medium schools
B. Fifty thousand rupees, for missionary institutions exclusively
C. Five lakh rupees, disbursed immediately and fully upon the Act's passage

**Answer: D.**
**Explanation:** The exact sum and delayed-implementation caveat are both precision points; the dispute over how it was eventually used belongs to Topic 09, resolved only by Macaulay's 1835 Minute on Education.

### Q41. The Charter Act of 1813 permitted which group to enter and operate in Company territory, reversing an earlier restrictive practice?

A. Christian missionaries
B. Non-Company Indian merchants only
C. French trading companies
D. Foreign diplomatic missions

**Answer: A.**
**Explanation:** This permissive missionary provision is a third, separate strand of the 1813 Act, alongside the trade-opening and Crown-sovereignty provisions.

### Q42. After the Charter Act of 1833, what was the Company's own legal status regarding trade?

A. It retained a reduced but still active trading role limited to tea and China
B. It became a purely administrative, non-commercial body holding its Indian possessions in trust for the Crown, with all commercial activity ended outright
C. It expanded its commercial activity to compensate for reduced administrative duties
D. It was permitted to trade freely in India but not with China

**Answer: B.**
**Explanation:** 1833 is the decisive break from 1813's partial opening -- the Company's commercial identity ends completely here, five decades before its administrative identity itself ends in 1858.

### Q43. Lord William Bentinck, already Governor-General of Bengal, became the first holder of which new title under the Charter Act, 1833?

A. Chairman of the Board of Control
B. Viceroy of India
C. Governor-General of India
D. Secretary of State for India

**Answer: C.**
**Explanation:** This all-India upgrade of the existing Bengal office is a distinct, separately examinable fact from the later 1858 addition of the Viceroy title to the same office.

### Q44. What was the specific mandate given to the Law Commission established under the Charter Act, 1833?

A. To manage the Company's remaining commercial assets
B. To try Nand Kumar-style forgery cases directly
C. To supervise missionary education institutions
D. To examine and codify Indian law into a unified, systematic body of codes, under Macaulay's chairmanship

**Answer: D.**
**Explanation:** This 1833 mandate set a codification process in motion that produced its major finished codes only after 1858, outside this topic's own 1757-1858 window.

### Q45. Which best describes the finished output of the 1833 Law Commission's codification mandate, within this topic's own 1757-1858 window?

D. Full completion of the Indian Penal Code by 1840

**Answer: A.**
**Explanation:** This is an important precision point -- 1833 is this topic's upstream trigger for a downstream codification story, not a completed achievement to credit to 1833 itself.

### Q46. The Charter Act of 1833's non-discrimination clause specifically barred disqualification from Company office on which grounds?

A. Educational qualification alone
B. Religion, place of birth, descent or colour
C. Prior criminal record only
D. Age and gender only

**Answer: B.**
**Explanation:** This is the clause's exact scope; its practical effect remained thin for decades, since removing a formal bar did not by itself create an accessible competitive mechanism.

### Q47. The Charter Act of 1853 continued Company rule under which distinctive condition, unlike the earlier Charter Acts?

A. A conditional renewal subject to a referendum in Bengal
B. A fixed fifty-year renewal term
C. Continuation only "until Parliament shall otherwise provide," with no fixed further term at all
D. Automatic permanent renewal with no possibility of future termination

**Answer: C.**
**Explanation:** This open-ended, terminable-at-will phrasing signals Parliament's readiness to end Company rule at any time, functioning as a structural precondition for 1858's comparatively swift transfer.

### Q48. Who sat among the additional legislative members added to the Governor-General's Council by the Charter Act, 1853?

A. Missionaries nominated by the Church of England
B. Elected Indian representatives from each presidency
C. Only serving military officers
D. Judges of the Calcutta Supreme Court and representatives drawn from the Madras, Bombay, Bengal and Agra (North-Western Provinces) administrations

**Answer: D.**
**Explanation:** These were British officials or Company nominees offering regional administrative input, not democratic representation; no Indian sat on this enlarged Council.

### Q49. What did the Charter Act of 1853 do to the Company Directors' power over civil-service recruitment?

A. It ended patronage nomination and opened recruitment to competition in principle, without itself designing or operating an examination system
B. It strengthened patronage nomination further
C. It transferred patronage power to the Governor-General personally
D. It abolished civil-service recruitment altogether

**Answer: A.**
**Explanation:** The Act's own text did not design the exam system; that required the separate 1854 Macaulay Committee, with the first actual examination held only in 1855.

### Q50. What was the specific function of the 1854 Macaulay Committee, distinct from the Charter Act of 1853 itself?

A. It negotiated the Treaty of Allahabad
B. It designed the syllabus, age limits and administrative rules for competitive civil-service recruitment under the principle the 1853 Act had opened
C. It drafted the Charter Act of 1858
D. It tried Nand Kumar's forgery case

**Answer: B.**
**Explanation:** This committee is the second of four steps in the 1833-1855 civil-service reform sequence, operationalising the principle the 1853 Act had only opened.

### Q51. In which year was the first actual competitive examination held for the Indian Civil Service under the new 1853-54 principle and mechanism?

A. 1833
B. 1858
C. 1855
D. 1853

**Answer: C.**
**Explanation:** This is the fourth and final step of the sequence -- 1833 promise, 1853 Act, 1854 committee, 1855 first examination -- and must never be collapsed into "1853" alone.

### Q52. Alongside Company rule itself, which two supervisory bodies did the Government of India Act, 1858 abolish?

A. The Supreme Court and the Sadar Diwani Adalat
B. The Legislative Council and the Law Commission
C. The Regulating Act's Council and the Amending Act's provisions
D. The Board of Control (created 1784) and the Company's own Court of Directors

**Answer: D.**
**Explanation:** These two bodies, both central to the 1784 dual-control design, were replaced by the Secretary of State for India and the statutory Council of India.

### Q53. The new office created by the 1858 Act to replace the Board of Control was described in the administrative practice of the period using which phrase?

A. "Fountain of authority"
B. "Custodian of the Crown"
C. "President of the Presidencies"
D. "Guardian of the Diwani"

**Answer: A.**
**Explanation:** This phrase, describing the new Secretary of State for India's centrality, reflects how completely political authority over Indian administration was concentrated in this single Crown minister after 1858.

### Q54. How many members composed the statutory Council of India created in 1858, and what proportion had prior East India Company service or directorship experience?

A. Six members, matching the earlier Board of Control's size exactly
B. Fifteen members, with an initial majority of seven having prior Company service or directorship experience
C. Twenty members, all newly appointed with no Company background whatsoever
