2. The owners describe the architecture as four limbs that must not be merged: protection from harm through the sexual-offences statute and the child-marriage statute; a specialised justice system under the juvenile-justice statute with its separate boards and committees; welfare and care through the umbrella mission that funds institutions, helpline, sponsorship and foster care; and independent statutory oversight through the child-rights commission that examines safeguards, inquires into violations and recommends measures; the analytical payoff is that a failure can be located in exactly one limb, so an answer that says child protection is weak has not yet said whether the defect is in the offence definition, in the adjudicating body, in the funded service or in the monitoring.
3. The Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act, 2012 defines a child as any person below eighteen years of age and is gender-neutral, so it protects all children below that age regardless of gender, and the owners flag as a recurring trap the belief that it protects only female children; the age boundary is a single bright line for this statute, which distinguishes it from the child-labour statute where fourteen and eighteen operate as two different thresholds and from the child-marriage statute where the ages recorded for a boy and a girl differ, so the examinable discipline is to state which statute's age rule is being used before using any age at all.
4. The owners record that the 2012 Act creates graded offences including penetrative sexual assault, aggravated penetrative sexual assault, sexual harassment and use of a child for pornography; that it mandates reporting by any person having knowledge of an offence; that it establishes Special Courts for speedy trial; and that it requires in-camera proceedings and child-friendly procedures with the assistance of an interpreter, a special educator or a support person; they also record that the Act covers offences committed through electronic means, which is the doorway through which the whole digital-era argument enters this topic rather than requiring a new statute.
5. The Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015 divides children into two categories that carry different institutions, different purposes and different outcomes: a child in conflict with law is one alleged or found to have committed an offence and is dealt with by a Juvenile Justice Board on a rehabilitative footing, while a child in need of care and protection is dealt with by a Child Welfare Committee which may order foster care, sponsorship, adoption or institutional care; the owners note that examiners frequently test whether a candidate holds this bifurcation, because collapsing the two turns a care jurisdiction into a criminal one in the answer itself.
6. The owners record the composition and the character of both bodies: the Juvenile Justice Board consists of a Metropolitan or Judicial Magistrate together with two social workers and has the power to conduct an inquiry and to dispose of cases concerning children in conflict with law, while the Child Welfare Committee consists of a Chairperson and four members and exercises the powers of a Bench of Magistrates in respect of children in need of care and protection; the practical consequence is that the Committee is not a court hearing an accusation and the Board is not a welfare authority, so the correct body must be named whenever the answer describes a decision about a child.
7. The owners define a heinous offence under the 2015 Act as one for which the minimum punishment under the general penal law is imprisonment of seven years or more, and they state the transfer mechanism with precision: for a child aged sixteen to eighteen alleged to have committed such an offence, the Board first conducts the preliminary assessment under Section 15 of the child's mental and physical capacity, ability to understand the consequences of the offence and the circumstances in which the offence was allegedly committed, and only after that statutory gateway may it order the transfer contemplated by Section 18(3), after which the Children's Court applies the Act's safeguards; adult treatment is therefore exceptional and never automatic, and the recurring trap is to describe it as automatic.
8. The owners record that the 2021 amendment to the juvenile-justice statute strengthened District Magistrate oversight of adoption and of child-care institutions and reclassified specified offences, and they attach an analytical caution to that change, namely that administrative accountability must not displace specialised child-sensitive decision-making; the examinable point is that the amendment moved certain decisions from a judicial or quasi-judicial route to an executive one in the name of speed and monitoring, so an answer about it must weigh faster clearance and closer inspection of institutions against the loss of a specialised forum.
9. The Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Amendment Act, 2016 sets two different rules that must not be merged: employment of a child below fourteen years is completely prohibited in any occupation or process, while an adolescent aged fourteen to eighteen is prohibited only from hazardous occupations and processes listed in the Schedule; the owners further record that the family-enterprise and child-artist exceptions carry statutory conditions and are not a general licence for child work, so the accurate formulation is a complete ban below fourteen, a hazard-limited ban between fourteen and eighteen, and narrow conditional exceptions, and the trap is to state a single ban for everyone below eighteen.
10. The owners record the subject matter of the two conventions that the audited Prelims ledger routes to this topic: Convention 138, the Minimum Age Convention, requires a national minimum age for admission to employment or work and the progressive abolition of child labour, while Convention 182, the Worst Forms of Child Labour Convention, requires urgent elimination of slavery-like practices, trafficking, forced recruitment, commercial sexual exploitation, illicit activities and work likely to harm children's health, safety or morals; the discipline is that one convention sets an age floor and the other targets categories of work irrespective of a single age rule, and no option, key or answer is recorded for that objective demand because the ledger records the official key as not held locally.
11. The Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006 records the age thresholds as below twenty-one for a boy and below eighteen for a girl, and it makes a child marriage generally voidable at the option of the party who was a child at the time of the marriage, while separately making specified situations void, namely those involving force, trafficking or an abducted child; the Act also provides for the notification of Child Marriage Prohibition Officers to prevent solemnisation and to seek injunctions; the examinable precision is that voidable is not the same as void, because a voidable marriage stands until the party who was a child elects to avoid it, whereas a void marriage has no legal existence from the outset.

13. 14. ##### Existing policy response
1. **POCSO applicability:** the Act covers offences committed through electronic means;
   NCPCR's POCSO e-Box portal allows online reporting of child sexual abuse.
2. **IT Rules (Intermediary Guidelines):** impose user-rule, due-diligence, notice/order,

4. **NCPCR monitoring:** the Commission tracks POCSO case pendency, issues advisories,

### PYQ DEMAND CARD 1 — 2025 General Studies Paper II Question 18

**Demand:** The National Commission for Protection of Child Rights has to address the challenges faced by children in the digital era; examine the existing policies and suggest measures the Commission can initiate to tackle the issue, 15 marks, 250 words, exactly as recorded in the audited 2024-2025 Mains routing ledger and as printed in the locally held official 2025 General Studies Paper II.

**Status:** Routed to this owner in the audited 2024-2025 Mains routing ledger as the owning topic. No official answer key exists for a Mains demand and none is claimed.

**Model answer (independently reasoned; no official key claimed):** Children face online grooming, sexual exploitation, coercive sharing of images, cyberbullying and exposure to harmful content; data extraction adds a distinct privacy challenge. Existing policy is not a blank slate. POCSO, 2012 covers sexual offences involving children below eighteen even when electronic media are used. NCPCR's e-Box enables reports of online sexual abuse; the national cybercrime reporting portal provides a channel for child sexual abuse material to reach police. Intermediary rules impose specified due-diligence, takedown and cooperation obligations, which should not be misdescribed as identical proactive scanning duties for every platform. The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 provides child-data safeguards, but the source's 21 July 2026 status assessment records a phased commencement and a scheduled 13 May 2027 date for substantive child-data duties. That dated assessment must be rechecked before describing current enforcement. Under its 2005 constituting Act, NCPCR can examine safeguards, inquire into complaints, inspect institutions and recommend measures; it cannot itself impose a platform sanction or prosecute an offender. It should establish a child-safe digital complaints monitoring cell, audit e-Box response and referral times, convene platforms on compliance and recommend cyber-safety modules for schools. It could press for age-appropriate design and coordinate readiness with the data-protection authority. Child-friendly evidence handling, confidentiality and links to counselling and Child Welfare Committees are essential. Measure progress by safe resolution and reduced exposure, not the number of advisories alone. Effective policy requires the Commission to connect existing police, platform, school and care responsibilities without claiming powers Parliament has not given it.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Distinguish a child in conflict with law from a child in need of care and protection under the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015, and explain why the distinction decides the institution, the purpose and the outcome. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer:** The Juvenile Justice Act, 2015 separates an alleged offender from a child who needs protection. A child in conflict with law is alleged or found to have committed an offence; the Juvenile Justice Board, comprising a Magistrate and two social workers, conducts a child-sensitive inquiry and ordinarily orders rehabilitation. A child in need of care and protection may be abandoned, abused or otherwise without adequate care. The Child Welfare Committee, chaired by a designated member with four others, decides on care, sponsorship, foster care, adoption-related measures or placement in an institution. The distinction determines procedure, safeguarding and outcome: sending an abused child before an offence-deciding Board misconstrues a need for care as guilt; treating an alleged offender only as a placement case bypasses a fair inquiry and appropriate rehabilitation. Both pathways must hear the child. Legal categories should follow the child's circumstances, not convenience of administration.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Examine whether the child-labour law of 2016 prohibits child work or regulates it. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer:** The 2016 amendment to child-labour law primarily prohibits employment below fourteen, but also regulates a different age group. It bars employment of a child under fourteen in occupations and processes; for adolescents aged fourteen to eighteen, prohibition focuses on hazardous occupations and processes specified in the Schedule. Neither age rule should be oversimplified. The conditional family-enterprise and child-artist exceptions must not hide school-displacing work. Enforcement therefore depends on inspection of hidden household and supply-chain work, school attendance and economic support to families, not merely on visible factory raids. The 2011 Census figure for workers aged five to fourteen is a dated count of main and marginal work, not a current total of hidden work. Age thresholds and exceptions must be read within this statute, not borrowed from POCSO's under-eighteen definition.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Critically examine the preliminary-assessment gateway for children aged sixteen to eighteen accused of heinous offences. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer:** The Juvenile Justice Act, 2015 permits an exceptional route for a child aged sixteen to eighteen alleged to have committed a heinous offence, defined by a minimum statutory punishment of at least seven years. Section 15 requires the Juvenile Justice Board to assess mental and physical capacity, ability to understand consequences and the circumstances of the alleged act. This is a gateway to deciding whether the case should go to a Children's Court under Section 18(3), not an automatic adult trial or a finding of guilt. The court must then apply the Act's safeguards. The rationale is to respond to grave violence without pretending that all older adolescents have identical maturity. Yet an assessment carried out without trained psychological support can confuse trauma, poverty or limited education with culpability. Public outrage cannot replace an individual inquiry or override the child's opportunity to be heard. Nor should a transfer erase the developmental purpose of the juvenile system: rehabilitation, review and safe placement remain relevant. Children in need of care and protection are a different category under the Child Welfare Committee; conflating their jurisdiction with this exceptional Board process is legally wrong. Better practice would supply independent expertise, record reasons for the capacity assessment and make review meaningful, while protecting victims throughout the proceedings. Exceptional transfer should remain evidence-based and child-sensitive rather than becoming a routine response to the seriousness of an allegation.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Assess whether prohibition alone can end child marriage in India. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer:** The Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006 creates deterrence, but legal prohibition cannot on its own remove the conditions that sustain early marriage. A girl below eighteen and a boy below twenty-one fall within its age rules. A child marriage is ordinarily voidable at the option of the party who was a child, not automatically void; marriages involving coercion, abduction or trafficking can fall within provisions making them void. Child Marriage Prohibition Officers can intervene before solemnisation and seek injunctions. These distinctions matter: wrongly treating every marriage as void can obscure a survivor's options and support claims. School dropout, insecurity during travel, household poverty, discriminatory norms and weak birth registration may still make a marriage seem protective or economically necessary to a family. A complaint route may expose a girl to retaliation if shelter, continuity of education and legal aid are absent. The NFHS-6 measure for 2023–24 on marriage before eighteen among women aged twenty to twenty-four is a dated retrospective survey indicator; it cannot by itself identify why every marriage occurred. Prevention should combine accessible schooling, safe transport, economic support and trusted reporting with officials able to act rapidly on threatened ceremonies. Post-marriage assistance must respect the affected person's safety and agency. Law sets a boundary; implementation and sustained alternatives determine whether families can cross from prohibition to changed practice.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Analyse how India's existing legal instruments address online harm to children, and identify where the framework is complete and where it is only impending. Answer in about 300 words.

**Model answer:** Digital child safety combines an existing offence framework with platform duties and an impending data-governance framework. POCSO, 2012 is gender-neutral and covers children below eighteen, including sexual offences committed through electronic means and use of a child for sexual material. A child can report abuse through the NCPCR e-Box; the national cybercrime portal routes reports of child sexual abuse material to police. These routes are not themselves trials or guaranteed takedowns. The intermediary rules require published user rules against prohibited child-sexual content and compliance with prescribed takedown and cooperation duties; obligations vary by intermediary category and applicable notice or order. There is no universal proactive scanning mandate for every platform. The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 provides child-data safeguards, with commencement under the 2025 Rules phased. The source assessment dated 21 July 2026 records that substantive child-data duties, including verifiable parental consent, were scheduled for 13 May 2027. That is a dated forward-looking position, not a claim about current implementation; commencement should be checked anew when advising in a later period. Nor does parental consent substitute for response to exploitation or grooming. Challenges include anonymous offenders, cross-platform distribution, victim identification, coercive sharing and the risk of re-traumatising a child during reporting. Police cyber units, platforms, Child Welfare Committees and specialised services need coordinated procedures for evidence preservation, removal and care. A digital-safety programme should measure safe reporting and assistance, not only notices issued. The framework addresses electronic sexual abuse now; future data duties have a different object and cannot be invoked as an already operative remedy without status verification. Agencies should keep child-safe records and verify whether referrals reach specialised support rather than treating complaints as resolved on receipt.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Evaluate the proposition that statutory oversight is the weakest link in India's child-protection system. Answer in about 300 words.

**Model answer:** Calling statutory oversight the single weakest link oversimplifies a system whose failures also arise in investigation, placements, education and household support. The National Commission for Protection of Child Rights, established under the Commissions for Protection of Child Rights Act, 2005, has a Chairperson and six members, at least two of them women. It reviews safeguards, examines complaints, inspects institutions and advises on implementation of POCSO, juvenile justice and children's education. Its recommendations and monitoring do not amount to court convictions or binding platform sanctions. In that sense follow-up is a real vulnerability: an inquiry is of little value if police fail to investigate, a Child Welfare Committee cannot secure a safe placement or an institution remains uninspected. District Magistrate oversight was strengthened by the 2021 juvenile-justice amendment, notably around adoption and child-care institutions, but speed must not eclipse specialised, child-sensitive decisions. Mission Vatsalya funds care institutions, sponsorship, foster care and the child helpline; it supports delivery rather than rewriting legal entitlements. Online harm adds multi-agency coordination problems: the Commission may monitor its e-Box, convene platforms and recommend school safety modules, but takedown powers and criminal investigations remain elsewhere. Child-labour exceptions and child-marriage prevention require local identification, safe reporting and schooling alongside legal prohibition. Oversight therefore matters as a diagnostic and accountability bridge, not as the only bottleneck. Publishing action-taken reports, strengthening independent inspection, recording children's views and tracking actual rescue, care quality and restoration would make its interventions useful. A functioning statutory monitor can identify breaches; frontline institutions and resource commitments must supply timely remedies. Distinguishing measured access from actual recovery is especially important for children without family support, whose reintegration requires continuity and independent safeguards.
