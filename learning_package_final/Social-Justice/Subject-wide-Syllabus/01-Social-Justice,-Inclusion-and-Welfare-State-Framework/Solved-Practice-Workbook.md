**Model answer:**

High growth and poor human development can coexist: aggregate output enlarges India's resources, but the conversion of income into health, education and dignity is unequal. Sen's capabilities distinguish real freedom from purchasing power.

The NFSA, 2013 makes covered households' foodgrain access a statutory claim; an eligible child's nutrition still depends on clean water, functioning anganwadis and maternal care. Article 46 directs special attention to SCs, STs and weaker sections, for whom discrimination and distance can obstruct access. Similarly, a school seat without teachers or a disabled student's accessible route does not secure learning. Unequal asset ownership and weak public services distribute growth's gains unevenly.

Growth is useful fiscal capacity, not proof of welfare. Disaggregate learning, nutrition, health and grievance outcomes by place and social group; expand quality services and remedy exclusions rather than equating GDP or registrations with development.

**Status:** Routed to this owner in the audited 2018-2023 Mains routing ledger, where the Core route is recorded as superseding the older Advanced ownership. No official answer key exists for a Mains demand and none is claimed.

**Model answer:**

India's human development has lagged economic development because income gains have not consistently crossed barriers of unequal assets, social hierarchy and weak services. Sen's capability approach distinguishes resources from the real freedom to live well.

First, caste, gender and regional inequalities shape who receives growth's returns. Article 46 recognises the special educational and economic needs of SCs, STs and weaker sections; an identical allowance need not offset unequal starting conditions. Second, an entitlement is not an outcome. NFSA, 2013 foodgrain rights help covered households, but identification errors, distant shops and poor complaints handling can prevent receipt. Third, registration and enrolment do not measure quality: a classroom without teaching or a clinic without medicines delivers few capabilities. Stigma in a ration queue can reduce both uptake and dignity. Finally, ownership of land and capital affects families' capacity to survive shocks; transfers relieve hardship without necessarily changing those foundations.

Growth finances social spending and statutory claims strengthen accountability, so a blanket claim that progress has failed would be misleading. Yet national averages obscure gaps among districts, genders and disability groups. Combine reliable universal schooling and primary health with additional targeted support, accessible delivery, anti-discrimination and disaggregated outcome monitoring. Human development requires converting economic resources into measurable, fairly shared freedoms.

**Model answer:**

Affirmative action has expanded formal opportunities, but a reserved seat is not automatically a usable opportunity. Constitutional equality must be judged by access, dignity and retention.

Article 46 directs special care for SCs, STs and weaker sections; reservations counter historical barriers in education and public employment. Yet first-generation students may arrive with unequal schooling, face unaffordable transport or discriminatory campuses, and leave before completing study. A fuller constitutional benefit requires redistribution, correction of wrongdoing and recognition of equal dignity together. Reserved positions alone cannot remedy inequalities in preparatory schooling and family resources.

### PYQ DEMAND CARD 4 — 2025 General Studies Paper II Question 16

**Status:** Routed to this owner in the audited 2024-2025 Mains routing ledger. No official answer key exists for a Mains demand and none is claimed.

**Model answer:**

Unequal ownership is a structural driver of poverty even amid rising production: people with land, housing or capital earn returns and absorb shocks, while landless workers depend on unstable wages. This helps explain India's poverty paradox of aggregate prosperity coexisting with persistent deprivation.

Property supplies collateral and bargaining power; caste- and gender-based obstacles to ownership reproduce intergenerational disadvantage. Article 46's special-care direction recognises unequal starting conditions. NFSA, 2013 foodgrain entitlements cushion consumption, but do not confer land, secure work or credit. Rawls's difference principle asks whether institutional inequalities improve the prospects of the least advantaged; continued destitution despite prosperity questions that arrangement. Sen's capability lens adds that nominal income or assets do not guarantee nutrition, learning or accessible healthcare.

Ownership, however, is not the only cause: illness, social exclusion, weak schooling and poor sanitation can impoverish even an asset-owning family. Conversely a transfer of land without secure title, irrigation or markets may yield little income. Protect women's property claims, secure tenure and affordable credit; combine these with health, education, skills and enforceable food rights. Measure assets and lived outcomes across caste, gender and region. Ownership inequality is a major cause, not a complete explanation of each poor household. Public services can partly offset the disadvantages of limited assets.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Distinguish distributive, corrective and recognitive justice, and show how the three-dimension test evaluates one Indian welfare intervention. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer:**

Distributive justice asks who receives resources; corrective justice asks how a specific wrong is undone; recognitive justice asks whose dignity and voice are denied. Abolition of manual scavenging needs all three.

Prohibition and enforcement address a wrongful practice, while compensation or rehabilitation remedies affected workers. Safe alternative livelihoods redistribute opportunity: a ban without replacement work risks displacing rather than ending the harm. Ending caste stigma, hearing workers' concerns and treating them as equal users of public services supplies recognition. Article 46's special concern for SCs and weaker sections reinforces support, but its directive alone cannot enforce prohibition.

An allocated benefit does not demonstrate sustained employment or freedom from humiliation. Evaluate actual cessation of unsafe work, stable livelihoods and restored dignity. A policy meeting only one dimension can leave the original injustice substantially intact.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Examine why formal legal equality is a necessary but insufficient condition for social justice. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer:**

Formal legal equality secures equal citizenship by forbidding arbitrary different rules for similarly placed persons. It is necessary, but identical rules can preserve unequal starting positions.

A hostel with only stairs excludes a student using a wheelchair despite formally equal admission. A school seat without transport cannot be used by a child in a remote tribal hamlet. Sen's capability distinction shows why identical resources need not yield identical real freedoms. The Preamble's justice commitments and Article 46's special direction for SCs, STs and weaker sections support differentiated help. Accessible buildings, transport and reservation can advance substantive participation rather than contradict equal status.

Such differentiation needs transparent eligibility and assessment: badly drawn boundaries can miss the most disadvantaged. Thus formal equality is the floor; access, remedy for discrimination and actual participation test social justice.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Assess the claim that India's welfare architecture has moved from discretionary charity to enforceable entitlement. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer:**

India has moved substantially from discretionary charity toward claimable rights, but legal recognition is not the same as enforceable delivery. Distinguish constitutional direction, statutory entitlement and executive scheme.

The NFSA, 2013 gives covered households a legal foodgrain claim, while the right-to-education framework makes elementary schooling claimable for eligible children. Article 46 directs promotion of SCs', STs' and weaker sections' educational and economic interests but is not itself a ration or scholarship entitlement. Manual-scavenging prohibition is a corrective legal intervention; where enforcement or alternative livelihoods fail, affected workers still lack effective relief. Many targeted benefits remain governed by changeable scheme rules rather than individually enforceable statutory guarantees.

Even statutory rights require accurate identification, staff, supply and accessible grievance redress. Informal workers may lack required documents; migrant families need portability; inaccessible schools and discriminatory queues can defeat formal eligibility. Announced allocations, cards and sanctioned seats do not demonstrate receipt or improved nutrition and learning. Social audits and household surveys provide checks on administrative totals, while appeals can turn a denied claim into a remedy. Adequate public finance is indispensable: a legal promise without a staffed delivery point is hollow.

The shift is real in legal language and citizen leverage, but incomplete across programmes and regions. Assess the full chain from claim through delivery to outcome; strengthen local remedies and publish disaggregated receipt. Entitlement has displaced charity only insofar as an eligible person can actually exercise the right.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Examine the universalism-versus-targeting choice in India's welfare design, including the errors that each design generates. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer:**

Universal and targeted welfare designs solve different problems, and both generate errors. Universal services remove the need to prove poverty; targeting directs scarce support to specified disadvantage.

A universal primary clinic reaches an informal worker without income papers and lowers exclusion and stigma. But subsidising well-off users raises fiscal cost and apparent inclusion error; nominal universality also fails if clinics are distant or inaccessible. Targeted scholarships help groups identified under Article 46's special-care principle. Yet incorrect beneficiary lists, changing income and absent identity documents can wrongly exclude entitled people. Ineligible inclusion diverts support; stricter proxy thresholds can reduce this leakage while worsening exclusion among volatile-income households. Migration makes portability a further test.

NFSA, 2013 foodgrains are legally claimable by covered households, not all residents; identification and grievance quality remain decisive. Registrations alone cannot quantify either error. Compare surveys, distribution records and social-audit findings, allowing corrections and appeals. Neither universalism nor targeting automatically guarantees dignified treatment at a local counter. A mixed design uses broadly available basic education and primary health alongside additional, portable and reviewable support where disadvantage is greater. A district must check both whether excluded people appeal and whether included people actually use the service, not simply whether funds are spent. Choose the balance by administrative capacity, fiscal constraints and the cost of missing a needy person.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Assess whether the capability approach supplies a more defensible standard of social justice than income-based measurement. Answer in about 300 words.

**Model answer:**

Income measures purchasing power, but social justice concerns whether a person can actually live, learn and participate with dignity. Sen's capability approach is the better ultimate evaluative standard because it tests conversion of resources into real opportunities; income remains an important, measurable input.

Equal earners do not necessarily have equal prospects. A disabled student may face costly inaccessible transport; another child may live beside a functioning school. Attendance registers measure enrolment rather than learning or freedom to continue study. An NFSA, 2013 ration card records entitlement, not whether a woman can collect food safely or obtain adequate nutrition. Clean water, primary care and freedom from caste discrimination shape what income can achieve. Nussbaum's threshold account asks what minimum capabilities should be secured for everyone, but a threshold still requires contested choices of indicators.

The capability view captures recognition as well as distribution: a humiliating service queue reduces agency even if grain is delivered. Rawls's difference principle tests gains for the least advantaged; Fraser's redistribution, recognition and representation expose whose experience defines success. Yet capabilities cannot be observed as easily as income. NFHS nutrition and health measures and surveys of learning are useful proxies, not complete accounts of freedom. A satisfied respondent may still face constrained choices; regional, gender and caste averages hide variation. Evidence of improved indicators alone cannot prove which intervention caused them.

Use income poverty to detect material shortages and guide transfers, but assess outcomes through health, learning, accessibility and dignified participation as well. Disaggregate by disability and social group, check exclusion through grievances and audit service quality. Capabilities provide a more defensible verdict, provided measurement limitations and the need for reliable income data remain explicit.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Assess the institutional conditions under which constitutional social-justice directives translate into measured capability gains. Answer in about 300 words.

**Model answer:**

Constitutional directions yield capability gains only if an institutional chain turns duties into effective services. Article 46 directs the State to advance SCs, STs and weaker sections; by itself it is not a deliverable scholarship or an enforceable job.

First, legislation or clear schemes must define eligible people, obligations and remedies. NFSA, 2013 makes foodgrain supply claimable for covered households; a discretionary grant depends on changing rules and budgets. Second, adequate finance, workers and infrastructure must translate legal provision into a usable service: a sanctioned anganwadi without staff or a school without transport does not produce nutrition or learning. Third, delivery must adapt to disadvantage. Migrants need portable access, disabled students accessible premises and caste-oppressed families protection from humiliating denial. Universal baseline clinics can limit exclusion while targeted assistance remedies unequal starting conditions.

Fourth, grievance redress, appeal and social audit must correct denials. Manual-scavenging prohibition without enforcement and rehabilitation leaves the original harm intact; recognition demands workers' voice and dignity alongside livelihood support. Fifth, count capabilities, not administrative entries. NFHS and PLFS survey evidence, social audits and registration data capture different links: triangulation can reveal a missing household or poor service quality. Break down outcomes by gender, caste, disability and region rather than presenting national averages as equal progress.

Incomes, sanitation and local capacity also affect health and education, so measured gains cannot automatically be attributed to one programme. Yet clear legal claims, sustained budgets, responsive frontline services, accessible remedies and independently checked outcomes make the constitutional promise plausibly effective. Where any link fails, the directive remains an aspiration despite high spending or impressive registration totals. In particular, independent household feedback should trigger budget and staff corrections, not merely furnish a publicity indicator.

### Evidence for further practice

- Constitutional baseline: Preamble, Part IV Articles 38–39, 41–43 and 46–47; Article 46 is a directive, whereas NFSA, 2013 and the Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009 create statutory claims for covered people.
- The ILO Social Protection Floors Recommendation No. 202 (2012) groups basic health care, child income security, working-age income security and older-person income security; it is not a directly enforceable Indian statute.
- The National Commission for Women, National Commission for Minorities, National Commission for Protection of Child Rights and National Human Rights Commission provide investigation/advice/recommendations; a recommendation is not automatically a binding delivery order.
- Test identification, enforcement, delivery, quality and outcome separately; NFHS/PLFS household evidence and social audits illuminate different failures from administrative registration. Keep reservation-category creamy-layer doctrine with the Polity owner.
