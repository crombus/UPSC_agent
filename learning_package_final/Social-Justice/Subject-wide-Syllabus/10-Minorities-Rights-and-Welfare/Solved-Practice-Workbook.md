# Minorities: Rights and Welfare — Solved Practice Workbook

## BASIC MCQS / REMEDIATION

40 distinct source-grounded application questions. All questions appear before the separate answer key. Legal, numerical and programme statements reflect the dated source owners cited below; an analytical scenario is not an official PYQ.

B. Article 30 applies only to religious endowments.

### Q11. A complaint of discrimination is submitted to the NCM. What may it do?

A. Set aside a Supreme Court judgment by its own order.

### Q12. Why distinguish the NCM from the minority-education commission?

### Q13. In which unit is Article 30 minority status ordinarily tested under TMA Pai (2002)?

D. An applicant's national EWS income bracket.

### Q14. A community is nationally small but a State majority. What is the Article 30 lesson?

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Examine the composition and mandate of the National Commission for Minorities and explain how it differs from the constitutional commissions. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer (150 words):**

The National Commission for Minorities (NCM) is a body under the National Commission for Minorities Act, 1992, not a constitutional commission like those for SCs, STs or backward classes. Its Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson and five Members evaluate minority development, monitor constitutional and legal safeguards, examine deprivation complaints, study discrimination and recommend remedies. For example, a linguistic school's grievance invokes Articles 29 and 30 and may also need the specialised National Commission for Minority Educational Institutions Act, 2004. The NCM can identify a pattern and press public authorities to respond; it cannot itself invalidate legislation, adjudicate all disputed rights or substitute for a court. Central notification of religious communities under section 2(c) of the 1992 Act is likewise different from Article 30's State-level minority test under TMA Pai (2002). Effective oversight therefore requires accessible complaints, transparent follow-up and outcome evidence alongside advice, not merely the existence of a commission.

**Model answer (270 words):**
