## PYQS AND ANSWER PRACTICE

### 2024 PRELIMS GS-I Q100 — VERIFIED DEMAND; OFFICIAL LETTER WITHHELD

Audited `_PYQ-ROUTING-PRELIMS-2024-2025.md` routes the **Pradhan Mantri Shram Yogi Maan-dhan (PM-SYM)** demand (2024 GS-I, Q100) here. The canonical Basic owner reproduces only the demand summary, not a verified complete original stem with four options or a final official Set-A option mapping. Accordingly no invented stem or official answer letter appears here. PM-SYM is a voluntary contributory pension for eligible unorganised workers: matching Government contributions and ₹3,000 monthly pension after 60 subject to scheme terms; an e-Shram registration does not itself enrol a worker.

The 2024 GS-III Labour Codes question is cross-owned by `Economy/basic/22` and `Economy/advanced/22`; the 2021 GS-I gig/women question belongs to Indian Society. No direct GS-II Mains PYQ is claimed. Six questions below are **original**, not official demands; answers to original MCQs are not official PYQ keys.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Explain why commencement, scheme notification and benefit delivery must be distinguished when assessing the Code on Social Security, 2020. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer (150 words):**

Code commencement does not establish a paid claim. The Code on Social Security was enacted in 2020 and commenced on 21 November 2025. Central Rules followed in May 2026 for Central-jurisdiction establishments; State rules and scheme notifications still need checking. The Code defines gig and platform workers and Section 114 provides an aggregator-contribution framework, but its turnover range is not evidence of a nationally collected levy. For any claimed benefit, identify the applicable scheme, notified rate, administrator and eligible worker; then trace collection, enrolment, claims, payment and appeal. e-Shram registration creates a UAN-based gateway, not automatic cover. A construction worker may instead claim State-notified BOCW support and a qualifying unorganised worker may enrol separately in contributory PM-SYM. A sound evaluation is dated and scheme-specific: it distinguishes a legal power, operational rules and actual protection of mobile workers without calling the commenced Code either a mere proposal or universally delivered welfare.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Distinguish statutory recognition of a platform worker from operational social-security coverage, and explain what evidence would show the difference. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer (145 words):**

A platform rider can meet the Code definition without receiving benefits. The definition covers paid services mediated through an online platform outside the traditional employer–employee relationship. Section 114 creates an aggregator-financing framework, but its 1–2% turnover range and 5% payout cap require an applicable notified rate and collection arrangement; a statutory range is not a payment receipt. To demonstrate operational coverage, identify a valid scheme, who enrolled, contributions actually collected, claim rules, individual payments and appeal outcomes. e-Shram UAN registration may aid convergence but does not itself confer insurance or PM-SYM pension. A private app policy may contain exclusions and is not necessarily a substitute for a statutory entitlement. State rules and worker movement across multiple platforms also matter. Recognition improves the legal starting point; funded, portable and contestable claims, measured with dated benefit data rather than worker projections, establish effective social security.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Critically examine the proposition that registration of an unorganised worker is necessary but not sufficient for social security. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer (242 words):**

Registration is an identification tool, not the social-security benefit. e-Shram, launched by the Ministry of Labour and Employment, gives unorganised workers a UAN-based record. It can help schemes find mobile workers, but an ID does not itself pay an accident claim, finance old-age support or guarantee eligibility. A worker wanting PM-SYM must meet that voluntary contributory pension's conditions and enrol separately; a building worker can pursue State BOCW welfare only through the applicable registration and board scheme. EPFO and ESIC protect covered workers under their respective eligibility rules, not every gig worker by virtue of a database entry.

The Code on Social Security, 2020 commenced on 21 November 2025, and final Central Rules followed in May 2026. ### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Assess whether an aggregator-financed contribution can deliver adequate social security for gig and platform workers. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer (249 words):**

Aggregator financing helps, but a rate alone does not secure workers. Section 114 of the commenced Code on Social Security provides for a Central-Government-notified contribution of 1–2% of aggregator annual turnover, capped at 5% of payments to gig and platform workers. Do not present either boundary as an already collected nationwide rate: the relevant scheme, notification, administrator and collections require date-specific verification. An aggregator with high turnover and a shifting workforce also raises questions about attribution when a courier works on several apps.

Adequacy has more dimensions than revenue. Accident and disability cover must have clear claim conditions and prompt payments; maternity and old-age protection address different risks. Voluntary PM-SYM enrolment is not a substitute for all such needs, while e-Shram registration is a delivery identifier, not a funded promise. Transparency over ratings and deactivation matters because lost access to work can undermine contribution continuity and a worker's ability to prove earnings. A small fixed levy without governance may produce unclaimed balances rather than security.

Design a notified rate with a sustainable fund, simple portable records, auditable platform reporting, worker voice and impartial appeals. Publish money collected and claims approved or rejected, and test adequacy against the cost and incidence of the covered risk. Fiscal sustainability and privacy matter: a single lifelong worker identity should minimise unnecessary disclosure and permit correction. Thus aggregator contributions are a plausible financing lever, conditional on operational proof and combined with genuine coverage, not evidence by themselves that the social-security gap has closed.

**Question:** Analyse the gap between legal recognition and effective protection for India's gig and platform workers, and identify where each corrective instrument must be applied. Answer in about 300 words.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Evaluate the claim that platform-worker inclusion requires more than registration and more than insurance. Answer in about 300 words.

**Model answer (284 words):**

Registration and insurance tackle different gaps; neither alone addresses platform labour's full risk. e-Shram's UAN records an unorganised worker for convergence but grants no automatic pension or health benefit. A platform may offer accident insurance, yet exclusions, claims delay, narrow disability definitions and lapse after deactivation can leave a rider unprotected. Income volatility, maternity and old-age needs persist even when a specific accident is covered. PM-SYM may supply a separate voluntary contributory old-age route to an eligible worker after qualifying contributions; it is not a universal benefit of app work.

The Code on Social Security, 2020 commenced on 21 November 2025; Central Rules followed in May 2026. Its platform-worker definition and Section 114 aggregator contribution range create a legal framework, not proof of an operational, adequately funded national gig scheme. Check the specific rate notification, rules, collections and payment evidence rather than asserting that a proposed contribution is already remitted. State variation in implementation and movement across multiple platforms create further eligibility risks.

Broader inclusion requires portable worker-controlled records and contributions, clear and sufficiently comprehensive risks covered, simple claim and appeal mechanisms and representation in benefit design. Algorithmic assignment and deactivation can determine income and access to records: reasoned explanations and review are social-security issues as well as fair-work issues. Minimise personal-data collection and allow correction without denying a mobile claimant for mismatched identifiers. Audit actual paid claims, rejected applications, contribution gaps and benefit adequacy by gender, State and work pattern rather than counting registrations. Publish the difference between accepted claims and cash actually received by workers, since fund balances can conceal failed delivery. The appropriate conclusion is conditional: recognition enables protection only when funded benefits, worker voice and practical contestability reinforce each other.
