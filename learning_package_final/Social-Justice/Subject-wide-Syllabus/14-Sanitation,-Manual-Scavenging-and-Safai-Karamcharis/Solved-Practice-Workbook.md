### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Distinguish manual scavenging from hazardous cleaning under the Prohibition of Employment as Manual Scavengers and their Rehabilitation Act, 2013, and examine the challenges in enforcing both prohibitions. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer (146 words):**

The 2013 PEMSR Act prohibits two related but different practices. Manual scavenging includes manually handling fresh human excreta in insanitary-latrine and specified settings; gloves do not make it lawful. Hazardous cleaning concerns manual sewer or septic-tank cleaning without prescribed protective equipment, devices and precautions. The Act also prohibits insanitary-latrine construction and requires identification and rehabilitation. A contractor cannot relabel unsafe tank entry ordinary sanitation. Enforcement falters when local surveys miss workers, contractors diffuse employer responsibility and equipment is unavailable or unused. NAMASTE, jointly implemented by the Social Justice and Housing ministries, profiles workers and supports PPE, training and mechanisation, but a profile is not proof of safety. Inspect individual worksites and contractual duties; prosecute prohibited practices, provide functional machines and protect workers against retaliation. Finally, link sanctions to stable alternative livelihoods: banning a task without enabling exit leaves a caste-linked workforce exposed to renewed hazardous employment.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Explain why the source and the status of the compensation tiers for sewer deaths must be stated precisely in a sanitation answer. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer (145 words):**

Compensation responds to harm; it does not prove safe work. *Safai Karamchari Andolan* (2014) supplied an earlier ₹10 lakh death baseline. The Supreme Court's *Dr Balram Singh v. Union of India* directions of 20 October 2023 increased sewer-death compensation to ₹30 lakh and prescribed at least ₹20 lakh for permanent disability and ₹10 lakh for other injury. Those are judicial directions, not a compensation schedule in the Prohibition of Employment as Manual Scavengers and their Rehabilitation Act, 2013. Confusing them could obscure an affected family's actual claim. The 2013 Act instead prohibits manual scavenging and hazardous cleaning and mandates identification and rehabilitation; NAMASTE supplies safety and mechanisation support. In an answer, identify the injury category and authority, then check incident recognition, responsible contractor and local body, claim processing and actual disbursal. No post-death payment converts an unsafe sewer entry into lawful employment or replaces prevention.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Critically examine the claim that manual scavenging persists in India because of an enforcement and caste-stigma failure rather than a legal vacuum. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer (234 words):**

A legal vacuum is not the chief explanation of continued manual scavenging. The PEMSR Act, 2013 criminalises employment for manual scavenging and hazardous sewer or septic-tank cleaning, prohibits insanitary latrines and provides identification and rehabilitation. *Dr Balram Singh* (2023) directed higher compensation for deaths and injury. Yet the number of laws cannot show compliance where work orders move through contractors, local authorities lack working suction equipment, prosecutions are rare and surveys omit workers whose recognition creates rehabilitation obligations. Deaths despite prohibition demand investigation, not a declaration that the occupation has vanished.

The persistence is also structured by caste. Dalit sub-castes historically channelled into this work may encounter stigma when seeking alternative employment; where statutory elements are present, caste-based harassment can additionally engage the PoA Act. The broader dignity, untouchability and forced-labour concerns connect to Articles 21, 17 and 23. But it would be wrong to assume every sanitation worker is a manual scavenger or every accident proves a PoA offence.

NAMASTE's worker profiling, safety training and mechanisation can reduce exposure; SRMS-linked rehabilitation and enterprise assistance support exit. Test actual safe entry, machine deployment, complaint investigation, disbursed compensation and sustained non-sanitation income rather than enrolment or machinery purchases alone. Independent incident reporting, procurement standards and contractor accountability must be paired with the workers' ability to refuse unsafe work without losing income. The failure is partly enforcement and caste-linked occupational sorting, not absence of formal prohibition.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Assess whether mechanisation as currently pursued can end hazardous cleaning, and identify what more would be required. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer (246 words):**

Mechanisation is necessary, but a machine purchase is not a safety system. NAMASTE, jointly implemented by MoSJE and MoHUA, promotes worker profiling, protective equipment, training and mechanised sanitation; support for worker enterprises can finance suction equipment. Where a sewer or septic tank can be serviced safely without entry, employers lose a common pretext for hazardous manual cleaning. But city-wide purchase totals do not tell us whether machines reach narrow lanes, are maintained or are available to every contractor. A poorly priced tender may reward manual shortcuts; training without equipment may give a worker responsibility without control.

The PEMSR Act, 2013 still prohibits manual scavenging and hazardous cleaning, while *Dr Balram Singh* (2023) directs compensation after sewer deaths and injuries. Neither a judicial payout nor a scheme registration can replace operational prevention. Require site-specific risk assessment, safe-work orders, adequate crews, inspected machinery, employer liability and independent records of unsafe entry, including subcontracted work. If an exceptional entry occurs, insist on prescribed gear and precautions rather than normalising bare-hand labour.

Mechanisation also cannot by itself end the caste-linked assignment of the most dangerous tasks. Survey all affected workers, provide realistic alternative training and access to capital and employment, and audit whether identified families obtain durable livelihoods. Measure reduced hazardous exposure and continued income, not just machine ownership or total PPE distributed. Thus mechanisation is one indispensable lever among prohibition, rehabilitation, compensation and accountable enforcement; treating it as a stand-alone technological cure merely moves risk down the contracting chain.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Analyse the sanitation-labour question as a four-lever problem and evaluate the institutional architecture that is supposed to hold the levers together. Answer in about 300 words.

**Model answer (273 words):**

Sanitation justice is a coordinated four-lever obligation, not a sequence in which compensation excuses failed prevention. First, the PEMSR Act, 2013 prohibits manual scavenging, hazardous cleaning and insanitary-latrine construction; State and district authorities must identify affected workers and enforce offences. Definitions matter: handling excreta in specified insanitary settings is distinct from unsafe sewer and septic-tank entry. Second, NAMASTE supports worker profiling, functional mechanised equipment, PPE and training under MoSJE–MoHUA implementation. Contractors and urban local bodies must be held to verifiable safe-work clauses: a central programme cannot inspect every manhole.

Third, rehabilitation under SRMS and convergent support should supply training, capital and genuine alternative earnings. An identified worker may remain in hazardous labour if loans fail, new employers discriminate or cash support ends. Fourth, compensation must reach victims' families: the Supreme Court in *Dr Balram Singh* (2023) directed ₹30 lakh for sewer deaths, at least ₹20 lakh for permanent disability and ₹10 lakh for other injury; these are judicial tiers, not a schedule in the 2013 Act. *Safai Karamchari Andolan* (2014) supplied the earlier death baseline.

The NCSK investigates complaints and recommends corrective action, but its present tenure through March 2028 is resolution-based, not the permanent statutory continuation of its 1993 origin. Distinguish its monitoring from police investigation, local procurement and judicial redress. Articles 17, 21 and 23 explain why concentrated caste-linked assignment implicates dignity and freedom, not merely technical hygiene; the PoA Act requires case-specific facts. Publish surveys, unsafe-work incidents, prosecuted contractors, working machines, rehabilitation outcomes and actual payouts. Record worksite refusals without penalising workers. Only simultaneous evidence of fewer unsafe entries and a durable occupational exit can justify claims of progress.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Evaluate the proposition that ending manual scavenging is a question of dignity rather than of sanitation infrastructure, and state what evidence would settle the question. Answer in about 300 words.

**Model answer (276 words):**

Dignity and infrastructure are complementary, not rival explanations. Under the PEMSR Act, 2013, manually removing excreta from insanitary settings is unlawful even if gloves are supplied; unsafe sewer or septic-tank cleaning is a distinct prohibited practice. A modern toilet network can still rely on Dalit workers to enter its sewerage system without prescribed equipment. Thus counting constructed toilets alone cannot answer whether caste-linked hazardous labour has ended. Conversely, a dignity declaration alone cannot clean tanks without working machines, trained crews and safe municipal contracts.

The four-lever test combines prohibition, mechanisation, rehabilitation and compensation. NAMASTE's MoSJE–MoHUA profiling, PPE and mechanisation address occupational exposure, while SRMS-linked support must give families earnings outside hereditary sanitation assignments. Contractor incentives, survey undercount and weak prosecution can break both chains. The NCSK's resolution-based monitoring and complaints role complements, but does not replace, enforcement by States, district officials and urban local bodies. The 2023 *Dr Balram Singh* judicial directions set ₹30 lakh for deaths and injury tiers; paying a victim's family is essential but cannot count as an injury prevented.

Evidence to adjudicate the proposition must follow people and worksites. Compare independently reported sewer entries and deaths, functioning equipment at sites, prosecution and complaint outcomes, genuine worker identification and stable income after rehabilitation. Disaggregate by caste where lawful and protect worker privacy and safety. Ask whether those denied risky assignments obtain equally paid alternatives and whether unregistered subcontracted workers can report hazards without losing work. A zero complaint count cannot establish safety where reporting exposes workers to dismissal. An infrastructure-only policy misses hierarchy; a recognition-only policy misses avoidable toxic exposure. Dignity requires both safe sanitation production and the freedom to exit it.
