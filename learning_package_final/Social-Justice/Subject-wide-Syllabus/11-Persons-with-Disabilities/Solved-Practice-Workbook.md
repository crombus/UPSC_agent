# Persons with Disabilities — Solved Practice Workbook

## BASIC MCQS / REMEDIATION

40 distinct source-grounded application questions. All questions appear before the separate answer key. Legal, numerical and programme statements reflect the dated source owners cited below; an analytical scenario is not an official PYQ.

### Q1. A public employer sees a qualified disabled applicant as a charitable beneficiary. Which legal frame applies?

A. The RPwD Act, 2016 frames equal participation as a right shaped by barriers, not charity.
B. Only the 1999 guardianship statute governs every employee.
C. Disability is defined solely by receipt of a device.
D. Article 16(4) itself defines disability accommodation.

### Q2. What does the 2016 Act's general disability definition emphasise?

A. Every temporary illness automatically qualifying for reservation.
B. Long-term impairment interacting with barriers that hinder equal participation.
C. Only income below an EWS threshold.
D. A medical diagnosis without social barriers as the entire test.

### Q3. A claimant has a disability but lacks 40% certified impairment. Which distinction matters?

A. All rights disappear unless certified at 40%.
B. Benchmark status is a constitutional SC category.
C. Disability status and benchmark-reservation eligibility are distinct; many rights are not limited to benchmark claimants.
D. A UDID number alone changes the medical percentage.

### Q4. What establishes benchmark disability for relevant statutory benefits?

A. Automatic inclusion of every applicant using public transport.
B. A certificate of family annual income only.
C. A hospital's informal sympathy letter.
D. Certification of 40% or more of a specified disability by the competent authority.

### Q5. What changed in category coverage from the 1995 law to the 2016 Act?

A. Specified categories expanded from seven to twenty-one under the later statute.
B. All disability categories were repealed in 2016.
C. The 1995 statute specified twenty-one and the 2016 statute seven.
D. Only visual impairment remained specified.

### Q6. Which is a specified-disability example easily missed by a purely mobility-focused design?

A. Only wheelchair use is recognised.
B. Autism spectrum disorder; the Act also covers diverse sensory and other specified disabilities.
C. EWS income certificates are specified disabilities.
D. Literacy is the Act's sole impairment test.

### Q7. A government office applies disability reservation to its posts. Which floor is in the owner?

A. Exactly 27% for disability under Article 16(4).
B. 5% of every private-sector job irrespective of statute.
C. At least 4% for persons with benchmark disabilities in government establishments and posts under the 2016 Act.
D. No reservation unless Article 342A is amended.

### Q8. A higher-education admission office asks which disability reservation floor is recorded.

A. 4% of every State's total population.
B. 10% of every school for EWS by the RPwD Act.
C. Reservation solely for applicants under the 1999 Trust Act.
D. 5% of seats in higher educational institutions for persons with benchmark disabilities.

### Q9. A school offers a ramp but refuses an accessible examination format to a specific learner. What duty is triggered?

A. Individual reasonable accommodation may be required despite generic accessibility provisions.
B. Universal design automatically excuses all individual adjustments.
C. The student must accept a charity grant instead of examination access.
D. An EWS land threshold defines a reasonable adjustment.

### Q10. How does section 2(y) constrain accommodation?

A. Every requested change is categorically mandatory regardless of burden.
B. Necessary and appropriate modification is owed unless it imposes a disproportionate or undue burden.
C. Only cash support counts as an adjustment.
D. The applicant bears an absolute duty to redesign public buildings.

### Q11. A supervisor describes denial of reasonable accommodation as merely bad manners. What is the legal error?

A. It is never legally relevant under the 2016 Act.
B. Only transport design can be discriminatory.
C. Denial can constitute disability discrimination under the rights-based framework.
D. All discrimination is cured by issuing a UDID card.

### Q12. What does Vikash Kumar v UPSC illustrate in this owner's use?

A. A court abolished all examination standards.
B. The 1999 Trust Act repealed the RPwD Act.
C. Every requested format always avoids any undue-burden inquiry.
D. Accommodation is an equality requirement rather than a discretionary charitable concession.

### Q13. Which pairing separates disability models?

A. Medical model foregrounds impairment; social model barriers; rights model duties and remedies.
B. Medical model means employer vacancies; social model means certificate income.
C. Rights model eliminates the need for accessibility.
D. Social model attributes all disadvantage to an applicant's willpower.

### Q14. A city plans a new metro station. Why design access from the outset?

A. A later portable card physically removes all steps.
B. Universal design accommodates diverse users initially and avoids relying solely on expensive retrofits.
C. Retrofitting is by definition the only statutory design method.
D. A wheelchair distribution camp substitutes for accessible platforms.

### Q15. An old station retrofits lifts but the announcement system remains inaccessible. What gap remains?

A. A lift automatically guarantees all information access.
B. ICT never forms part of accessibility policy.
C. Access requires built environment, transport and information/communication dimensions together.
D. Reservation quotas certify station accessibility.

### Q16. Which status accurately describes accessibility work after March 2024 in the owner?

A. All accessibility obligations expired in March 2024.
B. Only an undated press headline proves annual expenditure.
C. The 1999 Trust Act became a nationwide transport authority.
D. It proceeds through the Scheme for Implementation of the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act; avoid treating an earlier mission as a new standalone programme.

### Q17. What is UDID's administrative function?

A. Standardised, nationally portable disability certificate/card processes, not a guarantee of actual access.
B. Automatic reserved employment for every cardholder.
C. An alternative constitutional caste-list authority.
D. A census of every person with a disability regardless of registration.

### Q18. A State scheme rejects a valid portable certificate solely for lack of a second State card. Which issue arises?

A. UDID supersedes every scheme income rule automatically.
B. Portability in identification must be respected while checking the scheme's own eligibility rules.
C. A card itself proves employment outcomes.
D. Portability means no competent medical certification is ever needed.

### Q19. Which pair correctly distinguishes infrastructure from individual adjustment?

A. Both exclusively mean cash transfers.
B. An assistive device abolishes the public duty to design access.
C. Universal design sets broadly usable baselines; reasonable accommodation responds to a person's specific barrier.
D. A benchmark certificate replaces all accessible building standards.

### Q20. A college reports ramps but no accessible course materials. Which audit is best?

A. Count the number of issued ID cards only.
B. Count all nominal reserved seats as completed degrees.
C. Assume infrastructure accessibility can never be measured.
D. Assess ICT and pedagogical access as well as built environment and student-specific accommodations.

### Q21. What is the 2016 Act's legal nature relative to caste reservations?

A. Disability reservation is statutory and distinct from Articles 15(4)/16(4) backward-class reservation.
B. It is an amendment to the Central OBC List.
C. It gives SC/ST creamy-layer status to all disabled people.
D. It replaces Article 30 minority-institution rights.

### Q22. A disabled OBC applicant applies for a public post. What mistake must be avoided?

A. Assuming the RPwD quota is exactly the EWS quota.
B. Treating disability and caste as mutually exclusive identities; examine distinct applicable legal routes and notifications.
C. Denying all accommodation because an OBC benefit exists.
D. Giving the NCBC exclusive disability grievance powers.

### Q23. A person seeks a wheelchair under the aids/appliances route. Which scheme belongs to that purpose?

A. UDID is itself the wheelchair procurement scheme.
B. The accessibility framework issues all personal devices automatically.
C. ADIP supports purchase/fitting of aids and appliances subject to its guidelines.
D. The 2004 minority-education commission supplies wheelchairs under Article 30.

### Q24. Why is a delivered appliance not proof of inclusion?

A. Equipment delivery itself proves equal labour-market earnings.
B. Every device grants a reserved university place.
C. A card proves a building's ramps comply with standards.
D. Fit, usability, repair, accessible environment and actual participation still require monitoring.

### Q25. Which statutory scope is narrower than the RPwD Act's full disability schedule?

A. The National Trust Act, 1999 addresses autism, cerebral palsy, intellectual disability (statutory historical wording) and multiple disabilities.
B. The Trust Act covers all twenty-one RPwD categories identically.
C. The Trust Act is only the national transport code.
D. The 2016 Act abolished all guardianship support.

### Q26. An answer describes supported guardianship as universal substitute decision-making. Why caution?

A. A statutory trust automatically removes every person's legal capacity.
B. Support and rights must be tailored to the person's autonomy; the 1999 trust's scope is not every disability.
C. The same guardianship order is mandatory for all adults.
D. Informed preferences are irrelevant to a rights-based approach.

### Q27. Where can a complaint about implementation of disability safeguards go under the 2016 Act?

A. Only the NCBC under Article 338B.
B. Only the Central Waqf Council.
C. Chief Commissioner nationally and State Commissioners at State level, subject to their statutory functions.
D. A UDID database is the sole court of appeal.

### Q28. What is the limit of a commissioner's recommendation?

A. One complaint automatically enforces every building standard nationwide.
B. A commissioner issues caste certificates.
C. Commissioners can repeal the RPwD Act unilaterally.
D. Monitoring, complaints and recommendations do not themselves guarantee a court-equivalent remedy or accessible implementation.

### Q29. What do the 2011 Census figures on disability measure?

A. A dated population estimate affected by self-reporting and question design, not a timeless prevalence truth.
B. All disability prevalence in 2026 perfectly.
C. Only applicants who received ADIP devices.
D. An exact count of every eligible 40% benchmark applicant.

### Q30. Why cannot UDID registrations be substituted for disability prevalence?

A. UDID includes every unregistered person by definition.
B. Registration counts those identified/enrolled through a changing platform, not everyone with disability.
C. Registrations measure only job vacancies.
D. The Census is a live real-time UDID dashboard.

### Q31. A reservation post remains unfilled despite statutory quotas. What should an audit test?

A. Only how many cards were printed.
B. Only public speeches on awareness.
C. Post identification, accessible recruitment, accommodations and actual appointment rather than quota existence alone.
D. The size of the EWS asset ceiling.

### Q32. A private-sector job seeker faces exclusion. Which qualification matters?

A. All private posts automatically carry the identical statutory 4% reservation.
B. The 1995 law governs every 2026 job exclusively.
C. One public quota proves equal hiring everywhere.
D. Government-establishment reservation figures cannot be imputed wholesale to private-sector recruitment, though non-discrimination matters.

### Q33. What does Jeeja Ghosh supply as a judicial anchor?

A. Dignity and non-discrimination in public participation, beyond mere benefit disbursal.
B. A permanent numerical finding for 2026 prevalence.
C. A statutory amendment to the 1999 Trust Act.
D. A rule abolishing every accessibility standard.

### Q34. A department claims 10,000 UDID cards prove equal rights. What counter-indicator matters?

A. Count cards again with a different colour.
B. Measure accessible school completion, work and civic participation along with card coverage.
C. Assume every registered person has a job.
D. Treat device delivery as an official census.

### Q35. Why does sensitisation belong in implementation rather than just publicity?

A. Awareness posters by themselves replace the 2016 Act.
B. All disabled learners want identical adjustments.
C. Teachers, recruiters and officials must recognise barriers and perform accommodation duties without paternalism.
D. A training programme can waive non-discrimination law.

### Q36. What did the verified 2022 GS-II PYQ ask this owner to address?

A. An official 2026 answer letter for a Prelims question.
B. Only an EWS constitutional-amendment dispute.
C. An already adjudicated 2027 census total.
D. The RPwD Act, 2016 and sensitisation (Comment, 10 marks, 150 words); it is a Mains demand, not an objective key.

### Q37. A 2026 objective demand lists disability law, accessibility mission and a development corporation. What key status is recorded?

A. The local Set-A key is provisional and no final mapped answer letter is established in the owner.
B. A final official keyed letter can be guessed from the stem.

D. It was a 2022 GS-II Mains 20-marker.

### Q38. A designer argues every compliant ramp satisfies every individual need. Which reply holds?

A. No person can request an adjusted exam format.
B. Baseline standards and tailored accommodations are complementary, not substitutes.
C. A ramp alone guarantees hearing and cognitive access.
D. The RPwD Act contains only architectural duties.

### Q39. Which finding is an outcome rather than a programme output?

A. Number of UDID IDs printed.
B. Number of hearing aids purchased.
C. Increased accessible-course completion and sustained employment among eligible cohorts.
D. Amount of funds sanctioned for a mission.

### Q40. Which synthesis best avoids disability paternalism?

B. Record charity grants as the sole measure of equality.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Explain the concept of reasonable accommodation under the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 and its significance for inclusive education and employment. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer (148 words):**

Reasonable accommodation under RPwD Act section 2(y) means necessary, appropriate modification to enable equal enjoyment of rights without disproportionate or undue burden. It individualises a general promise: a university may supply an accessible exam format even when a building has a ramp; an employer may modify recruitment procedures or working arrangements while assessing the particular job. Denial is a discrimination issue, not merely a missed welfare benefit. Vikash Kumar v UPSC treats accommodation as equality rather than charity. The duty differs from universal design, which makes facilities usable for diverse people , and from benchmark certification, which controls certain reservation entitlements. Apply an interactive, assessment instead of assuming every person with the same impairment needs the same adjustment. Monitor admission, course completion and actual employment; neither an accommodation policy nor a UDID certificate alone demonstrates access. The undue-burden qualification limits particular requests, not the baseline commitment to non-discrimination.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Distinguish statutory reservation for persons with benchmark disabilities from constitutional reservation for the Scheduled Castes, the Scheduled Tribes and the other backward classes. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer (148 words):**

Disability reservation under the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 is statutory: at least 4% in government-establishment posts and 5% in higher educational institutions for benchmark disabilities, certified at 40% or more of a specified disability. It must not be presented as reservation under Articles 15(4) or 16(4), , or as the EWS route under Articles 15(6) and 16(6). A disabled OBC applicant may have distinct intersecting claims; eligibility and selection must follow the particular notice rather than an assumption that one identity erases another. Benchmark certification is not the general statutory definition of disability, which considers long-term impairment interacting with barriers; reasonable accommodation can be relevant beyond the reserved-seat category. Quotas offer entry, not guaranteed participation. Audit identification of posts, accessible examinations, adaptations and appointments, with grievance redress. A filled seat cannot by itself show that learning or employment continues on equal terms.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Critically examine the claim that the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 marks a paradigm shift from welfare to rights. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer (234 words):**

The Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 recasts disability from a discretionary welfare claim to enforceable equality: long-term impairments become disabling through social and institutional barriers. It replaced the 1995 framework's seven categories with twenty-one specified disabilities, widened recognition, prohibited discrimination including denial of reasonable accommodation, and provided benchmark-linked reservation of at least 4% in government posts and 5% in higher education. Section 2(y) requires appropriate individual adjustments unless disproportionate or undue burden arises; Vikash Kumar v UPSC makes the equality basis clear. Universal design and statutory accessibility duties extend to buildings, transport and information, whereas ADIP assistive devices address personal needs. The Chief Commissioner and State Commissioners can monitor safeguards and hear complaints; a portable UDID certificate helps identification but cannot physically remove a barrier. Yet statutory recognition is not full participation. Legacy buildings need costly retrofits, recruitment posts may go unidentified and training may remain inaccessible; persons outside the benchmark threshold still face discrimination. The National Trust Act, 1999 covers a narrower group and cannot represent every 2016 entitlement. Monitor accessible schooling, employment quality and complaints rather than only card counts. Rights language transforms the standard of accountability, but implementation depends on resources, trained duty-bearers and disabled persons' own choices, not paternalistic substitution. A wheelchair award cannot substitute for an accessible school or workplace; equally, reasonable accommodation must be negotiated with the rights-holder rather than imposed through family assumptions.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

**Model answer (246 words):**

The statutory gap arises along the delivery chain, not from a simple absence of rights. The 2016 RPwD Act recognises twenty-one specified disabilities and sets at least 4% reservation in government posts and 5% in higher education for benchmark-certified claimants. Yet an inaccessible application portal, unidentified suitable post, unusable exam or missing reasonable adjustment can prevent a qualified person from even reaching selection. Section 2(y)'s accommodation duty and Vikash Kumar v UPSC's equality reasoning oblige officials to respond to individual barriers rather than offer charity. Universal design would build accessibility in from the start, but retrofitting old transport, buildings and information systems is slow. After March 2024 the owner's cited accessibility implementation vehicle is SIPDA; programme status should not be mistaken for a completed barrier-free city. ### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Analyse the relationship between universal design, reasonable accommodation and accessibility standards in building a disability-inclusive public realm. Answer in about 300 words.

**Model answer (270 words):**

Universal design anticipates the widest usable range at the planning stage; reasonable accommodation responds to a particular person's remaining barrier. Neither renders the other redundant. A station with accessible circulation may still need usable visual and audio announcements; a college with ramps may need accessible course material and an adjusted exam format. The RPwD Act, 2016 defines reasonable accommodation in section 2(y) as necessary and appropriate adjustment absent disproportionate or undue burden. Denial can amount to discrimination, as the equality approach in Vikash Kumar v UPSC illustrates. Standards translate rights into common specifications for the built environment, transport and information/communication technology. Retrofitting legacy systems can be expensive and piecemeal; designing access into new projects usually reduces future exclusion. The owner's March 2024 status note places accessibility implementation through SIPDA, but a programme label is not evidence of universal compliance. UDID makes certification portable, whereas ADIP supports fitting of personal aids; neither discharges an operator's obligation to make a public interface usable. Meaningful audit tests the user's continuous journey: information before travel, accessible booking, a usable station, actual transit, accessible destination and an effective complaint mechanism. Use disabled persons' participation to detect design blind spots, allow individual adjustments when baseline standards fail, and assess affordability and maintenance alongside formal compliance. Legal access is valuable only if it leads to equal education, work and civic life across impairment groups. Organisational procurement standards should specify testing with diverse users, including sensory and cognitive disabilities. Accessible ICT must be verified on real services rather than a static compliance form; complaints and remedial work must be public and time-bound.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Evaluate the adequacy of India's disability data and identification architecture for rights-based policy. Answer in about 300 words.

**Model answer (285 words):**

Disability policy needs three non-interchangeable data layers: population prevalence, certified eligibility and lived outcomes. Census 2011 records a dated estimate shaped by self-reporting and limited questions; it cannot establish actual 2026 prevalence or the number of people who meet a specific statutory threshold. The 2016 RPwD Act specifies twenty-one disability categories; benchmark status requires at least 40% certified impairment for reservation, while its general definition focuses on long-term impairment interacting with barriers. UDID standardises nationally portable certificate and card processes but registration measures enrolled persons, not everyone with disability. ADIP delivery counts aids; a completed device fitting is not equal work or schooling. Build comparable disaggregated population surveys, accessible certification and transparent, privacy-protected administrative records; distinguish applications, rejections, appeals and actual participation. Track gender, impairment type, age, region and multiple disadvantage without treating an intersectional subgroup as a separate population estimate unless denominators exist. Link accessible-school completion, post identification, adjusted recruitment, employment retention and everyday access to buildings, transport and ICT. Inspect false exclusion of people without documents, changing definitions and the causal limits of before/after comparisons. Chief and State Commissioners can use complaint patterns as signals, but few complaints may indicate inaccessible redress rather than no discrimination. Preserve personal autonomy in data use, and report uncertainty instead of equating a card count with inclusion. Periodically assess whether gendered care burdens and rural distance distort certification rates, and record whether reasonable accommodation requests were met even among people who do not qualify for benchmark reservation. Keep eligibility review separate from the right to seek access. Publish methods and uncertainty with each dated release so apparent rises in registrations are not mistaken for a surge in underlying prevalence. Compare eligible applicants and certificate holders separately.
