### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Distinguish reservation for the other backward classes from reservation for the economically weaker sections with reference to their constitutional basis and identification criteria. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer (150 words):**

OBC and EWS reservations answer different disadvantages. Articles 15(4) and 16(4) support measures for socially and educationally backward classes; Article 342A governs the Central List, while the 105th Amendment (2021) protects State lists . Articles 15(6) and 16(6), inserted by the 103rd Amendment (2019), enable up to 10% EWS reservation for citizens outside SC/ST/OBC categories. Thus a poor OBC claimant cannot switch to EWS merely because of poverty. OBC non-creamy-layer assessment turns partly on parental status and, an income/wealth test; the ₹8 lakh ceiling notified in September 2017 is not the whole rule. Under the 31 January 2019 EWS criteria, gross family income below ₹8 lakh must also satisfy land, flat and plot exclusions. A matching rupee figure does not make these interchangeable tests. Both routes may expand first-generation entry, but document access, uneven subgroup take-up and later outcomes determine whether substantive mobility follows.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Explain the rationale of the creamy-layer exclusion within backward-class reservation and examine its limitations. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer (144 words):**

The OBC creamy-layer rule seeks to prevent relatively advanced families  from repeatedly capturing reserved opportunities. Indra Sawhney (1992) required this internal filter ; it must not be mechanically transferred to SC/ST reservation. Eligibility involves parental status as well as an income/wealth limb where applicable. The latter's ₹8 lakh ceiling, last notified in September 2017 , operates over three years; salary and agricultural-land income are not simply aggregated into it. Therefore excluding a first-generation student because of an isolated annual income number may be unlawful, while ignoring a parent's specified service status can wrongly admit an applicant. The filter improves distributive fairness but does not demonstrate equal opportunity across all OBC subgroups. Compare subgroup access and educational completion with appropriate denominators, maintain certificate appeal , and examine structural discrimination beyond parental income. Redistribution within a quota cannot alone create jobs or guarantee intergenerational mobility.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Critically examine the claim that the absence of published caste data undermines evidence-based backward-class policy. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer (235 words):**

The data gap weakens calibration, not the constitutional basis of backward-class protection. Article 342A structures Central-list identification, and the 105th Amendment (2021) confirms States' separate list-making for State purposes. Yet the last published all-caste census tables date to 1931; caste data collected in the 2011 Socio Economic and Caste Census were not officially released and validated for general policy use. An OBC scholarship register measures applicants or recipients, not a caste-specific population denominator. Without comparable denominators and educational and occupational indicators, researchers cannot reliably assess whether the 27% central OBC quota is concentrated among a few communities or whether excluded subgroups remain disadvantaged. The Rohini Commission, appointed in 2017 to examine sub-categorisation, illustrates the distribution question; recommendation is not implementation. The 30 April 2025 decision to include caste enumeration in the planned 2027 Census, as recorded by the owner on 21 July 2026, was a prospective decision, not released data. But census counts alone do not measure stigma, quality of schooling or selection barriers. Collect consistent disaggregated data with privacy safeguards; link administrative outcomes to carefully constructed population denominators, audit certificate and grievance pathways, and evaluate longitudinal mobility rather than assume that publication mechanically allocates fair shares. A sound baseline would record who is omitted by non-application, whether State and Central classifications differ and how first-generation students fare after entry. The absence of population totals must not become a pretext for abandoning already established protections.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Assess the constitutional reasoning by which reservation on a purely economic criterion was upheld in 2022 and the objections it left open. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer (250 words):**

In Janhit Abhiyan v Union of India (November 2022), a 3–2 Supreme Court majority upheld the 103rd Amendment's economically based EWS reservation. Articles 15(6) and 16(6) enable up to 10% in specified educational and public-employment settings, excluding SC/ST/OBC claimants. The majority accepted economic disadvantage as an independent affirmative-action criterion and did not regard exceeding the ordinary 50% ceiling as automatically destructive of the basic structure in this design. This resolves constitutional validity, not whether all economically disadvantaged people gain equal access. The minority's objection to excluding poor members of existing reserved categories directly tests substantive equality: the same poverty may be treated differently because one applicant also belongs to a historically disadvantaged category. The response is that distinct provisions address distinct forms of exclusion; the rejoinder is that poverty within reserved groups still merits effective support. Indra Sawhney (1992) concerns OBC reservation and the ordinary ceiling, so it cannot be quoted as having itself decided EWS validity. The 31 January 2019 income-and-asset notification further raises operational issues: an applicant below ₹8 lakh may fail an asset bar, and certificate availability varies. Upholding the amendment does not certify delivery or mobility; measure exclusion, verification, educational persistence and occupational outcomes separately. A later challenge to application rules should be distinguished from a challenge to the amendment itself; different scrutiny attaches to legislation, notification and certificate practice. Neither the majority nor the dissent supplies evidence that poverty by itself captures the full structure of caste disadvantage.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Analyse why reservation and scholarship coverage is an inadequate measure of social mobility, and what a better measurement architecture would require. Answer in about 300 words.

**Model answer (271 words):**

Reservation and scholarships chiefly measure inputs and first-stage access. A filled central OBC seat under the 27% framework or a PM-YASASVI scholarship to an eligible OBC/EBC/DNT student records entry or support, not a child's eventual position relative to their parents. True intergenerational mobility requires education completion, occupation, earnings and social dignity over time; a first-generation degree holder in insecure work illustrates why enrollment alone is insufficient. Build linked but privacy-protected cohorts with parental education and occupation at baseline, type of institution, persistence and graduation, employment quality and subsequent household outcomes. Separate Central and State-list populations after the 105th Amendment (2021), and disaggregate by subgroup, gender and geography; do not make recipients the denominator for a community's participation rate. The Rohini Commission's 2017 sub-categorisation mandate identifies concentration as a question, not proof that redistribution has happened. Published all-caste tables last dating to 1931 and unreleased/unvalidated 2011 caste data limit population-based comparisons; the planned 2027 enumeration was not final data as of July 2026. Compare eligible applicants, awardees, completers and non-applicants; audit certificates and appeals, school quality, hiring discrimination and regional job markets. Even a cohort association cannot alone establish reservation's causal effect: selection, family assets and local growth also matter. Use credible comparisons and qualitative evidence while preserving confidentiality; pursue both fair access and durable, dignified mobility. Retention can be undermined by fees, language barriers and social hostility even when admission is secured. Link scheme registers with schooling and labour-market surveys only with consent and safeguards, and publish disaggregated uncertainty rather than falsely precise success rates. Evaluate outcomes over multiple cohorts, not one recruitment cycle.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Evaluate the proposition that backward-class policy in India has solved identification but not distribution. Answer in about 300 words.

**Model answer (275 words):**

India has a developed legal architecture for recognising backwardness, but distribution remains contested. Articles 15(4) and 16(4) authorise backward-class measures; Article 342A structures the Central List, while the 105th Amendment (2021) secures State list-making for State purposes. The 102nd Amendment (2018) gave the NCBC constitutional status under Article 338B, but the commission advises and examines complaints; it cannot itself amend the Central List. Identification is therefore not completely “solved”: certification, State–Central divergence and the lack of a validated current caste denominator still constrain decisions. On distribution, Indra Sawhney (1992) required an OBC creamy-layer filter to prevent repeated capture by the advanced; parental status and applicable income rules must be applied correctly, not reduced to a shared ₹8 lakh number. The Rohini Commission's 2017 sub-categorisation mandate recognises unequal shares within the 27% central quota, but recommendations cannot be treated as implemented allocations. PM-YASASVI support for OBC, EBC and DNT students may widen educational access, yet notified components and State delivery vary. The 2011 caste data were not released/validated for general policy use, and the proposed 2027 enumeration remained prospective as of July 2026. Improve group-sensitive denominators, certificate appeals, transparent subgroup monitoring and longitudinal completion-to-employment tracking, subject to privacy and regional variation. Thus the proposition captures a serious distributive failure but overstates the completeness of initial identification and mistakes quota utilisation for social mobility. Better distribution requires transparent criteria, scheduled reviews and resources for beneficiaries outside the few communities with stronger access to urban schools and coaching. Assess not only reserved positions but also capacity in ordinary education and labour markets, lest redistributing a fixed quota simply rearrange scarcity without widening opportunity.
