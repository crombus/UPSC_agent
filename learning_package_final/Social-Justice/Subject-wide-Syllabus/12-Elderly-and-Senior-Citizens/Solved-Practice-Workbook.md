# Elderly and Senior Citizens — Solved Practice Workbook

## BASIC MCQS / REMEDIATION

40 distinct source-grounded application questions. All questions appear before the separate answer key. Legal, numerical and programme statements reflect the dated source owners cited below; an analytical scenario is not an official PYQ.

### Q1. A 61-year-old Indian seeks coverage under the 2007 maintenance statute. Which threshold matters?

A. A senior citizen is an Indian citizen who has attained 60 years under section 2(h).
B. The statutory minimum is 70 years.
C. Only those above 80 qualify as senior citizens.
D. Receipt of a pension alone defines age eligibility.

### Q2. What does maintenance include under the 2007 Act?

A. Only a monthly cash allowance.
B. Food, clothing, residence, medical attendance and treatment.
C. Only a place in a government-run home.
D. Only inpatient hospital bills.

### Q3. An older person has no children but owns property with legal heirs. Who may owe maintenance?

A. No one if the person has no biological child.
B. Only the National Commission for Minorities.
C. Children and qualifying relatives, including relevant legal heirs, under the Act's conditions.
D. Only the district hospital regardless of family obligations.

### Q4. Why is it inaccurate to say only sons must pay maintenance?

A. A pension grant extinguishes every family duty.
B. The statute forbids claims against any relative.
C. Only house owners can file a maintenance case.
D. The 2007 framework reaches children and specified relatives, not sons alone.

### Q5. Where does the 2007 Act provide summary adjudication?

A. A Maintenance Tribunal in every subdivision for relevant applications.
B. Only the Supreme Court as an original fact-finding tribunal.
C. The Central Waqf Council.
D. A medical-certificate desk without hearing powers.

### Q6. Which time frame characterises the Act's tribunal process?

A. An invariable instant judgment on application.
B. Ordinarily decision within 90 days, extendable in exceptional circumstances.
C. Mandatory ten-year consideration before listing.
D. No timeline under any State rule.

### Q7. How should the ₹10,000 monthly ceiling be described?

A. Every eligible claimant receives ₹10,000 automatically.
B. It is the central old-age pension for all over 60.
C. It is the Act's prescribed upper limit on the maintenance allowance; actual awards depend on circumstances.
D. It is a fixed income test for assistive devices.

### Q8. An older woman gives property to a carer who then refuses promised essentials. When can section 23 help?

A. Every gift is automatically void at age 60.
B. The maintenance tribunal automatically transfers all property to the State.
C. Any disagreement, without the statutory condition, compulsorily voids title.
D. Where transfer was subject to providing basic amenities/physical needs and the transferee fails, she may seek to void it.

### Q9. A gift deed contains no care condition. What caution follows?

A. Do not assume section 23's conditional-transfer remedy applies without examining the promised-care condition and facts.
B. Section 23 voids all gifts by older persons automatically.
C. The Act recognises only transfers made before age 60.
D. A helpline call by itself cancels registered title.

### Q10. What district-level institutional direction appears in the 2007 Act?

A. Every district must pay ₹150 as the sole welfare intervention.
B. States should establish and maintain at least one home per district for at least 150 indigent senior citizens.
C. Only Union authorities can open any home.
D. A hospitalisation card is equivalent to a functioning residential home.

### Q11. A State reports homes sanctioned but none accessible. What distinction is critical?

A. A sanction proves all homes have staff and food.
B. The 2019 Bill automatically built district homes.
C. A statutory establishment direction and functioning, dignified service delivery are different claims.
D. Every senior citizen prefers institutional placement.

### Q12. How should the 2019 amendment Bill be described at this owner's cutoff?

A. An enacted increase in every tribunal award.
B. A current automatic deduction power established in 2007.
C. A final court judgment on every elder-abuse complaint.
D. Proposed changes, introduced in December 2019 and later lapsed, not enacted 2007 Act provisions.

### Q13. A candidate cites a proposed direct deduction of maintenance from a relative's income. What qualification is needed?

A. It appeared in the lapsed amendment proposal, not an enacted power of that Bill.
B. It was mandatory in the 2007 original Act.
C. The 2026 Census implemented it administratively.
D. An AVYAY guideline can amend a statute.

### Q14. What is Atal Vayo Abhyuday Yojana (AVYAY) in the dated Ministry page?

A. A single universal cash pension for every citizen aged 60.
B. An umbrella for older persons' shelter, care and active participation, not a new constitutional pension right.
C. The statutory tribunal renamed.
D. A maternal health insurance scheme.

### Q15. Which AVYAY component supports senior-citizen homes?

A. Rashtriya Vayoshri Yojana as the only home builder.
B. Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan.
C. Integrated Programme for Senior Citizens.
D. Article 342A central list-making.

### Q16. Which AVYAY component supports State-level action planning?

A. The Supreme Court's EWS certificate portal.
B. Article 350B Special Officer.
C. The 2019 lapsed Bill as a currently operative scheme.
D. State Action Plan for Senior Citizens.

### Q17. An older person needs fitted assistive equipment for age-related infirmity. Which component is relevant?

A. Rashtriya Vayoshri Yojana, subject to age and economic eligibility guidelines.
B. The State Action Plan is itself a universal wheelchair delivery entitlement.
C. The 2007 tribunal directly manufactures aids.
D. Only hospitalisation coverage can supply aids.

### Q18. What income ceiling is recorded for Rashtriya Vayoshri's relevant guidance in this owner?

A. Every family with ₹8 lakh annual gross income receives an aid automatically.
B. Below poverty line or family income up to ₹15,000 per month for otherwise eligible persons aged 60+ with age-related infirmity.
C. No age or infirmity criterion applies.
D. The limit is the ₹200 central old-age pension.

### Q19. Which AVYAY component aims at productive engagement?

A. Only section 23 property revocation.
B. Only hospital discharge paperwork.
C. Senior Citizen Opportunities for Productive Engagement.
D. The 2027 caste enumeration programme.

### Q20. What is Seniorcare Ageing Growth Engine in this owner's umbrella account?

A. A judicial substitute for every Maintenance Tribunal.
B. An automatic pension of ₹10,000.
C. A State Waqf Board governance code.
D. An AVYAY component supporting innovation in ageing services, not a guaranteed income transfer.

### Q21. An elder fears financial abuse and isolation. What is Elderline's intended contact?

A. National senior-citizen helpline 14567 for information, grievances and emergency response.
B. The statutory maintenance ceiling is 14567 rupees.
C. A universal free residential-care guarantee.
D. A 2027 population census hotline.

### Q22. Why is a helpline call not the end of abuse prevention?

A. One call conclusively proves every household is safe.
B. Detection must connect to safety assessment, social workers, legal remedies and follow-up.
C. A referral automatically voids all property transfers.
D. Only financial abuse warrants intervention.

### Q23. What is the stated central pension assistance floor for eligible BPL people 60–79?

A. ₹500 per month plus identical State top-up everywhere.
B. ₹10,000 under the old-age-pension scheme for everyone.
C. ₹200 per month, with State top-ups varying; not the necessarily received total.
D. No pension before 80.

### Q24. What is the central-assistance figure for eligible BPL persons aged 80+?

A. ₹200 is the only possible total payment.
B. ₹15,000 monthly as a universal pension.
C. Exactly ₹10,000 fixed by a maintenance tribunal for everyone.
D. ₹500 per month; distinguish State supplements and dated central floor.

### Q25. Why should central old-age pension figures not be called a household's actual pension?

A. States may top up and eligibility and disbursement vary; central assistance alone does not show net received income.
B. All States pay exactly the same total.
C. Hospitalisation insurance is a cash pension.
D. A tribunal's maximum is automatically added to central pension.

### Q26. Which programme addresses geriatric care in the public-health system?

A. The 2019 lapsed Bill as an outpatient clinic programme.
B. National Programme for Health Care of the Elderly with service-continuity needs beyond hospital episodes.
C. The NCBC's State caste-list route.
D. Only the property-revocation tribunal.

### Q27. A citizen aged 70+ has a hospitalisation entitlement. What gap persists?

A. An inpatient card guarantees daily home care.
B. Hospitalisation automatically prevents social isolation.
C. Outpatient follow-up, medicines, rehabilitation and long-term support are not established merely by inpatient cover.
D. A maintenance award supplies all specialist treatment.

### Q28. Which dataset is the source owner's longitudinal ageing evidence?

A. A UDID registration count of all older Indians.
B. A single complaint register as national prevalence.
C. The proposed 2027 census as already final.
D. Longitudinal Ageing Study in India, with findings cited by wave/year and not treated as causal proof.

### Q29. An analyst infers elder-abuse prevalence from hotline calls. Why is this weak?

A. Under-reporting and selective contact make helpline counts an unsafe population denominator.
B. All abuse victims use a hotline every year.
C. Calls count households nationally without sampling.
D. No elder abuse occurs outside reported calls.

### Q30. A caregiver confiscates pension and pressures a property transfer. Which abuse type?

A. Only a medical impairment.
B. Financial/property abuse, potentially overlapping neglect or emotional coercion.
C. Always a voluntary market transaction.
D. By definition only physical violence.

### Q31. A family repeatedly isolates and humiliates an elder without hitting them. What applies?

A. Only physical injury counts as abuse.
B. Every disagreement legally amounts to section 23 revocation.
C. Emotional and psychological abuse can occur without physical assault.
D. A shelter grant proves dignity.

### Q32. Why can elder abuse stay hidden in a dependent household?

A. A lack of calls proves universally good family care.
B. Only homes can have abuse.
C. Section 23 itself collects representative prevalence data.
D. Care/housing dependence, shame and fear of abandonment deter reporting.

### Q33. What distinguishes neglect from an isolated missed pension payment?

A. Failure to provide needed care, potentially extending to abandonment, needs a contextual safety assessment.
B. Every delayed bank transfer is physical violence.
C. Cash transfers automatically supply all hands-on care.
D. Neglect is legally identical to a regional population projection.

### Q34. Migration and smaller families reduce co-resident care. What system pressure follows?

A. Maintenance tribunals alone supply daily nursing everywhere.
B. Formal home/community support and respite become important complements to informal family care.
C. Income security eliminates all eldercare labour.
D. Only cash pension amounts determine whether care is available.

### Q35. A hospital releases an older patient to an unsupported household. Which link is needed?

A. An inpatient admission is the entire care pathway.
B. A hotline replaces discharge planning and medication.
C. Continuity from geriatric treatment to rehabilitation, home care and social support.
D. Only a residential-home sanction provides family respite.

### Q36. Which view matches active ageing rather than passive welfare?

A. Require all older persons to take paid work.
B. Treat absence of diagnosed disease as full inclusion.
C. Withdraw maintenance rights from productive elders.
D. Support chosen civic, economic and social participation alongside care and income security.

### Q37. What is the 2002 Madrid ageing framework's analytical contribution in this owner?

A. Participation and dignity complement protection; policy must allow capabilities without denying dependency.
B. The 2007 Act was enacted by the Madrid conference.
C. All elderly citizens gained a statutory employment quota.
D. The UN plan fixed a national ₹200 pension.

### Q38. A ministry page lists 17.3 crore persons aged 60+ in 2026. How must it be labelled?

A. A verified enumerated population in 2026.
B. A projection on the official AVYAY page, not a counted 2026 census total.
C. The number of Elderline callers.
D. The number of AVYAY beneficiaries.

### Q39. A committee quotes 10.38 crore for older people. What date must accompany it?

A. It is a 2026 beneficiary count.
B. It is the number of 2020 PYQ candidates.
C. The official page reports this for 2011; later figures in that series are projections.
D. It is a statutory limit on home capacity.

### Q40. What is the audited 2020 GS-II ownership boundary?

A. The PYQ asks only for cash pensions.
B. The question was a 2026 Prelims multiple-choice item.
C. A maternal-health programme replaces the geriatric limb.
