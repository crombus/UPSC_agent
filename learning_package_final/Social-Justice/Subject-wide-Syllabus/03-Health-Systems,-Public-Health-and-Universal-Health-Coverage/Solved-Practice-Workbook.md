**Model answer:**

**Model answer:**

Universal health coverage means needed services of adequate quality without financial hardship. India's progress must therefore be assessed separately for population inclusion, effective service delivery and protection from health spending; no single card or clinic count settles all three.

Population: PM-JAY's core beneficiary group is eligibility-based, and its distinct 70-plus expansion increases reach among older citizens without making the whole programme universal for everyone. States can expand coverage, but geography and documentation still affect practical access. NHM's rural and urban wings support public facilities available more widely; a remote resident nevertheless needs reachable staff and transport. Service: Ayushman Arogya Mandirs, formerly HWCs, extend comprehensive primary care and screening, whereas PM-JAY purchases cashless secondary and tertiary hospitalisation for eligible beneficiaries. ASHAs and ANMs improve maternal follow-up and referrals. Yet equipment, medicines, doctor vacancies and continuity of chronic care determine whether listed packages become effective treatment. Public health interventions such as sanitation and surveillance are necessary even for insured households.

Finance: PM-JAY can reduce an eligible patient's inpatient bill, but outpatient drugs, diagnostics, travel and lost wages can still create out-of-pocket expenditure. National Health Accounts provide an official spending measure; a decline in the household share does not itself establish affordability for every income group. NHP, 2017 sets a 2.5% of GDP public-expenditure goal, not an achieved coverage figure. Monitor actual treatment denials, quality, catastrophic household spending and preventable disease, disaggregating rural, urban and social groups.

India has built complementary primary and inpatient pillars, but remaining eligibility, workforce and outpatient-finance gaps prevent an unqualified UHC verdict. Fund public primary care, strengthen referrals and enforce quality and grievance remedies, measuring people served rather than entitlements announced.

National financing improves health only when Union policy is converted into state and local service capacity. Health delivery depends on staffing, procurement, referral and regulation across levels, so a central allocation alone is not an outcome.
