#### 3. Detailed causal chain: the intergenerational trap (2024 PYQ Q7)

1. **Poverty** limits household capacity to purchase diverse, nutrient-dense food
   and invest in health/sanitation.
2. **Food insecurity** manifests as calorie deficit (hunger) and dietary
   monotony (lack of micronutrients).
3. **Undernutrition in early childhood** (especially first 1000 days) causes
   stunting (chronic) and wasting (acute), impairs cognitive development and
   weakens immunity.
4. **Impaired development** reduces school attendance, learning outcomes and
   skill acquisition.
5. **Low human capital** translates into low productivity and earnings in
   adulthood.
6. **Low earnings** perpetuate household poverty, which is then transmitted to
   the next generation (intergenerational trap).
7. **Breaking-the-cycle levers:**
   - Nutrition-specific: Poshan 2.0 (first-1000-days focus), PM POSHAN (school-
     age), micronutrient supplementation (IFA tablets).
   - Nutrition-sensitive: WASH convergence (SBM, JJM), income support (DBT,
     social security), agricultural diversification.
   - Portability: ONORC for migrant-worker inclusion.

### PYQ DEMAND CARD 1 — 2018 General Studies Paper II Question 17

**Demand:** How far do you agree that a focus on the availability of food rather than on human development policies is the cause of hunger in India, 15 marks, 250 words, exactly as recorded in the audited 2018-2023 Mains routing ledger.

**Status:** Routed to this owner in the audited 2018-2023 Mains routing ledger, where the Core route is recorded as superseding the older Advanced ownership. No official answer key exists for a Mains demand and none is claimed.

**Model answer:**

I agree only partly. National food availability matters, but hunger also depends on household access, bodily absorption and dietary use; human-development policy addresses these latter links.

Procurement and the public distribution system support supply. Under NFSA, 2013, priority-household members are entitled to 5 kg of grain per person monthly and Antyodaya Anna Yojana households to 35 kg per household monthly. Yet a migrant denied access despite a card remains hungry; ONORC addresses portability, not missing eligibility. Even regular grain cannot prevent anaemia caused by inadequate dietary diversity or stunting aggravated by infections and unsafe water. Poshan 2.0's anganwadis address early-childhood and maternal nutrition, while PM POSHAN provides meals for schoolchildren; sanitation and women's health are equally important. Targeted grain relieves immediate hunger, but local wages determine whether a family can buy pulses and vegetables; regular public health and clean water decide whether the resulting nutrients are absorbed. NFHS-5 (2019–21) recorded under-five stunting at 35.5%; the source's NFHS-6 (2023–24) figure is 29.3%, but changes across rounds cannot be attributed to grain allocations alone.

Conversely, an actual grain shortage would defeat even good health services. Availability is a necessary foundation, not a sufficient explanation of hunger. Secure affordable access, maternal and child care, dietary diversity and WASH while monitoring nutrition outcomes, not merely warehouse stocks.

**Model answer:**

Measured poverty and hunger may diverge because one captures purchasing power or multiple deprivations, while the other reflects dietary intake and the body's ability to absorb nutrients.

A household can cross an income-poverty line yet still lack diverse food, safe water or maternal health care. NFSA, 2013 grain rations cushion calorie access but do not alone correct anaemia or infection-related undernutrition. Conversely, subsidised grain can reduce immediate hunger even when earnings stagnate. Shrinking public expenditure on anganwadi workers, PM POSHAN meals, health or sanitation weakens the services that convert household income into nutrition. NFHS stunting and wasting record distinct chronic and acute outcomes; neither equals a monetary poverty count.

The relationship is therefore causal but not mechanical: altered survey methods, food prices and intrahousehold gender bias also affect comparisons. Maintain social spending across food, health, education and sanitation, and report poverty and nutrition indicators separately with their survey years.

### PYQ DEMAND CARD 3 — 2024 General Studies Paper II Question 7

**Demand:** Poverty and malnutrition create a vicious cycle, adversely affecting human capital formation. What steps can be taken to break the cycle, explain, 10 marks, 150 words, exactly as recorded in the audited 2024-2025 Mains routing ledger and reproduced in the Basic owner.

**Status:** Routed to this owner in the audited 2024-2025 Mains routing ledger. No official answer key exists for a Mains demand and none is claimed.

**Model answer:**

Poverty and malnutrition reinforce each other: poor diets and recurrent infections impair children's growth and learning; reduced future productivity then reproduces low income. Breaking the cycle requires intervention before and after birth, not simply distributing cereals.

During pregnancy and the first 1,000 days, anganwadi services under Poshan 2.0 can support supplementary nutrition, growth monitoring and counselling; maternal care and anaemia prevention protect both mother and infant. NFSA, 2013 makes foodgrains claimable for covered households, while PM POSHAN's cooked school meals support attendance and nutrition among eligible pupils. Safe water and sanitation reduce infection that prevents nutrient absorption. ONORC can help a migrant household use its existing ration entitlement away from home. Later, quality education and livelihoods support earnings, preventing intergenerational recurrence.

A card or meal count does not establish healthier growth. NFHS measures stunting, wasting and anaemia separately; disaggregate outcomes by district and group and remedy excluded families. Coordinate food, health, WASH and schooling rather than crediting one scheme for all gains.

**Model answer:**

Poverty restricts affordable food, health care and sanitation; malnutrition then lowers immunity, learning and later earning capacity, perpetuating poverty across generations. The first 1,000 days are especially consequential for physical and cognitive development.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Elucidate why measured poverty and measured hunger can move in different directions. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer:**

Monetary poverty measures income or spending; multidimensional poverty covers several deprivations; hunger and anthropometric indicators register dietary deficiency or its biological effects. Their trends need not coincide.

A household's earnings may rise above a poverty threshold while its child remains anaemic from an undiversified diet or stunted after repeated infections. NFSA, 2013 grain support may protect staple calories even when wages fall, while Poshan 2.0 and WASH determine whether nutrients are used. Stunting reflects low height for age after chronic deprivation; wasting reflects low weight for height after acute deprivation. NFHS survey rounds measure these outcomes, not the same individuals or denominators as income-poverty estimates.

Price shocks, unequal food allocation within households and different reference years can widen an apparent divergence. Compare like-for-like rounds and disaggregate by region and sex; do not infer that a fall in income poverty automatically ends malnutrition.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Assess whether India's food-security architecture has converted a discretionary scheme into an enforceable entitlement. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer:**

India's food-security architecture has made a real legal shift from a discretionary public-distribution scheme to claimable entitlements, but practical enforceability remains conditional on coverage and delivery.

NFSA, 2013 gives each member of a priority household 5 kg of foodgrain monthly and each Antyodaya Anna Yojana household 35 kg monthly. The Act also provides nutrition support across the life cycle and a food-security allowance where entitled grain or meals are not supplied. These legal specifications allow an eligible household to contest a denial rather than seek charity. Anganwadi nutrition under Poshan 2.0 and PM POSHAN school meals complement grain, but their implementation arrangements must not be confused with universal ration eligibility.

The right is bounded: not everyone is included on an NFSA list. Identification errors or outdated lists can deny access; electronic point-of-sale authentication, network failure and lack of documentation can also obstruct delivery. ONORC lets an already eligible migrant draw rations across state borders; it does not confer eligibility on someone excluded from the database. Nor does a subsidised grain claim ensure diverse diets or safe absorption. Local grievance officers, transparent registers and an effective allowance or supply remedy must make legal rights executable. Social audits can compare recorded distribution with actual household receipt.

Thus the statute transformed the legal position of covered people, but implementation and nutrition outcomes vary. Audit eligibility, quantity received, complaint resolution and child-health indicators separately before calling the transition complete.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Examine the claim that a focus on food availability rather than on human-development policy is the principal cause of hunger in India. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer:**

A singular emphasis on national grain availability cannot explain India's hunger. Availability matters, but a person needs economic and physical access, effective bodily absorption and nutritious use of food. I therefore accept the claim as a criticism of availability-only policy, not as proof that production is irrelevant.

Procurement, buffers and the NFSA, 2013 foodgrain entitlement protect staple supply. Yet a landless family may be unable to afford vegetables and protein even with subsidised cereals. A migrant with an NFSA card can lose practical access across borders unless ONORC portability and local delivery work. Diarrhoeal illness from unsafe water prevents nutrient absorption; maternal anaemia and poor breastfeeding or complementary feeding compromise early growth. Poshan 2.0 anganwadis address pregnancy and early childhood; PM POSHAN meals support eligible schoolchildren. Neither supplies clean water or a complete diet by itself. NFHS stunting measures chronic deprivation whereas wasting indicates acute undernutrition; the source records NFHS-5 under-five stunting at 35.5% and NFHS-6 at 29.3%. These are round-specific observations, not evidence that one scheme caused the change.

Supply shocks and inflation can still generate hunger, so resilient procurement and distribution are indispensable. But policies must also secure incomes, maternal care, sanitation, dietary diversity and usable entitlement for migrants. Diagnose the broken link with household and nutrition evidence rather than infer food security from stocks.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Assess the design of India's nutrition delivery system across the life cycle and the trade-offs it embodies. Answer in about 300 words.

**Model answer:**

India's nutrition architecture spans the life cycle but relies on separate institutions and different eligibility rules. Its promise is that staple security, care and school feeding can complement one another; its risk is that a person falls between their delivery systems.

Pregnancy and infancy require adequate maternal diets, counselling, breastfeeding support and infection prevention. Poshan 2.0, administered through the Ministry of Women and Child Development's anganwadi network, supplies supplementary nutrition and growth monitoring to eligible mothers and young children. This prioritises the first 1,000 days, when lasting stunting risk is high, but personnel vacancies and weak follow-up can turn monitoring into a register rather than treatment. For school-age children, PM POSHAN under the Ministry of Education provides cooked meals in eligible schools. Meals can support attendance but do not guarantee classroom learning or a complete diet outside school.

NFSA, 2013 gives covered priority households 5 kg grain per person monthly and Antyodaya Anna Yojana households 35 kg per household. It protects staple access; diverse proteins and micronutrients are separate requirements. ONORC makes an existing ration entitlement portable for migrants, but identification and authentication failures remain possible. Anaemia prevention, safe water, sanitation and primary care address nutrient use and illness; these cannot be replaced by more cereal. Stunting is chronic low height for age, wasting acute low weight for height, and anaemia a distinct haemoglobin deficit. One output measure cannot stand in for the others.

The design trades targeted fiscal concentration against exclusion, and digital portability against authentication risk. Coordinate anganwadi, school, ration and health services through shared referrals without treating enrolment as success. Disaggregate NFHS nutrition results by age, gender and district, and independently verify meal quality and actual receipt. A life-cycle chain is effective only if every transition remains accessible.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Assess whether portability and identification reform have reduced exclusion from India's food entitlement. Answer in about 300 words.

**Model answer:**

Portability and better identification can reduce exclusion from NFSA foodgrain entitlements, but they solve different problems and neither alone ensures that an eligible person receives food.

ONORC permits a ration-card holder to draw an existing entitlement outside their home state through Aadhaar-seeded records and electronic point-of-sale transactions. It addresses the migrant worker whose mobility otherwise severs access to a home-state ration shop. NFSA, 2013 distinguishes priority-household members' 5 kg monthly per person from Antyodaya Anna Yojana households' 35 kg monthly per household; portability moves these entitlements, not the person's coverage category. A migrant excluded from the underlying list gains nothing from a portable card they do not have. Even a listed family may face failed authentication, poor connectivity, an unstocked shop or hostile treatment. Children and older people dependent on another member's card may encounter additional practical barriers.

Identification reform can update stale lists and reduce wrongful inclusion, but demanding exact documents from informal workers, the homeless or recently relocated households may increase wrongful exclusion. Narrower thresholds redistribute scarce benefits but can miss unstable-income families; registrations or transaction totals cannot reveal everyone who was turned away. State governments and local fair-price-shop systems must maintain stock and accessible alternatives when digital checks fail. Display entitlements, provide receipt records, protect privacy, enable corrections and make grievance appeals reachable without travel costs.

Evaluate reforms through surveys of eligible households, refusal and complaint data and actual quantities collected across districts, not merely portable transaction counts. Nutrition outcomes also require Poshan 2.0, PM POSHAN, dietary diversity and WASH; ration portability cannot be credited with all changes in stunting. Thus mobility-linked exclusion can fall where infrastructure works, while list exclusion and frontline failure require separate remedies.
