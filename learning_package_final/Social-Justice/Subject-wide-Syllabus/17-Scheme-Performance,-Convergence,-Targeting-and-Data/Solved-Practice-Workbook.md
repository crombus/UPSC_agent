### SOURCE-GROUNDED EVIDENCE AND CLOSE-OPTION BOUNDARIES

- **Cycle and status:** This is the Social Justice capstone, integrating the previous sixteen topics rather than re-owning them. Targeting determines eligibility; delivery reaches a person; convergence combines services; outcome measurement checks change; correction feeds findings into redesign. A success at one stage does not prove success at another. These are owner-defined analytical stages, not separate statutory authorities.
- **NSAP:** The Ministry of Rural Development's non-contributory umbrella has five components: Indira Gandhi National Old Age Pension Scheme (IGNOAPS), Indira Gandhi National Widow Pension Scheme (IGNWPS), Indira Gandhi National Disability Pension Scheme (IGNDPS), National Family Benefit Scheme (NFBS: qualifying lump sum on death of the primary breadwinner) and Annapurna (food for elderly persons not covered by pension). Fixed central assistance may be supplemented by States. Eligibility must be checked under the relevant below-poverty-line and State routes, never assumed to be a single current SECC list.
- **Targeting and errors:** SECC 2011 used housing, land, assets and occupation deprivation proxies; it is distinct from the decennial Census. Its rural/urban data informs the core PM-JAY route, not the separate all-70-plus age route; caste data collected in SECC was not released or validated for general policy use. Inclusion error (Type I) admits ineligible persons; exclusion error (Type II) leaves eligible persons out. Static proxies miss migration and newly arising deprivation; self-declaration can increase inclusion, while rigid certificate or biometric checks can increase exclusion. Broad NFSA provision covers roughly two-thirds of people in the owner account and can reduce exclusion at greater cost; neither broad nor targeted design is universally best.
- **Delivery versus convergence:** DBT and JAM, PFMS and the single nodal agency arrangement address transfers and fund flow, not automatically service access. ANMs, ASHAs, anganwadi workers and panchayat secretaries work through distinct reporting lines; PRIs and urban local bodies implement locally. Horizontal coordination joins ministries at the same level; vertical coordination links Centre, State, district and block. The worked *analytical* PVTG household may need individual/community FRA rights, PM-JANMAN housing/road/water, PM POSHAN, Poshan 2.0, PM-JAY and NSAP; none of those entitlements becomes automatic merely because another is granted. Different databases, cadres, district authority/visibility and payment cycles can each obstruct convergence. Land-record mismatch blocking PM-KISAN, housing sanction without resolved malnutrition, and high ICDS enrolment with continuing anaemia are boundary *examples*, not measured case studies.
- **Measurement:** Administrative records are frequent and scheme-specific but omit non-enrollees; surveys compare sampled outcomes with a lag; a population Census is broader but infrequent. **Model answer:**
Awareness and involvement matter because a person cannot claim an unknown entitlement, and policymakers cannot correct exclusions they never hear about. But participation is a necessary, not sufficient, explanation of scheme performance.

At design, affected households can identify barriers that aggregate eligibility rules miss. Assisted correction and notice must accompany consultations. At delivery, an ASHA or anganwadi worker can explain entitlements, while panchayat-level meetings and social audits can expose non-payment or inaccessible hours. ### PYQ DEMAND CARD 2 — 2023 General Studies Paper II
**Demand:** Development and welfare schemes for the vulnerable are, by nature, discriminatory in approach. Do you agree? Give reasons. 15 marks; 250 words.
**Provenance:** Audited 2018–23 Mains routing ledger; wording checked against the locally held official 2023 GS-II paper. This is an original model, not a UPSC key.
**Model answer:**
I agree that welfare design differentiates between people, but not that every differentiation is unjust discrimination. Treating unlike vulnerabilities alike can perpetuate disadvantage; arbitrary rules or uncorrected denial make a justified classification oppressive in practice.

NSAP illustrates purposeful differentiation: its old-age, widow and disability pensions, death-related family benefit and Annapurna food support address different contingencies. A single uniform benefit would miss those needs. Similarly, the deprivation-based core PM-JAY route concentrates resources, while its separate all-70-plus route uses age; eligibility depends on the programme's purpose. Broad NFSA coverage lowers exclusion at higher fiscal cost and possible inclusion of non-poor people. Neither universalism nor targeting is automatically superior.

Yet classification is only as fair as its operation. SECC 2011 housing, land and asset proxies may not reflect newly acquired poverty; missing documents or failed authentication may deny a genuinely eligible claimant. These are exclusion errors, whereas admitting an ineligible claimant is inclusion error. Neither is cured by asserting that the policy favours the vulnerable. A land-record mismatch in a hypothetical multi-scheme household shows how administrative silos can ration access by accident. Under the privacy principle associated with *Puttaswamy*, data use requires legality, purpose, proportionality and safeguards; identity checking must not extinguish entitlement.

Thus, compensatory targeting is defensible if criteria track need, are publicly intelligible and can be challenged. Provide assisted offline access, timely updating, appeal and independent checks of who remains excluded. Judge discriminatory effect against actual distribution and remedy, not the scheme's benevolent title.
### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS
**Question:** Distinguish inclusion error from exclusion error in social-sector targeting, and explain how a deprivation-based targeting database affects each. Answer in about 150 words.
**Model answer:**
Inclusion error (Type I) admits an ineligible person; exclusion error (Type II) denies an eligible one. A threshold redistributes these risks.

SECC 2011 uses housing, assets, land and occupation as deprivation proxies, distinct from the decennial Census. Its criteria underpin the core PM-JAY route, so filtering can reduce leakage compared with self-declaration. But the proxies imperfectly reflect current need: mobility or new poverty can leave eligible households off the list. Document checks and failed authentication add exclusions even where deprivation is real. Conversely, apparently deprived households may qualify despite better present circumstances, so inclusion error survives.

The separate PM-JAY age-based route for everyone aged seventy and above is not SECC-limited. Broader eligibility can protect people at greater cost. Choose thresholds by programme purpose, publish rules and provide assisted claims and correction. A clean database is not proof that the needy obtain care.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS
**Question:** Critically examine the claim that India's social-sector schemes suffer from a convergence deficit at the household level. Answer in about 250 words.
**Model answer:**
The claim is persuasive because deprivation is simultaneous but delivery follows departments. Convergence requires horizontal coordination among ministries and vertical coordination across Centre, State, district and block; one axis cannot replace the other.

Purpose-limited records, named referral responsibility, aligned schedules and assisted correction address distinct bottlenecks. *Puttaswamy*'s legality and proportionality safeguards matter when linking sensitive records; an unchecked registry is not convergence.

District authority must be matched with local grievance review and independent survey/evaluation evidence; exhortations to 'coordinate better' cannot replace these mechanisms.

**Model answer:**
Performance is a chain, not a coverage number: identify need, deliver, coordinate, measure change and correct failure. Its bottleneck varies by stage.

At targeting, SECC 2011's deprivation proxies support the core PM-JAY route but can miss newly poor or mobile households. ### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS
**Question:** Evaluate the proposition that a rights-respecting data architecture is a precondition for, rather than a threat to, effective welfare delivery. Answer in about 300 words.
**Model answer:**
Rights-respecting data architecture is a condition of effective welfare, not an optional restraint on it. An inaccurate or inaccessible register can deny eligible people as surely as delayed funds can; unrestricted data pooling can add profiling and coercion to those failures. But safeguards should make legitimate coordination possible, not prohibit it.

NFSA's broad food-security coverage, roughly two-thirds of the population in the owner account, can protect households missed by fine-grained poverty tests, but it is not literal coverage of everyone. The core PM-JAY route uses SECC-derived deprivation and occupation criteria; a separate all-70-plus age-based route shows that one programme may combine logics. NSAP, a non-contributory MoRD umbrella, covers old age, widowhood, disability, death of a primary breadwinner and food support for some elderly people not receiving a pension. Central assistance may be supplemented by States, while actual eligibility follows the relevant below-poverty-line and State routes, not one imagined current national list.

SECC's 2011 vintage risks missing later deprivation; housing, land and assets are only proxies. Aadhaar can help establish identity or detect duplication but cannot prove need; a missing document or failed authentication must not cancel an otherwise valid claim. Conversely, unverified self-declaration can admit ineligible claimants. Design a federated architecture: update programme records through lawful, purpose-specific exchanges, minimise sensitive fields, log access, allow claimants to see and correct entries, and provide assisted offline enrolment and an appeal with a recorded decision. Census 2027 caste enumeration is ongoing, not a published eligibility list.
