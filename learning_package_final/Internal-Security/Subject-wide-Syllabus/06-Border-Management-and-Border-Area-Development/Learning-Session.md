# Border Management and Border Area Development — Complete Learning Session

> **GS paper:** III
> **Syllabus anchor:** security challenges and their management in border areas; applied mandates of security forces and agencies.
> **Core rule:** identify the boundary type and dominant threat first; then match guarding, lawful regulation, development and bilateral mechanisms without confusing an input with a security outcome.

### Authoritative legal, institutional and current checks, accessed 27 September 2026

- **MHA border map:** India reports 15,106.7 km of land borders with Bangladesh, China, Pakistan, Nepal, Myanmar, Bhutan and a claimed Afghanistan segment. Country totals and a fencing answer that counts only an International Border segment use different denominators.
- **Lead border-guarding forces:** BSF—Pakistan International Border and Bangladesh; ITBP—China frontier; SSB—Nepal and Bhutan; Assam Rifles—Myanmar. “One border, one force” identifies lead responsibility, not an exclusive monopoly: the Army, State police, Customs, immigration and intelligence agencies retain distinct roles.
- **BIM:** the officially approved Border Infrastructure and Management scheme period was 2021-22 to 2025-26 with an outlay of ₹13,020 crore. No official extension beyond that approved period was located by the review date; describe any later position only from a newer notification.
- **BADP:** an MHA Rajya Sabha reply dated 1 April 2026 says BADP is in its **sunset phase**, with funds allocated for committed liabilities. It still defines coverage as villages/towns within 0–10 km crow-fly distance from the first habitation at the international border in 16 States and 2 UTs.
- **VVP-I and VVP-II:** VVP-I was approved on 15 February 2023 for 662 selected northern-border villages. VVP-II was approved on 2 April 2025 as a Central Sector Scheme with an outlay of ₹6,839 crore for 1,954 selected villages in border blocks outside VVP-I, in 15 States and 2 UTs, for FY 2024-25 to 2028-29. These are not relabelled BADP projects.
- **ICPs/LPAI:** the Land Ports Authority of India Act, 2010 establishes LPAI. The official LPAI homepage, accessed 27 September 2026, listed 15 land ports for regulated passenger and goods movement; this is a dated operational inventory.
- **FMR:** The historical India–Myanmar FMR allowed eligible border residents within 16 km to cross with a border pass for visits up to two weeks. MHA announced on 8 February 2024 that the regime should be scrapped and recommended immediate suspension while MEA processed scrapping. The accessible official material reviewed did not supply one consolidated later instrument proving uniform field implementation; historical mechanics, announcement and current operation are therefore kept separate.
- **India–Bangladesh settlement:** the land transfer was implemented through the Constitution (One Hundredth Amendment) Act, 2015; the maritime boundary was delimited by the 7 July 2014 Annex VII arbitral award.
- **India–China management:** the 1993, 1996, 2005, 2012 and 2013 instruments created peace, confidence-building and consultation arrangements; they did not settle or mutually delineate the entire boundary.
- **Ladakh:** no official Sixth Schedule inclusion was found by the review date. Statutory Ladakh Autonomous Hill Development Councils are not Sixth Schedule Autonomous District Councils.

### Evidence limits

1. Border-length, fencing and infiltration figures must carry the date and denominator; the Pakistan International Border is not the same denominator as the Pakistan total that also reflects other sectors.
2. A fence, road, sensor, sanctioned project, pass portal or ICP is an input/capability; it does not by itself prove interdiction, lawful prosecution, development delivery or public trust.
3. “Illegal migrant,” refugee/asylum claimant, trafficking victim, smuggled person, infiltrator and local border resident are not interchangeable legal or analytical categories.
4. Intelligence attribution, apprehension, seizure and prosecution are different evidentiary stages.
5. The 8 February 2024 FMR decision must not be converted into an unsupported claim about a uniform final rule operating identically across the entire border.
6. This topic covers land-border management. Maritime defence, coastal policing and Sir Creek’s seaward consequences are developed in Topic 07.

## BASIC LEARNING SESSION

### Route map — ask five questions before prescribing a border solution

```text
What kind of line is it?
        ↓
What lawful movement must continue?
        ↓
What threat vector exploits terrain or procedure?
        ↓
Which force, civil agency and bilateral mechanism own each stage?
        ↓
Did the input produce delivery, interdiction, prosecution and trust?
```

### SESSION 1 — Border management is a governed interface, not a longer fence

Border **security** is the coercive core: surveillance, defence and interdiction. Border
**management** adds regulation of lawful passengers and goods, development of frontier
settlements, and bilateral mechanisms for disputes and incidents. Ashok Kumar Singh's
four-part formulation—guarding, regulation, development and bilateral institutions—is
therefore an answer framework, not a decorative definition.

Consider a legal trader at Petrapole, a trafficked child intercepted between crossings and
an armed infiltrator at the LoC. All three encounter the border, but Customs/LPAI
facilitation, victim-centred police action and military interdiction are different public
functions. A single "more fencing" prescription cannot answer them.

| Function | Named machinery | Success evidence | Common trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Guarding | BSF, ITBP, SSB, Assam Rifles; Army on military lines | detection-to-interdiction time, safe hand-over | equating deployment with zero leakage |
| Regulation | Customs, immigration, notified land ports/ICPs | lawful throughput plus reasoned refusal/seizure | treating LPAI as police or Customs |
| Development | BADP legacy, BIM infrastructure, VVP-I/VVP-II | working service, livelihood and retention | treating sanction as delivery |
| Bilateral management | flag meetings, hotlines, WMCC/SR process, treaties | incident resolution/de-escalation | calling management a final settlement |

**Exam line:** A secure frontier filters movement lawfully; it neither seals every kilometre
nor criminalises every mobile border community.

**Trap to reject:** "Development replaces guarding." Development can reduce vulnerability
and improve trust, but it cannot intercept a weapon, investigate trafficking or manage an
armed face-off.

### SESSION 2 — The legal character of the line determines the operating doctrine

An **International Boundary (IB)** is an agreed boundary. The **Line of Control (LoC)** is
the India-Pakistan military control line in Jammu and Kashmir, not an international
boundary. The **Line of Actual Control (LAC)** is associated with the unresolved
India-China boundary and differing perceptions in places. The **Actual Ground Position
Line (AGPL)** records deployments beyond NJ9842 in the Siachen sector.

```text
agreed IB → guarding + Customs/immigration + ordinary criminal process
LoC/AGPL  → military posture + infiltration control + ceasefire mechanisms
LAC       → patrol management + military readiness + diplomatic/military dialogue
open/community-linked border → selective identity/regulatory controls + local trust
```

MHA's planning total is **15,106.7 km**: Bangladesh 4,096.7; China 3,488;
Pakistan 3,323; Nepal 1,751; Myanmar 1,643; Bhutan 699; and Afghanistan
106 km claimed. A country total is not the denominator for a fencing answer that covers
only one IB segment.

**Evidence discipline:** A "porous" boundary may be legally settled but difficult to police;
an "open" border may permit specified movement while sovereignty and criminal law remain;
a managed LAC is not thereby mutually delineated.

### SESSION 3 — Western frontier: four segments, four operational problems

| Segment | Core problem | Principal actors/mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Pakistan IB/Radcliffe sector | tunnels, infiltration, arms/narcotics and other smuggling | BSF, State police, Customs/intelligence, physical and electronic grid |
| LoC | proxy infiltration and military escalation/ceasefire management | Army, intelligence, DGMO and field channels |
| AGPL/Siachen | extreme-altitude deployment and logistics | Armed Forces |
| Sir Creek | unresolved tidal-boundary interpretation with fishing and seaward effects | diplomacy, hydrography, Coast Guard/coastal agencies |

The sector distinction changes both evidence and remedy. A seizure near the IB establishes
an interdiction; it does not prove the sponsor or every downstream link. A fall in firing
along the LoC does not measure tunnelling, drone drops or inland networks. Sir Creek is a
roughly 96-km tidal boundary issue at the land-sea seam; India invokes a mid-channel/thalweg
reading while Pakistan relies on an eastern-bank reading. It is not a substitute for the
whole Pakistan-border analysis. The canonical segment approximations—about 2,308 km IB,
776 km LoC and 110 km AGPL—must remain source-attributed and must never be combined with a
single fencing denominator.

**Answer paragraph:** The Pakistan frontier is not one line. BSF-led IB control targets
cross-border crime and infiltration, the Army manages the LoC and AGPL military problem,
and Sir Creek requires negotiated delimitation plus humane fisher handling. This
segmentation prevents both wrong force attribution and misuse of a fencing statistic.

### SESSION 4 — China and Bangladesh demonstrate opposite boundary-management problems

On the China frontier, ITBP is the lead border-guarding force while the Army carries the
defence role. The 1993, 1996, 2005, 2012 and 2013 agreements created confidence-building
and consultation arrangements; they did not produce a final mutually delineated boundary.
Galwan (2020) and Yangtse/Tawang (2022) show why patrol protocols and crisis channels can
remain useful yet insufficient.

Bangladesh presents a settled-but-difficult-to-police frontier: riverine stretches, dense
habitation and cultivation close to the line complicate barriers and create frequent
legal/illegal mobility questions; the two countries also share 54 trans-boundary rivers in
the canonical account. The old enclave/adverse-possession issue was implemented
through the **Constitution (One Hundredth Amendment) Act, 2015**; the maritime boundary was
delimited by the **7 July 2014 Annex VII award**. Those matters must not be recycled as
current disputes.

> **Comparison:** China requires management without settlement; Bangladesh shows settlement
> without the disappearance of policing, migration, smuggling or trafficking challenges.

### SESSION 5 — FMR: learn the mechanics, then state the status precisely

The historical India-Myanmar **Free Movement Regime (FMR)** was a regulated exception to
ordinary passport/visa travel for eligible border residents. The official pre-2024 design
allowed qualifying people residing within **16 km** of the border to cross with a border
pass and remain for a limited visit of up to **two weeks**. Its purpose was to accommodate
ethnic, kinship and livelihood ties that pre-date the boundary; Assam Rifles and civil
authorities supplied the security/administrative interface.

```text
eligibility/residence proof
        ↓
border pass and authorised crossing/recording
        ↓
limited depth and duration
        ↓
return/monitoring
        ↘ misuse risks: insurgent movement, narcotics, arms, forged identity
```

On **8 February 2024**, MHA announced that the FMR should be scrapped to protect internal
security and demographic structure, stated that MEA was in the process of scrapping it,
and recommended immediate suspension meanwhile. The accessible official material reviewed
does not provide one consolidated later instrument proving identical field implementation
across the entire frontier. Therefore:

- do not write that the old 16-km entitlement simply continues unchanged;
- do not write that the press release alone proves every legal step and local procedure was
  completed instantly;
- do not infer legal status merely from the existence of an online border-pass portal.

**Policy problem:** regulation must distinguish insurgent/criminal misuse from ordinary
kinship, trade, humanitarian movement and trafficking victims. The answer is risk-based
identification and recorded crossings, not the false choice between an unmonitored frontier
and indiscriminate closure.

### SESSION 6 — Institutions: lead responsibility must connect to a federal case chain

The post-Kargil reform sought **one border, one force**: BSF for the Pakistan IB and
Bangladesh, ITBP for China, SSB for Nepal/Bhutan and Assam Rifles for Myanmar. The 2001
Group of Ministers' border-management task force was chaired by **Madhav Godbole**, and the
Department of Border Management became the coordinating civilian node.

Lead responsibility is not exclusivity. The Army retains defence roles on contested or
military lines; State police investigate crimes and maintain public order; Customs and
immigration regulate goods and persons; intelligence agencies warn; prosecutors and courts
convert evidence into legal outcomes. Article 355 and Union List Entry 2A explain Union
support, while State List Entries 1 and 2 retain public-order and police significance.
The Advanced owner also records Vikas Battalions under the Cabinet Secretariat in parts of
the China frontier, showing why the lead-force principle still requires command integration.

**Mechanism test:** follow one event. A BSF sensor detects a package; a patrol recovers it;
forensics links payload and device; State police register the applicable offence; specialist
agencies follow finance or organised networks; a court decides guilt. Deleting any hand-off
turns "coordination" into an acronym list.

### SESSION 7 — CIBMS, BOPs and counter-UAS: detection is only the first rung

The physical layer—fence, floodlighting, patrol road and Border Out Post—channels and
supports guarding. **CIBMS** adds electro-optical, thermal, radar or ground-sensor feeds,
communications and command-and-control. The Madhukar Gupta Committee's **grid border
protection** idea is wider still: depth intelligence, police links and rapid response are
connected instead of assuming that a continuous barrier is possible everywhere.

Border roads are executed through agencies including the Border Roads Organisation, CPWD
and State PWDs. The DS-DBO road, completed in 2019 in the canonical source, illustrates how
connectivity can change logistics; it does not by itself prove deterrence or settle the LAC.

For a hostile UAV, the operative sequence is:

```text
detect → classify → track → authorised neutralisation/interception
      → secure scene/payload → exploit telemetry/components
      → investigate finance/operator → assess sponsor cautiously
```

Jamming can interfere with lawful spectrum or aviation; kinetic action can create falling
debris; spoofing and capture require trained operators. Recovery of a foreign-made component
does not by itself establish the launching actor or State sponsorship.

**Exam trap:** sensor coverage, number of drones sighted and number shot down are different
indicators. The meaningful chain ends in response, evidence and disruption of the network.

### SESSION 8 — BOP and ICP answer different questions

A **Border Out Post** supports guarding between authorised crossings. An **Integrated Check
Post/land port** co-locates passenger and cargo facilities such as immigration, Customs,
security, quarantine, warehousing and scanning. The **Land Ports Authority of India Act,
2010** gives LPAI the infrastructure-management and coordination role; it does not confer
Customs, police or judicial powers.

The official LPAI homepage, accessed **27 September 2026**, lists **15 land ports**:
Attari, Agartala, Darranga, Dawki, Petrapole, Raxaul, Rupaidiha, Jogbani, Mankachar,
Golakganj, Moreh, Sutarkandi, Srimantapur, Sabroom and the Passenger Terminal Building at
Dera Baba Nanak. This is a dated operational inventory, not a permanent constitutional
number.

**Mechanism:** a functional ICP pulls legitimate traffic toward a recorded gateway, reduces
transaction costs, improves inter-agency hand-offs and leaves better audit trails. It cannot
eliminate informal routes, and throughput alone does not show lawful treatment or security.

### SESSION 9 — BADP, BIM and the two VVPs must never be collapsed

| Instrument | Design/status at 27 Sep 2026 | What not to claim |
|---|---|---|
| BADP | legacy border-area development; MHA says sunset phase, funds for committed liabilities | no new universal BADP pipeline |
| BIM | Central Sector umbrella for fencing, roads, floodlighting, BOPs and technology; approved 2021-22 to 2025-26 with ₹13,020 crore | no extension beyond the approved period without a later notification |
| VVP-I | Centrally Sponsored Scheme approved 15 Feb 2023; 662 selected northern-border villages | not the same geography or funding form as VVP-II |
| VVP-II | **Central Sector Scheme approved 2 Apr 2025; ₹6,839 crore; 1,954 selected villages; FY 2024-25 to 2028-29** | approval/outlay does not prove village-level delivery |

VVP-II covers strategic villages in blocks abutting international land borders other than
the northern-border geography already covered by VVP-I. Its official logic joins better
living conditions and livelihoods with safer borders, trans-border-crime control and
community participation. Convergence seeks all-weather roads, telecom, television and
electrification alongside livelihood and social infrastructure.

```text
approval/outlay → State/UT plan → project sanction → procurement/work
→ functional asset/service → sustained use → livelihood/trust/security effect
```

Only the first two rungs are proved by a Cabinet approval. A bridge sanctioned is not a
bridge delivered; an asset delivered is not proof of reduced crime. This **sanction ≠
delivery ≠ outcome** distinction is mandatory.

### SESSION 10 — Border communities, Ladakh and constitutional voice

Border residents are rights-holders and economic actors before they are intelligence
sources. Reliable roads, telecom, schools, health services, legal trade and grievance
redress can reduce out-migration and illicit-market dependence. Profiling, uncompensated
land loss or arbitrary gate restrictions can instead erode trust and voluntary reporting.
Village Defence Guards in J&K are a bounded local-security example: they operate under
district-police supervision and require vetting, training, weapons control and complaint
review; they are not a general model for deputising every border village.

Ladakh is the cleanest synthesis. It fronts both China and Pakistan sectors. **BADP/VVP**
are civilian development channels. **Operation Sadbhavana** is an Army civic-action
programme; official PIB material identifies Army Goodwill Schools, health, skills,
infrastructure and community-support activities. It is not BADP, VVP or a constitutional
autonomy scheme.

Ladakh Autonomous Hill Development Councils are statutory bodies. A Sixth Schedule
Autonomous District Council rests on **Articles 244(2) and 275(1)** and may receive specified
legislative, judicial and fiscal powers. Ladakh had not been included in the Sixth Schedule
on the evidence reviewed by 27 September 2026. The 2026 GS-III question therefore tests
three distinct channels—civil development, Army goodwill and constitutional representation.

**Answer line:** A "living border" is sustained when security presence, usable services and
political voice reinforce one another; none can be substituted for the other.

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

### ADVANCED BRANCH A — One-border-one-force is accountable primacy, not institutional solitude

The reform corrects diffuse responsibility by naming a lead guarding force. It cannot remove
the Army's defence function, State police powers, Customs/immigration mandates or judicial
control. The China frontier also illustrates practical overlap: ITBP guarding and Army
defence coexist. A mature answer asks whether command, intelligence, communications and
evidence transfer are integrated, not whether one badge is physically alone.

**Named evidence:** Kargil Review process; 2001 Group of Ministers; Madhav Godbole task
force. Follow a narcotics interception from the BSF patrol to State-police FIR, forensic
laboratory, financial investigation and court to show why supporting jurisdiction is
unavoidable. **Trap:** calling operational overlap proof that lead responsibility has no
value, or calling lead responsibility a legal monopoly.

### ADVANCED BRANCH B — Grid protection changes the unit of analysis

Linear security measures kilometres of fence. Grid protection maps routes, settlements,
water channels, tunnels, air vectors, roads, sensors, informants, police stations and
response teams in depth. CIBMS contributes the sensing/command layer, but maintenance,
bandwidth, trained operators and response time decide performance.

**Named mechanism:** Madhukar Gupta Committee recommendation. **Evidence test:** compare
alert-to-interception and recurrence, not merely installed sensor count. A riverine gap may
need patrol craft and community reporting; a tunnel-prone sector needs ground surveillance
and depth policing; a drone corridor needs air-domain sensors and forensic exploitation.
**Trap:** describing grid protection as a new name for continuous fencing.

### ADVANCED BRANCH C — Boundary settlement has three evidentiary levels

Peace-management agreements regulate conduct; delimitation describes an agreed line in law
or coordinates; demarcation marks it on the ground. The India-China agreements are
management instruments. The India-Bangladesh land settlement required treaty implementation
and the 100th Amendment; its maritime line followed a 2014 arbitral award.

**Analytical payoff:** diplomatic activity can be valuable without being falsely described
as final settlement. The 2015 constitutional amendment also demonstrates that an agreed
settlement may require domestic legal implementation before territorial exchange is complete.
**Trap:** using "disengagement", "delineation", "delimitation" and "demarcation"
interchangeably.

### ADVANCED BRANCH D — Development-security causation must be demonstrated

VVP-II's approved outlay and village count establish policy scope. They do not establish
functional roads, occupied homes, working telecom, livelihood retention or reduced
trans-border crime. A credible evaluation uses a results chain and compares village-level
service functionality, migration/retention, livelihood uptake, grievance resolution and
security indicators over time.

**Named evidence:** Cabinet approval of 2 April 2025; ₹6,839 crore; 1,954 villages;
FY 2024-25–2028-29. Compare a sanctioned telecom tower with actual uptime and emergency use,
or an approved road with all-weather access and maintenance. **Trap:** converting sanction
into delivery or a delivered asset into reduced trans-border crime without a causal test.

### ADVANCED BRANCH E — Hardening a community-linked border creates second-order risk

FMR reform may reduce an exploitable movement route, yet sudden or indiscriminate closure
can divert legitimate movement to unrecorded paths, separate kinship networks and weaken
cooperation. A calibrated design combines verified eligibility, authorised crossing points,
time/depth limits, digital and paper fallback, appeal, humanitarian screening and action
against arms/narcotics networks.

**Named status evidence:** MHA press release of 8 February 2024. **Unresolved point:** the
public material reviewed does not justify claiming one uniform final field regime. The
historical 16-km/two-week design should be taught as the pre-announcement mechanism, not
quietly converted into the present rule. **Trap:** using either the old rule or a portal's
existence as conclusive evidence of current entitlement.

### ADVANCED BRANCH F — Border infrastructure poses an ecological-security choice, not a veto

Roads and communications can improve defence mobility, disaster access and civilian
services, while fragile mountain and forest terrain can suffer slope failure, habitat
fragmentation and livelihood loss. The 2025 GS-IV application is answered through strategic
necessity, alternatives, cumulative impact, mitigation, consultation, reasoned decision,
monitoring and restoration—not by declaring either security or ecology absolute.

Project appraisal should compare route alignments, construction seasons, muck disposal,
watershed and slope effects, disaster access and local livelihood costs while protecting
genuinely sensitive operational detail. **Named evidence:** the 2025 GS-IV Q2(b) application.
**Trap:** treating "national security" as a reason to omit recorded reasons, mitigation and
post-construction monitoring.

### PYQ-ready close

India needs differentiated frontiers but integrated governance: credible guarding and crisis
management, visible lawful gateways, usable infrastructure, accountable policing and border
communities treated as partners and rights-holders. The proof is not money sanctioned or
technology installed; it is safer lawful movement, quicker interdiction, sustainable service
delivery and legitimate dispute handling.
