### PYQ 1 · 2020 GS-III · 15 marks · approximately 250 words

**Neutral rendering:** Analyse distinct security challenges on the India–Myanmar and India–Bangladesh borders and the Pakistan Line of Control.

**Model answer:** India's three frontiers differ in legal status, terrain and threat vector; calling all three simply “porous” loses the basis for a credible response.

**Myanmar:** Difficult terrain and ethnic communities living across the boundary complicate patrols and support legitimate kinship movement. Insurgent transit and narcotics networks exploiting proximity to the Golden Triangle require intelligence cooperation, Assam Rifles patrols and locally credible reporting. The Free Movement Regime facilitates eligible traditional movement; enforcement must distinguish residents from trafficking networks rather than criminalise community ties.

**Bangladesh:** This is a settled international boundary with dense settlement, cultivation close to the line and numerous riverine stretches. Migration disputes, cattle smuggling and other trans-border crime need coordinated BSF and police action, river surveillance and functional legal crossing points. Territorial questions concerning enclaves were addressed through the 2015 land-boundary settlement, following the 2014 maritime arbitral award. Neither settlement eliminates unlawful flows or river-water disagreements.

**Pakistan LoC:** Unlike the Bangladesh international boundary, the LoC is a military control line. Cross-LoC infiltration and ceasefire violations can threaten civilians; Army observation, layered intelligence and military communication channels must work together. Reduced firing alone would not prove that infiltration has ended.

Thus management must combine guarding, lawful regulation, border-area development and bilateral mechanisms according to the frontier. Secure mobility and community trust are enablers of intelligence, not dispensable concessions. Evaluate success separately through civilian safety, interdiction evidence and the preservation of legitimate movement; a fence or a ceasefire announcement is not itself an outcome.

### PYQ 2 · 2023 GS-III · 10 marks · approximately 150 words

**Neutral rendering:** Comment on adversarial use of UAVs to move arms, ammunition and drugs across India's borders and suggest countermeasures.

**Model answer:** A hostile unmanned aerial vehicle is a **delivery and reconnaissance vector**, not a self-evident identification of its operator. A small, low-flying platform can move arms, ammunition or narcotics over a fence and exploit short detection windows; a recovered payload may connect to a larger smuggling network.

Countermeasures must be layered: combine radar, electro-optical observation and human intelligence; link alerts to border guards, police and local response teams; use authorised, proportionate counter-UAS measures; preserve the device, payload and digital trail for forensic attribution and prosecution. Intelligence-sharing with the relevant neighbouring authorities, where practicable, targets organisers rather than only individual flights. Community tips require verification and protection from retaliation.

Detection is not interception; interception is not attribution; an equipment purchase is not proof of reduced trafficking. Performance should be measured through tested coverage, response time, evidentiary integrity and independently established disruption of networks, without inventing interception totals.

### PYQ 3 · 2024 GS-III · 15 marks · approximately 250 words

**Neutral rendering:** Examine conflicting issues and security challenges along India's China and Pakistan borders and explain BADP and the Border Infrastructure and Management (BIM) scheme. **Provenance:** Core companion identifies this as 2024 GS-III Q19 and records inspection of the printed stem.

**Model answer:** The China and Pakistan frontiers need different legal and operational vocabularies; an undelimited alignment cannot be treated as equivalent to a control line or a settled international boundary.

**China:** The disputed boundary and differing perceptions of the Line of Actual Control create patrol face-off risks. Aksai Chin and Arunachal Pradesh are distinct territorial flashpoints. The 2020 Galwan clash and 2022 Tawang face-off show why confidence-building arrangements, local command communication and diplomatic engagement must complement all-weather access, logistics and surveillance. Such mechanisms manage encounters; they do not alone settle competing claims.

**Pakistan:** The Radcliffe international-boundary sector faces smuggling, tunnelling and infiltration; the LoC combines military confrontation with infiltration and civilian exposure; the Siachen Actual Ground Position Line presents extreme-altitude deployment and logistics. Sir Creek remains a separate tidal-estuary boundary dispute. Responses therefore range from BSF policing and technological coverage to Army surveillance, ceasefire channels and bilateral negotiation.

**Development responses:** BADP addresses service, livelihood and connectivity deficits in eligible border villages; it is not a fencing scheme. BIM is the infrastructure umbrella supporting roads, fencing, floodlighting, communications and related management assets; it is not a village welfare programme. Their convergence can improve civilian access and response capacity, but scheme approvals neither prove completion nor establish a reduction in infiltration.

The India–Bangladesh land settlement illustrates what a negotiated territorial instrument can accomplish, not a quick template for an unresolved LAC. A credible strategy pairs security with maintained legal mobility, local participation and verifiable delivery, while keeping diplomatic settlement distinct from tactical conflict management.

### PYQ 4 · 2026 GS-III Q10 · 10 marks · approximately 150 words

**Neutral rendering:** In strategically situated Ladakh, discuss BADP and Army civic action for local goodwill, and the demand for Sixth Schedule protection. **Provenance:** Core companion records OCR verification against `books\mains\2026\QP-CSM-26-010926-GENERAL-STUDIES-PAPER-III.pdf`. This is a 2026 answer only; its developments must **not** be read back into earlier PYQs.

**Model answer:** Ladakh faces both China's disputed LAC and the Pakistan-facing LoC/AGPL sectors. Local trust therefore strengthens security as well as governance.

BADP, a civilian Central Government border-area development channel, supports eligible villages through basic services, connectivity and livelihoods; retaining viable settlements can sustain access and local knowledge. The Army's **Operation Sadbhavana** is a separate civic-action channel: Goodwill Schools, medical outreach and locally relevant skills can build relationships with remote communities. Neither expenditure nor outreach alone proves enduring goodwill.

After becoming a Union Territory without a legislature in 2019, Ladakh continued with **statutory** Autonomous Hill Development Councils. Local groups seek **Sixth Schedule constitutional** safeguards for land, tribal identity and meaningful self-government; existing councils are not themselves Sixth Schedule Autonomous District Councils. Improving ordinary council coverage or public services does not automatically satisfy that constitutional demand.

The balanced response combines accountable development and civic action with serious dialogue on autonomy and environmental protection, without confusing military assistance, civilian schemes and constitutional status.

## Six original Mains questions and model solutions

### Original 1 · 10 marks · target 150 words

**Question:** Distinguish border security from border management and explain the four-element management framework. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer:** Border **security** focuses on defending and guarding a frontier against illegal crossings and violence. Border **management** is wider: it reconciles protection with lawful movement, local welfare and relations with neighbouring states.

Its four linked elements are **guarding**, **regulation**, **development** and **bilateral mechanisms**. Guarding requires border-specific deployment: BSF on the Pakistan and Bangladesh international borders, ITBP on the China frontier, SSB on Nepal and Bhutan, and Assam Rifles on Myanmar. Regulation provides documented trade and lawful crossings through facilities such as Integrated Check Posts, rather than treating every traveller as a suspect. Development responds to isolation through border-village services and infrastructure, including BADP and appropriately targeted connectivity. Bilateral channels manage incidents and, where possible, disputes; India–Bangladesh's land settlement shows the value of an actual agreement, though a settlement does not eliminate smuggling.

The balance differs between the disputed China LAC, Pakistan LoC and open Nepal border. Thus fence length or sanctioned spending cannot substitute for measured safety, legitimate access and coordination.

### Original 2 · 10 marks · target 150 words

**Question:** Explain why BADP, BIM and the Vibrant Villages Programme must not be treated as one scheme. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer:** These initiatives can reinforce frontier resilience but differ in their **beneficiaries, instruments and implementation rules**. BADP addresses deficits in eligible border villages through local infrastructure, education, health and livelihoods: its central question is whether residents can remain and access services. BIM supports management infrastructure—roads, fencing, lighting, outposts, communication and surveillance—so its immediate users include guarding and response agencies. The Vibrant Villages Programme addresses selected frontier villages through its own village-level planning and convergence arrangements; it must be described using its official eligibility and non-overlap rules rather than silently folded into BADP.

A road may serve residents and patrols, but this overlap does not merge the schemes or prove benefits were delivered. Compare an input (approved funds), an output (usable asset or provided service) and an outcome (sustained access or improved safety). In a current-affairs answer, date the official scheme period and coverage before stating them; period-specific book figures are not reliable current figures. Development complements, rather than replaces, guarding and lawful border regulation.

### Original 3 · 15 marks · target 250 words

**Question:** Analyse security challenges along the Myanmar and Bangladesh borders and the Pakistan Line of Control. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer:** Distinct boundary regimes generate distinct threats. India–Myanmar and India–Bangladesh have international boundaries; the Pakistan Line of Control is a military control line, not an equivalent civilian crossing frontier.

**Myanmar:** Mountainous terrain, limited access and cross-border ethnic ties complicate both patrolling and legitimate family contact. Narcotics trafficking and insurgent transit call for Assam Rifles surveillance, local police intelligence and cooperation across the boundary. MHA announced a decision to scrap the Free Movement Regime in February 2024; a contemporary answer must distinguish that decision from notification and implementation. Livelihood alternatives, protected community reporting and regulated access matter because blanket suspicion can weaken intelligence.

**Bangladesh:** Dense settlements, cultivation and riverine gaps constrain continuous fencing. BSF–police cooperation, river patrols, legal trade routes and functioning customs/immigration facilities can target cattle smuggling, trafficking and irregular migration without assuming all movement is illicit. The land-enclave issue was settled through the 2015 Land Boundary Agreement and constitutional amendment, following the 2014 maritime award; water-sharing and trans-border crime still require attention. Describing the enclaves as an unresolved contemporary border dispute is anachronistic.

**LoC:** Infiltration and shelling expose civilians and require Army observation, intelligence fusion, protection of settlements and reliable military communication. Its ceasefire conditions and infiltration flows must be assessed separately: a period of reduced firing need not mean zero attempted crossings.

The common framework is guarding, regulation, development and bilateral contact; its mix differs by sector. Technology can detect a movement but needs rapid response and preserved evidence. Success must be evaluated against civilian security and lawful mobility, not an aggregate “sealed border” slogan.

### Original 4 · 15 marks · target 250 words

**Question:** Examine conflicting issues along the China and Pakistan borders and assess BADP and BIM as distinct responses. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer:** The India–China border is disputed, whereas the Pakistan-facing frontier consists of several legally and geographically different segments. This distinction should govern both the diagnosis and the use of development schemes.

**China:** Disputed claims in the western and eastern sectors and differing perceptions of the LAC produce encounters even where arrangements exist for restraint. Galwan (2020) and Tawang (2022) illustrate the limits of encounter-management mechanisms without a mutually settled boundary. Improve logistics, communications and incident de-escalation while keeping diplomatic negotiation separate from tactical patrolling. Better roads enhance access but cannot determine sovereignty.

**Pakistan:** Smuggling, tunnelling and illegal crossings are salient on the Radcliffe international-boundary stretch; infiltration, firing and civilian protection feature on the LoC; Siachen's AGPL requires high-altitude sustainment. Sir Creek raises a distinct disputed tidal-estuary alignment. BSF, Army, local police, intelligence agencies and bilateral channels therefore have different roles; simply extending the same fence everywhere ignores riverine and mountain constraints.

**BADP** supports eligible frontier villages' services and livelihoods. **BIM** builds border-management assets such as roads, floodlighting, fencing and communications. The former can make border communities more resilient; the latter can improve reach and response. Neither alone resolves the LAC or Sir Creek, and sanctioned projects should not be reported as completed security outcomes.

A differentiated strategy couples patrols and intelligence with development, legitimate trade and de-escalation. India–Bangladesh's territorial settlement demonstrates that agreement can resolve a defined boundary issue, but does not imply identical circumstances on China or Pakistan frontiers. The proper test is demonstrable safety and access alongside sustained dispute management.

### Original 5 · 20 marks · target 300 words

**Question:** Critically evaluate the one border, one force principle alongside technology and community participation. Answer in about 300 words.

**Model answer:** “One border, one force” is a post-Kargil organising principle intended to make lead responsibility clearer. It is useful, but only when interpreted as a coordination reform rather than a claim of exclusive operational control.

The conventional map assigns BSF the Pakistan and Bangladesh borders, ITBP the China frontier, SSB Nepal and Bhutan, and Assam Rifles Myanmar. A clear lead reduces duplication in patrol plans, information handover and infrastructure requests. Yet an Army role on the LoC and Siachen AGPL, state police powers over crime, customs at legal crossings and bilateral diplomacy cannot be abolished by a mnemonic. The disputed China LAC also involves military posture and diplomatic mechanisms; any account that treats ITBP's guarding lead as sole responsibility misreads the terrain and task.

Technology can close selected observation gaps. CIBMS combines sensors, cameras, communications and command systems; grid-style protection can connect a river gap or difficult stretch to patrols and intelligence. Hostile UAVs make low-altitude detection and evidence preservation important. But coverage can fail in terrain or weather, alarms need trained responders, and device recovery does not automatically identify a network. Test usable uptime, verified alerts, response times and admissible evidence rather than cite installation numbers as proof of security.

Communities are not merely surveillance assets. On the Nepal open border, lawful travel must remain possible; on the Myanmar frontier, kinship and trade needs complicate movement restrictions; near the Bangladesh frontier, cultivated plots and river routes matter. Accessible services, trusted complaint mechanisms and protected reporting can improve information while reducing abuse. Indiscriminate suspicion or closure undermines those gains.

The reform's defensible end-state is a named lead, written inter-agency handovers, intelligence and police integration, tested technology, and rights-respecting civilian engagement. Accountability should separately measure safety, legal mobility and community access. Neither a single uniform force model nor a technology procurement target is a substitute for boundary-specific outcomes.

### Original 6 · 20 marks · target 300 words

**Question:** Design a differentiated border-management strategy that balances security, legal mobility, infrastructure, area development and bilateral mechanisms. Answer in about 300 words.

**Model answer:** A viable strategy begins by classifying a border's **legal status, terrain, legitimate movement and dominant threat**, then assigns guarding, regulation, development and bilateral channels rather than prescribing a single fence.

On the **China LAC**, different perceptions and unresolved claims require clear patrol protocols, rapid local military communication, resilient road and communications access, and sustained diplomatic negotiation. Monitoring incidents can reduce escalation but must not be advertised as final demarcation. On the **Pakistan-facing frontier**, distinguish BSF-led enforcement against trafficking and infiltration on the international boundary, Army protection and ceasefire communication on the LoC, and high-altitude logistics on the AGPL. Sir Creek needs a negotiated boundary approach, not a mountain-security solution.

On **Bangladesh's settled boundary**, strengthen riverine observation, police–BSF coordination and lawful land-port processing through ICPs and LPAI, while preserving residents' access to fields and services. The 2014 maritime award and 2015 land settlement show that instruments can resolve particular territorial questions; they do not settle river-water sharing or eliminate crime. On the **Nepal open border**, preserve treaty-enabled travel with intelligence-led scrutiny of harmful networks. On **Myanmar**, Assam Rifles patrolling and anti-narcotics investigation should be paired with workable legal community mobility; any claim about the FMR's current implementation needs a dated notification. Bhutan's historical insurgent-camp experience warrants vigilance, not an unsupported assertion of present camps.

Across sectors, BADP-type village services and BIM-type physical assets should be planned together without merging their mandates; consider separately targeted village initiatives where eligible. Close unfenceable gaps with grid surveillance, verified alerts and a trained response, and protect community informants. Identify the force lead, state-police crime jurisdiction and diplomatic escalation channel in advance.

Publish separate indicators for operational availability, completed services, lawful crossing times, civilian safety and evidenced disruption of illicit networks. This prevents a sanctioned road, installed sensor or announced restriction from masquerading as a delivered, legitimate and durable security outcome.
