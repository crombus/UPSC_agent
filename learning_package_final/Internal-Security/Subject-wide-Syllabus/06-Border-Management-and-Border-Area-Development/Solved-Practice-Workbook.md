# Border Management and Border Area Development — Solved Practice Workbook

## Evidence and use note

This workbook is controlled by the Topic 06 Basic and Advanced owners, the Internal Security master framework and syllabus map, audited 2018–2026 routing ledgers, local official UPSC papers/keys, and the dated official sources listed in the companion learning session. Original questions are labelled as such. Scheme approval, expenditure, an interception, an arrest or an allegation is never converted into delivery, deterrence, conviction or attribution without evidence.

## BASIC MCQS / REMEDIATION

These **40 wholly rebuilt questions** form the workbook bank. Formats include single-best
answer, statement analysis, matching, chronology, legal distinction, scenario, evidence
inference and policy evaluation. Correct options rotate `A → B → C → D` exactly ten times.

### Q1. [Case application] A district plan combines patrols, a legal trade gate, village telecom and a flag-meeting protocol. What does this illustrate?

A. Border management as guarding, regulation, development and bilateral coordination.
B. Border defence as an exclusively military task with no civil component.
C. Customs modernisation without any security or community objective.
D. A development programme that can replace interdiction, diplomacy and legal border regulation across the district under one undifferentiated administrative chain.

**Answer: A.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — correct:** The four measures correspond to the complete border-management framework and answer different risks.
- **B — incorrect:** Military defence is only one layer; the plan expressly includes civil regulation and development.
- **C — incorrect:** Only the trade gate is a Customs-related element, so this description omits most of the design.
- **D — incorrect:** Services can reduce vulnerability, but they cannot replace lawful regulation, coercive capabilities, diplomacy or differentiated institutional mandates.

**Question-specific trap:** Do not shrink management to coercive guarding.

### Q2. [Match the lines] Which paired definitions survive legal scrutiny?

A. LAC—India-Pakistan military control line; AGPL—ordinary customs boundary.
B. LoC—India-Pakistan military control line; AGPL—deployment line beyond NJ9842.
C. IB—line of differing India-China perceptions; LoC—a settled international boundary recognised by treaty.
D. AGPL—India-Nepal open border; LAC—Sir Creek's tidal mid-channel.

**Answer: B.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** The LAC belongs to the India-China boundary problem, and the AGPL is not a customs boundary.
- **B — correct:** This preserves the distinct LoC and Siachen-sector meanings tested in border questions.
- **C — incorrect:** An IB is agreed, whereas the LoC is neither the India-China perception line nor a settled treaty boundary.
- **D — incorrect:** Neither the Nepal mobility arrangement nor Sir Creek defines these military lines.

**Question-specific trap:** Do not interchange lines created by different disputes and instruments.

### Q3. [Statements] Consider the following: 1. A legally settled border may remain porous. 2. An open border abolishes sovereignty. 3. A disputed border is necessarily densely populated. Which is correct?

A. Statements 1 and 2 only.
B. Statements 2 and 3 only.
C. Statement 1 only.
D. Statements 1, 2 and 3.

**Answer: C.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Statement 2 is false because facilitated movement does not extinguish State authority.
- **B — incorrect:** Both included statements are false; legal status, population and mobility are separate variables.
- **C — correct:** Porosity concerns enforceability, while openness and dispute status do not carry the claimed consequences.
- **D — incorrect:** The second and third propositions turn contingent features into universal rules.

**Question-specific trap:** Legal settlement, openness and physical porosity are independent variables.

### Q4. [Chronology] Which sequence correctly records settlement of India-Bangladesh boundary questions?

A. 2015 land settlement, followed by a 2020 maritime award and then the 100th Amendment.
B. 1993 LAC agreement, 2014 land exchange and 2015 Sir Creek award.
C. 2014 land-boundary amendment, followed by a 2015 Bay of Bengal plebiscite.
D. 2014 maritime arbitral award, then 2015 implementation of the land agreement through the 100th Amendment.

**Answer: D.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** The maritime award preceded, rather than followed, the constitutional implementation of the land settlement.
- **B — incorrect:** The LAC instrument concerns China, and neither Sir Creek nor the stated sequence fits Bangladesh.
- **C — incorrect:** The 100th Amendment implemented the land agreement; no Bay of Bengal plebiscite occurred.
- **D — correct:** This is the correct order and keeps maritime delimitation distinct from the enclave/land transfer.

**Question-specific trap:** Do not reverse the maritime-award and constitutional-implementation sequence.

### Q5. [Institution map] Which lead border-guarding-force allocation is accurate?

A. BSF—Pakistan IB and Bangladesh; ITBP—China; SSB—Nepal and Bhutan; Assam Rifles—Myanmar.
B. ITBP—Pakistan IB; SSB—China; Coast Guard—Myanmar; BSF—Nepal.
C. Army—every international boundary; State police—LoC and LAC exclusively.
D. LPAI—Bangladesh and Pakistan; NIA—China; Customs—Nepal and Bhutan.

**Answer: A.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — correct:** These are the designated lead guarding responsibilities, subject to military and civil-agency support.
- **B — incorrect:** Every force is assigned to the wrong frontier, and the Coast Guard is a maritime force.
- **C — incorrect:** The Army has defence roles, but routine lead guarding and policing are institutionally differentiated.
- **D — incorrect:** LPAI manages land-port infrastructure, NIA investigates specified offences and Customs regulates goods.

**Question-specific trap:** A designated lead force remains part of a multi-agency State.

### Q6. [Principle] What does 'one border, one force' most defensibly require?

A. Removal of State police and Customs from every district touching an international border.
B. A clearly accountable lead guarding force, while other competent authorities retain their statutory functions.
C. Transfer of LoC military command to the same CAPF that guards the Pakistan International Border, absorbing Army command and displacing the Army's defence mandate.
D. A constitutional prohibition on intelligence sharing across agencies.

**Answer: B.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Public order, investigation and goods regulation remain with their lawful authorities.
- **B — correct:** The principle clarifies primacy; it does not create an all-purpose monopoly over defence and justice.
- **C — incorrect:** The LoC is a military control line; lead-force doctrine neither assimilates it to the IB nor absorbs the Army's defence mandate.
- **D — incorrect:** The reform sought better coordination, not institutional isolation or an information-sharing ban.

**Question-specific trap:** Primacy improves accountability; it does not erase statutory mandates.

### Q7. [Federal application] A border force seizes narcotics and detains suspects inside a State. Which next step best completes lawful governance?

A. The force should itself try and sentence every accused at the BOP.
B. The Union should permanently assume all State police powers in the district.
C. Preserve evidence and transfer the case to competent investigators and courts.
D. Destroy the contraband, release all records to avoid inter-agency overlap and close the file at the outpost.

**Answer: C.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** A guarding force is not a trial court and cannot replace criminal adjudication.
- **B — incorrect:** A particular seizure does not justify erasing the constitutional distribution of police functions.
- **C — correct:** This connects interdiction to admissible evidence, investigation, prosecution and adjudication.
- **D — incorrect:** Destruction and closure without lawful sampling, documentation, transfer and orders would break the evidentiary chain.

**Question-specific trap:** An interception becomes a legal outcome only through evidence and adjudication.

### Q8. [FMR mechanics] Before the February 2024 policy announcement, which description best captures the historical India-Myanmar FMR?

A. Unlimited visa-free travel, residence and employment anywhere in both countries for every citizen.
B. A military ceasefire arrangement governing patrols along the LAC.
C. A customs concession available only to commercial trucks at an ICP.
D. Eligible 16-km residents used border passes for visits up to two weeks.

**Answer: D.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** The regime bounded eligible residents, distance and duration; it did not confer general travel, residence or employment rights.
- **B — incorrect:** FMR concerned civilian border mobility with Myanmar, not India-China military conduct.
- **C — incorrect:** It addressed eligible community movement and was not merely a cargo-tariff rule.
- **D — correct:** This states the historical residence, pass and duration mechanics without calling the frontier unregulated.

**Question-specific trap:** Historic FMR was bounded movement, not an unregulated international commons.

### Q9. [Status discipline] Which statement is supportable from MHA's 8 February 2024 release?

B. A complete bilateral termination instrument simultaneously criminalised every local crossing that day.
C. The former 16-km entitlement remains fully unchanged because a pass website is online.

**Answer: A.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — correct:** This mirrors the official release and keeps decision, suspension recommendation and final implementation distinct.
- **B — incorrect:** The release does not establish every later legal instrument, exception or field procedure.
- **C — incorrect:** A portal's existence cannot prove continuation of the earlier entitlement on identical terms.
- **D — incorrect:** Movement regulation neither delimits the boundary nor guarantees elimination of criminal networks.

**Question-specific trap:** Announcement, legal completion and field implementation are separate facts.

### Q10. [Policy design] Which response best balances FMR-related security and community concerns?

A. Treat every kinship visit as insurgent activity, close humanitarian screening and deny case-specific review, appeal or independent humanitarian assessment.
B. Use verified eligibility, authorised crossings, limited stays, appeals and targeted network action.
C. Restore unrecorded movement because regulation necessarily destroys local culture.
D. Delegate immigration status decisions to informal village militias without review.

**Answer: B.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Collective suspicion and denial of review or humanitarian assessment erase legal distinctions and can weaken community intelligence.
- **B — correct:** This regulates risk while preserving lawful mobility and an accountable status-determination process.
- **C — incorrect:** Cultural ties justify accommodation, not abandonment of identity, security and customs controls.
- **D — incorrect:** Coercive and status powers require lawful public authorities, evidence and review.

**Question-specific trap:** Security screening must remain individual, reviewable and compatible with lawful mobility.

### Q11. [System design] Which description of CIBMS is most accurate?

A. A welfare grant that finances schools and health centres in border villages.
B. A treaty forum that decides competing territorial claims.
C. An integration of sensors, communications, command systems and human response to supplement physical guarding.
D. A statutory authority that operates immigration counters at all land ports.

**Answer: C.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** That describes a development function rather than an electronic guarding architecture.
- **B — incorrect:** CIBMS is an operational technology system and has no boundary-adjudication mandate.
- **C — correct:** Its value lies in connecting detection with command and response, not in equipment alone.
- **D — incorrect:** LPAI manages land-port infrastructure; immigration remains a separate sovereign function.

**Question-specific trap:** CIBMS is a system-of-systems, not a single camera or welfare scheme.

### Q12. [Conceptual comparison] Why is grid protection broader than linear border security?

A. It replaces every patrol with a satellite, removes the need for police evidence and works identically in every terrain.
B. It measures success only by kilometres of fence completed.
C. It applies solely to high-altitude military lines where no settlement exists.
D. It links barriers, depth intelligence, sensors, routes, settlements and response teams across a network.

**Answer: D.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Technology cannot eliminate human response, investigation or legal responsibility, and terrain prevents one universal design.
- **B — incorrect:** Fence length is one input and misses routes, response and depth-area networks.
- **C — incorrect:** The grid idea is especially useful where terrain and habitation defeat a continuous linear barrier.
- **D — correct:** This captures the Madhukar Gupta Committee's systems logic rather than a wall metaphor.

**Question-specific trap:** Grid security measures routes and response in depth, not fence kilometres alone.

### Q13. [Close-option distinction] Which pairing correctly separates a BOP from an ICP?

A. BOP—guarding base between crossings; ICP—co-located regulated passenger and cargo gateway.
B. BOP—international court; ICP—military ceasefire line.
C. BOP—village-development grant; ICP—frontier-wide sensor network replacing Customs and immigration functions at designated crossing points.
D. BOP—Customs tariff; ICP—constitutional council for tribal areas.

**Answer: A.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — correct:** The first supports field guarding; the second organises lawful cross-border processing.
- **B — incorrect:** Neither facility performs adjudication of boundaries or constitutes a control line.
- **C — incorrect:** BADP/VVP concern development and CIBMS concerns sensors; an ICP instead co-locates lawful processing agencies.
- **D — incorrect:** The terms denote physical border institutions, not taxes or autonomous councils.

**Question-specific trap:** A BOP guards space; an ICP processes authorised flows.

### Q14. [Statutory role] Under the Land Ports Authority of India Act, 2010, LPAI principally does what?

**Answer: B.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** LPAI is not an operational military command.
- **B — correct:** Its statutory value is integrated infrastructure and facilitation, not absorption of sovereign functions.
- **C — incorrect:** Status determination and sentencing require competent executive/judicial processes.
- **D — incorrect:** Boundary negotiation belongs to the Union's diplomatic and political machinery.

**Question-specific trap:** Integrated infrastructure does not merge the powers of co-located agencies.

A. LAC → Palk Bay → McMahon Line → land port.

### Q17. [Treaty inference] What do the India-China agreements of 1993, 1996, 2005, 2012 and 2013 establish?

A. Rules and mechanisms for peace, confidence building and consultation, not final boundary settlement.
B. A mutually authenticated map of the entire LAC and a concluded international boundary binding both sides conclusively and resolving disputed sectors and differing perceptions.
C. Transfer of all defence responsibility from the Army to ITBP.
D. Compulsory international arbitration of every patrol face-off.

**Answer: A.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — correct:** The agreements manage conduct and communication while the underlying boundary remains unresolved.
- **B — incorrect:** Those instruments produced neither complete mutually agreed delineation nor a final settlement eliminating unresolved sectors and differing perceptions.
- **C — incorrect:** Lead guarding does not displace the Army's constitutional defence function.
- **D — incorrect:** The mechanisms are bilateral and do not create automatic third-party adjudication.

**Question-specific trap:** A confidence-building instrument manages conduct without settling title.

### Q18. [Evidence use] Galwan (2020) and Yangtse/Tawang (2022) most strongly support which conclusion?

A. Confidence-building agreements are legally identical to a settled boundary.
B. Management mechanisms can aid de-escalation yet cannot guarantee prevention where alignment remains disputed.
C. Every frontier incident proves failure of all diplomatic communication.
D. Physical fencing is the sole missing solution on the high-altitude LAC.

**Answer: B.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Conduct rules and final title/delimitation are different legal achievements.
- **B — correct:** The episodes demonstrate both the continuing need for mechanisms and their preventive limits.
- **C — incorrect:** An incident does not prove that every hotline, meeting or disengagement channel is useless.
- **D — incorrect:** Terrain, military posture, perceptions and diplomacy cannot be reduced to a fence deficit.

**Question-specific trap:** Use incidents to test mechanisms, not to declare diplomacy wholly useless.

### Q19. [Bangladesh frontier] How should the frontier be described after the 2014–15 settlements?

A. The enclave exchange and maritime boundary both remain wholly unresolved, including their constitutional and arbitral implementation.

**Option-by-option explanation:**

- **C — correct:** Settlement of title does not eliminate trans-border crime, mobility or policing questions.
- **D — incorrect:** BSF, not SSB, is the designated lead border-guarding force there.

### Q20. [Open-border reasoning] Which policy follows from the India-Nepal open-border character?

A. Abolish all identity and criminal-law checks because movement is lawful, even where specific trafficking indicators arise.
B. Treat every traveller as an irregular migrant unless a visa is produced.

**Answer: D.**

- **B — incorrect:** The arrangement cannot be analysed through an ordinary visa-only presumption.
- **C — incorrect:** SSB guarding does not extinguish State police and court functions.
- **D — correct:** Selective, evidence-led regulation protects both openness and security.

**Question-specific trap:** Open movement is compatible with targeted checks and ordinary criminal law.

### Q21. [Myanmar diagnosis] Which combination best captures the India-Myanmar frontier?

A. Rugged terrain, cross-border ethnic ties, insurgent routes, narcotics risk and contested movement rules.
B. A fully fenced urban border with no kinship, humanitarian movement, insurgent routes, narcotics networks or operational variation across sectors.
C. A maritime-only boundary administered by the Coast Guard.

**Answer: A.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — correct:** These interacting features explain why guarding, regulation and community policy must be combined.
- **B — incorrect:** Rugged terrain, kinship movement, security routes, narcotics risk and incomplete fencing vary across sectors and make the description untenable.
- **C — incorrect:** The frontier is a land border; maritime institutions do not replace its lead force.
- **D — incorrect:** No customs union exists, and Assam Rifles is the designated guarding force.

**Question-specific trap:** Myanmar requires security, humanitarian and community categories to be separated.

### Q22. [Legal classification] Which distinction between smuggling and trafficking in persons is correct?

A. Both labels turn exclusively on whether a fence was crossed.
B. Smuggling concerns illicit movement, often of goods; trafficking centres on exploitation and requires victim protection.
C. Trafficking applies only to contraband; smuggling applies only to children.
D. A person found with a smuggler can never be a trafficking victim.

**Answer: B.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Border crossing is only one fact; the protected interest and legal elements differ.
- **B — correct:** The exploitation element makes victim identification and assistance central to trafficking response.
- **C — incorrect:** The objects and legal concepts are reversed in this option.
- **D — incorrect:** Coercion, deception and exploitation may make a transported person a victim rather than offender.

**Question-specific trap:** Exploitation changes the trafficking response from simple interdiction to victim protection.

### Q23. [Status triage] A family crosses irregularly while one member alleges persecution and another appears controlled by an agent. What is the sound first approach?

A. Label the entire group infiltrators and deny any individual inquiry.
B. Treat the agent and family as identical victims without preserving evidence, screening individual claims, separating possible offences or deciding custody and referral.
C. Separate immigration status, protection claim, trafficking indicators and possible offences through individual screening.
D. Refer every question directly to LPAI for a final criminal verdict.

**Answer: C.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Collective labelling can obscure both genuine threat and people needing protection.
- **B — incorrect:** The suspected organiser's role, each protection claim, possible exploitation, custody and referral require individual screening and separate evidence.
- **C — correct:** This preserves security screening while applying the correct legal category to each person.
- **D — incorrect:** LPAI manages facilities and cannot adjudicate criminal guilt or protection claims.

**Question-specific trap:** One group crossing can contain legally different persons and roles.

### Q24. [Counter-UAS sequence] After a low-flying unknown drone is detected near a border village, what is the strongest operational order?

A. Announce foreign sponsorship, then search for the device.
B. Jam every local frequency permanently, then classify the object.
C. Destroy the payload after recording only its visible serial number, without preserving telemetry, controller links or launch-site evidence.
D. Track and classify, apply an authorised safe countermeasure, secure the scene and exploit forensic links.

**Answer: D.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Attribution requires evidence from the platform, control link, payload and wider network.
- **B — incorrect:** Indiscriminate interference creates safety and communications risks and reverses the decision order.
- **C — incorrect:** Destroying the payload and omitting telemetry, controller and launch-site evidence prevents reliable reconstruction and prosecution.
- **D — correct:** The sequence connects tactical safety with chain of custody and cautious attribution.

**Question-specific trap:** Counter-UAS action must protect aviation, spectrum and evidence.

### Q25. [Inference limit] A recovered drone carries foreign-manufactured components and narcotics. What can be concluded immediately?

A. An unlawful delivery was intercepted; operator and State sponsorship still require further evidence.
B. The manufacturing country legally directed the flight.
C. Every nearby resident participated in the smuggling network.
D. The border is fully sealed; the payload was recovered and no comparable route can remain undetected.

**Answer: A.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — correct:** Recovery proves the event and objects, while attribution remains a separate forensic/intelligence question.
- **B — incorrect:** Commercial component origin does not establish who assembled, launched or sponsored the platform.
- **C — incorrect:** Geographic proximity cannot substitute for individual participation evidence.
- **D — incorrect:** One recovery proves neither that comparable routes are closed nor that other attempts remain detectable.

**Question-specific trap:** Component origin is not operator or State attribution.

### Q30. [Evaluation] A ministry reports ₹500 crore sanctioned and 200 works approved under a border scheme. What is the strongest conclusion?

A. Security improved because financial sanction is itself an outcome.
B. Only approvals are proved; delivery, use and security impact need separate evidence.

- **D — incorrect:** A development release need not report convictions, and their absence proves neither non-delivery nor programme failure.

**Question-specific trap:** Never jump from a financial input to a social or security impact.

### Q33. [Institutional law] Which distinction between LAHDCs and Sixth Schedule councils is sound?

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Distinguish the International Boundary, Line of Control, Line of Actual Control and Actual Ground Position Line, and explain why the distinction matters for security management. *(Answer in 150 words.)*

**Model answer:**

These lines carry different legal and operational meanings. An **International Boundary** is an agreed boundary where guarding, Customs, immigration and regulated movement predominate. The **Line of Control** is the India–Pakistan military control line in J&K and is not an international boundary. The **Line of Actual Control** concerns the unresolved India–China boundary; differing perceptions create patrol friction. The **Actual Ground Position Line** records deployments beyond NJ9842 in the Siachen sector.

The distinction determines both threat and institution. BSF leads on the Pakistan International Border, while the Army has the primary operational role on the LoC/AGPL; ITBP guards the China frontier alongside the Army’s defence role. Fencing data for an IB cannot be applied to a military control line, and a confidence-building agreement cannot be treated as boundary settlement. Precise vocabulary therefore prevents wrong force attribution, wrong statistics and one-size-fits-all remedies.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Why can border-area development strengthen security without substituting for border guarding? *(Answer in 150 words.)*

**Model answer:**

Border development can reduce vulnerabilities exploited by hostile and criminal networks. Working roads and telecom improve response; schools, health services and livelihoods reduce forced out-migration and dependence on illicit markets; participation and grievance redress can strengthen voluntary reporting.

VVP-II is a concrete current example: approved on 2 April 2025 as a Central Sector Scheme, it carries ₹6,839 crore for 1,954 selected villages through FY 2028-29. VVP-I covers selected northern villages, while BADP is now in sunset phase. These facts establish policy design, not delivery.

Development cannot detect an infiltrator, interdict a drone, investigate trafficking or manage an LAC face-off. Those tasks need forces, intelligence, police, regulation and bilateral mechanisms. Guarding protects the space in which services function; credible services improve legitimacy and cooperation. Evaluation must therefore separate sanction, completed asset, actual service, livelihood/retention effect and security outcome.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** “An open or community-linked border requires more governance, not less sovereignty.” Analyse with reference to India’s Nepal and Myanmar frontiers. *(Answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer:**

An open or community-linked frontier permits or has historically permitted intense lawful movement; it does not remove territorial sovereignty, criminal law or the duty to protect residents.

On the Nepal border, longstanding mobility supports work, trade, family and cultural ties, while the same accessibility can be misused for transit, smuggling or evasion. SSB-led guarding must therefore be intelligence-led and selective, with State-police and regulatory support rather than indiscriminate closure.

The Myanmar frontier is harder: rugged terrain, ethnic communities across the boundary, insurgent routes, narcotics networks and instability create simultaneous security and humanitarian pressures. MHA announced on 8 February 2024 a decision to scrap the FMR, but exact current entitlements require the operative official rule; announcement and implementation cannot be collapsed. Assam Rifles’ border role must connect to State police, Customs, immigration and bilateral channels.

For both borders, governance means reliable identification and pass systems, notified crossings, facilitation of legitimate trade, victim-sensitive anti-trafficking procedures, data protection, appeal/grievance channels, roads and communications, and locally shaped livelihoods. It also means differentiated screening: an irregular migrant, refugee claimant, trafficked victim and insurgent are not the same legal category.

Thus, sovereignty is strengthened when the State can distinguish lawful from hostile movement fairly. Blanket suspicion may disrupt communities and weaken intelligence, while unregulated access permits exploitation; calibrated regulation avoids both failures.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Critically examine the “one border, one force” principle in India’s land-border architecture. *(Answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer:**

The post-Kargil “one border, one force” principle sought clear accountability by designating a lead Border Guarding Force: BSF for the Pakistan International Border and Bangladesh, ITBP for China, SSB for Nepal and Bhutan, and Assam Rifles for Myanmar. It reduces fragmented command, supports specialised training and gives each frontier an identifiable institutional owner.

Its value, however, is often misstated. A lead force cannot be the sole State actor. The Army retains defence responsibilities on contested frontiers and the LoC/AGPL; State police own public order and ordinary criminal investigation; Customs and immigration regulate goods and persons; intelligence agencies support prevention; courts adjudicate. Assam Rifles itself combines border and counter-insurgency roles under distinctive control arrangements. Even a single frontier varies by terrain and threat.

Therefore, implementation should be judged by unity of operational planning, communications, interoperable databases, joint drills, logistics, incident command and evidence hand-offs—not by eliminating legitimate overlap. Excessive diversion of a BGF from its principal task can dilute expertise, but rigid exclusivity can create seams that adversaries exploit.

The principle remains sound as accountable primacy. It should be reinforced through sector-specific standard operating procedures, State-level coordination, shared situational awareness and post-incident review, while preserving each agency’s statute, federal responsibility and judicial accountability.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Evaluate whether technology-led “smart borders” can overcome the limits of fencing in India. Suggest an integrated model. *(Answer in 300 words.)*

**Model answer:**

Fencing is useful for channeling movement, delaying intrusion and directing traffic toward lawful gates. It is nevertheless incomplete in riverine terrain, high mountains, dense habitation, tunnels and the low-altitude air domain. A “smart border” therefore shifts from a single line to a layered grid.

CIBMS-type architecture integrates cameras, thermal imagers, radar or ground sensors, communications and command-control with patrols and quick-response teams. Counter-UAS systems add detection, classification and authorised neutralisation against aerial delivery. Roads, bridges, floodlighting and BOPs reduce response time; ICPs provide auditable channels for lawful passengers and cargo. Intelligence and databases can connect a frontier alert to depth-area networks.

Technology does not remove five constraints. First, sensors create false positives and blind spots. Second, equipment needs power, communications, maintenance and trained operators. Third, detection is useless without timely interception. Fourth, a recovery must preserve chain of custody for police investigation and prosecution. Fifth, surveillance, jamming and identification must remain proportionate and lawful, particularly around communities and civilian aviation or communications.

An integrated model should begin with border-specific risk mapping rather than uniform procurement. It should combine physical barriers where feasible; redundant sensors and communications; joint BGF–Army–police–Customs protocols; rapid response; forensic exploitation; lawful ICPs and pass systems; local reporting protected from retaliation; and independent audits of uptime, response time, false alarms, interdictions and case outcomes.

Technology can reduce vulnerability and manpower exposure, but cannot settle a boundary, generate community trust or prove attribution. Smartness lies in the complete human–technical–legal chain, not in the device count.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Design a border-management strategy that reconciles national security, border-area development, federalism and individual rights. *(Answer in 300 words.)*

**Model answer:**

A defensible strategy begins by rejecting a uniform frontier. The LAC needs military preparedness and crisis mechanisms; the Pakistan International Border needs intelligence-led action against infiltration, tunnels and contraband; dense Bangladesh sectors need riverine surveillance and regulated trade; Nepal and Myanmar require calibrated treatment of lawful mobility and community ties.

**Security layer:** designate the lead BGF, retain the Army’s defence role, build terrain-specific BOP, road, fence, CIBMS and counter-UAS capability, and link every alert to a response and forensic chain. State police must investigate local networks; Customs, immigration and financial agencies should attack goods, persons and proceeds according to their mandates.

**Development layer:** distinguish programmes by status. BADP was in sunset phase in April 2026; BIM’s approved period ended with 2025-26 unless renewed; VVP-I and VVP-II provide current selected-village development channels. Planning should prioritise functional connectivity, health, education, communications and livelihoods through local institutions. Sanctions and expenditure must be audited against usability, retention, trust and vulnerability reduction.

**Federal and bilateral layer:** create shared operating pictures, joint protocols and regular Centre–State reviews while respecting State responsibility for police and public order. Use flag meetings, hotlines, working mechanisms, treaty implementation and regulated ICPs for cross-border incidents and lawful flows.

**Rights layer:** distinguish infiltrators, irregular migrants, refugee claimants, trafficking victims and residents; require individual screening, recorded reasons, proportionate force, lawful detention, compensation and grievance redress. Community participation must be voluntary and supervised.

Success should be reported through separate guarding, regulation, development, rights and cooperation indicators. This produces secure yet liveable frontiers and prevents either security absolutism or development-only complacency.
