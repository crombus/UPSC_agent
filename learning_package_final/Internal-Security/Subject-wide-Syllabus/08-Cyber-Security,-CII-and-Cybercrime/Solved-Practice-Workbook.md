# Cyber Security, CII and Cybercrime — Solved Practice Workbook

## Evidence and use note

The Topic 08 Basic and Advanced owners, audited routing ledgers, local official papers, the IT Act, CERT-In Directions/FAQ, MHA I4C material, MeitY/DoT notifications and Supreme Court judgments control this workbook. The forty MCQs are a rebuilt workbook-only bank. DPDP status is stated provision by provision as of 27 September 2026; future commencement is not backdated.

## BASIC MCQS / REMEDIATION

These **40 newly authored questions** replace the previous bank. They mix classification,
causal sequence, evidence, statutory trigger, current-law status, case application and
remedial formats. Keys rotate `A → B → C → D` exactly ten times.

### Q1. [Foundation] Which formulation best captures cyber security?

A. Protection of confidentiality, integrity, availability and resilient service delivery across people, process and technology.
B. Purchase of antivirus software for every government computer.
C. Criminal prosecution of all online conduct that causes annoyance, treated as the complete definition of national cyber security and resilience.
D. Permanent secrecy of every digital system, including public services.

**Answer: A.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — correct:** The definition covers security properties and continuity rather than one product or legal response.
- **B — incorrect:** Endpoint software is one control and cannot cover identity, networks, people, suppliers or recovery.
- **C — incorrect:** Liability requires a valid offence; section 66A's annoyance standard was struck down, and prosecution does not define resilience.
- **D — incorrect:** Security needs accountable access and availability, not indiscriminate secrecy.

**Question-specific trap:** Do not define cyber security as a product inventory.

### Q2. [Classification] Which distinction between a cyber incident and cybercrime is sound?

A. Every report to CERT-In automatically creates an FIR and criminal guilt.
B. An incident is technical; cybercrime requires a live offence and criminal process.
C. Only government systems can experience incidents; only citizens can suffer cybercrime or seek technical response.
D. A cybercrime exists only if NCIIPC declares the affected device CII.

**Answer: B.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Technical reporting, police registration and conviction are separate stages.
- **B — correct:** The same facts may trigger both chains, but their objects and institutional owners differ.
- **C — incorrect:** Public and private systems and persons can be involved in incidents, crimes and technical response.
- **D — incorrect:** Ordinary cybercrime does not depend on CII designation.

**Question-specific trap:** CERT-In reporting and criminal adjudication are different chains.

### Q3. [Type test] Which is a cyber-dependent offence?

A. Using a messaging app to arrange an otherwise conventional burglary, with the app serving only as coordination.
B. Publishing a lawful but unpopular policy opinion online.
C. Deploying malware to obtain unauthorised control of another person's system.
D. Purchasing a cyber-insurance policy after a risk assessment.

**Answer: C.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** The burglary can exist without the app; digital coordination merely makes it cyber-enabled.
- **B — incorrect:** Lawful expression is not transformed into crime by digital publication.
- **C — correct:** The prohibited conduct cannot exist without compromising a computer resource.
- **D — incorrect:** Insurance is a contractual risk-transfer measure, not criminal conduct.

**Question-specific trap:** Ask whether the offence depends on a computer or merely uses one.

### Q4. [Threshold] When can conduct fall within IT Act section 66F?

A. Whenever a website is temporarily slow during heavy traffic.
B. Whenever a server used in an offence is physically outside India.
C. Whenever money is lost through an ordinary online cheating scheme and the victim reports financial harm.
D. Defined conduct plus section 66F's specified security or terror intent.

**Answer: D.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Performance degradation without the statutory conduct and intent is insufficient.
- **B — incorrect:** Foreign location is not a substitute for the offence's mental and consequence elements.
- **C — incorrect:** Financial harm and reporting do not supply section 66F's security or terror intent; other offences ordinarily apply.
- **D — correct:** Section 66F is exceptional and requires its precise conduct-and-intent threshold.

**Question-specific trap:** Reserve section 66F for its exceptional statutory elements.

### Q5. [Terminology] Which use of 'cyberwar' is defensible?

A. Reserve it for exceptional, sufficiently attributed State-linked operations assessed against international-law effect thresholds.
B. Apply it to every phishing email that crosses a national border.
C. Use it as the current name of the offence formerly in section 66A.
D. Treat every CERT-In incident report as a declaration of armed conflict.

**Answer: A.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — correct:** The label requires more than technical hostility: attribution, State nexus and consequences matter.
- **B — incorrect:** Internet routing across borders does not convert fraud into warfare.
- **C — incorrect:** Section 66A was struck down and never defined cyberwar.
- **D — incorrect:** Incident reporting supports response and makes no such legal determination.

**Question-specific trap:** Cross-border routing is not cyberwar.

### Q6. [Ransomware] Why is double-extortion ransomware not only an availability problem?

A. It always destroys every backup before entering a network.
B. Attackers may steal data before encryption and threaten disclosure, compromising confidentiality as well.
C. Paying necessarily transfers legal title to the stolen data.
D. Encryption automatically proves that a foreign State sponsored the operation.

**Answer: B.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Backup impact must be examined; it is not universal or automatically proven.
- **B — correct:** Exfiltration plus encryption creates confidentiality and availability harms.
- **C — incorrect:** A ransom transaction provides no enforceable deletion or ownership assurance.
- **D — incorrect:** Malware behaviour does not itself identify the responsible actor or State.

**Question-specific trap:** Ransomware may compromise confidentiality before availability.

### Q7. [Recovery evidence] Which condition makes a backup a resilience control?

A. It appears in an asset register, even if never restored.
B. It remains permanently connected with the same administrator credentials.
C. It is segregated, integrity-tested and proven capable of restoring the required service within tolerance.
D. It is encrypted by the attacker together with production systems.

**Answer: C.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Documentation alone does not prove recoverability.
- **B — incorrect:** Shared access and connectivity can let the same compromise reach the backup.
- **C — correct:** Resilience depends on clean, tested and timely restoration.
- **D — incorrect:** An attacker-controlled copy cannot provide dependable recovery.

**Question-specific trap:** Backups count only when clean restoration is tested.

### Q8. [Risk model] Which causal chain expresses cyber risk correctly?

A. Consequence → conviction → vulnerability → threat actor.
B. Audit → insurance → zero incidents → attribution.
C. Section number → agency name; automatic security outcome follows without testing implementation, recovery or operational performance.
D. Threat actor/vector exploits vulnerability → consequence; controls reduce likelihood or impact.

**Answer: D.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** The order confuses cause, legal outcome and exposure.
- **B — incorrect:** Audits and insurance cannot guarantee zero events or identify an actor.
- **C — incorrect:** Legal architecture identifies authority but cannot demonstrate implementation, recovery or operational performance by itself.
- **D — correct:** This is the standard threat–vulnerability–consequence logic for risk treatment.

**Question-specific trap:** Risk links actor, vector, vulnerability and consequence.

### Q9. [Section 70] What defines Critical Information Infrastructure?

A. A resource whose failure would debilitate security, economy, public health or safety.
B. Any device owned by a ministry, regardless of consequence.
C. Every database containing more than a fixed record threshold set uniformly across sectors.
D. Only systems that have already suffered a foreign attack.

**Answer: A.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — correct:** The statutory definition is consequence-based.
- **B — incorrect:** Ownership alone does not establish criticality.
- **C — incorrect:** Section 70 uses debilitating consequences, not a universal cross-sector record-count threshold.
- **D — incorrect:** Preventive designation does not require a prior attack.

**Question-specific trap:** CII is defined by debilitating consequence.

### Q10. [Protected-system distinction] When does a resource acquire protected-system status?

A. When its operator describes it as mission-critical in a presentation.
B. When the appropriate Government declares it by notification under section 70.
C. When CERT-In publishes any vulnerability advisory affecting its software or the wider product family.
D. When a police complaint mentions the system.

**Answer: B.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Internal criticality language is not the statutory act of designation.
- **B — correct:** Notification creates the special legal status and access controls.
- **C — incorrect:** An advisory may concern a product family without notifying each deployed resource as a protected system.
- **D — incorrect:** A complaint records an allegation and cannot create protected-system status.

**Question-specific trap:** Protected-system status requires notification.

### Q11. [CERT-In mandate] Which function belongs principally to CERT-In under section 70B?

A. Notifying every CII asset as a protected system.
B. Trying cyber-terrorism offences in a designated court.
C. National cyber-incident information, alerts, directions and response coordination.
D. Operating first-originator traceability for messaging services and deciding the resulting users' criminal liability.

**Answer: C.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Protected-system notification is a governmental legal act, not CERT-In's general role.
- **B — incorrect:** Courts adjudicate offences; CERT-In is a technical response agency.
- **C — correct:** This is the national incident-response mandate.
- **D — incorrect:** Rule 4(2) duties fall on specified intermediaries under competent orders; CERT-In does not decide user guilt.

**Question-specific trap:** CERT-In is not police, court or the CII notifier.

### Q12. [NCIIPC mandate] Which description of NCIIPC is correct?

A. Citizen helpline for freezing online financial fraud.
B. National platform appeal tribunal under the IT Rules.
C. General police station for all internet offences.
D. Section 70A nodal agency under NTRO for protecting Critical Information Infrastructure.

**Answer: D.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** The 1930/CFCFRMS pathway belongs to the I4C-supported fraud response.
- **B — incorrect:** GACs hear intermediary grievance appeals and are unrelated to CII protection.
- **C — incorrect:** State/UT police retain ordinary criminal-investigation powers.
- **D — correct:** NCIIPC's mandate is specialised, preventive and CII-focused.

**Question-specific trap:** NCIIPC is CII-specific and does not operate the citizen complaint route.

### Q13. [Citizen-to-police chain] Which statement best describes I4C?

A. MHA coordination for reporting, analytics, forensics, training and State/UT policing.
B. The statutory CII-protection agency housed under NTRO.
C. The national technical incident-response body created by section 70B and operator of the State cyber-police network.
D. A court empowered to impose every IT Act sentence.

**Answer: A.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — correct:** I4C strengthens the criminal-justice chain without replacing State police or courts.
- **B — incorrect:** That is NCIIPC's section 70A role.
- **C — incorrect:** Section 70B describes CERT-In, which does not operate every State cyber-police station.
- **D — incorrect:** I4C is not a judicial body.

**Question-specific trap:** I4C supports State/UT policing rather than replacing federal structure.

### Q14. [Acronym firewall] Which institutional pairing preserves separate mandates?

A. NCCC—citizen complaint portal; NCSC—protected-system notification and emergency telecom-suspension authority.
B. NCCC—national threat situational awareness; NCSC—strategic cyber-security coordination.
C. NCCC—criminal court; NCSC—malware-cleaning centre.
D. NCCC—Rule 69A blocking committee; NCSC—Data Protection Board.

**Answer: B.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Citizen complaints, protected-system notification and telecom suspension belong to different legal and institutional routes.
- **B — correct:** The pairing keeps operational awareness distinct from high-level policy coordination.
- **C — incorrect:** Neither body performs the stated judicial or hygiene function.
- **D — incorrect:** Blocking procedure and DPDP adjudication are separate from both acronyms.

**Question-specific trap:** NCCC and NCSC have different awareness and coordination roles.

### Q15. [Sector scenario] Why does finance need a sectoral CSIRT such as CSIRT-Fin?

A. To replace CERT-In and State police permanently.
B. To declare every bank database a protected system.
C. To translate national alerts into sector-specific continuity, regulator and operator action.
D. To decide customer compensation conclusively without a contract, statutory basis or adjudicatory process.

**Answer: C.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Sectoral coordination complements national response and criminal investigation.
- **B — incorrect:** Protected-system status depends on notification, not sector-team declaration.
- **C — correct:** Payments and financial infrastructure have domain-specific dependencies and recovery needs.
- **D — incorrect:** Compensation depends on applicable contract, law and adjudication; a sectoral CERT cannot decide it conclusively.

**Question-specific trap:** Sectoral teams translate, not displace, national baselines.

### Q16. [Cyber hygiene] What is the Cyber Swachhta Kendra?

A. A covert military cyber command for offensive operations and classified wartime disruption of foreign networks.
B. A statutory tribunal hearing intermediary appeals.
C. A compulsory cyber-insurance pool for CII operators.
D. A botnet-cleaning and malware-analysis initiative providing user-facing hygiene support.

**Answer: D.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** The initiative provides civilian cyber hygiene and malware-cleaning support, not offensive wartime command.
- **B — incorrect:** Grievance Appellate Committees, not the Kendra, perform that appeal function.
- **C — incorrect:** It supplies tools and awareness rather than insurance.
- **D — correct:** This distinguishes prevention/hygiene from incident response and policing.

**Question-specific trap:** Cyber hygiene is preventive support, not offensive command.

### Q17. [Section match] Which set correctly maps IT Act powers?

A. 69—interception/monitoring/decryption; 69A—blocking public access; 69B—traffic-data monitoring for cyber security.
B. 69—CII notification; 69A—CERT-In; 69B—NCIIPC.
C. 69—data-protection consent; 69A—insurance; 69B—police trial.
D. 69—repealed with section 66A; 69A/69B—no longer operative.

**Answer: A.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — correct:** The three provisions authorise distinct executive measures subject to prescribed procedure.
- **B — incorrect:** CII, NCIIPC and CERT-In are mapped through sections 70, 70A and 70B.
- **C — incorrect:** Those subjects belong to different laws and institutions.
- **D — incorrect:** The Supreme Court struck section 66A, not these separate provisions.

**Question-specific trap:** Do not interchange sections 69–69B with sections 70–70B.

### Q18. [Current law] What is the status of section 66A of the IT Act?

A. It became the principal offence for ransomware in 2024.
B. Struck down in *Shreya Singhal* (2015); unavailable as current law.
C. It was renumbered as section 70A after the judgment; it now establishes NCIIPC.
D. It applies only to encrypted messages sent from abroad.

**Answer: B.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Ransomware must be charged through live specialised and general offences.
- **B — correct:** The Supreme Court invalidated the provision for unconstitutional vagueness/free-speech burden.
- **C — incorrect:** Section 70A is a distinct provision, not a post-judgment renumbering of struck-down section 66A.
- **D — incorrect:** The invalid provision has no such surviving territorial exception.

**Question-specific trap:** Never resurrect section 66A in a current-law answer.

### Q19. [Extraterritoriality] What does section 75 principally add?

A. Automatic proof that every foreign IP address belongs to a foreign government.
B. Universal Indian jurisdiction over any online act anywhere, without nexus.
C. Specified extraterritorial application where the offence/contravention involves a computer resource located in India.
D. A power for CERT-In to sentence foreign defendants.

**Answer: C.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Technical routing cannot prove identity or State responsibility.
- **B — incorrect:** The provision requires the statutory computer-resource connection.
- **C — correct:** It creates a jurisdictional basis, while evidence and international cooperation remain necessary.
- **D — incorrect:** CERT-In is not a criminal court.

**Question-specific trap:** Jurisdictional nexus is not attribution or enforceability abroad.

### Q20. [Criminal-law layer] After 1 July 2024, how should cyber-enabled cheating or intimidation be framed?

A. Only under the repealed IPC, because specialised digital offences no longer coexist with general criminal law for current offences.
B. Only as a CERT-In compliance breach, never a police matter.
C. Only through DPDP penalties because all cybercrime is personal-data processing.
D. Under relevant BNS provisions read with applicable IT Act offences, current procedure and electronic-evidence law.

**Answer: D.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** The BNS replaced the IPC for current general offences, while applicable IT Act offences continue to coexist.
- **B — incorrect:** Technical reporting does not displace the penal investigation.
- **C — incorrect:** DPDP has a different data-governance purpose and cannot absorb all offences.
- **D — correct:** This captures the layered special-plus-general criminal-law route.

**Question-specific trap:** Use BNS/current procedure with the specialised IT Act.

### Q21. [CERT-In timeline] What does the six-hour requirement measure?

A. Six hours from noticing a specified incident to the initial CERT-In report.
B. Maximum time for a court to convict the attacker.
C. Time allowed to prove foreign-State responsibility beyond doubt and complete attribution within the six-hour window.
D. A universal deadline for paying ransomware demands.

**Answer: A.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — correct:** The rule accelerates national awareness and permits later supplementation.
- **B — incorrect:** Criminal adjudication follows an entirely different and longer process.
- **C — incorrect:** Six hours governs initial incident reporting, not completion of technical, legal and political attribution.
- **D — incorrect:** The Directions do not prescribe ransom payment.

**Question-specific trap:** Six hours is an initial reporting deadline.

### Q22. [Logging duty] Which statement reflects the CERT-In Directions?

A. Logs may be disabled after an incident to protect privacy once storage or compliance costs become burdensome.
B. Enable and securely retain ICT logs for 180 days within India.
C. Only printed logs kept abroad satisfy the rule.
D. Log retention proves every user action was malicious.

**Answer: B.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Cost or privacy concerns do not justify post-incident log destruction that obstructs response and evidence preservation.
- **B — correct:** This is the current dated retention/location requirement.
- **C — incorrect:** The Directions concern ICT logs and specify Indian-jurisdiction retention.
- **D — incorrect:** Logs record events; interpretation and attribution still require analysis.

**Question-specific trap:** Retained logs create their own security and privacy duties.

### Q23. [Parallel reporting] Why might one ransomware event require both CERT-In reporting and a police complaint?

A. Because CERT-In and police are two names for the same body.
B. Because police manage technical recovery while CERT-In conducts the criminal trial.
C. The incident-response and penal-investigation chains have different purposes, powers and evidentiary outputs.
D. Because duplicate reports automatically guarantee compensation.

**Answer: C.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** CERT-In is under MeitY; ordinary investigation remains with competent law enforcement.
- **B — incorrect:** The functions are reversed and courts, not CERT-In, conduct trials.
- **C — correct:** Containment/coordination and offence investigation may arise from the same facts without merging.
- **D — incorrect:** Reporting creates process opportunities but no automatic financial remedy.

**Question-specific trap:** One event can require multiple non-duplicative reports.

### Q24. [Financial fraud] What is the best immediate citizen action after detecting an unauthorised online transfer?

A. Wait for a monthly CERT-In advisory before contacting anyone.
B. Post the suspected account details publicly; identify the alleged holder and crowdsource punishment before verification.
C. Report only to NCIIPC because every bank account is CII.
D. Notify 1930/NCRP and the bank promptly; preserve evidence for police.

**Answer: D.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Delay reduces the possibility of interrupting transaction flows.
- **B — incorrect:** Publishing unverified identity and account details can harm innocents, dissipate evidence and compromise investigation.
- **C — incorrect:** NCIIPC's protected-system role is not the citizen fraud-reporting channel.
- **D — correct:** Speed, bank coordination and preserved records support freezing and investigation.

**Question-specific trap:** Speed matters in fraud, but reporting does not guarantee recovery.

### Q25. [DPDP scope] Which proposition keeps the Act within its proper legal domain?

A. Digital personal-data governance; not the complete cyber-security code.
B. DPDP replaces sections 66F and 70A/70B of the IT Act.
C. DPDP makes every personal-data breach a proven act of cyber terrorism; identifiable individuals supply the required intent.
D. DPDP governs only paper records never digitised.

**Answer: A.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — correct:** The statute overlaps at breaches but answers a distinct privacy/data-governance question.
- **B — incorrect:** The IT Act's offence, CII and incident provisions remain separate.
- **C — incorrect:** Personal-data involvement does not supply section 66F's conduct and intent elements for cyber terrorism.
- **D — incorrect:** Its focus is digital personal data, including specified digitised processing.

**Question-specific trap:** DPDP is not India's complete cyber-security statute.

### Q32. [Zero trust] Which practice best reflects zero-trust architecture?

### Q33. [Attribution ladder] Which sequence is the most reliable?

A. Technical indicator → infrastructure/operator → legal actor → State responsibility/political attribution.
B. Political accusation → malware hash → automatic conviction → incident report.
C. Foreign server → hostile State → armed attack → retaliation.
D. Victim statement → NCIIPC notification → diplomatic sanction.

**Answer: A.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — correct:** Each rung requires additional evidence and supports a different confidence level.
- **B — incorrect:** The sequence reverses analysis and confuses evidence with legal outcome.
- **C — incorrect:** Infrastructure location cannot establish control, sponsorship or an armed-attack threshold.
- **D — incorrect:** Victim evidence and protected-system status do not automatically establish foreign responsibility.

**Question-specific trap:** Separate infrastructure evidence from operator and State responsibility.

### Q34. [Concentration risk] Why can a common cloud or managed-service provider create systemic cyber risk?

- **A — incorrect:** Operators retain governance, statutory and continuity accountability even when contracts allocate vendor responsibilities.
- **B — correct:** Concentration converts a supplier problem into a multi-sector dependency event.
- **C — incorrect:** Controls are possible, though architecture and contract determine their quality.
- **D — incorrect:** Public and private entities may both use shared services.

**Question-specific trap:** Shared providers can create correlated failure.

### Q35. [Audit inference] What can a completed cyber audit establish?

A. That the organisation will suffer no future incident.
B. The assessment proves every identified weakness was remediated; recovery was also tested successfully.
C. Findings at a point in time; remediation and operational performance require follow-up evidence.

- **B — incorrect:** Completion of the assessment is not proof that management closed every finding.
- **C — correct:** This separates assurance activity from remediation and resilience outcomes.
- **D — incorrect:** Incident origin and sponsorship require independent attribution evidence.

**Question-specific trap:** Audit completion is not remediation or recovery.

### Q36. [Insurance] What does individual cyber insurance principally do?

A. Prevent malware from executing on the insured device and guarantee restoration of every affected service subject to policy exclusions and limits.
B. Guarantee reimbursement for every loss irrespective of exclusions.
C. Replace police investigation and bank fraud controls.
D. Transfer defined financial costs—such as restoration, response or liability—subject to policy terms.

**Answer: D.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Insurance transfers defined financial loss after covered events; it neither prevents execution nor guarantees unlimited restoration.
- **B — incorrect:** Limits, exclusions, deductibles and security conditions govern actual coverage.

### Mains PYQ M1 — 2019 GS-III Q10 — DIRECT

**Exact official wording:** “What is CyberDome Project? Explain how it can be useful in controlling internet crimes in India.” *(150 words, 10 marks)*

**Demand:** define the Kerala model and evaluate utility without presenting it as the national cyber architecture.

**Model answer:**

CyberDome is Kerala Police’s public-private cyber centre of excellence. It connects police with technology professionals, ethical hackers, academia and firms for research, training, digital forensics, threat awareness and development of investigative tools.

Its value lies in closing specialised-skill gaps faster than police recruitment alone. Expert collaboration can support malware analysis, social-engineering awareness, vulnerability assessment, evidence extraction and training. It can also generate locally relevant tools and enable preventive work rather than only post-offence investigation. As a State model, it strengthens the police link in the cybercrime chain and can share practices with other jurisdictions.

However, CyberDome does not replace CERT-In’s national incident response, NCIIPC’s CII mandate, I4C’s national coordination or courts. Public-private participation requires vetting, confidentiality, access control, conflict safeguards and chain-of-custody discipline. Replication should therefore focus on governed collaboration and measurable case/capacity outcomes, not merely a branded centre.

### Mains PYQ M2 — 2020 GS-III Q9 — DIRECT

**Exact official wording:** “Discuss different types of cyber crimes and measures required to be taken to fight the menace.” *(150 words, 10 marks)*

**Demand:** classify the offences and connect prevention, rapid reporting, investigation, victim relief and resilient recovery.

**Model answer (140 words):**

Cybercrimes are **cyber-dependent** when the computer is the target or indispensable means—unauthorised access, malware, ransomware, botnets and denial-of-service. They are **cyber-enabled** when networks scale familiar offences—online cheating, identity theft, stalking, extortion, trafficking facilitation and financial fraud. Attacks on notified critical systems may trigger the protected-system framework; section 66F applies only when its cyber-terrorism conduct and intent are proved.

Countermeasures require secure configuration, patching, multi-factor authentication, least privilege, segmentation, tested offline backups and user awareness. Specified incidents must be reported to CERT-In; urgent financial fraud should be reported through **1930/NCRP**, while competent State/UT police investigate with preserved device, account, transaction and log evidence. **I4C** supports coordination and forensics; **NCIIPC** supports notified CII; sectoral regulators enforce domain controls.

Section 66A remains invalid. Success is reduced loss, restored service and adjudicated cases—not complaint, blocking or audit counts alone.

### Mains PYQ M3 — 2021 GS-III Q10 — DIRECT

**Exact official wording:** “Keeping in view India's internal security, analyse the impact of cross-border cyber attacks. Also discuss defensive measures against these sophisticated attacks.” *(150 words, 10 marks)*

**Demand:** connect effects to resilience and preserve attribution caution.

**Model answer:**

Cross-border cyber operations can disrupt power, telecom, finance, transport, health and government services; steal strategic or personal data; corrupt information; enable fraud; and erode public confidence. Interdependence creates cascades, while remote infrastructure, proxies and compromised machines obscure origin. Thus, a foreign technical trace does not by itself prove State direction or an armed attack.

Defence should begin with asset and dependency mapping, risk-based segmentation, strong identity and privilege controls, secure procurement/update chains, vulnerability management and tested offline recovery. CERT-In reporting and alerts, NCIIPC coordination for notified CII, sectoral CSIRTs and operator SOCs must share timely indicators. Joint exercises should test minimum-service restoration. Police and international-cooperation channels must preserve admissible evidence; diplomatic or counter-measures should match attribution confidence and consequence.

India should prioritise resilience while attribution develops. Measurable outcomes are containment time, service restoration, root-cause closure and recurrence reduction—not merely incident counts or retaliatory claims.

### Mains PYQ M4 — 2022 GS-III Q19 — DIRECT

**Exact official wording:** “What are the different elements of cyber security? Keeping in view the challenges in cyber security, examine the extent to which India has successfully developed a comprehensive National Cyber Security Strategy.” *(250 words, 15 marks)*

**Demand:** define elements, assess—not merely list—the architecture, and give a graded verdict on “comprehensive strategy.”

**Model answer:**

Cyber security protects confidentiality, integrity and availability, reinforced by authenticity, accountability, safety and resilience. Operationally it spans identify, protect, detect, respond, recover and learn across people, process, technology and supply chains.

India has built substantial components. The IT Act provides offences and powers; sections 70/70A/70B structure protected systems, NCIIPC and CERT-In. CERT-In’s 2022 Directions require rapid reporting and log retention. I4C and State police support the cybercrime chain; NCCC provides national situational awareness; sectoral CSIRTs and regulators address finance, power and other domains. Telecom cyber rules, the DPDP framework and cyber-hygiene programmes add network, data and citizen layers. The National Cyber Security Policy, 2013 remains a formal anchor.

Comprehensiveness remains qualified. No newer single publicly notified National Cyber Security Strategy was located by September 2026. Federal/private-operator coordination, skilled personnel, legacy systems, cloud and supply-chain concentration, small-entity compliance, attribution and cross-border evidence remain constraints. DPDP’s core obligations are also still in phased commencement, so notification cannot be equated with full enforcement.

India has therefore developed a broad cyber-security architecture, but not a fully integrated or uniformly mature outcome. The priority is enforceable baselines, cross-sector dependency exercises, tested recovery, metrics linking audits to remediation, and rights-respecting information sharing.

### Mains PYQ M5 — 2024 GS-III Q10 — APPLICATION

**Exact official wording:** “Describe the context and salient features of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.” *(150 words, 10 marks)*

**Demand:** state why the law was needed, its architecture and its exact current commencement status.

**Model answer:**

The DPDP Act, 2023 creates a framework for digital personal-data processing after reliance on narrower IT Act rules and constitutional privacy doctrine. It also reaches specified processing outside India linked to offering goods or services to Data Principals in India.

It includes notice and consent, specified legitimate uses, fiduciary and principal duties, additional obligations for Significant Data Fiduciaries, children’s safeguards, individual rights, a Data Protection Board, penalties and transfer restrictions by notification.

Current status is essential. Notifications dated 13 November 2025 phased commencement: Board-related provisions began then; the one-year tranche is due 13 November 2026; most core processing duties and rights begin 13 May 2027. Thus, on 27 September 2026 the regime is not fully in force. DPDP governs personal data; it does not replace CERT-In, NCIIPC, cybercrime law or CII protection.

## ORIGINAL MAINS PRACTICE

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Differentiate CERT-In, NCIIPC, I4C and NCCC in India’s cyber-security architecture. *(Answer in 150 words.)*

**Model answer:**

**CERT-In**, under IT Act section 70B and MeitY, is the national agency for cyber-incident information, alerts, emergency measures and response coordination. **NCIIPC**, under NTRO and section 70A, is the national nodal agency for protecting Critical Information Infrastructure and supporting protected-system owners. **I4C**, under MHA, coordinates the cybercrime response through reporting, analytics, forensics, training and interstate support; complaints go to competent State/UT police, and 1930 supports rapid financial-fraud reporting. **NCCC** generates national cyber-threat situational awareness and information sharing in the MeitY/CERT-In architecture.

The distinctions are functional. A ransomware event at a notified power system may require operator containment, CERT-In reporting, NCIIPC and sectoral coordination, while fraud against customers needs an I4C-supported police investigation. NCCC awareness is not an FIR, CERT-In reporting is not CII designation, and none of these bodies replaces a court. Clear hand-offs matter more than an acronym list.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Explain how Critical Information Infrastructure and a “protected system” differ under the IT Act, 2000. *(Answer in 150 words.)*

**Model answer:**

Critical Information Infrastructure is defined in section 70 by consequence: a computer resource whose incapacitation or destruction would have a debilitating impact on national security, the economy, public health or safety. It is therefore a risk concept tied to essential-service harm.

A **protected system** is a legal status. The appropriate Government may notify a computer resource that directly or indirectly affects CII; authorised access and prescribed security practices then apply, with enhanced penalties for unauthorised access. A power, banking or health-sector label does not automatically prove that every server in that sector has this notification.

NCIIPC is the section 70A nodal agency, but operators still implement controls and recovery, while CERT-In handles incidents under section 70B. The distinction prevents treating all important systems as notified or notification as resilience. Criticality asks what failure would do; protected status asks what Government has formally designated.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Assess the CERT-In six-hour incident-reporting rule from both security and rights perspectives. *(Answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer:**

CERT-In’s 28 April 2022 Directions require specified covered entities to report listed cyber incidents within six hours of noticing them or being brought to notice. They also require secure retention of ICT logs for a rolling 180 days within Indian jurisdiction. The official FAQ allows an initial report with available information and later supplementation.

The rule advances security by shortening detection-to-coordination time, enabling warning to other potential victims, preserving volatile evidence and helping identify campaigns crossing firms or sectors. A common timeline also reduces incentives to conceal incidents. Logs can support technical diagnosis and criminal investigation.

The burdens are real. A six-hour window may precede confident classification; rushed reports can be incomplete. Broad logging creates an attractive data target and imposes cost on smaller entities. Retained data may reveal user behaviour, so access, purpose, security and deletion controls matter. Duplicate reporting to CERT-In, sector regulators, police and—when commenced and applicable—the DPDP framework can create confusion.

Implementation should use clear incident lists, severity-based templates, secure automated reporting, protected good-faith disclosure, one designated contact, regulator harmonisation and audit of response value. Authorities should distinguish initial notification from final forensic conclusions and minimise unnecessary personal data.

Thus, rapid reporting is justified for shared situational awareness, but proportionality and secure handling determine whether it improves resilience without creating excessive compliance and privacy risk.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** “A cross-border cyberattack is not necessarily cyberwar.” Analyse. *(Answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer:**

“Cyberattack” describes hostile digital conduct; “cyberwar” implies an exceptional State-linked operation assessed against international-law thresholds. A phishing campaign, ransomware infection, espionage intrusion or service disruption may cross borders because infrastructure and victims are distributed, yet remain crime, intelligence activity or a lower-level hostile operation.

Three tests prevent escalation by label. **Attribution:** an IP address, language artefact or reused malware can point to infrastructure but not necessarily the operator, sponsor or State direction. **Effect:** theft, temporary disruption, physical damage and loss of life differ in severity; use-of-force or armed-attack analysis cannot be presumed. **Purpose and law:** section 66F cyber terrorism requires specified conduct and terror/sovereignty intent; ordinary fraud or unauthorised access follows other criminal provisions. Section 75 can provide an Indian nexus where a computer resource in India is involved, but foreign evidence still needs cooperation and admissibility.

India should therefore contain and recover first, preserve logs and malware, coordinate CERT-In/NCIIPC/sectoral actors, investigate through police and mutual-assistance channels, and make political or counter-measures proportionate to confidence and consequence. Public attribution should disclose enough reasoning without exposing sensitive capabilities.

Resilience is indispensable because structural anonymity cannot be abolished. Precision avoids both under-reaction to severe sabotage and dangerous over-reaction to an incident whose actor and legal threshold remain uncertain.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Design a resilience framework for a notified Critical Information Infrastructure system facing ransomware and supply-chain compromise. *(Answer in 300 words.)*

**Model answer:**

A notified protected system requires continuity of the essential service, not merely protection of its computers. Planning should begin with service, asset and dependency mapping, including cloud, telecom, power, identity, software suppliers and privileged administrators.

**Identify and protect:** classify critical functions and minimum service levels; apply secure procurement and update verification, software/component inventory, least privilege, multi-factor authentication, network and backup segmentation, offline recovery copies, vendor-access controls and tested manual fallback. Contracts must preserve log access, notification, subcontractor and exit duties.

**Detect and respond:** monitor identities, endpoints, networks and operational technology; correlate sector and national threat intelligence; maintain an exercised incident command. On ransomware, isolate affected segments without destroying evidence, revoke compromised credentials, preserve logs and malware, assess exfiltration, and report a specified incident to CERT-In within the applicable six-hour rule. NCIIPC coordinates the CII-protection dimension; the sectoral CSIRT/regulator and operator manage domain continuity; police/I4C investigate crime.

**Recover and learn:** restore only from integrity-tested clean copies, validate safety before reconnection, communicate honestly with dependent services and affected users, rotate secrets, close the root vulnerability and retest. Payment is not a recovery strategy because it gives no assurance of decryption or deletion.

**Governance:** separate incident facts from foreign-State attribution, protect retained personal data, document emergency access, and enable independent review. Board-level accountability should track mean detection/containment/restoration time, backup success, privileged-access exceptions, supplier remediation and recurrence—not audit counts alone.

Because CII failure cascades, conduct cross-sector exercises with power, telecom, finance and government dependencies. The decisive capability is graceful degradation and verified restoration under attack.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Critically evaluate India’s current cyber legal and institutional architecture, including the phased DPDP regime. *(Answer in 300 words.)*

**Model answer:**

India has developed a broad but fragmented cyber architecture. The IT Act supplies specialised offences and powers: section 66F cyber terrorism; sections 69/69A/69B interception, blocking and traffic monitoring; and sections 70/70A/70B for CII, NCIIPC and CERT-In. CERT-In’s 2022 Directions create rapid incident reporting and log duties. I4C and State police address cybercrime; NCCC supplies situational awareness; sectoral CSIRTs and regulators translate national baselines into continuity requirements. The Telecommunications Act and telecom-cyber rules add network and anti-fraud controls.

The DPDP Act adds a separate personal-data layer: lawful processing, fiduciary duties, individual rights, children and significant-fiduciary safeguards, Board enforcement and transfer restrictions by notification. Its current status is qualified. Board-related provisions commenced on 13 November 2025, but the one-year tranche is due 13 November 2026 and most core duties/rights on 13 May 2027. Notification therefore cannot be called full enforcement.

Strengths include statutory incident/CII institutions, national reporting, sector specialisation and growing citizen-fraud coordination. Gaps include no newer single publicly notified strategy replacing NCSP 2013, uneven State and small-entity capacity, legacy systems, skills, supply-chain/cloud concentration, overlapping reporting and structural attribution difficulty. Broad monitoring or retention also needs constitutional calibration after *Puttaswamy* and *Shreya Singhal*.

Reform should harmonise reporting, publish role and dependency maps, enforce baseline controls proportionately, protect good-faith disclosure, strengthen digital forensics and cross-border evidence, test backups and minimum-service recovery, and measure remediation and victim outcomes. A comprehensive framework is not the sum of agencies; it is their lawful, interoperable and demonstrably effective operation.
