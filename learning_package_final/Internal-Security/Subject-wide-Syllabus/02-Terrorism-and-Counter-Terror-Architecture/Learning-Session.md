### SESSION 1 — FOUNDATION — Meaning, purpose and audience

#### Visual

```text
political / religious / ideological objective
                    ↓
planned or organised violence / threat of violence
                    ↓
coercion of a government or intimidation of a wider public
                    ↓
physical harm + fear + publicity + institutional disruption
```

#### Explanation

The canonical working definition describes terrorism as the planned, organised and systematic use of violence as coercion for political, religious or ideological purposes. No universally accepted international definition removes every dispute, but an exam answer still needs a usable core: **coercive violence, a political or ideological purpose, and an audience wider than the immediate victim**.

An ordinary violent offence may produce fear, yet terrorism uses fear instrumentally to influence a government, community or political order. Conversely, a radical or unpopular belief is not by itself terrorism; the legal and security threshold concerns conduct, preparation, facilitation or incitement connected to violence under the applicable law.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Terrorism is violence designed not only to injure its immediate target but to coerce a wider political audience through fear.

**Must-write keywords:** coercion; wider audience; political/ideological purpose; violence; intimidation; legal threshold.

**Answer paragraph:** *Claim* — terrorism is a communicative form of political violence. *Evidence* — the canonical definition joins organised violence to political, religious or ideological coercion. *Analysis* — publicity and fear magnify harm beyond the attack site. *Qualification* — motive or belief alone does not establish the offence; statutory ingredients and evidence remain necessary.

**UPSC traps:** terrorism is not every violent crime; extremism is not automatically terrorism; a label is not a conviction.

### SESSION 2 — FOUNDATION — Terrorism, insurgency, LWE and organised crime

#### Visual

| Category | Dominant objective | Typical operating form | Decisive distinction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Terrorism | Coerce or intimidate for a political/ideological end | Spectacular or repeated violence aimed at a wider audience | A method and strategy of coercion |
| Insurgency | Challenge State authority or seek control/autonomy | Sustained armed political contest with an area or claimed social base | Broader political-military campaign |
| Left-Wing Extremism | Revolutionary capture/replacement of State power | Maoist organisation, guerrilla violence and parallel influence | Particular ideology and movement |
| Organised crime | Profit and durable illicit market power | Trafficking, extortion, laundering and protection networks | Material objective; terrorism may use its services |

#### Explanation

The terms overlap operationally but are not synonyms. Terrorism can be a tactic within an insurgency. LWE is a particular ideological insurgent/extremist phenomenon. Organised crime may finance or supply terrorism while pursuing profit rather than political transformation. The examiner rewards a **nexus analysis**, not conceptual merger.

The canonical India typology separates hinterland terrorism, J&K militancy, North-East insurgency and LWE. The last three have dedicated topics because their histories, political claims, geography and response requirements differ.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Classify by objective and organisation before classifying by weapon: identical violence can serve terrorist coercion, insurgent control or criminal profit.

**Must-write keywords:** method; movement; objective; social base; revolutionary strategy; profit motive; nexus, not identity.

**Answer paragraph:** A terror group may purchase arms from a trafficking network. The transaction links terrorism and organised crime, but the former seeks political coercion while the latter seeks profit; therefore finance and logistics can be jointly disrupted without collapsing their legal or analytical identities.

### SESSION 3 — CORE — External State, non-State and proxy actors

#### Visual

```text
sponsor State
  ├─ finance / sanctuary / training / weapons / intelligence
  └─ diplomatic or information cover
                    ↓
nominally non-State proxy or local module
                    ↓
operational deniability for sponsor + domestic harm in target State
```

#### Explanation

“State actor” and “non-State actor” describe formal identity; they do not prove operational independence. The canonical source uses the proxy-war idea to explain how a group can remain legally non-State while receiving material support from a State. That relationship gives the sponsor deniability and complicates attribution.

Attribution must be rung-specific. A government statement, intelligence assessment, investigative allegation, charge-sheet and judicial finding carry different evidentiary weight. Use verbs such as **alleged, investigated, chargesheeted, convicted** precisely.

A **sleeper cell** is a dormant operative or cell placed within a target environment and activated on instruction. Dormancy describes operational posture, not proof that every quiet associate belongs to such a cell.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Proxy warfare separates the visible perpetrator from the strategic sponsor; counter-terrorism must therefore disrupt both the operational cell and the enabling ecosystem.

**Must-write keywords:** state sponsorship; non-State front; proxy; sanctuary; deniability; attribution; evidentiary status.

**Answer paragraph:** *Claim* — external sponsorship can convert a domestic vulnerability into sustained violence. *Evidence* — finance, sanctuary, training and logistics can reach a nominally non-State group. *Analysis* — deniability lowers the sponsor's direct cost while extending the network's endurance. *Qualification* — current attribution must rest on a dated competent-agency or judicial record.

### SESSION 4 — CORE — Radicalisation and recruitment without profiling

#### Visual

```text
grievance / identity anxiety / ideological exposure
                    ↓  (not automatic)
trusted recruiter + selective narrative + social reinforcement
                    ↓
acceptance of violence → task allocation → facilitation/attack
                    ↑
         off-ramps: family, community, counselling,
         lawful online intervention, education, grievance access
```

#### Explanation

Radicalisation is a process, not a demographic label. A grievance does not mechanically cause violence; organisation, persuasion, network ties, opportunity and enabling resources convert susceptibility into mobilisation. Online content can accelerate exposure and networking, but offline relationships and local context often remain decisive.

Prevention should focus on behaviour and legally relevant risk rather than community identity. Community trust, credible counter-narratives, referral and counselling pathways, education and livelihood access, and proportionate investigation can reduce recruitment. Indiscriminate surveillance or stigmatisation can suppress cooperation and strengthen the persecutory narrative used by recruiters.

PRAHAAR's “attenuating conditions enabling terrorism” element gives this prevention logic a current policy anchor. Detailed platform governance belongs to Topic 09.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** The preventive target is the conversion pathway from grievance to organised violence, not the identity of the population in which recruiters operate.

**Must-write keywords:** susceptibility; mobilisation; recruiter; social reinforcement; off-ramp; counter-radicalisation; non-discrimination.

**Answer paragraph:** *Claim* — unemployment can create susceptibility but does not explain recruitment by itself. *Evidence* — the 2024 GS-IV case links unemployment, targeted online contact and local intermediaries. *Analysis* — intervention must break recruiter access and provide credible off-ramps. *Qualification* — criticism of government or religious identity cannot substitute for evidence of violent facilitation.

### SESSION 5 — CORE — The terrorism ecosystem and financing boundary

#### Visual

```text
ideology / strategic direction
        ↓
recruitment → finance → training → weapons → logistics
        ↓                                  ↓
communications / reconnaissance → target selection
        ↓
attack or attempted attack → publicity → fear → renewed recruitment
```

#### Explanation

Terrorism is an ecosystem rather than an isolated attacker. Funds may arise from State sponsorship, extortion, counterfeit currency, hawala, contraband, donations, front entities or organised-crime relationships. Finance supports travel, communication, safe houses, weapons and propaganda; it is not only the money used at the final attack stage.

The correct response is channel-specific: financial intelligence and tracing for transfers; customs and border interdiction for contraband; criminal investigation for predicate offences; sanctions and international cooperation for cross-border networks. Detailed AML/CFT architecture, FATF processes and attachment/confiscation belong to Topic 10.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Follow the network upstream—from attack to logistics, finance, recruitment and sponsor—because replacing one operative leaves the enabling system intact.

**Must-write keywords:** facilitation; logistics; hawala; FICN; front entity; predicate offence; follow the money; upstream disruption.

**UPSC trap:** seizure or provisional attachment is not confiscation; an allegation of funding is not proof of guilt.

### SESSION 6 — CORE — Counter-terrorism as a lifecycle

#### Visual

```text
PREVENT       PROTECT        DISRUPT        RESPOND
intelligence  hardening      interdict      contain/rescue
community     border/cyber   people/arms    preserve life
trust         security       money/comms    secure scene
     \______________  _____________________/
                    \/
             INVESTIGATE → PROSECUTE
             lawful evidence   fair trial
                    ↓
            RECOVER → LEARN → ADAPT
```

#### Explanation

Prevention, response and prosecution are different tasks. Intelligence and community trust may prevent an incident; protective security reduces vulnerability; disruption interrupts people, weapons, finance or communications; police and specialist forces respond; investigators convert leads into admissible evidence; prosecutors and courts determine liability; recovery restores services, assists victims and learns from failure.

Prevention is harder to measure because success is an event that did not occur. Arrests and seizures are visible outputs, but they do not alone establish deterrence, reduced recruitment, conviction or resilience. A balanced answer therefore uses indicators at multiple stages.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** A counter-terror architecture fails when warning, response, investigation and prosecution are treated as interchangeable boxes rather than a connected chain with accountable hand-offs.

**Must-write keywords:** prevention; protection; disruption; first response; chain of custody; prosecution; recovery; resilience.

**Answer paragraph:** MAC may share a warning, but the State police must assess and act lawfully; after an incident, scene preservation and forensics must convert the warning into evidence. The court, not the intelligence body, determines guilt. This chain explains why more data without better hand-offs does not guarantee security.

### SESSION 7 — CORE — Post-26/11 institutional evolution

#### Visual

```text
26/11 Mumbai attacks (2008): coordination and response gaps exposed
        ├─ NIA Act, 2008 → national scheduled-offence investigation
        ├─ MAC strengthened → intelligence sharing
        ├─ NATGRID developed → authorised data access/analysis
        ├─ four NSG hubs → faster specialist contingency response
        ├─ coastal-security strengthening → Topic 07
        └─ NCTC proposed → federal and intelligence/arrest objections
```

#### Explanation

The institutional build-out answered different failures. NIA investigates scheduled offences. MAC and SMAC share intelligence. NATGRID supports authorised access to connected data; it neither arrests nor prosecutes. Four post-26/11 NSG hubs at Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai and Hyderabad improved regional availability for exceptional counter-terror, counter-hijack and hostage-rescue contingencies; NSG is not a routine police force. NCTC sought stronger apex coordination but its proposed search/arrest/investigation design generated State and institutional objections.

The crucial lesson is **functional differentiation**. Intelligence bodies warn; police respond and initially investigate; NIA may investigate scheduled offences under statute; prosecutors present evidence; Special Courts adjudicate; NSG performs specialist intervention.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Post-26/11 reform created a network of specialised functions, not one omnipotent counter-terror agency.

**Must-write keywords:** functional differentiation; first responder; intelligence fusion; scheduled offence; contingency force; hand-off.

### SESSION 8 — CORE — Centre–State federal architecture

#### Visual

```text
State List Entry 1: public order     State List Entry 2: police
                     ↓ primary responsibility
State Police: local intelligence, first response, scene, witnesses, ordinary investigation
                     ↕ coordination / assistance
Union: Entry 2A deployment in aid of civil power + central intelligence + NIA statutory role
                     ↓
Article 355 duty does not erase State competence or ordinary accountability
```

#### Explanation

Public order and police are State subjects. Local police ordinarily arrive first, secure the scene, preserve evidence, manage witnesses and maintain order. Terror networks cross jurisdictions, so the Union supplies intelligence coordination, central investigation under statute, specialist force, finance, technology and forces in aid of civil power.

The NCTC controversy crystallised two objections: bypassing State policing competence, and giving an intelligence body direct arrest powers. Coordination is essential; centralisation is not automatically the only design. MAC/SMAC and case-specific NIA jurisdiction represent a more cooperative architecture.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Cooperative federalism is an operational necessity: national reach must reinforce, not hollow out, the local police functions on which admissible evidence and public trust depend.

**Must-write keywords:** State List Entries 1 and 2; Union List Entry 2A; Article 355; aid to civil power; cooperative federalism.

### SESSION 9 — CORE — NIA, Special Courts and State police

#### Visual

| Stage | Primary role | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| State-police action | first response, local intelligence, scene and ordinary policing | national attribution or guilt |
| NIA investigation | investigates and prosecutes offences in the NIA Act Schedule when statutory route is invoked | conviction merely because NIA took over |
| Special Court | conducts trial under the NIA Act framework | that every listed/designated person is guilty |
| High Court appeal | judicial appellate scrutiny | automatic reversal or confirmation |

#### Explanation

NIA was constituted under the NIA Act, 2008. The Central Government may direct it to investigate a scheduled offence; State authorities retain their legal duties and must assist. NIA is an investigating and prosecuting agency, not an intelligence fusion centre or tactical assault force.

The present Schedule includes the UAPA and specified statutes/offences concerning atomic energy, hijacking, aviation and maritime safety, weapons of mass destruction, explosive substances, cyber terrorism, trafficking, counterfeit currency and prohibited arms. The 2019 amendment expanded specified extra-territorial reach, added offences to the Schedule and enabled designation of Courts of Session as Special Courts.

Under the NIA Act framework, a Special Court is presided over by a judge appointed on the recommendation of the relevant High Court's Chief Justice; it has Court-of-Session powers, gives scheduled trials precedence and is subject to High Court appeal. Jurisdiction, investigation, prosecution and adjudication nevertheless remain distinct.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** National jurisdiction solves the cross-border and inter-State investigation problem only if local evidence, forensic quality and prosecutorial continuity survive the transfer.

**Must-write keywords:** scheduled offence; concurrent statutory role; State assistance; Special Court; day-to-day trial; appeal; admissible evidence.

### SESSION 10 — CORE — Legal evolution and the current dual route

#### Visual

```text
TADA: 1985–1995, lapsed amid misuse concerns
        ↓
POTA: 2002–2004, repealed amid misuse concerns
        ↓
UAPA: 1967 law; anti-terror framework widened in 2004/2008/2012/2019
        +
NIA Act, 2008; amended 2019
        +
BNS, 2023 in force 1 July 2024
Section 113 defines terrorist act in general penal law
        ↓
SP-or-above decides registration under BNS 113 or UAPA
        ↓
choice of statute ≠ proof of guilt; UAPA remains in force
```

#### Explanation

TADA and POTA show that emergency-style powers create a recurring necessity–misuse debate. The present architecture centres on UAPA and the NIA Act, alongside the BNS general offence. BNS section 113 did not repeal UAPA; it created concurrent legal pathways whose procedural and institutional consequences differ.

The forum-choice rule should be described exactly: the BNS explanation assigns the choice between section 113 and UAPA to an officer not below the rank of Superintendent of Police. It does not declare every BNS terror case a State-only matter or every UAPA case an automatic NIA case.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** India's legal architecture has widened from special anti-terror statutes to concurrent special and general-law routes, making transparent forum choice a new accountability question.

**Must-write keywords:** special law; general penal law; concurrent routes; SP-rank decision; sanction; bail; due process.

### SESSION 11 — CORE — UAPA 2019 and the evidence-rung firewall

#### Visual

```text
organisation in First Schedule / individual in Fourth Schedule
                    ↓ executive listing or designation
application for removal → Review Committee route
                    ↓
separate criminal process, if any:
investigation → arrest → charge-sheet → trial → conviction/acquittal
                    ↓
property chain:
identification → seizure/attachment → adjudication → confiscation, if ordered
```

#### Explanation

The 2019 UAPA amendment allows the Central Government to add an individual to the Fourth Schedule. This is an executive designation with statutory removal and review mechanisms; it is not a judicial conviction. Proscription of an organisation and designation of an individual are also distinct acts under different schedules.

The amendment permits an NIA officer of Inspector rank or above to investigate specified UAPA offences and changes approval for seizure or attachment in NIA-investigated cases to the Director General of NIA. These are powers and process rules, not outcome data.

Section 43D(5) establishes a stringent bail threshold for specified UAPA offences when the court finds reasonable grounds to believe the accusation prima facie true. Section 45 requires prior sanction for cognisance/prosecution under the relevant chapters. Bail, sanction, trial and conviction should not be collapsed.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Anti-terror law must be read as a ladder of distinct legal acts; moving from one rung to the next requires its own authority, evidence and safeguard.

**Must-write keywords:** First Schedule; Fourth Schedule; designation; denotification; Review Committee; section 43D(5); section 45; attachment; conviction.

### SESSION 12 — CORE SYNTHESIS — PRAHAAR, rights and measurable security

#### Visual

```text
PRAHAAR — 23 February 2026
  ├─ prevention
  ├─ response
  ├─ aggregating national capacities
  ├─ human-rights and rule-of-law process
  ├─ attenuating conditions enabling terrorism
  ├─ aligning/shaping international efforts
  └─ recovery and resilience
                    ↓
works through existing institutions; does not create a new arresting agency
                    ↓
measure separately: readiness, disruption, prosecution, trust and resilience
```

#### Explanation

PRAHAAR supplies a current umbrella for the complete lifecycle. Its value is conceptual integration: preventive intelligence and attenuating conditions; swift proportionate response; institutional capacity aggregation; human-rights and rule-of-law process; international cooperation; and recovery/resilience. Its text also recognises cyber, drone and dark-web/cryptocurrency-enabled threats.

A rights safeguard is not an external obstacle to security. Legality, necessity, proportionality, non-discrimination, reasoned authorisation, judicial review, fair trial and accountability sustain public cooperation and the evidentiary quality needed for durable convictions. Conversely, safeguards do not prevent targeted lawful force against an imminent threat.

Use multiple metrics: time taken to share and act on intelligence; protected-site readiness; disruption of specific networks; quality and timeliness of investigation; trial outcomes; repeat recruitment; victim recovery; community reporting and absence of retaliatory abuse. Do not infer impact from a launch, arrest or seizure alone.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Durable counter-terrorism joins operational speed to constitutional legitimacy, because public trust is both a rights outcome and an intelligence asset.

**Must-write keywords:** PRAHAAR; whole-of-government; whole-of-society; proportionality; prevention counterfactual; output–outcome distinction; resilience.

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

> Use this block only after the Basic answer can stand independently.

### A1. Coordination is not centralisation

Real-time coordination is indispensable, but an apex body with direct search and arrest powers is not the only design. The NCTC episode shows two separate concerns: federal bypass of State police and concentration of coercive power in an intelligence body. MAC/SMAC and case-specific NIA jurisdiction preserve more visible functional boundaries, though they depend on State capacity and cooperative behaviour.

### A2. State police are structurally, not ceremonially, important

Local police usually reach the scene first, know local networks, preserve evidence, protect witnesses and maintain order. A central investigation cannot repair evidence never collected or trust destroyed at first contact. Capacity-building for State police is therefore national counter-terror policy, not merely a State administrative issue.

### A3. Designation and prosecution serve different legal logics

Individual designation is preventive and signalling-oriented; prosecution is punitive and evidence-tested. The security rationale is that organisational bans can be evaded through renaming. The rights concern is that executive listing carries serious practical and reputational effects before a criminal judgment. A strong answer states both and names the removal/review route.

### A4. BNS–UAPA forum choice after 1 July 2024

BNS section 113 introduced a general-law terrorist-act offence while UAPA remained the special statute. The SP-rank choice can affect the applicable procedure, sanction, bail framework and possible central-agency route. This is a governance question about reasons, consistency and review; registration under either law is not proof of the accusation.

### A5. Bail is an operational rights issue

UAPA section 43D(5)'s prima-facie-true threshold can make pre-trial liberty the immediate constitutional concern. The correct analysis is not “security versus all bail,” but whether investigation is timely, evidence is scrutinised, delay is controlled and courts can apply statutory and constitutional safeguards.

### A6. Prevention has a measurement asymmetry

Response generates visible counts—operations, arrests, seizures. Prevention generates a counterfactual. Credible evaluation therefore combines process indicators (timeliness, coverage, referral capacity), intermediate outcomes (disrupted recruitment or finance pathways) and long-run outcomes (lower repeat violence and stronger public reporting), while avoiding causal claims from a single trend.

### A7. Technology widens both threat and response

Drones, encrypted communications, cyber intrusion, dark-web markets and virtual assets can lower distance and coordination costs. The answer should identify what function technology serves—recruitment, reconnaissance, finance, delivery or propaganda—and then match a lawful response. “Use AI” or “ban social media” is not an architecture.

### A8. International cooperation is operational only when translated domestically

The UNSC CTC, sanctions regimes, FATF standards, information exchange and mutual legal assistance matter because networks cross borders. The **2022 Delhi Declaration** specifically addresses terrorist use of new and emerging technologies. Their domestic value depends on timely listing, financial investigation, admissible evidence, extradition/assistance and fair prosecution. International declaration is not domestic implementation.

### A9. Security–rights feedback loop

Overbroad coercion can damage community reporting, increase perceived injustice and assist recruitment narratives. Under-reaction can expose citizens and weaken confidence in the State. The calibrated position is targeted, intelligence-led and reviewable coercion alongside preventive social measures and effective justice.

### A10. Advanced answer method

```text
1. Define the threat and legal/status boundary.
2. Identify actor, sponsor, objective and wider audience.
3. Trace recruitment, finance, logistics, technology and target pathway.
4. Match each link to the competent institution and legal power.
5. Separate intelligence, investigation, prosecution and judgment.
6. Test federal competence, necessity, proportionality and accountability.
7. Distinguish policy/output from measured security and rights outcomes.
8. Conclude with prevention + lawful coercion + resilience.
```

## CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

### Definition and distinctions

- Terrorism: planned/organised violence used for political, religious or ideological coercion of a wider audience.
- Belief or dissent alone is not terrorism; conduct and statutory ingredients matter.
- Terrorism = coercive method; insurgency = sustained armed political challenge; LWE = Maoist revolutionary movement; organised crime = profit-oriented network.
- Nexus does not mean identity.

### Actor and ecosystem

- State actor, non-State actor and State-sponsored proxy are distinct.
- Proxy support may include finance, sanctuary, training, weapons, intelligence and diplomatic cover.
- Sleeper cell: dormant operative/cell activated on instruction; dormancy is an operational description, not guilt.
- Attribution ladder: intelligence assessment → investigation → charge-sheet → trial → judgment.
- Ecosystem: ideology/grievance → recruiter/network → mobilisation → finance/logistics/training → attack/propaganda → renewed recruitment.
- Prevent the conversion pathway; do not profile communities.

### Counter-terror lifecycle

- Prevent: intelligence, trust, counter-radicalisation and vulnerability reduction.
- Protect: sites, borders, coast, cyber systems and public spaces.
- Disrupt: operatives, weapons, finance, communications and facilitators.
- Respond: police containment; NSG only for exceptional specialised contingencies.
- Investigate: State police or NIA under the applicable legal route.
- Prosecute/adjudicate: prosecutor and competent/Special Court.
- Recover: victims, services, communication, review and resilience.

### Institutional mandate map

- State police: local intelligence, first response, scene, witnesses, ordinary policing.
- MAC/SMAC: 24×7 intelligence sharing; no arrest or trial.
- NATGRID: authorised data-access/analysis support; no independent coercive power.
- NIA: investigation/prosecution of scheduled offences under the NIA Act; not intelligence fusion or tactical response.
- NSG: exceptional counter-terror, counter-hijack and hostage-rescue contingency force; post-26/11 hubs include Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai and Hyderabad; not routine police.
- NCTC: proposed apex design kept in abeyance after federal and intelligence/arrest objections; do not call it operational.
- CTCR Division: policy, legal administration, coordination and counter-radicalisation functions; not a substitute for field agencies.

### Federal spine

- State List Entry 1: public order; Entry 2: police.
- Union List Entry 2A: Union force deployed in aid of civil power.
- Article 355: Union duty; does not erase ordinary State responsibility.
- Cooperative federalism = national reach + local capacity + clear hand-offs.

### Legal chronology and close-option rules

- UAPA 1967 remains the principal special anti-terror statute; canonical amendment chronology: 1969, 1972, 1986, 2004, 2008, 2012 and 2019.
- TADA 1985–1995 and POTA 2002–2004 are no longer in force.
- UAPA 2019: individual in Fourth Schedule; NIA Inspector-rank investigation; DG-NIA approval for attachment/seizure in NIA cases.
- Organisation proscription ≠ individual designation.
- Designation ≠ arrest ≠ charge-sheet ≠ conviction.
- Seizure/attachment ≠ confiscation.
- NIA Amendment 2019: specified extra-territorial reach, expanded Schedule and Sessions Courts designated as Special Courts.
- BNS section 113, in force 1 July 2024, coexists with UAPA; SP-or-above chooses registration route.
- UAPA section 43D(5): stringent bail threshold; section 45: prior sanction framework.

### PRAHAAR and rights

- PRAHAAR released 23 February 2026; seven elements: prevention, response, capacity aggregation, human rights/rule of law, attenuating enabling conditions, international alignment, recovery/resilience.
- It is policy, not a new investigating or arresting agency.
- Rights test: legality, necessity, proportionality, non-discrimination, due process, review and accountability.
- Trust is both a constitutional value and an intelligence asset.

### Measurement and PYQ route

- Input/power: law, budget, personnel, database.
- Output: warning shared, operation, arrest, seizure, charge-sheet.
- Outcome: lawful disruption, timely trial, reduced repeat recruitment/violence, restored services and trust.
- Never infer outcome from policy launch or investigative output.
- PYQ spine: definition → manifestation/actor → ecosystem → matched institutions/laws → federal-rights limits → measurable resilience.

### Qualified conclusion

Terrorism is a networked form of coercive political violence. India therefore needs a networked but accountable response: preventive intelligence and social resilience, precise disruption, capable State policing, lawful NIA investigation, specialist contingency response, fair prosecution, international cooperation and recovery. The architecture succeeds only when security outputs mature into durable, rights-respecting outcomes.
