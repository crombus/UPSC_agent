### Evidence limits

1. The placement–layering–integration sequence is a useful typology, not the statutory test in
   section 3 PMLA and not a claim that every case visibly passes through all three stages.
2. An STR, sanctions-screening match, blockchain alert, customs mismatch or cash seizure is a
   lead. Attribution, source, control, knowledge/purpose and chain of custody still need proof.
3. “Clean-clean” is a book shorthand for apparently lawful origin and transfer before unlawful
   diversion. It does **not** mean the ultimate terrorist use is lawful.
4. Hawala, crypto, cash, trade and charities are channels or sectors, not offences by identity.
5. The normal section 8 route links confiscation to the Special Court’s final finding, but
   section 8(7) permits a property decision where trial cannot be conducted for specified
   reasons. Therefore “confiscation can occur only after conviction” is too absolute.
6. Current legal text, subordinate rules, notifications, regulator directions, FATF standards,
   UN sanctions and court judgments are kept in separate categories.

## BASIC LEARNING SESSION

### Master analytical map — follow value, prove status

```text
SOURCE                     PURPOSE                     CHANNEL
lawful / unlawful          personal / commercial      bank / cash / hawala
                                                      trade / VDA / NPO
       \                       |                         /
        └────────────── transaction trail ─────────────┘
                               ↓
REPORTING ENTITY: CDD + beneficial owner + monitoring + records + report
                               ↓
FIU-IND: receive → analyse → disseminate financial intelligence
                               ↓
COMPETENT INVESTIGATION
predicate offence: police/CBI/NCB/DRI/NIA, as jurisdiction permits
money laundering: ED | terror offence: police/NIA | regulatory breach: regulator/FIU
                               ↓
INTERIM ACTION
freeze / seize / provisional attachment ≠ guilt ≠ confiscation
                               ↓

                               ↓

                               ↓

```

### SESSION 1 — Terror financing, money laundering and proliferation financing are distinct

| Issue | Defining question | Source of value | Main legal-policy route |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Money laundering (ML)** | Is a person involved in a process/activity connected with proceeds of crime? | necessarily linked to criminal activity relating to a scheduled offence | PMLA |
| **Terrorist financing (TF)** | Was value raised, provided, received, moved or made available with the required terrorist purpose/knowledge? | may be lawful or unlawful | UAPA offences, section 51A targeted measures; PMLA where criminal proceeds are laundered |
| **Proliferation financing (PF)** | Is value financing prohibited WMD-related activity? | source can vary | WMD Act section 12A and FATF/UN targeted-financial-sanctions framework |
| **Black money/tax evasion** | Was income or wealth concealed from lawful tax/accounting systems? | may arise from lawful or unlawful activity | tax/FEMA/other laws; PMLA only if its own proceeds-and-scheduled-offence test is met |

The direction of inquiry differs. Laundering starts from **criminal origin** and asks how the
proceeds were handled. Terror financing starts from **terror purpose, beneficiary or knowledge**
and asks how value was raised, stored or moved. Thus:

```text
criminal source → conceal/use/project proceeds              = laundering
lawful source   → terrorist purpose                         = TF without prior laundering
criminal source → laundering → terrorist purpose            = both may apply
lawful source   → lawful beneficiary/use                    = neither, absent another offence
```

**Example:** a salary donation knowingly diverted to a listed terrorist organisation may create
a terror-finance issue without being “proceeds of crime” at source. Narcotics proceeds passed
through fronts and then supplied to terrorists may engage both the predicate offence/PMLA chain
and UAPA.

**Answer-grabbing line:** *Money laundering is source-centred; terrorist financing is
purpose-centred, though one transaction chain can satisfy both.*

**Must-write keywords:** proceeds of crime · scheduled offence · lawful or unlawful origin ·
terror purpose/knowledge · separate legal routes.

**Paragraph build:** Claim—ML and TF are not synonyms. Evidence—PMLA section 3 requires a process
connected with proceeds of crime, while UAPA sections 17/40 address terrorist funding from any
source. Analysis—lawful-origin funds can evade crime-origin filters. Qualification—the State
must still prove the statutory mental and factual links.

### SESSION 2 — PMLA begins with the scheduled-offence/proceeds gateway

**Scheduled or predicate offence:** an offence listed in the PMLA Schedule. Criminal activity
relating to it must produce property or value capable of being “proceeds of crime”.

**Proceeds of crime:** section 2(1)(u) covers property derived or obtained, directly or
indirectly, from criminal activity relating to a scheduled offence; it also covers the value of
such property and statutory equivalent-value situations. Unexplained wealth, tax non-compliance
or ownership of an expensive asset is not automatically proceeds of crime.

**Section 3 conduct:** the current text covers knowing involvement in one or more processes or
activities connected with proceeds, including concealment, possession, acquisition, use,
projecting or claiming as untainted. The 2019 Explanation also treats the process/activity as
continuing while a person is enjoying the proceeds through the listed modes. It is therefore
wrong to say that prosecution must prove “projection as untainted” in every case.

**Section 4 punishment:** the current Act prescribes rigorous imprisonment from three to seven
years and fine; the maximum extends to ten years where the proceeds relate to an offence in
paragraph 2 of Part A of the Schedule (the NDPS-linked category). The current text no longer
supports the book-period claim of a universal ₹5 lakh fine ceiling.

```text
scheduled criminal activity
          ↓
identified property/value derived or obtained from it
          ↓
knowing involvement in a section 3 process/activity
          ↓
money-laundering allegation
```

**Judicial boundary:** *Vijay Madanlal Choudhary* bars PMLA action on a merely notional
assumption that a scheduled offence occurred. *Pavana Dibbur* clarifies that the laundering
accused need not also be an accused in the predicate case; however, if the scheduled case ends
entirely in acquittal/discharge or is quashed, the linked proceeds foundation cannot survive.

**Answer-grabbing line:** *PMLA does not punish wealth in the abstract; it punishes knowing
dealing with property whose criminal genealogy runs through a scheduled offence.*

**Must-write keywords:** section 2(1)(u) · section 3 · scheduled offence · criminal activity ·
property/value · continuing activity · knowledge.

**Paragraph build:** Claim—PMLA is derivative in source but autonomous in the conduct it
criminalises. Evidence—sections 2(1)(u) and 3 plus *Pavana Dibbur*. Analysis—a later handler may
be liable without committing the predicate crime. Qualification—if the predicate foundation
disappears completely, a notional proceeds case cannot remain.

### SESSION 3 — Placement, layering and integration: useful map, limited legal force

| Stage | Analytical meaning | Typical control focus | Essential limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Placement** | illicit value first enters or is converted within a financial/commercial channel | cash controls, account monitoring, source-of-funds checks | some proceeds are non-cash or already inside the formal system |
| **Layering** | transactions, entities or assets distance value from origin/control | beneficial ownership, linked-account and cross-border analysis | one complex transaction is not automatically layering |
| **Integration** | value appears available as legitimate wealth, income, business or property | asset-income mismatch, transaction purpose and source tracing | legitimate appearance does not establish lawful origin |

The model describes a common laundering pattern; it does not define section 3. Stages can overlap,
occur in another order or leave no separate visible placement stage. TBML may move and disguise
value in the same trade cycle. Terror financing with lawfully earned money may involve no
laundering stage at all.

**Safe illustration:** criminal cash is deposited or converted; ownership is obscured through
linked transactions; the value later appears as a business receipt or asset. The purpose is to
identify control failures, not reproduce evasion instructions.

**Answer-grabbing line:** *Placement–layering–integration is an investigative lens, not a
statutory checklist.*

**Must-write keywords:** typology · overlap · non-linear · section 3 elements · traceability ·
beneficial owner.

**Paragraph build:** Claim—the three-stage model structures detection. Evidence—cash entry,
transactional distancing and legitimate-looking re-entry. Analysis—each stage suggests a
different control. Qualification—legal guilt depends on proceeds, knowledge and section 3
conduct, not on proving all three labels.

### SESSION 4 — Terror-finance sources and channels must be kept separate

**Source** answers where value came from; **channel** answers how it was moved; **end-use**
answers why it was provided. One source can use many channels and one channel can carry lawful
or unlawful value.

| Source pattern | Possible channel | What must be proved |
|---|---|---|
| external sponsorship or overseas supporter | formal remittance, courier, hawala, trade or VDA | originator/control, recipient and terror nexus |
| extortion or “taxation” by an armed group | cash collection, local business or hawala | coercion, collector-network link and control of value |
| narcotics/organised-crime proceeds | cash, fronts, trade or VDA | predicate crime, proceeds trail and laundering/terror use |
| high-quality FICN | courier/distribution network | counterfeit quality, knowing handling and monetary-stability/terror link where alleged |
| donation, charity, business income or personal funds | bank, payment service, NPO or cash | diversion, knowledge/purpose and beneficiary; lawful origin is not a defence to terror use |
| crowdfunding or online solicitation | payment account, platform or VDA | campaign controller, donor knowledge where relevant, destination and end-use |

Canonical Indian examples show different funding models: external sponsorship and diaspora/
charity routes have appeared in the J&K context; extortion and parallel taxation are important in
North-East insurgency and LWE; narcotics, counterfeit currency and organised crime can supply
criminal proceeds. These are historical typologies, not claims that every current organisation
uses every route.

**Answer-grabbing line:** *Effective CFT maps a three-part chain—source, movement channel and
terror end-use—rather than assuming that detecting “dirty money” is enough.*

**Must-write keywords:** source–channel–end-use · external sponsorship · extortion · FICN ·
donations · lawful-origin diversion.

**Paragraph build:** Claim—funding models vary by threat. Evidence—external support differs from
local extortion or lawful-origin donation. Analysis—controls must be channel-specific.
Qualification—historical examples cannot prove a present network without current evidence.

### SESSION 5 — Channel-specific evidence: suspicion must become a provable chain

| Channel | Useful indicators/evidence | What the indicator cannot prove alone |
|---|---|---|
| **Hawala / informal value transfer** | parallel ledgers, broker communications, cash–bank interfaces, reciprocal settlements, travel and counterparty links | absence of a conventional wire does not prove terrorism; a code/ledger needs attribution and corroboration |
| **TBML** | invoice–shipping–customs comparison, price/quantity/quality anomalies, duplicate financing, third-party payment and related-party ownership | a valuation mismatch may arise from quality, timing, currency or error; intent and proceeds must be shown |
| **VDA/crypto** | blockchain graph, wallet attribution, VDA-service-provider KYC, Travel Rule data, device/IP evidence and fiat on/off-ramp records | an address is not a person until attributed; privacy tools or cross-chain activity alone do not prove terror purpose |
| **Cash/courier/FICN** | seizure memo, note forensics, serial/pattern analysis, CCTV/travel, fingerprints/devices and recipient link | possession or counterfeit detection alone does not prove the ultimate terror beneficiary |
| **NPO/charity** | donor and beneficiary records, governance, project/end-use documents, sanctions screening, linked persons and unexplained diversion | charitable status, foreign contribution or religious purpose is not itself evidence of abuse |
| **Front/shell business** | beneficial owner, director/control links, books, inventory, tax/customs records, circular flows and commercial rationale | incorporation, loss-making activity or cash intensity alone is not guilt |

**Evidence ladder**

```text
red flag/report
   ↓
identity and beneficial ownership
   ↓
source/predicate link OR terrorist purpose/beneficiary
   ↓
movement, control and end-use
   ↓
corroboration + lawful collection + chain of custody
   ↓
charge/prosecution → judicial finding
```

**Answer-grabbing line:** *A red flag asks a question; only corroborated evidence answers it.*

**Must-write keywords:** attribution · beneficial ownership · source of funds · purpose ·
corroboration · chain of custody · false positive.

**Paragraph build:** Claim—financial intelligence is strongest when fused with non-financial
evidence. Evidence—invoice, wallet, device, travel and beneficiary records can converge.
Analysis—convergence links value to a person and purpose. Qualification—no single red flag
should substitute for proof.

### SESSION 6 — Preventive controls and the current amendment/notification rail

Reporting entities form the preventive layer. Under sections 11A–12 and the PML Rules they
identify clients and beneficial owners, understand the relationship and business, maintain
reconstructable records, monitor transactions and furnish prescribed reports to FIU-IND.

| Instrument/date | Verified change | Correct legal label |
|---|---|---|
| **PMLA, 2002; in force 1 July 2005** | principal AML offence, investigation, attachment, adjudication and confiscation framework | Act |
| **2019 section 3 Explanation/current text** | listed processes are alternatives; laundering is continuing while proceeds are enjoyed through them | Act amendment/current statutory text |
| **WMD Amendment Act, 2022** | inserted section 12A prohibition and targeted action against proliferation financing | Act amendment |
| **7 March 2023 VDA notification** | specified VDA services became designated activities/reporting-entity business | notification under PMLA; not a new section 3 offence |
| **3 and 9 May 2023 notifications** | specified client transactions by practising professionals and specified TCSP activity brought within reporting duties | activity-specific notifications |
| **Current consolidated PML Rules** | BO identification: company/partnership more than 10%; unincorporated body more than 15%; trust beneficiaries 10% or more; foreign-PEP definition and risk controls | subordinate rules |
| **FIU VDA Guidelines, updated 8 January 2026** | registration/compliance, CDD, monitoring, reporting, sanctions screening and transfer-information duties | operational guidance |
| **RBI Commercial Banks–KYC Directions, 2025; updated 18 September 2026** | sectoral risk-based KYC/AML/CFT duties for commercial banks | regulator direction |

No separate 2024–2026 PMLA amending Act was identified in the official DOR/India Code material
reviewed. The later changes above must therefore be cited by their correct category—rule,
notification or regulatory guideline—not casually called a new PMLA offence.

An STR covers suspicion, including attempted transactions, regardless of whether the transaction
was in cash. It must not be equated with an FIR or a finding of guilt. Beneficial ownership asks
which natural person ultimately owns, controls or benefits; it is not satisfied merely by naming
the company or account holder.

**Answer-grabbing line:** *AML prevention is gatekeeping before prosecution: know the customer,
identify the natural controller, monitor behaviour, preserve records and report suspicion.*

**Must-write keywords:** reporting entity · CDD · beneficial owner · ongoing monitoring · STR ·
record reconstruction · risk-based approach.

**Paragraph build:** Claim—preventive compliance narrows anonymity before crime is proved.
Evidence—sections 11A–12, current Rule 9 thresholds and VDA/professional notifications.
Analysis—better identity and transaction data improves attribution. Qualification—compliance
data remains an input and must be lawfully tested.

### SESSION 7 — Institutional roles: intelligence, investigation, supervision and judgment

| Actor | Proper role | It does not do |
|---|---|---|
| **Reporting entities** | CDD, beneficial-owner verification, monitoring, records and prescribed reports | decide criminal guilt |
| **FIU-IND** | receive, process, analyse and disseminate financial intelligence; supervise reporting compliance through statutory powers | register an ordinary police FIR, try PMLA guilt or confiscate property |
| **RBI / SEBI / IRDAI / other notified regulators** | issue sector-specific AML/CFT directions and supervise regulated entities | investigate every underlying offence or convict |
| **State police / CBI / NCB / Customs-DRI and other competent agencies** | register and investigate the relevant scheduled/predicate crime within jurisdiction | convert every suspected asset into PMLA proceeds without the legal link |
| **NIA** | investigate/prosecute NIA Act scheduled national-security cases when the statutory takeover/entrustment route applies, including UAPA terror-funding and qualifying high-quality FICN cases | act as India’s general FIU or routine AML supervisor |
| **Enforcement Directorate (ED)** | investigate section 3 laundering, exercise statutory search/seizure/arrest powers, provisionally attach property and file prosecution complaint | adjudicate its own attachment or pronounce guilt |
| **Adjudicating Authority** | test the property case and confirm or release attachment/freezing after notice and hearing | conduct the criminal trial |
| **Special Court** | try PMLA and connected scheduled-offence matters as the Act provides; decide guilt and property consequences | treat an STR or attachment order as conclusive proof |

**Coordination chain:** reporting entity → FIU intelligence → predicate agency and/or ED/NIA →
prosecutor → court. Intelligence may trigger investigation; admissible evidence must sustain the
case.

**FEMA boundary:** ED also administers FEMA, but FEMA contravention is a civil/adjudicatory route;
it is not interchangeable with criminal liability under PMLA.

**Answer-grabbing line:** *India’s AML/CFT architecture is a relay, not a single super-agency.*

**Must-write keywords:** FIU intelligence · predicate agency · ED investigation · NIA scheduled
case · regulator supervision · Adjudicating Authority · Special Court.

**Paragraph build:** Claim—institutional precision prevents inflated claims. Evidence—FIU
analyses, ED investigates, the Authority adjudicates property and the Special Court tries guilt.
Analysis—each hand-off requires data and evidentiary continuity. Qualification—coordination
cannot erase statutory jurisdiction or fair-process safeguards.

### SESSION 8 — Attachment, adjudication, conviction and confiscation are different rungs

```text
information / predicate case
          ↓
ED reason to believe + section 5 provisional attachment
          ↓
complaint to Adjudicating Authority
          ↓
notice + reply + hearing + finding
          ↓
confirmation OR release
          ↓
Special Court trial
     ┌────┴─────────┐
offence established  not established
     ↓               ↓
confiscation         release
     ↓
restoration to legitimate claimant/victim where statutory conditions are met

Exception: section 8(7) lets the Special Court decide confiscation or release where trial
cannot be conducted because of death, proclaimed-offender status or another statutory reason.
```

**Provisional attachment:** temporarily prohibits transfer, conversion, disposition or movement
of property. It preserves the res; it does not prove title, guilt or final forfeiture.

**Adjudication:** the Adjudicating Authority examines whether the property is involved in money
laundering for continuation/release of the restraint. This property-stage finding is not the
criminal conviction.

**Trial and confiscation:** the Special Court determines the offence. Section 8(5) provides the
ordinary confiscation route when the offence is found established; section 8(6) provides release
where it is not. Section 8(7) is the limited non-conviction property route when trial cannot be
conducted. Restoration protects qualifying legitimate claimants.

**Rights safeguard:** *Pankaj Bansal* requires a copy of written grounds of arrest under section
19 as a matter of course; *Vijay Madanlal* also stresses the proceeds-of-crime foundation.

**Answer-grabbing line:** *The rule-of-law test is not how much property is announced as attached,
but whether lawful restraint matures into timely adjudication, a fair trial and the correct final
property order.*

**Must-write keywords:** reason to believe · provisional · notice/hearing · confirmation ·
Special Court · section 8(5)/(6)/(7) · release/restoration.

**Paragraph build:** Claim—asset restraint and criminal guilt are separate. Evidence—sections 5
and 8 divide ED, Authority and Court functions. Analysis—early restraint prevents dissipation
while independent review protects property rights. Qualification—normal conviction-based
confiscation has a narrow section 8(7) exception.

### SESSION 9 — UAPA terror-funding, FICN and UN sanctions form a separate route

UAPA section 17 punishes raising/providing/collecting funds for terrorist acts, including funds
from legitimate or illegitimate sources; section 40 addresses raising funds for a terrorist
organisation. Section 51A empowers targeted freezing, seizure or attachment, prohibition on
making funds/resources available and entry/transit measures.

High-quality counterfeit Indian currency becomes part of the terror-law route only when the
statutory conditions are met, including the monetary-stability intent in UAPA section 15.
Ordinary counterfeit offences are now located in Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 sections 178–182.
Counterfeit possession therefore cannot be labelled terror financing without evidence of the
required link.

| Route | Target | Trigger/effect |
|---|---|---|
| **UNSC 1267/1989** | ISIL (Da’esh)/Al-Qaida associated persons/entities | international targeted sanctions; domestic implementation |
| **UNSC 1988** | Taliban-associated persons/entities | separate UN sanctions list |
| **UNSCR 1373** | domestic/foreign designation requests satisfying national legal test | each State freezes without delay under domestic mechanism |
| **UAPA section 51A procedure** | designated/suspected terrorist assets and services within statutory scope | Indian freeze/seize/attach/prohibition and review/unfreezing route |
| **FATF country monitoring** | a jurisdiction’s AML/CFT/CPF system | standards, peer review and action-plan pressure; not an individual asset-freeze order |

The MHA procedure requires list updates, identity verification, action without delay when a true
match is established, communication to regulators/FIU/police, and a route to unfreeze assets
frozen through mistaken identity. A designation is preventive status, not conviction.

**Answer-grabbing line:** *UN sanctions target listed persons and entities; FATF evaluates
jurisdictions and systems—neither should be described as the other.*

**Must-write keywords:** UAPA sections 17/40/51A · lawful or illegitimate source · targeted
financial sanctions · 1267/1989 · 1988 · 1373 · true match · delisting/unfreezing.

**Paragraph build:** Claim—terror finance has an offence route and a preventive sanctions route.
Evidence—UAPA sections 17/40 and section 51A procedure. Analysis—funds can be stopped before a
criminal trial while prosecution proceeds separately. Qualification—identity accuracy,
exemptions, review and delisting protect lawful interests.

### SESSION 10 — FATF sets standards and tests implementation; it does not investigate cases

FATF is an intergovernmental standard-setter established in 1989. Its 40 Recommendations cover
AML, CFT and counter-proliferation financing. India became a FATF member in 2010 and also
participates in APG and EAG.

```text
40 Recommendations
       ├─ criminalisation + confiscation
       ├─ preventive measures + beneficial ownership
       ├─ FIU, supervision and law enforcement
       ├─ targeted financial sanctions + NPO risk
       ├─ VAs/VASPs
       └─ international cooperation
                    ↓
MUTUAL EVALUATION: peer review
       ├─ technical compliance: laws/rules/institutions
       └─ effectiveness: outcomes in practice (11 Immediate Outcomes)
                    ↓
FOLLOW-UP: progress reporting/re-rating as applicable
```

Technical ratings ask whether the legal framework meets Recommendations; effectiveness ratings
ask whether the system produces outcomes. FATF is not a police force, prosecutor, court, UN
sanctions committee or treaty-enforcement body.

**Compliance by members:** national risk assessment; criminalise ML/TF/PF; CDD, beneficial-owner
and record rules; STR systems and an operational FIU; risk-based supervision; investigation,
prosecution and confiscation; targeted financial sanctions; VASP controls; NPO measures
proportionate to identified risk; and international cooperation.

**Answer-grabbing line:** *FATF converts a transnational collective-action problem into common
standards, peer scrutiny and reputational incentives; domestic law still supplies coercive power.*

**Must-write keywords:** 40 Recommendations · technical compliance · 11 Immediate Outcomes ·
peer review · risk-based approach · follow-up.

**Paragraph build:** Claim—formal compliance and effective outcomes are different. Evidence—a
Mutual Evaluation rates both. Analysis—good laws can coexist with weak supervision or delayed
trials. Qualification—FATF pressure operates through members and markets, not direct conviction.

### SESSION 11 — Grey list, black list, follow-up and sanctions must never be conflated

| FATF process | Official expression | Meaning | What it does **not** automatically mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| “Grey list” | jurisdictions under increased monitoring | strategic deficiencies plus commitment to an agreed action plan and increased monitoring | FATF does not call for blanket enhanced due diligence or wholesale de-risking merely because of this list |
| “Black list” | high-risk jurisdictions subject to a call for action | serious strategic deficiencies; enhanced due diligence and, in the most serious cases, countermeasures are called for | not a UN designation of every national/person from that country |
| Mutual-evaluation follow-up | regular or enhanced follow-up | frequency/intensity of post-evaluation reporting and possible re-rating | not a grey/black-list label |
| UN targeted sanctions | named persons/entities under Security Council regimes | asset freeze and other targeted measures implemented domestically | not a FATF country-compliance grade |

**Likely effects of grey listing—analytical, not automatic:** higher compliance scrutiny, slower
or costlier correspondent relationships, risk repricing and reputational pressure can affect
capital, trade or remittance channels. Magnitude varies by country and private response. A FATF
listing itself is not a universal trade embargo.

**Current India status, checked 27 September 2026:** the latest FATF monitored-jurisdiction
statements available are dated **19 June 2026**; India appears on neither the increased-monitoring
list nor the call-for-action list. Separately, its 2024 Mutual Evaluation remains in regular
follow-up.

**Answer-grabbing line:** *Listing concerns strategic deficiencies; follow-up concerns
post-evaluation reporting; UN sanctions concern designated targets.*

**Must-write keywords:** increased monitoring · action plan · call for action · enhanced due
diligence · countermeasures · regular follow-up · no automatic sanctions.

**Paragraph build:** Claim—“grey”, “black” and “regular follow-up” answer different institutional
questions. Evidence—FATF’s separate monitored-jurisdiction and MER processes. Analysis—conflation
produces false claims about sanctions. Qualification—market actors may still respond more
cautiously on a risk basis.

### SESSION 12 — India’s 2024 Mutual Evaluation: credible framework, outcome gaps

FATF published India’s joint FATF–APG–EAG Mutual Evaluation on **19 September 2024** and placed
India in **regular follow-up**, requiring a report back to Plenary in three years. The assessment
recognised strengths including risk understanding, use of financial intelligence, access to
beneficial-ownership information, asset recovery/confiscation efforts, domestic/international
cooperation and the formalisation/financial-inclusion gains associated with India’s digital
public infrastructure. JAM is relevant as a traceability and inclusion aid, not proof that cash
or exclusion risks have disappeared.

The balanced answer must also retain the report’s improvement priorities:

- conclude pending ML and TF prosecutions and apply effective, proportionate and dissuasive
  sanctions;
- improve risk-based supervision and implementation in parts of the non-financial sector and
  VDA ecosystem;
- improve implementation of cash restrictions in the precious-metals/stones sector;
- apply a risk-based, focused and proportionate approach to NPO terrorist-financing risk,
  including outreach rather than indiscriminate treatment of the whole sector as suspect.

```text
laws + institutions + reporting        = technical capacity
financial intelligence + restraint     = disruption capacity
completed trial + sanction + recovery  = adjudicated effectiveness
rights + review + proportionality      = legitimacy and durability
```

The correct verdict is neither “FATF cleared India completely” nor “attachments show failure”.
Regular follow-up records a favourable overall assessment with specific unfinished work. India’s
absence from the June 2026 grey/black lists is a separate current fact.

**Answer-grabbing line:** *India’s next-generation AML/CFT challenge is to convert strong
detection and disruption into timely, proportionate and reviewable adjudicated outcomes.*

**Must-write keywords:** 19 September 2024 · regular follow-up · report in three years ·
prosecution backlog · sanctions · NPO outreach · supervision · outcome.

**Paragraph build:** Claim—India has credible architecture but uneven effectiveness. Evidence—the
2024 MER praises intelligence and asset recovery while flagging trials, sanctions and NPO
outreach. Analysis—the bottleneck lies after detection. Qualification—follow-up status is not a
permanent certificate and must be rechecked when FATF publishes the next report.

### SESSION 13 — Integrated response: prevent, detect, disrupt, prove and review

| Stage | Priority |
|---|---|
| **Risk assessment** | separate threat type, source, channel, geography, actor and beneficiary |
| **Prevention** | proportionate CDD/EDD, beneficial ownership, sanctions screening, records, VASP and trade controls |
| **Detection** | quality STRs, trade-data and blockchain analysis, cash/FICN forensics, multi-agency fusion |
| **Investigation** | parallel financial investigation with predicate/terror case; lawful attribution and evidence preservation |
| **Disruption** | time-bound freeze/seizure/attachment where statutory tests are met |
| **Adjudication** | independent review, reasoned orders, appeal, timely prosecution and proportionate sanction |
| **Recovery** | confiscation/release/restoration under the correct final order; cross-border cooperation |
| **Prevention feedback** | use typologies and case outcomes to improve sector controls without blanket de-risking |

**Channel-specific reforms**

1. Integrate customs, shipping, banking and beneficial-owner data for TBML risk analysis.
2. Keep hawala enforcement focused on unlicensed value transfer, settlement networks and the
   proved criminal/terror link rather than cultural labels.
3. Apply FIU-IND’s current VDA framework, Travel Rule and cross-border VASP cooperation while
   using on-chain traceability and off-chain attribution together.
4. Improve cash/FICN forensic, border and financial-intelligence coordination; prove the terror
   nexus separately.
5. Protect legitimate NPO activity through FATF Recommendation 8’s risk-based, focused and
   proportionate approach.
6. Invest in predicate-offence quality, prosecutors and Special Courts so interim disruption
   matures into final outcomes.
7. Publish disaggregated metrics: reports, investigations, attachments, confirmations,
   prosecutions, convictions, confiscations, releases/restorations and time taken.

**Master-framework application:** classify the hostile actor and objective; identify the
financial vulnerability; name the competent institution; separate legal power from operational
performance; state the coordination or rights constraint; propose a calibrated remedy.

**Answer-grabbing line:** *“Follow the money” succeeds only when financial intelligence is
converted into admissible evidence, lawful restraint and a timely final order.*

**Must-write keywords:** risk-based · parallel financial investigation · inter-agency hand-off ·
international cooperation · due process · outcome metrics.

**Paragraph build:** Claim—more alerts alone will not defeat illicit finance. Evidence—the
reporting/FIU/agency/court chain and FATF’s prosecution concern. Analysis—weak hand-offs create
attachment without resolution. Qualification—speed must remain compatible with review,
proportionality and legitimate financial access.

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

### ADVANCED BRANCH A — “Clean-origin” finance creates a purpose-detection problem

Crime-origin screening works best where the source itself leaves an illegal marker. Lawful
salary, business income or donations diverted later to a terrorist may pass ordinary source-of-
wealth checks. Detection must therefore combine beneficiary/network analysis, purpose, unusual
behaviour, sanctions screening and end-use evidence. The book’s “clean-clean” label is useful
only as shorthand for lawful-looking origin and transfer; it must never imply lawful end-use.

**Advanced trap:** demanding a predicate offence for every UAPA terror-funding case. PMLA needs
the proceeds gateway; UAPA’s terror-purpose offences do not.

### ADVANCED BRANCH B — The laundering accused and predicate accused need not be identical

*Pavana Dibbur* separates personal identity from the property foundation. A professional or
later recipient who knowingly assists concealment/use may face section 3 even if not charged
with the scheduled offence. Yet the case still depends on property derived from criminal
activity relating to a scheduled offence. If that entire predicate case is extinguished by
acquittal/discharge/quashing, the linked PMLA basis falls.

This resolves two opposite errors: “only the predicate offender can launder” and “ED can create a
predicate crime by inference”.

*Vijay Madanlal* also upheld the core ECIR, section 50 summons/evidence and section 45 twin-bail-
condition architecture. These holdings do not make an ECIR identical to an FIR or make every
statement conclusive. *Pankaj Bansal* subsequently required written grounds of arrest, showing
that strong investigative powers and enforceable procedural safeguards must be read together.

### ADVANCED BRANCH C — Disruption and adjudication use different success metrics

Freezing or attachment can stop dissipation and protect a future remedy; a completed
investigation, trial, conviction and confiscation establish a different kind of success.
Counting only attached value may reward long unresolved cases. Counting only convictions may
ignore justified early disruption. A sound dashboard therefore reports every rung, age of cases,
release/restoration and appellate outcomes.

**Rights link:** *Pankaj Bansal* makes written grounds of arrest a statutory-constitutional
safeguard. Timeliness and fairness are not external obstacles to AML/CFT effectiveness; they
make final outcomes durable.

### ADVANCED BRANCH D — TBML and VDA laundering require different analytics

TBML hides value in price, quantity, quality, shipment or payment relationships; its core
evidence lies across customs, invoices, logistics, financing and ownership. VDA analysis begins
with on-chain flows but needs off-chain attribution through VDA service-provider records,
devices, communications and fiat interfaces. Neither channel is inherently anonymous or
invisible, and neither should be reduced to the same “technology” remedy.

The current FIU VDA guidelines add registration, CDD, monitoring, sanctions-screening,
reporting and transfer-information duties. International interoperability remains essential
because providers, wallets and counterparties can span jurisdictions.

### ADVANCED BRANCH E — NPO protection requires precision, not blanket suspicion

FATF’s revised Recommendation 8 requires focused, proportionate and risk-based measures for the
subset of NPOs exposed to terrorist-financing abuse. Governance, donor/beneficiary transparency,
end-use verification and targeted outreach can reduce risk. Wholesale account closure or
labelling the entire sector suspicious damages humanitarian activity, financial inclusion and
useful intelligence.

India’s 2024 MER recommendation on NPO outreach should therefore be read with, not against, the
global anti-de-risking principle.

### ADVANCED BRANCH F — International cooperation has four different jobs

1. **Standards/evaluation:** FATF and FATF-style regional bodies.
2. **Financial intelligence:** FIU-to-FIU exchange, including Egmont channels.
3. **Evidence and assets:** MLATs, letters of request, extradition and cross-border restraint/
   recovery under applicable law.
4. **Targeted sanctions:** UN Security Council regimes implemented through domestic law.

One instrument cannot substitute for another. FATF does not execute a freeze; an FIU exchange is
not admissible proof by itself; an MLAT does not create the domestic offence; and a UN listing
does not constitute conviction.

## CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

### Core distinctions

- **ML:** proceeds of scheduled criminal activity + knowing section 3 process/activity.
- **TF:** funds/value from lawful or unlawful origin + terrorist purpose/knowledge.
- **PF:** financing prohibited WMD-related activity; WMD Act section 12A.
- **Black money/tax evasion:** separate field; PMLA applies only when its own gateway is met.
- Placement–layering–integration is a non-mandatory typology, not section 3’s legal test.

### PMLA chain

- PMLA, 2002 = Act 15 of 2003; in force 1 July 2005.
- Section 2(1)(u): proceeds derived/obtained from criminal activity relating to a scheduled
  offence, value thereof and statutory equivalent-value reach.
- Section 3: concealment/possession/acquisition/use/projection/claiming are separate listed
  processes; continuing-activity Explanation.
- Section 4: rigorous imprisonment of three to seven years plus fine; maximum ten years for the
  paragraph 2 Part A category.
- *Vijay Madanlal*: no notional predicate/proceeds case.
- *Pavana Dibbur*: laundering accused need not be predicate accused; extinguished predicate
  foundation benefits the linked PMLA accused.
- ED PAO → Adjudicating Authority confirmation/release → Special Court finding →
  section 8(5) confiscation or 8(6) release; section 8(7) is the trial-impossible exception.
- *Pankaj Bansal*: written grounds of arrest must be furnished.

### Prevention and institutions

- Reporting entity: CDD, beneficial owner, monitoring, records, prescribed reports.
- STR = intelligence lead, not FIR/charge/conviction.
- FIU-IND: receive/process/analyse/disseminate; Director also addresses reporting compliance.
- Regulators: sector rules and supervision. Police/CBI/NCB/Customs-DRI/NIA: competent predicate
  or terror investigation. ED: PMLA. Authority: property adjudication. Special Court: trial.
- 2023: VDA services, specified professional transactions and TCSP activities added to reporting
  coverage; current rules tighten BO identification. 2026 FIU VDA guidelines are guidance, not a
  new laundering offence.

### Channel-and-evidence grid

- Hawala → ledgers + communications + settlement/counterparty links; no bank wire does not erase
  evidence.
- TBML → invoice/shipping/customs/payment/ownership comparison; discrepancy alone is not intent.
- VDA → on-chain graph + attributed wallet/VASP/device/fiat evidence; address alone is not identity.
- Cash/FICN → seizure + forensics + network + beneficiary/purpose; possession alone is not TF.
- NPO/donation → donor-beneficiary-governance-end-use evidence; apply focused, proportionate risk
  controls, not blanket suspicion.

### Terror sanctions and FATF

- UAPA sections 17/40 = funding offences; section 51A = preventive targeted measures.
- UNSC 1267/1989 and 1988 lists + UNSCR 1373 requests are not FATF grey/black lists.
- FATF = 40 Recommendations + technical compliance + effectiveness/11 Immediate Outcomes +
  follow-up.
- Grey = increased monitoring/action plan; no FATF call for blanket EDD solely due listing.
- Black = call for action; EDD and, in serious cases, countermeasures.
- India: MER published 19 September 2024 → regular follow-up → report back in three years.
- As checked 27 September 2026, India is absent from the latest FATF grey/black lists dated
  19 June 2026.

### Final answer spine

Define ML versus TF → classify source/channel/end-use → identify evidence → map reporting
entity/FIU/predicate agency/ED or NIA/court → separate interim from final status → add FATF/UN
international layer → diagnose prosecution, supervision or coordination gap → propose
risk-based, rights-compatible reform.
