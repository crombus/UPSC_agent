# North-East Insurgency and Peace Processes — Solved Practice Workbook

## Evidence and use note

The Basic and Advanced owners, master framework, syllabus mapping, all 2018–2026 PYQ ledgers, local official UPSC OCR evidence and dated MHA/India Code/Supreme Court material control this workbook. Original practice is not labelled as PYQ. Every peace instrument retains its date, faction and rung; current AFSPA, FMR and UPF/KNO claims preserve expiry or evidence limitations.

## BASIC MCQS / REMEDIATION

### Q1. Which approach best reflects the internal-security diversity of the North-East?

A. Identify the State, community, faction, political demand and conflict stage before choosing a response.
B. Treat the region as a single insurgency because terrain, borders and recurring violence are shared.
C. Classify each conflict as resource-driven because development deficits exist in several States.
D. Use the newest accord in one State as the legal model for other neighbouring conflicts.

**Answer: A.**

**Option-specific explanation:**
- **A — correct:** The region contains heterogeneous conflicts that cannot share one diagnosis.
- **B — incorrect:** Shared geography does not erase different histories, demands and factions.
- **C — incorrect:** Identity, autonomy and territorial claims often have greater causal fit.
- **D — incorrect:** Settlement instruments are group- and jurisdiction-specific.

### Q2. A faction seeks greater council powers within India, while another seeks sovereignty. What is the correct analytical move?

A. Merge them because armed methods make their constitutional objectives effectively identical.
B. Treat autonomy and sovereignty as distinct demands requiring different responses.
C. Route both to a development package without negotiating political authority.
D. Describe both as cross-border crime because the groups operate near Myanmar.

**Answer: B.**

**Option-specific explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Method does not erase differences in the desired end-state.
- **B — correct:** The content of the demand controls the appropriate instrument.
- **C — incorrect:** Economic measures cannot substitute for political design.
- **D — incorrect:** Location does not determine the political objective.

### Q3. What role does historical isolation play in the canonical explanation?

**Option-specific explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Historical experience cannot support such a present generalisation.
- **B — incorrect:** A single-factor causal claim exceeds the source.
- **C — correct:** The account uses history as context and still requires later political mobilisation.
- **D — incorrect:** Later policies are central to the causal sequence.

**Model answer (136 words; limit 150):**

A **Suspension of Operations** is a temporary, monitored cessation of hostilities under ground rules on camps, arms, movement and conduct. It enables dialogue but does not settle the political demand or establish disarmament.

AFSPA can be an exceptional aid, not a permanent substitute for ordinary law. Its legitimacy depends on shrinking with threat and remaining accountable while in force.
