### Evidence limits

1. A ceasefire/SoO, framework agreement, Memorandum of Settlement and implemented settlement are different rungs.
2. Cadre surrender/disbandment is an implementation indicator, not proof that every political, territorial, rehabilitation or inter-community issue is resolved.
3. AFSPA's statute, a disturbed-area notification, geographic withdrawal and repeal are different legal acts.
4. The latest UPF/KNO instrument found expired on 3 September 2026; no continuity is asserted.
5. The FMR decision is not converted into a fully verified operational abolition without a governing notification.
6. Historical foreign-link and group-strength statements are labelled as such and not treated as current.

### Learning map

```text

              ↓

              ↓
heterogeneous State/faction-specific insurgencies
```

### SESSION 1 — FOUNDATION — Why the North-East cannot be treated as one conflict

#### Visual

| Axis | Examples of variation | Why it changes the response |
|---|---|---|
| political demand | sovereignty, autonomy, statehood, council powers, representation | settlement instrument must match the demand |
| social geography | valley/hill, tribal/non-tribal, inter-State kinship | one group's accommodation can affect another |
| organisation | faction, ceasefire participant, pro-talks group, armed holdout | status must be named group by group |
| external link | sanctuary, kinship, arms/drug route, migration | border measure has security and livelihood effects |
| conflict stage | active violence, SoO, framework, MoS, implementation | signature is not settlement delivery |

#### Explanation

“North-East insurgency” is a regional heading, not a homogeneous organisation or cause. Naga, Bodo, Mizo, Tripura and Manipur conflicts differ in historical experience, ethnic geography, constitutional demand, factional structure and external connection. A sound answer names the State and group instead of transferring one accord or cause across the region.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** The North-East is a mosaic of identity and autonomy conflicts whose common geography does not erase their distinct political demands.

**Must-write keywords:** heterogeneity; State-specific; faction-specific; identity; autonomy; territorial claim; inter-ethnic externality.

**Answer paragraph:** *Claim* — a uniform settlement template can reproduce conflict. *Evidence* — the region uses statehood, Sixth-Schedule councils, Article 371 protections, SoOs, frameworks and MoS instruments. *Analysis* — each changes a different distribution of power. *Qualification* — even a tailored accord needs implementation and neighbouring-community consent.

### SESSION 2 — FOUNDATION — Historical isolation, Assamisation and statehood

#### Visual

```text
colonial excluded-area administration + missionary access
                   ↓
weak political/social integration with wider nationalist movement
                   ↓
post-Independence identity insecurity
                   +
1960 Assamese official-language policy (“Assamisation” fear)
                   ↓
statehood as accommodation:
Nagaland 1963 → Meghalaya/Manipur/Tripura 1972
              → Mizoram/Arunachal Pradesh 1987
```

#### Explanation

The canonical account treats historical isolation as a foundational condition, not a complete cause. Post-Independence language and assimilation anxieties sharpened identity claims. Statehood then became a major accommodation tool, but political reorganisation did not settle every territorial, factional or inter-community demand.

Pre-statehood histories also differ. Nagaland's statehood arose through parliamentary legislation, alongside the Thirteenth Amendment's special constitutional protection; it should not be said to have been created by the constitutional amendment itself.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Statehood reduced the scale of some centre–periphery conflicts, yet it also relocated unresolved identity and boundary questions inside new States.

**Must-write keywords:** excluded areas; identity formation; Assamisation; statehood; accommodation; residual claims.

### SESSION 3 — CORE — Constitutional accommodation is plural

#### Visual

```text
Sixth Schedule — Articles 244(2), 275(1)
   └─ tribal areas of Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram
      → Autonomous District/Regional Councils

Article 371A → Nagaland: specified customary, religious/social,
               civil/criminal-justice and land/resource protections

Article 371C → Manipur: hill-area committee and Governor-related mechanism
```

#### Explanation

The Sixth Schedule provides autonomous district and regional councils with legislative, judicial, revenue and administrative functions within its constitutional field. It does not apply across the whole North-East. Nagaland's distinct protection is Article 371A; Manipur's hill-area arrangement is Article 371C.

Autonomy can address representation and cultural security, but it can also create new boundary, resource and minority-within-minority concerns. The design question is therefore powers, territory, finance, safeguards and review—not the word “autonomy” alone.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** North-East accommodation works through differentiated constitutional instruments; substituting one schedule or article for another produces both legal and analytical error.

**Must-write keywords:** Sixth Schedule; Articles 244(2), 275(1), 371A, 371C; ADC; territorial competence; minority-within-minority.

### SESSION 4 — CORE — Drivers: identity, borders, development and inter-ethnic conflict

#### Visual

```text
identity/autonomy grievance
   + historical isolation / weak representation
   + porous terrain / cross-border kinship and sanctuary
   + arms-drug routes / corruption / weak administration
   + demographic-change fear / rumour
                   ↓
factional mobilisation → coercion/violence → displacement
                   ↓
retaliatory identity hardening and further fragmentation
```

#### Explanation

The canonical drivers include alienation, deprivation, ethnic continuity across the Myanmar border, difficult terrain, historical safe havens, arms and drug flows, underdevelopment, corruption and demographic-change anxieties. Myanmar is the principal foreign-link case in the source; Bangladesh, Nepal, Bhutan and China appear as historically varying transit, sanctuary or support references and must not be presented as a uniform current network. These factors vary by place and period and should not be applied collectively to every community.

The 2012 Bodo–Muslim clashes are a historical illustration of demographic fear and provocative messages amplifying violence; the canonical source records displacement exceeding four lakh. Use the episode to show the interaction of identity, rumour and displacement, not to generalise about a whole population or invent a casualty figure.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Border porosity enables movement, but identity mobilisation and weak institutions determine whether mobility becomes an insurgent supply line or ordinary social exchange.

**Must-write keywords:** alienation; cross-border kinship; sanctuary; trafficking; demographic anxiety; rumour; displacement.

### SESSION 5 — CORE — India–Myanmar border and the FMR status firewall

#### Visual

```text
shared ethnicity + difficult terrain + village trade/kinship
                     ↓
historical FMR: movement up to 16 km without passport/visa
                     ↓
misuse concerns: insurgent movement + irregular migration + narcotics
                     ↓
8 February 2024: decision to scrap announced
                     ↓
current legal/operational position → verify notification/pass rules
```

#### Explanation

The border connects Arunachal Pradesh, Nagaland, Manipur and Mizoram with Myanmar and crosses social landscapes older than the international boundary. Regulation can reduce sanctuary, trafficking and unauthorised movement, but abrupt restriction can burden kinship, local trade and trust.

The 8 February 2024 announcement is a decision-stage fact. No formal source located in this review establishes the complete present legal status of every earlier FMR entitlement. The live border-pass system is evidence of administrative management, not proof of the exact legal route by which the former regime was displaced.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Border policy must distinguish hostile mobility from legitimate community movement; security improves through verified identity and regulated access, not through legal ambiguity.

**Must-write keywords:** FMR; 16 km; decision/notification/operation; border pass; kinship; sanctuary; narcotics; lawful mobility.

### SESSION 6 — CORE — Composite response: accommodation and coercion run together

#### Visual

```text
group/faction abjures violence → ceasefire/SoO + dialogue + monitoring
                          ↘
                           political/autonomy settlement → implementation
                          ↗
group continues violence → targeted security + policing + border denial

both tracks + development + rights + reconciliation
```

#### Explanation

The canonical “composite strategy” does not mean coercion first and talks later. The State may negotiate with one faction while acting against another that continues violence. Development, connectivity and constitutional accommodation operate alongside security, not after all risk disappears.

A ceasefire permits dialogue; it does not concede the political demand. Targeted enforcement protects civilians and the negotiating space; it should not become collective punishment. The strategic unit is the named faction and conduct.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Successful peace policy separates the group willing to enter lawful politics from the faction continuing coercion, while keeping a credible route from ceasefire to settlement and implementation.

**Must-write keywords:** composite strategy; talks; abjure violence; targeted enforcement; faction; monitoring; reconciliation.

### SESSION 7 — CORE — The peace-instrument ladder

#### Visual

| Rung | Instrument | What it establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ceasefire / Suspension of Operations | temporary cessation under ground rules | disarmament or political settlement |
| 2 | framework agreement | agreed principles for further negotiation | settled territory, competencies or timeline |
| 3 | Memorandum of Settlement / accord | signed commitments and implementation design | delivery merely by signature |
| 4 | implementation | laws, councils, funds, rehabilitation, disbandment and monitoring actually delivered | absence of successor disputes |

#### Explanation

SoO ground rules commonly regulate designated camps, arms custody, movement and extortion. Compliance needs monitoring. Prolonged ceasefire without political progress can generate frustration or factionalism. A framework narrows the negotiating field but leaves core details open. A settlement is stronger, yet peace becomes durable only through verified implementation and reconciliation.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Count peace processes by the rung reached and implemented, not by the number of signatures.

**Must-write keywords:** SoO; ground rules; framework; MoS; implementation; disarmament; rehabilitation; monitoring.

### SESSION 8 — CORE — Accord map, 2015–2025

#### Visual

| Year | Instrument | Conflict/State | Status-safe description |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 2015 | NSCN (IM) Framework Agreement | Naga process | broad principles; final settlement not established |
| 2019 | NLFT(SD) agreement | Tripura | signed settlement; MHA reports cadres joined mainstream |
| 2020 | Bru-Reang agreement | Mizoram–Tripura displacement | permanent-settlement framework; delivery tracked separately |
| 2020 | Bodo MoS | Assam | settlement commitments; surrender/disbandment reported, implementation separate |
| 2021 | Karbi Anglong MoS | Assam | settlement and mainstreaming commitments; delivery separate |
| 2022 | Assam–Meghalaya MoU | inter-State boundary | boundary instrument, not insurgent-group accord |
| 2022 | Adivasi peace agreement | Assam | agreement with armed groups; reintegration/delivery separate |
| 2023 | DNLA/DPSC MoS | Assam | signed settlement and reported return to mainstream |
| 2023 | UNLF agreement | Manipur | first agreement with a valley-based Manipuri armed group; implementation separate |
| 2023 | pro-talks ULFA MoS | Assam | signed settlement; does not cover every ULFA faction by implication |
| 2024 | TIPRA tripartite agreement | Tripura | agreement on indigenous people's issues; implementation separate |
| 2025 | revised UPF/KNO SoO ground rules | Manipur | valid to 3 September 2026; no later renewal found |

#### Explanation

The map is deliberately heterogeneous. The 2015 Naga instrument is a framework, the Manipur instrument was a time-bound SoO, and several Assam/Tripura instruments are settlements with different implementation mechanisms. The same region—and even the same State—can occupy several rungs simultaneously.

Pre-decade anchors are the Shillong Accord (1975), Assam Accord (1985), Mizo Peace Accord (1986) and Bodoland Territorial Council accord (2003). The Mizo settlement is a standard example because electoral and institutional implementation followed signature.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** The North-East peace process is a portfolio of differently matured bargains, not a single linear transition from insurgency to peace.

**Must-write keywords:** dated map; faction coverage; implementation committee; territorial competence; reintegration; successor demand.

### SESSION 9 — CORE — Accord delivery, reconciliation and successor conflicts

#### Visual

```text
signed commitment
      ↓
legal/constitutional change + institution/council
      ↓
finance + administrative capacity + cadre rehabilitation
      ↓
inter-community consultation + grievance resolution
      ↓
monitored compliance and political participation
      ↓
durable settlement OR implementation gap / successor conflict
```

#### Explanation

Implementation has several dimensions: cessation of violence, surrender and arms management, institutional change, fiscal delivery, land or territorial arrangements, rehabilitation, political participation and inter-community acceptance. Progress on one does not establish the others.

Accommodation can create externalities. A council boundary, reservation or territorial arrangement benefiting one group may concern neighbours or minorities within the new unit. Inclusive consultation and review mechanisms are therefore peace safeguards, not obstacles to settlement.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** A settlement becomes peace only when its institutional benefits are delivered without transferring insecurity to another community.

**Must-write keywords:** implementation matrix; fiscal delivery; disbandment; political participation; reconciliation; successor demand; third-party effects.

### SESSION 10 — CORE — AFSPA: authority, necessity and accountability

#### Visual

```text
Section 3 → time/place-specific disturbed-area notification
                  ↓
Section 4 → specified force, arrest and search powers in that area
                  ↓
hand-over + investigation + record/reason + proportionality
                  ↓
Section 6 → previous Central sanction before prosecution

notification shrinks/expires ≠ AFSPA repeal
AFSPA power ≠ immunity from investigation
```

#### Explanation

AFSPA is activated territorially through a section 3 disturbed-area notification. Section 4 supplies specified powers; section 6 requires previous Central sanction before prosecution for acts done or purportedly done under the Act. A withdrawn notification ends operation in that area but does not repeal the statute.

*Naga People's Movement of Human Rights* (1997) upheld AFSPA while making operational safeguards binding. *EEVFAM* (2016) held that there is no absolute immunity for alleged excessive or retaliatory force and that allegations can be investigated. Operational necessity and accountability must therefore be analysed together.

As of 27 September 2026, notifications effective through 30 September covered: **Nagaland**—the whole of Dimapur, Niuland, Chumoukedima, Mon, Kiphire, Noklak, Phek, Peren and Meluri plus specified police-station areas in five other districts; **Arunachal Pradesh**—Tirap, Changlang and Longding plus Namsai, Mahadevpur and Chowkham police-station areas in Namsai district; **Manipur**—the State except 13 police-station areas: Imphal, Lamphel, City, Singjamei, Patsoi and Wangoi (Imphal West); Porompat, Heingang and Irilbung (Imphal East); Thoubal; Bishnupur and Nambol; and Kakching. The exact footprint must be rechecked after expiry.

Union escalation must also be distinguished. Deployment under Union List Entry 2A is aid to civil power while the State government remains; Article 356 concerns President's Rule and supersession of the State executive. A current Manipur constitutional-status claim requires a dated Gazette or official source.

The Irom Sharmila hunger strike is a historical legitimacy illustration of sustained civil opposition to AFSPA. It establishes the depth of the accountability debate, not the legal invalidity of the Act.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** AFSPA is a notified operational power bounded by place, time, necessity and accountability; it is neither ordinary policing nor a charter of impunity.

**Must-write keywords:** sections 3, 4, 6; disturbed area; previous sanction; minimum necessary force; investigation; notification/repeal distinction.

### SESSION 11 — CORE — Institutions: State police, Assam Rifles, NEC and DoNER

#### Visual

| Institution | Primary relevance | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| State police | local intelligence, public order, investigation, first response | cannot be replaced by episodic central deployment |
| Assam Rifles | India–Myanmar border and counter-insurgency roles | dual-task pressure raises “one force, one mandate” debate |
| Central forces/Army | aid to civil power and specialised operations | civil authority and legal accountability remain |
| North Eastern Council | statutory regional planning/coordination body, Act of 1971; restructured 2002 | development body, not a ceasefire monitor or police force |
| DoNER | department created 2001; ministry from 2004 | regional development coordination, not insurgent negotiation by itself |

#### Explanation

The Assam Rifles case illustrates mandate overload: border guarding and counter-insurgency draw on different deployment patterns, training and accountability. Historical battalion splits in secondary material should not be repeated as current figures without official confirmation.

Development institutions can reduce isolation and improve connectivity, but a budgetary arrangement or project announcement is not implementation. The canonical source records the 10% Gross Budgetary Support earmark, Special Category status and the Non-Lapsable Central Pool of Resources as book-period fiscal mechanisms; their present form must be checked before current use. The Vibrant Villages Programme, announced in 2022, is a cross-owned border-development example rather than a peace accord. Peace talks remain a political-security function; regional planning bodies support the conditions in which settlements can endure.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** The institutional question is not how many agencies exist, but whether each has one clear role, interoperable hand-offs and accountable delivery.

**Must-write keywords:** State-police primacy; Assam Rifles; dual mandate; NEC Act 1971; 2002 restructuring; DoNER 2001/2004.

### SESSION 12 — CORE SYNTHESIS — Current security picture and durable peace

#### Visual

```text
lower violence / surrender / disbandment
                ↓ useful security indicators
accord implementation + institutional functioning
                ↓ political indicators
return, rehabilitation, inter-community trust and rights
                ↓ social indicators
ordinary policing and open civic life without exceptional coercion
                ↓ durable peace
```

#### Explanation

MHA's April 2026 update reports that, compared with 2014, insurgency incidents in 2025 were lower by 82%, security-force casualties by 90% and civilian deaths by 97%; it also lists accord-related surrender/disbandment milestones. These are significant regional indicators but do not prove which single measure caused the change or that every State, faction or ethnic relationship is settled.

The safest current formulation is differentiated: several conflicts have moved to signed settlement and implementation; the Naga process has a framework without a final settlement in the official material checked; Manipur's UPF/KNO instrument reached its stated expiry on 3 September 2026 without a later renewal located; AFSPA coverage remained time- and area-specific through 30 September 2026; FMR implementation status remains legally unresolved in the sources checked.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Normalcy is not a regional average; it is the State- and community-specific ability to conduct politics, movement, policing and development through ordinary law.

**Must-write keywords:** differentiated status; attributed metric; ordinary law; implementation; reconciliation; residual risk; recheck date.

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

### A1. Accommodation and coercion are parallel tracks

Negotiation with a faction that abjures violence can coexist with targeted action against another faction continuing coercion. A sequential “force first, talks later” model misses the simultaneous, group-specific character of the regional strategy.

### A2. Identity-driven and resource-driven conflicts require different tools

North-East insurgencies are predominantly identity, territory, autonomy and representation disputes. Development matters, but political-structural accommodation has greater causal fit than treating the region as an LWE-style resource conflict.

### A3. The instrument rung determines the claim

SoO manages violence; a framework records principles; an MoS specifies commitments; implementation changes institutions and behaviour. The same State can host all four conditions across different groups. “Peace process progress” must therefore be disaggregated.

### A4. Settlement externalities

Territorial or council powers granted to one community can create fear among neighbouring or internal minorities. Boundary commissions, representation guarantees, resource-sharing rules and accessible review reduce the risk that one settlement becomes another conflict's grievance.

### A5. AFSPA's paired constitutional problem

Deployed forces need effective powers, yet coercive power without investigation and remedy erodes legitimacy. Section 6 sanction is a prosecution filter, not a prohibition on investigation. Notification renewal should be based on area-specific necessity and publicly intelligible reasoning.

### A6. Border regimes are internal-security-dependent variables

FMR demonstrates a two-way relation: porous movement can facilitate insurgency, while internal ethnic conflict can drive tighter border policy. The answer should evaluate security gain, administrative feasibility and kinship/livelihood cost together.

### A7. “One force, one mandate” and Assam Rifles

Border guarding requires persistent line presence; counter-insurgency requires mobile area operations. A dual mandate can dilute both. Reform options include clearer prioritisation, resources and command protocols or reassignment—but current force numbers require official verification.

### A8. Implementation dashboard

Track: verified ceasefire compliance; arms/camp management; law/council creation; fiscal release and expenditure; rehabilitation; return/displacement; political participation; inter-community incidents; ordinary policing; complaint and remedy. A signature count is not a dashboard.

### A9. Current-status discipline

- UPF/KNO: latest located SoO validity ended 3 September 2026; subsequent status unresolved.
- AFSPA: cited notifications expire 30 September 2026 unless withdrawn earlier; recheck before reuse.
- FMR: decision announcement verified; complete operative legal status not established.
- Accord outcomes: use MHA's dated reported milestones; do not infer causation from regional decline alone.

### A10. Advanced answer method

1. Disaggregate State, group, faction and demand.
2. Identify historical, identity, border and governance causes.
3. Select constitutional, ceasefire, settlement, security and development tools.
4. Place each peace instrument on the correct rung.
5. State current expiry/implementation status and source date.
6. Test third-party effects, AFSPA accountability and border-community costs.
7. Conclude with reconciliation and ordinary constitutional governance.

### Qualified conclusion

Durable peace in the North-East is negotiated and administered group by group.
