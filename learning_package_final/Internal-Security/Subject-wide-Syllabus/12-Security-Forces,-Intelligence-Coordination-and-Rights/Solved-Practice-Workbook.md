# Security Forces, Intelligence Coordination and Rights — Solved Practice Workbook

## Evidence and use note

This workbook is controlled by Topic 12’s canonical Basic and Advanced owners, the Internal
Security Master Framework and syllabus map, audited PYQ routing, local official UPSC papers, the
Constitution and force statutes, the NIA and AFSPA Acts, MHA Annual Report 2024–25, current
March 2026 AFSPA notifications and the cited Supreme Court judgments.

The **40 original MCQs** below are distinct and use direct, statement, matching, sequence,
scenario, evidence-status and institutional-boundary formats. Correct options rotate
`A → B → C → D` exactly ten times. Every option is explained.

The objective PYQs are separate from the 40 originals. The 2023 Series-A answer is from the
UPSC-issued key scan and is independently supported by MHA material. The locally held 2026
Series-A key is **provisional**, not an official final UPSC key.

## BASIC MCQS / REMEDIATION

### Q1. [Constitutional allocation] Which statement most accurately describes India’s internal-security division of responsibility?

A. Public order and police are State subjects; Union List Entry 2A permits Union-force deployment
in aid of civil power.
B. Public order is a Union subject, while police is concurrently administered by Union and State
governments in each district.
C. Article 355 transfers ordinary police administration to the Union whenever a State reports an
internal disturbance requiring assistance.
D. Union List Entry 8 requires central-intelligence clearance before a State police force may
investigate an inter-State security offence.

**Answer: A.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — correct:** State List Entries 1–2 place public order and police with States; Entry 2A
  enables Union-force deployment in aid of civil power.
- **B — incorrect:** Neither allocation is stated correctly.
- **C — incorrect:** Article 355 creates a Union duty; it does not automatically transfer police
  administration.
- **D — incorrect:** Entry 8 concerns the Central Bureau of Intelligence and Investigation; it is
  not a universal clearance clause.

**Question-specific trap:** “Union responsibility” does not mean “Union monopoly”.

### Q2. [Article 33] What is the most accurate effect of Article 33?

A. It directly authorises Union forces to arrest civilians whenever they are deployed in aid of
civil power.
B. It lets Parliament restrict specified personnel’s fundamental rights by law to secure duty and
discipline.
C. It lets State legislatures suspend Article 21 for residents of every notified disturbed area.
D. It converts personnel of public-order and intelligence organisations into members of the
military services.

**Answer: B.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Operational powers require a separate law; Article 33 concerns service
  personnel’s rights.
- **B — correct:** This is Article 33’s constitutional function.
- **C — incorrect:** Article 21 is not suspended through Article 33 or an AFSPA notification.
- **D — incorrect:** Rights restrictions do not alter administrative or military classification.

**Question-specific trap:** a discipline provision is not a coercive-power provision.

### Q3. [Administrative taxonomy] Why is it unsafe to assert that “CAPF” always denotes one fixed seven-force legal category?

A. The Constitution lets each State define which Union forces count as CAPFs within its
territory.
B. CAPFs exist solely under executive orders and therefore have no force-specific parliamentary
statutes.
C. MHA sources use different groupings; each force should be identified by statute, control and
task.
D. CAPF is the constitutional category covering State Armed Police and India Reserve Battalions
across India.

**Answer: C.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** “CAPF” is not a State-wise constitutional definition.
- **B — incorrect:** The named forces have force-specific statutes.
- **C — correct:** MHA’s older web grouping and Annual Report 2024–25 classify AR/NSG
  differently; source and date therefore matter.
- **D — incorrect:** State armed units are not CAPFs.

**Question-specific trap:** administrative nomenclature can vary even when each force’s legal
identity remains stable.

### Q4. [State Armed Police] Which proposition is correct?

A. A State Armed Police battalion becomes a CAPF whenever a Central scheme funds its raising or
equipment.
B. State Armed Police acquire nationwide jurisdiction over NIA Scheduled Offences whenever sent
outside their home district.
C. India Reserve Battalions are State-recruited units placed permanently under Ministry of
Defence operational command.
D. State Armed Police and reserve units provide armed support under State control without
becoming central forces.

**Answer: D.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Financial assistance does not transfer constitutional or command ownership.
- **B — incorrect:** Their powers arise under applicable State and criminal law, not a nationwide
  NIA mandate.
- **C — incorrect:** India Reserve Battalions are raised within State/UT structures.
- **D — correct:** Their armed role does not change their State character.

**Question-specific trap:** equipment and function do not determine constitutional ownership.

### Q5. [Border-force matching] Which set is correctly matched?

A. BSF—Pakistan and Bangladesh; ITBP—China; SSB—Nepal and Bhutan.
B. BSF—China and Myanmar; ITBP—Nepal and Bhutan; SSB—Pakistan.
C. BSF—Nepal and Bhutan; ITBP—Pakistan and Bangladesh; SSB—China.
D. BSF—Myanmar and Bangladesh; ITBP—Nepal and Bhutan; SSB—China.

**Answer: A.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — correct:** It matches the standard border-guarding allocation.
- **B — incorrect:** China belongs with ITBP, Myanmar with Assam Rifles and Nepal/Bhutan with SSB.
- **C — incorrect:** SSB, ITBP and BSF have been interchanged.
- **D — incorrect:** Myanmar belongs with Assam Rifles, Nepal/Bhutan with SSB and China with ITBP.

**Question-specific trap:** remember the border, then distinguish border guarding from military
defence.

### Q6. [Specialised components] Which statement about RAF and CoBRA is correct?

A. Both are independent CAPFs, each constituted and regulated through its own parliamentary
statute.
B. Both are CRPF components: RAF addresses riot/public-order contingencies and CoBRA
LWE/insurgency tasks.
C. RAF belongs to State Armed Police, while CoBRA is a specialised combat formation of the Army.
D. Both are operational arms of the Intelligence Bureau used for inter-State intelligence
sharing.

**Answer: B.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** They are not separate statutory CAPFs.
- **B — correct:** MHA Annual Report 2024–25 identifies both as specialised CRPF wings.
- **C — incorrect:** Neither classification is correct.
- **D — incorrect:** They are operational units, not intelligence platforms.

**Question-specific trap:** a distinctive name or uniform does not prove institutional
independence.

### Q7. [CISF mandate] Which deployment best fits CISF’s primary institutional role?

A. Territorial investigation of all offences reported within an industrial township and its
adjoining district.
B. Overseas collection and strategic assessment concerning threats to Indian industrial
interests.
C. Security of a nuclear installation, major airport or other vital establishment under the CISF
framework.
D. Trial and adjudication of sabotage offences arising inside a protected critical
installation.

**Answer: C.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Territorial crime investigation belongs to the competent police.
- **B — incorrect:** External intelligence is not CISF’s mandate.
- **C — correct:** Installation and critical-infrastructure security is CISF’s defining role.
- **D — incorrect:** Courts adjudicate; CISF does not.

**Question-specific trap:** guarding an installation and investigating an offence at that
installation are different functions.

### Q8. [Assam Rifles] Which description is accurate?

A. North-Eastern States jointly administer it, while their police chiefs rotate operational
command.
B. The Ministry of Defence administers it, while Railways exercises operational command.
C. MHA administers it, ITBP commands it, and its primary role is guarding the India–China
frontier.
D. MHA administers it; Army operational control supports Indo-Myanmar border guarding and
counter-insurgency.

**Answer: D.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** It is a Union force, not a consortium under rotating State-police command.
- **B — incorrect:** MHA administers Assam Rifles; Railways has no operational-command role.
- **C — incorrect:** Its operational control is with the Army, and its border role concerns
  Indo-Myanmar, not India–China.
- **D — correct:** It captures the documented dual arrangement and dual mandate.

**Question-specific trap:** dual control is not dual sovereignty; identify administrative and
operational chains separately.

### Q9. [Railway security] Which distinction is correct?

A. RPF provides statutory railway security; GRP, as State police, handles ordinary policing and
crime investigation.
B. GRP is a Union armed force under Railways, while RPF is assigned to State police cadres.
C. RPF alone investigates every cognizable offence on railway premises, irrespective of State
police jurisdiction.
D. RPF and GRP are one statutory force operating under different names in different railway
zones.

**Answer: A.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — correct:** It preserves the statutory RPF role and State-police character of GRP.
- **B — incorrect:** The classifications are reversed.
- **C — incorrect:** RPF security functions do not erase police jurisdiction.
- **D — incorrect:** They are institutionally distinct.

**Question-specific trap:** protection of railway property/passengers is not the same as complete
territorial policing.

### Q10. [Maritime division of labour] Which allocation is most accurate?

on shore.

**Answer: B.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** It reverses the military/law-enforcement distinction.
- **B — correct:** It states the three-layer allocation.
- **C — incorrect:** Criminal jurisdiction does not confer naval command.
- **D — incorrect:** Coast Guard and Navy are separate MoD armed services/forces; neither is an MHA
  CAPF.

**Question-specific trap:** one maritime incident can engage three institutions without merging
their powers.

### Q11. [NSG and SPG] Which comparison is correct?

A. NSG and SPG are interchangeable Union units available for ordinary district law-and-order
deployment.
B. SPG investigates terrorist offences, while NSG conducts their prosecution before designated
Special Courts.
C. NSG handles specialised counter-terror/anti-hijack response; SPG provides statutory proximate
security.
D. Both are State Armed Police formations placed temporarily under Union operational control.

**Answer: C.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Both have narrow specialised roles.
- **B — incorrect:** Investigation/prosecution belongs to police/NIA and prosecutors/courts.
- **C — correct:** It states their distinct statutory purposes.
- **D — incorrect:** Both are Union organisations.

**Question-specific trap:** “security of a person” and “counter-terror response” are not the same
mandate.

### Q12. [Intelligence cycle] Which sequence is institutionally sound?

A. Dissemination → operational action → collection → source grading → judicial conviction →
feedback.
B. Collection → prosecution → priority setting → intelligence assessment → field verification →
trial.
C. Arrest → raw reporting → analysis → judicial review → dissemination → renewed tasking.
D. Tasking → collection → collation → analysis → dissemination/action → feedback and re-tasking.

**Answer: D.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Conviction is a judicial outcome, not an intelligence-cycle stage.
- **B — incorrect:** Prosecution cannot precede tasking and collection.
- **C — incorrect:** Arrest is not the starting point of intelligence production.
- **D — correct:** It preserves the full iterative cycle.

**Question-specific trap:** intelligence is a feedback loop, not a one-way data dump.

### Q13. [Information versus intelligence] Which statement is correct?

A. Information becomes intelligence after corroboration, contextual analysis and confidence
assessment.
B. Any report from a classified source is automatically admissible evidence.
C. Intelligence is simply a larger volume of unfiltered data.
D. An intelligence assessment is a judicial finding because it has already been analysed.

**Answer: A.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — correct:** Analysis and source evaluation distinguish intelligence from raw reporting.
- **B — incorrect:** Classification does not establish relevance, authenticity or admissibility.
- **C — incorrect:** Volume without validation can increase noise.
- **D — incorrect:** Courts, not intelligence agencies, determine guilt.

**Question-specific trap:** assessment improves decision value, not evidentiary status.

### Q14. [IB–R&AW boundary] Which pairing is most accurate?

A. IB handles external military intelligence, while R&AW directs State police investigation of
domestic offences.
B. IB covers domestic intelligence and R&AW external intelligence; neither is a police or
prosecution agency.
C. IB prosecutes NIA cases, while R&AW administers NATGRID access for State police.
D. IB guards international borders, while R&AW protects designated critical installations.

**Answer: B.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** It misstates both domains.
- **B — correct:** It captures the public functional distinction without inventing coercive powers.
- **C — incorrect:** Special Courts/prosecution and NATGRID are separate functions.
- **D — incorrect:** Those tasks belong to named forces, not these intelligence agencies.

**Question-specific trap:** intelligence remit does not imply police powers.

### Q15. [NTRO boundary] Which is the safest evidence-based statement?

agency.

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** No such police statute or general arrest power is established.
- **B — incorrect:** It is not a court.
- **C — correct:** It states the public role and preserves the information boundary.
- **D — incorrect:** NATGRID and NTRO are different institutions.

**Question-specific trap:** classified operational detail is not a licence to fill gaps from
memory.

### Q16. [DIA boundary] Which statement is correct?

A. DIA is the civilian prosecution authority for offences alleged against members of the armed

### Q17. [MAC/SMAC] What is the principal institutional purpose of MAC and SMAC?

A. Continuous intelligence sharing and coordination at central and State levels.
B. Taking cognizance of Scheduled Offences and conducting trial.
C. Maintaining the sole national database of admissible criminal evidence.
D. Replacing State Special Branches and district police chains of command.

**Answer: A.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — correct:** MHA reports MAC centrally and SMAC at State level as intelligence-sharing
  mechanisms.
- **B — incorrect:** Courts take cognizance and try cases.
- **C — incorrect:** They are not evidence repositories with exclusive legal status.
- **D — incorrect:** They coordinate; they do not abolish State institutions.

**Question-specific trap:** sharing is neither investigation nor command.

### Q18. [NATGRID] Which statement is accurate as per MHA Annual Report 2024–25?

A. NATGRID independently gathers human intelligence and arrests persons selected through its
analytical system.
B. NATGRID links approved users to designated data providers; it is a connectivity platform, not
an investigating agency.

restrictions.

- **C — incorrect:** Access is for approved users, not unrestricted.
- **D — incorrect:** MAC/SMAC continue to perform intelligence-sharing functions.

**Question-specific trap:** a connected platform is not a coercive agency.

### Q19. [NIA jurisdiction] Which statement best states NIA’s legal scope?

- **C — correct:** It states both the source and limit of NIA’s powers.
- **D — incorrect:** Special Courts adjudicate and sentence.

**Question-specific trap:** “national agency” does not mean general federal police jurisdiction.

### Q20. [State police and NIA] A State police station registers information disclosing a possible Scheduled Offence. Which sequence is correct?

A. State police must suspend every investigative step while the Central Government decides
whether NIA should enter.
B. MAC assumes the investigation automatically because the information concerns a possible
national-security offence.

D. State police continue until NIA takes up the case; State power otherwise remains subject to
the Act.

**Answer: D.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** NIA Act section 6(7) requires continuation until NIA takes up the case.
- **B — incorrect:** MAC shares intelligence; it does not investigate.
- **C — incorrect:** NATGRID neither entrusts cases nor determines courts.
- **D — correct:** It reflects sections 6 and 10 of the NIA Act.

**Question-specific trap:** central review of a case does not create an investigative vacuum.

### Q21. [Proof chain] Which sequence best protects both security and fair trial?

A. Intelligence lead → lawful verification/FIR → forensic custody → charge-sheet → prosecution →
court verdict.
B. Intelligence lead → public accusation → detention order → conviction → later collection of
supporting material.
C. Database match → automatic charge-sheet → Special Court cognizance → conviction without
witness testing.
D. Operational recovery → media disclosure → presumption of guilt → prosecutorial confirmation
without independent proof.

**Answer: A.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — correct:** It keeps intelligence, investigation, prosecution and adjudication in lawful
  order.
- **B — incorrect:** It reverses proof and verdict.
- **C — incorrect:** A database match requires verification and admissible evidence.
- **D — incorrect:** Public or agency assertion cannot replace trial.

**Question-specific trap:** official action has a procedural status; it is not self-proving guilt.

### Q22. [Multi-agency command] Which arrangement is most likely to reduce both operational and evidentiary failure?

hand-offs.

hands.

**Answer: B.**

**Option-by-option explanation:**
- **A — incorrect:** Parallel orders and records create gaps and duplication.

- **C — incorrect:** Sharing platforms have no such universal command power.
- **D — incorrect:** Late ownership weakens chain of custody.

**Question-specific trap:** “joint” should describe coordination, not dissolved responsibility.

### PYQ P4 — 2019 GS-III Q19 — APPLICATION / FULL; TOPIC 02 PRIMARY

**Official wording:** “Indian Government has recently strengthened the anti-terrorism laws by
amending the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act (UAPA), 1967 and the NIA Act. Analyze the
changes in the context of prevailing security environment while discussing the scope and reasons
for opposing the UAPA by human rights organizations.” *(Answer in 250 words; 15 marks.)*

**Demand:** explain both 2019 amendments, justify their security logic and evaluate the rights
critique.

**Model answer:**

The 2019 amendments responded to networked terrorism whose organisers, finance, digital links and
operations may cross organisations and borders.

The UAPA amendment empowered the Central Government to designate an **individual**, not only an
organisation, as a terrorist in the Fourth Schedule. It also enabled NIA officers of Inspector
rank or above to investigate UAPA cases and, in NIA-investigated cases, shifted approval for
seizure/attachment of property from the State DGP to the Director General, NIA. The NIA amendment
extended the Act to specified offences committed outside India affecting Indian citizens or
Indian interests, expanded the Schedule to specified human-trafficking, counterfeit-currency,
prohibited-arms, cyber-terrorism and Explosive Substances Act offences.

The security case is speed, individual accountability, interstate reach and a clearer response to
offences that use transnational networks. Yet rights organisations oppose the risk of executive
designation before conviction, reputational and practical consequences, broad statutory language,
stringent bail effects, prolonged process, federal concerns and possible misuse against dissent.

The answer is neither impunity nor indiscriminate power: reasoned designation, prompt disclosure
of grounds compatible with security, effective review/de-listing, strict Scheduled-Offence
thresholds, judicial scrutiny, time-bound investigation and compensation/accountability for abuse.
Designation is not conviction; intelligence is not evidence; and NIA jurisdiction is not blanket
federal control over all crime.

**Why this earns marks:** it identifies the amendments, presents both security and liberty cases
and supplies enforceable safeguards.

### PYQ P5 — 2024 GS-III Q19 — APPLICATION / FULL; TOPIC 06 PRIMARY

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Distinguish the Armed Forces, Central Armed Police Forces, State Armed Police and
specialised statutory forces in India. Why does the distinction matter operationally?
*(Answer in 150 words.)*

**Execution plan:** category → examples/legal source → role → operational consequence.

**Model answer:**

India’s security bodies differ by constitutional location, statute and task. The Army, Navy and

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Explain the intelligence cycle and distinguish the roles of IB, R&AW, NTRO, DIA,

**Execution plan:** cycle → body map → legal firewall → conclusion.

**Model answer:**

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** “Unity of effort, not institutional merger, is the central requirement of India’s
internal-security command architecture.” Discuss. *(Answer in 250 words.)*

**Execution plan:** constitutional base → coordination problem → operating design → safeguards →
verdict.

**Model answer:**

India’s command architecture is necessarily plural. Public order and police belong to States,
while the Union controls defence, central agencies and forces that may aid civil power. Threats,
however, cross districts, States, borders, digital networks and financial systems. The objective
must therefore be unity of effort without erasing lawful ownership.

Fragmentation creates delayed intelligence, duplicated surveillance, incompatible communications,
uncertain field command and broken evidence custody. Assam Rifles’ administrative/operational
split, Army–border-force interfaces and multi-agency counter-terror operations show that multiple
chains may be necessary but impose a coordination cost.

An effective design assigns a lead **by function**: MAC/SMAC for sharing; the competent civil
authority and police for public order; the appropriate force for protection/intervention; State
police or NIA for investigation; and the prosecutor/court for trial. Written tasking, liaison
officers, a common operating picture, interoperable secure communications and pre-operation
evidence protocols should connect these functions. State police must continue local investigation
until a lawful NIA takeover, preventing a responsibility gap.

Centralisation is not a universal cure. The NCTC controversy showed the federal cost of combining
intelligence and coercive powers without accepted safeguards. Reform should instead strengthen
State capacity, MAC/SMAC feedback, lawful data access, joint exercises, after-action review and
rights accountability.

Unity of command is useful within an operation; constitutional diversity remains outside it.

**Why this earns marks:** it directly evaluates merger versus coordination and supplies an
executable operating design.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Police reform in India is an internal-security requirement as much as a governance
requirement. Examine with reference to major commissions and *Prakash Singh*.
*(Answer in 250 words.)*

**Execution plan:** why police matter → reform genealogy → seven directions → implementation gap
→ measured way forward.

**Model answer:**

State police are the first responders, local intelligence network, scene preservers, investigators
and witness interface for almost every internal-security threat. Weak tenure, skills, forensics or
accountability therefore affects both public order and national-security case quality.

The National Police Commission (1977–81) placed insulation, accountability and professional
investigation on the reform agenda. The Ribeiro Committee examined implementation; the
Padmanabhaiah Committee stressed modernisation, training, community relations and investigation;
the Malimath Committee connected investigation to prosecution, victims, witnesses and trial; and
the Soli Sorabjee Committee produced the Model Police Act, 2006.

In *Prakash Singh* (2006), the Supreme Court directed State Security Commissions; merit-based DGP
selection and minimum tenure; tenure for key field officers; separation of investigation from law
and order; Police Establishment Boards; State/district Police Complaints Authorities; and a
National Security Commission for central police leadership questions.

Implementation remains uneven because policing is a State subject, reforms affect political and
administrative control, and institutions can exist without independence or resources. MHA Annual
Report 2024–25 records that BPR&D was preparing a new draft Model Police Bill, not that a new
national law had been enacted.

States should legislate functional safeguards, fund investigators/forensics/prosecution, publish
tenure and complaint outcomes, train for rights and technology, and measure trust and trial
quality—not merely arrests.

**Why this earns marks:** it connects reform history, binding directions, implementation and
security outcomes.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Critically examine AFSPA’s powers, notification system and accountability safeguards.
How can necessity and proportionality be operationalised without disabling legitimate security
action? *(Answer in 300 words.)*

**Execution plan:** legal trigger → powers → safeguards/jurisprudence → current map → reforms →
balanced conclusion.

**Model answer:**

AFSPA is an exceptional aid-to-civil-power framework, not a permanent transfer of civil
government. Under section 3 of the 1958 Act, the Governor, Administrator or Central Government
may notify the whole or part of the relevant State/UT as disturbed when armed-force assistance is
considered necessary.

Section 4 permits specified ranks, within that notified area, to use force after the warning
required by clause (a) in the defined public-order situation; destroy specified arms positions;
arrest without warrant on the statutory basis; and enter/search for stated purposes. Section 5
requires hand-over to the nearest police station with the least possible delay and a circumstances
report. Section 6 requires prior Central sanction for specified proceedings. Jammu and Kashmir
has a separate 1990 Act, whose sanction provision is section 7.

These powers are bounded. *Naga People’s Movement* upheld the Act but required review of disturbed
area continuation at least every six months and strict observance of operational Dos and Don’ts.
*EEVFAM* rejected absolute immunity or a licence to kill and required thorough inquiry into
alleged excessive or retaliatory force causing death. Article 21, judicial review, police
investigation, court process and command discipline continue. PHRA section 19 gives NHRC a
narrower armed-forces procedure, which remains an accountability limitation.

As of 28 September 2026, March notifications cover specified parts of Assam, Arunachal Pradesh,
Manipur and Nagaland for six months from 1 April; they require rechecking after 30 September.

Operational proportionality needs threat-specific written orders, graduated force, warning and
precaution where feasible, medical aid, body/scene records, prompt police hand-over, independent
death/injury inquiry, reasoned sanction decisions and periodic territorial review. Compensation,
discipline and prosecution must follow proved abuse.

Thus the defensible balance is effective force under reviewable law: exceptional power should
shrink with necessity and remain traceable at every use.

**Why this earns marks:** it states exact provisions, case-law controls, current temporal status
and implementable safeguards.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Design a reform framework that combines intelligence technology, professional

arrest-and-deployment bias. *(Answer in 300 words.)*

**Execution plan:** objective → technology → people/process → oversight/community → dashboard →
qualification.

**Model answer:**

The objective is not maximum surveillance or force presence, but timely prevention and lawful
case resolution with public confidence.

**Technology:** MAC/SMAC should transmit time-stamped, graded warnings with acknowledgement and
action feedback. NATGRID and other interoperable systems need purpose limitation, role-based

NHRC/SHRC, functioning Police Complaints Authorities, legislative/budget scrutiny and protected
audit of intelligence systems. Community liaison, accessible complaints, witness protection,
non-discriminatory verification and reasoned post-operation communication encourage voluntary
reporting without turning residents into coerced informants.

**Dashboard:** retain input/activity figures—staff, training, alerts, searches and arrests—but add
warning-to-action time, false-positive correction, inter-agency hand-off time, forensic
turnaround, lawful charge-sheet quality, trial completion and appeal sustainability. Security
outcomes should track prevented attacks, reduced recurrence and network replacement; rights
outcomes should track custodial harm, unlawful-force findings, complaint disposal, remedy and
trust.

Each indicator needs a denominator and time period. Fewer complaints may mean safer policing or
fear, while a high conviction percentage among concluded trials says nothing by itself about all
registered cases. Balanced measurement aligns capability with legality and learning.

**Why this earns marks:** it integrates tools, personnel, institutions, community and a
methodologically sound outcome framework.
