### Evidence limits that govern this package

1. **Administrative labels are not constitutional categories.** “CAPF”, “Central Police
   Organisation”, “paramilitary” and “armed force of the Union” do not have identical scope.
2. **Intelligence mandate is not coercive power.** No arrest, search, seizure, investigation or
   prosecution power is attributed to IB, R&AW, NTRO, DIA, MAC, SMAC or NATGRID without a
   public legal source.
3. **A current notification is not permanent law.** The AFSPA map below is date-stamped.
4. **A lead is not proof.** Intelligence, FIR, arrest, seizure, charge-sheet, trial and conviction
   are separate stages.
5. **Activity is not outcome.** Meetings, deployments, alerts, searches and arrests must not be
   reported as convictions, reduced violence or public trust.

## BASIC LEARNING SESSION

### Master analytical map

```text
CONSTITUTIONAL ALLOCATION
State: public order + police                 Union: defence + central intelligence
                         \                   /
                          \                 /
                           COOPERATIVE SUPPORT
                 armed forces/CAPFs in aid of civil power
                                  │
                                  ▼
                    FUNCTION-SPECIFIC ARCHITECTURE
     intelligence → sharing/data → prevention/response → investigation
                                  → prosecution → adjudication
                                  │
                                  ▼
                         RIGHTS AND LEGITIMACY
 legality → necessity → proportionality → record → review → remedy
                                  │
                                  ▼
                           MEASURED OUTCOMES
 prevention + lawful case quality + fair trial + lower harm + public trust
```

The architecture succeeds only when the correct institution performs the correct function under
the correct law and transfers responsibility without losing intelligence, evidence or
accountability.

### SESSION 1 — Constitutional allocation and the vocabulary trap

#### Visual first

| Constitutional location | What it allocates | Exam consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **State List Entry 1** | Public order, subject to the Union-force exclusion | State executive and police remain the ordinary first line |
| **State List Entry 2** | Police, including railway and village police | Investigation and local law enforcement are primarily State functions |
| **Union List Entries 1–2** | Defence and Union armed forces | Army, Navy and Air Force are national-defence institutions |
| **Union List Entry 2A** | Deployment of a Union armed force/controlled force in a State in aid of civil power | Central deployment assists; it does not erase civil authority |
| **Union List Entry 8** | Central Bureau of Intelligence and Investigation | Constitutional competence exists, but each body still needs its actual legal/executive basis |
| **Article 355** | Union duty to protect States against external aggression and internal disturbance | Supports Union responsibility; not a blank cheque to displace federal limits |
| **Article 33** | Parliament may restrict specified service personnel’s Part III rights for duty and discipline | It concerns personnel discipline, not an operational power over civilians |

#### The vocabulary ladder

```text
ARMED FORCES (ordinary usage)
Army | Navy | Air Force
        │ defence role; Union control
        ▼
CENTRAL ARMED POLICE / CENTRAL SECURITY FORCES
BSF | CISF | CRPF | ITBP | SSB | source-dependent AR/NSG grouping
        │ border / internal-security aid / installation / specialist tasks
        ▼
STATE POLICE
civil police | investigation | traffic | special branches
        │
        └─ STATE ARMED POLICE / RESERVE / INDIA RESERVE BATTALIONS
           armed support under State control; not CAPFs
        ▼
SPECIALISED STATUTORY FORCES
NSG | SPG | RPF | Coast Guard
different parent ministries, laws and missions
```

**Core explanation:** “CAPF” is an administrative umbrella, not a phrase in the Constitution.
Several force Acts use the legal expression **armed force of the Union**, but that does not turn
the force into the Army, Navy or Air Force. Conversely, a State Armed Police battalion remains
part of the State policing structure even when organised and equipped for armed contingencies.

**Current nomenclature caution:** the MHA web page, last updated 3 April 2019, lists seven names
under CAPFs. The later MHA Annual Report 2024–25, section 7.23, speaks of five CAPFs, one CPMF
(Assam Rifles) and NSG as a specialised strike force. In an exam, write the **name, statute,
administrative home and task** instead of staking the answer on a universal count.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** India’s security architecture is constitutionally federal and
> functionally networked; labels matter less than lawful mandate, civil primacy and a clean hand-off.

**Must-write keywords:** State List Entries 1–2 · Union List Entries 1–2A and 8 · Article 355 ·
Article 33 · aid of civil power · civil primacy · administrative taxonomy.

**Paragraph build:** **Claim**—internal security is not constitutionally centralised.
**Evidence**—public order and police are State subjects, while Entry 2A permits Union forces in aid
of civil power and Article 355 imposes a Union duty. **Analysis**—effective response therefore
requires support and coordination rather than institutional substitution. **Qualification**—a
specific central statute, such as the NIA Act, may create defined concurrent powers.

**Trap check:** Article 355 is a duty; Article 33 is a personnel-rights provision; Entry 2A is a
deployment competence. None is a general police statute.

### SESSION 2 — Armed Forces, CAPFs and their statutory mandates

#### Visual first: legal-mandate matrix

| Force | Public legal basis | Administrative/operational location | Primary exam fingerprint | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Army | Army Act, 1950; Union defence entries | Ministry of Defence | external defence; may aid civil power | not ordinary crime investigator |
| Navy | Navy Act, 1957 | Ministry of Defence | naval defence and sea control | not coastal police |
| Air Force | Air Force Act, 1950 | Ministry of Defence | air defence and air power | not civil aviation police |
| BSF | BSF Act, 1968 | MHA | Pakistan/Bangladesh border guarding; aid duties | not State police |
| ITBP | ITBP Force Act, 1992 | MHA | India–China frontier/high-altitude guarding | Army retains military-defence role |
| SSB | SSB Act, 2007 | MHA | Nepal and Bhutan borders | open-border policing still needs State/customs coordination |
| CRPF | CRPF Act, 1949 | MHA | internal-security and public-order assistance | State police retain ordinary legal primacy |
| CISF | CISF Act, 1968 | MHA | critical/vital installation security | not a general territorial police |
| Assam Rifles | Assam Rifles Act, 2006 | MHA administration; Army operational control | Indo-Myanmar border and North-East counter-insurgency | dual control needs explicit coordination |
| NSG | NSG Act, 1986 | MHA | specialised counter-terror and anti-hijack response | not routine law-and-order deployment |

The MHA report identifies **RAF** and **CoBRA** as specialised wings of CRPF. They are not
additional CAPFs. A force may receive election, riot-control, counter-insurgency or disaster
support tasks without losing its statutory identity.

#### “Who guards what?” recall rail

```text
Pakistan + Bangladesh → BSF
China frontier        → ITBP
Nepal + Bhutan        → SSB
Myanmar               → Assam Rifles
critical installations→ CISF
internal-security aid → CRPF
CT/anti-hijack crisis → NSG
```

**Command lesson:** border guarding, military defence, public order and criminal investigation
can occur in the same theatre, but each remains a different function. The answer must identify
who leads each function and how information/evidence passes between them.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** A crowded theatre does not justify a blurred mandate: unity of effort
> requires differentiated roles, not institutional sameness.

**Must-write keywords:** enabling Act · border guarding · aid to civil power · statutory identity ·
dual control · specialised wing · operational deconfliction.

**Paragraph build:** **Claim**—force deployment should follow the problem’s legal and geographic
character. **Evidence**—BSF, ITBP, SSB and Assam Rifles have distinct frontier assignments, while
CRPF assists internal security and CISF protects installations. **Analysis**—correct allocation
reduces duplication and preserves expertise. **Qualification**—the State police and civil
administration remain indispensable for local law, investigation and public accountability.

**Trap check:** the Army is not a CAPF; State Armed Police are not central forces; RAF and CoBRA
are CRPF components; “paramilitary” is not a safe catch-all.

### SESSION 3 — Separate and specialised forces: do not force them into one box

#### Visual first

| Organisation | Governing source/control | Correct task | Frequent confusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SPG** | SPG Act, 1988; Cabinet Secretariat | proximate security to protectees defined by the Act | not general VIP security and not a CAPF merely because armed |
| **RPF** | RPF Act, 1957; Ministry of Railways | railway property, passenger area and passenger security | GRP is State police and handles ordinary policing/crime jurisdiction on railways |
| **Indian Coast Guard** | Coast Guard Act, 1978; Ministry of Defence | maritime-zone security, law enforcement and assistance to civil authorities | Navy defends against military threats; coastal police perform State policing |
| **NSG** | NSG Act, 1986; MHA | specialised counter-terror/anti-hijack intervention | not the first responder to every terror incident |
| **Home Guards** | respective State/UT Acts and Rules | voluntary auxiliary to State police and emergency/civil-defence support | no all-India Central Home Guards Act |
| **Border Wing Home Guards** | State/UT Home Guards framework | auxiliary support in border areas | not a substitute for BSF or a separate CAPF |

MHA Annual Report 2024–25 records Home Guards as a voluntary auxiliary to State police and says
18 Border Wing Home Guards battalions had been raised in border States. This dated figure
supports the 2023 Prelims distinction; it does not turn Home Guards into a Union force.

#### Three-layer maritime example

```text
MILITARY THREAT AT SEA       → Navy
MARITIME LAW-ENFORCEMENT     → Coast Guard + authorised agencies
COASTAL / LOCAL CRIME CASE   → State coastal/local police
                              ↓
                    evidence hand-off + prosecution
```

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Specialisation improves response only when the specialist enters at
> the right threshold and exits through a documented hand-off to the lawful investigator.

**Must-write keywords:** SPG Act · RPF–GRP distinction · Coast Guard–Navy–coastal police ·
Home Guards auxiliary · specialist threshold · hand-off.

**Paragraph build:** **Claim**—specialised forces solve narrow high-risk problems.
**Evidence**—NSG handles counter-terror contingencies, SPG proximate security, RPF railway
security and Coast Guard maritime law-enforcement tasks. **Analysis**—using them as generic police
creates role confusion. **Qualification**—joint operations remain necessary, but criminal
investigation and court proof must follow the competent law.

**Trap check:** an Act calling a body an “armed force of the Union” does not make every such body
a military service or a CAPF.

### SESSION 4 — State police and State Armed Police are the local backbone

#### Visual first

```text
LOCAL WARNING / COMPLAINT
          ↓
beat staff + Special Branch + district intelligence
          ↓
FIRST RESPONSE
civil police → secure life/site → register lawful case
          ↓
ARMED SUPPORT, IF REQUIRED
State Armed Police / reserve / IR battalion / CAPF aid
          ↓
INVESTIGATION
scene + witnesses + digital/forensic chain + case diary
          ↓
PROSECUTION AND TRIAL
prosecutor → court → conviction/acquittal → appeal
```

State police are structurally irreplaceable because they hold local knowledge, receive first
complaints, preserve scenes, identify witnesses and build the ordinary criminal case. State Armed
Police and reserve units provide armed, riot-control, guard and reinforcement capacity under State
control; they do not become CAPFs because the Centre assists their raising or modernisation.
Police organisation is governed through the applicable State/UT law: the Police Act, 1861
continues in some jurisdictions, while others use replacement State Police Acts.

**Why central force success can still fail in court:** an operational unit may neutralise a threat
or recover material, but an investigator must lawfully document the scene, seizure, witnesses,
forensics and accused linkage. A broken hand-off converts tactical success into evidentiary
weakness.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Central capacity can reinforce a State, but only professional local
> policing can convert security presence into routine rule of law.

**Must-write keywords:** first responder · local intelligence · scene preservation · State Armed
Police · India Reserve Battalion · investigation separation · prosecution quality.

**Paragraph build:** **Claim**—State-police capacity is the system’s binding constraint.
**Evidence**—State List Entries 1–2 and the NIA Act’s preservation of State investigative powers.
**Analysis**—central deployments cannot replace everyday registration, witnesses, forensics and
court liaison. **Qualification**—scheduled offences may lawfully move to NIA, and Union forces may
assist under Entry 2A.

**Trap check:** deployment, arrest and investigation are not one act; the unit that apprehends a
suspect need not be the final investigating agency.

### SESSION 5 — The intelligence cycle and the information–evidence firewall

#### Visual first

```text
1 DIRECTION / PRIORITY
          ↓
2 COLLECTION
HUMINT | SIGINT | IMINT | OSINT | financial/technical inputs
          ↓
3 PROCESSING / COLLATION
deduplicate | translate | time/place/identity checks
          ↓
4 ANALYSIS
source reliability + corroboration + alternatives + confidence
          ↓
5 DISSEMINATION
need-to-know warning / assessment / lead
          ↓
6 DECISION AND ACTION
protect | surveil lawfully | interdict | investigate
          ↓
7 FEEDBACK / RE-TASKING / REVIEW
what was right, missed, late, unlawful or unusable?
```

| Stage | What it is | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| Information | raw report, observation or data point | verified intelligence |
| Intelligence | assessed information with context and confidence | admissible proof by itself |
| Lead | basis to verify or investigate lawfully | finding of guilt |
| Evidence | material proved through lawful collection, relevance, authenticity and custody | agency assertion |
| Charge-sheet | investigator’s final report/allegation | conviction |
| Conviction | judicial finding after trial standard is met | synonym for arrest or prosecution |

**Collection disciplines:** source grading, corroboration, legal authorisation, minimisation,
translation/context and protection of sources. **Analysis disciplines:** separate fact from
assumption, record confidence, test alternative hypotheses and issue time-sensitive warnings.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Intelligence reduces uncertainty for action; evidence survives legal
> scrutiny for adjudication.

**Must-write keywords:** tasking · collection · collation · analysis · dissemination · feedback ·
source reliability · confidence level · intelligence lead ≠ evidence.

**Paragraph build:** **Claim**—coordination failure often begins before operations.
**Evidence**—MAC/SMAC share assessed inputs, while police/NIA must build a lawful evidentiary
record. **Analysis**—if uncertainty and source limits disappear during hand-off, intelligence is
either ignored or over-treated as proof. **Qualification**—secrecy may protect sources, but a
prosecution must still prove admissible facts independently.

**Trap check:** more data is not necessarily better intelligence; a correct warning is not by
itself a prosecutable case.

### SESSION 6 — IB, R&AW, NTRO, DIA, MAC, SMAC, NATGRID and NIA: exact boundaries

#### Visual first: function map

| Body | Publicly supportable role | What this package does **not** attribute |
|---|---|---|
| **IB** | domestic intelligence and security assessment under MHA | statutory arrest, ordinary police investigation or prosecution |
| **R&AW** | external intelligence under Cabinet Secretariat | domestic police jurisdiction or prosecution |
| **NTRO** | technical-intelligence organisation; public operational detail is limited | invented surveillance, arrest, search or prosecution powers |
| **DIA** | tri-service defence-intelligence coordination within HQ Integrated Defence Staff | civilian crime investigation or public-order command |
| **MAC** | central, round-the-clock multi-agency intelligence sharing under the IB architecture | investigation, arrest or operational command over States |
| **SMAC** | State-level sharing node linked to the MAC system | replacement of State Special Branch/police |
| **NATGRID** | authorised connectivity between user agencies and designated data providers | intelligence collection in its own right, arrest, charge-sheet or adjudication |
| **NIA** | statutory investigation and prosecution of **Scheduled Offences** under the NIA Act | general intelligence collection or blanket control of all serious crime |

#### NATGRID current anchor

MHA Annual Report 2024–25 says the solution **presently connects 11 Central User Agencies and
State/UT Police of all 28 States and 8 Union Territories with 11 providing organisations**.
That proves current connectivity as reported; it does not prove that every query is accurate,
necessary or converted into a successful prosecution.

#### NIA legal firewall

- Section 3 gives NIA officers police powers for investigation of Scheduled Offences and arrest of
  persons concerned in those offences.
- Section 6 provides the entrustment/takeover route and requires State police to continue until NIA
  actually takes up the investigation.
- Section 7 permits association of the State or transfer back with prior Central approval.
- Section 10 preserves State power to investigate and prosecute Scheduled Offences, subject to the
  Act.
- NIA Special Courts try cases; the court, not NIA, convicts.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** India’s intelligence architecture is a relay—collection, assessment,
> sharing and data access precede investigation, but none can borrow the next institution’s powers.

**Must-write keywords:** domestic · external · technical · defence intelligence · MAC/SMAC ·
NATGRID authorised access · Scheduled Offence · concurrent jurisdiction · no invented powers.

**Paragraph build:** **Claim**—institutional overlap is manageable when functions remain explicit.
**Evidence**—IB/R&AW/NTRO/DIA assess different domains; MAC/SMAC share; NATGRID connects data;
NIA investigates Scheduled Offences. **Analysis**—the sequence joins information without
collapsing legal mandates. **Qualification**—classified detail may remain unavailable, so an
answer must not infer coercive powers from intelligence status.

**Trap check:** NIA is not an intelligence agency; MAC is not an investigator; NATGRID is not a
database-owning police force; DIA is not a domestic police body.

### SESSION 7 — Command, control and cooperative-federal coordination

#### Visual first: incident architecture

```text
POLITICAL / LEGAL AUTHORITY
Union responsibility + State public-order responsibility
                         │
                         ▼
DESIGNATED CIVIL LEAD
State government / district administration / competent statutory agency
                         │
            ┌────────────┼────────────┐
            ▼            ▼            ▼
 intelligence cell   operations cell   investigation/prosecution cell
 IB/MAC/SMAC         police/CAPF/NSG   State police/NIA + prosecutor
            └────────────┼────────────┘
                         ▼
COMMON OPERATING PICTURE + WRITTEN TASKS + EVIDENCE HAND-OFF
                         ▼
AFTER-ACTION, RIGHTS AND OUTCOME REVIEW
```

#### Seven coordination rules

1. **Name one lead for each function**, not one super-agency for everything.
2. **Preserve civil primacy** when Union forces act in aid of civil power.
3. **Use written tasking and common terminology** so a threat input becomes an assigned action.
4. **Deploy liaison officers and interoperable secure communications** across control rooms.
5. **Pre-plan evidence custody** before an operation: scene owner, seizure memo, device handling,
   medical aid and investigator hand-over.
6. **Escalate by law and threshold:** local police → specialised State/Central support → NIA only
   through the Scheduled-Offence route.
7. **Review failure and harm:** missed warnings, delay, friendly-force conflict, civilian injury,
   unlawful access and case collapse.

#### Recurring fault lines

| Fault | Consequence | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| delayed/siloed intelligence | warning arrives unactioned | time stamp, priority, receipt and action feedback |
| dual/multiple command | duplicated patrols or gaps | area/task matrix and named operational commander |
| Centre–State mistrust | under-sharing or resistance | MAC/SMAC liaison plus respect for State legal role |
| tactical–investigation break | weak chain of custody | joint pre-operation evidence plan |
| platform dependence | data error becomes operational error | source validation, human review and correction |
| credit/blame competition | incomplete common picture | shared after-action review and auditable task log |

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Unity of command is desirable in an operation; unity of constitutional
> ownership is neither possible nor necessary.

**Must-write keywords:** cooperative federalism · civil primacy · lead agency · liaison officer ·
common operating picture · evidence hand-off · after-action review.

**Paragraph build:** **Claim**—the problem is usually coordination, not institutional absence.
**Evidence**—MAC/SMAC provide sharing, Entry 2A permits Union aid and NIA law specifies takeover
and State assistance. **Analysis**—written task allocation can integrate actors without erasing
their mandates. **Qualification**—centralisation without State legitimacy or local capacity can
reduce cooperation.

**Trap check:** an intelligence-sharing hub does not command field units; an operational commander
does not decide guilt; a central deployment does not automatically transfer the criminal case.

### SESSION 8 — Police reform: commissions, *Prakash Singh* and the implementation test

#### Visual first: reform chronology

```text
Police Act, 1861 legacy
        ↓
National Police Commission, 1977–81
insulation + accountability + professional investigation
        ↓
Ribeiro Committee, 1998–99
implementation of reform safeguards
        ↓
Padmanabhaiah Committee, 2000
modernisation + training + community policing + investigation
        ↓
Malimath Committee, 2000–03
criminal investigation + prosecution + victim/witness/trial reform
        ↓
Soli Sorabjee Committee → Model Police Act, 2006
        ↓
Prakash Singh judgment, 22 September 2006
seven binding directions
        ↓
MHA Annual Report 2024–25
BPR&D preparing a new draft Model Police Bill; finalisation still pending in report
```

#### The seven *Prakash Singh* directions

1. **State Security Commission** for policy, performance evaluation and insulation from
   unwarranted influence.
2. Merit-based **DGP selection** from an empanelled panel and minimum two-year tenure.
3. Minimum two-year tenure for key operational officers—IG Zone, DIG Range, district SP and SHO,
   subject to specified grounds.
4. **Separation of investigation from law and order**, initially in larger urban areas.
5. **Police Establishment Board** for specified personnel matters and recommendations.
6. **Police Complaints Authorities** at district and State levels for serious misconduct.
7. **National Security Commission** for selection/placement and service-condition questions of
   chiefs of Central Police Organisations.

#### Reform is a triangle

```text
AUTONOMY FROM ILLEGITIMATE PRESSURE
                 /\
                /  \
               /    \
 PROFESSIONAL CAPACITY —— EXTERNAL ACCOUNTABILITY
```

Autonomy without accountability risks impunity; accountability without tenure and resources can
become symbolic; modern equipment without investigation/prosecution reform raises activity, not
justice.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** India does not lack a police-reform blueprint; it lacks uniform,
> enforceable conversion of autonomy, capability and accountability into station-level practice.

**Must-write keywords:** National Police Commission · Ribeiro · Padmanabhaiah · Malimath · Model
Police Act 2006 · State Security Commission · fixed tenure · separation of investigation · PCA.

**Paragraph build:** **Claim**—police reform is a governance and internal-security reform.
**Evidence**—the seven judicial directions address political control, tenure, specialisation,
personnel management and complaints. **Analysis**—together they improve both operational quality
and legitimacy. **Qualification**—police is a State subject and implementation remains uneven;
an announced Model Bill is not enacted law.

**Trap check:** the directions are not merely a recommendation; a commission report is not itself
law; a Police Complaints Authority is not the same as departmental vigilance.

### SESSION 9 — AFSPA: threshold, powers and legal boundaries

#### Visual first

```text
SECURITY CONDITION REVIEW
          ↓
SECTION 3 GAZETTE NOTIFICATION
Governor / Administrator / Central Government
whole or part declared “disturbed area”
          ↓
SECTION 4 POWERS IN THAT AREA
warning + force in specified public-order situation
destroy specified arms/fortified positions
arrest on cognizable-offence basis/reasonable suspicion
enter/search for statutory purposes
          ↓
SECTION 5 HAND-OVER
nearest police station + least possible delay + circumstances report
          ↓
ORDINARY CASE / REVIEW SYSTEM
police investigation + court process + command/judicial/human-rights review
          ↓
SECTION 6
previous Central sanction before prosecution/suit/legal proceeding
```

#### What the 1958 Act does—and does not do

- **Section 3** activates the Act through a notified disturbed-area declaration; AFSPA does not
  apply merely because a place faces insurgency.
- **Section 4(a)** permits specified ranks, after such due warning as considered necessary, to use
  force even causing death against defined contraventions involving prohibited assembly or
  weapons. It is not an unrestricted “shoot-to-kill” clause.
- **Sections 4(b)–(d)** cover destruction of specified arms positions/hide-outs, arrest without
  warrant on the statutory basis and entry/search without warrant for the stated purposes.
- **Section 5** requires hand-over to the nearest police station with the least possible delay and
  a report of circumstances.
- **Section 6** requires previous Central Government sanction for prosecution, suit or other legal
  proceeding concerning acts done or purportedly done under the Act.
- **Jammu and Kashmir** is governed by the separate 1990 Act. Its prior-sanction provision is
  section 7; do not cite section 6 across both statutes.

#### Three limits on power

| Limit | Meaning |
|---|---|
| territorial | only a currently notified disturbed area |
| functional | only the purposes and actors specified by the Act |
| constitutional/judicial | Article 21, judicial review and the Supreme Court’s necessity/accountability safeguards continue |

> **Answer-grabbing line:** AFSPA enlarges operational authority within a notified area; it does
> not create a legal vacuum.

**Must-write keywords:** disturbed-area notification · Section 3 · due warning · Section 4 ·
least possible delay · Section 5 · prior sanction · Section 6 · J&K Act section 7.

**Paragraph build:** **Claim**—AFSPA is exceptional but legally bounded.
**Evidence**—notification is a precondition, section 4 states defined powers, section 5 restores
police custody and section 6 regulates prosecution. **Analysis**—the statute therefore overlays,
rather than replaces, civil and judicial processes. **Qualification**—the sanction rule and
restricted NHRC procedure can make accountability difficult in practice.

**Trap check:** “disturbed area” notification and AFSPA power are separate stages; prior sanction
is not a judicial declaration that the act was lawful.

### SESSION 10 — AFSPA safeguards and the current notified map

#### Judicial safeguard rail

```text
Naga People’s Movement (1997)
constitutionality upheld
→ declaration review at least every six months
→ Army “Dos and Don’ts” must be followed
→ action remains reviewable
```

### SESSION 11 — Rights, custody, accountability and the proof ladder

#### Custodial safeguard table

| Safeguard | Binding source/effect |
|---|---|
| grounds of arrest and bail information | BNSS section 47; Article 22 |
| visible identification and arrest memorandum | BNSS section 36; *D.K. Basu* |
| inform relative/friend and record compliance | BNSS section 48 |
| meet an advocate during interrogation, though not throughout | BNSS section 38 |
| medical examination and injury record/copy | BNSS section 53 |
| health and safety in custody | BNSS section 56 |
| prompt production before Magistrate | BNSS sections 57–58; Article 22 |
| police-station CCTV and oversight | *Paramvir Singh Saini* (2020) |
| independent encounter-death process | *PUCL v. State of Maharashtra* (2014) |

#### Accountability architecture

```text
INTERNAL
supervision | standing orders | discipline | after-action review
          +
EXECUTIVE / LEGISLATIVE
ministerial responsibility | budget/audit | rules | parliamentary scrutiny
          +
INDEPENDENT / JUDICIAL
courts | Magistrate | NHRC/SHRC | Police Complaints Authority
          +
PUBLIC LEGITIMACY
complaint access | reasoned disclosure | witness/victim protection | community trust
```

**PHRA section 19:** for complaints involving the armed forces, NHRC follows a special procedure:
it may seek a Central Government report and then make recommendations. This is narrower than its
ordinary inquiry route. **Article 33** concerns restrictions on specified personnel’s rights for
duty and discipline; it does not remove civilian rights.

#### The proof ladder

```text
raw information
   ↓
assessed intelligence / warning
   ↓
lawful verification + FIR / case registration
   ↓
arrest / search / seizure
   ↓
forensic authentication + chain of custody + witnesses
   ↓
charge-sheet / final report
   ↓
prosecutor presents case
   ↓
court takes cognizance / frames charge / conducts trial
   ↓
conviction OR acquittal
   ↓
appeal / finality
```

MHA’s NIA figures as on 31 March 2025 illustrate the distinction: **657 cases registered;
521 charge-sheeted; 157 trials concluded; 150 trials resulting in conviction**. These numbers
must not be collapsed into one “success” count, and a conviction-rate claim must state its
denominator.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Rights safeguards are not post-operation charity; they are the
> architecture that makes intelligence actionable, evidence admissible and authority legitimate.

**Must-write keywords:** Article 21 · Article 22 · D.K. Basu · BNSS 36/38/47/48/53/56–59 ·
PHRA section 19 · PCA · chain of custody · charge-sheet ≠ conviction.

**Paragraph build:** **Claim**—procedural rights increase security effectiveness.
**Evidence**—arrest memoranda, medical records, CCTV, prompt production and independent inquiry
protect both the detainee and the evidentiary record. **Analysis**—lawful process reduces false
implication, coerced confession and trial failure while sustaining community reporting.
**Qualification**—formal safeguards require staffing, preservation of records and independent
follow-through.

**Trap check:** an official press release about arrest or charge-sheet is authoritative about that
stage only; it does not establish guilt.

### SESSION 12 — Technology, training, community legitimacy and outcome measurement

#### Visual first: capability must be paired with control

| Capability | Operational value | Required safeguard |
|---|---|---|
| MAC/SMAC secure sharing | faster warning circulation | source grading, need-to-know access, receipt/action feedback |
| NATGRID authorised queries | cross-database link analysis | purpose limitation, role-based access, query logs, correction and audit |
| CCTNS/ICJS and digital case tools | continuity across police, forensic, prosecution and courts | accurate entry, interoperable identifiers, cyber security and evidentiary integrity |
| CCTV/body-worn/video records | reconstruction and deterrence | coverage, retention, tamper protection, retrieval and independent review |
| digital forensics/analytics | device, network and financial attribution | lawful acquisition, hashing/custody, validation and human review |
| drones/GIS/sensors | situational awareness and safer deployment | authorised purpose, deconfliction, privacy and false-positive review |

Technology cannot repair unclear law, poor data or weak supervision. A database hit must be
verified; an algorithmic correlation is not identity proof; secrecy cannot eliminate access logs.

#### Training matrix

1. **Law and rights:** arrest, search, AFSPA limits, crowd control, gender/child safeguards.
2. **Investigation:** scene management, interviewing, forensics, digital evidence and prosecution
   consultation.
3. **Intelligence:** source evaluation, structured analysis, alternative hypotheses and warning.
4. **Joint operations:** interoperable communications, maps, liaison and evidence hand-off.
5. **Context:** local language, terrain, social conflict, misinformation and community engagement.
6. **Personnel resilience:** stress management, ethical leadership, fatigue and mental-health
   support.

#### Community legitimacy cycle

```text
fair contact + accessible complaint
        ↓
greater willingness to report and testify
        ↓
better local intelligence and evidence
        ↓
more precise operations, fewer false positives
        ↓
greater trust and lower recruitment space for violent actors
```

Community policing is not intelligence outsourcing. It requires voluntary cooperation, protection
against retaliation, non-discrimination and a visible remedy when personnel abuse power.

#### Outcome dashboard: do not reward activity alone

| Level | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Inputs** | staffing, training hours, vehicles, forensic capacity, secure links |
| **Activities** | patrols, meetings, alerts, searches, arrests, joint exercises |
| **Intermediate quality** | warning-to-action time, false-positive rate, evidence hand-off time, forensic turnaround, complaint disposal, tenure stability |
| **Justice outcomes** | lawful charge-sheets, trial completion, conviction/acquittal with reasons, appeal sustainability |
| **Security outcomes** | attacks prevented, network capability reduced, recurrence/displacement monitored |
| **Rights/legitimacy outcomes** | custodial harm, unlawful-force findings, compensation/compliance, community trust and reporting willingness |

No single metric is sufficient. A high arrest count may reflect enforcement activity, overbreadth
or both; a high conviction rate may use only concluded trials as denominator; lower reporting may
mean less crime or less trust.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Modernisation is complete only when better sensors and weapons are
> matched by better judgment, evidence, supervision and public confidence.

**Must-write keywords:** role-based access · audit log · human review · chain of custody ·
de-escalation · local language · community trust · activity–outcome distinction.

**Paragraph build:** **Claim**—technology is an institutional multiplier, not an institutional
substitute. **Evidence**—NATGRID connects authorised users and data providers, while CCTV and
digital forensics can strengthen traceability. **Analysis**—benefit depends on lawful access,
accurate data, trained users and prosecutable outputs. **Qualification**—unchecked surveillance,
poor data or target chasing can damage rights and intelligence quality.

**Trap check:** a portal’s launch, number of connected agencies, meeting count or arrest total is
an output; it is not proof of safer communities or fairer justice.

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

### ADVANCED BRANCH A — Functional reform is different from centralisation

The NCTC episode demonstrates that giving a central intelligence-led body coercive powers can
trigger a genuine federal objection. A more durable route is to strengthen existing functions:
State police capacity; MAC/SMAC sharing; NATGRID access controls; NIA’s statutory case route;
joint operational SOPs; and prosecutor/forensic support. This does not rule out central action;
it subjects central action to a defined trigger and hand-off.

### ADVANCED BRANCH B — Intelligence oversight need not mean operational disclosure

IB and R&AW do not have public enabling statutes defining their complete mandate, powers and
oversight. NTRO’s public charter is also limited. The reform question is therefore not “secrecy or
oversight”, but **which cleared forum can review legality, mandate, expenditure, rights complaints
and systemic failure without exposing sources or methods**. A statutory charter can improve
capability by clarifying tasking and lawful access as well as improving accountability.

### ADVANCED BRANCH C — Command multiplicity is sometimes necessary but never free

Assam Rifles’ administrative/operational split, the Army–ITBP interface and the
Navy–Coast Guard–coastal-police architecture show that different threats require different
competences. The cost is coordination load: duplicated surveillance, delayed escalation,
uncertain evidence ownership and competing chains. The cure is not automatic merger; it is an
explicit task, geography, command and evidence matrix.

### ADVANCED BRANCH D — Capability and accountability are mutually reinforcing

Use-of-force review, arrest safeguards, medical records, CCTV, complaints authorities and
prosecution scrutiny can expose misconduct, but they also protect lawful personnel from false
allegations and produce evidence that survives trial. Under-investing in accountability therefore
weakens intelligence trust, witness cooperation and operational legitimacy.

### ADVANCED BRANCH E — Performance measurement can distort behaviour

Targets based only on arrests, recoveries, encounters, alerts or cases registered invite
over-production of visible activity. A balanced dashboard needs:

```text
speed + accuracy + legality + case quality + final adjudication
     + reduced harm + public trust + learning from failure
```

Every indicator needs a denominator, time period and ownership. Conviction rate among concluded
trials is not conviction among all registered cases; fewer complaints may mean improvement or
fear; more intelligence inputs may mean better collection or more noise.
