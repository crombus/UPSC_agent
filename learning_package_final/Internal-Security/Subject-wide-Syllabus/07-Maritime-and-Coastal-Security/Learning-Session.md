### Authoritative legal, institutional and current checks, accessed 27 September 2026

- **Coastline:** the Government revised India’s official coastline length from the older 7,516.6 km measure to **11,098.81 km** through a Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways circular dated 29 April 2025. This is a measurement revision, not physical coastal expansion.
- **Maritime zones:** the Territorial Waters, Continental Shelf, Exclusive Economic Zone and Other Maritime Zones Act, 1976 provides the 12-nautical-mile territorial sea, 24-nautical-mile contiguous zone and 200-nautical-mile EEZ framework. Territorial sovereignty is not the same as EEZ sovereign rights; innocent passage remains an international-law limit in the territorial sea.
- **Indian Navy:** after 26/11, Government assigned it overall maritime-security responsibility, including coastal and offshore security, in coordination with the Coast Guard, State marine police and other agencies. Overall responsibility does not make it the ordinary police, Customs authority or port operator.
- **Indian Coast Guard:** the Coast Guard Act, 1978 constitutes an armed force of the Union. Its statutory duties include protecting offshore installations, assisting fishermen, preserving the marine environment, assisting Customs against smuggling and enforcing applicable maritime law.
- **State/UT Marine Police:** MHA describes coastal police as responsible up to 12 nautical miles under the coastal-security architecture. This is an operational layer, not a claim that no central statute or agency can act there.
- **MDA architecture:** NC3I links coastal surveillance nodes; the Information Management and Analysis Centre (IMAC), inaugurated in November 2014, is the national data-fusion hub. The Navy’s July 2025 NMDA contract is an upgrade project, not evidence that every planned capability is already operational.
- **IFC-IOR:** hosted by the Indian Navy at Gurugram since 22 December 2018, it is a regional information-sharing hub. It is not the same institution as IMAC and has no general arrest/prosecution power.
- **Coordination:** Joint Operations Centres operate at Mumbai, Visakhapatnam, Kochi and Port Blair. The first National Maritime Security Coordinator was appointed in 2022, not 2020.
- **Training:** MHA established the National Academy of Coastal Policing at Okha as a subordinate office by order dated 7 July 2026. It addresses training capacity; it does not itself settle command or equipment gaps.
- **Vessels and fishers:** official systems include ReALCraft registration/licensing, identity documentation and a national rollout of satellite-based vessel communication/transponder support. Registration, identity and live tracking are separate coverage questions.
- **Current port law:** the Merchant Shipping Act, 2025 and Indian Ports Act, 2025 entered the current legal landscape in 2026. A December 2025 official decision set out a Bureau of Port Security design under section 13 of the Merchant Shipping Act; staffing, rules and operational maturity must be verified separately before claiming outcomes.
- **Offence law:** the Suppression of Unlawful Acts against Safety of Maritime Navigation and Fixed Platforms on Continental Shelf Act, 2002 addresses specified unlawful acts against ships/platforms. The Maritime Anti-Piracy Act, 2022 covers piracy on the high seas as statutorily defined, including the EEZ, and provides authorised seizure and designated-court machinery.

**Official links used:** [revised coastline](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2198800&reg=3&lang=1) · [Coast Guard charter](https://indiancoastguard.gov.in/charter-of-duties) · [post-26/11 architecture](https://pib.gov.in/newsite/PrintRelease.aspx?relid=111871) · [IMAC/NC3I](https://pib.gov.in/newsite/PrintRelease.aspx?relid=111697) · [NMDA contract](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2143191) · [IFC-IOR](https://ifcior.indiannavy.gov.in/about_us) · [NACP order](https://www.mha.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-07/NACPOrderEng_07072026.pdf) · [Coastal Security Scheme evidence](https://www.mha.gov.in/MHA1/Par2017/pdfs/par2025-pdfs/RS23072025/405.pdf) · [Maritime Anti-Piracy Act](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/19621?view_type=browse) · [SUA Act](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/2009) · [BoPS decision](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2206443&reg=3&lang=2).

### Evidence limits

1. The earlier 7,516.6 km coastline figure remains a historical measurement; use 11,098.81 km for current answers and identify the methodology revision when comparing.
2. Avoid an uncited current percentage for India’s trade or hydrocarbons moved by sea; strategic dependence can be established without a fragile number.
3. “Jurisdiction up to 12 nm” describes the coastal-police layer; Navy, Coast Guard, Customs and central criminal statutes may also operate according to law.
4. An AIS/radar track, registered vessel or transponder signal creates awareness; it does not prove identity, intent, interception or conviction.
5. A government decision to constitute an institution, a signed NMDA contract or a training academy is an input/capability, not proof of full operational effect.
6. Piracy, armed robbery at sea, maritime terrorism, smuggling, trafficking, illegal fishing and naval aggression have different legal elements and response owners.

## BASIC LEARNING SESSION

### Navigation chart — solve the location problem before the agency problem

```text
asset or activity at risk
        ↓
internal waters / territorial sea / contiguous zone / EEZ / high seas
        ↓
defence / terrorism / piracy / crime / safety / pollution / cyber incident
        ↓
Navy + Coast Guard + Marine Police + port/Customs/immigration
        ↓
awareness → decision → interdiction/rescue → evidence → recovery
```

### SESSION 1 — The revised coastline changes planning, not territory

The Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways revised India's official coastline length on
**29 April 2025** to **11,098.81 km**, using a standardised measurement based on
Electronic Navigation Charts and stakeholder-agreed terms of reference. The familiar
**7,516.6 km** figure is therefore **superseded for current use**. The difference reflects
method and scale—especially the treatment of coastal sinuosity and islands—not physical
expansion of India. The architecture spans nine coastal States and coastal Union Territories;
the revised national length is a planning denominator, not a claim that every segment has
the same hazard, traffic or policing requirement.

Maritime security protects the wider ocean system: sea lanes, ships, offshore assets,
maritime zones and ports. Coastal security is the near-shore subset concerned with approaches,
landing points, territorial waters and the shoreward hand-off. Port security protects the
ship–facility–cargo–digital interface. Naval defence addresses military threats.

**Named evidence:** MoPSW circular, 29 April 2025.
**Exam trap:** do not combine the coastline in kilometres with the EEZ breadth in nautical
miles, and never present 7,516.6 km as the current figure.

### SESSION 2 — The maritime-zone ladder is a jurisdiction map

| Zone | Extent from baseline | Legal idea | Typical security relevance |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Internal waters | landward of baseline | sovereignty | ports, harbours, ordinary domestic law |
| Territorial sea | up to 12 nm | sovereignty subject to innocent passage | close-coast policing, safety, interdiction |
| Contiguous zone | up to 24 nm | specified preventive/enforcement control | customs, fiscal, immigration, sanitary laws |
| EEZ | up to 200 nm | sovereign resource rights and specified jurisdiction | offshore assets, fisheries, surveillance |
| High seas | beyond national zones as applicable | freedoms plus international-law duties | piracy and transnational cooperation |

The key distinction is **sovereignty** versus **sovereign rights**. India does not acquire
territorial sovereignty over the entire EEZ. Nor is innocent passage permission for activity
prejudicial to the coastal State's peace, good order or security.

**Answer move:** state the zone, identify the protected legal interest, then name the actor
and statute. The physical reach of a Navy or Coast Guard vessel does not itself create an
unlimited legal power.

### SESSION 3 — Threat labels decide offence, jurisdiction and evidence

Piracy under the **Maritime Anti-Piracy Act, 2022** follows the UNCLOS private-ends/high-seas
model used by the statute, including its stated EEZ reach. Comparable violence in internal or
territorial waters is not automatically piracy and may be armed robbery at sea or another
domestic offence. The **SUA Act, 2002** addresses specified unlawful acts against the safety
of maritime navigation and fixed platforms; it is central to maritime-terrorism analysis.

| Question | Piracy | Maritime terrorism | Smuggling/trafficking |
|---|---|---|---|
| Purpose | private ends | political/ideological coercion | illicit goods / exploitation of persons |
| Location trigger | statutory high-seas setting | ship, platform, port or coast under applicable law | route and offence-specific |
| Proof focus | act, location, ships/persons, private end | intent, target, organisation and consequence | cargo/person, knowledge, network and victim status |

**Trap:** "violence at sea" is an operational description, not a complete charge. A boarding
team must preserve position, flag, crew, communications, cargo and custody evidence so that
interception becomes a sustainable prosecution.

### SESSION 4 — The Navy, Coast Guard and Marine Police are connected, not interchangeable

The **Indian Navy** holds overall maritime-security responsibility and military defence/sea
control. The **Indian Coast Guard**, constituted under the Coast Guard Act, 1978, performs
constabulary, safety and environmental duties: offshore protection, assistance to fishermen,
search and rescue, marine-environment protection, assistance to Customs against smuggling and
enforcement of applicable maritime law. State/UT **Marine Police** provide the close-coast
criminal-law/public-order layer commonly described up to 12 nautical miles.

```text
suspect track in EEZ → IMAC/JOC cue → ICG shadow/board
→ vessel enters territorial waters/lands cargo
→ Marine/State Police FIR + Customs/specialist investigation
→ prosecution and court
```

The rings overlap because incidents move. "Up to 12 nm" helps locate the State marine-police
layer; it does not exclude lawful action by central agencies. Overall Navy responsibility does
not make the Navy the port operator or ordinary investigating police.

### SESSION 5 — Ports are cyber-physical gateways

A port joins seaward approach, harbour control, berth, cargo, crew, fuel, rail/road exits and
digital operating systems. **DG Shipping** administers applicable ship and port-facility
standards, including the **International Ship and Port Facility Security (ISPS) Code**.
Port authorities/operators and Port Facility Security Officers maintain facility plans;
CISF provides physical protection where deployed; Customs controls goods; immigration
controls persons; police investigate; Navy/Coast Guard supply seaward security and intelligence.

The **Merchant Shipping Act, 2025** and **Indian Ports Act, 2025** modernised the legal
landscape. The official decision to constitute a Bureau of Port Security is an institutional
design input; rules, staffing, audits and corrective action show implementation.

**Mechanism:** compromise of a terminal operating system or Vessel Traffic Management System
can halt cargo or create collision/safety risk without breaching a gate. Segmentation, vendor
control, offline fallback and tested recovery are therefore coastal-security measures.

### SESSION 6 — Post-26/11 architecture: why each node exists

```text
1974 Customs Marine Organisation → smuggling response
1977 interim Coast Guard → Coast Guard Act, 1978
2005 Coastal Security Scheme → marine-police stations/boats/jetties
26/11 Mumbai attacks, 2008 → national architecture reset
2009 Sagar Prahari Bal + Navy overall responsibility
2014 IMAC/NC3I operational architecture
2018 IFC-IOR regional information sharing
2022 first National Maritime Security Coordinator + Piracy Act
2025 NMDA upgrade contract
7 Jul 2026 National Academy of Coastal Policing, Okha
```

Four **Joint Operations Centres**—Mumbai, Visakhapatnam, Kochi and Port Blair—provide
continuous Navy-Coast Guard-Marine Police coordination. The Cabinet-Secretary-led
**NCSMCS** supplies apex review. The first **National Maritime Security Coordinator** was
appointed in **2022**, not 2020.

The **Indian Maritime Security Strategy, 2015** supplies the Navy's strategic framework for
maritime interests, threats, coordination and sea-lane security. It should not be mistaken
for a statute transferring police, port or Customs powers.

The chronology is reactive because major additions followed exposed vulnerabilities. That
does not make them irrational; it means maintenance, horizon scanning and after-action
closure must prevent the next reform from waiting for the next attack.

### SESSION 7 — NC3I, IMAC and IFC-IOR form different information layers

**NC3I** is the communications and sensor network linking coastal surveillance nodes.
**IMAC**, inaugurated in November 2014, fuses national feeds—coastal radar, AIS and other
agency data—into a common operating picture. **IFC-IOR**, established at Gurugram on
22 December 2018, is the outward-facing regional information-sharing hub with partner
countries and liaison officers.
Port Vessel Traffic Management Systems add harbour tracks, while the Navy's **Integrated
Underwater Harbour Defence and Surveillance Systems** protect major naval-harbour approaches.

| Capability | Question answered | What remains |
|---|---|---|
| radar/AIS/community report | what track or anomaly exists? | identity and intent |
| NC3I/IMAC fusion | how do national feeds correlate? | tasking and lawful power |
| IFC-IOR exchange | what do regional partners know? | domestic verification/enforcement |
| patrol/air/boarding asset | can India reach and act? | evidence and adjudication |

The Navy's July 2025 NMDA contract is an upgrade commitment, not proof of completed
deployment. Data quality, false tracks, cyber security, watchkeeper training and serviceable
response assets determine whether awareness becomes security.

### SESSION 8 — Small craft and fishing communities are an identity-and-trust problem

**ReALCraft** records registration/licensing; a crew identity document identifies a person;
a transponder can report location when fitted, powered, maintained and used. None of these
alone proves present crew, intent or lawful activity. Small craft, informal landing points and
equipment cost/maintenance remain harder than tracking large commercial vessels.

Fishing communities know local craft, weather and landing patterns and can provide early
warning. They also hold livelihood, dignity, privacy and consular interests. Palk Bay is a
settled-boundary but unresolved livelihood/compliance problem; straying, IUU fishing,
trafficking and hostile infiltration require different treatment.

The canonical material also records distress/security contact channels—**1554 for the Coast
Guard and 1093 for Marine Police**—as examples of how reporting must return a safety benefit
to the community. Treat these as source-period operational details and verify before field use.

**Exam line:** fisher trust is an operational capability when registration also improves
safety, records can be corrected, distress support works and enforcement avoids collective
suspicion.

### SESSION 9 — Boundary cases and international cooperation must retain ownership labels

The India-Bangladesh maritime boundary was delimited by the **2014 Annex VII award**.
**Sir Creek** remains an unresolved India-Pakistan tidal-boundary question with seaward
delimitation and fishing implications. The Palk Bay line is settled even though fisher arrests
and livelihood conflict continue. **Katchatheevu**, transferred through the 1974 India-Sri
Lanka agreement, remains domestically contentious without making the international maritime
line legally undelimited. These cases prevent the common mistake that every maritime dispute
is an undelimited boundary.

The **International Maritime Organization (IMO)** sets international shipping, safety,
security and pollution-prevention norms—SOLAS, the ISPS Code and MARPOL are key examples.
It does not command India's coastal police. Maldives matters to India because its location
near Indian Ocean sea lanes can support maritime-domain awareness, search and rescue and
regional stability; it does not receive Indian enforcement jurisdiction.

The 2023 GS-II IMO and 2024 GS-II Maldives questions are therefore explicitly
**APPLICATION** routes owned primarily by International Relations. Topic 07 supplies only
the domestic-security implementation link.

### SESSION 10 — Readiness is the whole chain, including recovery

| Stage | Operational question | Useful indicator |
|---|---|---|
| Prevent | Were vessels, crew, cargo, ports and networks risk-managed? | verified coverage and closed vulnerabilities |
| Prepare | Were people, boats, SOPs and backups exercised? | serviceability, sea-time, drill remediation |
| Respond | Did warning produce timely lawful action? | decision/interception/rescue time |
| Prove | Was physical/digital evidence preserved? | viable investigation and case outcome |
| Recover | Did port, communications or offshore service resume safely? | restoration time and recurrence control |

The **National Academy of Coastal Policing**, established by MHA order on 7 July 2026,
addresses training. It does not by itself provide boats, fuel, jetties, staffing, command
clarity or sea experience. Likewise, an exercise proves that a scenario was run; readiness
requires identified gaps to be assigned, corrected and retested.

Use **Sea Vigil** for the national-scale exercise and **Sagar Kavach** for recurring
coastal-security drills as named examples. Their evidentiary value lies in after-action
findings and corrective closure, not participation numbers.

**Qualified conclusion:** maritime resilience is the ability to see, decide, act, prove and
recover across sea and shore while respecting navigation, livelihood and due process.

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

### ADVANCED BRANCH A — Layered jurisdiction is a hand-off problem

Geographic rings are useful teaching aids, but an actual case moves across them. Detection
may occur in the EEZ, boarding may be by the Coast Guard, landing may trigger State-police
jurisdiction, cargo may invoke Customs, and prosecution may depend on digital evidence from
a port system. The advanced issue is a common incident picture, designated command and
documented evidence transfer—not artificial institutional exclusivity.

Use the Coast Guard Act, 1978 for constabulary duties, the post-26/11 Navy responsibility
for overall coordination, and a JOC for the common watch. **Mechanism:** track → task →
board/rescue → hand over → investigate → try. **Trap:** drawing concentric rings and then
claiming that no other competent agency may act within a ring.

### ADVANCED BRANCH B — Awareness-heavy reform can leave an interdiction deficit

Radar, AIS, NC3I and IMAC improve visibility. They do not create a serviceable patrol craft,
trained boarding team, fuel, legal authority or safe evidence procedure. Audit
`track-to-classification`, `classification-to-tasking` and `tasking-to-interception` times.
The July 2025 NMDA contract should be described as an upgrade project until commissioned
capability is officially demonstrated.

An IMAC alert that cannot be correlated with vessel records, assigned to a ready asset or
converted into a lawful boarding remains awareness without control. **Named evidence:** the
2025 Navy NMDA contract. **Trap:** reporting procurement value, sensor count or connected
agency count as proof of operational interception.

### ADVANCED BRANCH C — Small-craft identity is probabilistic

Registration links a declared vessel to a record; identity documents link a holder to a
record; a transponder supplies a signal. Flags can be false, devices can fail and crews can
change. Combine records, harbour departure data, visual identification, community knowledge
and risk-based boarding. The trap is converting a database match into proof of intent.

ReALCraft is the registration/licensing layer; AIS or satellite transponders provide
architecture-dependent live information; fishermen supply contextual knowledge. Each source
can contradict another and should trigger verification rather than automatic hostility.
**Evidence test:** measure coverage, functioning equipment, false matches and successful
identity resolution separately.

### ADVANCED BRANCH D — Command reform and training reform are different

The canonical recommendation to make the Coast Guard a single coastal-security authority is
a policy proposal, not current transfer of every Navy, police, port or Customs power. NACP
addresses the separate marine-police training deficit. Assess command, law, staffing,
equipment and training as different variables.

MHA's **7 July 2026** order establishes NACP at Okha as a subordinate office; it does not
prove every coastal unit has trained crews or adequate sea time. **Trap:** using a training
institution to answer the separate question of who commands a mixed Navy–ICG–police incident.

### ADVANCED BRANCH E — International law prevents zone inflation

Territorial-sea sovereignty remains subject to innocent passage; contiguous-zone powers are
specified; EEZ rights are functional; piracy has offence/location elements; hot pursuit has
conditions. Security urgency does not create a borderless power to stop, seize or prosecute.
Precise legal classification increases, rather than weakens, operational legitimacy.

Use the Maritime Zones Act, 1976 for the zone ladder, the Maritime Anti-Piracy Act, 2022 for
defined piracy and the SUA Act, 2002 for unlawful acts against ships/platforms. **Trap:**
calling any violent act at sea "piracy" or describing the EEZ as sovereign territory.

### ADVANCED BRANCH F — Ports convert maritime security into national resilience

Ports are network hubs: a cyber failure can stop cargo, customs records, navigation or gate
operations; a physical incident can interrupt energy and manufacturing supply chains.
Continuity planning must identify priority berths, manual fallbacks, alternate communications,
vendor dependencies and recovery authority. ISPS compliance is a baseline, not proof that
the port can recover from a combined cyber-physical event.

The Merchant Shipping Act, 2025, Indian Ports Act, 2025, DG Shipping's ISPS administration
and CERT-In's incident framework occupy different layers. **Evidence test:** restoration time,
safe cargo release and root-cause closure. **Trap:** treating a compliance certificate or
Bureau of Port Security announcement as proof of tested continuity.
