### Authoritative legal, institutional and current checks, accessed 27 September 2026

- **Instrument of Accession:** signed on 26 October 1947; the accession subjects were Defence, External Affairs and Communications, with ancillary matters in the Instrument's Schedule.
- **UN Security Council Resolution 47, 21 April 1948:** placed withdrawal of tribesmen and Pakistani nationals not normally resident in the State, and associated Pakistan-side action, before later Indian force reduction and plebiscite arrangements. The sequence was not implemented.
- **Simla Agreement, 2 July 1972:** converted the former ceasefire line into the Line of Control and committed the parties to bilateral peaceful settlement. The UN continues UNMOGIP under its interpretation; India officially maintains that its mandate lapsed after Simla. State both positions.
- **DGMO joint statement, 25 February 2021:** reaffirmed strict observance of ceasefire agreements/understandings along the LoC and other sectors from midnight 24/25 February. It is a military understanding, not a treaty or Kashmir settlement.
- **Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019:** effective 31 October 2019; created the Union Territory of Jammu and Kashmir with a legislature and the Union Territory of Ladakh without one. Section 32 excludes State List Entries 1 and 2—public order and police—from the J&K Assembly's legislative competence.
- **Article 370/35A:** C.O. 272 and C.O. 273 changed the constitutional position in August 2019; Article 35A, introduced through the 1954 application order, ceased to operate. The Supreme Court's 11 December 2023 judgment upheld the constitutional actions and required Assembly elections by 30 September 2024. The 2024 elections were held; ECI/PIB reported 63.88% overall turnout. No official restoration-of-statehood notification was found by the review date, so J&K is described as a Union Territory with legislature.
- **J&K-specific AFSPA, 1990:** a separate statute from the 1958 North-East Act. Section 3 concerns disturbed areas, section 4 powers, section 6 hand-over of arrested persons/property to police, and section 7 previous Central sanction before prosecution.
- **Village Defence Guards Scheme, 2022:** superseded the 1995 VDC order and operates under district police supervision; local deterrence must be paired with screening, training, weapons control and accountability.
- **NIA Pahalgam case:** official 2025/2026 releases are used only as chargesheet/investigation-status evidence of an alleged Pakistan-linked conspiracy and local facilitation; no allegation is converted into conviction.
- **Kashmiri migrant relief/rehabilitation, Rajya Sabha answer of 25 March 2026:** cash/ration assistance and transit-accommodation progress are delivery inputs/outputs, not proof of voluntary safe return or complete rehabilitation.
- **CPEC:** MEA's official position is that it passes through Indian territory under Pakistan's illegal occupation and raises sovereignty and territorial-integrity objections.

**Official links used:** [J&K Reorganisation Act](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/indiacode/bitstream/123456789/12030/1/A2019-34.pdf) · [Article 370 judgment](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2019/29796/29796_2019_1_1501_49019_Judgement_11-Dec-2023.pdf) · [J&K/Ladakh Affairs](https://www.mha.gov.in/en/divisionofmha/jammu-kashmir-and-ladakh-affairs) · [2024 Assembly turnout](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2061605) · [DGMO statement](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1700682) · [J&K AFSPA, 1990](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/1953) · [VDG Scheme record](https://www.mha.gov.in/sites/default/files/2023-07/Annexure7_28072023.pdf) · [CPEC official position](https://www.mea.gov.in/lok-sabha?dtl/32353/QUESTION+NO606+BRI+AND+CPEC) · [UNSC Resolution 47](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/111955) · [UNMOGIP fact sheet](https://peacekeeping.un.org/en/factsheet/unmogip).

### Evidence limits

1. The constitutional validity and doctrine of Articles 370/35A belong primarily to Polity; this topic teaches the minimum exact status needed for operational-security analysis.
2. Election turnout, tourist visits, projects, arrests and chargesheets are separate indicators; none proves that another event caused “normalcy.”
3. A quiet LoC does not prove zero infiltration, drone delivery, terror finance, local recruitment or hinterland violence.
4. Historical group names, leadership and strength in the textbook are not treated as current.
5. Official allegations and chargesheets are not convictions; property attachment is not confiscation.
6. Rehabilitation assistance is not equivalent to safe, voluntary and durable return.

### SESSION 1 — FOUNDATION — Accession, Resolution 47 and the LoC

#### Visual

```text
26 Oct 1947 → Instrument of Accession
              Defence + External Affairs + Communications
                     ↓
1947–48 conflict → ceasefire line
                     ↓
UNSC Resolution 47, 21 Apr 1948
  Pakistan-side withdrawal sequence → later Indian reduction/plebiscite
  sequence not implemented
                     ↓
1971 war → Simla Agreement, 2 Jul 1972 → Line of Control
```

#### Explanation

The historical princely State comprised Jammu, the Kashmir Valley, Ladakh, Gilgit and Baltistan, with sharply different social and strategic settings. “Pakistan-occupied Kashmir” is India's term for territory under Pakistan's control; Pakistan uses “Azad Jammu and Kashmir” for part of that area. The accession and later territorial dispute are foundations, but a GS-III answer should not become a constitutional essay. Resolution 47 established a sequence; citing a plebiscite without the preceding withdrawal conditions is incomplete. The Simla Agreement created the bilateral LoC framework after the 1971 war.

UNMOGIP requires a two-position statement: the UN continues the mission under its interpretation, while India maintains that its mandate lapsed after the Simla Agreement. The LoC is not the internationally recognised border, and the ceasefire line/LoC terminology should be dated.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** J&K's internal-security problem sits inside an unresolved territorial dispute, but its operational analysis begins with the mechanisms that convert that dispute into violence.

**Must-write keywords:** Instrument of Accession; 26 October 1947; Resolution 47; sequencing; Simla Agreement; Line of Control; bilateralism.

### SESSION 2 — FOUNDATION — Proxy war as a strategic adaptation

#### Visual

```text
limits of conventional war (1965, 1971)
                  ↓
low-intensity / proxy strategy
                  ↓
sponsor support: training + finance + weapons + sanctuary
                  ↓
nominally non-State groups + infiltrators + local facilitators
                  ↓
violence with deniability and lower direct interstate cost
```

#### Explanation

The canonical account presents Pakistan's proxy strategy as an adaptation after conventional-war defeats, described in the source through the phrase “bleed India through a thousand cuts.” It treats the earlier Punjab militancy playbook as a model later replicated in Kashmir. Historical examples include Lashkar-e-Taiba, Jaish-e-Mohammed and Hizbul Mujahideen; their present strength, leadership and specific case role must be verified from dated competent sources.

A proxy relationship separates sponsor, armed group, infiltrator, local facilitator and propagandist. Each role requires distinct evidence. “Pakistan-sponsored” is an analytical conclusion only when tied to material, communication, financing, origin, training or agency findings.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Proxy warfare turns an external strategic contest into an internal policing and legitimacy challenge by separating the sponsor from the visible perpetrator.

**Must-write keywords:** low-intensity warfare; State sponsorship; proxy; deniability; external aid; attribution ladder.

### SESSION 3 — CORE — Evolution of the conflict ecosystem

#### Visual

```text
disputed 1987 election → legitimacy trigger
         +
Afghan-war spillover / externally trained cadres
         +
sponsor infrastructure across the LoC
                     ↓
insurgency and terrorism from late 1980s
                     ↓
post-2008 adaptation: crowds, propaganda and legitimacy contest
                     ↓
current mix: infiltration + local modules + finance + drones/digital media
```

#### Explanation

The 1987 election dispute is a trigger, not a complete root cause. External training and support intensified an indigenous political rupture. Later tactics widened from kinetic militancy to crowd mobilisation, stone-pelting, propaganda and information effects; current claims about these tools require dated evidence.

The large-scale displacement of Kashmiri Pandits in the 1990s is a central demographic and rehabilitation consequence. The canonical estimate of nearly four lakh is a book-period figure; use it as attributed historical evidence, not a current population count.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** The J&K threat has adapted from infiltration-led militancy to a mixed ecosystem of proxy violence, local facilitation, finance and legitimacy contest.

**Must-write keywords:** trigger/root cause; external reinforcement; tactical adaptation; information contest; displacement; rehabilitation.

### SESSION 4 — CORE — The cross-border operating chain

#### Visual

```text
sponsor / handler / training or logistics node
                     ↓
LoC route, tunnel, drone or other delivery channel
                     ↓
infiltrator / local module
                     ↓
OGW or facilitator: shelter, transport, reconnaissance,
communication, recruitment, finance, weapons or warning
                     ↓
attack / attempted attack → propaganda → renewed recruitment
```

#### Explanation

Infiltration is one route within a wider ecosystem. Tunnels and drones can move weapons, narcotics or other material without moving a person. Terror finance supports logistics and recruitment. Local facilitation connects external supply to terrain, targets and social networks. Radicalisation and propaganda can sustain the pipeline even when cross-LoC firing falls.

An **over-ground worker** is an alleged non-combatant facilitator, not a legal label for dissent or sympathy. Investigation must prove the specific act and mental element. The 2025 Pahalgam NIA material is usable only as an investigation/chargesheet example of alleged external conspiracy and local facilitation.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** The operational unit is the cross-border-to-local chain; intercepting an infiltrator without dismantling finance, facilitators and recruitment leaves the system renewable.

**Must-write keywords:** infiltration attempt; successful infiltration; drone delivery; tunnel; handler; OGW; logistics; terror finance; local module.

### SESSION 5 — CORE — Ceasefire is one metric, not the conflict

#### Visual

```text
November 2003 ceasefire understanding
                ↓
25 February 2021 DGMO reaffirmation
                ↓
governs cross-LoC firing / border tranquillity

separately measure:
infiltration attempts | successful infiltration | drones/tunnels
local recruitment | terror finance | hinterland incidents
```

#### Explanation

The DGMO statement reaffirmed existing agreements and ceasefire observance from midnight 24/25 February 2021 and retained hotline/flag-meeting mechanisms. It is a military understanding, not a treaty or political settlement.

Reduced firing is a real humanitarian and security gain for border residents. It cannot be used as proof that the proxy ecosystem ended. Each metric has a different source, denominator and operational meaning.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** A quieter LoC measures restraint by armed forces across the line; it does not by itself measure the movement of people, money, weapons or ideas beneath that threshold.

**Must-write keywords:** 2003 understanding; DGMO reaffirmation; 25 February 2021; firing; infiltration; independent metrics.

### SESSION 6 — CORE — Security and intelligence architecture

#### Visual

| Function | Principal actors | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| LoC defence/guarding | Army in forward areas; BSF in assigned sectors | external-facing security differs from civil policing |
| local intelligence/first response | J&K Police and local administration | allegations must become lawful evidence |
| intelligence fusion | IB/MAC and participating agencies | warning is not conviction |
| scheduled-offence investigation | NIA or competent police through statute | takeover/charge-sheet is not judgment |
| tactical contingency | specialist forces where required | not routine governance |
| adjudication | competent/Special Courts | courts determine guilt |

#### Explanation

The security grid must connect border warning to local response, scene preservation, digital/financial evidence, prosecution and victim support. The 2019 reorganisation makes the command relationship structurally different from an ordinary State: police and public order are outside the J&K Assembly's legislative competence and run through the Lieutenant Governor/Union framework.

Intelligence-led precision matters because indiscriminate labelling weakens community reporting. Force can disrupt an imminent threat, but policing and justice must hold the gain.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Border defence stops entry, police expose the local network, investigators prove the case and courts determine guilt; no institution can substitute for the whole chain.

**Must-write keywords:** security grid; J&K Police; Army; BSF; MAC; NIA; chain of custody; Lieutenant Governor.

### SESSION 7 — CORE — 2019 constitutional and administrative change

#### Visual

```text
pre-August 2019
Article 370 framework + Article 35A through 1954 Order
                     ↓
5–6 August 2019: C.O. 272 / C.O. 273
                     ↓
31 October 2019: Reorganisation Act effective
  ├─ UT of J&K → legislature
  └─ UT of Ladakh → no legislature
                     ↓
J&K Assembly: State List except Entries 1 and 2
              + applicable Concurrent List
public order/police → outside Assembly competence
```

#### Explanation

Before 2019, Article 370 structured application of the Constitution to J&K, and Article 35A—inserted through the 1954 Presidential Order—enabled the State legislature to define permanent residents and special rights. The August 2019 orders altered that constitutional position; the Reorganisation Act created two Union Territories from 31 October 2019.

The Supreme Court on 11 December 2023 upheld the constitutional actions and directed Assembly elections by 30 September 2024. Elections were held; official reporting recorded 63.88% turnout. As of this review, no official statehood-restoration notification was found, so J&K remains a Union Territory with legislature.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** The operational security consequence of 2019 is not an abstract claim of integration but a changed chain of authority in which police and public order remain outside the elected Assembly's legislative field.

**Must-write keywords:** C.O. 272; C.O. 273; 31 October 2019; UT with legislature; section 32; Entries 1 and 2; statehood status.

### SESSION 8 — CORE — What can and cannot count as “mainstreaming”

#### Visual

```text
legal integration → application of constitutional/statutory framework
political participation → elections, turnout, representative institutions
security → violence/infiltration/recruitment/finance metrics
development → completed access and service use
rights → equality, remedy, accountable policing
rehabilitation → safe voluntary return + livelihood + dignity

one improving dimension ≠ proof of the others or of a single cause
```

#### Explanation

“Mainstreaming” is multidimensional and normatively contested. The 2026 PYQ explicitly asks for positive impacts of the 2019 changes, so a candidate should present verified post-change developments while avoiding unsupported causation. The 2024 Assembly election and its 63.88% turnout support a democratic-participation claim; they do not prove that every political grievance disappeared or that constitutional change alone caused participation.

Similarly, application of central laws is a legal fact; a project is an input; tourist visits are economic activity; lower recorded violence is a security metric; safe return is a rehabilitation outcome. Use each for its own proposition.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Normalcy is a dashboard, not a slogan: political participation, civilian safety, lawful policing, livelihoods, rights and voluntary return must be measured separately.

**Must-write keywords:** legal integration; participation; service delivery; attribution; post hoc; normalcy dashboard; residual risk.

### SESSION 9 — CORE — Hearts and Minds as operational legitimacy

#### Visual

```text
civilian protection + proportionate operations
              ↓
grievance redress + local participation + truthful communication
              ↓
services/livelihoods + youth opportunity
              ↓
rehabilitation/return + remedy for abuse
              ↓
trust → voluntary reporting → weaker recruitment/facilitation
```

#### Explanation

“Hearts and Minds” is not publicity. It means making lawful institutions safer, more responsive and more credible than the coercive network. Security operations protect access; local government and public services make it useful; accountability and remedy make it legitimate.

The approach must distinguish peaceful criticism, alienation, radical belief, facilitation and violent participation. Overbroad labelling can create grievance and reduce information flow. Under-enforcement leaves communities exposed to intimidation. The operational balance is behaviour-based, evidence-led and proportionate.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Trust is not the soft alternative to security in J&K; it is the mechanism through which communities deny concealment, recruitment and legitimacy to violent networks.

**Must-write keywords:** civilian protection; participation; grievance redress; truthful communication; proportionality; voluntary intelligence.

### SESSION 10 — CORE — Development and displaced-person rehabilitation

#### Visual

```text
assistance / project / job / accommodation
                  ↓ output
access + safety + livelihood + schooling + community acceptance
                  ↓ rehabilitation
voluntary durable return or locally chosen settlement
                  ↓ outcome
```

#### Explanation

Historical policy anchors include the Prime Minister's Reconstruction Plan (2004), UDAAN skilling and the 2008 Kashmiri Pandit migrant package. Current names, amounts and completion must come from dated official material.

The 25 March 2026 parliamentary reply records cash assistance of ₹3,250 per person per month subject to ₹13,000 per family, ration support, and 6,000 approved transit accommodations for migrant employees, of which 4,112 had been constructed and 3,257 allotted. These are dated delivery inputs/outputs. They do not establish that every displaced family can or wishes to return safely, or that social and emotional loss has been repaired.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Rehabilitation succeeds when assistance becomes secure agency—a person can choose return or settlement with livelihood, dignity and freedom from renewed coercion.

**Must-write keywords:** displacement; relief; transit accommodation; livelihood; safe voluntary return; choice; dignity; output/outcome.

### SESSION 11 — CORE — Village Defence Guards and the rights boundary

#### Visual

```text
remote-village threat + delayed formal response
                   ↓
VDG local volunteers under district-police framework
  ├─ possible gain: presence, warning, deterrence
  └─ risk: vetting, weapons control, bias, escalation, accountability
                   ↓
training + roster + storage/audit + command + complaint + review
```

#### Explanation

The 2022 Village Defence Guards Scheme superseded the earlier 1995 VDC order. Local knowledge and presence can improve warning and self-protection in remote areas, but arming civilians introduces risks of misuse, communal bias, accidental escalation and blurred command.

The scheme must remain police-supervised, with transparent selection, training, weapon/ammunition accounting, clear activation rules, incident reporting, periodic review and accessible remedies. VDGs supplement; they do not replace professional police.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Community defence can close a response-time gap only when the State retains command, records and accountability over every weapon and deployment.

**Must-write keywords:** VDG Scheme 2022; SP/SSP supervision; vetting; training; weapons audit; supplementary role; accountability.

### SESSION 12 — CORE — J&K AFSPA and lawful coercion

#### Visual

```text
J&K AFSPA, 1990 — separate from AFSPA, 1958
  ├─ s.3 → disturbed-area declaration
  ├─ s.4 → specified operational powers
  ├─ s.6 → arrested persons/property handed to police
  └─ s.7 → previous Central sanction before prosecution
                     ↓
legality + necessity + proportionality + record + investigation + remedy
```

#### Explanation

The J&K statute has a structure parallel to, but section numbering different from, the 1958 North-East Act. This is a classic close-option trap. Exceptional powers respond to armed threat; they do not erase duties of minimum necessary force, hand-over, investigation and accountability.

The correct analysis is not “AFSPA or human rights.” Under-reaction can expose citizens and forces; unlawful or indiscriminate action can deepen alienation, weaken evidence and assist propaganda. Legitimate security joins capability with review.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Exceptional power is sustainable only when its territorial basis, operational use and accountability chain remain visible and reviewable.

**Must-write keywords:** J&K AFSPA 1990; separate statute; sections 3/4/6/7; hand-over; previous sanction; proportionality.

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

### A1. Deniability is the design logic of proxy war

A sponsor gains strategic effect while avoiding the cost and attribution of open war. The response must therefore connect recovered material, communication, finance, training and handler evidence rather than rely on labels. Diplomatic attribution and criminal proof serve different forums.

### A2. Ceasefire violations can be instrumental

The canonical source treats firing as a way to keep the issue active, tie down forces and assist infiltration. Even so, a current incident should not be attributed to that strategy without dated evidence. The broader lesson is to separate deliberate facilitation from routine friction.

### A3. The proxy toolkit adapts

Conventional infiltration can coexist with drones, tunnels, local modules, encrypted messaging, finance and crowd/information tactics. Match the response to the function: border denial, anti-drone capability, financial tracing, community policing, digital evidence and strategic communication.

### A4. 2019 changed operational federalism

UT status may reduce one Centre–State coordination layer, but centralisation can weaken local political ownership if participation, accountability and services do not keep pace. Elections restored an elected Assembly; police and public order nevertheless remain outside its legislative competence.

### A5. Causation discipline for post-2019 claims

A later improvement may be consistent with several causes: security operations, intelligence, ceasefire, changing external conditions, administration, economic activity or political participation. State the verified trend, identify plausible mechanisms and avoid claiming the constitutional change alone caused the outcome without evidence.

### A6. Rehabilitation is a choice-and-safety outcome

Cash, ration, housing or jobs are inputs and outputs. Durable rehabilitation requires safety, livelihood, education, property access, community acceptance, remedy and voluntary choice over return or settlement. A completion percentage cannot represent each dimension.

### A7. Community defence is a governance trade-off

VDGs may improve local presence and warning, but weak vetting, biased deployment or poor weapons control can create harm. Police command, training, records, rotation, complaint and review determine whether the model supports or undermines legitimacy.

### A8. UN and bilateral tracks coexist as contested positions

The UN maintains UNMOGIP; India treats the Simla Agreement as making the issue bilateral and the mission's mandate obsolete. Presenting one institutional position as universally accepted is inaccurate. Operational LoC monitoring, diplomatic doctrine and criminal counter-terror action should remain distinct.

### A9. “Hearts and Minds” requires hard indicators

Test civilian safety, grievance disposal, service uptime, local recruitment, information voluntarily shared, complaint investigation, election participation and rehabilitation—not publicity volume. Trust is demonstrated through behaviour and institutional use.

### A10. Advanced answer method

1. State the precise historical/legal boundary.
2. Define sponsor, proxy, infiltrator, facilitator and local recruit.
3. Trace movement, finance, logistics, information and violence separately.
4. Allocate border, intelligence, police, investigative and judicial roles.
5. Add development, participation, rehabilitation and rights.
6. Use dated, disaggregated metrics and correct status verbs.
7. Conclude with accountable normalcy, not absolute closure.
