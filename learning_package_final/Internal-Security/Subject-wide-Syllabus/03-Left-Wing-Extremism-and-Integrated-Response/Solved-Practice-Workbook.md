## BASIC MCQS / REMEDIATION

### Questions — attempt before consulting the key

#### MCQ 1. Origin versus current footprint

B. Naxalbari is the name of a political doctrine, not a place connected with the movement's origin.
C. A movement's place of origin always remains its operational centre, but district boundaries may change.
D. Naxalism began only after the CPI (Maoist) merger, so an earlier place of origin is impossible.

#### MCQ 2. Two different dates

## PYQS AND ANSWER PRACTICE

The 2018, 2020 and 2022 items below use the audited ledger's **neutral demand rendering**, not invented verbatim wording. The 2025 question is verbatim in the canonical Core owner. Marks and practice lengths follow that ledger; solutions below are independent, question-specific models.

### PYQ 1 — 2018 GS-III, Q10 · 10 marks · 150 words

**Audited demand:** Explain LWE challenges and the Government's counter-strategy.

**Model answer:** Left-Wing Extremism seeks political power through armed Maoist insurgency; it is not a secessionist movement. Its challenge is both coercive and institutional. Armed groups threaten civilians, police and infrastructure, while extortion and obstructed roads, schools and health services weaken public authority. Land alienation, insecure forest rights and absent grievance redress supply exploitable grievances; they do not make every deprived person an insurgent.

The Government's response must therefore join security with legitimate governance. State police, supported by Central capacity and inter-State coordination, protect communities and reopen access. Civil administration must sustain that access through services and accountable local institutions. MHA's National Policy and Action Plan (2015) links security and development with a **third prong**: the rights and entitlements of local communities. FRA-based forest-right recognition and PESA's Fifth Schedule governance safeguards matter here, but enactment alone is not implementation. A decline in violence is progress; durable success requires safe civilian life and functioning, trusted administration.

**Examiner focus:** Connect threat mechanism to an assigned response; do not replace the rights prong with a list of forces.

### PYQ 2 — 2020 GS-III, Q19 · 15 marks · 250 words

**Audited question in Core:** “What are the determinants of left-wing extremism in the Eastern part of India? What strategy should the Government of India, civil administration and security forces adopt to counter the threat in the affected areas?”

**Model answer:** LWE in eastern India cannot be inferred from poverty alone. In affected regions, land alienation, uncertain forest use, weak compensation, poor services and exclusion make it easier for armed organisers to claim legitimacy. Mineral and infrastructure projects may also create extortion opportunities. Conversely, coercion and attacks on connectivity can preserve the underdevelopment the movement claims to oppose. These are interacting determinants, not a deterministic description of every mineral-rich district.

The **Government of India** should coordinate support to States under the 2015 National Policy and Action Plan: security assistance, connectivity and development, and protection of local rights and entitlements. Scheme funding should be assessed by usable roads, communications and services, not sanctions alone. The **civil administration** must remain present after security access is restored: register and hear grievances, sustain schools and healthcare, and implement forest-right recognition under FRA, 2006 and applicable Gram Sabha safeguards under PESA, 1996. Neither Act proves its own delivery.

**State police and supporting security forces** must protect civilians, lawfully secure access and coordinate across State boundaries rather than merely displace violence across a trijunction. The Greyhounds precedent illustrates the value of trained State capacity, not a substitute for civil authority. Clear–hold–develop captures the sequencing: force can open access, but police and civil administration must hold it before rights and services can be delivered. Success should be evaluated through civilian protection and sustained governance as well as incident trends.

**Examiner focus:** Assign distinct roles to all three actors named in the question; state why grievances and coercive organisation must both be addressed.

### PYQ 3 — 2022 GS-III, Q20 · 15 marks · 250 words

**Audited demand:** Discuss Naxalism as an internal-security threat and the required multilayered response.

**Model answer:** Naxalism challenges the State's capacity to protect citizens and sustain constitutional authority. Maoist groups seek power through protracted armed struggle, not a separate sovereign territory. Threats to people and public infrastructure, extortion, and interference with local services and panchayats can establish coercive parallel authority. This is more than an occasional law-and-order incident. Yet recruitment also draws on unresolved land and forest-rights grievances and weak access to justice; economic deprivation alone does not produce insurgency.

A layered response must make security and legitimacy mutually reinforcing. State police, with lawful Central support and inter-State coordination, protect civilians and create safe access; an intelligence lead must still be converted into evidence through due process. District administration then holds the area with continuing police, school, health and grievance-redress presence. Development means services people can use, rather than only road or telecom projects sanctioned on paper. FRA, 2006 offers a route to forest-right recognition, and PESA, 1996 provides local-governance safeguards in applicable Fifth Schedule areas; their implementation must be demonstrated, not presumed.

Finally, surrender is only an event: durable rehabilitation requires safety, livelihoods and social reintegration over time. Public trust depends on accountable conduct toward residents, not only the reduction of incidents. The 2015 national policy's security, development and rights prongs express this logic. The strongest result is a sustained end to coercive parallel authority alongside functioning local self-government.

**Examiner focus:** “Multilayered” requires at least security, civil governance, rights and reintegration, with a reason each layer needs the others.

### PYQ 4 — 2025 GS-III, Q10 · 10 marks · 150 words

**Verified question:** “The Government of India recently stated that Left Wing Extremism (LWE) will be eliminated by 2026. What do you understand by LWE and how are the people affected by it? What measures have been taken by the government to eliminate LWE?”

**Model answer:** LWE is a Maoist armed movement seeking political power through protracted struggle, not secession. It harms civilians through coercion, extortion, disrupted schools and clinics, livelihood insecurity and weakened local governance. State measures must also safeguard civilian rights.

The Government's National Policy and Action Plan (2015) combines **security**, **development** and **rights and entitlements**. State police and supporting forces protect access; roads and communications help restore services. District police and civil officials must then remain accessible: a temporary security gain does not by itself keep services open. FRA, 2006 and PESA, 1996 provide forest-rights and local-governance routes where applicable, but statutes alone do not prove delivery. Surrender-and-rehabilitation measures offer an exit from violence; counting surrenders alone cannot establish reintegration. The stated 2026 deadline is a target, not evidence of completion. Judge progress by civilian safety, functioning services, delivered rights and reduced violence.

**Current-status note (outside the 2025 exam answer):** Core records PIB's qualified June 2026 assessment “effectively free from Left-Wing Extremism”; do not import this later fact into a 2025 answer as though it were known when the paper was set.

### ORIGINAL MAINS PRACTICE WITH MODEL SOLUTIONS

#### Original 1 · 10 marks · about 150 words

**Question:** Explain why LWE is not merely a law-and-order problem.

**Model answer:** Police must prevent violence, but treating LWE solely as an arrest problem mistakes a symptom for the whole mechanism. Maoist groups seek political power through armed insurgency and can impose extortion and parallel authority. Land alienation, insecure forest claims, failures of compensation and inaccessible services create grievances that organisers may exploit; poverty is neither a sufficient cause nor a justification for violence.

Singh's account of obstructed roads and electricity shows a feedback loop: weak governance facilitates coercion, and coercion keeps governance weak. State police can restore safe access, but district administration must remain to resolve claims, provide schools and healthcare, and enable local self-government. MHA's 2015 policy accordingly combines security, development and local rights. FRA and PESA provide relevant rights and procedural routes, not evidence of universal delivery. The measure of success is both fewer harms and credible constitutional governance experienced by residents.

**Why it meets the directive:** Defines the security threat, explains why deprivation and governance matter, then connects each cause to an institutionally appropriate response.

#### Original 2 · 10 marks · about 150 words

**Question:** Describe how people are affected by LWE and identify the State's immediate duties.

**Model answer:** Civilians may face threats, extortion and displacement; attacks or coercive interference can deny access to schools, clinics, livelihoods, roads and local decision-making. Residents also need protection from excessive or indiscriminate State action. An incident count therefore cannot describe the entire human cost.

The State's immediate duty is lawful civilian protection, including safe access to essential services and an accountable route to report harm. State police and district officials must coordinate so that a protected route is followed by working healthcare, education and grievance offices rather than a temporary presence. Relevant land and forest claims should be heard through lawful processes, including FRA recognition and applicable PESA safeguards; neither statutory enactment nor a project announcement proves delivery. For those leaving armed groups, safety must be followed by livelihood and reintegration support. Restoring trust requires residents to encounter the State as a reliable protector, not simply as a force that passes through.

**Why it meets the directive:** Separates the forms of harm from distinct immediate protection, service and rights duties.

#### Original 3 · 15 marks · about 250 words

**Question:** Analyse the determinants of LWE in eastern India and assign roles to Government, civil administration and security forces.

**Model answer:** The presence of an armed organisation, not poverty by itself, turns grievances into sustained insurgency. Land alienation, insecure forest access, poor compensation, weak service provision and exclusion can make recruitment claims more credible. In mineral-rich areas, infrastructure and extraction also create extortion opportunities. Organised interference with roads and administration can then reproduce the very deprivation on which recruitment feeds. The determinants are thus structural *and* strategic; they cannot be reduced to a single district characteristic.

The Union government's role is to support coordination across States and resource the 2015 national policy's security, development and community-rights prongs, evaluating implementation rather than allocations alone. State governments must enable lawful policing and administrative follow-through, including coherent approaches across shared boundaries. District civil administration should hear land and forest claims, maintain health and education facilities, and implement relevant FRA and PESA processes rather than assuming legislation equals local benefit.

State police and supporting security forces protect residents and access to infrastructure within legal safeguards. **Question:** Examine the LWE response through clear–hold–develop, inter-State coordination and surrender-to-rehabilitation outcomes.

**Model answer:** These three lenses address different failure points in an otherwise integrated response. **Clear–hold–develop** starts by establishing lawful access where armed coercion has impeded civilian movement. Development is effective only when a road, school or clinic can be used safely and people can assert rights. **Inter-State coordination** matters because administrative boundaries need not contain a mobile armed organisation. The historical Dandakaranya experience and the Greyhounds precedent show both the value of capable State policing and the risk that a one-State success becomes displacement into a neighbour. Coordination should therefore be judged by regional civilian protection and compatible governance follow-through, not a single district's falling incident count. Civil administration remains responsible for FRA forest-right procedures and applicable PESA local-governance safeguards; a police operation cannot decide a land claim.

**Surrender and rehabilitation** must also be separated. The act of surrender is countable, whereas protection from retaliation, skills, livelihoods and social reintegration require sustained observation. A scheme announcement or payment is not by itself proof of reintegration. This distinction matters to recruitment: an unsafe return can weaken public trust in future offers to leave violence.

The three lenses reinforce one another. If clearing is followed by an administrative vacuum, if violence shifts across a border, or if a surrender is mistaken for a completed life transition, aggregate security improvement may be fragile. A credible end-state joins lower coercion to accessible services, delivered rights and sustained reintegration, assessed with dated and appropriately scoped evidence.
