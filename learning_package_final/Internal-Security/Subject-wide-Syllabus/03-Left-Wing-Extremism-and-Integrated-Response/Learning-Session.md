### Evidence limits

1. Historical cadre strengths, district counts, leadership descriptions and force posture in the textbook are not presented as current.
2. Official decline figures are security indicators, not proof that dispossession, weak services or justice deficits have ended.
3. “Effectively free” is retained as an attributed official assessment, not an unqualified analytical verdict.
4. A district's inclusion or removal from an administrative list changes scheme/resource treatment; it does not itself prove durable normalcy.
5. Surrender is an event; rehabilitation and reintegration are longitudinal outcomes.

### SESSION 1 — FOUNDATION — Naxalbari, Maoism and the political objective

#### Visual

```text
Naxalbari area, Darjeeling district — May 1967
          ├─ Charu Majumdar
          └─ Kanu Sanyal
                   ↓
Maoist protracted people's war
                   ↓
armed insurgency + mass mobilisation + strategic alliances
                   ↓
capture/replacement of State power; not a secessionist project
```

#### Explanation

“Naxalism” derives from the Naxalbari, Khoribari and Phansidewa police-station areas of Darjeeling district, West Bengal, where the movement began in May 1967 under leaders including Charu Majumdar and Kanu Sanyal. Left-Wing Extremism is the wider current umbrella for Maoist-inspired armed violence.

The canonical ideological objective is to capture State power through a “Protracted People's War,” combining armed insurgency, mass mobilisation and alliances; the source associates it with Mao's proposition that political power grows out of the barrel of a gun and treats bearing arms as non-negotiable. This distinguishes LWE from separatist movements seeking a different sovereign State: LWE contests who governs India and by what revolutionary order.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** LWE is not secessionism at India's edge; it is a Maoist project to displace constitutional authority from within through protracted armed struggle.

**Must-write keywords:** Naxalbari; May 1967; Maoism; protracted people's war; armed struggle; mass mobilisation; capture of State power.

**Answer paragraph:** *Claim* — ideology determines the movement's end-state. *Evidence* — the canonical account identifies capture of State power, not territorial secession. *Analysis* — counter-insurgency must therefore contest parallel authority and mobilisation, not merely guard a border. *Qualification* — current organisational strength cannot be inferred from its historical doctrine.

### SESSION 2 — FOUNDATION — Organisation and five-phase expansion

#### Visual

```text
Preparatory → Perspective → Guerrilla → Base → Liberated phase
     |            |            |          |          |
  survey       organise     mobile war   durable    claimed
  terrain      support      /cells       influence  control
```

#### Explanation

The five-phase strategy is an organisational theory of expansion, not a verified map of present control. It shows why LWE combines political work, front activity, recruitment, guerrilla violence and attempts at parallel governance.

CPI (Maoist) was formed in 2004 through the merger of the People's War Group and Maoist Communist Centre of India. Current status must be stated legally: CPI (Maoist) is listed in the First Schedule to UAPA. Listing does not establish the guilt of any named person without evidence and adjudication.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Maoist violence is the armed edge of a staged political-organisational strategy; dismantling a squad is not identical to dismantling its recruitment and authority system.

**Must-write keywords:** five phases; front organisation; guerrilla; base; parallel authority; CPI (Maoist); UAPA First Schedule.

### SESSION 3 — CORE — Structural causes without deterministic poverty

#### Visual

| Structural condition | Security relevance | Necessary qualification |
|---|---|---|
| insecure land tenure / failed ceiling implementation | grievance and weak State legitimacy | poverty does not automatically produce LWE |
| forest-rights exclusion | livelihood and identity conflict | statutory right ≠ delivered right |
| acquisition/displacement without fair process | disruption and distrust | each project and claim needs evidence |
| mineral extraction with weak benefit-sharing | extortion opportunity and local grievance | minerals do not mechanically cause violence |
| poor roads, services and administration | vacuum for parallel authority | connectivity without accountable delivery can disappoint |
| exclusion, unemployment and justice deficits | recruitment susceptibility | organisation converts grievance into mobilisation |

#### Explanation

The canonical cause-set includes land ceiling evasion, encroachment on government/community land, non-regularisation of traditional rights, acquisition without appropriate compensation, disruption of *jal–jangal–zameen*, poverty, unemployment, infrastructure deficits, governance failure, exclusion and alienation. Its mineral-belt illustration names coal, iron, bauxite, manganese, nickel and copper as resources around which extraction and extortion opportunities may arise.

These are vulnerabilities, not automatic causes. Maoist organisation, coercion, ideology and extortion convert them into a security challenge. This dual explanation avoids blaming affected communities and avoids romanticising the armed movement.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Grievance supplies susceptibility; organisation, coercion and a political project convert it into sustained extremism.

**Must-write keywords:** land; forest; displacement; mineral belt; governance vacuum; alienation; organised conversion.

### SESSION 4 — CORE — Tribal, land, forest and mining dimensions

#### Visual

```text
customary use / habitation / community resources
                     ↓
FRA 2006: recognition process begins through Gram Sabha
                     +
PESA 1996 in Fifth Schedule areas
  ├─ consultation before land acquisition/resettlement
  └─ mandatory recommendation for specified minor-mineral concessions
                     ↓
delivery gap → distrust / litigation / displacement risk
                     ↓
rights-compliant administration reduces exploitable vulnerability
```

#### Explanation

FRA and PESA address different dimensions. FRA recognises specified individual and community forest rights and gives the Gram Sabha a central role in initiating determination. PESA adapts Panchayat governance to Scheduled Areas, protects customary resources and requires consultation before land acquisition; its recommendation is mandatory for specified minor-mineral licences, leases and auction concessions.

Mining can create two simultaneous pathways: legitimate economic opportunity and, where consultation, compensation, rehabilitation or benefit-sharing fail, grievance and coercive space. Maoist extortion of contractors and projects is an organisational strategy; it should not be described as community consent.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** In LWE areas, rights delivery is security capacity because lawful control of land, forests and local decision-making contests the legitimacy of parallel authority.

**Must-write keywords:** FRA 2006; PESA 1996; Fifth Schedule; Gram Sabha; consultation; minor-mineral recommendation; compensation; benefit-sharing.

**UPSC trap:** enactment is not implementation; consultation is not identical to consent; minor forest produce is not the same as minor minerals.

### SESSION 5 — CORE — Instrumental underdevelopment and parallel governance

#### Visual

```text
weak administration / inaccessible services
             ↓
coercive entry + parallel taxation / Jan Adalat / intimidation
             ↓
extortion from roads, mining and contractors
             ↓
obstruction of roads/electricity/water/governance
             ↓
continuing isolation → easier recruitment and control
```

#### Explanation

Underdevelopment is both an enabling condition and, in the canonical account, a condition the movement may deliberately maintain. Extortion is easier in resource-rich project areas, while obstruction of roads, electricity, water supply and local institutions can preserve isolation and dependence.

Parallel taxation or “justice” is coercive authority, not evidence of democratic legitimacy. The canonical book-period example of Panchayat representatives being forced to resign, along with intimidation, infrastructure damage and restricted movement, shows how the movement can weaken constitutional governance and increase costs for the population it claims to defend.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** LWE can feed on deprivation while reproducing it; the State must therefore defeat both coercive organisation and the administrative vacuum it exploits.

**Must-write keywords:** instrumental underdevelopment; extortion economy; parallel taxation; Jan Adalat; infrastructure obstruction; governance vacuum.

### SESSION 6 — CORE — How people are affected

#### Visual

| Dimension | Harm to affected people |
|---|---|
| life and security | killings, intimidation, forced recruitment and exposure to operations |
| development | damaged roads, schools, communications and blocked public works |
| livelihood | extortion, disrupted markets, forest/land insecurity and displacement |
| democracy | coerced voting, weakened Panchayats and fear-driven silence |
| rights and justice | abuse by armed groups and risk of disproportionate State action |
| future opportunity | interrupted education, health access, mobility and investment |

#### Explanation

The civilian is not a passive background to an LWE contest. People may face Maoist coercion, State-security operations, extortion, infrastructure destruction, displacement and delayed services. A balanced answer identifies both the insurgent's agency and the State's duty to use lawful, proportionate force.

“People affected” also includes families of victims, surrendered cadres requiring protection, local officials, elected representatives and communities whose everyday choices are constrained by fear. Development must create real access and voice, not merely expenditure.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** The decisive measure of normalcy is whether residents can use roads, schools, markets, Gram Sabhas, police and courts without coercion from either armed actors or unaccountable authority.

**Must-write keywords:** civilian protection; coercion; service denial; displacement; local democracy; access; dignity.

### SESSION 7 — CORE — Integrated policy: security, development and rights

#### Visual

```text
NATIONAL POLICY AND ACTION PLAN, 2015
    ├─ SECURITY → protect people, build police/intelligence capacity
    ├─ DEVELOPMENT → roads, telecom, banking, education, health, skills
    └─ RIGHTS/ENTITLEMENTS → land, forest, representation, justice
                     +
TRUST/COMMUNICATION → administration + civil society + credible grievance redress
```

#### Explanation

MHA's 2015 policy is explicitly multi-pronged: security measures, development interventions, and rights and entitlements of local communities. The canonical source separately identifies psychological operations—better understood as legitimate communication, trust restoration and engagement rather than manipulation.

Security creates access; administration holds the space; services and rights make the gain durable. Development without protection may be obstructed or captured. Security without rights can produce alienation. Rights without capable administration may remain paper entitlements.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** The integrated response is not “security plus welfare”; it is a sequence in which lawful security opens space, administration holds it, and rights-based development makes constitutional authority preferable.

**Must-write keywords:** three-pronged policy; rights and entitlements; trust restoration; convergence; area-specific delivery.

### SESSION 8 — CORE — Clear, hold and develop

#### Visual

```text
CLEAR                         HOLD                         DEVELOP
remove immediate         resident police/civil       rights + services +
coercive threat          administration + roads      livelihoods + voice
      ↓                         ↓                          ↓
temporary access         durable State presence      legitimate normalcy

failure at HOLD → cadres return / services cannot function / gains reverse
```

#### Explanation

Clearing is an operational capability. Holding requires permanent, competent and accountable police and civil presence. Developing requires functioning schools, health services, roads, telecom, banking, livelihoods and rights institutions. The middle step is frequently decisive because mobile forces can leave before civil administration becomes reliable.

District de-listing is an administrative classification that can affect scheme eligibility and force allocation. It should trigger calibrated tapering and monitoring, not an inference that grievances or residual networks have disappeared.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Territory is not sustainably recovered when a force passes through; it is recovered when ordinary institutions can remain, deliver and command voluntary public cooperation.

**Must-write keywords:** clear; hold; develop; administrative presence; reversibility; tapering; durable normalcy.

### SESSION 9 — CORE — Security architecture and inter-State coordination

#### Visual

```text
State police / specialised State units → local lead
                ↕
CRPF / CoBRA → central assistance and jungle-warfare capability
                ↕
intelligence + unified planning + common operating picture
                ↓
boundary/trijunction pursuit and hold plan across States
```

#### Explanation

The Dandakaranya illustration spans Chhattisgarh, Odisha, Maharashtra and the historical undivided-Andhra operational space. Pressure in one State can displace cadres into another if intelligence, pursuit, surrender policy and area-holding plans differ.

Greyhounds demonstrate the value of a dedicated, trained State force with local terrain knowledge. CoBRA and locally recruited formations such as the Bastariya Battalion add specialised capacity, but central force deployment cannot replace State-police intelligence, investigation or civil administration.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** An LWE theatre follows forests and networks rather than State boundaries; the response must be locally led but operationally interoperable across them.

**Must-write keywords:** trijunction; displacement effect; Greyhounds; CRPF; CoBRA; Bastariya Battalion; unified planning; State-police primacy.

### SESSION 10 — CORE — SAMADHAN and scheme architecture

#### Visual

```text
S  Smart Policing and Leadership
A  Aggressive Strategy
M  Motivation and Training
A  Actionable Intelligence
D  Dashboard for Development and Key Performance Indicators
H  Harnessing Technology for Development and Security
A  Action Plan for Each Theatre
N  No Access to Financing
```

#### Explanation

SAMADHAN, announced in 2017, is a strategic mnemonic, not a standalone spending scheme. It converts the integrated doctrine into operational questions: leadership, intelligence, training, technology, theatre-specific planning, development dashboards and financial denial.

Scheme instruments serve different functions: SRE reimburses eligible security-related expenditure, SIS supports special forces/intelligence and infrastructure, Special Central Assistance fills critical public-infrastructure gaps, road and mobile projects improve access, fortified police stations support holding capacity, and Civic Action Programme supports local engagement. A sanction or outlay is an input; completed access and trusted use are delivery and outcome questions.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** SAMADHAN is useful only when its letters become a coordinated district plan rather than eight disconnected slogans.

**Must-write keywords:** SAMADHAN; SRE; SIS; Special Central Assistance; road connectivity; mobile towers; fortified police stations; Civic Action Programme.

### SESSION 11 — CORE — Surrender, rehabilitation and trust restoration

#### Visual

```text
surrender (verifiable event)
        ↓ screening + legal processing + immediate protection
rehabilitation (skills, livelihood, safety, counselling)
        ↓
reintegration (social acceptance + non-return + lawful citizenship)
```

#### Explanation

Surrender produces a countable event; rehabilitation is a multi-year process. State packages can differ, and inconsistent approaches may be exploited through movement across borders. Screening is needed to distinguish genuine disengagement from tactical surrender, while protection is needed against reprisal.

Trust restoration also requires grievance access, conduct accountability, culturally competent administration and credible communication. The canonical shift from project-oriented to individual-oriented civic action illustrates that infrastructure and personal welfare address different needs; neither alone proves public legitimacy.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** A surrendered weapon is an output; a former cadre living safely and lawfully without re-recruitment is the rehabilitation outcome.

**Must-write keywords:** surrender; screening; rehabilitation; reintegration; protection from reprisal; recidivism; trust.

### SESSION 12 — CORE SYNTHESIS — Current decline without premature victory

#### Visual

```text
MHA December 2025:
1,936 incidents (2010) → 218 (2025): reported 89% decline
1,005 deaths   (2010) →  93 (2025): reported 91% decline
                     ↓
PIB June 2026: “effectively free from Left-Wing Extremism”
                     ↓
retain qualification
≠ zero residual risk
≠ completed FRA/PESA delivery
≠ completed rehabilitation
≠ irreversible constitutional governance
```

#### Explanation

The official decline is substantial and should be acknowledged. The canonical file also preserves an earlier MHA **2024 baseline**—73% lower violence, 86% lower deaths and 38 affected districts—which must not be re-dated as a 2026 result. Security incidence, death and district-classification data answer limited questions; they do not directly measure title recognition, Gram Sabha voice, service quality, extortion pressure, rehabilitation or hidden network capacity.

The 2025 examination stem reproduced the Government's then-stated 2026 elimination objective. Later PIB material used “effectively free,” which is an attributed, qualified official assessment. An answer written after that date should neither repeat the target as pending nor convert the later phrase into literal zero risk.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** India's LWE achievement is a major reduction in organised violence; its durability will be proved by whether constitutional administration, rights and livelihoods remain after exceptional security support recedes.

**Must-write keywords:** 2010 baseline; 2025 measured decline; effectively free; residual risk; root-cause resolution; reversibility.

### Historical examples and status discipline

- The 2010 Dantewada ambush, in which 76 CRPF personnel were killed, and the 2013 Sukma attack, in which 27 people including senior political leaders were killed, are canonical **historical capability examples**, not current force-strength evidence.
- “Red Corridor” and book-period Dandakaranya descriptions are useful geographic concepts, not current official district lists.
- Current casualty, cadre, surrender, district, road, tower or allocation figures require a dated official source.

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

### A1. Instrumental underdevelopment

Maoists may benefit from the deprivation they invoke by obstructing roads, utilities and administration and extracting rent from projects. This corrects a one-way causal story: underdevelopment can aid recruitment, while the organisation can also sustain underdevelopment.

### A2. Security-first versus development-first

The defensible synthesis is sequencing, not a binary. Some protection and access are prerequisites for delivery; holding and rights-based administration should follow rapidly so coercive gains do not become occupation without legitimacy. Different districts can require different sequences.

### A3. Clear, hold and develop are distinct capabilities

Clear is tactical; hold is institutional; develop is distributive and rights-based. Incident decline primarily supports a security claim. Resident administration, functioning Gram Sabhas and accessible services support a holding claim. Rights realisation and resilient livelihoods support a development claim.

### A4. Inter-State displacement and policy arbitrage

Differing pursuit, surrender and negotiation approaches can shift cadres and incentives across boundaries. Joint intelligence, compatible protocols and theatre plans are necessary, but States still need room to adapt rehabilitation to local conditions.

### A5. Rights delivery as counter-insurgency capacity

FRA and PESA are not welfare footnotes. They shape whether land, forest and mineral decisions are experienced as lawful and participatory. Their weak implementation can reopen the legitimacy gap even after violence falls.

### A6. Rehabilitation quality

Count surrender, screening, legal disposition, skills, livelihood, protection, social acceptance and non-return separately. Generous immediate benefits without protection or viable work can produce tactical surrender or re-recruitment; punitive exclusion can deter genuine exit.

### A7. Psychological operations versus trustworthy communication

Strategic communication should rebut coercive propaganda with verifiable delivery and accessible grievance mechanisms. Propaganda unsupported by experience damages credibility. Trust is earned through conduct, not manufactured by publicity.

### A8. De-listing and tapering

Because district classification directs resources, abrupt withdrawal after de-listing can weaken the holding phase. A taper based on residual network, service and rights indicators is analytically safer than treating the administrative change as final victory.

### A9. Current-status evidence ladder

```text
target announced → period-end administrative assessment
       → recorded incidents/deaths → residual network evidence
       → rights/service/reintegration outcomes → durable normalcy
```

The December 2025 figures and June 2026 wording occupy different rungs. Neither alone proves every later rung.

### A10. Advanced answer method

1. Define LWE and separate it from secessionism.
2. State ideology and organisational strategy.
3. Distinguish structural grievance from organisational conversion.
4. Explain civilian, governance and development effects.
5. Apply security–hold–development–rights sequencing.
6. Allocate roles across State police, administration and Union support.
7. Add surrender/reintegration and inter-State coordination.
8. Grade current claims and conclude on durable constitutional governance.

## CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

### Origin, ideology and organisation

- Naxalbari, Khoribari and Phansidewa areas, Darjeeling district, May 1967; Charu Majumdar and Kanu Sanyal.
- LWE/Naxalism: Maoist armed strategy to capture State power, not secession; “political power grows out of the barrel of a gun” is the canonical ideological slogan.
- Protracted people's war: armed insurgency + mass mobilisation + alliances.
- Five phases: Preparatory → Perspective → Guerrilla → Base → Liberated.
- CPI (Maoist): 2004 merger of PWG and MCCI; listed in UAPA First Schedule.

### Cause–organisation distinction

- Structural: land tenure, forest rights, displacement/compensation, mineral governance, poverty, unemployment, infrastructure and justice deficits.
- Conversion: cadre organisation, ideology, coercion, recruitment, extortion and parallel authority.
- Poverty is neither necessary nor sufficient; organisation matters.
- Instrumental underdevelopment: the movement can obstruct development and maintain the condition it exploits.

### Tribal, land, forest and mining

- FRA 2006: recognition of specified individual/community forest rights; Gram Sabha initiates determination.
- PESA 1996: Scheduled-Area self-governance; consultation before land acquisition/resettlement; mandatory recommendation for specified minor-mineral concessions.
- Consultation ≠ consent; minor forest produce ≠ minor minerals; enactment ≠ delivery.
- Coal, iron, bauxite, manganese, nickel and copper illustrate the mineral-belt link; mining can create livelihood and revenue, grievance and extortion opportunities simultaneously.

### People affected

- Violence, intimidation, recruitment and competing coercion.
- Extortion, displacement and livelihood disruption.
- Damage/denial affecting roads, electricity, water, schools and health.
- Weakened Panchayats, justice access and freedom of political choice.

### Integrated response

- MHA National Policy and Action Plan, 2015: security + development + rights/entitlements.
- Canonical three tracks: security + development + trust/psychological engagement.
- Clear → hold → develop; the holding gap drives reversibility.
- SAMADHAN: Smart Policing and Leadership; Aggressive Strategy; Motivation and Training; Actionable Intelligence; Dashboard for Development and KPIs; Harnessing Technology; Action Plan for Each Theatre; No Access to Financing.
- Schemes/instruments: SRE, SIS, Special Central Assistance, road/mobile connectivity, fortified police stations and Civic Action Programme.
- Forces: State police lead locally; Greyhounds model; CRPF/CoBRA and Bastariya Battalion add specialised capacity.

### Federal and reintegration safeguards

- Coordinate across trijunctions; avoid displacement into a weaker neighbouring jurisdiction.
- Surrender = event; rehabilitation = protected multi-year process; reintegration = durable outcome.
- Operations require legality, necessity, proportionality, civilian protection and accountability.
- Development outlay ≠ access; district de-listing ≠ grievance resolution.

### Current status

- MHA December 2025: 1,936 incidents/1,005 deaths in 2010 versus 218 incidents/93 deaths in 2025; reported declines 89% and 91%.
- Earlier MHA 2024 baseline in the owner: violence −73%, deaths −86%, 38 affected districts; do not relabel it as a 2026 result.
- PIB June 2026: “effectively free from Left-Wing Extremism” — retain quotation and attribution.
- Do not infer zero incidents, zero residual capability, completed rights delivery or irreversible peace.

### PYQ answer spine

```text
definition/objective → causes + organisational conversion
→ impact on people/governance → security + hold + development + rights
→ coordination + rehabilitation → dated metrics
→ residual-risk and legitimacy qualification
```

### Qualified conclusion

LWE's decline is a major security achievement, but the durable end-state is not a target date or district label. It is the disappearance of coercive parallel authority alongside functioning Gram Sabhas, trusted police, accessible justice, delivered rights and livelihoods that persist without exceptional force protection.
