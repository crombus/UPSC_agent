### SESSION 1 — IDENTITY: WHAT NITI AAYOG LEGALLY IS

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** NITI Aayog is an executive policy institution created by Union Cabinet resolution on 1 January 2015.

**Technical definition:** It is non-constitutional and non-statutory, with recommendatory rather than legislative, fiscal-transfer or coercive authority.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> NITI Aayog’s legal identity explains both its policy flexibility and its limits.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **NITI Aayog**
- **1 January 2015**
- **Cabinet resolution**
- **executive body**
- **non-constitutional**
- **non-statutory**
- **recommendatory**
- **no fund allocation**

**How to use them:** Frame the answer through NITI Aayog; define 1 January 2015, connect Cabinet resolution with executive body to explain the mechanism, and use non-constitutional for the decisive comparison or qualification.

[FACT] NITI Aayog is the **National Institution for Transforming India**. The Union Cabinet constituted it by resolution on **1 January 2015**, replacing the Planning Commission.

[FACT] It is **non-constitutional** because no constitutional Article creates it. It is **non-statutory** because no Act of Parliament creates it. It is an **executive institution** because its source is a Union Cabinet resolution.

[ANALYSIS] “Extra-constitutional” is sometimes used descriptively for an institution outside the constitutional text, but “unconstitutional” would be wrong. The executive may create advisory and coordinating institutions within its authority.

[LIMIT] Executive creation means its composition and internal architecture can be changed more easily than those of a constitutional or statutory body. It does not mean that every recommendation it makes automatically becomes law.

### SESSION 2 — WHY WAS THE INSTITUTIONAL SHIFT ATTEMPTED?

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** The shift sought a more federal, flexible and evidence-oriented development-policy institution.

**Technical definition:** The 2015 resolution replaced directive resource planning with shared vision, bottom-up inputs, strategy, coordination, monitoring and knowledge support.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> India changed the planning institution because economic diversity and federal governance no longer suited a uniform command model.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **Planning Commission**
- **institutional shift**
- **cooperative federalism**
- **bottom-up planning**
- **strategy**
- **monitoring**
- **2015 resolution**

**How to use them:** Frame the answer through Planning Commission; define institutional shift, connect cooperative federalism with bottom-up planning to explain the mechanism, and use strategy for the decisive comparison or qualification.

[FACT] The 2015 resolution described a need to replace centralised planning assumptions with an institution suited to a diverse, market-linked and federal polity.

[FACT] The official objectives include a shared vision of national priorities with active State involvement, credible plans developed from village level upward, long-term strategic frameworks, monitoring and feedback, inter-sectoral coordination, attention to vulnerable sections, knowledge partnerships, technology and capacity building.

[ANALYSIS] The central shift was from **directive resource planning** to **indicative strategy and coordination**. It sought to treat States as policy partners rather than only recipients of plan approval and assistance.

[LIMIT] A bottom-up mandate does not itself prove bottom-up practice. Agenda-setting, information asymmetry and fiscal dependence can still make engagement Union-led.

### SESSION 5 — GOVERNING COUNCIL: COOPERATIVE FORUM, NOT A SECOND CHAMBER

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** Official Governing Council wording includes Chief Ministers of all States; Chief Ministers of Union Territories with legislatures; and Lieutenant Governors or Administrators of the other Union Territories as the relevant notification provides.

**Technical definition:** Its decisions or consensus do not become statutes, taxes or enforceable State duties merely because the meeting was chaired by the Prime Minister.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Official Governing Council wording includes Chief Ministers of all States; Chief Ministers of Union Territories with legislatures; and Lieutenant Governors or Administrators of the other Union Territories as the relevant notification provides.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **Governing Council**
- **Cooperative Forum**
- **Not A Second Chamber**
- **executive-resolution framework**
- **Constitution**
- **membership basis**

**How to use them:** Frame the answer through Governing Council; define Cooperative Forum, connect Not A Second Chamber with executive-resolution framework to explain the mechanism, and use Constitution for the decisive comparison or qualification.

[FACT] Official Governing Council wording includes Chief Ministers of all States; Chief Ministers of Union Territories with legislatures; and Lieutenant Governors or Administrators of the other Union Territories as the relevant notification provides. NITI’s Vice-Chairperson, full-time members, ex-officio members and notified special invitees also participate within the official design.

[FACT] Its institutional purpose is to evolve a shared vision of national priorities and strategies with active involvement of States and Union Territories.

[ANALYSIS] The Council gives development policy a high-level Union-State forum. Its political authority comes from participation by heads of government, not from a power to enact law.

[LIMIT] It is not Rajya Sabha, not a constitutional legislature and not a treaty-making body. Its decisions or consensus do not become statutes, taxes or enforceable State duties merely because the meeting was chaired by the Prime Minister.

### Verified routed PYQ 1 - UPSC GS-III 2018, Q11 - 15 marks, 250 words

**Verified question:** “How are the principles followed by the NITI Aayog different from those followed by the erstwhile Planning Commission in India?”

**Directive decode:** “How different” requires a criterion-based comparison, not two disconnected descriptions. “Principles” prioritises philosophy, federal method, planning logic, resource role and institutional influence. A high-scoring answer must also note continuity in legal status.

### Evidence-led model solution

NITI Aayog and the erstwhile Planning Commission were both created by Union Cabinet resolutions and were therefore non-constitutional and non-statutory. The major change was not legal form but governing principle: from directive resource planning to advisory, cooperative and evidence-based coordination.

First, the Planning Commission worked through centrally framed Five-Year Plans and State plan negotiations; NITI’s 2015 resolution instead emphasises a shared national vision, active State participation and planning from the village level upward. This changes States from negotiated recipients within a central plan to partners in the Governing Council. The qualification is that agenda-setting and fiscal dependence can still make consultation Union-led.

Second, the Commission combined plan formulation with substantial resource-allocation influence. NITI has no corresponding authority to allocate plan grants; constitutional devolution remains with the Finance Commission process and budgetary control with competent governments. NITI therefore relies on persuasion rather than purse strings.

Third, fixed Five-Year Plans gave way to flexible strategies, missions, policy reports and feedback-based correction. DMEO, the SDG Index and Aspirational Districts illustrate monitoring, evaluation and competitive federalism. Yet rankings can suffer from data lag, gaming and indicator bias.

Fourth, the Commission’s relationship with the National Development Council centred on national plan approval, while NITI’s Governing Council is a continuing development-policy forum whose consensus is not law.

Thus, the shift is from command, allocation and uniform planning toward strategy, cooperative and competitive federalism, knowledge and outcomes. It gains flexibility and State dialogue but loses direct fiscal leverage; its success depends on credibility, participation and implementation follow-through.

**Why this earns marks:** The model compares principles by common criteria, names the legal-status continuity, uses programme evidence and evaluates the loss of fiscal leverage.

**How to improve/compress:** Use a six-row comparison—source, federal method, planning horizon, resource role, monitoring instrument and limitation—then close with the persuasion-versus-purse trade-off.

### M4. 15 marks, 250 words - “Competitive federalism can improve performance, but it can also corrupt measurement.” Discuss with reference to NITI Aayog.

### Demand

Explain the incentive mechanism, benefits, Goodhart risk and safeguards.

### Model solution

Competitive federalism uses comparison among States, districts or blocks to create reputational and administrative incentives. NITI Aayog operationalises it through indices, peer benchmarking and delta rankings.

[FACT] The Aspirational Districts model combines convergence, collaboration and competition, while delta ranking highlights incremental improvement. [ANALYSIS] This can motivate an initially weak district because recent progress, not only absolute level, gains visibility. Public comparison can also direct political attention and spread replicable practice. [LIMIT] delta improvement does not mean the district has reached an adequate absolute standard.

[FACT] The SDG India Index translates multiple development goals into comparable subnational indicators. [ANALYSIS] A common framework can expose gaps and encourage States to learn from peers. [LIMIT] a composite rank depends on data coverage, indicator selection, normalisation and weights; small rank differences may be substantively meaningless.

## CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

### 1. Identity and legal boundary

- **Full form:** National Institution for Transforming India.
- **Created:** Union Cabinet Resolution, **1 January 2015**.
- **Nature:** executive, non-constitutional, non-statutory.
- **Replaced:** Planning Commission; did **not** replace Finance Commission, GST Council, ISC, SFCs or local bodies.
- **Cannot by itself:** legislate, tax, bind States, divide the tax pool, allocate constitutional grants or enact a Governing Council consensus.
- **Answer line:** “NITI’s influence is strategic, coordinative and informational; legal implementation remains with the competent authority.”

### 2. Foundational mandate

- shared national priorities with active State participation;
- credible planning from village level upward;
- strategic and long-term policy frameworks;
- monitoring, evaluation, feedback and mid-course correction;
- inter-sectoral and inter-departmental coordination;
- special attention to vulnerable sections;
- knowledge, innovation and external partnerships;
- technology upgradation and capacity building.
- **Trap:** broad mandate does not equal enforceable jurisdiction.

### 3. Composition recall

- Prime Minister: Chairperson.
- Governing Council: State CMs; CMs of UTs with legislatures; LGs/Administrators of other UTs as notified; relevant NITI categories.
- Vice-Chairperson: appointed by PM.
- Full-time members.
- Part-time members: up to two under foundational resolution, rotational academic/research source.
- Ex-officio Union Ministers: up to four under foundational resolution; July 2024 order listed four.
- CEO: PM-appointed, fixed tenure, Secretary rank under resolution.
- Special invitees: separate category; do not merge with ex-officio members.
- **Current control:** omit officeholder names unless checked on the exact exam date.

### 4. Governing and Regional Councils

- Governing Council = apex federal development-policy forum.
- It is not Rajya Sabha, a legislature or a binding adjudicatory body.
- Consensus -> competent Union/State action -> law/rule/notification/budget where required.
- Regional Councils = specific multi-State/regional issue + specified tenure.
- Regional Council is not Article 263 ISC and not statutory Zonal Council.

### 5. Planning Commission comparison

| Head | Planning Commission | NITI Aayog |
|---|---|---|
| source | 1950 Cabinet resolution | 2015 Cabinet resolution |
| status | non-constitutional/non-statutory | same |
| method | Five-Year Plans, top-down tendency | flexible strategy, bottom-up mandate |
| State role | plan negotiation/recipient within central framework | Governing Council partner |
| finance | plan-resource allocation influence | no plan-grant allocation |
| instrument | plan and outlay | advice, data, M&E, coordination |
| leverage | purse plus plan approval | persuasion and reputation |

- **NDC:** plan-approval role effectively lapsed; do not invent formal statutory abolition.
- **Twelfth Plan:** 2012-17, final Five-Year Plan.
- **Plan/non-plan:** abolished in Union Budget 2017-18; do not attribute solely to NITI.

### 6. Cooperative and competitive federalism

- Cooperative = joint diagnosis, shared priority, role allocation, review.
- Tools: Governing Council, Regional Councils, State Support Mission, knowledge support.
- Competitive = common indicators, ranking, delta, peer learning, reputation.
- Best design: cooperation sets fair rules; competition operates within them.
- Limits: central agenda-setting, uneven participation, gaming, capacity inequality and non-binding follow-up.

### 7. State and local linkage

- State Support Mission supports State visions, analytics, capacity and institutional engagement.
- State Institutions for Transformation may be new or strengthened existing bodies; not constitutionally mandatory.
- Success test: mandate, staffing, data, budget link and action tracking.
- Local bodies retain constitutional/State-law roles under Parts IX and IX-A.
- Aspirational district/block administration is not a substitute for elected decentralised planning.

### 8. Programme mechanism cards

- **ADP:** convergence + collaboration + competition; indicators; delta improvement; district focus.
- **ABP:** granular block-level extension; reveals intra-district gaps; higher data/capacity burden.
- **SDG Index:** localisation and comparison; always cite edition; no causation from rank.
- **DMEO:** monitoring/evaluation and evidence ecosystem; findings need departmental uptake.
- **AIM:** initiative under NITI; AIM 2.0 continuation officially approved in November 2024 through March 2028.
- **SSM/SIT:** State strategy and capacity support.
- **Boundary:** coordination/monitoring by NITI does not mean sole field implementation.

### 9. Monitoring and data logic

```text
INPUT -> ACTIVITY -> OUTPUT -> OUTCOME -> IMPACT
```

- Monitoring: progress and target tracking.
- Evaluation: relevance, design, efficiency, causal contribution and impact.
- Delta: incremental change; level: current absolute position.
- Dashboard risks: missing data, lag, bad proxy, gaming and context-free comparison.
- Safeguards: metadata, audit, revision history, peer grouping, mixed evidence and citizen feedback.

### 12. Reform recall

- predictable Council calendar and State agenda proposals;
- public action-taken matrix;
- co-designed indicators and comparable peer groups;
- published metadata, revisions and independent validation;
- DMEO publication and reasoned ministry/State response;
- State institutions linked to Cabinet and budget;
- local elected institutions included where devolved subjects are affected;
- annual mandate-performance report and parliamentary visibility;
- preserve flexibility; do not recreate command planning.

### 14. PYQ and answer spine

- **2018 GS-III Q11:** same legal status -> different federal principle -> different planning method -> different resource role -> NDC/GC transition -> monitoring instruments -> leverage/data qualification -> graded verdict.
- **2019 Prelims AIM route:** institutional ownership is NITI; local held official key unavailable, so do not mislabel a third-party key as official.
- **Universal line:** “NITI replaced directive plan-and-allocation influence with advisory strategy, federal coordination and evidence; the gain is flexibility and partnership, while the cost is dependence on credibility and uptake.”

### 15. Ten final traps

1. Non-constitutional does not mean unconstitutional.
2. No NITI Aayog Act creates the institution.
3. NITI does not replace Article 280 Finance Commission.
4. Governing Council consensus is not law.
5. Regional Councils are issue-specific, not permanent Zonal Councils.
6. NITI did not alone abolish plan/non-plan classification.
7. A dashboard output is not necessarily an outcome.
8. A rank is not causal evidence.
9. NITI coordination is not sole implementation.
10. A Viksit Bharat theme is an agenda signal, not a measured result.
