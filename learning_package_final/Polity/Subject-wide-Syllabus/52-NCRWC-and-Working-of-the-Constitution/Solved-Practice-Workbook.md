### ANSWERS AND OPTION-SPECIFIC EXPLANATIONS — ORIGINAL MCQS 1–40

#### MCQ 1 — Correct answer: A

**Option-specific explanations:**
- **A — Correct:** The Union resolution created a temporary advisory reviewer.
- **B — Incorrect:** A parliamentary joint committee differs in source and lacks independent Article 368 power.
- **C — Incorrect:** No constitutional amendment created a permanent NCRWC.
- **D — Incorrect:** The body neither replaced the Constitution nor claimed founding power.

**Examiner trap 1:** Institutional origin determines legal force: an executive commission is not a constitutional body.

#### MCQ 2 — Correct answer: B

- **D — Incorrect:** The 91st Amendment came later and did not create NCRWC.

**Examiner trap 2:** Do not reverse constitution, adoption and presentation dates.

#### PYQ 1 — 2021 GS-II Q1 — supporting/cross-owned

**Question:** “Constitutional Morality” is rooted in the Constitution itself and is founded on its essential facets. Explain the doctrine of “Constitutional Morality” with the help of relevant judicial decisions.

**Demand decoding:** Define constitutional morality and explain its judicial application; a review commission is context, not the source of the doctrine.

**Model solution:** Constitutional morality is fidelity to the Constitution's text, structure, values and the restraints of
public office, rather than compliance with transient social or political morality. It requires equal
citizenship, reasoned power and respect for institutional roles. In *Government of NCT of Delhi v.
Union of India* (2018), the Supreme Court linked it to constitutional trust, collaborative federalism
and responsible government. *Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India (2018)* held that constitutional morality must protect
liberty and equality against exclusionary social morality. *Indian Young Lawyers Association* (2018)
used equality and dignity to test religious exclusion, while later opinions demonstrate that the
concept requires disciplined legal reasoning rather than personal preference. The NCRWC's focus on
accountability, restraint and effective rights illustrates the same performance question: does
institutional conduct realise constitutional commitments? Constitutional morality therefore makes a
written Constitution a lived discipline of limited, answerable government.

**Why this earns marks:** The Delhi and *Navtej* decisions show distinct institutional and equality applications rather than using morality as a slogan.

**How to improve this answer:** Explain explicitly that constitutional morality is not identical to public opinion; keep the NCRWC example subordinate to judgments.

**Compression:** Definition → Delhi (institutional roles) → *Navtej* (equal citizenship) → doctrinal limit.

#### PYQ 2 — 2019 GS-II Q12 — supporting/cross-owned

**Question:** “Parliament's power to amend the Constitution is a limited power and it cannot be enlarged into absolute power.” In the light of this statement explain whether Parliament under Article 368 of the Constitution can destroy the Basic Structure of the Constitution by expanding its amending power.

**Demand decoding:** Decide whether Article 368 can enlarge itself into an unlimited power; distinguish procedural compliance from substantive invalidity.

**Model solution:** Article 368 gives Parliament a wide constituent power, but not authority to destroy the Constitution's
identity. *Kesavananda Bharati (1973)* held that every provision may be amended while the basic
structure cannot be damaged or destroyed. The limitation is substantive: democracy, rule of law,
judicial review, federalism, separation of powers and other foundational features cannot be abrogated
through the amendment procedure. *Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain* applied the doctrine to invalidate
an amendment damaging free elections and judicial review. *Minerva Mills (1980)* struck clauses that
sought unlimited amending power and excluded review, reasoning that limited amending power and judicial
review are themselves basic features. Parliament therefore cannot use Article 368 to convert a limited
power into an absolute one; a self-validating amendment would erase the distinction between a
constituted amending body and original constituent power. This boundary also controlled NCRWC's express
mandate: review could recommend reform within parliamentary democracy but could not authorise rewriting
basic structure. Adaptability survives, but constitutional continuity sets its outer limit.

The procedural safeguards of special parliamentary majorities and, for specified federal
provisions, ratification by at least half the States add democratic and federal consent; they
do not remove substantive review. This is why NCRWC's suggested institutional changes required
different competent routes and no Commission recommendation itself became an amendment.

**Why this earns marks:** *Kesavananda* establishes the limit and *Minerva Mills* applies it directly to an attempt to remove that limit.

**How to improve this answer:** Keep the NCRWC mandate as an illustration, not as legal authority for the basic-structure rule.

**Compression:** Limited amending power → attempted self-expansion → *Minerva Mills* → ratification is not a cure.

#### PYQ 3 — 2024 GS-II Q1 — supporting/cross-owned

**Question:** Examine the need for electoreal reforms as suggested by various commities with particular reference to ‘one nation–one election’ principle. *(Spellings as printed.)*

**Demand decoding:** Examine proposals from multiple committees, then evaluate simultaneous elections against federalism and responsible government; NCRWC is only one source.

**Model solution:** Electoral reform must address opaque finance, criminalisation and weak

Savings cannot outweigh accountability. Strengthen finance disclosure and ECI capacity

#### PYQ 4 — 2025 GS-II Q11 — supporting/cross-owned

**Question:** “Constitutional morality is the fulcrum which acts as an essential check upon the high functionaries and citizens alike....” In view of the above observation of the Supreme Court, explain the concept of constitutional morality and its application to ensure balance between judicial independence and judicial accountability in India.

**Demand decoding:** Apply constitutional morality specifically to the tension between judicial independence and misconduct accountability.

**Model solution:** Constitutional morality means fidelity to constitutional values, procedures and institutional limits.
For judges, it protects decisional independence from executive, legislative and popular retaliation:
security of tenure, protected conditions of service, judicial review and institutional control support
this function. *Kesavananda Bharati (1973)* and *Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Association v
Union of India* (2015) locate judicial independence within basic
structure. Yet independence is not insulation from standards. The same morality demands transparent
selection criteria, reasoned judgments, consistent recusal, ethical disclosure, fair complaint
procedures and timely discipline. *Government of NCT of Delhi* and *Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India (2018)* show that public
power must be exercised through role fidelity, equality and reason-giving.

Removal under Articles 124(4)–(5) is an exceptional parliamentary safeguard, while the Restatement of
Values and in-house procedure operate below that threshold. Their non-statutory and opaque features
leave an accountability gap. Reform should preserve judicial primacy against executive capture while
adding a permanent appointments secretariat, published criteria, recorded reasons, diversity data and
an independent, fair complaints process. Constitutional morality thus requires independence in
adjudication and accountability in the constitution and exercise of judicial office; neither executive
control nor corporate judicial secrecy satisfies it.

**Why this earns marks:** It separates independence of decisions from transparency of appointments, reasons and complaints.

**How to improve this answer:** Contrast the NCRWC's proposed judicial commission with the later invalidated NJAC without implying either governs present appointments.

**Compression:** Basic-structure independence → NJAC holding → removal versus everyday complaints → balanced reform.

### ORIGINAL MAINS PRACTICE 2 — 10 MARKS, 150 WORDS

**Question:** Distinguish constitutional review, amendment and replacement, with reference to the NCRWC.

**Demand decoding:** Distinguish requires separate sources of authority, legal effects and limits.

<!-- ORIGINAL-MAINS-2-ANSWER-START -->
Constitutional review diagnoses how text and institutions operate and proposes repair; its report does not itself alter law. The NCRWC performed this advisory function under an executive resolution and within parliamentary democracy and basic structure.

Constitutional amendment is different. Parliament acts under Article 368 through prescribed special majorities; amendments affecting specified federal provisions also require ratification by at least half the States. Even a procedurally valid amendment remains subject to the basic-structure doctrine developed in Kesavananda Bharati and Minerva Mills.

Replacement claims authority to establish a new constitutional order and therefore requires a fresh constituent mandate. NCRWC neither possessed nor asserted that power. Its exercise is best understood as constitutional maintenance: identify failure, select the least legally sufficient route and preserve constitutional identity. Basic structure therefore limits destructive amendment but does not freeze all reform.

### ORIGINAL MAINS PRACTICE 4 — 15 MARKS, 250 WORDS

**Question:** Analyse why the working of the Constitution cannot be judged from constitutional text alone.

**Demand decoding:** Analyse requires a causal framework connecting formal rules to institutional behaviour and outcomes.

<!-- ORIGINAL-MAINS-4-ANSWER-START -->
Constitutional text allocates power and protects rights, but institutions, conventions, incentives, political culture and administrative capacity determine whether those commitments become lived outcomes.

Legislative working depends on sitting time, committee scrutiny, financial control, opposition space and restraint in ordinance use. Electoral working requires control of criminalisation, transparent campaign finance, internal party democracy and credible anti-defection adjudication. Executive working turns on cabinet responsibility, reasoned discretion, professional civil services, transparency and anti-corruption enforcement.

Federal working requires consultation, predictable fiscal relations, restrained use of Governors and Article 356, and functioning intergovernmental forums. Local self-government needs actual transfer of functions, funds and functionaries. Judicial working concerns timely appointments, independence, accountability, access and compliance; tribunal efficiency cannot displace High Court judicial review recognised in L. Chandra Kumar.

The NCRWC's 120/100-day parliamentary sitting proposal is a benchmark for time, not proof
of productive debate. Likewise, its proposal for a 10 per cent ministry ceiling cannot
be cited as the enacted 15 per cent ceiling under the 91st Amendment. These gaps show
why performance and implementation must be verified independently of a commission report.

Cases such as S.R. Bommai, D.C. Wadhwa and Krishna Kumar Singh demonstrate that formally available powers can be constitutionally corrosive when conventions and limits are ignored. Conversely, vacancies or poor implementation may reflect capacity rather than defective text. A sound diagnosis therefore asks whether failure lies in authority, restraint, delivery or correction, and then selects a proportionate reform route.

**Question:** Critically evaluate the NCRWC as a constitutional reform exercise and audit the implementation of its recommendations.

**Demand decoding:** Critically evaluate requires achievements, limits, differentiated implementation evidence and a graded verdict.

<!-- ORIGINAL-MAINS-5-ANSWER-START -->
The NCRWC was a major exercise in constitutional maintenance. Constituted on 22 February 2000 and reporting on 31 March 2002, it reviewed fifty years of institutional experience while expressly preserving parliamentary democracy and basic structure. RTI and Lokpal statutes occupy related fields but require independent attribution.

The NCRWC succeeded more as a structured diagnosis and reform menu than as a single implemented programme. Selective adoption, modification or rejection is constitutionally legitimate when supported by evidence, deliberation and the competent legal route.

### ORIGINAL MAINS PRACTICE 6 — 20 MARKS, 250 WORDS

**Question:** Design a route-sensitive constitutional reform framework for recurring failures in Indian governance.

**Demand decoding:** Design requires a decision framework, sectoral applications, constitutional limits and implementation controls.

<!-- ORIGINAL-MAINS-6-ANSWER-START -->
Reform should begin with diagnosis, not an automatic demand for constitutional amendment. First identify whether the failure concerns legal authority, institutional restraint, capacity, incentives or corrective remedies.

Where the defect is procedural, House rules and conventions can improve Bill scrutiny, opposition time, reason-giving and restraint in ordinance use. Capacity failures such as vacancies, weak court infrastructure, poor records or unstable civil-service tenure require finance, staffing, technology and administrative redesign. Statutory gaps call for ordinary legislation: political-finance disclosure, party regulation, tribunal structure, whistle-blower protection and integrity institutions are examples.

Article 368 should be used only when constitutional text or allocation must change. Amendments affecting provisions specified in the proviso to Article 368(2) additionally require ratification by at least half the States. Judicial interpretation applies open-textured provisions and protects rights, federalism, judicial review and basic structure, but courts cannot replace a complete legislative policy. Federal disputes also require consultation through institutions such as the Inter-State Council, while local devolution depends heavily on State law and administration.

Every proposal should identify the competent actor, finance, timetable, transparency mechanism, review forum and outcome metric. It must then be tested against rights, democratic legitimacy, federal balance, basic structure and implementation capacity.

The NCRWC illustrates this plural route logic. Constitutional durability comes from matching remedy to defect: under-constitutionalisation evades law, but over-constitutionalisation creates rigidity and mistakes poor working for defective text.
