### SESSION 1 - PARTIES AS DEMOCRATIC INTERMEDIARIES: FUNCTIONS BEFORE LAW

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
CITIZENS + interests
        |
        v
aggregation -> programme/manifesto -> candidate selection -> election
        |                                      |
        v                                      v
opposition/scrutiny <-> legislature <-> government coordination
        |
        v
accountability at the next election
```

#### DEFINITION

A political party is a voluntary association that seeks public power through elections in order to translate social preferences into programmes, candidates, government or organised opposition.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> Political parties are not merely contestants at election time; they are the democratic transmission belt linking dispersed citizens to governable choices and accountable public power.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

**interest aggregation | political recruitment | programme | representation | government formation | opposition | accountability**

#### WRITE THE PARAGRAPH: CLAIM -> NAMED EVIDENCE -> ANALYSIS -> QUALIFICATION

**Claim:** Parties make representative democracy workable by reducing millions of preferences into contestable governing alternatives. **Named evidence:** India's parliamentary system relies on parties to nominate candidates, organise legislative support, form ministries and sustain an Opposition; Article 75(3) collective responsibility operates politically through organised majority support. **Analysis:** Without aggregation and coordination, elections could choose individuals but could not reliably produce programmes, ministries or sustained legislative accountability. **Qualification:** Parties mediate representation imperfectly: centralised tickets, opaque finance or identity mobilisation can distort rather than faithfully transmit citizen preferences.

#### EVIDENCE AND EXPLANATION

- Core functions: articulate and aggregate interests; recruit leaders; select candidates; mobilise voters; frame manifestos; coordinate government; discipline legislators; scrutinise through opposition; socialise citizens into constitutional competition.
- A party differs from a pressure group because it seeks governmental office and contests elections; a pressure group ordinarily seeks to influence office-holders without itself assuming general governmental responsibility.
- A recognised party is not a constitutional office. Public importance does not convert the organisation into the State, and legal privileges do not make its symbol private property.

#### PRELIMS TRAP

- Do not define a party only as an election machine or treat every organised political association as a recognised party.

#### MAINS USE

- Use this function chain as the introduction to answers on internal democracy, opposition, manifestos or regulation.

#### RECAP

- citizens -> aggregation -> candidates/programme -> government/opposition -> accountability

### SESSION 2 - PARTY-SYSTEM CLASSIFICATIONS AND INDIA'S FEDERALISED MULTI-PARTY ORDER

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
PARTY SYSTEMS
|
+-- one-party system -> legal/actual monopoly; no competitive alternation
+-- two-party system -> two principal governing competitors; smaller parties may exist
+-- multi-party system -> several relevant competitors; coalitions become possible
    |
    +-- dominant-party phase -> competition exists, one party repeatedly prevails
    +-- coalition phase -> no party alone controls a majority
    +-- federalised competition -> different effective systems at Union and State levels
```

#### DEFINITION

A party system is the patterned interaction among parties, voters and institutions; it is classified by effective competition, not by merely counting registered organisations.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> India is best understood as a multi-party and federalised party system whose national pattern can coexist with very different State-level contests.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

**one-party | two-party | multi-party | dominant-party system | coalition | federalised competition | effective parties**

#### WRITE THE PARAGRAPH: CLAIM -> NAMED EVIDENCE -> ANALYSIS -> QUALIFICATION

**Claim:** India's party system cannot be captured by a single nationwide headcount because electoral relevance varies across levels and States. **Named evidence:** Rajni Kothari's 'Congress system' described early one-party dominance within a competitive order; the post-1967 fragmentation, 1977 alternation, 1989-2014 coalition era and later renewed national dominance did not erase strong State parties. **Analysis:** First-past-the-post rules encourage constituency concentration, while social and linguistic diversity and federal arenas sustain multiple territorially rooted competitors. **Qualification:** Labels such as 'dominant' or 'coalition era' are analytical periodisations, not constitutional categories, and must be tied to a date and level of government.

#### EVIDENCE AND EXPLANATION

- One-party dominance is not a one-party State: opposition legally competes and alternation remains possible.
- A two-party system means two principal competitors, not the legal exclusion of all smaller parties.
- India's national, State and sub-State arenas may show bipolar competition, triangular contests or coalition blocs simultaneously.

#### PRELIMS TRAP

- Do not equate multi-party system with permanent coalition government, or one-party dominance with constitutional monopoly.

#### MAINS USE

- Use federalised competition to explain regional parties, coalition bargaining and why national/state labels do not predict every policy position.

#### RECAP

- competition pattern -> effective parties -> level/territory -> dominance or coalition -> qualified label

### SESSION 3 - CONSTITUTIONAL LOCATION: NARROW REFERENCES, NOT A GENERAL PARTY CODE

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
CONSTITUTION
|
+-- Arts 102(2), 191(2) -> Tenth Schedule disqualification bridge
+-- Tenth Schedule -> political party / original party / legislature party
+-- Arts 75(1B), 164(1B), 361B -> bounded post-defection office disabilities
|
+-- does NOT create a comprehensive code for registration, finance,
    internal elections, recognition or symbols
```

#### DEFINITION

The Constitution recognises political parties chiefly through the anti-defection framework; most party regulation comes from statutes, election rules and the Symbols Order.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> The Constitution constitutionalises party-linked legislative discipline, but it does not constitutionalise the whole political party as an office or comprehensively regulate its internal life.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

**Tenth Schedule | Articles 102(2), 191(2) | Articles 75(1B), 164(1B), 361B | bounded cross-link | no comprehensive party law**

#### WRITE THE PARAGRAPH: CLAIM -> NAMED EVIDENCE -> ANALYSIS -> QUALIFICATION

**Claim:** Constitutional references to parties are function-specific rather than a charter of general party governance. **Named evidence:** Articles 102(2) and 191(2) import Tenth-Schedule disqualification; Articles 75(1B), 164(1B) and 361B attach temporary ministerial or remunerative-political-post disabilities to the specified disqualified member. **Analysis:** These provisions protect legislative stability and deter rewards for defection, but they do not confer recognition, allot symbols or supervise organisational elections. **Qualification:** The full paragraph-by-paragraph anti-defection code belongs to Topic 42; here it is used only to mark the boundary between party discipline and general party regulation.

#### EVIDENCE AND EXPLANATION

- Political party and legislature party are distinct: the first is the electoral organisation; the second is its members in a particular House.
- Anti-defection is not a general licence for the party leadership to control every speech, organisational dispute, membership issue or financing decision.
- A recognised party remains an association governed by applicable law, not a constitutional authority equivalent to the ECI or a House.

#### PRELIMS TRAP

- Do not say political parties were wholly absent from today's constitutional text, or that the Tenth Schedule regulates party registration and finance.

#### MAINS USE

- Use the constitutional-statute-order three-layer map to prevent source confusion in regulatory answers.

#### RECAP

- Constitution -> defection only; statute -> registration/finance; ECI Order -> recognition/symbol

### SESSION 4 - SECTION 29A REGISTRATION: WHO APPLIES, WHEN AND WITH WHAT DECLARATION

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
association/body of individual Indian citizens
        |
        v
application to ECI -> signed by chief executive
        |             -> presented/sent to Secretary, ECI
        |             -> normally within 30 days of formation
        v
name + head office + address + office-bearers + membership strength
+ local units + legislative representation
        |
        v
party constitution must affirm Constitution + socialism + secularism
+ democracy + sovereignty + unity + integrity
```

#### DEFINITION

Registration is the statutory entry process under Section 29A of the Representation of the People Act, 1951 for an association or body of individual Indian citizens calling itself a political party.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> Section 29A is an entry-and-identity gateway: it records the association as a political party after disclosure and constitutional-allegiance requirements, but does not award electoral recognition.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

**Section 29A | individual citizens of India | 30 days | chief executive | Secretary ECI | party constitution | constitutional allegiance**

#### WRITE THE PARAGRAPH: CLAIM -> NAMED EVIDENCE -> ANALYSIS -> QUALIFICATION

**Claim:** Registration begins with a time-bound, particularised application rather than automatic self-description. **Named evidence:** Section 29A(2)-(5) requires a post-commencement body to apply within thirty days of formation, through its chief executive, with organisational particulars and a memorandum/rules containing the specified allegiance clause. **Analysis:** The particulars enable electoral administration to identify the organisation and its responsible officers, while the declaration sets a constitutional minimum for entry. **Qualification:** ECI guidelines add documentary and public-notice procedures; those administrative requirements should not be confused with every word of the enacted subsection.

#### EVIDENCE AND EXPLANATION

- The historical sixty-day route in Section 29A(2)(a) concerned bodies existing when the 1988 amendment commenced; newly formed bodies use the thirty-day route in clause (b).
- Required statutory particulars include name, State of head office, communication address, president/secretary/treasurer and other office-bearers, membership strength and categories, local units and legislative representation.
- Under the ECI registration guidelines checked on 8 September 2026, the proposed party name is also publicly advertised in two national and two local daily newspapers on two days so objections may be filed during the prescribed thirty-day window.
- The party constitution must bear true faith and allegiance to the Constitution as by law established, socialism, secularism and democracy, and uphold India's sovereignty, unity and integrity.

#### PRELIMS TRAP

- Registration is not incorporation of the association, recognition as a National/State party, or a certificate that every later act is constitutional.

#### MAINS USE

- Quote the application-plus-allegiance design to show both ECI entry control and its limited reach.

#### RECAP

- Indian-citizen association -> 30-day application -> particulars -> allegiance clause -> ECI hearing/decision

### SESSION 5 - ECI DECISION AND LIMITED DEREGISTRATION: INSTITUTE OF SOCIAL WELFARE

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
SECTION 29A DECISION
particulars -> relevant factors -> reasonable hearing -> register / refuse
                                              |
                                              v
GENERAL CANCELLATION POWER? -> NO
NARROW CORRECTION SPACE -> fraud in registration / party declared unlawful /
                          registration order incapable of remaining effective
                                              |
                                              v
internal democracy or later misconduct alone != free-standing cancellation power
```

#### DEFINITION

Deregistration is cancellation of Section 29A status; the Supreme Court has held that the ECI lacks a general power to cancel registration at will.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> The ECI's quasi-judicial power to register is real, but Indian National Congress (I) v Institute of Social Welfare prevents it from becoming an unenumerated power of political extinction.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

**quasi-judicial | reasonable opportunity | final decision | limited deregistration | fraud | unlawful association | Institute of Social Welfare**

#### WRITE THE PARAGRAPH: CLAIM -> NAMED EVIDENCE -> ANALYSIS -> QUALIFICATION

**Claim:** Section 29A gives the ECI a reasoned entry decision but not a broad continuing merits jurisdiction over every party failure. **Named evidence:** Indian National Congress (I) v Institute of Social Welfare (2002) treated registration under Section 29A as quasi-judicial and rejected a general implied deregistration power, preserving only narrow exceptional situations such as fraud or supervening illegality. **Analysis:** This protects associational competition from discretionary cancellation but leaves a regulatory gap where internal-election, inactivity or compliance failures do not fit a recognised exception. **Qualification:** The judgment does not immunise parties from other laws, recognition consequences, tax consequences, criminal law or a competent declaration that an association is unlawful.

#### EVIDENCE AND EXPLANATION

- Section 29A(6)-(8) permits further particulars, requires a reasonable opportunity of hearing and directs the Commission to register or refuse; the decision is communicated and declared final by the statute.
- After registration, material changes in name, head office, office-bearers, address or other material matters must be communicated without delay under Section 29A(9).
- The absence of general cancellation power is one reason reform proposals call for a comprehensive party law with defined grounds, hearing and appeal.

#### PRELIMS TRAP

- Do not infer a cancellation power from the ECI's broad Article 324 role when Section 29A and the binding judgment delimit registration.

#### MAINS USE

- Use the case to frame the central paradox: regulated entry, weak continuing organisational supervision.

#### RECAP

- ECI may register/refuse after hearing -> no at-will cancellation -> narrow exceptions -> legislative gap

### SESSION 6 - REGISTRATION, RECOGNITION AND SYMBOL ALLOTMENT: THREE DISTINCT QUESTIONS

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
1 REGISTRATION              2 RECOGNITION                 3 SYMBOL ALLOTMENT
Sec 29A, RPA 1951         Symbols Order 1968            Symbols Order + Rules 5/10
Who is a registered       Has performance crossed       Which reserved/free symbol
political party?          National/State criteria?      goes to candidate?
        |                         |                              |
        +-------------------------+------------------------------+
                                  v
                    never use the terms interchangeably
```

#### DEFINITION

Registration identifies a political party under statute; recognition gives a performance-based National or State status; symbol allotment assigns the applicable reserved or free symbol in an election.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> A party can be validly registered yet unrecognised, and a symbol can be allotted without converting that candidate's organisation into a recognised party.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

**registration | recognition | registered-unrecognised | reserved symbol | free symbol | candidate set up by party**

#### WRITE THE PARAGRAPH: CLAIM -> NAMED EVIDENCE -> ANALYSIS -> QUALIFICATION

**Claim:** The three-stage architecture separates legal entry, electoral performance and ballot identification. **Named evidence:** Section 29A governs registration, while the Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968 classifies recognised parties and reserved/free symbols under Article 324 read with Rules 5 and 10 of the Conduct of Elections Rules, 1961. **Analysis:** Separation permits open entry while reserving continuing privileges for demonstrable electoral support and maintaining a manageable ballot. **Qualification:** Recognition is date-sensitive and State-specific where applicable; registration alone neither guarantees a reserved symbol nor the privileges of recognition.

#### EVIDENCE AND EXPLANATION

- A recognised political party is either a National party or a State party under the Symbols Order.
- A registered-unrecognised party remains registered but has not met, or does not currently retain, recognition criteria.
- Candidate nomination and symbol rules ask whether the candidate is validly 'set up' by the party; a mere claim of support is not enough.

#### PRELIMS TRAP

- Do not write 'registered national party' as though Section 29A itself grants national recognition.

#### MAINS USE

- Use a three-column distinction table in any answer on ECI control over parties.

#### RECAP

- Sec 29A entry != performance recognition != election-specific allotment

### SESSION 7 - NATIONAL AND STATE RECOGNITION: OFFICIAL, DATE-CONTROLLED CRITERIA

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
NATIONAL PARTY - satisfy any one
1) >=6% valid votes in each of 4+ States (LS/Assembly) + >=4 LS members
2) >=2% of Lok Sabha seats + returned candidates from >=3 States
3) recognised State party in >=4 States

STATE PARTY - satisfy any one in that State
1) >=6% Assembly votes + >=2 Assembly members
2) >=6% Lok Sabha votes + >=1 Lok Sabha member
3) >=3% Assembly seats or 3 seats, whichever is more
4) >=1 Lok Sabha seat per 25 seats (or fraction) allotted to State
5) >=8% valid votes in State at last LS or Assembly general election
```

#### DEFINITION

Recognition is the performance-based status awarded under the Election Symbols Order, not a constitutional rank or permanent title.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> National and State recognition are alternative-test categories under the Symbols Order: the safest answer states the criteria with an access date and avoids freezing a volatile party list.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

**paragraphs 6A/6B | alternative conditions | valid votes | returned members | four States | three States | eight per cent | date control**

#### WRITE THE PARAGRAPH: CLAIM -> NAMED EVIDENCE -> ANALYSIS -> QUALIFICATION

**Claim:** Recognition combines vote share and territorial or seat performance so that privileges track demonstrated electoral presence. **Named evidence:** The ECI's official Political Parties and Election Symbols compilation, checked for this repair on 8 September 2026, sets alternative National and State tests in the amended Symbols Order. **Analysis:** The alternatives capture both broad vote presence and concentrated seat effectiveness, while State recognition respects territorially bounded support. **Qualification:** Status and party lists change after ECI review; no current list or count is frozen here, and any future answer should verify the operative ECI order.

#### EVIDENCE AND EXPLANATION

- For the National six-per-cent route, the additional four Lok Sabha members are a separate condition; do not import the three-State requirement from the two-per-cent Lok Sabha route.
- For the State Assembly-seat route, the formula is three per cent of Assembly seats or three seats, whichever is more.
- Recognition affects symbol reservation and specified electoral facilities; it does not create constitutional office or immunity from general law.

#### PRELIMS TRAP

- Do not combine fragments of different alternatives into a new test, or treat six per cent in one State as National recognition.

#### MAINS USE

- Use criteria briefly in Prelims-oriented answers; in Mains, focus on why performance-based privileges coexist with open registration.

#### RECAP

- alternative tests -> performance + territorial spread -> ECI review -> date-sensitive status

### SESSION 8 - RESERVED AND FREE SYMBOLS: ELECTORAL ENTITLEMENT, NOT PRIVATE PROPERTY

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
SYMBOL POOL
|
+-- RESERVED -> exclusively reserved for recognised party in relevant area
|               -> allotted to candidate validly set up by that party
|
+-- FREE -> not reserved -> available under ECI allotment rules
                -> used by independents / registered-unrecognised candidates
|
+-- legal purpose -> voter identification + fair, efficient ballot
    not trademark ownership or divisible private property
```

#### DEFINITION

A reserved symbol is reserved for a recognised party under the Symbols Order; a free symbol is any other notified symbol available for allotment under election rules.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> An election symbol is a regulated electoral identifier whose use depends on the Symbols Order and valid party authorisation, not a proprietary asset owned in perpetuity.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

**reserved symbol | free symbol | exclusive allotment | party candidate | legal entitlement | voter identification**

#### WRITE THE PARAGRAPH: CLAIM -> NAMED EVIDENCE -> ANALYSIS -> QUALIFICATION

**Claim:** Symbol reservation converts recognition into a stable visual cue for voters while keeping ownership within public electoral law. **Named evidence:** The Symbols Order classifies symbols as reserved or free, and Sadiq Ali held that a symbol is not property to be divided between rival co-owners. **Analysis:** The rule protects ballot clarity and party continuity but allows the ECI to resolve competing claims in the public interest of election administration. **Qualification:** A State party's reserved symbol is tied to its recognised area, subject to the Order's concessions and directions; it is not automatically an all-India exclusive private mark.

#### EVIDENCE AND EXPLANATION

- Recognised-party candidates receive the party's reserved symbol when validly set up.
- Registered-unrecognised parties and independents ordinarily choose from notified free symbols subject to priority and allotment rules.
- The symbol identifies the candidate on the ballot; recognition and allotment do not settle all civil disputes over organisational assets or names.

#### PRELIMS TRAP

- Do not say the ECI 'gives ownership' of a symbol or that every registered party gets a permanently reserved symbol.

#### MAINS USE

- Use the property-versus-entitlement distinction to explain why Paragraph 15 adjudication is legitimate.

#### RECAP

- recognition -> reservation -> valid candidate authorisation -> ballot use, not ownership

### SESSION 9 - PARAGRAPH 15 SPLITS: SADIQ ALI AND THE MAJORITY TEST

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
RIVAL GROUPS EACH CLAIM THE RECOGNISED PARTY
        |
        v
ECI satisfied dispute exists -> notice + evidence + hearing
        |
        +-- organisational wing support
        +-- legislative wing support
        +-- party constitution / aims where workable
        |
        v
which group IS the party for Symbols Order purposes?
        |
        v
binding direction for symbol allotment; symbol cannot be divided
```

#### DEFINITION

Paragraph 15 empowers the ECI to decide which rival section or group of a recognised party is that party for the purposes of the Symbols Order.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> Paragraph 15 does not reward the first claimant; it requires the ECI to identify the party through evidence, with organisational and legislative majorities as valuable but context-sensitive tests.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

### DIRECT VERIFIED UPSC PYQ - 2022 GS-II Q13 - 15 marks - 250 words

**Question (exact from the official paper):** "While the national political parties in India favour centralisation, the regional parties are in favour of State autonomy." Comment.

**Official-key discipline:** UPSC does not publish an official descriptive answer key or model answer. The response below is an original examiner-oriented model; only the question wording and paper metadata are official.

**Demand decode:** "Comment" requires a reasoned position, causal explanation, counter-evidence and a qualified verdict. Do not merely list National and State parties.

**Model answer:**

The proposition captures a recurring tendency, not a permanent ideological division. National parties seeking Union power benefit from uniform programmes, central fiscal leverage and a coordinated national organisation. State-rooted parties depend more directly on linguistic, cultural, regional and fiscal claims; they therefore press for devolution, a restrained use of the Governor's office and greater consultation in Union decisions.

The coalition period after 1989 made this logic visible. Regional parties used parliamentary leverage to carry State claims into Union cabinets and bargaining over policy. They thereby deepened representative federalism, although coalition agreements could also be opaque and transactional.

Yet party position matters as much as party label. A national party in State opposition may demand autonomy; a regional party may centralise power within its own State or support Union centralisation as a coalition partner. Recognition under the Symbols Order is an electoral status, not an ideological command. Constitutional institutions therefore matter more than partisan promises: fiscal devolution, intergovernmental consultation, judicial review and federalism's basic-structure protection create durable restraints.

Thus, the statement is analytically useful but overbroad. Party incentives shift with territorial base, organisation and office; State autonomy must rest on rule-bound institutions and internally federal political parties.

**Why this earns marks:** It gives a qualified answer immediately, explains mechanisms, supplies coalition-era evidence, reverses the stereotype with counter-cases and ends institutionally.

**PYQ route:** tendency -> national incentives -> regional incentives -> coalition evidence -> reversal -> constitutional safeguard -> qualified conclusion.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 - 10 marks - 150 words

**Question:** Distinguish registration, recognition and symbol allotment of political parties in India. Answer in 150 words.

**Model answer:**

<!-- ORIGINAL-MAINS-1-ANSWER-START -->
India uses three separate legal gates. Registration under Section 29A of the Representation of the People Act, 1951 records an association of individual Indian citizens as a political party after organisational particulars, a constitutional-allegiance clause and an ECI hearing. It does not itself confer National or State status.

Recognition is performance-based under the Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968. A registered party becomes a recognised National or State party only by satisfying an operative alternative vote-and-seat criterion. Recognition is date-sensitive and brings specified electoral privileges.

Symbol allotment is the election-stage assignment governed by the Symbols Order and Conduct of Elections Rules. A recognised party's duly set-up candidate ordinarily receives its reserved symbol; other eligible candidates use free symbols under allotment rules.

Thus, entry, electoral status and ballot identification are connected but not interchangeable. A registered-unrecognised party remains lawful without possessing a permanently reserved symbol.

**Question:** Why can the Election Commission not ordinarily deregister a political party at will? Answer in 150 words.

**Model answer:**

<!-- ORIGINAL-MAINS-2-ANSWER-START -->

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 - 15 marks - 250 words

**Question:** Political parties are over-regulated at the electoral edge but under-regulated internally. Discuss.

**Model answer:**

<!-- ORIGINAL-MAINS-3-ANSWER-START -->

Sanctions should be graded and reviewable.

India's problem is not absence of regulation but its location. Law carefully regulates the party's public electoral interface while leaving the gate through which leaders and candidates emerge comparatively opaque.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 - 15 marks - 250 words

**Question:** Examine the constitutional significance of the 2024 electoral-bonds judgment for political-party finance.

**Model answer:**

<!-- ORIGINAL-MAINS-4-ANSWER-START -->
Association for Democratic Reforms v Union of India (2024) shifted political finance from administrative convenience to constitutional voter information. The 2018 Electoral Bond Scheme allowed bank-issued bearer instruments and shielded donor-recipient links from public scrutiny. The enabling amendments also altered disclosure under the Representation of the People Act and company law.

The five-judge Constitution Bench held that non-disclosure burdened the voter's right to information under Article 19(1)(a). Political contributions can indicate access, influence and possible quid pro quo; informed electoral choice therefore extends beyond candidate biography to party finance. The Court found the restriction disproportionate because less restrictive transparency-compatible means could address cash donations. It separately invalidated the removal of the corporate contribution cap as manifestly arbitrary under Article 14, recognising the heightened influence of companies.

The remedy was operational, not merely declaratory: bond issuance stopped, the State Bank of India had to furnish purchaser and recipient data, and the ECI had to publish it. Yet the judgment did not enact a complete campaign-finance code, fix a donation ceiling or establish public funding.

Its constitutional significance is thus twofold: transparency is a component of meaningful voting, and formally equal corporate donation rules may still be arbitrary when they permit disproportionate influence. The next step is interoperable donor, party, company and expenditure disclosure with audit and proportionate enforcement, while protecting genuinely small-donor privacy.

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

### Advanced 1 - Effective-number and cleavage analysis

Counting registered parties exaggerates competition because many have negligible electoral effect. Advanced comparison may distinguish the number of parties from the number of **effective** competitors weighted by votes or seats. In India, social cleavages are not automatically parties: caste, class, religion, language and region become politically salient through organisation, coalition and candidate strategy. The same cleavage can be cross-cut by welfare, leadership or alliance. Use this framework only after the core legal and functional answer is complete.

### Advanced 2 - Catch-all, cadre and cartel tendencies

A **cadre party** emphasises committed organisation and ideological training; a **mass party** builds broad membership; a **catch-all party** dilutes sharp ideology to assemble diverse voters. A **cartel-party** argument suggests established parties may use State resources and regulation to protect collective advantages. These are comparative ideal types, not fixed labels for every Indian party. An organisation can combine cadre pockets, charismatic leadership and catch-all election strategy.

### Advanced 3 - Regulatory design beyond command-and-control

Party regulation can use disclosure, benefit conditions, graded sanctions, independent audit and member remedies rather than direct control of ideology. Recognition privileges and tax benefits can be linked to timely public filings. Cancellation should remain exceptional because entry barriers can entrench incumbents. An independent appellate tribunal or designated court can review serious sanctions while routine organisational disputes first use internal processes.

### Advanced 4 - Public funding choices

State funding may be direct cash, reimbursement, tax credits or in-kind support such as broadcast time and electoral material. The Indrajit Gupta Committee supported partial in-kind assistance, but India has not enacted a complete public-funding system. Funding without expenditure control, candidate transparency and internal democracy may subsidise existing inequality. Any allocation formula must avoid permanently privileging recognised incumbents over new entrants.

### Advanced 5 - Coalition and manifesto institutions

Pre-poll alliances communicate a prospective governing choice; post-poll coalitions may be necessary but can obscure the mandate. Published common-minimum programmes, coalition agreements and periodic implementation reports can improve responsibility without making every promise judicially enforceable. Manifesto costing should disclose assumptions and trade-offs, but the ECI should not become the arbiter of substantive economic ideology.

### Advanced 6 - A proportionate party-law model

A defensible statute would separate: **entry** under Section 29A; **continuing disclosure**; **minimum internal procedure**; **recognition and symbols**; **candidate integrity**; **finance**; **digital campaigning**; and **sanctions/appeals**. It should protect small parties through simple compliance tiers, publish machine-readable data, and reserve cancellation for fraud, unlawful objects or persistent grave violation proved through due process. The democratic test is whether regulation improves informed choice without freezing political competition.

## CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

### Democratic role and party-system classification

- Political party: voluntary association seeking public power through elections.
- Functions: interest aggregation, recruitment, candidate selection, manifesto, mobilisation, government formation, opposition, legislative coordination and accountability.
- Party system concerns effective competition: one-party, two-party, multi-party, dominant-party and coalition forms.
- India: competitive multi-party and federalised; State systems may be bipolar or multi-cornered even when the national pattern differs.
- Rajni Kothari's Congress system described early dominance inside a competitive order, not a one-party State.

### Section 29A registration decoder

- Applicant: association/body of individual citizens of India calling itself a political party.
- New body applies within thirty days of formation; historical sixty-day clause covered bodies existing at commencement.
- Chief executive signs; application goes to Secretary, ECI.
- Particulars: name, head office State, address, office-bearers, membership strength/categories, local units and legislative representation.
- ECI guidelines checked on the control date add publication of the proposed name in two national and two local daily newspapers on two days and a thirty-day objection opportunity.
- Party rules must affirm Constitution, socialism, secularism, democracy, sovereignty, unity and integrity.
- ECI may seek more particulars; reasonable hearing precedes register/refuse decision.
- Material changes must be communicated without delay.
- Registration does not incorporate the association, grant recognition or approve every later act.

### Limited deregistration

- Indian National Congress (I) v Institute of Social Welfare (2002): Section 29A decision is quasi-judicial; no general ECI cancellation power.
- Narrow fraud or supervening-unlawfulness situations are distinguishable.
- Other law may impose tax, recognition, criminal or election consequences.
- Reform need: defined continuing duties, graded sanctions, hearing and appeal; proposals are not current law.

### Recognition and symbol rules - controlled to 8 September 2026

- National recognition, any one: 6% valid votes in each of 4+ States at relevant LS/Assembly elections + 4 LS members; 2% Lok Sabha seats with returned candidates from at least 3 States; or State-party recognition in at least 4 States.
- State recognition, any one: 6% Assembly votes + 2 MLAs; 6% Lok Sabha votes in State + 1 MP; 3% Assembly seats or 3 seats whichever more; 1 MP per 25 seats or fraction allotted to State; or 8% valid votes in State at last LS/Assembly general election.
- Criteria/status are order- and date-sensitive; no volatile party list is memorised here.
- Reserved symbol: recognised-party entitlement for duly set-up candidates.
- Free symbol: other notified symbols allotted under ECI rules.
- Symbol is regulated electoral entitlement, not private property.

### Split and institutional boundary

- Paragraph 15: ECI decides which rival group of a recognised party is the party for Symbols Order purposes.
- Sadiq Ali: organisational and legislative majority tests are valuable and relevant, not a legislature-only ownership rule.
- Kanhaiya Lal Omar: Symbols Order valid under Article 324 read with Rules 5 and 10.
- Subhash Desai: political party and legislature party differ; party authorises whip/leader; Speaker and ECI occupy distinct fields.
- ECI symbol decision does not retrospectively cure defection; Tenth-Schedule two-thirds merger is not Paragraph 15 recognition.

### Finance, tax and bond chronology

- Section 29B: voluntary contributions subject to company-law and foreign-source restrictions.
- Section 29C: treasurer reports each contribution exceeding Rs 20,000 to ECI before income-return due date; failure loses tax relief for that year.
- Rs 20,000 is a disclosure trigger, not donation cap, cash threshold or spending ceiling.
- Section 13A exemption is conditional on books, donor records, audit, permitted banking route, return and Section 29C compliance.
- Electoral Trusts Scheme 2013: approved intermediary; at least 95% eligible annual contributions distributed to Section 29A parties; trust != bond.
- 2017 amendments -> 2018 Electoral Bond Scheme -> ADR v Union of India, 15 February 2024.
- ADR: anonymity/non-disclosure invalid under Article 19(1)(a); unlimited corporate-funding change manifestly arbitrary under Article 14; SBI disclosure and ECI publication ordered.
- Judgment did not enact a complete finance code; never invent current donation totals.

### Candidate gatekeeping and campaign

- Internal democracy: membership rolls, periodic organisational elections, leadership contestability, transparent tickets, inclusion and appeal.
- No comprehensive enacted internal-democracy code; ECI cannot generally manage or cancel at will.
- ADR/PUCL: candidate affidavit and voter right to know.
- Rambabu Singh Thakur: party publication of criminal antecedents and reasons based on merit, not mere winnability.
- Lily Thomas: conviction-based legislator disqualification boundary; not party deregistration.
- NOTA expresses rejection but does not automatically void an ordinary parliamentary/Assembly poll.
- Section 77: candidate account distinct from general party campaign; 40 notified leaders for recognised party, 20 for another; travel exclusion is bounded.
- Paid news, MCC and manifesto promises require separate legal analysis.
- S. Subramaniam Balaji: manifesto promises not automatically corrupt practices under then statutory text; ECI guidelines followed. All manifestos are not contracts and all welfare promises are not bribery.

### Opposition, federalism, digital politics and RTI

- Opposition: scrutiny, alternatives, committees, questions and peaceful alternation.
- Discipline supports collective responsibility; overbroad whips can weaken deliberation. Narrow-whip reform is not current law.
- National-centralising/regional-autonomy is a qualified tendency shaped by territorial base, organisation and office.
- Coalition politics can deepen federal bargaining but raise coordination and transparency costs.
- Digital reforms: verified sponsor, ad library, expenditure trail, deepfake response, privacy and due process.
- CIC Full Bench, 3 June 2013: six named parties held public authorities; compliance and final judicial settlement remain contested. Do not state a universal settled RTI rule.

1. Constitution/Tenth Schedule does not register parties.
2. Section 29A registration does not grant a reserved symbol.
3. Recognition criteria are alternatives; do not combine fragments.
4. Three-State spread belongs to the 2% Lok Sabha-seat route.

6. Symbol is not private property.
7. Paragraph 15 identity dispute is not Tenth-Schedule defection.
8. ECI lacks general at-will deregistration.
9. Section 29C threshold is not a cap.
10. Electoral trust is not electoral bond.
11. Star-campaigner travel exclusion is not a blanket rally-cost exemption.
12. CIC 2013 order is not a settled Supreme Court ruling for every party.
