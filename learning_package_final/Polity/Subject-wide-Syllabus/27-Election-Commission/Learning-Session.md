## BASIC LEARNING SESSION

### SESSION 1 - ARTICLE 324: A CONSTITUTIONAL TRUST INSTITUTION

```text
EQUAL ELECTORAL CITIZENSHIP
           |
accurate roll -> fair candidature -> neutral campaign
           |                              |
      secure poll -> auditable count -> lawful result
           |
LEGITIMATE REPRESENTATIVE GOVERNMENT
```

**Definition.** The Election Commission of India (ECI) is the permanent constitutional body in Article 324 that controls electoral rolls and conducts elections to Parliament, State legislatures, the President and the Vice-President.

**Answer line.** The ECI does not choose rulers; it protects the conditions in which electors can choose them freely.

**Keywords.** Article 324; superintendence, direction and control; free and fair elections; constitutional trust; legal limits.

**ECI-specific argument.**
- **Claim:** Article 324 creates an independent electoral manager because the incumbent executive cannot be the final judge of the contest in which it participates.
- **Named evidence:** The constitutional text vests the complete supervisory triad - superintendence, direction and control - in the ECI.
- **Analysis:** A single constitutional authority can coordinate rolls, personnel, polling, counting and emergency corrective action across India.
- **Qualification:** Independence is not sovereignty; the ECI remains bound by the Constitution, enacted election law, natural justice and judicial review at the proper stage.

**Evidence.** Article 324(1) expressly includes preparation of electoral rolls and conduct of the four named classes of elections.

**Prelims trap.** Article 324 does not cover Panchayat, municipal, cooperative-society or internal party organisational elections.

**Mains use.** Begin an independence answer with the conflict-of-interest reason for an autonomous election manager, then evaluate appointment, staff, finance and reason-giving.

**Recap.** Broad constitutional supervision + statutory discipline + reviewable action = credible electoral administration.

### SESSION 2 - THE DECODED CONSTITUTIONAL SPINE: ARTICLES 325-329, 103, 192, 243K AND 243ZA

| Provision | Learner-facing rule | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Article 325 | one general electoral roll; no exclusion or special roll only on religion, race, caste or sex | citizenship, age, residence and lawful disqualifications still apply |
| Article 326 | adult suffrage for Lok Sabha and State Assemblies | voting age is 18 after the 61st Amendment Act, 1988 |
| Articles 327-328 | Parliament legislates; States have a subordinate field for their legislatures | State law yields to parliamentary law |
| Article 329 | delimitation/allotment bar and election-petition channel | not blanket immunity from judicial review |
| Articles 103/192 | President/Governor decides specified sitting-member disqualification questions after obtaining ECI opinion | Tenth Schedule belongs to Speaker/Chairman |
| Articles 243K/243ZA | State Election Commission controls Panchayat/municipal elections | ECI and SEC are distinct constitutional bodies |

**Definition.** Part XV distributes rule-making, management and dispute resolution instead of concentrating every election function in the ECI.

**Answer line.** Article 324 supplies the manager, Articles 325-328 supply equality and legislative structure, and Article 329 protects the timetable while preserving the post-result remedy.

**Keywords.** general roll; adult suffrage; parliamentary law; election petition; ECI opinion; State Election Commission.

**ECI-specific argument.**
- **Claim:** The constitutional design separates electoral administration from local-body management and final judicial adjudication.
- **Named evidence:** Articles 243K and 243ZA assign local polls to SECs; Articles 103 and 192 create a narrow ECI opinion role.
- **Analysis:** Separation prevents Article 324 from becoming an all-purpose election clause and preserves federal and adjudicatory boundaries.
- **Qualification:** The President or Governor is the formal decision-maker under Articles 103/192, while the ECI opinion has controlling constitutional importance; defection is outside this route.

**Evidence.** The 61st Amendment Act, 1988 changed Article 326 from twenty-one to eighteen years.

**Prelims trap.** The SEC is appointed by the Governor and its Commissioner has a High-Court-judge-like removal protection; it is not a regional office of the ECI.

**Mains use.** Use this allocation map to distinguish ECI, SEC, legislature, court and Speaker before discussing reform.

**Recap.** Different actor, different election, different legal source.

### SESSION 3 - COMPOSITION, MULTI-MEMBER EVOLUTION AND T.N. SESHAN

```text
25 Jan 1950        Oct 1989       Jan 1990       Oct 1993 onward
CEC only  -------> CEC + 2 ECs -> CEC only ----> CEC + 2 ECs
                                                collegial majority
```

**Definition.** Article 324 permits a CEC and such number of other Election Commissioners as the President fixes, subject to parliamentary law; when ECs exist, the CEC is Chairman.

**Answer line.** The Commission is collegial: the CEC chairs but does not command two constitutionally inferior votes.

**Keywords.** CEC; other ECs; Chairman; equal decisional power; majority; multi-member Commission.

**ECI-specific argument.**
- **Claim:** Collegiality reduces person-centred election administration without erasing the CEC's distinct constitutional safeguards.
- **Named evidence:** *T.N. Seshan v. Union of India* (1995) upheld the multi-member Commission and the equality of members in decision-making.
- **Analysis:** Majority decision supplies internal deliberation and continuity while preventing unilateral control by the Chair.
- **Qualification:** Functional equality coexists with removal asymmetry and the CEC's chairmanship.

**Evidence.** Section 18 of Act 49 of 2023 seeks unanimity where possible and otherwise adopts majority opinion.

**Prelims trap.** The Constitution does not permanently fix the number of other ECs at two.

**Mains use.** Write "first among equals in chairmanship, not decisional supremacy" and immediately explain the removal difference.

**Recap.** Chairmanship is organisational; Commission business is collegial.

### SESSION 4 - APPOINTMENT LAW: 1991 FRAMEWORK, ANOOP BARANWAL AND ACT 49 OF 2023

```text
Article 324(2): President appoints, subject to parliamentary law
                      |
no appointment law -> Anoop Baranwal (2 Mar 2023)
                      |
         PM + LoP/largest opposition leader + CJI
                 only until Parliament legislates
                      |
Act 49 of 2023, commenced 2 Jan 2024
Search: Law Minister + 2 Secretary-rank members -> panel of five
Select: PM + LoP/largest opposition leader + PM-nominated Cabinet Minister
                      |
            President appoints by warrant
```

**Definition.** The 2023 Act is the current appointment, term, service-condition and business statute; it repealed the Election Commission (Conditions of Service of Election Commissioners and Transaction of Business) Act, 1991.

**Answer line.** *Anoop Baranwal* filled a constitutional vacuum temporarily; Parliament then occupied Article 324(2)'s express law-making space, leaving the independence quality of the enacted design open to constitutional review.

**Keywords.** interim arrangement; Search Committee; panel of five; Selection Committee; executive majority; President's warrant.

**ECI-specific argument.**
- **Claim:** A statutory process improves legality but does not by itself settle institutional-independence concerns.
- **Named evidence:** Sections 5-8 of Act 49 of 2023 set eligibility, a Law-Minister-led search and a PM-LoP-Cabinet-Minister selection committee; section 8 permits consideration outside the search panel.
- **Analysis:** The process is rule-bound and includes Opposition participation, yet the executive controls two of three selection votes and the search stage.
- **Qualification:** *Anoop Baranwal* did not permanently constitutionalise CJI membership; it expressly operated only until Parliament made a law.

**Current status - checked through 7 September 2026.** The Act operates. The Supreme Court's official order of 22 March 2024 refused interim interference and treated observations as tentative. No later official stay or final merits judgment was located; therefore do not describe the Act as upheld or struck down.

**Evidence.** Section 5 requires Secretary-equivalent rank, integrity and knowledge of and experience in election management and conduct.

**Prelims trap.** The enacted Search Committee is headed by the Minister of Law and Justice, not the Cabinet Secretary.

**Mains use.** Separate parliamentary competence, process strengths, independence risks and current judicial status.

**Recap.** Temporary CJI panel ended with legislation; constitutional adequacy remains a distinct question.

### SESSION 5 - TENURE, SALARY, SERVICE CONDITIONS, REMOVAL AND REGIONAL COMMISSIONERS

| Office | Term/status | Removal |
|---|---|---|
| CEC | six years or age 65, whichever earlier; no reappointment | like a Supreme Court judge |
| other EC | same statutory term and salary | President only on CEC recommendation |
| Regional Commissioner | assistance office under Article 324(4) | President only on CEC recommendation |

**Definition.** Article 324(5) constitutionalises the core security rules; Act 49 of 2023 supplies the present term, salary and related conditions.

**Answer line.** Equality in vote does not mean equality in removal protection.

**Keywords.** six years; sixty-five; no reappointment; aggregate six-year cap; Supreme Court judge salary; disadvantageous variation.

**ECI-specific argument.**
- **Claim:** The CEC has the strongest security because removal follows the same manner and grounds as a Supreme Court judge.
- **Named evidence:** Article 324(5) protects the CEC's tenure and bars disadvantageous variation of service conditions after appointment; section 11 of the 2023 Act restates the removal routes.
- **Analysis:** A high threshold shields the institutional chair from ordinary executive pleasure.
- **Qualification:** Other ECs are protected from direct removal but remain dependent on a CEC recommendation; equalising protection is a reform proposal, not current law.

**Evidence.** Section 9: six years or sixty-five, no reappointment, and no more than six years in aggregate when an EC becomes CEC. Section 10: salary equal to a Supreme Court judge. The constitutional non-variation safeguard is textually for the CEC; the Act also protects incumbents at commencement from disadvantageous change.

**Prelims trap.** Six years/65 is statutory, not written into Article 324.

**Mains use.** Evaluate appointment and removal together: a secure exit cannot fully cure a partisan entry process.

**Recap.** Strong CEC security + weaker EC security + common statutory term.

### SESSION 6 - SECRETARIAT, STAFF DEPENDENCE AND ARTICLE 324(6)

```text
ECI headquarters
     |
Chief Electoral Officers -> District Election Officers/Returning Officers
     |                                  |
registration machinery             Presiding/Polling Officers
     |
staff supplied by Union/States when ECI requests under Article 324(6)
```

**Definition.** The ECI is a permanent constitutional body but conducts mass elections through personnel and infrastructure substantially drawn from Union and State administrations.

**Answer line.** Formal independence can be weakened if an autonomous Commission depends on governments for staff, secretariat capacity and finance.

**Keywords.** Article 324(6); President; Governor; staff availability; deemed deputation; permanent secretariat; budget autonomy.

**ECI-specific argument.**
- **Claim:** Article 324(6) creates a constitutional duty to supply staff but not a wholly independent election civil service.
- **Named evidence:** The President or Governor must make staff available when the ECI requests it for its constitutional functions.
- **Analysis:** ECI control during election duty promotes neutrality, but recruitment, career incentives and infrastructure remain located in ordinary government systems.
- **Qualification:** Administrative dependence does not prove partisan conduct; the reform case is preventive institutional design.

**Evidence.** *Anoop Baranwal* urged consideration of a permanent secretariat and charged expenditure, but that appeal did not itself amend the Constitution or budget law.

**Prelims trap.** Article 324 does not contain an Article-322-style blanket clause charging all ECI expenditure on the Consolidated Fund of India.

**Mains use.** Propose an independent secretariat, predictable finance, transparent deployment criteria and protection for officers acting under ECI control.

**Recap.** Staff duty is constitutional; institutional dependence remains an independence issue.

### SESSION 7 - ELECTION TIMETABLE, BY-ELECTIONS, ADJOURNMENT, FRESH POLL AND COUNTERMAND

```text
ECI schedule -> statutory notification -> nominations -> scrutiny -> withdrawal
      -> campaign -> poll -> count -> result -> election petition

disruption ladder:
local interruption -> adjourned poll
destroyed/lost voting material -> poll void + fresh poll
booth capturing -> adjournment/countermand as statute permits
candidate death in recognised-party setting -> statutory postponement route
```

**Definition.** The ECI fixes and supervises the programme, while formal notifications and corrective steps must follow the Constitution, RPA 1951 and Conduct of Elections Rules, 1961.

**Answer line.** Election scheduling is broad supervision; countermanding is not free-standing political discretion.

**Keywords.** schedule announcement; notification; section 151A; sections 52, 57, 58, 58A; repoll; recorded reasons.

**ECI-specific argument.**
- **Claim:** Speed is essential, but each disturbed-poll remedy must be tied to its statutory trigger.
- **Named evidence:** RPA 1951 sections 57, 58 and 58A distinguish adjournment, destruction/loss and booth capturing.
- **Analysis:** Graduated remedies preserve votes already lawfully cast while correcting only the damaged stage.
- **Qualification:** Final validity of a returned candidate's election belongs to the High Court petition route, not an unlimited post-result ECI power.

**Evidence.** Section 151A ordinarily requires a by-election within six months, except where the remaining term is under one year or ECI, in consultation with the Central Government, certifies difficulty from unavoidable circumstances.

**Prelims trap.** "Every vacancy must be filled within six months without exception" is false.

**Mains use.** Show the sequence, identify the precise disruption and state why proportional correction protects both integrity and continuity.

**Recap.** Programme power is broad; corrective power is trigger-specific.

### SESSION 8 - RPA 1950: ALLOCATION, DELIMITATION AND INCLUSIVE ELECTORAL ROLLS

```text
RPA 1950
|-- allocation of seats and constituency framework
|-- electoral-roll preparation and revision
|-- section 19: age + ordinary residence
|-- sections 21-23: revision, correction/deletion, inclusion
|-- four qualifying dates: 1 Jan | 1 Apr | 1 Jul | 1 Oct
+-- Delimitation Commission remains a distinct statutory authority
```

**Definition.** The RPA 1950 builds the representative and voter-list infrastructure on which the RPA 1951 election process operates.

**Answer line.** Roll integrity has two equal limbs: include every eligible citizen and remove only ineligible entries through fair procedure.

**Keywords.** ordinary residence; qualifying date; claims and objections; notice; inclusion; deletion; Delimitation Commission.

**ECI-specific argument.**
- **Claim:** Accurate rolls are the first electoral safeguard because a secure poll cannot repair wrongful exclusion.
- **Named evidence:** Sections 21-23 create revision, correction/deletion and inclusion procedures; the Election Laws (Amendment) Act, 2021 enabled four qualifying dates.
- **Analysis:** More frequent entry points reduce age-based exclusion, while notice and hearing protect against mass or erroneous deletion.
- **Qualification:** Aadhaar may assist identity verification under the statutory framework but is not proof of citizenship; non-submission cannot be treated mechanically.

**Current judicial control.** In *Association for Democratic Reforms v. ECI*, 2026 INSC 564 (27 May 2026), the Supreme Court recognised ECI power to conduct special intensive revision and a limited electoral-purpose citizenship inquiry, but not final citizenship adjudication; genuine citizenship doubts belong to the competent Citizenship Act authority.

**Evidence.** Article 325 forbids discriminatory roll exclusion, but it does not erase citizenship, age, residence or lawful disqualification requirements.

**Prelims trap.** The ECI participates in delimitation as the statute provides; it is not the ordinary sole Delimitation Commission.

**Mains use.** Evaluate accuracy, inclusion, notice, accessible appeal, migration, disability access and audit of deletions.

**Recap.** Inclusion and purification are complementary, not competing, duties.

### SESSION 9 - RPA 1951: CONDUCT, QUALIFICATIONS, DISQUALIFICATIONS, CORRUPT PRACTICES AND DISPUTES

| RPA 1951 field | High-yield provisions |
|---|---|
| candidature and conduct | nominations, scrutiny, polling, counting, results |
| disqualification | sections 8, 8A, 9, 9A, 10, 10A and related remedies |
| corrupt practices | section 123 |
| party registration | section 29A |
| expenses | sections 77-78 and section 10A consequence |
| election disputes | sections 80-116A; High Court then Supreme Court appeal |

**Definition.** The RPA 1951 governs the operative contest and post-result challenge, whereas the RPA 1950 primarily structures representation and rolls.

**Answer line.** The ECI administers the election, but courts determine an election petition and serious corrupt-practice liability through the statutory route.

**Keywords.** conviction; corrupt practice; election expense account; election petition; High Court; section 116A appeal.

**ECI-specific argument.**
- **Claim:** Electoral administration and electoral adjudication are deliberately separated.
- **Named evidence:** Sections 80 and 80A assign election petitions to the High Court; section 116A permits appeal to the Supreme Court.
- **Analysis:** The ECI can prevent, monitor and refer violations without becoming the final court deciding whether a returned election is void.
- **Qualification:** ECI gives constitutionally required opinions in specified disqualification routes and may exercise statutory remedial powers, but cannot invent new grounds.

**Evidence.** *Lily Thomas v. Union of India* (2013) invalidated section 8(4), ending the sitting-member shield against immediate conviction-based disqualification.

**Prelims trap.** A stay of sentence alone is not the same as a stay of conviction for disqualification consequences.

**Mains use.** Distinguish substantive grounds, decision-maker, remedy and timing.

**Recap.** RPA 1951 regulates the contest; the High Court protects the post-result judicial remedy.

### SESSION 10 - NOMINATIONS, AFFIDAVITS AND CRIMINAL ANTECEDENTS

```text
nomination -> affidavit -> public disclosure -> scrutiny
      |             |
qualification       criminal cases | assets/liabilities | education
      |
false/defective disclosure -> statutory/court consequence, not arbitrary rejection
```

**Definition.** Candidate disclosure converts the voter's freedom of expression into an informed electoral choice.

**Answer line.** Disclosure is not a declaration of guilt; it is a democratic information remedy against concealed risk.

**Keywords.** Form 26; Article 19(1)(a); criminal antecedents; assets and liabilities; party publicity; informed choice.

**ECI-specific argument.**
- **Claim:** ECI's affidavit architecture strengthens accountability before conviction-based disqualification becomes available.
- **Named evidence:** *Union of India v. Association for Democratic Reforms* (2002) and *PUCL v. Union of India* (2003) protected voter access to candidate information.
- **Analysis:** Information permits electoral sanction where criminal trials are slow and helps media and civil society verify conflicts and wealth growth.
- **Qualification:** Pending cases do not equal guilt, and nomination rejection must follow statute rather than moral suspicion.

**Evidence.** *Public Interest Foundation v. Union of India* (2018) required prominent disclosure and repeated publicity; the 13 February 2020 follow-up required parties to publish reasons for selecting candidates with pending criminal cases, based on qualifications rather than mere winnability.

**Prelims trap.** The Supreme Court did not create a blanket pre-conviction ban on every person facing a criminal charge.

**Mains use.** Pair faster trials and accurate disclosure with safeguards against politically motivated cases.

**Recap.** Know, scrutinise, decide - without replacing presumption of innocence.

### SESSION 11 - ELECTION EXPENDITURE, PAID NEWS, MEDIA AND SOCIAL MEDIA

| Risk | ECI-linked response | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| candidate overspending | account, ceiling, observers and monitoring teams | party expenditure is not governed by one identical candidate ceiling |
| paid news | Media Certification and Monitoring Committees; expense attribution where proved | evidence and legal route remain necessary |
| political advertising | pre-certification rules for covered electronic/social-media ads | ECI is not a general media censor |
| misinformation/deepfakes | MCC directions plus applicable election, criminal and technology law | lawful speech and platform jurisdiction matter |

**Definition.** Campaign-finance and media regulation seeks a level playing field by making influence visible, attributable and reviewable.

**Answer line.** Money power is not only expenditure volume; it is hidden sponsorship, surrogate publicity and unequal amplification.

**Keywords.** sections 77-78; section 10A; paid news; MCMC; pre-certification; social media; audit trail.

**ECI-specific argument.**
- **Claim:** ECI monitoring is strongest when administrative detection connects to a clear statutory consequence.
- **Named evidence:** RPA 1951 requires candidate expense accounts and permits disqualification for failure to lodge them as required.
- **Analysis:** Observers, flying squads and media monitoring can identify hidden expenditure before it distorts the campaign.
- **Qualification:** ECI cannot create unlimited party-spending offences or platform regulation without legislative authority.

**Evidence.** Paid news may be added to a candidate's account on evidence; separate bribery, false-statement or other offences require their own elements.

**Prelims trap.** Candidate expenditure ceiling and total political-party campaign expenditure are not the same legal category.

**Mains use.** Recommend real-time disclosure, third-party spending rules, transparent digital-ad libraries and reasoned enforcement.

**Recap.** Detect -> attribute -> apply the correct legal consequence.

### SESSION 12 - POLITICAL-PARTY REGISTRATION AND LIMITED DEREGISTRATION

```text
association applies under RPA 1951 section 29A
            |
constitution, office-bearers, prescribed particulars,
allegiance to constitutional principles
            |
         ECI registration
            |
registration != recognition != reserved symbol
```

**Definition.** Section 29A registration gives an association legal status as a political party for election law; recognition is a separate Symbols Order status.

**Answer line.** The ECI has a registration power, not a general supervisory power to dissolve political parties.

**Keywords.** section 29A; registered unrecognised party; recognition; fraud; limited review; internal democracy.

**ECI-specific argument.**
- **Claim:** Limited deregistration protects political association from an unenumerated administrative death penalty.
- **Named evidence:** *Indian National Congress (I) v. Institute of Social Welfare* (2002) held that ECI lacks a general power to deregister, subject to narrow situations such as fraud, section 29A(9)-type consequences or a party becoming unlawful under competent law.
- **Analysis:** The holding respects statutory limits but leaves weak enforcement against dormant, non-compliant or internally undemocratic parties.
- **Qualification:** Deregistration, derecognition and symbol consequences are distinct; reform requires legislation with hearing and appeal.

**Evidence.** Registration applications must contain the statutory constitutional-allegiance declaration.

**Prelims trap.** Violation of an ECI guideline does not automatically prove a general implied power to cancel registration.

**Mains use.** Propose audited accounts, periodic compliance, internal-election disclosure and a narrowly drafted deregistration law.

**Recap.** Registration opens the legal door; recognition and symbols are later, separate questions.

### SESSION 13 - RECOGNITION, SYMBOLS AND PARTY SPLITS

```text
registered party
     |
Symbols Order tests -> recognised National/State party OR unrecognised party
     |
reserved symbol / free symbol rules
     |
rival groups claim old party -> paragraph 15 adjudication
organisational wing + legislative wing + continuity evidence
```

**Definition.** The Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968 structures recognition, symbol reservation/allotment and disputes between rival groups of a recognised party.

**Answer line.** A symbol dispute is an electoral-identity adjudication, not a judicial settlement of every property or constitutional dispute inside a party.

**Keywords.** Symbols Order 1968; paragraph 15; recognised party; reserved symbol; legislative wing; organisational wing.

**ECI-specific argument.**
- **Claim:** Symbol adjudication protects voter clarity when competing factions claim the same electoral identity.
- **Named evidence:** *Kanhiya Lal Omar v. R.K. Trivedi* (1985) upheld the Symbols Order as a valid exercise connected with the Conduct of Elections Rules and Article 324.
- **Analysis:** A central decision prevents rival candidates from presenting indistinguishable party signals on the ballot.
- **Qualification:** ECI applies the Order's electoral tests and evidence; it does not acquire unlimited authority over all internal party governance.

**Evidence.** Recognition depends on notified electoral-performance criteria, not merely registration age or national ambition.

**Prelims trap.** Every registered party does not receive a permanently reserved symbol.

**Mains use.** Link transparent evidentiary tests, reasoned orders and internal-democracy reform.

**Recap.** Section 29A registers; the Symbols Order recognises and allocates.

### SESSION 14 - MODEL CODE OF CONDUCT: FAST, NON-STATUTORY AND LEGALLY BOUNDED

```text
schedule announced
       |
MCC begins -> party/candidate conduct | meetings | processions | polling day
       |      observers | party in power | manifestos
complaint -> response -> censure/restraint/administrative correction
       |
if conduct also violates statute -> police/prosecution/other legal route
       |
election process completed -> MCC ends
```

**Definition.** The MCC is a consensus-based, non-statutory campaign code enforced by the ECI through Article 324 supervision and existing legal powers.

**Answer line.** The MCC is not a penal statute, but the same act may independently violate the RPA, Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita or another law.

**Keywords.** Kerala 1960; 1968-69 evolution; schedule announcement; level playing field; non-statutory; statutory overlap.

**ECI-specific argument.**
- **Claim:** Non-statutory form gives preventive speed during a short campaign.
- **Named evidence:** ECI's MCC manual traces evolution from the 1960 Kerala code and later inter-party consensus; Article 324 supports rapid administrative action.
- **Analysis:** Transfers, official advertising restraint and campaign directions can prevent irreversible advantage before a criminal trial could conclude.
- **Qualification:** Speed cannot excuse inconsistent treatment, vague orders or punishment unsupported by law.

**Evidence.** *Abhiram Singh v. C.D. Commachen* (2017) broadly construed section 123(3)'s prohibition on appeals to religion, race, caste, community or language; this is statutory corrupt-practice law, not merely MCC etiquette.

**Prelims trap.** "MCC has no legal effect because it is non-statutory" is as wrong as "every MCC breach is a crime."

**Mains use.** Support a hybrid reform: retain flexible MCC, codify selected serious fields, publish complaint timelines and reasoned outcomes.

**Recap.** Fast code + existing law + transparent reasons.

### SESSION 15 - EVM, VVPAT, RULES 49MA/56D AND THE 2024-25 JUDICIAL CONTROL

```text
Ballot Unit -> Control Unit records vote -> VVPAT displays slip -> sealed drop box
                                            |
Rule 49MA: claimed mismatch -> declaration + test-vote procedure
Rule 56D: Returning Officer may order slip count on application; slip count controls discrepancy
                                            |
counting protocol + mandatory sample verification + court-directed safeguards
```

**Definition.** EVM-VVPAT combines electronic recording with a voter-visible paper audit trail retained for verification.

**Answer line.** Electoral technology must be assessed through architecture, custody, audit and remedy - neither presumed fraudulent nor declared infallible.

**Keywords.** VVPAT; paper trail; Rule 49MA; Rule 56D; Symbol Loading Unit; burnt-memory/microcontroller verification.

**ECI-specific argument.**
- **Claim:** Verifiability, not a return to manual ballots by assertion, is the constitutional response to technology distrust.
- **Named evidence:** *Subramanian Swamy v. ECI* (2013) required phased VVPAT introduction; *Association for Democratic Reforms v. ECI* (26 April 2024) rejected return to paper ballots and universal 100% VVPAT counting while adding safeguards.
- **Analysis:** Preserving Symbol Loading Units and enabling specified candidate-requested technical verification creates an evidence route without making every result depend on full manual recount.
- **Qualification:** The 2024 judgment did not certify every future procedure immune from review; specific evidence and statutory remedies remain relevant.

**Evidence.** The 7 May 2025 order clarified manufacturer-engineer diagnostic checking/certification, preservation of requested machines and a candidate-requested mock-poll display procedure within the Court's protocol.

**Prelims trap.** A VVPAT slip is displayed briefly and retained in the box; the voter does not take it away.

**Mains use.** Add public audit statistics, accessible technical explanations, custody transparency and rapid complaint resolution.

**Recap.** Secure device + paper audit + observable procedure + review.

### SESSION 16 - ARTICLE 324 POWER: MOHINDER SINGH GILL, A.C. JOSE AND NATURAL JUSTICE

```text
Is the field covered by Constitution, Act or Rules?
           |
     YES -> ECI obeys the law; cannot supplant it
           |
      NO -> Article 324 may fill the genuine gap
           |
action must serve free/fair election + rest on relevant material
           |
natural justice adjusted to urgency + reasons + judicial review
```

**Definition.** Article 324 is plenary within the election field, but its residual power operates where law is silent, not contrary to an occupied statutory field.

**Answer line.** Article 324 is a reservoir, not a rival legislature.

**Keywords.** gap-filling; supplement not supplant; occupied field; natural justice; relevant material; proportionality.

**ECI-specific argument.**
- **Claim:** Residual power is necessary because legislation cannot anticipate every election emergency.
- **Named evidence:** *Mohinder Singh Gill v. CEC* (1978) recognised Article 324's reservoir character; *A.C. Jose v. Sivan Pillai* (1984) held that ECI cannot override enacted rules.
- **Analysis:** Together they permit decisive action in a vacuum while preserving Parliament's Article 327 authority and the rule of law.
- **Qualification:** Election urgency can shape when and how hearing occurs, but it does not abolish fairness, reasons or review.

**Evidence.** *Mohinder Singh Gill* also insists that an order stands on the reasons contained in it; later affidavits cannot freely replace the original basis.

**Prelims trap.** "Plenary" does not mean the ECI can amend the RPA or Conduct of Elections Rules by instruction.

**Mains use.** Apply a four-step test: legal field, genuine gap, electoral necessity, procedural fairness.

**Recap.** Fill silence; obey text; record reasons.

### SESSION 17 - ARTICLE 329, ELECTION PETITIONS AND THE HIGH COURT ROUTE

```text
pre-result electoral process
     |
Article 329(b) anti-interruption rule
     |
result declared
     |
election petition: RPA 1951 ss.80, 80A, 81, 83
     |
High Court: dismiss / void election / declare another elected where conditions exist
     |
Supreme Court appeal: section 116A
```

**Definition.** Article 329 protects the election timetable by channelling challenges to parliamentary and State-legislature elections into the statutory post-result petition.

**Answer line.** The Constitution delays ordinary judicial interruption; it does not abolish judicial remedy.

**Keywords.** *N.P. Ponnuswami*; election petition; forty-five days; section 100; material effect; High Court; section 116A.

**ECI-specific argument.**
- **Claim:** The anti-interruption rule prevents fragmented litigation from stopping a democratic timetable.
- **Named evidence:** *N.P. Ponnuswami v. Returning Officer* (1952) treated the petition as the exclusive challenge route; *Election Commission v. Ashok Kumar* (2000) allowed limited judicial action that facilitates rather than obstructs the process.
- **Analysis:** Courts protect both continuity and legality by calibrating timing and remedy.
- **Qualification:** President and Vice-President election disputes go directly to the Supreme Court under Article 71 and their special statute, not this High Court route.

**Evidence.** Section 100 includes disqualification, corrupt practice, improper nomination decisions, vote irregularity and material non-compliance grounds.

**Prelims trap.** Article 329 is not a general bar on every election-related writ at every stage and for every office.

**Mains use.** Structure answers as constitutional bar -> petition procedure -> void grounds -> appeal -> delay reform.

**Recap.** Do not derail the poll; provide a full post-result court remedy.

### SESSION 18 - ELECTORAL REFORM: INDEPENDENCE, CLEAN POLITICS AND INFORMATION INTEGRITY

| Reform field | Deficit | Bounded response |
|---|---|---|
| appointment | executive predominance and weak public reasons | plural committee, independent search, dossier time, recorded comparative reasons |
| removal/secretariat | EC asymmetry and staff dependence | fair inquiry, protected dissent, permanent secretariat and predictable finance |
| expenditure | hidden third-party/digital spending | real-time disclosure, ad libraries, audit and proportionate penalties |
| criminalisation | slow trials and opaque selection | special courts, truthful disclosure, party reasons, no guilt by accusation |
| party democracy | weak internal accountability | statutory minimums with autonomy and appeal |
| misinformation | deepfakes and opaque amplification | rapid provenance notices, platform transparency and speech safeguards |
| MCC | flexibility but inconsistent reasons | hybrid codification and public complaint ledger |

**Definition.** Electoral reform improves the ecosystem of competition rather than granting the ECI unbounded power.

**Answer line.** The best reform combines institutional independence with transparent, reviewable exercise of existing authority.

**Keywords.** independence; accountability; criminalisation; political finance; internal democracy; misinformation; MCC debate; simultaneous elections.

**ECI-specific argument.**
- **Claim:** More power without clearer law and reasons may deepen contestation rather than trust.
- **Named evidence:** The appointment debate after *Anoop Baranwal*, disclosure cases, *Lily Thomas*, *Public Interest Foundation* and the 2024 EVM judgment expose different links in the trust chain.
- **Analysis:** Reform must assign the right tool to the right institution - ECI administration, legislative standards, court adjudication and party compliance.
- **Qualification:** Simultaneous elections are only a bounded cross-link here; efficiency claims must be tested against federal mandates, mid-term dissolution and accountability.

**Evidence.** *Abhiram Singh* addresses communal appeals; *Public Interest Foundation* addresses disclosure; neither authorises ECI to legislate new disqualifications.

**Prelims trap.** Delimitation Commission, SEC, High Court and government are not subordinate departments of the ECI.

**Mains use.** End with a sequenced package: independent entry, secure operation, transparent enforcement, quick courts and informed voters.

**Recap.** Trust is an ecosystem: entry rules + clean contest + auditable vote + timely remedy.

### PYQ-M1 - UPSC 2018 GS-II Q1

**Official-paper wording held locally:** "In the light of recent controversy regarding the use of Electronic Voting Machines (EVM), what are the challenges before the Election Commission of India to ensure the trustworthiness of elections in India?" (10 marks, 150 words)

**Model answer.** Trustworthiness is an ecosystem, not only a machine question. First, roll accuracy requires inclusion, notice before deletion and accessible correction. Second, the ECI must ensure neutral staff, secure custody and transparent counting. Third, money power, paid news, criminal antecedents and misinformation can make a technically secure poll substantively unfair. Fourth, technology needs visible audit. *Subramanian Swamy v. ECI* (2013) made VVPAT a confidence safeguard; *ADR v. ECI* (2024) retained EVM-VVPAT, rejected universal counting and added preservation and candidate-requested verification measures. Fifth, MCC complaints require prompt, comparable reasons. Yet unsupported fraud allegations should not be amplified. The answer is auditable procedure: publish aggregate verification outcomes, strengthen observer and candidate access, explain safeguards in plain language and provide swift legal remedies. The ECI earns trust not by demanding faith but by making each link - roll, campaign, poll, count and complaint - independently verifiable.

### PYQ-M2 - UPSC 2019 GS-II Q11

**Official-paper wording held locally:** "On what grounds a people's representative can be disqualified under the Representation of Peoples Act, 1951? Also mention the remedies available to such person against his disqualification." (15 marks, 250 words)

**Model answer.** The RPA 1951 operationalises the "law made by Parliament" ground in Articles 102 and 191. Its principal disqualifications include conviction for specified offences under section 8; corrupt practices under section 8A; dismissal for corruption or disloyalty under section 9; subsisting government contracts under section 9A; office under a government company under section 10; and failure to lodge election-expense accounts under section 10A. *Lily Thomas v. Union of India* (2013) invalidated section 8(4), so a sitting legislator no longer receives an automatic grace period after a qualifying conviction.

Remedies depend on the source. An appellate stay of conviction, not merely suspension of sentence, can halt conviction-based consequences. Section 11 empowers the ECI to remove or reduce specified disqualification where the statute permits. A sitting-member question under Articles 103 or 192 goes to the President or Governor after obtaining the ECI's opinion. An election-petition judgment is appealable to the Supreme Court under section 116A. Defection is different: the Tenth Schedule assigns it to the Speaker or Chairman.

The system therefore combines strict grounds with layered review; its weakness is delay. Time-bound courts and reasoned ECI opinions should improve remedy without diluting proof.

### PYQ-M3 - UPSC 2020 GS-II Q1

**Official-paper wording held locally:** "There is a need for simplification of procedure for disqualification of persons found guilty of corrupt practices under the Representation of Peoples Act. Comment." (10 marks, 150 words)

**Model answer.** Simplification is justified because the present chain can outlast the elected term, but the evidentiary threshold must remain strict. A section 123 corrupt practice is proved in a High Court election petition; section 99 may name guilty persons; section 8A disqualification then proceeds through the President acting on the ECI's opinion. Multiple stages protect fairness but reduce deterrence when hearings and references are delayed. Article 324 cannot authorise the ECI to bypass the petition and summarily declare guilt. Reform should therefore simplify time, service and coordination: dedicated election benches, digital filing and evidence, a published timetable for the court-ECI-President sequence, and reasoned decisions. Because the consequence can exclude a citizen from electoral participation for years, natural justice and appellate review remain essential. Simplify procedure, not proof.

### PYQ-M4 - UPSC 2022 GS-II Q11

**Official-paper wording held locally:** "Discuss the procedures to decide the disputes arising out of the election of a Member of the Parliament or State Legislature under the Representation of the People Act, 1951. What are the grounds on which the election of any returned candidate may be declared void? What remedy is available to the aggrieved party against the decision? Refer to the case laws." (15 marks, 250 words)

**Model answer.** Article 329(b) channels a challenge to a parliamentary or State-legislature election into the RPA election-petition route. *N.P. Ponnuswami v. Returning Officer* (1952) explains the anti-interruption principle: ordinary litigation should not derail an election midway.

Under sections 80 and 80A, an election is questioned only by petition tried by the High Court. Section 81 generally requires presentation within forty-five days; section 83 requires material facts and full particulars of corrupt practice. The statutory trial follows with necessary parties and strict pleading requirements.

Section 100 permits an election to be declared void for lack of qualification or disqualification; corrupt practice by the returned candidate or election agent; improper rejection of nomination; and material effect caused by improper acceptance, corrupt practice by others, improper reception/refusal/rejection of votes, or non-compliance with the Constitution, Act, Rules or lawful orders. The High Court may dismiss, void the election or, where statutory conditions are met, declare another candidate elected. Section 116A provides appeal to the Supreme Court.

*Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain* (1975) protected free and fair elections and judicial review. The design rightly preserves the poll timetable, but time-bound adjudication is necessary so victory does not exhaust the remedy.

### PYQ-M5 - UPSC 2022 GS-II Q15

**Exact official-paper wording held locally:** "Discuss the role of the Election Commission of India in the light of the evolution of the Model Code of Conduct." (15 marks, 250 words)

**Model answer.** The MCC is a consensus-based, non-statutory code through which the ECI converts Article 324 supervision into rapid campaign-time standards. ECI material traces its origin to the 1960 Kerala election and its national evolution through the 1968-69 period and later party consultations.

From schedule announcement until completion, the ECI regulates meetings, processions, polling-day conduct, manifestos and misuse of official machinery; receives complaints; seeks responses; censures or restrains campaigners; and orders administrative correction. Its strength is preventive speed: transfers, advertising restraint or a campaign direction can remove an unfair advantage before ordinary litigation concludes.

The MCC is not itself a complete penal code. Bribery, undue influence, communal appeals and other conduct may separately attract the RPA 1951, BNS or another statute. *Abhiram Singh v. C.D. Commachen* (2017), for example, interprets section 123(3)'s corrupt-practice prohibition, not a free-standing MCC offence.

Weaknesses are inconsistent response time, limited direct sanctions and insufficient reasons. Full criminal codification may sacrifice speed. A hybrid reform should retain flexible preventive rules, codify serious misuse of state resources, and publish complaint timelines, reasons and statutory referrals. The ECI's legitimacy requires both immediacy and equal treatment.

### PYQ-M6 - UPSC 2024 GS-II Q1

**Exact wording preserved in the local owner:** "Examine the need for electoral reforms as suggested by various committees with particular reference to 'one nation - one election' principle." (10 marks, 150 words)

**Model answer.** Electoral reform is needed in political finance, criminalisation, roll integrity, ECI independence and dispute delay; simultaneous elections are one contested proposal within that larger agenda. Supporters cite lower recurring administrative cost, fewer repeated MCC periods and policy continuity. Yet synchronisation must address Articles 83 and 172, mid-term dissolution, ECI capacity and the federal risk that State mandates become absorbed by a national campaign. Committee recommendations cannot replace constitutional consent. Priority should first go to transparent donations and digital ads, time-bound criminal and election trials, accurate rolls and a credible appointment process. Simultaneous elections should proceed only with a workable remainder-term or constructive-confidence design, federal consultation and judicial safeguards. Efficiency is valuable, but it cannot override representative accountability.

### PYQ-M7 - UPSC 2025 GS-II Q1

**Official-paper wording held locally:** "Discuss the 'corrupt practices' for the purpose of the Representation of the People Act, 1951. Analyze whether the increase in the assets of the legislators and/or their associates, disproportionate to their known sources of income, would constitute 'undue influence' and consequently a corrupt practice." (10 marks, 150 words)

**Model answer.** Section 123 treats bribery, undue influence, specified identity or religious appeals, promotion of enmity, false candidate statements, prohibited transport, excess expenditure and prohibited public-servant assistance as corrupt practices. Undue influence under section 123(2) targets direct or indirect interference with the free exercise of an electoral right. Disproportionate growth in a legislator's or associate's assets may raise disclosure, tax or anti-corruption concerns, but without an electoral nexus showing coercion or interference with voter freedom it is not automatically undue influence. *ADR* and *PUCL* instead support full candidate-information disclosure and verification. If illicit assets finance a proven inducement, bribery or another corrupt practice may arise on its own elements. The correct response is rigorous disclosure and investigation, not stretching a defined electoral wrong beyond its text.

### ORIGINAL MAINS PRACTICE - EXACTLY SIX MODELS

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 - Explain why Article 324 is a reservoir of power but not a source of authority contrary to election law. (10 marks, 150 words)

**Model answer.** Article 324 grants the ECI plenary superintendence, direction and control so that unforeseen gaps do not paralyse a free and fair election. In *Mohinder Singh Gill v. CEC* (1978), the Supreme Court treated it as a reservoir where legislation is silent. This permits urgent, election-specific safeguards based on relevant material. However, Parliament possesses election-law competence under Article 327. *A.C. Jose v. Sivan Pillai* (1984) therefore held that ECI may supplement, not supplant, the Act and Rules. The Commission also owes context-sensitive natural justice and recorded reasons. Thus, Article 324 is decisive in a genuine legal vacuum but obedient where the field is occupied. Its legitimacy lies in combining operational flexibility with the rule of law.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 - Distinguish the constitutional position of the CEC from that of the other Election Commissioners. (10 marks, 150 words)

**Model answer.** The CEC and other ECs are functionally equal members of a multi-member Commission. *T.N. Seshan v. Union of India* (1995) upheld collegial equality; section 18 of Act 49 of 2023 seeks unanimity and otherwise uses majority opinion. The CEC is Chairman, not a unilateral superior. Security, however, is asymmetric. The CEC can be removed only in the same manner and on the same grounds as a Supreme Court judge, and Article 324 protects the CEC against disadvantageous variation of service conditions. Other ECs have the same statutory salary and term framework but may be removed by the President only on the CEC's recommendation. Thus, equality of vote coexists with unequal tenure protection. Reform should protect collegial dissent without making serious misconduct practically unaccountable.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 - Critically examine the appointment architecture under Act 49 of 2023. (15 marks, 250 words)

**Model answer.** Article 324(2) expressly permits Parliament to regulate appointment. Act 49 of 2023 therefore validly replaces the earlier executive convention and the temporary *Anoop Baranwal* arrangement.

The design has four stages. Section 5 limits eligibility to Secretary-equivalent persons of integrity with election-management knowledge and experience. Section 6 creates a Law-Minister-led Search Committee with two Secretary-rank members and a panel of five. Section 7 establishes the Prime Minister, Lok Sabha Opposition leader and a PM-nominated Union Cabinet Minister as the Selection Committee. Section 8 allows transparent self-regulated procedure and consideration beyond the panel; the President appoints by warrant.

Its strengths are a statutory process, express qualifications and Opposition participation. Its weaknesses are executive control of the search, a two-to-one executive majority, an outside-panel power and no express duty to publish comparative reasons. These features may reduce perceived independence even if individual appointments are meritorious.

*Anoop Baranwal* did not permanently require the CJI; it operated only until Parliament legislated. As of 7 September 2026, the Act operates and no official final merits judgment or stay was located; the 22 March 2024 order refused interim interference.

Reform should publish criteria, give members adequate dossiers, record comparative reasons, strengthen search independence and protect meaningful Opposition participation. Legality is established by statute; legitimacy depends on credible insulation from the incumbent executive.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 - The Model Code of Conduct gains from flexibility but loses legitimacy through opacity. Discuss. (15 marks, 250 words)

**Model answer.** The MCC is a non-statutory, consensus-based code operating through Article 324 from schedule announcement until completion. Its flexibility is an institutional strength. The ECI can rapidly stop misuse of official machinery, restrain campaigning, seek responses, transfer officers and refer independently illegal conduct without awaiting a full criminal trial. Such preventive speed protects a level playing field when delayed correction would be meaningless.

Yet flexibility creates legitimacy risks. Direct sanctions are limited, similar complaints may receive different timelines, short orders may not reveal the standard applied, and MCC language can blur with statutory offences. A code cannot itself create crimes or disqualifications. Bribery, communal appeals and other conduct must satisfy the RPA, BNS or another applicable law. *Abhiram Singh* demonstrates that statutory corrupt-practice adjudication remains judicial.

Full codification may replace swift prevention with slow litigation. A hybrid is preferable: retain flexible campaign etiquette; legislate clear sanctions for serious misuse of government resources and repeated non-compliance; distinguish preventive from punitive orders; provide a short hearing where feasible; and publish a searchable complaint, timeline, reason and referral ledger.

The MCC should remain quick, but not inscrutable. Transparent consistency converts moral authority into durable institutional trust.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 - Evaluate the ECI as an electoral-trust ecosystem rather than merely an election-day manager. (20 marks, 250 words)

**Model answer.** Article 324 makes the ECI responsible for the complete conditions of electoral choice, not only polling day. Trust begins with inclusive, accurate rolls under the RPA 1950. It continues through neutral staff under Article 324(6), lawful nominations, candidate disclosure after *ADR/PUCL*, expenditure and paid-news monitoring, MCC enforcement and secure EVM-VVPAT custody. It ends with transparent counting, reasoned corrective orders and the High Court election-petition remedy under Article 329 and the RPA 1951.

This ecosystem explains why machine security alone is insufficient. *Subramanian Swamy* and the 2024 *ADR v. ECI* judgment strengthened verifiability, but a voter excluded from the roll or exposed to hidden money power remains disenfranchised in substance. Similarly, broad Article 324 authority cannot replace law: *Mohinder Singh Gill* permits gap-filling, while *A.C. Jose* bars statutory contradiction.

Institutional independence also has multiple links - appointment, removal, collegiality, secretariat, finance and transparent reasons. Act 49 of 2023 creates a lawful appointment process but leaves an executive-dominance debate; Article 324(6) supplies staff without creating an autonomous cadre.

Reform must therefore be sequenced: independent search and reasoned selection; permanent secretariat and predictable finance; deletion safeguards and accessible roll appeals; real-time political-finance and digital-ad disclosure; comparable MCC decisions; audited technology; and time-bound election courts. The ECI earns authority when every stage is lawful, observable and correctable.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 - Propose a balanced reform programme for political parties, criminalisation and campaign information without granting unbounded power to the ECI. (20 marks, 250 words)

**Model answer.** Reform must respect the division between legislation, ECI administration, party autonomy and judicial adjudication.

First, Parliament should strengthen party transparency: periodic audited accounts, beneficial-donor disclosure, digital-ad libraries, minimum internal-election and membership rules, and a narrowly drafted deregistration regime with notice and appeal. *Institute of Social Welfare* shows why ECI cannot invent a general cancellation power under section 29A.

Second, criminalisation requires information and speed rather than guilt by accusation. *ADR/PUCL* support affidavits; *Public Interest Foundation* requires prominent publicity and party reasons; *Lily Thomas* ensures immediate qualifying conviction consequences. Dedicated courts and sanctions for false disclosure should complement protection against politically motivated cases.

Third, campaign information needs real-time expenditure reporting, third-party spending rules, paid-news attribution on evidence, platform ad transparency and rapid deepfake provenance notices. ECI should administer these standards, but speech restrictions and offences need clear law.

Fourth, MCC reform should be hybrid: retain preventive flexibility, codify serious misuse of official machinery, and publish complaint timelines and reasons. Symbol disputes should remain under the Symbols Order with reasoned paragraph 15 analysis, not become general control of party property or ideology.

Finally, oversight must be reciprocal: a more independent ECI requires transparent selection, protected staff, published enforcement standards and timely judicial review. The objective is accountable electoral competition, not an omnipotent regulator.


## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH - NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

### ADVANCED 1 - APPOINTMENT DESIGN BEYOND A BINARY CJI-DEBATE

```text
eligible pool -> independent search -> plural selection -> recorded reasons
             -> President's warrant -> parliamentary/public accountability
```

**Definition.** Appointment design is the institutional method used to insulate selection from partisan control while retaining democratic accountability.

**Answer line.** CJI membership is one design option; the constitutional test is credible independence across search, deliberation and reasons.

**Keywords.** independent search; plural selection; conflict rule; dossier time; recorded reasons.

**ECI-specific argument.** **Claim:** appointment legitimacy depends on the whole funnel. **Named evidence:** Act 49 of 2023 combines an executive-led search, Opposition participation and an outside-panel power. **Analysis:** a lawful process can still produce perceived dependence if one actor controls the shortlist and voting majority. **Qualification:** judicial membership is not the only constitutionally permissible safeguard.

**Evidence.** *Anoop Baranwal* used a CJI-inclusive arrangement only until Parliament legislated.

**Prelims trap.** A plural committee is not automatically non-executive or independent.

**Mains use.** Test breadth of pool, conflicts, dossier access, comparative reasons and dissent.

**Recap.** Institutional incentives matter more than one famous officeholder on the committee.

### ADVANCED 2 - ROLL REVISION, CITIZENSHIP AND EXCLUSION RISK

```text
electoral eligibility inquiry
      !=
final citizenship adjudication
```

**Definition.** Roll-revision proportionality balances accurate citizenship-based enrolment with protection against wrongful exclusion.

**Answer line.** An electoral eligibility inquiry may protect roll purity, but it cannot become a parallel citizenship court.

**Keywords.** limited inquiry; notice; exclusion error; competent authority; accessible proof.

**ECI-specific argument.** **Claim:** roll revision requires asymmetric-error safeguards. **Named evidence:** 2026 INSC 564 permits limited electoral inquiry but reserves final citizenship adjudication to the competent authority. **Analysis:** wrongful inclusion affects roll accuracy, while wrongful exclusion immediately removes political voice. **Qualification:** procedural protection does not prevent lawful deletion after evidence and hearing.

**Evidence.** Aadhaar identifies a person but is not proof of citizenship.

**Prelims trap.** An ECI roll decision is not a universal legal declaration that a person is not an Indian citizen.

**Mains use.** Recommend notice, reasoned orders, correction windows, public deletion data and assisted documentation.

**Recap.** Verify eligibility without converting administration into final status adjudication.

### ADVANCED 3 - POLITICAL PARTIES AS PUBLIC ACTORS AND PRIVATE ASSOCIATIONS

```text
association autonomy <---- balance ----> public electoral function
```

**Definition.** Political parties are private associations performing indispensable public electoral functions.

**Answer line.** Public consequences justify transparency standards, but association autonomy bars unlimited ECI management.

**Keywords.** internal democracy; association autonomy; public function; proportional regulation; appeal.

**ECI-specific argument.** **Claim:** party regulation must be express and proportionate. **Named evidence:** section 29A authorises registration, while *Institute of Social Welfare* rejects a general implied deregistration power. **Analysis:** disclosure and minimum democratic standards protect voters without making ECI the party's permanent administrator. **Qualification:** serious cancellation requires legislated grounds, hearing and appeal.

**Evidence.** Symbols Order recognition regulates electoral status separately from registration.

**Prelims trap.** A political party is not a constitutional organ merely because it contests elections.

**Mains use.** Propose periodic internal elections, membership rules, audited accounts and independent dispute resolution.

**Recap.** Regulate the public electoral function without extinguishing lawful association.

### ADVANCED 4 - MCC CODIFICATION OPTIONS

| Model | Gain | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| fully non-statutory | speed and flexibility | uneven reasons and weak direct sanctions |
| full penal code | certainty | litigation delay and over-criminalisation |
| hybrid | codify serious repeat fields; retain preventive code | boundary drafting remains difficult |

**Definition.** MCC codification asks which campaign standards should become statutory while preserving rapid preventive administration.

**Answer line.** Codify serious and repetitive abuse, not every campaign etiquette rule.

**Keywords.** hybrid model; preventive restraint; punitive finding; reasoned order; complaint ledger.

**ECI-specific argument.** **Claim:** hybrid codification best combines speed and certainty. **Named evidence:** current MCC action relies on Article 324, while bribery and communal appeals already use statutory routes. **Analysis:** selected sanctions can deter misuse of official machinery without forcing every complaint into criminal litigation. **Qualification:** vague penal drafting may chill speech and slow immediate correction.

**Evidence.** MCC and section 123 can apply to related conduct but remain legally distinct.

**Prelims trap.** Statutory overlap does not transform the whole MCC into an Act.

**Mains use.** Separate preventive orders, statutory referrals, hearings and publication duties.

**Recap.** Preserve rapid correction; add law and reasons where coercive consequences are serious.

### ADVANCED 5 - AN ELECTORAL-TRUST AUDIT

```text
INPUTS          PROCESS             OUTPUT             REMEDY
appointments -> neutral staff -> auditable count -> reasoned order/court
rolls        -> fair campaign -> transparent result -> timely correction
```

**Definition.** An electoral-trust audit tests every institutional input, process, output and remedy.

**Answer line.** Trust fails at the weakest link, whether appointment, roll, campaign, machine, order or court delay.

**Keywords.** ecosystem; input; process; audit; remedy; reciprocal accountability.

**ECI-specific argument.** **Claim:** ECI performance cannot be judged by polling-day security alone. **Named evidence:** disclosure cases, MCC practice, VVPAT judgments and Article 329 petitions regulate different stages. **Analysis:** an accurate machine cannot cure an excluded voter or hidden expenditure, while a good roll cannot cure an unauditable count. **Qualification:** ecosystem analysis must still identify the exact legal owner of each failure.

**Evidence.** ECI, SEC, Delimitation Commission, government and courts perform distinct functions.

**Prelims trap.** Electoral accountability does not mean executive control of the Commission.

**Mains use.** Ask six questions: who appoints, who staffs, what is disclosed, which rule applies, what reasons exist, and what remedy is timely.

**Recap.** Independence and accountability are complements when both are legally structured.

## CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

### CONSTITUTIONAL SPINE AND INSTITUTIONAL BOUNDARIES

- Article 324: ECI controls rolls and elections to Parliament, State legislatures, President and Vice-President.
- Article 325: one general roll; no exclusion or special claim only on religion, race, caste or sex.
- Article 326: adult suffrage for Lok Sabha/Assemblies; 61st Amendment Act, 1988 lowered age from 21 to 18.
- Articles 327-328: Parliament legislates; State field is subordinate to parliamentary law.
- Article 329: anti-interruption design plus election-petition remedy; not total immunity.
- Articles 103/192: President/Governor decides specified sitting-member disqualification after ECI opinion.
- Articles 243K/243ZA: SEC, not ECI, controls Panchayat and municipal polls.

### COMPOSITION, APPOINTMENT AND SECURITY

- Single-member from 1950; temporary multi-member 1989-90; three-member continuously from October 1993.
- *T.N. Seshan* (1995): multi-member Commission valid; CEC chairs, members decide collegially.
- *Anoop Baranwal* (2023): PM + Opposition leader + CJI only until Parliament enacted law.
- Act 49 of 2023 commenced 2 January 2024 and repealed the 1991 Act.
- Search Committee: Law Minister + two Secretary-rank-or-above members -> five names.
- Selection Committee: PM + LoP/largest opposition leader + PM-nominated Union Cabinet Minister.
- Eligibility: Secretary-equivalent rank, integrity, election-management knowledge/experience.
- Term: six years or 65; no reappointment; aggregate six-year cap if EC becomes CEC.
- Salary: equal to Supreme Court judge under enacted section 10.
- CEC removal: like Supreme Court judge; EC/Regional Commissioner: President only on CEC recommendation.
- Article 324 protects CEC service conditions from disadvantageous post-appointment variation.
- Current status through 7 September 2026: Act operative; 22 March 2024 interim refusal; no located final merits ruling/stay.

### ADMINISTRATION, STAFF AND TIMETABLE

- Article 324(6): President/Governor supplies staff on ECI request; no separate constitutional election cadre.
- Schedule announcement and statutory notification are distinct steps.
- Section 151A: by-election ordinarily within six months; remaining-term and unavoidable-circumstance exceptions.
- Sections 57, 58, 58A: adjournment/fresh poll/booth-capturing remedies; use exact trigger.
- Countermanding is not an unbounded Article 324 discretion.

### RPA 1950, ROLLS AND DELIMITATION

- RPA 1950: allocation/constituency framework and electoral rolls.
- Section 19: age and ordinary residence; sections 21-23: revision, correction/deletion and inclusion.
- Four qualifying dates: 1 Jan, 1 Apr, 1 Jul, 1 Oct.
- Roll integrity = inclusion + lawful purification + notice + accessible remedy.
- 2026 INSC 564: limited electoral-purpose citizenship inquiry, not final citizenship adjudication.
- Delimitation Commission is distinct; ECI participates as law provides.

### RPA 1951, CANDIDATES AND DISPUTES

- RPA 1951: conduct, qualifications/disqualifications, expenses, corrupt practices, parties and petitions.
- Sections 8-10A: principal statutory disqualification grounds.
- *Lily Thomas* (2013): section 8(4) shield invalid; qualifying conviction consequence immediate.
- Section 123: corrupt practices; *Abhiram Singh* (2017) broad identity/religious-appeal control.
- Affidavits: *ADR* (2002), *PUCL* (2003); criminal-antecedent publicity: *Public Interest Foundation* (2018/2020).
- Article 329(b) -> sections 80/80A High Court petition -> section 116A Supreme Court appeal.
- *Ponnuswami* (1952): no mid-process derailment; *Ashok Kumar* (2000): limited facilitating review.

### PARTIES, SYMBOLS AND MCC

- Section 29A registration != recognition != reserved symbol.
- *Institute of Social Welfare* (2002): no general deregistration power; narrow exceptions.
- Symbols Order 1968 and paragraph 15 govern recognised-party splits.
- *Kanhiya Lal Omar* (1985): Symbols Order valid.
- MCC: non-statutory, begins with schedule announcement, ends with completion.
- MCC breach may overlap with RPA/BNS/other law; code alone is not a full penal statute.
- Reform: retain speed, publish reasons, codify selected serious fields.

### MONEY, MEDIA AND TECHNOLOGY

- Candidate expense account/ceiling != identical party-spending ceiling.
- Paid news: evidence -> monitoring -> possible expense attribution/legal action.
- Social media: ad certification, disclosure and applicable law; ECI is not universal platform regulator.
- *Subramanian Swamy* (2013): VVPAT paper trail.
- *ADR v. ECI* (2024): no paper-ballot return or universal count; limited preservation/verification safeguards.
- 2025 clarification: diagnostic certification and candidate-requested protocol.
- Technology answer: architecture + custody + audit + complaint remedy.
