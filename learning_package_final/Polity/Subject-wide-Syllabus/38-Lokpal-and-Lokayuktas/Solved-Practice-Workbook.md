### Separate solved MCQ key

### MCQ 1 — A

**Answer:A.**

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A:** Correct. Section 3 establishes Lokpal under parliamentary legislation; sections 20 and 35 separate its process role from criminal adjudication.
- **B:** A resolution cannot substitute for the 2013 parliamentary Act.
- **C:** CVC is a separate statutory vigilance body, not Lokpal's parent agency.
- **D:** Special Courts, not Lokpal, adjudicate criminal guilt.

**Examiner trap 1:** Do not convert statutory status into either executive-resolution status or constitutional-court status.

### MCQ 2 — B

**Answer:B.**

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A:** The statutory ceiling is eight Members, not ten.
- **B:** Correct. Section 3 fixes one Chairperson and a ceiling of eight Members; it neither guarantees all seats are filled nor adds the CVC.
- **C:** The Act sets a maximum, not a requirement that all eight posts be filled.
- **D:** CVC is separate and not an ex officio Lokpal Member.

**Examiner trap 2:** Read 'not exceeding' as a ceiling, not a mandatory filled strength.

### MCQ 3 — C

**Answer:C.**

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A:** The representation floor applies to all Members, not only non-judicial ones.
- **B:** The judicial half is calculated among Members, not with the Chairperson added.
- **C:** Correct. Section 3 uses Members as the denominator for both the judicial share and the SC/ST/OBC/minority/women floor; the categories may overlap.
- **D:** Judicial Members need not all be serving judges; eligibility includes former judges.

**Examiner trap 3:** UPSC may change the denominator, replace a floor with an exact quota, or require serving judges.

### MCQ 4 — D

**Answer:D.**

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A:** A former CJI is eligible but not the only possible Chairperson.
- **B:** A Supreme Court Judge is eligible but eminent persons may also qualify.
- **C:** The Act permits former judges and eligible eminent persons, not only serving CJIs.
- **D:** Correct. The Act provides judicial routes and an eminent-person route linked to the integrity, ability and expertise criteria for a non-judicial Member.

**Examiner trap 4:** The Chairperson's office is not reserved exclusively for a former CJI.

### MCQ 5 — A

**Answer:A.**

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A:** Correct. Section 3 specifies at least 25 years in anti-corruption policy, public administration, vigilance, finance including insurance and banking, law or management.
- **B:** The special-knowledge threshold is twenty-five years in specified fields, not any thirty years.
- **C:** Judicial service is a distinct eligibility route; ten years does not meet non-judicial expertise.
- **D:** Being an MP is a disqualification during office, not a twenty-year qualification.

**Examiner trap 5:** Do not replace a listed-field expertise test with generic seniority.

### MCQ 6 — B

**Answer:B.**

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A:** Serving parliamentarians are expressly disqualified.
- **B:** Correct. Retirement from public service is not itself a bar; section 3(4) separately lists legislative office, local-body membership, removal/dismissal, age and incompatibilities.
- **C:** A sitting municipal member is barred by the elected-office rule.
- **D:** A person dismissed from government service is excluded by section 3.

**Examiner trap 6:** Do not confuse prior public service with disqualifying removal from service.

### MCQ 7 — C

**Answer:C.**

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A:** President and CVC are not both members of this five-person recommendation committee.
- **B:** Speaker rather than Rajya Sabha Chairman sits on it; CVC is not a member.
- **C:** Correct. Section 4 names the Prime Minister as chair, Speaker, Lok Sabha LoP, CJI or nominated Supreme Court judge, and one eminent jurist.
- **D:** Home Minister and Cabinet Secretary are not members of this Lokpal committee.

**Examiner trap 7:** A plausible office from the CVC or CBI appointment process is not automatically a Lokpal selector.

### MCQ 8 — D

**Answer:D.**

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A:** The CJI alone does not nominate the eminent jurist.
- **B:** The Search Committee produces a candidate panel, not the final jurist nomination.
- **C:** Joint parliamentary election is not section 4's nomination procedure.
- **D:** Correct. Section 4(1)(e) assigns nomination to the President on the recommendation of the first four members of the Selection Committee.

**Examiner trap 8:** The President is the formal nominator, but the recommendation source is statutorily specified.

### MCQ 9 — A

**Answer:A.**

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A:** Correct. Section 4(3) requires at least seven persons with listed expertise, a 50 per cent diversity floor, and permits selection beyond its recommended panel.
- **B:** Search proposes names; the President appoints following the Selection Committee recommendation.
- **C:** Expertise extends beyond serving judges and includes diverse fields.
- **D:** At least seven experts is different from five constitutional office-holders.

**Examiner trap 9:** Search and selection are different stages; the Search Committee is neither appointing authority nor exclusive gatekeeper.

### MCQ 10 — B

**Answer:B.**

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A:** A vacancy does not transfer the appointment power to the Search Committee.
- **B:** Correct. Section 4(2) validates action despite a Selection Committee vacancy, and Common Cause held the Act workable without waiting for proposed amendments.
- **C:** A vacancy does not itself invalidate the proceedings under section 4(2).
- **D:** The Act has no automatic permanent largest-opposition-party substitution for the LoP seat.

**Examiner trap 10:** Do not turn a vacancy-saving clause into a textual largest-opposition-party substitution.

### MCQ 11 — C

**Answer:C.**

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A:** The five-year period ends earlier if the Member turns seventy.
- **B:** The age ceiling is seventy and the maximum term five years.
- **C:** Correct. Section 6 fixes five years from entering office or attainment of age 70, whichever occurs earlier.
- **D:** Four years is CVC's tenure rule, not Lokpal's five-year rule.

**Examiner trap 11:** Lokpal tenure must not be borrowed from the CVC, CIC or Election Commission.

### MCQ 12 — D

**Answer:D.**

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A:** Ordinary second terms are barred; only the limited Member-to-Chairperson route remains.
- **B:** Post-office employment restrictions prevent this unrestricted Union appointment.
- **C:** The statutory post-tenure restriction includes immediate electoral candidacy.
- **D:** Correct. Section 8 bars ordinary reappointment and specified assignments/employment/elections, while allowing Member-to-Chairperson elevation within a five-year aggregate.

**Examiner trap 12:** The Member-to-Chairperson exception does not restart the tenure clock.

### MCQ 13 — A

**Answer:A.**

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A:** Correct. Section 37 places proved misbehaviour on a petition-reference-Supreme Court inquiry route and bars Lokpal from inquiring into its own Chairperson or Member.
- **B:** A Cabinet-only inquiry would bypass the Supreme Court safeguard.
- **C:** An internal bench vote cannot replace Presidential reference and Supreme Court inquiry.
- **D:** An ordinary Lok Sabha majority is not the section 37 misbehaviour route.

**Examiner trap 13:** Use section 37's own threshold; do not import impeachment or CVC formulas.

### MCQ 14 — B

**Answer:B.**

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A:** A critical editorial is not an enumerated removal ground.
- **B:** Correct. Insolvency is listed, along with paid outside employment during tenure and qualifying infirmity of mind or body.
- **C:** Dissent within Lokpal is not insolvency, paid employment or infirmity.
- **D:** Policy disagreement is not a statutory ground for direct Presidential removal.

**Examiner trap 14:** Direct grounds are narrow statutory facts, not political dissatisfaction.

### MCQ 15 — C

**Answer:C.**

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A:** CVC unanimity cannot replace the full Lokpal bench vote.
- **B:** There is no two-House parliamentary precondition to starting this inquiry.
- **C:** Correct. Section 14 requires full-bench consideration and approval by at least two-thirds, in addition to subject exclusions and in-camera treatment.
- **D:** The CJI does not personally conduct the statutory preliminary inquiry.

**Examiner trap 15:** PM coverage is neither ordinary identical treatment nor complete immunity.

### MCQ 16 — D

**Answer:D.**

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A:** The Act prescribes confidentiality on dismissal, not mandatory parliamentary publication.
- **B:** The records cannot be made available merely because the complainant requests publication.
- **C:** A thirty-day public-release rule does not supersede the express bar.
- **D:** Correct. The second proviso to section 14(1)(a) creates this specific non-publication rule for a dismissed PM inquiry.

**Examiner trap 16:** Do not generalise the PM-specific secrecy rule to every Lokpal proceeding.

### MCQ 17 — A

**Answer:A.**

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A:** Correct. Sita Soren held that bribery is not protected by Articles 105(2) or 194(2), while the constitutional immunity for the legislative act itself remains.
- **B:** MPs are included subject to the genuine speech/vote constitutional exception.
- **C:** Article 105(2) still protects genuine speech and votes, unlike the bribery transaction.
- **D:** The Court rejected a bribery shield; it did not repeal Article 105(2).

**Examiner trap 17:** Separate the protected speech/vote from the independently complete offence of bribery.

### MCQ 18 — B

**Answer:B.**

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A:** Departments and Special Courts do not become interchangeable trial authorities.
- **B:** Correct. The first two provisos to section 20(1) create a split CVC route: A/B reporting to Lokpal and C/D processing under the CVC Act.
- **C:** The Act specifies CVC's Groups A/B and C/D split for preliminary-inquiry routing.
- **D:** Referral is not automatic trial before assessing the prima facie case.

**Examiner trap 18:** The route changes by service group and by stage; CVC does not become the trial court.

### MCQ 19 — C

**Answer:C.**

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A:** The threshold is not restricted to UN grants; other foreign sources can qualify.
- **B:** Domestic gifts alone do not satisfy this foreign-source limb.
- **C:** Correct. Clause (h) uses a foreign-source donation threshold above Rs 10 lakh in a year, subject to any higher amount formally notified.
- **D:** A domestic government grant is tested under other clauses with their own conditions.

**Examiner trap 19:** Do not confuse clause (h)'s express foreign threshold with clause (g)'s notified income condition.

### MCQ 20 — D

**Answer:D.**

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A:** Retirement does not erase covered acts committed in public office.
- **B:** Private conduct unrelated to the former covered capacity is not invariably within section 14.
- **C:** Re-employment is not required for scrutiny of conduct during covered service.
- **D:** Correct. Section 14 repeatedly uses 'is or has been', while its explanation ties the complaint to the period of covered public service.

**Examiner trap 20:** Post-tenure coverage follows the time of conduct, not a general lifelong public-servant label.

### MCQ 21 — A

**Answer:A.**

- **C:** The alleged wrongdoer has no veto on complaint submission.
- **D:** An advocate is not mandatory for every complaint.

**Examiner trap 21:** Complaint formalities regulate access; they do not predetermine truth or guilt.

- **C:** Criminal investigation is not merely a departmental disciplinary process.
- **D:** Neither a preliminary inquiry nor police investigation is itself the criminal trial.

**Examiner trap 22:** Do not use 'inquiry', 'investigation' and 'trial' as interchangeable words.

- **D:** A thirty-day preliminary deadline is not the statutory ninety-day clock.

**Examiner trap 23:** The report deadline and overall preliminary-inquiry period are distinct clocks.

**Examiner trap 24:** A prima facie decision is a routing decision, not a criminal verdict.

**Answer: B.**

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A:** The Lokpal and Lokayukta appointing frameworks are distinct, not a common anti-corruption panel.
- **B:** Section 4's Union committee chooses Lokpal nominees; State statutes supply State appointment routes.
- **C:** DSPE State consent governs police jurisdiction, not ombudsman appointments.
- **D:** State statutory appointment committees are not replaced by a uniform popular election.

**Examiner trap 34:** Identify the State and its law before naming a Lokayukta appointing authority.

### MCQ 35 — C

**Answer: C.**

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A:** One State statute cannot amend the parliamentary Act for all States.
- **B:** A Lokayukta's investigative architecture is not universally dependent on an automatic CBI reference.
- **C:** State-law design can vary; compare investigative and referral powers against each actual State Act.
- **D:** Section 63 imposes establishment by State law but no uniform investigative code.

**Examiner trap 35:** A common title does not prove identical powers across States.

### MCQ 36 — D

**Answer: D.**

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A:** Neither ombudsman type is established as a constitutional body with uniform removal procedures.
- **B:** The CBI Director's section 4A selection panel does not select State Lokayuktas.
- **C:** A State Lokayukta does not terminate Lokpal's statutory Union jurisdiction.

**Examiner trap 36:** Union establishment and State-law obligation do not imply identical tenure or selection.

## PYQS AND ANSWER PRACTICE

### Verified direct Prelims PYQ - Civil Services Preliminary Examination 2025, GS Paper I, Set A, Question 98

> Consider the following statements about Lokpal:
>
> I. The power of Lokpal applies to public servants of India, but not to the Indian public servants posted outside India.
> II. The Chairperson or a Member shall not be a Member of the Parliament or a Member of the Legislature of any State or Union Territory, and only the Chief Justice of India, whether incumbent or retired, has to be its Chairperson.
> III. The Chairperson or a Member shall not be a person of less than forty-five years of age on the date of assuming office as the Chairperson or Member, as the case may be.
> IV. Lokpal cannot inquire into the allegations of corruption against a sitting Prime Minister of India.
>
> Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
> (a) III only
> (b) II and III
> (c) I and IV
> (d) None of the above statements is correct

**Official Set-A key: A - III only.** The local official UPSC paper and answer-key PDF were checked directly.

| Statement | Verdict | Statutory reason |
|---|---|---|
| I | False | Section 1(3) applies the Act to public servants in and outside India. |
| II | False as a compound statement | The office-holding disqualification is correct, but section 3 does not restrict the Chairperson to only the CJI. |

**PYQ trap:** A compound statement fails if one limb is false; statement II uses a true disqualification to conceal a false exclusivity claim.

### Original solved Mains practice

The following six questions are original practice, not UPSC PYQs. The models are deliberately capped at the stated limits.

### Mains 1 - 10 marks | 150 words

**Question:** "Lokpal is an ombudsman, not a corruption court." Explain.

**Demand:** Separate Lokpal's screening, investigation-related and sanction powers from the Special Court's adjudication of guilt.

**Model answer**

Under the Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013, Lokpal receives corruption complaints against specified public servants and directs their processing; it does not try criminal charges. Section 20 distinguishes a preliminary inquiry, which checks whether a prima facie case exists, from investigation, which collects evidence. Lokpal can refer cases to the DSPE/CBI and supervise *referred* investigations under section 25. Under section 23 it may grant sanction for prosecution; section 29 also permits provisional attachment. These are substantial coercive and coordinating powers, not merely non-binding advice.

But prosecution must be proved before a Special Court under section 35. Sections 30–31 subject attachment and confiscation to that court's control. Neither a complaint, adverse inquiry, sanction nor attachment establishes guilt; the accused retains procedural protections. Calling Lokpal a “corruption court” would confuse institutional scrutiny with adjudication. It is an ombudsman with enforcement-linked powers, while conviction and sentence remain judicial.

**Why this earns marks:** Distinguishes inquiry, investigation, sanction and attachment from the Special Court's trial and conviction.

### Mains 2 - 10 marks | 150 words

**Question:** Explain the special statutory safeguards governing a corruption complaint against the Prime Minister.

**Demand:** State the excluded PM subject areas, the full-bench voting threshold and confidential disposal rules; distinguish safeguards from total immunity.

**Model answer**

Section 14 of the Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act includes a serving or former Prime Minister: high office is not personal immunity. Yet an allegation relating to international relations, external or internal security, public order, atomic energy or space falls outside this jurisdiction. A complaint on another subject cannot proceed to inquiry unless the full Lokpal bench considers it and at least two-thirds of its Members approve. The inquiry is held *in camera*; if the Lokpal dismisses it, its records cannot be published or made available.

These filters protect sensitive State functions and deter politically motivated complaints without excluding every corruption allegation against a Prime Minister. Their cost is reduced public scrutiny at the entry stage. Careful reasons and observance of the statutory threshold are therefore indispensable: accountability survives, but only within the Act's narrower subject-matter and procedure.

**Why this earns marks:** Identifies exactly which complaints are excluded and which may proceed after enhanced screening.

### Mains 3 - 15 marks | 250 words

**Question:** Analyse the Lokpal-CVC-CBI relationship. Why is it misleading to describe it as a simple hierarchy?

**Demand:** Trace the CVC's differentiated A/B versus C/D route, DSPE's referred-case role and the limits of Lokpal's superintendence.

**Model answer**

Lokpal, CVC and CBI/DSPE do not form a single chain of command: complaint screening, vigilance inquiry and criminal investigation have different statutory owners. Under section 20 of the 2013 Act, Lokpal may order a preliminary inquiry or investigation. It routes specified Groups A–D preliminary-inquiry complaints to the CVC; the report concerning Groups A/B returns for Lokpal's decision, whereas Groups C/D follow the CVC Act route. CVC thus does not become a mere Lokpal branch.

For a criminal probe Lokpal can refer the matter to the DSPE. Section 25 gives it superintendence over *Lokpal-referred* DSPE cases and protects continuity by requiring approval for transfer of the investigating officer. But the anti-dictation proviso prevents instructions to investigate or dispose of a case in a predetermined manner. Nor does this confer general control over unrelated CBI investigations. The CBI supplies investigative expertise; Lokpal's Prosecution Wing/agency and sanction power under sections 12 and 23 support prosecution, while section 35 leaves trial to Special Courts.

The counterargument is that such divided responsibility invites delay and blurred accountability. Shared case tracking and reasoned referrals could reduce that risk without replacing the statutory separation of vigilance, investigation, prosecution and adjudication with an imagined superior–subordinate hierarchy.

**Why this earns marks:** Traces both the A/B versus C/D route and the limited referred-case superintendence, then evaluates fragmentation.

### Mains 4 - 15 marks | 250 words

**Question:** Evaluate whether Lokpal's appointment, tenure and removal design adequately protects independence.

**Demand:** Evaluate appointment, fixed tenure and removal individually, including where implementation can defeat formal independence.

**Model answer**

The 2013 Act gives Lokpal insulation at three points, but statutory design cannot by itself guarantee operational autonomy. Section 4's Selection Committee brings together the Prime Minister, Lok Sabha Speaker, Leader of Opposition, CJI or nominee and an eminent jurist; a socially diverse Search Committee assists it. This distributes appointment power, although the executive retains influence and selectors need not confine themselves to search-panel names. *Common Cause* (2017) made clear that a vacancy must not become a veto on implementing the Act.

Section 6 limits tenure to five years or age seventy. Section 8's bar on ordinary reappointment and specified later offices/elections reduces dependence on an appointing authority's favour; a Member's possible elevation to Chairperson remains subject to an aggregate five-year ceiling. For misbehaviour, section 37 requires a petition by at least 100 MPs, Presidential reference and Supreme Court inquiry, rather than dismissal at executive will. By contrast, insolvency, paid outside employment and qualifying infirmity permit direct presidential removal under the Act.

These checks are meaningful, but slow selections, vacancies and inadequate staff can defeat independence without formally violating tenure or removal rules. Transparent selection, stable multidisciplinary staffing and reasoned public reporting are therefore as necessary as legal insulation. The design substantially protects tenure; its adequacy must also be judged by whether Lokpal can act promptly and independently.

**Why this earns marks:** Weighs each appointment, tenure and removal safeguard against its specific operational weakness.

### Mains 5 - 20 marks | 250 words

**Question:** "The Lokpal Act is broad in jurisdiction but filtered in operation." Critically analyse.

**Demand:** Test breadth of covered persons against exclusions for protected functions, time limits and complaint/inquiry screening.

**Model answer**

“Broad” describes who may be complained against; “filtered” describes what allegation can proceed and how. Section 14 covers the Prime Minister, Union Ministers, MPs, Groups A–D officers, former office-holders and qualifying functionaries of government-linked or foreign-funded entities. It does not turn Lokpal into a general forum against every private person: statutory thresholds and, where applicable, State consent matter.

The Prime Minister is included, but international relations, external/internal security, public order, atomic energy and space allegations are excluded. Other PM complaints require full-bench consideration, two-thirds approval and *in-camera* inquiry. MPs' genuine parliamentary speech and votes attract Article 105(2) protection; *Sita Soren* (2024) distinguishes bribery from that privilege. Neither office has a blanket corruption exemption.

Even an eligible allegation must clear the 2020 Complaint Rules, section 20's hearing and preliminary-inquiry/investigation route and section 53's seven-year limit; section 46 addresses good-faith complainants. A preliminary inquiry tests whether there is a case to investigate; it is not the criminal trial, and sanction cannot substitute for proof in a Special Court. Screening guards reputation and due process, but restrictive entry or delay can impede valid cases, especially when investigation depends on another agency. Thus reach on paper does not measure enforcement: effective capacity and reviewable, fair case disposal are needed to turn wide jurisdiction into accountability.

**Why this earns marks:** Separates personal coverage, substantive immunity and procedural admissibility rather than equating jurisdiction with conviction.

### Mains 6 - 20 marks | 250 words

**Question:** Assess the strengths and weaknesses of the Lokpal-Lokayukta framework and suggest reforms consistent with federalism and due process.

**Demand:** Assess Union enforcement and State-law Lokayukta variation separately; match each reform to a failure without displacing State autonomy or due process.

**Model answer**

The Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013 offers a national route for corruption complaints while leaving State ombudsmen to State law. At the Union level, section 20 routes preliminary inquiry and investigation; sections 23 and 25 provide prosecution sanction and limited superintendence over referred DSPE cases. Sections 29–35 link attachment to Special Court scrutiny and trial. Coverage of senior office-holders is therefore backed by mechanisms, not just a symbolic promise. Plural selection, fixed tenure, section 37's protected removal process, charged expenditure, CAG audit and section 48 reporting help resist direct interference.

Yet a complaint-led institution without a general express *suo-motu* route relies on outside investigators. Vacancies, thin staffing, delay and opaque disposals can erode formal independence; strengthening prosecution must not treat inquiry findings as criminal guilt. Federally, section 63 requires State legislation but specifies no uniform powers or composition. Maharashtra and Karnataka demonstrate space for different models, but residents may receive unequal protection.

Reforms should prioritise timely appointments, multidisciplinary staff, interoperable Lokpal–CVC–CBI case tracking and anonymised stage-wise reporting. States could adopt a model minimum Lokayukta standard without imposing one central institutional design. Update asset-declaration practice within the applicable rules; if new *suo-motu* or investigative powers are proposed, require recorded reasons, recusal, a hearing and judicial review. A stronger framework should narrow implementation gaps while retaining State choice and the accused's due process.

**Why this earns marks:** Couples distinct Union and State weaknesses with reforms that do not bypass federal autonomy or fair process.
