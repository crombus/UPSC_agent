# Polity Topic 38 - Lokpal and Lokayuktas

**Subject:** Polity | **GS Paper:** GS-II | **Control date:** 8 September 2026

**Source control:** canonical Basic/Core and optional Advanced owners; official UPSC 2025 GS-I paper and local official Set-A key; the Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013; the 2016 amending Act; Lokpal (Complaint) Rules, 2020; official Gazette material on the statutory wings; the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 as amended; verified State statutes used only for contrast; and proposition-controlled Supreme Court authorities. Volatile incumbents and live complaint, vacancy, disposal or conviction figures are omitted.

**Core thesis:** Lokpal is a statutory complaint-and-process institution with wide but qualified anti-corruption jurisdiction. It can route inquiry and investigation, supervise referred DSPE matters, grant sanction in its statutory field and activate attachment/prosecution mechanisms, but it does not itself convict, sentence, automatically dismiss officials, investigate every private person, control all CBI cases or override parliamentary privilege generally.

#### Evidence

[FACT] The Lokpal is a **statutory body**, established for the Union by the Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013. It is not created by the Constitution and is not a constitutional court, police force or general public-grievance commission.

[FACT] Section 2 defines a complaint as one made in the prescribed form alleging that a covered public servant committed an offence punishable under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988. This makes the institution corruption-specific.

[ANALYSIS] The ombudsman idea supplies the accountability logic, but the enacted Indian model is more than a classical recommendatory grievance office. It can route preliminary inquiry and investigation, supervise the DSPE/CBI in referred matters, grant prosecution sanction, use attachment machinery and direct prosecution under the Act.

[LIMIT] These powers do not make Lokpal the final judge of criminal guilt. Investigation, prosecution and adjudication remain legally distinct stages; conviction or acquittal belongs to the competent court.

#### Claim -> named evidence -> analysis -> qualification

- **Claim:** The 2013 Act is the result of a long institutional and legislative evolution rather than a product of one protest.
- **Named evidence:** Swedish ombudsman, 1809; First Administrative Reforms Commission, 1966; repeated Lokpal Bills from 1968; 2011 anti-corruption movement; Act 1 of 2014.
- **Analysis:** The chronology explains why the Indian model combines ombudsman accountability with specialised inquiry, investigation, prosecution and judicial stages.
- **Qualification:** The 2011 movement accelerated political salience, but Parliament's Act, later amendment and rules are the legal sources.

#### Evidence

[FACT] The modern ombudsman institution is conventionally traced to Sweden in 1809. Local Polity sources explain its diffusion to other jurisdictions as a mechanism for receiving complaints about administrative wrongdoing.

[FACT] India’s First Administrative Reforms Commission, in its 1966 work on redress of citizens’ grievances, recommended two institutions: a **Lokpal at the Union level** and **Lokayuktas at the State level**.

[FACT] Successive Lokpal Bills beginning in 1968 repeatedly lapsed, were withdrawn or failed to complete enactment. The political and civil-society mobilisation of 2011 formed part of the bounded context in which the later Bill was debated.

[FACT] The Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act received presidential assent on **1 January 2014** and came into force on **16 January 2014** through Gazette notification.

[ANALYSIS] The long legislative history shows a recurring design conflict: how to create an institution strong enough to scrutinise high public office without merging complainant, investigator, prosecutor and judge.

[LIMIT] Civil-society mobilisation explains political salience; it is not the legal source of the institution. The enacted statute, rules and judicial interpretation control the present framework.

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** The President appoints Lokpal leadership after a statutory selection process.

**Technical definition:** The Selection Committee combines the PM, Speaker, Lok Sabha LoP, CJI or nominee and eminent jurist; the Search Committee assists, and section 4(2) prevents a vacancy from invalidating appointment.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> The President appoints Lokpal leadership after a statutory selection process.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **President**
- **Selection Committee**
- **Search Committee**
- **Common Cause v. Union of India (2017)**


#### Claim -> named evidence -> analysis -> qualification

- **Claim:** Appointment is formally plural but not free from executive influence.
- **Named evidence:** Section 4 Selection Committee and Search Committee; Common Cause v. Union of India, judgment dated 27 April 2017.
- **Analysis:** Executive, legislative, judicial and eminent-jurist participation broadens input, while the Search Committee supplies an expert screening layer.
- **Qualification:** Section 4(2) prevents a vacancy from invalidating appointment; Common Cause did not rewrite the Act to create a permanent largest-opposition-party substitute.

#### Evidence

[FACT] The President appoints the Chairperson and Members on the recommendation of the Selection Committee.

[FACT] Section 4 names five positions:

1. Prime Minister — Chairperson;
2. Speaker of the Lok Sabha;
3. Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha;
4. Chief Justice of India or a Supreme Court Judge nominated by the CJI; and
5. one eminent jurist nominated by the President on the recommendation of the first four.

[FACT] Section 4 names the **Leader of Opposition in the House of the People**. It does not insert the leader of the single largest opposition party as a statutory substitute.

[FACT] Section 4(2) instead states that no appointment is invalid merely because of a vacancy in the Selection Committee.

[FACT] The Selection Committee constitutes a Search Committee of **at least seven persons** of standing and relevant expertise. Not less than fifty per cent of its members must come from the same listed diversity categories. The Selection Committee may consider a person not recommended by the Search Committee.

[ANALYSIS] Search identifies a panel; Selection makes the recommendation; the President makes the appointment. Treating the Search Committee as the final appointing body reverses the statutory sequence.

### MCQ 1. Legal identity

Which statement best describes the Lokpal?

A. It is a statutory anti-corruption ombudsman whose powers and limits arise from the 2013 Act.
B. It is an executive body created only by a government resolution.
C. It is the investigative division of the CVC.
D. It is a constitutional court for corruption offences.

**Answer: A.**

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A:** Correct. Section 3 establishes Lokpal under parliamentary legislation; sections 20 and 35 separate its process role from criminal adjudication.
- **B:** Incorrect. The proposition 'It is an executive body created only by a government resolution.' conflicts with the controlling rule: Section 3 establishes Lokpal under parliamentary legislation; sections 20 and 35 separate its process role from criminal adjudication.
- **C:** Incorrect. The proposition 'It is the investigative division of the CVC.' conflicts with the controlling rule: Section 3 establishes Lokpal under parliamentary legislation; sections 20 and 35 separate its process role from criminal adjudication.
- **D:** Incorrect. The proposition 'It is a constitutional court for corruption offences.' conflicts with the controlling rule: Section 3 establishes Lokpal under parliamentary legislation; sections 20 and 35 separate its process role from criminal adjudication.

**Examiner trap 1:** Do not convert statutory status into either executive-resolution status or constitutional-court status.

The Lokpal is a statutory Union anti-corruption ombudsman under the 2013 Act. Its authority is significant but process-oriented.

**Claim:** Lokpal can move a complaint toward accountability. **Named evidence:** sections 20, 23, 25 and 29 permit inquiry or investigation routing, prosecution sanction, supervision of referred DSPE matters and provisional attachment. **Analysis:** these powers make it more than a merely recommendatory grievance office. **Qualification:** each power is bounded by statute, hearing requirements and court control.

Therefore, Lokpal is a powerful institutional gateway and coordinator, but the Special Court alone may convict and sentence.

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

### 1. Ombudsman models and the Indian hybrid

The classical ombudsman commonly investigates maladministration and reports or recommends. India's Lokpal is a statutory hybrid: it receives a prescribed corruption complaint, can trigger preliminary inquiry or investigation, supervises referred DSPE matters, grants sanction in its statutory field, invokes attachment machinery and directs prosecution. This comparative label helps explain the institution; it cannot enlarge the Act.

### 2. Independence-accountability tension

| Independence device | Accountability device | Persistent tension |
|---|---|---|
| Plural Selection Committee | Search and selection procedure | Executive and political actors remain central |
| Fixed non-renewable tenure | Removal through Supreme Court inquiry | Vacancies can weaken capacity without formally altering tenure |
| Charged administrative expenses | Budget, CAG audit and parliamentary laying | Resource dependence may continue in implementation |
| Referred-case CBI safeguards | Anti-dictation proviso and court review | Insulation must not become operational micromanagement |
| In-camera PM process | High threshold and confidentiality | Protection from abuse can reduce visible reasons |

### 3. Section 17A of the Prevention of Corruption Act

Section 17A, inserted in 2018, generally requires previous approval before an inquiry or investigation into an offence alleged to relate to a recommendation made or decision taken by a public servant in discharge of official functions, subject to the statutory on-the-spot arrest exception. It is an external PC Act gate that an investigating agency must consider; it is not a Lokpal conviction rule. Its defence is protection of honest decision-making; its risk is delay or executive shielding. Any answer should distinguish section 17A approval from section 19 sanction for prosecution and from Lokpal's section 23 power.

### 4. Higher-judiciary jurisdiction: date-controlled issue

On 20 February 2025, the Supreme Court stayed the Lokpal order dated 27 January 2025 that had asserted jurisdiction over a sitting High Court judge. The interim order raised a serious institutional question and preserved confidentiality. No final merits proposition is claimed here as at 8 September 2026. This issue should be used only as a qualified current-law illustration, not as settled expansion or exclusion.

### 5. Reform tests

A reform deserves support only if it answers four questions: Does it strengthen lawful capacity? Does it preserve natural justice? Does it provide auditable reasons and judicial review? Does it respect section 63's State-law structure? These tests favour timely appointments, professional staff, interoperable case tracking, aggregate stage-wise disclosure, current asset-declaration rules and model minimum Lokayukta safeguards. They caution against giving an institution undefined power merely because existing enforcement is slow.

## CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

### Identity and evolution

- Lokpal is a **statutory**, Union-level, corruption-specific institution under the Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013; it is not constitutional.
- Sweden, 1809 -> First ARC, 1966 -> repeated Bills from 1968 -> 2011 anti-corruption mobilisation -> enactment in 2013 -> assent 1 January 2014 -> commencement 16 January 2014.
- Political mobilisation explains urgency; statute, amendment, rules and judgments determine law.

### Composition, selection and service

| Head | Exact recall |
|---|---|
| Size | Chairperson + not more than eight Members |
| Judicial share | 50% of Members |
| Representation | Not less than 50% of Members from SC/ST/OBC/minorities/women |
| Chair routes | CJI/former CJI; SC judge/former SC judge; qualified eminent person |
| Judicial Member | SC judge/former judge or HC Chief Justice/former Chief Justice |
| Other Member | Impeccable integrity, outstanding ability, >=25 years listed expertise |
| Minimum age | 45 |
| Selection | PM, LS Speaker, LS LoP, CJI/nominee, eminent jurist |
| Search Committee | At least seven experts; diversity floor; panel not exclusive |
| Appointment | President by warrant |
| Term | Five years or age 70, earlier |
| Removal | Section 37 Supreme Court inquiry route plus narrow direct grounds |

**Common Cause (2017):** section 4(2) made the Act workable despite a Selection Committee vacancy; the Court did not legislate a permanent substitute for the LoP.

### Jurisdiction

- PM/former PM: covered with five excluded domains, full bench, two-thirds approval, in-camera inquiry and special record secrecy if dismissed.
- Union Ministers and MPs: covered.
- MPs: section 14(2) preserves Article 105(2) for speech/vote; **Sita Soren (2024)** denies bribery immunity without abolishing legislative privilege.
- Groups A-D Union officials: covered, with section 20 CVC routing.
- Former public servants: conduct must relate to the period in covered capacity.
- Bodies created by Parliament or Central-controlled/financed: conditional section 14(1)(f) coverage.
- Other societies/associations/trusts: clause (g) government-finance and notified-income conditions; clause (h) foreign-source donation above Rs 10 lakh/year or higher notified amount.
- Section 14(3): connected abettors, bribe-givers, bribe-takers and conspirators may be reached; State-service proviso may require State consent.

### Complaint-to-court route

```text
Complaint Rules 2020 filing
 -> scrutiny
 -> Lokpal chooses preliminary inquiry or investigation
 -> CVC/Inquiry Wing/agency as statute directs
 -> report + hearing + >=3-Member bench
 -> investigation / departmental action / closure
 -> investigation report to court + copy to Lokpal
 -> sanction / charge-sheet / closure / departmental route
 -> Prosecution Wing or agency
 -> Special Court alone determines guilt and sentence
```

| Clock | Rule |
|---|---|
| Preliminary report | 60 days from reference |
| Preliminary inquiry | Ordinarily 90 days + further 90 days for recorded reasons |
| Investigation | Six months + extensions not exceeding six months at a time, reasons recorded |
| Special Court trial | One-year target; three-month reasoned extensions; total not beyond two years |
| Complaint limitation | Seven years from date of alleged offence |

### CVC, CBI and powers

- Groups A/B preliminary inquiry: CVC reports to Lokpal; Groups C/D: CVC proceeds under CVC Act.
- Section 25 superintendence concerns only Lokpal-referred DSPE matters.
- Lokpal cannot dictate investigation/disposal in a particular manner.
- Transfer of a DSPE officer investigating a referred case requires Lokpal approval.
- Search, civil-court inquiry powers, safe custody of records and attachment are statutory process tools, not proof of guilt.
- Provisional attachment: recorded reasons, maximum 90 days, immediate Special Court involvement; confirmation application within 30 days.
- Special Court controls confirmation/confiscation and restoration consequences.

### Assets, false complaints and accountability

- Current section 44 after the 2016 substitution: every public servant declares assets and liabilities in the prescribed form and manner.
- Section 45 creates a conditional rebuttable presumption for wilful non-declaration or misleading information.
- Section 46 requires a false and frivolous or vexatious complaint, Special Court cognizance through the specified complainant, and preserves good-faith complaints.
- Section 13 charges specified administrative expenses on the Consolidated Fund of India.
- Sections 40-43: budget, parliamentary appropriation/grants, accounts, CAG audit and returns.
- Section 48: annual report to President; Parliament receives it with reasons where advice was not accepted.

### Lokayuktas

- Section 63 requires a State, if it had not already done so, to establish a Lokayukta by State law within one year of commencement.
- It does **not** create one national State template.
- Maharashtra's 1971 enactment is a verified historical early State model with Lokayukta/Upa-Lokayukta inquiry and reporting provisions.
- Karnataka's 1984 Act supplies a distinct Lokayukta/Upalokayukta, investigation and prosecution design.
- Always compare exact State statutes on composition, appointment, jurisdiction, tenure, removal, investigation, prosecution, reporting and recommendation effect.

1. Statutory, not constitutional.
2. Maximum eight Members, not always eight.
3. Percentage denominator is Members.
4. PM included with filters, not wholly immune.
5. Article 105(2) survives; bribery immunity does not.
6. Complaint route is not a general express suo-motu power.
7. CVC and CBI are not Lokpal departments.
8. Referred-case superintendence is not control of all CBI cases.
9. Sanction, attachment and charge-sheet are not conviction.
10. Section 63 does not standardise every Lokayukta.
