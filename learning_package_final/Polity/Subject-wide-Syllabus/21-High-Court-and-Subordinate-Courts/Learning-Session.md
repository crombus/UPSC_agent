## BASIC LEARNING SESSION

### SESSION 1 - INTEGRATED JUDICIAL HIERARCHY AND CONSTITUTIONAL ORIGIN

#### VISUAL FIRST - CONCEPT GATEWAY

```text
1774 Regulating Act -> 1861 High Courts Act -> 1862 Calcutta/Bombay/Madras
                                   |
                                   v
1950 Constitution: Supreme Court -> High Courts -> subordinate courts
```

Caption: The diagram fixes this session's controlling legal relationship before detail.

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** India uses one integrated judicial hierarchy in which High Courts are constitutional courts for States and territorial jurisdictions, while district courts form the principal trial and appellate layer below them.

**Technical definition:** Part VI, Chapter V contains Articles 214-231 on High Courts; Chapter VI contains Articles 233-237 on subordinate courts.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> The High Court is simultaneously a constitutional remedies court and the institutional head of the State judiciary.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **integrated judiciary**
- **Part VI**
- **constitutional court**
- **district judiciary**
- **judicial federalism**

#### HIGH-COURT-SPECIFIC ANSWER UNIT

**Claim:** India combines national legal unity with territorially distributed constitutional adjudication.

**Named evidence:** Articles 214-231 and 233-237, read with the Supreme Court's appellate position.

**Analysis:** A litigant usually encounters constitutional legality through a territorially accessible High Court, while a common apex court preserves doctrinal coherence across the Union.

**Qualification:** Integration does not mean unitary administration: States retain statutory court structures and service rules within constitutional limits.

#### EVIDENCE

- [FACT] The first chartered High Courts at Calcutta, Bombay and Madras opened in 1862; Allahabad followed in 1866.
- [FACT] High Courts exercise constitutional, civil, criminal, appellate, supervisory and administrative functions from different legal sources.
- [FACT] The constitutional design separates High Courts from the State executive while linking them to the Supreme Court through appeal and precedent.

#### PRELIMS TRAP

Do not describe a High Court as merely the State's highest appellate court; Articles 215, 226, 227, 228 and 235 create distinct constitutional functions.

#### MAINS USE

Use the integrated-yet-decentralised design as the introduction to any answer on High Courts or district-judiciary independence.

#### MINI RECAP

One hierarchy; multiple territorial constitutional courts; local adjudication under High Court control.

### SESSION 2 - ARTICLE 214, COMMON HIGH COURTS AND THE REPEALED ARTICLE 232

#### VISUAL FIRST - CONCEPT GATEWAY

```text
ARTICLE 214 default: a High Court for each State
                 |
                 +--> ARTICLE 231 exception: Parliament may establish one common High Court
                 |                         for two or more States or States plus Union Territories
                 |
                 +--> ARTICLE 232: repealed by the Seventh Amendment, 1956
```

Caption: The diagram fixes this session's controlling legal relationship before detail.

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** The Constitution states a State-wise default but permits Parliament to create a common High Court, so the number of High Courts need not equal the number of States and Union Territories.

**Technical definition:** Article 214 supplies the default; Article 231 authorises a common High Court; the Constitution (Seventh Amendment) Act, 1956 reorganised the provisions and repealed Article 232.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> Article 214 is a default rule, not a one-State-one-court numerical guarantee.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **Article 214**
- **Article 231**
- **common High Court**
- **Seventh Amendment**
- **Article 232 repealed**

#### HIGH-COURT-SPECIFIC ANSWER UNIT

**Claim:** The Constitution permits shared High Court institutions without diluting each constituent territory's access to constitutional justice.

**Named evidence:** Articles 214 and 231 and the Constitution (Seventh Amendment) Act, 1956.

**Analysis:** A common High Court can pool judicial capacity and maintain regional coherence while Parliament adjusts references to Governors, legislatures and territorial jurisdiction.

**Qualification:** Common jurisdiction does not erase the distinct laws, executive authorities or subordinate judicial services of the participating States.

#### EVIDENCE

- [FACT] India has 25 High Courts as of 7 September 2026; several serve more than one State or Union Territory.
- [FACT] Article 231 permits a common court for two or more States or for States and Union Territories.
- [FACT] Article 232 is no longer operative and should not be presented as a current power.

#### PRELIMS TRAP

Never infer one High Court for every State from Article 214 without reading Article 231.

#### MAINS USE

Use common High Courts to show that constitutional federalism can share institutions while preserving territorial legal identities.

#### MINI RECAP

Article 214 default; Article 231 shared institution; Article 232 repealed.

### SESSION 3 - ARTICLE 216 COMPOSITION, SANCTIONED STRENGTH AND CAPACITY

#### VISUAL FIRST - CONCEPT GATEWAY

```text
HIGH COURT
  +-- Chief Justice
  +-- other Judges: number not fixed in the Constitution
  +-- sanctioned strength: institution-specific
  +-- working strength: date-sensitive
  +-- vacancies: sanctioned minus working, not a permanent fact
```

Caption: The diagram fixes this session's controlling legal relationship before detail.

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** Every High Court consists of a Chief Justice and such other judges as are appointed; the Constitution does not prescribe a uniform court strength.

**Technical definition:** Article 216 states the composition, while sanctioned posts and appointments arise through institutional and governmental processes rather than a single constitutional number.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> A wide constitutional jurisdiction is only as effective as the bench strength and registry capacity that can exercise it.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **Article 216**
- **Chief Justice**
- **sanctioned strength**
- **working strength**
- **vacancy**

#### HIGH-COURT-SPECIFIC ANSWER UNIT

**Claim:** Judicial capacity converts formal constitutional power into timely remedies.

**Named evidence:** Article 216 and the Department of Justice High Court strength statement dated 1 July 2026.

**Analysis:** Vacancies reduce available benches, delay hearings and increase administrative burdens, thereby weakening access even though jurisdiction remains legally intact.

**Qualification:** A dated vacancy snapshot diagnoses capacity but cannot by itself establish the causes of pendency or future strength.

#### EVIDENCE

- [FACT] The 1 July 2026 Department of Justice statement records 1,122 sanctioned posts, 781 working judges and 341 vacancies.
- [FACT] Each figure is expressly date-bound and must be reverified before later reuse.
- [FACT] Registry staffing, infrastructure, procedure and government litigation also affect disposal.

#### PRELIMS TRAP

Do not treat sanctioned strength, working strength and pendency as interchangeable statistics.

#### MAINS USE

Deploy the dated 2026 figures only as evidence of implementation strain after explaining the constitutional design.

#### MINI RECAP

Article 216 fixes composition, not a national judge count; capacity data must carry a date.

### SESSION 4 - ARTICLE 217 APPOINTMENT AND THE COLLEGIUM BRIDGE

#### VISUAL FIRST - CONCEPT GATEWAY

```text
CONSTITUTIONAL TEXT                 JUDICIALLY DEVELOPED PROCESS
President appoints                    HC Chief Justice + 2 senior colleagues initiate
after consultation with       ->     State and Union inputs are processed
CJI + Governor                         CJI + 2 senior-most SC judges consider HC appointment
+ HC Chief Justice for puisne judge   -> President formally appoints
```

Caption: The diagram fixes this session's controlling legal relationship before detail.

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** The President appoints High Court judges after the consultations specified in Article 217; the collegium is a judicially developed method for giving those consultations institutional content.

**Technical definition:** Article 217 text, the Second Judges Case (1993), Third Judges Presidential Reference (1998), the Memorandum of Procedure and the NJAC judgment (2015) must be kept in separate legal layers.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> High Court appointments are constitutionally executive in form but judicially structured to protect decisional independence.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **Article 217**
- **consultation**
- **collegium**
- **judicial primacy**
- **Memorandum of Procedure**

#### HIGH-COURT-SPECIFIC ANSWER UNIT

**Claim:** Judicial primacy seeks to prevent appointments from becoming instruments of executive control.

**Named evidence:** Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Association (Second Judges Case, 1993) and Special Reference No. 1 of 1998.

**Analysis:** The collegium converts individual consultation into plural judicial assessment while retaining executive verification and the President's formal appointment.

**Qualification:** The collegium's composition is not printed in Article 217, and executive participation is not constitutionally eliminated.

#### EVIDENCE

- [FACT] The originating High Court Chief Justice consults the two senior-most colleagues.
- [FACT] At the Supreme Court stage, High Court appointments are considered by the CJI and two senior-most Supreme Court judges.
- [FACT] The 99th Amendment and NJAC Act were invalidated in 2015 because the enacted design damaged judicial independence; the ruling did not declare every conceivable appointments commission unconstitutional.

#### PRELIMS TRAP

Do not write that Article 217 itself creates a CJI-plus-two collegium.

#### MAINS USE

Separate text, case law and administrative procedure before evaluating transparency or delay.

#### MINI RECAP

President appoints; Article 217 lists consultees; Judges Cases create judicial primacy.

### SESSION 5 - QUALIFICATIONS, OATH AND POST-TENURE PRACTICE

#### VISUAL FIRST - CONCEPT GATEWAY

```text
ARTICLE 217(2) ELIGIBILITY
Indian citizen
   +-- 10 years judicial office in India
   OR
   +-- 10 years advocate of one or more High Courts

ARTICLE 219 oath -> Governor or authorised person
ARTICLE 220 -> former permanent judge: SC/other HCs only, not own former HC/subordinate forums
```

Caption: The diagram fixes this session's controlling legal relationship before detail.

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** A High Court judge must be an Indian citizen and satisfy one of two ten-year professional routes; there is no distinguished-jurist route.

**Technical definition:** Article 217(2) governs eligibility, Article 219 the Third Schedule oath, and Article 220 restricts post-tenure pleading or acting by a former permanent judge.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> High Court eligibility is professional and experience-based, not a political nomination category.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **citizenship**
- **ten years**
- **judicial office**
- **High Court advocate**
- **Article 220**

#### HIGH-COURT-SPECIFIC ANSWER UNIT

**Claim:** The qualification design draws judges from both adjudicative service and experienced advocacy.

**Named evidence:** Articles 217(2), 219 and 220 and the Third Schedule.

**Analysis:** Dual entry broadens institutional experience while the oath binds every judge to the Constitution and impartial performance.

**Qualification:** The Constitution specifies neither a minimum appointment age nor a distinguished-jurist route for High Courts.

#### EVIDENCE

- [FACT] Judicial-office and advocacy periods are constitutionally recognised routes, subject to the computation rules in Article 217.
- [FACT] The oath is administered before the Governor or a person appointed by the Governor.
- [FACT] Article 220's practice restriction applies to a former permanent High Court judge and preserves practice before the Supreme Court and other High Courts.

#### PRELIMS TRAP

Do not import the Supreme Court's distinguished-jurist eligibility route into Article 217.

#### MAINS USE

Use the two-source recruitment design when discussing diversity, professional competence and career pathways.

#### MINI RECAP

Citizen plus one ten-year route; Governor-administered oath; bounded post-retirement practice.

### SESSION 6 - TENURE, REMOVAL, SALARIES AND INSTITUTIONAL INDEPENDENCE

#### VISUAL FIRST - CONCEPT GATEWAY

```text
SECURITY OF OFFICE
appointment -> oath -> tenure to 62
                  |
                  +-- resignation to President
                  +-- removal: Art 218 + Art 124(4)-(5)
                  +-- elevation or transfer

FINANCIAL SHIELD
Art 221 conditions + charged High Court expenses under Art 229(3)
```

Caption: The diagram fixes this session's controlling legal relationship before detail.

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** A High Court judge ordinarily serves until sixty-two and cannot be removed by an ordinary executive order.

**Technical definition:** Article 218 applies the Supreme Court removal provisions in Article 124(4)-(5); Article 221 protects salary and service conditions, subject to constitutional exceptions including Financial Emergency.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> Judicial independence rests on security against retaliation, not on immunity from lawful accountability.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **retirement 62**
- **proved misbehaviour**
- **incapacity**
- **special majority**
- **charged expenditure**

#### HIGH-COURT-SPECIFIC ANSWER UNIT

**Claim:** Difficult removal and protected conditions insulate adjudication from short-term political displeasure.

**Named evidence:** Articles 217, 218, 221, 229(3), 360 and the Judges (Inquiry) Act, 1968.

**Analysis:** A judge who cannot be dismissed or financially penalised by the litigant executive is better placed to enforce constitutional limits.

**Qualification:** Independence does not bar investigation, parliamentary removal, recusal norms, reasoned criticism or lawful institutional discipline.

#### EVIDENCE

- [FACT] Removal requires proved misbehaviour or incapacity and the Article 124(4) special-majority address followed by presidential order.
- [FACT] Administrative expenses of the High Court are charged on the Consolidated Fund of the State.
- [FACT] Article 360 permits constitutionally authorised salary-reduction directions during a Financial Emergency.

#### PRELIMS TRAP

A High Court judge is neither removable by the Governor nor subject to the President's pleasure.

#### MAINS USE

Organise independence under personnel, finance, jurisdiction and administration, then add accountability limits.

#### MINI RECAP

Tenure to 62; stringent removal; protected conditions; charged institutional expenses.

### SESSION 7 - ARTICLE 222 TRANSFER OF HIGH COURT JUDGES

#### VISUAL FIRST - CONCEPT GATEWAY

```text
PUBLIC-INTEREST TRANSFER
CJI-led institutional consultation
       |
       +-- four senior-most Supreme Court judges
       +-- transferor High Court Chief Justice
       +-- transferee High Court Chief Justice
       |
President transfers under Article 222 -> compensatory allowance where provided
```

Caption: The diagram fixes this session's controlling legal relationship before detail.

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** The President may transfer a judge from one High Court to another after consulting the Chief Justice of India.

**Technical definition:** Article 222 supplies the constitutional power; Sankalchand Himmatlal Sheth, the Judges Cases and the Memorandum of Procedure supply safeguards and present consultation practice.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> Transfer is constitutionally available for public interest but constitutionally dangerous if used as punishment.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **Article 222**
- **public interest**
- **CJI consultation**
- **no consent requirement**
- **non-punitive**

#### HIGH-COURT-SPECIFIC ANSWER UNIT

**Claim:** Plural judicial consultation converts transfer from unilateral executive leverage into an institutionally reviewable process.

**Named evidence:** Union of India v. Sankalchand Himmatlal Sheth (1977) and Special Reference No. 1 of 1998.

**Analysis:** Transfers may address local entrenchment or administrative needs, yet opaque timing can undermine judicial morale and public confidence.

**Qualification:** The judge's consent is not an express constitutional condition, but absence of consent does not license mala fide or punitive transfer.

#### EVIDENCE

- [FACT] Article 222 places formal authority in the President after consultation with the CJI.
- [FACT] Current procedure uses consultation with the four senior-most Supreme Court judges and the Chief Justices of both affected High Courts.
- [FACT] Compensatory allowance may be provided when a judge is transferred.

#### PRELIMS TRAP

Do not confuse transfer of a High Court judge under Article 222 with posting or transfer of district judges under Articles 233 and 235.

#### MAINS USE

Balance mobility and national integration against transparency, judge-specific fairness and decisional independence.

#### MINI RECAP

President transfers; CJI-led plural consultation; consent not textual; punishment impermissible.

### SESSION 8 - ACTING, ADDITIONAL AND RETIRED JUDGES: ARTICLES 223, 224 AND 224A

#### VISUAL FIRST - CONCEPT GATEWAY

```text
TEMPORARY CAPACITY TOOLS
Art 223 -> Acting Chief Justice during vacancy/absence/inability
Art 224 -> Additional Judge for temporary business/arrears, maximum two years
         -> Acting Judge when a permanent judge cannot perform duties
Art 224A -> retired HC judge sits on CJ request + prior President consent + judge consent
```

Caption: The diagram fixes this session's controlling legal relationship before detail.

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** The Constitution supplies temporary judicial capacity without converting every short-term need into a permanent appointment.

**Technical definition:** Articles 223, 224 and 224A create distinct offices with different triggers, authorities and duration limits.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> Temporary judges are constitutional safety valves, not substitutes for timely permanent appointments.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **acting Chief Justice**
- **additional judge**
- **acting judge**
- **retired judge**
- **prior presidential consent**

#### HIGH-COURT-SPECIFIC ANSWER UNIT

**Claim:** Temporary appointments protect continuity when leadership, workload or judge availability creates an immediate gap.

**Named evidence:** Articles 223, 224 and 224A.

**Analysis:** Each mechanism targets a different capacity problem, allowing the court to function while preserving the ordinary Article 217 appointment structure.

**Qualification:** Repeated dependence on temporary appointments can mask structural vacancies and must not normalise insecure judicial tenure.

#### EVIDENCE

- [FACT] An additional judge under Article 224 may be appointed for a period not exceeding two years.
- [FACT] An acting judge addresses the absence or inability of a permanent judge.
- [FACT] Under Article 224A, the Chief Justice requires prior presidential consent and the retired judge's consent; while sitting, the retiree has the jurisdiction, powers and privileges of a judge but is not otherwise deemed a judge of that High Court.

#### PRELIMS TRAP

Article 224A concerns retired High Court judges; Article 128 is the separate Supreme Court provision.

#### MAINS USE

Use these provisions to distinguish emergency continuity from durable capacity reform.

#### MINI RECAP

223 leadership gap; 224 workload or absence; 224A consensual retired-judge recall.

### SESSION 9 - ARTICLE 215 COURT OF RECORD AND CONTEMPT

#### VISUAL FIRST - CONCEPT GATEWAY

```text
ARTICLE 215
court of record
   +-- authoritative record and evidentiary value
   +-- power to punish contempt of itself
            |
            +-- Contempt of Courts Act, 1971 regulates procedure/definitions
            +-- constitutional source remains Article 215
```

Caption: The diagram fixes this session's controlling legal relationship before detail.

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** Every High Court is a court of record and possesses constitutional contempt power.

**Technical definition:** Article 215 is the constitutional source; the Contempt of Courts Act, 1971 defines and regulates civil and criminal contempt without extinguishing inherent constitutional authority.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> Contempt protects the administration of justice, not the personal prestige of judges.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **Article 215**
- **court of record**
- **civil contempt**
- **criminal contempt**
- **fair criticism**

#### HIGH-COURT-SPECIFIC ANSWER UNIT

**Claim:** Contempt jurisdiction protects obedience to orders and public confidence in adjudication.

**Named evidence:** Article 215 and the Contempt of Courts Act, 1971.

**Analysis:** The power enables a constitutional court to secure compliance and prevent conduct that substantially obstructs justice.

**Qualification:** Fair and accurate reporting, fair criticism and statutory defences prevent contempt from becoming a shield against democratic scrutiny.

#### EVIDENCE

- [FACT] Civil contempt principally concerns wilful disobedience of judgments, decrees, directions, orders, writs or undertakings.
- [FACT] Criminal contempt requires the statutory tendency to scandalise, prejudice proceedings or obstruct administration of justice.
- [FACT] The 1971 Act supplies procedure and limits while Article 215 preserves constitutional status.

#### PRELIMS TRAP

Do not say the 1971 Act creates the High Court's contempt power; it regulates a power already conferred by Article 215.

#### MAINS USE

Frame contempt as a rule-of-law power bounded by free speech, statutory defences and proportionality.

#### MINI RECAP

Record authority plus contempt power; institution-protecting, not reputation-protecting.

### SESSION 10 - ARTICLE 225 AND THE PLURAL SOURCES OF HIGH COURT JURISDICTION

#### VISUAL FIRST - CONCEPT GATEWAY

```text
HIGH COURT JURISDICTION IS NOT FROM ONE CLAUSE
Constitution: Arts 215, 225-228, 235
Letters Patent / charter history
Parliamentary statutes
State statutes within competence
CPC / criminal procedure / special enactments
High Court rules
```

Caption: The diagram fixes this session's controlling legal relationship before detail.

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** High Court jurisdiction is a layered product of constitutional provisions, pre-Constitution jurisdiction preserved by Article 225, statutes and court rules.

**Technical definition:** Article 225 continues existing jurisdiction and the law administered, subject to the Constitution and competent legislation, while removing the former revenue-matter restriction.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> The Constitution creates essential High Court powers but does not codify every original or appellate jurisdiction in one place.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **Article 225**
- **Letters Patent**
- **statutory jurisdiction**
- **court rules**
- **revenue matters**

#### HIGH-COURT-SPECIFIC ANSWER UNIT

**Claim:** Jurisdictional diversity reflects historical charters and legislation rather than constitutional inconsistency.

**Named evidence:** Article 225, applicable Letters Patent, the Code of Civil Procedure and criminal procedure statutes.

**Analysis:** The preserved-and-alterable structure allows specialised jurisdiction to evolve while constitutional writ and supervisory powers remain independently protected.

**Qualification:** A jurisdiction found in one chartered High Court or State statute cannot be universalised to every High Court.

#### EVIDENCE

- [FACT] Original civil jurisdiction is charter- and statute-specific.
- [FACT] Civil and criminal appeals arise through procedural and special statutes.
- [FACT] Constitutional powers under Articles 226 and 227 are not merely inherited statutory powers.

#### PRELIMS TRAP

Never state that all High Courts possess identical ordinary original civil jurisdiction.

#### MAINS USE

Distinguish constitutional minimum powers from variable statutory and charter-based jurisdiction.

#### MINI RECAP

Article 225 preserves; legislation shapes; Articles 226-228 constitutionally add and protect.

### SESSION 11 - ORIGINAL, APPELLATE, CIVIL AND CRIMINAL JURISDICTION

#### VISUAL FIRST - CONCEPT GATEWAY

```text
CASE ENTERS HIGH COURT
  +-- ORIGINAL: only where Constitution/charter/statute confers it
  +-- APPELLATE: civil/criminal appeals under governing law
  +-- REVISION/REFERENCE: procedure-specific correction
  +-- DEATH SENTENCE: Sessions Court sentence requires High Court confirmation
  +-- ELECTION/COMPANY/ADMIRALTY/COMMERCIAL: statute-specific routes
```

Caption: The diagram fixes this session's controlling legal relationship before detail.

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** High Courts hear some matters first instance and many by appeal, but the exact mix differs by constitutional text, charter, parliamentary law, State law and procedure.

**Technical definition:** Original civil, criminal, testamentary, admiralty, company, commercial, election, appellate and revisional powers must be traced to their governing source.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> High Court jurisdiction is functionally broad but territorially and statutorily differentiated.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **original jurisdiction**
- **appellate jurisdiction**
- **revision**
- **confirmation**
- **source-specific**

#### HIGH-COURT-SPECIFIC ANSWER UNIT

**Claim:** The High Court combines constitutional review with ordinary appellate correction because legality and factual adjudication meet at the State level.

**Named evidence:** Article 225, procedural codes, Representation of the People Act, 1951 and High Court-specific charters/statutes.

**Analysis:** This mixed docket permits legal uniformity across district courts but also creates workload tension between constitutional and ordinary cases.

**Qualification:** Original civil jurisdiction and bench arrangements are not uniform nationwide and must be stated as court-specific.

#### EVIDENCE

- [FACT] A Sessions Court may impose a death sentence, but execution requires High Court confirmation under criminal procedure.
- [FACT] Election petitions concerning Parliament and State legislatures are tried by High Courts under the 1951 Act.
- [FACT] Commercial Divisions in High Courts operate only where the 2015 statute and original civil jurisdiction conditions apply.

#### PRELIMS TRAP

Do not equate 'highest court in the State' with exclusive original jurisdiction over all major civil or criminal disputes.

#### MAINS USE

Use the source matrix to avoid sweeping claims and to explain workload composition.

#### MINI RECAP

Broad functions, variable sources, no universal original-civil template.

### SESSION 12 - ARTICLE 226 AND THE ARTICLE 32 COMPARISON

#### VISUAL FIRST - CONCEPT GATEWAY

```text
ARTICLE 32                         ARTICLE 226
Supreme Court                      High Court
Fundamental Right to move Court    constitutional power; relief discretionary
Fundamental Rights                 Fundamental Rights + any other legal purpose
all-India court                    territorial/cause-of-action connection
not displaced by Art 226           SC power preserved by Art 226(4)
```

Caption: The diagram fixes this session's controlling legal relationship before detail.

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** Article 226 empowers High Courts to issue directions, orders and the five named writs for Fundamental Rights and for any other legal purpose.

**Technical definition:** Article 226 is wider in remedial purpose than Article 32 but territorially bounded and ordinarily discretionary; Article 32 itself is a Fundamental Right.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> Article 32 guarantees a national constitutional door; Article 226 places a wider legal-remedy door closer to the citizen.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **Article 226**
- **any other purpose**
- **Article 32**
- **discretion**
- **territorial nexus**

#### HIGH-COURT-SPECIFIC ANSWER UNIT

**Claim:** Article 226 makes the High Court the everyday constitutional court for both rights and ordinary public-law legality.

**Named evidence:** Articles 32 and 226 and L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India (1997).

**Analysis:** The wider subject matter allows High Courts to correct unlawful detention, jurisdictional error and public-duty failure without forcing every claimant into the Supreme Court.

**Qualification:** Wider purpose does not mean unlimited territory, automatic relief or an appeal over every administrative decision.

#### EVIDENCE

- [FACT] Both courts may issue habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari and quo warranto.
- [FACT] Article 226(4) expressly states that the High Court power does not derogate from the Supreme Court's Article 32 power.
- [FACT] Judicial review under Articles 226/227 is part of the Basic Structure.

#### PRELIMS TRAP

Say 'Article 226 is wider in purpose', not simply 'wider in every respect'.

#### MAINS USE

Compare nature, protected interests, territorial reach, discretion and institutional role.

#### MINI RECAP

Same writ vocabulary; different constitutional nature, purpose and territorial operation.

### SESSION 13 - ARTICLE 226(2): TERRITORIAL CAUSE OF ACTION

#### VISUAL FIRST - CONCEPT GATEWAY

```text
RESPONDENT LOCATION --------------------+
                                         |
CAUSE OF ACTION wholly/partly in territory --> High Court may exercise Article 226
                                         |
PETITIONER RESIDENCE ALONE --------------X  no automatic jurisdiction
```

Caption: The diagram fixes this session's controlling legal relationship before detail.

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** A High Court may act when the cause of action arises wholly or partly within its territory even if the government, authority or person is located elsewhere.

**Technical definition:** Article 226(2) uses a cause-of-action nexus; material facts necessary to establish the legal claim, not merely residence or convenience, create territorial connection.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> Article 226 follows the dispute's material facts, not merely the respondent's headquarters.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **Article 226(2)**
- **cause of action**
- **material facts**
- **territorial nexus**
- **forum conveniens**

#### HIGH-COURT-SPECIFIC ANSWER UNIT

**Claim:** Cause-of-action jurisdiction prevents remote authority location from defeating an otherwise territorially rooted public-law claim.

**Named evidence:** Kusum Ingots & Alloys Ltd. v. Union of India (2004).

**Analysis:** The rule expands effective access where part of the legal wrong occurs locally, while allowing courts to reject artificial or trivial territorial links.

**Qualification:** Even when a small part of the cause arises, discretionary doctrines such as forum conveniens may make another High Court the appropriate forum.

#### EVIDENCE

- [FACT] Service of an order may matter only when it forms part of the material cause of action.
- [FACT] The situs of the authority is no longer the sole territorial test.
- [FACT] Residence of the petitioner by itself is insufficient.

#### PRELIMS TRAP

## PYQS AND ANSWER PRACTICE

### Audited previous-year routes

#### PYQ-M1 - UPSC GS-II 2024, Q2 - direct access-to-justice route

**Question (verified exact wording):** "Explain and distinguish between Lok Adalats and Arbitration Tribunals. Whether they entertain civil as well as criminal cases?"

**10 marks | 150 words**

**Model answer:** Lok Adalats are statutory settlement forums under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987; arbitral tribunals are consensual adjudicatory forums under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996. An ordinary Lok Adalat facilitates compromise in pending or pre-litigation disputes. Its consensual award is final, binding and decree-like; if settlement fails, it cannot decide the contest on merits. It may handle civil disputes and compoundable criminal offences, never non-compoundable offences. An arbitral tribunal derives authority from an arbitration agreement, hears an arbitrable civil or commercial dispute and renders an award subject to limited statutory challenge; it does not conduct criminal prosecution. Permanent Lok Adalats are a distinct exception for specified public-utility-service disputes: after failed conciliation they possess bounded adjudicatory power under the 1987 Act. Thus both reduce ordinary-court burden, but one is public access-oriented settlement and the other private consensual adjudication.

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes source, consent, method, outcome and civil/criminal boundaries.

#### PYQ-M2 - UPSC GS-II 2025, Q13 - supporting appointments route

**Verified neutral demand:** Critically examine India's collegium system of judicial appointments in comparison with the United States.

**15 marks | 250 words**

**Model answer:** India's higher-judiciary appointment system seeks insulation from political control through judicial primacy. Article 217 formally vests High Court appointment in the President after specified consultations; the Second Judges Case (1993) and Third Judges Reference (1998) converted consultation into a collegium process. High Court proposals begin with the Chief Justice and two senior colleagues, then reach the Supreme Court collegium of the CJI and two senior-most judges, alongside executive inputs. The 2015 NJAC judgment invalidated the enacted replacement because judicial independence was imperilled.

The United States uses presidential nomination and Senate confirmation. Public hearings and political accountability are clearer, but partisan polarisation can dominate selection. India's model better limits direct party control, yet opacity, delay, weak diversity disclosure and diffused responsibility reduce legitimacy.

Reform should preserve judicial primacy while publishing criteria and timelines, creating a permanent appointments secretariat, widening the search pool, recording privacy-sensitive reasons and requiring timely executive responses. The objective is accountable independence, not transplanting a politically polarised confirmation model.

**Why this earns marks:** It compares actors, values, risks and feasible reform without implying that every commission is unconstitutional.

### Exactly six original Mains questions with model answers

#### Original Q1 - 10 marks, answer in not more than 150 words

**Question:** Explain why Article 226 makes the High Court the everyday constitutional court.

**Model answer:** Article 226 makes the High Court the everyday constitutional court because it combines local access with broad remedial purpose. Unlike Article 32, which is itself a Fundamental Right and primarily enforces Fundamental Rights in the Supreme Court, Article 226 authorises writs for Fundamental Rights and "any other purpose." Article 226(2) also permits jurisdiction where material cause of action arises wholly or partly within the territory. Citizens can therefore challenge unlawful detention, refusal of statutory duty, jurisdictional error and invalid public action near the place of injury. L. Chandra Kumar (1997) further protects review under Articles 226/227 as part of the Basic Structure. Yet the power is not limitless: relief is discretionary, territorial nexus is necessary, and an efficacious alternative remedy ordinarily invites restraint subject to recognised exceptions. Article 226 is thus wider in purpose, locally accessible and constitutionally indispensable.

#### Original Q2 - 10 marks, answer in not more than 150 words

**Question:** Distinguish Articles 226 and 227.

**Model answer:** Article 226 is principally a public-law remedial jurisdiction; Article 227 is a supervisory jurisdiction over territorial courts and tribunals. Under Article 226, a High Court issues directions, orders and writs to enforce Fundamental Rights and other legal rights. Territorial cause of action matters, and relief remains discretionary. Article 227 enables judicial and administrative superintendence, including correction of grave jurisdictional or procedural failure, calling returns and framing general rules, subject to the Armed-Forces-law exception. Radhey Shyam (2015) cautions against treating ordinary civil-court orders as Article 226 writ targets; correction ordinarily lies under Article 227 or statutory procedure. Neither provision creates a routine second appeal. L. Chandra Kumar protects their review role as part of the Basic Structure. In short, Article 226 asks whether public power has violated law; Article 227 keeps inferior adjudicatory institutions within lawful bounds.

#### Original Q3 - 15 marks, answer in not more than 250 words

**Question:** Examine how Articles 233-235 protect subordinate-judiciary independence.

**Model answer:** Articles 233-235 distribute recruitment and career control so that the State executive cannot dominate judges who routinely decide cases involving government. Article 233 places appointment, posting and promotion of district judges with the Governor in consultation with the High Court. For direct recruitment, clause (2) requires the constitutional advocacy threshold and High Court recommendation. Article 234 governs recruitment below district judge through Governor-made rules after consultation with both the State Public Service Commission and High Court. Article 235 then vests control over district and subordinate courts in the High Court, covering posting, promotion, leave and disciplinary authority.

Chandra Mohan (1966) connected this design to Article 50 and rejected executive officers entering district judgeship through an unconstitutional route. Ranga Muhammad (1967) located transfer of serving district judges within Article 235, and Nripendra Nath Bagchi treated disciplinary control as integral.

The arrangement is not exclusive judicial administration: State law, finance, infrastructure, formal gubernatorial orders and valid appeal rights remain relevant. High Court control must also observe natural justice. The constitutional achievement is therefore shared appointment with judicially secured career control, preserving both democratic administration and adjudicative independence.

#### Original Q4 - 15 marks, answer in not more than 250 words

**Question:** Critically examine constitutional safeguards for High Court independence.

**Model answer:** High Court independence rests on mutually reinforcing personnel, financial, jurisdictional and administrative safeguards. Article 217 provides presidential appointment through constitutionally specified consultation; the Judges Cases add judicial primacy. Judges serve until sixty-two and do not hold office during executive pleasure. Article 218 applies the stringent Article 124(4)-(5) removal route based on proved misbehaviour or incapacity. Article 221 protects service conditions, while Article 229 places registry appointments with the Chief Justice and charges High Court administrative expenses on the State Consolidated Fund. Article 222 transfer requires CJI consultation. Articles 215, 226, 227 and 235 protect contempt authority, judicial review, superintendence and control over the district judiciary.

Practical independence nevertheless faces collegium opacity, delayed appointments, perceived punitive transfers, weak diversity, vacancies and infrastructure dependence. Total secrecy is not required for independence, while total disclosure may harm candidate privacy. Reform should preserve judicial primacy but institutionalise criteria, timelines, a permanent secretariat, diversity search, reasoned communication and predictable infrastructure funding.

Thus the Constitution strongly protects adjudication from direct political retaliation; accountable and timely administration must make that formal protection effective.

#### Original Q5 - 20 marks, answer in not more than 250 words

**Question:** Evaluate the case for an All India Judicial Service.

**Model answer:** Article 312 enables an All India Judicial Service only after the Rajya Sabha declares by a two-thirds majority of members present and voting that national interest requires it, followed by parliamentary law. Article 312(3) excludes posts below district judge. No AIJS exists as of 7 September 2026.

The case for AIJS is regular recruitment, common minimum standards, a wider national talent pool, planned training and greater mobility. It may reduce recurring district-judge vacancies and diversify recruitment beyond local professional networks. National data and a predictable calendar could support workforce planning.

However, district judging demands command of State law, local language and procedure. States and High Courts also raise federal, reservation, seniority, promotion and cadre-control concerns. A central examination cannot repair courtrooms, staff, prosecution or retention. Most importantly, AIJS design cannot dilute High Court consultation and control under Articles 233 and 235.

A viable model would remain district-judge-level, allocate officers to State cadres, require local-language competence, protect State reservation, preserve High Court appraisal and promotion, and coordinate training through judicial academies. AIJS is therefore defensible as a carefully federal professional service, not as centralisation of the subordinate judiciary.

#### Original Q6 - 20 marks, answer in not more than 250 words

**Question:** Judicial pendency is a governance problem, not merely a judicial problem. Analyse with reference to High Courts and subordinate courts.

**Model answer:** Pendency is the accumulated stock of unresolved cases; delay is the time taken in an individual case. Both arise from interactions across government, courts and litigants. The Department of Justice's 1 July 2026 snapshot recorded 1,122 sanctioned High Court posts, 781 working judges and 341 vacancies, showing a capacity gap, but vacancies alone do not explain the queue.

Government departments generate repetitive litigation and delayed appeals. Police investigation, prosecution, summons, forensic support and prison production affect criminal trials. Court staff, records, translation, infrastructure, adjournments and fragmented case management affect throughput. Complex procedure and unequal legal assistance increase citizen costs.

Reform must therefore combine timely appointments under Articles 217 and 233-234, High Court control under Article 235, Malik Mazhar recruitment calendars, differentiated case tracks, reasoned adjournment control, mediation where lawful, government-litigation discipline and staff/infrastructure investment. e-Courts Phase III can digitise records, filing and payments, while NJDG supports diagnosis. Yet digital-only design can exclude poor, rural, disabled or language-disadvantaged litigants.

The goal is not disposal at any cost. Rights-sensitive case management, adequate reasons and fair hearing must accompany speed. Pendency falls sustainably only when every institution producing or processing litigation is accountable.


## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH - NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

### A. Federalism and common High Courts

A common High Court demonstrates cooperative institutional federalism: judicial capacity may be shared even when legislative, executive and service structures remain distinct. The advantage is coherence and resource pooling; the risk is perceived distance, language exclusion or unequal bench access. The answer must therefore connect Article 231 with statute-specific territorial and bench design rather than treating a common court as administrative merger.

### B. Collegium reform without executive capture

The Second and Third Judges Cases prioritise judicial independence; the NJAC decision invalidated the enacted replacement. Reform questions should separate constitutional permissibility from institutional design. Transparent eligibility criteria, a permanent secretariat, documented timelines, diversity search, privacy-sensitive reasons and predictable executive responses can improve legitimacy without converting appointments into partisan confirmation.

### C. Writ discretion and proportional institutional restraint

Article 226 combines wide power with self-restraint. Territorial nexus, alternative remedy, delay, disputed facts and forum conveniens are distinct filters. A High Court should intervene early against void action, rights violations or natural-justice failure, yet avoid becoming the first appellate authority for every statutory dispute. The analytical phrase is 'jurisdiction remains; discretionary exercise is structured'.

### D. Professionalising the district judiciary

Recruitment must be read with training, mentoring, language competence, promotion, infrastructure and court management. Rejanish expands a direct-recruitment eligibility pathway; AIJA 2025 adds prospective practice experience for entry-level civil judges; Malik Mazhar targets calendars. None alone resolves vacancies. Reform succeeds only when Articles 233-235 control is paired with predictable State funding and data-based workforce planning.

### E. Digital constitutionalism

e-Courts can reduce distance and administrative opacity, but digital procedure must preserve open justice, accessible formats, language support, privacy, cyber-security and meaningful hearing. Hybrid access and e-Sewa Kendras matter because device ownership is not equivalent to procedural capability. Use technology as an institutional multiplier, never as evidence that adjudicative capacity or equality is already solved.
