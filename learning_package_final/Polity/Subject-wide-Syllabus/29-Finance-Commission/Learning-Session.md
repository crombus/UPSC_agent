## BASIC LEARNING SESSION

### SESSION 1 - Fiscal federalism and the two imbalances

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
CONSTITUTIONAL ASSIGNMENT
Union: broader, buoyant tax bases       States: service-heavy expenditure duties
                 \                         /
                  VERTICAL FISCAL GAP
                           |
                    Union-State sharing

State A: higher capacity             State B: lower capacity / higher cost
                 \                         /
                 HORIZONTAL FISCAL GAP
                           |
                    inter se allocation
```

#### Definition

**Fiscal federalism** is the constitutional and institutional allocation of revenue powers, expenditure responsibilities, transfers and borrowing space across levels of government.

#### Answer line

> The Finance Commission is a periodic constitutional equalisation mechanism: it addresses both the Union-State resource gap and unequal fiscal capacity among States without becoming a permanent spending ministry.

#### Must-write keywords

`vertical fiscal imbalance` - `horizontal fiscal imbalance` - `equalisation` - `autonomy` - `rule-based transfers`

#### Finance-Commission-specific paragraph model

- **Claim:** India needs a Finance Commission because constitutional tax assignment and service responsibility do not automatically match.
- **Named evidence:** Article 280 creates a periodic Commission; the Sixteenth Finance Commission describes equity and efficiency as guides to horizontal devolution.
- **Analysis:** Rule-based sharing reduces ad hoc bargaining, while equalisation helps comparable public services despite unequal tax capacity and costs.
- **Qualification:** A fiscal gap is not measured from tax assignment alone; own revenue, devolution, grants, borrowing and expenditure obligations must be read together.

#### Evidence and explanation

- **[FACT] Vertical imbalance** asks how the shareable pool is divided between the Union and all States collectively.
- **[FACT] Horizontal imbalance** asks how the States' collective share is divided among individual States.
- **[ANALYSIS] Equalisation does not mean identical rupee transfers; it means adjusting for unequal capacity and cost so minimum public services become more comparable.
- **[LIMIT] The Commission is central, but Article 282 grants, Centrally Sponsored Schemes, GST arrangements, borrowing and State own revenues also shape fiscal federalism.

#### Prelims trap

Vertical means Union versus States; horizontal means among States. Reversing them loses easy marks.

#### Mains use

Use this session to introduce any answer on fiscal federalism, devolution, grants or State autonomy.

#### Recap

Assignment creates two gaps; Article 280 supplies a periodic, reasoned balancing process.


### SESSION 2 - Decoded constitutional revenue spine

#### VISUAL FIRST

| Provision | What it does | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|
| Article 269 | Union levies/collects specified inter-State taxes and assigns them to States | assigned taxes are not the Article 270 pool |
| Article 269A | inter-State GST is levied/collected by Union and apportioned | connects GST settlement with Article 270 |
| Article 270 | shareable Union taxes distributed between Union and States | source of the divisible pool |
| Article 271 | Parliament may add a Union-purpose surcharge | proceeds belong wholly to Union |
| Article 279 | defines net proceeds and makes CAG certification final | fixes the distributable base |
| Article 279A | creates GST Council | tax-policy coordination, not Finance Commission |
| Articles 280-281 | Commission and parliamentary laying | recommendation plus accountability |

#### Definition

The **constitutional revenue spine** is the linked set of provisions that identifies assigned taxes, apportioned GST, shareable taxes, exclusions, net proceeds, the recommending body and disclosure to Parliament.

#### Answer line

> A precise answer begins with the tax route: identify whether revenue is assigned under Article 269, apportioned under Article 269A, shared under Article 270 or excluded as an Article 271 surcharge.

#### Must-write keywords

`Article 269` - `Article 269A` - `Article 270` - `Article 271` - `Article 279` - `Article 279A`

#### Finance-Commission-specific paragraph model

- **Claim:** The divisible pool is a constitutionally delimited base, not a synonym for Union gross tax revenue.
- **Named evidence:** Articles 270(1), 270(1A), 271 and 279 define inclusion, GST cross-links, exclusion and certification.
- **Analysis:** The classification determines whether and how revenue reaches States; the Commission recommends shares within that legal architecture.
- **Qualification:** Exact treatment can depend on the levy and constitutional clause; avoid saying that every Union tax or every cess is shareable.

#### Evidence and explanation

- **[FACT] Article 270** covers specified Union taxes and duties and, through clause (1A), the relevant GST collected under Article 246A(1) and the Union-apportioned part under Article 269A.
- **[FACT] Article 269A** places inter-State GST in a Union collection/apportionment mechanism framed by parliamentary law on GST Council recommendations.
- **[FACT] Article 271** authorises a surcharge for Union purposes; its proceeds form part of the Consolidated Fund of India and are excluded from sharing.
- **[FACT] Article 279** defines net proceeds as proceeds minus collection cost; the CAG ascertains and certifies them, and the certificate is final.

#### Prelims trap

The GST Council under Article 279A does not decide the Finance Commission's vertical or horizontal devolution formula.

#### Mains use

Draw this table before discussing cesses, GST or the divisible pool; it prevents category errors.

#### Recap

Classify the levy first, then identify the pool, then apply vertical and horizontal shares.


### SESSION 3 - Article 280: appointment, composition and functions

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
PRESIDENT
   |
   +-- every fifth year OR earlier if necessary
   |
   +-- appoints Chair + four other members
   |
   +-- receives recommendations on:
       (a) vertical + horizontal tax sharing
       (b) Article 275 grant principles
       (bb) Panchayat augmentation
       (c) Municipality augmentation
       (d) other referred sound-finance matters
```

#### Definition

The **Finance Commission** is a periodic constitutional body constituted by the President under Article 280 to recommend tax distribution, grant principles, local-body augmentation and referred matters of sound finance.

#### Answer line

> Article 280 supplies the institution's fixed constitutional core; the President's terms of reference may add matters under clause (3)(d), but cannot erase the listed duties.

#### Must-write keywords

`President` - `every fifth year or earlier` - `Chairman plus four` - `Article 280(3)` - `sound finance`

#### Finance-Commission-specific paragraph model

- **Claim:** The Commission combines a mandatory constitutional mandate with a flexible presidential reference power.
- **Named evidence:** Article 280(1), (2) and (3)(a)-(d).
- **Analysis:** Fixed functions protect federal predictability, while clause (d) lets each award address contemporary fiscal risks.
- **Qualification:** The President appoints members, but Parliament - not the President alone - is constitutionally authorised to prescribe qualifications by law.

#### Evidence and explanation

- **[FACT] Periodicity:** within two years of constitutional commencement and thereafter every fifth year, or earlier if the President considers necessary.
- **[FACT] Composition:** one Chairman and four other members.
- **[FACT] Article 280(3)(a):** distribution between Union and States and allocation among States of their respective shares.
- **[FACT] Article 280(3)(b), Article 280(3)(bb), Article 280(3)(c) and Article 280(3)(d):** Article 275 grant principles, Panchayat augmentation, Municipality augmentation and additional presidential references in the interests of sound finance.

#### Prelims trap

Do not write 'five members plus a Chairman'; the total is five: Chairman plus four.

#### Mains use

Use the constitutional/statutory split when the question asks how the Commission is constituted.

#### Recap

Article 280 fixes why, when, how large and what core tasks; the 1951 Act supplies operational detail.


### SESSION 4 - Finance Commission Act, 1951: qualifications and procedure

#### VISUAL FIRST

| Statutory field | Controlled rule |
|---|---|
| Chairman | experience in public affairs |
| other-member pools | High Court judge/qualified person; government finance and accounts; financial matters and administration; economics |
| personal interest | President must be satisfied no prejudicial interest exists |
| disqualification | unsound mind, undischarged insolvent, moral-turpitude conviction, prejudicial interest |
| term | period specified by President; reappointment permitted; resignation to President |
| procedure/powers | Commission determines procedure; specified civil-court powers for inquiry |

#### Definition

The **Finance Commission (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act, 1951** operationalises Article 280 by prescribing member qualifications, integrity safeguards, service conditions, procedure and evidentiary powers.

#### Answer line

> The Constitution creates the body; Parliament's 1951 Act supplies the expertise and inquiry framework needed to turn a federal mandate into an evidence-based award.

#### Must-write keywords

`Act 33 of 1951` - `public affairs` - `government finance and accounts` - `reappointment` - `civil-court powers`

#### Finance-Commission-specific paragraph model

- **Claim:** Expert composition matters because devolution requires legal, accounting, administrative and economic judgment.
- **Named evidence:** Sections 3-8 of the Finance Commission (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act, 1951.
- **Analysis:** Plural expertise improves the Commission's ability to test State memoranda, revenue projections, grant conditions and fiscal risks.
- **Qualification:** Civil-court-like powers support evidence gathering; they do not transform the Commission into a court or make its recommendations judicial decrees.

#### Evidence and explanation

- **[FACT] Section 3** lists the Chairman's public-affairs qualification and four alternative qualification categories for other members.
- **[FACT] Sections 4-5** address prejudicial interests and disqualifications.
- **[FACT] Section 6** permits the President to specify the term; members are eligible for reappointment and may resign to the President.
- **[FACT] Section 8** lets the Commission determine its procedure and grants listed civil-court powers such as summoning witnesses and requiring documents.

#### Prelims trap

Do not claim that Article 280 itself lists the four specialist qualifications; they come from the 1951 Act.

#### Mains use

A strong constitution answer separates constitutional architecture from statutory mechanics.

#### Recap

Article 280 creates; the 1951 Act qualifies, screens and equips.


### SESSION 5 - Advisory character, Article 281 and implementation

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
Commission recommendation
        |
        v
report submitted to President
        |
        v
Article 281: report + explanatory memorandum laid before each House
        |
        +-- accepted recommendation
        +-- accepted in principle
        +-- taken note of
        +-- to be examined separately
        |
        v
President's order / Budget / law / guideline / release
```

#### Definition

The Commission's recommendations are **advisory**: Article 281 requires public parliamentary explanation of action taken, not automatic legal self-execution of every recommendation.

#### Answer line

> The Finance Commission is non-binding in formal law but weighty in constitutional politics because rejection or modification must confront a reasoned, public, federal expert report.

#### Must-write keywords

`advisory` - `Article 281` - `explanatory memorandum` - `acceptance` - `implementation`

#### Finance-Commission-specific paragraph model

- **Claim:** Constitutional weight and legal enforceability are different dimensions.
- **Named evidence:** Article 281 and the February 2026 Explanatory Memorandum on FC-16.
- **Analysis:** Public reasons, budget consequences and recurring federal negotiations make recommendations harder to disregard than ordinary advice.
- **Qualification:** Laying the report does not itself appropriate money, amend the Constitution, issue every operational guideline or prove actual release.

#### Evidence and explanation

- **[FACT] Article 281** requires the President to cause every recommendation, together with an explanatory memorandum on action taken, to be laid before each House of Parliament.
- **[CURRENT] FC-16's report and Explanatory Memorandum were laid on 1 February 2026.
- **[CURRENT] The Memorandum uses distinct status language: accepted, accepted in principle, taken note of, to be examined separately and to be acted upon in due course.
- **[LIMIT] Implementation may require a Presidential order under Article 270, Budget provision, statutory authority, ministry guidelines, State compliance and actual release.

#### Prelims trap

Do not write that Parliament 'approves' the report under Article 281; the constitutional verb is laying, accompanied by an explanatory memorandum.

#### Mains use

Status vocabulary is an excellent qualification in any current-affairs answer.

#### Recap

Recommendation -> response -> legal/budget instrument -> release: never collapse the chain.


### SESSION 6 - Divisible pool, vertical devolution and cesses

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
UNION GROSS TAX REVENUE
   - Article 271 surcharges
   - specific-purpose cesses excluded by Article 270 design
   - Article 269 / 269A assigned-apportioned routes
   - Union Territory share where applicable
   - cost of collection
                 |
                 v
      CAG-CERTIFIED NET PROCEEDS
                 |
          DIVISIBLE POOL
          /            \
      Union share    States' vertical share
```

#### Definition

**Vertical devolution** is the recommended percentage of the divisible pool assigned collectively to States; it is not the States' percentage of gross tax revenue.

#### Answer line

> The headline vertical share must always be read with its base: exclusions and collection cost determine how much revenue the percentage actually reaches.

#### Must-write keywords

`gross tax revenue` - `net proceeds` - `divisible pool` - `41 per cent` - `cess` - `surcharge`

#### Finance-Commission-specific paragraph model

- **Claim:** Cesses and surcharges create a base-versus-rate problem in fiscal federalism.
- **Named evidence:** Articles 270, 271 and 279; FC-16 Report Chapter 7; Explanatory Memorandum paragraphs 3-4.
- **Analysis:** Even an unchanged vertical percentage can yield a different effective transfer relative to gross tax revenue if the shareable base changes.
- **Qualification:** FC-16 rejected the claim that cesses and surcharges had almost wiped out the FC-14 increase, yet acknowledged that their rise reduced devolution below what it would otherwise have been.

#### Evidence and explanation

- **[CURRENT] FC-16 recommended retaining States' vertical share at 41 per cent of net proceeds; the Union accepted it.
- **[CURRENT] FC-16 recommended annual Union disclosure of Article 279 CAG-certified net-proceeds data; the Union accepted it.
- **[FACT] FC-16 calculated the divisible pool at an average 89.2 per cent of gross tax revenue in the FC-13 period, 82.1 per cent in FC-14 and 78.3 per cent in FC-15.
- **[FACT] FC-16 did not recommend including cesses/surcharges in the pool or imposing a numerical cap; it treated such change as requiring constitutional reconsideration and retained the existing design.

#### Prelims trap

41 per cent is not 41 per cent of gross tax revenue, and it is not a State-wise share.

#### Mains use

Use the base-versus-rate distinction to evaluate State autonomy without making an exaggerated 'no benefit' claim.

#### Recap

Net pool first; vertical rate second; effective transfer and grants last.


### SESSION 7 - Horizontal devolution and the FC-16 formula

#### VISUAL FIRST

| FC-16 criterion | Weight | Operating idea |
|---|---:|---|
| per-capita GSDP distance | 42.5% | larger gap from benchmark -> stronger equalisation |
| population, Census 2011 | 17.5% | scale of service need |
| demographic performance | 10% | inverse population growth, 1971-2011 |
| area | 10% | cost/scale, with 1.5% floor |
| forest | 10% | weighted forest stock plus increase |
| contribution to GDP | 10% | square-root-transformed GSDP share |

#### Definition

**Horizontal devolution** allocates the States' collective vertical share among the twenty-eight States through a weighted formula balancing equity, need, cost and contribution.

#### Answer line

> FC-16's horizontal formula remains equalisation-heavy, but adds a bounded contribution-to-GDP criterion and changes how demography, area and forest are measured.

#### Must-write keywords

`per-capita GSDP distance` - `2011 population` - `inverse growth` - `1.5 per cent floor` - `weighted forest` - `GDP contribution`

#### Finance-Commission-specific paragraph model

- **Claim:** The formula is not a simple reward table; each criterion converts State data into a relative share before weights are combined.
- **Named evidence:** FC-16 Report paragraphs 8.84-8.97 and Technical Note, Table 8.8; accepted in the Explanatory Memorandum.
- **Analysis:** The 42.5 per cent distance weight preserves redistribution, while the 10 per cent GDP contribution term makes productive contribution visible without allocating directly in proportion to output.
- **Qualification:** Weights are award-specific. Do not carry FC-15's tax/fiscal-effort criterion into FC-16.

#### Evidence and explanation

- **[CURRENT] Population:** each State's share of the 2011 Census population of twenty-eight States.
- **[CURRENT] Demographic performance:** per-capita amount proportional to inverse population growth between 1971 and 2011, then multiplied by 2011 population.
- **[CURRENT] Forest:** 80 per cent weighted forest-area share plus 20 per cent share in increase from 2015 to 2023; VDF/MDF/OF weights are 1.0/0.65/0.30 and decline is treated as zero.
- **[CURRENT] GDP contribution:** square root of State GSDP divided by the sum of square roots, using average nominal GSDP for 2018-19 to 2023-24 excluding 2020-21.

#### Prelims trap

Demographic performance is not current TFR in FC-16; tax/fiscal effort is not a separate FC-16 criterion.

#### Mains use

Use the formula to structure equity-versus-efficiency analysis and quote only verified weights.

#### Recap

Six criteria, 100 per cent: distance 42.5, population 17.5, and four 10s.


### SESSION 8 - Article 275 grants and transfer design

#### VISUAL FIRST

| Transfer | Legal base | Allocation character |
|---|---|---|
| tax devolution | Articles 270 and 280(3)(a) | formula-based share of net proceeds |
| grants-in-aid | Articles 275 and 280(3)(b) | need/principle-based grant from CFI |
| public-purpose grants | Article 282 | discretionary Union or State spending power |
| tied grant | grant conditions specify use | less recipient flexibility |
| untied grant | broad permitted use | greater recipient discretion |
| performance grant | release linked to verified outcome/condition | incentive plus compliance |

#### Definition

**Grants-in-aid** are transfers distinct from tax devolution; Article 280 recommends principles, while Article 275 authorises grants charged on the Consolidated Fund of India to States determined to need assistance.

#### Answer line

> Tax devolution supports general autonomy; grants can equalise residual need, finance merit goods or reward performance, but conditions must be transparent and proportionate.

#### Must-write keywords

`Article 275` - `grants-in-aid` - `Consolidated Fund of India` - `tied` - `untied` - `performance grant`

#### Finance-Commission-specific paragraph model

- **Claim:** A well-designed transfer system uses devolution for predictable general resources and grants for clearly identified residual problems.
- **Named evidence:** Articles 275(1), 280(3)(b) and 282; FC-16 Chapters 9-11.
- **Analysis:** Separating instruments makes accountability clearer: devolution follows a formula, while grant design can specify purpose, entry condition, performance or matching responsibility.
- **Qualification:** Article 275 includes constitutionally specific provisos concerning Scheduled Tribes and Scheduled Areas; do not reduce it to a generic discretionary subsidy.

#### Evidence and explanation

- **[FACT] Article 280(3)(b)** asks the Commission to recommend principles governing Article 275 grants and sums payable to States for purposes outside the provisos to Article 275(1).
- **[FACT] Article 275** permits different sums for different States determined to need assistance and charges the grants on the Consolidated Fund of India.
- **[FACT] Article 282** is a separate public-purpose spending power; it is not the constitutional source of tax devolution.
- **[ANALYSIS] Tied grants protect a national service objective; untied grants protect State choice; performance grants can incentivise results but may penalise low-capacity States unless design accounts for starting conditions.

#### Prelims trap

Do not add grants to the vertical-devolution percentage or describe grants as a State's constitutional share of one pooled percentage.

#### Mains use

Use the instrument table whenever a question asks whether fiscal transfers improve autonomy.

#### Recap

Devolution distributes a pool; grants address a purpose or gap; Article 282 remains a separate channel.


### SESSION 9 - Local bodies: Union Finance Commission and State Finance Commission

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
Article 243-I: Governor constitutes State Finance Commission every fifth year
        |
        +-- reviews Panchayat finances and recommends State-level sharing/grants
        |
Article 243-Y: applies the SFC finance-review logic to Municipalities
        |
Article 280(3)(bb)/(c): Union FC recommends measures to augment
State Consolidated Funds, based on SFC recommendations
        |
State allocation + onward transfer + local accounts/audit
```

#### Definition

Local-body augmentation under Article 280 is an indirect federal chain: the Union Finance Commission strengthens State Consolidated Funds so States can supplement Panchayats and Municipalities.

#### Answer line

> The Union Finance Commission is not a national municipal allocator; local fiscal decentralisation remains State-mediated and constitutionally linked to State Finance Commissions.

#### Must-write keywords

`Article 243-I` - `Article 243-Y` - `Article 280(3)(bb)` - `Article 280(3)(c)` - `augmentation`

#### Finance-Commission-specific paragraph model

- **Claim:** Local fiscal federalism depends on two Commissions operating at different constitutional levels.
- **Named evidence:** Articles 243-I, 243-Y and 280(3)(bb)/(c); FC-16 Chapter 10.
- **Analysis:** A strong SFC supplies local needs and devolution evidence; the Union FC can then design augmentation without substituting for State responsibility.
- **Qualification:** The Constitution currently retains the words 'on the basis of the recommendations made by the Finance Commission of the State'. FC-16 recommended deleting that phrase by amendment, but no such amendment was located by the control date.

#### Evidence and explanation

- **[FACT] Article 243-I** requires the Governor to constitute an SFC within one year of the 73rd Amendment's commencement and every fifth year thereafter.
- **[FACT] Article 243-Y** requires the SFC constituted under Article 243-I to review Municipal finances and make recommendations.
- **[CURRENT] FC-16 linked local grants to duly constituted bodies, public accounts/audit and timely SFC constitution plus State-legislature action-taken reporting.
- **[CURRENT] The Union accepted FC-16's detailed local-body grant design; rural-local-body operational guidelines were officially reported as issued on 21 May 2026.

#### Prelims trap

Do not say the Union Finance Commission directly divides money among every Panchayat or Municipality.

#### Mains use

Use the chain to explain why delayed SFCs weaken both decentralisation and grant eligibility.

#### Recap

SFC diagnoses and recommends within the State; Union FC augments the State fund; States transfer onward.


### SESSION 10 - Disaster-risk financing

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
RISK FINANCE
|
+-- State level: SDRF + SDMF = Rs 2,04,401 crore (2026-31)
|      +-- SDRF Rs 1,63,521 crore
|      +-- SDMF Rs 40,880 crore
|
+-- National level: NDRF + NDMF = Rs 79,406 crore (2026-31)
|
+-- data/eligibility: NDMIS validation from 2027-28
```

#### Definition

**Disaster-risk financing** is the ex ante and ex post funding architecture for response, relief, recovery, reconstruction, preparedness and mitigation under the Disaster Management Act and Finance Commission recommendations.

#### Answer line

> FC-16 continues the shift from relief-only thinking to a response-plus-mitigation architecture, while tying later access to better data and utilisation discipline.

#### Must-write keywords

`SDRF` - `SDMF` - `NDRF` - `NDMF` - `risk index` - `NDMIS`

#### Finance-Commission-specific paragraph model

- **Claim:** Disaster grants are a specialised FC function referred through the President's terms of reference, not a free-standing Article 280 clause naming each fund.
- **Named evidence:** FC-16 Chapter 11 and Explanatory Memorandum paragraphs 20-25; MHA guidelines dated 31 July 2026.
- **Analysis:** Separate response and mitigation windows reduce the risk that all fiscal attention is absorbed by post-disaster relief.
- **Qualification:** Award-period corpus, annual allocation, release and utilisation are different quantities; do not present the five-year figure as one-year spending.

#### Evidence and explanation

- **[CURRENT] State corpus:** Rs 2,04,401 crore for SDRF and SDMF together; Union share Rs 1,55,915.85 crore and State share Rs 48,485.15 crore.
- **[CURRENT] Cost sharing:** 75:25 for non-Northeastern and Hilly States and 90:10 for Northeastern and Hilly States.
- **[CURRENT] State corpus split:** SDRF:SDMF = 80:20; Rs 1,63,521 crore and Rs 40,880 crore.
- **[CURRENT] National corpus:** Rs 79,406 crore for NDRF and NDMF; the Union accepted the recommendations and MHA issued operational guidelines on 31 July 2026.

#### Prelims trap

Rs 2,04,401 crore is the combined State-level corpus, not the national-level corpus.

#### Mains use

Use this as a GS-III bridge: constitutional transfer design meets climate adaptation, mitigation and accountable data.

#### Recap

Separate level, fund, period and purpose before quoting any disaster-finance number.


### SESSION 11 - Evolution from FC-14 and FC-15

#### VISUAL FIRST

| Award | Vertical share | Exam-relevant change |
|---|---:|---|
| FC-13 | 32% | pre-jump baseline |
| FC-14, 2015-20 | 42% | large rise in predictable, relatively untied tax devolution |
| FC-15, 2021-26 | 41% | one-point adjustment associated with J&K reorganisation; six-criterion formula |
| FC-16, 2026-31 | 41% | continuity vertically; new contribution-to-GDP criterion horizontally |

#### Definition

The **evolution of Finance Commission awards** shows continuity in equalisation but changing balances between autonomy, demographic fairness, ecology, effort and contribution.

#### Answer line

> The major recent story is not a linear rise in the headline percentage; it is the interaction of vertical share, horizontal criteria, grants and the size of the divisible base.

#### Must-write keywords

`FC-14` - `42 per cent` - `FC-15` - `41 per cent` - `continuity and change`

#### Finance-Commission-specific paragraph model

- **Claim:** FC-14 enlarged untied fiscal space; FC-15 recalibrated the share and formula; FC-16 retained 41 per cent but redesigned several criteria.
- **Named evidence:** FC-14 and FC-15 reports; FC-16 Chapters 7-9.
- **Analysis:** Comparative analysis reveals how an award can preserve overall vertical balance while changing who receives what and through which instrument.
- **Qualification:** Do not infer each State's fiscal outcome from the collective percentage; horizontal share, tax collection, grants and expenditure pressures differ.

#### Evidence and explanation

- **[FACT] FC-14** raised the States' collective share from 32 to 42 per cent for 2015-20.
- **[FACT] FC-15** recommended 41 per cent for 2021-26 and used population, area, forest and ecology, income distance, demographic performance, and tax and fiscal effort.
- **[CURRENT] FC-16** retained 41 per cent and removed a separate tax/fiscal-effort criterion while adding contribution to GDP.
- **[ANALYSIS] Evolution should be assessed through autonomy, equalisation, incentives and transfer composition rather than one percentage alone.

#### Prelims trap

The one-point fall from 42 to 41 is not evidence that FC-15 rejected fiscal decentralisation wholesale.

#### Mains use

This comparison directly answers the 2021 Mains PYQ and enriches current FC-16 analysis.

#### Recap

Compare rate, formula, grants and context - never only the headline rate.


### SESSION 12 - Sixteenth Finance Commission: recommendation, acceptance and action

#### VISUAL FIRST

| FC-16 item | Recommendation | Union response / implementation control |

| award period | 2026-27 to 2030-31 | commenced 1 Apr 2026 |
| vertical share | 41% | accepted |
| horizontal formula | six criteria, Table 8.8 | accepted |

#### Definition

A sound FC award balances equalisation, State autonomy, incentive compatibility, macro-fiscal stability and transparent accounting.

- **Claim:** Transfer design changes behaviour: unconditional resources expand choice, while conditions can improve services or distort priorities.

A credible award equalises capacity, rewards genuine effort and exposes fiscal risk.


### SESSION 14 - Institutional distinctions

#### VISUAL FIRST

|---|---|---|---|
| Union Finance Commission | Article 280 | periodic tax-sharing/grant recommendations | set GST rates or audit accounts |
| GST Council | Article 279A | cooperative recommendations on GST | decide FC vertical/horizontal shares |

#### Definition

Institutional clarity means identifying each body's source, membership, function, output and legal effect before making a comparison.

#### Answer line

#### Must-write keywords

`Article 280` - `Article 279A` - `CAG` - `RBI` - `NITI Aayog` - `State Finance Commission`

#### Finance-Commission-specific paragraph model

- **Claim:** Overlapping fiscal subjects do not make institutions interchangeable.
- **Named evidence:** Constitutional provisions and official institutional mandates.
- **Analysis:** Correct institutional routing clarifies accountability: the actor who recommends may not be the actor who legislates, budgets, certifies, audits or releases.
- **Qualification:** The fact that two bodies make recommendations does not mean the Supreme Court's holding on one automatically governs the other.

#### Evidence and explanation

- **[FACT] Finance Commission:** temporary periodic constitutional commission appointed by the President.
- **[FACT] GST Council:** continuing Union-State constitutional forum chaired by the Union Finance Minister with weighted voting under Article 279A.
- **[FACT] CAG:** final constitutional certifier of net proceeds under Article 279 and auditor of public accounts.
- **[FACT] RBI:** publishes State-finance analysis and supports government debt/cash management but does not award constitutional devolution shares.
- **[FACT] GST compensation:** the Compensation to States Act, 2017 created a statutory transition guarantee and compensation-cess mechanism; the protected compensation period ended on 30 June 2022. It belongs to GST Council/statutory history, not Article 280's constitutional core.

#### Prelims trap

NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission as a policy institution; it did not replace or absorb Article 280.

#### Mains use

Institutional comparison is a high-return method for both Prelims elimination and Mains analysis.

#### Recap

Ask: constitutional source, recurring/permanent form, output, recipient and legal effect.

### MCQ 1. Article 280 requires the President to constitute a Finance Commission:

A. every fifth year or earlier if the President considers necessary
B. every five years only after a parliamentary resolution

**Answer:** A

- **D:** State demand may influence politics but is not the constitutional trigger.

A. Chairman plus three members
B. Chairman plus four other members

**Answer:** B

- **B:** Article 280(1) fixes a total of five: one Chairman and four others.
- **C:** This would create six persons and is a standard numerical distractor.

### PYQ 1 - UPSC GS-II 2018, Q14 - 15 marks, 250 words

**Question:** How is the Finance Commission of India constituted? What do you know about the terms of reference of the recently constituted Finance Commission? Discuss.

**Demand:** constitution + question-time Fifteenth Commission terms of reference + significance/limits.

**Model solution:**  
The Finance Commission is a periodic constitutional body under Article 280. The President constitutes it every fifth year or earlier, and appoints a Chairman and four other members. Article 280(2) authorises Parliament to prescribe qualifications; the Finance Commission (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act, 1951 requires public-affairs experience for the Chairman and provides judicial, government-finance, financial-administration and economics pools for the other members.

Its permanent constitutional reference is to recommend vertical and horizontal distribution of shareable net tax proceeds, principles governing Article 275 grants, measures augmenting State Consolidated Funds for Panchayats and Municipalities on the basis of State Finance Commission recommendations, and other presidentially referred matters in the interests of sound finance.

In the question's 2018 setting, the recently constituted Fifteenth Finance Commission was also asked to examine Union-State finances, grants, local bodies, disaster management and fiscal consolidation in a changed tax and policy environment. Additional terms can widen inquiry but cannot displace Article 280's core.

Recommendations remain advisory. Yet Article 281 requires the report and an explanatory memorandum on action taken to be laid before Parliament, giving the award transparency and political weight.

### PYQ 2 - UPSC GS-II 2021, Q3 - 10 marks, 150 words

**Question:** How have the recommendations of the 14th Finance Commission of India enabled the States to improve their fiscal position?

**Model solution:**  
The Fourteenth Finance Commission improved State fiscal space principally by raising their collective share in the divisible pool from 32 to 42 per cent for 2015-20.

First, the larger formula-based share increased predictable and relatively untied resources, enabling States to align spending with local priorities. Second, States gained more directly from buoyancy in shareable Union taxes. Third, greater general-purpose funds supported State-heavy functions such as health, education, agriculture and local infrastructure. Fourth, the change accompanied a wider shift away from plan-era discretionary assistance and placed greater responsibility on States for expenditure choices.

However, gains varied with each State's horizontal share, actual Union tax collection, grants, Centrally Sponsored Schemes, own revenue and debt pressures. Thus, FC-14 strengthened autonomy and budget predictability, but sustainable fiscal improvement still required State revenue effort and accountable spending.

### PYQ 5 - UPSC GS-II 2025, Q14 - adjacent owner

**Question:** Examine the evolving pattern of Centre-State financial relations in the context of planned development in India. How far have recent reforms impacted fiscal federalism in India?

**Model solution:**  
Centre-State finance has moved from a dual plan/non-plan transfer system toward a more rule-based but still mixed architecture. During planned development, the Planning Commission shaped plan assistance and Centrally Sponsored Schemes while Finance Commissions handled tax sharing, grants and non-plan revenue gaps. Overlap increased State dependence on Union discretion.

Recent reforms altered this balance. FC-14 raised vertical devolution from 32 to 42 per cent, enlarging untied resources. The Planning Commission was replaced by NITI Aayog and the plan/non-plan expenditure distinction ended. GST created an Article 279A coordination forum but pooled substantial indirect-tax autonomy. FC-15 set 41 per cent and FC-16 retained it for 2026-31 while revising horizontal criteria and strengthening local-body, disaster and transparency measures.

The impact is substantial but incomplete. Formula-based transfers aid autonomy and equalisation; however, cesses and surcharges reduce the shareable base, conditional schemes constrain flexibility, delayed SFCs weaken local finance and off-budget liabilities obscure risk. Reform should therefore combine transparent net-proceeds data, rationalised schemes, credible debt reporting and stronger State-local devolution.

### ORIGINAL SOLVED MAINS PRACTICE - EXACTLY SIX

### Original Mains 1 - 10 marks, 150 words

**Question:** Distinguish vertical and horizontal devolution and explain why both are necessary.

**Model solution:**  
Vertical devolution divides the Article 270 divisible pool between the Union and all States collectively; horizontal devolution distributes the States' share among individual States.

The first corrects the mismatch between the Union's stronger tax base and States' service-heavy responsibilities. FC-16's accepted 41 per cent vertical share is the current award rule for 2026-31. The second addresses unequal fiscal capacity and service-delivery cost across States. FC-16 therefore uses per-capita GSDP distance, population, demographic performance, area, forest and contribution to GDP.

Vertical sharing protects aggregate State fiscal space; horizontal sharing advances equalisation while recognising scale, ecology and contribution. Yet neither percentage alone determines outcomes: the divisible-pool base, grants, own revenues, debt and expenditure pressures matter. A balanced federation therefore needs both an adequate collective share and a transparent inter se formula.

### Original Mains 2 - 10 marks, 150 words

**Question:** Why are Finance Commission recommendations advisory but politically weighty?

**Model solution:**  
Article 280 directs the Finance Commission to recommend; it does not make every proposal self-executing law. Implementation may require a Presidential order, Budget appropriation, legislation, guidelines and State compliance. Hence the recommendations are advisory.

Their political and constitutional weight is nevertheless high. The President appoints an expert body at regular intervals; Union and State governments submit evidence; the report gives public reasons for a national distribution formula. Article 281 then requires the recommendations and an explanatory memorandum on action taken to be laid before each House of Parliament.

FC-16 illustrates the distinction: the Union accepted the 41 per cent share and horizontal formula, accepted State borrowing ceilings only in principle, took note of the no-revenue-deficit-grant assessment and left other items for separate examination. Thus, non-binding advice remains difficult to ignore because deviation must be publicly justified amid recurring federal negotiations.

### Original Mains 3 - 15 marks, 250 words

**Question:** Analyse the equity-efficiency balance in the Sixteenth Finance Commission's horizontal formula.

**Model solution:**  
The Sixteenth Finance Commission distributes the States' collective share through six criteria, combining equalisation, need, cost, ecology, demographic transition and economic contribution.

Equity remains dominant. Per-capita GSDP distance carries 42.5 per cent and gives larger relative allocation to States farther below the benchmark, correcting unequal fiscal capacity. Census 2011 population has 17.5 per cent, reflecting service scale. Area has 10 per cent with a 1.5 per cent floor, partly recognising cost disability.

Efficiency and externalities are integrated rather than isolated. The 10 per cent forest variable combines weighted forest stock with forest increase in an 80:20 ratio, compensating national ecological benefits and rewarding expansion. Demographic performance, also 10 per cent, uses inverse population growth between 1971 and 2011. Contribution to GDP receives 10 per cent, but square-root transformation moderates the dominance of large economies.

Compared with FC-15, tax/fiscal effort is removed as a separate criterion and GDP contribution is introduced. This makes growth contribution visible, while income distance preserves redistribution.

The balance is contestable: output can reflect inherited advantages, while strong equalisation may weaken visible reward for effort. Yet the formula avoids either extreme. Its legitimacy depends on transparent data, stable methods and recognition that horizontal shares do not alone determine State fiscal outcomes.

### Original Mains 4 - 15 marks, 250 words

**Question:** Examine the constitutional and practical relationship between the Union Finance Commission and State Finance Commissions.

**Model solution:**  
The Union Finance Commission and State Finance Commissions form a two-level fiscal-decentralisation chain but are not hierarchical versions of one institution.

Under Article 280, the President constitutes the Union Finance Commission to recommend Union-State tax sharing, Article 275 grant principles and measures augmenting State Consolidated Funds for Panchayats and Municipalities. Articles 280(3)(bb) and (c) presently require the local-body measures to be based on State Finance Commission recommendations.

Article 243-I requires the Governor to constitute an SFC every fifth year to review Panchayat finances and recommend State-local tax sharing, assignment of revenues and grants. Article 243-Y extends this review to Municipalities. Thus, the SFC diagnoses intra-State needs, while the Union Commission augments the State-level resource envelope; the State makes allocations and onward transfers.

In practice, delayed SFCs, uneven data and weak action-taken reporting impair this chain. FC-16 responded by linking local-grant eligibility to timely SFC constitution, public accounts and a State-legislature action-taken report. It also recommended deleting the constitutional phrase requiring Union FC reliance on SFC recommendations, but no amendment was located by 8 September 2026.

Reform should retain State responsibility while improving SFC regularity, common accounting, audited data and prompt transfer. The Union Commission should supplement, not substitute for, constitutionally decentralised State-local finance.

### Original Mains 5 - 20 marks, 250 words

**Question:** Critically examine the cesses-and-surcharges debate in Indian fiscal federalism.

**Model solution:**  
Cesses and surcharges expose the difference between a devolution rate and its tax base. Article 271 permits Parliament to levy surcharges for Union purposes, while Article 270 excludes such surcharges and specific-purpose cesses from the divisible pool. States therefore receive the recommended percentage of a legally narrower base than gross tax revenue.

States argue that growing excluded levies weaken the effective value of tax devolution and reduce untied fiscal space. FC-16's data show the divisible pool averaging 89.2 per cent of gross tax revenue in the FC-13 period, 82.1 per cent in FC-14 and 78.3 per cent in FC-15. This supports a base-erosion concern.

However, the Commission rejected the stronger claim that the FC-14 increase had been almost wiped out: total Finance Commission transfers remained materially above the FC-13 period. It also noted the Union's need for targeted fiscal capacity and that cess-funded schemes can benefit States.

FC-16 did not recommend including excluded levies in the pool or imposing a numerical cap, viewing such options as constitutionally problematic. Reform should therefore begin with annual CAG-certified net-proceeds disclosure, transparent utilisation, purpose-specific accounting and sunset review. Any inclusion or cap must follow the correct constitutional route.

The balanced conclusion is that exclusions are constitutionally valid, yet their scale and duration remain legitimate questions of federal accountability.

### Original Mains 6 - 20 marks, 250 words

**Question:** Evaluate the Sixteenth Finance Commission as an instrument of cooperative fiscal federalism.

**Model solution:**  
The Sixteenth Finance Commission combines continuity in vertical sharing with significant redesign in horizontal devolution, local finance, disaster funding and fiscal transparency.

Its accepted 41 per cent State share preserves a predictable untied core for 2026-31. Horizontally, 42.5 per cent per-capita GSDP distance keeps equalisation central, while Census 2011 population, inverse 1971-2011 demographic performance, area, weighted forest and square-root-transformed GDP contribution balance need, cost, ecology and growth.

The Commission recommended no revenue-deficit, sector-specific or State-specific grants, but proposed Rs 7,91,493 crore for local bodies, Rs 2,04,401 crore for State disaster funds and Rs 79,406 crore for national disaster funds. Accepted local grants combine basic, performance, tied and untied components; later guidelines began operationalisation. It also sought annual disclosure of CAG-certified net proceeds and stronger debt transparency.

The design nevertheless raises concerns. Cesses and surcharges continue outside the pool; conditions may burden low-capacity States; delayed SFCs weaken local transfers; and several fiscal-roadmap proposals were accepted only in principle or left for separate examination. Recommendations remain advisory and implementation depends on orders, budgets, guidelines and compliance.

FC-16 is therefore a substantial cooperative-federal instrument, not a complete settlement. Its success should be judged by transparent implementation, real State autonomy, equalising outcomes, timely local transfers and honest whole-government debt accounts.

## CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

### Core constitutional identity

- Article 280: President constitutes a Finance Commission every fifth year or earlier.
- Total composition: Chairman plus four other members.
- Core job: vertical sharing, horizontal allocation, Article 275 grant principles, local-body augmentation and additional referred sound-finance matters.
- Recommendations are advisory; constitutional weight comes from expertise, reasons, recurring federal negotiation and Article 281 disclosure.

 -> horizontal formula -> State receipt

### Constitution versus 1951 Act

- Constitution: body, periodicity, total size, appointing authority and functions.
- 1951 Act: Chairman's public-affairs experience; other-member expertise pools; prejudicial-interest screen; disqualification; term/reappointment/resignation; procedure and civil-court inquiry powers.
- Trap: civil-court powers do not make the Commission a court.

### Vertical and horizontal devolution

- Vertical = Union versus all States; FC-16 accepted share = 41 per cent of the divisible pool.
- Horizontal = among twenty-eight States.
- FC-16 formula: GSDP distance 42.5; population 17.5; demographic performance 10; area 10; forest 10; GDP contribution 10.
- Demography = inverse population growth, 1971-2011; not current TFR.
- Area = 1.5 per cent floor.
- Forest = 80:20 weighted stock/increase; VDF/MDF/OF = 1.0/0.65/0.30.
- GDP contribution = square-root-transformed GSDP share.

### Grants, local bodies and disaster finance

- Devolution is a tax share; grants are separate transfers.
- Article 275 principles are recommended by FC; Article 282 remains a separate public-purpose channel.
- Tied = specified use; untied = broader choice; performance = release linked to conditions/results.
- SFC: Governor, Article 243-I; Article 243-Y municipal review.
- Union FC augments State Consolidated Funds; it does not directly allocate to every local body.
- FC-16 local grants: Rs 7,91,493 crore, 2026-31; basic:performance 80:20; half of basic tied.
