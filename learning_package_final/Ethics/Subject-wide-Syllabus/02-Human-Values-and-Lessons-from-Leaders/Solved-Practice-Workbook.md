### KEYED EXPLANATIONS AND EXAMINER TRAPS

#### MCQ 1 — A

- **A — correct.** A value is defined by two properties, endurance and trans-situationality. Only a standard that persists over time and governs choice across unrelated situations qualifies; that is precisely what distinguishes it from a mood or an attitude.
- **B — wrong.** A strong feeling about one object is the signature of an attitude, not a value. Feeling is at the base of the value ladder and is the least stable level; it can coexist with dishonest conduct in an unrelated file.
- **C — wrong.** Accurate definitional knowledge is cognitive content. Knowing what bribery is and which provision punishes it establishes information, and information has never been sufficient for conduct.
- **D — wrong.** A notified conduct rule is an external, enforceable standard sitting above the value on the ladder. It describes what the organisation requires, not what the individual holds.

> **Trap:** The stem offers an emotional report ("she has always disliked") and invites the candidate to accept it as evidence of a value. The examinable test is structural — endurance plus trans-situationality — not the intensity of the feeling reported.

#### MCQ 2 — B

- **A — wrong.** Statement 1 reverses the two terms. It is the *attitude* that is object-specific and the *value* that is trans-situational, so accepting statement 1 endorses the standard confusion this topic exists to correct.
- **B — correct.** Statement 1 is reversed and therefore false, while statement 2 is exactly the diagnostic illustration: a favourable evaluation attached to one object leaves conduct in an unrelated situation untouched, which is what "object-specific" means in practice.
- **C — wrong.** This accepts the reversal in statement 1. Both statements cannot be correct because statement 2's illustration only makes sense on the corrected definitions, where the attitude is the narrow item.
- **D — wrong.** Statement 2 is a straightforward and correct application of the value–attitude distinction, so rejecting both statements discards a true one.

> **Trap:** Two-statement items in this area routinely swap "value" and "attitude" in one clause. Read the definitional statement first and check the direction of the distinction before evaluating the illustration.

#### MCQ 25 — A

- **A — correct.** The doctrine claims that reality is many-sided and that no single standpoint captures it whole, which makes consultation an information requirement. It says nothing about the relativity of right and wrong and supplies no reason to postpone a decision indefinitely.
- **B — wrong.** The doctrine's administrative translation is precisely what the 2025 paper's "relevance in the contemporary world" demand invites, and epistemic humility about partial standpoints is directly applicable to a contested public decision.
- **C — wrong.** Syadvada is conditional predication — prefixing an assertion with *syat*, "in some respect" — and its administrative form is stating the standpoint from which a finding holds. It is a discipline of expression, not a licence to defer.
- **D — wrong.** Unanimity is not the standard for a lawful administrative decision, and a consultation without a closing date becomes a queue of vetoes that disadvantages precisely those least able to keep attending.

> **Trap:** This is the single most common error on Mahavir in GS-IV. Fix it with one sentence: many-sidedness concerns the complexity of truth-claims, not the equivalence of right and wrong.

#### Solved PYQ 1 — 2024 GS-IV, Q2(a) — 10 marks — 150 words — **directly owned**

**Question (verbatim official English wording):** "It is not enough to talk about peace, one must believe in it; and it is not enough to believe in it is one must act upon it." In the present context, the major weapon industries of the developed nations are adversely influencing continuation of number of wars for their own self-interest, all around the world. What are the ethical considerations of the powerful nations in today's international arena to stop continuation of ongoing conflicts.

**Source note:** exact English text from the locally held official PDF `books\mains\05 UPSC 2024 Paper-IV_Final 1.pdf`, question 2(a), page 2. The printed source itself carries the ungrammatical clause "and it is not enough to believe in it is one must act upon it"; the artefact is preserved rather than silently corrected.

**Ownership note.** ✅ The 2024–2025 routing ledger lists Ethics Topic 02 as the **first owner** of 2024 GS-IV Q2, with Topic 13 as co-owner for Q2(b). The stem is a **values-to-action ladder** — talk, belief, action — which is exactly this topic's translation problem applied to states rather than to officials.

**Demand decoding.** The stem supplies a three-rung ladder and an empirical premise, and then asks a single question: what are the *ethical considerations* of powerful nations. Two obligations follow. First, the ladder must be used, not merely quoted: an answer must show why belief without action is the failure the quotation names. Second, "ethical considerations" requires named principles applied to state conduct, not a survey of conflicts.

**Model answer (exam length — 147 words)**

The quotation sets a ladder: profession, conviction, action. Declaring peace costs nothing; believing it commits nothing; only acting on it surrenders an advantage. The ethical test for a powerful state is therefore whether it accepts a cost.

Four considerations follow. Non-maleficence limits arms transfers where diversion or use against civilians is foreseeable, not merely where it is proven. Consistency forbids condemning aggression selectively by alliance. Trusteeship of collective institutions requires that a permanent seat be exercised for the system rather than for the seat-holder. Transparency requires published end-use verification, since profit made invisible cannot be examined.

India's own position illustrates the standard: strategic autonomy, restraint in first use of force and support for negotiated settlement are commitments carrying real cost.

The limitation is candid. States pursue security and industrial employment, so ethical restraint must be built into enforceable export-control and verification regimes rather than left to declaration.

**Why this earns marks.** It uses the ladder analytically, names four specific ethical considerations rather than sentiments, supplies an India-centric anchor, and closes by converting the value into an institutional requirement instead of an appeal.

**Expansion bank (not part of the timed answer).** At greater length, add: Gandhi's means–ends coupling from ARC Box 2.8, since "commerce without morality" and "science without humanity" name this exact conduct; the distinction between an ethical duty and an enforceable treaty obligation; and the Kautilyan point that where an actor's gain is unobservable, disclosure regimes matter more than declarations.

**How to improve a weak attempt.** Replace any general condemnation of arms manufacturers with one named ethical principle plus the verification mechanism that would give it effect, and end on the trade-off between security interest and restraint rather than on a plea for peace.

**Model answer (exam length — 141 words)**

The proposition redefines success as a net figure. An achievement is to be judged not only by what was gained but by what was surrendered to gain it. Applied to administration, it converts a target into a question about its price.

A district that reports full sanitation coverage by excluding hamlets from the denominator, or a police station that improves its disposal rate by refusing to register complaints, has achieved a number by surrendering truthfulness and equal access. The Second ARC's own insistence that ethical behaviour lies in "adoption in action" rather than in declared standards points the same way.

Operationally, this argues for measuring what was given up: exception registers, exclusion audits, complaint-refusal records and grievance data reported alongside achievement data.

The limitation is real. Every decision surrenders something, and outcomes still matter. The test is whether what was surrendered was a value or merely a preference.

**Why this earns marks.** It converts an inspirational line into a measurement design, supplies two concrete Indian instances, proposes named instruments, and prevents the principle from becoming an argument against targets altogether.

**Expansion bank.** Add the espoused-versus-enacted diagnostic; the Kautilyan point that unobservable conduct requires disclosure design rather than exhortation; and the observation that recording refusals of improper pressure is the positive counterpart of recording exclusions.

**How to improve a weak attempt.** Replace the paraphrase of the quotation with one named performance indicator that conceals a cost, and one register that would reveal it.

#### Routed Demand 4 — 2020 GS-IV Q5(a) — 10 marks — neutral rendering — **directly owned**

**Neutral rendering of the routed demand:** Discuss the main factors responsible for gender inequality in India, and the contribution of Savitribai Phule to its removal.

**Source / ownership:** ⚠️ Neutral rendering from the 2018–2023 routing ledger; the 2020 GS-IV paper is not held locally, so no verbatim claim is made. ✅ Routed to Ethics Topic 02 as first listed owner, with Topic 13 as co-owner for Q5(b).

**Demand decoding.** **Discuss** requires two linked deliverables in one answer: a causal account of gender inequality, and a contribution that acts on that account. The link is the whole test — a biography that does not connect to the named factors answers only half.

**Model answer (exam length — 149 words)**

**How to improve a weak attempt.** Replace a tribute paragraph with the causal link — name the factor, then name the institution built against it, then state what one institution could not achieve.

#### Cross-topic anchor 1 — 2024 GS-IV, Q3(a) — 10 marks — 150 words

**Question (verbatim official English wording):** Given below are three quotations of great thinkers. What do each of these quotations convey to you in the present context. (a) "Learn everything that is good from others, but bring it in, and in your own way absorb it, do not become others." -Swami Vivekananda

**Source note:** exact English text from the locally held official PDF, question 3(a), pages 2–3.

**Model answer (exam length — 147 words)**

Vivekananda's instruction is therefore ethical self-respect joined to openness — informed adaptation, neither transplant nor refusal.

**Why this earns marks.** It converts the quotation into a decision procedure, supplies a concrete Indian illustration, and states the principle's own misuse — the qualification most answers omit.

#### Cross-topic anchor 3 — 2025 GS-IV, Q3(a) — 10 marks — 150 words

**Question (verbatim official English wording):** "Those who in trouble untroubled are, Will trouble trouble itself." – Thiruvalluvar

**Source note:** exact English text from the locally held official PDF `books\mains\UPSC Mains 2025 GS Paper 4.pdf`, question 3(a), page 2.

**Ownership note.** ⚠️ Routed to Topics 06 and 07. Cross-topic anchor; not counted as owned. ⚠️ The wording is a modern paraphrase rather than a named historic translation of **Tirukkural 623** (Chapter 63, *Iṭukkaṇ Aḻiyāmai*); G. U. Pope renders it "Who griefs confront with meek, ungrieving heart, / From them griefs, put to grief, depart."

**Model answer (exam length — 149 words)**

The couplet makes composure an active force. A calm mind does not deny danger; it prevents fear and confusion from multiplying the original problem. For a civil servant, equanimity is therefore operational rather than decorative, because accurate assessment and humane communication are hardest exactly when they matter most.

In a cloudburst or a communal rumour, an officer who stays composed verifies facts before announcing them, activates response teams in sequence, states clearly what is known and unknown, protects vulnerable groups first, and records decisions for later scrutiny. Panic spreads misinformation and invites arbitrary action; disciplined calm allows police, health workers and local representatives to be coordinated rather than merely instructed.

Equanimity has a boundary. It is not passivity, emotional coldness, or delay disguised as patience. An officer who remains serene while failing to order rescue or hear grievances has inverted the teaching. Composure must accompany urgency, not replace it.

**Why this earns marks.** It treats composure as a mechanism with named crisis behaviours, and it supplies the limitation — equanimity is not passivity — that the quotation itself does not contain.

#### Cross-topic anchor 4 — 2025 GS-IV, Q3(b) — 10 marks — 150 words

**Question (verbatim official English wording):** "The greatest discovery of my generation is that a human being can alter his life by altering his attitudes." – William James

The claim is that disposition is authored rather than inherited: what a person can do is bounded partly by how the situation is construed, and construal can be changed by deliberate effort.

Attitudes are therefore a real lever, and a bounded one.

#### Cross-topic anchor 5 — 2025 GS-IV, Q3(c) — 10 marks — 150 words

**Question (verbatim official English wording):** "The strength of a society is not in its laws, but in the morality of its people." – Swami Vivekananda

**Source note:** exact English text from the locally held official PDF, question 3(c), page 3.

**Ownership note.** ⚠️ Routed to Topics 06 and 07. Cross-topic anchor; not counted as owned. ⚠️ The line is set by UPSC as Vivekananda's but is **not traceable** to the *Complete Works*. The nearest documented statement of the theme, from "Our Duty to the Masses" (23 June 1894), is: "No country on earth has so many laws, and in no country are they so little regarded."

**Model answer (exam length — 149 words)**

The proposition holds that law is necessary but not sufficient: a statute is enforced by people, interpreted by people and obeyed largely without supervision, so its practical strength depends on the standards its society has internalised.

The claim has real administrative force. The Second ARC concedes that rules "cannot provide for all situations" and that discretion widens with seniority, and its Preface states that "one cannot mandate honesty". Where a value is absent, enforcement must observe everything — which is impossible and expensive.

But the proposition, taken strictly, understates the same report's diagnosis. It locates official corruption in opportunity, monopoly and unobservability rather than in popular morality, and it warns against an overemphasis on values that assumes nothing can be done until values are restored.

The defensible synthesis is reciprocal: law without internalised morality is under-enforced because enforcement itself needs honest enforcers, while morality without law is unenforceable at scale.

**Why this earns marks.** It accepts the proposition's force, then tests it against the primary report's own structural diagnosis, and closes with a reciprocal synthesis rather than an endorsement.

#### Cross-topic anchor 6 — 2025 GS-IV, Q4(b) — 10 marks — 150 words

**Question (verbatim official English wording):** What are the major teachings of Mahavir? Explain their relevance in the contemporary world.

**Source note:** exact English text from the locally held official PDF, question 4(b), page 3.

**Ownership note.** ⚠️ Routed to Topics 04 and 06. Cross-topic anchor; not counted as owned. Doctrinal depth belongs to Topic 06 and to the Philosophy Jainism file; this topic's contribution is the value-to-administration translation.

**Model answer (exam length — 144 words)**

Mahavir's teachings comprise five great vows and two doctrines about truth. The vows are ahimsa, non-injury; satya, truthfulness; asteya, non-stealing; brahmacharya, restraint; and aparigraha, non-possessiveness. The doctrines are anekantavada, the many-sidedness of reality, and syadvada, conditional predication, which prefixes an assertion with *syat*, "from a standpoint".

Their contemporary relevance is specific. Ahimsa supplies a proportionality test in enforcement and eviction. Satya requires accurate reporting rather than favourable figures. Aparigraha is the ethical core of probity, since office is held in trust and not owned, which is why conflict-of-interest and anti-nepotism rules exist. Anekantavada makes multi-stakeholder consultation an information requirement rather than a formality. Syadvada disciplines official note-writing: state the standpoint from which a finding holds.

One precision point and one caution. Jain tradition contrasts Parshvanatha's four restraints with Mahavir's five. And anekantavada is epistemic humility, not moral relativism or a licence to defer decisions.

**Why this earns marks.** It answers both halves, translates each teaching into a named administrative practice rather than a sentiment, and closes with the precision point and the standard trap.

### ORIGINAL MAINS PRACTICE WITH MODEL SOLUTIONS

**Format note.** ✅ The 10-mark items below follow the verified GS-IV Section-A format (150 words). ✅ The 20-mark items follow the verified GS-IV Section-B format, which is a **case study** (250 words). ⚠️ The 15-mark items are **extended analytical practice and are not a GS-IV paper format**; they are retained to satisfy the repository's 10/15/20 requirement and to build depth.

#### Original Mains Practice 1 — 10 marks — 150 words

**Question:** "Values are inculcated, not instructed." Explain the mechanisms through which family, society and educational institutions actually transmit values, and identify the failure mode of each. (Answer in 150 words)

**Model answer (146 words)**

Transmission works through five mechanisms, of which explicit instruction is the weakest alone. Modelling transmits by observed conduct: a child copies how a scarce resource is divided at home, and a junior copies what a senior actually does. Reinforcement transmits by what is rewarded. Identification transmits through belonging — the Second ARC notes that where office brings prestige, dismissal "means disgrace for the entire family", giving relatives an incentive to restrain the officer. Instruction transmits content. Reflective revision transmits by testing inherited standards against experience.

Each has a failure mode. The family models acquisitiveness and produces nepotism. Society transmits resignation; the ARC records corruption being regarded as "inevitable" and resistance as "futile". Schools reproduce hierarchy through the hidden curriculum whatever the syllabus prints. Instruction fails when taught as recall.

Diagnosis therefore precedes remedy: name the failing mechanism, because a modelling failure is not cured by a curriculum.

**Why this earns marks.** It names five mechanisms with a primary-source anchor for the strongest, attaches a specific failure mode to each institution, and closes with the diagnostic rule rather than a general appeal.

#### Original Mains Practice 2 — 10 marks — 150 words

**Question:** Examine the proposition that the Fundamental Duties in Article 51A are an effective instrument for inculcating values in Indian citizens. (Answer in 150 words)

**Model answer (147 words)**

Article 51A, inserted by the 42nd Amendment in 1976 on the Swaran Singh Committee's recommendation and extended to eleven duties by the 86th Amendment in 2002, is a genuine value instrument in one respect and a weak one in another.

Its strength is declaratory and educative. It converts contested standards into a public text: 51A(b) requires citizens to cherish the ideals of the freedom struggle, 51A(e) to renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women, and 51A(h) to develop scientific temper. Indira Gandhi's stated justification was to make people conscious of duties as they are of rights.

Its weakness is enforceability. The duties are non-justiciable, carry no legal sanction, and Parliament expressly declined the Committee's proposals for a penalty and for immunity of such a penalty law from challenge.

The verdict is therefore bounded: effective as declared standard and interpretive aid, ineffective as command.

**Why this earns marks.** It supplies exact amendment history, cites three duties with their content, uses the rejected penalty clause as proof of design intent, and reaches a bounded verdict on both limbs.

#### Original Mains Practice 3 — 10 marks — 150 words

**Question:** "A quotation is worth writing in a GS-IV answer only when it changes what an officer is required to do." Justify this statement with suitable examples. (Answer in 150 words)

**Model answer (143 words)**

The statement is justified because the examinable skill is translation, not recall. A teaching that leaves an officer's obligations unchanged has added no information to the answer.

Two examples show the difference. Gandhi's "commerce without morality" changes an obligation: it directs a procurement officer facing under-invoicing pressure to record the pressure on file and refer the deviation to the tender committee, converting restraint into an auditable act. Kautilya's five propositions, as the Second ARC quotes them, change a design obligation: disgorgement and reassignment for proven dishonesty, but published minimum tenure as a reward for integrity.

By contrast, an answer that merely asserts that great leaders valued honesty leaves every procedure exactly where it was.

The qualification is that translation must be honest. Where a quotation's provenance is untraceable, the idea should be answered with a clause of caution, never with an invented citation.

**Why this earns marks.** It defends the proposition with two contrasting worked examples, names the resulting obligations precisely, and closes with the attribution discipline that the practice itself requires.

#### Original Mains Practice 4 — 15 marks — extended analytical practice (not a GS-IV paper format) — 250 words

**Question:** Compare Gandhi's character-centred account of ethical failure with Kautilya's opportunity-centred account, as both are recorded in the Second ARC's Fourth Report. What balanced integrity design follows for a district administration? (Answer in about 250 words)

**Model answer (250 words)**

The Second ARC records both registers and uses both. Gandhi's, at Box 2.8, presents the Seven Social Sins — politics without principles through to worship without sacrifice — one fault repeated seven times: a legitimate end pursued after its ethical means has been detached. Kautilya's, in Annexure-I(2), presents five propositions: custody makes tasting inevitable, appropriation is as unobservable as a fish drinking water, intent is more concealed than the path of birds, wrongful gains must be disgorged and the officer changed in his works, and those who increase wealth in just ways must be made permanent in office.

The comparison is not of quality but of domain. Gandhi addresses conduct where observation cannot reach — the residue the Commission concedes at paragraph 2.2.4 when it says rules "cannot provide for all situations" and that discretion widens with seniority. Kautilya addresses the observable structure that determines how large that residue is.

Each fails alone. Character-first reasoning drifts into the "overemphasis on values and character" the Commission warns against at paragraph 1.4, whose assumption is that nothing can be done until values are restored. Design-first reasoning drifts into surveillance that demoralises honest officers and induces decision-avoidance.

A balanced district design follows. Shrink the residue: publish eligibility criteria, auto-allot files, timestamp stages, and end single-officer monopoly on discretionary decisions. Fill it: require recorded reasons for every deviation, and grade their quality in appraisal. Reward integrity through published minimum-tenure norms, and make one senior recusal visible each year, since modelling transmits fastest.

**Why this earns marks.** It reproduces both accounts accurately from the primary report, resolves the comparison by domain rather than by preference, names each register's failure mode, and converts the synthesis into four concrete district-level instruments.

#### Original Mains Practice 5 — 15 marks — extended analytical practice (not a GS-IV paper format) — 250 words

**Question:** Critically examine the claim that exemplar-based ethics — teaching values through the lives of great leaders — is an unreliable foundation for public administration. (Answer in about 250 words)

**Model answer (249 words)**

The claim has real force. Exemplar reasoning fails in six identifiable ways. It becomes a quotation scrapbook, in which sayings replace rules. It permits selective quotation, since a substantial corpus usually supports opposite administrative choices. It invites anachronism, as when Kautilya is credited with designing a vigilance commission rather than diagnosing an incentive problem. It slides into hero-worship, treating admired character as a warrant for any inferred position. It enables moral substitution — the Second ARC itself warns at paragraph 1.4 against an overemphasis on values and character whose implicit assumption is that "until values are restored, nothing much can be done", a counsel of postponement. And it tempts fabricated provenance, since several lines UPSC has set, including those attributed to Sardar Patel, William James and Justice Potter Stewart, cannot be traced to a primary source.

But the claim overstates. Exemplars do work that neither codes nor sanctions can do. They make a standard memorable under time pressure. They make it credible, by demonstrating that a real person kept it under pressure — something no notification can establish. And they extend moral imagination beyond the current rulebook. The Constitution recognises this at Article 51A(b), which makes cherishing the ideals of the freedom struggle a citizen duty.

The reasoned verdict is bounded rather than dismissive. Exemplars are inputs to conduct, not guarantees of it. They earn their place when each is carried through to a named administrative rule with a stated limitation, and lose it the moment admiration replaces mechanism.

**Why this earns marks.** It takes the critical side seriously with six named failure modes and a primary-source warning, then supplies the counter-case with a constitutional anchor, and ends on a bounded verdict rather than on either dismissal or celebration.

#### Original Mains Practice 6 — 20 marks — GS-IV Section-B case study — 250 words

**Case:** You are the Deputy Commissioner of a district. A large private school, run by a trust whose chairperson is a politically influential person, has been sanctioned land at a concessional rate on condition that it reserves a quarter of its seats for children from disadvantaged families. An inspection you ordered finds that these children are seated in a separate section, taught by the least experienced staff, and required to eat the mid-day meal after the fee-paying children have finished. The trust's records are formally compliant: the quota is filled and no rule expressly prohibits sectioning. The chairperson tells you, politely, that any adverse finding will be challenged and that your predecessor "understood the practical difficulties". A parents' group has approached you but is afraid of losing seats. The district education officer advises that a show-cause notice would be legally fragile.

**(a)** Identify the stakeholders and the values in conflict. **(b)** Evaluate at least three options. **(c)** State your decision, its implementation, and the residual risk. (Answer in 250 words)

**Model answer (248 words)**

**Stakeholders and values.** The quota children and their parents hold claims to equal dignity and non-discrimination; fee-paying parents hold an interest in continuity; the trust holds a legitimate expectation of lawful process; the teachers are subject to allocation decisions; and the public holds an interest in enforcement of a concession condition. The conflict is between formal compliance and substantive equality, and between avoiding legal fragility and refusing to normalise segregation.

**Options.** First, accept the record. It is safe and wrong: it treats the quota as a headcount and permits the hidden curriculum to teach hierarchy daily, which is precisely how a school transmits values. Second, issue an immediate show-cause notice. It is principled but, as advised, legally fragile on the sectioning point, and it exposes the complaining parents. Third, act on the enforceable instrument. The concession was granted on a condition; conditions are enforceable on their own terms, and the meal and staffing practices are documentable facts.

**Decision.** Adopt the third option, with the first two elements of the second. Record the inspection findings with photographs and timings. Issue a notice under the land-concession condition, not under a general moral standard. Direct integrated sections and a common meal sitting from the next term, with staffing allocation returned by lot. Protect the parents by acting on the inspection record rather than on their complaint.

**Residual risk.** Litigation, and pressure on my posting. Mitigation: a speaking order, a copy to the sanctioning department, and a compliance review in ninety days.

**Why this earns marks.** It names stakeholders and values precisely, evaluates three genuinely distinct options including the tempting safe one, selects the enforceable instrument rather than the moral one, protects the vulnerable complainant, and states an honest residual risk with mitigation.

#### Original Mains Practice 7 — 20 marks — GS-IV Section-B case study — 250 words

**Case:** You head a training academy for a state civil service. Your predecessor introduced a well-regarded ethics module, and completion rates are near total. Yet a confidential survey of officers three years after training reports that recorded refusals of improper pressure are almost non-existent, that the appraisal counts only disposal volume, and that two of the academy's own senior faculty have relatives who regularly clear examinations they help set. A junior faculty member brings you this survey and asks that it be suppressed, fearing that publishing it will end the module's funding. The state government has asked you for a note on "strengthening value education" ahead of a budget decision.

**(a)** What are the ethical issues? **(b)** What options are available and what would each achieve? **(c)** What will you do, and what safeguards will you build? (Answer in 250 words)

**Model answer (249 words)**

**Ethical issues.** Three, and they are distinct. First, an integrity issue inside the academy: faculty relatives clearing examinations the faculty help set is a conflict of interest regardless of outcome. Second, an honesty issue about the survey: suppressing inconvenient evidence to protect a programme is the reasoning the programme exists to prevent. Third, a design issue: near-total completion alongside near-zero recorded refusals shows a value taught at the formation step and contradicted at the reinforcement step.

**Options.** Suppress and seek funding. It protects the module and destroys the academy's standing to teach integrity; it also models concealment to every officer who later learns of it. Publish and blame the service. It is honest but shifts responsibility away from the institution that trained them. Publish with a remedy attached. It uses the survey as the evidence base for the note the government has asked for.

**Decision.** Take the third. Send the survey to government as an annexure to the note. Recommend one appraisal change — grade the quality of recorded reasoning in discretionary decisions, audited on a sample — because conduct is repeated when it is rewarded, and no training budget defeats an appraisal form. Simultaneously order faculty recusal from any examination involving a relative, publish the recusal register, and record it as the academy's first application of the module.

**Safeguards and residual risk.** Protect the junior faculty member by attributing the survey institutionally. Risk: funding may still be cut. Mitigation: present the recusal reform as evidence that the academy corrects itself.

**Why this earns marks.** It separates three genuinely different ethical issues, refuses the tempting protective option with a stated reason, converts the diagnosis into one high-leverage institutional reform, protects the person who raised the matter, and concedes the residual risk with a mitigation.
