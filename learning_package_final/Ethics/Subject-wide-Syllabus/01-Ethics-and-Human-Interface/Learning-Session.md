### SESSION 1 — The Essence: What Ethics *Is* in Human Action

**Session 1 of 17 · Stage: Foundation · Syllabus limb: essence of ethics in human actions**

#### VISUAL FIRST


*What the visual shows:* the ARC's definition has two halves. The first half is the standard; the second half is the machinery that converts a standard into conduct. Candidates who write only the first half write a dictionary entry, not an ethics answer.

#### Core teaching

✅ **Definition (ARC 1.1, verbatim sense):** "Ethics is a set of standards that society places on itself and which helps guide behaviour, choices and actions." The Commission immediately adds that it "is painfully aware that standards do not, by themselves, ensure ethical behaviour; that requires a robust culture of integrity."

✅ **The crux, in the report's own words:** "The crux of ethical behaviour does not lie in bold words and expressions enshrined as standards, but in their adoption in action, in sanctions against their violations, in putting in place competent disciplinary bodies to investigate allegations of violations and impose sanctions quickly and in promoting a culture of integrity."

✅ **Etymology (ARC 1.2):** "ethics" comes from the Greek *ethikos*, "arising from habit". "Corrupt" comes from the Latin *corruptus*, "to break or destroy". The ARC then makes a bitter play on the etymology: "It is unfortunate that corruption has, for many, become a matter of habit."

⚠️ **Why the etymology is analytically useful, not decorative:** if the essence of ethics is *habit*, then ethical conduct is a disposition built by repetition inside an institution, and a one-off training module or a printed code cannot produce it. This is the bridge from the definition to the ARC's later institutional argument.

✅ **Scale of corruption (ARC 1.2):** the report distinguishes "grand corruption involving persons in high places" from "retail corruption touching the everyday life of common people". Retail corruption is precisely the human interface this topic is about.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> Ethics is not the language a society uses about itself but the conduct it is able to reproduce under pressure; the Second ARC therefore defines it as standards a society places on itself, and then warns that standards alone "do not ensure ethical behaviour".

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

`standards society places on itself` · `guide behaviour, choices and actions` · `robust culture of integrity` · `adoption in action, not bold words` · `sanctions against violations` · `competent disciplinary bodies` · `ethikos = arising from habit` · `corruptus = to break or destroy` · `grand corruption vs retail corruption`

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH: claim -> named evidence -> analysis -> qualification

**Claim:** The essence of ethics lies in enforced habit, not in declared standards.
**Named evidence:** The Second ARC's Fourth Report, *Ethics in Governance* (January 2007), defines ethics at paragraph 1.1 as standards a society places on itself, and in the same paragraph states that the "crux of ethical behaviour does not lie in bold words... but in their adoption in action, in sanctions against their violations" and in "competent disciplinary bodies".
**Analysis:** The definition is therefore two-part — a normative half and an enforcement half — and each half fails without the other: a code without sanction becomes decoration, while sanction without an internalised standard becomes arbitrary punishment.
**Qualification:** The ARC nevertheless does not reduce ethics to deterrence; the same paragraph requires "promoting a culture of integrity", which is a slower, socialising process that no disciplinary body can deliver by itself.

#### ADMINISTRATIVE APPLICATION IN INDIA

⚠️ A department that publishes an integrity pledge, but has no timeline for disciplinary inquiries and no record of a single sanction, satisfies only the first half of ARC 1.1. The measurable test of "adoption in action" is the disposal time of disciplinary cases, not the number of pledges signed.

#### UPSC TRAP

❌ Writing "ethics means moral values" as the definition. → The examiner is testing whether you know that the ARC's definition is *social* (standards a society places on itself) and *operational* (adoption, sanction, machinery, culture). "Moral values" is the vaguest available answer.

#### RECAP

Ethics = society's own standards + adoption in action + sanction + competent machinery + culture of integrity. Corruption is the failure of that whole package, not merely of personal character.

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH: claim -> named evidence -> analysis -> qualification

**Claim:** India's ethics-in-governance framework is a post-liberalisation, treaty-era construction rather than an ancient inheritance.
**Named evidence:** The 2nd ARC's Fourth Report (January 2007) itself cites, at paragraph 1.14, the UNCAC adopted by the UN General Assembly in October 2003, the ADB-OECD Anti-Corruption Action Plan signed by the Government of India, the World Bank's refusal to fund tainted projects, and the 2006 IMF–World Bank Singapore joint statement; at 1.15 it names the Right to Information Act as recently enacted.
**Analysis:** The clustering of these instruments within roughly five years shows that the Indian framework was consolidated under simultaneous external treaty pressure and internal transparency demand, not by gradual moral evolution.
**Qualification:** The report's own status statements are dated: India had signed but not ratified UNCAC when the report was written, and ratification followed only on 9 May 2011, so the report must be quoted as a 2007 vantage point rather than as current treaty status.

#### ADMINISTRATIVE APPLICATION IN INDIA

⚠️ In an answer on "why do we still need ethics reform after so many laws", the timeline itself is the argument: the instruments arrived in a compressed decade, while the ARC's diagnosed structural causes (Session 7) are of much longer standing, so legal supply outran cultural absorption.

#### UPSC TRAP

❌ Writing that India "ratified UNCAC in 2005" or that India is a party to the OECD Anti-Bribery Convention. → India **signed** UNCAC on 9 December 2005 and **ratified** it on 9 May 2011; India is **not** a party to the OECD Anti-Bribery Convention, 1997. The ARC's "signed" wording is a 2007 statement.

#### RECAP

Two clocks: ancient words, recent institutions. Anchor every institutional claim to 2001–2011, and anchor the report itself to January 2007 with nine chapters and three terms of reference, of which one was deferred.

*What the visual shows:* the four levels are not four topics but four different questions about the same act. GS-IV lives in the normative and applied rows; the descriptive row supplies facts and the meta-ethical row supplies at most a sentence.

#### Core teaching

⚠️ **Descriptive ethics** gives an empirical account of what a society or group actually believes, approves or does — for example a survey of officials' attitudes to gift-taking. It reports; it neither justifies nor excuses.

⚠️ **Normative ethics** asks what one *ought* to do and argues the answer from a standard — duty, consequence, virtue, justice or care. It is developed as a subject in Topic 08; here you need only the fact that a normative claim requires a ground.

⚠️ **Meta-ethics** examines what moral words mean and whether moral judgments can be true — objectivism versus relativism, fact versus attitude. It is conceptually genuine but, in a 150-word GS-IV answer, worth at most one sentence.

⚠️ **Applied ethics** works a normative theory out for a specific field: administrative ethics, medical ethics, environmental ethics, AI ethics, business ethics. This is where GS-IV mostly sits, and it is why the paper is full of officers, hospitals, contractors and clearances rather than abstract puzzles.

⚠️ **The load-bearing move — the is/ought gap.** The single most useful thing this fourfold does in an answer is to block the inference from "this is the practice here" to "this is therefore acceptable". Naming the level converts a vague moral protest into a precise objection: *you have offered me a descriptive premise for a normative conclusion.*

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> Every ethical dispute in administration begins with a category error: a descriptive fact about what is customary is offered as a normative reason for what is right, and the first duty of an ethical officer is to refuse that substitution.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

`descriptive ethics` · `normative ethics` · `meta-ethics` · `applied ethics` · `is-ought gap` · `social fact is not a justification` · `administrative ethics as applied ethics` · `GS-IV is normative and applied`

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH: claim -> named evidence -> analysis -> qualification

**Claim:** Naming the level of ethical enquiry is not academic housekeeping; it decides whether an argument is valid.
**Named evidence:** When a state training institute reports that field staff regard small facilitation payments as normal, the report is descriptive ethics — an accurate account of belief; the ARC's own paragraph 1.2, which observes that "corruption has, for many, become a matter of habit", is the same kind of descriptive observation.
**Analysis:** The ARC does not treat that habituation as a defence; it treats it as the problem to be corrected, which is precisely the refusal to slide from description to justification.
**Qualification:** Descriptive findings are still indispensable, because a corrective designed without knowing what officials actually believe will misfire; the rule is that description sets the agenda while normative argument sets the standard.

#### ADMINISTRATIVE APPLICATION IN INDIA

⚠️ A district team that surveys customary festival-gift practice and then asks whether procurement officers may accept such gifts has run two enquiries. The survey answers a descriptive question; the procurement question is applied-normative and must be answered from duty, conflict-of-interest and public-confidence grounds, not from the survey.

#### UPSC TRAP

❌ Treating meta-ethics as the "highest" and therefore most impressive level. → In GS-IV it is the least rewarded. A candidate who spends 40 words on moral realism in a 150-word applied question has mispriced the paper.

#### RECAP

Four questions, one act: what is believed, what ought to be done, what moral words mean, what this field must do. Never let the first answer the second.

### SESSION 4 — Dimensions of Ethics II: The Six Working Dimensions of a Public Decision

**Session 4 of 17 · Stage: Core · Syllabus limb: dimensions of ethics · Directly serves the owned 2024 GS-IV Q1(b)**

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
              ONE ADMINISTRATIVE DECISION, SIX ETHICAL DIMENSIONS
              ===================================================
        (a District Officer finalising a welfare beneficiary list)

   DIMENSION            THE QUESTION IT FORCES           WHAT IT BLOCKS
   ---------            ----------------------           --------------
1. DUTY / RIGHTS   ->   "Is this person being treated  -> using a citizen as
                         as an end, with an entitlement?"  a means or a favour
2. CONSEQUENCES    ->   "Who is harmed, how much,      -> good intention with
                         how certainly?"                  foreseeable damage
3. JUSTICE /       ->   "Are like cases treated        -> silent, unexplained
   FAIRNESS             alike; is exclusion reasoned?"    discrimination
4. VIRTUE /        ->   "What does this decision       -> situational courage,
   CHARACTER            require me to become?"            selective honesty
5. CARE /          ->   "What does the most            -> procedural correctness
   EMPATHY              vulnerable claimant need?"        that is humanly blind
6. ACCOUNTABILITY  ->   "Can I state my reasons in     -> decisions that only
   / TRANSPARENCY       public and be reviewed?"          survive in secrecy

   +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
   |  NO SINGLE DIMENSION DECIDES.  Rigid duty ignores hardship;        |
   |  outcome-only reasoning rationalises unfair means; care alone      |
   |  becomes favouritism.  The dimensions are CHECKS ON EACH OTHER.    |
   +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

*What the visual shows:* the six dimensions are not a list to be recited but a sequence of filters applied to one decision, each catching a failure the others miss.

#### Core teaching

⚠️ The examiner's phrase "dimensions of ethics" is the syllabus's own wording and was set directly in 2024. It asks for the **respects in which an action can be ethically evaluated**, not for a list of virtues. Six dimensions carry the whole GS-IV paper:

1. **Duty and rights** — the deontological dimension. The citizen holds an entitlement; the officer holds an obligation. This dimension is the reason a beneficiary cannot be treated as an object of discretionary kindness.
2. **Consequences and harm** — the teleological dimension. It asks who bears the loss, how severe it is and how foreseeable it was. It is the dimension that makes "I meant well" insufficient.
3. **Justice and fairness** — the distributive dimension. Like cases alike; unequal treatment must be reasoned and recorded.
4. **Virtue and character** — the agent-centred dimension: integrity, courage, impartiality, self-restraint. It asks what a decision habituates the officer into becoming.
5. **Care and empathy** — the relational dimension: attention to the specific vulnerable claimant rather than to the average applicant.
6. **Accountability and transparency** — the public-reason dimension: reasons capable of surviving disclosure, appeal and audit.

⚠️ **The synthesis you must write:** no dimension is self-sufficient. Rigid rule-following can ignore an exceptional hardship; outcome-only reasoning can rationalise unfair means; care without fairness becomes favouritism; transparency without care becomes bureaucratic exposure of the weak. The examinable position is a *reasoned plural* one: apply all six, name the conflict, and justify the trade-off.

✅ **Why this belongs to Topic 01 and not to Topic 08:** Topic 08 owns the internal architecture of deontology, consequentialism and virtue ethics as *theories*. Topic 01 owns the prior question the syllabus actually prints — the *dimensions* along which any human action, in any field, can be ethically appraised.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> A public decision is not ethical because it satisfies one dimension well; it is ethical because it survives duty, consequence, fairness, character, care and public accountability simultaneously — and where they conflict, because the officer named the conflict instead of hiding it.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

`duty and rights` · `consequences and harm` · `justice and fairness` · `virtue and character` · `care and empathy` · `accountability and transparency` · `reasoned plural verdict` · `treat the citizen as an end, not a means` · `reasons capable of public scrutiny`

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH: claim -> named evidence -> analysis -> qualification

**Claim:** The dimensions of ethics function as mutual checks, so a decision defended on a single dimension is usually the least defensible.
**Named evidence:** A District Officer finalising a beneficiary list who applies published eligibility criteria, hears objections, protects personal data and records reasons for inclusion and exclusion has satisfied duty, fairness and accountability together; a compassionate but unrecorded insertion of a favoured applicant satisfies only care.
**Analysis:** The unrecorded insertion fails precisely where care is strongest, because it removes the reviewability that protects every other claimant with an equally sympathetic story.
**Qualification:** This does not make compassion illegitimate; it requires compassion to travel through a lawful route — a documented hardship provision, a reasoned relaxation, or a referral — so that it can be extended to like cases rather than to chosen ones.

#### ADMINISTRATIVE APPLICATION IN INDIA

⚠️ Apply the six-dimension filter to a routine file: a transfer request, a licence renewal, a tender evaluation. The dimension that fails first is usually accountability — the decision is defensible but its reasons were never written down, which converts a good decision into an unverifiable one.

#### UPSC TRAP

❌ Answering "dimensions of ethics" with a list of values (honesty, integrity, punctuality). → Values are *contents*; dimensions are *respects of evaluation*. The 2024 question asked how these dimensions "shape ethical decision-making in the professional context", which requires the filter, not the list.

#### RECAP

Six dimensions, one decision: duty, consequence, fairness, character, care, accountability. Name the conflict; justify the trade-off; record the reasons.

```text
     "...those who, under the guise of friendship, accept favors, which offend
      the spirit of the law but do not violate its letter"
     "...among so many influential people, morality has become identical with
      legality. We are certainly in a tragic plight if the accepted standard by
      which we measure the integrity of a man in public life is that he keep
      within the letter of the law."
     Source: Senate Ethics Manual, 2003 edition, p.6, cited in ARC 4th Report Box 2.3
```

*What the visual shows:* six terms that ordinary speech merges, separated by the distinct test each applies, plus the four-case table that lets you answer "is legal always ethical?" in one line.

#### Core teaching

⚠️ **Ethics vs morality.** Ethics is the *systematic, reasoned* study or standard of right conduct; morality is the set of beliefs and practices *actually held* by a person or society. The two are used interchangeably in ordinary Mains prose, and that is acceptable — but the moment a question says "distinguish", the distinction must be available on demand.

⚠️ **Values** are enduring evaluative commitments that orient conduct before any rule is consulted. They are the "guiding stars" the ARC speaks of at 1.5.

⚠️ **Law** is publicly enacted and enforceable. Its jurisdiction is conduct, not motive; its instrument is sanction, not persuasion.

⚠️ **Propriety** is conformity to what the office and the occasion make *appropriate*. It is the test that catches conduct which breaks no rule but damages the credibility of the office. Accepting a lavish private dinner from a bidder may involve no bribe and violate no provision, yet fail propriety because of how it appears to a reasonable citizen.

⚠️ **Conscience** is reflective inner moral judgment. It is indispensable as a check on the self, and insufficient as a public justification: conscience cannot by assertion cancel a valid law, and an officer who invokes it must accept the consequences that Topic 10 examines.

✅ **The report's own authority for the legality/propriety gap.** ARC Box 2.3, "Need for Ethical Code", quotes Senator Fulbright asking "What should be done about men who do not directly and blatantly sell the favors of their offices for money and so place themselves within the penalties of the law? How do we deal with those who, under the guise of friendship, accept favors, which offend the spirit of the law but do not violate its letter?" and warning that "morality has become identical with legality... a tragic plight if the accepted standard by which we measure the integrity of a man in public life is that he keep within the letter of the law." The cited source is the Senate Ethics Manual, 2003 edition, page 6.

⚠️ **Boundary note:** Box 2.3 sits in the ARC's Chapter 2 and is the anchor of Topic 10 (sources of ethical guidance). It is used here only to establish the legality/morality/propriety separation, which Topic 01 owns as part of the essence of ethics.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> Legality is the floor of public conduct and propriety is its ceiling; the space between them is where reputations, and public trust, are actually lost — which is why the Second ARC quotes the warning that "morality has become identical with legality".

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

`ethics = reasoned standard` · `morality = held belief and practice` · `values = enduring evaluative commitments` · `law = enacted, enforceable` · `propriety = fitness to office and occasion` · `conscience = reflective inner judgment` · `spirit vs letter of the law` · `ARC Box 2.3, Senate Ethics Manual 2003` · `legality is the floor, not the ceiling`

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH: claim -> named evidence -> analysis -> qualification

**Claim:** Legal compliance is a necessary but insufficient standard for public office, because propriety applies a test law cannot.
**Named evidence:** Box 2.3 of the Second ARC's Fourth Report reproduces Senator Fulbright's question about those who "under the guise of friendship, accept favors, which offend the spirit of the law but do not violate its letter", sourced to the Senate Ethics Manual, 2003 edition, page 6.
**Analysis:** The passage identifies a class of conduct that is unreachable by penal law yet corrosive of confidence, because the injury is to the *appearance* of impartiality, and appearance is what makes an impartial decision believable to the citizen who lost.
**Qualification:** Propriety must not become vague moral policing of private life; it applies where there is a nexus with the office, entrusted power or public perception, and its remedies are disclosure, recusal and avoidance rather than punishment.

#### ADMINISTRATIVE APPLICATION IN INDIA

⚠️ A procurement officer invited to a bidder's private dinner should record the invitation, decline or disclose it, and recuse where the nexus is direct. The correct administrative instrument is a conflict-of-interest disclosure entry, not a self-certification of good intentions.

#### UPSC TRAP

❌ "If it is legal, no ethical question arises." → Three separate tests exist. ARC Box 2.3 exists precisely to reject the equation of morality with legality, and the examiner sets this as a distractor almost every year.

#### RECAP

Six words, six tests. Legal is not the same as moral, and moral is not the same as proper. Where they diverge, name which test the conduct fails and prescribe the matching remedy — sanction for illegality, argument for immorality, disclosure and recusal for impropriety.

#### Core teaching

⚠️ **The individual determinants**, in the order in which they act on a person:

- **Conscience and reflective judgment** — the internal reviewer that makes an officer uncomfortable before, not after, the audit.
- **Family and early socialisation** — the first site of habituation; ethics as *ethikos* begins here.
- **Education** — not moral instruction alone but the trained capacity to give and take reasons. This is what makes Session 7 necessary and prevents Topic 01 from becoming a moral sermon.

📰 **Live instrument:** Mission Karmayogi's stated design — competency-based capacity building covering behavioural as well as functional competencies, and a rules-based to roles-based shift — is an explicit attempt to act on the individual determinant of professional culture at national scale, with 1.49 crore users reported by PIB on 6 February 2026.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> India's ethical deficit is rarely a deficit of values; the Second ARC records that "a sense of right and wrong is intrinsic to our culture and civilization", which means the operative question is not whether officials know what is right but what it costs them to do it.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

`conscience` · `family and early socialisation` · `civil education` · `values exist in abundance (ARC 1.5)` · `peer and professional culture` · `incentive structure` · `good behaviour rewarded / bad behaviour punished (ARC 1.4)` · `behavioural competencies` · `rules-based to roles-based`

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH: claim -> named evidence -> analysis -> qualification

**Claim:** Individual determinants explain the *capacity* for ethical action, not its *frequency*.
**Named evidence:** The Second ARC states at paragraph 1.5 that values "exist in abundance in our society" and that "a sense of right and wrong is intrinsic to our culture and civilization", while at 1.4 it describes how conduct shifts when good behaviour is "fraught with difficulties" and bad behaviour is "extravagantly rewarded".
**Analysis:** Read together, these two paragraphs locate the problem not in a shortage of moral knowledge but in a reward structure that taxes integrity, which is why exhortation-only reform reliably underperforms.
**Qualification:** The inference must not be pushed into determinism: the same paragraph insists that values are needed "to serve as guiding stars", so incentive redesign without value formation produces compliance that collapses the moment surveillance lapses.

#### ADMINISTRATIVE APPLICATION IN INDIA

⚠️ An honest officer who is transferred after refusing an irregular sanction, while a pliant colleague receives a coveted posting, teaches the entire cadre a lesson no training module can undo. The ethical intervention is posting-and-transfer policy, not another workshop.

#### UPSC TRAP

❌ Reducing the "determinants of ethics" limb of the syllabus to family and education alone. → The syllabus word is *determinants*, and the ARC's own analysis is overwhelmingly institutional. An answer that stops at upbringing has answered half a question.

#### RECAP

Person, family, education, culture, peers, incentives. Values are abundant; the scarce good is a structure in which acting on them is not punished.

*What the visual shows:* one risk-creating bundle, three Indian aggravators and two general multipliers. This is the single most quotable causal chain in the whole topic.

#### Core teaching

✅ **The bundle (ARC 1.5).** Public servants — "elected or appointed" — are "endowed with authority to make decisions and impact on human lives and exercising the power to determine allocation of resources". "Public office and control over public purse offer enormous temptation and opportunity to promote private gain at public cost." The ARC's conclusion from this is institutional: "creation of institutions and designing of incentives are of utmost importance in promoting ethical conduct of public servants."

✅ **Aggravator 1 — colonial legacy (ARC 1.6).** "There is a colonial legacy of unchallenged authority and propensity to exercise power arbitrarily. In a society which worships power, it is easy for public officials to deviate from ethical conduct."

✅ **Aggravator 2 — asymmetry of power (ARC 1.6).** Nearly 90% of people are in the unorganized sector, "quite a number of them lead a precarious existence, depending on subsistence wages with no job security", while "nearly 70% of the organized workers with job security and regular monthly wage are employed by the state directly or through public sector undertakings". Such employees are "educated" in a largely illiterate and semiliterate society, are economically better off than most, and their employment "comes with all the trappings of power". The consequence is stated as a mechanism: the asymmetry "reduces societal pressure to conform to ethical behaviour".

⚠️ **Mandatory qualification on the figures.** These are the report's *2007 illustrative* percentages. They must never be cited as current labour-force statistics. For a present-day answer, cite the mechanism and pair it with a current, independently verified source such as the latest Economic Survey formalisation data.

✅ **Aggravator 3 — early policy choice (ARC 1.7).** "As a conscious choice, the Indian state in the early decades after Independence chose a set of policies whose unintended consequence was to put the citizen at the mercy of the State." Over-regulation, severe restrictions on economic activity, excessive state control, near-monopoly of government in many sectors and an economy of scarcity "all created conditions conducive to unbridled corruption". Subsidies and beneficiary-oriented programmes under power asymmetry "converted the public servant into patron and master and reduced most citizens into mendicants", which simultaneously "enhanced opportunities to indulge in corruption and reduced the citizens' capacity to resist extortionary demands".

✅ **Multiplier 1 (ARC 1.8).** "It is generally recognized that monopoly and discretion increase the propensity to corruption while competition and transparency reduce corruption."

✅ **Multiplier 2 (ARC 1.9).** "The more remotely power is exercised from the people, the greater is the distance between authority and accountability." The report adds two specific observations: "For a large democracy, India probably has the smallest number of final decision makers", and "Local Government is not allowed to take root and power has been concentrated both horizontally and vertically in a few hands", with the net result of "weakened citizenry and mounting corruption".

⚠️ **Note the asymmetry of the two aggravators 1 and 3.** The first is inherited and cultural; the third was a *deliberate policy choice with an unintended consequence*. The ARC is careful to say "unintended", and an answer that presents planning-era policy as intentionally corrupt misreads the source.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> The Indian human interface is not ethically fragile by accident: a colonial habit of unchallenged authority, an asymmetry of power that removed societal pressure, and a scarcity economy that turned the official into a patron together converted routine administration into a site of extraction.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

`authority + discretion + control over the public purse` · `enormous temptation and opportunity` · `colonial legacy of unchallenged authority` · `asymmetry of power` · `reduces societal pressure to conform` · `patron and master / citizens into mendicants` · `capacity to resist extortionary demands` · `monopoly and discretion increase, competition and transparency reduce` · `distance between authority and accountability` · `smallest number of final decision makers`

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH: claim -> named evidence -> analysis -> qualification

**Claim:** India's corruption problem is structurally produced at the point of contact, not merely imported by dishonest individuals.
**Named evidence:** The Second ARC identifies three aggravators at paragraphs 1.6–1.7 — a colonial legacy of "unchallenged authority", an "enormous asymmetry of power" that "reduces societal pressure to conform to ethical behaviour", and an early-decades policy regime of over-regulation and scarcity that "converted the public servant into patron and master and reduced most citizens into mendicants".
**Analysis:** Each aggravator works on a different variable — the officer's sense of impunity, the citizen's bargaining power and the transaction's scarcity value — so they compound rather than merely add, which explains why single-instrument reforms disappoint.
**Qualification:** The report's supporting percentages are 2007 illustrations rather than current data, and its account of policy is explicitly of an "unintended consequence", so the argument must be carried by the mechanism and not by the numbers or by any imputation of bad faith to the planners.

#### ADMINISTRATIVE APPLICATION IN INDIA

⚠️ In a land-mutation counter, all three aggravators are visible at once: the clerk's authority is effectively unreviewable in practice, the applicant is typically an unorganised-sector claimant who cannot afford delay, and the record itself is scarce and non-substitutable. That is why land administration remains high-risk even after economy-wide liberalisation.

#### UPSC TRAP

❌ Quoting "90% unorganised" or "70% employed by the state" as current statistics. → They are the ARC's 2007 illustrative framing. Cite them as the report's framing, with the year, or replace them with current verified data.

#### RECAP

One bundle (authority + discretion + purse), three Indian aggravators (colonial legacy, asymmetry, scarcity-era regulation), two multipliers (monopoly/discretion versus competition/transparency; over-centralisation).

*What the visual shows:* the ceiling on all administrative reform. Without this session, an answer will over-promise what transparency alone can deliver.

#### Core teaching

✅ **The claim (ARC 1.12).** "Perhaps the most important determinant of the integrity of a society or the prevalence of corruption is the quality of politics. If politics attracts and rewards men and women of integrity, competence and passion for public good, then the society is safe and integrity is maintained. But if honesty is incompatible with survival in politics, and if public life attracts undesirable and corrupt elements seeking private gain, then abuse of authority and corruption become the norm."

✅ **The consequence for reform.** "In such a political culture and climate, desirable initiatives will not yield adequate dividends. Competition and decentralization certainly reduce corruption in certain sectors. But if the demand for corruption is fuelled by inexhaustible appetite for illegitimate funds in politics, then other avenues of corruption will be forcibly opened up. As a result, even as corruption declines in certain areas, it shifts to other, sometimes more dangerous, areas in which competition cannot be introduced and the state exercises a natural monopoly."

✅ **The prescription.** "What is needed with liberalisation is corresponding political and governance reform to alter the incentives in politics and public office and to promote integrity and ethical conduct."

⚠️ **How to use this in an answer.** This paragraph is the qualification that converts an ordinary answer into a graded one. Any claim you make about transparency, competition, e-governance or decentralisation should be followed by the migration caveat: reduced supply of opportunity in one sector does not reduce demand for illegitimate funds, so the pressure relocates to sectors where the state is a natural monopoly — land, mining leases, discretionary permissions, policing, regulatory clearances.

⚠️ **A precise inference, clearly labelled.** If corruption were purely a matter of individual character, then liberalisation — which changed incentives and not characters — should not have reduced it. ARC 1.8 records that it did. That is the strongest available internal argument that the ARC's own diagnosis is structural rather than dispositional.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> The Second ARC's most uncomfortable finding is that administrative reform has a political ceiling: competition and decentralisation reduce corruption in some sectors, but where the demand for illegitimate funds persists, corruption does not disappear — it migrates to the sectors in which the state is a natural monopoly.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

`quality of politics is the most important determinant` · `honesty incompatible with survival in politics` · `inexhaustible appetite for illegitimate funds` · `other avenues will be forcibly opened up` · `natural monopoly sectors` · `migration, not elimination` · `corresponding political and governance reform`

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH: claim -> named evidence -> analysis -> qualification

**Claim:** Anti-corruption reform that ignores political finance buys displacement rather than reduction.
**Named evidence:** ARC paragraph 1.12 states that if "the demand for corruption is fuelled by inexhaustible appetite for illegitimate funds in politics, then other avenues of corruption will be forcibly opened up", so that corruption "shifts to other, sometimes more dangerous, areas in which competition cannot be introduced and the state exercises a natural monopoly".
**Analysis:** This reframes measured sectoral success — the telecom or railway-reservation improvement of paragraph 1.8 — as a change in the *location* of rent extraction unless the demand side is simultaneously addressed, which is why land, mining and clearance-related corruption can rise while consumer-facing corruption falls.
**Qualification:** The paragraph does not counsel despair about administrative reform; the ARC's own remedy is that liberalisation must be accompanied by "corresponding political and governance reform", so the correct conclusion is joint sequencing rather than abandonment of structural correctives.

#### ADMINISTRATIVE APPLICATION IN INDIA

⚠️ A state that digitises driving-licence issuance and simultaneously retains discretionary, unpublished norms for change-of-land-use permissions has demonstrated the migration effect inside a single administration: the visible counter is cleaned, the invisible file is not.

#### UPSC TRAP

❌ Concluding that "competition and technology have solved corruption in India". → ARC 1.8 records sectoral gains; ARC 1.12 immediately limits them. Quote both, in that order, and the answer reads as balanced rather than naive.

#### RECAP

Quality of politics is the ARC's "most important determinant". Structural correctives work sectorally; without political-finance reform, corruption migrates to natural-monopoly sectors.

*What the visual shows:* one distinction that changes the diagnosis, the victim, the evidence problem and the remedy. It is the most reusable analytical wedge this topic supplies.

#### Core teaching

✅ **The report's framing (ARC 1.13).** "All forms of corruption are reprehensible and we need to promote a culture of zero-tolerance of corruption. But some forms of corruption are much more pernicious than others and deserve closer attention."

✅ **Coercive corruption.** "In a vast majority of cases of bribery, the citizen is a victim of extortion and is compelled to pay a bribe in order to get a service to which he is entitled." The report adds the reason resistance fails: "Experience has taught most citizens that there is a vicious cycle of corruption operating and they often end up losing much more by resisting corruption." Hence "the citizen is an unwilling victim of coercive corruption".

✅ **Collusive corruption.** "But there are several cases of collusion between the bribe giver and corrupt public servant. In such cases of collusive corruption, both parties benefit at immense cost to society." The named examples are: "Awarding of contracts for public works and procurement of goods and services, recruitment of employees, evasion of taxes, substandard projects, collusive violation of regulations, adulteration of foods and drugs, obstruction of justice and concealing or doctoring evidence in investigation".

✅ **The trend statement.** "As the economy is freed from state controls, extortionary corruption declines and collusive corruption tends to increase. We need to fashion strong and effective instruments to deal with this growing menace of collusive corruption, which is undermining the very foundations of our democracy and endangering society."

⚠️ **Why the distinction is ethically, not merely legally, important.** In coercive corruption the citizen's autonomy is overridden; the ethical wrong is extortion through public power and the citizen deserves protection and restitution. In collusive corruption the citizen is a co-author of the wrong; the ethical wrong is a joint appropriation of public goods and both parties are culpable. Treating the two identically either re-victimises the coerced or under-punishes the colluder.

⚠️ **A boundary caution.** The categories can blur in practice: a firm that first faced extortion may later seek an illegal advantage. Labels must follow the facts of each transaction, not a stereotype about bribe-givers.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> The same act of bribery can have two entirely different ethical structures: in coercive corruption the citizen is, in the Second ARC's words, "an unwilling victim", while in collusive corruption "both parties benefit at immense cost to society" — and a remedy designed for one will fail the other.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

`coercive corruption` · `unwilling victim of extortion` · `vicious cycle of corruption` · `entitled service` · `collusive corruption` · `both parties benefit at immense cost to society` · `procurement, recruitment, tax evasion, adulteration, doctoring evidence` · `extortionary declines, collusive increases with liberalisation` · `zero tolerance`

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH: claim -> named evidence -> analysis -> qualification

**Claim:** Liberalisation changed the composition of corruption rather than merely its quantity.
**Named evidence:** The Second ARC states at paragraph 1.13 that "as the economy is freed from state controls, extortionary corruption declines and collusive corruption tends to increase", and lists procurement, recruitment, tax evasion, substandard projects, adulteration and the doctoring of evidence as collusive forms.
**Analysis:** This matters because collusive corruption has no complaining witness — both parties gain — so detection must shift from grievance channels to audit, asset scrutiny and whistle-blower protection, which are institutionally more expensive than a complaint counter.
**Qualification:** The trend is a tendency, not a law: coercive extraction persists wherever an entitled service remains scarce, discretionary and non-substitutable, which is why land, policing and certification counters continue to generate extortion despite economy-wide liberalisation.

#### ADMINISTRATIVE APPLICATION IN INDIA

⚠️ Two files on the same officer's desk require opposite instruments. A pension release delayed until payment is coercive: the remedy is a time-bound service guarantee, an escalation route and protection for the complainant. A road-work completion certificate issued for substandard work is collusive: the remedy is third-party technical audit and investigation of both the certifier and the contractor.

#### UPSC TRAP

❌ "All corruption is the citizen's fault for paying a bribe." → ARC 1.13 expressly describes the extorted citizen as "an unwilling victim" who "often ends up losing much more by resisting corruption". Blaming the coerced payer inverts the report.

#### RECAP

Coercive = extraction for an entitlement, citizen is a victim, complaint-led remedies. Collusive = joint gain at society's cost, no complainant, audit-led remedies. Liberalisation shifts the mix toward the second.

*What the visual shows:* private and public relationships are not two moral standards but two role-structures; the ethical failures at the interface are all forms of importing a private structure into a public role.

#### Core teaching

⚠️ **The structural difference.** Private relationships are legitimately partial — you may prefer your own child. Public relationships are fiduciary: authority and resources are held *for* others, so partiality is a breach rather than a virtue. The syllabus's phrase "ethics in private and public relationships" asks precisely for this contrast, and for what happens when the boundary is crossed.

⚠️ **The three crossing points.**
1. **Nepotism and favouritism** — private partiality imported into a public role. A senior who says "everyone here prioritises relatives, so it is right" has offered a descriptive premise (Session 3) for a normative conclusion, in a role where partiality is structurally forbidden.
2. **Conflict of interest** — a private stake in a public decision. The wrong is not that the officer decided badly; it is that the decision can no longer be *shown* to be impartial. Hence the remedies are disclosure, recusal and reallocation rather than self-assurance.
3. **Gifts, hospitality and post-retirement benefit** — private benefit that a reasonable observer could read as a price for a public act. This is exactly the class Senator Fulbright described in ARC Box 2.3 as favours "under the guise of friendship" that "offend the spirit of the law but do not violate its letter".

⚠️ **The reasonable-citizen test.** The workable public test is not "did I feel biased?" but "could I state this reason publicly, and would the citizen who lost accept it as a reason?" Feeling impartial is a private fact; being demonstrably impartial is a public one.

✅ **The report's own extension (ARC 1.17).** The ARC explicitly refuses to confine ethics to government: an across-the-board effort must include "corporate ethics and ethics in business", with a demanded "paradigm shift from the pejorative 'business ethics' to 'ethics in business'"; ethics "in every profession, voluntary organization and civil society structure as these entities are now vitally involved in the process of governance"; and "finally, there should be ethics in citizen behaviour because such behaviour impinges directly on ethics in government and administration."

⚠️ **The reciprocity point that earns marks.** ARC 1.17's last clause makes the citizen a duty-bearer, not only a rights-holder. The private relationship of a citizen to public goods — tax honesty, refusal to offer a bribe, truthful declarations — is part of the same ethical system. An answer that treats the citizen purely as a victim has missed the report's own final move.

⚠️ **Private virtue does not automatically transfer.** A personally generous officer may still run an opaque office; a personally austere officer may still be procedurally unfair. Role morality is learned, and this is why codes of conduct and codes of ethics (Topic 16) exist alongside personal character.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> Private life permits partiality and public office forbids it; almost every ethical failure at the human interface is the importation of a private relationship structure — loyalty, gratitude, friendship — into a role that is held in trust for strangers.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

`partiality is legitimate in private, a breach in public` · `fiduciary role` · `entrusted power` · `nepotism and favouritism` · `conflict of interest: disclose, recuse, reallocate` · `appearance of bias` · `reasonable-citizen test` · `ARC 1.17: ethics in business, professions, civil society, citizen behaviour` · `paradigm shift from business ethics to ethics in business`

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH: claim -> named evidence -> analysis -> qualification

**Claim:** Ethical governance cannot be built on official conduct alone, because the private side of the relationship also carries duties.
**Named evidence:** Paragraph 1.17 of the Second ARC's Fourth Report extends the ethical demand to "corporate ethics and ethics in business", to "every profession, voluntary organization and civil society structure", and states that "there should be ethics in citizen behaviour because such behaviour impinges directly on ethics in government and administration".
**Analysis:** This closes the loop the earlier paragraphs open: if corruption is a transaction, then a supply-side reform confined to officials leaves the demand side — firms seeking irregular advantage and citizens offering inducements — structurally untouched.
**Qualification:** The reciprocity must not be used to equalise blame where power is unequal; the same report describes the extorted citizen at paragraph 1.13 as "an unwilling victim", so citizen duty applies with full force to collusive conduct and only weakly where the citizen is coerced.

#### ADMINISTRATIVE APPLICATION IN INDIA

⚠️ An officer whose relative applies for a contract in the officer's own jurisdiction should record the relationship in writing, transfer the file to a designated alternative authority, and ensure the decision is signed by that authority. The written record is the ethical act; the private assurance of fairness is not.

#### UPSC TRAP

❌ Answering "ethics in private and public relationships" as "be honest at home and at work". → The examinable content is the *structural asymmetry* — permitted partiality versus fiduciary impartiality — plus the three crossing points and ARC 1.17's extension to business, professions, civil society and citizen behaviour.

#### RECAP

Private = permitted partiality; public = fiduciary impartiality. Crossings are nepotism, conflict of interest and gifts. ARC 1.17 extends the duty to business, professions, civil society and the citizen.

*What the visual shows:* the corrective is not "honesty" but the removal of three gaps — alternative, information and discretion — each with the report's own named evidence.

#### Core teaching

✅ **The general finding (ARC 1.8).** "The experience of the past six decades in our country and elsewhere offers us valuable lessons in curbing corruption. It is generally recognized that monopoly and discretion increase the propensity to corruption while competition and transparency reduce corruption. This has been dramatically witnessed in India in the wake of economic liberalization. As competition came in and choice expanded, corruption plummeted."

✅ **The named sectors.** "Telephones, steel, cement, sugar and even two-wheelers are among the many sectors, which have seen enhanced supply and choice, reducing or even eliminating corruption." Reproduce the full list — many answers drop sugar and two-wheelers, which is exactly the level of specificity the examiner rewards.

✅ **The technology finding.** "Similarly, wherever technology and transparency have been introduced, corruption has been significantly contained. Computerization and access to information have made many services from railway reservation to issuing of driving licenses increasingly free from corruption."

✅ **The named Indian initiatives (ARC 1.15).** The report cites three as "steps in the right direction": the Supreme Court's ruling that candidates contesting elections must file details of "their wealth, educational qualifications and criminal antecedents along with their nomination papers"; the Right to Information Act, described as "recently enacted" and "a potent weapon to fight corruption"; and "the introduction of information communication technologies, e-governance initiatives and automation of corruption prone processes in administration".

⚠️ **The precise mechanism.** A bribe is priced by the absence of an alternative. Competition creates an alternative; transparency destroys the information asymmetry that lets an official invent a requirement; automation removes the discretionary step at which the requirement could be invented. This is why the corrective works without any change in personal character — the point that makes the ARC's diagnosis structural.

⚠️ **The boundary case that earns marks.** Monopoly by itself is not the driver. A single-window, IT-enabled utility payment counter is a monopoly and can still be low-corruption because it has no discretion and leaves an audit trail. The operative variable is **discretion without transparency**.

⚠️ **The exclusion limit.** Automation shifts the failure mode rather than eliminating it: a digitally excluded claimant may now need an intermediary, which recreates a rent at one remove. The safeguard is an assisted, staffed offline channel with the same time guarantee.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> The Second ARC's most-quoted finding is deliberately unsentimental: corruption fell in telephones, steel, cement, sugar and two-wheelers not because officials became more honest but because citizens acquired an alternative — which is why reducing discretion is more durable than moral exhortation.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

`monopoly and discretion increase; competition and transparency reduce` · `telephones, steel, cement, sugar, two-wheelers` · `railway reservation and driving licences` · `computerization and access to information` · `wealth, educational qualifications and criminal antecedents` · `RTI Act as a potent weapon` · `automation of corruption-prone processes` · `discretion without transparency is the real driver` · `digital exclusion recreates the rent`

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH: claim -> named evidence -> analysis -> qualification

**Claim:** Reducing discretion is a more durable anti-corruption instrument than moral exhortation.
**Named evidence:** ARC paragraph 1.8 records that after liberalisation "as competition came in and choice expanded, corruption plummeted" in telephones, steel, cement, sugar and two-wheelers, and that computerisation made services "from railway reservation to issuing of driving licenses increasingly free from corruption".
**Analysis:** In each case the change was to the citizen's options and to the officer's discretionary step, not to anyone's character, which demonstrates that the rent was created by the structure of the transaction rather than by individual dishonesty.
**Qualification:** The corrective is bounded in two ways the same report supplies — paragraph 1.12 warns that suppressed demand migrates to natural-monopoly sectors, and automation itself can exclude the digitally weak, so an assisted offline channel and continued oversight remain necessary.

#### ADMINISTRATIVE APPLICATION IN INDIA

⚠️ Sequence a district-level intervention in the ARC's own order: publish the fee and the timeline, remove the discretionary step by automating eligibility checks, generate a time-stamped acknowledgement, and provide a staffed assisted counter for applicants who cannot transact online.

#### UPSC TRAP

❌ Writing that "liberalisation is unrelated to ethics" or, conversely, that "liberalisation ended corruption". → ARC 1.8 credits competition and transparency with real sectoral reductions; ARC 1.12 immediately caps the claim. Both sentences belong in the same answer.

#### RECAP

Remove the three gaps — alternative, information, discretion. Quote the five sectors and the two services. Then cap the claim with migration and digital exclusion.

#### Core teaching

✅ **The claim (ARC 1.10).** "It is well recognized that every democracy requires the empowerment of citizens in order to hold those in authority to account. An answer that names only RTI has named one-sixth of the corrective.

⚠️ **The structural argument.** Because ARC 1.6 identifies asymmetry of power as an aggravating cause, the empowerment instruments are not optional good-governance additions; they are the specific inverse of a specific diagnosed cause. Where those are absent, the empowerment mechanism can itself be captured by the locally powerful, reproducing the asymmetry inside the remedy. ⚠️ **Ownership boundary.** RTI's statutory architecture is Topic 15; citizens' charters and work culture are Topic 17. Topic 01 owns why empowerment is *ethically necessary*, not the provisions.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> Citizen empowerment is not a courtesy extended by a benevolent state; because the Second ARC identifies asymmetry of power as a cause of corruption, information, charters, consultation and social audit are the precise structural inverse of a diagnosed cause.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

**Named evidence:** The Second ARC names six instruments at paragraph 1.10 — Right to Information, effective citizens' charters, opportunity and incentives for a proactive citizenry, stakeholders' involvement in delivery, public consultation in decision-making, and social auditing — and states that they "dramatically curbed corruption and promoted integrity and quality of decision making".
**Analysis:** Each instrument dismantles one component of the asymmetry the report itself diagnoses at paragraph 1.6 — information, entitlement, initiative, design, reason-giving and verification — so the set is corrective by construction rather than by aspiration.
**Qualification:** Their effectiveness is conditional on literacy, awareness and organisational capacity, so without facilitation and proactive disclosure the instruments can be captured by locally powerful users and reproduce the very asymmetry they were meant to correct.

#### ADMINISTRATIVE APPLICATION IN INDIA

❌ Treating RTI as the whole of citizen empowerment. → The ARC names six instruments in one sentence; the marks lie in the completeness of the list and in explaining which asymmetry each one corrects.

Six instruments, six asymmetries corrected, one capture risk. Empowerment is the inverse of a diagnosed cause.

*What the visual shows:* two arms multiplying, not adding. This is the structure that lets you refuse the false choice between "moral reform" and "strict punishment".

#### Core teaching

✅ **The claim (ARC 1.11).** "In the ultimate analysis, the state and a system of laws exist in order to enforce compliance and promote desirable behaviour. Therefore, enforcement of rule of law and deterrent punishment against corruption are critical to build an ethically sound society. A detailed analysis of our anti-corruption mechanisms and the causes of their failure is necessary in order to strengthen the forces of law and deter the corrupt public servants."

✅ **The unfinished agenda (ARC 1.16).** "Much more remains to be done however, and beyond the realm of existing regulation." The report then names four specific requirements: benami properties of corrupt public servants "need to be forfeited, as also the assets illegally acquired from corrupt practices"; "Whistleblower legislation has to be put in place to protect informants against retribution"; the institutional framework for investigating corrupt practices must be strengthened; and exemplary punishment must be awarded "thereby raising the risk associated with corrupt behaviour".

⚠️ **Status discipline — the most common factual error in this session.** In January 2007 these were *recommendations*, not law. Writing about them today requires separating what the ARC recommended from what has since been enacted, and Topics 19 and 21 own the statutory detail. Never write "the ARC enacted" or imply that the report itself created a legal duty.

⚠️ **The analytical structure.** Prevention reduces opportunity; punishment raises expected cost. Corruption persists where opportunity is high *and* risk is low. Attacking only one factor leaves a positive product, which is the clean way to reject both "just teach values" and "just punish harshly".

⚠️ **The self-critical clause.** ARC 1.11 does not assume that the existing anti-corruption machinery works; it demands "a detailed analysis of... the causes of their failure". Citing this clause signals that you have read the report rather than a summary, and it licenses a paragraph on delay, sanction-for-prosecution bottlenecks and low conviction rates without drifting outside Topic 01's scope.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> The Second ARC refuses the choice that most answers make for it: prevention lowers the opportunity for corruption and punishment raises its risk, and because the two multiply rather than add, removing only one of them leaves the incentive to be corrupt fully intact.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

`enforcement of rule of law` · `deterrent punishment` · `causes of their failure` · `benami property forfeiture` · `assets illegally acquired` · `whistleblower legislation against retribution` · `strengthen investigating framework` · `exemplary punishment raises the risk` · `preventive and punitive, never either/or`

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH: claim -> named evidence -> analysis -> qualification

**Claim:** An anti-corruption strategy that is only preventive is as incomplete as one that is only punitive.
**Named evidence:** Paragraph 1.11 of the Second ARC's Fourth Report states that "enforcement of rule of law and deterrent punishment against corruption are critical to build an ethically sound society", while paragraph 1.16 adds forfeiture of benami and illegally acquired assets, whistleblower protection "against retribution", a strengthened investigative framework and exemplary punishment that raises "the risk associated with corrupt behaviour".
**Analysis:** Because corrupt conduct responds to expected opportunity multiplied by expected risk, systemic redesign that lowers opportunity while enforcement remains slow simply lowers the price of corruption instead of ending it.
**Qualification:** The same paragraph 1.11 warns that the existing machinery is not to be assumed effective and demands analysis of "the causes of their failure", so punitive escalation without procedural reform of investigation, sanction and trial will raise announced risk without raising real risk.

#### ADMINISTRATIVE APPLICATION IN INDIA

⚠️ A department that reduces its discretionary steps but leaves disciplinary proceedings pending for years has completed the preventive arm and abandoned the punitive one. The measurable test is median disposal time of disciplinary and prosecution-sanction cases, not the number of circulars issued.

#### UPSC TRAP

❌ Presenting ARC 1.16's items as existing law of 2007. → They are recommendations in the report's own words ("has to be put in place", "need to be forfeited"). Statutory developments after 2007 belong to Topics 19 and 21 and must be dated separately.

#### RECAP

Two arms, multiplied. Prevention lowers opportunity, punishment raises risk, and the ARC demands that the failure of existing machinery itself be analysed.

#### Core teaching

✅ **Approach 1 (ARC 1.4).** "The first is overemphasis on values and character. Many people lament the decline in values and the consequent rise in corruption. The implicit assumption is that until values are restored, nothing much can be done to improve the conduct of human beings."

✅ **Approach 2 (ARC 1.4).** "The second approach is based on the belief that most human beings are fundamentally decent and socially conscious, but there is always a small proportion of people, which cannot reconcile individual goals with the good of society. Such deviant people tend to pursue personal gain at the cost of public good and the purpose of organized government is to punish such deviant behaviour. If good behaviour is consistently rewarded and bad behaviour consistently punished, the bulk of the people follow the straight and narrow path."

✅ **The incentive corollary, in the report's words.** "However, if good behaviour is not only not rewarded, but is actually fraught with difficulties and bad behaviour is not only not punished, but is often extravagantly rewarded, then the bulk of the people tend to stray from the honourable path."

✅ **The synthesis (ARC 1.5).** "In the real world, both values and institutions matter. Values are needed to serve as guiding stars, and they exist in abundance in our society. A sense of right and wrong is intrinsic to our culture and civilization. But values need to be sustained by institutions to be durable and to serve as an example to others. Values without institutional support will soon be weakened and dissipated. Institutions provide the container, which gives shape and content to values. This is the basis of all statecraft and laws and institutions."

⚠️ **How to deploy it.** This is the single most transferable paragraph in GS-IV. Any question of the form "why do public servants behave unethically", "what shapes ethical conduct", "is moral education enough" or "can law make people honest" can open with the two contrary approaches and close with the container metaphor. It is also the safest way to avoid the two commonest failure modes: the moralising essay and the purely legalistic list.

⚠️ **The distinction to protect.** The ARC does not say values are unimportant. It says they are *abundant but perishable* without institutional containment. An answer that presents the ARC as dismissing character has misread paragraph 1.5.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> The Second ARC settles the oldest quarrel in public ethics in a single image: "institutions provide the container, which gives shape and content to values", so that values without institutional support "will soon be weakened and dissipated".

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

`overemphasis on values and character` · `deviant minority` · `both values and institutions matter` · `values are guiding stars` · `values exist in abundance` · `values without institutional support will soon be weakened and dissipated` · `institutions provide the container` · `basis of all statecraft` · `army of public servants, elected or appointed`

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH: claim -> named evidence -> analysis -> qualification

**Claim:** Neither moral renewal nor deterrence alone explains or corrects ethical failure in public administration.
**Named evidence:** Paragraph 1.4 of the Second ARC's Fourth Report sets out the two "somewhat contrary" approaches — an overemphasis on declining values, and the punishment of a deviant minority — and paragraph 1.5 answers that "in the real world, both values and institutions matter", because "values without institutional support will soon be weakened and dissipated" while "institutions provide the container, which gives shape and content to values".
**Analysis:** The container metaphor is doing analytical work: it explains why identical individuals behave differently across departments, and why an organisation can degrade quickly when its supervisory and disciplinary machinery weakens even though its recruitment pool is unchanged.
**Qualification:** Containment is not sufficient by itself either, since the same paragraph insists values are needed "to serve as guiding stars"; an institution that secures compliance without conviction produces conduct that lapses as soon as monitoring does.

#### ADMINISTRATIVE APPLICATION IN INDIA

⚠️ Two districts recruit from the same cadre and the same training academy but differ sharply in grievance-redress performance. The variable is not the officers' values but the container: published timelines, a functioning review meeting, and a supervisor who reads the pendency list.

#### UPSC TRAP

❌ "The ARC blamed declining values for corruption." → The report explicitly rejects a single-cause explanation at 1.4–1.5 and insists that values and institutions are jointly necessary.

#### RECAP

Two contrary one-cause theories, one two-input synthesis. Guiding stars plus containers. Quote the container sentence verbatim.

#### Core teaching

⚠️ **Comprehensive National Power (CNP)** is a composite of material and non-material capacities. Treating it as a synonym for military strength is the standard error. The examinable components are economic, military/strategic, socio-cultural or soft, technological/scientific and diplomatic.

✅ **The economic mechanism is the one you can source.** ARC 1.8's finding — that transparency and reduced discretion cut corruption after liberalisation — supports the claim that ethical governance lowers the risk premium a corrupt economy pays in investment and borrowing costs. The Report's Preface makes the same point in its own terms: improved governance, "non-expropriation, contract enforcement, and decrease in bureaucratic delays and corruption can raise the GDP growth rate significantly."

⚠️ **The military/strategic mechanism.** Integrity in defence procurement ensures capability is bought on merit rather than on commission, and apolitical professionalism keeps the chain of command trusted. Both are inferences from general procurement ethics, not ARC findings, and must be labelled as such.

✅ ⚠️ **The socio-cultural mechanism.** ARC 1.10's citizen-empowerment logic builds internal legitimacy; the further step that legitimacy becomes external soft power is an inference.

⚠️ **The technological mechanism.** Research integrity — no fabrication, no plagiarism — and transparent data/AI governance norms sustain the credibility on which technological power depends. Topic 13 owns AI ethics in detail.

⚠️ **The diplomatic mechanism.** A state that honours treaty and humanitarian commitments accumulates credibility that neither military nor economic calculation can purchase.

⚠️ **The thesis.** Ethics is not a decorative "soft" addition to national power and not a substitute for material capacity. Each material component has a credibility multiplier that ethical governance produces, while social harmony is the component to which ethics contributes most directly.

⚠️ **The honest limitation.** The causal link between ethical governance and national power is indirect and operates over a longer horizon than a single policy cycle, which is why it should be argued as a mechanism rather than asserted as a measured effect.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> Ethics does not substitute for economic or military capacity; it decides how much of that capacity is actually usable, because a state whose commitments, procurements and courts are believed can convert the same resources into far more power than one that is not believed.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

`CNP is composite, not military alone` · `economic, military, socio-cultural, technological, diplomatic` · `risk premium` · `procurement integrity` · `apolitical professionalism` · `legitimacy becomes soft power` · `research integrity` · `honoured commitments` · `capability multiplier, not substitute` · `social harmony is the most direct contribution`

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH: claim -> named evidence -> analysis -> qualification

**Claim:** Ethical governance is a multiplier of national power rather than a moral ornament attached to it.
**Named evidence:** The Second ARC's Preface argues that "improved governance in the form of non-expropriation, contract enforcement, and decrease in bureaucratic delays and corruption can raise the GDP growth rate significantly", and paragraph 1.8 records that transparency and reduced discretion actually curbed corruption in named sectors after liberalisation.
**Analysis:** Both statements identify credibility as the transmission channel: contracts that are enforced and permissions that are predictable convert the same physical and financial resources into higher realised capacity, and the same logic extends to procurement quality in defence and to trust in research output.
**Qualification:** The link is indirect and slow, and no single index establishes it, so the argument should be presented as a mechanism supported by the report's own reasoning rather than as a quantified national-power effect.

#### ADMINISTRATIVE APPLICATION IN INDIA

⚠️ A defence acquisition decided on a documented technical evaluation, with dissent recorded and integrity pacts enforced, produces both a better platform and a more trusted service. The ethics is not adjacent to the capability; it is a condition of it.

#### UPSC TRAP

❌ Equating Comprehensive National Power with military power, or answering the question with a paragraph of patriotic generalities. → Name the five components, then supply one distinct mechanism per component. The mechanism column is where the marks are.

#### RECAP

Five components, five mechanisms, one thesis: ethics multiplies usable capacity; social harmony is its most direct contribution; the causal link is indirect and slow.

#### Core teaching

✅ **The paper's actual shape**, verified directly from the locally held official PDFs: in both `books\mains\05 UPSC 2024 Paper-IV_Final 1.pdf` and `books\mains\UPSC Mains 2025 GS Paper 4.pdf`, every Section-A sub-question carries "(Answer in 150 words) 10", and every Section-B case study carries "(Answer in 250 words)" for 20 marks. There is no 15-mark question in GS-IV.

⚠️ **The five-move spine.** (1) One or two lines of context naming the concept precisely. (2) Two or three core dimensions or mechanisms drawn from Sessions 4, 7 and 8. ⚠️ **The selectable evidence pool** — choose one or two per answer, never all four.

| Claim | Named evidence | Significance | Limitation to state |
|---|---|---|---|

✅ **Case-study boundary.** This topic does **not** carry a rival 20-mark format. Every GS-IV Section-B case study must be built on the eight-element architecture owned by `22_Case-Study-Method-and-Answer-Architecture.md`: facts, stakeholders, constraints, at least three realistic options with the strongest objection to the chosen one steelmanned, named ethical evaluation, decision, implementation and communication, and residual-risk mitigation. Topic 01 supplies the ethical-evaluation content used inside element five.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING LINE

> A GS-IV answer is graded on whether the examiner can see the mechanism, the named evidence and the limit; a moving conclusion with none of these reads as an essay on goodness rather than as an answer on ethics.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

`define and state a thesis` · `mechanism before example` · `one named illustration per dimension` · `explicit counterpoint` · `qualified verdict` · `150 words / 10 marks Section A` · `250 words / 20 marks Section B` · `eight-element case-study architecture (Topic 22)` · `at least three options` · `residual-risk mitigation`

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH: claim -> named evidence -> analysis -> qualification

**Claim:** Structure, not eloquence, is what distinguishes a high-scoring GS-IV theory answer.
**Named evidence:** The 2024 GS-IV question printed at Q1(b) asks the candidate to "Explain the key dimensions of ethics that influence human actions" and then to "Discuss how these dimensions shape ethical decision-making in the professional context" — two directives, therefore two structural obligations in 150 words.
**Analysis:** A candidate who answers only the first directive can score at most half the marks regardless of quality, because the second directive requires movement from taxonomy to application in a named professional setting.
**Qualification:** Structure does not license mechanical templating; the same five-move spine must be filled with topic-specific evidence, since a visibly reusable skeleton with generic content reads as preparation rather than as thinking.

#### ADMINISTRATIVE APPLICATION IN INDIA

⚠️ Rehearse the spine on one file type until it is automatic — for example a beneficiary-list finalisation — then transfer it to procurement, transfer and clearance questions. The examiner rewards the transferable mechanism, not the memorised example.

#### UPSC TRAP

❌ Importing the case-study architecture into a 150-word theory answer, or writing a theory essay in place of a case study. → Section A wants mechanism and verdict in 150 words; Section B wants stakeholders, at least three options, decision and residual risk in 250 words. Mismatching the format is a self-inflicted loss.

#### RECAP

Five moves, one directive decoder, four selectable evidence blocks, one counterpoint template, and a hard boundary against importing Topic 22's case-study format.

### ETHICS DEEP-REVIEW CORE CONTROL

- **Must remember:** ethics is reasoned evaluation of right conduct; morality is a person's or community's held code; values are enduring evaluative commitments; law is publicly enforceable authority; propriety is fitness to office and occasion; conscience is reflective moral judgment. Overlap in usage never makes these terms synonyms.
- **Close distinction:** legality, morality and propriety apply different tests. Lawful action can remain ethically deficient; conscience cannot cancel valid law by assertion; and constitutional morality is a public justificatory discipline rather than an official's private preference. Constitutional morality is owned by Topic 14 with Topic 10 as a guidance-source cross-link.
- **Evidence and authority limit:** administrative examples must identify the power exercised, the affected citizen, the duty owed, the incentive at work and the institutional safeguard available. ARC claims retain their paragraph numbers and the report's January 2007 date; Mission Karmayogi figures retain their 6 February 2026 release date; neither becomes timeless proof.
- **Syllabus completeness check:** essence (Sessions 1, 5), determinants (Sessions 6, 7, 8), consequences (Sessions 9, 10), dimensions (Sessions 3, 4), private and public relationships (Session 11), correctives and synthesis (Sessions 12–15), application (Sessions 16, 17), chronology (Session 2).

### CORE DRILLS 1–24

#### MCQ 1

The Second ARC's Fourth Report defines ethics and then immediately qualifies the definition. Which statement most accurately reproduces the *qualification* the Commission attaches?

- **A.** The crux of ethical behaviour lies not in bold words enshrined as standards but in their adoption in action, in sanctions against violations, in competent disciplinary bodies that act quickly, and in promoting a culture of integrity.
- **B.** Ethical behaviour follows automatically once a comprehensive code of conduct has been notified for every organ of government, because a standard published in the official gazette both informs officials of their obligations and supplies the disciplinary authority with a ready basis for action in every case of deviation.
- **C.** Ethics is a purely private matter of conscience, so public standards can only describe conduct and never improve it.
- **D.** Standards guarantee ethical behaviour provided that the society enforcing them is economically developed.

**Answer:** A

**Option-by-option explanation**

- **A — correct.** ARC 1.1 states that ethics is "a set of standards that society places on itself", then warns that "standards do not, by themselves, ensure ethical behaviour; that requires a robust culture of integrity", and locates the crux in adoption in action, sanctions, competent disciplinary bodies acting quickly, and a culture of integrity. All four elements appear in this option.
- **B — wrong.** Notification of a code is exactly the "bold words and expressions enshrined as standards" that ARC 1.1 says is *not* the crux. The report's whole point is that a notified code without enforcement machinery changes nothing.
- **C — wrong.** The ARC's definition is explicitly social, not private: standards are what "society places on itself". Treating ethics as purely private contradicts the definition in the first sentence of the chapter.
- **D — wrong.** The report attaches no economic precondition to ethical behaviour. It attaches an institutional one — sanctions and disciplinary machinery — and it separately argues that ethical governance itself raises growth, which is the reverse of this option's causal order.

> **Trap:** The stem asks for the *qualification*, not the definition. Candidates who scan for the familiar phrase "standards that society places on itself" pick a definitional option and lose the mark that the second half of ARC 1.1 was set to test.

#### MCQ 2

Consider the following two statements about the etymology used in the Second ARC's Fourth Report.

1. "Ethics" derives from the Greek *ethikos*, meaning "arising from habit".
2. "Corrupt" derives from the Latin *corruptus*, meaning "to break or destroy".

Which of the statements is/are correct?

- **A.** 1 only
- **B.** Both 1 and 2
- **C.** 2 only
- **D.** Neither 1 nor 2

**Answer:** B

**Option-by-option explanation**

- **A — wrong.** Statement 1 is correct, but rejecting statement 2 discards the second half of ARC 1.2, which gives *corruptus* precisely the sense "to break or destroy".
- **B — correct.** ARC 1.2 supplies both derivations in a single sentence, and then uses them together: because ethics arises from habit, the report calls it "unfortunate that corruption has, for many, become a matter of habit".
- **C — wrong.** Statement 2 alone is incomplete; the Greek derivation in statement 1 is equally the report's own and is the one that carries the analytical weight about habituation.
- **D — wrong.** Both derivations are stated verbatim in ARC 1.2, so rejecting both contradicts the primary source.

> **Trap:** Two-statement questions in this area often reverse the languages, attaching Latin to "ethics" and Greek to "corrupt". Fix the pairing by meaning: *habit* is Greek, *breaking* is Latin.

#### MCQ 3

The Second ARC's terms of reference on ethics in governance covered three heads. Which statement correctly describes how the Fourth Report actually dealt with them?

- **A.** All three heads — vigilance and corruption, the political executive and permanent civil service relationship, and codes of conduct for the different organs of government — were deferred in their entirety to the Commission's later report on Civil Services Reforms, so that the Fourth Report confined itself to a statement of general ethical principles.
- **B.** Only the head on codes of conduct was examined, and the other two were dropped entirely.
- **C.** Vigilance and corruption and codes of conduct were examined in considerable detail, while the political executive–permanent civil service relationship was deferred to the report on Civil Services Reforms.
- **D.** The relationship between the political executive and the permanent civil service was the sole subject examined in detail.

**Answer:** C

**Option-by-option explanation**

- **A — wrong.** ARC 1.19 says the opposite: two of the three heads were examined "in considerable detail" in this very Report.
- **B — wrong.** Vigilance and corruption was examined too. This option drops head A, which supplies the entire anti-corruption architecture of the report.
- **C — correct.** ARC 1.18 lists the three heads — (A) Vigilance and Corruption, (B) Relationship between Political Executive and Permanent Civil Service, (C) Code of Conduct for different organs of Government — and ARC 1.19 states that "items A & C" were examined in considerable detail while "item B will be dealt with comprehensively in the ARC's report on Civil Services Reforms".
- **D — wrong.** Head B is exactly the one that was *not* examined here. This option inverts the report's own statement.

> **Trap:** The deferred head is the politically most interesting one, which is why candidates assume it was the report's centrepiece. Remember the pairing: A and C were done here; B went to Civil Services Reforms.

#### MCQ 4

Arrange the following in correct chronological order.

1. India ratifies the United Nations Convention against Corruption.
2. The UN General Assembly adopts the United Nations Convention against Corruption.
3. The Second ARC's Fourth Report, *Ethics in Governance*, is published.
4. India endorses the ADB-OECD Anti-Corruption Action Plan for Asia and the Pacific.

- **A.** 2 – 4 – 3 – 1
- **B.** 4 – 3 – 2 – 1
- **C.** 1 – 2 – 4 – 3
- **D.** 4 – 2 – 3 – 1

**Answer:** D

**Option-by-option explanation**

- **A — wrong.** It places the UNCAC adoption of October 2003 before the ADB-OECD endorsement of 30 November 2001, reversing the first two events.
- **B — wrong.** It puts the January 2007 report before the October 2003 adoption of UNCAC, although the report itself cites that adoption at paragraph 1.14 as an existing instrument.
- **C — wrong.** It places India's ratification first. Ratification occurred on 9 May 2011, after every other event in the list.
- **D — correct.** ADB-OECD endorsement, 30 November 2001; UNCAC adopted by the UN General Assembly, 31 October 2003; the Second ARC's Fourth Report, January 2007; India's ratification of UNCAC, 9 May 2011.

> **Trap:** India *signed* UNCAC on 9 December 2005 but *ratified* it only on 9 May 2011, which is why the report speaks of a convention India had joined without India being a full party. Treating signature as ratification collapses two events six years apart.

#### MCQ 5

A state training institute reports that a large share of field staff regard small facilitation payments as normal practice. An officer argues that the practice is therefore acceptable. Which is the most precise description of the error?

- **A.** A descriptive finding about prevailing belief has been used as a normative justification, crossing the is–ought gap.
- **B.** A meta-ethical claim about the meaning of "wrong" has been mistaken for an empirical survey result.
- **C.** An applied-ethics conclusion has been drawn without first settling a question of moral semantics.
- **D.** The officer has correctly applied normative ethics but has used an inadequate sample.

**Answer:** A

**Option-by-option explanation**

- **A — correct.** The survey establishes what staff in fact believe, which is descriptive ethics. The officer's conclusion is about what may be done, which is normative. Moving from the first to the second without a justifying standard is the is–ought error.
- **B — wrong.** No claim about the meaning or truth-status of moral language has been made. Meta-ethics is not engaged at any point in the scenario.
- **C — wrong.** Applied ethics does not require moral semantics to be settled first; the ARC itself proceeds directly to administrative application without any meta-ethical preliminary.
- **D — wrong.** The defect is logical, not statistical. Even a perfectly representative sample of belief would establish nothing about what ought to be done.

> **Trap:** The word "therefore" is the whole question. Candidates who focus on the plausibility of facilitation payments miss that the stem is testing the inference, not the practice.

#### MCQ 6

Two probationers argue about whether the word "integrity" can be true or false in the way a factual statement can, without reference to any pending decision. Their debate belongs to which level of ethical enquiry?

- **A.** Descriptive ethics
- **B.** Meta-ethics
- **C.** Applied ethics
- **D.** Normative ethics

**Answer:** B

**Option-by-option explanation**

- **A — wrong.** Descriptive ethics would require them to be reporting what some group actually believes about integrity, which is not what the stem describes.
- **B — correct.** They are debating the meaning and truth-status of a moral term, which is exactly the meta-ethical question of whether moral language states facts or expresses attitudes.
- **C — wrong.** Applied ethics requires a concrete field and a live decision. The stem expressly removes any pending decision.
- **D — wrong.** Normative ethics would require them to be arguing what someone ought to do and on what ground; they are arguing about what a word does.

> **Trap:** Meta-ethics feels like the "deepest" level and is therefore over-selected in questions where a decision is actually at stake. Use the reverse test as well: when a live administrative decision is present, meta-ethics is almost never the answer.

#### MCQ 7

A hospital administrator must decide whether to publish a surgery waiting list despite staff discomfort. Which level of ethical enquiry is she conducting, and why?

- **A.** Descriptive ethics, because the administrator's first task is to record how staff and patients presently feel about disclosure, and an accurate account of those prevailing attitudes is by itself what settles whether the waiting list should be published.
- **B.** Meta-ethics, because the meaning of fairness is contested.
- **C.** Applied ethics, because a normative standard of fairness and transparency is being worked out for a specific institutional decision.
- **D.** None, because the question is purely administrative rather than ethical.

**Answer:** C

**Option-by-option explanation**

- **A — wrong.** Staff discomfort is a fact in the background of the decision, not the object of the enquiry. She is not conducting a survey.
- **B — wrong.** She is applying a standard of fairness, not analysing what the word "fairness" means or whether it can be true.
- **C — correct.** Applied ethics is normative theory worked out for a definite field — here, medical and administrative practice — by testing a concrete decision rather than by naming a theory.
- **D — wrong.** The decision allocates a scarce public good and affects identifiable persons unequally, which makes it ethical as well as administrative; the two are not alternatives.

> **Trap:** "Purely administrative" is a standing distractor in GS-IV. Almost every allocation decision carries duty, fairness and accountability dimensions, so the option that denies an ethical question is nearly always wrong.

| 1. Consequences | i. Silent, unexplained unequal treatment |
| 2. Justice and fairness | ii. A procedurally correct decision that ignores the most vulnerable claimant |
| 3. Care and empathy | iii. Good intentions accompanied by foreseeable harm |
| 4. Accountability | iv. A decision that survives only while it remains unexamined |

- **A.** 1-i, 2-ii, 3-iii, 4-iv
- **B.** 1-ii, 2-iii, 3-iv, 4-i
- **C.** 1-iv, 2-i, 3-ii, 4-iii
- **D.** 1-iii, 2-i, 3-ii, 4-iv

**Answer:** D

**Option-by-option explanation**

- **A — wrong.** It attaches unequal treatment to the consequences dimension. Unequal treatment is caught by justice and fairness, which asks whether like cases are treated alike.
- **B — wrong.** It makes accountability responsible for detecting unequal treatment. Accountability tests whether reasons can be stated publicly and reviewed; fairness tests the distribution itself.
- **C — wrong.** It assigns non-reviewability to the consequences dimension, whereas foreseeable harm is precisely what consequence-reasoning exists to detect.
- **D — correct.** Consequences catch foreseeable harm despite good intentions; justice and fairness catch unexplained unequal treatment; care and empathy catch procedural correctness that is blind to the vulnerable; accountability catches decisions that survive only in the absence of scrutiny.

> **Trap:** Matching questions on dimensions punish candidates who memorise the list without the function of each item. Learn each dimension by the *failure it detects*, not by its name.

#### MCQ 9

A procurement officer attends a lavish private dinner hosted by a firm bidding for a contract in his jurisdiction. No payment is made and no rule is shown to be violated. Which assessment is most defensible?

- **A.** The conduct may satisfy legality yet fail propriety, because it creates a reasonable apprehension of bias in a decision held in trust for others.
- **B.** No ethical issue arises, because ethical assessment begins only after a rule has been breached.
- **C.** The conduct is unethical only if the officer subjectively feels influenced by the hospitality.
- **D.** The conduct is automatically criminal because any hospitality from a bidder is an illegal gratification.

**Answer:** A

**Option-by-option explanation**

- **A — correct.** Legality, morality and propriety are separate tests. Propriety asks what the office and occasion make appropriate, and its remedies are disclosure, recusal and avoidance rather than punishment.
- **B — wrong.** This is the equation that ARC Box 2.3 exists to reject, quoting the warning that "morality has become identical with legality" in public life.
- **C — wrong.** The public test is not the officer's private feeling but whether a reasonable citizen who lost the contract could regard the decision as impartial. Subjective confidence is unverifiable.
- **D — wrong.** Criminality requires the elements of the relevant offence to be made out. Asserting automatic criminality both overstates the law and, by making the bar impossibly high, would in practice acquit most improper conduct.

> **Trap:** The stem deliberately removes both payment and rule-breach so that the only available failure is propriety. Candidates who look for a bribe find none and wrongly conclude that nothing is wrong.

Which statement most accurately reflects the Second ARC's position on prevention and punishment?

#### MCQ 21

A widow's sanctioned pension is withheld by a clerk until a payment is made. Which classification and remedy pairing is correct?

- **A.** Coercive corruption, in which the citizen is an unwilling victim of extortion for an entitled service; the matched remedies are a time-bound service guarantee, published fees, an escalation route and protection of the complainant.
- **B.** Collusive corruption, because a payment has passed between a private person and a public servant, the Commission's test for collusion being simply whether money changed hands outside the sanctioned fee, irrespective of whether the payer sought an entitlement or an irregular advantage.
- **C.** Neither category applies, because no contract or procurement is involved.
- **D.** Collusive corruption, because the citizen has voluntarily paid and is therefore an accomplice.

**Answer:** A

**Option-by-option explanation**

- **A — correct.** ARC 1.13 defines coercive corruption as extraction for a service to which the citizen is entitled and calls the payer "an unwilling victim"; the remedies follow from the fact that the victim has both an interest and a motive to complain.
- **B — wrong.** The passing of money does not by itself make a transaction collusive. Collusion requires that both parties gain at society's cost, which is absent where the payer receives only what she was already owed.
- **C — wrong.** ARC 1.13 places the "vast majority of cases of bribery" in the coercive category and does not confine corruption to contracts or procurement.
- **D — wrong.** Voluntariness in the formal sense is not the test; the report explains that citizens pay because resistance costs them "much more", which is coercion, not complicity.

> **Trap:** The presence of a payment invites automatic classification as collusion. Classify by *who gains*: if the payer gets only her entitlement, the transaction is extortion.

#### MCQ 25

*Targets the error: "ethics and morality are always technically identical."*

Which statement gives the most precise distinction available on demand?

- **A.** Ethics is the systematic, reasoned examination of standards of right conduct, while morality denotes the beliefs and practices actually held by persons or communities; the terms overlap in ordinary usage but must be separated when a claim is being critically assessed.
- **B.** Ethics applies to public life and morality applies only to private life.
- **C.** Ethics is codified in law, whereas morality is never written down.
- **D.** Morality is superior to ethics because it is derived from religious and community teaching tested over centuries, whereas ethics is a modern academic construction that produces argument without obligation and therefore cannot bind an officer who remains unpersuaded by the reasoning offered.

**Answer:** A

**Option-by-option explanation**

- **A — correct.** The distinction is between a reasoned standard and a held practice. It is available on demand without denying that the words are used interchangeably in ordinary Mains prose.
- **B — wrong.** Both terms operate in both domains. A private promise raises an ethical question; a public office raises moral ones. The public/private line is a different distinction, addressed in the private-versus-public relationships session.
- **C — wrong.** Ethics is not defined by codification. Codes of ethics exist, but so do unwritten ethical standards, and much morality is in fact written down in religious and community codes.
- **D — wrong.** Ranking the two is not a distinction, and grounding morality solely in religion ignores its social, familial and professional sources.

> **Trap:** The error is over-correction. Having learned that the terms differ, candidates then deny that they ever overlap, which produces an equally inaccurate answer.

#### MCQ 28

*Targets the error: "if an act is legal, no ethical question arises."*

Which response is best supported by the Second ARC's own material?

- **A.** Legality settles the matter, because public servants can only fairly be judged by enforceable rules published in advance, and any additional standard of propriety would expose an officer to censure for conduct that no provision prohibited at the time it occurred, which is itself unfair.
- **B.** Ethics and legality diverge only in private life, never in official decisions.
- **C.** Ethical evaluation applies only where a court has already found a violation.
- **D.** Legality, morality and propriety are three separate tests; ARC Box 2.3 quotes the warning that "morality has become identical with legality" precisely in order to reject that equation, and describes favours that "offend the spirit of the law but do not violate its letter".

**Answer:** D

**Option-by-option explanation**

- **A — wrong.** This is the very position Box 2.3 was included to criticise, and it is described there as a "tragic plight" for public life.
- **B — wrong.** The Fulbright passage concerns public officials and official favours, so the divergence is illustrated in official life, not excluded from it.
- **C — wrong.** Making judicial finding a precondition would postpone ethical evaluation until after litigation, which is both impractical and contrary to the preventive logic of the whole report.
- **D — correct.** It states the three-test separation and supplies the report's own authority for it, with the letter-versus-spirit formulation.

> **Trap:** Candidates who know the three tests still choose a legality-first option when the stem describes conduct that is clearly lawful. The correct move is to name propriety as the failing test and prescribe disclosure or recusal.

#### MCQ 29

*Targets the paired errors: "liberalisation is unrelated to ethics" and "liberalisation ended corruption."*

Which statement holds both halves of the Second ARC's position together?

- **A.** Competition, choice and transparency demonstrably reduced corruption in named sectors after liberalisation, but where demand for illegitimate funds persists, corruption migrates to natural-monopoly sectors rather than disappearing.
- **B.** Liberalisation is an economic policy with no ethical consequences whatever.
- **C.** Liberalisation eliminated corruption in India across all sectors.
- **D.** Liberalisation increased corruption everywhere by weakening state supervision.

**Answer:** A

**Option-by-option explanation**

- **A — correct.** It joins ARC 1.8's sectoral finding to ARC 1.12's migration warning, which is exactly the balanced position the report holds.
- **B — wrong.** ARC 1.8 attributes a measurable fall in corruption to competition and choice, so denying any ethical consequence contradicts the report directly.
- **C — wrong.** ARC 1.12 states that corruption "shifts to other, sometimes more dangerous, areas", which forecloses any claim of elimination.
- **D — wrong.** The report records reductions, not universal increases. It does warn of new risks, but as relocation rather than as an across-the-board rise.

> **Trap:** The two errors sit on opposite sides, and a candidate correcting one usually falls into the other. Write both sentences — the sectoral gain and the migration caveat — in that order.

#### MCQ 30

*Targets the error: "decentralisation always reduces corruption."*

Which qualification is best supported?

- **A.** Decentralisation is ethically neutral and has no effect on the citizen–official interface.
- **B.** Decentralisation shortens the distance between authority and accountability, but without matching capacity, transparency and audit it can create many small local monopolies of discretion in place of one large one.
- **C.** Decentralisation guarantees accountability because local officials are personally known to the citizens they serve, are answerable at village or ward meetings, and can be confronted immediately about a delayed file, which removes the anonymity on which extraction at a distant office depends.
- **D.** Decentralisation is undesirable because it multiplies the number of decision-makers.

**Answer:** B

**Option-by-option explanation**

- **A — wrong.** ARC 1.9 treats over-centralisation as a corruption-increasing factor, so proximity of power is not ethically neutral.
- **B — correct.** It preserves the benefit the report identifies while adding the capacity, transparency and audit conditions without which local discretion simply replaces central discretion.
- **C — wrong.** Personal acquaintance can as easily produce local capture and pressure as accountability; familiarity is not a substitute for a record and a review route.
- **D — wrong.** ARC 1.9 in fact criticises India for having "probably the smallest number of final decision makers" for a large democracy, so multiplying decision points is not itself the problem.

> **Trap:** Distance and accountability are two different variables. Decentralisation reliably reduces the first; it improves the second only when capacity, transparency and audit accompany it.

#### MCQ 32

*Targets the error: treating Comprehensive National Power as a synonym for military strength.*

Which framing is most defensible in a GS-IV answer?

#### Solved PYQ 1 — 2024 GS-IV, Q1(b) — 10 marks — 150 words — **directly owned**

**Question (verbatim official English wording):** "Ethics encompasses several key dimensions that are crucial in guiding individuals and organizations towards morally responsible behaviour." Explain the key dimensions of ethics that influence human actions. Discuss how these dimensions shape ethical decision&making in the professional context.

**Source note:** exact English text from the locally held official PDF `books\mains\05 UPSC 2024 Paper-IV_Final 1.pdf`, question 1(b), page 2. The printed source itself carries `decision&making`; the artefact is preserved rather than silently corrected.

**Demand decoding.** Two directives, therefore two obligations. **Explain** requires the dimensions to be named and defined as *respects of evaluation*, not as a list of virtues. **Discuss** requires movement from taxonomy to a named professional setting, showing how the dimensions operate on an actual decision. An answer that stops after the list forfeits roughly half the marks whatever its quality.

**Model answer (exam length — 149 words)**

Ethics evaluates action against standards, so its dimensions are the respects in which any decision can be judged. Six carry professional practice: duty and rights, which treat the citizen as an entitlement-holder; consequences, which ask who is harmed and how foreseeably; justice and fairness, which require like cases to be treated alike; virtue, which tests character under pressure; care, which attends to the most vulnerable claimant; and accountability, which demands reasons capable of public scrutiny.

They shape decisions by functioning as mutual checks. A district officer finalising a beneficiary list applies published criteria, hears objections, protects personal data and records reasons for exclusion, thereby satisfying duty, fairness and accountability together. Compassion may prompt help but cannot justify a secret insertion.

No dimension decides alone: rigid rules ignore hardship, outcome-only reasoning rationalises unfair means, and care without fairness becomes favouritism. Sound professional judgment names the conflict and justifies the trade-off.

**Why this earns marks.** It defines "dimensions" correctly, supplies all six, converts them into a named Indian administrative act, and closes with the plural-checks verdict that answers the second directive.

**Expansion bank (not part of the timed answer).** If the question is set at greater length, add: the four levels of ethical enquiry as the prior classification, with the is–ought caution; the ARC 1.1 point that standards require adoption in action, sanctions and competent disciplinary machinery; and the observation that personal good intention cannot cure a biased procedure or an unreviewable decision.

**How to improve a weak attempt.** Replace any abstract sentence about "moral values" with one named dimension plus the specific administrative failure it detects, and end with a trade-off rather than with a restatement of the opening.

#### Solved PYQ 2 — 2025 GS-IV, Q1(b) — 10 marks — 150 words — **cross-topic anchor**

**Question (verbatim official English wording):** "Constitutional morality is not a natural sentiment but a product of civil education and adherance of the rule of law." Examine the significance of constitutional morality for public servant highlighting the role in promoting good governance and ensuring accountability in public administration.

**Source note:** exact English text from the locally held official PDF `books\mains\UPSC Mains 2025 GS Paper 4.pdf`, question 1(b), page 2. The printed source carries `adherance`; the spelling is preserved.

**Ownership note.** ⚠️ The routing ledger assigns this question to Ethics Topics 13 and 14, and the official syllabus mapping records that "Constitutional morality is Topic 14 primary with Topic 10 as a guidance-source cross-link." It is retained here as a cross-topic anchor, not claimed as Topic 01 property, because its premise — that constitutional morality is *produced* by civil education and rule-of-law practice rather than felt naturally — is exactly the values-plus-institutions synthesis of ARC 1.5.

**Demand decoding.** **Examine** requires a position plus its limits. The stem supplies the thesis; the candidate must show *how* constitutional morality operates, and must cover both listed functions — good governance and accountability — with an administrative illustration.

**Model answer (exam length — 148 words)**

Constitutional morality is disciplined fidelity to constitutional values and lawful procedure rather than to personal preference, majority sentiment or official convenience. Because it is acquired through civic education and rule-of-law practice, it must be converted into daily conduct: equality, dignity, liberty, fair procedure and accountability.

It promotes good governance in three ways. It restrains arbitrary power, since published criteria, hearings and recorded reasons displace discretionary favour. It protects minorities and vulnerable persons when local prejudice is popular. It makes decisions auditable, because the officer can name the value and rule supporting the action. A District Magistrate allocating flood relief must apply transparent need-based criteria across caste, religion and political affiliation, record exclusions and provide grievance review.

It does not require mechanical rule-worship. Exceptional hardship may demand humane discretion, but discretion must stay reasoned, non-discriminatory and reviewable. Constitutional morality is thus the bridge between legal authority and legitimate administration.

**Why this earns marks.** It accepts the stem's premise, converts it into three named governance mechanisms, supplies an Indian illustration, and qualifies with the discretion caveat instead of ending on a slogan.

**Link back to Topic 01.** The claim that constitutional morality is "not a natural sentiment" is the ARC's own position in different words: values exist in abundance but are perishable, so institutions must contain them.

#### Routed Demand 1 — 2020 GS-IV Q1(a) — 10 marks — neutral rendering

#### Routed Demand 2 — 2020 GS-IV Q1(b) — 10 marks — neutral rendering

**Neutral rendering of the routed demand:** Examine education as a pervasive tool for individual development and social transformation, with reference to NEP 2020.

#### Routed Demand 3 — 2020 GS-IV Q2(a) — 10 marks — neutral rendering

**Neutral rendering of the routed demand:** Hatred is destructive of wisdom and conscience — do you agree? Justify your answer.

**Source / ownership:** ⚠️ Neutral rendering from the 2018–2023 routed-demand ledger; the paper is not held locally. Routed to Ethics Topic 01 and Topic 05, with Topic 05 owning the emotional-intelligence limb.

**Demand decoding.** **Do you agree / justify** requires an explicit side, defended against the strongest objection rather than merely asserted.

**Model answer (exam length — 150 words)**

I agree, with one qualification. Hatred is destructive of wisdom because it fixes judgment before evidence: the hating official already knows who is guilty, so contrary facts are discounted and the decision is made by the emotion rather than by the file. It is destructive of conscience because conscience works by imagining the other person's standpoint, which hatred forbids.

**Why this earns marks.** It takes a side, explains two distinct mechanisms of damage, supplies an administrative illustration, and separates hatred from legitimate moral indignation.

#### Routed Demand 4 — 2022 GS-IV Q2(a) — 10 marks — neutral rendering

**Neutral rendering of the routed demand:** Explain how a positive and a negative mindset can lead to different interpretations of rules and regulations.

**Source / ownership:** ⚠️ Neutral rendering from the 2018–2023 routed-demand ledger; the paper is not held locally. Routed to Topic 03 (attitude — primary) and Ethics Topic 01. Retained here for its human-interface consequence.

**Demand decoding.** **Explain** requires the mechanism by which an attitude changes interpretation, then a case where the two readings diverge in their effect on a citizen.

**Model answer (exam length — 147 words)**

A mindset decides what an official believes a rule is *for*. A positive mindset treats regulation as an instrument for securing fairness, safety and public trust, so ambiguity is resolved towards the rule's purpose. A negative mindset treats regulation chiefly as an obstacle, a source of leverage or a shield against responsibility, so ambiguity is resolved towards refusal.

The consequence is unequal service from identical rules. Faced with an incomplete application, the first officer identifies the missing document, records a deficiency memo and fixes a date; the second returns the file unexplained, converting a curable defect into a fresh journey for the applicant. Neither has broken a rule.

The qualification is that positivity is not indulgence. Purposive interpretation must stay within the rule's text and be recorded, otherwise discretion becomes personal. The ethical standard is a reading that serves the rule's object and can be stated publicly.

**Why this earns marks.** It locates the difference in interpretation rather than in compliance, illustrates identical rules producing unequal outcomes, and guards against the "positive attitude means bending rules" misreading.

#### Routed Demand 5 — 2022 GS-IV Q2(b) — 10 marks — neutral rendering

**Neutral rendering of the routed demand:** Discuss ethics in human actions and its role in resolving conflicts in everyday functioning.

**Source / ownership:** ⚠️ Neutral rendering from the 2018–2023 routed-demand ledger; the paper is not held locally. Routed to Topic 03 and Ethics Topic 01.

**Demand decoding.** **Discuss** requires a working definition of ethics in action, then its specific contribution to conflict resolution, then its limits.

**Model answer (exam length — 148 words)**

Ethics in human action means choosing conduct that can be justified by standards — honesty, fairness, non-harm, empathy, duty and accountability — rather than by preference or convenience. Everyday conflicts involve not only competing interests but unequal power, incomplete information and questions of respect.

Ethics contributes three things to their resolution. It converts a clash of wills into a comparison of reasons, so the stronger party cannot simply prevail. It identifies whose interest is an entitlement and whose is a preference, which fixes the order of priority. It supplies procedures — hearing, disclosure, recorded reasons, review — that make the outcome acceptable even to the party that loses.

Its limits must be stated. Ethical reasoning cannot settle a dispute where facts are contested, and it cannot substitute for authority where a lawful decision is required. Its function is to make the exercise of authority defensible, not to remove the need for it.

**Why this earns marks.** It defines ethics in action operationally, gives three named contributions to conflict resolution, and closes with the honest limit that ethics does not replace lawful authority.

#### Original Mains Practice 1 — 10 marks — 150 words

**Question:** Distinguish ethics from morality. Why does the distinction matter to a public servant? Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer (147 words)**

Ethics is the systematic, reasoned examination of standards of right conduct; morality is the set of beliefs and practices a person or community actually holds. The words overlap in ordinary use, but the distinction matters because a widespread practice is not thereby justified.

For a public servant, morality explains why colleagues treat a festival gift, a political recommendation or preferential treatment as normal. Ethics asks a further question: is the conduct fair, compatible with public duty, non-harmful and publicly defensible? A revenue officer cannot prioritise a relative's mutation file merely because family loyalty is socially valued; public office requires impartiality and equal access.

The distinction must not become contempt for community values, which often reveal genuine hardship and guide humane delivery. But where custom conflicts with constitutional equality or public trust, the officer must reason ethically and act through lawful procedure rather than defer to prevailing belief.

**Why this earns marks.** It defines both terms, states the practical stake, gives a named administrative example, and qualifies against the opposite error of dismissing community morality altogether.

#### Original Mains Practice 2 — 10 marks — 150 words

**Question:** Why is conduct that is legal not necessarily proper in public office? Illustrate. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer (146 words)**

Legality means conformity with enacted law; propriety asks whether conduct suits the responsibilities and the visible standing of the office. The gap matters because rules cannot enumerate every conflict, and public confidence can be lost before any violation is proved.

A district procurement officer may lawfully attend a private dinner hosted by a bidder where no payment or formal favour is established. The event can still create a reasonable apprehension of bias and compromise the officer's independence. The prudent response is disclosure, recusal where the nexus is direct, and avoidance of hospitality a citizen could fairly read as influence. Similar reasoning covers informal meetings, use of official information and public praise for connected firms.

Propriety must not become vague policing of private life; it requires a nexus with office, entrusted power or public perception. Legal compliance is therefore the floor of public conduct, not its ceiling.

**Why this earns marks.** It separates the two tests, illustrates with a lawful but improper act, prescribes the matching remedy, and guards the boundary against moral policing.

#### Original Mains Practice 3 — 10 marks — 150 words

**Question:** "Values without institutional support will soon be weakened and dissipated." Examine this proposition with reference to Indian public administration. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer (149 words)**

The proposition, drawn from the Second ARC's Fourth Report, holds that values are necessary but perishable, and that institutions supply the container which gives them shape and content.

Indian experience supports it. Two districts recruiting from the same cadre and academy can differ sharply in grievance redress; the variable is rarely the officers' values and usually the container — published timelines, a functioning review meeting and a supervisor who reads the pendency list. The Commission's own observation that corruption fell where competition, transparency and computerisation removed discretion points the same way: incentives changed, characters did not.

The proposition must not be read as dismissing character. The same paragraph insists values are needed as guiding stars and exist in abundance in our society.

The defensible verdict is therefore joint: institutional design without value formation buys compliance that lapses when monitoring lapses, while values without containment dissipate exactly as the report warns.

**Why this earns marks.** It uses the report's own metaphor, supplies a comparative Indian illustration and a sourced mechanism, protects the value half of the synthesis, and ends with a two-sided verdict.

#### Original Mains Practice 4 — 15 marks — extended analytical practice (not a GS-IV paper format)

**Question:** Evaluate the claim that corruption is structurally produced rather than merely a failure of character. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer (243 words)**

Corruption is partly a failure of character, but it is largely structurally enabled. The Second ARC connects risk to a specific bundle — public authority, discretion and control over public funds — and then to three Indian aggravators: a colonial legacy of unchallenged authority, an enormous asymmetry of power, and an early-decades policy regime of over-regulation and scarcity that converted the public servant into patron and master while reducing the citizen's capacity to resist extortionary demands.

A character-only explanation recommends moral renewal. It is necessary, because integrity, empathy and courage do influence conduct under pressure, and the Commission itself insists values are needed as guiding stars. Yet it cannot explain why corruption fell in telephones, steel, cement, sugar and two-wheelers, or in railway reservation and driving-licence issuance, where competition, transparency and computerisation changed incentives while leaving characters untouched. In land administration, time-stamped digital records, published fees and an appeal route similarly reduce a clerk's scope to demand speed money from a vulnerable applicant.

Structural reform has real limits. Automation can exclude digitally weak citizens and recreate a rent through intermediaries. Decentralisation can produce many small local monopolies of discretion rather than one large one. Most seriously, the Commission warns that where political demand for illegitimate funds persists, corruption migrates to natural-monopoly sectors instead of disappearing.

The false choice must therefore be rejected. Character makes integrity possible; institutions make it durable and verifiable; and political-finance reform sets the ceiling on what administrative redesign alone can achieve.

**Why this earns marks.** It evaluates rather than asserts, supplies the report's full sectoral evidence, tests the structural claim against three named limits, and closes with a layered verdict instead of a compromise formula.

#### Original Mains Practice 5 — 15 marks — extended analytical practice (not a GS-IV paper format)

**Question:** Distinguish coercive from collusive corruption and draw out the implications for the ethical response. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer (250 words)**

Coercive corruption occurs when a public functionary extracts payment for a service to which the citizen is already entitled; the Second ARC calls such a citizen an unwilling victim, noting that resistance costs delay, harassment, lost opportunity, lost time and wages, uncertainty and at times danger to life or limb. Collusive corruption occurs when giver and taker jointly secure private gain while society bears the loss.

The examples differ accordingly. A clerk withholding a sanctioned pension until payment is made is coercion: the widow seeks no improper advantage and is constrained by need. An engineer and contractor certifying substandard road work is collusion; the Commission's own list extends to procurement, recruitment, tax evasion, collusive violation of regulations, adulteration of food and drugs, obstruction of justice and the doctoring of evidence.

The ethical implications diverge sharply. Coercion overrides the citizen's autonomy, so the response is protective: time-bound service guarantees, published fees, single-window delivery, automation of the discretionary step, a safe complaint channel and protection of the complainant. Collusion is a joint appropriation with no complaining witness, so the response is investigative: procurement transparency, third-party technical audit, asset and beneficial-interest scrutiny, whistle-blower protection and prosecution of both sides.

Two qualifications matter. The categories blur, since a firm first extorted may later seek advantage, so labels must follow facts, not stereotypes. And the Commission records that as the economy is freed from controls, extortionary corruption declines while collusive corruption tends to increase — which is why audit capacity now matters more than complaint counters.

**Why this earns marks.** It distinguishes by who gains, reproduces the report's own example list, derives two different remedy sets from the distinction, and adds the blurring and trend qualifications.

#### Original Mains Practice 6 — 20 marks — 250 words

**Question:** Citizen empowerment is not a welfare add-on but an ethical redesign of the human interface. Discuss. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer (248 words)**

Citizen empowerment is an ethical redesign because it changes the relationship between the holder of public power and the person dependent on it. At the human interface the official controls information, time, certification and access. Where an unorganised or poor citizen cannot question those controls, a formal entitlement becomes dependence and discretion becomes extraction — which is precisely the asymmetry of power the Second ARC identifies as an aggravating cause of corruption.

The Commission names six correctives in a single sentence: Right to Information, effective citizens' charters, opportunity and incentives for a proactive citizenry, stakeholders' involvement in the delivery of public services, public consultation in decision-making, and social auditing. Each dismantles a distinct component of the asymmetry. Information ends the official's informational monopoly. A meaningful charter converts a favour into a declared standard by naming the service, fee, timeline, responsible officer and grievance route. Stakeholder involvement addresses design capture; consultation forces reasons before rather than after the decision; social audit tests the record against lived reality. In a rural works programme, reading the muster roll aloud in the village exposes entries that a closed departmental file conceals.

Because empowerment is the exact inverse of a diagnosed cause, it is structural rather than supplementary. Two limits must still be stated: every instrument presupposes literacy, awareness, time and organisational capacity, so elite capture can reproduce the asymmetry inside the remedy; and the Commission insists that prevention be joined to enforcement. Empowerment therefore promotes dignity and substantive equality, not merely administrative efficiency.

**Why this earns marks.** It argues from a diagnosed cause to a matched remedy, reproduces the complete six-instrument list with a distinct function for each, adds an Indian illustration, and states the capture and enforcement limits.

#### Original Mains Practice 7 — 20 marks — 250 words

**Question:** How can constitutional morality and role-based civil-service capacity together improve the ethical quality of public administration? Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer (247 words)**

Constitutional morality supplies the normative compass of public administration; role-based capacity supplies the ability to practise it where authority meets citizen need. The first requires fidelity to equality, dignity, liberty, fair procedure and accountable power even when prejudice or convenience points elsewhere. The second recognises that ethical administration depends on functional and behavioural competence, not on rules memorised in isolation.

A constitutionally disciplined public servant gives reasons, avoids discrimination, hears affected persons and keeps discretion reviewable. Capacity determines whether that intention survives contact with a real file. Mission Karmayogi is the current national instrument: the Department of Personnel and Training describes the National Programme for Civil Services Capacity Building as focused on the citizen–government interface, on functional and behavioural competencies, and on a shift from a rules-based to a roles-based civil service, delivered through six pillars including the iGOT-Karmayogi platform. A Press Information Bureau release dated 6 February 2026 reports 1.49 crore users, 4,342 courses, 7.26 crore completions and 23 languages; these are dated release figures, not an undated programme total.

The complementarity is exact. A welfare officer handling disability benefits needs procedural knowledge, data discipline, empathy, communication and the courage to refuse a politically favoured but ineligible claim. Capacity without constitutional morality becomes efficient exclusion; morality without capability remains an aspiration that cannot deliver timely justice.

The honest limit is that training changes competence faster than it changes incentives, so posting, transfer and disciplinary practice must reward the conduct the training teaches.

**Why this earns marks.** It pairs a normative and an operational input, grounds the operational half in a dated official source with its status stated, supplies a concrete service illustration, and ends on the incentive limit rather than on praise for the programme.

#### Original Mains Practice 8 — 20 marks — GS-IV Section-B case study — 250 words

**Case.** You are the Sub-Divisional Magistrate of a sub-division where mutation of agricultural land is a notified time-bound service. A widow, Kamala, has applied three times over eight months. The dealing clerk returns her file each time citing a fresh "deficiency". Her neighbour tells her the file will move for a payment of a fixed sum. Kamala is illiterate, is losing a crop season, and now wants only her mutation, not a complaint. Your office has a single dealing clerk for the whole sub-division, and the previous SDM's transfer followed a complaint against the same clerk.

**Question:** Identify the stakeholders and the values in conflict, evaluate at least three options, state your decision with reasons, and describe implementation and residual-risk mitigation. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer (243 words)**

**Stakeholders and interests.** Kamala — an entitled service, an unlost crop season, and freedom from retaliation. Other applicants in the queue — equal, non-extractive treatment. The dealing clerk — due process before adverse action. My office and the revenue administration — lawful record, credibility and continuity of service. The state — deterrence and revenue integrity.

**Values in conflict.** Immediate relief for a vulnerable citizen versus systemic deterrence; procedural fairness to the clerk versus urgency; my duty to act on credible information versus Kamala's wish not to complain.

**Options.** (1) Grant relief only: dispose of the mutation myself. It rescues Kamala but leaves the extraction machinery intact for the next applicant. (2) Act punitively only: place the clerk under inquiry immediately. It signals deterrence but, on hearsay alone, is procedurally weak and would stall every pending file in a single-clerk office. (3) Combine relief with systemic correction and a properly founded inquiry.

**Decision.** Option 3. Dispose of Kamala's mutation within the notified period through a recorded order that lists the deficiencies raised and their disposal.

**Implementation.** Publish the mutation checklist, fee and timeline at the counter; require every deficiency memo to be issued in writing with the applicant's acknowledgement; audit the sub-division's returned files for repeat-deficiency patterns; and open proceedings on that documented evidence rather than on rumour.

**Residual risk and mitigation.** Kamala may face retaliation — monitor her file personally and record my order. The office may slow down — seek a second dealing hand and set weekly pendency review.

**Why this earns marks.** It uses the eight-element architecture owned by Topic 22, refuses a two-option framing, protects the coerced citizen without prejudging the clerk, and converts a single grievance into a documented systemic correction.

#### Original Mains Practice 9 — 20 marks — GS-IV Section-B case study — 250 words

**Case.** You head a district health society. A firm supplying diagnostic kits has been technically qualified by your evaluation committee. During the evaluation you learn that the firm's promoter is the brother-in-law of your immediate superior, that the firm hosted a "product familiarisation dinner" attended by two committee members, and that the firm's bid is the lowest and technically strongest on paper. Procurement is urgent: a seasonal outbreak is under way, and any delay will cost testing capacity. No rule has demonstrably been broken.

**Question:** Identify the ethical issues, evaluate at least three options, decide, and state your safeguards. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer (240 words)**

**Ethical issues.** Three overlap. First, a conflict of interest through my superior's relationship, which I did not create but now know of. Second, an impropriety — hospitality accepted by evaluators from a bidder — which offends the spirit of impartial evaluation even if no rule is shown to be violated. Third, a genuine consequentialist urgency: delay costs testing capacity during an outbreak.

**Stakeholders.** Patients awaiting testing; other bidders entitled to a fair process; the two committee members; my superior; and the institution, whose future procurements depend on present credibility.

**Options.** (1) Proceed and record nothing, relying on the bid being lowest and best. This maximises speed but leaves the award indefensible if the relationship later surfaces. (2) Cancel and re-tender. This protects appearances but sacrifices real patients to a defect that can be cured procedurally. (3) Proceed with disclosure, reconstitution and independent verification.

**Decision.** Option 3. Record the relationship and the dinner in writing on the file; recuse the two members who attended and replace them; have the technical evaluation re-validated by the reconstituted committee; and place the award before the society with the disclosure attached.

**Steelmanned objection.** It will be said that reconstitution is delay dressed as ethics. The answer is that a documented one-week revalidation is far cheaper than a cancelled contract mid-outbreak.

**Safeguards and residual risk.** Publish the comparative statement; provide for staggered delivery with acceptance testing; and retain the option of an emergency alternative supplier if performance falters.

**Why this earns marks.** It names the propriety failure that no rule catches, refuses both the "no rule broken" and the "cancel everything" extremes, steelmans the strongest objection to its own decision, and secures the outcome with verifiable safeguards.

⚠️ **Advanced analytical claim.** The human interface is not a fixed moral test but a *structurally produced site*: its ethical temperature moves with discretion, transparency, centralisation and the integrity of the political market, not with exhortation alone.

### A2. Structural versus dispositional causation

⚠️ The ARC's diagnosis privileges structural causes — discretion, monopoly, centralisation — over a purely dispositional "bad people" account, while conceding that both operate together. The internal evidence is paragraph 1.8: liberalisation changed incentives, not characters, and corruption fell. A purely dispositional theory cannot generate that prediction.

⚠️ **The displacement corollary.** Reducing corruption in competitive sectors while leaving natural-monopoly sectors — land, policing, discretionary permissions — untouched relocates rather than reduces aggregate corruption. This is ARC 1.12's warning stated as a policy corollary.

⚠️ **The empowerment corollary.** Because the asymmetry-of-power argument is a *causal* claim, citizen-empowerment tools are not optional good-governance additions but structurally necessary correctives. Topics 15 and 17 own their statutory and service-delivery detail.

### A3. International and domestic reform architecture, with corrected status

✅ ARC 1.14 names the contemporaneous international correctives: the UN Convention against Corruption, adopted by the UN General Assembly in October 2003; the ADB-OECD Anti-Corruption Action Plan for Asia and the Pacific, "signed by the Government of India"; the World Bank's refusal to fund projects whose implementation is tainted by corrupt practices; and the 2006 IMF–World Bank Singapore joint statement with major multilateral financial institutions on a framework for preventing and combating fraud and corruption.

📰 **Status update on the report's 2007 framing.** UNCAC was adopted by UNGA resolution 58/4 on 31 October 2003. India signed it on 9 December 2005 and **ratified it on 9 May 2011** (UN Treaty Collection / UNODC status list), so the report's "signed" position is historical. India endorsed the ADB-OECD Anti-Corruption Action Plan on 30 November 2001. India is **not** a party to the separate OECD Anti-Bribery Convention, 1997 — the two instruments must not be conflated.

### A4. Boundary cases that refine the diagnosis

- ⚠️ **Discretionary decentralisation.** MPLADS-style discretionary schemes, examined in Topic 18, show how even a well-intentioned decentralisation instrument can recreate a coercive or collusive interface where discretion is not matched by transparency.
- ⚠️ **Automation without market competition.** Land-record digitisation initiatives such as Bhoomi and Gyandoot, discussed in Topic 23, show the ARC's competition/transparency corrective operating without literal market competition: removing discretion through automation reduces the asymmetry at the counter directly.
- ⚠️ **The transparent monopoly.** A single-window, IT-enabled utility payment counter is a monopoly and can still be low-corruption. Monopoly per se is not the driver; **discretion without transparency** is. Monopoly with automation and an audit trail can be clean.

### A5. Limitations and trade-offs of the structural programme

- ⚠️ Structural correctives can be undermined where the political market itself demands illegitimate funds; systemic reform without political-finance reform has a ceiling (ARC 1.12).
- ⚠️ Decentralisation reduces the *distance* between authority and accountability but can, without matching capacity and audit, create many small local monopolies of discretion instead of one large one.
- ⚠️ Citizen-empowerment tools presuppose a threshold of literacy, awareness and organisational capacity that is unevenly distributed; elite capture can reproduce the asymmetry inside the empowerment mechanism itself.

### A6. Advanced factual anchors

- ✅ The report frames three specific historical aggravating factors — colonial legacy, asymmetry of power, and post-Independence over-regulation (paragraphs 1.6–1.7).
- ⚠️ Coercive corruption is generally easier to bring to book than collusive corruption because the coerced payer retains an incentive to testify, whereas in collusion neither party will complain. The mechanism follows from ARC 1.13's own definitions; the statutory mechanics belong to Topic 19.
- ✅ The report frames the "quality of politics" — not law or administration — as "perhaps the most important determinant" of societal integrity (paragraph 1.12).

### A7. Advanced traps

- ❌ Structural reform alone (competition, information technology) is sufficient to end corruption. → ARC 1.12 warns that persisting political demand relocates corruption into non-competitive, natural-monopoly sectors.
- ❌ Decentralisation always reduces corruption. → It reduces the distance between authority and accountability but does not guarantee accountability without transparency and capacity.
- ❌ The 90%-unorganised-sector figure is a current labour-force statistic. → It is a dated 2007 illustrative figure inside the report.

### A8. Current-anchor discipline

📰 When a question asks for present-day analysis, cite the structural-cause arguments — asymmetry of power, over-centralisation — alongside current, independently verified governance data such as the latest Economic Survey's employment and formalisation statistics, rather than the ARC's 2007 illustrative figures.

### A9. Advanced Mains framework

**Central thesis.** The ethical quality of the human interface is a function of discretion, transparency, centralisation and political-market integrity — not of character alone; durable reform therefore combines value formation with institutional redesign.

1. Define ethics as habit-based standard and corruption as its failure, precisely.
2. State the ARC's two competing causal theories, then its own institutions-plus-values synthesis.
3. Explain the three Indian-specific structural aggravators.
4. Apply the coercive/collusive distinction to classify the dilemma actually posed.
5. Recommend correctives across all three levers — competition and transparency, citizen empowerment, and enforcement — while flagging the political-market ceiling on purely administrative reform.

### The ARC definition and what it actually requires

- ✅ Ethics = "a set of standards that society places on itself" guiding "behaviour, choices and actions" (ARC 1.1).
- ✅ Standards alone do not ensure ethical behaviour; the crux lies in **adoption in action**, **sanctions against violations**, **competent disciplinary bodies acting quickly**, and **promoting a culture of integrity** (ARC 1.1).
- ✅ Corruption = "an important manifestation of the failure of ethics" (ARC 1.2).
- ✅ *ethikos* = arising from habit (Greek); *corruptus* = to break or destroy (Latin) (ARC 1.2).
- ✅ Grand corruption (persons in high places) vs retail corruption ("touching the everyday life of common people") (ARC 1.2).

### Dimensions of ethics — two layers

**Layer 1: four levels of enquiry.** Descriptive (what is in fact believed) · Normative (what ought to be done, and on what ground) · Meta-ethical (what moral words mean, whether they can be true) · Applied (what a specific field must do). GS-IV is normative and applied; meta-ethics is worth at most one line. The is–ought gap is the load-bearing rule: a social fact is not a justification.

**Layer 2: six working dimensions of a public decision.** Duty and rights (citizen as entitlement-holder) · Consequences (who is harmed, how foreseeably) · Justice and fairness (like cases alike; reasoned exclusion) · Virtue and character (what the decision habituates) · Care and empathy (the most vulnerable claimant) · Accountability and transparency (reasons capable of public scrutiny). They function as **mutual checks**; the examinable verdict is a reasoned plural one that names the conflict.

### The precision grid and the three tests

| Term | Test applied |
|---|---|
| Ethics | Can this conduct be justified by argument? |
| Morality | What does this person or community actually hold right? |
| Values | What do I consistently prize across situations? |
| Law | Does an enacted rule permit or forbid this? |
| Propriety | Would a reasonable citizen see this as suited to the office? |
| Conscience | Can I answer for this to myself on reflection? |

- Legal is not the same as moral, and moral is not the same as proper. Remedy map: illegality → sanction; immorality → argument; impropriety → disclosure, recusal, avoidance.
- ✅ ARC Box 2.3 (Senator Fulbright; Senate Ethics Manual, 2003 ed., p.6): favours accepted "under the guise of friendship, which offend the spirit of the law but do not violate its letter"; "morality has become identical with legality... a tragic plight if the accepted standard... is that he keep within the letter of the law."

### Determinants — individual

- Conscience · family and early socialisation · civil education · religion, philosophy and cultural inheritance · peer and professional culture · incentive structure · personality.
- ✅ ARC 1.5: values "exist in abundance in our society"; "a sense of right and wrong is intrinsic to our culture and civilization". The scarce input is the condition under which acting on values is survivable.
- ✅ ARC 1.4 incentive rule: consistent reward and punishment keep "the bulk of the people" on the honourable path; where good behaviour is "fraught with difficulties" and bad behaviour "extravagantly rewarded", they "stray".
- 📰 Mission Karmayogi acts on professional culture at scale: citizen–government interface, functional **and** behavioural competencies, rules-based to roles-based shift, six pillars including iGOT-Karmayogi.

### Determinants — structural

- ✅ **The bundle (1.5):** public office = authority + discretion + control over the public purse → "enormous temptation and opportunity to promote private gain at public cost"; hence "creation of institutions and designing of incentives are of utmost importance".
- ✅ **Aggravator 1 (1.6):** colonial legacy of "unchallenged authority and propensity to exercise power arbitrarily"; "in a society which worships power, it is easy for public officials to deviate".
- ✅ **Aggravator 2 (1.6):** asymmetry of power — ~90% in the unorganized sector; ~70% of organized workers with job security and regular monthly wage employed by the state directly or through PSUs; employment carries "all the trappings of power" → "reduces societal pressure to conform to ethical behaviour". **2007 illustrative figures; never cite as current data.**
- ✅ **Aggravator 3 (1.7):** over-regulation, severe restrictions, excessive state control, near-monopoly, economy of scarcity, subsidies under asymmetry → "converted the public servant into patron and master and reduced most citizens into mendicants"; reduced "the citizens' capacity to resist extortionary demands". The consequence is expressly **unintended**.
- ✅ **Multiplier 1 (1.8):** monopoly + discretion increase; competition + transparency reduce. Named sectors: **telephones, steel, cement, sugar, two-wheelers**; named computerised services: **railway reservation, driving licences**.
- ✅ **Multiplier 2 (1.9):** over-centralisation widens the distance between authority and accountability; "India probably has the smallest number of final decision makers"; local government "not allowed to take root" → "weakened citizenry and mounting corruption".
- ✅ **Ceiling (1.12):** "quality of politics" is "perhaps the most important determinant"; where demand for illegitimate funds persists, "other avenues of corruption will be forcibly opened up" and corruption shifts to **natural-monopoly** sectors. Prescription: "corresponding political and governance reform".

### Consequences — six channels plus the positive mirror

1. **Transactional (1.13):** delays, harassment, lost opportunity, lost time and wages, uncertainty, "at times, potential danger of loss of life or limb" — the costs of *resisting*.
2. **Distributive (1.2):** retail corruption falls hardest on common people.
3. **Relational (1.7):** patron-and-master versus mendicant.
4. **Systemic (1.16):** black money, serious economic offences and fraud, money laundering "leading even to the funding of terrorist activities against the State".
5. **Legitimacy (1.3):** interventions seen as "mere posturing" and as "handy weapons for partisan, political use"; corruption regarded as inevitable; cynicism "bodes ill for our democratic system itself".
6. **Developmental (Preface):** "non-expropriation, contract enforcement, and decrease in bureaucratic delays and corruption can raise the GDP growth rate significantly"; six governance-quality measures listed, of which **rule of law** and **absence of graft** are "most directly significant".

**Positive mirror:** predictability lowers transaction risk; equal treatment builds social harmony and lowers enforcement cost; reviewable reasons reduce disputes and speed disposal.

### Coercive versus collusive corruption (1.13)

| | Coercive | Collusive |
|---|---|---|
| Who pays | Citizen entitled to the service | Bribe-giver seeking the irregular outcome |
| Citizen's position | "an unwilling victim of coercive corruption" | Beneficiary |
| Who gains | Only the official | "both parties benefit at immense cost to society" |
| Detection | Victim has a motive to complain | No complaining witness |
| ARC examples | Extraction for an entitled service | Contracts for public works; procurement of goods and services; recruitment; tax evasion; substandard projects; collusive violation of regulations; adulteration of foods and drugs; obstruction of justice; concealing or doctoring evidence |
| Remedy | Time-bound guarantee, published fee, single window, automation, safe complaint channel, protection of complainant | Procurement transparency, third-party technical audit, asset and beneficial-interest scrutiny, whistle-blower protection, investigate both sides |

- ✅ Trend: as the economy is freed from state controls, extortionary corruption declines and collusive corruption tends to increase — "undermining the very foundations of our democracy".
- ⚠️ Boundary: categories blur; labels follow facts, not stereotypes about bribe-givers.

### Private and public relationships (1.17)

- Private relationships permit partiality; public office is **fiduciary** and forbids it. Resources are entrusted, not owned; discretion is delegated power, not liberty.
- Three crossing points: **nepotism/favouritism**, **conflict of interest** (disclose → recuse → reallocate, in writing), **gifts, hospitality and post-retirement offers**.
- The public test is not "did I feel biased?" but "can I state this reason publicly, and would the citizen who lost accept it?"
- ✅ ARC 1.17: ethics in governance "has a much wider import"; the effort must include corporate ethics with a "paradigm shift from the pejorative 'business ethics' to 'ethics in business'", ethics in "every profession, voluntary organization and civil society structure", and "ethics in citizen behaviour because such behaviour impinges directly on ethics in government and administration".
- ⚠️ Reciprocity limit: citizen duty binds fully in collusive conduct, weakly where the citizen is coerced.

### The three corrective levers

1. **Structural:** remove the alternative gap (competition and choice), the information gap (transparency, published rule and fee) and the discretion gap (automation). ✅ ARC 1.15 named initiatives: Supreme Court ruling requiring candidates to file **wealth, educational qualifications and criminal antecedents** with nomination papers; the **RTI Act**; and **ICT/e-governance/automation of corruption-prone processes**.
2. **Citizen empowerment (1.10), six instruments:** Right to Information · effective citizens' charters · opportunity and incentives for a proactive citizenry · stakeholders' involvement in delivery · public consultation in decision-making · social auditing. ARC's verdict: they "dramatically curbed corruption and promoted integrity and quality of decision making". ⚠️ Elite-capture limit applies.
3. **Prevention AND punishment (1.11, 1.16):** "enforcement of rule of law and deterrent punishment... are critical"; also demanded is "a detailed analysis of our anti-corruption mechanisms and the causes of their failure". ✅ 1.16 **recommendations of January 2007** (not then law): forfeiture of benami properties and illegally acquired assets; whistleblower legislation "against retribution"; a stronger investigative framework; exemplary punishment "raising the risk associated with corrupt behaviour".

### The synthesis (1.4–1.5)

- Approach 1: "overemphasis on values and character" — nothing improves until values are restored.
- Approach 2: the "deviant minority" — most are decent; organised government punishes the few.
- ✅ ARC's own position: "In the real world, both values and institutions matter." Values are "guiding stars" and "exist in abundance"; but "values without institutional support will soon be weakened and dissipated", because "institutions provide the container, which gives shape and content to values. This is the basis of all statecraft and laws and institutions."

### Ethics and Comprehensive National Power

| Component | Mechanism by which ethics strengthens it |
|---|---|
| Economic | Predictable rule of law and low graft cut the risk premium; the Preface links non-expropriation, contract enforcement and reduced delay to GDP growth |
| Military / strategic | Procurement integrity buys capability on merit, not commission; apolitical professionalism keeps the chain of command trusted |
| Socio-cultural / soft | Citizen empowerment builds internal legitimacy, which becomes external credibility |
| Technological | Research integrity and transparent data/AI norms sustain confidence in results |
| Diplomatic | Honoured treaty and humanitarian commitments accumulate credibility force cannot buy |

⚠️ Thesis: ethics **multiplies** usable capacity rather than substituting for it; social harmony is its most direct contribution; the causal link is indirect and slower than one policy cycle.

### Standing examiner traps

1. Defining ethics as "moral values" rather than ARC 1.1's social-operational definition.
2. Treating ethics and morality as always identical — or over-correcting into "never overlapping".
3. "If it is legal, no ethical question arises" — rejected by Box 2.3.
4. Attributing a single "decline of values" cause to the ARC — rejected at 1.4–1.5.
5. Blaming the coerced citizen — contradicted at 1.13.
6. Quoting the 90%/70% figures as current statistics — they are 2007 illustrative framing.
7. Claiming liberalisation ended corruption — capped by the migration effect at 1.12.
8. Equating Comprehensive National Power with military strength alone.
