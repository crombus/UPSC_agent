## BASIC MCQS / REMEDIATION

### MCQ 01 — Instrument taxonomy

A government requires every covered fuel supplier to pay a legally fixed amount for each tonne of CO2 embodied in the fuel sold. There is no tradable emissions cap. Which instrument is this?

A. Carbon tax
B. Cap-and-trade ETS
C. Baseline-and-credit system
D. Voluntary offset programme

**Answer: A**

**Explanation:** A carbon tax directly fixes the price per unit of emissions or carbon content; the quantity response is not fixed in advance. A cap-and-trade ETS fixes a covered quantity and creates allowances. Baseline-and-credit creates credits for outperforming a benchmark. A voluntary offset programme issues or transacts credits without the stated tax liability.

---

### MCQ 02 — Assertion and reason: ETS

**Assertion (A):** A conventional cap-and-trade ETS gives stronger ex-ante control over covered emissions quantity than a carbon tax.
**Reason (R):** The regulator fixes the total allowance supply while market trading discovers the allowance price.

A. Both A and R are correct, but R does not explain A
B. Both A and R are correct, and R explains A
C. A is correct, but R is incorrect
D. A is incorrect, but R is correct

**Answer: B**

**Explanation:** In a conventional ETS, the cap constrains aggregate covered emissions and allowance scarcity produces a market price; therefore the reason explains the assertion. Price collars, reserves and borrowing rules can modify outcomes, but the core quantity-price distinction remains. A carbon tax instead fixes the rate while emissions respond.

### MCQ 03 — Intensity baseline

An industrial unit’s output rises by 20%, while its emissions intensity falls by 10%. Ignoring all other changes, which conclusion is correct?

A. Absolute emissions fall by 10% because intensity determines total emissions
B. Absolute emissions stay unchanged because output growth offsets intensity improvement
C. Absolute emissions rise by 8%, so intensity improvement does not ensure an absolute decline
D. Absolute emissions cannot be estimated without the initial output and intensity values

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Initial emissions may be written as output × intensity. New emissions are 1.20 × 0.90 = 1.08 of the initial level, an 8% rise. The example captures the central baseline-and-credit distinction: intensity can improve while total emissions increase. It does not imply that all intensity systems cause growth; it shows that they do not guarantee an absolute ceiling.

### MCQ 04 — Offset as a use

Which statement most accurately describes an offset?

A. An allowance used to comply with an absolute emissions cap
B. A tax payment made on the buyer’s own fuel emissions
C. A removal unit that must always use permanent geological storage
D. An external credit used to compensate for emissions under specified claim rules

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** “Offset” describes how a credit is used—to compensate for emissions elsewhere—not a single underlying market design or project type. Offsets may represent avoidance, reduction or removal. They are not allowances, tax payments or necessarily permanent removals. Integrity depends on baseline, additionality, leakage, durability, ownership and claim accounting.

### MCQ 05 — Allowance versus credit

Which pairing is correct?

A. Allowance—permission within a cap; credit—verified mitigation against a baseline or target
B. Allowance—verified removal; credit—unlimited permission outside a capped system
C. Allowance—unit used only voluntarily; credit—unit used only for legal compliance
D. Allowance and credit—identical units once serialised and entered in any registry

**Answer: A**

**Explanation:** An allowance originates from the cap and authorises a specified amount of covered emissions. A credit originates from an eligible reduction, avoidance or removal measured against a rule-defined reference. Either unit may be tradable or admitted into compliance depending on law, but origin and environmental meaning remain different. Registry entry does not erase this distinction.

### MCQ 06 — Compliance and voluntary demand

Which factor best distinguishes a compliance market from a voluntary market?

A. Whether each unit is expressed in tonnes of carbon-dioxide equivalent
B. Whether demand follows a legal obligation or a non-mandated choice
C. Whether transactions occur through an electronic registry and trading platform
D. Whether independent private verifiers assess the underlying mitigation activity

**Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Compliance demand follows a legal obligation to pay, surrender units or meet a target. Voluntary demand follows a non-mandated purchase or claim. Both markets can use tCO2e, electronic registries and private third-party verification. A government may also operate a voluntary crediting mechanism, so public/private management is not the decisive test.

### MCQ 07 — Additionality

A solar project was already legally required, fully financed and under construction before any carbon-credit application. Which integrity concern is most direct?

A. Failure of geological storage containment after credit issuance
B. Overallocation of allowances under a capped trading programme
C. Non-additionality because the project may proceed without credit revenue
D. Double use of a serialised unit after its first retirement

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Additionality asks whether the credited outcome would occur in the claimed amount without the crediting intervention. A pre-required, financed and commenced project may fail that test, although the governing methodology controls the legal result. Geological leakage concerns storage, overallocation concerns allowances, and double use concerns the unit after issuance.

### MCQ 08 — Leakage

A forest-credit project stops logging inside its boundary, but timber extraction shifts to an unprotected neighbouring forest because market demand is unchanged. This is best described as:

A. Reversal within the credited forest
B. Double issuance by two registries
C. A corresponding adjustment
D. Activity-shifting leakage

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** The intervention displaced emissions outside its accounting boundary, which is leakage. A reversal would release carbon previously stored inside the credited pool. Double issuance concerns duplicate units for the same outcome. A corresponding adjustment is international accounting under Article 6 and does not describe displaced logging.

### MCQ 09 — Permanence

Which measure most directly manages reversal risk in a forest-credit programme?

A. Withholding a buffer reserve to replace credits lost after future reversals
B. Auctioning extra allowances whenever an ETS market price falls
C. Charging a carbon tax on fuel consumed by the credit buyer
D. Authorising the credit as an ITMO without continued storage monitoring

**Answer: A**

**Explanation:** A buffer reserve pools or withholds units so losses can be compensated under programme rules. It manages but does not eliminate reversal risk. Allowance auctions and taxes address pricing, not biological permanence. Article 6 authorisation cannot substitute for continued monitoring or remedy a physical reversal.

### MCQ 10 — Validation and verification

Consider the following statements:

1. Validation normally assesses project design and eligibility before registration.
2. Verification normally assesses monitored outcomes after implementation.
3. Registry entry itself independently proves that the project was additional.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. 1 only
B. 1 and 2 only
C. 2 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3

**Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Statements 1 and 2 correctly distinguish ex-ante validation from ex-post verification. Statement 3 is incorrect: a registry records units and their lifecycle, but additionality depends on the methodology, evidence, validation and programme decision. Even a verified unit can later face durability or claim-accounting questions.

### MCQ 11 — Credit lifecycle

Which sequence is most appropriate for a project-based credit?

A. Issuance → baseline selection → validation → monitoring → final retirement
B. Monitoring → issuance → project design → verification → programme registration
C. Design/validation → registration → monitoring/reporting → verification → issuance → final use
D. Retirement → issuance → verification → monitoring → validation of project design

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Project design establishes boundary, baseline and monitoring; validation assesses it; registration admits the activity; monitoring/reporting creates evidence; verification checks the result; issuance creates serialised units; transfer and surrender/retirement/cancellation determine final use. The other sequences place issuance or final use before the evidence needed to support them.

### MCQ 12 — Double counting

The same serialised credit is retired by Company X, but the registry later allows Company Y to retire that identical serial number again. Which failure has occurred?

A. Baseline inflation
B. Carbon leakage
C. Double claiming between two national NDCs
D. Double use of the same unit

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** The identical unit has been used twice, so this is double use. Baseline inflation would overstate the underlying mitigation before issuance. Leakage concerns emissions outside the project boundary. Double claiming can occur even with different records when two actors or countries claim the same outcome; the scenario specifically identifies reuse of one serial number.

### MCQ 13 — Scope of corresponding adjustment

Which statement about a corresponding adjustment is correct?

A. It prevents both Parties counting the same authorised international outcome toward NDC achievement
B. It proves the activity would not occur without revenue from carbon credits
C. It guarantees stored carbon remains permanently outside the atmosphere
D. It converts any privately issued voluntary credit into an internationally transferred outcome

**Answer: A**

**Explanation:** Corresponding adjustments are Paris-accounting controls against international double claiming of authorised outcomes. They do not test the counterfactual, monitor physical storage or automatically authorise private credits. An outcome becomes relevant to Article 6 accounting only under the applicable authorisation, tracking and reporting rules.

### MCQ 14 — Article 6 map

Which mapping is correct?

A. Article 6.2—non-market work programme; Article 6.4—bilateral accounting only; Article 6.8—UN crediting mechanism
B. Article 6.2—cooperative approaches; Article 6.4—UNFCCC-supervised crediting; Article 6.8—non-market approaches
C. Article 6.2—Kyoto CDM; Article 6.4—carbon tax; Article 6.8—allowance auction
D. Article 6.2—adaptation finance only; Article 6.4—technology transfer only; Article 6.8—ITMO registry

**Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Article 6.2 governs cooperative approaches and ITMO accounting; Article 6.4 establishes the Paris Agreement crediting mechanism; Article 6.8 provides the framework for non-market approaches. Kyoto CDM, national taxes and allowance auctions are not the three Article 6 branches.

### MCQ 15 — SOP and OMGE

Consider the following statements regarding Article 6.4:

1. Five per cent of issued A6.4ERs is transferred as an in-kind share of proceeds for the Adaptation Fund.
2. At least two per cent of issued A6.4ERs is cancelled for overall mitigation in global emissions.
3. Both percentages are identically mandatory for Article 6.2 cooperative approaches.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. 1 only
B. 2 and 3 only
C. 1 and 2 only
D. 1, 2 and 3

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Statements 1 and 2 reflect the Article 6.4 rules: SOP supports adaptation and OMGE withholds units from use. Statement 3 is incorrect. Under Article 6.2, adaptation contributions and OMGE action are strongly encouraged and reported, but the Article 6.4 mandatory percentages do not automatically apply.

### MCQ 16 — 6.2 versus 6.4

Which statement correctly distinguishes adaptation finance and OMGE under Articles 6.2 and 6.4?

A. Both adaptation finance and OMGE are prohibited under Article 6.2 cooperation
B. Both are mandatory under Article 6.2 at the Article 6.4 percentages
C. Article 6.4 contains no mandatory adaptation or overall-mitigation provisions
D. Article 6.4 mandates them; Article 6.2 strongly encourages comparable action

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** Decision 3/CMA.3 makes the Article 6.4 in-kind adaptation share and minimum OMGE cancellation mandatory. Decision 2/CMA.3 strongly encourages participating Parties to contribute to adaptation and deliver OMGE in cooperative approaches. The two branches therefore share objectives but not identical legal obligations.

### MCQ 17 — Legacy CER firewall

Consider the following statements about limited use of legacy CERs under the Article 6.4 transition arrangements:

1. Eligible CERs must originate from CDM activities registered on or after 1 January 2013.
2. Their use is limited to a Party’s first or first updated NDC.
3. The host Party must apply a corresponding adjustment to these pre-2021 CERs.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. 1 and 2 only
B. 2 and 3 only
C. 1 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3

**Answer: A**

**Explanation:** The post-1 January 2013 registration threshold and first/first-updated-NDC limit form the legacy-unit firewall. The eligible units are identified as pre-2021 reductions in the mechanism registry. Under the adopted transition rule, the host Party does not apply a corresponding adjustment to this limited CER lane; these units are not equivalent to newly authorised A6.4ERs.

### MCQ 18 — CDM activity transition dates

Which date pair is correct for transition of eligible CDM activities to Article 6.4?

A. Project request by 31 December 2025; host approval by 31 December 2026
B. Project request by 31 December 2023; host approval deadline extended to 30 June 2026

**Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Activity participants had to request transition by 31 December 2023. The host-Party approval deadline was extended from 31 December 2025 to 30 June 2026. Meeting deadlines did not guarantee transition: the activity still had to satisfy the applicable mechanism requirements.

### MCQ 19 — India’s statutory basis

Which legal pairing is correct for India’s Carbon Credit Trading Scheme?

A. Electricity Act section 3 creates CCCs, while the Environment Act creates CCTS
B. Forest law authorises CCC trading, and CERC directly issues every certificate
C. Energy Conservation Act sections 14(w) and 14AA enable CCTS and CCC issuance/trading
D. CCTS rests only on an executive guideline without authority in a statute

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:** The Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022 inserted the statutory architecture. Section 14(w) empowers the Central Government to specify the carbon credit trading scheme; section 14AA allows the Government or authorised agency to issue CCCs to compliant registered entities and enables purchase/sale under the scheme. Other laws support target notification and trading regulation but do not replace this basis.

### MCQ 20 — Institutional roles

Which institutional mapping is correct?

A. Grid Controller sets GEI targets; MoEFCC operates power exchanges; BEE adjudicates trades
B. CERC validates projects; NSC-ICM maintains unit accounts; Grid Controller notifies targets
C. MoEFCC issues every trade order; BEE only collects taxes; CERC manages the registry
D. NSC-ICM recommends; BEE administers; Grid Controller registers; CERC regulates trading

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** The CCTS assigns governance and recommendation to NSC-ICM, administration and specified issuance/procedure functions to BEE, registry operation to Grid Controller of India, and trading regulation to CERC. MoEFCC notifies GEI targets under the Environment (Protection) Act after the prescribed recommendation chain.

### MCQ 21 — CCTS design

Which description of the CCTS compliance mechanism is correct?

A. It uses intensity targets: outperformance can earn CCCs; shortfalls require CCC surrender
B. It imposes an economy-wide absolute cap and auctions allowances to every emitting entity
C. It functions only as a voluntary forestry-credit programme for non-obligated entities
D. It charges a carbon tax that CERC collects from all industrial fuel users

**Answer: A**

**Explanation:** Obligated entities receive entity-specific GEI targets measured per unit of equivalent output. Performance better than target can support CCC issuance; a shortfall creates a purchase/surrender need. This is not an economy-wide cap, a forestry-only programme or a tax. Absolute emissions may rise even while intensity falls.

### MCQ 22 — GEI notification timeline

Which chronology is correct?

A. Iron-and-steel final targets were notified in 2022 and repealed when CCTS began
B. Four-sector rules: October 2025; final expansion: January 2026; iron/steel draft: June 2026
C. All sector targets became final with the original CCTS notification in June 2023
D. CERC notified entity-level GEI targets through its trading regulation in February 2026

**Answer: B**

**Explanation:** G.S.R. 739(E) of 8 October 2025 covered aluminium, cement, chlor-alkali and pulp/paper. G.S.R. 25(E) of 13 January 2026 added secondary aluminium entities, refinery, petrochemical and textile schedules. The 26 June 2026 iron-and-steel text was a draft. CERC regulates trading rather than notifying entity GEI schedules.

### MCQ 26 — Offset methodologies

Which statement correctly reflects BEE’s official methodology list reviewed on 30 September 2026?

A. No methodology had been approved for any offset sector by that date
B. Twelve were listed, but none covered CCUS despite its approved sectoral scope
C. Every approved sectoral scope already had at least one operational methodology

### MCQ 27 — CCUS chain

Which sequence best represents a complete CCUS chain?

A. Credit trading → carbon tax payment → capture → forest retirement
B. Capture → unit retirement → allowance auction → transport to market
C. Capture → conditioning/compression → transport → use/storage → monitoring and closure
D. Baseline selection → ITMO authorisation → oxygen production → forest buffer

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:** CCUS is a physical value chain. CO2 must be separated, conditioned, transported and then used or stored; storage requires injection, monitoring, closure and liability arrangements. A climate assessment follows measured carbon through this chain and includes the energy penalty and upstream/downstream emissions.

### MCQ 28 — Capture methods

Consider the following pairs:

1. Post-combustion — separation from flue gas after combustion
2. Pre-combustion — separation after fuel conversion into a hydrogen-rich stream and CO2
3. Oxy-fuel — combustion in near-pure oxygen, yielding mainly CO2 and water vapour

Which of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

A. 1 only
B. 1 and 2 only
C. 2 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** All three pairs are correct. Post-combustion can suit retrofits but treats dilute flue gas. Pre-combustion integrates fuel conversion and separation. Oxy-fuel simplifies the exhaust composition but oxygen production consumes energy. The methods are not interchangeable across every industrial process.

### MCQ 29 — Utilisation duration

Captured CO2 is converted into synthetic aviation fuel and later released when the fuel burns. Which description is most accurate?

A. It recycles carbon and may displace fossil feedstock, but is not durable removal
B. It permanently stores the carbon in a deep geological formation after combustion
C. It is net-negative regardless of the energy and hydrogen production pathways
D. It creates an emission allowance within a conventional cap-and-trade programme

**Answer: A**

**Explanation:** Fuel use returns the carbon to the atmosphere, so the pathway may recycle rather than permanently store CO2. Its benefit depends on low-carbon hydrogen/energy and the displaced counterfactual. It is not geological storage, automatic negative emissions or an allowance.

### MCQ 30 — Geological storage

Consider the following statements:

1. Deep saline formations and depleted oil/gas reservoirs are major geological storage options.
2. Storage assurance requires caprock integrity, well management, plume/pressure monitoring and post-closure responsibility.
3. A theoretical regional capacity estimate is identical to permitted, appraised and injectible capacity.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. 1 only
B. 1 and 2 only
C. 2 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3

**Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Statements 1 and 2 reflect IEA storage guidance. Statement 3 is false: theoretical pore volume must be narrowed through site characterisation, injectivity testing, regulation, infrastructure and economics before it becomes bankable capacity. Legacy wells and pressure interference can further reduce usable capacity.

### MCQ 31 — Capture rate versus avoided emissions

A plant reports that its capture unit removes 90% of CO2 from a selected flue stream. Why may lifecycle emissions avoided be lower?

A. Capture percentages are reported in tonnes of oil equivalent rather than CO2
B. Captured CO2 no longer requires compression, transport, monitoring or final storage
C. Energy, upstream emissions, uncaptured streams and downstream leakage lower net benefit
D. Every point-source capture facility is legally and technically a DAC facility

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:** A capture rate is a boundary-specific engineering metric. Additional energy/fuel, upstream supply, other plant emissions, transport and storage performance determine net avoided emissions. The unit is tCO2, not tonnes of oil equivalent, and point-source capture is not DAC.

### MCQ 32 — CCS versus CDR

With reference to CCS and carbon dioxide removal, which statement is correct?

A. All fossil-source CCS removes CO2 already dispersed through ambient air
B. Every captured-carbon use keeps carbon outside the atmosphere permanently
C. CDR refers exclusively to afforestation and other biological sinks
D. Point-source CCS usually avoids emissions; credible DACCS or BECCS can remove atmospheric CO2

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** Point-source CCS intercepts emissions before release, so it is normally emissions avoidance. DACCS begins with ambient air, and BECCS begins with biogenic uptake; with low lifecycle emissions and durable storage, both can yield net removal. CCU duration varies, and CDR includes biological, geochemical and engineered methods.

### MCQ 33 — DAC pathways

Which comparison is correct?

A. Solid DAC generally uses lower-temperature heat; liquid DAC can require high-temperature regeneration
B. Liquid DAC needs heat but neither electricity, compression nor downstream transport
C. Solid DAC captures concentrated flue gas and is therefore post-combustion capture
D. Both pathways guarantee permanent removal even when captured CO2 enters fuel

**Answer: A**

**Explanation:** IEA distinguishes S-DAC, which can use lower-temperature heat, from L-DAC designs that may require high-temperature heat up to about 900°C. Both need energy and downstream handling. DAC captures ambient air, unlike post-combustion flue gas capture, and permanent removal requires durable storage and net lifecycle benefit.

### MCQ 34 — DAC cost evidence

Which use of the IEA 2022 estimate is correct?

A. “Every DAC project costs exactly USD 125/tCO2 including transport, storage and monitoring.”
B. “IEA estimated USD 125-335/tCO2 capture cost for a large current-technology plant, with wide uncertainty.”
C. “The range is India’s official floor and forbearance price for Carbon Credit Certificates.”

**Explanation:** The IEA figure is a dated, modelled capture-cost range, sensitive to technology, energy, configuration and finance. It is not a universal project quote, a full transport/storage cost, an Indian market price or a ranking against every mitigation option. Correct use preserves source, year, boundary and uncertainty.

### MCQ 35 — DAC and synthetic fuel

With reference to DAC-derived synthetic fuel and DACCS, which statement is correct?

A. Air-captured CO2 in synthetic fuel stays isolated after the fuel is burned
B. Synthetic fuel from DAC can be made without hydrogen or additional energy inputs
C. DAC fuel recycles carbon; DAC with durable storage can deliver atmospheric removal
D. Every commercial use of DAC automatically requires an Article 6 corresponding adjustment

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Fuel production combines captured carbon with hydrogen, and combustion releases CO2 again; this is potentially lower-carbon recycling, not durable storage. DACCS can create removal if lifecycle emissions are deducted and storage is secure. Article 6 treatment depends on authorisation and use, not the mere technology label.

### MCQ 36 — BECCS conditions

When can BECCS credibly produce net-negative emissions?

A. Any combustion of biomass is net-negative even when carbon is not captured
B. High crop yield alone proves net removal despite land-use and supply-chain emissions
C. Captured biogenic CO2 in a short-lived product is automatically permanent storage
D. Sustainable biomass uptake, lifecycle accounting, high capture and durable storage yield net withdrawal

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** BECCS combines biological uptake with capture and storage. Land-use change, cultivation, processing, transport, conversion emissions, incomplete capture and storage performance must all be counted. Biomass combustion alone is not removal, and short-lived use does not secure durable storage.

### MCQ 37 — Land-based removal

With reference to land-based carbon claims, which statement is correct?

A. Afforestation adds stocks but can reverse; avoided deforestation usually prevents a counterfactual emission
B. Afforestation is permanent by definition and cannot damage biodiversity or local rights
C. Avoided deforestation is technically identical to direct air capture with geological storage
D. Biochar durability is fixed and independent of feedstock, pyrolysis conditions or soil

**Answer: A**

**Explanation:** Afforestation/reforestation can remove CO2 through photosynthesis but may reverse through fire, drought, harvest or land-use change and can create land/biodiversity trade-offs. Avoided deforestation is ordinarily an avoided-emissions claim. Biochar stability varies with production and environmental conditions.

### MCQ 38 — Enhanced weathering and ocean alkalinity

Which shared challenge most directly applies to enhanced weathering and ocean alkalinity enhancement?

A. Neither method requires mineral extraction, chemical reaction or large material flows
B. Mining scale, energy, reaction measurement, ecological effects and MRV uncertainty challenge both
C. Both methods are mature allowance markets governed by an absolute emissions cap
D. Both methods necessarily cause immediate CO2 leakage from a geological reservoir

**Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Both approaches accelerate geochemical carbon uptake and may require very large mineral/material flows. Grinding, transport, impurities, reaction rates, attribution and ecological effects complicate lifecycle accounting. ### MCQ 39 — Ocean CDR

Why does ocean fertilisation require particular caution?

A. Ocean fertilisation cannot alter marine productivity, nutrient cycles or food webs
B. Each tonne of nutrient creates one verified tonne of permanent deep-ocean storage
C. Export depth, permanence, ecosystems, deoxygenation and transboundary governance remain uncertain
D. It is the mandatory crediting activity created by Paris Agreement Article 6.8

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Stimulated surface growth does not guarantee that carbon sinks deeply or remains stored. Article 6.8 is a cooperation framework, not a mandate to deploy ocean fertilisation.

### MCQ 40 — Mitigation hierarchy

Which policy package is most defensible?

A. Prioritise offsets and postpone gross reductions until future removal becomes inexpensive
B. Treat short-lived forest storage as permanently equivalent to fossil carbon emissions
C. Apply CCUS to every source even where direct substitution is cleaner and cheaper
D. Avoid/reduce first, price remaining emissions, target difficult-source CCUS, then neutralise residuals durably

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** This hierarchy limits mitigation deterrence and distinguishes gross reduction, point-source avoidance and atmospheric removal. It does not ban markets or capture; it places them in roles consistent with their mechanisms and risks. Durable removals should neutralise residual emissions, not excuse avoidable emissions.

### PYQ-P1 — UPSC Prelims 2021, GS-I, Q29

**Ownership:** Application/cross-owned — carbon measurement in buildings, not a carbon-market design question.
**Local official paper:** `QP-CSP-21-GeneralStudiesPaper-I-121021.pdf`.

The ‘Common Carbon Metric’, supported by UNEP, has been developed for

A. assessing the carbon footprint of building operations around the world
B. enabling commercial farming entities around the world to enter carbon emission trading
C. enabling governments to assess the overall carbon footprint caused by their countries
D. assessing the overall carbon footprint caused by the use of fossil fuels by the world in a unit time

**Key label: INFERRED ANSWER — NOT OFFICIALLY VERIFIED LOCALLY**
**Most defensible answer: A**

**Explanation:** UNEP’s Common Carbon Metric was developed as a common method for measuring energy use and reporting greenhouse-gas emissions from building operations. ### PYQ-M1 — UPSC Mains 2025, GS-III, Q7

**Ownership:** Direct.
**Local official paper:** `UPSC Mains 2025 GS Paper 3 3.pdf`; local official text extract also checked.
**Directive/limit:** Explain · 10 marks · 150 words.

> **What is Carbon Capture, Utilization and Storage (CCUS)? What is the potential role of CCUS in tackling climate change?**
> *(Answer in 150 words.)*

**Model answer (145 words):**

CCUS separates CO2 from an industrial or power stream, transports it, and then uses it or stores it in a monitored geological formation.

Its strongest climate role is in **hard-to-abate process emissions**. Cement calcination, some steel and fertiliser processes can retain residual CO2 after efficiency, electrification and renewable substitution. Capture can prevent these emissions, while shared pipelines and saline/depleted-reservoir storage can support industrial clusters. Paired with sustainable bioenergy, capture and durable storage may also remove atmospheric CO2.

However, stack capture rate is not lifecycle abatement. Solvent regeneration, compression and transport use energy; short-lived utilisation or enhanced oil recovery can weaken benefits; storage needs site appraisal, MRV and long-term liability. Ordinary fossil-source CCUS avoids emissions—it is not automatically negative emissions.

Hence, CCUS should complement rapid direct reduction and be prioritised for difficult process/residual emissions, with every claimed tonne verified across the full chain.

**Why this earns marks:** It defines the complete chain, gives sector-specific utility, distinguishes avoidance from removal, and balances role with energy, storage, MRV and lock-in limits.

## ORIGINAL MAINS PRACTICE

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 marks

**Question:** Distinguish a carbon tax, cap-and-trade, baseline-and-credit and an offset. Why does confusing their units weaken climate policy? *(Answer in 150 words.)*

**Demand decoding:** Define four instruments, compare price/quantity/unit logic, then explain the policy consequence of conflation.

**Model answer (149 words):**

A **carbon tax** fixes a legal price per tCO2e or fuel-carbon content; emissions quantity responds. **Cap-and-trade** fixes an aggregate covered-emissions cap and creates allowances, whose price is discovered through trading. **Baseline-and-credit** sets a performance benchmark or intensity target and issues credits for verified outperformance; it need not cap absolute emissions. An **offset** is a credit used to compensate for emissions outside the buyer’s own reduction boundary.

Their units therefore encode different claims. An allowance is permission to emit within a cap; a credit represents a reduction, avoidance or removal relative to a baseline. Treating them as interchangeable hides whether mitigation is absolute or intensity-based, whether the counterfactual is credible, and whether storage can reverse.

For example, India’s CCTS compliance leg issues CCCs against GEI targets; it is not an EU-style allowance cap. Sound policy must separately disclose cap stringency, baseline/additionality, leakage, permanence, MRV and final surrender or retirement.

**Why this earns marks:** It answers every taxonomy limb, uses the allowance-credit distinction and applies it to India without overclaiming.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 marks

**Question:** Analyse how Articles 6.2, 6.4 and 6.8 of the Paris Agreement seek cooperation while protecting environmental integrity. Include the transition from the Kyoto CDM. *(Answer in 250 words.)*

**Demand decoding:** Compare all three branches, explain accounting safeguards and percentages, then present the two CDM transition lanes with a balanced verdict.

**Model answer (237 words):**

Article 6 creates three distinct cooperation routes. **Article 6.2** permits Party-designed cooperative approaches. A host-authorised mitigation outcome used internationally becomes an ITMO and is tracked through reporting, registries and a **corresponding adjustment**, preventing both Parties from counting the same outcome toward NDC achievement. This accounting safeguard, however, cannot cure a false baseline, non-additionality or reversal.

**Article 6.4** establishes a UNFCCC-supervised crediting mechanism. Approved methodologies, host approval, validation, verification and the mechanism registry govern A6.4 emission reductions. Authorised units used internationally enter Article 6.2 accounting; non-authorised mitigation-contribution units remain host-country mitigation. Integrity also includes a **5% in-kind share of proceeds** for the Adaptation Fund and cancellation of at least **2%** for overall mitigation in global emissions. Under 6.2, analogous adaptation contributions and OMGE action are strongly encouraged, not imposed at those percentages.

**Article 6.8** provides non-market cooperation on mitigation, adaptation, finance, technology and capacity-building without transferring mitigation outcomes.

The CDM transition has a firewall. Activity-transition requests closed on 31 December 2023 and host approvals on 30 June 2026 after extension. Separately, only CERs from activities registered on/after 1 January 2013 may enter the legacy lane, identified as pre-2021 reductions and usable only for a first/first-updated NDC; no host corresponding adjustment applies.

Thus Article 6 can lower cooperation costs, but credibility depends on conservative baselines, human-rights safeguards, transparent authorisation, durable storage and separation of old Kyoto units from new Paris mitigation.

**Why this earns marks:** It compares every branch, uses exact safeguards/dates, and qualifies accounting integrity with project integrity.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 marks

**Question:** “CCUS is a chain whose climate value is determined by its weakest link.” Discuss with reference to capture technology, transport, utilisation, geological storage, MRV and liability. *(Answer in 250 words.)*

**Demand decoding:** Follow the full chain and show why a high stack-capture rate is insufficient.

**Model answer (224 words):**

CCUS begins with source characterisation and CO2 separation, but climate benefit survives only if every downstream link works. **Post-combustion** capture treats flue gas; **pre-combustion** separates CO2 after fuel conversion; **oxy-fuel** produces a concentrated CO2-water exhaust but requires energy-intensive oxygen. Solvent regeneration, compression and extra fuel mean a 90% stack-capture rate can deliver lower lifecycle abatement.

Captured CO2 must be dehydrated, compressed and transported. Pipelines suit steady cluster-scale flows, while ships offer flexibility for offshore storage. Impurities, corrosion, pressure, rupture risk and mismatched capture-storage schedules can strand the chain.

“Utilisation” needs a duration test. Synthetic fuels, beverages and many chemicals re-release carbon; mineral products may store a fraction longer; enhanced oil recovery requires full oil-combustion accounting. Therefore CCU is not automatically sequestration.

For storage, IEA identifies deep saline formations and depleted reservoirs as major options. Site appraisal must establish capacity, injectivity, caprock integrity and legacy-well condition. MRV then measures injected mass, plume movement, reservoir pressure and possible leakage through operation and closure.

Liability rules must allocate remediation, compensation, financial security and post-closure stewardship between capture operator, transporter, storage operator and State. Without these rules, future taxpayers may inherit risk.

CCUS is most defensible for difficult cement, steel and fertiliser process emissions, after efficiency, electrification and material substitution. Ordinary fossil-source CCUS avoids new emissions; only sustainable BECCS or DAC with durable storage can remove atmospheric CO2.

**Why this earns marks:** It follows the complete physical/legal chain, gives named sectoral use and ends with the avoidance-removal distinction.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 marks

**Question:** Evaluate whether India’s Carbon Credit Trading Scheme can deliver cost-effective industrial decarbonisation without sacrificing environmental integrity. *(Answer in 300 words.)*

**Demand decoding:** Present design strengths, integrity and implementation risks, dated status, and a prioritised way forward.

**Model answer (294 words):**

India’s CCTS can lower industrial mitigation costs if targets and credits represent scarce, credible tonnes.

**Design strengths:** The Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022 gives statutory authority through sections 14(w) and 14AA. The 2023 scheme, amended in December 2023, combines a mandatory compliance leg with a voluntary offset leg. It uses entity-level GEI targets, independent ACVAs, a Grid Controller registry and CERC-regulated power-exchange trading. Final target rules dated 8 October 2025 and 13 January 2026 cover seven unique sectors, while CERC’s February 2026 regulations separate compliance and offset segments.

**Cost-effectiveness:** Firms with cheaper abatement can outperform targets and sell CCCs; high-cost firms can buy them. Intensity benchmarks may accommodate output growth and encourage technology diffusion.

**Integrity risks:** An intensity system does not cap absolute emissions. Generous baselines, product-mix changes or weak meters can create surplus credits without deep decarbonisation. Offset projects add additionality, leakage, reversal and double-claim risks. BEE’s official list showed 12 methodologies by 30 September 2026, but no CCUS methodology, illustrating that sector approval is not issuance readiness. Verification capacity, registry interoperability and penalties must be credible. Market power, illiquidity and price volatility also matter; the CERC framework still requires approved transaction rules and a floor/forbearance band.

**Status discipline:** Notification is not achievement. The reviewed public portals did not provide a verified CCC issuance total, market price or traded volume, so operational success cannot yet be asserted.

**Way forward:** publish absolute emissions beside intensity; use conservative, periodically tightening benchmarks; disclose unit vintage and methodology; require robust ACVA oversight; create reversal buffers for land projects; prevent cross-scheme double issuance; publish issuance, surrender and trade data; and align export-sector decarbonisation with technology, finance and just-transition support.

Thus CCTS can be efficient, but MRV stringency—not the existence of trading—will determine atmospheric value.

**Why this earns marks:** It evaluates law, economics, integrity and exact status, then links each reform to a diagnosed weakness.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 marks

**Question:** Compare DACCS, BECCS, afforestation, biochar, enhanced weathering and ocean-based carbon removal. How should India and the world govern their resource, justice and mitigation-deterrence risks? *(Answer in 300 words.)*

**Demand decoding:** Compare mechanisms/durability, identify method-specific limits, then propose a hierarchy and justice safeguards.

**Model answer (297 words):**

CDR methods differ in mechanism, durability and social footprint; a common “one tonne” label can conceal this.

**DACCS** chemically captures ambient CO2 and stores it geologically. It is measurable and potentially durable, but needs heat/electricity, sorbents, compression and storage. IEA’s 2022 large-plant capture estimate of USD 125-335/tCO2 is uncertain and excludes a universal full-chain price.

**BECCS** combines biomass uptake with capture and geological storage. It can be net-negative only after accounting for land-use change, cultivation, transport, conversion and incomplete capture. Large deployment can compete for land, food, water and biodiversity.

**Afforestation/reforestation** offers co-benefits when ecologically designed, but carbon can reverse through fire, drought, pests or clearing; monocultures can damage biodiversity and rights. **Biochar** stores a stable fraction of pyrolysed biomass in soils, yet durability depends on feedstock, process and soil.

**Enhanced weathering** spreads reactive minerals to accelerate CO2-consuming reactions. Its promise is durable storage, but mining, grinding energy, dust/metals, reaction rates and field-scale MRV constrain it. **Ocean alkalinity enhancement, fertilisation and related biological approaches** may expand ocean uptake, but export to depth, permanence, ecosystem effects, deoxygenation and transboundary governance remain uncertain.

Governance should follow five principles. First, avoid and reduce gross emissions before relying on removals. Second, reserve durable removals for residual emissions and keep separate ledgers for reductions, avoidance and removal. Third, match credit duration to storage risk through buffers, replacement duties and long monitoring. Fourth, require free, prior and informed participation, secure tenure, benefit sharing, food-water-biodiversity safeguards and liability funding. Fifth, publish lifecycle energy/material use and uncertainty, with independent verification.

This prevents **mitigation deterrence**—using promised future removal to delay feasible cuts—and avoids shifting land, pollution and reversal risks onto vulnerable communities or future generations. A diversified, cautiously scaled portfolio is preferable to dependence on any speculative single method.

**Why this earns marks:** It compares every named method, uses a dated IEA evidence point, integrates IPCC-style trade-offs and provides a justice-centred governance hierarchy.
