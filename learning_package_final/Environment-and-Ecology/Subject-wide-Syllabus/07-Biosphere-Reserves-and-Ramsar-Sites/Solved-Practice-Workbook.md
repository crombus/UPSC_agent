## BASIC MCQS / REMEDIATION

### MCQ 01 — National designation and WNBR status

Which statement correctly distinguishes an Indian Biosphere Reserve from inclusion in UNESCO’s World Network of Biosphere Reserves?

A. India may designate a Biosphere Reserve nationally, while WNBR inclusion follows a separate nomination and UNESCO decision.
B. UNESCO inclusion automatically converts the entire reserve into a National Park under Indian law.
C. A State Government can inscribe a site directly in the WNBR without a national nomination.
D. Every nationally designated Biosphere Reserve must enter the WNBR within ten years.

**Answer: A**

**Option analysis**

- **A — Correct.** National designation and WNBR recognition are separate stages.
- **B — Incorrect.** UNESCO recognition does not create a Wild Life (Protection) Act category.
- **C — Incorrect.** WNBR nominations are submitted by the national government.
- **D — Incorrect.** Ten years concerns periodic review of an existing WNBR site, not compulsory admission.

**Remediation:** Memorise the chain: **national designation → optional national nomination → UNESCO decision**.

### MCQ 02 — Three biosphere functions

Which triad states the three mutually reinforcing functions of a Biosphere Reserve?

A. Policing, prosecution and ecological compensation
B. Conservation, development and logistic support
C. Designation, boundary restriction and delisting
D. Tourism, relocation and species translocation

**Answer: B**

**Option analysis**

- **A — Incorrect.** These are enforcement or mitigation actions, not the MAB function triad.
- **B — Correct.** Conservation, sustainable development and support for research/monitoring/education are the three functions.
- **C — Incorrect.** These are possible status processes, not reserve functions.
- **D — Incorrect.** None is a universal defining function of every Biosphere Reserve.

**Remediation:** “Logistic support” means knowledge support, not transport infrastructure.

### MCQ 03 — Zonation

Which sequence moves from the most conservation-focused zone to the principal cooperation landscape for sustainable livelihoods?

A. Buffer → transition → core
B. Transition → core → buffer
C. Core → buffer → transition
D. Core → transition → buffer

**Answer: C**

**Option analysis**

- **A — Incorrect.** It begins with the intermediate zone and ends with the strictest zone.
- **B — Incorrect.** It reverses the functional gradient.
- **C — Correct.** Core protection is followed by compatible buffer activity and the transition cooperation area.
- **D — Incorrect.** The buffer, not the transition area, normally adjoins or surrounds the core.

**Remediation:** Use the functional gradient: **protect → support-compatible activity → cooperate and develop sustainably**.

### MCQ 04 — Transition-area application

A. Core area
B. Strict nature reserve outside the Biosphere Reserve
C. Buffer area used only for scientific sampling

**Answer: D**

**Option analysis**

- **A — Incorrect.** A core is intended for long-term conservation and minimal disturbance.
- **B — Incorrect.** The facts place the settlement inside the reserve’s cooperation landscape.
- **C — Incorrect.** Buffer functions are compatible with conservation, but the settlement–livelihood emphasis identifies transition.
- **D — Correct.** Transition areas integrate communities, sustainable production and conservation goals.

**Remediation:** Transition does not mean unregulated; it means negotiated sustainable use.

### MCQ 11 — Ramsar wetland definition

C. Marine water not exceeding six metres in depth at low tide

**Answer: C**

**Option analysis**

- **A — Incorrect.** The definition includes natural/artificial and fresh/brackish/salt systems.
- **B — Incorrect.** Temporary wetlands and flowing waters are included.
- **C — Correct.** Article 1.1 expressly includes shallow marine water to that depth.
- **D — Incorrect.** Ramsar status does not require prior WPA notification.

**Remediation:** Ramsar’s wetland concept is hydrological and ecological, not restricted to “lakes”.

### 2018 GS-III Q7 — Direct

**Question:** What is wetland? Explain the Ramsar concept of “wise use” in the context of wetland conservation. Cite two examples of Ramsar sites from India. *(10 marks, 150 words)*

**Provenance:** official local paper `GENERAL-STUDIES-PAPER-III.pdf`, code `EGT-G-GST`, printed Q7.

**Answer status:** No official model answer is held locally; the solution below is independently authored from the cited official instruments.

**Model answer**

Under Ramsar Article 1.1, a wetland includes marsh, fen, peatland or water, whether natural or artificial, permanent or temporary, static or flowing, fresh, brackish or salt; it also covers marine water up to six metres deep at low tide.

“Wise use” means maintaining ecological character through ecosystem approaches within sustainable development. It rejects both unrestricted exploitation and a universal no-use model. At site level, it requires protection of hydrology and catchment links, pollution control, regulated resource use, stakeholder participation and monitoring.

Chilika Lake illustrates integrated restoration: hydrological intervention, monitoring and community-linked fisheries preceded its removal from the Montreux Record in 2002. Keoladeo National Park, another Ramsar Site, remains on that Record, showing that designation alone does not guarantee ecological condition.

In India, wise use is delivered through applicable domestic law, especially the Wetlands Rules, State/UT Wetland Authorities and site management plans.

**Why this earns marks:** It defines the ecological unit, explains rather than merely names wise use, supplies two differentiated Indian examples and adds the domestic implementation bridge.

### 2021 GS-I Q6 — Direct route, application demand

**Question:** What are the environmental implications of the reclamation of water bodies into urban land use? Explain with examples. *(10 marks, 150 words)*

**Provenance:** official local paper `QP-CSM-21-GENSTUDIESPAPER-I-110122.pdf`, code `HXS-G-GSU`, printed Q6.

**Answer status:** No official model answer is held locally; the solution below is independently authored.

**Model answer**

Reclaiming an urban water body replaces a dynamic hydrological space with impermeable land. First, it removes storm-water storage and drainage connectivity, increasing runoff and flood peaks. Second, it reduces sediment and nutrient retention, so untreated inflows move faster into rivers and groundwater systems. Third, loss of marsh vegetation and shallow-water mosaics destroys fish, bird and amphibian habitat and weakens urban cooling. Fourth, construction can obstruct feeder channels, alter recharge or discharge patterns and intensify subsidence or water scarcity. It also displaces fishing, grazing and cultural uses.

Pallikaranai Marsh shows why retaining an urban wetland matters for Chennai’s flood resilience and biodiversity. East Kolkata Wetlands demonstrate the opposite opportunity cost: conversion would destroy a wastewater-recycling, fisheries and livelihood system.

Cities should map historical water spread and feeder drains, prevent encroachment, regulate the zone of influence, restore hydrology before beautification, treat sewage and involve dependent communities in monitoring.

**Why this earns marks:** It links reclamation to hydrology, pollution, biodiversity, climate and livelihoods, uses Indian examples and offers a process-based remedy.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Distinguish national Biosphere Reserve designation from inclusion in UNESCO’s World Network of Biosphere Reserves. Explain the legal significance of the distinction in India. *(Answer in 150 words.)*

**Model answer**

A national Biosphere Reserve is designated by India as a landscape framework combining conservation, sustainable development and logistic support through core, buffer and transition functions. WNBR status follows a second process: India nominates a qualifying site, UNESCO’s MAB bodies scrutinise it, and the MAB International Coordinating Council decides inclusion.

Enforceable restrictions in a core commonly arise from an overlapping National Park, Sanctuary or forest-law status. Buffer and transition parcels may remain under varied forest, revenue, community or private tenure. UNESCO recognition also leaves sovereignty with India and adds periodic review rather than direct international enforcement.

PIB recorded 18 national Biosphere Reserves but only 13 WNBR members on 3 November 2025. Thus, an answer must state the status, underlying law and boundary separately.

**Why this earns marks:** It compares process, count and legal effect instead of using “UNESCO Biosphere Reserve” loosely.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Explain how Ramsar site boundary, wise use and domestic legal protection interact. *(Answer in 150 words.)*

**Model answer**

A Ramsar Site is not merely its visible water spread. Article 2.1 requires a described, mapped boundary and permits connected riparian/coastal zones, islands and deeper water. The Ramsar Information Sheet records the criteria, ecological character, threats and management context.

Within that boundary, wise use means maintaining ecosystem components, processes and services through sustainable management. Fishing, tourism or biomass use is therefore neither automatically prohibited nor automatically permitted; compatibility depends on hydrology, pollution load, habitat and monitoring.

Ramsar listing creates an international commitment but not a new Indian land category. The Wetlands Rules, 2017, site notification, State/UT Wetland Authority and any overlapping wildlife, forest or CRZ law supply enforceable controls. MoEFCC guidance allows the whole Ramsar site to be delineated while each overlapping parcel remains under its competent law.

Thus, boundary identifies the ecological unit, wise use supplies the objective and domestic law delivers compliance.

**Why this earns marks:** It integrates treaty geography, management principle and Indian enforceability.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Compare Biosphere Reserves, Ramsar Sites, protected areas and World Heritage Sites as conservation instruments. *(Answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer**

The four instruments may overlap spatially but answer different questions.

A Biosphere Reserve is a people–nature landscape organised around conservation, sustainable development and logistic support. Its core–buffer–transition design accommodates different intensities of use. India may designate one nationally and separately nominate it to UNESCO’s World Network.

A Ramsar Site is a precisely mapped wetland meeting at least one international criterion. Its governing principle is wise use—maintenance of ecological character within sustainable development. Listing entails reporting and management commitments but does not itself create an Indian land category.

A National Park, Wildlife Sanctuary, Conservation Reserve or Community Reserve is a statutory protected area under the Wild Life (Protection) Act. Its notification directly determines authority, rights and permitted activities. This is the strongest place-based domestic legal layer among the four.

A World Heritage Site is inscribed under the 1972 Convention for Outstanding Universal Value. It attracts international conservation obligations and scrutiny, while land control continues through domestic law and the property’s management system.

Sundarban illustrates cumulative status: Biosphere Reserve, Tiger Reserve/National Park, Ramsar wetland and World Heritage property coexist with non-identical purposes and boundaries.

Therefore, the examiner should compare parent instrument, spatial design, rights effect, management authority and review mechanism. International recognition can raise visibility and accountability, but statutory enforcement and ecological outcomes remain nationally delivered.

**Why this earns marks:** It compares all requested instruments through common analytical dimensions and a named overlap example.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Assess the Montreux Record, Ramsar Advisory Missions and CEPA as accountability tools under the Ramsar system. *(Answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer**

Ramsar accountability begins with ecological character—the combination of wetland components, processes and benefits/services at a stated time.

The Montreux Record flags listed sites where that character has changed, is changing or is likely to change because of human interference. It creates visibility and priority attention without removing Ramsar status. Keoladeo National Park and Loktak Lake remain India’s current entries.

A Ramsar Advisory Mission is a voluntary technical-assistance mechanism requested by the Contracting Party. Experts examine causes, governance and restoration options; they do not take over the site or impose sanctions. Chilika Lake demonstrates the corrective potential: restoration, a 2001 mission review and documented improvement preceded its removal from the Record in 2002.

CEPA supplies the social infrastructure. Resolution XV.6 (2025) expands it as Communication, Capacity Building, Education, Participation and Awareness. It can improve local knowledge, institutional skill, stakeholder participation and compliance—elements without which a technical plan remains administrative paper.

The limitations are equally important. Montreux listing can persist without adequate finance; a mission depends on national consent and follow-through; CEPA cannot substitute for pollution control or environmental flows.

Together, the three tools work best as a cycle: disclose change, obtain diagnosis, build shared capacity, implement restoration and monitor outcomes.

**Why this earns marks:** It explains each mechanism, uses current Indian status, provides a restoration example and evaluates limits.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Evaluate the Biosphere Reserve model as an instrument for reconciling biodiversity conservation, research and community rights in India. *(Answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer**

The Biosphere Reserve model is designed to reconcile objectives that a single exclusionary boundary cannot. The core secures long-term conservation; the buffer supports compatible research, monitoring and education; the transition area enables settlements, livelihoods and sustainable resource management. Logistic support also turns the landscape into a learning system for climate adaptation and biodiversity governance.

Its first strength is scale: ecological processes, corridors and human dependence often extend beyond a National Park. Second, UNESCO’s framework requires participation arrangements, a management policy and a designated coordination mechanism. Third, WNBR inclusion adds international learning and a ten-year review. India’s 18 national Biosphere Reserves and 13 WNBR members, recorded on 3 November 2025, also show that national designation and international recognition can proceed at different speeds.

The model’s weakness is legal fragmentation. Biosphere Reserve status is not a separate WPA category. A core commonly relies on existing wildlife or forest law, while buffer and transition areas may contain diverse tenures and agencies. Without finance, benefit sharing and conflict resolution, zonation becomes a map rather than governance. Conservation can also lose legitimacy if customary rights are ignored; conversely, calling a use “traditional” cannot excuse ecological damage.

India should link zonation to enforceable land-use plans, recognised rights, community institutions, ecological monitoring and periodic public review.

Thus, the model is valuable not because it avoids trade-offs, but because it provides a framework to negotiate and measure them.

**Why this earns marks:** It evaluates design, legal limits, evidence, rights and a practical reform route within the word limit.
