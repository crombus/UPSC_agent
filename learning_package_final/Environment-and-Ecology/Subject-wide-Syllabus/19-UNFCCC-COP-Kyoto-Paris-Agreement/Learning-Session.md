## BASIC LEARNING SESSION

### 1. The treaty family: one framework, two later instruments

#### Visual first

```text
UNFCCC, 1992/1994
framework objective + principles + institutions + general commitments
        |
        +--> KYOTO PROTOCOL, 1997/2005
        |    quantified commitments for a listed developed-country group
        |    two commitment periods + Kyoto mechanisms
        |
        +--> PARIS AGREEMENT, 2015/2016
             universal participation through nationally determined contributions
             common procedural cycle: NDC -> transparency -> stocktake -> stronger NDC
```

**Answer-grabbing line:** Global climate law did not replace one treaty with another; it layered Kyoto’s differentiated target regime and Paris’s universal NDC cycle beneath the UNFCCC framework.

**Must-write keywords:** framework convention · related legal instruments · differentiated commitments · universal participation · nationally determined

The UNFCCC was adopted on **9 May 1992** and entered into force on **21 March 1994**. It establishes the objective, principles, institutions and continuing negotiating process. It does not itself allocate a treaty-wide numerical emissions cut to every Party. Kyoto and Paris are separate treaties under this framework: their Parties, governing bodies and legal obligations must be identified separately.

**How to write the paragraph:**
**Claim:** the architecture is layered. **Evidence:** UNFCCC Article 2 supplies the objective; Kyoto Article 3 supplied quantified commitment periods; Paris Article 4 supplies successive NDCs. **Analysis:** each instrument solves a different governance problem. **Qualification:** a later COP or CMA decision implements its parent treaty but does not silently rewrite treaty text.

**Exam traps**

- UNFCCC ≠ Kyoto Protocol ≠ Paris Agreement.
- “Adopted at a COP” does not make an agreement merely a COP decision.
- Paris universal participation does not abolish the Convention’s annexes.

### 2. UNFCCC objective, principles and differentiated commitments

#### Objective and principle map

```text
ARTICLE 2 OBJECTIVE
stabilise GHG concentrations
        -> prevent dangerous anthropogenic interference
        -> allow ecosystems to adapt naturally
        -> protect food production
        -> enable sustainable economic development

ARTICLE 3 GUIDANCE
equity + CBDR-RC + developed-country lead
special needs/circumstances of vulnerable developing Parties
precaution despite scientific uncertainty
sustainable development
supportive, open international economic system
```

**Answer-grabbing line:** CBDR-RC does not divide responsibility into “action” and “no action”; it combines common participation with differentiated leadership, capability and support.

**Must-write keywords:** Article 2 · equity · common but differentiated responsibilities and respective capabilities · precaution · sustainable development · developed-country lead

#### Annex architecture and Article 4 commitments

| Group | Meaning | Core additional responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| **All Parties** | Every Party to the Convention | Inventories; national programmes; technology, sinks, adaptation, research, education and communications under Article 4.1 |
| **Annex I** | Industrialised countries and economies in transition listed in the Convention | Developed-country mitigation lead and enhanced reporting under Article 4.2 |
| **Annex II** | OECD members listed in Annex II; a subset of Annex I | New and additional finance and technology-transfer duties under Article 4.3–4.5 |
| **Non-Annex I** | Parties not listed in Annex I, mainly developing countries; India belongs here | General commitments apply; implementation is linked by Article 4.7 to effective finance and technology support and development priorities |

Annex I and Annex II are Convention categories. **Annex B** is a Kyoto schedule of quantified commitments; it is not a synonym for Annex I. Paris operates a universal NDC system alongside, not instead of, the Convention annexes.

**How to write the paragraph:**
**Claim:** differentiation is institutional, not rhetorical. **Evidence:** Annex II carries finance and technology duties beyond Annex I’s mitigation lead; Article 4.7 links developing-country implementation to support. **Analysis:** ambition and means of implementation are legally connected. **Qualification:** non-Annex I status never means absence of Convention commitments.

### 3. Institutions: COP, subsidiary bodies, Secretariat and finance

#### Institutional flow

```text
COP — supreme Convention body
 |
 +--> SBSTA — scientific and technological advice
 +--> SBI   — implementation, reporting, finance, capacity and budget work
 +--> Secretariat — meetings, documents, communications and technical support
 +--> Financial Mechanism — resources under COP guidance/accountability
```

**Answer-grabbing line:** The UNFCCC process separates political decision-making, scientific advice, implementation review, administration and finance rather than lodging all functions in “the COP”.

**Must-write keywords:** Article 7 COP · Article 8 Secretariat · Article 9 SBSTA · Article 10 SBI · Article 11 Financial Mechanism

| Institution or route | Exact treaty-architecture role |
|---|---|
| **COP** | Reviews implementation and adopts decisions necessary to promote effective implementation of the Convention |
| **SBSTA** | Provides scientific and technological advice; connects negotiations with bodies such as the IPCC without becoming the IPCC |
| **SBI** | Assesses implementation, communications, transparency, finance, capacity-building and administrative matters |
| **Secretariat** | Organises sessions, compiles/transmits reports and supports the treaty process; headquartered in Bonn |
| **GEF and GCF** | Operating entities of the Convention’s Financial Mechanism; both serve the Paris Agreement |
| **LDCF and SCCF** | Special funds administered by the GEF |
| **Adaptation Fund** | Created in the Kyoto architecture; serves Paris from 2019 while transition arrangements continue |
| **Fund for Responding to Loss and Damage** | Operationalised at COP28/CMA 5 as an entity entrusted with operation of the Financial Mechanism; accountable to COP and CMA |
| **Standing Committee on Finance** | Assists COP/CMA with coherence, guidance, reviews and climate-finance assessments |

**Finance firewall:** this topic tests where finance duties and entities sit. Fund replenishments, project pipelines, needs estimates and delivery performance belong to later finance/current-affairs owners.

**Trap:** the Adaptation Fund is not interchangeable with the GCF, and neither is identical to the Fund for Responding to Loss and Damage.

### 4. COP, CMP and CMA — membership and legal effects

#### Governing-body matrix

| Body | Who decides? | Instrument served | Typical output |
|---|---|---|---|
| **COP** | Parties to the UNFCCC | Convention | COP decisions; may adopt protocols or agreements through applicable procedures |
| **CMP** | Parties to the Kyoto Protocol | Kyoto Protocol | Kyoto implementation, accounting, compliance and mechanism decisions |
| **CMA** | Parties to the Paris Agreement | Paris Agreement | NDC, transparency, stocktake, finance and Article 6 implementation decisions |

Non-Parties to Kyoto or Paris may attend the relevant CMP/CMA as observers but do not take decisions for that instrument.

#### Legal-effect ladder

```text
TREATY ARTICLE
  binding for a Party after consent + entry into force
        |
FORMAL AMENDMENT
  follows treaty amendment/acceptance rules
        |
COP / CMP / CMA DECISION
  authoritative implementation or procedural act under delegated treaty powers
  legal effect depends on parent provision and wording
        |
POLITICAL DECLARATION / PRESIDENCY ROADMAP / PLEDGE
  political effect; not automatically treaty law
        |
DELIVERY EVIDENCE
  actual finance, policy, emissions or adaptation outcome
```

**Answer-grabbing line:** A COP-family decision is neither legally irrelevant nor automatically equivalent to a treaty amendment; its effect is decision-specific and bounded by the parent instrument.

**Must-write keywords:** supreme body · serving as meeting of Parties · delegated authority · implementation decision · formal amendment · political declaration

The Paris Agreement itself was adopted through **decision 1/CP.21**, but the Agreement is a treaty distinct from that cover decision. Likewise, the Glasgow Climate Pact and first Global Stocktake outcome are CMA decisions, not amendments to the Paris Agreement. A decision can operationalise reporting, accounting or mechanisms where the treaty authorises the CMA to elaborate rules; it cannot bypass formal amendment procedures to rewrite treaty provisions.

**How to write the paragraph:**
**Claim:** legal character depends on source. **Evidence:** Paris Articles 13–14 create transparency and stocktake mandates; CMA decisions supply detailed modalities. **Analysis:** delegated rules can have operational consequences. **Qualification:** a declaration signed by some governments, such as the COP28 health declaration, remains distinct from a negotiated COP/CMA decision.

### 5. Treaty life cycle: adoption to withdrawal and re-entry

#### Status sequence

```text
ADOPTION -> text agreed
SIGNATURE -> preliminary endorsement; normally not full consent to be bound
RATIFICATION / ACCEPTANCE / APPROVAL -> post-signature consent to be bound
ACCESSION -> consent without prior signature; same legal effect as ratification
ENTRY INTO FORCE -> treaty threshold/date makes obligations operative
IMPLEMENTATION -> international and domestic action
WITHDRAWAL -> notice + treaty waiting period
RE-ENTRY -> new instrument + treaty entry rule
```

**Answer-grabbing line:** Treaty history must use legal verbs precisely: adoption creates text, ratification creates consent, entry into force activates obligations, and implementation proves neither compliance nor outcome by itself.

**Must-write keywords:** signature · ratification · accession · entry into force · depositary · withdrawal · re-entry

| Instrument | Adopted | Entered into force | Entry rule |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| UNFCCC | 9 May 1992 | 21 March 1994 | 90 days after the 50th instrument |
| Kyoto Protocol | 11 December 1997 | 16 February 2005 | 55 Convention Parties including Annex I Parties accounting for at least 55% of listed 1990 CO₂ emissions |
| Paris Agreement | 12 December 2015 | 4 November 2016 | 30 days after 55 Parties accounting for at least 55% of global GHG emissions |

Paris Article 28 allows withdrawal after three years from entry into force for that Party; it takes effect one year after depositary receipt unless a later date is specified. The United States first withdrew effective **4 November 2020**, accepted the Agreement again on **20 January 2021**, re-entered on **19 February 2021**, and its second withdrawal became effective **27 January 2026**. Re-entry is not automatic: under Article 21.3, the Agreement enters into force for a later-joining State 30 days after deposit of ratification, acceptance, approval or accession.

**Trap:** withdrawal from the UNFCCC is treated by Paris Article 28.3 as withdrawal from Paris too; withdrawal from Paris alone does not equal withdrawal from the Convention.

### 6. Kyoto Protocol: targets, Annex B, AAUs and commitment periods

#### Kyoto operating chain

```text
ANNEX B percentage
  x base-year emissions
  x years in commitment period
  = ASSIGNED AMOUNT
  -> denominated through AAUs (1 AAU = 1 tCO2e)
  -> emissions + removals accounting
  -> trading/project units supplement domestic action
  -> compliance assessment
```

**Answer-grabbing line:** Kyoto was binding because it converted differentiated responsibility into time-bound assigned amounts for covered Parties, not because every Convention Party received the same cap.

**Must-write keywords:** quantified emission limitation or reduction commitment · Annex B · assigned amount · AAU · commitment period · supplemental

- **First commitment period:** 2008–2012; Annex I Parties acting under Annex B commitments were to reduce aggregate covered GHG emissions by at least **5% below 1990 levels**.
- **Annex B:** records Party-specific percentages. It must not be confused with Convention Annex II.
- **AAU:** an Assigned Amount Unit represents one tonne CO₂-equivalent within the assigned-amount accounting system; it is not a project credit.
- **Monitoring and compliance:** national systems, inventories, registries, expert review and a compliance system support the target architecture.

#### Doha Amendment

The Doha Amendment was adopted at CMP 8 on **8 December 2012**. It established the second commitment period, **1 January 2013–31 December 2020**, with an aggregate ambition of at least **18% below 1990 levels** for participating listed Parties. It entered into force on **31 December 2020** after the acceptance threshold was met. The commitment period has ended, but the Kyoto Protocol itself remains in force and the CMP continues; “Kyoto expired in 2020” is therefore inaccurate.

**How to write the paragraph:**
**Claim:** Kyoto combined legal clarity with limited coverage. **Evidence:** Article 3, Annex B and assigned amounts fixed quantified obligations for periods. **Analysis:** this made accounting and compliance more determinate than under a pledge-only model. **Qualification:** coverage weakened because the United States never ratified, Canada withdrew effective 15 December 2012, and major developing emitters had no Kyoto-style quantified caps.

### 7. Kyoto mechanisms: CDM, JI and international emissions trading

| Mechanism | Treaty basis | Participants/location | Unit or asset | Core purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Joint Implementation (JI)** | Article 6 | Project in another Annex I Party | ERU | Annex I cooperation through verified project reductions/removals |
| **Clean Development Mechanism (CDM)** | Article 12 | Project hosted by a non-Annex I Party | CER | Sustainable development for host + partial compliance for Annex I user |
| **International Emissions Trading (IET)** | Article 17 | Eligible Annex B Parties | AAUs and other eligible Kyoto units | Transfer surplus units, supplemental to domestic action |

**Answer-grabbing line:** Kyoto’s three mechanisms differ by host, participant and unit: CDM generates CERs in developing hosts, JI generates ERUs within Annex I, and emissions trading transfers assigned-amount and other eligible units.

**Must-write keywords:** CER · ERU · AAU · additionality · supplemental to domestic action · registry

The CDM also channelled a share of proceeds towards adaptation. Its experience created institutions and methodologies later relevant to Paris Article 6.4, but Article 6.4 is not simply the CDM renamed.

#### Limits and criticisms

- **Coverage:** binding caps excluded the United States and developing-country emitters.
- **Political durability:** Canada’s withdrawal and limited second-period participation reduced reach.
- **Surplus units:** economic transition produced “hot air” concerns where assigned amounts exceeded plausible business-as-usual emissions.
- **Project integrity:** additionality, baseline choice, uneven geographic distribution and sustainable-development quality were contested.
- **Market demand:** weak demand and low prices undermined incentives.

**Balanced verdict:** Kyoto built the first serious international carbon-accounting and compliance infrastructure; its narrow participation prevented it from becoming a complete global mitigation regime.

### 8. Paris Agreement: temperature goal, NDCs and the ambition cycle

#### Article 2 and Article 4

```text
TEMPERATURE PURPOSE
well below 2°C + pursue efforts toward 1.5°C
        |
EVERY PARTY
prepare + communicate + maintain successive NDC
        |
pursue domestic mitigation measures
        |
report under Article 13
        |
collective Global Stocktake under Article 14
        |
next NDC every five years
progression + highest possible ambition
```

**Answer-grabbing line:** Paris makes participation and process universal while leaving the content of mitigation ambition nationally determined.

**Must-write keywords:** Article 2 · Article 4.2 · successive NDC · progression · highest possible ambition · national circumstances · five-year cycle

#### Binding process versus nationally determined substance

| Treaty obligation | What Paris does not do |
|---|---|
| Each Party **shall** prepare, communicate and maintain successive NDCs | It does not assign one identical percentage cut to all Parties |
| Each Party **shall pursue** domestic mitigation measures with the aim of achieving its NDC | It does not guarantee achievement of the NDC |
| Each successive NDC **will represent progression** and reflect highest possible ambition | It sets no universal numerical formula for progression |
| Each Party communicates an NDC every five years and may strengthen it at any time | The GST does not replace national decision-making |

Developed Parties should continue economy-wide absolute targets; developing Parties are encouraged to move over time towards economy-wide targets in light of national circumstances. The Article 15 committee is facilitative, expert-based, non-adversarial and non-punitive.

**How to write the paragraph:**
**Claim:** Paris resolves a participation problem through a procedural bargain. **Evidence:** Article 4 binds NDC preparation, maintenance and pursuit of domestic measures while national ambition remains self-set. **Analysis:** sovereignty increases participation but risks aggregate inadequacy. **Qualification:** transparency and stocktake generate information and pressure, not an internationally imposed national target.

### 9. Adaptation, finance, transparency, stocktake and loss and damage

#### Paris pillar map

| Article | Function | Precision point |
|---|---|---|
| **7 — Adaptation** | Global goal: enhance adaptive capacity, strengthen resilience and reduce vulnerability | Adaptation is country-driven and context-specific; it is not the same as residual loss and damage |
| **8 — Loss and damage** | Averting, minimising and addressing impacts, including extreme and slow-onset events | Decision 1/CP.21 paragraph 51 says Article 8 does not involve or provide a basis for liability or compensation |
| **9 — Finance** | Developed Parties shall provide resources for mitigation and adaptation; others are encouraged voluntarily | Scaled-up finance should aim at balance between adaptation and mitigation |
| **13 — Transparency** | Inventories, progress, support information, technical expert review and facilitative multilateral consideration | Flexibility applies to developing Parties that need it in light of capacities; reporting is not proof of achievement |
| **14 — Global Stocktake** | Collective assessment of mitigation, adaptation and support, in light of equity and best available science | First held in 2023; every five years; not a country compliance judgment |

**Answer-grabbing line:** Paris links nationally determined action to internationally common evidence: transparency tracks individual action, while the stocktake diagnoses the collective gap.

**Must-write keywords:** global goal on adaptation · enhanced transparency framework · flexibility · collective progress · equity · best available science · averting/minimising/addressing

#### First Global Stocktake — UAE Consensus, COP28/CMA 5

Decision **1/CMA.5** called on Parties to contribute, in a nationally determined manner, to:

- transitioning away from fossil fuels in energy systems, in a just, orderly and equitable manner;
- tripling renewable-energy capacity globally by 2030;
- doubling the global average annual rate of energy-efficiency improvements by 2030.

This is not a binding universal fossil-fuel phase-out schedule. COP28 also operationalised the Fund for Responding to Loss and Damage and adopted the UAE Framework for Global Climate Resilience.

### 10. Article 6: three pathways and exact accounting safeguards

#### Article 6 fork

```text
VOLUNTARY COOPERATION
 |
 +-- 6.2 COOPERATIVE APPROACHES
 |     ITMOs + authorisation + reporting + corresponding adjustments
 |
 +-- 6.4 CMA-SUPERVISED MECHANISM
 |     A6.4ERs + Supervisory Body + 5% adaptation levy + minimum 2% OMGE cancellation
 |
 +-- 6.8 NON-MARKET APPROACHES
       cooperation without transfer of mitigation outcomes
```

**Answer-grabbing line:** Article 6 is not one carbon market: it separates Party-led transfers, a central crediting mechanism and non-market cooperation.

**Must-write keywords:** ITMO · authorisation · corresponding adjustment · A6.4ER · share of proceeds · overall mitigation in global emissions · non-market approach

#### Article 6.2

- Parties may use **internationally transferred mitigation outcomes (ITMOs)** towards NDCs or authorised other international mitigation purposes.
- Participating Parties must authorise, report and apply **corresponding adjustments** so the same mitigation is not counted by both the first-transferring and using Party.
- For a tCO₂e accounting balance, the first-transferring Party adds the authorised transfer and the using Party subtracts the amount used.
- Under 6.2, adaptation contributions and cancellation for overall mitigation are **strongly encouraged**, not fixed mandatory percentages.

#### Article 6.4

- CMA-supervised mechanism with a Supervisory Body; aims to promote mitigation, sustainable development and an overall mitigation in global emissions.
- **Share of proceeds for adaptation:** **5% of issued A6.4ERs** is forwarded to the Adaptation Fund, alongside monetary/administrative arrangements.
- **OMGE:** a **minimum 2% of issued A6.4ERs** is mandatorily cancelled; these units cannot be used to offset another emission.
- Host authorisation determines whether units can be used internationally; corresponding-adjustment rules then apply to authorised first transfers.

#### Article 6.8

Non-market approaches support coordinated mitigation, adaptation, finance, technology and capacity-building without tradable mitigation outcomes. “Non-market” does not mean “no cooperation”.

**Integrity test:** additionality + conservative baseline + no double counting + permanence/reversal safeguards + sustainable-development and rights safeguards + transparent registry.

**Trap:** the 5% share of proceeds and minimum 2% OMGE cancellation belong to **Article 6.4**, not automatically to every Article 6.2 transfer.

India’s Panchamrit announcement at Glasgow was a national political announcement; India’s 2022 updated NDC was the later formal treaty communication. Do not merge them.

### 12. Baku and Belém: current officially verified outcomes

#### COP29/CMA 6 — Baku, 2024

Decision on the New Collective Quantified Goal:

1. **All-actor scale-up:** calls on all actors to enable finance to developing country Parties from all public and private sources to at least **USD 1.3 trillion per year by 2035**.
2. **Core goal:** sets a goal, with developed country Parties taking the lead, of at least **USD 300 billion per year by 2035** for developing country Parties, from a wide variety of public/private, bilateral/multilateral and alternative sources.
3. **Status discipline:** USD 300 billion and USD 1.3 trillion are differently framed goals; neither proves money delivered.
4. **Article 6:** CMA 6 adopted further guidance for 6.2, 6.4 and 6.8, advancing authorisation, reporting, registry and mechanism operation.

#### COP30/CMA 7 — Belém, 2025

The adopted **Global Mutirão** decision:

- launched the voluntary **Global Implementation Accelerator** and **Belém Mission to 1.5**;
- reaffirmed the Baku finance pathway;
- called for efforts to at least **triple adaptation finance by 2035** and urged developed Parties to increase their collective provision trajectory;
- linked NDC implementation with investment plans, technical assistance and national development strategies.

CMA 7 also adopted **59 Belém Adaptation Indicators**. They are voluntary, non-prescriptive, non-punitive, facilitative, country-driven and not a basis for comparing Parties.

**Fossil-fuel qualification:** the negotiated Global Mutirão decision contains no fossil-fuel roadmap. The COP30 Presidency launched a separate roadmap process on transitioning away from fossil fuels; it is a presidency initiative outside the adopted package, not a treaty amendment or negotiated CMA mandate.

**Answer-grabbing line:** Belém’s examinable story is implementation and adaptation measurement, not a falsely claimed binding fossil-fuel phase-out.

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

### A. Kyoto–Paris design trade-off

⚠️ **Inference:** Paris traded Kyoto’s stronger top-down specificity for broader participation and political durability. This is not simply “binding versus non-binding”: Paris contains binding procedural duties, while Kyoto’s quantified obligations covered a narrower set of participating Parties.

### B. Decision law and institutional authority

Treaty bodies often adopt detailed rules because the treaty expressly delegates elaboration. Article 6 accounting guidance and Article 13 transparency modalities therefore matter operationally. Yet their authority remains rooted in treaty consent; they are not free-standing amendments.

### C. Transparency cannot manufacture ambition

Article 13 can improve data quality and comparability; Article 14 can expose collective gaps. Neither mechanism supplies finance, enacts domestic policy or automatically raises an NDC. The Paris “ratchet” works through information, diplomacy, domestic politics and support.

### D. Carbon-market integrity

Article 6 can reduce the cost of cooperation only if baselines are conservative, reductions additional, reversals addressed and double counting prevented. A corresponding adjustment protects accounting integrity; it does not by itself prove the underlying mitigation is real or socially just.

### E. Finance and equity

The Baku goal combines a developed-country-led USD 300 billion goal with a broader all-actor USD 1.3 trillion scale-up. Belém reaffirmed these pathways and called for tripling adaptation finance by 2035. The analytical question is not only quantity but grants versus loans, access, predictability, additionality and actual delivery—detail owned by later climate-finance topics.

### F. Current-status discipline

- U.S. Paris withdrawal is effective from 27 January 2026; future re-entry would require a fresh instrument and 30-day entry period.
- India’s 2031–35 NDC is active in the registry; target values remain Topic 20.
- COP30’s fossil-fuel roadmap is a presidency process outside the negotiated Global Mutirão decision.
- Kyoto’s commitment periods ended; the Protocol did not cease to exist.

## CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

### Treaty spine

- **UNFCCC:** adopted 9 May 1992; in force 21 March 1994; Article 2 objective; Article 3 principles; Article 4 differentiated commitments.
- **Kyoto:** adopted 11 December 1997; in force 16 February 2005; Annex B assigned amounts; periods 2008–12 and 2013–20.
- **Paris:** adopted 12 December 2015; in force 4 November 2016; universal NDC cycle.

### UNFCCC architecture

- Objective: stabilise GHG concentrations while protecting adaptation, food security and sustainable development.
- CBDR-RC = common action + differentiated responsibility/capability + developed lead.
- Annex II ⊂ Annex I; India = non-Annex I.
- COP political body; SBSTA science/technology; SBI implementation; Secretariat administration.
- Financial Mechanism: GEF/GCF operating entities; FRLD entrusted entity; Adaptation Fund is a separate Kyoto-origin route serving Paris.

### Legal-status traps

- COP ≠ CMP ≠ CMA.
- Treaty/article ≠ amendment ≠ implementing decision ≠ declaration/roadmap ≠ delivery.
- Adoption ≠ signature ≠ ratification/accession ≠ entry into force.
- Paris withdrawal: three-year eligibility + one-year notice; later re-entry = fresh instrument + 30 days.

### Kyoto recall

- Annex B percentages → assigned amount → AAUs.
- First period: 2008–12, aggregate at least 5% below 1990.
- Doha: adopted 2012; second period 2013–20; at least 18% below 1990; in force 31 December 2020.
- CDM: non-Annex I host/CER; JI: Annex I project/ERU; IET: Annex B unit transfer.
- Strength: quantified targets and accounting. Limits: coverage, durability, surplus-unit and project-integrity concerns.

### Paris recall

- Article 2: well below 2°C; pursue 1.5°C.
- Article 4: prepare, communicate, maintain successive NDCs; pursue domestic measures; progression; highest possible ambition; five-year cycle.
- Article 7 adaptation; Article 8 loss and damage; paragraph 51 of decision 1/CP.21 excludes liability/compensation basis.
- Article 9 finance; Article 13 transparency; Article 14 collective GST every five years.
- GST ≠ national compliance ruling.

### Article 6 exact facts

- **6.2:** ITMOs; authorisation; reporting; corresponding adjustments.
- **6.4:** CMA mechanism; **5%** issued units to Adaptation Fund; **minimum 2%** cancelled for OMGE.
- **6.8:** non-market cooperation.
- 6.2 has encouragement, not the same mandatory 5%/2% levies.

### Milestone line

`Bali process → Copenhagen political accord → Cancun institutions → Durban universal mandate → Warsaw loss-and-damage mechanism → Paris Agreement → Glasgow pact/rulebook → COP27 fund decision → UAE first GST → Baku NCQG → Belém implementation/adaptation indicators`

### Current verified anchors

- **Baku:** USD 300bn/year by 2035 goal with developed Parties leading; USD 1.3tn/year all-actor scale-up.
- **Belém:** Global Implementation Accelerator; Belém Mission to 1.5; call to triple adaptation finance by 2035; 59 voluntary adaptation indicators.
- **Fossil-fuel precision:** UAE decision says “transitioning away”; Belém presidency roadmap is outside negotiated text.
- **India firewall:** treaty status and negotiating principles here; target values and domestic implementation in Topic 20.

### Mains conclusion

> The Paris system broadened participation beyond Kyoto’s narrow quantified-target model, but universality is credible only when transparency improves evidence, Article 6 preserves environmental integrity, and differentiated finance and technology convert nationally determined ambition into verified delivery.
