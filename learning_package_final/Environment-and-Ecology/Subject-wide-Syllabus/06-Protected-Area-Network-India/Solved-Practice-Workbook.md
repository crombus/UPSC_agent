## BASIC MCQS / REMEDIATION

### MCQ 01 — The four statutory PA categories

Which set contains only the four protected-area categories created in Chapter IV of the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972?

A. National Park, Wildlife Sanctuary, Conservation Reserve and Community Reserve
B. National Park, Tiger Reserve, Elephant Reserve and Eco-Sensitive Zone
C. Wildlife Sanctuary, Biosphere Reserve, Ramsar Site and Community Reserve
D. National Park, Conservation Reserve, Tiger Reserve and World Heritage Site

**Answer: A**

**Option analysis**

- **A — Correct.** These are the four Chapter IV place-based categories.
- **B — Incorrect.** Tiger Reserve is a separate statutory tiger framework; Elephant Reserve and ESZ arise from different instruments.
- **C — Incorrect.** Biosphere Reserve and Ramsar Site are not Chapter IV categories.
- **D — Incorrect.** Tiger Reserve and World Heritage status do not belong to the four-category list.

**Remediation:** Start every problem by identifying the parent statute and legal label.

### MCQ 02 — Sanctuary rights settlement

After an admitted claim to a right over land proposed for a Wildlife Sanctuary, which course is available to the Collector under the Act?

A. The claim must be rejected at the preliminary stage because no private or customary right may continue within any Sanctuary.
B. The land may be excluded, the right acquired/surrendered, or the right allowed to continue after consultation.
C. The claim automatically becomes a community forest right managed by the Gram Sabha.
D. Final notification must precede any inquiry into the nature or extent of the right.

**Answer: B**

**Option analysis**

- **A — Incorrect.** Section 24(2)(c) permits continuation of an admitted right in a Sanctuary.
- **B — Correct.** It states the three statutory routes accurately.
- **C — Incorrect.** The WPA claims process does not automatically convert a claim into a different statutory right.
- **D — Incorrect.** Claims inquiry and disposal ordinarily precede the final section 26A notification.

**Remediation:** Sanctuary = rights may continue; National Park = that continuation route is excluded. A Sanctuary intention notification also activates sections 27–33A immediately and requires recorded-rights-based fuel, fodder and forest-produce arrangements pending settlement.

### MCQ 03 — National Park rights

Which statement best distinguishes the final notification of a National Park from that of a Wildlife Sanctuary?

A. A National Park can be notified only on Central Government land that is already free from every recorded claim or customary right.
B. A National Park requires no inquiry into pre-existing rights.
C. Rights over included land must vest in the State; section 24(2)(c)’s continuation option does not apply.
D. A National Park can retain grazing rights if the Chief Wildlife Warden records them.

**Answer: C**

**Option analysis**

- **A — Incorrect.** State-controlled land can be notified through section 35.
- **B — Incorrect.** Sections 19–26A substantially apply to rights inquiry and extinguishment.
- **C — Correct.** This is the decisive rights-settlement distinction.
- **D — Incorrect.** National Park grazing is prohibited, subject only to the narrow authorised-transport exception.

**Remediation:** The National Park test is not “fewer people”; it is the statutory vesting and activity regime. Since 1 April 2023, sections 27–33A operate from the intention notification and the State must make recorded-rights-based fuel, fodder and forest-produce arrangements pending settlement.

### MCQ 04 — Boundary alteration

Which statement correctly distinguishes the WPA boundary-alteration safeguards?

A. Both boundaries may be altered by a State executive order after consulting the Chief Wild Life Warden.
B. Both boundaries require a recommendation of the National Board for Wild Life.
C. A Sanctuary requires a State-Legislature resolution, while a National Park requires an NBWL recommendation.
D. A Sanctuary requires an NBWL recommendation, while a National Park requires a State-Legislature resolution.

**Answer: D**

**Option analysis**

- **A — Incorrect.** Neither safeguard is reduced to an executive consultation with the Chief Wild Life Warden.
- **B — Incorrect.** Section 26A(3) uses the NBWL route for a Sanctuary, but section 35(5) uses a State-Legislature resolution for a National Park.
- **C — Incorrect.** It reverses the two statutory safeguards.
- **D — Correct.** It accurately separates section 26A(3) from section 35(5).

**Remediation:** Memorise the split rule: **WLS → NBWL; NP → State Legislature**.

### 2023 GS-I Q15 — Direct owner

**Question:** Identify and discuss the factors responsible for diversity of natural vegetation in India. Assess the significance of wildlife sanctuaries in rain forest regions of India. *(15 marks, 250 words)*

**Provenance:** official local paper `QP-CSM-23-GENERAL-STUDIES-PAPER-I-180923.pdf`, code `SKYC-G-GSU/35`, printed Q15; routed directly to Topic 06.

**Demand decoding**

- **Identify and discuss:** name the physical and biogeographic controls, then explain how they produce variation.
- **Assess:** show the ecological value of Sanctuaries in rainforest regions and their limits.

**Model answer**

India’s vegetation diversity arises from the interaction of tropical latitude with exceptional relief and monsoon variability. Rainfall ranges from arid western conditions to very wet Western Ghats and Northeast; temperature changes with latitude and altitude; Himalayan elevation creates subtropical, temperate and alpine belts. Relief and aspect modify moisture and insolation, while soils, geology, drainage and coastal conditions support grassland, mangrove, evergreen and deciduous formations. Biogeographic history, fire, grazing, cultivation and fragmentation further reshape the natural mosaic.

Wildlife Sanctuaries in rainforest regions protect this diversity at habitat scale. Pakke in the Eastern Himalaya and Wayanad in the Western Ghats retain multilayered forest, endemic species and breeding refuges. Their canopy, soil and stream systems store carbon, regulate water, reduce erosion and sustain pollinators and forest-dependent livelihoods. ## ORIGINAL MAINS PRACTICE

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Distinguish a National Park from a Wildlife Sanctuary with reference to notification, rights and activity regulation. *(Answer in 150 words.)*

**Model answer**

A Wildlife Sanctuary and a National Park are both WPA protected areas, but their rights regimes differ. For a Sanctuary, the State issues an intention notification, the Collector determines claims, and an admitted right may be excluded, acquired/surrendered or allowed to continue after consultation with the Chief Wildlife Warden. The final section 26A notification then fixes limits and date.

For a National Park, sections 19–26A broadly apply, but the option permitting continuation of a right is excluded. All rights over included land must vest in the State before final notification under section 35(4). Activity control is also stricter: Sanctuary grazing may be regulated, controlled or prohibited, whereas National Park grazing is prohibited subject to a narrow authorised-entry exception. The boundary safeguards differ: Sanctuary alteration needs an NBWL recommendation under section 26A(3), while National Park alteration needs a State-Legislature resolution under section 35(5).

Thus, the decisive distinction is not the category name alone but how rights are settled and continuing use is controlled.

**Why this earns marks:** It compares the exact statutory stages and avoids the vague “NP is stricter” answer.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Why must Biosphere Reserve, Ramsar Site and World Heritage Site not be treated as substitutes for a protected area under Indian wildlife law? *(Answer in 150 words.)*

**Model answer**

The labels answer different legal questions. A WPA protected area is created by domestic notification and directly regulates rights, entry, extraction and management. A Biosphere Reserve is a conservation-and-development framework using core, buffer and transition functions; it may contain National Parks or Sanctuaries but does not replace their statutes. A Ramsar Site is an internationally recognised wetland carrying a wise-use commitment; enforceable controls arise from Indian wetland, wildlife, forest and planning law. A World Heritage Site is inscribed for Outstanding Universal Value under the 1972 Convention, while land and activity regulation remains domestic.

Madhav illustrates overlap: the National Park supplies the WPA category, the Tiger Reserve adds section 38V management and Sakhya Sagar adds Ramsar recognition. These labels can strengthen scrutiny and coordination, but none automatically settles tenure or converts the others.

Therefore, an answer must identify the parent instrument and explain overlap without legal substitution.

**Why this earns marks:** It compares all three designations with a precise Indian example.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Compare Tiger Reserves and Elephant Reserves as conservation designs. Why is connectivity especially important for elephant conservation? *(Answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer**

Tiger and Elephant Reserves both focus conservation effort, but their legal architecture differs. A Tiger Reserve is notified by the State on NTCA recommendation under section 38V. It has a statutory core or Critical Tiger Habitat, a buffer, a Tiger Conservation Plan and detailed relocation safeguards. Boundary alteration and de-notification face NTCA–NBWL locks.

An Elephant Reserve is a Project Elephant landscape designation rather than a separate Chapter IV WPA category. Its restrictions depend on the underlying National Park, Sanctuary, reserved forest, revenue or private-land status. The official 2023 Atlas recorded 33 Elephant Reserves over 80,777 km².

Connectivity is especially important because elephant herds use large, seasonal and often inter-State ranges to access food, water and breeding areas. The 2023 official report ground-validated 150 corridors but cautioned that this was a minimum and that exact boundaries were not demarcated. Roads, railways, power lines, fencing and land-use conversion can therefore turn an identified corridor into a functional bottleneck, increasing conflict and mortality.

Policy should translate evidence into land-use safeguards: notify suitable Government links as Conservation Reserves, use community agreements on private land, redesign linear infrastructure, restore bottlenecks and coordinate compensation and conflict response across States.

Thus, tiger law secures a defined core–buffer landscape, while elephant conservation succeeds only when wider multi-use landscapes remain permeable under several laws.

**Why this earns marks:** It compares legal status, uses dated official evidence and explains the ecological mechanism behind the policy difference.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Explain the present legal position on Eco-Sensitive Zones after the Supreme Court’s 2023 judgment. Assess the case for site-specific zonation. *(Answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer**

Eco-Sensitive Zones are Environment (Protection) Act regulatory buffers around National Parks and Sanctuaries, not WPA protected areas. The 2011 guidelines organise activities into prohibited, regulated and permissible categories through site notifications.

In June 2022, the Supreme Court directed a default minimum one-kilometre ESZ. On 26 April 2023, it modified that blanket approach. The direction does not apply where MoEFCC has issued a draft or final notification, received a proposal, or where protected areas lie on inter-State borders or share boundaries. Governments must follow the 2011 guidelines and the relevant site notification. The Court nevertheless retained a hard rule: mining is impermissible inside an NP/WLS and within one kilometre of its boundary.

Site-specific zonation is justified because edge threats differ. A quarry beside a breeding habitat, a long-settled village, an urban-edge Sanctuary and a transboundary forest cannot be managed through identical geometry. Expert scrutiny can calibrate width and activity controls to slope, watershed, wildlife movement and settlement patterns.

However, flexibility can become dilution if notifications are delayed, baselines weak or regulated activities routinely cleared. Public disclosure, cumulative-impact assessment, functioning monitoring committees and enforceable no-go areas are therefore essential.

The correct balance is a strong ecological presumption against damaging edge uses combined with transparent, evidence-based local zonation.

**Why this earns marks:** It states the current rule, retains the mining exception and offers a balanced proportionality analysis.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** “Creating an inviolate tiger core is a legal process of ecological proof, rights recognition, consent and rehabilitation—not merely a relocation package.” Critically examine. *(Answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer**

Section 38V seeks viable tiger breeding habitat, but it does not authorise automatic displacement. A critical tiger habitat must be identified through scientific and objective criteria within National Parks or Sanctuaries. Before Scheduled Tribes or other forest dwellers are resettled or their rights affected, six cumulative safeguards apply.

First, recognition and determination of land and forest rights must be complete. Second, State agencies, with affected people’s consent and independent ecological and social expertise, must establish that local activities cause irreversible damage threatening tigers and habitat. Third, reasonable coexistence options must be found unavailable. Fourth, a livelihood-secure resettlement or alternative package must be prepared. Fifth, informed consent of the Gram Sabha and affected persons is required. Finally, land and facilities at the new site must be provided before existing rights are disturbed.

This design reconciles habitat integrity with procedural and distributive justice. It also prevents a cash offer from substituting for title, services, livelihood and community consent. In practice, however, unequal information, delayed rights recognition, pressure to accept packages and weak post-relocation monitoring can undermine formal voluntariness.

Reform should sequence FRA and WPA processes, publish ecological and social assessments, provide independent facilitation and legal aid, allow genuinely viable coexistence where possible, and audit both household welfare and habitat recovery after relocation.

An inviolate core is legitimate only when ecological necessity and rights-compliant procedure are jointly proved; failure on either side weakens conservation durability.

**Why this earns marks:** It follows the statutory sequence, critically tests implementation and reaches a qualified verdict.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Protected-area expansion is necessary but insufficient for biodiversity conservation in India. Discuss with reference to connectivity, marine governance, community participation and management effectiveness. *(Answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer**

Protected-area expansion secures legal authority and representative habitat. NR-7 records 1,134 PAs for 2025, demonstrating wider coverage. Yet notification is an input, not proof of ecological outcome.

First, isolated “islands” cannot sustain dispersal, gene flow or climate-range shifts. Conservation Reserves, Community Reserves, Tiger Conservation Plans and land-use safeguards must keep corridors functional; the 2023 elephant report’s 150 identified corridors are not automatically secured boundaries.

Second, marine effectiveness requires governance beyond a site. PIB recorded 130 notified Marine Protected Areas in December 2023, but mobile species and pressures cross boundaries. WPA protection must coordinate with fisheries rules, CRZ regulation, pollution control, shipping and upstream catchment management.

Third, legitimacy affects compliance. Sanctuary rights may continue; Community Reserve committees directly manage; tiger relocation requires rights completion, ecological proof, informed consent and rehabilitation. Exclusion without due process can shift conservation costs onto vulnerable communities, while participation without enforceable habitat rules can also fail.

Finally, WII’s Management Effectiveness Evaluation asks whether planning, staff, finance, patrolling, habitat work, conflict response and monitoring produce outputs and outcomes. A notified but understaffed PA with blocked corridors and unresolved rights remains a paper achievement.

India should therefore combine ecologically representative expansion with MEE-linked funding, current management plans, corridor and seascape planning, transparent rights processes and species/habitat outcome monitoring.

The correct metric is not area notified alone, but connected, equitable and measurably effective conservation.

**Why this earns marks:** It uses official figures, covers all four demanded dimensions and converts critique into an implementable framework.
