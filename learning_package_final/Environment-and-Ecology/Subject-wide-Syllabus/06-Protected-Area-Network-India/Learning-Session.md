### 1. The status firewall: one landscape can carry several labels

**Answer-grabbing line**

> India’s conservation map is layered: a place-based legal category under the Wild Life (Protection) Act may be overlain by species management, surrounded by an Environment (Protection) Act buffer, classified by an IUCN management objective and internationally recognised under a treaty without any one label replacing the others.

**Must-write keywords:** parent law; notification; underlying tenure; overlay; international designation; management objective; overlap.

| Label | Legal or institutional source | What it does | What it does **not** automatically do |
|---|---|---|---|
| National Park / Sanctuary / Conservation Reserve / Community Reserve | WPA, Chapter IV | Creates an Indian statutory protected-area category | Does not determine an IUCN category or international designation |
| Tiger Reserve | WPA section 38V | Creates a statutory tiger core–buffer framework and Tiger Conservation Plan | Does not convert every hectare into a National Park |
| Elephant Reserve | Project Elephant approval/notification | Focuses landscape planning and conservation effort | Is not a separate Chapter IV WPA category |
| Eco-Sensitive Zone | Environment (Protection) Act/Rules notification | Regulates the surrounding landscape | Is not part of the PA merely because it adjoins it |
| IUCN category Ia–VI | Global management-objective classification | Describes the primary management objective | Does not itself create Indian legal restrictions |
| Biosphere Reserve / Ramsar / World Heritage | Programme or treaty recognition | Adds zonation, wise-use or outstanding-value commitments | Does not by itself replace domestic law or settle private rights |

**Examiner trap:** “Tiger Reserve is merely administrative” is too weak; section 38V makes it statutory. “Tiger Reserve is the fifth Chapter IV PA category” is also wrong.

**Write the paragraph**

> **Claim:** A conservation label must be read with its parent instrument. **Evidence:** Madhav is simultaneously a National Park, part of a Tiger Reserve notified in March 2025, and contains the Ramsar-listed Sakhya Sagar. **Analysis:** Each label answers a different question—land protection, tiger management and wetland recognition. **Qualification:** Overlap increases governance obligations but does not make the three legal effects interchangeable.

### 2. The four WPA categories: land, consent and notification

**Answer-grabbing line**

> The four categories are not a simple “strict to weak” ladder: National Park and Sanctuary differ mainly in rights settlement and activity control, while Conservation and Community Reserves extend statutory conservation through consultation, consent and participatory management.

**Must-write keywords:** State Government; intention notification; Collector; admitted rights; Government land; volunteered land; consultation; management committee.

| Dimension | Wildlife Sanctuary | National Park | Conservation Reserve | Community Reserve |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Main provision | Sections 18–26A | Section 35 | Section 36A | Section 36C |
| Ordinary notifying authority | State Government | State Government | State Government | State Government |
| Typical land | Any eligible area; special route for reserve forest/territorial waters | Sanctuary or other eligible area | Government-owned land, especially adjoining/linking PAs | Private/community land volunteered by community or individual; outside NP/WLS/Conservation Reserve |
| Prior social step | Claims inquiry and settlement | Claims inquiry and extinguishment/vesting | Consultation with local communities | Voluntary offer is the entry condition |
| Rights outcome | Collector may exclude land, acquire/surrender rights, **or allow a right to continue** after consultation with the Chief Wildlife Warden | Section 24(2)(c), which permits continuation of a right, is excluded; all rights over included land must vest in the State before final notification | No NP-style compulsory rights-extinguishment sequence is built into section 36A | Ownership does not transfer merely by notification; land-use change needs committee resolution plus State approval |
| Management centre | Chief Wildlife Warden manages and protects through an approved management plan; in Scheduled/FRA areas the plan requires due Gram Sabha consultation | State/CWLW regime with stricter section 35 controls | Committee **advises** the Chief Wildlife Warden | Committee is the statutory management authority and prepares/implements the plan |

#### Ordinary State route and special Central route

- The table states the **ordinary** rule. Section 38 permits the Central Government to declare a Sanctuary or National Park where the State leases or otherwise transfers an area under its control to the Centre.
- The 2022 amendment inserted section 38(2A), allowing the Centre to declare a Conservation Reserve in such a transferred area when section 36A conditions are met.
- No parallel section 38 power was created for a Community Reserve. Its section 36C route remains State notification following a voluntary community/individual offer.

#### Notification flow

```text
SANCTUARY
State intention notification, s.18
        ↓
ss.27–33A apply immediately; State supplies recorded-rights-based
fuel, fodder and forest produce pending settlement, s.18A
        ↓
Collector appointed within 30 days; proclamation within 60 days;
claims invited for two months, ss.18B and 21
        ↓
Collector records claims, ss.19–25
        ↓
exclude land / acquire or surrender right / allow right to continue
        ↓
final limits and date, s.26A

NATIONAL PARK
State intention notification, s.35(1)
        ↓
ss.27–33A protections operate immediately, s.35(3A)
        ↓
ss.19–26A apply, BUT no s.24(2)(c) continuation route
        ↓
pending final settlement, State supplies fuel/fodder/forest produce
in terms of recorded rights, s.35(3B)
        ↓
claims disposed + all included-land rights vest in State
        ↓
final limits and date, s.35(4)
```

Where acquisition is chosen, the 2022 amendment updates the statutory reference to the **Right to Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement Act, 2013**.

**Close-option correction:** The two categories have different boundary locks. A **Wildlife Sanctuary** boundary may be altered only on the **recommendation of the National Board for Wild Life** under section 26A(3). A **National Park** boundary may be altered only through a **resolution passed by the State Legislature** under section 35(5).

### 3. Rights, access, grazing and management are separate questions

**Answer-grabbing line**

> “Rights settled” does not mean “all activity permitted,” and “strictly protected” does not mean “no management action”: entry, grazing, extraction, habitat intervention and tourism each follow a different statutory control.

**Must-write keywords:** recognised right; permit; regulation; grazing; forest produce; bona fide need; management purpose.

| Question | Sanctuary | National Park |
|---|---|---|
| Can an admitted right continue? | Yes, if allowed under section 24(2)(c) | No continuation route under section 24(2)(c) |
| Grazing | Chief Wildlife Warden may regulate, control or prohibit it in wildlife interests | Grazing is prohibited; livestock entry is narrowly excepted for authorised transport |
| Destruction/removal/diversion | Prohibited except through the statutory permission process for better management | Section 35(6) imposes the corresponding strict permit-and-NBWL consultation route |
| Forest produce | Controlled by the Act and permit/management regime | Removal is generally barred; if lawfully removed, the proviso permits personal bona fide needs of people living in and around the park, not commercial use |
| Day-to-day authority | Chief Wildlife Warden through the approved management plan; Gram Sabha consultation is due where Scheduled/FRA provisions apply | Applicable sanctuary-control provisions plus section 35 |

#### Participatory categories

- A **Conservation Reserve Management Committee** advises the Chief Wildlife Warden; it includes local panchayat, department and civil-society representation.
- A **Community Reserve Management Committee** is itself responsible for conserving, maintaining and managing the reserve. On community land, the 2022-amended provision requires **not less than five** Panchayat/Gram Sabha nominees plus one State Forest/Wildlife representative. On private land, the committee instead includes the owner, a State Forest/Wildlife representative and the concerned Panchayat or tribal-community representative.
- A Community Reserve does not create an unrestricted extraction zone. Existing use, NTFP collection and cultivation remain subject to the Act, other applicable law and the management plan; a change in land use needs committee and State approval.
- Current section 29 also clarifies that a permitted continuing right, including bona fide drinking and household water use by local communities until rights are settled, is not treated as prohibited habitat destruction.

**Examiner trap:** The Chief Wildlife Warden manages sanctuaries, but does not replace the Community Reserve Management Committee as the governing authority of a Community Reserve.

### 4. Marine protected areas: same categories, additional jurisdiction

**Answer-grabbing line**

> A “Marine Protected Area” describes ecological setting, not a fifth WPA category: legal protection normally comes through a Marine National Park, Sanctuary or another Chapter IV category, supplemented by coastal and fisheries regulation.

**Must-write keywords:** territorial waters; Central concurrence; Chief Naval Hydrographer; fishermen’s occupational interests; innocent passage; seascape; CRZ.

#### Territorial-water safeguards

For a Sanctuary that includes territorial waters:

1. the State must obtain prior concurrence of the Central Government;
2. limits are determined in consultation with the Chief Naval Hydrographer;
3. adequate measures must protect local fishermen’s occupational interests; and
4. innocent passage of vessels is not affected.

Section 35 applies the territorial-water route to a Marine National Park as far as relevant. Section 36A expressly allows a Conservation Reserve to protect **landscapes and seascapes**.

#### Governance map

```text
WPA CATEGORY
Marine NP / WLS / Conservation Reserve
        +
EPA / CRZ NOTIFICATION
coastal ecological regulation
        +
FISHERIES LAW AND STATE DEPARTMENTS
gear, season, effort and livelihood regulation
        =
actual marine governance
```

PIB’s **18 December 2023** answer recorded **130 notified Marine Protected Areas** across coastal States and islands. It separately recorded **106 Important Coastal and Marine Biodiversity Areas** as identified and prioritised. An ICMBA is a scientific priority area; it is not thereby a notified PA.

**Examples:** Gulf of Mannar Marine National Park, Gulf of Kachchh Marine National Park, Gahirmatha Marine Wildlife Sanctuary and the Dugong Conservation Reserve illustrate different legal routes.

### 5. Tiger Reserves: statutory overlay, core–buffer and NTCA

**Answer-grabbing line**

> A Tiger Reserve is a section 38V statutory landscape whose core seeks inviolate breeding habitat and whose buffer seeks ecological integrity, dispersal and coexistence; neither component erases the underlying land category or local-rights procedure.

**Must-write keywords:** section 38V; NTCA recommendation; Tiger Conservation Plan; critical tiger habitat; buffer; Gram Sabha; dispersal corridor; section 38W.

#### Legal architecture

```text
NTCA recommendation
        ↓
State Government notifies Tiger Reserve
        ↓
TIGER CONSERVATION PLAN
├─ viable tiger, co-predator and prey populations
├─ compatible land use
├─ livelihoods of local people
└─ links between PAs / tiger reserves as dispersal corridors

TIGER RESERVE
├─ CORE / CRITICAL TIGER HABITAT
│  ├─ NP/WLS area
│  ├─ scientific and objective criteria
│  └─ required to be kept inviolate without bypassing rights safeguards
└─ BUFFER / PERIPHERAL AREA
   ├─ lesser degree of habitat protection
   ├─ integrity + dispersal
   └─ coexistence; limits set with Gram Sabha and expert consultation
```

Section 38XA, inserted by the 2022 amendment, states that the tiger chapter is **in addition to, not in derogation of**, the Sanctuary/National Park provisions applying to components of a Tiger Reserve. The statutory overlay therefore strengthens rather than replaces the underlying category.

#### Relocation is conditional, not automatic

No Scheduled Tribe or other forest dweller may be resettled or have rights adversely affected for an inviolate tiger area unless all statutory conditions are met:

1. recognition/determination of rights and acquisition of land or forest rights is complete;
2. State agencies, with affected people’s consent and ecological/social-scientist consultation, establish irreversible damage and threat to tiger/habitat;
3. other reasonable coexistence options are found unavailable through the same consent-and-expert process;
4. a livelihood-protecting resettlement/alternative package is prepared;
5. informed consent of the Gram Sabha and affected persons is obtained; and
6. facilities and land at the resettlement site are provided before existing rights are disturbed.

**Boundary lock:** section 38W requires NTCA recommendation and NBWL approval for boundary alteration. De-notification additionally requires public interest and approval of both bodies.

**Dated anchor:** Madhav Tiger Reserve, notified in March 2025, became the 58th reserve in the July 2025 NTCA record; NR-7 materials still reported 58 in 2026.

### 6. Elephant Reserves and corridors: conservation designation without a new WPA category

**Answer-grabbing line**

> Elephant conservation is legally strongest where landscape planning is translated into protection under existing wildlife, forest and land-use laws; the Elephant Reserve label and corridor identification do not themselves reproduce the tiger core–buffer statute.

**Must-write keywords:** Project Elephant; Steering Committee; overlap; wide-ranging species; corridor; indicative boundary; multi-State coordination.

| Dimension | Tiger Reserve | Elephant Reserve |
|---|---|---|
| Source | WPA section 38V | Project Elephant administrative/conservation framework |
| Authority | State notification on NTCA recommendation | Approval/notification through Project Elephant’s Steering Committee and States |
| Spatial design | Statutory core/critical habitat + buffer | Landscape/reserve mosaic; no equivalent statutory core–buffer formula |
| Rights rule | Detailed section 38V(5) safeguards | Underlying WPA/forest/revenue/private-land status controls rights |
| Connectivity | Tiger Conservation Plan expressly covers linking areas | Corridors and multi-use landscapes are central because elephant ranges are large and fluid |

The 2023 official Atlas recorded **33 Elephant Reserves across 80,777 km²**. The 2023 corridor report recorded **150 corridors in 15 elephant-range States**, warned that this is a **minimum** number, showed only indicative directions and stated that exact boundaries had not yet been demarcated. Identification therefore does not equal land acquisition, PA notification or secured functionality.

PIB’s 14 December 2023 answer explains that Elephant Reserves overlap Tiger Reserves, Sanctuaries and Reserved Forests; infrastructure is regulated through those existing Acts, rules and guidelines.

### 7. Eco-Sensitive Zones: a regulatory buffer, not another PA

**Answer-grabbing line**

> An ESZ is a site-specific Environment (Protection) Act regulatory belt around a National Park or Sanctuary; its purpose is to grade land use at the edge, not to extend the core’s complete prohibition into every neighbouring settlement.

**Must-write keywords:** EPA 1986; Rule 5; site-specific notification; prohibited; regulated; permissible; edge effect; one-kilometre mining bar.

#### Current judicial sequence

| Stage | Legal position |
|---|---|
| Supreme Court, 3 June 2022 | Default minimum one-kilometre ESZ direction around each NP/WLS |
| Supreme Court, 26 April 2023 | Uniform rule modified: paragraph 56.1 does not apply where a draft/final ESZ notification exists, a proposal has reached MoEFCC, or PAs lie on inter-State/shared boundaries |
| Rule retained | Mining is impermissible inside an NP/WLS and within one kilometre of its boundary |
| Operational rule | Governments must follow the 2011 Guidelines and each PA’s notification for prohibited, regulated and permissible activities |

Therefore:

- there is **no uniform statutory ten-kilometre ESZ**;
- an ESZ need not be one kilometre everywhere;
- the residual one-kilometre default cannot be quoted without the 2023 exceptions;
- not every livelihood activity is prohibited; and
- the one-kilometre mining bar remains a distinct judicial rule.

**Write the paragraph**

> **Claim:** ESZ law moved from a universal buffer presumption to notification-led proportionality. **Evidence:** the 2023 *Godavarman* judgment exempted notified/proposed and shared-boundary cases from the 2022 blanket direction but retained the one-kilometre mining prohibition. **Analysis:** this protects edge ecology while allowing site-specific treatment of settlements and infrastructure. **Qualification:** the applicable notification and its activity schedule must be checked for the particular PA.

### 8. Corridors and connectivity: ecological function needs a legal route

**Answer-grabbing line**

> A corridor is an ecological function before it is a legal category; it becomes secure only when land-use planning, a PA notification, easement, acquisition, forest law or community agreement preserves movement in practice.

**Must-write keywords:** dispersal; gene flow; metapopulation; stepping stone; bottleneck; permeability; linear infrastructure; landscape.

#### Why connectivity matters

```text
ISOLATED PA
small population → inbreeding risk → no recolonisation → local extinction risk

CONNECTED LANDSCAPE
core habitat ↔ corridor/stepping stone ↔ core habitat
          gene flow + dispersal + climate-range shift
```

#### Legal routes

- **Conservation Reserve:** Government land adjoining or linking PAs; community consultation; useful statutory corridor route.
- **Community Reserve:** volunteered private/community land; useful where local tenure and conservation commitment coincide.
- **Tiger Conservation Plan:** must address ecologically compatible uses in linking areas.
- **ESZ:** can reduce edge pressure but is not automatically a through-corridor.
- **Elephant corridor identification:** supplies evidence and priority; it does not itself create title or restrictions.
- **Linear-infrastructure mitigation:** wildlife passages, alignment changes, speed controls and fencing design maintain permeability; a map line without these measures may remain a paper corridor.

**Examiner trap:** “All corridors are Conservation Reserves” is false; “Conservation Reserves can never be corridors” is also false.

### 9. IUCN management categories versus Indian legal categories

**Answer-grabbing line**

> IUCN Ia–VI categories classify the primary management objective of a protected area; Indian labels classify it under domestic law. Similar names do not establish legal or analytical equivalence.

**Must-write keywords:** management objective; not a hierarchy; governance type; domestic law; Category II; Category IV; Category V; Category VI.

| IUCN category | Primary idea |
|---|---|
| Ia | Strict Nature Reserve |
| Ib | Wilderness Area |
| II | Large natural/near-natural area protecting ecological processes, with compatible education/recreation |
| III | Natural Monument or Feature |
| IV | Habitat/Species Management Area, often needing active intervention |
| V | Protected Landscape/Seascape shaped by long-term people–nature interaction |
| VI | Protected Area with Sustainable Use of Natural Resources |

Three controls:

1. **Not a hierarchy:** Ia is not inherently “better” than VI; objectives differ.
2. **Name is not proof:** an Indian “National Park” is not automatically IUCN Category II merely because Category II carries the same English label.
3. **Governance is separate:** government, shared, private and indigenous/local-community governance types can occur across management categories.

**Application:** A Community Reserve could pursue an objective resembling V or VI, but the applicable Indian rights and management powers still come from sections 36C–36D, not from IUCN.

### 10. Protected Area versus Biosphere Reserve, Ramsar and World Heritage

**Answer-grabbing line**

> International recognition can raise standards, monitoring and visibility, but enforceable land-use controls still depend on the Indian statute, rules and site-specific management regime operating beneath the label.

| Instrument | Core purpose | Legal effect in India | Can overlap a WPA PA? |
|---|---|---|---|
| WPA Protected Area | Statutory place-based wildlife/habitat protection | Direct domestic rights, entry, activity and management consequences | It is the domestic base category |
| Biosphere Reserve | UNESCO MAB model combining conservation, research and sustainable development through core–buffer–transition functions | Indian programme/administrative zonation; not itself a Chapter IV WPA category | Yes; core commonly includes an NP/WLS |
| Ramsar Site | Wetland of International Importance and wise-use commitment | International designation; domestic force comes from applicable Indian wetland, wildlife, forest and planning law | Yes, or it may lie outside a PA |
| World Heritage Site | Outstanding Universal Value under the 1972 Convention | International inscription and conservation obligation; no automatic conversion into NP/WLS | Yes |

**Madhav example**

```text
MADHAV LANDSCAPE
├─ Madhav National Park → WPA land category
├─ Madhav Tiger Reserve → section 38V tiger framework
└─ Sakhya Sagar → Ramsar wetland within the National Park
```

The 2026 Prelims route tests precisely this non-substitution principle.

### 11. Governance, rights and community participation

**Answer-grabbing line**

> Durable conservation requires lawful restrictions, recognised tenure, credible consent and shared incentives; exclusion without due process creates conflict, while participation without enforceable habitat safeguards can leave biodiversity exposed.

**Must-write keywords:** rights recognition; due process; Gram Sabha; management committee; coexistence; rehabilitation; distributive justice; procedural legitimacy.

#### Participation ladder

```text
INFORMATION
    ↓
CONSULTATION
    ↓
REPRESENTATION ON COMMITTEE
    ↓
CONSENT FOR SPECIFIED DECISION
    ↓
SHARED / COMMUNITY MANAGEMENT
```

- Sanctuary rights settlement may permit a recognised right to continue.
- Conservation Reserve declaration requires community consultation; its committee advises State management.
- Community Reserve notification depends on a voluntary offer; its committee manages the reserve.
- Tiger-buffer limits involve Gram Sabha consultation; core relocation requires informed consent and prior completion of rights and rehabilitation conditions.
- Forest Rights Act recognition and WPA habitat protection must be sequenced rather than treated as mutually cancelling statutes.

#### Relocation test for a Mains answer

Ask:

1. Were individual and community rights recognised?
2. Was irreversible ecological damage scientifically established?
3. Were coexistence alternatives examined?
4. Was consent real, informed and household-specific as well as Gram Sabha-based?
5. Were land, services and livelihood facilities delivered before movement?
6. Is post-relocation welfare and habitat recovery monitored?

**Qualification:** The statute creates demanding safeguards; a claim that every relocation is unlawful or that every notified package proves voluntariness is equally unsound.

### 12. Notification versus effectiveness

**Answer-grabbing line**

> Notification secures a legal boundary; conservation effectiveness is demonstrated only when management inputs and social legitimacy produce measurable habitat, species and connectivity outcomes.

**Must-write keywords:** designation; management plan; staffing; budget; enforcement; participation; MEE; output; outcome; adaptive management.

#### Results chain

```text
NOTIFICATION
legal boundary / authority
        ↓
PLANNING
objectives / zonation / rights / threats
        ↓
INPUTS
staff / finance / data / equipment
        ↓
PROCESS
patrolling / habitat work / participation / conflict response
        ↓
OUTPUT
encroachment controlled / corridor treated / compensation delivered
        ↓
OUTCOME
habitat condition + viable populations + connectivity + equitable coexistence
```

WII’s MEE approach exists because declaration alone does not always produce adequate protection. A count can answer **how many places are notified**; it cannot by itself answer:

- whether boundaries are demarcated;
- whether a management plan is current and funded;
- whether rights and compensation are settled;
- whether a corridor remains permeable;
- whether species and habitat trends improve; or
- whether marine/fisheries authorities are coordinated.

#### Balanced evaluation framework

| Dimension | Indicator |
|---|---|
| Legal | final notification, boundary and enforceable authority |
| Ecological | habitat quality, prey/resource base, invasive species, fire/water regime |
| Spatial | corridor permeability, buffer condition, climate refugia |
| Administrative | staff, finance, intelligence, prosecution, monitoring |
| Social | rights settlement, compensation, participation, conflict burden |
| Outcome | population trend, occupancy, breeding, ecosystem function |

**Conclusion line:** India needs both representative expansion and effective management; neither a larger count nor a single exclusionary model is a sufficient conservation strategy.

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

### A. Protected-area law as governance of people

The four categories distribute authority differently. Sanctuary law allows a recognised right to continue; National Park notification requires included-land rights to vest in the State; Conservation Reserve law consults communities but retains CWLW-centred management; Community Reserve law gives the committee direct management responsibility. The analytical issue is therefore not “people versus animals” in the abstract, but **which right, decided by whom, through which process, for what ecological objective**.

### B. ESZ proportionality after 2023

The 2022 order supplied a strong ecological presumption for a minimum buffer. The 2023 judgment accepted that expert scrutiny and site-specific notifications must account for settlements, infrastructure, shared boundaries and varying landscapes. A good answer uses both propositions: edge protection is necessary, but uniform geometry is not always rational. The separate one-kilometre mining prohibition remains the hard floor.

### C. Marine governance fragmentation

Marine species and fish stocks move through fluid boundaries. The WPA protects sites and listed wildlife; CRZ rules regulate coastal ecological areas; fisheries laws govern harvest; ports, shipping and pollution engage still other authorities. A marine PA can therefore be legally notified yet ecologically ineffective if fishing effort, upstream pollution, shipping or climate-driven range shifts remain unmanaged outside its boundary.

### D. Tiger and elephant spatial design

Tiger core–buffer law is suited to protecting breeding habitat and prey systems while managing dispersal outward. Elephant ranges are often larger, seasonal and multi-State. Their conservation depends more heavily on corridor permeability, crop-conflict governance and infrastructure design. This is a difference in spatial ecology, not a claim that tigers need no corridors or elephants need no secure refuges.

### E. Data limits

- PA totals change with new notifications and must carry a source date.
- “Area notified” is not “area effectively managed.”
- Tiger core boundaries, elephant corridor use and human–wildlife conflict data require site-specific evidence.
- An IUCN category, UNESCO inscription or Ramsar listing cannot be used as a proxy for Indian statutory restrictions.
- The current-count reconciliation in this session is explicit; do not hide an arithmetic inference as a separately published datum.

## CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

### Legal category rail

- WPA Chapter IV: Sanctuary, National Park, Conservation Reserve, Community Reserve.
- Tiger Reserve: statutory section 38V framework, but not one of those four categories.
- Elephant Reserve: Project Elephant designation; protection depends on underlying law.
- ESZ: EPA/Rules regulatory belt; not a WPA PA.
- Corridor: ecological function; not automatically a legal category.

### Notification and rights

- Sanctuary: section 18 intention → Collector claims → section 26A final notification.
- Sanctuary intention notification immediately activates sections 27–33A and recorded-rights-based alternative arrangements; the Collector appointment/proclamation/claim windows are 30 days/60 days/two months.
- Sanctuary admitted right: exclude land, acquire/surrender, or allow continuation.
- Sanctuary management: approved plan; due Gram Sabha consultation where Scheduled/FRA provisions apply.
- National Park: section 24(2)(c) continuation route excluded; included-land rights vest in State before final notification.
- National Park intention notification activates sections 27–33A immediately; pending settlement, the State must arrange fuel, fodder and forest produce according to recorded rights.
- Conservation Reserve: Government land; local-community consultation; useful for adjacent/linking areas.
- Community Reserve: volunteered private/community land; committee manages; land-use change needs committee resolution + State approval; community-land and private-land committee compositions differ after the 2022 amendment.
- Special section 38 route: Centre may declare NP/WLS and, after the 2022 amendment, a Conservation Reserve on transferred State-controlled land; not a Community Reserve.
- WLS boundary alteration: NBWL recommendation, s.26A(3).
- NP boundary alteration: State-Legislature resolution, s.35(5).

### Activity and management

- Sanctuary grazing: regulate/control/prohibit.
- National Park grazing: prohibited, subject to narrow authorised-entry exception.
- Sanctuary: CWLW controls, manages and maintains.
- Conservation Reserve committee: advises CWLW.
- Community Reserve committee: management authority and plan maker.

### Marine safeguards

- Territorial-water Sanctuary: prior Central concurrence, Naval Hydrographer consultation, fishermen’s occupational interests, innocent passage.
- Marine PA is an ecological descriptor; use WPA category + CRZ/fisheries law.
- PIB 18 December 2023: 130 notified MPAs; 106 ICMBAs only identified/prioritised.

### Tiger framework

- State notifies on NTCA recommendation.
- Section 38XA preserves the underlying NP/WLS law; the tiger framework is additional.
- Core/CTH: scientific/objective criteria; inviolate purpose; rights safeguards.
- Buffer: integrity, dispersal, coexistence; Gram Sabha/expert consultation.
- Relocation: rights complete + irreversible damage + no coexistence option + package + informed consent + facilities first.
- Boundary change: NTCA recommendation + NBWL approval.
- De-notification: public interest + both approvals.
- Madhav: March 2025; 58th in NTCA July 2025 record.

### Elephant framework

- 33 Elephant Reserves; official 2023 Atlas area: 80,777 km².
- 150 corridors in 15 States, 2023; minimum count, indicative direction, exact boundaries not demarcated.
- Identification/notification under Project Elephant does not create a tiger-style statutory core–buffer regime.

### ESZ

- EPA/Rules, not WPA.
- Site-specific prohibited/regulated/permissible schedule.
- 2023 Court: blanket one-kilometre rule modified for notified/proposed/shared-boundary cases.
- Mining barred inside NP/WLS and within one kilometre.
- No universal ten-kilometre rule.

### IUCN and recognition firewall

- Ia strict; Ib wilderness; II ecological processes; III natural monument; IV habitat/species management; V landscape/seascape; VI sustainable use.
- IUCN categories = management objectives, not hierarchy or Indian law.
- Indian National Park ≠ automatic IUCN II.
- Biosphere Reserve = core–buffer–transition programme.
- Ramsar = wetland international importance/wise use.
- World Heritage = outstanding universal value.
- All may overlap a WPA PA without replacing it.

### Dated count control

- NR-7 graphic: **1,134 PAs (2025)**; **309 Community Reserves**.
- PIB 16 March 2026: **106 NPs, 574 WLSs, 58 Tiger Reserves, 33 Elephant Reserves**.
- Reconciled Conservation Reserves: **145**, explicitly derived from the four-category total.
- WII’s 27 November 2023 baseline was 1,014; do not reuse it as a 2026 total.

### Final answer line

> India’s protected-area network succeeds when the correct legal category settles rights lawfully, species and marine overlays fit ecological scale, ESZs and corridors maintain landscape permeability, communities share legitimate authority and management effectiveness—not notification alone—shows improved conservation outcomes.
