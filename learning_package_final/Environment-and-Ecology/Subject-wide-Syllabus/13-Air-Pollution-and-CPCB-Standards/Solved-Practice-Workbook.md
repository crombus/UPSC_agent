# Air Pollution and CPCB Standards — Solved Practice Workbook

> **Scope:** exactly 40 original MCQs, all routed Topic 13 PYQs, and six original Mains questions.
> **MCQ key design:** strict A → B → C → D rotation, repeated ten times; A/B/C/D each occurs exactly ten times.
> **PYQ discipline:** wording and options were checked against official UPSC papers. A key is called official only where a local official key record was available.
> **Current-status cut-off:** NCAP performance reported by MoEFCC to Rajya Sabha on 6 August 2026; extant GRAP schedule revised 21 November 2025.

## BASIC MCQS / REMEDIATION

### MCQ 1

Which one of the following is predominantly a **secondary** pollutant in the lower atmosphere?

A. Ground-level ozone
B. Carbon monoxide
C. Lead-bearing dust
D. Sulphur dioxide

**Answer: A**

**Explanation:** Ground-level ozone is formed through sunlight-driven reactions involving NOx and VOCs/CO. CO, lead-bearing dust and SO₂ can be emitted directly. Ozone may be transported, but its defining urban-pollution pathway is atmospheric formation rather than direct tailpipe emission.

---

### MCQ 2

Which reaction pathway most directly explains an important fraction of secondary inorganic PM₂.₅?

A. Carbon monoxide combining with lead particles
B. Ammonia neutralising nitric or sulfuric acid
C. Ozone mechanically breaking coarse road dust
D. Benzene condensing without atmospheric oxidation

**Answer: B**

**Explanation:** NH₃ neutralises nitric and sulfuric acids to form ammonium nitrate and ammonium sulfate, both important fine-particle components. CO does not combine with Pb in this manner; ozone does not create road dust mechanically; and secondary organic aerosol from VOCs generally involves oxidation rather than unreacted benzene simply condensing.

---

### MCQ 3

Consider the following statements:

1. PM₂.₅ and PM₁₀ are aerodynamic size fractions rather than single chemical substances.
2. Particulate matter may be emitted directly or formed from gaseous precursors.
3. Every PM₂.₅ particle necessarily originated from combustion.
4. A PM₁₀ measurement can contain a fine-particle fraction.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. 1 and 3 only
B. 2 and 4 only
C. 1, 2 and 4 only
D. 1, 2, 3 and 4

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Statements 1, 2 and 4 are correct. PM₂.₅ can arise from combustion, but also from secondary sulfate, nitrate, ammonium and organic aerosol; therefore statement 3 is too absolute. PM₂.₅ is nested within the broader PM₁₀ aerodynamic fraction.

---

### MCQ 4

Which sequence best represents daytime photochemical ozone formation?

A. O₃ photolysis → SO₂ release → nitrate formation → NO removal
B. VOC condensation → NH₃ photolysis → O₂ loss → O₃ emission
C. NO₂ deposition → oxygen released from soil → direct O₃ emission; VOCs then remove every oxidant from the air
D. NO₂ photolysis → atomic oxygen combines with O₂ → O₃ forms; VOC-derived radicals help recycle NO to NO₂

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** NO₂ absorbs sunlight and dissociates, allowing atomic oxygen to combine with O₂. VOC/CO oxidation produces radicals that convert NO back to NO₂ without consuming all newly formed ozone, permitting accumulation. The other sequences misstate sources or reactions.

---

### MCQ 5

Which statement about particulate exposure is most accurate?

A. PM₂.₅ generally penetrates deeper into the respiratory tract, but toxicity also depends on composition, dose and vulnerability.
B. PM₁₀ is always chemically more toxic than PM₂.₅.
C. Particle diameter alone identifies the source sector.
D. Ambient PM concentration at one station is identical to every resident's inhaled dose across indoor, roadside and workplace microenvironments.

**Answer: A**

**Explanation:** Smaller aerodynamic size favours deeper penetration, while metals, organics, acidity, dose and host factors also influence harm. Neither size fraction has one fixed chemistry or source. Personal exposure further depends on time, place, indoor infiltration and activity.

---

### MCQ 6

Which pollutant-effect pair is correctly matched?

A. Carbon monoxide — principal driver of acid deposition
B. Ground-level ozone — foliar injury and reduced crop productivity
C. Lead — beneficial micronutrient at all ambient levels
D. Ammonia — depletion of stratospheric ozone as its main local effect

**Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Tropospheric ozone damages leaf tissue, photosynthesis and crop/forest productivity. Acid deposition is driven mainly by sulfur and nitrogen compounds, not CO. Lead is a toxic metal. NH₃ is important for ammonium PM and reactive-nitrogen deposition/eutrophication, not chiefly stratospheric ozone depletion.

---

### MCQ 7

Consider the following pairs:

1. Classical smog — cool, humid, sulfur-rich smoke
2. Photochemical smog — NOx, VOCs and sunlight
3. Classical smog — oxidising ozone/PAN mixture as the defining feature
4. Photochemical smog — ground-level ozone and secondary oxidants

Which pairs are correctly matched?

A. 1 and 2 only
B. 3 and 4 only
C. 1, 2 and 4 only
D. 1, 2, 3 and 4

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Pairs 1, 2 and 4 are correct. Classical London-type smog is traditionally sulfurous, particle-rich and described as reducing; the oxidising ozone/PAN mixture belongs to photochemical smog, making pair 3 incorrect.

### MCQ 8

During a strong near-surface temperature inversion over a city, which outcome is most likely, other things equal?

A. Faster vertical dilution of emissions
B. Automatic chemical destruction of primary pollutants
C. Conversion of every coarse particle into PM₂.₅
D. Suppressed vertical mixing and higher near-surface concentration

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** An inversion creates stable stratification, capping the mixing layer and trapping emissions near the surface. It changes dispersion; it neither destroys all pollutants nor mechanically converts all particle sizes.

---

### MCQ 9

Which statement correctly distinguishes rainout from washout?

A. Rainout incorporates pollutants within cloud processes; washout scavenges them below cloud.
B. Rainout is dry deposition; washout is gravitational settling.
C. Rainout concerns gases only; washout concerns particles only.
D. Both terms describe wet scrubbers fitted to industrial stacks and therefore do not involve atmospheric clouds or precipitation.

**Answer: A**

**Explanation:** Rainout is in-cloud incorporation, whereas washout is below-cloud scavenging by falling precipitation. Both can affect gases and particles depending on solubility and size. They are atmospheric removal pathways, not stack controls.

---

### MCQ 10

For a household using a smoky solid-fuel stove, which intervention best follows the pollution-control hierarchy?

A. Rely only on an outdoor city AQI alert
B. Replace the polluting fuel/stove and add effective source ventilation
C. Close all ventilation while combustion continues
D. Measure room PM continuously while leaving the same fuel, stove and ventilation pathway unchanged

**Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Substitution/elimination and source capture are stronger than warning or measurement alone. Closing ventilation can increase indoor concentration, and an outdoor AQI does not measure the stove-generated indoor exposure.

### MCQ 11

Which one of the following lists only pollutants covered by the 2009 NAAQS?

A. PM₂.₅, methane, carbon dioxide, mercury and black carbon
B. PM₁₀, black carbon, methane and radon
C. Benzene, benzo(a)pyrene, arsenic and nickel
D. CO₂, ozone, mercury and dioxins

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Benzene, particulate-phase BaP, arsenic and nickel are four of the twelve NAAQS pollutants. Methane, CO₂, black carbon as a separate standard, radon, mercury and dioxins are not in the 2009 NAAQS list.

### MCQ 12

Which pair reproduces the 2009 NAAQS correctly?

A. PM₂.₅ annual 60 µg/m³; PM₁₀ annual 40 µg/m³, both in every area category
B. O₃ 8-hour 180 µg/m³; 1-hour 100 µg/m³
C. CO 8-hour 4 µg/m³; 1-hour 2 µg/m³
D. PM₁₀ 24-hour 100 µg/m³; PM₂.₅ 24-hour 60 µg/m³

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** The 24-hour PM values are 100 and 60 µg/m³ respectively. Annual PM₁₀/PM₂.₅ values are 60/40. O₃ is 100 for 8 hours and 180 for 1 hour. CO is expressed in mg/m³: 2 for 8 hours and 4 for 1 hour.

### MCQ 13

Under the 2009 NAAQS, which annual standards are lower in an ecologically sensitive area than in industrial/residential/rural/other areas?

A. SO₂ and NO₂
B. PM₁₀ and PM₂.₅
C. Benzene and BaP
D. Arsenic and nickel

**Answer: A**

**Explanation:** Annual SO₂ is 50 versus 20 µg/m³, and annual NO₂ is 40 versus 30 µg/m³. The listed standards for both PM fractions, benzene, BaP, arsenic and nickel are the same across the two area columns.

### MCQ 14

Which statement reflects the official NAAQS compliance note?

A. Every short-term measurement must remain below the limit without exception.
B. Short-term limits apply 98% yearly; the allowed 2% exceedance cannot occur on consecutive monitoring days.
C. Annual means require one reading each month.
D. Two consecutive exceedances automatically establish annual non-attainment without any need for further regular or continuous monitoring.

**Answer: B**

**Explanation:** CPCB's notification states the 98%/2% rule and bars exceedance on two consecutive monitoring days. Annual arithmetic means require at least 104 measurements taken twice weekly at uniform intervals. Consecutive exceedance triggers regular/continuous monitoring and investigation; it is not identical to an annual-mean determination.

### MCQ 15

Which NAAQS pollutant does **not** enter India's eight-pollutant National AQI?

A. Ammonia
B. Lead
C. Benzene
D. Ozone

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:** AQI includes PM₁₀, PM₂.₅, NO₂, O₃, CO, SO₂, NH₃ and Pb. Benzene, BaP, arsenic and nickel have annual NAAQS but are outside the AQI pollutant set.

### MCQ 16

At one station the sub-indices are PM₂.₅ 286, PM₁₀ 230, NO₂ 92 and O₃ 118. Subject to adequate data, the reported AQI is:

A. 181, the arithmetic mean rounded down
B. 230, because PM₁₀ always controls AQI
C. 118, because ozone uses an 8-hour average
D. 286, the highest pollutant sub-index

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** India's AQI uses the maximum operator. The highest available sub-index is 286 for PM₂.₅. Different averaging times are already incorporated in pollutant-specific sub-index calculation and do not justify averaging the sub-indices.

### MCQ 17

Which data condition is required by CPCB for calculating an overall AQI?

A. At least three pollutants, one PM fraction, and 16 hours for each sub-index.
B. All twelve NAAQS pollutants, each with a complete annual arithmetic mean and no missing hourly observations.
C. Only PM₂.₅ and PM₁₀, each with an annual mean.
D. Any one pollutant if its reading exceeds NAAQS.

**Answer: A**

**Explanation:** CPCB specifies at least three pollutants with one PM fraction and at least 16 hours for a sub-index. Individual pollutant sub-indices may still be shown when overall AQI data are insufficient.

### MCQ 18

Which AQI breakpoint pair is correct?

A. PM₂.₅ “Good”: 0–50 µg/m³
B. PM₂.₅ “Satisfactory”: 31–60 µg/m³
C. PM₁₀ “Poor”: 101–250 µg/m³
D. NO₂ “Very Poor”: 181–280 µg/m³

**Answer: B**

**Explanation:** PM₂.₅ Good is 0–30 and Satisfactory 31–60. PM₁₀ 101–250 is Moderate; Poor is 251–350. NO₂ 181–280 is Poor; Very Poor is 281–400.

### MCQ 19

Which conclusion can legitimately be drawn from a city's real-time AQI?

A. Every individual in the city received the same dose.
B. All twelve NAAQS pollutants complied with every averaging period.
C. It reports the worst available sub-index for that place/time, subject to data limitations.
D. It identifies the source with highest national emissions as the cause of the local controlling sub-index, without apportionment.

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:** AQI is a communication index based on available monitored pollutants. It is not a personal exposure measure, a complete NAAQS compliance finding, or a source-apportionment result. CPCB also cautions that real-time analyser data may be unscreened for statutory use.

### MCQ 20

Which matching is correct?

A. NAMP manual station — continuous measurement of stack emissions at a single industrial source
B. CAAQMS — annual industrial consent document
C. OCEMS — personal exposure during commuting
D. NAMP/CAAQMS — ambient concentration; OCEMS — source emission

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** NAMP and CAAQMS observe ambient air at receptor locations, with different temporal resolution. OCEMS/CEMS monitors source emissions. Consent is a legal instrument, and personal exposure requires different measurement design.

---

### MCQ 21

Which statement correctly distinguishes an emission inventory from source apportionment?

A. An inventory estimates emissions; apportionment estimates contributions to ambient concentration.
B. An inventory measures personal dose; apportionment sets legal standards.
C. An inventory is always receptor-based; apportionment uses only fuel sales.
D. They are interchangeable names for the AQI.

**Answer: A**

**Explanation:** Inventories use activity data, emission factors, surveys and source monitoring. Source apportionment uses ambient measurements and dispersion/receptor models to attribute receptor concentration. Neither is an AQI or personal-dose measure.

### MCQ 22

A study finds that crop-residue burning contributed substantially to Delhi PM₂.₅ during a particular November episode. Which inference is defensible?

A. It must remain the dominant source in every month, at every receptor and for every pollutant across the NCR.
B. The share is specific to episode, pollutant, place and method; it is not a fixed annual share.
C. Local vehicle and dust controls are therefore irrelevant.
D. The measured concentration share is identical to the health-burden share.

**Answer: B**

**Explanation:** Source shares change with season, meteorology, pollutant and receptor. An episodic finding cannot erase year-round local/regional sources or be equated automatically with exposure and health burden.

### MCQ 23

Consider the following under the Air Act, 1981:

1. CPCB lays down standards for air quality.
2. SPCBs inspect industrial plants and enforce source-emission requirements.
3. Prior consent is relevant for an industrial plant in an air-pollution-control area.
4. AQI categories are themselves specified as criminal offences.

Which are correct?

A. 1 and 4 only
B. 2 and 3 only
C. 1, 2 and 3 only
D. 1, 2, 3 and 4

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Statements 1–3 reflect sections 16, 17 and 21. AQI is a CPCB communication framework, not an offence schedule under the Air Act.

### MCQ 24

What is the most accurate role of the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 in air-pollution control?

A. It replaces every function of SPCBs under the Air Act.
B. It creates the National AQI categories, pollutant breakpoints and daily colour bands directly in the enacted statutory text.
C. It applies only to indoor household smoke.
D. It gives the Union umbrella powers, directions and rule-based standards that complement the Air Act.

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** The EPA complements rather than abolishes the Air Act architecture. Sections 3, 5 and 6 support measures, directions and standards; the Environment (Protection) Rules provide sector-specific and general emission/discharge standards.

### MCQ 25

Which statement about CAQM is correct?

A. It covers NCR and defined adjoining areas affecting NCR air and prevails in specified conflicts.
B. It is a voluntary committee for every Indian city.
C. It replaced CPCB, every SPCB and every Pollution Control Committee for all air, water and waste matters throughout India.
D. It administers WHO guidelines as binding Indian law.

**Answer: A**

**Explanation:** The 2021 Act defines adjoining areas in Haryana, Punjab, Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh linked to NCR air impacts and gives CAQM strong coordinating/direction powers. Its jurisdiction is regional, not nationwide, and WHO AQGs are not Indian statutes.

### MCQ 26

Which institution-function pair is best matched?

A. MoEFCC — computes every citizen's personal dose
B. SPCB/PCC — consent, inspection and local industrial enforcement within the applicable legal framework
C. CAQM — notifies every NAAQS value for all India under section 16(2)(h) of the Air Act and revises WHO guidelines

**Answer: B**

**Explanation:** SPCBs/PCCs are central to on-ground consent and enforcement. CPCB notifies/coordinates national ambient standards and technical systems; MoEFCC handles national policy/rules; CAQM has a regional NCR mandate; WHO issues global health guidance.

### MCQ 28

Which description of the 21 November 2025 GRAP schedule is correct?

A. Stage III begins at AQI 301 and cancels Stage I measures.
B. GRAP operates as the same permanent national programme in all NCAP cities and is triggered by each city's annual PM₁₀ mean.

### MCQ 29

Which package most directly targets vehicular emissions?

A. BS-VI, inspection/maintenance and PUC strengthening, vapour recovery, public transport/electrification
B. Liming acidified lakes, designating wetlands, installing fish ladders and expanding irrigation canals as the principal urban vehicle-emission package
C. Only anti-smog guns at construction sites
D. Only taller industrial stacks

**Answer: A**

**Explanation:** The listed measures address fuel/engine exhaust, evaporative VOCs, gross emitters and travel activity. Anti-smog guns target local dust; taller stacks redistribute rather than eliminate emissions; the ecological measures are not a primary vehicle-control package.

### MCQ 30

Which industrial-control pairing is most appropriate?

A. SO₂ — road vacuum sweeping
B. PM — electrostatic precipitator/bag filter; SO₂ — flue-gas desulfurisation; NOx — combustion or catalytic controls
C. NOx — only increasing chimney height so the same emission mass is dispersed farther, without combustion or post-combustion controls
D. Fugitive dust — disabling enclosure and suction

**Answer: B**

**Explanation:** PM capture, sulfur removal and NOx-specific combustion/post-combustion controls match pollutant mechanisms. Stack height does not reduce total emissions, and fugitive controls require enclosure, capture and housekeeping.

### MCQ 31

Which set represents a balanced crop-residue strategy?

A. Penalties and prosecution alone, without machinery access, agronomic support, straw markets or changes in the harvest-to-sowing constraint
B. Cloud seeding as a permanent substitute for residue management
C. In-situ management, ex-situ supply chains, crop/incentive reform, monitoring and proportionate enforcement
D. Treating seasonal fires as the sole year-round urban source

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:** A durable strategy combines feasible alternatives, demand for straw, agronomic/economic change and enforcement. Punishment alone ignores incentives; cloud seeding is episodic; and seasonal contribution should not erase local and other regional sources.

### MCQ 32

For construction dust, which intervention most clearly acts at source?

A. Publishing citywide AQI and health advisories after dust has left the site, without changing storage, handling or transport
B. Moving the monitor farther from the site
C. Raising a nearby stack
D. Covering material and transport, wheel washing, enclosure/barriers and controlled handling

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** Preventing entrainment and tracking addresses the source pathway. Monitoring reports pollution but does not control it; relocating a monitor hides rather than reduces exposure; a stack is irrelevant to most fugitive construction dust.

### MCQ 33

A factory cuts stack PM emissions by 40%, but a nearby community's measured PM₂.₅ changes little because regional secondary aerosol and traffic dominate. Which conclusion is best?

A. Emission reduction occurred, but ambient concentration and exposure outcomes depend on the full source-atmosphere system.
B. The stack control certainly failed because a valid source-emission reduction must produce an identical percentage fall at every ambient receptor.
C. Ambient concentration and source emission are identical quantities.
D. Health benefits must equal exactly 40%.

**Answer: A**

**Explanation:** Source control may be real even when the receptor response is diluted by other sources and chemistry. The case demonstrates why emission, concentration, exposure and health outcome must be evaluated separately.

### MCQ 34

Which is an **outcome**, rather than merely an input or output, of a clean-air programme?

A. Funds released for buses, charging infrastructure and monitoring equipment, whether or not these are used effectively
B. Comparable annual PM₁₀ concentration falls after quality-controlled monitoring
C. Number of action plans uploaded
D. Number of monitors purchased

**Answer: B**

**Explanation:** A defensible concentration reduction is an intermediate environmental outcome. Funds are inputs; plans and purchased monitors are outputs. A final health outcome would require exposure and epidemiological evidence beyond the concentration trend.

### MCQ 35

Which statement correctly compares WHO 2021 AQGs and Indian NAAQS?

A. WHO values automatically amend Indian law.
B. An annual WHO value may be compared with a one-hour NAAQS without qualification.
C. WHO AQGs guide health; NAAQS are notified standards, so match pollutant, unit and averaging time.
D. Both frameworks contain the same pollutants, averaging periods, legal force and compliance rule for every monitored location.

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:** WHO recommendations inform health ambition but do not legally replace NAAQS. Comparisons require like-for-like metrics; the pollutant sets, averaging periods and compliance designs differ.

### MCQ 36

Which statement about artificial rainfall for pollution control is most accurate?

A. It permanently eliminates vehicle, industrial, dust and biomass emissions even after seeded precipitation has stopped.
B. It works without suitable clouds.
C. It can remove every gas and particle with equal efficiency.
D. With seedable clouds it may aid temporary scavenging, but it cannot replace source control.

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** Cloud seeding is meteorologically contingent and episodic. Precipitation may scavenge some particles/soluble gases while transferring material to surfaces; ongoing emissions and non-scavenged pollutants remain.

### MCQ 37

Which pair is principally associated with acid-rain formation?

A. Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides
B. Carbon monoxide and lead
C. Ozone and methane alone
D. Ammonia and elemental carbon alone

**Answer: A**

**Explanation:** Oxidation of sulfur and nitrogen oxides forms sulfuric and nitric acids. NH₃ can neutralise these acids and influence particulate ammonium, but SO₂ and NOx are the principal acid-rain precursors in this framing.

### MCQ 38

In an ammonia-rich airshed with abundant nitric acid, reducing agricultural NH₃ emissions would most directly tend to:

A. Increase ammonium nitrate formation because reducing ammonia automatically creates more gaseous nitric acid and particles in every season
B. Constrain formation of ammonium nitrate, though the response may be non-linear
C. Convert PM₂.₅ into PM₁₀ mechanically
D. Eliminate all ozone irrespective of NOx and VOC chemistry

**Answer: B**

**Explanation:** NH₃ is the base needed to neutralise nitric acid into particulate ammonium nitrate. Chemical equilibrium, temperature and precursor availability can make the response non-linear. The measure does not mechanically resize particles or independently eliminate ozone.

### MCQ 39

During a winter episode, which evidence package best supports source-specific action?

A. One citywide AQI number, without pollutant composition, station metadata, meteorology or any source-specific evidence
B. A national annual emission total only
C. Quality-controlled concentration/speciation, meteorology, a current inventory and receptor/dispersion modelling
D. A social-media attribution without method or season

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:** Combining measurements, composition, weather and complementary models provides a defensible attribution chain. AQI alone communicates severity; a national total lacks local receptor resolution; unsupported attribution is not evidence.

### MCQ 40

Which dashboard would best evaluate a city clean-air plan?

A. Expenditure, equipment procurement and meeting counts, without any comparable pollution, exposure or health result
B. Daily AQI without source or meteorological context
C. Number of penalties without compliance or pollution trends
D. Inputs and outputs plus comparable emissions, ambient concentration, exposure distribution and health/ecosystem indicators

**Answer: D**

**Explanation:** A complete dashboard follows the causal chain from capacity and implementation to environmental and welfare outcomes, while controlling for data quality and meteorology. Single administrative or AQI counts cannot establish policy effectiveness.

## Solved Prelims PYQs

### PYQ 1 — UPSC Prelims 2019, GS-I, Q35

**Ownership:** Application — biomass/crop-residue emissions
**Provenance:** Official UPSC 2019 GS-I paper; routed in `_PYQ-ROUTING-PRELIMS-2018-2023.md`.
**Key label:** **INFERRED ANSWER — NOT OFFICIALLY VERIFIED LOCALLY (high confidence)**

Consider the following:

1. Carbon monoxide
2. Methane
3. Ozone
4. Sulphur dioxide

Which of the above are released into atmosphere due to the burning of crop/biomass residue?

A. 1 and 2 only
B. 2, 3 and 4 only
C. 1 and 4 only

### PYQ 11 — UPSC Mains 2020, GS-III, Q17

**Ownership:** Direct — NCAP
**Provenance:** Official UPSC Mains 2020 GS-III paper; routed in `_PYQ-ROUTING-MAINS-GS3-GS4-2018-2023.md`.
**Question:** What are the key features of the National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) initiated by the Government of India?
**Limit:** 250 words | **Marks:** 15

**Demand:** Identify programme design, target, scale, instruments, monitoring and implementation limitation; preserve the historical target applicable to the 2020 question.

**Model answer**

The National Clean Air Programme, launched by MoEFCC in January 2019, is India's national framework for reducing urban particulate pollution through coordinated national, State and city action.

**Key features**

- **Target and baseline:** it originally sought a 20–30% reduction in PM₁₀ and PM₂.₅ concentrations by 2024, using 2017 as the reference, while recognising that targets were indicative rather than a separate penal statute.
- **City focus:** non-attainment cities prepare city-specific clean-air action plans based on their source mix.
- **Multi-sector approach:** plans cover vehicles, road and construction dust, industry, waste/biomass burning and household fuels rather than treating pollution as a single-source problem.
- **Evidence architecture:** expansion of ambient monitoring, emission inventories and source-apportionment studies is intended to connect action with actual local contributors.
- **Federal coordination:** MoEFCC/CPCB provide the national framework; SPCBs, urban bodies and sector departments implement and monitor actions.
- **Convergence and review:** programme resources are combined with transport, urban, energy and waste schemes; progress is reviewed through programme-management systems.

**Assessment:** NCAP correctly shifts policy from episodic response to source-oriented planning, but its effectiveness depends on representative monitoring, enforceable sectoral action and municipal/SPCB capacity. Targets or uploaded plans are outputs, not proof of attainment.

**Conclusion:** NCAP's value lies in making clean air a measurable, city-specific and inter-sectoral governance programme; its success must be judged by comparable concentration, exposure and health outcomes.

**Why this earns marks:** It answers “key features,” gives the original target and institutional design, adds implementation analysis and avoids using a later target anachronistically.

### PYQ 13 — UPSC Mains 2022, GS-III, Q7

**Ownership:** Direct — photochemical smog
**Provenance:** Official UPSC Mains 2022 GS-III paper; routed in `_PYQ-ROUTING-MAINS-GS3-GS4-2018-2023.md`.
**Question:** Discuss in detail the photochemical smog emphasizing its formation, effects and mitigation. Explain the 1999 Gothenburg Protocol.
**Limit:** 150 words | **Marks:** 10

**Model answer**

Photochemical smog is an oxidising mixture formed when sunlight acts on NOx and VOCs/CO. NO₂ photolysis produces atomic oxygen, which forms ground-level O₃; VOC-derived radicals recycle NO to NO₂, permitting O₃ accumulation. PAN, aldehydes and secondary aerosols accompany it in stagnant air.

It irritates airways, aggravates asthma and reduces lung function; ozone also injures leaves and lowers crop/forest productivity.

Mitigation requires NOx and VOC control: cleaner fuels/engines, public transport, vapour recovery, solvent controls, industrial combustion standards, leak detection and chemistry-aware airshed planning. Measures must reflect whether an area is NOx- or VOC-sensitive.

The 1999 Gothenburg Protocol operates under the UNECE Convention on Long-range Transboundary Air Pollution. It uses a multi-pollutant, multi-effect approach to reduce sulfur, NOx, VOCs and ammonia causing acidification, eutrophication and ground-level ozone; its 2012 amendment added particulate commitments. India is not governed by this regional protocol.

**Why this earns marks:** It covers mechanism, effects, controls and the Protocol's institutional location, pollutant scope and India-related qualification.

### Original 1 — 10 marks

**Question:** Distinguish source emission, ambient concentration and personal exposure. Why can a fall in one fail to produce an equal fall in the others?
**Limit:** 150 words

**Model answer**

**Emission** is pollutant mass released by a source, such as g/s from a stack. **Ambient concentration** is pollutant mass per air volume at a receptor and averaging period. **Exposure** is the concentration contacted across locations, time and activities.

The three are linked but not proportional. A factory may cut stack PM, yet regional secondary aerosol, traffic and dust can keep neighbourhood concentration high. Conversely, favourable wind or rain may lower concentration without a durable emission reduction. Two residents under the same AQI can have different exposure because one cooks with biomass or works beside traffic.

Evaluation must separately track source compliance, quality-controlled ambient trends and population exposure. Meteorology, chemistry, transport, indoor infiltration and behaviour mediate each transition.

**Conclusion:** clean-air success is not one number; it is a verified chain from lower emissions to lower concentration, equitable exposure reduction and better health.

**Why this earns marks:** It defines all terms, demonstrates two-way non-equivalence with named examples and reaches a qualified evaluation principle.

### Original 2 — 10 marks

**Question:** Why are emission inventories and source-apportionment studies complementary rather than interchangeable tools for urban air-quality management?
**Limit:** 150 words

**Model answer**

An **emission inventory** estimates how much each source emits, commonly using activity data, emission factors, surveys, GIS grids and CEMS. Its output is mass by pollutant, place and time. **Source apportionment** estimates how much measured ambient pollution at a receptor comes from source categories, using chemical composition, source profiles, meteorology, dispersion or receptor models.

They answer different questions. A large emissions source may contribute less at a receptor because of distance, stack height, wind or deposition; secondary PM may form from several precursor sectors. Conversely, road dust may dominate local PM₁₀ concentration despite a smaller regional mass total.

CPCB's model framework therefore combines a season-aware inventory, ambient/speciation monitoring, dispersion modelling and receptor validation before prioritising measures.

**Conclusion:** inventory identifies the emissions burden; apportionment identifies the receptor burden. Policy needs both, with uncertainty ranges and no transfer of one city's seasonal shares to another.

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes method, unit and policy use, then explains why atmospheric processes prevent substitution.

### Original 3 — 15 marks

**Question:** Evaluate India's NAAQS–AQI–monitoring architecture. How can it be made more reliable for regulation and public health?
**Limit:** 250 words

**Model answer**

India's architecture performs three different functions. The **2009 NAAQS** provide notified pollutant-, unit- and averaging-period standards for twelve pollutants. The **National AQI** converts eight short-term pollutants into sub-indices and reports the maximum as a public warning. **NAMP/manual stations and CAAQMS** generate ambient evidence, while OCEMS monitors sources.

**Strengths**

- NAAQS create a common legal benchmark, including toxic pollutants beyond PM.
- AQI makes complex data understandable and identifies the controlling pollutant.
- Continuous stations enable episode response; manual networks support longer trends.
- The 6 August 2026 official network count—593 continuous and 1,053 manual stations—shows major expansion.

**Gaps**

- AQI excludes benzene, BaP, arsenic and nickel and is not a personal-exposure measure.
- CPCB cautions that raw real-time analyser data may not be statutory evidence without scrutiny.
- Station siting and density can miss roadside, industrial and low-income hotspots.
- Annual means can hide episodes; one AQI can hide multi-pollutant burdens.
- NAAQS and WHO AQGs differ substantially for PM, requiring transparent health review rather than casual numerical comparison.

**Reform**

Create a quality-assured regulatory data layer separate from provisional real-time display; publish calibration/downtime metadata; expand speciation, rural and hotspot coverage; integrate source CEMS, inventories and apportionment; add exposure cohorts and health surveillance; and report uncertainty.

**Conclusion:** AQI should warn, NAAQS should regulate, and monitoring should prove outcomes. Reliability comes from keeping these roles distinct but interoperable.

**Why this earns marks:** The answer evaluates each layer, uses exact official evidence, identifies limitations and offers mechanism-linked reforms.

**Question:** Air pollution is simultaneously a health, ecosystem and economic problem. Discuss with pollutant-specific examples and source-specific controls.

Air pollution is not a single-PM problem; different pollutants connect sources to multiple damages.

### Original 5 — 20 marks

**Question:** Critically evaluate India's air-pollution governance architecture through an input–output–outcome lens. Refer to the Air Act, EPA, CPCB–SPCB/PCC system, CAQM, NCAP and GRAP.
**Limit:** 300 words

**Model answer**

India has a layered architecture, but institutional activity must be separated from environmental outcome.

**Legal and institutional design**

The Air Act, 1981 assigns national coordination and air-quality standard-setting to CPCB and consent, inspection and source enforcement to SPCBs; PCCs perform delegated Union-Territory functions. The Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 adds Union rule-making, industry standards and binding directions. The CAQM Act, 2021 creates an NCR airshed authority whose directions prevail in specified conflicts. NCAP supplies long-term city planning, whereas GRAP supplies cumulative, forecast-based episode response.

**Input–output record**

Inputs include grants, staff, monitors, CEMS, buses, residue-management machinery and dust-control equipment. Outputs include city plans, inspections, stations, road sweeping, compliant equipment and GRAP orders. These improve state capacity but do not themselves prove cleaner air.

**Outcome evidence**

MoEFCC's 6 August 2026 reply assessed 130 NCAP cities: 108 had lower annual PM₁₀ than 2017–18, 72 reduced above 20%, 26 above 40%, and 14 met NAAQS. This demonstrates broad but uneven progress; it does not justify claiming universal attainment of the revised “up to 40% or NAAQS by 2025–26” goal.

**Critical gaps**

- fragmented implementation across transport, agriculture, power and municipalities;
- weak comparability where stations, siting or data completeness change;
- GRAP's reactive restrictions cannot replace structural action;
- source shares vary seasonally, while city averages conceal exposure inequality;
- consent or equipment installation may not yield sustained compliance.

**Way forward**

Use airshed budgets, quality-assured monitoring, season-specific inventory/apportionment, transparent source compliance, exposure/health indicators and performance finance tied to verified outcomes. Strengthen municipal/SPCB capacity while preserving CAQM's regional coordination.

**Conclusion:** the architecture is institutionally comprehensive but outcome credibility depends on closing the chain from legal direction to emissions, concentration, exposure and health.

**Why this earns marks:** It covers every named institution, applies the required evaluation lens, uses current official results and gives a balanced verdict.

### Original 6 — 20 marks

**Question:** Design an integrated clean-air strategy for a non-attainment city affected by traffic, industry, crop-residue smoke, construction dust and household pollution. Include measurement, implementation and equity safeguards.
**Limit:** 300 words

**Model answer**

The city should begin with an **airshed diagnosis**, not a generic action list.
