### Legal-currency box

1. **Solid waste:** the **Solid Waste Management Rules, 2026** superseded the 2016 Rules,
   except for things already done or omitted, and took effect on **1 April 2026**. The 2016 Rules
   remain indispensable for historical PYQs and for understanding the transition, but are not the
   current operating rules.
2. **Plastic waste:** the **Plastic Waste Management Rules, 2016** remain the principal rules,
   read with amendments through **G.S.R. 73(E), 23 January 2025** at this review cut-off.
3. **E-waste:** the **E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022**, effective **1 April 2023**, remain the
   principal rules, read with the 2023 amendments and **G.S.R. 164(E), 8 March 2024**.
4. A **rule**, **amendment**, **portal procedure**, **registration**, **certificate** and
   **verified material outcome** are different legal/evidentiary objects. Never substitute one for
   another.

## BASIC LEARNING SESSION

## 1. From a linear waste chain to circular material flow

### 1.1 Visual first

```text
LINEAR THROUGHPUT
virgin material → product → short use → mixed collection → dump/burn/unsafe processing

MATERIAL-CIRCULAR ROUTE
avoid demand
   ↓
reduce material + design for durability/disassembly
   ↓
reuse / refill / repair / refurbish
   ↓
segregate at source
   ↓
separate collection → sorting/MRF → authorised processor
   ↓
material recycling → secondary raw material
   ↓
recovery only for suitable non-recyclable fraction
   ↓
safe treatment and disposal of irreducible residue
```

The statutory SWM hierarchy places **prevention, reduction, reuse, recycling, recovery and
disposal** in that order. Circularity therefore does not mean “collect more waste” or “burn waste
for energy”. It means retaining product, component and material value at the highest practicable
level before accepting recovery or disposal.

### 1.2 Why source segregation is the hinge

Mixed waste converts potentially useful material into a contaminated stream:

```text
food/wet waste + paper/plastic + sanitary/special-care waste
        ↓ mixing
lower material quality + worker exposure + odour/leachate + poor compost + larger residue

separate streams at source
        ↓
wet → compost/biomethanation
dry → sorting/reuse/recycling
sanitary/special-care → protected collection + appropriate treatment
```

Segregation is not the final outcome; it is the condition that makes later processing safe and
credible.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** A circular waste system protects value before it manages residue:
> prevention and reuse conserve embedded material and energy, while source segregation preserves
> the quality needed for safe recycling.

**Must-write keywords:** waste hierarchy · lifecycle · embedded value · source segregation ·
material flow · residual fraction · circularity.

## 2. Exact vocabulary: activity is not outcome

| Term | Exact exam meaning | What it does **not** prove |
|---|---|---|
| **Generation** | Waste comes into existence at household, commercial, manufacturing or end-of-life stage | That it has entered a lawful chain |
| **Segregation** | Sorting and separate storage of defined streams | That the streams remain separate in transport |
| **Collection** | Physical taking/aggregation of waste from a generator or collection point | Processing, recycling or safe disposal |
| **Transportation** | Environmentally sound movement in suitable vehicles/containers | That the destination is authorised or operational |
| **Sorting / MRF** | Separation of recyclable and non-recyclable fractions | Transformation into a new material |
| **Processing** | Scientific handling for reuse, recycling or transformation | Necessarily material recycling |
| **Recycling** | Transformation of segregated waste into new material/product or raw material | Reuse, refurbishing, energy recovery or disposal |
| **Recovery** | Reclaiming material or, lower in the hierarchy, useful energy | Circularity automatically; residues and emissions remain |
| **Refurbishing** | Extending the working life of the same EEE for the same intended use | Final recycling; e-waste EPR is deferred, not extinguished |
| **Treatment** | Changing physical, chemical or biological characteristics to reduce harm/volume | Recovery of value in every case |
| **Disposal** | Final safe destination for residual/inert or otherwise authorised non-recoverable fractions | Open dumping, littering or unverified burning |

### 2.1 The proof ladder

```text
entity registered
   ≠ waste collected
   ≠ waste processed
   ≠ material recycled
   ≠ EPR certificate generated
   ≠ certificate purchased/extinguished
   ≠ independently verified environmental outcome
```

A registration proves that an entity has entered the regulatory system. A certificate records a
compliance instrument generated under prescribed rules. A verified outcome additionally needs
material-flow evidence, mass balance, authorised destination, capacity, audit and safe residue
management.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Waste governance fails when documentary compliance is treated as a
> substitute for physical material flow.

**Must-write keywords:** chain of custody · mass balance · authorised destination · certificate
integrity · physical audit · throughput · residue.

## 3. Three principal streams and the overlap firewall

| Stream | Current principal framework | Primary accountability spine | Key exclusion/firewall |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary solid waste | SWM Rules, 2026 | Generator segregation → local-body collection → registered processing → residual-only landfill | Industrial, hazardous, biomedical, e-waste, battery and radioactive waste remain under separate rules |
| Plastic waste | PWM Rules, 2016 as amended | Generator/local-body duties + producer/importer/brand-owner EPR + registered plastic waste processors | An item containing plastic is not automatically a prohibited SUP item or the same EPR category |
| E-waste | E-Waste Rules, 2022 as amended | Producer EPR through registered recyclers; manufacturer/refurbisher/recycler duties | Waste batteries and packaging plastics remain under their own rules |

All three are subordinate legislation under the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**, but they
are not one combined code. The material stream, responsible actor and lifecycle stage must be
identified before selecting the rule.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** A common parent statute does not collapse separate material streams,
> actors and end-of-life obligations into one rule.

**Must-write keywords:** stream-specific law · obligated entity · lifecycle stage · parent statute
· overlap firewall.

## 4. Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016 — historical and PYQ baseline

### 4.1 Exact coverage after the 2020 amendment

The 2016 Rules applied to urban local bodies, urban-agglomeration outgrowths, census towns,
notified areas and industrial townships, Railways, airports/airbases, ports/harbours, defence
establishments, SEZs, government organisations and notified pilgrimage/religious/historical
places, plus domestic, institutional, commercial and other non-residential generators in those
areas. The 2020 amendment inserted **villages with population more than 3,000**.

They excluded industrial waste, hazardous waste/chemicals, biomedical waste, e-waste, lead-acid
batteries and radioactive waste governed separately.

### 4.2 Waste-generator duties

Under rule 4, a generator had to:

- segregate into **biodegradable, non-biodegradable and domestic hazardous** streams;
- wrap used sanitary waste and place it with dry/non-biodegradable waste;
- keep C&D and horticultural waste separately;
- not throw, burn or bury waste in streets, public spaces, drains or water bodies;
- pay the local user fee;
- give advance notice for an unlicensed gathering above 100 persons and manage its segregated
  waste;
- hand street-vending waste to the notified collection system.

Resident/market associations, gated communities and institutions above **5,000 sq m**, hotels and
restaurants had source-segregation duties and were to process biodegradable waste through
composting or biomethanation on-site **as far as possible**.

### 4.3 Bulk waste generator

The 2016 definition covered specified government, public/private, health, educational, lodging,
commercial, market, worship and sports premises with average generation **exceeding 100 kg/day**.
It did not use floor-area and water-consumption alternatives; those are features of the 2026
Rules.

### 4.4 Local-body duties

The local authority/Panchayat was required to build the operating chain:

```text
bye-laws + user fee + spot fine
 → door-to-door segregated collection
 → recognise/integrate waste pickers and SHGs
 → MRFs + domestic-hazardous deposition centres
 → separate transport
 → decentralised wet-waste processing
 → recycling / RDF / co-processing where suitable
 → residual-only sanitary landfill
 → biomining/bioremediation or scientific capping of old dumps
```

It also had to provide training and PPE to workers and prevent intermixing during transport.

### 4.5 Processing and landfill firewall

- Biodegradable waste: composting, vermicomposting, biomethanation/anaerobic digestion or other
  bio-stabilisation.
- Dry recyclable waste: MRF and authorised recyclers.
- Suitable non-recyclable combustible fraction: RDF, co-processing or waste-to-energy subject to
  standards.
- Landfill: only non-usable, non-recyclable, non-biodegradable, non-combustible, non-reactive
  inert waste and processing rejects/residues; mixed waste was to stop going to landfill.
- Non-recyclable waste with calorific value **1,500 kcal/kg or more** was not to be landfilled.

### 4.6 Exact amendments

| Notification | Exact high-yield change |
|---|---|
| G.S.R. 298(E), 10 April 2019 | Rule 15(h): recyclable-waste bin colour changed from **white to blue** |
| S.O. 1152(E), 19 March 2020 | “Ministry of Urban Development” replaced by **Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs**; villages above 3,000 added; Central implementation duty added; local bodies required compartmentalised, covered collection/transport from listed premises |

> **Answer-grabbing line:** The 2016 Rules shifted the municipal system from mixed collection and
> dumping toward generator segregation, decentralised processing, waste-picker integration and
> residual-only sanitary landfilling.

**Must-write keywords:** three streams · >100 kg/day · 5,000 sq m · MRF · biomethanation ·
residual-only landfill · biomining · blue bin.

## 5. Solid Waste Management Rules, 2026 — current law

### 5.1 Legal status and coverage

S.O. 388(E), 27 January 2026, superseded the 2016 Rules and commenced on **1 April 2026**. It
applies to urban **and rural** local bodies and public/private entities and generators within their
jurisdictions, including listed government, transport, industrial-notified, commercial and
institutional areas. Separate-rule exclusions remain.

### 5.2 Four-stream source segregation

Every generator must separate:

1. **wet waste**;
2. **dry waste**;
3. **sanitary waste**; and
4. **special care waste**.

Special-care waste includes specified household discards such as paint/pesticide containers,
lamps, expired medicines, mercury thermometers, waste batteries, needles/syringes and contaminated
gauze. This collection stream does not erase the downstream battery, biomedical or hazardous-waste
firewall.

### 5.3 Current bulk waste generator test

An listed institutional, commercial or residential-society entity is a bulk waste generator if it
meets **any one** of these:

- floor area **20,000 sq m or above**;
- water consumption **40,000 litres/day**; or
- solid-waste generation **100 kg/day**.

Bulk generators must register on the centralised portal, manage dry/sanitary/special-care waste
through the authorised chain, process wet/horticultural waste in a decentralised manner, file
returns and avoid unregistered entities.

### 5.4 Extended Bulk Waste Generator Responsibility

New bulk generators must set up adequate wet-waste processing. An existing generator unable to do
so may obtain a local-body exemption and procure **Extended Bulk Waste Generator Responsibility
(EBWGR) certificates**. The local body generates certificates only against waste collected,
transported and sent to registered processors/recyclers; for wet waste, processor reporting must
precede generation. Certificates remain valid for **three years**.

This is a compliance route, not permission to mix waste or proof that every claimed kilogram
became a useful product.

### 5.5 Local body, processing and MRF duties

Local bodies/concessionaires must register, preserve segregation through collection and transport,
use separate compartments/arrangements, hand waste to registered facilities, protect workers with
training/PPE and file public returns. Registered MRFs may also act as drop-off/deposition points
for plastic, e-waste and other EPR streams, but must channel them under their separate rules.

### 5.6 Current landfill rule

Only non-usable, non-recyclable, non-biodegradable, non-combustible and non-energy-recoverable dry
waste, inert waste, processing rejects/residues and residual waste may enter sanitary landfill.
**Wet waste and C&D waste may not.**

During a temporary processing-capacity gap, wrongly routed waste attracts a weight-based landfill
user fee. That fee must be **higher than collection, transportation and processing costs**, making
segregation/processing the cheaper route. It is not a permanent licence for mixed dumping.

### 5.7 Legacy waste and RDF

- Existing dumpsites/GVPs must be mapped and assessed by **31 October 2026**. At this cut-off that
  is a statutory deadline, not an achieved national outcome.
- Biomining/bioremediation must follow CPCB guidance, destination records, leachate control, fire
  prevention and audit.
- For specified solid-fuel units within the notified distance, suitable segregated combustible
  fraction/agri-residue above 1,500 kcal/kg has a phased **6% → 10% → 15%** fuel-substitution
  schedule. Do not replace this with a generic “5% to 15%” claim.
- Waste sent to WtE must meet at least 1,500 kcal/kg; RDF sent to cement plants at least
  3,000 kcal/kg under the rule.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** The 2026 Rules add rural coverage, four-stream segregation, digital
> registration, EBWGR and stronger economic disincentives against landfilling recoverable waste,
> but their success still depends on verified processing and local capacity.

**Must-write keywords:** supersession · four streams · rural coverage · 20,000 sq m ·
40,000 L/day · 100 kg/day · EBWGR · three-year validity · landfill user fee.

## 7. Plastic categories, restrictions and actor map

### 7.1 Current EPR categories

| Category | Current coverage |
|---|---|
| **I** | Rigid plastic packaging |
| **II** | Flexible single-layer or plastic–plastic multilayer packaging; sheets/covers, carry bags, sachets and pouches |
| **III** | Multilayered packaging with at least one plastic layer and at least one non-plastic layer |
| **IV** | Packaging sheets/carry bags **and commodities** made of compostable plastics |
| **V** | Packaging sheets/carry bags **and commodities** made of biodegradable plastics |

Category III is not “any package with several plastic layers”; that belongs to Category II.

### 7.2 Identified SUP prohibition

From **1 July 2022**, manufacture, import, stocking, distribution, sale and use were prohibited for:

- ear buds with plastic sticks; balloon sticks; plastic flags; candy and ice-cream sticks;
  polystyrene/thermocol for decoration;
- plates, cups, glasses, forks, spoons, knives, straws, trays and stirrers;
- wrapping/packing film around sweet boxes, invitation cards and cigarette packets; and
- plastic/PVC banners below **100 microns**.

This is an item-specific prohibition, not a ban on all single-use plastic or all plastic packaging.

### 7.3 Thickness and material rules

- Plastic carry bags: **75 microns from 30 September 2021** and **120 microns from
  31 December 2022**.
- Non-woven plastic carry bags: at least **60 GSM** from 30 September 2021.
- Plastic sheets/covers for packaging: generally at least **50 microns**, subject to the
  functionality provision in the rule.
- Plastic sachets for gutkha, tobacco and pan masala remain prohibited.
- Compostable/biodegradable products require CPCB certification and prescribed labels; a
  “biodegradable” marketing claim without the rule's standard/certificate is not an exemption.

### 7.4 Actor map

| Actor | Core role |
|---|---|
| Generator | Minimise, segregate, avoid litter and hand over through the authorised chain |
| Local body / Panchayat | Collection and processing infrastructure, annual assessment, SUP enforcement, waste-picker engagement |
| Producer/importer/brand owner | Category-wise EPR, returns, registration, collection or certificate compliance |
| Plastic waste processor | Registered recycling, industrial composting, waste-to-energy/oil or co-processing; certificates and returns |
| Raw-material manufacturer/importer/seller | Registration, sell only to registered entities, invoice/quarterly-report duties, no supply for prohibited SUP |

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Plastic policy uses two different levers: an item-specific prohibition
> for selected low-utility/high-litter products and lifecycle EPR for packaging that remains legal.

**Must-write keywords:** Category I–V · item-specific prohibition · 120 microns · 60 GSM ·
PIBO · PWP · raw-material traceability.

## 8. Plastic EPR — targets, certificates and compensation

### 8.1 Current 2026–27 obligations

The category-wise EPR target has been **100% since 2023–24**. For financial year **2026–27**:

| Obligation | I | II | III | IV |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Minimum recycling/industrial composting as % of EPR target | 70 | 50 | 50 | 70 |
| Mandatory recycled content as % of plastic manufactured/imported for the year | 40 | 10 | 5 | — |

For Category I rigid packaging used by brand owners, minimum reuse in 2026–27 is:

- **15%** for packaging from 0.9 up to below 4.9 litre/kg; and
- **75%** for packaging of 4.9 litre/kg or more.

Category V biodegradable plastic follows its separate certificate/biodegradation route; do not
invent a Category V percentage by extending the I–IV table.

### 8.2 Registration

The centralised portal covers producers (including micro/small for registration), importers,
brand owners, plastic waste processors, raw-material manufacturers/importers and manufacturers of
compostable/biodegradable items. Current rule 13 uses **one-time registration** for PIBOs and PWPs.
No registered entity should deal with an entity whose registration is mandatory but absent.

Micro/small producers' EPR quantity is assigned to the raw-material manufacturer/importer that
supplied them, while the producer retains the recycled-content obligation. Micro/small brand
owners remain outside the Schedule II obligated-brand-owner category. Keep registration and
obligation separate.

### 8.3 Certificate chain

```text
registered PWP receives segregated plastic
   → processes it within authorised capacity
   → reports category-wise quantity / end use
   → certificate generated on portal
   → obligated entity acquires and files it
   → portal cross-checks both sides
   → lower reconciled quantity counts
   → CPCB/SPCB audit may verify physical evidence
```

Only registered PWPs issue processing certificates, except the prescribed self-declaration route
for plastic used in road construction. A certificate cannot exceed installed capacity and must
remain category-specific.

The 2024 amendment provides an electronic trading platform and a certificate exchange band:
the CPCB-fixed highest and lowest prices equal **100% and 30%**, respectively, of the applicable
environmental compensation.

The special 2023 paragraph allowing CPCB-authorised purchase across categories expressly ceased
at the end of **2025–26**. It is not a current 2026–27 relaxation.

### 8.4 End-of-life and environmental compensation

Only plastic that cannot be recycled should go to prescribed end-of-life routes such as road
construction, waste-to-energy, waste-to-oil or co-processing. Feedstock chemicals produced for
new plastic may fall within recycling under the 2022 definition; merely calling a thermal process
“chemical recycling” does not prove that condition.

Environmental compensation:

- follows polluter-pays;
- does not erase the unfulfilled EPR obligation;
- carries the shortfall forward for up to three years; and
- if cured, returns **75% within one year, 60% within two years and 40% within three years**;
  thereafter the amount is forfeited.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Plastic EPR converts producer responsibility into a category-wise
> digital certificate market; its credibility therefore depends on whether portal entries match
> real, authorised and environmentally sound material throughput.

**Must-write keywords:** 100% EPR target · 70/50/50/70 · 40/10/5 · reuse 15/75 · one-time
registration · lower figure · 30–100% price band · 75/60/40.

## 9. E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022 — scope and actors

### 9.1 Status and application

G.S.R. 801(E), 2 November 2022, superseded the 2016 e-waste rules and took effect on
**1 April 2023**. It applies to manufacturers, producers, refurbishers, dismantlers and recyclers
dealing with listed EEE, components, consumables, parts and spares.

It does not apply to:

- waste batteries under the Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022;
- packaging plastics under the PWM Rules;
- micro enterprises as defined by the MSMED Act; or
- radioactive waste under atomic-energy law.

### 9.2 Actor duties

| Actor | Exact functional role |
|---|---|
| Manufacturer | Portal registration; collect manufacturing e-waste; ensure recycling/disposal; returns |
| Producer | Portal registration; obtain/meet EPR target; awareness; returns |
| Refurbisher | Register; extend same-use product life; send refurbishment waste to registered recycler; returns |
| Recycler | Register; use CPCB-compliant process; send unrecycled fractions to other registered recyclers; send residues to authorised TSDF; maintain auditable records |
| Dismantler | May work for a recycler under CPCB guidelines; gives dismantled material only to registered recycler |
| Bulk consumer | Gives e-waste only to registered producer, refurbisher or recycler |

Only **manufacturer, producer, refurbisher and recycler** are portal-registration categories.
A dismantler is within the rule's application but is not a fifth portal category.

### 9.3 Informal-worker and State duty

State/UT labour authorities must recognise/register dismantling and recycling workers, assist
group formation, build skills and undertake annual health-and-safety monitoring. This makes worker
transition part of the legal design, not merely a welfare add-on.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** The 2022 Rules separate collection reach from hazardous processing:
> material may be collected widely, but EPR is fulfilled only through the registered recycling
> chain.

**Must-write keywords:** Schedule I · registered recycler · dismantler boundary · bulk consumer ·
TSDF · worker recognition.

## 10. E-waste EPR, targets and certificates

### 10.1 Current target

For financial year **2026–27**, Schedule III requires recycling of **70%** of the quantity of the
relevant EEE placed on the market in year **Y–X**, where X is the CPCB-notified average product
life.

Special cases:

- recent sellers: from 2025–26 onward, **20% of sales two financial years earlier** until the
  normal average-life method applies;
- imported used EEE: **100%** EPR at end of life if not re-exported;
- solar PV modules/panels/cells: no Schedule III/IV recycling target; manufacturers/producers must
  store generated waste through **2034–35** under CPCB guidance.

### 10.2 Recycling certificate

CPCB generates an EPR certificate through the portal in favour of a registered recycler. Eligible
quantity is derived from verified end product multiplied by a prescribed conversion factor. CPCB's
implementation framework currently uses key recovered end products such as iron, aluminium,
copper and gold.

- Validity: **two years from the end of the financial year of generation**.
- Producer buys certificates online from registered recyclers and files quarterly returns.
- Producer and recycler data are cross-checked; the lower figure counts where they differ.
- A producer may buy up to current liability + prior shortfall + **5% of current liability**.
- Purchase extinguishes the matched certificate; portal availability is not proof of environmental
  performance without audit.

### 10.3 Refurbishing certificate

Refurbishing extends the same product's life. Its certificate:

1. defers the producer's EPR for the CPCB-set extended-life period;
2. returns only **75% of the deferred quantity** to the later recycling obligation; and
3. never extinguishes EPR by itself—final extinguishment requires registered recycling and an EPR
   certificate.

### 10.4 Compensation and trading

E-waste environmental compensation does not absolve EPR. If a shortfall is cured, the return is:

- **85% after one year**;
- **60% after two years**;
- **30% after three years**; and
- none thereafter.

G.S.R. 164(E), 8 March 2024, permits one or more regulated exchange platforms and fixes the
certificate price ceiling/floor at **100% and 30%** of environmental compensation.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** An e-waste certificate is a metal- and mass-balance-linked compliance
> instrument; refurbishment postpones the recycling obligation but cannot convert product-life
> extension into final material recovery.

**Must-write keywords:** Y–X · 70% · 100% used-import duty · two-year certificate · +5% purchase
cap · refurbish deferment · 75% later liability · 85/60/30.

## 11. RoHS and current e-waste amendments

### 11.1 Core RoHS rule

New listed EEE/components/consumables/parts/spares must not exceed:

- **0.1% by weight in homogeneous material** for lead, mercury, hexavalent chromium,
  polybrominated biphenyls and polybrominated diphenyl ethers; and
- **0.01%** for cadmium.

Producers self-declare compliance and maintain technical documentation. CPCB may conduct random
sampling at the producer's cost; non-compliant products require corrective action and withdrawal
or recall.

### 11.2 Amendment map

| Amendment | Exact exam use |
|---|---|
| G.S.R. 61(E), 30 January 2023 | RoHS information supplied as and when CPCB requires; added specified solar and medical-device exemptions |
| G.S.R. 534(E), 24 July 2023 | Secure refrigerant management; conversion-factor rule for multiple recycling end products; revised RoHS exemption schedules/timelines |
| G.S.R. 164(E), 8 March 2024 | Updated dismantler definition; return/report extension up to nine months by Central order; regulated certificate-exchange platforms and price band |

The July 2023 amendment exempts specified Schedule II-C EEE placed on the market on or before
**1 April 2025**, and specified compatible parts/spares until **1 April 2028** where compliant
alternatives are unavailable. Do not describe RoHS as an absolute exemption-free ban.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** E-waste law controls both ends of the lifecycle: RoHS reduces hazardous
> input at design/manufacture, while EPR and authorised recycling control end-of-life material flow.

**Must-write keywords:** homogeneous material · 0.1% · cadmium 0.01% · self-declaration · random
sampling · recall · refrigerant.

## 12. Battery, biomedical, hazardous and C&D overlap firewall

```text
waste battery
   → Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022

infectious/pathological/sharps waste from healthcare activity
   → Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016

hazardous recycling residue / TSDF-bound waste
   → Hazardous and Other Wastes Rules, 2016

construction and demolition waste
   → Environment (Construction and Demolition) Waste Management Rules, 2025

packaging around EEE
   → Plastic Waste Management Rules

discarded listed EEE itself
   → E-Waste Rules
```

An EEE can therefore produce several regulated outputs after dismantling. The product is not
assigned one permanent legal identity that follows every separated fraction.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Waste classification follows the material and hazard after separation;
> one discarded product can enter several lawful downstream regimes.

**Must-write keywords:** fraction-specific routing · battery firewall · biomedical sharps · TSDF ·
C&D separation.

## 13. Informal sector, worker safety and just transition

Waste pickers, kabadi networks and informal dismantlers often provide collection reach, sorting
knowledge and livelihoods. Yet open burning, crude acid leaching, uncontrolled breaking and
unprotected handling transfer toxic risk to workers and neighbourhoods.

The correct policy is neither exclusion nor romanticisation:

```text
recognise + register workers/groups
 → identity, contracts and fair payment
 → source-separated collection and aggregation
 → PPE, training, health surveillance and social protection
 → channel material to registered MRFs/recyclers
 → prohibit unsafe processing
 → reskill/upgrade enterprises for formal recovery roles
```

SWM law requires integration/training/PPE; e-waste law assigns State labour authorities worker
recognition, group formation, skills and annual safety monitoring. EPR finance can support this
transition only if contracts and certificate value reach collectors rather than stopping at
formal intermediaries.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** A just waste transition formalises hazardous processes without
> informalising livelihoods.

**Must-write keywords:** recognition · aggregation · occupational health · PPE · cooperative/SHG ·
fair value capture · authorised processing.

## 14. How to evaluate policy: input, output and outcome

| Level | Examples | What it proves |
|---|---|---|
| Input | Rules, portal, finance, staff, land | Capacity/authority exists |
| Output | Registrations, collection vehicles, MRFs, plants, certificates | Activity or infrastructure exists |
| Intermediate result | Source-segregation rate, verified tonnes processed, plant uptime, residue destination | Chain is functioning |
| Environmental outcome | Less dumping/burning/leachate/toxic exposure; secondary material displaces virgin input | Harm and material loss are reduced |
| Social outcome | Safer work, stable livelihoods, cleaner neighbourhoods, fair service | Transition is inclusive |

### 14.1 Delhi and Indore — exact analytical contrast

**Delhi:** Ghazipur, Bhalswa and Okhla are legacy-dump remediation cases. MCD uses biomining and
land reclamation; its official 2025 planning documents set site-specific target dates, while the
public dashboard still showed continuing operations in September 2026. Treat the case as
**remediation in progress**, not completed success or total failure.

**Indore:** the transferable feature is segregation-led door-to-door collection, processing
discipline, citizen/ULB enforcement and wet-waste valorisation. PIB recorded the **550 TPD**
source-segregated wet-waste Bio-CNG plant inaugurated on 19 February 2022. This is evidence of a
processing model, not proof that every material fraction has zero residue.

```text
Delhi: mixed legacy stock → biomining/remediation → land recovery
Indore: source segregation → separate collection → processing/value recovery

lesson: cure is necessary, but prevention is cheaper and more durable
```

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Delhi demonstrates the cost of correcting a historic mixed-waste
> stock; Indore demonstrates the advantage of preventing that stock through segregation-first
> flow management.

**Must-write keywords:** legacy stock · biomining · prevention-first · door-to-door segregation ·
dated status · no outcome overclaim.

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

## 16. EPR is principally a verification problem

EPR changes incentives by assigning lifecycle liability upstream, but certificate trading
separates the producer from the physical waste handled by a recycler. That flexibility can lower
compliance cost and support specialised processors. It also creates risks:

- false input invoices or output sales;
- certificates beyond installed/actual capacity;
- double counting;
- category or material misclassification;
- unsafe residue disposal after valuable fractions are removed; and
- exclusion of informal collectors from certificate value.

The response is not to reject markets but to strengthen material accounting:

```text
purchase/source evidence
 + weighment
 + input composition
 + process yield/conversion factor
 + sale of recovered output
 + residue destination
 + capacity/energy consistency
 + surprise physical audit
 = defensible certificate
```

## 17. Technology neutrality with a residue test

Pyrolysis, plasma gasification, incineration, waste-to-oil, co-processing and biomethanation are
not interchangeable. Their suitability depends on feedstock, contamination, moisture, calorific
value, emissions, energy balance and residue destination.

- Recycling retains material value.
- Feedstock recovery may count as recycling only where the legal definition is met.
- Energy recovery is below recycling in the hierarchy.
- Incineration reduces volume but creates flue-gas and ash-management duties.
- Plasma/pyrolysis labels do not eliminate sorting or pollution-control requirements.

**Technology test:** What enters? What useful output leaves? Where do carbon, toxics and residues
go? What monitoring proves the claim?

## 18. Flow control versus legacy-stock remediation

EPR mainly governs **new product/waste flows**. Dumpsites, contaminated land and orphaned/historical
e-waste are accumulated **stocks**. Stopping new leakage does not remove the stock; cleaning the
stock without reforming current flow recreates it.

```text
FLOW POLICY: design + EPR + segregation + collection + authorised processing
STOCK POLICY: inventory + risk ranking + biomining/remediation + safe destination + monitoring
```

## CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

### Material-flow spine

- Hierarchy: prevent → reduce → reuse → recycle → recover → dispose.
- Reuse/repair/refurbish preserve more embedded value than destructive recycling.
- Segregation preserves material quality and worker safety.
- Collection ≠ processing ≠ recycling ≠ recovery ≠ disposal.
- Registration/certificate ≠ verified outcome.

### Solid-waste legal spine

- 2016: three streams; >100 kg/day bulk definition; ULB collection/MRF/processing; residual-only
  landfill.
- 2019: recyclable bin white → blue.
- 2020: MoHUA name; villages >3,000; covered compartmentalised transport.
- 2026: supersedes from 1 April; urban+rural; four streams.
- Current BWG: 20,000 sq m or 40,000 L/day or 100 kg/day.
- EBWGR certificate: local-body generated against registered processing; valid three years.
- Landfill: no wet/C&D; wrong waste user fee higher than collection+transport+processing.
- Dumpsite mapping deadline: 31 October 2026—deadline, not achieved outcome.

### Plastic spine

- Principal PWM Rules 2016; current amendments through G.S.R. 73(E), 23 January 2025.
- Categories: I rigid; II flexible plastic-only; III plastic + non-plastic multilayer; IV
  compostable; V biodegradable.
- Identified SUP prohibition from 1 July 2022; not a blanket ban.
- Carry bags 120 microns from 31 December 2022; non-woven 60 GSM.
- FY 2026–27: recycling I/II/III/IV = 70/50/50/70.
- Recycled content I/II/III = 40/10/5.
- Rigid reuse = 15% (0.9–<4.9 L/kg), 75% (≥4.9 L/kg).
- Plastic EC return = 75/60/40; payment does not erase EPR.
- Certificate price band = 30–100% of EC.
- Cross-category paragraph 15A expired after 2025–26.

### E-waste spine

- Rules 2022 effective 1 April 2023; amendments through G.S.R. 164(E), 8 March 2024.
- Registrants: manufacturer, producer, refurbisher, recycler; dismantler is not a fifth portal
  category.
- FY 2026–27 normal target = 70% of EEE placed in market in Y–X.
- Recent sellers = 20% of sales two years earlier; used imports = 100%.
- Solar PV: no target; storage through 2034–35.
- EPR certificate valid two years; producer purchase ceiling includes current + arrears + 5%.
- Refurbishing defers EPR; 75% later returns; final recycling extinguishes it.
- E-waste EC return = 85/60/30.
- RoHS: 0.1% for Pb/Hg/Cr6+/PBB/PBDE; Cd 0.01%, subject to schedules/exemptions.

### Worker and overlap spine

- Integrate collectors; formalise hazardous processing.
- Recognition + fair contracts + PPE + health monitoring + skills + social protection.
- Batteries, biomedical waste, hazardous residues and C&D waste retain separate rule routes.
- One product can split into several legally distinct downstream fractions.

### Final examiner line

> Judge waste policy by avoided generation, source-segregated material retained in productive use,
> verified authorised processing, safely managed residue and protected workers—not by registrations,
> installed plants or certificates alone.
