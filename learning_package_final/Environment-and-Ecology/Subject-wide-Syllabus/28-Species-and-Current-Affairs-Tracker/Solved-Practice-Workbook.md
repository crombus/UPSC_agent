## SOLVED PREVIOUS-YEAR QUESTIONS — 16 ROUTED DEMANDS

### PYQ 1 — 2018 Prelims GS-I, Q86 · Shared route

**Stem** (restored from the local official scan `QP-CSP-18-GS-I-C.pdf`; the scan prints the four
option letters as a block before the four option texts, so letters are re-paired in printed order):

> Why is a plant called *Prosopis juliflora* often mentioned in news?
> (a) Its extract is widely used in cosmetics.
> (b) It tends to reduce the biodiversity in the area in which it grows.
> (c) Its extract is used in the synthesis of pesticides.
> (d) None of the above

**Key status:** official 2018 key **not held locally**.

**Reasoned outcome: (b).** *Prosopis juliflora* is an introduced, fast-spreading, nitrogen-fixing
leguminous tree that forms dense thickets, suppresses native grasses and shrubs through shading and
allelopathy, and draws down shallow soil moisture. Indian reporting about it is almost entirely
invasion-ecology reporting — encroachment on grassland and commons, and knock-on effects on grazing
and on grassland birds.

**Why each alternative fails.** (a) There is no prominent cosmetic industry built on its extract, and
that is not why it is in the news. (c) Pesticidal synthesis is associated in Indian reporting with
neem, not mesquite. (d) Since (b) is defensible, "none of the above" cannot be.

**Tracker link:** application card — invasive alien species. The tracker contributes identity
discipline, not a conservation-status field; *Prosopis* is not a threatened taxon.

### PYQ 2 — 2019 Prelims GS-I, Q31 · Shared route

**Stem** (local official scan `csp-p1.pdf`):

> Recently, there was a growing awareness in our country about the importance of Himalayan nettle
> (*Girardinia diversifolia*) because it is found to be a sustainable source of
> (a) anti-malarial drug (b) biodiesel (c) pulp for paper industry (d) textile fibre

**Key status:** official 2019 key **not held locally**.

**Reasoned outcome: (d).** The nettle's stem yields a long bast fibre — locally called *allo* — that
is retted, hand-spun and woven in the mid-Himalaya. The item entered the news through rural
livelihood and handloom programmes, which is a fibre story.

**Why each alternative fails.** (a) Antimalarial attention in India attaches to *Artemisia* and to
cinchona, not to nettle. (b) Biodiesel feedstock reporting concerns *Jatropha* and used cooking oil.
(c) Paper pulp in India is a bamboo, eucalyptus and agro-residue question; nettle is harvested in
quantities far too small for pulping.

**Tracker link:** application card — economic botany and non-timber forest produce.

### PYQ 3 — 2020 Prelims GS-I, Q72 · Shared route

**Stem** (local official scan `CSP_2020_GS_Paper-1.pdf`):

> With reference to Indian elephants, consider the following statements:
> 1. The leader of an elephant group is a female.
> 2. The maximum gestation period can be 22 months.
> 3. An elephant can normally go on calving till the age of 40 years only.
> 4. Among the States in India, the highest elephant population is in Kerala.
> Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
> (a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 4 only (c) 3 only (d) 1, 3 and 4 only

**Key status:** official 2020 key **not held locally**.

**Reasoned outcome: (a).** Elephant herds are matriarchal, led by the oldest experienced female.
Gestation in *Elephas maximus* runs to roughly 22 months, the longest of any land mammal. Statement 3
is false — cows continue to calve well past 40. Statement 4 is false; Kerala holds a substantial
population but is not the highest-ranked State. The all-India ranking is not restated without a
dated source.

**Why each alternative fails.** (b) imports the false Kerala claim. (c) rests only on the false
calving ceiling. (d) compounds both errors while dropping the gestation fact.

**Tracker link:** flagship longitudinal card — *Elephas maximus*. Note the firewall: this question is
about the species' **biology**, not about its IUCN category or Schedule.

### PYQ 4 — 2020 Prelims GS-I, Q74 · Application route

**Stem** (local official scan):

> With reference to India's biodiversity, Ceylon frogmouth, Coppersmith barbet, Gray-chinned minivet
> and White-throated redstart are
> (a) Birds (b) Primates (c) Reptiles (d) Amphibians

**Key status:** official 2020 key **not held locally**.

**Reasoned outcome: (a).** All four names belong to birds: a nocturnal insectivore of the Western
Ghats and Sri Lanka, a widespread metallic-calling barbet, a montane forest passerine of the
minivet group, and a Himalayan chat. The item tests whether unfamiliar vernacular bird names can be
placed in the right class.

**Why each alternative fails.** (b) Indian primate names are macaques, langurs, the lion-tailed
macaque and the hoolock gibbon — none of these. (c) and (d) Reptile and amphibian common names in
India follow entirely different conventions (skinks, geckos, pit vipers; bush frogs, caecilians).

**Tracker link:** application card — faunal identification, no status fields.

### PYQ 5 — 2020 Prelims GS-I, Q77 · Shared route

**Stem** (local official scan; option letters degraded as "(b).", "(ec)", "(ad)" and restored in
printed order, cross-checked against the Hindi column of the same page):

> Which of the following are the most likely places to find the musk deer in its natural habitat?
> 1. Askot Wildlife Sanctuary 2. Gangotri National Park 3. Kishanpur Wildlife Sanctuary
> 4. Manas National Park
> Select the correct answer using the code given below:
> (a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 3 and 4 only (d) 1 and 4 only

**Key status:** official 2020 key **not held locally**.

**Reasoned outcome: (a).** The musk deer is a high-altitude Himalayan animal of temperate and
sub-alpine forest and scrub. Askot (Uttarakhand, Kumaon Himalaya) and Gangotri National Park
(Uttarakhand, upper Bhagirathi) both sit in that altitudinal band. Kishanpur is a terai sanctuary of
the Gangetic plain and Manas lies on the Brahmaputra plain at the Bhutan foothills — both far below
musk-deer habitat.

**Why each alternative fails.** (b), (c) and (d) each admit at least one lowland site, which is the
precise error the question is constructed to catch.

**Tracker link:** shared card — the species supplies the habitat band, the protected-area owner
supplies the site identities.

### PYQ 6 — 2021 Prelims GS-I, Q21 · Application route

**Stem** (local official scan `QP-CSP-21-GeneralStudiesPaper-I-121021.pdf`):

> Which one of the following is used in preparing a natural mosquito repellent?
> (a) Congress grass (b) Elephant grass (c) Lemongrass (d) Nut grass

**Key status:** official 2021 key **not held locally**.

**Reasoned outcome: (c).** Lemongrass (*Cymbopogon* spp.) yields citral- and citronellal-rich
essential oils; citronella-type oils are the basis of widely sold plant-derived repellents.

**Why each alternative fails.** (a) Congress grass (*Parthenium hysterophorus*) is an invasive weed
notorious for dermatitis and allergic rhinitis — a health hazard, not a repellent. (b) Elephant grass
(*Pennisetum purpureum*) is a fodder and bioenergy grass. (d) Nut grass (*Cyperus rotundus*) is a
persistent agricultural sedge weed with traditional medicinal use, but not repellent use.

**Tracker link:** application card — economic botany.

### PYQ 7 — 2021 Prelims GS-I, Q23 · Application route

**Stem** (local official scan):

> Consider the following animals: 1. Hedgehog 2. Marmot 3. Pangolin
> To reduce the chance of being captured by predators, which of the above organisms rolls up/roll up
> and protects/protect its/their vulnerable parts?
> (a) 1 and 2 (b) 2 only (c) 3 only (d) 1 and 3

**Key status:** official 2021 key **not held locally**.

**Reasoned outcome: (d).** Hedgehogs contract a specialised circular muscle to curl into a spine-
covered ball; pangolins roll into a tight ball armoured by overlapping keratin scales, with the head
tucked inside. Marmots are burrowing ground squirrels whose escape response is to run to a burrow
and give alarm calls.

**Why each alternative fails.** (a) and (b) include the marmot, which does not use a rolling defence.
(c) drops the hedgehog, the textbook example of the behaviour.

**Tracker link:** application card — defensive adaptation.

### PYQ 8 — 2021 Prelims GS-I, Q63 · Application route

**Stem** (local official scan; the scan prints the four option letters as a block before the four
option texts, so letters are re-paired in printed order):

> Consider the following statements:
> 1. Moringa (drumstick tree) is a leguminous evergreen tree.
> 2. Tamarind tree is endemic to South Asia.
> 3. In India, most of the tamarind is collected as minor forest produce.
> 4. India exports tamarind and seeds of moringa.
> 5. Seeds of moringa and tamarind can be used in the production of biofuels.
> Which of the statements given above are correct?
> (a) 1, 2, 4 and 5 (b) 3, 4 and 5 (c) 1, 3 and 4 (d) 1, 2, 3 and 5

**Key status:** official 2021 key **not held locally**.

**Reasoned outcome: (b).** Statement 1 is false twice over: *Moringa oleifera* belongs to
Moringaceae, not to the legume family, and it is deciduous over much of its range. Statement 2 is
false: *Tamarindus indica* originated in tropical Africa and was carried into South Asia long enough
ago to be assumed native. Statements 3, 4 and 5 are each sound — tamarind is collected as minor
forest produce, both commodities are exported, and both seeds yield oil usable as biofuel feedstock.

**Why each alternative fails.** (a), (c) and (d) each retain statement 1 or statement 2, which are the
two planted errors.

**Tracker link:** application card — economic botany and biogeographic origin.

### PYQ 9 — 2022 Prelims GS-I, Q45 · Application route

**Stem** (local official scan `GENERAL STUDIES PAPER I.pdf`). **OCR restoration declared:** *both*
local extractions of this page drop the quoted subject term from the stem, printing
"With reference to ______ sometimes mentioned in the news". The missing term is restored as
**"Gucchi"** from corroborating renderings of the same official question; the restoration is flagged
rather than silently inserted, and the three statements and four options below are exactly as the
local scan prints them.

> With reference to "Gucchi" sometimes mentioned in the news, consider the following statements:
> 1. It is a fungus. 2. It grows in some Himalayan forest areas.
> 3. It is commercially cultivated in the Himalayan foothills of north-eastern India.
> Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
> (a) 1 only (b) 3 only (c) 1 and 2 (d) 2 and 3

**Key status:** official 2022 key **not held locally**.

**Reasoned outcome: (c).** Gucchi is the morel, an ascomycete fungus of the genus *Morchella*. It
fruits in temperate Himalayan forest, chiefly in Jammu and Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand,
and is gathered by hand. Commercial cultivation has not been established at scale, and the
north-eastern foothills are not its harvesting zone.

**Why each alternative fails.** (a) discards the true habitat statement. (b) and (d) both accept the
false cultivation claim, which is the single error the question plants.

**Correction recorded.** An earlier framing of this route described the organism as a Himalayan
"caterpillar fungus". That is a different taxon — *Ophiocordyceps sinensis*, the parasitic
*keeda jadi* of alpine meadows. The official 2022 stem, read locally, is about the morel. The two are
kept apart here precisely because conflating them is the kind of identity error the tracker exists to
prevent.

**Tracker link:** application card — fungal diversity and non-timber forest produce.

### PYQ 10 — 2023 Prelims GS-I, Q14 · Application route

**Stem** (local official scan `QP_CS_Pre_Exam_2023_280523.pdf`):

> Consider the following fauna: 1. Lion-tailed Macaque 2. Malabar Civet 3. Sambar Deer
> How many of the above are generally nocturnal or most active after sunset?
> (a) Only one (b) Only two (c) All three (d) None

**Key status:** official 2023 key **not held locally**.

**Reasoned outcome: (b).** The lion-tailed macaque is a diurnal, canopy-dwelling Western Ghats
primate that forages by day and sleeps at night. The Malabar civet is nocturnal. Sambar are
crepuscular to nocturnal, feeding largely at dusk and through the night. Two of the three therefore
qualify.

**Why each alternative fails.** (a) would require two of the three to be diurnal; only the macaque is.
(c) wrongly makes a diurnal primate nocturnal. (d) contradicts well-documented civet and sambar
activity patterns.

**Tracker link:** application card — activity rhythm.

### PYQ 11 — 2023 Prelims GS-I, Q15 · Application route

**Stem** (local official scan):

> Which of the following organisms perform waggle dance for others of their kin to indicate the
> direction and the distance to a source of their food?
> (a) Butterflies (b) Dragonflies (c) Honeybees (d) Wasps

**Key status:** official 2023 key **not held locally**.

**Reasoned outcome: (c).** The waggle dance of *Apis* encodes two quantities on the vertical comb:
the angle of the waggle run relative to vertical gives the bearing to the food source relative to the
sun, and the duration of the waggle run encodes distance.

**Why each alternative fails.** (a) Butterflies are not eusocial and do not recruit nestmates.
(b) Dragonflies are solitary aerial predators with no recruitment communication. (d) Some wasps are
social and recruit, but the waggle dance is specific to honeybees.

**Tracker link:** application card — animal communication.

### PYQ 12 — 2023 Prelims GS-I, Q17 · Application route

**Stem** (local official scan):

> Consider the following statements regarding the Indian squirrels:
> 1. They build nests by making burrows in the ground.
> 2. They store their food materials like nuts and seeds in the ground.
> 3. They are omnivorous.
> How many of the above statements are correct?
> (a) Only one (b) Only two (c) All three (d) None

**Key status:** official 2023 key **not held locally**.

**Reasoned outcome: (a).** The familiar Indian squirrels are palm squirrels (*Funambulus* spp.),
arboreal and scansorial animals that build globular leaf-and-fibre nests in trees, roof spaces and
wall crevices rather than digging burrows, and that do not rely on underground caching in the way
temperate ground squirrels do. They are, however, genuinely omnivorous — fruit, seeds, nuts, buds,
insects and occasionally eggs. Only statement 3 survives.

**Why each alternative fails.** (b) and (c) accept at least one of the burrowing or ground-caching
claims. (d) denies the well-supported omnivory.

**Confidence note.** This is a reasoned outcome on behavioural grounds, not a key assertion; no
official 2023 key is held locally.

**Tracker link:** application card — feeding and nesting ecology.

### PYQ 13 — 2023 Prelims GS-I, Q19 · Application route

**Stem** (local official scan):

> Which one of the following makes a tool with a stick to scrape insects from a hole in a tree or a
> log of wood?
> (a) Fishing cat (b) Orangutan (c) Otter (d) Sloth bear

**Key status:** official 2023 key **not held locally**.

**Reasoned outcome: (b).** Orangutans modify sticks — stripping, shortening and sharpening them — to
probe cavities for insects and to extract seeds, and the behaviour is socially transmitted within
populations.

**Why each alternative fails.** (a) The fishing cat hunts by scooping fish directly with its paws.
(c) Otters famously use stones to crack shellfish in some species, but that is percussion, not stick
probing. (d) The sloth bear's adaptation is anatomical, not technological: a gap in the incisors and
a mobile snout let it suck termites straight out of a nest.

**Tracker link:** application card — tool use.

### PYQ 14 — 2024 Prelims GS-I, Q25 · Application route · **official key held locally**

**Stem** (local official paper `2024-GS1-Set A`; the scan prints option letters and texts in separate
blocks, re-paired here in printed order):

> Consider the following statements:
> 1. Lions do not have a particular breeding season.
> 2. Unlike most other big cats, cheetahs do not roar.
> 3. Unlike male lions, male leopards do not proclaim their territory by scent marking.
> Which of the statements given above are correct?
> (a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3

**Official Series-A key held locally: (a).** Read directly from the local `Ans-2024-GS1` Series-A
table.

**Solution: (a).** Lions are aseasonal breeders; cubs are born year-round and birth timing is driven
by pride dynamics rather than by a calendar season. The cheetah lacks the specialised laryngeal and
hyoid apparatus that lets *Panthera* species roar, and instead purrs continuously, chirps and yelps —
which is also why the cheetah sits outside *Panthera*. Statement 3 inverts a well-documented fact:
male leopards mark territory intensively by urine spraying, tree scraping and cheek rubbing.

**Why each alternative fails.** (b) and (c) each retain the inverted leopard claim, and (b) also drops
the true lion statement. (d) accepts all three, including the false one.

**Tracker link:** application card — big-cat behaviour. Note that the question tests behaviour, not
conservation status, which is why no IUCN or Schedule field is engaged.

### PYQ 15 — 2025 Prelims GS-I, Q37 · **Direct route** · **official key held locally**

**Stem** (local official paper `2025-GS1-Set A`; the scan's Roman numerals are degraded and are
restored below — the cleaner of the two local extractions prints option (c) unambiguously as
"II only"):

> Regarding Peacock tarantula (Gooty tarantula), consider the following statements:
> I. It is an omnivorous crustacean.
> II. Its natural habitat in India is only limited to some forest areas.
> III. In its natural habitat, it is an arboreal species.
> Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
> (a) I only (b) I and III (c) II only (d) II and III

**Official Series-A key held locally: (d).** Recovered from the page-1 Series-A table of the local
`Ans-2025-GS1` extraction. One export of the same document had lost that page's table and begins at
the Series-B table; the page-1 extraction was used, and the two are distinguished explicitly rather
than assumed interchangeable.

**Solution: (d).** Statement I fails twice: *Poecilotheria metallica* is a theraphosid spider —
Arachnida, not Crustacea — and it is a predator of insects and other small invertebrates rather than
an omnivore. Statement II is the species' defining fact: its natural occurrence is confined to a
small, fragmented forest tract in Andhra Pradesh in the Eastern Ghats. Statement III is also correct —
it is arboreal, sheltering in holes and crevices of standing trees.

**Why each alternative fails.** (a) rests solely on the false crustacean claim. (b) keeps that claim
while adding a true one. (c) drops the arboreal habit, which the stem explicitly asserts and which is
true.

**Tracker link:** full-field species card. Current status fields: IUCN **Critically Endangered**
(assessment year recorded as unverified at cutoff); genus in **CITES Appendix II from CoP18, 2019**;
Wildlife Protection Act entry recorded as unverified rather than asserted.

### PYQ 16 — 2026 Prelims GS-I, Q36 · **Direct route** · **provisional key only**

**Stem** (local official paper `2026-GS1-Set A`; the scan prints "(ec)" for "(c)" and runs digits into
words, restored below without altering content):

> Consider the following statements about *Rhynchostylis retusa* (Foxtail orchid):
> 1. It is an epiphytic orchid.
> 2. The species is endemic to North-east India.
> 3. It is the State flower of Arunachal Pradesh and Assam.
> Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
> (a) 1 only (b) 1 and 3 (c) 2 and 3 (d) 3 only

**Key status: PROVISIONAL.** The locally held `Ans-2026-GS1-Provisional` Series-A table records
**B** for Q36. This is the **provisional** Commission key; it is labelled provisional here, in the
learning session and in the tree chart, and is not upgraded to a final key anywhere in this package.

**Solution consistent with the provisional key: (b).**

- **Statement 1 — correct.** *Rhynchostylis retusa* is an epiphyte. It anchors on bark and takes
  water and nutrients from air, rain and accumulated debris through velamen-covered aerial roots. It
  takes nothing from the host's tissues, so it is not a parasite.
- **Statement 2 — incorrect, and this is what the question turns on.** The species' native range runs
  from southern Yunnan and south-west Guizhou in China across India, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh,
  Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Thailand, Laos, Cambodia, Vietnam, Peninsular Malaysia, Indonesia and the
  Philippines. A taxon distributed across South and South-East Asia cannot be endemic to north-east
  India. The examiner is testing whether cultural prominence in Assam is mistaken for a range limit.
- **Statement 3 — correct.** It is the State flower of **both** Arunachal Pradesh and Assam, where it
  is the *kopou phul* of Bihu.

**Why each alternative fails.** (a) drops the true State-flower statement. (c) accepts the false
endemism claim. (d) discards the epiphytic habit, which the stem states correctly.

**Tracker link:** full-field species card. CITES scope: Orchidaceae is listed at family level in
Appendix II, with some taxa in Appendix I and specified annotation-based exceptions; the exact
annotation text was not read first-party at cutoff and is recorded as unverified.

## ORIGINAL MCQ BANK — 40 ITEMS

Keys run strictly **A → B → C → D**, repeating ten times. Every item is independently answerable;
the rotation is a bookkeeping convention, not a hint.

### Q1

In a species current-affairs record, which one of the following pairs of dates must be recorded as
two separate fields for the same entry?

A. The date the source itself attaches to the fact, and the date on which that source was opened and
checked.
B. The date of the first newspaper headline, and the date of the earliest wire-service report of the
same event.
C. The date of the parliamentary debate, and the date of the ministerial press conference that
followed it.
D. The date a species was first formally described, and the date its English common name came into
general use.

**Answer: A.**
- **A** is correct: the source's publication, assessment or effective date and the user's verification
  date answer different questions — how current the fact is, and how recently it was confirmed.
- **B** is wrong: two secondary reports of one event are duplicates, not independent fields, and
  neither is the source of record.
- **C** is wrong: these are two events, each of which would generate its own record if relevant; they
  are not two dates on one fact.
- **D** is wrong: nomenclatural history is taxonomic background and has no bearing on how current a
  status claim is.

### Q2

Consider the following fields of a species tracker record:

1. Population trend
2. Source and its publication date
3. Scientific name
4. CMS Appendix

Which of the above may legitimately remain unfilled without rendering the record unusable?

A. 1 and 2 only
B. 1 and 4 only
C. 2 and 3 only
D. 3 and 4 only

**Answer: B.**
- **A** is wrong: dropping the source and its date removes the only thing that makes the entry
  checkable.
- **B** is correct: a population trend may be genuinely unverified, and the CMS Appendix is simply not
  applicable to a non-migratory taxon.
- **C** is wrong: the scientific name is the record's identity anchor and cannot be left open.
- **D** is wrong: for the same reason, dropping the scientific name defeats the record.

### Q3

Consider the following statements about the recheck-by field:

1. It is set according to how quickly the underlying register changes.
2. It is distinct from the date on which the source was last opened.
3. Once it has passed, the entry may still be cited provided it was first-party when created.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

A. None
B. Only one
C. Only two
D. All three

**Answer: C.**
- **A** is wrong: at least the first statement is plainly sound.
- **B** is wrong: the first two statements are both sound.
- **C** is correct: refresh frequency should track the volatility of the register, and the recheck-by
  date looks forward while the verification date looks back.
- **D** is wrong: the third statement defeats the purpose of the field. Being first-party when created
  says nothing about whether the fact still holds.

### Q4

Consider the following pairs of status verb and the strongest claim it supports:

1. Designated — a legal or administrative status now exists
2. Allocated — a facility is physically operating
3. Assessed — an evaluation has been carried out and published
4. Announced — an intention has been stated

Which of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

A. 1, 2 and 3 only
B. 2 and 4 only
C. 1 and 2 only
D. 1, 3 and 4 only

**Answer: D.**
- **A** is wrong: it admits pair 2, which overstates allocation.
- **B** is wrong for the same reason, while also discarding two sound pairings.
- **C** is wrong: it keeps the mismatched pair and drops two correct ones.
- **D** is correct: allocation commits capacity or money; it does not establish that anything has been
  built or is running. The other three pairings are exact.

### Q5

A single sentence reads: "India's Ramsar total reached 101 when Glaw Lake was designated on
3 August 2026." Which discipline does this sentence quietly abandon?

A. The separation between a dated news trigger and a current status that needs its own as-on date.
B. The separation between the scientific binomial of an organism and the vernacular name used for it
locally.
C. The separation between a global assessment of a species and an assessment of one of its named
subpopulations.
D. The separation between the date on which a source was last checked and the date by which it must
be checked again.

**Answer: A.**
- **A** is correct: the designation is an event dated 3 August 2026; "India has 101 sites" is a state
  that must carry its own as-on date, because it can change the next week.
- **B** is wrong: sites carry place names, and no scientific binomial is at issue.
- **C** is wrong: no assessment unit is involved in a designation count.
- **D** is wrong: neither of those dates appears in the sentence at all.

### Q6

**Statement-I:** A tracker record may legitimately be filed with an empty population field.
**Statement-II:** The Wild Life (Protection) Amendment Act, 2022 created a dedicated Schedule to carry
species listed in the CITES Appendices.

Which one of the following is correct in respect of the above statements?

A. Both are correct, and Statement-II explains Statement-I.
B. Both are correct, but Statement-II does not explain Statement-I.
C. Statement-I is correct, but Statement-II is not.
D. Statement-II is correct, but Statement-I is not.

**Answer: B.**
- **A** is wrong: the amendment's schedule architecture has nothing to do with why a population
  estimate may be unavailable.
- **B** is correct: both propositions hold, but they are unrelated. Population figures depend on survey
  cycles; the Schedule change is a legislative matter.

## ORIGINAL MAINS PRACTICE — SIX MODEL ANSWERS

**Word-count convention.** Counts are produced by the regular expression
`\b[A-Za-z0-9]+(?:[-'][A-Za-z0-9]+)*\b` applied to the model answer text only, excluding the question
line and this note. A curly apostrophe (’) is treated as a **separator**, so a word written with one
splits into two tokens; a straight apostrophe (') and a hyphen (-) **join**, so `India's` and
`cross-layer` each count as one. The model answers below use straight apostrophes and hyphens
throughout, so that the printed count is reproducible by running that expression over the text.

### M1 — 10 marks (limit 150 words)

**Question.** Explain why a species current-affairs record must keep the news trigger and the current
status as two separately dated fields.

**Model answer.**

A record that fuses the two cannot age honestly. The news trigger is an event: Glaw Lake in Arunachal
Pradesh was designated on 3 August 2026, and that date never changes. The current status is a
condition: India held 101 Ramsar sites as on 1 October 2026, and that figure can change with the next
designation. Fusing them freezes a count to an event date, which is how superseded totals survive for
years in answer scripts. Separation also forces the right verb. Designation is an administrative act;
it does not by itself evidence management effectiveness, and the 58 tiger reserves recorded since
Madhav's notification in March 2025 carry the same caution. The cost is modest, since one extra field
is all that is required. The discipline matters most where registers move quickly, and less where a
status is genuinely stable.

**Word count (convention above): 140 / 150.**

### M2 — 10 marks (limit 150 words)

**Question.** Examine why an IUCN Red List category, a Wildlife Protection Act Schedule and a CITES
Appendix cannot be treated as interchangeable descriptions of a species.

**Model answer.**

Each answers a different question and binds a different actor. The IUCN category is a scientific
estimate of extinction risk, dated to an assessment; the Great Indian Bustard is Critically
Endangered, yet that carries no legal force. A Schedule under the Wild Life (Protection) Act, whose
amended schedules took effect on 1 April 2023, creates Indian criminal and administrative
consequences. A CITES Appendix regulates international trade: the genus Poecilotheria entered
Appendix II in 2019 because of collection for the ornamental pet trade, not because trade listings
rank extinction risk. Treating them as synonyms produces confident errors, such as inferring
Schedule I from a Critically Endangered label. The registers do often converge for flagship species,
and that convergence is why the conflation feels safe. It is still unsafe, because the verification
route for each claim is different.

**Word count (convention above): 136 / 150.**

### M3 — 15 marks (limit 250 words)

**Question.** Using the Great Indian Bustard, analyse how scientific assessment, domestic law,
judicial enforcement and international convention interact in India's species conservation.

**Model answer.**

The bustard is the clearest Indian case in which four governance layers act on one population, and
their interaction explains the outcome.

Science sets the alarm. The species is assessed as Critically Endangered, with BirdLife reporting a
global figure in the range of 50 to 249 mature individuals. That assessment has no operative force,
but it establishes the factual premise every other layer relies on.

Domestic law supplies consequence. The bird sits in Schedule I of the Wild Life (Protection) Act,
which criminalises hunting and trade. Schedule I, however, protects individuals rather than the
semi-arid grassland the species needs, and grassland is the habitat class least represented in
India's protected-area network. Law therefore reaches the bird without reaching its problem.

Courts supplied the missing instrument, then qualified it. In M.K. Ranjitsinh, the Supreme Court
first ordered overhead power lines underground across bustard habitat, and on 21 March 2024 modified
that blanket direction into an expert-committee assessment of area-specific feasibility. The same
judgment recognised a right against the adverse effects of climate change under Articles 14 and 21,
showing a court balancing species protection against renewable-energy deployment rather than treating
either as absolute.

International convention adds reach without enforcement: listings in CITES Appendix I and CMS
Appendix I, the latter agreed at Gandhinagar in 2020, address trade and range-state cooperation.

The limits are real: no verified post-judgment compliance record or current captive-breeding figure
is available, so the case demonstrates institutional design rather than a measured recovery.

**Word count (convention above): 246 / 250.**

### M4 — 15 marks (limit 250 words)

**Question.** Discuss how India's recent wetland and protected-area designations should be evaluated,
distinguishing administrative expansion from conservation outcome.

**Model answer.**

Designation counts measure a government's willingness to confer status. They do not measure what
happens inside the boundary, and an answer that treats the two as the same mistakes an input for a
result.

The expansion is genuine and recent. India reached 100 Ramsar sites on 5 June 2026 with the Jai
Prakash Narayan Bird Sanctuary at Surha Tal in Ballia district, Uttar Pradesh, and 101 on 3 August
2026 with Glaw Lake, Arunachal Pradesh's first such site. The tiger-reserve network stood at 58 after
Madhav in Madhya Pradesh was notified in March 2025. Cold Desert in Himachal Pradesh joined the
UNESCO World Network of Biosphere Reserves in 2025, taking that subset to 13 of India's 18 notified
reserves. As on March 2025 the wider network recorded 106 national parks and 573 wildlife
sanctuaries.

Each of those is a status, conferred on a date, by an authority. Outcome evidence is a different
class of claim and comes from monitoring: the snow-leopard assessment released in January 2024 put
the national estimate at 718 and reported that much of the species' range lies outside protected
areas, which is an outcome finding that no designation count could have produced.

The honest evaluation is therefore two-part. Credit the expansion, because legal status is a
precondition for most interventions. Then ask what is being measured inside it, and say plainly when
no verified monitoring figure exists, as is the case for most of the newly designated wetlands at
present.

**Word count (convention above): 244 / 250.**

### M5 — 20 marks (limit 300 words)

**Question.** Evaluate the proposition that evidence discipline - naming source, date and status
verb, and recording what could not be verified - is itself an analytical skill rather than a clerical
habit.

**Model answer.**

The proposition holds, because each element of the discipline removes a specific class of error, and
removing error is analysis rather than bookkeeping.

Naming the source fixes accountability. A figure attributed to a Press Information Bureau release or
to a Wildlife Institute of India assessment can be checked; the same figure floating free cannot be
distinguished from a remembered approximation. Dating fixes currency. Until recently this package
described UNCCD COP17 at Ulaanbaatar as scheduled for 17 to 28 August 2026; the session has since
concluded, adopting 34 decisions and deferring four agenda items to the next Conference. The
underlying words were never wrong, only undated, and an undated claim decays silently.

The status verb fixes the strength of the claim. Designated, allocated, released and achieved mark
genuinely different positions on the path from intention to reality. India's cheetah programme
illustrates the distance: animals were released at Kuno National Park in September 2022 and a second
site was established at Gandhi Sagar in April 2025, which is translocation under management, not a
restored wild population.

Recording what could not be verified is the hardest element and the most analytical. On 1 October
2026 no first-party conservation database could be read end to end from this working environment, so
every IUCN assessment year in this package remains open, and no species-level Schedule entry is
asserted. Declaring that is more informative than filling the fields plausibly.

The proposition has limits. Discipline cannot rescue a weak argument, and an answer that lists
provenance instead of reasoning will score poorly. Its value is conditional on the claim mattering to
the argument being made.

Within that limit, calibration is visible to an examiner, and a candidate who says what they do not
know is more credible on what they do.

**Word count (convention above): 294 / 300.**

### M6 — 20 marks (limit 300 words)

**Question.** Critically examine how static ecological concepts and dated conservation facts should be
integrated, drawing on India's 2025 species-discovery compilations and its 2026 wetland milestone.

**Model answer.**

Integration works when the dated fact is used as evidence for a conceptual claim, and fails when it is
attached as decoration.

Consider the 2025 discovery compilations. India added 709 animals to its faunal record, of which 483
were new to science and 226 were first records for the country, taking documented animal diversity to
1,05,953 species; the floral compilation added 353 plant taxa, 221 new to science and 132 new
distributional records. Read carelessly, this becomes a claim that biodiversity increased, which is
false. Read against the concept of survey effort, it supports a defensible argument: the rate at which
previously undescribed taxa are still being found indicates how incompletely the baseline is known,
which in turn explains why impact assessment at a project site so often proceeds without a species
inventory. The concept supplies the question; the figures supply the measurement.

The wetland milestone works the same way. Glaw Lake in Arunachal Pradesh became India's 101st Ramsar
site on 3 August 2026 and the state's first. The concept of a Ramsar designation explains what this
does and does not do: it commits a party to wise use and to reporting, and it does not by itself alter
land tenure, upstream abstraction or catchment land use. Pairing the date with the mechanism yields an
argument. Citing the date alone yields a fact that any list could supply.

Two cautions follow. First, the direction of integration matters: concepts should select the facts,
not the reverse, or an answer becomes a news summary. Second, the pairing must be refreshed, since
both the site count and the discovery totals will move.

Used this way, currency is an argumentative asset. Used otherwise, it is ballast.

**Word count (convention above): 284 / 300.**
