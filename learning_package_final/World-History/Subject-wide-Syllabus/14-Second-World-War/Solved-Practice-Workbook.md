### TRANSPARENT ZERO-DIRECT-PYQ AUDIT

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Why was the Battle of Britain strategically decisive? Answer in about 150 words.

**Model thesis:** It denied Germany a quick western settlement, preserved Britain as an Allied base and ensured Germany entered its Soviet war without ending the first front.

**Claim → named evidence → analysis → qualification:**

- RAF resistance, radar, German range limits and the shift to bombing London prevented invasion and kept Britain in the war.
- The attack on the USSR created the multi-front war Germany could not sustain.

**Qualified conclusion:** It denied Germany a quick western settlement, preserved Britain as an Allied base and ensured Germany entered its Soviet war without ending the first front.

**Demand decoding:** The directive **answer** requires a direct position on “Why was the Battle of Britain strategically decisive? Answer in about 150 words.”, every clause and time boundary, chronological-causal organisation, named evidence, a counter-position and a qualified global verdict.

**Detailed examiner-grade model status:** The solved content above is the executable answer base. Preserve its thesis, named chronology, spatial evidence, causal mechanism, counter-position and qualification rather than replacing it with a generic narrative.

**Executable exam-length answer / compression plan:** For a 10-mark answer, open with a two-sentence thesis and a spatial-temporal frame; organise three named events, treaties, actors, regions, institutions or attributed interpretations as claim → evidence → analysis → qualification; reserve the final lines for a graded conclusion.

**Why this earns marks:** The answer obeys the directive, distinguishes structure, trigger and outcome, connects evidence to mechanism, includes non-European agency and avoids teleology, presentism and monocausal explanation.

**How to improve this answer:** For “Why was the Battle of Britain strategically decisive? Answer in about 150 words.”, replace the weakest generalisation with one additional named treaty, regional case, primary record or attributed scholarly position and state what that evidence cannot prove.

**Detailed examiner-grade model answer:**

The Battle of Britain (July–September 1940) denied Germany the quick western settlement it needed before expanding the war. France fell in June, but Britain resisted.

The RAF prevented the Luftwaffe from gaining the air superiority needed for invasion. Radar gave warning of incoming attacks, while German aircraft faced range limitations; Hitler's shift towards bombing London also reduced pressure on RAF airfields. These interacting factors helped keep Britain in the war. Survival preserved an Allied base and a maritime supply lifeline, rather than merely defending one island.

When Germany launched Operation Barbarossa against the USSR on 22 June 1941, Britain remained an active enemy. The campaign in the east therefore enlarged an unresolved war rather than following a secure peace in the west. Britain later served as a base for offensives against German-occupied Europe.

The battle alone did not defeat Germany: Soviet resistance, Atlantic supply and American resources were indispensable later. Its strategic significance was negative but fundamental—it prevented a swift Axis victory and made eventual multi-front Allied pressure possible.

**Claim → named evidence → analysis → qualification:**

- The Normandy landings opened the western front in France and accelerated Germany's two-front collapse.
