### PYQ DEMAND CARD 1 — 2023 GS-I · 10 marks · 150 words

**Demand:** Bring out the socio-economic effects of railways in different countries.

**Status:** Verified neutral demand from the local owner.

**Model solution:** Railways magnified the political economy they entered. In Britain they integrated coal, iron, engineering and a national market; in continental catch-up economies they often supported state-led market integration; in the United States they opened a settler frontier while accelerating indigenous dispossession; in Russia and Japan they served compressed strategic modernisation; and in colonies they commonly moved export commodities and administrative power toward ports. Across settings they accelerated urbanisation, migration, standard time and new occupations. Thus the technology was common, but ownership and economic purpose determined whether integration or extraction dominated.

**Detailed examiner-grade model answer:**

Railways had different socio-economic effects because routes, ownership and political power differed. In Britain, linking coalfields, iron production, engineering centres and consumers strengthened an already industrialising national market. For later continental industrialisers, connecting regional markets could assist industrial catch-up, although tracks alone could not supply capital or institutions.

In the United States, railways enabled migration and settler expansion while accelerating Indigenous dispossession: gains to traders and settlers therefore cannot stand for universal welfare. In Russia and Meiji Japan, railway building formed part of state-supported strategic modernisation. In colonial economies, lines often joined producing hinterlands to ports, moving raw materials outward and manufactures or administrative power inward; integration could coexist with extraction. Across these settings, faster journeys encouraged urbanisation, new occupations and more standardised time. Railways were thus an enabling technology, not an independent guarantee of equitable development: the distribution of their benefits reflected the political economy in which they operated.

### PYQ DEMAND CARD 2 — 2024 GS-I · 15 marks · 250 words

**Demand:** How far was the Industrial Revolution in England responsible for the decline of handicrafts and cottage industries in India?

**Status:** Exact wording reproduced by the World History owner; final India verdict belongs to Modern Indian History.

**Detailed examiner-grade model answer:**

England's Industrial Revolution was a major enabling cause of pressure on Indian handicrafts, but it cannot by itself explain the extent or distribution of their decline. The spinning jenny, water frame and power loom raised textile output; improved steam power and the factory system allowed more concentrated, standardised production. Canals, steam boats and later railways lowered the cost of moving those goods. Cheaper machine-made cloth thus became a formidable competitor to hand-spun and hand-woven production, while British industry sought both raw materials and markets abroad.

Yet a price advantage did not automatically displace every craft in India. Colonial tariff and monopoly arrangements shaped whose goods obtained market access and on what terms. Revenue demands and changes in purchasing power affected artisan customers, while the loss of court patronage damaged particular luxury trades. Transport penetration changed exposure to imported goods differently across regions. Coarse rural production and specialised luxury work could survive where their markets or qualities differed from mass-produced cloth. Nor should textile competition be mechanically extended to every kind of handicraft.

The correct degree of responsibility is therefore substantial but conditional: English industrialisation supplied the scale and cost advantage; colonial policy and changing local demand mediated its effects in India. A judgement about how far individual Indian industries declined requires Indian regional and craft evidence, not merely an English invention chronology.
