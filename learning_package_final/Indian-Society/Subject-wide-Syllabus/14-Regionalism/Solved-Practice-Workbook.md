### PYQ DEMAND CARD 1 — 2020 GS-I Q10

**Demand:** Audited rendering: argue whether regionalism in India follows rising cultural assertiveness. 10 marks, 150 words; no verbatim claim.

**Model answer:** Cultural assertiveness can give a region's distinct language or history a political voice, but regionalism is not its automatic or exclusive consequence. Regionalism begins when a shared regional interest is organised into a demand for recognition, autonomy or a more equitable resource share. The States Reorganisation Act, 1956 illustrates accommodation of linguistic assertion *within* the Union, refuting the equation of cultural voice with disloyalty. A distinct region may also prosper materially yet seek language recognition, while a culturally similar sub-region may mobilise against poor roads or weak representation. These cases separate the identity and deprivation mechanisms. In a district demanding both a recognised dialect and transport access, a purely symbolic concession leaves the material complaint alive. Conversely, roads do not substitute for representative voice. Thus rising assertiveness is one significant pathway to regionalism; local opportunity, development and institutions decide its form and intensity. Examine the demand's actual content rather than label every regional movement secessionist.

**Examiner check:** Argues the proposition, cites 1956, gives contrary development pathway and a qualified verdict.

### PYQ DEMAND CARD 2 — 2024 GS-I Q17

**Demand (verified in canonical Basic owner):** What is regional disparity? How does it differ from diversity? How serious is the issue of regional disparity in India? 15 marks, 250 words.

**Model answer:** Regional disparity is uneven development between places—income, services, infrastructure, human development or fiscal capacity. Diversity is the coexistence of different linguistic, cultural, ecological and historical identities. Their causal independence is crucial: culturally similar districts can have unequal transport access, whereas states with distinct languages may offer comparably good health services.

The disparity is serious because unequal public goods shape the opportunities available to residents before individual effort can matter. A lagging hill district within an otherwise better-performing state may face a much longer journey to a clinic or market. That illustrates both spatial disadvantage and the danger of judging all residents by a state's average. The NITI Aayog SDG India Index, edition 2023–24 (the canonical owner's latest verified edition as of July 2026, not asserted as latest today), gives a multi-indicator framework for comparing states and UTs; a state-level composite can mask differences among districts and between health, education and infrastructure. Without a checked series one should not invent a current rank or numerical gap. Differences in per-capita income, service access and fiscal capacity should be examined with consistent dates and comparable units before claiming scale or trend.

The response is targeted infrastructure and human-development investment, transparent fiscal priorities and district-level monitoring. Recognition of a language may answer identity but cannot build a road. Conversely, redistributive spending should not erase legitimate cultural claims. Disparity remains a substantial governance problem, but its severity is spatially and sectorally uneven; cultural diversity itself is not the problem.

**Examiner check:** Answers each of the three clauses in order; provides paired counterexamples, bounded evidence and a policy conclusion.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Distinguish regionalism from sub-regionalism and show why the distinction changes the policy response. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer:** Regionalism mobilises a recognised region's interest in the wider polity; sub-regionalism mobilises a smaller area's identity or grievance *within* that region. A state-wide campaign for linguistic recognition is the former; a district cluster alleging neglect by its own state capital is the latter. Scale alone is not enough to diagnose cause: the district may need equitable clinics, fair representation or recognition of a dialect, possibly all three. The 1956 States Reorganisation illustrates accommodation of language across state boundaries, but does not prescribe another boundary whenever one district lags. Use district-level service and representation evidence before choosing devolution, infrastructure investment or statehood discussion. A state's aggregate NITI Aayog SDG India Index score may conceal a poorly served district. Responding to sub-regional deprivation with only a state-wide slogan can reproduce the original grievance.

**Examiner check:** Scale, drivers, illustration and corresponding instrument are all differentiated.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Explain why a constitutional statehood demand should not be treated as automatically anti-national. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer:** A statehood demand seeks a new constituent state inside the Union, whereas secession seeks separation from it. The distinction turns on the movement's actual political objective, not the emotive phrase 'separate state'. India's 1956 linguistic reorganisation shows that recognised boundaries can be changed to accommodate identity without rejecting national membership. A sub-region complaining of weak representation at its state capital might ask for statehood, but its claim still requires examination of administrative viability, minority interests within the proposed unit and development alternatives. Autonomy and decentralisation can address some complaints without creating a new state; they are options, not inevitable steps towards secession. Nor does lawful constitutional framing prove that every demand is sound on its merits. Dialogue, disaggregated evidence and a reasoned constitutional process are preferable to assigning disloyalty to all petitioners.

**Examiner check:** Defines both forms, grounds the argument in reorganisation and qualifies statehood's costs.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Examine the claim that diversity and regional disparity are causally independent axes. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer:** Diversity describes differences in language, ecology, culture or history; disparity measures differences in developmental outcomes. They can coincide, but one is not evidence of the other. Two states with distinct languages may both have accessible primary care; two districts speaking the same language may differ markedly in road connectivity. These are counterexamples to the claim that distinct identity *causes* backwardness.

The economic geography of investment, terrain, public-service delivery and access to markets can diverge from the map of linguistic groups. For the 2024 GS-I demand, therefore, define each axis before assessing the seriousness of unequal opportunities. The NITI Aayog SDG India Index 2023–24 offers state-wise multi-indicator comparisons, but its aggregate state score cannot reveal all district-level service deficits. A hill district with poor clinic access may be hidden within a better-performing state. Conversely a culturally distinct prosperous region can still make a legitimate recognition claim; additional roads would not answer a language grievance.

Policy must follow diagnosis. For disadvantage, compare dated income, health, education, infrastructure and fiscal-capacity indicators and target gaps. For identity, assess representative voice and lawful accommodation, including the kind of linguistic reorganisation seen in 1956. Where a sub-region reports both neglected infrastructure and an unrecognised dialect, combine approaches instead of presuming every sub-region shares identical drivers. The axes are analytically independent, not empirically unable to interact: institutions and politics may bring them together in particular histories.

**Examiner check:** Defines axes, tests both off-diagonal cases, states an interaction limit and matches policy to evidence.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Examine the limits of a composite index as evidence of how serious regional disparity is in India. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer:** A composite index can establish comparable patterns across reporting units, not the entire lived severity of spatial disadvantage. The NITI Aayog SDG India Index 2023–24 combines goal-linked indicators for states and UTs; it is a useful starting benchmark, provided the edition, unit and component are specified. The canonical Basic owner verified that edition as the latest *in July 2026*; a new rank should be checked against a current official publication.

Three limits matter. First, a state average conceals district and sub-regional deficits: remote hill districts may have much poorer transport access than their state's score suggests. Second, aggregation can offset a weak health component with stronger performance elsewhere, concealing the precise service needing repair. Third, a score does not establish why outcomes differ: terrain, past investment, governance and labour markets require independent investigation. A linguistic map is likewise not a development measure. Culturally similar districts can be economically unequal; culturally different states can perform similarly.

For the 2024 GS-I question 'How serious?', triangulate comparable dated indicators of income, infrastructure, schooling, health and fiscal capacity at state and district scales. Identify who within a region is excluded and whether gaps persist across periods rather than infer a trend from a single ranking. Use the index to locate patterns and evaluate intervention; do not turn a relative category into a made-up rupee or percentage gap. The conclusion may reasonably call disparity a serious, uneven governance challenge without claiming that every region suffers equally.

**Examiner check:** Defends index utility while showing spatial, component and causal limitations.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Assess how identity-driven and disparity-driven regionalism require different responses. Answer in about 300 words.

**Model answer:** Regionalism expresses an organised claim about a region's interests. Its policy significance depends on whether the grievance concerns recognition, unequal outcomes or both. Diversity of language does not itself show poverty; poor transport does not require a different language.

An identity-driven movement can demand linguistic status or better political voice despite satisfactory economic outcomes. The 1956 linguistic States Reorganisation demonstrates one mode of accommodation inside the Union, although reorganisation is neither obligatory nor costless in every case. Consultation, accessible administrative language and representative inclusion may be less disruptive alternatives. By contrast, a culturally similar district cluster complaining of low clinic access and poor roads needs disaggregated investment, transparent allocation and monitoring. Offering merely a cultural celebration would not shorten journeys to treatment. The NITI Aayog SDG India Index 2023–24 can locate broad interstate contrasts, but district data are required to detect lagging parts of a strong-performing state; do not mistake the index for every resident's experience.

Some sub-regional claims compound a distinctive dialect, weak representation and unmet infrastructure needs. For them recognition without service delivery leaves material grievance intact, while spending without voice leaves the identity complaint unanswered. A new state may move the centre of decision-making nearer to residents, but can create a new periphery or impose administrative costs; decentralisation and targeted area investment deserve comparison before redrawing borders. The state's response must also distinguish autonomy within the Union from secession, not presume an automatic progression among movement forms.

An evidence-led sequence is therefore: record the actual demand, compare dated regional and within-state indicators, hear excluded groups within the region, choose recognition and/or redistribution, and evaluate both access and representation afterward. Balanced development and cultural accommodation are complements when both drivers exist, not interchangeable cures.

**Examiner check:** Separate mechanisms, matched instruments, Indian institutional anchor, compounded case and costs/limits are explicit.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Assess reorganisation as a response to regional assertion in India. Answer in about 300 words.

**Model answer:** Reorganisation can recognise a territorial identity and relocate political decision-making, but is not a universal solution to regional disparity. The States Reorganisation Act, 1956 substantially accommodated linguistic demands within India; it is evidence that regional assertion is compatible with national integration. It does not show that every distinct district needs a state, nor that every created state becomes economically equal.

Consider a sub-region asserting a distinct dialect and poor access to its state capital. A new unit could provide stronger representation and administrative proximity. But to evaluate the proposal one must compare decentralisation or autonomy within the existing state, district-level budget reallocations and the fiscal/administrative cost of a new capital and bureaucracy. If the underlying grievance concerns irrigation and clinics rather than exclusion from political voice, a territorial split alone may leave it unresolved. Even a new state can develop a marginalised border district, reproducing the original sub-regional pattern.

A serious appraisal begins by disaggregating development measures. The NITI Aayog SDG India Index 2023–24 is a state-level comparative source; it cannot settle the distribution of services inside the proposed territory or justify a current rank without checking for a later edition. Consultation should include minorities and remote communities within both proposed units, not only the most organised movement leadership. Constitutional statehood, autonomy and secession are distinct demands, with different implications; reorganisation should not be confused with a concession to secession.

Reorganisation is most defensible where representation and effective administration improve demonstrably and viable institutions accompany the change. Recognition should be paired with monitored investment where disparity also motivates assertion. The verdict is conditional: redraw boundaries when their prospective benefit is supported by granular evidence and alternatives fail, but assess social inclusion and implementation after the change rather than declare success at the moment of creation.

**Examiner check:** Historical evidence, decision criteria, comparison with alternatives, intraregional risks and qualified verdict answer 'assess'.
