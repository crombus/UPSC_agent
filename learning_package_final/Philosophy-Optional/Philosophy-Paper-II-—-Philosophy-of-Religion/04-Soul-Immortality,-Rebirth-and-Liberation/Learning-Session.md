### 0A. SOUL, SELF, PERSON AND CONTINUITY-BEARER ⚠️

| Model | What the person/self is | Survival implication | Main pressure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Substance dualism | an immaterial thinking substance distinct from body | the soul may survive bodily death | interaction, individuation and dependence on brain/body |
| Hylomorphism | the soul is the organising form of a living body | isolated disembodied survival is harder; resurrection restores the embodied person | how the same person is reconstituted |
| Bundle/no-substantial-self | a series or bundle of physical/mental events without a permanent owner | causal or psychological continuity may replace substance survival | responsibility and “who” survives |
| Embodied-person view | the person is an irreducibly embodied psychophysical unity | survival requires resurrection, re-creation or another continuity relation | replica and duplication problems |

**Personal-identity criteria:** bodily continuity, memory/psychological
continuity, soul-substance and causal continuity answer different questions.
Hick's replica thought experiment tests whether a unique psychophysical
duplicate with memory and character is survival; the possibility of two
replicas exposes the difference between qualitative similarity and numerical
identity.

#### The Six Survival-Terms and the Necessity Question

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **CORE DISTINCTION:** Immortality means the soul's survival after bodily death, rebirth means karmically conditioned re-becoming, and liberation means release from the cycle of birth and death. **Core argument/criticism:** Buddhist anātman (no permanent self) shows that causal continuity can sustain rebirth without an immortal substance. **Final verdict:** Immortality is sufficient for rebirth in substantial-self theories but is not universally necessary.
#### LAYER 1 - SIMPLE START

The whole syllabus item turns on refusing to treat six words as synonyms. Read
this map first; everything else hangs on it.

```text
SIX WORDS, SIX MEANINGS -- never swap them
-------------------------------------------------------------
  IMMORTALITY    the soul does not die when the body dies
  SURVIVAL       something of me continues after death
  REBIRTH        re-becoming in a new life, driven by karma
  REINCARNATION  a SOUL re-enters flesh (needs a substance)
  RESURRECTION   ONE life; God raises the whole person again
  LIBERATION     exit from the cycle itself (many names)
-------------------------------------------------------------
  test chain:  IMMORTALITY --> REBIRTH --> LIBERATION
               is each one NEEDED for the next?
  the shock:   Buddhism has rebirth AND liberation with
               NO immortal soul at all
```

Think of six different bus routes that lazy speakers all call "the bus". If you
board the wrong one you end up in the wrong city. In this topic, half the marks
are lost simply by merging two of these terms. The examiner's favourite move is
to ask whether one term is a *necessary condition* for the next.

> MEMORY: I -> R -> L. Immortality, Rebirth, Liberation. Ask of each arrow: is
> the earlier term necessary, sufficient, or neither, for the later one?

### SESSION 2 — IMMORTALITY OF THE SOUL AND PLATO'S ARGUMENTS

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** [!IMPORTANT] Immortality is the continued existence of the soul after bodily death.

**Technical definition:** Plain version: Plato argues that because the soul is the kind of thing that is simple and life-giving, it is not the kind of thing that can fall apart or die.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Plato's arguments support different claims—pre-existence, affinity or simplicity—and none should be treated as an uncontested proof of personal immortality.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **soul**
- **simplicity argument**
- **recollection**
- **affinity argument**
- **cyclical argument**
- **personal identity**

**How to use them:** Reconstruct Plato's arguments separately and test whether incorporeality, simplicity or recollection establishes the survival of this individual person.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **EXAM-READY LINE:** Immortality is the continued existence of the soul after bodily death. **Core argument/criticism:** Plato's strongest route makes soul essentially life-bearing, but the inference still depends on contested assumptions about simplicity, substance and identity. **Final verdict:** Plato supplies a cumulative case for survival, not an uncontested demonstration.
#### LAYER 1 - SIMPLE START

Plato's Phaedo offers four separate arguments that the soul cannot die. Treat
them as four branches, not one lump.

```text
        PLATO -- four a priori proofs (Phaedo)
        ======================================
  [1] OPPOSITES     living and dead arise from each other
                    -> souls must persist between lives
  [2] RECOLLECTION  we judge equal sticks by an Equality we
                    never saw -> the soul knew it BEFORE birth
  [3] AFFINITY      the soul is simple, invisible, like the
                    Forms -> it does not decompose
  [4] FORM OF LIFE  the soul essentially brings Life, so it
                    cannot admit its opposite, death
        --------------------------------------
  bridge needed: pre-existence (1,2) is NOT yet survival;
                 each proof must reach BOTH directions
  critique:      affinity/simplicity are ASSUMED; Kant --
                 the soul is not a knowable substance
```

Plain version: Plato argues that because the soul is the kind of thing that is
simple and life-giving, it is not the kind of thing that can fall apart or die.
The Indian systems agree the self is eternal, but from very different premises.

> MEMORY: Plato's four = O-R-A-F: Opposites, Recollection, Affinity, Form of Life.

#### LAYER 2 - CORE UPSC

#### 1. IMMORTALITY OF THE SOUL ✅
- **Plato (Phaedo — 2023 PYQ) — a priori proofs:** ✅ 1. **Argument from Opposites (cyclical):** opposites come from opposites; living→dead and dead→living, so souls persist. 2. **Argument from Recollection (anamnesis):** learning = recollecting knowledge the soul had before birth → soul pre-exists. 3. **Argument from Affinity:** the soul is simple, immaterial, akin to the eternal Forms → indissoluble/immortal. 4. **Argument from the Form of Life:** the soul essentially participates in Life, so cannot admit its opposite (death).
- **Critique:** the affinity/simplicity claims are assumptions; Kant — the soul is not a knowable substance (paralogism). ⚠️
- **Indian:** ātman (Vedānta), jīva (Jain), and the Nyāya self are **eternal** in their respective systems. The *Gītā* 2.23 uses the canonical image that weapons, fire, water and wind cannot destroy the embodied self. ✅

---
#### 8.1 Plato's immortality arguments
- **Doctrine statement.** ✅ In the *Phaedo*, Plato presents the soul as pre-existent and akin to intelligible, non-composite reality, culminating in the claim that soul essentially brings life and cannot admit death.
- **Argument.** ✅ Cyclical argument: living and dead arise from each other. Recollection: grasp of equality exceeds sensory instances and indicates pre-natal acquaintance. Affinity: soul resembles invisible, stable Forms more than composite body. Final argument: what essentially participates in Life cannot receive its contrary.
- **Presupposition.** ⚠️ Learning is recollection, likeness to the simple supports indestructibility, and essential predicates exclude contraries.
- **Distinction.** ✅ Pre-existence does not by itself prove post-mortem survival; each argument must bridge both directions.
- **Canonical example.** ✅ Equal sticks never perfectly instantiate Equality, yet we judge their deficiency by a standard not derived from them alone.
- **Objection → reply.** ✅ Simmias' harmony analogy makes soul dependent on body; Plato replies that soul governs body and recollection implies prior existence.
#### LAYER 5 - RAPID REVISION

- Four proofs: Opposites, Recollection, Affinity, Form of Life.
- Pre-existence (1,2) is not survival: flag the bridge.
- Kill-shot critique: simplicity is assumed; Kant -- no knowable substance.
- Simmias = harmony objection; Plato's reply = soul governs body.

> WRONG: Blurring the four arguments into "Plato thought the soul is eternal".
> That caps you at the average band.

#### 1.1 Arguments for immortality and their limits

| Argument family | Core reasoning | Main objection |
|---|---|---|
| Simplicity/indivisibility | an immaterial simple soul has no parts into which it can decompose | simplicity is not established; even a simple subject could cease or depend on embodiment |
| Identity/continuity | stable agency, responsibility or first-person unity requires a persisting subject | psychological or causal continuity may explain persistence without an immortal substance |
| Moral order | complete justice or the highest good requires survival beyond one life | postulation expresses moral hope, not theoretical demonstration |
| Desire/fulfilment | a deep natural desire for unending fulfilment points toward a possible object | desires can be frustrated or socially formed; existence does not follow from longing |
| Religious experience/testimony | experiences interpreted as contact with the dead or an eternal self support survival | interpretation, diversity and alternative explanations prevent demonstrative force |

**Body/brain objection:** systematic dependence of memory, personality and
agency on bodily conditions pressures separable-soul theories. It does not by
itself deductively prove that no subject can survive, but it shifts the burden
to an account of interaction, identity and evidence.

### SESSION 3 — REBIRTH, KARMA AND SAṂSĀRA

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** Rebirth is renewed embodied or conditioned existence within a karmic series; traditions disagree whether an enduring soul or only causal continuity connects lives.

**Technical definition:** Karma supplies the action-consequence structure and rebirth supplies its trans-life field, while reincarnation specifically presupposes a substantial self that is re-embodied.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Rebirth links action to consequences beyond one life, yet its philosophical meaning depends on what—soul, subtle body or causal series—is said to continue.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **karma**
- **rebirth**
- **cycle of becoming (saṃsāra)**
- **moral continuity**
- **substantial self**
- **causal fruition**

**How to use them:** Explain rebirth as the temporal field of karmic fruition, while distinguishing theories that transmit an enduring self from those that transmit only causal conditions.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **CORE DISTINCTION:** Rebirth is karmically conditioned re-becoming within saṃsāra (the cycle of birth and death). **Core argument/criticism:** Karma (action and moral causation) explains why consequences may mature beyond one life, while Buddhism replaces a transmigrating soul with causal continuity. **Final verdict:** Rebirth requires a continuity-bearer, but that bearer need not be an unchanging soul.
#### LAYER 1 - SIMPLE START

Rebirth is the wheel: deeds leave traces, traces shape the next birth, and the
wheel turns until liberation stops it.

```text
        THE WHEEL OF KARMA AND REBIRTH (samsara)
             _____________________________
            /                             \
       DEEDS (karma)                 BIRTH-CONDITIONS
       act + its unseen potency  ->  reflect past deeds
       (adrsta / apurva)                   |
            ^                              v
       SAMSKARAS carried by          a LIFE is lived and
       the subtle body               fresh karma is sown
            ^                              |
            \____________ DEATH ___________/
       three karmas: sancita (stored) | prarabdha (ripening
                     now) | agami (future)
       NOT fatalism: purusakara (effort) works within daiva
       exit only by LIBERATION (knowledge / grace / discipline)
```

Plain version: karma is not fate. It is a moral book-keeping in which today's
choices set tomorrow's starting conditions, and rebirth is simply the fact that
the ledger runs longer than one lifetime.

> MEMORY: Karma needs rebirth. If fruits mature beyond one life, there must be a
> further life for them to mature IN -- otherwise justice is impossible.

#### LAYER 2 - CORE UPSC

#### 2. REBIRTH (Karma-Saṃsāra) ✅
- **Doctrine:** karmically conditioned continuity extends across lives until liberation. Vedānta, Nyāya and Jainism assign continuity to an enduring self or soul; Buddhism uses causal continuity without a permanent self. ✅
- **Rebirth in Karma theory (2023 PYQ):** rebirth widens the temporal field in which karmic consequences can mature beyond one life. “Debt” is only an analogy and must not replace each school's causal account. ✅
- **Gītā (2025 PYQ):** the imperishable soul *"casts off worn-out bodies and enters new ones"* — rebirth presupposes the deathless self.
- **The Buddhist challenge (2022, 2024 PYQ):** Buddhism denies a permanent soul (**anātman/anattā**) yet affirms rebirth — what is reborn is **not a self but a causal continuum** of momentary states (a flame passing from candle to candle; *pratītyasamutpāda*). → **rebirth WITHOUT an immortal soul.** ✅
- **"Is immortality a necessary condition for rebirth?" (2022, 2024, 2025):** ⚠️
  - *Substantialist systems (Vedānta/Nyāya/Jain):* **YES** — a persisting soul is what transmigrates.
  - *Buddhism:* **NO** — continuity of a causal stream suffices; no unchanging soul needed. → the decisive counter-example.

- Buddhism: rebirth WITHOUT a soul -> continuity, not substance.
- Not fatalism: purusakara within daiva.

> WRONG: Saying Buddhism denies continuity. It denies a permanent substance-self,

### SESSION 4 — BUDDHIST REBIRTH WITHOUT A PERMANENT SELF

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** [!IMPORTANT] Buddhist rebirth is the causal continuation of dependently arisen aggregates without anātman (a permanent self).

**Technical definition:** Doctrine statement. ✅ Rebirth is causal continuity of dependently arisen aggregates, not transmigration of a numerically identical substance.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

#### LAYER 2 - CORE UPSC

#### 8.5 Nyāya apavarga and Advaita jīvanmukti
- **Doctrine statement.** ✅ Nyāya liberation is final cessation of pain and pain-generating qualities; Advaita *jīvanmukti* is freedom through knowledge while bodily life continues.
- **Argument.** ✅ Nyāya traces suffering through defect, activity, rebirth and false knowledge; true knowledge reverses the chain. Advaita knowledge destroys ignorance immediately, while *prārabdha* karma accounts for the residual body.
- **Presupposition.** ⚠️ Nyāya treats consciousness as adventitious to self; Advaita treats consciousness as self's essence and karma as unable to survive liberating knowledge except in already-fructifying form.
- **Distinction.** ✅ *Jīvanmukti* differs from *videhamukti*; apavarga differs from positive heavenly pleasure.
- **Canonical example.** ✅ The potter's wheel continues briefly after the push ceases: bodily momentum after ignorance is destroyed.
- **Objection → reply.** ⚠️ If knowledge destroys karma, why does embodiment remain? Advaita distinguishes accumulated and future karma from already-fructifying *prārabdha*.
#### 8.9 Sāṃkhya and Yoga: *kaivalya*, and how it differs from every other liberation > Cross-link: Paper I — Sāṃkhya and Paper I — Yoga. This module carries only what the *religion* clause needs; the tattva-derivation belongs to Paper I.

- **Doctrine statement (Sāṃkhya).** ✅ Reality is exhausted by **two** kinds of principle: *puruṣa* — plural, inactive, attributeless **pure consciousness**, mere witness (*sākṣin*), by nature already isolated (*kevala*) — and *prakṛti* — one, unconscious, active, constituted by the three *guṇas*. Bondage is **not** a real bond but ***aviveka***: the failure to discriminate puruṣa from *buddhi*, so that prakṛti's states are misattributed to consciousness.
- **Argument.** ✅ (1) The *Sāṃkhya-kārikā* opens from the fact of the **threefold suffering** (*ādhyātmika* — bodily/mental; *ādhibhautika* — from other beings; *ādhidaivika* — from natural/cosmic forces) and the failure of ordinary and ritual means to end it permanently. (2) Effects pre-exist in their cause (*satkāryavāda*), so evolution is transformation of prakṛti, not creation. (3) Consciousness cannot be a product of the unconscious, hence puruṣa is distinct. (4) Liberation is ***viveka-khyāti*** — discriminative discernment — after which prakṛti ceases to display herself to that puruṣa. (5) The result is ***kaivalya***: aloneness, the standing of consciousness in its own nature.
- ✅ **The doctrine that wins marks:** *Sāṃkhya-kārikā* 62–63 states that, strictly, **no puruṣa is bound, released or transmigrating**; it is *prakṛti*, in her manifold forms, who binds herself, transmigrates and releases herself. Liberation is therefore the correction of a **misattribution**, not an event in consciousness.
- ✅ **Jīvanmukti in Sāṃkhya:** *SK* 67 uses the **potter's wheel that continues to spin after the potter's hand is withdrawn** — the body persists on residual momentum after discriminative knowledge; *SK* 68 gives *videha-kaivalya* at the fall of the body. ⚠️ Note the parallel with Advaita's *prārabdha* image and the difference in metaphysics: for Advaita the residue is karmic, for Sāṃkhya it is the guṇas' momentum.
- **Doctrine statement (Yoga).** ✅ Pātañjala Yoga accepts Sāṃkhya's metaphysics and adds a **practical psychology and an Īśvara**. *Yoga-sūtra* 1.2: yoga is the **restriction of the modifications of the mind (*citta-vṛtti-nirodha*)**; 1.3: then the seer abides in its own nature. The five *kleśas* (2.3) are *avidyā, asmitā, rāga, dveṣa, abhiniveśa*, with *avidyā* as the field of the rest. The eightfold path (*aṣṭāṅga*: *yama, niyama, āsana, prāṇāyāma, pratyāhāra, dhāraṇā, dhyāna, samādhi*) moves from *saṃprajñāta* to *asaṃprajñāta samādhi*, through *dharma-megha-samādhi* (4.29) to kaivalya. ✅ *YS* 4.34 defines **kaivalya** as the counter-flow (*pratiprasava*) of the guṇas now empty of purpose for puruṣa — or, equivalently, the power of consciousness (*citi-śakti*) established in its own nature.
- ✅ **Yoga's Īśvara is not a creator.** *YS* 1.24 defines Īśvara as a **special puruṣa** untouched by *kleśa*, *karma*, *vipāka* and *āśaya*; he is the teacher of the ancient teachers and the object of *Īśvara-praṇidhāna*, which is **one optional means among others**, not the ground of the world. This makes Yoga the decisive Indian case of **theism without creation** — route to Religion without God.
- **Distinction (the whole point of this module).** ✅ *Kaivalya* is **isolation**, and it is not: Advaita's *mokṣa* (realised **identity** with Brahman), Viśiṣṭādvaita's *mokṣa* (**communion** with God), Nyāya's *apavarga* (cessation of pain **and of consciousness**), Jain *kaivalya/siddhatva* (**perfected omniscient individuality**), or Buddhist *nirvāṇa* (**cessation of craving**, no self). ⚠️ Note the terminological trap: Jainism *also* uses *kevala/kaivalya* — but there it means **omniscience (*kevala-jñāna*)**, the opposite of Sāṃkhya's contentless isolation.
- **Objection → reply.** ⚠️ (a) **Plurality of puruṣas:** if each puruṣa is attributeless and inactive, nothing distinguishes them, so Sāṃkhya's pluralism collapses into Advaita's one. *Reply:* difference is established by the observed difference of birth, death and experience — a reply Advaitins find question-begging. (b) **Why does prakṛti act at all?** *Reply:* she acts "for the sake of puruṣa", illustrated by the milk that flows unconsciously for the calf — but a purpose in the unconscious is exactly what Nyāya objects to. (c) **Is a contentless liberation desirable?** *Reply:* Sāṃkhya explicitly denies that kaivalya is bliss; it is the *ending* of the threefold suffering, and offering more would be dishonest.
- **Verdict formula.** ⚠️ "Sāṃkhya-Yoga gives the sharpest Indian statement that liberation is **epistemic correction, not ontological change** — nothing is added, nothing is destroyed, and consciousness was free all along. Its cost is a liberation with no positive content and a dualism whose two terms cannot explain their own association."

- kaivalya = isolation of purusa; SK 62-63 = prakrti binds/releases, not purusa.

### SESSION 9 — JÑĀNA, KARMA, BHAKTI AND PATHS TO LIBERATION

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** Knowledge, disciplined action and devotion are distinct liberating means whose hierarchy and combination vary with each school's account of ignorance, karma and grace.

**Technical definition:** The paths to liberation are school-relative disciplines: knowledge removes ignorance, action purifies or fulfils duty, and devotion relates the aspirant to a personal Lord.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

#### LAYER 2 - CORE UPSC

#### 8.6 Bhakti as a path to liberation (2018 Q7(c) owner-module)
- **Doctrine statement.** ✅ *Bhakti* is loving devotion to a personal God held, in the theistic Vedāntas and the Bhakti traditions, to be a **direct means (*sādhana*) of liberation** — and, crucially, one available irrespective of caste, gender, language or scholastic training.
- **Textual base (cite precisely).** ✅ *Bhagavad Gītā* chs. 7–12 and the concluding *carama-śloka* **18.66** (*sarva-dharmān parityajya mām ekaṃ śaraṇaṃ vraja* — abandon all dharmas, take refuge in Me alone); **9.22** on the Lord securing what the exclusively devoted lack; **9.32** on the accessibility of the path. ✅ *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* **7.5.23** lists the **nine limbs (*navadhā bhakti*)**: *śravaṇa* (hearing), *kīrtana* (praising), *smaraṇa* (remembering), *pāda-sevana* (service at the feet), *arcana* (ritual worship), *vandana* (salutation), *dāsya* (servitude), *sakhya* (friendship), *ātma-nivedana* (self-offering). ✅ *Nārada Bhakti Sūtra* defines bhakti as of the nature of supreme love for the Lord; *Śāṇḍilya Bhakti Sūtra* as supreme attachment to Īśvara.
- **Typology (marks lie in the distinctions).** ✅ *Parā* (higher, disinterested) vs *aparā*/*gauṇī* (preliminary, ritual-supported); ***vaidhī*** (governed by scriptural injunction) vs ***rāgānugā*** (spontaneous, following the passion of exemplary devotees — Gauḍīya); **saguṇa bhakti** (Tulsīdās, Sūrdās, Mīrābāī — devotion to a form) vs **nirguṇa bhakti** (Kabīr, Nānak, Dādū — devotion to a formless ultimate, which shows bhakti does **not** logically require an iconic God); the five *bhāvas* of Gauḍīya rasa-theory (*śānta, dāsya, sakhya, vātsalya, mādhurya*).
- **The systematic Vedāntic positions.** ✅ - **Rāmānuja:** bhakti is *dhruvānusmṛti* — steady, uninterrupted loving meditation; because it presupposes qualifications not everyone has, he admits ***prapatti*/*śaraṇāgati*** (unconditional self-surrender) as an equally efficacious and universally accessible means. Liberation is finally by **divine grace**. - ✅ The famous Śrīvaiṣṇava split over how grace and effort combine: ***mārjāra-nyāya*** (the cat carries the kitten — grace alone, Teṅkalai) vs ***markaṭa-nyāya*** (the young monkey must cling — grace plus effort, Vaḍakalai). This is the single most efficient way to show you understand the grace/effort problem. - **Madhva:** bhakti is *jñāna-pūrvaka-sneha* — affection **preceded by knowledge** of the Lord's supremacy; it is both the means and, in graded form, the content of liberation; *prasāda* (grace) is decisive and *taratamya* fixes the degree.
  - **Vallabha:** *puṣṭi-mārga*, the path of nourishing grace, with grace unearned by works.
  - **Caitanya (Gauḍīya):** *prema-bhakti* is not merely a means to mokṣa but a **fifth and higher *puruṣārtha*** — the devotee may even prefer service to liberation. - **Advaita (Śaṃkara):** bhakti/*upāsanā* is **preparatory, not directly liberating** — it produces *citta-śuddhi* (purification) and one-pointedness, qualifying one for *jñāna*, which alone removes ignorance. ❓ The often-quoted definition of bhakti as *svasvarūpānusandhānam* ("investigation into one's own true nature") comes from the *Vivekacūḍāmaṇi*, whose ascription to Śaṃkara is disputed — attribute it to the Advaita tradition, not to Śaṃkara personally.
- **Argument that bhakti is a genuine and sufficient path.** ⚠️ (1) **Accessibility:** it dispenses with Sanskritic learning and ritual entitlement, which is why the movement is vernacular and socially open (Āḻvārs and Nāyanārs, Basavaṇṇa's Vīraśaiva, Kabīr, Nānak, Mīrā, Tukārām). (2) **Whole-person engagement:** it enlists affect and will, not intellect alone, and so transforms conduct as well as cognition. (3) **The incapacity argument:** if bondage is beginningless, the bound jīva cannot lift itself; only grace can, and bhakti is the posture in which grace is received. (4) **Textual warrant:** Gītā 18.66 makes surrender the final teaching of the text.
- **Objection → reply.** ⚠️ (a) **Love presupposes duality**, so bhakti cannot deliver Advaitic identity. *Reply:* Advaita agrees — and therefore classifies bhakti as means, not end; the theistic Vedāntas simply deny that identity is the goal. (b) **Emotionalism:** devotion without discernment can degenerate into sentiment or fanaticism. *Reply:* the traditions build in *vaidhī* discipline, scriptural regulation and the *jñāna-pūrvaka* requirement (Madhva). (c) **If grace decides, effort is otiose.** *Reply:* the cat/monkey dispute is precisely the tradition's own recognition of the problem; neither side eliminates the other term. (d) **Bhakti presupposes a saguṇa God** and so is unavailable to Buddhism, Jainism or Mīmāṃsā. *Reply:* nirguṇa bhakti shows the requirement is a *personal focus*, not an iconic form — but the objection stands against strictly non-theistic systems.
- **Verdict formula.** ⚠️ "Bhakti is a complete path in the theistic Vedāntas and a preparatory discipline in Advaita. Its philosophical significance is not emotional but **structural**: it relocates liberation from achievement to reception, and thereby makes mokṣa a matter of grace rather than of qualification."
#### 8.7 Jñāna, karma and bhakti — how the three means are related (2020 Q8(c) owner-module)
- **The exact question.** ⚠️ Are knowledge, action and devotion **alternative** routes (choose one), **sequential** stages (one leads to the next), or **combined** (all three jointly)? Every Vedāntic school answers differently, and naming the disagreement *is* the answer.
- **The analytic apparatus (deploy this early; it is what separates a 7 from a 12).** ⚠️ - **Direct means (*sākṣāt-sādhana*)** vs **auxiliary/remote aid (*ārād-upakāraka*)**. - **Means** vs **qualification (*adhikāra*)**: karma may qualify one for knowledge without being a means to liberation. - **Production model** vs **removal model** of liberation: if mokṣa is a *result to be produced*, action can produce it; if it is the *removal of an error*, only knowledge can remove it. Śaṃkara's entire case rests on the second.
- **The positions.** ✅ - **Śaṃkara (Advaita):** ***jñāna* alone liberates.** Mokṣa is not a producible effect (*sādhya*) but the eternally accomplished nature of the self (*siddha*), obscured by *avidyā*; ignorance is destroyed only by knowledge, as darkness by light. He therefore **rejects *jñāna-karma-samuccaya-vāda*** — the doctrine that knowledge and ritual action jointly produce liberation. Karma and *upāsanā* remain indispensable *preparations*: they generate *citta-śuddhi* and the *sādhana-catuṣṭaya* (*viveka*, *vairāgya*, the six inner accomplishments, *mumukṣutva*); knowledge then matures through *śravaṇa–manana–nididhyāsana*.
  - **Rāmānuja (Viśiṣṭādvaita):** **sequential culmination** — *karma-yoga* purifies, *jñāna-yoga* discloses the self's nature as a mode of God, and both flow into *bhakti-yoga*, which is the direct means; *prapatti* is the accessible alternative. Rāmānuja therefore accepts a form of *samuccaya* that Śaṃkara denies.
  - **Madhva (Dvaita):** knowledge of the Lord's supremacy is prior; bhakti ripened from it is the means; *prasāda* is decisive.
  - **Pūrva-Mīmāṃsā:** **karma alone** — the Veda is essentially injunctive, and knowledge-passages are subordinate to ritual action. This is the *pūrvapakṣa* Śaṃkara is arguing against, and naming it shows you know why the dispute exists.
  - ✅ **The Gītā's own signal:** 5.4–5 states that only the immature (*bālāḥ*) declare Sāṃkhya (knowledge) and Yoga (action) to be different; one who reaches by one reaches by the other. The Gītā thus supports **convergence**, which is why three great commentators could each claim it — **Śaṃkara for *jñāna*, Rāmānuja for *bhakti*, and Tilak (*Gītā-Rahasya*) for *karma***. - ✅ **Vivekananda:** the four yogas (*jñāna, bhakti, karma, rāja*) are **temperament-matched** routes to the same realisation — a psychological, not a metaphysical, pluralism.
- **Presupposition.** ⚠️ That liberation has a single determinate nature, so that the means-question has one right answer; the moment liberation itself is plural (Advaitic identity vs Vaiṣṇava communion), the means necessarily diverge.
- **Objection → reply.** ⚠️ **Objection:** the "convergence" reading is diplomatic mush — knowledge that dissolves individuality and devotion that preserves it cannot terminate in the same state. **Reply:** they converge in *function* (removal of egoic self-appropriation) while diverging in *terminus*; the honest answer says exactly this rather than harmonising. ⚠️ **Second objection:** if karma cannot liberate, why does the Gītā command action? **Reply:** *niṣkāma-karma* — action without appropriation of the fruit — is not liberating *by producing* mokṣa but by dismantling the agency-sense that sustains bondage.
- **Verdict formula.** ⚠️ "In the Indian tradition all three are *means*, but not means of the same kind. Karma qualifies, bhakti receives, jñāna removes. Whether they combine or exclude one another is settled not by piety but by one's answer to a prior question — is liberation *produced* or *uncovered*?"

#### LAYER 2 - CORE UPSC

#### 8.8 Resurrection versus rebirth — and rebirth versus reincarnation
- **Doctrine statement.** ✅ **Resurrection** is the divine re-constitution of the whole embodied person at an eschatological end; **rebirth** is the karmically driven re-embodiment of a continuant within a beginningless series. They are not variants of one idea: they differ in number of lives, mechanism, judge, telos and identity-bearer.
- **Two Western strands that are often wrongly merged.** ✅ **Immortality of the soul** is Greek/Platonic: the soul is naturally deathless and death releases it from the body. **Resurrection of the dead** is Hebrew–Christian–Islamic: the person is a **psychosomatic unity**, so survival requires God to restore the whole person; death is real and defeat is reversed only by divine act. **Oscar Cullmann** (*Immortality of the Soul or Resurrection of the Dead?*, 1955) argues the two are theologically **opposed**, not complementary — a high-value citation.
- **Comparative grid.**

| Axis | Resurrection | Rebirth (Indian) |
|---|---|---|
| Number of lives | ✅ One earthly life | ✅ Beginningless series |
| Mechanism | Divine act at the end of history | *Karma* maturing through *saṃsāra* |
| Judge | God, in a final judgement | The karmic law itself; in Nyāya/Rāmānuja God *ratifies* rather than decrees |
| Telos | Restored embodied life in communion with God | **Exit** from the series — mokṣa, nirvāṇa, kaivalya, apavarga |
| Identity-bearer | The person re-constituted by God (soul as form, or replica) | The ātman/jīva — or, in Buddhism, a causal stream |
| Body | Essential; a "spiritual body" is still a body | Discarded like clothing (Gītā 2.22); the subtle body (*sūkṣma-śarīra*) carries dispositions |
| Direction of time | Linear, eschatological | Cyclical, with a linear exit |
| Moral function | Accountability before a personal God | Self-regulating desert without an external prosecutor |

- **The identity problem, both ways.** ⚠️ *For resurrection:* the **gap problem** (what exists between death and resurrection?) and the **re-assembly problem** (the classical "cannibal" objection — if matter is shared between bodies, whose atoms are raised?). Three standard replies: **Aquinas** — the rational soul is the *form* of the body and secures continuity; **Peter van Inwagen** — God preserves the body (or a core of it) and leaves a simulacrum; **John Hick's replica theory** — an exact psychophysical replica, with continuity of memory and character, appearing in a different space, is *the same person* for all purposes that matter. *For rebirth:* the **memory objection** — no recollection of prior lives; the reply distinguishes psychological memory from metaphysical continuity and appeals to latent *saṃskāras*/*vāsanās*.
- ⚠️ **Rebirth vs reincarnation (2019 Q8(a) demands this).** In careful usage, **reincarnation** (*re-enfleshing*) presupposes a **substantial soul** that re-enters a new body — apt for Vedānta, Nyāya and Jainism. **Rebirth** is the wider, more neutral term, and is the only correct term for **Buddhism**, which affirms re-becoming (*punarbhava*) without any transmigrating substance. Sanskrit registers the same range: *punarjanma* (rebirth), *punarāvṛtti* (return), *saṃsāra* (the wandering itself). ✅ The Gītā's *pitṛyāna* teaching (9.21 — those who exhaust their merit "return to the world of mortals") shows that even heaven is a *within-saṃsāra* stage, not resurrection.
- **Objection → reply.** ⚠️ **Objection to resurrection:** a replica is a duplicate, not a survivor — and if God could make one replica, he could make two, which destroys identity. **Reply:** Hick restricts the case to a *unique* replica and argues that in the absence of a rival claimant our ordinary criteria are satisfied; critics answer that possibility of duplication already shows the criterion is not identity. ⚠️ **Objection to rebirth:** it is empirically untestable and morally hazardous. **Reply:** untestability is shared with resurrection, and explanatory karma does not license social judgement of the sufferer.
- **Verdict formula.** ⚠️ "Resurrection and rebirth answer *different* questions. Resurrection asks how a person can survive when the person is essentially embodied; rebirth asks how moral desert can be discharged when a single life is too short. Neither is a version of the other, and the identity problem — how the later being is the *same* — is unresolved on both sides, though for opposite reasons."

#### 10. CRITICISMS AND REPLIES

1. **Memory objection.** ⚠️ Lack of past-life memory challenges identity. **Reply:** memory is neither necessary nor sufficient for metaphysical continuity; unconscious dispositions may connect lives. 2. **Duplication objection.** ⚠️ If psychological continuity branches, identity becomes indeterminate. **Reply:** substance theories invoke one bearer; Buddhist theory accepts continuity without numerical identity. 3. **Moral-desert objection.** ⚠️ Rebirth can assign suffering to unverifiable past fault. **Reply:** karmic explanation need not legitimise social punishment; compassion remains obligatory. 4. **Liberation paradox.** ⚠️ Who seeks liberation if Advaita denies individuality or Buddhism denies self? **Reply:** conventional agency operates until insight removes the misconception sustaining bondage.
#### 11. COMMON UPSC TRAPS

1. ⚠️ Do not equate immortality, resurrection and rebirth.
2. ⚠️ Do not say Buddhism denies continuity; it denies permanent substance-self.
3. ⚠️ Do not describe Nyāya apavarga as positive bliss without qualification.
4. ⚠️ Do not make all Indian liberation doctrines merger with God.
5. ⚠️ Do not use the flame analogy as proof; it illustrates a causal-continuity model.
6. ⚠️ In jīvanmukti, distinguish knowledge destroying ignorance from *prārabdha* sustaining embodiment.
7. ⚠️ Do not treat **bhakti** as mere emotion. Its philosophical content is the shift from *achievement* to *reception* (grace), and the *mārjāra*/*markaṭa* dispute is the tradition's own statement of the grace–effort problem.
8. ⚠️ Do not say "Advaita rejects bhakti". Advaita makes it **preparatory** (*citta-śuddhi*, *upāsanā*), not directly liberating — a difference of role, not of value. 9. ⚠️ Do not answer the jñāna/karma/bhakti question with "all three are equal". Name the disagreement: Śaṃkara rejects *jñāna-karma-samuccaya*; Rāmānuja accepts a graded *samuccaya* culminating in bhakti; Mīmāṃsā holds karma alone.
10. ⚠️ Do not conflate **rebirth** with **reincarnation**. Reincarnation presupposes a substantial soul re-entering flesh; rebirth is the wider term and the only correct one for Buddhism (*punarbhava*).
11. ⚠️ Do not present **resurrection** as rebirth in heaven. It is one life, a bodily re-constitution by divine act, and a final judgement — the opposite structure to *saṃsāra*. 12. ⚠️ Do not treat *kaivalya* as a synonym for mokṣa. In Sāṃkhya-Yoga it is **isolation of contentless consciousness**; in Jainism the cognate *kevala* means **omniscience**. The same syllable, opposite content.
13. ⚠️ Do not call Yoga's Īśvara a creator. *YS* 1.24 makes him a *special puruṣa* untouched by karma — an object of *praṇidhāna*, not a cause of the world. 14. ⚠️ Do not say Sāṃkhya's puruṣa is bound and released. *SK* 62–63 says the opposite; the misattribution is the whole doctrine.
#### LAYER 5 - RAPID REVISION

- Resurrection: one life, psychosomatic person, God restores, judgement,
  Cullmann's opposition.
- Rebirth (wider) vs reincarnation (assumes a substance; wrong for Buddhism).
- Identity replies: Aquinas (soul=form), van Inwagen (core preserved), Hick
  (unique replica); rebirth -> memory != continuity.
- Fourteen canonical traps sit in the CORE slice -- the answer spine retrieves them.

#### Solved PYQ 1 - 2018 Q5(b), 10 marks

**Question:** How far is it plausible to argue that the anti-theistic religions’ stand on the concept of rebirth is philosophically significant?

**Model solution**

**Thesis.** The anti-theistic religions (Buddhism, and in its own way Jainism)
are philosophically significant precisely because they show that rebirth is
detachable both from a creator-God and from an immortal soul-substance.

- **Doctrine.** Buddhism affirms re-becoming (*punarbhava*) while denying a permanent self (*anatman*): what continues is a dependently arisen causal stream, not a transmigrating substance. Jainism has an eternal *jiva* but no
  God who administers karma; the moral law is self-regulating.
- **Significance (three claims).** (1) Karma needs no divine judge -- desert is
  discharged by an impersonal law, answering the problem of evil differently from
  theism. (2) Rebirth needs no immortal soul -- continuity, not substance,
  suffices (the flame-to-flame model). (3) Liberation (*nirvana*, *kevala*)
  requires neither grace nor union with God.
- **Objection -> reply.** If nothing permanent persists, who is reborn and who is
  liberated? Reply: the later stream is "neither the same nor wholly other";
  moral responsibility rests on causal connectedness, not identity of substance.

**Verdict.** The stand is significant because it separates the *machinery* of
karma-rebirth-liberation from theism and from soul-substance, proving that the
Indian moral cosmos is not logically wedded to either.

> MEMORY: Why this earns marks -- it reads "significant" as a modal/structural
> claim (what rebirth does NOT require) rather than as praise, and names the
> exact anti-substantialist and anti-theistic points.

#### Solved PYQ 2 - 2018 Q7(c), 15 marks

**Question:** Evaluate the concept of Bhakti (Devotion) as a pathway to attain liberation.

**Model solution**

**Thesis.** Bhakti is a complete path to liberation in the theistic Vedantas and
a preparatory discipline in Advaita; its philosophical contribution is structural
-- it relocates liberation from achievement to reception.

- **Define the criterion.** A "pathway" must be assessed for accessibility,
  sufficiency and terminus -- not merely described.
- **Textual base.** *Gita* 18.66 (*carama-sloka*: abandon all dharmas, take refuge in Me alone), 9.22 and 9.32 on accessibility; *Bhagavata* 7.5.23 lists the nine limbs (*navadha bhakti*): *sravana, kirtana, smarana, pada-sevana, arcana, vandana, dasya, sakhya, atma-nivedana*.
- **Typology (marks lie here).** *para* vs *apara*; *vaidhi* vs *raganuga*; *saguna* (Tulsidas, Mira) vs *nirguna* (Kabir, Nanak) -- the latter showing
  bhakti needs a personal focus, not an iconic form.
- **Systematic positions.** Ramanuja: bhakti as *dhruvanusmrti*, with *prapatti*
  as the accessible alternative; grace decisive; *marjara* vs *markata* dispute on grace/effort. Madhva: *jnana-purvaka-sneha*. Caitanya: *prema* as a fifth *purusartha*. Advaita: bhakti is *citta-suddhi*, preparatory not liberating.
- **Objection -> reply.** Love presupposes duality, so bhakti cannot deliver
  Advaitic identity -> Advaita agrees and so classes it as means, not end; the
  theistic schools deny identity is the goal at all.

**Verdict.** Bhakti's significance is not emotional but structural: it makes moksa
a matter of grace (reception) rather than qualification (achievement).

> MEMORY: Why this earns marks -- criterion-first evaluation, precise verse
> citation in order, the grace/effort dispute named, and the achievement->
> reception thesis stated explicitly.

#### Solved PYQ 3 - 2019 Q8(a), 20 marks

**Question:** State and explain the doctrines of Karma, Rebirth and Reincarnation in Hinduism.

**Model solution**

**Thesis.** Karma, rebirth and reincarnation are linked but non-identical: karma
is the mechanism, rebirth the field, and "reincarnation" a school-specific
substantialist reading that Buddhism refuses. Define each before expounding.

- **Karma.** Act together with its unseen potency (*adrsta/apurva*); classified as *sancita* (stored), *prarabdha* (ripening now) and *agami* (future). It is moral causation, not fatalism -- *purusakara* (effort) operates within *daiva*.
- **Rebirth.** *Punarjanma* within beginningless *samsara*; the subtle body (*suksma-sarira*) carries *samskaras* across lives. *Gita* 2.22 (worn-out
  garments) and 9.21 (return from heaven when merit is exhausted) show even heaven
  is a within-*samsara* stage.
- **Reincarnation vs rebirth.** Reincarnation (*re-enfleshing*) presupposes a
  SUBSTANTIAL soul re-entering a body (Vedanta, Nyaya, Jainism). Rebirth is the
  wider, neutral term and the ONLY correct one for Buddhism's *punarbhava* without
  a transmigrating self.
- **Contrast.** Resurrection -- one life, divine re-constitution, final judgement
  (Cullmann's opposition of Greek immortality and Hebrew resurrection).
- **Criticism -> reply.** Memory objection and victim-blaming risk; reply --
  memory is not continuity, and karma explains without licensing social blame.

**Verdict.** Karma supplies the mechanism, rebirth the field, and reincarnation a
substantialist reading of that field which Buddhism rejects.

> MEMORY: Why this earns marks -- it obeys "state AND explain" by defining all
> three separately, then interlocking them, and it nails the reincarnation/rebirth
> distinction the question is built to test.

#### Solved PYQ 4 - 2020 Q5(b), 10 marks

**Question:** The concept of ‘Immortality’ is a basic presupposition of religion. Elaborate your answer.

**Model solution**

**Thesis.** Immortality functions as a basic presupposition of religion because
most traditions require some post-mortem continuant to bear desert, judgement or
liberation -- but the presupposition takes at least three different forms, and
Buddhism shows it is not strictly necessary.

- **Why presupposed.** Moral seriousness (desert beyond this life), the hope of
  liberation/salvation, and the meaningfulness of ritual for the dead all seem to
  need something that survives death.
- **Three forms.** (1) Greek/Platonic natural immortality of a simple soul; (2)
  Semitic resurrection, where the psychosomatic person is re-constituted by God
  (not a natural property but a divine act -- Cullmann, 1955); (3) Indian eternal
  *atman/jiva* transmigrating until liberation.
- **The counter-example.** Buddhism grounds desert and liberation on a causal
  stream (*anatman*), so immortality is a common but not indispensable
  presupposition -- continuity is what is really required.

**Verdict.** "Immortality" is a widespread and structurally important
presupposition of religion, but the deeper presupposition is CONTINUITY of a
morally accountable subject, which Buddhism secures without an immortal soul.

> MEMORY: Why this earns marks -- it elaborates the claim (as asked) but refuses to
> overclaim, distinguishing the forms of the presupposition and citing the
> Buddhist limiting case.

#### Solved PYQ 5 - 2020 Q8(c), 15 marks

**Question:** Do you accept that Knowledge, Action and Devotion are the means to attain liberation in Indian tradition? Discuss.

**Model solution**

**Thesis.** Knowledge, action and devotion are all means to liberation, but means
of DIFFERENT kinds; whether they are alternative, sequential or combined is
settled by a prior question -- is liberation produced or uncovered?

- **Apparatus (deploy early).** Direct means (*saksat-sadhana*) vs remote aid (*arad-upakaraka*); means vs qualification (*adhikara*); production model vs
  removal model of moksa.
- **Positions.** *Purva-Mimamsa*: karma alone (the *purvapaksa*). *Sankara*: jnana alone removes *avidya*; he rejects *jnana-karma-samuccaya*; karma and *upasana*
  only purify (*sadhana-catustaya*; *sravana-manana-nididhyasana*). *Ramanuja*: karma -> jnana -> bhakti, with *prapatti* as the accessible alternative -- a graded *samuccaya*. *Madhva*: knowledge-grounded bhakti, *prasada* decisive.
- **The Gita's signal.** 5.4-5: only the immature call Samkhya (knowledge) and
  Yoga (action) different -- which is why Sankara, Ramanuja and Tilak could each
  claim the text. Vivekananda's four yogas match temperament (psychological, not
  metaphysical, pluralism).
- **Objection -> reply.** "Convergence" is diplomatic mush -> reply: they converge
  in FUNCTION (removing egoic self-appropriation) but diverge in TERMINUS (identity
  vs communion); say this rather than harmonising.

**Verdict.** Karma qualifies, bhakti receives, jnana removes; their relation
follows from each school's answer to whether liberation is produced or uncovered.

> MEMORY: Why this earns marks -- it names the disagreement instead of harmonising,
> and uses the production/removal cut to explain WHY schools differ.

#### Solved PYQ 6 - 2021 Q6(a), 20 marks

**Question:** Discuss the concept of immortality of soul with special reference to Hindu tradition.

**Model solution**

**Thesis.** In the Hindu tradition the immortality of the soul is affirmed across
schools, but on different grounds and with different consequences for liberation;
it is best read as the eternity of a continuity-bearer rather than one uniform
doctrine.

- **Vedanta.** The *atman* is eternal and self-luminous; *Gita* 2.23 gives the
  canonical image that weapons, fire, water and wind cannot destroy the embodied
  self. Advaita: *atman* = Brahman; Visistadvaita: the *jiva* is an eternal mode
  of God.
- **Nyaya-Vaisesika.** The self is an eternal substance, but consciousness is
  adventitious to it -- hence *apavarga* is cessation of pain and of consciousness.
- **Samkhya-Yoga.** *Purusa* is eternal, plural, contentless witness, isolated by nature (*kevala*).
- **Comparison with the West.** Plato argues immortality a priori (Phaedo); the
  Hindu case rests on scriptural testimony and substance-metaphysics, not on the
  Forms.
- **Critique -> reply.** The soul's simplicity does not entail indestructibility
  (Kant's paralogism), and there is no memory of past lives -> reply: continuity is
  carried by *samskaras* in the subtle body, distinct from psychological memory.

**Verdict.** Hindu "immortality of the soul" is the eternity of a continuity-
bearer (*atman/jiva/purusa*), variously conceived, and it grounds -- but does not
by itself deliver -- liberation.

> MEMORY: Why this earns marks -- it keeps "Hindu tradition" plural (Vedanta,
> Nyaya, Samkhya) instead of collapsing it, and cites the verified Gita 2.23 image.

#### Solved PYQ 7 - 2021 Q6(b), 15 marks

**Question:** Elucidate the concept of liberation according to Advaita Vedānta. Explain the role of knowledge in the attainment of liberation.

**Model solution**

**Thesis.** For Advaita, liberation (*moksa*) is the realisation of the *atman*'s identity with Brahman through the removal of ignorance (*avidya*); knowledge (*jnana*) is therefore the sole direct means.

- **Nature of liberation.** *Moksa* is not a producible effect but the eternally accomplished nature of the self, obscured by *avidya*. It comes as *jivanmukti*
  (freedom while living, the body coasting on *prarabdha* like a spun wheel) and *videhamukti* (at the body's fall).
- **Role of knowledge.** Since bondage is ignorance, only knowledge can remove it,
  as light removes darkness. Sankara rejects *jnana-karma-samuccaya*; action and *upasana* generate *citta-suddhi* and the *sadhana-catustaya*, and knowledge matures through *sravana-manana-nididhyasana*.
- **Provenance (Griffiths).** Bondage is a beginningless superimposition
  (*adhyasa*) -- the rope taken for a snake -- so *moksa* is "not acquired but
  acknowledged"; knowledge only removes the error.
- **Objection -> reply.** If knowledge destroys karma, why does the body remain?
  -> already-fructifying *prarabdha* is distinguished from accumulated and future
  karma.

**Verdict.** Advaitic liberation is a cognitive-existential awakening, and
knowledge is the direct means because liberation is uncovered, not produced.

> MEMORY: Why this earns marks -- it answers BOTH obligations (the concept AND the
> role of knowledge), and grounds the "knowledge alone" claim in the removal model.

#### Solved PYQ 8 - 2021 Q8(b), 15 marks

**Question:** Discuss and evaluate the doctrine of Karma as an essential postulate of Hinduism.

**Model solution**

**Thesis.** The doctrine of karma is an essential postulate of Hinduism because it
supplies a self-regulating moral order that makes rebirth intelligible and desert
just; it is defensible against fatalism and moral-luck objections but remains
empirically unverifiable.

- **Statement.** Every morally qualified act leaves an unseen potency
  (*adrsta/apurva*) that matures as pleasure or pain; *sancita/prarabdha/agami*
  classify the debt. Karma requires rebirth, since fruits mature beyond one life.
- **Why a postulate.** It grounds moral responsibility, explains the distribution
  of fortune without a capricious deity, and secures the justice of *samsara*.
- **Evaluation.** Strength -- it is not fatalism: *purusakara* operates within *daiva*; present effort shapes *agami*. Weakness -- unverifiability, and the
  moral-luck/victim-blaming risk.
- **Objection -> reply.** Karma blames the sufferer -> reply: it EXPLAINS
  distribution without LICENSING social judgement; compassion remains obligatory.

**Verdict.** Karma is postulated because it does indispensable work -- justice,
responsibility, the intelligibility of rebirth -- but its warrant is inferential
and moral, not empirical.

> MEMORY: Why this earns marks -- it both expounds AND evaluates (as asked),
> defending karma against fatalism while conceding unverifiability.

#### Solved PYQ 9 - 2022 Q5(c), 10 marks

**Question:** “Immortality of Soul is a necessary postulate for rebirth.” Critically examine with reference to Buddhism.

**Model solution**

**Thesis.** The claim that immortality of the soul is a necessary postulate for
rebirth is false as stated: a persisting soul is SUFFICIENT for rebirth but not
NECESSARY, and Buddhism is the decisive counter-example.

- **The substantialist assumption.** In Vedanta, Nyaya and Jainism an eternal
  *atman/jiva* is exactly what transmigrates, so there immortality is presupposed.
- **The Buddhist refutation.** Buddhism denies a permanent self (*anatman*) yet affirms rebirth (*punarbhava*): what continues is a dependently arisen causal
  stream, illustrated by a flame passing candle to candle. Rebirth therefore
  requires CONTINUITY, not an unchanging substance.
- **Objection -> reply.** If no same agent persists, who is reborn and who reaps
  the fruit? -> the later stream is "neither the same nor wholly other"; moral
  responsibility rests on causal connectedness.

**Verdict.** Immortality of a substantial soul is sufficient but not necessary for
rebirth; what rebirth strictly needs is a continuity-bearer, and schools disagree
only about whether that bearer must be a substance. Buddhism proves the point.

> MEMORY: Why this earns marks -- it delivers a precise MODAL verdict (sufficient
> not necessary) and uses Buddhism as a counter-example, not merely an exception.

#### Solved PYQ 10 - 2022 Q7(b), 15 marks

**Question:** Is it possible to have an idea of Liberation without the conception of a real agent? In this context, discuss the difference between Advaita and Viśiṣṭādvaita systems of thought.

**Model solution**

**Thesis.** An idea of liberation without a "real agent" is coherent, and the
Advaita-Visistadvaita dispute is exactly a dispute over whether the liberated
subject is an ultimately real individual agent.

- **The paradox.** If Advaita denies ultimate individuality, who is bound and who
  is liberated?
- **Advaita.** *Moksa* is the sublation of a MISTAKEN individuality, not the annihilation of an entity; conventional agency (*vyavaharika*) operates until
  liberating knowledge removes the misidentification. The "agent" is real
  empirically, unreal absolutely -- so liberation needs no ultimately real agent.
- **Visistadvaita.** The *jiva* is a real, eternal mode of God, a genuine agent
  throughout, bound by karma and freed into communion/service; it never becomes
  God. So it faces no agent-paradox but pays with irreducible plurality.
- **Objection -> reply.** Advaita's conventional agency looks ad hoc -> reply: the
  two-truths framework is doing principled work, distinguishing the standpoint of
  ignorance from that of realisation.

**Verdict.** Liberation without a real (ultimate) agent is coherent on Advaita and
denied on Visistadvaita; the difference is not about liberation's possibility but
about the ontological status of the one who is free.

> MEMORY: Why this earns marks -- it reframes the "real agent" question as the very
> axis dividing the two schools, giving each its own answer rather than blending.

#### Solved PYQ 11 - 2022 Q8(b), 15 marks

**Question:** Discuss the nature of Soul and Bondage according to Jainism.

**Model solution**

**Thesis.** In Jainism the soul (*jiva*) is an eternal, conscious substance whose
bondage is the literal adhesion of fine karmic MATTER; liberation is the complete
stoppage and shedding of that matter.

- **Nature of the soul.** Every living being is an eternal conscious *jiva*, of
  the size of the body it occupies, whose intrinsic nature is infinite knowledge,
  perception, bliss and energy (*ananta-catustaya*), currently obscured.
- **Bondage machinery.** Passions and action ATTRACT karmic particles (*asrava*); they BIND to the soul (*bandha*); restraint STOPS the influx (*samvara*); austerity SHEDS accumulated karma (*nirjara*); the freed *jiva* manifests its full qualities and rises to the summit of the universe (*siddhatva*).
- **Distinction.** Jain liberation PERFECTS individuality -- neither Advaitic
  identity nor Buddhist cessation without a self. Jain *kevala* = omniscience, not Samkhya's contentless *kaivalya*.
- **Objection -> reply.** How does non-material consciousness interact with quasi-
  material karma? -> Jainism treats extension and embodiment as MODES of the *jiva*
  during bondage; the reply is contested but internally consistent.

**Verdict.** The Jain soul is eternal and intrinsically omniscient, and bondage/
liberation are a quasi-physical process of accretion and removal of karmic matter.

> MEMORY: Why this earns marks -- it delivers BOTH nouns the question names (soul
> AND bondage), with the *asrava-bandha-samvara-nirjara* machinery in order.

#### Solved PYQ 12 - 2023 Q6(a), 20 marks

**Question:** Critically examine Plato’s apriori proofs for the immortality of the soul.

**Model solution**

**Thesis.** Plato's four Phaedo arguments make the soul's immortality suggestive
but not demonstrable; assessed separately, each either reaches only pre-existence
or rests on an undefended assumption.

- **(1) Opposites (cyclical).** Living and dead arise from each other, so souls
  persist between lives. *Assessment:* establishes recurrence, not the soul's
  indestructibility; the analogy from physical opposites is weak.
- **(2) Recollection (*anamnesis*).** We judge equal sticks by an Equality never
  perfectly given in sense, so the soul knew it before birth. *Assessment:* proves
  (at most) PRE-existence, not post-mortem survival.
- **(3) Affinity.** The soul is simple, invisible and akin to the Forms, so it
  does not decompose. *Assessment:* likeness is not identity; simplicity does not
  entail indestructibility.
- **(4) Form of Life.** The soul essentially brings Life and cannot admit its
  opposite, death. *Assessment:* the strongest, but it assumes the soul is
  essentially life-giving -- the point at issue.
- **Objection -> reply.** Simmias' harmony analogy makes the soul a bodily
  attunement -> Plato replies the soul RULES the body and pre-exists it.
- **External critique.** Kant's paralogism: the soul is not a knowable substance.

**Verdict.** The arguments are mutually supporting rather than individually
conclusive; they render immortality intelligible, not proven.

> MEMORY: Why this earns marks -- "critically examine" is obeyed by FOUR separate
> assessments plus Simmias and Kant, not one summary verdict.

**Verdict.** *Apavarga* is the cessation of pain and of the very consciousness
that makes pain possible -- the sharpest Indian statement of liberation as
release rather than beatitude.

**Thesis.** With reference to the *Bhagavad Gita*, immortality of the soul is
presented as the presupposition of rebirth: precisely because the embodied self is
imperishable, it "casts off worn-out bodies and enters new ones". Yet, read
philosophically, this shows immortality is sufficient for rebirth, not
universally necessary.

- **The Gita's teaching.** 2.23 -- weapons, fire, water and wind cannot destroy
  the self; 2.22 -- the self changes bodies as a person changes worn-out garments.
  Rebirth here PRESUPPOSES the deathless *atman*.
- **The mechanism.** Driven by karma, the imperishable self takes new embodiments
  within *samsara*; 9.21 shows even heavenly sojourn ends in return.
- **The wider verdict.** The Gita's model is substantialist, so for IT immortality
  is necessary. But Buddhism affirms rebirth without an immortal soul, so the
  necessity is school-relative: the Gita states a sufficient condition, not a law
  binding every rebirth theory.
- **Objection -> reply.** Does the Gita PROVE immortality? -> No; it ASSERTS it as
  scriptural teaching and illustrates it (the garment image is illustration, not
  demonstration).

**Verdict.** In the Gita, immortality of the soul is the necessary ground of
rebirth; across the traditions, it is sufficient but not necessary -- Buddhism is
the reservation the honest answer records.

> while still delivering the modal verdict and the Buddhist reservation.

#### Original Mains 1

> Original question | Philosophy Paper II, Philosophy of Religion | 10 marks | answer in ~150 words

**Question:** Distinguish rebirth from reincarnation, and explain why the distinction is decisive for Buddhism.

**Model solution**

**Thesis.** Rebirth is the wider, more neutral term; reincarnation is the
narrower term that assumes a substantial soul re-entering flesh -- which is why
Buddhism accepts rebirth but not reincarnation.

- **Reincarnation** (*re-enfleshing*) presupposes an enduring soul-substance that
  passes from body to body; it fits Vedanta, Nyaya and Jainism.
- **Rebirth** (*punarjanma/punarbhava*) names only re-becoming within *samsara*,
  and can be cashed out with or without a substance.
- **Why decisive for Buddhism.** Buddhism affirms *punarbhava* while denying a permanent self (*anatman*); what continues is a dependently arisen causal stream
  (flame-to-flame), "neither the same nor wholly other". To call this
  "reincarnation" would smuggle back the very soul-substance Buddhism denies.

**Verdict.** Use "rebirth" as the genus and reserve "reincarnation" for the
substantialist species; only then is Buddhist *punarbhava* correctly described.

#### Original Mains 2

> Original question | Philosophy Paper II, Philosophy of Religion | 15 marks | answer in ~250 words

**Question:** Examine whether 'liberation' can be treated as a single concept across the Indian schools.

**Model solution**

**Thesis.** 'Liberation' is a family-resemblance term, not a single concept:
once the school-specific destinations are separated, the apparent Indian consensus
turns out to be a shared vocabulary covering incompatible states.

- **The parity bench.** *Moksa* (Advaita) is realised identity with Brahman; *moksa* (Visistadvaita) is eternal communion with God; *apavarga* (Nyaya) is cessation of pain AND of consciousness; *kaivalya* (Samkhya-Yoga) is the isolation of contentless consciousness; *kevala/siddhatva* (Jain) is perfected omniscient individuality; *nirvana* (Buddhism) is the cessation of craving with
  no self affirmed.
- **The consciousness test.** These states differ precisely on what survives:
  Nyaya keeps a self but no consciousness; Advaita keeps non-dual bliss; Samkhya
  keeps contentless awareness; Jainism keeps omniscience; Buddhism affirms no self.
  A single concept cannot span "no consciousness" and "infinite bliss".
- **What they share.** Only a formal core: cessation of bondage and exit from
  *samsara*. That is a family resemblance, not a common essence.
- **Objection -> reply.** Perhaps all are "freedom from suffering"? Reply: even
  this masks a production/removal split and the identity/isolation divide.

**Verdict.** Treat 'liberation' as a genus with irreducibly different species;
fixing the exact term against the parity bench is the first task in any answer.

#### Original Mains 3

> Original question | Philosophy Paper II, Philosophy of Religion | 20 marks | answer in ~400 words

**Question:** Compare the Western resurrection tradition and the Indian rebirth tradition on the problem of personal identity across death.

**Model solution**

**Thesis.** Resurrection and rebirth answer different questions and face the
identity problem from opposite directions; neither is a version of the other, and
on both the question "is the later being the SAME?" remains unresolved.

- **Two structures.** Resurrection (Hebrew-Christian-Islamic) is ONE life: the
  person is a psychosomatic unity, so survival needs God to re-constitute the whole
  person, and history ends in judgement. Rebirth (Indian) is a beginningless
  series in which karma matures across lives until liberation. Oscar Cullmann
  (1955) argues Greek immortality and Hebrew resurrection are theologically
  OPPOSED, not complementary.
- **Identity for resurrection.** The gap problem (what exists between death and
  resurrection?) and the re-assembly ("cannibal") problem (shared atoms) threaten
  bodily continuity. Standard replies: Aquinas -- the rational soul is the FORM of
  the body and secures continuity; van Inwagen -- God preserves a core of the body
  or leaves a simulacrum; Hick -- an exact, unique psychophysical replica,
  continuous in memory and character, is the same person for all that matters. The
  duplication objection (God could make two) says the criterion is then not
  identity.
- **Book support.** Lynne Rudder Baker ("Death and the Afterlife", Oxford Handbook
  of Philosophy of Religion) sets out four candidate criteria -- same soul, same
  body, same composite, or continuity of memory -- and surveys Origen's "seed",
  Augustine's re-collected "statue" and Aquinas's soul-as-form; Bernard Williams's
  reduplication argument and H. H. Price's image-world sharpen the difficulty.
- **Identity for rebirth.** The memory objection (no recollection of prior lives)
  is met by distinguishing psychological memory from metaphysical continuity and
  appealing to latent *samskaras*; in Buddhism identity is dropped altogether in
  favour of a causal stream "neither the same nor wholly other".
- **Contrast.** Resurrection keeps the body essential; rebirth discards it like a
  garment (*Gita* 2.22). Resurrection needs a divine agent; rebirth needs only the
  self-regulating law of karma.

**Verdict.** Resurrection asks how an essentially embodied person can survive;
rebirth asks how moral desert can be discharged when one life is too short. Both
leave the sameness of the later being contested -- for resurrection because of the
gap and duplication, for rebirth because of the missing memory and (in Buddhism)
the deliberate abandonment of numerical identity.

#### PYQ-Mapped Model-Answer Skeletons

#### 6.1 — "Is immortality of the soul a necessary condition for rebirth? (w.r.t. Buddhism)" (2022, 10m)
```
Intro : substantialist assumption — a soul must persist to transmigrate.
Body  : Vedānta/Nyāya/Jain — YES, eternal ātman/jīva is what is reborn;
        Buddhism — NO: anātman; what continues is a causal stream (pratītyasamutpāda),
        rebirth like flame-to-flame, not a soul.
Assess: rebirth needs CONTINUITY, not necessarily an unchanging soul.
Concl : immortality is sufficient but not necessary — Buddhism proves the point.
```

#### 6.2 — "Nature of jīvanmukti in Advaita Vedānta." (2025, 10m)
```
Intro : Advaita — mokṣa is realising ātman = Brahman; ends avidyā.
Body  : jīvanmukti = liberated WHILE living; jñāna destroys ignorance now;
        body persists by prārabdha karma (spun-wheel) → videhamukti at death.
Assess: liberation is a cognitive-existential transformation, not a post-mortem reward.
Concl : freedom is present realisation, not a future place.
```

#### 6.3 — "Evaluate the concept of Bhakti (Devotion) as a pathway to attain liberation." (2018, 15m)
```
Intro : bhakti = loving devotion to a personal God, held to be a DIRECT means of mokṣa.
Body  : textual base — Gītā 18.66 (carama-śloka), 9.22; Bhāgavata 7.5.23 navadhā bhakti;
        Nārada/Śāṇḍilya Bhakti Sūtras.
        typology — parā/aparā; vaidhī/rāgānugā; saguṇa (Tulsīdās, Mīrā) vs nirguṇa (Kabīr, Nānak).
        systematic — Rāmānuja (dhruvānusmṛti + prapatti; grace); mārjāra vs markaṭa nyāya;
        Madhva (jñāna-pūrvaka-sneha); Vallabha (puṣṭi); Caitanya (prema as fifth puruṣārtha);
        Advaita (preparatory — citta-śuddhi, not direct means).
Assess: strengths — accessibility across caste/gender/language; whole-person engagement;
        grace answers the incapacity of a beginninglessly bound jīva.
        objections — love presupposes duality; emotionalism; grace vs effort; requires a
                personal God.
Concl : philosophically, bhakti relocates liberation from achievement to RECEPTION — that, not
        emotion, is its contribution.
```

#### 6.4 — "Are Knowledge, Action and Devotion the means to liberation in Indian tradition?" (2020, 15m)
```
Intro : the real question is whether they are alternative, sequential or combined.
Tools : sākṣāt-sādhana vs ārād-upakāraka; means vs adhikāra; PRODUCTION vs REMOVAL model of mokṣa.
Body  : Mīmāṃsā — karma alone (the pūrvapakṣa).
        Śaṃkara — jñāna alone removes avidyā; rejects jñāna-karma-samuccaya; karma/upāsanā only
                  purify (sādhana-catuṣṭaya; śravaṇa-manana-nididhyāsana).
        Rāmānuja — karma → jñāna → bhakti, with prapatti as the accessible alternative.
        Madhva — knowledge-grounded bhakti; prasāda decisive.
        Gītā 5.4–5 — only the immature call Sāṃkhya and Yoga different; hence Śaṃkara, Rāmānuja
                  and Tilak could each claim the text.
        Vivekananda — four yogas matched to temperament (psychological, not metaphysical,
                pluralism).
Assess: they converge in FUNCTION (dismantling egoic appropriation) and diverge in TERMINUS
        (identity vs communion) — say this rather than harmonising.
Concl : all three are means, of different kinds — karma qualifies, bhakti receives, jñāna removes.
```

#### 6.5 — "Karma, Rebirth and Reincarnation in Hinduism." (2019, 20m)
```
Intro : three linked but non-identical concepts — define each before expounding.
B1    : Karma — act + its unseen potency (adṛṣṭa/apūrva); sañcita / prārabdha / āgāmi;
        moral causation, not fatalism (puruṣakāra vs daiva).
B2    : Rebirth — punarjanma within beginningless saṃsāra; subtle body carries saṃskāras;
        Gītā 2.22 (garments) and 9.21 (return from heaven when merit is exhausted).
B3    : Reincarnation vs rebirth — reincarnation presupposes a SUBSTANTIAL soul re-enfleshed
        (Vedānta, Nyāya, Jainism); rebirth is the wider term and the only correct one for
        Buddhism's punarbhava without a transmigrating self.
Contrast: resurrection — one life, divine re-constitution, final judgement (Cullmann's opposition
        of Greek immortality and Hebrew resurrection).
Crit  : memory objection; unverifiability; victim-blaming risk — and the replies.
Concl : karma supplies the mechanism, rebirth the field, and "reincarnation" a school-specific
        substantialist reading that Buddhism refuses.
```

#### The Six Survival-Terms and the Necessity Question

#### LAYER 3 - ADVANCED

The necessity question is a modal question, so answer it with modal vocabulary.

> ANALYSIS: Is immortality of a substantial soul NECESSARY for rebirth? In the
> substantialist systems (Vedanta, Nyaya, Jainism) an eternal atman or jiva is
> exactly what transmigrates, so there immortality is presupposed. But Buddhism
> secures re-becoming through a causal stream alone. So a persisting soul is
> SUFFICIENT for rebirth, not NECESSARY -- Buddhism is the decisive counter-case.

> ANALYSIS: Is rebirth necessary for liberation? Liberation is defined as exit
> from the cycle, so it presupposes bondage-across-lives and something that is
> freed. Substantial systems answer yes; Buddhism shows nirvana is coherent even
> without an eternal soul, so "a robust conception of liberation" does not
> logically require an immortal substance -- only a continuity that can end.

Because the several liberation-words name different destinations, "liberation"
behaves like a family-resemblance term rather than one fixed state: identity
(Advaita), communion (Visistadvaita), cessation (Nyaya apavarga, Buddhist
nirvana), isolation (Samkhya-Yoga kaivalya) and perfected individuality (Jain
kevala) share a family label but not a single content.

> WRONG: Treating the one-screen map as one school's doctrine. It is a
> comparative scaffold; each cell must be cashed out in that school's own terms.

#### Immortality of the Soul: Plato's Four Proofs and the Western Critique

#### LAYER 3 - ADVANCED

Grade the four arguments separately, because the examiner does.

> ANALYSIS: The cyclical and recollection arguments establish only pre-existence;
> they do not yet prove post-mortem survival. Plato needs the affinity and
> Form-of-Life arguments to bridge the second direction, and even these rest on
> the assumption that what is simple cannot decompose -- simplicity does not by
> itself entail indestructibility. Kant's paralogism formalises the complaint:
> the soul is not a knowable substance, so its permanence cannot be demonstrated.

> ANALYSIS: Simmias' harmony objection (same dialogue) makes the soul an
> attunement of the body, which would perish with it. Plato replies that the soul
> rules the body rather than depending on it, and that recollection presupposes a
> soul already in existence before the body -- an attunement cannot pre-exist its
> instrument.

> BOOK: In "Death and the Afterlife" (Oxford Handbook of Philosophy of Religion),
> Lynne Rudder Baker frames Plato's position as NATURAL immortality -- the soul is
> deathless by its own nature and death merely releases it -- and contrasts it
> with the Semitic view, where survival is a divine ACT, not a property of the
> soul. This is why Plato belongs on the "immortality" side and resurrection on
> the other.

The Indian comparison earns marks: atman (Vedanta), jiva (Jain) and the Nyaya
self are all eternal, but by scriptural testimony and substance-metaphysics, not
by Plato's Forms. The Gita 2.23 image (weapons, fire, water, wind cannot destroy
the self) is a canonical illustration -- paraphrase it; do not treat it as proof.

#### Rebirth: Karma, Samsara and the Buddhist Challenge

#### LAYER 3 - ADVANCED

> ANALYSIS: Rebirth is the trans-life field in which karmic consequences can mature; debt is only an analogy. The
> 2023 question ("significance of rebirth in the theory of Karma") is really
> asking: why does karma REQUIRE rebirth? Because desert outruns a single life.

> ANALYSIS: Then comes the Buddhist twist. Buddhism denies a permanent soul
> (anatman) yet affirms rebirth: what is reborn is not a self but a causal
> continuum of momentary states, a flame passing candle to candle. So rebirth
> requires CONTINUITY, not necessarily an unchanging soul -- immortality is
> sufficient but not necessary. Say exactly this for 2022 Q5(c) and 2025 Q7(c).

Guard two objections in advance:

> WRONG: Reading karma as fatalism. The tradition insists on purusakara (personal
> effort) operating within daiva (the given): prarabdha fixes the hand you are
> dealt, agami is the hand you are now playing.

> ANALYSIS: The moral-desert or "victim-blaming" objection says karma licenses
> blaming the sufferer for an unverifiable past fault. Reply: karma EXPLAINS
> distribution without LICENSING social judgement; compassion remains obligatory,
> and the explanatory claim is separable from any prescriptive one.

Provenance discipline: the Gita's garment image (2.22) and the pitryana teaching
(9.21, merit exhausted -> return to the mortal world) are verified verses -- use
them as illustration; even heaven is a within-samsara stage, not resurrection.

#### Buddhist Rebirth Without a Soul: Anatman and Causal Continuity

#### LAYER 3 - ADVANCED

The engine is dependent origination (pratityasamutpada): all conditioned factors
are impermanent; no aggregate is a self; craving and karmic formations condition
future consciousness; so continuity occurs without an unchanging owner.

> ANALYSIS: The standard objection -- "if no same agent persists, who receives
> the karmic fruit?" -- is answered by the causal-descent formula: the later
> stream is neither the same nor wholly other than the earlier. Moral
> responsibility needs causal connectedness, not substance-identity.

> WRONG: Calling anatman nihilism. Buddhism denies an independent, permanent self,
> not conventional persons or the reality of consequences.

> BOOK: Philip L. Quinn, writing on religious diversity in the Oxford Handbook of
> Philosophy of Religion, contrasts the Christian hope of UNION WITH GOD with
> Buddhist nirvana, which he characterises as the ceasing-to-be of the individual
> self rather than its perfection -- precisely the anti-substantialist point the
> 2018 "anti-theistic religions" question rewards.

This subtopic also carries the 2018 Q5(b) demand: the philosophical significance
of the anti-theistic (Buddhist, Jain) stand on rebirth is that it PROVES rebirth
is separable from theism and from an immortal soul -- a self-regulating moral
order needs neither a God who judges nor a substance that transmigrates.

#### Liberation Across Schools: the Parity Bench

#### LAYER 3 - ADVANCED

> ANALYSIS: Jivanmukti (Advaita) is liberation WHILE LIVING -- the knower of
> Brahman is free now; the body continues on prarabdha momentum (a spun wheel)
> until it drops at videhamukti. Liberation is thus a cognitive-existential
> transformation, not a post-mortem place or reward.

> ANALYSIS: For the "robust liberation" stem (2024 Q6(a)): liberation presupposes
> bondage-across-lives (rebirth) and something liberated (a continuity-bearer).
> Substantial systems make that bearer a soul; Buddhism makes it a stream. So a
> robust conception needs continuity that can END, not an immortal substance.

> BOOK: Paul J. Griffiths (Oxford Handbook of Philosophy of Religion) reads
> Sankara's moksa as "not acquired but acknowledged": since bondage is only
> avidya, liberation adds nothing and destroys nothing -- knowledge simply
> removes an error, and the self is disclosed as having been free all along.

> WRONG: Treating kaivalya as a synonym for moksa, or Jain kevala as Samkhya
> kaivalya. Same syllable, opposite content -- Jain kevala = omniscience; Samkhya
> kaivalya = contentless isolation. Nyaya apavarga is cessation, not bliss.

Provenance: the §5A bench and the school table are the canonical evidence; keep
the seven rows distinct and your script cannot collapse into "all Indian schools
seek union with God" -- which is simply false for Nyaya, Samkhya and Buddhism.

#### Vedantic Atman, Rebirth and Moksa: Advaita versus Visistadvaita

#### LAYER 3 - ADVANCED

> ANALYSIS: The "real agent" question (2022 Q7(b)) is sharp. If Advaita denies
> ultimate individuality, who is bound and who is liberated? Advaita answers:
> conventional agency operates until knowledge removes the misidentification;
> moksa is not the annihilation of an entity but the sublation of a MISTAKEN
> individuality. Visistadvaita rejects this: the jiva is a real agent throughout,
> bound by karma and freed into service -- so it has no agent-paradox to solve.

> BOOK: Paul J. Griffiths (Oxford Handbook of Philosophy of Religion) locates
> Sankara's bondage in avidya as a beginningless SUPERIMPOSITION (adhyasa) -- the
> rope mistaken for a snake -- so moksa is "not acquired but acknowledged". This
> is the cleanest way to state why, for Advaita, knowledge alone liberates.

> ANALYSIS: The role of knowledge (2021 Q6(b)) follows directly: since bondage is
> ignorance, only knowledge can remove it (darkness by light); devotion and works
> merely purify (citta-suddhi) and qualify. For Ramanuja knowledge discloses the
> self as God's mode but culminates in bhakti and grace.

> ANALYSIS: The memory objection -- no recollection of prior lives -- is met by
> distinguishing psychological memory from metaphysical continuity and appealing
> to latent samskaras; empirical warrant remains contested, so concede it.

Provenance: the Gita garment image is a canonical illustration of embodiment, not
an independent demonstration of the eternal self.

#### Jain Jiva and Bondage: the Karmic-Matter Ladder

#### LAYER 3 - ADVANCED

> ANALYSIS: The 2022 Q8(b) demand is TWO nouns -- "soul AND bondage" -- so deliver
> both: the jiva metaphysics (eternal, conscious, exactly body-sized during
> embodiment) AND the asrava/bandha/samvara/nirjara machinery. Missing either
> half caps the mark.

> ANALYSIS: Jain liberation PERFECTS individuality. It is neither Advaitic
> identity (the soul does not merge into Brahman) nor Buddhist cessation (a real
> perfected self remains). The freed jiva manifests ananta-catustaya and rises to
> the summit of the universe.

> WRONG: Treating Jain kevala as Samkhya kaivalya. Jain kevala = omniscience
> (kevala-jnana), the fullest content; Samkhya kaivalya = contentless isolation.

> ANALYSIS: The interaction problem: how does non-material consciousness bind with
> quasi-material karma? Jainism's answer is to treat extension and embodiment as
> MODES of the jiva during bondage -- consciousness is intrinsically the soul's,
> and the karmic "matter" is the obscuring medium, not a second substance the soul
> must reach across. Note this is a concession-shaped reply; flag it as contested.

Provenance: the dust-on-an-oily-body image is the standard canonical illustration
of how passions make karma adhere -- use it as a model, not a demonstration.

#### Cessation and Isolation: Nyaya Apavarga, Advaita Jivanmukti and Samkhya-Yoga Kaivalya

#### LAYER 3 - ADVANCED

> ANALYSIS: Nyaya traces suffering through defect -> activity -> rebirth -> false
> knowledge; true knowledge reverses the chain, ending pain AND the qualities
> (including consciousness) that sustain embodied experience. So apavarga is a
> negative state -- do not call it positive bliss.

> ANALYSIS: Advaita's jivanmukti turns on a clean split: liberating knowledge
> destroys ignorance IMMEDIATELY, but already-fructifying prarabdha karma keeps
> the body going -- the potter's wheel spins on after the hand withdraws. Hence
> the objection "if knowledge destroys karma, why does the body remain?" is met by
> distinguishing accumulated and future karma from prarabdha.

> ANALYSIS: Samkhya's most examinable doctrine is SK 62-63: strictly, NO purusa is
> bound, released or transmigrating; it is prakrti who binds, transmigrates and
> releases herself. Liberation is viveka-khyati -- discriminative discernment --
> correcting a MISATTRIBUTION, not an event in consciousness. SK 67-68 give the
> potter's-wheel jivanmukti and videha-kaivalya at death.

> WRONG: Calling Yoga's Isvara a creator. YS 1.24 makes Isvara a special purusa
> untouched by karma -- an object of pranidhana, one optional means, not the
> ground of the world. Yoga is the decisive Indian case of theism without
> creation.

Provenance: SK 1 (threefold duhkha), 62-63, 67-68 and YS 1.2/1.3/1.24/4.34 are
verified numbers; the potter's-wheel image is shared with Advaita but the residue
differs (karmic for Advaita, gunas' momentum for Samkhya). Note the parallel and
the difference -- that contrast is a top-band discriminator.

#### Paths to Liberation: Bhakti, and the Jnana-Karma-Bhakti Question

#### LAYER 3 - ADVANCED

Bhakti (2018 Q7(c)): loving devotion to a personal God, held to be a DIRECT means
and, crucially, open irrespective of caste, gender, language or learning.

> ANALYSIS: Its philosophical significance is structural, not emotional: it
> relocates liberation from ACHIEVEMENT to RECEPTION. If bondage is beginningless,
> the bound jiva cannot lift itself, so only grace can -- and bhakti is the posture
> in which grace is received. The marjara-nyaya (cat carries kitten: grace alone)
> vs markata-nyaya (monkey must cling: grace plus effort) dispute IS the
> tradition's own statement of the grace-effort problem.

> WRONG: Treating bhakti as mere emotion, or saying "Advaita rejects bhakti".
> Advaita makes bhakti PREPARATORY (citta-suddhi, upasana), qualifying one for
> jnana -- a difference of role, not of value.

The three-means question (2020 Q8(c)) is decided by a prior question:

> ANALYSIS: Is liberation PRODUCED or UNCOVERED? If produced, action can produce
> it (Mimamsa). If it is the removal of an error, only knowledge removes it --
> Sankara's whole case, and why he rejects jnana-karma-samuccaya. Ramanuja accepts
> a graded samuccaya culminating in bhakti (prapatti as the accessible
> alternative); Madhva makes knowledge-grounded bhakti decisive under prasada.
> Deploy saksat-sadhana (direct means) vs arad-upakaraka (remote aid) early.

> ANALYSIS: The Gita's own signal (5.4-5) says only the immature call Samkhya and
> Yoga different, which is why Sankara, Ramanuja and Tilak could each claim the
> text. They converge in FUNCTION (dismantling egoic appropriation) and diverge in
> TERMINUS (identity vs communion). Say this rather than harmonising.

> BOOK: The nirguna strand (Kabir, Nanak) shows bhakti needs a PERSONAL focus, not
> an iconic form -- so the "bhakti requires a saguna God" objection is blunted for
> theists, though it still holds against strictly non-theistic systems.

Provenance: cite Gita 18.66 (carama-sloka), 9.22, 9.32; Bhagavata 7.5.23
(navadha bhakti, nine limbs in order). The Vivekacudamani definition of bhakti is
of DISPUTED ascription -- say "the Advaita tradition", never "Sankara wrote".

#### Resurrection vs Rebirth vs Reincarnation; Inter-School Debates, Criticisms and Traps

#### LAYER 3 - ADVANCED

> ANALYSIS: Two Western strands are wrongly merged. Immortality of the soul is
> Greek/Platonic (soul naturally deathless). Resurrection is Hebrew-Christian-
> Islamic: the person is a PSYCHOSOMATIC unity, so survival needs God to restore
> the whole person. Oscar Cullmann (1955) argues the two are theologically
> OPPOSED, not complementary -- a high-value citation.

> BOOK: Lynne Rudder Baker (Oxford Handbook, "Death and the Afterlife") lays out
> four candidate criteria of personal identity across death -- same soul, same
> body, same composite (soul + body), or continuity of memory -- and surveys the
> Christian reassembly tradition: Origen's "seed", Augustine's re-collected
> "statue", and Aquinas's soul-as-FORM securing bodily continuity.

> BOOK: Peter van Inwagen presses the re-assembly ("cannibal") problem -- if atoms
> are shared between bodies, whose are raised? -- and suggests God preserves a core
> of the body or substitutes a simulacrum. Bernard Williams's reduplication
> argument and H. H. Price's "image-world" of the next life both show how fragile
> the bodily and memory criteria are.

> ANALYSIS: John Hick's replica theory answers the identity problem: an exact
> psychophysical replica, continuous in memory and character, appearing in another
> space, is the same person for all purposes that matter. Objection: a replica is
> a duplicate, and God could make TWO; Hick restricts the case to a UNIQUE replica,
> but critics say the mere possibility of duplication shows the criterion is not
> identity.

> ANALYSIS: For rebirth the identity worry is the memory objection; reply by
> separating psychological memory from metaphysical continuity (latent samskaras/
> vasanas). The gap problem (resurrection) and the memory gap (rebirth) leave the
> identity question unresolved on BOTH sides -- for opposite reasons.

### FINAL CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES - Soul: Immortality, Rebirth and Liberation

#### A. The Six Survival-Terms (never merge)

- **Immortality** = the soul does not die with the body (Plato; Indian eternal self).
- **Survival** = something of me continues after death (the generic claim).
- **Rebirth** = karmically driven re-becoming; the WIDER, neutral term.
- **Reincarnation** = a SUBSTANTIAL soul re-enfleshed (Vedanta, Nyaya, Jain).
- **Resurrection** = one life; God re-constitutes the whole embodied person.
- **Liberation** = exit from the cycle; a FAMILY term (moksa / apavarga / kaivalya /
  nirvana / kevala).
- **Modal spine:** immortality of a substantial soul is SUFFICIENT for rebirth,
  not NECESSARY (Buddhism). What rebirth needs is a continuity-bearer.

#### B. Immortality - Plato and the West

- Four Phaedo proofs: **Opposites** (cyclical), **Recollection** (pre-existence), **Affinity** (simple, akin to the Forms), **Form of Life** (cannot admit death).
- Bridge: recollection/opposites give only PRE-existence, not survival.
- Critique: simplicity does not entail indestructibility; Kant's paralogism -- the
  soul is not a knowable substance. Simmias' harmony objection; Plato: soul rules body.
- Baker (Oxford Handbook): Plato = NATURAL immortality; Semitic = survival by divine ACT.

#### C. Rebirth, Karma, Samsara

- Karma = act + unseen potency (*adrsta/apurva*); **sancita / prarabdha / agami**.
- Rebirth = the field where fruits maturing beyond one life are discharged.
- Not fatalism: **purusakara** works within **daiva**. Subtle body carries *samskaras*.
- Verified verses: *Gita* 2.22 (garments), 9.21 (return from heaven).

#### D. Buddhist Continuity Without a Soul

- **anatman** + **pratityasamutpada**: rebirth = a causal STREAM, not a traveller.
- Models: flame-to-flame, milk-to-curd (continuity, not identity).
- "Neither the same nor wholly other" answers who-reaps-the-fruit.
- anatman is not nihilism. Quinn (Oxford Handbook): nirvana = ceasing-to-be of the
  individual self, contrasted with Christian union-with-God. 2018 = significance of
  the anti-theistic stand.

#### E. Liberation Parity Bench (seven destinations)

| Term | School | What ends | Consciousness |
|---|---|---|---|
| moksa | Advaita | avidya | non-dual bliss |
| moksa | Visistadvaita | karmic bondage | personal, communion |
| apavarga | Nyaya-Vaisesika | pain + its causes | NONE |
| kaivalya | Samkhya-Yoga | aviveka | pure, contentless |
| kevala/siddhatva | Jainism | karmic matter | omniscient |
| nirvana | Buddhism | craving, dukkha | no self stated |
| resurrection | Semitic | death | embodied, before God |

- **jivanmukti** (free while living; body on *prarabdha*) vs **videhamukti** (at death).
- Griffiths: Advaitic moksa "not acquired but acknowledged".

#### F. Vedanta - Advaita vs Visistadvaita

- Advaita: *atman* = Brahman; bondage = *avidya/adhyasa*; means = *jnana*;
  liberation = identity; individuality sublated (not annihilated).
- Visistadvaita: *jiva* = eternal mode of God; means = bhakti + prapatti, by grace;
  liberation = communion; jiva persists.
- The "real agent" problem is Advaita's to solve (conventional agency until insight).

#### G. Jain Jiva and Bondage Ladder

- *jiva* = eternal, conscious, body-sized; intrinsic **ananta-catustaya**.
- **asrava** (influx) -> **bandha** (bondage) -> **samvara** (stoppage) -> **nirjara** (shedding) -> **kevala/siddhatva** (perfected, rises to universe-top).
- Interaction problem: extension/embodiment are MODES of jiva during bondage (contested).
- Jain *kevala* = omniscience (NOT Samkhya's contentless *kaivalya*).

#### H. Cessation and Isolation - Apavarga, Jivanmukti, Kaivalya

- Nyaya **apavarga** = cessation of pain AND consciousness (adventitious consciousness).
- Advaita **jivanmukti** = knowledge frees now; *prarabdha* coasts the body (spun wheel).
- Samkhya **kaivalya** = isolation of *purusa*; **SK 62-63** = *prakrti* binds/releases, not *purusa*; **SK 67-68** potter's wheel + *videha-kaivalya*.
- **YS 1.24** = Isvara is a special *purusa*, not a creator (theism without creation).

#### I. Paths - Bhakti and the Three Means

- **Karma qualifies, bhakti receives, jnana removes** - three means of different kinds.
- Production vs removal model decides the DIRECT means; *saksat-sadhana* vs *arad-upakaraka*.
- Sankara rejects *jnana-karma-samuccaya*; Ramanuja accepts a graded one -> bhakti; Mimamsa = karma alone; *Gita* 5.4-5 = convergence (function, not terminus).
- **marjara** (grace alone) vs **markata** (grace + effort). *Vivekacudamani* ascription
  DISPUTED -> "the Advaita tradition".
- Verified: *Gita* 18.66, 9.22, 9.32; *Bhagavata* 7.5.23 (nine limbs).

#### J. Resurrection vs Rebirth vs Reincarnation

#### N. Fourteen Common Traps (one line each)

1. Do not equate immortality, resurrection and rebirth.
2. Buddhism denies permanent substance-self, not continuity.
3. Nyaya apavarga is not positive bliss.
4. Not all Indian liberation is merger with God.
5. The flame analogy illustrates, it does not prove.
6. In jivanmukti separate knowledge (frees) from prarabdha (sustains body).
7. Bhakti is not mere emotion; it shifts achievement -> reception.
8. Advaita makes bhakti preparatory, not rejected.
9. Do not say "all three means are equal"; name the disagreement.
10. Rebirth is not reincarnation (substance vs wider term).
11. Resurrection is not rebirth in heaven.
12. kaivalya (Samkhya isolation) is not kevala (Jain omniscience).
13. Yoga's Isvara (YS 1.24) is not a creator.
14. Samkhya's purusa is not bound/released -- prakrti is (SK 62-63).

#### O. Answer Spine (objection -> reply)

- memory gap -> memory is not metaphysical continuity; latent *samskaras*.
- duplication -> substance theory keeps one bearer; Buddhism drops numerical identity.
- moral desert -> karma explains distribution, does not license social blame.
- who seeks moksa? -> conventional agency operates until insight removes the error.
- replica = a copy? -> Hick restricts to a UNIQUE replica meeting our criteria.
- re-assembly -> Aquinas (soul = form) / van Inwagen (God keeps a core).
- resurrection = rebirth? -> no: one life, divine act, final judgement.
