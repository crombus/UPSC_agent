### 0A. VERSIONS, BURDENS AND REPLY-TYPES ⚠️

| Test | Question the examiner is really asking | Where the marks sit |
|---|---|---|
| Version | Which problem is on the table — logical, evidential, existential, horrendous, animal or distributional? | Naming the version in the first two lines |
| Burden | Must the theist show consistency, restore probability, or answer the sufferer? | Matching the reply to the burden |
| Reply-type | Is the reply a **defence** (possible reason), a **theodicy** (actual reason), a **revision** of the God-concept, or a **dissolution** that removes the defendant? | The distinction that separates a 60% script from a 70% script |
| Cost | What has the reply given up — omnipotence, personality, the creator, or moral confidence? | The closing verdict |
| Scope | Does the reply cover moral evil only, natural evil too, animal suffering, or the amount and distribution? | The residue paragraph |
| Ethics of reply | Does the answer respect lament and the duty to relieve suffering? | The qualification that prevents a callous conclusion |

**Burden rule:** the logical problem is defeated by showing a *possible* morally sufficient reason; the evidential problem is not, because possibility does not restore probability. A script that answers Mackie and thinks it has answered Rowe has skipped an entire burden.

⚠️ A theodicy is never refuted merely by having a cost. It is *measured* by its cost. State the cost explicitly and grade the reply against it.

### Origin and chronology of the debate

⚠️ A chronology is examinable here because the replies are historically ordered: each defence answers a specific earlier formulation, and scripts that float free of the sequence misattribute positions.

| Stage | Date | Development |
|---|---|---|
| The dilemma transmitted by Lactantius, *De Ira Dei* 13, traditionally ascribed to Epicurus | early 4th century CE, reporting a much older tradition | willing but not able, able but not willing, neither, or both — the classical shape of the question. ❓ No surviving Epicurean text contains it |
| Augustine, *Enchiridion* and *City of God* | late 4th–early 5th century | evil as privation of good (*privatio boni*); moral evil from defective will; the Fall; "all evil is either sin or punishment for sin" |
| Irenaeus' image/likeness theology | 2nd century, recovered later | humans made in God's *image* and required to grow into his *likeness* — the theological seed of soul-making |
| Leibniz, *Théodicée* | 1710 | the word *theodicy* itself; the best possible compossible world |
| John Keats' journal-letter to George and Georgiana Keats | spring 1819 | coins **"The vale of Soul-making"** in preference to "a vale of tears" |
| Whitehead, *Process and Reality* | 1929 | creativity as ultimate; the dipolar God; "the fellow-sufferer who understands" |
| Hartshorne, *The Divine Relativity* (1948) and *Omnipotence and Other Theological Mistakes* (1984) | 1948–1984 | classical omnipotence argued to be incoherent |
| Mackie, "Evil and Omnipotence", *Mind* | 1955 | the logical problem as a set of inconsistent propositions plus the paradox of omnipotence |
| Hick, *Evil and the God of Love* | 1966 | the Augustinian/Irenaean typology, epistemic distance, and the modern soul-making theodicy |
| Plantinga, *God, Freedom, and Evil* | 1974 | the free-will **defence**; transworld depravity establishes possibility, never actuality |
| Griffin, *God, Power, and Evil: A Process Theodicy* (1976) and *Evil Revisited* (1991) | 1976–1991 | persuasive power as metaphysical necessity, not divine self-limitation |
| Rowe, "The Problem of Evil and Some Varieties of Atheism" | 1979 | the evidential argument and the suffering fawn |
| Wykstra's CORNEA | 1984 | the epistemic-access condition that blocks the *noseeum* inference |
| Adams, *Horrendous Evils and the Goodness of God* | 1999 | goodness *to persons*; defeat within a life rather than global balancing off |

### SESSION 1 — WHICH PROBLEM IS BEING ASKED?

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** The problem of evil is the challenge of showing how the suffering we actually observe can be squared with a God who is at once all-powerful, all-knowing and perfectly good.

**Technical definition:** The problem is a family of distinct arguments — logical, evidential, existential and distributional — each of which places a different burden on the theist and is answered by a different type of reply.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> There is no single problem of evil: there is a logical charge of inconsistency, an evidential charge of improbability and an existential charge about the sufferer's own life, and a reply that answers one leaves the others standing.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **logical problem**
- **evidential problem**
- **existential and moral burden**
- **defence**
- **theodicy**
- **morally sufficient reason**

**How to use them:** Open by naming the version, state its burden, choose the reply-type designed for that burden, and close by saying what the reply leaves untouched.

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** A defence shows that God *could* have a reason; a theodicy claims to say what the reason *is*; only the second is refuted by showing the proposed reason to be false.

```text
   │ LOGICAL      Are the propositions strictly inconsistent?     │
```

⚠️ **This four-row grid is the single most examiner-pleasing device in the whole topic.** Almost every UPSC stem on this clause ("what is the logical problem", "critically examine", "explain the main tenets") is answered by first locating the version and then matching the reply.

##### 1. Three burdens, stated exactly

✅ The full classical profile is **omnipotent + omniscient + perfectly good + creator**, confronting **evil exists**. Omniscience is not one of the three propositions in Mackie's familiar set, but it blocks the reply that evil escaped divine notice.

| Burden | What must be shown | Who carries it |
|---|---|---|
| **Logical** | The set is inconsistent, or a possible morally sufficient reason restores compatibility | Mackie presses it; Plantinga answers it |
| **Evidential** | The observed amount, kinds or distribution of suffering lowers — or does not lower — the probability of God | Rowe presses it; theodicies and Wykstra answer it |
| **Existential / moral** | A response respects lament, agency and the sufferer rather than merely balancing a world-total | Adams and the anti-theodicy tradition press it |

⚠️ Plantinga's defence addresses the first burden only. It does not by itself explain the actual quantity of suffering or make the divine reason probable. Saying so is itself a mark-earning move.

##### 2. Defence versus theodicy — the distinction that separates bands

| | **Defence** | **Theodicy** |
|---|---|---|
| Claim | God *could* have a morally sufficient reason | This *is* God's morally sufficient reason |
| Burden discharged | Logical consistency | Explanatory adequacy |
| Success condition | Bare logical possibility suffices | The proposed reason must be plausible and true |
| Failure condition | Only if the story is impossible | If the proposed reason is false or insufficient |
| Standard instances | Plantinga's free-will defence; skeptical theism | Augustine, Hick, Leibniz, process theodicy |
| Examiner value | Explains why Plantinga needs no evidence for transworld depravity | Explains why every theodicy carries a stated cost |

> 🔑 **Mnemonic — "COULD versus IS":** a defence says God **could** have a reason; a theodicy says what the reason **is**.

##### 3. The ethics of replying at all ⚠️

✅ The canonical control is explicit: a causal explanation is never moral permission to withhold aid. Whatever its metaphysics, no reply to evil cancels the present duties of compassion, protection and relief.

- Protest or **anti-theodicy** refuses to justify suffering at all, on the ground that justification can corrupt truthfulness and displace resistance.
- The reply is that protest diagnoses the *ethics* of explanation but does not by itself settle the logical or evidential argument.
- ⚠️ The examiner rewards a script that states this without moralising: philosophy of religion asks whether the propositions are compatible; pastoral care asks what to say to a mourner. Confusing the two questions produces either a callous answer or an empty one.

##### 4. Why UPSC cares

✅ The syllabus phrase is a bare two words — *Problem of Evil* — and the examiner therefore tests whether the candidate can *impose* structure on it:

```text
NAME       →  the version of the problem the stem is about
RECONSTRUCT→  the set or the probability judgement as numbered steps
MATCH      →  the reply-type designed for that version
COST       →  what the reply gives up: omnipotence, personality, creator, moral confidence
RESIDUE    →  what it still leaves unexplained
```

##### UPSC traps

1. ⚠️ Do not treat the inconsistent triad as self-contradictory without stating the bridging premises.
2. ⚠️ Do not call Plantinga's defence a proof of God's actual reason; it establishes possibility only.
3. ⚠️ Do not answer an evidential stem with a purely logical reply — the burden has changed.
4. ⚠️ Do not present a theodicy as refuted merely because it has a cost; state the cost and grade it.
5. ⚠️ Do not moralise in place of arguing, or argue as though the sufferer were absent; the topic owns both burdens.
6. ⚠️ Do not begin an answer with a definition of God; begin with the version of the problem, then the God-concept it presupposes.

##### Revision notes

- The problem of evil is a family of arguments, not one argument.
- The logical version alleges inconsistency; the evidential version alleges improbability.
- The existential version asks whether a particular life can be a great good to the person living it.
- The distributional version asks why *this* sufferer, and is India's structural strength.
- A defence needs only possibility; a theodicy claims actuality and therefore carries risk.
- Omniscience blocks the "God did not notice" reply even though it is not in Mackie's three propositions.
- Every reply must be graded by its cost as well as its coverage.
- Causal explanation is never moral permission to withhold help.
- Version-matching in the first two lines is the cheapest mark in the topic.
- The clause is examined once in every paper from 2019 to 2025, so structure is worth more than novelty.

### SESSION 2 — KINDS OF EVIL AND THE CLASSICAL DILEMMA

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** Evil in this debate means suffering and wrongdoing of any kind, divided by its source into what free agents do and what nature does.

**Technical definition:** **Moral evil** is harm traceable to the misuse of creaturely freedom; **natural evil** is suffering not caused by any will; **horrendous evil** is a technically defined subset that threatens the meaning of a whole life; **animal evil** is pre-human and pre-moral pain.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> The classification is not decorative: each theodicy was built for a particular kind of evil, so a script that does not sort the evil first cannot match a reply to it afterwards.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **moral evil**
- **natural evil**
- **horrendous evil**
- **animal and pre-human suffering**
- **the Lactantius-transmitted dilemma**
- **quantity and distribution**

**How to use them:** Sort the evil in the stem, then state which replies survive that sorting and which are immediately out of scope.

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** Free will explains what agents do; it does not explain earthquakes, and it explains nothing at all about a fawn burned in a forest fire.

⚠️ **Sorting first is worth marks.** The 2023 stem is about moral evil and punishment; the 2025 stem is about a theodicy that covers both kinds; the animal case is the standing residue in every 20-marker.

##### 1. Moral and natural evil ✅

| | Moral evil | Natural evil |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | Harm arising from human wrongdoing | Suffering not caused by any will |
| Standard examples | Cruelty, injustice, betrayal, oppression | Earthquakes, disease, congenital disorder |
| Free-will defence | ✅ Direct application | ⚠️ No direct application |
| Augustinian account | ✅ Defective will; sin | ⚠️ Appeal to a fallen created order — ❓ historically and scientifically strained |
| Soul-making | ✅ Growth through freely chosen response | ✅ The law-governed environment in which growth is possible |
| Process account | ✅ Finite agency deviating from the lure | ✅ Finite causal powers, since power is intrinsically distributed |
| Karma | ✅ Moral causation of deeds | ✅ Explanation extended across lives, including animal births |

##### 2. The dilemma as it is actually transmitted ❓

✅ **Attribution discipline.** The famous "if God is willing but not able… able but not willing…" dilemma is **traditionally ascribed to Epicurus but transmitted through Lactantius, *De Ira Dei* 13**. ❓ No surviving Epicurean text contains it.

**Safe formulation for the exam:** *"the dilemma transmitted by Lactantius and traditionally ascribed to Epicurus."*

```text
   IS GOD WILLING TO PREVENT EVIL BUT NOT ABLE?          →  then not omnipotent
   IS GOD ABLE BUT NOT WILLING?                          →  then not perfectly good
   IS GOD NEITHER ABLE NOR WILLING?                      →  then why call this God?
   IS GOD BOTH ABLE AND WILLING?                         →  then whence evil?
```

⚠️ The dilemma is rhetorically powerful and logically incomplete: it presupposes exactly the bridging premises that Session 3 makes explicit. Quoting it without those premises is what separates a lament from an argument.

##### 3. Quantity, kind and distribution ⚠️

Three separate complaints hide inside "there is too much evil":

- **Amount:** even granting that some hardship is needed, the scale seems unnecessary. **Reply:** epistemic limits and long-range goods. **Rejoinder:** unknown reasons cannot become a blanket licence.
- **Kind:** certain evils — horrendous and animal — appear to serve no developmental function at all.
- **Distribution:** why *this* person? ⚠️ This is the question on which Western theodicy is weakest and the karma frameworks are structurally strongest, because rebirth widens the temporal field of desert.

##### 4. Why UPSC cares

✅ The examiner uses the classification to test scope discipline. A candidate who writes "free will explains evil" without qualification has silently claimed to explain earthquakes, congenital disease and animal predation — and has invited the single most common rejoinder in the topic.

##### UPSC traps

1. ⚠️ Do not use "horrendous evil" loosely for "very bad evil"; Adams' sense is technical.
2. ⚠️ Do not attribute the four-horn dilemma to a surviving Epicurean text; route it through Lactantius.
3. ⚠️ Do not treat natural evil as a subspecies of moral evil without an argument; that is the Augustinian move and it needs defending.
4. ⚠️ Do not answer animal suffering with the free-will defence — it does not apply, and saying so earns marks.
5. ⚠️ Do not collapse amount, kind and distribution into one complaint; they have different replies.
6. ⚠️ Do not describe graphic detail; the philosophical distinction needs no illustration of that sort.

##### Revision notes

- Moral evil comes from misused freedom; natural evil comes from law-governed nature.
- Horrendous evil is technically defined by its threat to a whole life's goodness for the person living it.
- Animal and pre-human suffering predates moral agency and defeats both free will and soul-making for the sufferer.
- The four-horn dilemma reaches us through Lactantius, *De Ira Dei* 13; ascription to Epicurus is traditional, not textual.
- The dilemma presupposes bridging premises it does not state.
- Amount, kind and distribution are three complaints, not one.
- Distribution is the structural advantage of the karma frameworks.
- Every theodicy must be scored against the kinds of evil it actually covers.
- Sorting the evil in the first paragraph is what makes the rest of the answer matchable.
- The 2023 stem is about moral evil; the animal residue belongs in the critical section, not the exposition.

### SESSION 3 — MACKIE'S LOGICAL PROBLEM AND THE BRIDGING PREMISES

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** The logical problem claims that believing in an all-powerful, wholly good God while admitting that evil exists is not merely odd but strictly self-contradictory.

**Technical definition:** ✅ **J. L. Mackie**, "Evil and Omnipotence", *Mind* (1955), argues that the propositions *God is omnipotent*, *God is perfectly good* and *evil exists* form an inconsistent set — but only once two additional principles are supplied.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> The three famous propositions are not formally contradictory by themselves; the contradiction is manufactured by two bridging principles, and the entire logical debate is a fight over those two principles.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **inconsistent set**
- **bridging premise**
- **quasi-logical rules**
- **adequate and fallacious solutions**
- **morally sufficient reason**
- **formal contradiction**

**How to use them:** Write the propositions as a numbered set, add the bridges explicitly, then show that denying either bridge dissolves the contradiction — and say what that denial costs.

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** The set becomes inconsistent only with the bridging premises; deny either and the contradiction dissolves — but the theist then owes an account of what a good, omnipotent being *would* tolerate, which is exactly where the evidential problem restarts.

```text
   THE SET AS USUALLY PRINTED               THE SET AS IT MUST BE WRITTEN
   ──────────────────────────               ─────────────────────────────
   (1) God is omnipotent                    (1) God is omnipotent
   (2) God is perfectly good                (2) God is omniscient
   (3) Evil exists                          (3) God is perfectly good
                                            (4) Evil exists
   ↳ NOT a formal contradiction              + BRIDGE A: a perfectly good being
     — nothing in (1)–(3) entails             eliminates every evil it can,
     the negation of any other                unless it has a morally
                                              sufficient reason
                                            + BRIDGE B: an omnipotent being has
                                              no non-logical limit on what it
                                              can prevent
                                            ↳ NOW the set is inconsistent
   ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   THE THEIST'S THREE EXITS
   deny BRIDGE A  → there IS a morally sufficient reason (defence/theodicy)
   deny BRIDGE B  → omnipotence is limited (process, finite God)
   deny (4)'s force → evil is privation / empirical-level only (Augustine, Advaita)
```

##### 1. Mackie's own frame ✅

- Mackie presents the theologian as facing a set of beliefs that cannot all be true, and sorts the standard replies into **adequate solutions** — which abandon one of the beliefs — and **fallacious solutions**, which appear to keep all three but smuggle in an abandonment.
- ✅ Examples of what Mackie calls fallacious: "good cannot exist without evil", "evil is necessary as a means to good", "the universe is better with some evil", and "evil is due to human free will" — each, he argues, either limits omnipotence or redefines goodness.
- ⚠️ The examiner does not require Mackie's full taxonomy, but a script that names the adequate/fallacious framing shows it has read the argument rather than the slogan.

##### 2. Why the bare triad is not a contradiction ✅

A formal contradiction requires that the propositions, plus logic alone, entail both *p* and *not-p*. Nothing in "God is omnipotent", "God is perfectly good" and "evil exists" does that. What supplies the entailment are two *quasi-logical* rules about goodness and power:

| Bridge | Statement | How it is attacked |
|---|---|---|
| **A — goodness** | A perfectly good being eliminates evil as far as it can | Insert "unless it has a morally sufficient reason for permitting it" — the whole space of defences and theodicies |
| **B — power** | An omnipotent being can eliminate every evil whose prevention is logically possible | Deny that free creatures who always freely choose good are within God's power to actualise (Plantinga); or deny classical omnipotence outright (Hartshorne, Griffin) |

⚠️ The logically possible/logically impossible boundary matters: almost every theist accepts that omnipotence does not extend to the self-contradictory. The dispute is over whether *a world of significantly free agents who never go wrong* falls inside or outside that boundary.

##### 3. What answering the logical problem does and does not achieve ⚠️

```text
   SHOW a POSSIBLE morally sufficient reason
        → the SET is consistent
        → the logical problem is DEFEATED
        → and NOTHING follows about how much suffering is likely
   The atheist then reformulates:
        "granted it is possible — but is it PROBABLE?"
        → Rowe 1979, Session 8
```

⚠️ This is the highest-value sentence available in the 2020 question: the logical problem is now widely regarded as answered, and the live debate has migrated to the evidential form. Stating the migration is worth more than repeating the triad a second time.

##### 4. Why UPSC cares

✅ The 2020 stem is exactly *"What is the logical problem of evil? Discuss."* The marks are in the **bridging premises** and in the distinction between formal inconsistency and evidential improbability. A script that writes the three propositions, declares them contradictory and moves to free will has conceded the analytical core of the question.

##### UPSC traps

1. ⚠️ Do not assert contradiction without the bridges; that is the single commonest failure on the 2020 stem.
2. ⚠️ Do not attribute the logical problem to Rowe or the evidential problem to Mackie.
3. ⚠️ Do not say Mackie "proved God does not exist"; he argued a set of beliefs is inconsistent.
4. ⚠️ Do not treat "omnipotence includes the logically impossible" as the standard theist position; almost no one holds it.
5. ⚠️ Do not omit omniscience; without it the theist can reply that God did not know.
6. ⚠️ Do not finish without noting the migration to the evidential problem.

##### Revision notes

- Mackie, "Evil and Omnipotence", *Mind*, 1955, is the canonical statement.
- The three propositions alone are not formally contradictory.
- Bridge A: a good being eliminates evil as far as it can. Bridge B: omnipotence has no relevant limit.
- Mackie sorts replies into adequate solutions and fallacious solutions.
- Inserting "unless there is a morally sufficient reason" is the theist's principal move.
- Omniscience blocks the ignorance reply and belongs in the full profile.
- Answering the logical problem establishes consistency only, never probability.
- Denying Bridge B costs classical omnipotence — the process route.
- Denying the reality of evil costs the seriousness of suffering — the privation and Advaita routes.
- The live philosophical debate has migrated to the evidential problem; say so.

### SESSION 4 — THE PARADOX OF OMNIPOTENCE

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** The paradox of omnipotence asks whether an all-powerful being can create something it cannot afterwards control — and points out that either answer seems to limit its power.

**Technical definition:** ✅ Mackie's *Mind* (1955) formulation distinguishes **first-order omnipotence** (unlimited power to act on things) from **second-order omnipotence** (unlimited power to determine what powers exist), and argues that they cannot both be unrestricted.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> The paradox is not a word-game about stones: it asks whether God's sovereignty is the power to control everything or the power to create genuinely uncontrollable things — and the free-will defence needs the second at the price of the first.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **first-order and second-order omnipotence**
- **self-binding**
- **logically possible**
- **sovereignty**
- **significant freedom**
- **restriction that dissolves the paradox**

**How to use them:** Write the paradox as a dilemma with both horns and both costs, then state the restriction that dissolves it and what that restriction concedes.

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** If God cannot make beings he cannot control, his second-order power is limited; if he can and does, his first-order power over them is limited — so unrestricted omnipotence in both senses is incoherent, and theism must choose which sense it means.

```text
   CAN AN OMNIPOTENT BEING CREATE A THING IT CANNOT AFTERWARDS CONTROL?
                              │
        ┌─────────────────────┴─────────────────────┐
       YES                                         NO
        │                                           │
   then there is now something                 then there is something
   it CANNOT do — control that thing           it CANNOT do — make such a thing
        │                                           │
   FIRST-ORDER power limited                   SECOND-ORDER power limited
        └─────────────────────┬─────────────────────┘
                              │
   MACKIE: unrestricted omnipotence in BOTH senses is incoherent
                              │
   STANDARD RESTRICTIONS THAT DISSOLVE IT
   (a) omnipotence = power over the LOGICALLY POSSIBLE only
   (b) omnipotence = second-order sovereignty, freely SELF-BOUND in creation
   (c) omnipotence is abandoned as a classical attribute (process theodicy)
                              │
   LINK TO EVIL: the free-will defence REQUIRES restriction (a) or (b) —
   God creates significantly free agents and cannot causally guarantee
   that they always choose well.  That is a chosen self-limitation
   for Plantinga, and a METAPHYSICAL NECESSITY for Griffin.
```

##### 1. The two orders of power ✅

| Order | What it covers | If unrestricted | If restricted |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First-order** | Power to act on and control existing things | God can override every creaturely choice | Creatures possess genuine, uncoerced agency |
| **Second-order** | Power to determine what powers exist at all | God can create beings with irrevocable independence | God cannot make genuinely independent beings |

⚠️ The paradox bites because theism traditionally wants both. The resolution always sacrifices something: either the scope of omnipotence, or the reality of creaturely freedom.

##### 2. Three standard restrictions and their costs ⚠️

- **Logical-possibility restriction:** omnipotence extends only to what is logically possible; a self-contradictory task names no task at all. **Cost:** modest, and almost universally accepted — but it does not by itself settle whether "free agents who never go wrong" is logically possible for God to actualise.
- **Self-binding sovereignty:** God's second-order power is exercised *in* creating free agents, and the resulting first-order limitation is chosen, not imposed. **Cost:** it makes divine restraint voluntary, which re-opens the question why God does not intervene in the worst cases.
- **Abandonment:** process theism denies classical omnipotence outright — power is intrinsically distributed and God's power is persuasive by metaphysical necessity. **Cost:** a God who cannot guarantee any final victory over evil.

##### 3. Why the paradox belongs in an evil answer ✅

The 2021 stem is *"Discuss the paradox of omnipotence of God in the context of the existence of evil."* The connection is precise:

```text
   IF omnipotence is unrestricted in the FIRST-ORDER sense
        → God could prevent every moral evil without loss
        → the free-will defence FAILS
   IF omnipotence is restricted by genuine creaturely freedom
        → the free-will defence WORKS
        → but "omnipotent" now means something the objector may refuse to grant
   THEREFORE: the paradox is the hinge on which the free-will defence turns.
```

⚠️ Route the *independent definition and coherence* of omnipotence to Notions of God; this file owns the attribute only insofar as evil places it under pressure.

##### 4. Why UPSC cares

✅ The examiner wants a paradox stated as a dilemma with both horns, both costs, and then the restriction that dissolves it. A script that reduces the question to "can God make a stone too heavy to lift?" and stops has produced a puzzle, not an analysis, and has not connected the paradox to evil at all.

##### UPSC traps

1. ⚠️ Do not present the paradox only in the stone form; use the first-order/second-order distinction.
2. ⚠️ Do not claim the paradox disproves God; it shows that unrestricted omnipotence in both senses is incoherent.
3. ⚠️ Do not forget the connection to evil — the 2021 stem explicitly demands it.
4. ⚠️ Do not conflate Plantinga's *chosen* restraint with Griffin's *metaphysically necessary* limitation.
5. ⚠️ Do not drift into the general coherence of omnipotence; that belongs to Notions of God.
6. ⚠️ Do not treat "omnipotence covers the logically impossible" as a serious theistic option.

##### Revision notes

- Mackie's paradox appears in the same 1955 *Mind* article as the logical problem.
- First-order omnipotence is power over things; second-order omnipotence is power over what powers exist.
- Both cannot be unrestricted; the dilemma has a cost on each horn.
- The logical-possibility restriction is standard and cheap but not by itself decisive.
- Self-binding sovereignty makes restraint voluntary and re-opens the intervention question.
- Process theism abandons classical omnipotence entirely.
- The free-will defence presupposes some restriction; that is why the paradox belongs here.
- Plantinga: God *chooses* not to override. Griffin: God *cannot* coerce.
- The attribute's independent coherence belongs to Notions of God.
- Answer 2021 as a two-horned dilemma plus a dissolving restriction, explicitly tied to evil.

### SESSION 5 — PLANTINGA'S FREE-WILL DEFENCE AND DIVINE RESPONSIBILITY

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** The free-will defence says that a world containing genuinely free people who sometimes choose wrongly is better than a world of guaranteed good behaviour, so God can be good and still permit moral evil.

**Technical definition:** ✅ **Alvin Plantinga**, *God, Freedom, and Evil* (1974), argues that significant creaturely freedom is a great good whose possibility includes morally wrong choice, so it is **possible** that God could not actualise a world containing moral good without also containing moral evil.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Plantinga's defence does not tell us why anyone actually suffers; it shows only that the theist's beliefs are not self-contradictory — and that modest claim is exactly what the logical problem required.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **significant freedom**
- **libertarian freedom**
- **transworld depravity**
- **defence, not theodicy**
- **moral good and moral evil**
- **divine responsibility**

**How to use them:** Reconstruct the defence as numbered premises, state that it needs only possibility, answer the "why not always-good free agents" objection with transworld depravity, and then state the residue: natural evil.

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** A world of programmed beneficence lacks the moral authorship of a world containing genuinely responsible agents, so significant freedom is a good whose price is the possibility of its misuse.

```text
   PLANTINGA'S FREE-WILL DEFENCE — AS PREMISES
   (1) A world with significantly free creatures is more valuable, other
       things equal, than a world with no free creatures at all.
   (2) Significant freedom entails the genuine availability of alternatives:
       an agent free with respect to an action can perform it or refrain.
   (3) God cannot CAUSALLY DETERMINE a creature to freely do only what is
       right — that description is self-cancelling.
   (4) Therefore God can create free persons only by accepting the
       POSSIBILITY that some will choose wrongly.
   (5) Therefore moral evil is COMPATIBLE with divine omnipotence and
       perfect goodness.
                              │
   OBJECTION (Mackie): why not create free agents who ALWAYS freely
   choose the good?  That is logically possible, so omnipotence covers it.
                              │
   REPLY (transworld depravity): it is POSSIBLE that every creaturely
   essence God could instantiate would go wrong in some circumstance.
   If so, a world of free agents who never go wrong was not available
   to God to actualise.
                              │
   STATUS OF THE REPLY: POSSIBILITY ONLY.  Plantinga never claims
   transworld depravity is ACTUAL — and does not need to.
                              │
   RESIDUE: natural evil.  The defence is silent about earthquakes
   unless it is extended by a further hypothesis.
```

##### 1. What "defence" means here ✅

- The defence's target is Mackie's **logical** claim, and its success condition is bare logical possibility. That is why Plantinga is entitled to a hypothesis he does not assert to be true.
- ✅ Contrast: a theodicy claims to identify God's *actual* morally sufficient reason and is therefore refuted if the proposed reason is false.
- ⚠️ Presupposition to state: libertarian freedom must be coherent and sufficiently valuable. A compatibilist who denies premise (3) rejects the defence at its root; naming that is a mark-earning move.

##### 2. Transworld depravity, stated carefully ✅

⚠️ The technical claim is that a creaturely essence suffers **transworld depravity** if, whatever circumstances God places it in where it acts freely, it goes wrong at least once. Plantinga's claim is that it is *possible* that every essence God could instantiate suffers from it.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Does Plantinga assert it is actual? | ❌ No. Possibility suffices for a defence |
| Does it explain the amount of suffering? | ❌ No |
| Does it answer the evidential argument? | ❌ No; that is Session 8 |
| What does it do? | ✅ Blocks Mackie's "God could have made only-good free agents" reply |

##### 3. The 2022 stem: absolute creation and human responsibility ⚠️

The 2022 question runs: *"If God is the Absolute Creator, then the responsibility of the evil cannot belong to the human agent. Critically examine."*

```text
   THE STEM'S ARGUMENT                       THE REPLIES
   God creates everything, including     (a) FREE-WILL DEFENCE: God creates the
   the agent, its powers and the             CAPACITY, the agent supplies the
   circumstances of its choice               ACT; authorship of the act is the
        │                                    agent's
   therefore every act traces back       (b) PRIVATION: God creates natures,
   to the creator                            not the defect in them; evil is a
        │                                    lack, not a created substance
   therefore human responsibility        (c) FOREKNOWLEDGE ≠ CAUSATION: knowing
   is an illusion                            what will freely occur does not
                                             make it occur
                                         (d) PROCESS: God does not have the
                                             unilateral power the stem assumes
   ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   REJOINDER RETAINED: ⚠️ knowingly choosing to instantiate the whole
   system, foreseeing its outcomes, remains morally relevant even if it
   is not causation.  A first-class script concedes this and grades it.
```

⚠️ **Scope check first.** The stem is about **moral** evil and creaturely responsibility. Natural evil is relevant only as the residue that the free-will reply cannot reach.

##### 4. Why UPSC cares

✅ The examiner is testing whether the candidate knows what a defence is *for*. The commonest error is to present transworld depravity as a discovery about human nature and then to attack it as empirically unsupported — an objection that misses the modal point entirely.

##### UPSC traps

1. ⚠️ Do not call the free-will defence a theodicy; Plantinga explicitly offers a defence.
2. ⚠️ Do not present transworld depravity as an actual fact about people.
3. ⚠️ Do not use the defence against the evidential problem; the burden there is probability.
4. ⚠️ Do not omit natural evil as the acknowledged residue.
5. ⚠️ Do not merge Augustine's theological free-will account with Plantinga's modal argument; they belong to different centuries and different projects.
6. ⚠️ Do not answer the 2022 stem with theology; it is a question about agency, authorship and responsibility.

##### Revision notes

- *God, Freedom, and Evil* (1974) is the canonical source.
- The defence answers Mackie's logical charge and needs only possibility.
- Significant freedom entails genuinely available alternatives.
- God cannot causally determine a creature to act freely; the description cancels itself.
- Transworld depravity is a possibility claim, never an actuality claim.
- The defence is strongest for moral evil and silent about natural evil.
- Libertarian freedom is the presupposition a compatibilist will deny.
- The 2022 stem is about authorship: God creates the capacity, the agent supplies the act.
- Foreknowledge does not cause free action, but knowingly instantiating the system remains morally relevant.
- A world of programmed beneficence lacks moral authorship — that is the sentence to write.

### SESSION 6 — AUGUSTINE: PRIVATION, DEFECTIVE WILL, SIN AND PUNISHMENT

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** Augustine argues that evil is not a thing God made but a hole in something good — a failure of what ought to be there — and that human wrongdoing and its consequences explain the rest.

**Technical definition:** ✅ Evil is **privation of a due good (*privatio boni*)**, not a positive substance; culpable evil originates in **defective will**; and the printed 2023 thesis is that *"all evil is either sin or the punishment for sin."*

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Augustine's achievement is metaphysical rather than pastoral: he shows how evil can be real without being created, but he does not thereby show why a good creator permitted the conditions in which the privation occurs.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **privation of good (*privatio boni*)**
- **defective will**
- **parasitic on good**
- **the Fall**
- **sin and punishment for sin**
- **convertibility of being and goodness**

**How to use them:** Reconstruct the metaphysics before objecting to it — the 2023 directive requires the author's own system first.

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** Calling blindness a privation does not call it unreal: it is a real lack in a subject that ought to possess sight, and the reality of the lack is precisely what makes it evil.

```text
   AUGUSTINE'S ARGUMENT — AS PREMISES
   (1) Being, insofar as it is being, is good.
   (2) God creates beings; God does not create evil substances.
   (3) Corruption is parasitic: it depends on a good nature to corrupt.
   (4) Moral fault is DISORDERED WILLING — the will turning from a
       greater good to a lesser one.
   (5) Therefore evil is PRIVATION, not a rival creative principle.
                              │
   THE 2023 THESIS: "all evil is either sin or the punishment for sin"
   ┌────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────┐
   │ MORAL EVIL = SIN               │ NATURAL EVIL = PUNISHMENT      │
   │ misuse of created freedom      │ a fallen, disordered order     │
   │ the will's own defect          │ consequent on the Fall         │
   └────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────┘
                              │
   CANONICAL EXAMPLE: rot is not a rival substance to wood;
   it is the loss of the wood's proper integrity.
                              │
   OBJECTIONS THAT MUST BE STATED
   ⚠️ innocent suffering and animal pain resist a punishment reading
   ⚠️ "privation" is metaphysically strained for intense positive pain
   ⚠️ inherited guilt is morally harder to defend than inherited condition
   ⚠️ ❓ a fallen natural order is historically and scientifically strained
```

##### 1. Privation stated so that it is not a denial of suffering ✅

⚠️ **The commonest misreading is that privation makes pain imaginary.** It does not. A privation is a *real absence of a good that ought to be present in this subject*. Blindness is not nothing; it is a determinate lack in a creature whose nature calls for sight.

| Claim | What it establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Evil is privation | Evil has no independent creative source; dualism is avoided | That any particular privation was justified |
| Being and goodness are convertible | God's creation is wholly good as created | That the created order is presently undamaged |
| Corruption is parasitic | Evil presupposes and depends on good | That the parasite is harmless or unreal |

##### 2. The inherited-guilt control ⚠️

✅ Two readings of inherited fallenness must be separated:

- **Personal inherited guilt:** later persons are personally culpable for an ancestor's act. ⚠️ Morally the harder claim to defend.
- **Inherited damaged condition:** later persons inherit a disordered condition and a disordered social-natural order without personal guilt. ⚠️ This reduces the inherited-guilt objection — but it still owes an account of why a good creator permits the condition and its unequal effects.

⚠️ A first-class 20-mark script names both readings and shows that the second is the defensible one *and* that it does not close the question.

##### 3. Where the account is strongest and weakest ⚠️

```text
   STRONGEST                                WEAKEST
   ─────────                                ───────
   metaphysical economy: no evil            innocent suffering: infants,
   principle, no dualism                    congenital disease
   moral evil: defective will               animal pain, pre-human suffering
   preserves the creator's goodness         a "fallen order" preceding humanity
   explains dependence of evil on good      the retributive framing of natural evil
                              │
   CONTRAST TO DEPLOY: Hick's soul-making replaces PUNISHMENT with
   DEVELOPMENT; Indian karma replaces a single Fall with a
   beginningless series that answers DISTRIBUTION.
```

##### 4. Why UPSC cares

✅ The 2023 stem is a quotation with the directive *Critically discuss*, so the examiner is buying (i) faithful reconstruction of Augustine's own system, then (ii) evaluation. ❓ Treat the printed sentence as the object of analysis rather than as a verified verbatim translation; the safest formulation attributes the doctrine to Augustine and analyses the printed stem as given.

##### UPSC traps

1. ⚠️ Do not say privation makes pain imaginary.
2. ⚠️ Do not object before reconstructing; the directive requires the system first.
3. ⚠️ Do not present the printed sentence as a certified verbatim translation.
4. ⚠️ Do not treat inherited guilt and inherited condition as the same claim.
5. ⚠️ Do not forget that a fallen order preceding human history is ❓ historically and scientifically strained.
6. ⚠️ Do not conclude that Augustine simply fails; conclude that he preserves God's goodness for moral evil while remaining incomplete for natural and innocent suffering.

##### Revision notes

- Sources: Augustine, *Enchiridion* and *City of God*.
- Evil is privation of a due good, not a created substance.
- Being and goodness are convertible; corruption is parasitic on a good nature.
- Moral fault is disordered willing — turning from a greater good to a lesser.
- The 2023 thesis divides evil into sin and punishment for sin.
- Rot in wood is the canonical example of parasitic privation.
- Privation is a *real* lack, not an illusion of suffering.
- Inherited condition is defensible; inherited personal guilt is not.
- Innocent and animal suffering are the standing objections.
- Verdict: preserves divine goodness for moral evil; incomplete for natural and innocent suffering.

### SESSION 7 — HICK'S IRENAEAN SOUL-MAKING

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** Soul-making theodicy says the world is not meant to be a comfortable place but a school: God creates people unfinished, at a distance from himself, in a world with real risk, so that they can grow into genuine goodness.

**Technical definition:** ✅ **John Hick**, *Evil and the God of Love* (1966), develops the **Irenaean** line — humans are created in God's *image* and must grow into his *likeness* — through **epistemic distance**, genuine freedom and a stable, law-governed order.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Ready-made virtue is not achieved virtue: courage requires danger and compassion requires another's need, so a world engineered to prevent all suffering would also prevent the goods that make persons.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **soul-making**
- **image and likeness (Irenaeus)**
- **epistemic distance**
- **law-governed world**
- **vale of Soul-making (Keats)**
- **eschatological fulfilment**

**How to use them:** State the developmental rationale, distinguish it from punishment, add epistemic distance and nomic regularity, then price the eschatological reply.

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** Soul-making is developmental, not retributive: suffering is not deserved payment but the environment in which freely achieved virtue becomes possible.

```text
   HICK'S ARGUMENT — AS PREMISES
   (1) Ready-made virtue is not ACHIEVED virtue.
   (2) Courage, compassion and trust require real difficulty,
       real need and real risk.
   (3) A world in which such virtues are possible must be
       LAW-GOVERNED and must therefore permit harmful consequences.
   (4) Humans are created IMMATURE, at an EPISTEMIC DISTANCE from
       God, so that faith and love are freely reached, not compelled.
   (5) Therefore suffering can be the ENVIRONMENT of soul-making.
                              │
   IRENAEAN vs AUGUSTINIAN TYPOLOGY (Hick's own division)
   ┌──────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────┐
   │ AUGUSTINIAN              │ IRENAEAN                     │
   │ humanity created perfect │ humanity created IMMATURE    │
   │ FALL from that state     │ GROWTH toward likeness       │
   │ evil = privation + sin   │ evil = the environment of    │
   │ backward-looking         │        development           │
   │ retributive framing      │ forward-looking, teleological│
   └──────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────┘
                              │
   ATTRIBUTION DISCIPLINE ✅
   "The vale of Soul-making" is JOHN KEATS'S phrase, from his
   journal-letter to George and Georgiana Keats, spring 1819,
   where he prefers it to "a vale of tears".
   → IRENAEUS supplies the THEOLOGY (image/likeness)
   → KEATS supplies the PHRASE
   → HICK supplies the modern THEODICY
   Do NOT attribute the phrase to Irenaeus or call it Hick's coinage.
                              │
   OBJECTIONS
   ⚠️ scale and proportionality: much suffering builds nothing
   ⚠️ animal suffering: the animal does not morally grow
   ⚠️ the reply is ESCHATOLOGICAL — universal fulfilment — and
      therefore depends on contested afterlife commitments
```

##### 1. Epistemic distance, stated precisely ✅

⚠️ Epistemic distance is not divine hiding for its own sake. It is the claim that if God's reality were overwhelmingly evident, response would be compelled rather than freely given, and the relationship sought would be impossible. A world that is *religiously ambiguous* is therefore a condition of free faith.

⚠️ **Boundary note:** the wider question of divine hiddenness as an independent argument, and the epistemology of faith, belong to *Reason, Revelation and Faith*. Here epistemic distance appears only as a structural component of Hick's theodicy.

##### 2. Why the world must be law-governed ⚠️

```text
   IF nature were adjusted ad hoc to prevent every harm
        → no reliable consequences
        → no genuine deliberation, planning or responsibility
        → no moral agency at all
   THEREFORE a stable nomic order is a condition of agency,
   and such an order will sometimes injure.
                              │
   COST: this explains the KIND of world, not the AMOUNT of pain in it.
   Session 8 and Session 11 press exactly that gap.
```

##### 3. Coverage and cost ⚠️

| Feature | Soul-making |
|---|---|
| Moral evil | ✅ Growth through freely chosen response |
| Natural evil | ✅ The environment of growth — its principal advantage over the free-will defence |
| Animal suffering | ⚠️ Weak: instrumentalises animals or leaves them out |
| Horrendous evil | ⚠️ Weak: some suffering destroys rather than builds persons |
| Attribute preserved | ✅ Purposive goodness |
| Chief cost | ⚠️ Proportionality, and dependence on a universal eschatological fulfilment |

##### 4. Why UPSC cares

✅ Soul-making is the theodicy most often reduced to a slogan. The canonical trap is explicit: do not reduce Hick to "suffering makes us stronger". The marks are in **epistemic distance**, **freedom**, and the **law-governed order** — the three components that convert a platitude into a theodicy.

##### UPSC traps

1. ⚠️ Do not reduce Hick to "suffering makes us stronger".
2. ⚠️ Do not attribute "vale of Soul-making" to Irenaeus or present it as Hick's coinage.
3. ⚠️ Do not confuse the developmental rationale with retributive punishment.
4. ⚠️ Do not omit epistemic distance; it is what makes the freedom genuine.
5. ⚠️ Do not present the eschatological reply as uncontroversial; it depends on contested afterlife commitments.
6. ⚠️ Do not claim soul-making solves animal suffering; it instrumentalises it at best.

##### Revision notes

- *Evil and the God of Love* (1966) is the source of "epistemic distance" and the Irenaean/Augustinian typology.
- Irenaeus supplies the image/likeness theology of growth.
- Keats coined "The vale of Soul-making" in the spring 1819 journal-letter; Hick adopted it.
- Ready-made virtue is not achieved virtue.
- Courage requires danger; compassion requires another's need.
- A law-governed world is a condition of agency and will sometimes injure.
- The theodicy is developmental and forward-looking, not retributive.
- It covers natural evil better than the free-will defence does.
- Its costs are proportionality, animal suffering and dependence on universal fulfilment.
- Hick's typology is itself examinable: Augustinian looks back to a Fall, Irenaean looks forward to a likeness.

### SESSION 8 — ROWE'S EVIDENTIAL PROBLEM AND GRATUITOUS EVIL

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** The evidential problem grants that God and evil are not strictly contradictory, and argues instead that the sheer amount and pointlessness of suffering makes God's existence unlikely.

**Technical definition:** ✅ **William Rowe**, "The Problem of Evil and Some Varieties of Atheism" (1979), argues from instances of intense suffering for which no justifying good is discernible to the conclusion that gratuitous evil probably exists, and therefore that an omnipotent, wholly good being probably does not.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Once the logical charge is answered, the argument does not disappear; it changes shape, and the theist who has proved consistency has still not made divine permission probable.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **evidential problem**
- **gratuitous / pointless evil**
- **the suffering fawn**
- **probability rather than contradiction**
- **inductive inference**
- **the *noseeum* step**

**How to use them:** Set out the argument as an inference with an explicit inductive step, identify the fawn as an *evidential* case, then show that the theist's reply must attack either the inductive step or the probability judgement.

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** Rowe's fawn is not a deductive contradiction but an evidential case: it asks not whether God *could* have a reason, but whether it is reasonable to think one exists.

```text
   ROWE'S EVIDENTIAL ARGUMENT — AS STEPS
   (1) There exist instances of intense suffering that an omnipotent,
       omniscient being could have prevented without thereby losing
       some greater good or permitting some equally bad or worse evil.
   (2) An omniscient, wholly good being would prevent the occurrence
       of any intense suffering it could, unless it could not do so
       without losing some greater good or permitting some equally
       bad or worse evil.
   (3) Therefore there does not exist an omnipotent, omniscient,
       wholly good being.
                              │
   THE CANONICAL CASE — THE SUFFERING FAWN
   a fawn is trapped in a forest fire started by lightning, is
   severely burned, and lies suffering for days before dying;
   no human observes it, and no moral growth follows from it.
                              │
   WHAT THE CASE IS FOR:  premise (1) — the EVIDENTIAL claim
   WHAT IT IS NOT:        a deductive contradiction
                              │
   THE THEIST'S THREE ROUTES
   (a) attack the INFERENCE from "no reason seen" to "no reason"
       → SKEPTICAL THEISM (Session 9)
   (b) supply a POSITIVE REASON → theodicy (Sessions 6, 7, 12)
   (c) revise the GOD-CONCEPT so premise (2) no longer applies
       → process theism, finite God, non-creator systems
                              │
   ⚠️ (a) and (b) sit AWKWARDLY together: to say "I cannot see the
   reason and neither can you" while also saying "here is the reason"
   is a tension the examiner rewards you for noticing.
```

##### 1. Why the burden really has changed ✅

| | Logical problem | Evidential problem |
|---|---|---|
| Charge | The set is inconsistent | Theism is improbable given the evidence |
| Needed to defeat it | A **possible** morally sufficient reason | A reason to think permission is **likely** |
| Adequate reply | Defence | Theodicy, skeptical theism, or concept-revision |
| Rowe's own position | Grants consistency | Presses probability |
| Standard error | Applying Plantinga here | Applying a probability reply to Mackie |

⚠️ Rowe himself frames the argument as supporting a *variety* of atheism that acknowledges the theist may be rational; the argument claims evidential force, not demonstration.

##### 2. The inductive step, isolated ⚠️

```text
   "I can see no justifying reason for this evil."
                    ↓  ← THIS STEP is the whole battleground
   "There is no justifying reason for this evil."
                    ↓
   "Gratuitous evil exists."
                    ↓
   "An omnipotent, wholly good God probably does not exist."
```

⚠️ The step is an inference from ignorance to absence. It is not automatically fallacious: we make such inferences constantly and correctly when we would expect to see the thing if it were there. Everything therefore turns on whether we *would* expect to discern a divine justifying reason — which is precisely Wykstra's question.

##### 3. Why the fawn was chosen ⚠️

The case is deliberately constructed to strip away every standard theodicy at once:

- No human free will is involved — the free-will defence is out of scope.
- No one learns anything from it — soul-making has no purchase for the sufferer.
- The animal cannot be said to deserve it — a retributive reading is unavailable.
- It is unobserved — even indirect human moral benefit is removed.

##### 4. Why UPSC cares

✅ The distinction between the logical and evidential problems is the discipline the examiner enforces across this whole clause: the canonical trap is to conflate logical inconsistency, evidential improbability and the omnipotence paradox, and to match each theodicy to the version it addresses. A script that knows the fawn is evidential has already avoided the most heavily penalised error in the topic.

##### UPSC traps

1. ⚠️ Do not present Rowe's fawn as a deductive contradiction.
2. ⚠️ Do not answer the evidential argument with the free-will defence alone.
3. ⚠️ Do not say the evidential argument "proves" atheism; it claims probabilistic force.
4. ⚠️ Do not treat the inference from unseen reason to absent reason as obviously fallacious; argue the case.
5. ⚠️ Do not deploy skeptical theism and a positive theodicy in the same paragraph without noticing the tension.
6. ⚠️ Do not omit the reason the fawn case was built the way it was.

##### Revision notes

- Rowe, "The Problem of Evil and Some Varieties of Atheism", 1979.
- The argument grants logical consistency and attacks probability.
- Premise (1) is the evidential claim; the fawn is its illustration.
- Premise (2) is a claim about what a wholly good being would do.
- The inductive step runs from "no reason seen" to "no reason exists".
- The fawn is designed to exclude free will, soul-making, desert and observer benefit.
- The theist may attack the inference, supply a reason, or revise the God-concept.
- Attacking the inference is skeptical theism; supplying a reason is theodicy.
- The two replies sit awkwardly together; say so.
- Version-confusion between Mackie and Rowe is the most penalised error in this clause.

### SESSION 9 — SKEPTICAL THEISM AND CORNEA

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** Skeptical theism does not try to say why God permits suffering. It argues that we are in no position to tell whether a justifying reason exists, so our failure to find one proves very little.

**Technical definition:** ✅ Skeptical theism attacks the *inference* in the evidential argument — from "I can see no justifying reason for this evil" to "there is no justifying reason". Its principal proponents are **Stephen Wykstra**, **William Alston**, **Michael Bergmann** and **Daniel Howard-Snyder**.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Skeptical theism is the strongest available defensive reply to the evidential argument and the most corrosive one: it neutralises the atheist's inference only by conceding that the moral structure of the universe is opaque to us.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **CORNEA**
- **the *noseeum* inference**
- **representativeness theses**
- **defence, not theodicy**
- **cognitive access**
- **moral-scepticism objection**

**How to use them:** State CORNEA or Bergmann's theses explicitly; never let "God's ways are mysterious" stand in for the position.

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** One may claim that it *appears* that p is absent only if it is reasonable to believe that, given one's cognitive situation, p would likely be discernible if it were there.

```text
   WYKSTRA'S CORNEA (1984)
   Condition of ReasoNable Epistemic Access
   ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
   "It appears that p is absent" is a legitimate claim ONLY IF it is
   reasonable to believe that, given one's cognitive situation,
   p WOULD LIKELY BE DISCERNIBLE if it were there.
                              │
   BERGMANN'S REPRESENTATIVENESS THESES
   our sample of (i) possible GOODS, (ii) possible EVILS, and
   (iii) the ENTAILMENT RELATIONS between them is NOT
   representative of the total space
                              │
   THEREFORE the NOSEEUM inference —
        "no see 'um, so none there" — is UNWARRANTED for goods
        that would justify divine permission
                              │
   ANALOGIES
   ALSTON:  a novice examines a grandmaster's move, sees no point
            in it, and has no warrant to conclude there is none
   WYKSTRA: an infant cannot see why a painful procedure is done;
            that inability is no evidence that there is no reason
                              │
   WHAT IT IS:  a DEFENCE against the EVIDENTIAL argument
   WHAT IT IS NOT: a theodicy; and it does not target the LOGICAL problem
```

##### 1. Presupposition and scope ⚠️

- **Presupposition:** that the relevant justifying goods would plausibly exceed human cognitive reach, and that the theist may appeal to that gap without special pleading.
- **Scope:** ✅ it targets the **evidential**, not the **logical**, problem. ✅ It is compatible with also holding a positive theodicy, though the two sit awkwardly together — the optional residue is precisely that a candidate should not deploy both in the same paragraph.

##### 2. The three standing objections, with replies and residues ⚠️

| Objection | Statement | Reply | Residue |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Moral scepticism** (Michael Almeida, Bruce Russell, Erik Wielenberg) | If we cannot judge that an evil is gratuitous, we equally cannot judge that we ought to *prevent* it — perhaps God permits it for an unseen good | Obligation is fixed by what we *know*, not by unknown all-things-considered value | ⚠️ Critics find the reply unstable; the duty to rescue is exactly what the scepticism seems to unsettle |
| **Divine deception / parity** | The same scepticism undercuts confidence in divine promises, revelation and God's goodness | Restrict the scepticism to the value-entailment domain only | ⚠️ The restriction looks *ad hoc* |
| **Bracketing / unfalsifiability** | If extended, no empirical evidence could ever count against theism | — | ⚠️ The position becomes unfalsifiable, which is a cost even if it is not a refutation |

##### 3. Indian parity ⚠️

Structurally analogous moves exist in the Indian material:

- The Nyāya and Vedāntic appeal that the workings of **unseen moral potency (*adṛṣṭa*)** are **beyond the senses (*atīndriya*)** and known only through scriptural testimony (*śabda*).
- The Buddhist treatment of the precise ripening of karma as one of the **imponderables (*acinteyya*)** — matters not profitable to speculate about.

⚠️ The parallel is genuine, and it inherits the same objection: a mechanism placed permanently beyond checking cannot be evidence either for or against.

##### 4. Why UPSC cares

✅ The canonical trap is explicit: **do not let "God's ways are mysterious" stand in for skeptical theism**. Either state CORNEA or Bergmann's representativeness theses, or do not use the position at all. A named, formulated epistemic principle is worth several marks; a gesture at mystery is worth none — and mystery without an access principle is an unfalsifiable appeal, of which skeptical theism is the disciplined version.

##### UPSC traps

1. ⚠️ Do not present skeptical theism as a theodicy; it denies the atheist's inference.
2. ⚠️ Do not substitute "God works in mysterious ways" for CORNEA.
3. ⚠️ Do not apply it to the logical problem; it addresses the evidential one.
4. ⚠️ Do not omit the moral-scepticism cost; it is the position's standing price.
5. ⚠️ Do not treat the *noseeum* inference as always fallacious; CORNEA specifies exactly when it fails.
6. ⚠️ Do not present the Indian parallel as an endorsement; it inherits the same unfalsifiability objection.

##### Revision notes

- Proponents: Wykstra, Alston, Bergmann, Howard-Snyder.
- CORNEA (1984) is the Condition of ReasoNable Epistemic Access.
- Bergmann's three theses deny that our sample of goods, evils and their relations is representative.
- The *noseeum* inference — "no see 'um, so none there" — is the target.
- Alston's grandmaster and Wykstra's infant are the canonical analogies.
- It is a defence, not a theodicy, and it addresses the evidential problem only.
- Objection 1: it threatens ordinary moral reasoning and the duty to rescue.
- Objection 2: it undercuts confidence in divine promises unless restricted *ad hoc*.
- Objection 3: extended far enough, it makes theism unfalsifiable.
- Indian parallels: *adṛṣṭa* as *atīndriya*; karmic ripening among the *acinteyya*.

### SESSION 10 — HORRENDOUS EVILS: BALANCING OFF VERSUS DEFEAT

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** Some evils are so devastating that even if the world as a whole comes out well, the person who lived through them may have no reason to think their own life was worth living — and that is a different problem from the general one.

**Technical definition:** ✅ **Marilyn McCord Adams**, *Horrendous Evils and the Goodness of God* (1999), defines a **horrendous evil** as one whose participation — as perpetrator or as victim — gives the participant *prima facie* reason to doubt whether their life could be **a great good to them on the whole**.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Global balancing is not personal defeat: a world can be good on the whole while a particular life is not, and a God who is good *to persons* owes an answer at the level of the person.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **horrendous evil (technical sense)**
- **balancing off**
- **defeat (Chisholm's term)**
- **goodness to persons**
- **global versus local/personal**
- **incommensurable good**

**How to use them:** Use Adams' definition in its exact sense, distinguish balancing off from defeat, and state that compensation after suffering is not automatically justification for permitting it.

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** A later good can compensate for loss while leaving the evil merely balanced; Adams' stronger requirement is that a horrendous evil be *defeated within the person's life as a whole*.

```text
   ADAMS' ARGUMENT — AS PREMISES
   (1) Standard theodicies work at the GLOBAL level: they seek a reason
       why God permits evil IN GENERAL, balancing it within the world's
       total value.
   (2) But horrendous evils threaten the INDIVIDUAL'S LIFE AS A WHOLE:
       a global balance can be struck while a particular person's life
       remains, for them, a net disaster.
   (3) A God who is good TO PERSONS — not merely a good PRODUCER OF
       WORLDS — must therefore DEFEAT the horrendous evil WITHIN the
       frame of the individual's life.
   (4) Only resources internal to a particular religion — divine
       identification with human suffering, beatific intimacy with God
       as an INCOMMENSURABLE good — can accomplish that defeat.
                              │
   THE DISTINCTION THAT EARNS THE MARKS
   ┌───────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ BALANCING OFF             │ DEFEAT (Chisholm's term)           │
   │ the good OUTWEIGHS the    │ the evil becomes an ORGANIC PART   │
   │ evil in a sum             │ of a whole whose value it          │
   │ evil remains EXTERNAL     │ CONTRIBUTES to                     │
   │ to the good               │ evil is INTEGRATED, not offset     │
   └───────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────┘
   ┌───────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ GLOBAL defeat             │ LOCAL / PERSONAL defeat            │
   │ at the level of the world │ within THIS life, for THIS person  │
   │ what theodicies deliver   │ what Adams says is required        │
   └───────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────┘
                              │

   evil externally balanced; it does not make the life a great good
   TO THEM from within.
```

##### 1. The definition, used correctly ✅

⚠️ The canonical trap is explicit: **do not use "horrendous evil" loosely for "very bad evil"**. Adams' cases involve grave betrayal, coerced participation in severe wrongdoing and devastating violence against intimate relations — and graphic detail is unnecessary for the philosophical distinction. What makes an evil horrendous is not its intensity as such but the *doubt it gives the participant about the worth of their own life on the whole*.

##### 2. Presupposition, objection and reply ⚠️

| Element | Content |
|---|---|
| **Presupposition** | ⚠️ That goodness-to-persons is a distinct, non-negotiable requirement; that there are incommensurable goods — for Adams, relationship with God — that can defeat rather than merely counterbalance; and, controversially, that the theodicist may use religion-specific resources rather than only "generic theism" |
| **Objection 1** | ⚠️ The reply is purely eschatological and unverifiable, and it seems to make the horrendous evil instrumentally necessary |
| **Reply 1** | ✅ Adams denies instrumentality: the evil need not be *required*; it must be *defeated* |
| **Objection 2** | ⚠️ Using religion-specific resources abandons the neutral philosophical arena |
| **Reply 2** | ✅ The atheological argument targets a *particular* theism, so a particular theism may answer |
| **Canonical example** | ✅ A person coerced into grave harm against someone they love: no aggregate of unrelated goods automatically makes that life a great good to that person from within |

##### 3. Indian parity — the highest-value paragraph in a 20-marker ⚠️


### SESSION 11 — ANIMAL SUFFERING AND THE PRE-HUMAN PROBLEM

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** Animals were being torn apart, starving and dying of disease for hundreds of millions of years before any human being existed — and none of the usual explanations for suffering apply to them.

**Technical definition:** ✅ Animal pain from predation, disease and pre-human natural processes is a hard form of natural evil because **no free-will defence and no soul-making rationale plainly applies** to a creature that neither chooses nor grows morally.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

##### 1. Indian parity ✅

- **Karma-saṃsāra** offers a distinctive distribution-account by extending moral causation to **animal births (*tiryak-yoni*)**.
- **Jainism** ascribes living selves graded sense-capacity and grounds **non-injury (*ahiṃsā*)** in that recognition.
- **Buddhism** places animals within the same field of **suffering (*duḥkha*)** and extends **compassion (*karuṇā*)** to sentient beings.

⚠️ The cost is symmetrical to karma's general cost: the explanation is unverifiable and must not be used to infer that present suffering is deserved.

##### 2. Objection and reply within the karma route ⚠️

```text
   OBJECTION: karma explains distribution but is itself the thing in
   question; and an animal cannot LEARN from a fruit it cannot understand.

   response it demands — compassion, non-injury — is unaffected by

                              │
```

5. ⚠️ Do not use karma to infer that a suffering creature deserved it.
6. ⚠️ Do not omit the fawn's status as an evidential rather than deductive case.

### SESSION 12 — PROCESS THEODICY AND THE OTHER NON-CLASSICAL REPLIES

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** Process theodicy answers evil by changing the God-concept rather than by justifying permission: God is not the kind of being who could simply stop things, because power is spread through reality rather than concentrated in one place.

**Technical definition:** ✅ Process thought limits unilateral divine control: God **persuades** finite actualities and **shares** their suffering. God is **dipolar** — a *primordial nature* that envisages possibilities and supplies each occasion its **initial aim**, and a *consequent nature* that receives and preserves what the world becomes.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Process theodicy purchases God's innocence at the price of God's sovereignty — and a first-class answer states the trade rather than concealing it.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **dipolar God**
- **primordial and consequent nature**
- **initial aim**
- **persuasion, not coercion**
- **fellow-sufferer who understands**
- **metaphysical necessity, not self-limitation**

**How to use them:** Number the tenets, name Whitehead, Hartshorne, Griffin and Cobb separately, and state the modal difference from the free-will defence.

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** Griffin's God *cannot* coerce; Plantinga's God *chooses* not to intervene — the difference is modal, and it is the whole difference between a theodicy and a defence here.

```text
   PROCESS THEODICY — THE TENETS, NUMBERED
   (1) CREATIVITY, not God, is the ultimate; actual entities are
       self-creative occasions of experience.
   (2) God is DIPOLAR —
         PRIMORDIAL NATURE : the ordered envisagement of possibilities,
                             supplying each occasion its INITIAL AIM
         CONSEQUENT NATURE : God's reception and preservation of what
                             the world actually becomes
   (3) POWER IS INTRINSICALLY DISTRIBUTED: to be actual is to have
       some self-determination.
   (4) THEREFORE divine power is PERSUASIVE BY METAPHYSICAL NECESSITY
       (Griffin) — NOT by self-limitation.  ← the line that separates
       process theodicy from the free-will defence.
   (5) EVIL = finite occasions DEVIATING from the divine lure;
       God is not culpable, because God could not unilaterally prevent it.
   (6) God is "the FELLOW-SUFFERER WHO UNDERSTANDS" (Whitehead) —
       solidarity, not detachment.
                              │
   NAME THE LAYER YOU ARE USING — DO NOT WRITE "PROCESS (WHITEHEAD)" AND STOP
   ┌────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ WHITEHEAD      │ Process and Reality (1929): creativity ultimate; │
   │                │ dipolar God; initial aim; consequent nature;     │
   │                │ "fellow-sufferer who understands"                │
   ├────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ HARTSHORNE     │ The Divine Relativity (1948); Omnipotence and    │
   │                │ Other Theological Mistakes (1984): classical     │
   │                │ omnipotence is INCOHERENT — to have ALL power is │
   │                │ to leave none to anything else; "surrelativism"/ │
   │                │ panentheism makes God supremely RELATED, not     │
   │                │ supremely UNAFFECTED                             │
   ├────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ GRIFFIN        │ God, Power, and Evil: A Process Theodicy (1976); │
   │                │ Evil Revisited (1991): persuasion is a           │
   │                │ METAPHYSICAL NECESSITY, not a chosen restraint   │
   ├────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
   │ COBB           │ A Christian Natural Theology: the theological    │
   │                │ systematisation                                  │
   └────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                              │
   CRITICISM: a God who cannot guarantee victory over evil may be
   unworthy of worship; the metaphysics is contested; classical theists
   DENY that actuality entails self-determination.
   REPLY: divine solidarity, and the OBJECTIVE IMMORTALITY of achieved
   value in the consequent nature — but the objection does not disappear.
```

##### 1. Process is a theodicy, not a defence ✅

⚠️ This is a precise classificatory point and it is examinable. Process theodicy **claims to identify God's actual situation** — it is not offering a merely possible story. That is why it is refutable in a way Plantinga's defence is not, and why its metaphysical premises must be defended rather than merely entertained.

##### 2. The surrounding reply-space ⚠️

| Response | Precise claim | Main cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Greater-good / stable-order** | Some goods — freedom, reliable agency, courage, compassion — require risk, regular law and the possibility of loss | May explain the *kind* of world without explaining the *amount* or *distribution* of suffering |
| **Leibnizian best possible world** | A perfect God chooses the best total compossible order, not a world containing every local good | There may be no unique best world; world-total optimisation can neglect goodness to particular persons |
| **Eschatological fulfilment** | No finite suffering is final; later fulfilment, restoration or union can prevent a life from being finally wasted | Compensation after suffering is not automatically justification for permitting it |
| **Divine mystery** | Finite minds may fail to grasp providential reasons | Without an access principle this becomes an unfalsifiable appeal; skeptical theism is the disciplined evidential version |
| **Protest / anti-theodicy** | Refusing to justify suffering can protect truthfulness, lament and the moral priority of resistance | Protest diagnoses the ethics of explanation but does not by itself settle the logical or evidential argument |

⚠️ **Compensation is not defeat.** A later good can compensate for loss while leaving the evil externally balanced. Adams' stronger requirement is that a horrendous evil be defeated within the person's life as a whole. Pastoral responsibility therefore requires listening, protection and action before speculative explanation; a theodicy never licenses passivity toward preventable harm.

##### 3. Finite God and the general revision strategy ⚠️

```text
   THE REVISION STRATEGY IN GENERAL
   weaken ONE element of the classical profile and the problem loosens:
        omnipotence → process, finite God (goodness preserved)
        personality → impersonal Absolute, Advaita (evil at the
                      empirical level; the ultimate is untouched)
        creatorship → Buddhism, Jainism, Mīmāṃsā (no defendant at all)
                              │
   EVERY revision purchases relief with a RELIGIOUSLY SIGNIFICANT LOSS.
   Naming the loss is what converts description into evaluation.
```

##### 4. Why UPSC cares

✅ The 2025 stem is *"Explain the main tenets of the Process Theodicy as an explanation of the problem of evil."* The directive **explain the main tenets** buys enumerated doctrine, correctly attributed. The canonical trap is explicit: do not write "process theodicy (Whitehead)" and stop — name the layer you are using. Numbering the six tenets and attributing Whitehead, Hartshorne and Griffin separately is the difference between a competent and a strong script.

##### UPSC traps

1. ⚠️ Do not write that the process God is simply powerless; divine power is persuasive and relational.
2. ⚠️ Do not attribute the whole position to Whitehead; name Hartshorne and Griffin's distinct contributions.
3. ⚠️ Do not confuse Griffin's metaphysical necessity with a chosen self-limitation.
4. ⚠️ Do not call process theodicy a defence; it claims God's actual situation.
5. ⚠️ Do not let "God's ways are mysterious" pass as an argument; without an access principle it is unfalsifiable.
6. ⚠️ Do not present eschatological compensation as automatic justification.

##### Revision notes

- Whitehead, *Process and Reality* (1929): creativity ultimate; dipolar God; initial aim; consequent nature; fellow-sufferer.
- Hartshorne, *The Divine Relativity* (1948) and *Omnipotence and Other Theological Mistakes* (1984): classical omnipotence is incoherent.
- Griffin, *God, Power, and Evil* (1976) and *Evil Revisited* (1991): persuasion is metaphysically necessary.
- Cobb, *A Christian Natural Theology*: the theological systematisation.
- To be actual is to have some self-determination, so power is intrinsically distributed.
- Evil is deviation from the divine lure; God is not culpable.
- Process is a theodicy, not a defence — it claims actuality.
- Greater-good, best-world, eschatology, mystery and anti-theodicy each carry a stated cost.
- Compensation is not defeat.
- Verdict: God's innocence is purchased at the price of God's sovereignty; state the trade.

### SESSION 13 — THE INDIAN RESPONSE: KARMA, THE SCHOOLS AND THEIR COSTS

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** The Indian traditions mostly do not ask why God allows suffering. They ask where suffering comes from and how to get out of it, and they answer with a self-operating moral law that runs across lives.

**Technical definition:** ✅ **Karma-saṃsāra** holds that suffering is the fruit of one's own past deeds across lives and that the moral law is **self-operating**, so evil raises no problem for a creator's goodness: it is cosmic justice rather than divine cruelty.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> India's structural advantage is not that it explains evil better but that it answers a different question: not why evil is permitted, but why *this* person suffers — and it can drop the defendant altogether.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **karma-saṃsāra**
- **self-operating moral law**
- **karma-superintendent (*karmādhyakṣa*)**
- **beginninglessness**
- **empirical and ultimate levels (*vyāvahārika/pāramārthika*)**
- **victim-blaming control**

**How to use them:** Distinguish the theistic from the non-theistic karma accounts, use beginninglessness precisely, and always attach the moral caution.

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** Karma is not one doctrine across the Indian schools: Mīmāṃsā, Buddhist and Jain accounts need no creator, while Nyāya and theistic Vedānta assign God a just administrative role rather than authorship of deserts.

```text
   THE INDIAN MOVE — STRUCTURALLY
   WESTERN QUESTION : why does a good God PERMIT evil?
   INDIAN QUESTION  : how does BONDAGE ORIGINATE and how is it ENDED?
                              │
   ┌──────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────┐
   NON-THEISTIC ROUTES                       THEISTIC ROUTES
   Buddhism, Jainism, Mīmāṃsā                Nyāya, Rāmānuja
   no divine distributor required            God dispenses karmic fruit
   the law operates by itself                JUSTLY as karma-superintendent
   → THERE IS NO DEFENDANT                     (karmādhyakṣa)
                                             → God is a JUDGE, not an
```

##### 1. What karma explains that Western theodicy does not ✅

| Question | Western resource | Indian resource | Where they diverge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Why is there evil at all? | Free will; privation; soul-making | Ignorance (*avidyā*); inflow of karmic matter (*āsrava*); craving (*taṇhā*) | The West asks about *permission*; India about the *origination of bondage* |

##### 2. The moral controls — non-negotiable ⚠️

✅ **Beginninglessness.** Karma traditions commonly treat the action–consequence series as beginningless, so they need not identify a first wrongful act. Beginninglessness blocks one regress question but does not verify any claimed past-life cause.

✅ **Distributor.** Non-theistic Buddhist, Jain and Mīmāṃsā accounts do not require a divine distributor; Nyāya and theistic Vedānta assign God a just administrative role without making God the arbitrary author of deserts.

⚠️ **Epistemic limit.** The inference from "this person suffers" to "this person deserves it for a specific past act" is unavailable to ordinary observers. Using karma that way is epistemically unsupported and converts explanation into victim-blaming.

⚠️ **Residual pressure.** Karma addresses distribution more directly than the free-will defence, but natural suffering, collective harms and unequal starting conditions still press the fairness and verification of the mechanism.

✅ **Duty of compassion.** Whatever its metaphysics, karma does not cancel present duties of compassion, protection and relief; causal explanation is not moral permission to withhold aid.

##### 3. Standing criticisms and replies ⚠️

```text
   CRITICISM 1: victim-blaming
        REPLY: classical Indian ethics makes compassion obligatory;
        explanatory karma does not authorise social judgement.
        RESIDUE: the misuse is common enough to require the caution
        to be stated in the answer itself.
   CRITICISM 2: infinite regress of karma
        REPLY: the series is beginningless by doctrine, so the demand
        for a first term is misplaced.
        RESIDUE: beginninglessness is a stipulation, not a verification.
   CRITICISM 3: the a-morality of an impersonal law
        REPLY: Nyāya supplies an intelligent just dispenser; Buddhism
        supplies conditionality without a moral legislator.
        RESIDUE: the two replies pull in opposite directions and
        cannot be combined in one answer.
```

⚠️ Route personal identity, rebirth mechanics and the metaphysics of liberation to *Soul, Immortality, Rebirth and Liberation*. This file owns karma **only** as an explanation of suffering and its distribution.

##### UPSC traps

2. ⚠️ Do not use karma to infer that a particular sufferer deserves their suffering.
3. ⚠️ Do not treat beginninglessness as evidence for any past-life cause.
4. ⚠️ Do not say Advaita denies suffering; it places it at the empirical level.
5. ⚠️ Do not let karma answer Adams' question; it answers distribution, not personal worth.
6. ⚠️ Do not import rebirth mechanics; they belong to the Soul topic.

##### Revision notes

- Karma-saṃsāra: suffering is the fruit of one's own past deeds across lives; the law is self-operating.
- Its structural advantage is *distribution* — why this person — which Western theodicy answers weakly.
- Nyāya and Rāmānuja: God dispenses karmic fruit justly as *karmādhyakṣa*, a judge rather than an arbitrary cause.
- Mīmāṃsā, Buddhism and Jainism need no divine distributor at all.
- Advaita: evil is real at the empirical level and sublated at the ultimate level.
- The series is beginningless, which blocks a regress but verifies nothing.
- Victim-blaming is the standing moral cost; state the control explicitly.
- Compassion, protection and relief remain obligatory regardless of causal explanation.
- Natural suffering, collective harm and unequal starting conditions still press the mechanism.
- India can drop the defendant; the West cannot — that is the comparative sentence to write.

### SESSION 14 — EVIL AND THE PROFANE AS RELIGION-FORMING CATEGORIES

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** This session asks a completely different question from the rest of the topic: not whether evil disproves God, but whether religion itself is *built out of* the categories of evil, impurity and the ordinary.

**Technical definition:** ✅ On the sociological-phenomenological analysis, religion is constituted not by belief in God but by a **binary partition of the world into the sacred and the profane**, and by a practical response to **evil, suffering and impurity** as that which must be diagnosed, bounded and overcome.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Evil and the profane found religion **structurally rather than doctrinally**: they supply the predicament, the boundary and the soteriological task without which religious practice would be unintelligible.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **sacred and profane**
- **hierophany**
- **world-axis (*axis mundi*)**
- **the numinous (*mysterium tremendum et fascinans*)**
- **predicament → boundary → institution → soteriology**
- **profane ≠ evil**

**How to use them:** Treat the question as *functional*, not theological; run the four-step founding sequence; and correct the profane/evil confusion in the first paragraph.

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** Durkheim's profane is the *ordinary*, not the wicked — and conflating them is the commonest error in this question.

##### Revision notes

- Durkheim, *The Elementary Forms of Religious Life* (1912): the sacred/profane classification; religion as beliefs and practices relative to sacred things uniting a moral community, a church; deliberately God-free.
- Otto, *The Idea of the Holy* (1917): the numinous, *mysterium tremendum et fascinans*; the object-side.

### SESSION 15 — CONCEPT-DEPENDENCE AND COMPARATIVE SYNTHESIS

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** The problem of evil only bites on a very particular idea of God. Change the idea and the problem shrinks, moves or disappears entirely — which tells us the problem is really a test of a concept.

**Technical definition:** ⚠️ The problem is **concept-dependent**: it presses only on a God who is *simultaneously omnipotent, wholly good and creator*. Weaken any of those and the argument loses its grip — at a religiously significant price.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> The problem of evil is a stress-test of a *particular* God-concept, not of religion as such — which is why the same suffering that refutes one theology leaves another entirely untouched.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **concept-dependence**
- **finite God**
- **impersonal Absolute**
- **no creator**
- **religiously significant loss**
- **assessment grid**

**How to use them:** Run the classical set against four God-concepts, show it dissolving in each, and price each dissolution.

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** Weaken omnipotence (process), weaken personality (Advaita), or remove the creator (Buddhism, Jainism, Mīmāṃsā) and the problem dissolves — but each dissolution purchases relief with a religiously significant loss.

2. **Natural evil.** ⚠️ Free will does not explain earthquakes or disease. **Reply:** stable natural law enables agency. **Rejoinder:** stability does not explain every extreme distribution.
3. **Divine responsibility.** ⚠️ Creating foreknown wrongdoers seems culpable. **Reply:** foreknowledge does not cause free action. **Rejoinder:** knowingly choosing the whole system remains morally relevant.

5. ⚠️ Do not list theodicies without their costs; the grid is the answer.
6. ⚠️ Do not omit the Indian bridge in a 15- or 20-marker; it is the highest-value comparative move available.

### SESSION 16 — ANSWER ARCHITECTURE FOR THE PROBLEM OF EVIL

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** This session converts everything above into a writing method: how to open, what to include at each mark level, which directive demands what shape, and how to finish with a graded verdict rather than a pious sentence.

**Technical definition:** ⚠️ An answer architecture specifies, for each directive and mark band, the obligatory content, the ordering, the evidence density and the form of the conclusion.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Always state *which version* of the problem you are answering in the first two lines: theodicies are version-specific, and a mismatched theodicy scores as an error rather than as content.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **version identification**
- **directive decoding**
- **graded verdict**
- **cost statement**
- **quotation discipline**
- **residue paragraph**

**How to use them:** Open with the version, structure by the directive, close with a graded verdict that names the cost and the residue.

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** A theodicy is not refuted by its cost but is *measured* by it — so name the cost, then grade the reply against the version of the problem it was built for.

```text
   THE UNIVERSAL SPINE FOR THIS CLAUSE
   (1) NAME the version        logical · evidential · existential ·
                               horrendous · animal · distributional
   (2) STATE the set or the    with the BRIDGING PREMISES, or with the
       probability judgement   inductive step made explicit
   (3) RECONSTRUCT the reply   as numbered premises, correctly attributed
   (4) OBJECT and REPLY        the strongest objection, then the
                               proponent's best answer, then the residue
   (5) COMPARE                 one deliberate Indian or Western comparator
   (6) GRADE                   what it explains · what it preserves ·
                               what it costs · what it leaves untouched
```

##### 1. Answer architecture by mark band ✅

**10 marks**
```text
State the exact problem → identify the God-concept it presupposes →
one response with its presupposition → one objection →
verdict on COMPATIBILITY, not a devotional conclusion.
```

**15 marks**
```text
Logical/evidential frame → the doctrine's argument →
canonical example → objection and reply →
Indian comparator → assess the explanatory cost.
```

**20 marks**
```text
Define evil and state the inconsistent set → strongest theodicy in full →
rival theodicy or Indian view → two objections with replies →
distinguish DEFENCE from THEODICY → graded verdict.
```

⚠️ **Band budgets.** 10 marks ≈ 150 words: version, set, one reply, one objection, one-line verdict. 15 marks ≈ 220–260 words: add the canonical example, the reply, and the Indian comparator. 20 marks ≈ 320–360 words: add a second objection with its reply, the defence/theodicy distinction, and the residue before a criterion-named conclusion.

**Graded-verdict templates:**

- ⚠️ *Logical problem:* "The set is inconsistent only with the bridging premises; deny either and the contradiction dissolves — but the theist then owes an account of what a good omnipotent being *would* tolerate, which is where the evidential problem restarts."
- ⚠️ *Theodicy stem:* "The theodicy answers the version of the problem it was designed for and leaves ___ untouched; its cost is ___, and a theodicy is not refuted by its cost but is measured by it."
- ⚠️ *Concept-dependence stem:* "The problem is a stress-test of one God-concept, not of religion. Weaken omnipotence (process), weaken personality (Advaita), or remove the creator (Buddhism, Jainism, Mīmāṃsā) and it dissolves — but each dissolution purchases relief with a religiously significant loss."

1. ⚠️ Do not open with a definition of God; open with the version of the problem.
2. ⚠️ Do not use a devotional conclusion where a graded verdict is required.
3. ⚠️ Do not quote the four-horn dilemma as a surviving Epicurean text.
4. ⚠️ Do not exceed the band budget; a 10-marker with four theodicies has no analysis in it.
5. ⚠️ Do not omit the residue paragraph in a 20-marker; the examiner is looking for it.

- Always name the version of the problem in the first two lines.

## BASIC MCQS / REMEDIATION

### 32 original MCQs — 24 core diagnostics and 8 remedial error-targeting drills

Philosophy Optional is examined only at the Mains stage, so **no question below is a Prelims previous-year question**. All thirty-two are original UPSC-style diagnostics written against this owner's doctrine.

**Answer placement:** each correct option was fixed **independently**, so the key sequence is unpatterned rather than rotated and no cycle such as `ABCD` — or its reverse — can be used to guess. Options are matched for length, specificity, grammar and visible formatting so that the answer cannot be spotted by shape alone. Every question carries four question-specific option explanations and one unique examiner trap.

**Coverage:** the twenty-four core questions run across the whole printed clause — *Problem of Evil* — covering the four versions of the problem and their burdens, the defence/theodicy distinction, the classification of evil, quotation and attribution discipline, Mackie's bridging premises and his adequate/fallacious framing, the paradox of omnipotence, Plantinga's free-will defence with transworld depravity, divine responsibility under absolute creation, Augustinian privation with the sin-and-punishment thesis and the inherited-guilt control, Hick's soul-making with epistemic distance and the Irenaean/Augustinian typology, concept-dependence, Rowe's evidential argument and its inductive step, Wykstra's CORNEA with the costs of skeptical theism, Adams' horrendous evils with balancing off versus defeat, animal and pre-human suffering, process theodicy with its named layers, the surrounding reply-space of greater-good, best-world, eschatological, mystery and anti-theodicy responses, the Indian karma frameworks across theistic and non-theistic schools, and evil and the profane as religion-forming categories. The eight remedial drills attack the errors that cost marks every year on this clause.

#### MCQ 1. Which version of the problem is on the table

A script answers a stem about the sheer quantity of undeserved suffering in the world by proving that God's existence and the existence of evil are not strictly contradictory. What has gone wrong?

A. Nothing has gone wrong, because establishing logical consistency is sufficient to answer every formulation of the problem of evil.

B. The script has answered the existential burden when the stem in fact raised the logical burden about a formal inconsistency.

C. The script has answered the logical burden when the stem raised the evidential burden, where consistency does not restore probability.

D. The script has confused moral with natural evil, which is a classificatory rather than a version-matching failure in this case.

**Answer: C.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: consistency answers Mackie alone, and Rowe explicitly grants consistency before pressing the improbability of divine permission.
- **B:** Incorrect: this inverts the two burdens, and the existential burden concerns whether a particular life can be a great good to the person living it.
- **C:** Correct: quantity and distribution belong to the evidential version, whose burden is probability, so a bare consistency proof leaves the charge standing.
- **D:** Incorrect: the classification of evil is a separate discipline, and the stem's fault here is the mismatch of reply-type to version rather than of kind.

**Examiner trap 1:** Candidates treat "the problem of evil" as one argument; the examiner treats it as four, and awards the first marks to whoever names the version in the opening two lines.

#### MCQ 2. What a defence claims and what a theodicy claims

Which statement states the difference between a defence and a theodicy correctly?

A. A defence identifies God's actual reason for permitting evil, whereas a theodicy shows only that some such reason is logically available.

B. A defence and a theodicy differ only in tone, the first being philosophical and the second devotional, but their success conditions are identical.

C. A defence applies to natural evil while a theodicy applies to moral evil, so the two divide the field between them by subject matter.

D. A defence shows that God could have a morally sufficient reason, whereas a theodicy claims to identify what that reason actually is.

**Answer: D.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: this reverses the two, and it makes Plantinga's modest possibility claim into an assertion he explicitly declines to make.
- **B:** Incorrect: the success conditions differ sharply, since bare logical possibility suffices for a defence but never for a theodicy.
- **C:** Incorrect: the division is by claim-strength and not by subject matter, and both defences and theodicies address moral and natural evil.
- **D:** Correct: a defence discharges the consistency burden with a possible reason, while a theodicy asserts an actual reason and is refuted if that reason fails.

**Examiner trap 2:** Using "defence" and "theodicy" interchangeably caps a script at the average band, because the whole force of Plantinga's argument depends on his needing only possibility.

#### MCQ 3. Sorting the evil before selecting the reply

Consider the following statements about the classification of evil and the scope of the standard replies.

1. The free-will defence applies directly to moral evil and not directly to natural evil.
2. Hick's soul-making extends to natural evil by treating it as the environment of moral growth.
3. The free-will defence covers animal suffering because predation is a misuse of creaturely freedom.
4. Augustine reaches natural evil only by appealing to a fallen order, which is historically strained.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. 1, 2 and 4 only, because the scope of each reply is fixed by the kind of evil it was built to explain.

B. 1 and 3 only, because freedom is the single source of all evil once creaturely agency is admitted.

C. 2, 3 and 4 only, because natural and animal suffering are treated identically by every developmental theodicy.

D. 1 and 2 only, because Augustine makes no attempt whatever to extend his account to natural evil.

**Answer: A.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Correct: statements 1, 2 and 4 each state the scope of a reply accurately, and statement 3 fails because animals neither choose nor misuse freedom.
- **B:** Incorrect: statement 3 is false, and the canonical rule is that answering animal suffering with the free-will defence is itself a marked error.
- **C:** Incorrect: statement 3 remains false, and soul-making covers animal suffering only instrumentally through human virtue toward animals.
- **D:** Incorrect: Augustine does extend the account to natural evil through the fallen order, which is why statement 4 belongs in the correct set.

**Examiner trap 3:** A script that writes "free will explains evil" without qualification has silently claimed to explain earthquakes, congenital disease and predation, and has invited the topic's commonest rejoinder.

#### MCQ 4. Attribution and quotation discipline in this clause

Which pairing of a formulation with its correct source is accurate?

A. The four-horn dilemma is preserved in a surviving Epicurean treatise, and "the vale of Soul-making" was coined by Irenaeus in his image-and-likeness theology.

B. The four-horn dilemma is reported by Lactantius, and "the vale of Soul-making" is Hick's own coinage in *Evil and the God of Love*.

C. The four-horn dilemma reaches us through Lactantius, *De Ira Dei* 13, and "the vale of Soul-making" is John Keats's phrase from his spring 1819 journal-letter.

D. The four-horn dilemma is Mackie's restatement in *Mind*, and "the vale of Soul-making" belongs to Chisholm's analysis of defeat and balancing off.

**Answer: C.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: no surviving Epicurean text contains the dilemma, and Irenaeus supplies the image-and-likeness theology rather than the phrase itself.
- **B:** Incorrect: the Lactantius half is right, but Hick adopted an existing phrase and never presented it as his own invention.
- **C:** Correct: the dilemma is transmitted through Lactantius and traditionally ascribed to Epicurus, while Keats coined the phrase that Hick later borrowed.
- **D:** Incorrect: Mackie restates the logical problem rather than the dilemma, and Chisholm supplies the technical term "defeat" used by Adams.

**Examiner trap 4:** Attribution errors are cheap to avoid and expensive to make, because a misassigned phrase signals to the examiner that the doctrine behind it was never read.

#### MCQ 5. What actually makes Mackie's set inconsistent

Which statement identifies the role of the bridging premises correctly?

A. The bridging premises are optional rhetorical devices, since "God is omnipotent", "God is perfectly good" and "evil exists" already entail a formal contradiction.

B. Two additional principles are needed — that a good being eliminates evil as far as it can, and that omnipotence has no relevant limit on prevention.

C. The bridging premises assert that evil is a positive substance, without which the set of propositions could not be brought into conflict at all.

D. Only one bridging premise is required, namely that God's goodness must be recognisable to the creatures whose suffering is in question.

**Answer: B.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: nothing in the three propositions plus logic alone entails both a proposition and its negation, which is what formal contradiction requires.
- **B:** Correct: the goodness bridge and the power bridge together generate the inconsistency, and each is the target of a distinct family of theistic replies.
- **C:** Incorrect: the substantiality of evil is Augustine's target rather than Mackie's premise, and the set does not require it.
- **D:** Incorrect: recognisability is a separate hiddenness issue, and one bridge alone leaves the power side of the argument entirely unsupported.

**Examiner trap 5:** Declaring the triad self-contradictory and moving straight to free will concedes the analytical core of the 2020 question before the answer has properly begun.

#### MCQ 6. Mackie's own framing of the standard replies

In "Evil and Omnipotence" Mackie divides proposed solutions into adequate and fallacious ones. What is his criterion?

A. An adequate solution abandons at least one of the disputed propositions, whereas a fallacious solution only appears to retain all of them.

B. An adequate solution is one drawn from revealed theology, whereas a fallacious solution relies on philosophical reasoning alone about divine attributes.

C. An adequate solution answers the evidential form of the argument, whereas a fallacious solution addresses only its weaker logical formulation.

D. An adequate solution is accepted by most theists, whereas a fallacious solution has been abandoned by the mainstream theological tradition.

**Answer: A.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Correct: Mackie treats the honest exits as those that give up a proposition, and classifies replies that quietly limit power or redefine goodness as fallacious.
- **B:** Incorrect: revelation plays no part in Mackie's taxonomy, which is conducted entirely in terms of the consistency of a set of propositions.
- **C:** Incorrect: the evidential formulation postdates Mackie's article by more than two decades and is not what his classification is about.
- **D:** Incorrect: popularity is not a logical criterion, and Mackie's charge is precisely that widely held replies are among the fallacious ones.

**Examiner trap 6:** Naming Mackie's adequate/fallacious framing shows the examiner that the article was read rather than the slogan, and it costs one sentence to include.

#### MCQ 7. The paradox of omnipotence, stated as a dilemma

Which statement expresses Mackie's paradox of omnipotence most accurately?

A. If God is omnipotent, God can perform logically contradictory tasks, and the impossibility of such tasks therefore disproves divine omnipotence outright.

B. If God cannot make beings God cannot control, second-order power is limited; if God can and does, first-order power over them is limited.

C. If God is omnipotent, God must intervene in every case of suffering, and the observed absence of intervention therefore establishes that God is not good.

D. If God creates free beings, God thereby ceases to be omniscient about their future choices, which is the real limitation the paradox exposes.

**Answer: B.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: almost no theist claims omnipotence covers the self-contradictory, so this attacks a position that the debate does not actually contain.
- **B:** Correct: the paradox turns on the incompatibility of unrestricted first-order and second-order power, and each horn carries its own cost.
- **C:** Incorrect: this restates the goodness bridge of the logical problem rather than the paradox about the structure of power itself.
- **D:** Incorrect: the foreknowledge issue is a distinct problem, and Mackie's paradox concerns power over things rather than knowledge of them.

**Examiner trap 7:** Reducing the 2021 question to the heavy-stone puzzle produces a riddle rather than an analysis and leaves the required connection to evil entirely unmade.

#### MCQ 8. The premise structure of the free-will defence

Which reconstruction of Plantinga's free-will defence is correct?

A. Significant freedom is a great good, it entails genuine alternatives, God cannot causally determine a free creature to choose only rightly, so moral evil is possible alongside perfect goodness.

B. Significant freedom is a great good, God therefore creates only agents who will in fact choose rightly, and the moral evil we observe must accordingly be an illusion.

C. Significant freedom is a great good, and since God foresees every misuse of it, God bears no responsibility for any consequence that follows from creaturely choice.

D. Significant freedom is a great good, so God permits moral evil in order to produce the greater good of courage, compassion and mature moral character.

**Answer: A.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Correct: this is the defence's actual chain, and step three is the self-cancelling description that blocks a causally guaranteed world of only-right choices.
- **B:** Incorrect: nothing in the defence denies the reality of moral evil, and denying it would abandon the proposition the argument is meant to accommodate.
- **C:** Incorrect: foreknowledge is a separate issue, and Plantinga does not argue that divine foresight discharges divine responsibility.
- **D:** Incorrect: this is Hick's developmental rationale rather than Plantinga's modal one, and it converts a defence into a theodicy.

**Examiner trap 8:** Blending Plantinga's modal argument with Hick's developmental one produces a hybrid that neither author holds and that no objection in the literature actually targets.

#### MCQ 9. The modal status of transworld depravity

What exactly does Plantinga claim about transworld depravity?

A. That it is an empirically established feature of human psychology, confirmed by the observed frequency of wrongdoing in every known society.

B. That it is a necessary truth about creaturely essences, so that no possible free creature could ever refrain from wrongdoing in any circumstance.

C. That it is a theological doctrine equivalent to the Augustinian Fall, transmitted to every human being through inherited guilt from an ancestral act.

D. That it is possibly true of every creaturely essence God could instantiate, which is enough to block Mackie's demand for only-good free agents.

**Answer: D.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: the claim is modal rather than empirical, and Plantinga needs no evidence about actual human behaviour to make the defence work.
- **B:** Incorrect: necessity is far stronger than the argument requires, and Plantinga is careful not to assert that transworld depravity is even actual.
- **C:** Incorrect: the Augustinian Fall is a theological narrative about historical origin, whereas transworld depravity is a claim about possible worlds.
- **D:** Correct: possibility suffices for a defence, and that possibility is precisely what defeats the demand for a world of free agents who never go wrong.

**Examiner trap 9:** Attacking transworld depravity as empirically unsupported misses the modal point entirely and shows that the difference between defence and theodicy was never grasped.

#### MCQ 10. Absolute creation and the location of responsibility

The 2022 stem asserts that if God is the Absolute Creator, responsibility for evil cannot belong to the human agent. Which combination of replies is philosophically appropriate?

A. God creates the capacity while the agent supplies the act; evil is a privation rather than a created thing; and foreknowledge does not amount to causation.

B. God creates every act directly, so responsibility is genuinely divine, and the human agent is correctly described by the stem as a mere instrument.

C. The stem is answered by pointing out that natural evil exists, since earthquakes show that not all suffering can be traced to any agent at all.

D. The stem collapses because omnipotence is incoherent, so there is no Absolute Creator whose responsibility could be discussed in the first place.

**Answer: A.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Correct: these are the three standard replies about authorship, metaphysics and knowledge, and together they locate responsibility with the acting agent.
- **B:** Incorrect: conceding the stem outright abandons the critical directive, which asks for examination rather than acceptance of the claim.
- **C:** Incorrect: natural evil is outside the stem's scope, which concerns moral evil and the agency of the human being who commits it.
- **D:** Incorrect: this is the process route, which changes the subject rather than examining the conditional the stem actually asserts.

**Examiner trap 10:** The strongest scripts concede the rejoinder — that knowingly instantiating the whole system remains morally relevant — instead of pretending the free-will reply closes the matter.

#### MCQ 11. What the privation account establishes

Which statement about Augustine's privation account is correct?

A. It establishes that suffering is an illusion produced by a defective perspective, since what has no positive being can have no real effect on a creature.

B. It establishes that evil has an independent creative source opposed to God, which is why the created order contains both good and evil substances.

C. It establishes that God is the direct cause of natural evil while remaining the indirect cause of moral evil through the freedom given to creatures.

D. It establishes that evil depends parasitically on good and has no created substance of its own, without thereby justifying any particular permitted deprivation.

**Answer: D.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: a privation is a real lack in a subject that ought to possess the missing good, and blindness is not thereby rendered unreal.
- **B:** Incorrect: metaphysical dualism is exactly what the privation account is designed to avoid, since being as being is held to be good.
- **C:** Incorrect: the account denies that God creates evil at all, and it treats natural evil through the fallen order rather than direct causation.
- **D:** Correct: the account secures metaphysical economy and divine innocence in creation, while leaving the separate question of permission unanswered.

**Examiner trap 11:** The commonest misreading is that privation trivialises pain; a script that says so has attacked a position no serious Augustinian holds.

#### MCQ 12. The sin-and-punishment thesis and the inherited-guilt control

Which treatment of the 2023 thesis is philosophically strongest?

A. Accept the thesis without qualification, since Augustine's authority settles the matter and the printed stem reproduces his considered position exactly.

B. Reconstruct the thesis, then distinguish inherited personal guilt from an inherited damaged condition, and note that the second is more defensible but still owes an explanation.

C. Reject the thesis immediately, since innocent suffering is obvious and no further reconstruction of Augustine's metaphysical framework is therefore required.

D. Restate the thesis as a claim about natural law, since disease and disaster are the punishments that the sentence must have been intended to describe.

**Answer: B.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: the directive is *critically discuss*, and unqualified acceptance forfeits the evaluative half of a twenty-mark question.
- **B:** Correct: the directive requires the system before the objection, and the guilt-versus-condition distinction is the sharpest available critical instrument.
- **C:** Incorrect: objecting before reconstruction breaches the directive and loses the marks reserved for faithful exposition of the author's position.
- **D:** Incorrect: this narrows the thesis to natural evil alone, whereas the sentence explicitly partitions all evil into sin and its punishment.

**Examiner trap 12:** Treat the printed sentence as the object of analysis rather than as a verified verbatim translation; the doctrine is Augustinian, but the exact wording is the examiner's, not a certified rendering.

#### MCQ 13. The three components of Hick's soul-making

Which set of elements is indispensable to Hick's theodicy?

A. Retributive justice, a historical Fall, and the eventual restoration of an original perfection that human beings once actually possessed.

B. Divine hiddenness as an end in itself, the denial of libertarian freedom, and a purely eschatological account of all present goods.

C. The compensation of victims, the punishment of wrongdoers, and the proportioning of happiness to virtue within the present life.

D. Genuine freedom, an epistemic distance from God that leaves the world religiously ambiguous, and a stable law-governed order that can injure.

**Answer: D.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: these are the Augustinian type's components, which Hick explicitly contrasts with the forward-looking Irenaean type he defends.
- **B:** Incorrect: epistemic distance serves freedom rather than being an end in itself, and Hick requires libertarian freedom rather than denying it.
- **C:** Incorrect: this is Kant's structure of the highest good, and soul-making is developmental rather than retributive or distributive.
- **D:** Correct: these three convert the platitude that hardship builds character into a theodicy with a stated rationale and a stated cost.

**Examiner trap 13:** Reducing Hick to "suffering makes us stronger" discards the three components that carry the argument and leaves nothing for an objection to engage with.

#### MCQ 14. Varying the God-concept

The 2024 stem claims that the problem of evil is a direct offshoot of how God is conceptualised in a system. Which treatment best fulfils the directive?

A. Assert that all religions face the problem equally, since suffering is universal and no conceptual variation can alter the force of the argument.

B. Deny the claim outright, since the reality of suffering is independent of theology and therefore cannot depend on any concept of the divine.

C. Run the classical set against classical theism, a finite or process God, an impersonal Absolute and non-creator systems, and price each dissolution.

D. Concede the claim and conclude that theism is refuted, since a problem that varies with the concept cannot be a genuine philosophical problem at all.

**Answer: C.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: non-creator systems face no charge of divine culpability, so the claim of uniform force cannot survive a single comparative test.
- **B:** Incorrect: the reality of suffering is not in dispute; what varies is whether that suffering constitutes an objection to a given God-concept.
- **C:** Correct: showing the dependence requires varying the concept, and the critical half is naming the religiously significant loss each variation incurs.
- **D:** Incorrect: concept-dependence relocates the cost into the theology rather than refuting theism, and the theodicies exist precisely to keep all the attributes.

**Examiner trap 14:** Listing the dissolutions without their costs answers only half the 2024 question, because "critically discuss" buys the price list as much as the mechanism.

#### MCQ 15. The Irenaean and Augustinian types

Which contrast between Hick's two types is stated correctly?

A. The Augustinian type is developmental and teleological, while the Irenaean type is retributive and explains suffering as deserved punishment for sin.

B. The Augustinian type looks back to a fall from an original perfection, while the Irenaean type looks forward to growth from immaturity into likeness.

C. The Augustinian type denies human freedom altogether, while the Irenaean type makes freedom the sole explanation of both moral and natural evil.

D. The Augustinian type is a defence needing only possibility, while the Irenaean type is a theodicy claiming to identify God's actual purposes.

**Answer: B.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: this reverses the two types and would make Irenaeus the source of the retributive framing that Hick contrasts with his own.
- **B:** Correct: the backward-looking Fall and the forward-looking image-to-likeness growth are exactly the axis on which Hick's typology turns.
- **C:** Incorrect: Augustine grounds moral evil in defective will, which presupposes rather than denies creaturely freedom.
- **D:** Incorrect: both are theodicies claiming actual reasons, and the defence category belongs to Plantinga and to skeptical theism instead.

**Examiner trap 15:** The typology is itself examinable content; naming it converts a list of theodicies into a structured account of how the tradition divides.

#### MCQ 16. Rowe's argument and its inductive step

Which statement about the evidential argument is correct?

A. It is a deductive demonstration whose conclusion follows necessarily once the existence of any instance of intense suffering has been conceded.

B. It concerns only moral evil, since natural suffering cannot be described as gratuitous in the absence of an agent who might have prevented it.

C. It begins from the claim that some suffering serves no discernible justifying good, and its weakest point is the step from unseen reason to absent reason.

D. It succeeds only if the theist grants that God's reasons must be transparent to human beings, which no classical theist has ever been willing to concede.

**Answer: C.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: Rowe presents the argument as evidential rather than demonstrative, and he allows that a theist may remain rational in rejecting it.
- **B:** Incorrect: the fawn is a case of natural evil, and it was chosen precisely because no human agency is involved anywhere in it.
- **C:** Correct: the inductive step from "no justifying reason is seen" to "no justifying reason exists" is exactly what skeptical theism attacks.
- **D:** Incorrect: the argument requires only that such reasons would probably be discernible, which is weaker than a demand for full transparency.

**Examiner trap 16:** Presenting the fawn as a deductive contradiction collapses the logical and evidential versions and triggers the most heavily penalised error in this clause.

#### MCQ 17. Wykstra's condition, stated exactly

Which formulation states CORNEA correctly?

A. One may claim that p is absent whenever no evidence for p has been produced, since the burden of proof always rests on the party asserting existence.

B. One may claim that it appears that p is absent only if it is reasonable to believe that p would likely be discernible, given one's cognitive situation, were it there.

C. One may claim that p is absent only if a competent authority within the relevant religious tradition has confirmed that no such thing has ever been observed.

D. One may claim that p is absent only if the claim can be verified by a repeatable procedure, which no claim about divine purposes could ever satisfy.

**Answer: B.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: this is a generic burden-of-proof rule, whereas CORNEA is a specific condition on when an appearance of absence licenses a claim of absence.
- **B:** Correct: the Condition of ReasoNable Epistemic Access ties the legitimacy of a *noseeum* claim to the likelihood of discernment in one's actual cognitive position.
- **C:** Incorrect: appeals to religious authority form no part of Wykstra's epistemology, which is framed entirely in terms of cognitive access.
- **D:** Incorrect: a verificationist criterion would prove far too much and would also eliminate the theistic claims Wykstra is defending.

**Examiner trap 17:** Substituting "God works in mysterious ways" for a stated epistemic principle converts a defensible philosophical position into an unfalsifiable appeal worth no marks.

#### MCQ 18. What skeptical theism costs and what it covers

Consider the following statements about skeptical theism.

1. It is a defence rather than a theodicy, because it attacks an inference instead of identifying God's reason.
2. It targets the evidential argument rather than the logical problem.
3. Its principal cost is that the same scepticism threatens ordinary moral judgement and the duty to rescue.
4. It is refuted outright by the observation that we routinely infer absence from non-observation.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. 1 and 4 only, because the inference from non-observation to absence is a standard and reliable form of everyday reasoning.

B. 2, 3 and 4 only, because skeptical theism claims to state God's reasons while denying that we can identify them.

C. 1 and 2 only, because the moral-scepticism objection was withdrawn once the scope restriction to value-entailments was proposed.

D. 1, 2 and 3 only, because the *noseeum* inference is legitimate in some domains and CORNEA specifies exactly when it fails.

**Answer: D.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: statement 4 misstates the position, since CORNEA concedes that such inferences are often sound and specifies the condition under which they are not.
- **B:** Incorrect: statement 1 is dropped here even though it is true, and the description offered of skeptical theism contradicts its defensive character.
- **C:** Incorrect: the moral-scepticism objection has not been withdrawn, and critics find the value-entailment restriction unstable and *ad hoc*.
- **D:** Correct: the position is a defence against the evidential argument, and its standing price is the threat it poses to ordinary moral confidence.

**Examiner trap 18:** Deploying skeptical theism and a positive theodicy in the same paragraph is self-undermining, because one says the reason is unknowable while the other names it.

#### MCQ 19. Horrendous evils and the distinction that answers them

Which statement captures Adams' position accurately?

A. A horrendous evil is any evil of exceptional magnitude, and it is answered by showing that the world's total value nevertheless exceeds its total disvalue.

B. A horrendous evil is one caused by human cruelty rather than by nature, and it is answered by the free-will defence supplemented by eventual divine judgement.

C. A horrendous evil gives the participant *prima facie* reason to doubt their life is a great good to them, and requires defeat within that life rather than global balancing.

D. A horrendous evil is one for which no explanation is available, and it is answered by conceding that theodicy has failed and that only silence is appropriate.

**Answer: C.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: magnitude alone is not the criterion, and a global sum is precisely the response Adams argues cannot answer the person's own question.
- **B:** Incorrect: perpetrators as well as victims are participants, and the definition is fixed by the threat to a life's overall worth rather than by causal origin.
- **C:** Correct: the definition is technical and the required response is personal-level defeat, which Chisholm's term distinguishes from mere balancing off.
- **D:** Incorrect: Adams offers a positive account through divine identification and beatific intimacy rather than a concession that no answer exists.

**Examiner trap 19:** Using "horrendous evil" as a loose intensifier discards the one distinction — defeat within a life versus balancing across a world — that the term was coined to carry.

#### MCQ 20. Which replies survive the animal-suffering case

Which assessment of the pre-human suffering problem is correct?

A. Free will and retribution fail immediately, soul-making applies only instrumentally through human virtue, and nomic regularity alone partly survives on the Western side.

B. Soul-making succeeds completely, because the suffering animal acquires the virtues of endurance and patience that constitute moral growth in any sentient creature.

C. The problem dissolves once we accept that animals lack sentience altogether, a position now settled by consensus in both philosophy and the empirical sciences.

D. The problem is answered by the fallen-order reply, which is the historically and scientifically best-supported explanation of pre-human natural suffering.

**Answer: A.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Correct: the animal neither chooses nor morally grows, so only the stable-order reply retains any purchase, and even that explains kind rather than amount.
- **B:** Incorrect: moral growth requires an agent capable of freely chosen virtue, which is exactly what the animal sufferer is not.
- **C:** Incorrect: the neo-Cartesian premise about higher-order awareness is philosophically and empirically contested rather than settled.
- **D:** Incorrect: a cosmic fall preceding human history is historically and scientifically strained and is among the weakest replies available.

**Examiner trap 20:** Naming the animal case as the point where two major theodicies both run out is itself a mark-earning move, and omitting it leaves a twenty-marker without its residue paragraph.

#### MCQ 21. Process theodicy and its named layers

Which statement correctly assigns a claim to its process author?

A. Whitehead argued that classical omnipotence is incoherent, Hartshorne introduced the initial aim, and Griffin coined the phrase "fellow-sufferer who understands".

B. Whitehead supplied the dipolar God with primordial and consequent natures, Hartshorne argued omnipotence is incoherent, and Griffin made persuasion a metaphysical necessity.

C. Whitehead defended a self-limiting God who chooses restraint, Hartshorne systematised the theology, and Griffin treated evil as privation of a due good.

D. Whitehead denied that God has any nature at all, Hartshorne restored divine immutability, and Griffin argued that evil is necessary for the greater good.

**Answer: B.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: the incoherence argument is Hartshorne's, the initial aim is Whitehead's, and the fellow-sufferer phrase closes *Process and Reality*.
- **B:** Correct: this assigns the dipolar structure, the incoherence argument and the metaphysical-necessity move to their actual authors.
- **C:** Incorrect: chosen self-limitation is Plantinga's structure rather than Whitehead's, and privation belongs to Augustine rather than to Griffin.
- **D:** Incorrect: the dipolar doctrine is Whitehead's own, and process thought explicitly rejects both immutability and the greater-good instrumentalisation of evil.

**Examiner trap 21:** Writing "process theodicy (Whitehead)" and stopping forfeits the marks reserved for naming which layer of the position is doing the work in the argument.

#### MCQ 22. The reply-space around the classical theodicies

Which pairing of a response with its principal cost is accurate?

A. The Leibnizian best-world response costs nothing philosophically, because a perfect being must by definition select the unique optimum among possible worlds.

B. Eschatological fulfilment costs nothing, since a later restoration automatically converts any permitted suffering into a justified means to that restoration.

C. The greater-good and stable-order response may explain the *kind* of world without explaining the *amount* or *distribution* of suffering within it.

D. Protest or anti-theodicy costs nothing, because refusing to justify suffering settles the logical and evidential arguments in the objector's favour at once.

**Answer: C.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: there may be no unique best world, and world-total optimisation can neglect goodness to the particular persons who bear the losses.
- **B:** Incorrect: compensation after suffering is not automatically justification for permitting it, which is exactly the balancing-versus-defeat point.
- **C:** Correct: risk and regular law can be shown necessary for certain goods without thereby accounting for the observed quantity or allocation of harm.
- **D:** Incorrect: protest diagnoses the ethics of explanation but leaves the logical and evidential arguments themselves entirely unsettled.

**Examiner trap 22:** Every response in this space has a stated cost; a script that lists responses without costs has described the literature instead of evaluating it.

#### MCQ 23. Karma across the Indian schools

Which statement about the Indian responses to suffering is correct?

A. Mīmāṃsā, Buddhist and Jain accounts require no divine distributor, while Nyāya and theistic Vedānta assign God a just administrative role over karmic fruit.

B. Every Indian school treats karma as administered by a personal creator God, which is why the Indian tradition faces the same problem of evil as classical theism.

C. Karma is presented in all schools as a doctrine with an identifiable first act, since a beginningless series would make the assignment of desert impossible.

D. Advaita denies that suffering occurs at all, and therefore has no need of any account of the empirical distribution of pain among embodied beings.

**Answer: A.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Correct: the theistic and non-theistic accounts differ precisely on whether a divine dispenser is required, and Nyāya's Lord allots according to desert rather than arbitrarily.
- **B:** Incorrect: the non-theistic schools drop the defendant entirely, which is the structural reason the Indian material can dissolve the classical problem.
- **C:** Incorrect: the series is standardly treated as beginningless, which blocks the demand for a first wrongful act without verifying any particular past cause.
- **D:** Incorrect: Advaita locates suffering at the empirical level and treats it as sublated only under liberating knowledge at the ultimate level.

**Examiner trap 23:** Presenting karma as a single theistic doctrine across the schools is the canonical error, and it also destroys the comparison that makes the Indian material worth citing.

#### MCQ 24. Evil and the profane as religion-forming categories

Which statement about the 2019 owner-module is correct?

A. The profane is a synonym for the evil, so Durkheim's binary is a moral classification of actions into the permitted and the forbidden.

B. Otto's holy and Durkheim's sacred are interchangeable terms, since both denote the social classification through which a moral community is constituted.

C. Evil and the profane found religion structurally by supplying the predicament, the boundary, the institutions and the soteriological task.

D. Eliade's hierophany describes the moral judgement by which a community declares certain acts sinful and therefore excludes them from communal life.

**Answer: C.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: Durkheim's profane is simply the ordinary and non-consecrated, and conflating it with the wicked is the commonest error on this stem.
- **B:** Incorrect: Otto's numinous holy is the object-side of religious response, whereas Durkheim's sacred is a social classification.
- **C:** Correct: the four-step sequence is the functional account the directive demands, and it founds religion rather than theism.
- **D:** Incorrect: hierophany is the irruption of the sacred generating sacred space and time, not a moral verdict on conduct.

**Examiner trap 24:** Answering this functional-sociological stem with theodicies answers a different question, because the 2019 examiner asks what evil and the profane *do*, not whether they disprove God.

### Remedial MCQs — common-error repair

These eight drills target the specific errors that the canonical trap list identifies as recurring on this clause. Attempt them only after the twenty-four core diagnostics.

#### MCQ 25. Repairing the mismatch of reply to version

A candidate answers a stem on the amount and distribution of suffering by reconstructing the free-will defence in full. Which correction is required?

A. None; the free-will defence is the standard reply to every formulation, and its coverage of moral evil is sufficient for any stem on suffering.

B. The candidate should have used the free-will defence but added a devotional conclusion, since an evidential stem calls for pastoral rather than analytic treatment.

C. The candidate should have replaced the free-will defence with the Augustinian privation account, which alone addresses the quantity of suffering in the world.

D. The candidate should have named the evidential version and answered with a theodicy, skeptical theism or a revised God-concept rather than a bare consistency defence.

**Answer: D.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: the defence answers only the logical charge, and the amount of suffering is untouched by any argument that establishes bare possibility.
- **B:** Incorrect: a devotional conclusion is penalised at every band, and the fault here is structural rather than one of tone.
- **C:** Incorrect: privation addresses the metaphysical status of evil rather than its quantity, so the substitution repairs nothing.
- **D:** Correct: the evidential burden is probability, and only a positive reason, a blocked inference or a changed God-concept can discharge it.

**Examiner trap 25:** Version-mismatch is invisible to the candidate and obvious to the examiner, which is why naming the version explicitly in the opening is worth more than any additional content.

#### MCQ 26. Repairing "the triad is self-contradictory on its face"

A script asserts that omnipotence, perfect goodness and the existence of evil are contradictory without further argument. What must be supplied?

A. A definition of evil as a positive substance, since only a substantial evil could stand in genuine logical opposition to divine goodness.

B. The two bridging principles about what a good being does and what an omnipotent being can do, without which no formal contradiction arises.

C. An empirical survey of the quantity of suffering, since the contradiction emerges only once the scale of the world's evil is established.

D. A demonstration that God's goodness must be recognisable to creatures, since an unrecognisable goodness could not conflict with anything observable.

**Answer: B.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: substantiality is Augustine's target rather than a requirement of Mackie's argument, which is about the consistency of propositions.
- **B:** Correct: the goodness and power bridges convert a merely uncomfortable set into an inconsistent one, and each is separately contestable.
- **C:** Incorrect: quantity belongs to the evidential version, and the logical charge is meant to hold even for a single instance of evil.
- **D:** Incorrect: recognisability concerns divine hiddenness, which is a distinct issue and no part of the logical formulation.

**Examiner trap 26:** The 2020 stem is answered in the bridges; a script that omits them has written the question's premise rather than its answer.

#### MCQ 27. Repairing "privation means the pain is not real"

A script argues that because Augustine calls evil a privation, Augustinian theodicy denies the reality of suffering. What is the correct repair?

A. The script is right, and this is precisely why the privation account was abandoned by later Christian theology in favour of the Irenaean approach.

B. The script should add that privation applies only to moral evil, so that natural suffering remains fully real while moral evil is merely an absence.

C. The script should recognise that a privation is a real lack of a good that ought to be present, so blindness is a determinate deprivation rather than an illusion.

D. The script should replace privation with the process account, since only a God of persuasive power can take the reality of suffering seriously.

**Answer: C.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: the account was not abandoned, and its central claim concerns the metaphysical status of evil rather than the phenomenology of pain.
- **B:** Incorrect: privation is not restricted to moral evil, and the proposed split has no basis in the Augustinian framework.
- **C:** Correct: the account denies evil an independent substance while affirming that the lack is real in a subject whose nature calls for the missing good.
- **D:** Incorrect: substituting a different theodicy avoids the misreading instead of correcting it, and the question asks for a repair.

**Examiner trap 27:** Rot in wood is the canonical illustration: the rot is not a rival substance, and it is not therefore imaginary — it is the real loss of the wood's proper integrity.

#### MCQ 28. Repairing "soul-making just means suffering makes us stronger"

Which repair restores Hick's argument?

A. Add epistemic distance, genuine freedom and a stable law-governed order, and distinguish the developmental rationale from retributive punishment.

B. Add the claim that every instance of suffering produces a proportionate increase in virtue, which is what makes the theodicy explanatorily complete.

C. Add the claim that Irenaeus coined the phrase "vale of Soul-making", which supplies the patristic authority the modern version otherwise lacks.

D. Add the observation that soul-making covers animal suffering directly, since animals also develop resilience through the hardships they endure.

**Answer: A.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Correct: these three components convert a platitude into a theodicy with a rationale, and the contrast with punishment fixes its forward-looking character.
- **B:** Incorrect: Hick makes no proportionality claim, and asserting one exposes the theodicy to the scale objection in its strongest form.
- **C:** Incorrect: the phrase is Keats's from the spring 1819 journal-letter, and attributing it to Irenaeus is a marked factual error.
- **D:** Incorrect: the animal does not morally grow, so extended soul-making reaches animals only instrumentally through human virtue toward them.

**Examiner trap 28:** Keats supplied the phrase, Irenaeus the image-and-likeness theology and Hick the modern theodicy; collapsing the three is the single commonest attribution error in this topic.

#### MCQ 29. Repairing "the process God is simply powerless"

Which statement corrects the misdescription?

A. The process God possesses full classical omnipotence but voluntarily declines to use it, which is why evil occurs without divine culpability.

B. The process God has no relation to the world at all, so questions of power and culpability do not arise within the process framework.

C. The process God is powerless in the strict sense, and process writers accept that this makes their deity unworthy of worship by their own admission.

D. The process God exercises persuasive power by supplying each occasion its initial aim, and cannot coerce because to be actual is to have some self-determination.

**Answer: D.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: voluntary restraint is Plantinga's structure, whereas Griffin's point is that the limitation is metaphysically necessary rather than chosen.
- **B:** Incorrect: the consequent nature makes God supremely related to the world, which is the opposite of detachment.
- **C:** Incorrect: the worship objection is raised by critics and answered by appeal to solidarity and objective immortality, not conceded by process writers.
- **D:** Correct: persuasion through the initial aim is a real and maximal form of power, limited by the distributed self-determination of actual entities.

**Examiner trap 29:** The modal difference is the examinable point: Griffin's God *cannot* coerce, while Plantinga's God *chooses* not to intervene.

#### MCQ 30. Repairing "karma shows that this person deserved it"

A script infers from a person's suffering that they must have committed a specific wrong in a previous life. What is the correct repair?

A. The inference is unavailable to ordinary observers, and using karma this way converts explanation into victim-blaming without any epistemic warrant.

B. The inference is sound but should be stated compassionately, since the doctrine's truth is unaffected by the distress that stating it may cause.

C. The inference is valid only in theistic schools, where a divine dispenser guarantees that each specific suffering matches a specific past act.

D. The inference should be replaced by the claim that suffering is illusory, which is the Advaitic solution to the difficulty the script has created.

**Answer: A.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Correct: the mechanism is not observationally accessible, so no particular desert can be read off a particular case, and the misuse is a moral as well as epistemic error.
- **B:** Incorrect: the inference is not sound, and presenting an unwarranted judgement gently does not make it warranted.
- **C:** Incorrect: even where God allots fruit justly, the specific karmic history remains beyond the senses and known only through testimony.
- **D:** Incorrect: Advaita places suffering at the empirical level rather than denying it, and the substitution introduces a second error.

**Examiner trap 30:** Karma does not cancel present duties of compassion, protection and relief; a causal explanation is never moral permission to withhold aid, and saying so protects the answer's ethical credibility.

#### MCQ 31. Repairing "the profane is the evil"

A script treating the 2019 stem writes that the profane is the wicked and the sacred the righteous. What is the correct repair?

A. Replace the pair with the pure and impure, since ritual pollution is the only category that Durkheim's sociology actually recognises.

B. Distinguish four categories — evil as moral or natural harm, the profane as the ordinary, the impure as the ritually polluting and the demonic as the anti-sacred.

#### MCQ 32. Repairing "God's ways are mysterious" as a philosophical position

A script offers divine mystery as its answer to the evidential argument. What is the correct repair?

A. Replace mystery with the free-will defence, since a defence is always preferable to a theodicy when the burden is one of probability.

B. Retain mystery and add that it is unfalsifiable, since unfalsifiability protects the claim from any possible empirical objection.

## PYQS AND ANSWER PRACTICE

### Solved conceptual drills and distinction loops

Five short drills that fix the distinctions the examiner tests before any full answer is attempted.

**Drill 1 — Defence or theodicy?** Plantinga offers a **defence**: he claims only that a morally sufficient reason is *possible*, so no evidence for transworld depravity is required. Hick offers a **theodicy**: he claims to identify God's *actual* purpose, and is therefore refuted if soul-making is not in fact the purpose.

**Drill 2 — Explanation or justification?** Karma *explains* why this person suffers by locating a cause; it does not thereby *justify* the suffering or license withholding aid. Confusing the two is how an explanatory doctrine becomes victim-blaming.

**Drill 3 — Privation or illusion?** Privation says evil is a real lack of a due good, not a created substance. Blindness is a determinate deprivation in a creature whose nature calls for sight; it is not an illusion, and Augustine never says it is.

**Drill 4 — Balancing off or defeat?** Balancing off outweighs the evil within a larger sum, leaving it external to the good. Defeat — Chisholm's term — integrates the evil into a whole whose value it contributes to. Adams demands the second, and demands it *within the individual life*.

**Drill 5 — Skeptical theism or positive theodicy?** Skeptical theism says we cannot tell whether a justifying reason exists. A theodicy says what the reason is. Holding both in the same paragraph is self-undermining, and the examiner notices.

### PYQ 1 — 2019, Q6(b), 15 marks

#### Exact question

**"What role do the concepts of evil and profane play to provide a firm foundation to religion?"**

#### Demand decoding

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Directive | *What role do A and B play to provide a firm foundation to…* — a **functional** account of how negative categories constitute religion, not a definition of either term |
| Core issue | Whether religion is founded on a diagnosis of what is wrong rather than on belief in God |
| Scope boundary | This is the sociological-phenomenological question; the theistic problem of evil is **not** what is asked |
| Ideal thesis | Evil and the profane found religion **structurally rather than doctrinally** — and they found *religion*, not *theism* |
| Non-negotiables | Durkheim, Otto and Eliade correctly distinguished; the profane ≠ evil correction; the four-step founding sequence; Indian parity with its rider |

#### Model answer (≈250 words)

The question is functional, not theological: it asks what work negative categories do in constituting religion. **Durkheim**, in *The Elementary Forms of Religious Life* (1912), holds that all known religious beliefs presuppose a classification of all things into two opposed classes, **profane** and **sacred**, and defines religion as a unified system of beliefs and practices relative to sacred things, uniting adherents into a single moral community, a church. The definition is deliberately God-free. ⚠️ A correction must be made at once: the **profane is the ordinary, not the wicked**; evil, impurity and the demonic are three further and distinct categories.

**Otto**'s *The Idea of the Holy* (1917) supplies the object-side — the **numinous**, *mysterium tremendum et fascinans* — while **Eliade**'s *The Sacred and the Profane* (1957) shows the sacred irrupting as **hierophany**, generating sacred space around an *axis mundi* and recoverable sacred time.

The founding sequence follows. A religion first names a **predicament** — sin, impurity, suffering (*duḥkha*), ignorance (*avidyā*), karmic bondage. The predicament is marked by a **boundary** — pure and impure, sacred and profane — which generates ritual, prohibition and moral code. The boundary generates **institutions**: priesthood, initiation, purification, penance. The predicament generates a **soteriology**, the ordered path out.

Indian material is the clearest case: the Buddhist **First Noble Truth** makes suffering the explicit foundation of an entire religion; Jain *āsrava* and *bandha* name the predicament and *saṃvara* and *nirjarā* the exit. ⚠️ India's founding defect is more often ignorance or bondage than moral evil, which yields diagnosis-and-insight rather than sin-and-forgiveness.

**Verdict:** the binary is an ideal type, not a law, and wonder and gratitude are independent religious roots; but evil and the profane found religion **structurally** — they supply the predicament and the boundary without which soteriology would be idle. They found religion, not theism.

#### Why this earns marks

- **Structure:** functional framing → Durkheim with the profane correction → Otto and Eliade → four-step sequence → Indian parity → ideal-type qualification → verdict.
- **Doctrine:** sacred/profane classification; the God-free definition of religion; the numinous; hierophany, *axis mundi*, recoverable time.
- **Distinction:** evil ≠ profane ≠ impure ≠ demonic, made in the first paragraph rather than as an afterthought.
- **Comparison:** the Buddhist First Noble Truth as the world's clearest case of evil as religion-founding.

### PYQ 2 — 2020, Q6(b), 15 marks

#### Exact question

**"What is the logical problem of evil? Discuss."**

#### Demand decoding

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Directive | *What is… Discuss* — state the **set** precisely, then evaluate; a lament about suffering earns nothing |
| Core issue | Whether the theist's propositions are *formally* inconsistent |
| Scope boundary | The logical version only; the evidential problem appears at the end as the place the debate migrates to |
| Ideal thesis | The set is inconsistent **only with the bridging premises**, and the whole debate is a fight over those premises |
| Non-negotiables | Mackie 1955; the numbered set including omniscience; both bridges; adequate/fallacious framing; Plantinga's defence; the migration to Rowe |

#### Model answer (≈255 words)

The logical problem alleges that a set of theistic beliefs cannot all be true. **J. L. Mackie**, "Evil and Omnipotence" (*Mind*, 1955), sets out the propositions: God is **omnipotent**; God is **perfectly good**; and **evil exists**. The full classical profile adds **omniscience**, which blocks the reply that evil escaped divine notice.

Crucially, these propositions are **not formally contradictory by themselves**. Nothing in them, plus logic alone, entails both a proposition and its negation. The contradiction is manufactured by two **bridging principles**: first, that a perfectly good being eliminates evil as far as it can; second, that an omnipotent being has no relevant limit on preventing any evil whose prevention is logically possible. Mackie sorts the replies into **adequate solutions**, which honestly abandon one proposition, and **fallacious solutions**, which appear to keep all three while covertly limiting power or redefining goodness.

The theist has three exits, each attacking a bridge. Insert "**unless there is a morally sufficient reason**" into the goodness bridge, and the whole space of defences and theodicies opens. Deny the power bridge and one gets **Plantinga's free-will defence**: significant freedom entails genuine alternatives, God cannot causally determine a creature to freely choose only rightly, so a world containing moral good may be unavailable without moral evil. Plantinga needs only **possibility**, which is why transworld depravity is asserted as possible and never as actual. Denying the reality of evil — privation, or the empirical level in Advaita — is the third and most costly exit.

**Verdict:** the logical problem is widely regarded as answered; the live debate has migrated to **Rowe's evidential** form, where consistency no longer suffices.

#### Why this earns marks

- **Structure:** numbered set → why it is not a formal contradiction → the two bridges → the three exits → verdict and migration.
- **Doctrine:** Mackie's article and date; the adequate/fallacious taxonomy; omniscience in the full profile.
- **Argument:** the bridges are written as separate premises, so the objections have something determinate to attack.
- **Distinction:** defence versus theodicy, deployed to explain why possibility suffices for Plantinga.
- **Technical terms:** inconsistent set, bridging premise, morally sufficient reason, transworld depravity.
- **Criticism:** the third exit's cost — denying evil's reality — is stated rather than passed over.
- **Conclusion:** naming the migration to the evidential problem is the highest-value sentence available in this question.

#### Compact skeleton and timing

```text
1 min  : Define the logical problem as a consistency charge
3 min  : The propositions as a numbered set, including omniscience
3 min  : Why the bare set is not a formal contradiction
3 min  : Bridge A and Bridge B, written out
3 min  : Three exits — morally sufficient reason, Plantinga, privation/levels
1 min  : Mackie's adequate/fallacious framing
1 min  : Verdict — answered; the debate migrates to Rowe
Total  : 15 minutes
```

#### Common mistakes and examiner penalties

- Asserting contradiction without the bridges: **concedes the analytical core of the question**.
- Confusing Mackie's charge with Rowe's: **version error, the most penalised fault in this clause**.
- Writing that Mackie "proved God does not exist": **overstatement; he argued a set is inconsistent**.
- Omitting omniscience: **leaves the ignorance reply open and weakens the profile**.
- Presenting Plantinga as supplying God's actual reason: **misdescribes a defence as a theodicy**.
- Ending without the migration: **loses the one sentence that shows command of the current debate**.

### PYQ 3 — 2021, Q5(e), 10 marks

#### Exact question

**"Discuss the paradox of omnipotence of God in the context of the existence of evil."**

#### Demand decoding

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Directive | *Discuss the paradox of…* — a paradox stated as a **dilemma with both horns and both costs**, then the restriction that dissolves it |
| Core issue | Whether unrestricted power over things and unrestricted power over what powers exist can coexist |
| Scope boundary | The context clause is mandatory: the paradox must be connected to evil, not discussed in the abstract |
| Ideal thesis | Unrestricted omnipotence in both senses is incoherent, and the free-will defence requires the restriction |
| Non-negotiables | Mackie 1955; first-order and second-order power; the three restrictions; the Plantinga/Griffin modal contrast |

#### Model answer (≈160 words)

**Mackie** (*Mind*, 1955) frames the paradox as a dilemma: can an omnipotent being create things it cannot afterwards control? If it **cannot**, its **second-order** power — power to determine what powers exist — is limited. If it **can and does**, its **first-order** power over those things is limited. Unrestricted omnipotence in both senses is therefore incoherent.

Three restrictions dissolve it. Omnipotence may extend only to the **logically possible**, which almost all theists concede. Or it may be **second-order sovereignty freely self-bound** in the act of creating free agents. Or it may be abandoned, as in **process theism**, where power is intrinsically distributed and divine power is persuasive by metaphysical necessity.

The connection to evil is exact. The **free-will defence** requires one of these restrictions: God creates significantly free agents and cannot causally guarantee that they choose well. For Plantinga this restraint is **chosen**; for Griffin it is **metaphysically necessary**.

**Verdict:** the paradox does not refute theism; it forces theism to specify which sense of omnipotence it is defending, and to pay for the choice.

#### Why this earns marks

- **Structure:** dilemma → both horns with costs → three restrictions → the link to evil → graded verdict.
- **Doctrine:** the first-order/second-order distinction, which is the paradox's actual content.
- **Argument:** each horn is priced rather than merely stated.
- **Distinction:** chosen self-limitation versus metaphysical necessity, named with their authors.
- **Technical terms:** second-order sovereignty, self-binding, logically possible, persuasive power.
- **Criticism:** the self-binding route re-opens the intervention question, which the verdict acknowledges.
- **Conclusion:** the paradox forces a specification rather than a refutation — the correctly graded ending for ten marks.

#### Compact skeleton and timing

```text
1 min  : State the dilemma in one sentence
2 min  : Horn 1 and Horn 2, each with its cost
3 min  : The three restrictions, with the process option last
2 min  : The link to evil via the free-will defence
1 min  : Plantinga chooses; Griffin cannot
1 min  : Verdict — specification, not refutation
Total  : 10 minutes
```

#### Common mistakes and examiner penalties

- Using only the heavy-stone form: **a riddle rather than an analysis**.
- Omitting the connection to evil: **the stem's context clause is explicit and its omission is penalised**.
- Claiming the paradox disproves God: **overstatement; it shows an incoherence in one construal**.
- Merging Plantinga's chosen restraint with Griffin's necessity: **conflates a defence with a theodicy**.
- Drifting into the general coherence of omnipotence: **that belongs to Notions of God**.
- Exceeding the band: **a ten-marker with four theodicies has no analysis left in it**.

### PYQ 4 — 2022, Q6(c), 15 marks

#### Exact question

**"If God is the Absolute Creator, then the responsibility of the evil cannot belong to the human agent. Critically examine."**

#### Demand decoding

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Directive | *Critically examine [a quoted thesis]* — fidelity to the claim's **scope** before evaluation |
| Core issue | Whether absolute creation transfers moral authorship from the creature to the creator |
| Scope boundary | The stem concerns **moral** evil and human agency; natural evil is the residue, not the subject |
| Ideal thesis | The conditional fails as stated, but its rejoinder — that knowingly instantiating the system remains morally relevant — survives |
| Non-negotiables | Free-will defence; privation; foreknowledge ≠ causation; the process alternative; the retained rejoinder |

#### Model answer (≈255 words)

The stem asserts a conditional: absolute creation entails that human agents cannot be responsible for evil. Its scope must be fixed first — it concerns **moral evil**, since no one attributes an earthquake to a human agent.

Three replies resist the conditional. First, the **free-will defence**: God creates the *capacity* for choice, while the agent supplies the *act*. Significant freedom entails genuine alternatives, and God cannot causally determine a creature to freely choose rightly — the description cancels itself. A world of programmed beneficence lacks the moral authorship of a world containing genuinely responsible agents. Second, **Augustine's privation account**: God creates natures, all of which are good as created; evil is a **privation of a due good** arising in a **defective will**, so what God creates and what goes wrong are not the same item. Third, **foreknowledge is not causation**: knowing what an agent will freely do does not make the agent do it.

A fourth route rejects the antecedent. **Process theism** denies that God has the unilateral power the stem presupposes: power is intrinsically distributed, so no being determines another's becoming.

⚠️ The critical half must concede what survives. Even granting that God does not cause the act, **knowingly choosing to instantiate the whole system, foreseeing its outcomes, remains morally relevant**. Foreknowledge does not cause free action, but it does not discharge the decision to create.

**Verdict:** the conditional is false as stated — creaturely authorship is preserved — but it is not empty. It correctly identifies a residual divine responsibility for the *system*, which the free-will defence answers by pricing rather than by denying.

#### Why this earns marks

- **Structure:** fix the scope → three replies to the conditional → one reply rejecting the antecedent → the retained rejoinder → graded verdict.
- **Doctrine:** capacity versus act; *privatio boni*; the self-cancelling description of determined freedom.
- **Argument:** each reply is attached to the exact step of the conditional it blocks.
- **Distinction:** causing an act versus instantiating a system in which the act occurs.
- **Technical terms:** significant freedom, defective will, privation, unilateral power.
- **Criticism:** the rejoinder is conceded rather than suppressed, which is what "critically" buys.
- **Conclusion:** false as stated but not empty — a graded verdict rather than a flat rejection.

#### Compact skeleton and timing

```text
1 min  : Fix the scope — moral evil, human agency
4 min  : Free-will defence: capacity vs act; the self-cancelling description
3 min  : Privation: God creates natures, not defects
2 min  : Foreknowledge is not causation
2 min  : Process denial of the antecedent
2 min  : The retained rejoinder about instantiating the system
1 min  : Verdict — false as stated, not empty
Total  : 15 minutes
```

#### Common mistakes and examiner penalties

- Accepting the conditional: **abandons the critical directive**.
- Answering with natural evil: **out of scope; the stem is about human agency**.
- Suppressing the rejoinder: **produces an uncritical defence and caps the script below the strong band**.
- Treating foreknowledge as the whole issue: **the stem is about creation, not omniscience**.
- Using the process reply as the only answer: **changes the subject rather than examining the claim**.
- Ending with a devotional sentence: **penalised at every band on this clause**.

### PYQ 5 — 2023, Q8(a), 20 marks

#### Exact question

**"All evil is either sin or punishment for sin." — St. Augustine. Critically discuss.**

#### Demand decoding

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Directive | *"Quotation." — Critically discuss* — reconstruct the author's own system **first**, then test it |
| Core issue | Whether the sin-and-punishment partition covers all evil |
| Scope boundary | Augustine's framework in full, then the standing objections; rivals appear as comparators, not as substitutes |
| Ideal thesis | The thesis preserves divine goodness for moral evil but is incomplete for natural, innocent and animal suffering |
| Non-negotiables | Privation metaphysics; defective will; the Fall; the guilt-versus-condition distinction; Hick and karma as comparators; ❓ status of the printed wording |

#### Model answer (≈350 words)

Augustine's sentence partitions all evil into **sin** and its **punishment**. The metaphysics behind it must be stated before it is tested. Being, insofar as it is being, is **good**; God creates beings, not evil substances; corruption is **parasitic**, depending on a good nature to corrupt; and moral fault is **disordered willing**. Evil is therefore **privation of a due good (*privatio boni*)**, not a rival creative principle. ⚠️ Privation does not make pain unreal: rot is not a rival substance to wood but the real loss of the wood's proper integrity, and blindness is a determinate lack in a creature whose nature calls for sight.

On this basis the partition works as follows. **Moral evil is sin** — the misuse of created freedom. **Natural evil is punishment** — the consequence of a fallen, disordered order following the Fall.

The critical half has four parts. First, **innocent suffering**: infants and the congenitally afflicted fit a retributive reading poorly. Second, **animal and pre-human suffering**: no free-will defence and no retributive account applies to a creature that neither chooses nor sins, and ❓ a cosmic fall preceding human history is historically and scientifically strained. Third, **the metaphysics of privation** is strained for intense positive pain, which does not feel like an absence. Fourth, and most sharply, **inherited guilt**: the claim that later persons are *personally guilty* for an ancestor's act is far harder to defend than the claim that they inherit a **damaged condition** and a disordered social-natural order. ⚠️ The second reading reduces the objection but still owes an account of why a good creator permits the condition and its unequal effects.

Two comparators sharpen the verdict. **Hick's soul-making** replaces punishment with **development**: humans are created immature, at an epistemic distance, in a law-governed world where courage and compassion become possible. **Indian karma** replaces a single Fall with a **beginningless** series and thereby answers the **distribution** question — why *this* person — that Augustine cannot; its own cost is unverifiability and the risk of victim-blaming.

**Verdict:** treating the printed sentence as the object of analysis rather than as a certified translation ❓, the thesis succeeds for moral evil and preserves divine goodness in creation, but it is incomplete for natural, innocent and animal suffering, where a developmental or distributional account does more work.

#### Why this earns marks

- **Structure:** metaphysics first → the partition → four objections → two comparators → graded verdict.
- **Doctrine:** convertibility of being and goodness; parasitic corruption; defective will; the Fall.
- **Argument:** the partition is derived from the metaphysics rather than asserted alongside it.
- **Distinction:** inherited guilt versus inherited condition — the sharpest available critical instrument on this stem.
- **Technical terms:** *privatio boni*, disordered willing, epistemic distance, beginninglessness.
- **Comparison:** Hick on development and karma on distribution, each with its own cost stated.
- **Conclusion:** success-with-remainder rather than flat refutation, plus the quotation-discipline note.

#### Compact skeleton and timing

```text
2 min  : State the thesis and announce the reconstruct-then-test order
5 min  : Privation metaphysics as four premises + rot/blindness examples
3 min  : The partition: sin and punishment
5 min  : Four objections — innocence, animals, strained privation, inherited guilt
3 min  : Hick on development; karma on distribution, each with cost
2 min  : Verdict + quotation-discipline note
Total  : 20 minutes
```

#### Common mistakes and examiner penalties

- Objecting before reconstructing: **breaches the directive and forfeits the exposition marks**.
- Saying privation makes pain imaginary: **attacks a position no Augustinian holds**.
- Treating the printed sentence as a certified verbatim translation: **overstates the textual claim**.
- Merging inherited guilt with inherited condition: **loses the sharpest critical distinction available**.
- Omitting animal suffering: **the residue the examiner expects in a twenty-marker**.
- Concluding that Augustine simply fails: **ungraded verdict; the correct finding is success-with-remainder**.

### PYQ 6 — 2024, Q6(c), 15 marks

#### Exact question

**"The problem of evil is a direct offshoot of how God is conceptualised in a system." Critically discuss.**

#### Demand decoding

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Directive | *Critically discuss* a thesis about **dependence** — show the dependence by varying the God-concept, then price each variation |
| Core issue | Whether the argument's force is fixed by the concept of God under defence |
| Scope boundary | Four God-concepts, not a survey of every theodicy; the theodicies enter as the attempt to keep all attributes |
| Ideal thesis | The claim is correct, but concept-dependence is not a solution: it relocates the cost into the theology |
| Non-negotiables | The classical set; process/finite God; Advaita's levels; non-creator systems; the cost of each dissolution |

#### Model answer (≈255 words)

The thesis is correct, and its demonstration is straightforward. State the classical set: God is **omnipotent**, **omniscient**, **perfectly good** and **creator**, and evil exists; the contradiction requires the bridging principles that a good being eliminates evil as far as it can and that omnipotence has no relevant limit. The argument bites only where all four attributes are held together.

Now vary the concept. Weaken **omnipotence** and the **process** God, whose power is persuasive by metaphysical necessity, is simply not the kind of being who could have prevented the evil unilaterally — the charge lapses. Weaken **personality** and **Advaita's** *nirguṇa* Brahman is untouched: evil is real at the empirical level (*vyāvahārika*) and sublated under liberating knowledge at the ultimate level (*pāramārthika*). Remove the **creator** and Buddhism, Jainism and Mīmāṃsā have **no defendant** at all: suffering is explained by conditions and by deeds, and the moral law is self-operating. Only classical theism carries the full burden.

⚠️ The critical half is the price list. The process route surrenders sovereignty and any guarantee of final victory over evil. Advaita risks minimising present suffering. Non-creator systems give up providence, divine rescue and a personal guarantor of justice. Concept-dependence is therefore not a *solution*: it moves the cost from the argument into the theology. The theodicies — free-will, soul-making, privation — exist precisely because some theists refuse to pay that price and try to keep every attribute.

**Verdict:** the problem is a stress-test of a *particular* God-concept, not of religion as such — which is why the same suffering that refutes one theology leaves another entirely untouched.

#### Why this earns marks

- **Structure:** state the set → vary the concept four ways → price each dissolution → verdict.
- **Doctrine:** the bridging premises; persuasive power; the two-level Advaitic ontology; self-operating moral law.
- **Argument:** the dependence is *shown* by variation rather than merely asserted.
- **Distinction:** dissolution versus solution — concept-dependence relocates rather than removes the difficulty.
- **Technical terms:** *nirguṇa*, *vyāvahārika*, *pāramārthika*, persuasive power, self-operating law.
- **Comparison:** three non-classical routes, each with a named tradition and a named loss.
- **Conclusion:** stress-test of one God-concept, not of religion — the exact verdict the stem invites.

#### Compact skeleton and timing

```text
1 min  : Accept the thesis and announce the method — vary the concept
3 min  : The classical set with its bridges
3 min  : Weaken omnipotence — process
2 min  : Weaken personality — Advaita's two levels
3 min  : Remove the creator — Buddhism, Jainism, Mīmāṃsā
2 min  : The price list for all three
1 min  : Verdict — stress-test of a concept, not of religion
Total  : 15 minutes
```

#### Common mistakes and examiner penalties

- Listing dissolutions without costs: **answers half the question**.
- Concluding that theism is refuted: **misreads dependence as defeat**.
- Saying Advaita denies suffering: **factual error; it locates suffering at the empirical level**.
- Treating non-creator systems as evasions: **they answer a different question and should be graded, not dismissed**.
- Surveying every theodicy: **the stem asks about God-concepts, not about the catalogue of replies**.
- Omitting the bridges: **the variation has nothing determinate to act on**.

### PYQ 7 — 2025, Q6(b), 15 marks

#### Exact question

**"Explain the main tenets of the Process Theodicy as an explanation of the problem of evil."**

#### Demand decoding

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Directive | *Explain the main tenets of X* — **enumerated doctrine, correctly attributed**; number the tenets |
| Core issue | How a revision of omnipotence answers evil without justifying permission |
| Scope boundary | Process thought specifically; other non-classical revisions appear only in the closing evaluation |
| Ideal thesis | Process theodicy purchases God's innocence at the price of God's sovereignty |
| Non-negotiables | Six tenets; Whitehead, Hartshorne, Griffin and Cobb named separately; the Griffin/Plantinga modal contrast; the worship objection |

#### Model answer (≈260 words)

Process theodicy answers evil by **revising omnipotence**, not by justifying permission. Its tenets are six.

**(1) Creativity, not God, is the ultimate**; actual entities are self-creative occasions of experience. **(2) God is dipolar**: the **primordial nature** is the ordered envisagement of possibilities, supplying each occasion its **initial aim**; the **consequent nature** receives and preserves what the world becomes. **(3) Power is intrinsically distributed**: to be actual is to possess some self-determination. **(4) Divine power is therefore persuasive by metaphysical necessity** — for **Griffin**, God *cannot* coerce, which is what separates process theodicy from the free-will defence, where God *chooses* not to intervene. **(5) Evil is the deviation of finite occasions from the divine lure**; God is not culpable because God could not unilaterally prevent it. **(6) God is "the fellow-sufferer who understands"** (**Whitehead**) — solidarity, not detachment.

The layers must be named separately. **Whitehead**, *Process and Reality* (1929), supplies the dipolar God, the initial aim and the consequent nature. **Hartshorne**, *The Divine Relativity* (1948) and *Omnipotence and Other Theological Mistakes* (1984), argues that classical omnipotence is **incoherent**: to have all the power is to leave none to anything else. **Griffin**, *God, Power, and Evil* (1976) and *Evil Revisited* (1991), supplies the metaphysical-necessity move. **Cobb**'s *A Christian Natural Theology* systematises the theology.

Criticism: a God who cannot guarantee victory over evil may be **unworthy of worship**; the metaphysics is contested; and classical theists deny that actuality entails self-determination. The replies are divine solidarity and the **objective immortality** of achieved value in the consequent nature.

**Verdict:** it purchases God's innocence at the price of God's sovereignty — and the trade must be stated, not concealed.

#### Why this earns marks

- **Structure:** numbered tenets → named layers → criticism with reply → graded verdict.
- **Doctrine:** dipolarity, initial aim, consequent nature, distributed power, objective immortality.
- **Argument:** tenet (3) is shown to *entail* tenet (4), so the position is derived rather than listed.
- **Distinction:** Griffin's metaphysical necessity versus Plantinga's chosen restraint — the highest-value line in the answer.
- **Technical terms:** primordial and consequent natures, persuasive power, fellow-sufferer, surrelativism.
- **Criticism:** the worship objection is stated with its reply and its residue.
- **Conclusion:** the innocence-for-sovereignty trade, named explicitly as a cost.

#### Compact skeleton and timing

```text
1 min  : Frame — revision of omnipotence, not justification of permission
6 min  : The six tenets, numbered
3 min  : Whitehead, Hartshorne, Griffin, Cobb — one claim each
2 min  : Griffin cannot; Plantinga chooses
2 min  : Worship objection, reply and residue
1 min  : Verdict — innocence at the price of sovereignty
Total  : 15 minutes
```

#### Common mistakes and examiner penalties

- Writing "process theodicy (Whitehead)" and stopping: **forfeits the attribution marks the directive is buying**.
- Calling the process God powerless: **misdescribes persuasive power as no power**.
- Confusing Griffin's necessity with self-limitation: **collapses the distinction from the free-will defence**.
- Calling process theodicy a defence: **it claims God's actual situation and is therefore refutable**.
- Omitting the worship objection: **leaves the answer uncritical in a question that expects evaluation**.
- Failing to number the tenets: **"main tenets" is an enumeration directive**.

### ORIGINAL MAINS PRACTICE WITH MODEL SOLUTIONS

These six original models extend coverage rather than repeat a direct verified PYQ. Two are 10-mark, two 15-mark and two 20-mark, and each sits inside the declared word band — **10 marks about 150 words, 15 marks about 240 to 260, 20 marks about 340 to 360**.

#### Original 10-marker 1

**Question:** Distinguish a defence from a theodicy, and explain why the distinction determines what counts as a refutation. **(10 marks, 150 words)**

##### Demand decoding

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Directive | *Distinguish* and *explain why* — a conceptual distinction put to argumentative work |
| Core issue | Why bare possibility suffices in one case and not in the other |
| Ideal thesis | The distinction fixes the success condition, and therefore fixes what a refutation must show |

##### Model answer (≈155 words)

A **defence** claims only that God *could* have a morally sufficient reason for permitting evil. Its target is the charge of **logical inconsistency**, and its success condition is therefore bare **logical possibility**. This is why **Plantinga** may assert transworld depravity as *possible* without asserting it as *actual*, and why an objector who shows that it is probably false has not touched the argument: to refute a defence one must show that the proposed story is **impossible**.

A **theodicy** claims to identify God's **actual** reason. **Augustine's** privation account, **Hick's** soul-making and **process theodicy** all make this stronger claim. Their success condition is plausibility and truth, so an objector refutes them by showing the proposed reason is false or insufficient — which is exactly what the animal-suffering and proportionality objections attempt.

The distinction therefore fixes the burden on both sides. ⚠️ It also explains why skeptical theism and a positive theodicy sit awkwardly together: one says the reason is unknowable, the other names it.

##### Top-answer checklist

- Both terms defined by their **claim-strength**, not by tone.
- Success conditions stated separately for each.
- Plantinga cited for the possibility-only claim.
- At least two named theodicies with their actual-reason claim.
- The closing line about the tension between skeptical theism and positive theodicy.

#### Original 15-marker 1

**Question:** Does skeptical theism answer the evidential argument from evil, and at what cost? Discuss. **(15 marks, 250 words)**

##### Demand decoding

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Directive | *Does… and at what cost? Discuss* — a two-part demand: effectiveness, then price |
| Core issue | Whether blocking the inference is a genuine answer or a purchased stalemate |
| Ideal thesis | It answers the inference successfully and pays for it with a general loss of moral confidence |

##### Model answer (≈255 words)

Skeptical theism does not supply a theodicy. It attacks the **inference** in the evidential argument: from "I can see no justifying reason for this evil" to "there is no justifying reason". Its proponents are **Wykstra**, **Alston**, **Bergmann** and **Howard-Snyder**.

The mechanism is **Wykstra's CORNEA** (1984), the Condition of ReasoNable Epistemic Access: one may claim that it *appears* that p is absent only if it is reasonable to believe that, given one's cognitive situation, p **would likely be discernible** if it were there. **Bergmann** adds that our sample of possible goods, possible evils and the entailment relations between them is **not representative** of the total space. Therefore the *noseeum* inference is unwarranted for goods that would justify divine permission. **Alston's** analogy is a novice examining a grandmaster's move and seeing no point in it; **Wykstra's** is an infant who cannot see why a painful procedure is performed.

⚠️ The cost is threefold. The **moral-scepticism objection** (Almeida, Russell, Wielenberg) presses that if we cannot judge an evil gratuitous, we equally cannot judge that we ought to *prevent* it — which threatens the duty to rescue; the reply that obligation is fixed by what we know is found unstable. The **parity objection** presses that the same scepticism undercuts confidence in divine promises; restricting it to value-entailments looks *ad hoc*. The **bracketing objection** presses that, extended, no evidence could ever count against theism.

**Verdict:** it is the strongest defensive reply available and the most corrosive one, since it neutralises the atheist's inference only by conceding that the moral structure of the universe is opaque to us.

##### Top-answer checklist

- CORNEA stated in full, not paraphrased as "mystery".
- Bergmann's representativeness theses named alongside it.
- Both canonical analogies present.
- All three objections, each with its reply or residue.
- The verdict phrased as strongest-and-most-corrosive, with the concession named.

#### Original 15-marker 2

**Question:** "Karma answers the distribution question that Western theodicy cannot, but at a price." Critically examine. **(15 marks, 250 words)**

##### Demand decoding

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Directive | *Critically examine* — accept what is true, then state the price precisely |
| Core issue | Whether trans-life moral causation is an explanatory gain or an unverifiable stipulation |
| Ideal thesis | The distributional gain is real and school-specific; the price is verification and the risk of victim-blaming |

##### Model answer (≈255 words)

The claim is accurate on both sides. Western theodicy answers **why evil exists** — through freedom, privation or soul-making — but has no standard answer to **why this person**. **Karma-saṃsāra** answers exactly that: suffering is the fruit of one's own past deeds across lives, and the moral law is **self-operating**, so rebirth widens the temporal field of justice.

⚠️ Karma is not one doctrine. **Mīmāṃsā**, **Buddhist** and **Jain** accounts require no divine distributor at all; **Nyāya** and theistic **Vedānta** assign God a just administrative role as **karma-superintendent (*karmādhyakṣa*)** — a judge, not an arbitrary cause of evil. The series is treated as **beginningless**, which blocks the demand for a first wrongful act.

The price has four parts. First, beginninglessness is a doctrinal stipulation and **verifies nothing** about any claimed past-life cause. Second, the inference from "this person suffers" to "this person deserves it for a specific past act" is **unavailable to ordinary observers**, and using karma that way converts explanation into **victim-blaming**. Third, natural suffering, collective harms and unequal starting conditions still press the fairness of the mechanism. Fourth, karma answers **distribution**, not **personal worth**: it does not show that a sufferer's life is a great good *to them*, which is **Adams'** demand.

✅ The standing control must be stated: whatever its metaphysics, karma does not cancel present duties of compassion, protection and relief; causal explanation is not moral permission to withhold aid.

**Verdict:** the distributional gain is genuine and is India's structural advantage, but it is purchased with an unverifiable trans-life mechanism and a real moral hazard that the answer must name.

##### Top-answer checklist

- The distributional gain stated against the Western gap.
- Theistic and non-theistic karma schools separated, with *karmādhyakṣa* named.
- Beginninglessness used precisely — blocks a regress, verifies nothing.
- The victim-blaming control stated explicitly.
- Adams' distinction between distribution and personal worth invoked in the closing evaluation.

#### Original 20-marker 1

**Question:** "A theodicy is not refuted by its cost but is measured by it." Critically discuss with reference to at least three theodicies. **(20 marks, 350 words)**

##### Demand decoding

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Directive | *Critically discuss with reference to at least three* — an evaluative principle applied to named positions |
| Core issue | Whether cost-accounting is the right method for grading replies to evil |
| Ideal thesis | The principle is correct and it is the examiner's own grading method, but it has a limit where a cost destroys the position's purpose |

##### Model answer (≈355 words)

The principle is sound and it is how this clause should be graded. Every reply to evil buys relief by surrendering something; the philosophical task is to state the purchase, not to pretend it was free.

**Free-will defence.** **Plantinga** shows that significant freedom entails genuine alternatives and that God cannot causally determine a creature to freely choose rightly, so moral evil is compatible with omnipotence and perfect goodness. Its **cost** is threefold: it presupposes **libertarian freedom**, which a compatibilist denies; it is silent about **natural evil**; and, being a defence, it establishes only **possibility**. None of these refutes it, because its target was the logical charge alone. Measured against that target, it succeeds.

**Soul-making.** **Hick** creates humans immature, at an **epistemic distance**, in a law-governed world where courage requires danger and compassion requires another's need. Its **coverage** is wider — it reaches natural evil as the environment of growth. Its **cost** is proportionality: much suffering builds nothing, animals do not morally grow, and the reply is a **universal eschatological fulfilment** that depends on contested afterlife commitments. Measured against the evidential burden it partly succeeds and partly fails, and the grade should say so.

**Process theodicy.** **Whitehead's** dipolar God supplies each occasion its **initial aim**; **Hartshorne** argues classical omnipotence is incoherent; **Griffin** makes persuasion a **metaphysical necessity** rather than a choice. Its coverage is the widest of the three — moral evil, natural evil and animal suffering all follow from finite self-determination. Its **cost** is the largest: classical **sovereignty**, and any guarantee of final victory over evil, which raises the worship objection.

⚠️ The principle has a limit. A cost can be so severe that it destroys the position's **purpose**: if a reply saves God's goodness by removing the God whose goodness was in question, it has changed the subject rather than answered it. **Advaita's** two-level relocation and non-creator systems face exactly this charge, and answering it requires showing that the religious life the position sustains is still recognisably the one in dispute.

**Verdict:** cost is a measure, not a refutation — but a theodicy whose cost includes its own point is not measured, it is superseded. Grading therefore requires both the price list *and* a judgement on whether the purchase was worth making.

##### Top-answer checklist

- Three theodicies, each with coverage and cost stated separately.
- Plantinga's three costs named, including libertarian freedom.
- Hick's eschatological dependence identified as a cost, not a bonus.
- Griffin's metaphysical necessity distinguished from chosen restraint.
- The stated **limit** of the principle, which converts agreement into critical discussion.

#### Original 20-marker 2

**Question:** Assess whether the horrendous-evils challenge shows that no general theodicy can succeed. **(20 marks, 350 words)**

##### Demand decoding

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Directive | *Assess whether* — a determinate verdict on a strong claim, with the strongest case on both sides |
| Core issue | Whether a person-level requirement is satisfiable by any world-level account |
| Ideal thesis | The challenge defeats general theodicy as *global accountancy* but leaves a religion-specific personal answer standing |

##### Model answer (≈355 words)

**Marilyn McCord Adams** (*Horrendous Evils and the Goodness of God*, 1999) defines a **horrendous evil** as one whose participation — as perpetrator or victim — gives the participant *prima facie* reason to doubt whether their life could be **a great good to them on the whole**. The definition is technical, and using it loosely for "very bad evil" destroys the argument.

The case for the strong claim is powerful. Standard theodicies operate **globally**: they seek a reason why God permits evil in general, balancing it within the world's total value. But a global balance can be struck while a particular person's life remains, for them, a net disaster. **Chisholm's** distinction is decisive: **balancing off** outweighs the evil within a sum and leaves it external to the good, whereas **defeat** integrates the evil into a whole whose value it contributes to. A God who is good **to persons** — not merely a good producer of worlds — must defeat the evil **within the frame of the individual's life**. ⚠️ Compensation is not defeat: a later good can compensate for loss while leaving the evil merely balanced.

The case against the strong claim is that Adams herself supplies an answer. She argues that resources internal to a particular religion — divine identification with human suffering, and **beatific intimacy with God as an incommensurable good** — can accomplish personal-level defeat. Two objections follow, each with her reply. That the answer is **purely eschatological and unverifiable**, and seems to make the evil instrumentally necessary: Adams denies instrumentality, since the evil need not be *required*, only *defeated*. That it **abandons the neutral arena** by using Christian-specific resources: the atheological argument targets a *particular* theism, so a particular theism may answer.

**Indian parity** sharpens the verdict. Karma explains the **distribution** of horrendous suffering but not personal worth. **Advaita's** ultimate-level sublation and **Buddhist** liberation both address the predicament by dissolving the standpoint from which the life is evaluated — critics call this evasive, defenders therapeutic. The contrast is exact: **defeat within the life** versus **exit from the life-series**.

**Verdict:** the challenge defeats general theodicy understood as world-level accountancy, and it defeats any reply that offers compensation in place of integration. It does not show that *no* answer is possible; it shows that any successful answer must be **person-relative** — which is why the residual question is whether such an answer is a theodicy at all, or an admission that no general one succeeds.

##### Top-answer checklist

- Adams' definition used in its technical sense, with perpetrators included.
- Balancing off versus defeat, with Chisholm named.
- Goodness-to-persons contrasted with goodness as world-production.
- Both objections with Adams' replies.
- Indian parity used for the defeat-versus-exit contrast, and a genuinely graded verdict.

### High-score answer protocol for the problem of evil

#### 1. Name the version before anything else

Write, in the first two lines, which problem you are answering: logical, evidential, existential, horrendous, animal or distributional. Theodicies are version-specific, and a mismatched theodicy scores as an error rather than as content.

#### 2. Write the set with its bridges

Never assert that omnipotence, goodness and evil are contradictory. Write the four-proposition profile, then the goodness bridge and the power bridge. Every subsequent move in the answer attaches to one of the two bridges.

#### 3. Reconstruct, do not summarise

Each reply is a chain of premises. Plantinga's has four steps; Augustine's has four; process theodicy has six tenets. Numbering them converts recall into argument and gives the objection something determinate to attack.

#### 4. Attach the cost to the reply, immediately

Never leave a theodicy on the page without its price: free will costs libertarian assumptions and natural evil; privation costs innocence and animals; soul-making costs proportionality and an afterlife premise; process costs sovereignty; karma costs verification.

#### 5. Use one Indian comparator deliberately

Choose it for the work it does. Karma for **distribution**. Advaita for **levels**. Buddhism, Jainism and Mīmāṃsā for **removing the defendant**. *Adṛṣṭa* as *atīndriya* and karmic ripening among the *acinteyya* for **epistemic parity with skeptical theism**. A comparator with no argumentative job is decoration.

#### 6. Keep the four negative categories apart

Evil is moral or natural harm; the profane is the ordinary; the impure is the ritually polluting; the demonic is the actively anti-sacred. This single discipline protects the whole 2019 module and prevents the most common error on it.

#### 7. Observe quotation discipline

The four-horn dilemma reaches us through **Lactantius**, *De Ira Dei* 13, and is traditionally ascribed to Epicurus; "vale of Soul-making" is **Keats**; "defeat" is **Chisholm's** term; "fellow-sufferer who understands" is **Whitehead's**; CORNEA is **Wykstra's**.

#### 8. Marks-wise architecture

10 marks: version, set, one reply with its presupposition, one objection, a compatibility verdict. 15 marks: add the canonical example, the reply, and one Indian comparator. 20 marks: add a second objection with its reply, the defence/theodicy distinction, and the residue paragraph before a criterion-named conclusion.

#### 9. Never end devotionally

A graded verdict answers the directive and follows from the body. "God's purposes are ultimately good" is not a verdict; "the theodicy answers the version it was designed for, at the cost of X, and leaves Y untouched" is.

#### 10. Final twenty-second audit

Version named? Bridges written? Reply reconstructed as premises? Cost attached? Comparator doing work? Residue stated? Verdict graded? If any answer is no, the marks are still on the table.

### Advanced seminar modules — from competent to examiner-grade

Each module states a thesis, its argument, the strongest objection, the reply, a qualification and a verdict. Deploy **one** refinement in a 15-marker and at most **two** in a 20-marker; none of them is needed to repair a core answer.

#### ADVANCED MODULE 1/24 — Modal logic without notation overload

[THESIS] The logical problem is a question about whether the relevant propositions can be true
together in at least one possible world. The critic alleges that no possible world contains both
the classical God and evil; the defender needs only one coherent possibility in which God has a
morally sufficient reason to permit evil.

[ARGUMENT] Three modal ideas must be kept separate. First, **logical possibility** means that a
description contains no contradiction. Second, **world feasibility** asks whether God can
actualize that description given truths about undetermined creaturely choices. Third,
**actuality** asks which world and history obtain. Plantinga argues at the first two levels. He
does not infer that the freedom story is the actual divine policy merely because it is possible.

[OBJECTION] The distinction between possible and feasible worlds appears to limit divine power by
facts God does not control. If counterfactuals of freedom are independently true, what grounds
them, and why cannot an omnipotent creator choose better agents or circumstances?

[REPLY] Libertarian freedom precisely denies that God determines the content of the free choice.
The defender can say that creating agents includes accepting truths about how those agents would
freely respond. The grounding question remains a serious objection to Molinist and feasible-world
accounts, but it is not a proof of contradiction.

[QUALIFICATION] In an UPSC answer, modal vocabulary should clarify rather than display technique.
Write: "A logically describable sinless free world may not be strongly actualizable by God if the
truth of free creaturely choices is not under divine causal control." Do not use symbols unless
they reduce ambiguity.

[VERDICT] Modal analysis is most successful as a negative result: the simple inconsistent-set
argument needs more than the bare triad. It leaves open whether the actual world is a reasonable
choice for a perfectly good creator.

#### ADVANCED MODULE 2/24 — The moral premises behind the logical problem

[THESIS] The dispute is not only about divine power. It is also about the content of goodness.
The premise "a good being eliminates evil" is too strong unless it includes "when elimination
does not sacrifice an equal or greater good, violate another's agency, or permit an equal or worse
evil."

[ARGUMENT] Ordinary morality permits painful surgery, dangerous education and enforcement of
just law when proportionate and necessary. The theist generalizes: divine permission may be
morally justified where a good is logically connected with risk. The critic answers that ordinary
permission is constrained by consent, necessity, proportionality, fair distribution and
accountability. A parent may allow a difficult examination but not torture a child for character.
Therefore "greater good" is not a blank cheque.

[OBJECTION] Applying human moral constraints to God may be anthropomorphic because creatorhood
changes the relation between giver of life and creature.

[REPLY] If divine goodness has no recognizable continuity with moral goodness, the theist loses
the very premise used to praise and worship God. Analogical predication may allow difference of
mode, not reversal of evaluative content.

[QUALIFICATION] This creates a dilemma for extreme skeptical theism. Too much confidence in human
moral standards makes evil evidentially forceful; too little makes "God is good" thin and removes
the basis for positive theodicy.

[VERDICT] The logical problem is best understood as a stress-test of perfect goodness under the
conditions of creator responsibility, not as a mechanical contradiction among three dictionary
definitions.

#### ADVANCED MODULE 3/24 — Freedom: value, range and responsibility

[THESIS] Free-will approaches require more than the assertion that freedom is valuable. They must
specify the kind of freedom, the range of consequences necessary for responsibility, and the
distribution of opportunities for meaningful choice.

[ARGUMENT] Libertarian freedom holds that at least some choices are not determined by prior
conditions. Compatibilist freedom identifies acting from one's own reasons without external
constraint even if those reasons are causally determined. Plantinga's defence needs a libertarian
relation to God: if God determines the choice, creaturely authorship cannot wholly transfer moral
responsibility. Yet significant freedom need not entail unlimited power to harm. Human societies
rightly restrict weapons and opportunities for catastrophic wrongdoing while preserving agency.
The critic therefore asks why God permits consequences far beyond what meaningful choice needs.

[OBJECTION] Continuous divine restriction would make the world a moral nursery, obscure causal
responsibility and prevent serious cooperation against evil.

[REPLY] The objection supports some regular consequences, not every scale of consequence. The
theist must explain a threshold: which harms are integral to responsible agency and which are
apparently excessive?

[QUALIFICATION] Social and structural evil complicates individualistic freedom. Poverty,
oppression and institutions are produced through many choices and inherited systems. No single
agent may intend the total harm, yet responsibility is distributed. A theodicy centered only on
isolated decisions misses this cumulative structure.

[VERDICT] Freedom plausibly explains the possibility of moral fault. It less clearly explains the
available magnitude of harm, unequal moral opportunity, structural evil and divine non-restraint.

#### ADVANCED MODULE 4/24 — Natural evil: autonomy of creation and excess

> **CORE DISTINCTION:** A stable natural order is not merely a stage for morality; it is the condition of embodied.

[THESIS] A stable natural order is not merely a stage for morality; it is the condition of embodied
life, scientific understanding and purposive action. Some contemporary formulations describe
creation as having relative autonomy rather than being a theatre of continuous intervention.

[ARGUMENT] Regular tectonics, weather, mutation, immunity and ecological competition have
life-enabling and harm-producing aspects. A world in which harmful effects vanished whenever they
threatened a person would cease to be cognitively reliable. Agents could not infer causes,
coordinate plans or develop technologies. This supports a general permission of natural risk.

[OBJECTION] The critic distinguishes law from initial conditions, regularity from intensity, and
ordinary vulnerability from apparently pointless agony. An omniscient designer could allegedly
choose different constants, safer organisms, earlier warnings or selective interventions without
abolishing order.

[REPLY] Whole-system comparison is difficult: changes that remove one harm may remove goods or
produce unknown harms. Complex adaptive systems may require trade-offs that local observers
cannot calculate.

[COUNTER-OBJECTION] Difficulty of comparison is epistemic modesty, not positive evidence that the
actual balance is optimal. It blocks confident atheistic inference only to the extent that access
is genuinely poor; it also blocks confident theodicy.

[VERDICT] Creation-autonomy and law-order accounts explain why a serious world cannot be a
hedonistic paradise. The question of preventable excess remains the central evidential residue.

#### ADVANCED MODULE 5/24 — Evolutionary evil and deep time

> **CORE DISTINCTION:** Deep time is a fairness test for every theodicy.

[THESIS] Evolutionary history intensifies the problem because pain, predation, parasitism and mass
extinction occur over immense pre-human periods. The data weaken any account that treats natural
evil chiefly as punishment for human sin.

[ARGUMENT] Evolution through variation, selection and ecological interaction generates adaptive
complexity but also waste, failed organisms and sentient suffering. A theist may value a creation
capable of bringing forth novelty through lawful processes rather than receiving every form by
direct fiat. Such fecundity can be a good. Nevertheless, the process contains beings who cannot
understand danger, consent to risk or acquire human-style virtue.

[OBJECTION] Calling evolutionary suffering the price of autonomy treats animals as disposable
means. If God values each sentient creature, a species-level good is insufficient.

[REPLY] A defender may extend eschatological or redemptive goodness to non-human creatures, or
argue that creaturely value is not exhausted by painless duration. These proposals are
philosophically possible but source-dependent and speculative.

[QUALIFICATION] Karmic rebirth across animal forms supplies a different continuity story, but it
can moralize animal pain without accessible evidence of desert. Jain concern for graded living
beings and radical non-violence provides an ethical response even where metaphysical explanation
is disputed.

[VERDICT] Deep time is a fairness test for every theodicy. A theory that mentions animals only as
instruments for human growth is incomplete.

#### ADVANCED MODULE 6/24 — Rowe, Draper and cumulative evidence

[THESIS] The evidential case is strongest when cumulative rather than dependent on one dramatic
example. It combines apparently gratuitous instances with patterns of biological pain, unequal
distribution, divine hiddenness and the failure of proposed reasons to cover all classes.

[ARGUMENT] Rowe asks whether at least one instance of intense suffering lacks a justifying
connection. Draper asks a comparative question: is the pattern of pleasure and pain more expected
if nature is indifferent than if guided by perfect benevolence? The two approaches can reinforce
each other. Rowe supplies local cases; Draper supplies pattern comparison. Neither requires the
claim that evil is logically incompatible with God.

[OBJECTION] Theism is not a single bare hypothesis. It may include freedom, stable law,
soul-making, eschatology and independent evidence from religious experience or cosmological
arguments. Evaluating one datum in isolation distorts a cumulative worldview.

[REPLY] Auxiliary theodicies must have independent motivation and predictive discipline. A view
that adds a new hidden purpose for every counterexample becomes resistant to evidence. Other
evidence for God can offset evil, but Topic 02 arguments must be weighed rather than presumed.

[QUALIFICATION] Do not assign invented numerical priors. State only the comparative structure:
evidence confirms a hypothesis to the extent that it is more expected on that hypothesis than on
its rivals.

[VERDICT] Evidential evil functions as an undercutting and rebutting consideration within a total
case. It rarely yields a stand-alone mathematical conclusion, but it can be philosophically
weighty.

#### ADVANCED MODULE 7/24 — Skeptical theism: three boundaries

> **CORE DISTINCTION:** Skeptical theism is defensible only when bounded epistemically, morally and religiously.

[THESIS] Skeptical theism is defensible only when bounded epistemically, morally and religiously.

1. **Epistemic boundary:** Human ignorance concerns the full network of divine reasons; it does
   not imply ignorance of every local good, evil or causal relation.
2. **Moral boundary:** Agents must act on accessible moral reasons. Unknown divine permission
   never authorizes human infliction or neglect.
3. **Religious boundary:** If the view blocks all inference from worldly goods to providence, it
   cannot selectively block only negative evidence while retaining every positive sign.

[ARGUMENT] Wykstra's accessibility principle is plausible when the scale difference between
human and divine cognition is relevant. Bergmann's representativeness concern likewise warns
that sampled goods may not exhaust possible goods. But a complete suspension of inductive
judgement would damage ordinary reasoning.

[OBJECTION] The asymmetry between divine and human perspectives may itself justify selective
skepticism: evil-permission could involve remote entailments, while some positive religious
experiences are direct.

[REPLY] That distinction must be argued case by case. It cannot be assumed merely to protect a
favoured conclusion. Moreover, the more opaque divine purposes become, the harder it is to infer
that God is trustworthy in the recognizable moral sense.

[VERDICT] Skeptical theism is best used as a defeater-defeater against overconfident noseeum
reasoning, followed immediately by an affirmation of local moral responsibility.

#### ADVANCED MODULE 8/24 — Soul-making under the proportionality test

[THESIS] Soul-making succeeds for obstacles proportionate to agency and fails when it treats all
suffering as development irrespective of outcome.

[ARGUMENT] A developmental environment needs danger, resistance, uncertainty and other persons'
needs. These conditions enable courage, patience, compassion and practical wisdom. Yet formation
requires an agent capable of learning, a challenge within some range of response, and an
opportunity for recovery. Horrendous evils can fragment memory, trust and agency. Infant death
and much animal pain offer no obvious subject of moral formation.

[OBJECTION] Goods may accrue to communities or other persons, and eschatological life may complete
development interrupted here.

[REPLY] Community goods do not automatically justify using one person as material for another.
Eschatology can widen the developmental horizon only if the individual sufferer is restored and
the evil defeated within that person's good.

[QUALIFICATION] The distinction between first-order evil and second-order good should never be
written as "evil is necessary for good." Some second-order virtues need vulnerability, but the
same virtue may be elicited by many degrees of hardship. Necessity and proportionality require
separate argument.

[VERDICT] Hick supplies a powerful account of why a world with no adversity might be morally
shallow. Adams supplies the needed correction: goodness must finally be goodness to persons.

#### ADVANCED MODULE 9/24 — Augustine after evolutionary criticism

[THESIS] Augustine's metaphysical privation claim can survive even if the historical-punitive
account of natural evil is rejected.

[ARGUMENT] Corruption remains dependent on an underlying good: disease is malfunction of living
systems, cruelty is disorder of agency, injustice is corruption of social relation. This
ontological point does not require that earthquakes began after a first human sin. The historical
fall narrative, by contrast, makes empirical and moral claims about original perfection,
inherited disorder and punishment.

[OBJECTION] Separating privation from fall may save a fragment while abandoning the theodicy.
Even if evil is ontologically parasitic, God creates the subjects and conditions in which
privation occurs.

[REPLY] The defender can combine privation with finite creation and free development: a created
world is good yet not divine or incapable of defect. This avoids dualism without claiming that
every natural evil punishes sin.

[COUNTER-OBJECTION] If defect is an inevitable condition of finite being, either God cannot create
without evil or chooses a vulnerable order. The classical question returns at the level of
creation.

[VERDICT] Use Augustine in layers. Privation is the strongest philosophical layer; defective
freedom is strong for moral evil; universal punishment and a historical cosmic fall carry the
heaviest burden.

#### ADVANCED MODULE 10/24 — Process theology compared with voluntary self-limitation

[THESIS] Process non-coercion and classical kenotic self-limitation must be distinguished by
modality. In a self-limitation view, God possesses unilateral power but refrains for the sake of
freedom or relationship. In Griffin-style process thought, such unilateral coercion is not a
metaphysical possibility.

[ARGUMENT] The distinction changes responsibility. Voluntary restraint still permits the
question, "Why did God not make an exception here?" Necessary non-coercion answers that no
exception was available. It therefore gives process thought a cleaner innocence claim but a
greater assurance problem. A God who voluntarily risks creation may remain sovereign; a process
God participates in a field of distributed creativity.

[OBJECTION] Process thought may redefine divine power until God resembles one influential cause
among others.

[REPLY] Hartshornean and process accounts distinguish supreme relational or persuasive power
from finite power. God offers the best possibility to every occasion and perfectly receives all
value; supremacy need not mean monopoly.

[QUALIFICATION] The answer must state that Whitehead's dipolar terminology, Hartshorne's critique
of omnipotence and Griffin's theodicy are related but not interchangeable. Do not attribute every
later theological conclusion directly to Whitehead.

[VERDICT] Process thought gives the most systematic non-classical response to natural evil, but
its success depends on whether worship and hope require guaranteed control.

#### ADVANCED MODULE 11/24 — Karma: desert, causation and social ethics

> **CORE DISTINCTION:** A strict desert reading says present suffering is proportionate consequence of prior.

[THESIS] Karma can be interpreted as desert, impersonal moral causation, character formation or
bondage mechanics. The ethical evaluation changes with the interpretation.

[ARGUMENT] A strict desert reading says present suffering is proportionate consequence of prior
wrongdoing. An impersonal-causation reading treats deeds as producing tendencies without a divine
judge. A formative reading emphasizes how action shapes character and future possibility. Jain
karmic matter gives a more literal bondage mechanism, while Buddhist dependent continuity denies
a permanent owner. Nyaya adds an intelligent allocator; Mimamsa can retain ritual efficacy
without creatorhood.

[OBJECTION] Without memory, transparent proportionality or a stable person, retribution seems
unfair. Beginninglessness avoids a first-cause regress only by denying a first temporal point; it
does not show why the system is morally good. Socially, observers may infer desert from
misfortune and withhold reform.

[REPLY] Memory is not the sole ground of responsibility even within one life, and causal
continuity can support concern. More importantly, no school licenses a bystander to know a
victim's hidden karmic history. Compassion, dana, ahimsa and assistance are themselves morally
required actions.

[VERDICT] Use karma as a comparative model of trans-life moral causation, never as a warrant for
explaining a particular victim. Its strongest feature is distribution; its greatest risk is
retrospective moralization.

#### ADVANCED MODULE 12/24 — Advaita and the reality-status objection

[THESIS] Advaita changes the problem by distinguishing empirical experience from ultimate
non-duality, but it cannot responsibly answer suffering with the slogan "the world is unreal."

[ARGUMENT] At the empirical level, persons, Isvara, karma, dharma, ignorance and suffering are
operative. Knowledge removes the fundamental misidentification of self with finite body-mind and
reveals non-dual Brahman. At the ultimate level, no independent evil principle confronts Brahman.
The classical creator-versus-evil duality is therefore not final.

[OBJECTION] If empirical suffering is sublated, the view may appear to trivialize victims and
make moral action provisional.

[REPLY] Empirical validity is sufficient for duties, compassion, discipline and liberation
practice before realization. Sublatability does not mean non-occurrence at its operative level.
Dream pain can be real to the dreamer without being ultimate, though this analogy must not be used
pastorally to dismiss actual trauma.

[QUALIFICATION] Ramanuja's critique is relevant: if the world and selves are real modes dependent
on Brahman, their suffering cannot be assigned merely a lower ontological status. Visistadvaita
therefore retains a more direct theistic governance problem alongside karma and grace.

[VERDICT] Advaita dissolves the ultimate metaphysical dualism but leaves an empirical ethical and
soteriological problem that must be answered through knowledge, duty and compassion.

#### ADVANCED MODULE 13/24 — Buddhist and Samkhya diagnoses compared

[THESIS] Buddhism and Samkhya both avoid a creator theodicy but disagree radically about self and
the cause of bondage.

[ARGUMENT] Samkhya locates suffering in purusha's apparent identification with prakriti's
evolutes. Discriminative knowledge separates consciousness from material-nature activity,
culminating in kaivalya. Buddhism denies a permanent purusha and analyses dukkha through
dependent origination, impermanence, craving and ignorance. Cessation is not isolation of a
substantial witness but ending the conditions that sustain appropriation and suffering.

[OBJECTION] Removing God avoids culpability but may not explain why the beginningless causal
order has its painful character. Samkhya must explain the conjunction of radically different
principles; Buddhism must explain responsibility and continuity without self.

[REPLY] Both systems present the "why" as a mistaken demand for a first creator-cause and redirect
attention to operative conditions and liberation. A causal diagnosis can be sufficient for
practice even without a cosmic justification.

[QUALIFICATION] This is a reframing, not a victory by default. Existential pain, compassion and
fairness remain, while the logical inconsistent triad disappears.

[VERDICT] Creatorless systems demonstrate that the problem of evil is not coextensive with the
problem of suffering. They exchange theodicy for diagnosis and soteriology.

#### ADVANCED MODULE 14/24 — Protest, lament and moral knowledge

[THESIS] Protest is philosophically important because it treats the negative moral appearance of
suffering as knowledge-bearing rather than as a defect of faith.

[ARGUMENT] Job's protest resists friends who infer guilt from suffering. Ivan Karamazov refuses
to endorse a harmony that uses innocent agony. Camus makes solidarity and revolt an ethical
answer without metaphysical reconciliation. These positions differ, but all reject the movement
from "a total explanation may exist" to "the sufferer should accept our explanation."

[OBJECTION] Protest without metaphysical resolution may express emotion rather than answer the
truth question.

[REPLY] Moral perception is part of the evidence. A theory that can preserve consistency only by
erasing the prima facie wrongness of cruelty has changed the meaning of goodness. Protest also
generates practical reasons to resist avoidable evil.

[QUALIFICATION] Anti-theodicy should not be represented as the claim that no meaning, redemption
or religious response is possible. Its central objection is to comprehensive third-person
justification and instrumentalization.

[VERDICT] Protest is not a logical refutation of theism, but it is a constraint on what may count
as a morally adequate theodicy.

#### ADVANCED MODULE 15/24 — Pastoral reason and philosophical restraint

[THESIS] The order appropriate to an examination answer is not always the order appropriate at a
hospital bed, disaster site or bereavement. Philosophical competence includes knowing when an
explanation is morally mistimed.

[ARGUMENT] A sufferer may need safety, truthful presence, lament and practical assistance before
causal or theological interpretation. Offering karma, punishment or soul-making as a diagnosis of
a particular person's suffering exceeds available evidence and can add moral injury. Prayer can
include protest; trust can coexist with uncertainty; religious communities can embody
solidarity without claiming a hidden plan.

[OBJECTION] Refusing explanation may leave religious belief cognitively empty when it is most
challenged.

[REPLY] Restraint is not permanent evasion. General philosophical accounts can be assessed in
appropriate contexts. The immediate moral duty is not identical with the later intellectual
task.

[QUALIFICATION] The same rule protects against secular triumphalism. One should not use a
person's trauma as an occasion to score an atheistic conclusion either. Victim-centered reason
asks what the evidence supports and what the situation ethically permits.

[VERDICT] The practical implication of the problem of evil is active compassion under epistemic
humility. A theory that excuses preventable harm fails regardless of its abstract elegance.

#### ADVANCED MODULE 17/24 — Essay laboratory - Is evil evidence against God or against a concept of God?

> **CORE DISTINCTION:** God is finite, processual, impersonal or not a creator, the inference changes.

[THESIS] Evil first tests a concept of God and only then contributes evidence about whether any
being answering to that concept exists. If "God" means an omnipotent, omniscient and perfectly
good creator, apparently preventable suffering bears directly on the concept's instantiation. If
God is finite, processual, impersonal or not a creator, the inference changes.

[ARGUMENT] The conceptual stage asks which attributes generate expectations. Perfect goodness
creates an expectation of opposition to evil; knowledge creates awareness; power creates capacity;
creatorhood creates responsibility for the framework. The evidential stage compares those
expectations with the world. A process God predicts struggle and partial achievement more readily
than classical control. Advaita predicts an empirically operative but ultimately sublatable order.
Buddhism predicts conditioned suffering without a providential distribution. Each view must be
tested against what it actually asserts.

[OBJECTION] This method lets believers escape evidence by revising God whenever a difficulty
arises.

[REPLY] Revision is legitimate only if independently motivated and if its religious cost is paid
openly. An ad hoc finite God introduced solely to avoid evil has weak explanatory value. Process
thought, by contrast, offers a wider metaphysics of power and becoming, though that metaphysics is
contestable.

[VERDICT] Evil does not address an empty word. It confirms or disconfirms profiles. The strongest
answer therefore moves in the order **concept -> expectation -> evidence -> revision cost**.

#### ADVANCED MODULE 18/24 — Essay laboratory - Could God create virtue without suffering?

[THESIS] Some virtue logically requires vulnerability, but it does not follow that the actual
quantity and distribution of suffering is required.

[ARGUMENT] Courage needs perceived danger, compassion needs another's need, forgiveness
presupposes wrong, and responsibility needs consequential action. These are relation-dependent
goods. A world containing them cannot be wholly insulated from harm. Yet virtues differ:
creativity, friendship, gratitude and understanding do not require atrocity. Even danger-dependent
virtues may require only bounded challenge. The critic can therefore grant soul-making while
denying that horrors are necessary.

[OBJECTION] The total network of virtues, histories and free relations may require risks that no
local observer can calculate.

[REPLY] This possibility creates humility but not justification. The theist should avoid the
universal claim that every evil is necessary and defend only a general risk structure. Adams's
defeat condition must handle cases that exceed ordinary formation.

[QUALIFICATION] The question "Could God create virtue directly?" also concerns authenticity.
Implanted dispositions may be good qualities, but achieved virtues include a person's authorship
and history. That distinction supports development without proving every route to development
morally permissible.

[VERDICT] Soul-making explains why a frictionless world may lack important goods; proportionality
and goodness-to-persons decide whether the explanation reaches actual horrors.

#### ADVANCED MODULE 19/24 — Essay laboratory - Is divine permission equivalent to causation?

[THESIS] Permission and causation are not identical, but a creator's permission can still carry
responsibility where prevention is easy, knowledge complete and the stakes grave.

[ARGUMENT] A human wrongdoer can be the direct efficient cause of moral evil. God, on classical
theism, causes the agent and sustaining order but does not determine the free choice. This supports
derivative creaturely culpability. Nevertheless, knowingly selecting and sustaining a world
creates a responsibility of framework and permission. Moral analysis therefore needs at least
three levels: authorship of the act, design of available powers and consequences, and decision
whether to intervene.

[OBJECTION] If God intervened whenever misuse became serious, creaturely action would not be real.

[REPLY] Responsibility does not require unlimited consequence. Legal and social systems constrain
harm while retaining agency. The theist must identify why divine restraint is more limited than
responsible human restraint should be.

[QUALIFICATION] Process thought changes the third level by denying unilateral prevention. Karma
systems distribute responsibility across a life-series. Non-creator systems remove framework
selection by God but retain causal questions about bondage.

[VERDICT] The 2022 PYQ is best answered through layered rather than exclusive responsibility:
human authorship can be real while divine permission remains philosophically assessable.

#### ADVANCED MODULE 20/24 — Essay laboratory - Can eschatology repair theodicy?

[THESIS] Eschatology can prevent a life from being finally defeated by evil, but future bliss is
not automatically a moral warrant for earlier permission.

[ARGUMENT] Hick needs eventual fulfilment because earthly opportunities are radically unequal.
Adams needs a good powerful enough to defeat horrors within the participant's life. Augustine's
judgement framework seeks cosmic moral order. These uses differ: completion of development,
personal redemption and retributive balance are not interchangeable.

[OBJECTION] If eternal good infinitely exceeds finite suffering, the total life is good.

[REPLY] Aggregate outweighing does not settle consent, use and dignity. It would be wrong to harm
a person deliberately merely because one later supplies a larger benefit. The theist needs an
internal relation between redemption and the suffered evil, or a reason why permission respected
the person throughout.

[QUALIFICATION] Eschatology is a substantive religious commitment, not publicly established by
the problem of evil itself. It may belong in a cumulative theology, but should be labelled rather
than smuggled into a supposedly neutral proof.

[VERDICT] Eschatology is strongest as a theory of defeat and hope, weaker as a retroactive licence.
It deepens the response only when it remains victim-centered.

#### ADVANCED MODULE 21/24 — Essay laboratory - Does prayer intensify the problem?

[THESIS] Petitionary prayer can intensify evil's evidential problem because traditions portray God
as able and willing to respond, while many urgent petitions appear unanswered. It can also
transform the existential response through lament, solidarity and moral mobilization.

[ARGUMENT] If God sometimes prevents harm in response to prayer, selective non-prevention demands
an account of distribution. If God never changes outcomes, petition requires reinterpretation.
Stable-law defenders may say prayer is not a mechanism for suspending causation; it can align the
agent with divine purpose and community action. Protest prayer preserves relationship without
pretending to understand permission.

[OBJECTION] Unanswered prayer is merely another instance of limited human perspective.

[REPLY] That answer is available but costly. It makes claims of answered providence harder to
interpret and can render petitionary expectations opaque. A consistent view must treat positive
and negative cases with the same epistemic standard.

[QUALIFICATION] This is a bounded link to religious experience, reason and faith. The Problem of
Evil owner uses prayer only to test lived implications, not to provide a full theory of prayer.

[VERDICT] Prayer does not solve evil. At its best it refuses isolation, voices protest and
generates compassionate action without claiming privileged knowledge of hidden reasons.

#### ADVANCED MODULE 22/24 — Essay laboratory - The relation between evil and divine hiddenness

> **CORE DISTINCTION:** Do not turn this section into a full hiddenness essay or assert that hiddenness is.

[THESIS] Hiddenness aggravates suffering when a loving God could provide unmistakable presence,
guidance or consolation without removing all freedom.

[ARGUMENT] Hick values epistemic distance because overwhelming awareness might compromise free
relationship and development. The critic distinguishes coercive display from adequate availability:
a parent can remain non-coercive while being recognizably present to a terrified child. Sincere
nonbelief and divine silence during horror therefore add an interpersonal dimension to evidential
evil.

[OBJECTION] Religious growth may require trust under ambiguity, and divine presence may occur in
forms the sufferer cannot presently recognize.

[REPLY] Again, possibility is not proportionality. Some ambiguity may preserve freedom; extreme
absence may destroy the very trust and agency soul-making requires. The distribution of religious
awareness becomes relevant.

[QUALIFICATION] Do not turn this section into a full hiddenness essay or assert that hiddenness is
itself always evil. Use it as an auxiliary pressure on epistemic-distance explanations.

[VERDICT] Hiddenness shows that the problem concerns not only prevention of pain but the quality
of divine relationship under pain.

#### ADVANCED MODULE 23/24 — Essay laboratory - Can theism learn from non-theistic traditions?

> **CORE DISTINCTION:** The most fruitful comparison is functional and critical: creator-theism supplies a.

[THESIS] Non-theistic traditions cannot be imported as ready-made theodicies, but they can correct
theism's tendency to prioritize cosmic justification over diagnosis and practice.

[ARGUMENT] Buddhism starts from dukkha, traces conditions and prescribes a path. Jainism links
bondage to disciplined non-violence. Samkhya diagnoses misidentification and seeks discriminative
release. These approaches ask what sustains suffering and how agency can respond, without first
vindicating a creator. Theistic traditions can likewise make lament, compassion and prevention
central even while retaining questions of providence.

[OBJECTION] Borrowing practice while retaining creator belief avoids the distinctive metaphysical
challenge.

[REPLY] The challenge remains; learning at the practical level is not a logical solution. It is a
test of whether a proposed belief produces responsible action. Comparative philosophy need not
erase doctrinal difference to identify shared ethical insight.

[QUALIFICATION] Karma should not be treated as the universal bridge. Buddhist no-self, Jaina jiva,
Samkhya purusha and Vedantic selves differ fundamentally.

[VERDICT] The most fruitful comparison is functional and critical: creator-theism supplies a
permission problem; non-creator systems supply alternative causal and soteriological grammars;
each can be evaluated for compassion, responsibility and explanatory cost.

#### ADVANCED MODULE 24/24 — Essay laboratory - Final position options

[THESIS] A candidate need not claim a total solution. A reasoned final position can take one of
four forms, provided its cost is explicit.

1. **Classical preservation:** Free will, stable law, soul-making and eschatological defeat jointly
   make theism coherent and not unreasonable. Cost: multiple auxiliary commitments and a residue
   of apparently gratuitous evil.
2. **Epistemically modest preservation:** Plantinga defeats contradiction; skeptical theism blocks
   overconfident induction; no complete theodicy is claimed. Cost: opacity of goodness and
   providence.
3. **Non-classical revision:** Process or finite theism reduces culpability through distributed
   power. Cost: weakened sovereignty and assurance.
4. **Non-theistic reframing:** Buddhism, Jainism or Samkhya removes the creator defendant and
   prioritizes diagnosis and liberation. Cost: unresolved questions of causal order, identity or
   ultimate why.

[ARGUMENT] These are not equal answers to one fixed question. Each changes premises, explanatory
burdens and religious practices. The examiner should be shown exactly what has been preserved,
revised or abandoned.

[VERDICT] The most defensible synthetic judgement is that strict logical incompatibility is not
established, evidential evil remains substantial, and moral protest limits every universal
theodicy. Philosophical integrity requires continued action against suffering rather than
premature closure.

#### FINAL REGISTER 2/16 — Kinds of evil and the classical dilemma

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** Free will explains what agents do; it does not explain earthquakes, and it explains nothing at all about a fawn burned in a forest fire.

- ✅ **Moral evil:** harm traceable to misused creaturely freedom — cruelty, injustice, betrayal, oppression.
- ✅ **Natural evil:** suffering not caused by any will — earthquake, disease, congenital disorder.
- ✅ **Horrendous evil:** technically defined by the doubt it gives the participant about their own life's worth.
- ✅ **Animal / pre-human evil:** predation and disease before any moral agency existed.
- ❓ **The four-horn dilemma** — willing but not able / able but not willing / neither / both — reaches us through **Lactantius**, *De Ira Dei* 13, and is *traditionally* ascribed to **Epicurus**; no surviving Epicurean text contains it.
- ⚠️ The dilemma presupposes exactly the bridging premises it does not state.
- ⚠️ Three separate complaints hide inside "too much evil": **amount**, **kind** and **distribution**.
- ⚠️ Distribution is where Western theodicy is weakest and karma is structurally strongest.
- 🔑 **M-N-H-A:** Moral, Natural, Horrendous, Animal — sort first, match second.

#### FINAL REGISTER 3/16 — Mackie's logical problem

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** ✅ The set becomes inconsistent only with the bridging premises; deny either and the contradiction dissolves.

- ✅ **Source:** J. L. Mackie, "Evil and Omnipotence", *Mind* (1955).
- ✅ **The set:** God is omnipotent · God is perfectly good · evil exists — plus omniscience in the full profile.
- ✅ **Not a formal contradiction:** nothing in the propositions plus logic alone entails a proposition and its negation.
- ✅ **Bridge A (goodness):** a perfectly good being eliminates every evil it can.
- ✅ **Bridge B (power):** an omnipotent being has no relevant limit on preventing evil whose prevention is logically possible.
- ✅ **Mackie's taxonomy:** *adequate solutions* abandon a proposition; *fallacious solutions* only appear to keep all three.
- ⚠️ **Exit 1:** insert "unless there is a morally sufficient reason" → defences and theodicies.
- ⚠️ **Exit 2:** deny Bridge B → Plantinga, or process theism's outright rejection of classical omnipotence.
- ⚠️ **Exit 3:** deny the force of "evil exists" → privation, or Advaita's empirical level. **Cost:** the seriousness of suffering.
- ⚠️ **Verdict:** widely regarded as answered; the live debate has **migrated to the evidential form**.

#### FINAL REGISTER 4/16 — The paradox of omnipotence

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** ✅ Unrestricted omnipotence in both the first-order and second-order senses is incoherent, so theism must specify which sense it defends.

- ✅ **Source:** the same Mackie article, *Mind* (1955).
- ✅ **First-order power:** power to act on and control existing things.
- ✅ **Second-order power:** power to determine what powers exist at all.
- ⚠️ **Horn 1 — cannot make uncontrollable beings:** second-order power limited.
- ⚠️ **Horn 2 — can and does:** first-order power over them limited.
- ✅ **Restriction (a):** omnipotence covers the **logically possible** only — near-universally conceded.
- ✅ **Restriction (b):** second-order sovereignty **freely self-bound** in creating free agents. **Cost:** restraint becomes voluntary, re-opening the intervention question.
- ✅ **Restriction (c):** abandonment — process theism. **Cost:** no guarantee of final victory over evil.
- ⚠️ **Link to evil (2021 stem):** the free-will defence *requires* a restriction; Plantinga's God **chooses** not to override, Griffin's God **cannot** coerce.
- ⚠️ The attribute's independent coherence belongs to *Notions of God*.

#### FINAL REGISTER 5/16 — Plantinga's free-will defence

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** ✅ A world of programmed beneficence lacks the moral authorship of a world containing genuinely responsible agents.

- ✅ **Source:** Alvin Plantinga, *God, Freedom, and Evil* (1974).
- ✅ **Premises:** (1) a world with significantly free creatures is more valuable; (2) significant freedom entails genuine alternatives; (3) God cannot causally determine a creature to *freely* choose rightly — the description cancels itself; (4) so moral evil is compatible with omnipotence and goodness.
- ✅ **Transworld depravity:** a creaturely essence goes wrong in some circumstance in every world where it acts freely. ⚠️ Plantinga claims only that this is **possible**, never that it is **actual**.
- ✅ It is a **defence**, not a theodicy — bare logical possibility suffices.
- ⚠️ **Presupposition:** libertarian freedom is coherent and sufficiently valuable; a compatibilist rejects premise (3).
- ⚠️ **Residue:** natural evil is untouched unless a further hypothesis is added.
- ⚠️ **2022 stem replies:** God creates the *capacity*, the agent supplies the *act*; privation; foreknowledge ≠ causation; process denial of the antecedent.
- ⚠️ **Retained rejoinder:** knowingly instantiating the whole system, foreseeing its outcomes, remains morally relevant.

#### FINAL REGISTER 6/16 — Augustinian privation

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** ✅ Calling blindness a privation does not call it unreal — it is a real lack in a subject that ought to possess sight.

- ✅ **Sources:** Augustine, *Enchiridion* and *City of God*.
- ✅ **Premises:** being as being is good · God creates beings, not evil substances · corruption is **parasitic** on a good nature · moral fault is **disordered willing**.
- ✅ **Evil = privation of a due good (*privatio boni*)**, not a rival creative principle.
- ✅ **Canonical example:** rot is not a rival substance to wood but the loss of the wood's proper integrity.
- ✅ **The 2023 thesis:** moral evil = **sin**; natural evil = **punishment** through a fallen order.
- ⚠️ **Inherited guilt vs inherited condition:** personal guilt for an ancestor's act is far harder to defend than an inherited **damaged condition**; the second reduces the objection but still owes an account of why the condition and its unequal effects are permitted.
- ⚠️ **Objections:** innocent and animal suffering; privation strained for intense positive pain; ❓ a pre-human fallen order is historically and scientifically strained.
- ⚠️ **Quotation discipline:** treat the printed sentence as the object of analysis, not as a certified verbatim translation.
- ⚠️ **Verdict:** preserves divine goodness for moral evil; incomplete for natural and innocent suffering.

#### FINAL REGISTER 7/16 — Hick's Irenaean soul-making

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** ✅ Courage requires danger and compassion requires another's need, so a world engineered to prevent all suffering would prevent the goods that make persons.

- ✅ **Source:** John Hick, *Evil and the God of Love* (1966).
- ✅ **Premises:** ready-made virtue is not achieved virtue · the great virtues require real difficulty · a law-governed world must permit harm · humans are created immature at an **epistemic distance**.
- ✅ **Irenaeus** supplies the **image → likeness** theology; humans grow into likeness.
- ✅ **"The vale of Soul-making" is John Keats's phrase**, journal-letter to George and Georgiana Keats, spring 1819, preferred by him to "a vale of tears". Hick **borrowed** it. ⚠️ Do not attribute it to Irenaeus or call it Hick's coinage.
- ✅ **Typology:** Augustinian = backward-looking Fall from perfection; Irenaean = forward-looking growth from immaturity.
- ✅ **Coverage:** moral **and** natural evil — its advantage over the free-will defence.
- ⚠️ **Costs:** proportionality; animal suffering; dependence on a universal **eschatological fulfilment**.
- ⚠️ **Trap:** do not reduce it to "suffering makes us stronger"; the three components carry the argument.

#### FINAL REGISTER 8/16 — Rowe's evidential problem

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** ✅ Rowe's fawn is an evidential case, not a deductive contradiction.

- ✅ **Source:** William Rowe, "The Problem of Evil and Some Varieties of Atheism" (1979).
- ✅ **Premise 1:** there exist instances of intense suffering an omnipotent being could have prevented without losing a greater good or permitting an equally bad or worse evil.
- ✅ **Premise 2:** a wholly good being would prevent any intense suffering it could, absent such a reason.
- ✅ **Conclusion:** no omnipotent, omniscient, wholly good being exists.
- ✅ **The fawn:** trapped in a lightning-started fire, severely burned, suffering unobserved for days.
- ⚠️ **Why the case was built that way:** it removes free will, moral growth, desert and observer benefit at once.
- ⚠️ **The inductive step:** "no justifying reason seen" → "no justifying reason exists" — the whole battleground.
- ⚠️ **Three theist routes:** attack the inference (skeptical theism) · supply a reason (theodicy) · revise the God-concept.
- ⚠️ Routes 1 and 2 sit awkwardly together; noticing this earns marks.
- ⚠️ Rowe claims **evidential force**, not demonstration, and allows the theist may remain rational.

#### FINAL REGISTER 9/16 — Skeptical theism

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** ✅ One may claim it *appears* that p is absent only if p would likely be discernible, given one's cognitive situation, were it there.

- ✅ **Proponents:** Stephen Wykstra · William Alston · Michael Bergmann · Daniel Howard-Snyder.
- ✅ **CORNEA (1984):** Condition of ReasoNable Epistemic Access — the epistemic-access condition on any appearance-of-absence claim.
- ✅ **Bergmann's representativeness theses:** our sample of possible goods, possible evils and the entailment relations between them is **not representative** of the total space.
- ✅ **Target:** the *noseeum* inference — "no see 'um, so none there".
- ✅ **Analogies:** Alston's novice and the chess grandmaster; Wykstra's infant and the painful procedure.
- ✅ It is a **defence**, not a theodicy, and it targets the **evidential**, not the logical, problem.
- ⚠️ **Cost 1 — moral scepticism** (Almeida, Russell, Wielenberg): it threatens the judgement that we ought to prevent an evil; the "obligation is fixed by what we know" reply is found unstable.
- ⚠️ **Cost 2 — parity:** the same scepticism undercuts divine promises; restricting it to value-entailments looks *ad hoc*.
- ⚠️ **Cost 3 — bracketing:** extended, it makes theism unfalsifiable.
- ⚠️ **Indian parity:** *adṛṣṭa* as *atīndriya*, known only through testimony; karmic ripening among the Buddhist *acinteyya*. Same parallel, same objection.

#### FINAL REGISTER 10/16 — Horrendous evils

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** ✅ Global balancing is not personal defeat: a world can be good on the whole while a life is not.

- ✅ **Source:** Marilyn McCord Adams, *Horrendous Evils and the Goodness of God* (1999).
- ✅ **Definition:** an evil whose participation — as perpetrator **or** victim — gives the participant *prima facie* reason to doubt whether their life could be a great good **to them on the whole**.
- ✅ **Argument:** standard theodicies are **global**; a global balance can be struck while an individual life is a net disaster; a God good **to persons** must **defeat** the evil within that life; only religion-specific resources — divine identification, beatific intimacy as an **incommensurable good** — can do so.
- ✅ **Balancing off** = the good outweighs the evil in a sum, leaving it external. **Defeat** (Chisholm's term) = the evil becomes an organic part of a whole whose value it contributes to.
- ⚠️ **Compensation is not defeat.**
- ⚠️ **Objection 1:** purely eschatological, unverifiable, and seemingly instrumentalising. **Reply:** Adams denies instrumentality — the evil need not be *required*, only *defeated*.
- ⚠️ **Objection 2:** religion-specific resources abandon neutral ground. **Reply:** the atheological argument targets a *particular* theism.
- ⚠️ **Indian parity:** karma answers **distribution**, not personal worth; Advaita's sublation and Buddhist liberation dissolve the evaluating standpoint — critics say evasive, defenders say therapeutic.
- ⚠️ **Contrast to name:** defeat *within* the life versus *exit from* the life-series.

#### FINAL REGISTER 11/16 — Animal and pre-human suffering

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** ✅ Only nomic regularity survives on the Western side, and it explains the kind of world rather than the amount of pain in it.

- ✅ **Argument:** animal pain predates moral agency · it is not misused human freedom · the animal does not morally grow · it cannot be called retributive · therefore standard theodicies leave a major remainder.
- ⚠️ **Neo-Cartesian:** animals lack the higher-order awareness that makes pain matter — canvassed by **Michael Murray**, *Nature Red in Tooth and Claw* (2008). **Cost:** contested premise.
- ⚠️ **Nomic regularity:** a stable law-governed world is needed for any life at all. **Cost:** kind, not amount.
- ⚠️ **Extended soul-making:** danger makes stewardship toward animals possible. **Cost:** instrumentalises them.
- ⚠️ **Eschatological:** animal fulfilment in a final state. **Cost:** unverifiable and undeveloped.
- ⚠️ **Fallen order:** ❓ historically and scientifically strained.
- ✅ **Indian parity:** karma in **animal births (*tiryak-yoni*)**; Jain graded living selves grounding **non-injury (*ahiṃsā*)**; Buddhist **suffering (*duḥkha*)** and **compassion (*karuṇā*)** extended to sentient beings.
- ⚠️ **Objection:** an animal cannot learn from a fruit it cannot understand. **Reply:** karmic maturation is **causal, not pedagogical**; the compassion it demands is unaffected.
- ⚠️ Do not answer this with the free-will defence; saying so explicitly earns marks.

#### FINAL REGISTER 12/16 — Process theodicy and the non-classical reply-space

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** ✅ It purchases God's innocence at the price of God's sovereignty — state the trade.

- ✅ **Six tenets:** (1) creativity, not God, is ultimate · (2) God is **dipolar** — primordial nature supplying the **initial aim**, consequent nature receiving what the world becomes · (3) power is **intrinsically distributed** · (4) divine power is **persuasive by metaphysical necessity** (Griffin) · (5) evil is deviation from the divine lure · (6) God is "the **fellow-sufferer who understands**" (Whitehead).
- ✅ **Whitehead**, *Process and Reality* (1929) — dipolarity, initial aim, consequent nature, the fellow-sufferer.
- ✅ **Hartshorne**, *The Divine Relativity* (1948), *Omnipotence and Other Theological Mistakes* (1984) — classical omnipotence is **incoherent**; surrelativism/panentheism makes God supremely **related**, not supremely unaffected.
- ✅ **Griffin**, *God, Power, and Evil* (1976), *Evil Revisited* (1991) — persuasion is a **metaphysical necessity, not a self-limitation**.
- ✅ **Cobb**, *A Christian Natural Theology* — the theological systematisation.
- ✅ It is a **theodicy**, not a defence: it claims God's actual situation.
- ⚠️ **Criticism:** a God who cannot guarantee victory may be unworthy of worship; the metaphysics is contested. **Reply:** solidarity plus the **objective immortality** of achieved value.
- ⚠️ **Reply-space:** greater-good/stable order (kind not amount) · Leibnizian best world (no unique best; neglects persons) · eschatological fulfilment (compensation ≠ justification) · divine mystery (unfalsifiable without an access principle) · protest/anti-theodicy (diagnoses ethics, settles no argument).

#### FINAL REGISTER 13/16 — The Indian response

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** ✅ Karma is not one doctrine across the Indian schools: Mīmāṃsā, Buddhist and Jain accounts need no creator; Nyāya and theistic Vedānta assign God a just administrative role.

- ✅ **Karma-saṃsāra:** suffering is the fruit of one's own past deeds across lives; the moral law is **self-operating**, so evil raises no problem for a creator's goodness.
- ✅ **Structural advantage:** it answers **distribution** — why *this* person — which Western theodicy answers weakly.
- ✅ **Nyāya / Rāmānuja:** God dispenses karmic fruit justly as **karma-superintendent (*karmādhyakṣa*)** — a judge, not an arbitrary cause of evil.
- ✅ **Advaita:** evil is real at the **empirical level (*vyāvahārika*)** and sublated under liberating knowledge at the **ultimate level (*pāramārthika*)**; Brahman is untouched. ⚠️ Cost: risk of minimising suffering.
- ✅ **Beginninglessness:** the action–consequence series needs no first wrongful act. ⚠️ It blocks a regress question but **verifies nothing**.
- ⚠️ **Victim-blaming control:** the inference from "this person suffers" to "this person deserves it for a specific past act" is **unavailable to ordinary observers**.
- ⚠️ **Residual pressure:** natural suffering, collective harms and unequal starting conditions still press fairness and verification.
- ✅ **Duty of compassion:** karma does not cancel present duties of compassion, protection and relief.
- ⚠️ **Origination vocabulary:** ignorance (*avidyā*) · inflow (*āsrava*) · craving (*taṇhā*) — India asks about the **origination of bondage**, the West about **permission**.

#### FINAL REGISTER 14/16 — Evil and the profane as religion-forming categories

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** ✅ Evil and the profane found religion structurally rather than doctrinally — and they found religion, not theism.

- ✅ **Durkheim**, *The Elementary Forms of Religious Life* (1912): all known religious beliefs presuppose a classification of all things into two opposed classes, **profane** and **sacred**; religion is a unified system of beliefs and practices relative to sacred things, uniting adherents into a moral community, a **church**. Deliberately **God-free**.
- ✅ **Otto**, *The Idea of the Holy* (1917): the **numinous**, *mysterium tremendum et fascinans* — the **object-side**.
- ✅ **Eliade**, *The Sacred and the Profane* (1957): **hierophany**; sacred **space** around an *axis mundi*; recoverable sacred **time**.
- ⚠️ **Founding sequence:** predicament → boundary → institution → soteriology.
- ✅ **Indian parity:** Buddhist **First Noble Truth** (the clearest world case) · Jain *āsrava*/*bandha* with *saṃvara*/*nirjarā* · Advaita's *avidyā* · *śuddhi/aśuddhi* and *śauca* · *maṅgala/amaṅgala* · *pāpa/puṇya* · Mīmāṃsā ritual purity.
- ⚠️ **Rider:** India's founding defect is more often **ignorance or bondage** than **moral evil** — diagnosis-and-insight rather than sin-and-forgiveness.
- ✅ **Four distinct categories:** evil (harm) ≠ profane (the ordinary) ≠ impure (ritually polluting) ≠ demonic (actively anti-sacred).
- ⚠️ **Objections:** the binary is a Durkheimian imposition and Eliade's archaic ontology is unfalsifiable → treat as an **ideal type**; and religion also grows from wonder and gratitude.
- ✅ **Ownership:** this file owns the evil/profane pole; the sacred/holy-as-object pole belongs to *Religious Experience*.

#### FINAL REGISTER 15/16 — Concept-dependence and the comparative grid

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** ⚠️ The problem is a stress-test of a *particular* God-concept, not of religion as such.

| God-concept | Effect on the problem | Religiously significant loss |
|---|---|---|
| Classical theism | Full force; all three burdens live | None yet — the theodicies must do the work |
| Finite / process God | Dissolved at the logical level | Sovereignty; any guarantee of final victory |
| Impersonal Absolute (Advaita) | Relocated to the empirical level | A personal God to address; risk of minimising suffering |
| No creator (Buddhism, Jainism, Mīmāṃsā) | Removed — no defendant | Providence, divine rescue, a cosmic guarantor of justice |

| Theodicy | Evil explained | Attribute preserved | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free-will | Moral | Omnipotence + goodness | Natural evil left over |
| Augustinian privation | Moral, via the Fall | All attributes | Privation strained; innocence and animals |
| Soul-making | Moral + natural | Purposive goodness | Scale; animals; universalist eschatology |
| Process | All | Goodness, not omnipotence | Sacrifices classical omnipotence |
| Karma | All, including distribution | Cosmic justice; no creator-blame | Victim-blaming; verification; regress |
| Advaita | Empirical-level evil | Brahman untouched | Risk of minimising suffering |
| Skeptical theism | None — a defence | All attributes | Moral scepticism; unfalsifiability |

- ⚠️ **Four standing criticisms:** excess evil · natural evil · divine responsibility · karma and compassion — each with a reply *and* a rejoinder.
- ⚠️ Concept-dependence is **not a solution**; it relocates the cost from the argument into the theology.

#### FINAL REGISTER 16/16 — Answer architecture and quotation discipline

> **EXAM-READY LINE:** ⚠️ A theodicy is not refuted by its cost but is measured by it — name the cost, then grade the reply against the version it was built for.

- ⚠️ **Universal spine:** name the version → write the set with its bridges → reconstruct the reply as premises → object and reply → one deliberate comparator → grade by cost and residue.
- ⚠️ **10 marks:** exact problem → God-concept → one response with its presupposition → one objection → compatibility verdict. ≈150 words.
- ⚠️ **15 marks:** logical/evidential frame → the doctrine's argument → canonical example → objection and reply → Indian comparator → explanatory cost. ≈240–260 words.
- ⚠️ **20 marks:** define evil and state the set → strongest theodicy in full → rival theodicy or Indian view → two objections with replies → defence versus theodicy → graded verdict. ≈340–360 words.
- ⚠️ **Verdict ladder:** ≈40% asserts the triad · ≈50% distinguishes logical from evidential · ≈60% states the bridges and one full theodicy · ≈65–70% version-matched with costs · ≈70%+ adds defeat-versus-balancing-off, CORNEA or the animal gap **and** says what the theist has given up.
- ✅ **Quotation discipline:** dilemma → **Lactantius**, *De Ira Dei* 13 (traditionally Epicurus) · vale of Soul-making → **Keats** 1819 · defeat → **Chisholm** · fellow-sufferer → **Whitehead** · CORNEA → **Wykstra** 1984 · transworld depravity → **Plantinga** 1974, possibility only · sacred/profane → **Durkheim** 1912 · hierophany → **Eliade** 1957 · numinous → **Otto** 1917.
- ⚠️ **Safe formulations:** "The problem's force varies with the God-concept." · "A successful defence defeats contradiction; it need not make suffering morally transparent." · "Global balancing is not personal defeat." · "Evil and the profane found religion structurally, not doctrinally."
- ⚠️ **Never end devotionally.** A graded verdict answers the directive and follows from the body.

```ascii-master
   (1) There exist instances of intense suffering that an omnipotent, omniscient being
```

```ascii-master
   India MORALISES animal suffering; the West mostly BRACKETS it
                                    |
   EPISTEMIC PARITY WITH SKEPTICAL THEISM
   unseen moral potency (adrsta) is BEYOND THE SENSES (atindriya), known only through
```

#### ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 19/22: Evil and the profane — the three theorists and the sequence

```ascii-master
ASCII MASTER FLOW — PANEL 19/22: Evil and the profane — the three theorists and the sequence

   2019 Q6(b), 15 marks. THIS IS A FUNCTIONAL, NOT A THEOLOGICAL, QUESTION.
   It does not ask whether evil disproves God. It asks what evil and the profane DO.

   THE THREE CANONICAL THEORISTS — DO NOT INTERCHANGE THEM
   DURKHEIM  The Elementary Forms of Religious Life (1912) — SOCIAL SIDE
             all known religious beliefs presuppose a classification of all things into
             two opposed classes, PROFANE and SACRED; religion = a unified system of
             beliefs and practices RELATIVE TO SACRED THINGS, uniting adherents into a
             single moral community, a CHURCH.  The definition is deliberately GOD-FREE.
   OTTO      The Idea of the Holy (1917) — OBJECT SIDE
             the sacred is an A PRIORI category of response: the NUMINOUS,
             mysterium tremendum et fascinans
   ELIADE    The Sacred and the Profane (1957) — SPATIAL / TEMPORAL
             the sacred irrupts as HIEROPHANY, generating sacred SPACE (a fixed centre,
             AXIS MUNDI, against homogeneous profane extension) and sacred TIME
             (recoverable and repeatable, against irreversible profane duration)
                                    |
   THE FOUR-STEP FOUNDING SEQUENCE
   (1) PREDICAMENT  something is wrong: sin · impurity · suffering (duhkha) · ignorance
                    (avidya) · karmic bondage · alienation
   (2) BOUNDARY     pure/impure · sacred/profane · saved/lost -> ritual, prohibition, code
   (3) INSTITUTION  priesthood · initiation · purification · penance · community discipline
   (4) SOTERIOLOGY  the ordered path OUT
   => evil and the profane are not embarrassments TO religion but part of its STRUCTURAL
      FOUNDATION: without a diagnosis of what is wrong, salvation-language has no work to do.
                                    |
   FOUR CATEGORIES THAT MUST NOT BE MERGED
   EVIL = moral or natural harm | PROFANE = the ORDINARY, the non-consecrated
   IMPURE = the ritually polluting | DEMONIC = the actively anti-sacred
   ! DURKHEIM'S PROFANE IS NOT EVIL. A ploughed field, a market and a weekday are profane
   and none of them is wicked. Conflating them is the commonest error on this stem.
```
