### SESSION 1 — WHAT JAINISM IS: CONQUEROR (jina), FORD-MAKER (tīrthaṅkara) AND THE TEXTUAL ANCHOR

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** Jainism is a liberation-centred, non-Vedic realist philosophy that explains why a naturally omniscient soul is empirically ignorant, and how disciplined conduct removes that obscuration.

**Technical definition:** Jaina philosophy (*jaina-darśana*) is a pluralistic realism that treats every real as a substance (*dravya*) enduring through modes (*paryāya*), holds that reality is many-sided (*anekāntavāda*), disciplines finite judgement through standpoints (*naya*) and conditional predication (*syāt*), and explains bondage as the adhesion of subtle material action-matter (*karma-pudgala*) to the conscious soul (*jīva*), removable through the three jewels (*ratnatraya*).

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Jainism is best defined as a pluralistic realism with non-absolutist finite predication and a material theory of karmic bondage: reality has many objective aspects, finite judgement reaches it only under standpoints, and liberation is achieved by removing real karmic matter rather than by acquiring divine grace.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **conqueror (jina)** — one who has conquered the passions, not a creator
- **ford-maker (tīrthaṅkara)** — liberated teacher who re-establishes the crossing over rebirth
- **Umāsvāti / Umāsvāmī, *Tattvārthasūtra***
- **three jewels (ratnatraya)** — right vision, right knowledge, right conduct
- **non-theism about creation (anīśvaravāda)**
- **Veda-rejecting school (nāstika)**

**How to use them:** open by naming the system, not the sect. Say that Jainism is a *darśana* with a shared systematic text, then state the three jewels as its path formula, and only then add that its religious authority rests on perfected teachers rather than on a creator.

#### SIMPLE START — PLAIN-LANGUAGE GATEWAY

```text
   A SOUL THAT ALREADY HAS FULL KNOWLEDGE
                    |
        but it is covered by fine matter
                    |
                    v
   +----------------+----------------+
   |                                 |
   SEE RIGHTLY      KNOW RIGHTLY     ACT RIGHTLY      <- the three jewels
   |                                 |
   +----------------+----------------+
                    |
       covering matter stops coming in, then falls away
                    |
                    v
        THE SOUL SHINES AS IT ALWAYS WAS
```

**In simple words:** Jainism does not ask how a soul becomes great. It asks why an already perfect knower behaves like an ignorant one. Its whole system — ontology, logic and ethics — is built to answer that single question by identifying the covering, explaining how the covering arrives, and prescribing how it is stopped and shed.

| Remember first | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Jina* | "Conqueror" of passions; the school takes its name from the title, not from a founder-god |
| *Tīrthaṅkara* | Ford-maker: a liberated teacher who rebuilds the crossing, twenty-four in the present cycle |
| *Tattvārthasūtra* | First systematic Sanskrit exposition, authoritative for **both** major traditions |
| Path formula | Right vision + right knowledge + right conduct, taken together |
| What Jainism denies | A creator God, not soul, ethics, rebirth or spiritual perfection |

> **One-line memory:** Jainism is the science of removing a covering from a soul that is already luminous.

⚠️ **Caption.** Examiners repeatedly reward candidates who show that Jaina logic is *derived*, not decorative. Many-sidedness is a claim about the object; standpoint-relativity is a claim about the knower; the *syāt* particle is a claim about the sentence; and material karma explains why the knower is limited in the first place. Answer scripts that treat *anekāntavāda* as mere tolerance lose exactly this derivation.

#### 1. Umāsvāti / Umāsvāmī and the *Tattvārthasūtra*

✅ The *Tattvārthasūtra*, also called *Tattvārthādhigama-sūtra*, of **Umāsvāti** in Śvetāmbara usage and **Umāsvāmī** in Digambara usage, is the first systematic Sanskrit exposition of Jaina philosophy and is treated as authoritative by **both** major traditions.

✅ What it does, and why that matters in an answer:

1. It supplies a compact doctrinal grammar covering metaphysics, epistemology, ethics, bondage and liberation in one architecture.
2. Its cross-sectarian authority is unusual, because Śvetāmbara and Digambara traditions otherwise differ on canon, monastic practice and several doctrinal details.
3. It states the path through the **three jewels (ratnatraya)**: right vision (*samyag-darśana*), right knowledge (*samyag-jñāna*) and right conduct (*samyak-cāritra*).
4. It systematises the seven principles (*tattvas*), the five extended substances plus time, and the classification of knowledge (*jñāna*) — so Jainism cannot honestly be reduced either to "just ethics" or to "just seven-fold logic".

⚠️ **Presupposition worth stating.** An answer improves the moment Jainism is presented as a systematic *darśana* rather than as a collection of ascetic practices. The examiner is testing whether the candidate can locate a doctrine inside a system.

❓ **Sectarian-history objection.** Does a text accepted by both traditions erase real sectarian difference?
⚠️ **Reply.** It does not. It supplies a shared doctrinal framework *within which* disagreements about canon, practice and interpretation continue. Cite the text as a systematic anchor, never as proof of doctrinal uniformity.

✅ **Names to drop carefully, with what each name buys you:**

| Name | Tradition / period | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| **Kundakunda** | Digambara; *Samayasāra* | The real-standpoint / conventional-standpoint contrast (*niścaya-naya* / *vyavahāra-naya*) |
| **Siddhasena Divākara** | Early logic | Early systematisation of many-sidedness and conditional predication |
| **Samantabhadra** | Early logic | Early defence of *syādvāda* against the contradiction charge |
| **Akalaṅka** | Mature logic | Developed Jaina theory of means of valid knowledge (*pramāṇa*) |
| **Māṇikyanandi**, **Hemacandra**, **Yaśovijaya** | Later logic | The mature indirect-knowledge tradition, including recognition and reasoning |

⚠️ Do **not** invent *sūtra* numbers, dates or authorship certainties. Write "the *Tattvārthasūtra* states the path through right vision, right knowledge and right conduct" — that formulation is safe and complete.

#### 2. Conqueror, ford-maker and the shape of Jaina religious authority

✅ "Jaina" derives from **conqueror (jina)**: one who has conquered the passions and karmic bondage. A **ford-maker (tīrthaṅkara)** is a liberated teacher who re-establishes the crossing over the cycle of rebirth (*saṃsāra*) — not a creator, not a governor of the world, and not a granter of grace.

✅ The received framework:

1. Twenty-four ford-makers are recognised in the present cosmic cycle.
2. **Ṛṣabha** is named first, **Pārśva** twenty-third and **Vardhamāna Mahāvīra** twenty-fourth.
3. Mahāvīra **systematised and renewed an already existing tradition**; describing him as inventing Jainism is a factual error in the Jaina account itself.
4. Śvetāmbara and Digambara traditions differ on canon, monastic practice and some doctrinal details, while sharing the central grammar of soul, action-matter, many-sidedness, vows and liberation.

❓ **Historical caution.** Mahāvīra and Pārśva function as historical anchors, but precise dates and claims about the earliest ford-makers require source-specific qualification. A philosophy answer needs the framework, not legendary chronology; do not assert regnal or birth years you cannot source.

✅ **Philosophical consequence, and this is the examinable point:** Jainism combines an **uncreated cosmos** with religious authority grounded in **perfected teachers**. It is unorthodox (*nāstika*) because it rejects Vedic authority — not because it denies soul, ethics, rebirth or spiritual perfection.

| Sense of "Veda-rejecting (nāstika)" | Does Jainism qualify? |
|---|---|
| Rejects the authority of the Veda — the **primary classificatory sense** | ✅ Yes; this is why Jainism sits with Cārvāka and Buddhism in the unorthodox column |
| Denies life after death — a **secondary sense** | ❌ No; Jainism affirms rebirth, action-consequence and liberation |
| Modern vernacular "atheist" | ⚠️ Misleading; Sāṃkhya and Mīmāṃsā are non-theistic yet Veda-accepting |

> 🔑 **Trap, and it is examined:** Jainism and Cārvāka are both *nāstika*, but for different reasons and to different depths. Cārvāka is unorthodox in **both** senses; Jainism only in the **first**.

#### 3. Why Jainism denies a creator without denying divinity

✅ The argument, in the order it should be written:

1. The six substances are eternal in their substantial continuity.
2. Their changing modes explain ordinary production and destruction, so no creation from nothing is needed.
3. Action-matter (*karma*) explains embodied inequality and moral consequence without divine allocation.
4. A creator therefore adds no necessary explanatory work while raising further questions about motive, material, imperfection and responsibility.
5. Liberation depends on self-purification, not on grace.

✅ **Distinction to protect:** Jainism denies a *creator*, not divinity in the honorific sense. A conqueror (*jina*), a perfected soul (*siddha*) and a ford-maker (*tīrthaṅkara*) are worthy of reverence because of achieved perfection, not because they made or govern the universe.

⚠️ **Exam formula.** Jainism is a religion of self-effort within an uncreated moral cosmos: non-theistic about creation, yet neither materialist nor spiritually negative.

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH — claim → named evidence → analysis → qualification

> **Claim.** Jainism should be introduced as a systematic *darśana* whose religious authority is achieved rather than creative.
> **Named evidence.** The *Tattvārthasūtra* of Umāsvāti / Umāsvāmī is accepted across Śvetāmbara and Digambara traditions and states the path as right vision, right knowledge and right conduct taken together; the tradition's own title-word is *jina*, "conqueror of the passions", and a *tīrthaṅkara* is a ford-maker, not a maker of the world.
> **Analysis.** Because authority is achieved through the destruction of obscuring action-matter, the same architecture that yields the ethics also yields the epistemology: a soul freed from obscuration knows completely, so partial knowledge is a karmic condition rather than a permanent limit of the knower.
> **Qualification / link.** The shared text does not erase sectarian difference, and the earliest ford-makers cannot be treated as datable history; the safe claim is doctrinal and systematic, and it is exactly the claim the question rewards.

#### UPSC TRAPS IN THIS SUBTOPIC

1. ❌ Calling Mahāvīra the founder of Jainism. ✅ He is the twenty-fourth ford-maker who renewed an existing tradition.
2. ❌ Translating *nāstika* as "atheist". ✅ The primary criterion is rejection of Vedic authority.
3. ❌ Treating the *Tattvārthasūtra* as proof of sectarian uniformity. ✅ It is a shared systematic anchor, not a unifying canon.
4. ❌ Reducing Jainism to non-violence. ✅ Non-violence is derived from the plurality of souls and the mechanics of bondage.
5. ❌ Inventing *sūtra* numbers or ford-maker dates. ✅ Name the text and the doctrine, never fabricate the citation.

#### RAPID REVISION

- Textual anchor: **Umāsvāti / Umāsvāmī**, *Tattvārthasūtra*, cross-sectarian authority.
- Path formula: right vision **+** right knowledge **+** right conduct, jointly and not serially.
- No creator; liberated souls remain **numerically distinct** individuals.
- Unorthodox on the **Veda criterion only**, unlike Cārvāka.
- Never reduce the system to "seven viewpoints" or to "non-violence".

- Directly controls the "pluralistic and realistic" verdict demanded in **2025 Q7(c), 15 marks**.
- Keep sectarian history to two lines at most unless the question asks about sources.

### SESSION 2 — THEORY OF REALITY I: MANY-SIDEDNESS (anekāntavāda) AND THE REAL AS ORIGINATION, DECAY AND PERSISTENCE

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** A real thing is neither a frozen block nor a bare series of flashes; it keeps its identity while its states genuinely change, and it has more true aspects than any single sentence can capture.

**Technical definition:** **Many-sidedness (anekāntavāda)** is the metaphysical thesis that the real object is *ananta-dharmātmakaṃ vastu*, a thing constituted by innumerable aspects; and the real (*sat*) is defined as that which possesses **origination (utpāda)**, **decay (vyaya)** and **persistence (dhrauvya)** together.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> For the Jainas reality is defined, not merely described: the real is that which originates, decays and persists at once, so permanence belongs to it as substance and change belongs to it as mode — and it is precisely this composite structure that forces every finite judgement to be aspect-relative.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **many-sidedness (anekāntavāda)**
- **the real (sat) = origination + decay + persistence (utpāda-vyaya-dhrauvya)**
- **substance (dravya) · quality (guṇa) · mode (paryāya)**
- **thing with innumerable aspects (ananta-dharmātmakaṃ vastu)**
- **one-sidedness (ekāntavāda)** — the error being refused
- **aspect-realism, not subjectivism**

**How to use them:** state the definition of the real *first*, derive many-sidedness from it, and only then move to judgement. A script that begins with the seven predications and works backwards loses the derivation marks.

#### SIMPLE START — PLAIN-LANGUAGE GATEWAY

```text
       GOLD BRACELET                          GOLD RING
             |                                    ^
             |  melted and reshaped               |
             +------------------------------------+

   WHAT ENDED   : the bracelet-mode          -> decay      (vyaya)
   WHAT BEGAN   : the ring-mode              -> origination (utpāda)
   WHAT STAYED  : the gold itself            -> persistence (dhrauvya)

   The jeweller's customer who wanted a bracelet is disappointed.
   The customer who wanted a ring is delighted.
   The one who simply wanted GOLD is neither.        <- three true reports, one object
```

**In simple words:** the three onlookers are not contradicting one another; they are reporting the same object under three different aspects. Jainism generalises that ordinary situation into a metaphysics: a real thing genuinely gains a state, genuinely loses a state, and genuinely stays itself, all at the same time.

| Term | Easy meaning | What it prevents |
|---|---|---|
| Substance (*dravya*) | The enduring bearer | Prevents collapse into pure flux |
| Quality (*guṇa*) | Its abiding characteristic | Prevents a bare, featureless substratum |
| Mode (*paryāya*) | Its changing state | Prevents a static, inert absolute |
| Many-sidedness (*anekāntavāda*) | Aspects are really in the object | Prevents relativism about the object |

> **One-line memory:** the substance persists while the modes originate and perish.

⚠️ **Caption.** This diagram is the whole answer to "how is reality defined by the Jainas?" (2019 Q6(a)). The definition is chosen because it is the only one that saves *both* recognition and transformation, and the price of saving both is that judgement must become aspect-relative.

#### 1. Many-sidedness stated as a metaphysical thesis

✅ **Statement.** Many-sidedness (*anekāntavāda*) is the doctrine that reality is many-sided; no single unqualified proposition exhausts the nature of a real thing.

✅ **Argument, in five steps that can be written verbatim:**

1. Real things endure through change and exhibit multiple aspects.
2. Human cognition usually grasps only one aspect at a time.
3. Dogmatic one-sided claims therefore distort reality.
4. A sound metaphysics must allow a plurality of aspects within one real.
5. Hence reality is non-one-sided — *anekānta*.

⚠️ **Presupposition.** Being is richer than any single finite judgement.

✅ **Distinction that earns marks.** Many-sidedness is **not** indecision, tolerance or diplomacy. It is a **metaphysical** claim about the complexity of the object, logically prior to any theory of predication.

✅ **Example.** A jar is permanent as a substance across a stretch of time, changing in its modes, and describable by shape, use, material and temporal state. No one predicate says all of this at once.

✅ **Sceptical objection.** If every object has indefinitely many aspects, knowledge may become impossible.
⚠️ **Reply.** Knowledge is possible *precisely* through qualified standpoints rather than through absolutist simplification; partiality is a condition of finite knowing, not a defeat of it.

#### 2. The real (*sat*) as origination, decay and persistence

✅ **Statement.** The real (*sat*) is what possesses **origination (utpāda)**, **decay (vyaya)** and **persistence (dhrauvya)** together — the formula usually cited as *utpāda-vyaya-dhrauvya-yuktaṃ sat*.

✅ **Argument:**

1. Experience shows change: forms arise and pass.
2. Experience also shows continuity: something persists through those changes.
3. Pure permanence cannot explain transformation.
4. Pure flux cannot explain identity.
5. Therefore reality must include both persistence and modification.
6. This is articulated by saying that substance is characterised by origination, decay and permanence.

⚠️ **Presupposition.** Both common-sense identity and experienced change are philosophically significant and must be preserved rather than explained away.

✅ **Distinction.** This differs from Buddhist momentariness, which gives primacy to flux, and from strict monisms that absorb change into mere appearance.

✅ **The named objection.** How can opposite features — change and permanence — belong to one thing?
✅ **The reply.** They belong **in different respects**: permanence as substance (*dravya*), change as mode (*paryāya*). This single sentence disarms the contradiction charge at its root.

#### 3. Substance, quality and mode

✅ **Statement.** Every substance (*dravya*) possesses **qualities (guṇa)**, its essential and abiding characteristics, and **modes (paryāya)**, its changing states or modifications.

✅ **Argument:**

1. A substance must have stable features or it would not be what it is.
2. It must also undergo modal change or it could not participate in experience.
3. Therefore it is intelligible only as substance-with-qualities-and-modes.

✅ **Distinction, and this is examined.** *Guṇa* is **not** *paryāya*. Qualities belong to the substance as abiding characteristics; modes are the changing states in which those qualities appear.

✅ **Example.** Consciousness (*upayoga*) is a defining quality of the soul; pleasure, pain, delusion or liberated omniscience are **modes** in which the soul appears.

✅ **Buddhist nominalist objection.** Why not speak only of changing qualities and drop the substance?
⚠️ **Reply.** Because changing qualities still presuppose something whose changes they are; a series of states without a bearer cannot ground recognition or moral continuity.

| Level | Jaina term | Status | Test question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bearer | *dravya* | Persists | What is it that changed? |
| Abiding character | *guṇa* | Persists with the bearer | What makes it that kind of thing? |
| Changing state | *paryāya* | Originates and decays | What is it doing or undergoing now? |

#### 4. Causation: identity and novelty together

✅/⚠️ **Statement.** Jaina causation is standardly described as **both-existence-and-non-existence of the effect (sadasatkāryavāda)**: the effect exists in the cause with respect to enduring substance, and does not yet exist with respect to its new mode.

✅ **Argument:**

1. If the effect were wholly identical with the cause, production would add nothing.
2. If it were wholly non-existent and unrelated, anything could arise from anything.
3. In transformation the underlying substance persists while an earlier mode ceases and a new mode originates.
4. Therefore the effect is continuous with the cause as substance and novel as mode.

✅ **Example.** A clay jar is not produced from sheer nothing, because clay persists; yet the jar-form is genuinely new and was not previously present as that completed mode.

✅ **Comparison to write in one line.** Jaina causation mediates between Sāṃkhya's pre-existence doctrine (*satkāryavāda*) and Nyāya's new-production doctrine (*asatkāryavāda*) by applying many-sidedness to causal identity.

✅ **Objection.** Saying the effect both exists and does not exist appears contradictory.
✅ **Reply.** Existence and non-existence are indexed to different respects — substance and mode — so no unqualified contradiction is affirmed.

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH — claim → named evidence → analysis → qualification

> **Claim.** The Jaina definition of the real is chosen to save recognition and transformation simultaneously, and every later Jaina doctrine follows from that choice.
> **Named evidence.** The real is defined as *utpāda-vyaya-dhrauvya-yuktaṃ sat*; the standard illustration is the gold that loses its bracelet-mode, gains its ring-mode and persists as gold; and the same structure yields Jaina causation as *sadasatkāryavāda*, positioned between Sāṃkhya's *satkāryavāda* and Nyāya's *asatkāryavāda*.
> **Analysis.** Because permanence is predicated of the substance and change of the mode, the two apparently opposed predicates never occupy the same respect, which is why the Jaina can be a realist about change without becoming a momentarist and a realist about identity without becoming a static monist.
> **Qualification / link.** The defence works only if the substance–mode distinction is itself granted; a Buddhist who refuses aspect-realism will say the dilemma has been renamed rather than solved, which is exactly why the question usually continues into the theory of judgement.

#### UPSC TRAPS IN THIS SUBTOPIC

1. ❌ "Many-sidedness means every opinion is equally true." ✅ It means the *object* has many real aspects; judgements are still gradable.
2. ❌ Using *guṇa* and *paryāya* interchangeably. ✅ Quality abides; mode changes.
3. ❌ Presenting the gold example as mere illustration. ✅ It encodes the three-term definition of the real.
4. ❌ Calling Jaina causation *satkāryavāda*. ✅ It is *sadasatkāryavāda*, and the reason is the substance/mode index.
5. ❌ Starting the 2019 answer with the seven predications. ✅ Define the real first; judgement is the *reflection* of that definition.

#### RAPID REVISION

- *sat* = origination + decay + persistence, **together**.
- Substance persists; quality abides with it; mode originates and perishes.
- Many-sidedness is **ontology**, not tolerance.
- Causation: continuous as substance, novel as mode.
- The contradiction charge dies on the phrase "in different respects".

#### EXAM APPLICATION — WHERE THIS IS ASKED

- **2019 Q6(a), 20 marks** — "How is reality defined by the Jainas?" begins exactly here.
- **2025 Q7(c), 15 marks** — the realism half of "pluralistic and realistic" rests on this section.
- **2020 Q7(c), 15 marks** (Cārvāka-owned) — the Jaina half of the Cārvāka/Jaina contrast is this definition.

### SESSION 3 — THEORY OF REALITY II: SIX SUBSTANCES, SOUL AND NON-SOUL, AND MATTER

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** Jainism sorts everything that exists into six kinds of substance, splits them into the conscious and the non-conscious, and treats matter as the only substance that can be perceived by the senses.

**Technical definition:** Jaina ontology recognises six substances (*dravyas*) — soul (*jīva*), matter (*pudgala*), medium of motion (*dharma*), medium of rest (*adharma*), space (*ākāśa*) and time (*kāla*) — of which the first five are extended substances (*astikāyas*), while time is a substance that is **not** an *astikāya*.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Jaina pluralism is not a list but an explanatory scheme: consciousness, corporeality, motion, rest, accommodation and succession each require a distinct real, and the resulting six-substance ontology is what makes Jainism irreducibly plural without becoming arbitrary.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **soul (jīva) / non-soul (ajīva)**
- **six substances (ṣaḍ-dravya)**; **five extended substances (pañcāstikāya)**
- **space-points (pradeśa)**; **world-space (lokākāśa) / beyond-world space (alokākāśa)**
- **matter (pudgala)**; **atom (paramāṇu)**; **aggregate (skandha)**
- **infinite tetrad (ananta-catuṣṭaya)** — knowledge, perception, bliss, power
- **immobile (sthāvara) / mobile (trasa)** beings

**How to use them:** name the six, immediately flag that only five are *astikāyas*, then give each substance a one-line explanatory function. Listing without functions reads as memorisation.

#### SIMPLE START — PLAIN-LANGUAGE GATEWAY

```text
   Ask six different "why" questions about one ordinary event:
   a fish swims across a pond and then rests.

   WHY IS THERE AN EXPERIENCER AT ALL?          -> soul        (jīva)
   WHY IS THERE A BODY AND WATER?               -> matter      (pudgala)
   WHY IS MOVEMENT POSSIBLE?                    -> medium of motion (dharma)
   WHY IS RESTING POSSIBLE?                     -> medium of rest   (adharma)
   WHERE DOES ALL OF IT FIT?                    -> space       (ākāśa)
   WHY IS THERE A BEFORE AND AN AFTER?          -> time        (kāla)
```

**In simple words:** each substance answers a question that the others cannot. The media of motion and rest do **not** push or stop the fish; they are the standing conditions without which swimming and resting would be impossible at all — like water for a fish, not like a hand pushing it.

| Term | Easy meaning | Frequent error to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| *dharma-dravya* | Condition enabling motion | It does **not** mean merit or virtue here |
| *adharma-dravya* | Condition enabling rest | It does **not** mean sin or demerit here |
| *ākāśa* | Room or accommodation | Not "ether" in the Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika sense of a sound-substrate |
| *kāla* | Succession, duration | A substance, but **not** an extended substance |

> **One-line memory:** six substances, five extensions, one consciousness.

⚠️ **Caption.** Two examinable facts are locked into this single board. First, *kāla* is the only substance that is not an *astikāya*, which is why the tradition speaks of **five extended substances plus time**. Second, *pudgala* is the only substance with form (*rūpa*), which is precisely what allows the Jaina theory of material action-matter in Session 8.

#### 1. The six substances and their explanatory functions

✅ **Statement.** Jainism classically recognises six substances: **soul (jīva)**, **matter (pudgala)**, **medium of motion (dharma)**, **medium of rest (adharma)**, **space (ākāśa)** and **time (kāla)**.

| Substance | Basic sense | Explanatory role |
|---|---|---|
| **Soul (jīva)** | Conscious substance | Experiencer, knower, bearer of bondage and liberation |
| **Matter (pudgala)** | Material substance | Corporeality, karmic matter, bodies, sense-objects |
| **Medium of motion (dharma)** | Enabling condition | Makes movement possible |
| **Medium of rest (adharma)** | Enabling condition | Makes rest possible |
| **Space (ākāśa)** | Accommodation | Gives room to substances |
| **Time (kāla)** | Succession | Change, sequence, before-and-after |

✅ **The extension test, which is where marks are won and lost:**

| Substance | Extended substance (*astikāya*)? | Extension |
|---|---|---|
| Soul | ✅ Yes | Has space-points (*pradeśa*); in embodiment it is co-extensive with the body |
| Matter | ✅ Yes | Extended and aggregative |
| Medium of motion | ✅ Yes | Co-extensive with world-space (*lokākāśa*) |
| Medium of rest | ✅ Yes | Co-extensive with world-space |
| Space | ✅ Yes | World-space (*lokākāśa*) and beyond-world space (*alokākāśa*) |
| **Time** | ❌ **No** | A substance, but **an-astikāya**: it lacks extension across many space-points |

✅ **Safe doctrinal formulation:** time is a substance but not an extended substance; the *pañcāstikāya* list has five members while the *dravya* list has six.

❓ **Sectarian nuance, stated with care.** Digambara authors characteristically affirm real **time-atoms (kāla-aṇus)** associated with the space-points of world-space, whereas some Śvetāmbara discussions treat the substantial independence of time as disputed or conventional. Report the dispute; do not adjudicate it.

✅ **Named objection (Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika style).** Media of motion and rest look mysterious and unparsimonious.
⚠️ **Reply.** They are not forces or pushes but **enabling conditions** required for a coherent pluralistic cosmology; a fish swims because water permits swimming, and the medium of motion is the cosmic analogue of that permission.

#### 2. Soul and non-soul — the fundamental division

✅ **Statement.** The most fundamental ontological distinction is between **soul (jīva)**, the conscious, and **non-soul (ajīva)**, the non-conscious.

✅ **Argument:** experience distinguishes living, aware beings from inert entities; consciousness cannot be reduced to inert matter in Jaina thought; hence the universe divides into soul and non-soul.

✅ **Distinctions to write:** unlike Advaita, this is not a distinction *within* one underlying Brahman; unlike Cārvāka, consciousness is not reducible to material organisation.

✅ **The problem this immediately creates.** If the soul is immaterial, how can material action-matter affect it? That question is the engine of Session 8, and a good answer flags it here rather than pretending it does not arise.

#### 3. The nature of the soul

✅ **Statement.** Every soul is numerically distinct, beginningless, and intrinsically endowed with the **infinite tetrad (ananta-catuṣṭaya)** — infinite knowledge, infinite perception, infinite bliss and infinite power — although these are obscured by action-matter.

✅ **Argument:** liberation is meaningful only if the soul has a native capacity for perfection; bondage is therefore obscuration rather than annihilation of that nature; hence the soul is intrinsically luminous but contingently covered.

✅ **Distinction.** Jainism is pluralistic: there are **many** souls, not one universal self. It also differs from Nyāya, where the self becomes conscious through conjunction with the internal organ; in Jainism consciousness (*upayoga*) belongs to the soul intrinsically.

✅ **The classification of living beings, which grounds Jaina ethics:**

| Class | Sanskrit term | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Liberated soul | **mukta-jīva** | Free from every karmic obstruction |
| Bound soul | **baddha-jīva** | Embodied and karmically conditioned |
| Immobile bound being | **sthāvara** | One-sense life, with touch; classically earth-, water-, fire-, air- and plant-bodied |
| Mobile bound being | **trasa** | Two-, three-, four- or five-sense beings |

✅ **Spatiality, and the objection it invites.** An embodied soul has **space-points (pradeśa)** and is co-extensive with its body, like a lamp illuminating the room it occupies: the ant's soul fills an ant-body and the elephant's soul an elephant-body without becoming material. This contraction-and-expansion doctrine explains embodied awareness, but it creates a serious objection — how can an immaterial substance possess *changing* extension?

✅ **Sāṃkhya / Vedānta objection.** If the soul is naturally omniscient, ignorance becomes hard to explain.
✅ **Reply.** Action-matter as obscuring material is introduced precisely to explain the gap between intrinsic capacity and empirical limitation.

#### 4. Matter — the only substance with form

✅ **Statement.** **Matter (pudgala)** is the material substance and the **only** substance possessing form (*rūpa*); it includes atoms, bodies, sense-objects and karmic matter.

✅ **Atomic structure, stated exactly:**

1. The indivisible material atom (**paramāṇu**) is the smallest unit of matter.
2. Material aggregates (**skandha**) arise through combinations of atoms.
3. Matter possesses colour, taste, smell and touch; **sound is treated as a material modification** rather than as a separate immaterial quality — an explicit contrast with Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika, where sound is the quality of ether.
4. Jaina atoms are **not** divided into permanently different elemental kinds in the Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika manner; elemental differentiation belongs to developed modes and combinations.
5. Bodies, sense-organs, speech, breath, the mind as an embodied instrument, and karmic matter are all material configurations.

⚠️ **Etymological aid.** *Pudgala* is commonly explained through filling or combining (*pūraṇa*) and disintegrating (*galana*) — useful because it foregrounds matter's aggregative and transformable character.

✅ **Nyāya / Mīmāṃsā objection.** To call action-consequence "matter" seems strange.
✅ **Reply.** That strangeness is Jainism's originality: bondage is not merely a moral description but a real quasi-physical entanglement, and it is only possible because matter alone has form and can therefore adhere.

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH — claim → named evidence → analysis → qualification

> **Claim.** Jaina pluralism is disciplined rather than arbitrary, because each substance is admitted for a distinct explanatory task.
> **Named evidence.** The six substances are soul, matter, medium of motion, medium of rest, space and time; only five are extended substances, since time is *an-astikāya*; and only matter possesses form, which is why sound is classed as a material modification rather than as a quality of ether.
> **Analysis.** The scheme therefore answers six different questions — who experiences, what is corporeal, how movement and rest are possible, where things are accommodated and why there is succession — so removing any one member would leave a genuine explanatory gap rather than merely shortening a list.
> **Qualification / link.** The cost is real: the soul is said to expand and contract with the body, which Nyāya attacks as incompatible with eternality, and the substantial independence of time is presented differently across sectarian traditions, so the pluralism should be defended as explanatory rather than as self-evident.

#### UPSC TRAPS IN THIS SUBTOPIC

1. ❌ "Six substances, six *astikāyas*." ✅ Six substances, **five** *astikāyas*; time is excluded.
2. ❌ Reading *dharma* / *adharma* as merit and demerit. ✅ Here they are media of motion and rest.
3. ❌ Treating *ākāśa* as the Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika sound-substrate. ✅ In Jainism sound is material; space merely accommodates.
4. ❌ Saying the soul is "one". ✅ Souls are innumerable and remain numerically distinct even after liberation.
5. ❌ Treating the lamp analogy as decorative. ✅ It carries the *pradeśa* doctrine and the Nyāya objection attached to it.

#### RAPID REVISION

- Six substances; five extended; time is the exception.
- Soul: many, beginningless, intrinsically endowed with the infinite tetrad.
- Only matter has form; sound is a material modification.
- Immobile one-sense beings are morally significant — this is the metaphysical root of non-violence.
- Soul is co-extensive with its body; the lamp simile carries both the doctrine and its objection.

- **2025 Q7(c), 15 marks** — pluralism is argued from the six-substance scheme and the innumerability of souls.
- **2018 Q6(c), 15 marks** — bound and liberated souls presuppose the account of the soul given here.
- **2020 Q7(c), 15 marks** (Cārvāka-owned) — Cārvāka's four elements versus the Jaina six substances is the cleanest contrast available.

### SESSION 4 — KNOWLEDGE: THE JAINA INVERSION OF DIRECT AND INDIRECT, AND THE FIVE KINDS OF KNOWING

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** Jainism reverses the usual classroom expectation: seeing a pot with the eyes is only *conventionally* direct, because it depends on organs, mind and karmic limitation; what is truly direct is what the soul knows by itself.

**Technical definition:** Jaina epistemology divides valid knowledge into **direct (pratyakṣa)** and **indirect (parokṣa)**, where strict directness means knowing by the soul without mediation by sense or mind; ordinary sense-cognition is therefore only **conventional direct knowledge (sāṃvyavahārika pratyakṣa)**, and the five kinds of knowledge (*jñāna*) are graded by the removal of knowledge-obscuring action-matter.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> The Jaina theory of judgement is not a logical curiosity bolted onto a metaphysics: it follows necessarily from a theory of knowledge in which ordinary cognition is karmically partial, so that only omniscience could ever make an unconditioned assertion.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **direct (pratyakṣa) / indirect (parokṣa)**
- **conventional direct knowledge (sāṃvyavahārika pratyakṣa)**
- **sense-and-mind cognition (mati)**, **verbal/scriptural knowledge (śruta)**, **clairvoyant knowledge (avadhi)**, **knowledge of others' minds (manaḥparyāya)**, **omniscience (kevala)**
- **partial destruction-cum-subsidence (kṣāyopaśamika)**
- **wrong orientation (mithyā-darśana)**; **kumati**, **kuśruta**, **vibhaṅga**
- **recollection (smṛti)**, **recognition (pratyabhijñā)**, **reasoning that grasps pervasion (tarka / ūha)**

**How to use them:** deploy "conventional direct knowledge" early. It signals that you know Jaina *pratyakṣa* is not Nyāya *pratyakṣa*, which is the single most common source of lost marks on this subtopic.

#### SIMPLE START — PLAIN-LANGUAGE GATEWAY

```text
   ORDINARY EXPECTATION                   JAINA CLASSIFICATION
   ====================                   ====================

   eyes see a pot                         eyes see a pot
        |                                      |
        v                                      | depends on: sense organ + mind
   "this is DIRECT"                            |             + karmic veiling
                                               v

                                        (sāṃvyavahārika pratyakṣa)

   WHAT IS STRICTLY DIRECT?  the soul knowing without any organ standing in between
        avadhi  ->  manaḥparyāya  ->  kevala      (limited -> wider -> total)
```

**In simple words:** the Jaina test for "direct" is not vividness but **absence of a middleman**. Eyes and mind are middlemen. Remove karmic obscuration far enough and the soul knows without them; that, and only that, is direct in the strict sense.

| Term | Easy meaning | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| *pratyakṣa* (strict) | Soul knows without mediation | Explains why omniscience is the standard of full knowledge |
| *sāṃvyavahārika pratyakṣa* | Everyday, workable directness | Preserves ordinary life without conceding metaphysical finality |
| *kṣāyopaśamika* | Knowledge from partly removed veiling | The exact reason finite judgement is partial |

> **One-line memory:** directness is measured by freedom from mediation, not by brightness of appearance.

⚠️ **Caption.** Read the ladder downward and the point becomes unmistakable: the five kinds are **not** five independent means of valid knowledge in the Nyāya sense. They are stations on a single curve of karmic unveiling, which is why Jaina epistemology cannot be detached from Jaina soteriology.

#### 1. The primary division and its high-yield inversion

✅ **Statement.** Jaina epistemology distinguishes **direct (pratyakṣa)** and **indirect (parokṣa)** knowledge, but uses the word *pratyakṣa* in a non-ordinary way.

✅ **Argument:**

1. In the strict Jaina sense, *pratyakṣa* means knowledge the soul has directly, without mediation by senses or mind.
2. Ordinary sense-perception is therefore not ultimate direct knowledge; it is only **conventional direct knowledge (sāṃvyavahārika pratyakṣa)**.
3. Strictly, ordinary sense-and-mind cognition belongs with mediated cognition, because it depends on organs, mind, signs and karmic limitation.
4. ⚠️ This reverses the usual expectation that perception is the primary direct means of valid knowledge.

⚠️ **Presupposition.** Directness is measured by freedom from mediation and karmic obstruction, not by sensory vividness.

✅ **Distinction.** Do not map Jaina *pratyakṣa* mechanically onto Nyāya *pratyakṣa*; the same word carries a different doctrinal weight.

✅ **Nyāya objection.** Perception through sense-contact is the paradigm of direct cognition.
⚠️ **Reply.** Sense-contact is direct only pragmatically; metaphysically it is mediated by organs, mind and knowledge-obscuring action-matter.

#### 2. The five kinds of knowledge, exactly

| Kind of knowledge | Status | Meaning | Examinable point |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sense-and-mind cognition (mati)** | Indirect / conventional | Ordinary empirical cognition | Ordinary perception is **not** strict *pratyakṣa* |
| **Verbal or scriptural knowledge (śruta)** | Indirect | Sign-mediated knowledge derived from *mati* | Not blind faith; it depends on prior cognition and communication |
| **Clairvoyant knowledge (avadhi)** | Direct in the strict sense | Limited knowledge of material things beyond sense-range | Direct, but bounded |
| **Knowledge of others' mental modes (manaḥparyāya)** | Direct in the strict sense | Access to others' mental states | Higher than *avadhi*, still not omniscience |
| **Omniscience (kevala)** | Direct and complete | Unlimited, unmediated knowledge | The full overcoming of cognitive obstruction |

✅ **Argument:** *mati* and *śruta* are mediated and therefore indirect in the strict classification; *avadhi*, *manaḥparyāya* and *kevala* are direct because the soul knows without ordinary sense-and-mind mediation; the series maps a graded release from karmic veiling.

✅ **Distinction that must be written.** The five kinds are **not** five *pramāṇas* in the Nyāya sense; they are Jaina modes of knowing indexed to bondage and purification.

✅ **Cārvāka-style objection.** *Avadhi*, *manaḥparyāya* and *kevala* are unverifiable to ordinary experience.
⚠️ **Reply.** Jainism treats them as necessary consequences of its soul-and-action-matter theory; a critic who rejects that ontology may still reject them, and an honest answer records this as an external, not internal, defeat.

#### 3. Wrong cognition — where epistemology becomes ethics

✅ **Statement.** Jainism recognises **kumati (mati-ajñāna)**, **kuśruta (śruta-ajñāna)** and **vibhaṅga-jñāna** as wrong cognition.

✅ **Argument:**

1. Wrongness is not always mere absence of content.
2. Cognition becomes spiritually wrong when held under **wrong orientation (mithyā-darśana)**.
3. *Kumati* is misoriented sensory-mental cognition; *kuśruta* is misoriented verbal or scriptural cognition; *vibhaṅga-jñāna* is perverted clairvoyance.
4. ⚠️ The content may retain descriptive accuracy while its orientation remains false because it is governed by delusion.

✅ **Example.** A learned person may quote doctrine accurately and use it for pride, sectarian contempt or violence; the verbal cognition then functions as *kuśruta*.

✅ **Nyāya objection.** Calling a cognition wrong because of spiritual orientation confuses truth with moral psychology.
⚠️ **Reply.** Bondage affects the knower as a whole; epistemic correctness without right vision remains incomplete **for liberation**, which is the only standard Jainism is claiming to apply.

#### 4. Later Jaina logic and the indirect means of valid knowledge

✅ **Statement.** Jainism admits **inference (anumāna)** and **testimony (śabda)** within the indirect, and later Jaina logicians give a richer account of mediated knowing.

⚠️ **Content:**

1. ✅ **Akalaṅka**, **Māṇikyanandi**, **Hemacandra** and **Yaśovijaya** are the names to cite for the developed tradition.
2. ⚠️ Later Jaina logicians accept **recollection (smṛti)**, **recognition (pratyabhijñā)**, **reasoning that grasps pervasion (tarka / ūha)** and **inference (anumāna)** as valid indirect knowledge in appropriate contexts.
3. ✅ This is a notable contrast with Nyāya, which does **not** treat memory as an independent means of valid knowledge.
4. ⚠️ The Jaina move protects continuity, recognition and inferential preparation as genuine cognitive achievements without inflating them into omniscience.

✅ **Distinction.** Testimony is accepted as mediated knowledge, **not** as a creator-God's revelation; Jainism remains non-theistic about creation even while accepting scripture.

✅ **Example.** "This is the same teacher I met earlier" is not raw perception; it involves memory and recognition working together.

✅ **Nyāya objection.** Memory merely reproduces past cognition and is not a fresh means of knowledge.
⚠️ **Reply.** Recollection and recognition can be valid cognition when they successfully connect present awareness with a real object or past experience.

#### 5. The critical link: means of knowledge → standpoint → conditional assertion

✅ This is the derivation that converts a good answer into a very good one.

1. ✅ Reality is *ananta-dharmātmakaṃ vastu*: the real object has innumerable aspects, properties and modes.
2. ✅ Ordinary human cognition is **kṣāyopaśamika**, arising from the partial destruction-cum-subsidence of knowledge-obscuring action-matter, and is therefore necessarily partial.
3. ✅ A judgement made by a non-omniscient knower captures an aspect, not the whole object.
4. ✅ A partial judgement is true relative to its standpoint: this is **standpoint theory (nayavāda)**.
5. ✅ Absolutising a partial standpoint yields **one-sidedness (ekāntavāda)**, which Jainism identifies as the root error of rival schools.
6. ✅ Therefore assertion must carry the operator **syāt**, "from a certain standpoint": this is **conditional predication (syādvāda)**.
7. ✅ The sevenfold schema is the disciplined expression of that standpoint-relativity: **saptabhaṅgī**.
8. ✅ Only **omniscience (kevala-jñāna)** grasps all aspects at once, because only omniscience is no longer karmically partial.

⚠️ **Presupposition.** Finite cognition is not false because it is partial; it becomes false when it claims unconditioned finality.

✅ **Distinction.** Jaina relativity is **not** subjectivism: the aspects are really in the object, and the limitation is in the knower.

✅ **Śaṃkara-style objection.** If all assertions are standpoint-relative, the assertion of standpoint-relativity loses absolute force.
⚠️ **Reply.** Jainism accepts reflexive qualification — *syādvāda* is itself asserted *syāt* — and locates its force in disciplined anti-absolutism rather than in an unqualified dogma. Session 7 examines whether that reply succeeds.

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH — claim → named evidence → analysis → qualification

> **Claim.** Jaina conditional predication is an epistemological consequence, not a rhetorical device.
> **Named evidence.** Ordinary cognition is *kṣāyopaśamika*, arising from partial destruction-cum-subsidence of knowledge-obscuring action-matter; ordinary perception is only *sāṃvyavahārika pratyakṣa*; and only *kevala-jñāna*, which follows the destruction of the obscuring karmas, grasps all aspects at once.
> **Analysis.** It follows that unconditioned assertion is available exclusively to omniscience, so a finite knower who drops the *syāt* operator is not being decisive but is claiming a cognitive status the system says he does not possess.
> **Qualification / link.** The argument therefore stands or falls with the ontology of soul and obscuring action-matter; a Cārvāka who denies omniscience will read the whole apparatus as an elaborate defence of unverifiable claims, which is why "critically examine" questions must state the internal success and the external vulnerability separately.

#### UPSC TRAPS IN THIS SUBTOPIC

1. ❌ Calling ordinary perception *pratyakṣa* without qualification. ✅ It is *sāṃvyavahārika pratyakṣa*.
2. ❌ Listing the five kinds of knowledge as five *pramāṇas*. ✅ They are graded modes of unveiling.
3. ❌ Forgetting the false counterparts. ✅ *Kumati*, *kuśruta* and *vibhaṅga* are examinable.
4. ❌ Saying Jainism rejects inference. ✅ It accepts inference and testimony as **indirect**.
5. ❌ Missing the memory contrast. ✅ Later Jaina logic admits recollection and recognition; Nyāya does not.

#### RAPID REVISION

- Strict directness = no organ, no mind, no veil.
- Five kinds: *mati*, *śruta* (indirect); *avadhi*, *manaḥparyāya*, *kevala* (direct).
- Wrong cognition is orientation-relative, not merely content-relative.
- Later logic adds recollection, recognition and pervasion-grasping reasoning.
- The chain: innumerable aspects → partial cognition → standpoint → *syāt* → sevenfold schema.

#### EXAM APPLICATION — WHERE THIS IS ASKED

- **2023 Q5(a), 10 marks** — the "all human knowledge is empirical and therefore relative" prompt is answered *from* this session, by separating Jaina aspect-relativity from empiricist relativism.
- **2019 Q6(a), 20 marks** — the bridge from "reality" to "judgements" is the five-step derivation in section 5.
- **2022 Q6(b), 15 marks** — the absolutism charge is met by the *kevala-jñāna* clause established here.

### SESSION 5 — STANDPOINT THEORY (nayavāda): THE SEVEN STANDPOINTS

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** A standpoint is a legitimate but partial way of looking at a thing. Jainism classifies the main ways in which people take such partial views, so that disagreement can be diagnosed instead of merely continued.

**Technical definition:** **Standpoint theory (nayavāda)** holds that every ordinary judgement expresses a *naya*, a determinate perspective that captures one aspect of a many-sided real; classical Jainism enumerates **seven standpoints**, running from the practical and generic to the strictly verbal and function-conditioned.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> A standpoint is not an error but an unfinished truth: it becomes error only when it forgets its own conditions, and the sevenfold classification of standpoints exists precisely to make those conditions visible before a dispute hardens into dogma.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **standpoint theory (nayavāda)**; **standpoint (naya)**
- **naigama, saṃgraha, vyavahāra, ṛjusūtra, śabda, samabhirūḍha, evambhūta**
- **one-sidedness (ekāntavāda)** and **wrong standpoint (durnaya / nayābhāsa)**
- **real standpoint (niścaya-naya) / conventional standpoint (vyavahāra-naya)** — Kundakunda
- **substance-directed (dravyārthika) / mode-directed (paryāyārthika)**

**How to use them:** name the standpoints in order, then group them (first three substance-directed and practical, last four increasingly verbal and mode-directed). Grouping shows understanding; a bare list shows recall.

#### SIMPLE START — PLAIN-LANGUAGE GATEWAY

```text
   ONE MAN. FIVE TRUE DESCRIPTIONS. NO CONTRADICTION.

   "He is a human being."          -> generic class view          (saṃgraha)
   "He is this particular man."    -> practical, particular view   (vyavahāra)
   "He is singing right now."      -> present-state view           (ṛjusūtra)
   "He is called a singer."        -> the word used of him         (śabda)
   "'Singer' means one who sings." -> the word's exact sense       (samabhirūḍha)
   "He is a singer ONLY while
    actually singing."             -> function-in-act view         (evambhūta)

   Each is TRUE under its own condition.
   Each becomes FALSE the moment it says: "and nothing else is true of him."
```

**In simple words:** the standpoints move from the loosest, most practical way of speaking to the strictest, most literal one. Nobody is lying; they are answering different questions. Jainism only objects when one of them claims to be the whole story.

| Group | Standpoints | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Practical and substance-directed | *naigama*, *saṃgraha*, *vyavahāra* | Ordinary usage, class and particular |
| Temporal turn | *ṛjusūtra* | Restricts attention to the present state |
| Verbal and mode-directed | *śabda*, *samabhirūḍha*, *evambhūta* | Increasingly strict about words and actual function |

> **One-line memory:** partiality is not falsehood; absolutised partiality is.

#### 🖼️ VISUAL — THE SEVEN STANDPOINTS AS A NARROWING FUNNEL

```text
   WIDEST, MOST PRACTICAL
   +------------------------------------------------------------------+
   | 1 naigama       mixed practical / purposive view; blends general  |
   |                 and particular, often through intention           |
   +------------------------------------------------------------------+
   | 2 saṃgraha      generic view; attends to shared class-character   |
   +------------------------------------------------------------------+
   | 3 vyavahāra     practical particular view; ordinary dealings      |

   | 4 ṛjusūtra      present-state view; the thing as it now is        |   <- pivot:
   +------------------------------------------------------------------+      substance-
   | 5 śabda         verbal view; attends to the expression used       |      directed
   +------------------------------------------------------------------+      turns into
   | 6 samabhirūḍha  etymological view; distinct words, distinct sense |      mode-
   +------------------------------------------------------------------+      directed
   | 7 evambhūta     function-in-act view; the term applies only while |
   |                 the named function is actually being performed    |
   +------------------------------------------------------------------+
   NARROWEST, MOST LITERAL

   ANY standpoint that denies the others becomes a WRONG standpoint (nayābhāsa).
```

⚠️ **Caption.** The funnel makes the examinable structure visible: the first three are broadly substance-directed (*dravyārthika*), *ṛjusūtra* is the hinge, and the last three are verbal and mode-directed (*paryāyārthika*). Answers that reproduce this movement rather than a flat list read as analysis.

#### 1. Standpoint theory stated

✅ **Statement.** *Nayavāda* teaches that every ordinary judgement expresses a **naya**, a standpoint that captures one aspect of the real.

✅ **Argument:** reality is many-sided; finite knowers rarely grasp all sides at once; therefore judgements are aspectual rather than exhaustive; therefore a doctrine of standpoints is required to explain how partial truth is possible at all.

⚠️ **Presupposition.** Partiality is not falsehood; it becomes falsehood only when the partial is absolutised.

✅ **Distinction.** *Nayavāda* is **not** scepticism. It permits truth — qualified truth.

✅ **Example.** A jar may be described as clay, as container, as round, as breakable, as existing-now. Each statement is true from a standpoint.

✅ **Nyāya-style objection.** Standpoints may proliferate without limit.
⚠️ **Reply.** Jaina thinkers classify the principal standpoints precisely in order to discipline discourse rather than dissolve it — which is why there is a canonical list of seven and not an open-ended set.

#### 2. The seven standpoints, exactly

| Standpoint | Brief meaning | Worked illustration |
|---|---|---|
| **Naigama naya** | Common, practical or purposive standpoint; a mixed, unspecialised view that blends general and particular, often through intention | Saying "I am cooking rice" while only fetching water, because the whole purposive act is meant |
| **Saṃgraha naya** | Generic standpoint; emphasis on class-character | "A jar is a substance" — the class swallows the particular |
| **Vyavahāra naya** | Practical or particular standpoint; ordinary usage about specific things | "Bring **that** jar" — the particular is what matters |
| **Ṛjusūtra naya** | Momentary or present-state standpoint | "He is happy" said of the present state only, without past or future |
| **Śabda naya** | Verbal standpoint; attention to the linguistic expression used | Treating a change of word as marking a change of meaning |
| **Samabhirūḍha naya** | Etymological standpoint; strictly differentiated verbal view | Refusing to treat two synonyms as fully interchangeable because their derivations differ |
| **Evambhūta naya** | Actuality-conditioned verbal standpoint; a term applies when the function is being performed | "Singer" applies only while singing is actually occurring |

✅ **Argument.** The list shows that partiality can arise from pragmatic, generic, particular, temporal and linguistic conditioning — five different sources of one-sidedness, not one.

⚠️ **Presupposition.** Language itself contributes to one-sidedness if its scope is not critically examined.

✅ **Distinction that is directly examined.** Do **not** confuse the **seven standpoints** with the **sevenfold predication**. The former are epistemic perspectives; the latter are structured propositional forms. The numerical coincidence is a trap, not a correspondence.

✅ **Examiner's objection.** The verbal standpoints look over-scholastic.
⚠️ **Reply.** They matter because a large class of philosophical disputes are verbal absolutisations of context-bound usage; the last three standpoints are a diagnostic instrument for exactly that failure.

#### 3. The real and the conventional standpoint

✅ **Statement.** Kundakunda, the Digambara author associated with the *Samayasāra*, is standardly cited for the contrast between the **real standpoint (niścaya-naya)** and the **conventional standpoint (vyavahāra-naya)**.

⚠️ **Use.** The real standpoint describes the soul in its own intrinsic nature; the conventional standpoint describes it as embodied, active and karmically conditioned. Both are required: the first states what liberation restores, the second states what bondage does. Cite the pair when a question asks how the soul can be described both as pure and as bound.

✅ **Cross-check.** The substance-directed / mode-directed pair (*dravyārthika* / *paryāyārthika*) performs a similar service at the level of the object: the first attends to the enduring bearer, the second to changing states.

#### 4. Standpoint and its perversion

✅ A standpoint that asserts its own content **and** denies the remaining aspects ceases to be a *naya* and becomes a **wrong standpoint (nayābhāsa / durnaya)**. This is the technical form of **one-sidedness (ekāntavāda)**, which Jainism identifies as the shared defect of rival schools:

| Rival position | Absolutised standpoint | Jaina diagnosis |
|---|---|---|
| Strong permanence-monism | Substance-directed view taken absolutely | Change becomes appearance |
| Buddhist momentariness | Present-state view (*ṛjusūtra*) taken absolutely | Identity becomes fiction |
| Extreme nominalism | Verbal standpoints taken absolutely | The object dissolves into usage |
| Naive generic realism | Generic view (*saṃgraha*) taken absolutely | Particulars become dispensable |

⚠️ **Answer use.** This table converts "Jainism criticises other schools" from an assertion into a diagnosis: each rival is charged with elevating one legitimate standpoint into an exclusive one.

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH — claim → named evidence → analysis → qualification

> **Claim.** Standpoint theory is a diagnostic instrument, not an invitation to indecision.
> **Named evidence.** The canonical seven run *naigama*, *saṃgraha*, *vyavahāra*, *ṛjusūtra*, *śabda*, *samabhirūḍha* and *evambhūta*; Kundakunda's *Samayasāra* supplies the real and conventional standpoints; and a standpoint that denies the rest is technically reclassified as *nayābhāsa*.
> **Analysis.** Because each rival school can be shown to have absolutised one member of that list — momentariness the present-state view, permanence-monism the substance-directed view — the Jaina critique of others becomes a single repeatable argument rather than a series of unconnected complaints.
> **Qualification / link.** The instrument is only as good as the classification, and a critic may reasonably ask why exactly seven standpoints are canonical rather than five or nine; the Jaina answer is pragmatic and pedagogical, so the doctrine should be defended as disciplining discourse rather than as exhausting it.

#### UPSC TRAPS IN THIS SUBTOPIC

1. ❌ "Seven *nayas* = seven *bhaṅgas*." ✅ Perspectives versus propositional forms; never merge them.
2. ❌ Presenting *naya* as opinion. ✅ A *naya* is a conditioned truth-claim about a real aspect.
3. ❌ Ignoring *nayābhāsa*. ✅ The perversion of a standpoint is the technical name of one-sidedness.
4. ❌ Dropping *evambhūta*. ✅ It is the strictest standpoint and the most quotable illustration.
5. ❌ Treating *niścaya* / *vyavahāra* as Advaita's two truths. ✅ Both remain standpoints on **one real** soul, with no sublation.

#### RAPID REVISION

- Seven standpoints, in order, with the funnel from practical to function-in-act.
- First three substance-directed; *ṛjusūtra* the hinge; last three verbal and mode-directed.
- *Nayābhāsa* = standpoint plus denial of the rest.
- Kundakunda: real and conventional standpoints for the soul.
- Standpoints ≠ predications.

#### EXAM APPLICATION — WHERE THIS IS ASKED

- **2021 Q7(c), 15 marks** — "Explain the Jaina view of seven-fold (sapta-bhaṅgī) 'Naya'." The printed wording fuses the two doctrines, so the answer must **explain both** and expressly distinguish them.
- **2019 Q6(a), 20 marks** — standpoints are the middle term between reality and judgement.
- **2022 Q6(b), 15 marks** — the relativism charge is stated at the level of standpoints before it reaches predication.

### SESSION 6 — CONDITIONAL PREDICATION (syādvāda) AND THE SEVENFOLD PREDICATION (saptabhaṅgī)

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** Since every judgement is made from a standpoint, every assertion should carry a marker saying "in this respect". Combining assertion, denial and inexpressibility under that marker yields exactly seven possible forms.

**Technical definition:** **Conditional predication (syādvāda)** is the doctrine that assertions must be qualified by the operator **syāt** — "in some respect", "from a certain standpoint" — and the **sevenfold predication (saptabhaṅgī)** is the exhaustive schema generated by combining affirmation, negation and inexpressibility (*avaktavya*) under that operator.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> The particle *syāt* is not a hedge but an index: it states the respect, substance, place, time and mode under which a predication holds, so that the seven forms of Jaina judgement record conditions of truth rather than degrees of doubt.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **conditional predication (syādvāda)**; the operator **syāt**
- **sevenfold predication (saptabhaṅgī)**; **bhaṅga** (mode of predication)
- **inexpressible (avaktavya)**
- the four indices: **substance (dravya) · place (kṣetra) · time (kāla) · mode (bhāva)**
- **own-form (svarūpa) / other-form (pararūpa)**

**How to use them:** write the four indices early. They are the technical device that blocks the contradiction charge, and most scripts never mention them.

#### SIMPLE START — PLAIN-LANGUAGE GATEWAY

```text
   THE CLAY JAR ON THE TABLE

   "The jar EXISTS."     -> as clay, on this table, now, in jar-shape   = syād asti
   "The jar DOES NOT
    EXIST."              -> as gold, in another room, yesterday,
                            as a lump                                    = syād nāsti

   The two sentences never meet, because the four indices differ:

        SUBSTANCE (dravya) : clay, not gold
        PLACE     (kṣetra) : this table, not that room
        TIME      (kāla)   : now, not yesterday
        MODE      (bhāva)  : jar-shape, not lump-shape

   Contradiction needs SAME respect, SAME time, SAME sense.
   syāt guarantees that this never happens.
```

**In simple words:** the Jaina is not saying "the jar sort of exists". He is saying "the jar definitely exists **as this**, and definitely does not exist **as that**", and he is putting the conditions on the record instead of hiding them.

| Trap | Correct reading |
|---|---|
| *syāt* = "perhaps" | ❌ No. It is a definite assertion restricted by standpoint |
| *syādvāda* = scepticism | ❌ No. Scepticism withholds; *syādvāda* asserts with an index |
| *avaktavya* = mystical silence | ❌ No. It marks failure of **simultaneous unqualified** expression |
| Seven forms = seven truth-values | ❌ No. One truth, seven conditioned formulations |

> **One-line memory:** *syāt* fixes the respect; the seven *bhaṅgas* exhaust the combinations.

#### 🖼️ VISUAL — HOW SEVEN, AND ONLY SEVEN, FORMS ARISE

```text
   THREE BASIC MOVES                       COMBINATION LOGIC
   =================                       =================
   [1] affirm            asti              simple:      1  2  4        -> 3 forms
   [2] deny              nāsti             pair-wise:   1+2, 1+4, 2+4  -> 3 forms
   [4] inexpressible     avaktavya         all three:   1+2+4          -> 1 form
                                                                   TOTAL  7

   1. syād asti                        in some respect, it IS
   2. syād nāsti                       in some respect, it IS NOT
   3. syād asti ca nāsti ca            in some respect it is AND is not (successively)
   4. syād avaktavyam                  in some respect it is INEXPRESSIBLE
   5. syād asti ca avaktavyam          it is, and is inexpressible
   6. syād nāsti ca avaktavyam         it is not, and is inexpressible
   7. syād asti ca nāsti ca            it is, is not, and is inexpressible
      avaktavyam ca

   WHY SEVEN?  three primitives taken singly, in pairs, and all together
               = 3 + 3 + 1 = 7.  Not an arbitrary number, and not extendable.
```

⚠️ **Caption.** The combinatorial derivation is the difference between reciting the list and explaining it. Note the internal logic of forms 3 and 4: form 3 asserts existence and non-existence **successively**, under different indices; form 4 arises when both are demanded **simultaneously and without qualification**, at which point language cannot deliver a single unqualified predicate.

#### 1. Conditional predication stated

✅ **Statement.** *Syādvāda* is the doctrine that assertions should be made with the qualification **syāt** — "in some respect", "from a certain standpoint", "conditionally".

✅ **Translation trap, stated in full because it is repeatedly examined.** Although *syāt* can literally suggest "perhaps" or "may be", it does **not** express probability, doubt, agnosticism or scepticism here. It marks a **definite** assertion restricted by standpoint, substance, place, time and mode. "The jar conditionally exists" is not a guess that the jar probably exists.

✅ **Argument:**

1. Since reality is many-sided, categorical assertion is risky.
2. Since judgements arise from standpoints, language must register conditionality.
3. The particle *syāt* prevents absolutisation.
4. Hence *syādvāda* is the logical-linguistic expression of many-sidedness.

✅ **Distinction to write verbatim.** Many-sidedness = **metaphysical** doctrine; standpoint theory = theory of **perspectives**; conditional predication = mode of **assertion**; sevenfold predication = the **formal schema** of that assertion.

✅ **Śaṃkara-style objection.** Constant qualification weakens assertoric force.
⚠️ **Reply.** Jaina logic accepts that precision is preferable to dogmatic simplicity; and the qualification is informative, since it specifies *which* respect is in play.

#### 2. The seven predications, exactly and in order

✅ **Statement.** The *saptabhaṅgī* is the sevenfold schema of conditional predication produced by combining affirmation, negation and inexpressibility under the operator *syāt*.

1. **syād asti** — in some respect, it is.
2. **syād nāsti** — in some respect, it is not.
3. **syād asti ca nāsti ca** — in some respect, it is and it is not.
4. **syād avaktavyam** — in some respect, it is inexpressible.
5. **syād asti ca avaktavyam** — in some respect, it is and is inexpressible.
6. **syād nāsti ca avaktavyam** — in some respect, it is not and is inexpressible.
7. **syād asti ca nāsti ca avaktavyam** — in some respect, it is, is not, and is inexpressible.

✅ **Argument:** a thing may be affirmed under one standpoint; negated under another; both affirmed and negated under different references or times; called inexpressible where simultaneous exhaustive predication fails; and combinations of these yield seven basic structured forms.

⚠️ **Presupposition.** Contraries need not violate logic when they are indexed to standpoint, time, relation or mode.

✅ **Distinction.** "Inexpressible" does **not** mean mystical silence simpliciter. It means that an unqualified or simultaneous statement is not straightforwardly expressible.

✅ **Example.** A clay jar exists now on the table; it does not exist as a lump of raw clay; it both is and is not under different modal descriptions; and in a complex state of transition it may be called *avaktavya*.

#### 3. The four indices and the answer to the contradiction charge

✅ Opposed predicates are indexed to four determinants:

| Index | Sanskrit | What it fixes | Jar illustration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Substance | **dravya** | The material or kind under which the claim is made | It is clay; it is not gold |
| Place | **kṣetra** | The location relative to which it holds | It is on this table; it is not in that room |
| Time | **kāla** | The temporal reference | It is now; it was not yesterday |
| Mode | **bhāva** | The state or form | It is jar-shaped; it is not lump-shaped |

✅ **The formal reply.** Contradiction would arise only if affirmation and negation were made in the **same respect**, at the **same time**, in the **same sense**. The operator *syāt* blocks precisely that reading. Existence is asserted of the thing in its **own-form (svarūpa)**; non-existence is asserted of it in **other-form (pararūpa)**.

✅ **Named critics.** Śaṃkara in the *Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya* and Rāmānuja both press the charge that contradictory attributes cannot belong to the same thing in the same way. Session 7 develops those critiques and the Jaina replies in full.

#### 4. The blind men and the elephant — what the parable does and does not prove

✅ **Statement.** The familiar illustration captures the spirit of many-sidedness: each grasped part yields a partial truth wrongly elevated into a whole truth.

⚠️ **What it dramatises.** The difference between **partial correctness** and **exclusive completeness**.

✅ **Distinction.** The parable illustrates non-absolutism; it is **not** the formal doctrine, and it does not by itself establish that aspects are objectively in the elephant.

✅ **Example.** One touches the trunk and says "the elephant is like a serpent"; another the leg and says "like a pillar". Each is conditionally right and absolutely wrong.

✅ **Pedagogical objection.** The parable trivialises complex metaphysics.
⚠️ **Reply.** It is pedagogical, not probative; use it for one sentence to clarify the structure of partial truth, then return to the four indices for the argument.

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH — claim → named evidence → analysis → qualification

> **Claim.** The sevenfold predication is a completeness result about qualified assertion, not a licence for contradiction.
> **Named evidence.** Three primitives — affirmation, negation and inexpressibility — taken singly, pairwise and jointly yield exactly 3 + 3 + 1 = 7 forms; each is governed by the operator *syāt*; and the indices of substance, place, time and mode fix the respect, so that existence is predicated in own-form and non-existence in other-form.
> **Analysis.** Because the indices differ in every pair of opposed predications, the schema never asserts p and not-p in the same respect, which means the classical charge of violating non-contradiction misdescribes the doctrine before evaluating it.
> **Qualification / link.** The defence secures consistency but not comfort: Śaṃkara can still object that a doctrine which qualifies even itself has surrendered unconditional assertoric force, so a "critically examine" answer must concede that the Jaina buys logical safety at the price of assertoric strength.

#### UPSC TRAPS IN THIS SUBTOPIC

1. ❌ Translating *syāt* as "perhaps". ✅ It is a standpoint operator, not a probability marker.
2. ❌ Giving six or eight *bhaṅgas*, or the wrong fourth. ✅ The fourth is **syād avaktavyam**.
3. ❌ Treating *avaktavya* as a third truth-value. ✅ It marks inexpressibility under a simultaneous unqualified demand.
4. ❌ Omitting the four indices. ✅ Without them the contradiction charge cannot be answered.
5. ❌ Leading with the elephant parable. ✅ Use it once, briefly, after the formal account.

#### RAPID REVISION

- *syāt* = definite assertion **plus** stated respect.
- Seven forms: 3 simple + 3 pairwise + 1 joint.
- Fourth form is inexpressibility, not the conjunction.
- Four indices: substance, place, time, mode.
- Own-form affirms; other-form denies.

#### EXAM APPLICATION — WHERE THIS IS ASKED

- **2021 Q7(c), 15 marks** — reproduce all seven in order, then distinguish them from the seven standpoints.
- **2023 Q5(a), 10 marks** — the sevenfold judgement is examined against the claim that all knowledge is empirical and therefore relative.
- **2019 Q6(a), 20 marks** — this is the "view on judgements" half of the question.

```closure-flow

MECHANISM / ARGUMENT: Three primitives of affirmation, negation and inexpressibility taken singly, pairwise and jointly generate exactly seven forms, each governed by an operator that states the respect in which the predication holds.
```

### SESSION 7 — EVALUATING NON-ABSOLUTISM: SELF-REFUTATION, THE ABSOLUTISM CHARGE, AND THE PLURALIST-REALIST VERDICT

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** Critics say the Jaina position destroys itself: if everything must be qualified, then the doctrine of qualification must also be qualified, and then it says nothing definite. This session works out whether that objection succeeds.

**Technical definition:** The reflexivity problem asks whether *anekāntavāda* can be asserted without self-exemption; the absolutism charge, printed in 2022, asks whether non-absolutism covertly presupposes an absolute standpoint; and the classification question, printed in 2025, asks whether Jainism is properly described as pluralistic and realistic.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Jaina non-absolutism survives the self-refutation charge by paying a price it can afford and refusing one it cannot: it accepts that its own thesis is standpoint-qualified, but it denies that qualification means indeterminacy, because the aspects being qualified are objectively in the thing.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **self-refutation charge**; **reflexive qualification**
- **relativism versus non-absolutism**
- **omniscience (kevala-jñāna)** as the internal reference point
- **ontological realism / epistemic non-absolutism**
- **qualified realism**

**How to use them:** separate the two halves of every criticism — the *logical* half about consistency and the *epistemic* half about assertoric force. Jainism answers the first decisively and the second only partially, and saying so is what "critically" rewards.

⚠️ **Caption.** The three challenges are not variants of one complaint. A dissects consistency, B dissects presupposition, and C demands a classification. Scripts that answer all three with the same paragraph lose the discrimination marks.

#### 1. Challenge A — is many-sidedness self-refuting or trivial?

✅ **Statement.** Critics argue **either** that *anekāntavāda* is self-refuting, because the claim that "all reality is many-sided" is itself made absolutely, **or** that it is trivial, because everyone already admits contextual variation.

✅ **The critic's argument, in four steps:**

1. If every assertion must be qualified, the doctrine itself must be qualified.
2. If qualified, it may lose universal force.
3. If unqualified, it violates its own rule.
4. Alternatively, if it merely says that things can be looked at in different ways, it is philosophically thin.

⚠️ **Presupposition.** A doctrine of relativity must be able to explain its own status.

✅ **The Jaina reply, in four steps:**

1. Jainism need not deny that the doctrine itself is expressible from a standpoint.
2. This does not collapse it; it exhibits the very discipline it teaches.
3. The thesis is **not** that all statements are equally true, but that truth is aspect-conditioned.
4. Its philosophical bite lies in reconciling plurality with realism, not in banal relativism.

⚠️ **Honest assessment.** The consistency charge is answered. The triviality charge is answered only if the candidate shows the *work* the doctrine does — namely holding permanence and change together, and diagnosing rival schools as absolutised standpoints. Without that demonstration, the triviality charge has real force.

#### 2. Challenge B — does non-absolutism presuppose absolutism? (2022, 15 marks)

⚠️ **Statement of the challenge.** To say that reality is many-sided, one must already know absolutely that it is so; therefore *anekāntavāda* covertly presupposes absolutism.

✅ **The challenge, formalised:**

1. The doctrine claims universal scope.
2. Universal scope appears absolute.
3. Therefore the doctrine seems to rely on the very absolutism it criticises.

✅ **The Jaina reply, formalised:**

1. Non-absolutism does not deny more comprehensive knowledge; it denies **one-sided exclusivism by finite knowers**.
2. The doctrine is a methodological and ontological caution against *ekānta*, not a ban on all graded comprehensiveness.
3. Within Jainism, **omniscience (kevala-jñāna)** represents total knowledge, while ordinary discourse remains standpoint-bound.
4. Hence the charge succeeds only if one confuses finite judgement with omniscient cognition.

⚠️ **Assessment.** This is a subtle and strong reply: Jainism can admit a difference between omniscience and ordinary language without abandoning its critique of dogmatic partiality. ⚠️ The residue is that the reply relocates rather than removes the absolute: the omniscient standpoint now carries the unconditioned status that finite discourse is denied, and a critic may press that this is exactly what the objection alleged. State both, and then give a graded verdict.

#### 3. Challenge C — is Jaina philosophy pluralistic and realistic? (2025, 15 marks)

⚠️ **Statement.** Yes, Jainism is strongly **pluralistic** and broadly **realistic**, though both labels require nuance.

✅ **Why pluralistic:**

1. It accepts many real souls.
2. It accepts multiple irreducible substances.
3. It rejects monistic reduction.
4. Liberation is of many individual souls, not absorption into one absolute.

✅ **Why realistic:**

1. It treats substances, qualities and modes as objectively real.
2. The external world is not illusion.
3. Knowledge aims at real objects, though only under standpoints.

⚠️ **The necessary nuance.** Its realism is **qualified realism**, because judgement is standpoint-indexed. But qualification of *knowledge* does not imply unreality of the *object* — and that sentence is the hinge of the whole answer.

✅ **Realist objection.** If all knowledge is relative to standpoint, realism weakens.
⚠️ **Reply.** Jainism separates **ontological realism** from **epistemic non-absolutism**: reality is objective, but finite access to it is partial.

| Axis | Jaina position | Label earned |
|---|---|---|
| Number of ultimate reals | Many souls, six substances | Strong pluralism ✅ |
| Status of the external world | Real, not appearance | Realism ✅ |
| Status of aspects | Objectively in the thing | Aspect-realism ✅ |
| Status of finite judgement | Standpoint-indexed | Non-absolutism, hence **qualified** realism ⚠️ |
| Status of the omniscient standpoint | Unconditioned, internal to the system | Residual pressure from Challenge B ⚠️ |

#### 4. The graded verdict to close any critical question

**Strong ✅**

1. Jainism gives one of the clearest Indian accounts of how permanence and change can both be real.
2. Many-sidedness, standpoint theory and conditional predication form a disciplined sequence from ontology to epistemology to language.
3. Its material action-matter doctrine makes ethics causally serious and connects metaphysics with ascetic practice.
4. Its non-violence follows directly from plural souls and karmic bondage, not from sentiment alone.

**Weak ⚠️**

1. Material action-matter binding a conscious soul remains difficult for non-Jaina metaphysics.
2. Omniscience and the higher direct knowledges are hard to verify externally.
3. The system can appear over-taxonomic in karma-types, stages and standpoints.
4. Reflexively qualified conditional predication may appear to weaken its own universal claim.

**Genuinely contested ❓**

1. Sectarian nuances about the substantial independence of time differ in presentation.
2. Modern legal-ethical evaluation of *sallekhanā* requires case-specific verification before any detail is cited.
3. Historical dating of several Jaina thinkers should not be asserted without a separate source.

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH — claim → named evidence → analysis → qualification

> **Claim.** The absolutism charge fails as stated but succeeds in a weaker, more interesting form.
> **Named evidence.** The 2022 paper prints the claim that Jaina relativism "cannot be logically sustained without postulating absolutism"; the Jaina reply distinguishes finite standpoint-bound judgement from *kevala-jñāna*, which grasps all aspects at once because knowledge-obscuring karma has been destroyed.
> **Analysis.** Since the doctrine denies one-sided exclusivism by finite knowers rather than denying comprehensiveness as such, the charge as printed equivocates between two senses of "absolute", and once the equivocation is removed the alleged self-contradiction disappears.
> **Qualification / link.** What survives is a genuine cost rather than a contradiction: the unconditioned status has been relocated to omniscience, so a candidate should conclude that Jainism is consistent but not standpoint-free, which is precisely the graded verdict the directive "critically examine" is asking for.

#### UPSC TRAPS IN THIS SUBTOPIC

1. ❌ Answering 2022 by simply asserting that *syādvāda* is not relativism. ✅ Engage the printed claim and locate the equivocation.
2. ❌ Treating "relativism" and "non-absolutism" as synonyms. ✅ Relativism relativises truth; non-absolutism relativises exclusive claims.
3. ❌ Declaring Jainism realist without the qualification. ✅ Say "qualified realism" and explain the qualifier.
4. ❌ Ignoring omniscience in the 2022 answer. ✅ It is the pivot of the reply and the site of the residual objection.
5. ❌ Ending with summary rather than verdict. ✅ Critical directives demand a graded judgement.

- Three distinct challenges: consistency, presupposition, classification.
- Reflexive qualification is a feature, not a bug.
- *Kevala-jñāna* answers 2022 and simultaneously creates its residue.
- Ontological realism ≠ epistemic absolutism.
- Close every critical answer with strong / weak / contested.

### SESSION 8 — BONDAGE: ACTION-MATTER AS SUBTLE MATTER, AND THE MECHANICS OF ADHESION

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** In Jainism the moral residue of action is not a book-entry or an invisible force; it is extremely fine matter that is actually drawn to the soul and sticks to it, and the stickiness is supplied by the passions.

**Technical definition:** **Action and moral consequence (karma)** is a form of matter (*pudgala*): psychical action-matter (*bhāva-karma*) attracts material action-matter (*dravya-karma*), producing **psychic bondage (bhāvabandha)** and **material bondage (dravyabandha)** analysed under four parameters — type (*prakṛti*), duration (*sthiti*), intensity (*anubhāga / rasa*) and quantity (*pradeśa*).

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> The originality of Jaina soteriology lies in treating action and moral consequence not merely as a moral record but as subtle material bondage, so that liberation requires both the stoppage of fresh influx and the shedding of what has already adhered.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **material action-matter (karma-pudgala)**; **psychical (bhāva-karma) / material (dravya-karma)**
- **influx (āsrava) → bondage (bandha) → stoppage (saṃvara) → shedding (nirjarā) → liberation (mokṣa)**
- **psychic bondage (bhāvabandha) / material bondage (dravyabandha)**
- five causes: **wrong vision (mithyātva), non-restraint (avirati), carelessness (pramāda), passions (kaṣāya), activity (yoga)**
- **eight karma-types (karma-prakṛti)**; **destructive (ghātiyā) / non-destructive (aghātiyā)**
- **momentary bondage (īryāpathika) / durable bondage (sāmparāyika)**

**How to use them:** never write "karma" alone in a Jainism answer. Write **material action-matter** and then immediately distinguish it from Nyāya's *adṛṣṭa*, Mīmāṃsā's *apūrva*, Sāṃkhya's *saṃskāra* and Buddhist *vāsanā*. That single contrast is worth more than a paragraph of description.

#### SIMPLE START — PLAIN-LANGUAGE GATEWAY

```text
   A DRY CLOTH AND AN OILED CLOTH ARE WAVED IN THE SAME DUSTY ROOM.

   dust in the room          = fine karmic matter, everywhere available
   waving the cloth          = ACTIVITY of body, speech and mind   (yoga)
   the oil on the cloth      = PASSIONS: anger, pride, deceit, greed (kaṣāya)

   DRY CLOTH   -> dust touches, does not hold   -> momentary bondage (īryāpathika)
   OILED CLOTH -> dust sticks, deeply and long  -> durable bondage  (sāmparāyika)

   HOW MUCH dust arrives  -> decided by the WAVING   (activity)
   HOW LONG and HOW HARD
   it clings              -> decided by the OIL      (passion)
```

**In simple words:** activity opens the channel and determines how much matter arrives; passion determines how long it stays and how strongly it will fruit. This is why a Jaina monk's careful walking binds only momentarily while an angry act binds deeply.

| Term | Easy meaning |
|---|---|
| *āsrava* | Influx — karmic matter flowing toward the soul |
| *bandha* | Bondage — that matter actually adhering |
| *saṃvara* | Stoppage — closing the channel of fresh influx |
| *nirjarā* | Shedding — wearing away what has already adhered |
| *mokṣa* | Liberation — no karmic matter left at all |

> **One-line memory:** activity brings the dust; passion supplies the glue.

⚠️ **Caption.** Two divisions of labour are the examinable core. First, the inner state precedes and shapes the outer adhesion, which is why Jainism is not crude physicalism. Second, activity fixes *type and quantity* while passion fixes *duration and intensity* — the single most frequently mis-stated pairing in Jainism answers.

#### 1. Action and moral consequence as subtle material substance

✅ **Statement.** Jainism's distinctive thesis is that **action and moral consequence (karma) is a subtle material substance**, a form of matter (*pudgala*) that flows toward and binds the soul.

✅ **Argument:**

1. The soul's intrinsic omniscience is obscured in empirical life.
2. That obscuration requires a real binding factor.
3. The factor is action-matter, conceived not merely as moral tendency but as fine material accretion.
4. Thus bondage is ontologically real, not merely metaphorical.

⚠️ **Presupposition.** Ethical and cognitive defilement must have a concrete causal structure.

✅ **The trap that decides marks.** In Jainism action-matter is **material**, unlike presentations in which action-consequence is a moral force, a merit-demerit ledger or an unseen potency:

| School | Name of the moral residue | Ontological status |
|---|---|---|
| **Jainism** | *karma-pudgala* | ✅ Subtle **matter** that adheres to the soul |
| Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika | *adṛṣṭa* | Unseen quality of the self, not matter |
| Mīmāṃsā | *apūrva* | Unseen potency linking ritual act and result |
| Sāṃkhya-Yoga | *saṃskāra* | Dispositional trace within *prakṛti* |
| Buddhism | *vāsanā* | Mental-causal impression, no material particle |

✅ **Nyāya-Vedānta objection.** How can matter bind a conscious, immaterial soul?
⚠️ **Reply.** The embodied soul is not absolutely non-spatial: it has space-points (*pradeśa*), is co-extensive with the body, and is modified by psychical action-matter; karmic matter is attracted through the passions. ⚠️ A residual difficulty remains, because interaction between consciousness and matter still demands explanation — and an honest answer says so.

#### 2. The five causes of bondage

| Cause | Sanskrit | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Wrong vision | **mithyātva / mithyā-darśana** | Misapprehends soul, reality and the path |
| Non-restraint | **avirati** | Leaves conduct exposed to harmful desire and action |
| Carelessness | **pramāda** | Weakens vigilance even where doctrine is known |
| Passions | **kaṣāya** | Anger, pride, deceit and greed make adhesion deep and durable |
| Activity | **yoga** | Activity of body, speech and mind opens the channel of influx |

⚠️ **Causal ordering, which is the analytical point.** Wrong vision and ignorance sustain the passions; activity supplies the influx-channel; passion supplies the stickiness. This prevents the material account from becoming crude physicalism: **psychical disposition determines the depth and duration of material bondage**.

#### 3. Psychic and material bondage (2024, 15 marks)

✅ **Statement.** Bondage has two closely related forms: **psychic bondage (bhāvabandha)** and **material bondage (dravyabandha)**.

✅ **Argument:**

1. The soul first undergoes affective-volitional corruption through passion, attachment, aversion, delusion and wrong disposition.
2. This inner deformation is *bhāvabandha*.
3. Corresponding karmic particles then attach materially to the soul.
4. This actual material adhesion is *dravyabandha*.
5. Thus psychic and material bondage are distinct yet interlinked.

⚠️ **Presupposition.** Inner states are causally efficacious; moral psychology precedes material karmic accretion.

✅ **Distinction to write in exactly this form:**

- **bhāvabandha** = subjective, intentional, affective bondage — a **modification of the soul**.
- **dravyabandha** = objective, material bondage by karmic matter — an **adhesion of matter**.

✅ **Example.** Anger or greed in the soul is *bhāvabandha*; the karmic matter drawn and fixed by that passion is *dravyabandha*.

✅ **Analytical objection.** Why not collapse them into one process?
✅ **Reply.** The distinction lets Jainism explain **both** moral agency and ontological consequence: one is the soul's state, the other is the karmic material result. Collapse them and either the ethics or the ontology is lost.

#### 4. The fivefold soteriological sequence

| Stage | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Influx (āsrava)** | Karmic matter flows toward the soul |
| **Bondage (bandha)** | Karmic matter adheres to the soul |
| **Stoppage (saṃvara)** | Fresh inflow is blocked |
| **Shedding (nirjarā)** | Accumulated karma is worn away or exhausted |
| **Liberation (mokṣa)** | Complete release from all karma |

✅ **Argument:** wrong belief, passion and activity open the soul to influx; influx produces bondage; discipline blocks new inflow; austerity and purification burn off old karma; when all karmic matter is gone, liberation occurs.

✅ **Distinction, examined repeatedly.** *Saṃvara* and *nirjarā* are **not** the same: one stops new karma, the other removes old karma.

✅ **Example.** Damming a muddy stream that feeds a tank is like stoppage; dredging the sediment already deposited is like shedding.

#### 5. Seven principles and nine categories

✅ The seven **principles (tattvas)** are soul (*jīva*), non-soul (*ajīva*), influx (*āsrava*), bondage (*bandha*), stoppage (*saṃvara*), shedding (*nirjarā*) and liberation (*mokṣa*).

| Enumeration | Members | Exam significance |
|---|---|---|
| **Seven principles** | Soul, non-soul, influx, bondage, stoppage, shedding, liberation | Standard liberation-sequence framework |
| **Nine categories (padārtha)** | The seven **plus merit (puṇya) and demerit (pāpa)** | Makes moral valence explicit |

✅ **Argument:** merit and demerit are ethically important because meritorious and demeritorious states affect bondage; ⚠️ they can also be subsumed under influx and bondage, since both are forms of karmic involvement; ⚠️ hence some expositions use seven for soteriological mechanics and nine for moral classification.

⚠️ **Presupposition that must be written.** Even **good** karma remains karmic bondage if it keeps the soul within the cycle of rebirth.

✅ **Mīmāṃsā-style objection.** If merit is morally valuable, why treat it as part of bondage?
⚠️ **Reply.** Jainism distinguishes ethical improvement **within** the cycle of rebirth from final release **beyond** all karmic determination. Charity performed with attachment produces merit, but liberation requires non-attachment, not a pleasanter rebirth.

✅ **Liberation by subtraction — the governing image.** The soul does not manufacture omniscience; it removes what obscures its intrinsic capacity. The **infinite tetrad (ananta-catuṣṭaya)** — infinite knowledge, perception, bliss and power — is *revealed*, not produced. Cleaning a sooted lamp does not create light.

✅ **Sāṃkhya / Vedānta objection.** If the soul is intrinsically omniscient, why was it obscured at all?
✅ **Reply.** The soul-karma relation is **beginningless (anādi)** but not endless; like seed-and-tree or ore-and-gold, there is no first temporal term, yet purification can terminate the relation.

#### 6. The eight karma-types

| Class | Karma-type | What it obscures or determines | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Destructive (ghātiyā)** | **Jñānāvaraṇīya** | Knowledge | Blocks the soul's intrinsic knowing |
| **Destructive** | **Darśanāvaraṇīya** | Perception or apprehension | Blocks direct awareness |
| **Destructive** | **Mohanīya** | Right vision and right conduct | Most dangerous, because delusion misorients the path |
| **Destructive** | **Antarāya** | Energy, power, capacity to act | Obstructs the soul's effective power |
| **Non-destructive (aghātiyā)** | **Vedanīya** | Pleasure and pain | Determines felt experience without destroying essence |
| **Non-destructive** | **Nāma** | Body, form, embodiment | Determines the type of body |
| **Non-destructive** | **Gotra** | Status or family-standing | Determines high or low status conditions |
| **Non-destructive** | **Āyus** | Lifespan | Determines duration of embodied life |

✅ **Argument:** the first four are destructive because they obstruct knowledge, perception, right orientation and power; the last four are non-destructive because they structure embodied experience, body, status and lifespan. **Mohanīya** is the most dangerous, since delusion corrupts both right vision and right conduct; it is commonly divided into *darśana-mohanīya* and *cāritra-mohanīya*.

✅ **Distinction with a large pay-off.** The omniscient being at the **sayoga-kevalī** stage has destroyed the destructive karmas but still lives with remaining non-destructive karmas until final release. That single fact explains embodied omniscience — see Session 9.

✅ **Buddhist objection.** The taxonomy is metaphysically heavy compared with a mental-volitional account.
⚠️ **Reply.** Jainism accepts the heaviness because it wants a realist explanation of why cognition, embodiment, status, pleasure and lifespan are **all** karmically structured.

#### 7. The four parameters of bondage

| Parameter | Meaning | Principal determinant |
|---|---|---|
| **Type (prakṛti)** | Which kind of karma is bound | **Activity (yoga)** |
| **Quantity (pradeśa)** | How much karmic matter is bound | **Activity (yoga)** |
| **Duration (sthiti)** | How long the bondage lasts | **Passion (kaṣāya)** |
| **Intensity (anubhāga / rasa)** | How strongly it will fruit | **Passion (kaṣāya)** |

✅ **Consequence.** Activity **without** passion has a radically different karmic effect from activity **with** passion. This yields the contrast between **momentary bondage (īryāpathika bandha)**, arising from passion-free activity, and **durable bondage (sāmparāyika bandha)**, arising from passion-coloured activity.

✅ **Example.** A monk's careful walking involves bodily activity, but being free from passion it binds only momentarily; an angry act binds deeply because passion fixes the karma.

⚠️ **Presupposition.** The moral weight of an action depends not only on external movement but on passion, attachment and delusion.

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH — claim → named evidence → analysis → qualification

> **Claim.** Jaina bondage theory is a moral psychology and a material physics working as one mechanism, which is why it cannot be paraphrased as "karma is action".
> **Named evidence.** The five causes run wrong vision, non-restraint, carelessness, passion and activity; psychical action-matter attracts material action-matter, giving *bhāvabandha* and *dravyabandha*; and the four parameters divide labour so that activity fixes type and quantity while passion fixes duration and intensity, producing the contrast between *īryāpathika* and *sāmparāyika* bondage.
> **Analysis.** Because duration and intensity — the two features that actually determine future suffering — are governed by passion rather than by outward act, the doctrine explains why an identical bodily movement can be almost karmically inert in a disciplined agent and gravely binding in an angry one, which is exactly the result an ethics of intention requires.
> **Qualification / link.** The mechanism still owes an account of how formless consciousness and subtle matter make contact, and Nyāya presses precisely there; the defensible conclusion is that Jainism buys explanatory precision about moral consequence at the cost of an unresolved interaction problem.

#### UPSC TRAPS IN THIS SUBTOPIC

1. ❌ Writing "karma" without "material". ✅ Materiality is the Jaina thesis.
2. ❌ Assigning duration to activity. ✅ Duration and intensity belong to **passion**; type and quantity to **activity**.
3. ❌ Equating *bhāvabandha* with mere intention and *dravyabandha* with mere deed. ✅ One is the soul's modification, the other the matter's adhesion.
4. ❌ Treating merit as a step toward liberation. ✅ Merit improves the rebirth, not the release.
5. ❌ Confusing stoppage with shedding. ✅ Preventive versus eliminative.
6. ❌ Listing eight karmas without the destructive / non-destructive split. ✅ The split is what makes embodied omniscience intelligible.

#### RAPID REVISION

- Five causes: wrong vision, non-restraint, carelessness, passion, activity.
- Two bondages: psychic modification, material adhesion.
- Four parameters: type and quantity from activity; duration and intensity from passion.
- Eight karmas: four destructive, four non-destructive; delusion is the worst.
- Seven principles; nine categories add merit and demerit.
- Beginningless but terminable: *anādi* yet not endless.

### SESSION 9 — LIBERATION: THE THREE JEWELS, THE DISCIPLINE, THE FOURTEEN STAGES AND THE PERFECTED SOUL

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** Liberation is not something added to the soul. It is what remains when everything foreign has been stopped and shed. The path therefore has two jobs: close the door, and clean the room.

**Technical definition:** Liberation (*mokṣa*) is attained through the **three jewels (ratnatraya)** — right vision (*samyag-darśana*), right knowledge (*samyag-jñāna*), right conduct (*samyak-cāritra*) — realised through vows and disciplines that effect stoppage (*saṃvara*) and shedding (*nirjarā*), and mapped across **fourteen stages of spiritual development (guṇasthāna)** culminating in omniscience (*kevala-jñāna*) and the perfected soul (*siddha*).

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Jaina liberation is a subtraction and not an acquisition: because the soul's infinite knowledge, perception, bliss and power are intrinsic and merely obscured, the path is a graded removal of obscuring matter, and the liberated soul remains a numerically distinct individual rather than merging into any absolute.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **three jewels (ratnatraya)**
- **great vows (mahāvrata) / lesser vows (aṇuvrata)**
- **restraint (gupti), carefulness (samiti), virtues (dharma), contemplations (anuprekṣā), endurance of afflictions (pariṣaha-jaya), austerity (tapas)**
- **stages of spiritual development (guṇasthāna)**; **ladder of suppression (upaśama-śreṇi) / ladder of annihilation (kṣapaka-śreṇi)**
- **embodied omniscient (sayoga-kevalī) / activity-free omniscient (ayoga-kevalī)**
- **perfected soul (siddha)**; **summit of the world (siddha-śilā)**; **voluntary passionless death (sallekhanā / santhārā)**

**How to use them:** pair every ethical term with its karmic function. Vows are not moral decoration; they are the operational form of stoppage, and austerity is the operational form of shedding.

#### SIMPLE START — PLAIN-LANGUAGE GATEWAY

```text
   A ROOM WITH AN OPEN DOOR AND A FLOOR ALREADY THICK WITH DUST

   STEP 1  CLOSE THE DOOR        -> stoppage (saṃvara)
           vows, restraint, carefulness, virtues, contemplations, endurance
           result: no NEW dust enters

   STEP 2  SWEEP THE FLOOR       -> shedding (nirjarā)
           austerity, repentance, service, study, detachment, meditation
           result: OLD dust is removed

   STEP 3  THE ROOM IS THE ROOM  -> liberation (mokṣa)
           nothing has been added; something foreign has been removed

   WHY THE ORDER MATTERS: sweeping while the door stands open is endless work.
```

**In simple words:** Jainism refuses to let ethics and metaphysics come apart. Every vow has a job in the karmic machine, and the machine has two independent problems — arrival and accumulation — so the path needs two independent remedies.

| Discipline | Job |
|---|---|
| Vows, restraint, carefulness | Close the channel: **stoppage** |
| Austerity, repentance, meditation | Remove the deposit: **shedding** |
| Right vision | Aim the whole process correctly |

> **One-line memory:** stoppage is preventive, shedding is eliminative, liberation is what is left.

#### 🖼️ VISUAL — THE FOURTEEN STAGES AS ONE ASCENT WITH ONE FALL

⚠️ **Caption.** Stage 4 and stage 11 are the two places examiners probe. Stage 4 is the breakthrough of right vision **without** conduct; stage 11 is the trap of suppression, which necessarily falls back because covered delusion is not destroyed delusion. An answer that names both has understood the ladder rather than memorised it.

#### 1. The three jewels

✅ **Statement.** Liberation requires the **three jewels (ratnatraya)**: right vision (*samyag-darśana*), right knowledge (*samyag-jñāna*) and right conduct (*samyak-cāritra*).

✅ **Argument:** one must first see reality correctly; then know it correctly; then live in accordance with that insight. Mere knowledge without conduct does not stop influx; mere conduct without right view lacks orientation.

✅ **Distinction.** The three jewels are **not** sequential in a crude external sense; they are mutually reinforcing dimensions of one path, and the *Tattvārthasūtra* states them jointly.

✅ **Cārvāka-style objection.** Is "right faith" blind belief?
✅ **Reply.** No. *Samyag-darśana* is right apprehension or trustful orientation toward truth, not irrational fideism; it is the stage-4 breakthrough that makes the rest of the ladder possible.

#### 2. The great vows and their graded form

| Vow | Basic meaning | Soteriological relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **Non-violence (ahiṃsā)** | Non-injury in thought, speech and act | Central, because injury binds karma most deeply |
| **Truthfulness (satya)** | Truthful speech | Purifies speech and intention |
| **Non-stealing (asteya)** | Taking nothing not given | Curbs acquisitive harm |
| **Chastity (brahmacarya)** | Control of sensuality | Disciplines desire |
| **Non-possession (aparigraha)** | Non-attachment to possessions | Reduces grasping and karmic adhesion |

⚠️ **Presupposition.** Ethical discipline is **metaphysically effective**, not merely socially good.

✅ **Distinction.** In Jainism non-violence is not only a moral ideal but a **soteriological technology**: extreme care toward even one-sense beings follows directly from the pluralistic doctrine of souls established in Session 3.

✅ **Householder-pragmatic objection.** Such rigour is too severe for ordinary life.
⚠️ **Reply.** Jainism distinguishes **great vows (mahāvrata)** for ascetics from **lesser vows (aṇuvrata)** for householders, preserving the ideal while allowing graded practice — which is exactly what the fifth stage of the ladder records.

#### 3. The complete discipline of stoppage and shedding

✅ The five vows are central but do **not** exhaust the standard discipline:

| Discipline | Sanskrit | Main function |
|---|---|---|
| Restraint of body, speech and mind | **gupti** | Controls the three channels of activity |
| Carefulness in movement, speech, alms-seeking, handling objects and bodily functions | **samiti** | Prevents negligent injury and fresh influx |
| Ten virtues, including forgiveness, humility, truthfulness, purity, restraint, austerity, renunciation, non-attachment and celibacy | **dharma** | Replaces passion with disciplined character |
| Contemplations on the self, impermanence, solitude, influx, stoppage and related truths | **anuprekṣā / bhāvanā** | Reorients cognition and weakens attachment |
| Patient conquest of afflictions | **pariṣaha-jaya** | Prevents pain, hunger, heat, cold and insult from producing passion |
| Right conduct | **cāritra** | Integrates vision, knowledge, vows and equanimity |
| Austerity | **tapas** | Stops passion and accelerates the wearing away of stored karma |

✅ **Two forms of shedding.** Natural fruition wears karma out when it matures; disciplined austerity and purification **accelerate** shedding. External austerities regulate bodily consumption and exposure; internal austerities include repentance, humility, service, study, detachment, meditation and abandonment of bodily identification.

✅ **Distinction to write.** Stoppage is preventive; shedding is eliminative. Vows primarily close the channels of new influx, while austerity and purified conduct also exhaust karma already bound.

#### 4. The fourteen stages of spiritual development

| No. | Stage | Brief gloss |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **Mithyā-dṛṣṭi** | False vision; complete wrong orientation |
| 2 | **Sāsvādana / sāsādana** | Falling condition with a lingering taste of right vision |
| 3 | **Miśra / samyag-mithyā-dṛṣṭi** | Mixed right and wrong orientation |
| 4 | **Avirata samyag-dṛṣṭi** | Right vision without full restraint |
| 5 | **Deśavirata** | Partial restraint, especially lay vows |
| 6 | **Pramatta-saṃyata** | Full restraint with negligence |
| 7 | **Apramatta-saṃyata** | Full restraint without negligence |
| 8 | **Apūrva-karaṇa / nivṛtti-bādara** | Unprecedented purification; gross passions being transformed |
| 9 | **Anivṛtti-bādara** | Further advanced purification of gross passions |
| 10 | **Sūkṣma-samparāya** | Only subtle greed remains |
| 11 | **Upaśānta-moha** | Delusion suppressed, not destroyed |
| 12 | **Kṣīṇa-moha** | Delusion destroyed |
| 13 | **Sayoga-kevalī** | Omniscient embodied being with activity still present |
| 14 | **Ayoga-kevalī** | Activity ceases; momentary final state before perfection |

✅ **Reading the ladder:** stages 1–3 describe wrong, falling and mixed vision; stage 4 is decisive because right vision first becomes stable enough to orient the path; stages 5–7 discipline conduct through partial and full restraint; stages 8–10 refine passions from gross to extremely subtle; stages 11–12 mark the crucial difference between **suppression** and **destruction** of delusion; stages 13–14 explain how omniscience and final disembodiment are related.

✅ **Two ladders.** The **ladder of suppression (upaśama-śreṇi)** suppresses delusion and can reach stage 11, which **necessarily falls back** because suppressed delusion is not destroyed. The **ladder of annihilation (kṣapaka-śreṇi)** destroys delusion and reaches stage 12 and beyond.

✅ **Advaita-style objection.** A fourteen-stage ladder turns liberation into a mechanical sequence rather than insight.
⚠️ **Reply.** Insight, conduct and karmic purification must be integrated; right vision without progressive destruction of karma is incomplete, and the ladder simply records that integration honestly.

#### 5. Embodied omniscience and the Jaina answer to liberation-in-life

✅ **Statement.** At stage 13 the **sayoga-kevalī** possesses omniscience while embodied, because the **destructive (ghātiyā)** karmas have been destroyed while the **non-destructive (aghātiyā)** karmas remain. Stage 14, **ayoga-kevalī**, is the momentary cessation of activity immediately before perfection.

✅ **Distinction.** The *sayoga-kevalī* is the Jaina answer to "is liberation-in-life possible?": the embodied omniscient being is liberated-in-life in the Jaina sense, though the perfected state follows only after the remaining non-destructive karmas are exhausted.

✅ **Example.** A lamp whose cover has been removed shines fully even before it is moved; likewise the omniscient being knows fully while still embodied.

✅ **Rāmānuja-style objection.** Omniscience while embodied seems unintelligible if embodiment itself implies limitation.
⚠️ **Reply.** Jainism distinguishes destructive karmas, which obstruct essential knowledge, from non-destructive karmas, which sustain embodiment without blocking omniscience.

#### 6. Omniscience, liberation and the condition of the perfected soul

✅ **Omniscience (kevala-jñāna)** is perfect knowledge attained when the obstructive karmas are fully destroyed. The soul is intrinsically omniscient; ignorance is due to karmic obstruction; remove the obstruction and native luminosity manifests completely. Perfection is therefore **recovery, not production**.

✅ **Liberation (mokṣa)** is the final state in which the soul is freed from all karmic matter, regains infinite knowledge, perception, bliss and power, and rises to the **summit of the world (siddha-śilā)**, being no longer weighed down by karmic matter.

✅ **The condition of the liberated soul — the 2018 demand, stated exactly:**

| Question | Jaina answer |
|---|---|
| Does it retain individuality? | ✅ Yes. It is a **siddha**, numerically distinct; there is no merger |
| Does it retain knowledge? | ✅ Yes. Infinite knowledge and perception are fully manifest |
| Does it retain body or activity? | ❌ No. Disembodied, activity-free, beyond rebirth |
| Does it return? | ❌ No. Bondage is beginningless but terminable |
| Where is it? | ✅ At the summit of the world, in the Jaina cosmological picture |

✅ **Distinction.** Unlike Advaita liberation, this is **not** merger into impersonal Brahman. Unlike Buddhist *nirvāṇa*, it is the liberation of a distinct enduring soul.

⚠️ **Modern objection.** Spatial ascent of a liberated soul may seem mythic.
⚠️ **Reply.** Report the cosmological picture faithfully while separating canonical doctrine from modern philosophical evaluation; do not silently secularise the doctrine.

#### 7. Voluntary passionless death

✅ **Statement.** **Sallekhanā** or **santhārā** is the voluntary, gradual, ritually regulated relinquishment of food and passions undertaken when death is unavoidable or bodily decline is incurable.

✅ **Argument:**

1. Bondage tracks **passion**: passion fixes karmic matter more deeply than bare activity.
2. A death faced with equanimity minimises new influx and prevents panic, attachment and aversion from creating fresh bondage.
3. The aim is not to end life as such, but to end passion at the moment when life is already approaching its close.
4. The practice must be free from desire for death, desire to continue living, attachment to friends, recollection of pleasures and hope of reward.
5. If such motives enter, the act becomes a **transgression (aticāra)** of the vow's proper discipline.

✅ **Distinction from suicide (ātma-ghāta):** suicide is passion-driven, sudden, violent, often concealed and motivated by despair or aversion; *sallekhanā* is passion-free, non-violent, gradual, supervised and public.

❓ **Modern legal-ethical objection.** Critics in India have disputed whether *sallekhanā* is protected religious practice or an impermissible form of self-destruction.
⚠️ **Reply and evidence discipline.** Legal and ethical evaluation must distinguish intention, procedure, publicity, non-violence and the absence of death-desire. The controversy is a useful Paper II bridge to religion and morality, but **specific case names, holdings, dates and statutory details must not be asserted unless separately verified**.

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH — claim → named evidence → analysis → qualification

> **Claim.** The Jaina path is intelligible only as the operational counterpart of the bondage mechanism, which is why its ethics cannot be detached from its metaphysics.
> **Named evidence.** Vows, restraint (*gupti*), carefulness (*samiti*), the ten virtues, the contemplations and the endurance of afflictions all effect stoppage, while austerity effects shedding; the fourteen stages record the ascent; and the ladder of suppression necessarily falls from stage 11 because delusion there is merely covered, whereas the ladder of annihilation destroys it at stage 12.
> **Analysis.** Because suppression and destruction produce the same appearance but opposite futures, the doctrine gives Jainism a principled account of spiritual relapse that a purely insight-based soteriology cannot supply, and it also explains why omniscience can coexist with embodiment once only the non-destructive karmas remain.
> **Qualification / link.** The account remains internally taxonomic and externally unverifiable: a critic can accept the moral psychology while rejecting the karmic physics, so the strongest available conclusion is that the path is coherent within Jaina ontology rather than demonstrable outside it.

#### UPSC TRAPS IN THIS SUBTOPIC

1. ❌ Saying the liberated soul merges into a universal consciousness. ✅ It remains numerically distinct.
2. ❌ Treating stage 11 as an achievement. ✅ It necessarily falls back.
3. ❌ Making the three jewels a temporal sequence. ✅ They are joint conditions.
4. ❌ Reducing the discipline to five vows. ✅ Restraint, carefulness, virtues, contemplations, endurance and austerity all belong.
5. ❌ Citing a specific *sallekhanā* judgment. ✅ Report the controversy without unverified legal detail.
6. ❌ Saying omniscience requires disembodiment. ✅ Stage 13 is embodied omniscience.

#### RAPID REVISION

- Three jewels, jointly; right vision is the stage-4 breakthrough.
- Great vows for ascetics, lesser vows for householders.
- Stoppage = vows and restraint; shedding = austerity.
- Fourteen stages; two ladders; stage 11 falls, stage 12 does not.
- *Sayoga-kevalī* = embodied omniscient = the Jaina liberation-in-life.
- *Siddha* keeps individuality; no merger, no return.

### SESSION 10 — JAINISM AMONG ITS RIVALS: NAMED CRITICISMS, REPLIES AND THE ANSWER ARCHITECTURE

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

**Plain-language definition:** Every major Indian school attacked Jainism at a different joint. This session collects the named attacks, the Jaina replies, and an honest verdict on how much force each attack retains.

**Technical definition:** The dialectical ledger (*pūrvapakṣa–siddhānta*) of Jainism records objections from Śaṃkara, Rāmānuja, Dharmakīrti-style Buddhist argument, Nyāya, Sāṃkhya-Vedānta and Cārvāka, each paired with the Jaina reply and its residual force.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Jainism is best evaluated as a rigorous qualified realism: internally systematic, ethically powerful and logically subtle, yet vulnerable to external critiques of contradiction, soul-matter interaction and unverifiable higher knowledge.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **opponent's position (pūrvapakṣa) / established conclusion (siddhānta)**
- **Śaṃkara**, **Rāmānuja**, **Dharmakīrti**, **Nyāya**, **Sāṃkhya-Vedānta**, **Cārvāka**
- **causal efficacy (arthakriyā)**
- **residual force** — what survives the reply

**How to use them:** never write "critics say". Name the critic, state the argument in its strongest form, give the Jaina reply, then grade what survives. This three-move discipline is what separates a 9 from a 12 on a 15-marker.

#### SIMPLE START — PLAIN-LANGUAGE GATEWAY

```text
   SIX CRITICS, SIX DIFFERENT JOINTS OF THE SAME SYSTEM

   Śaṃkara        -> attacks the LOGIC          "you affirm and deny together"
   Rāmānuja       -> attacks the OBJECT         "opposed real attributes in one thing"
   Dharmakīrti    -> attacks the SOUL           "permanent yet changing cannot act"
   Nyāya          -> attacks the SOUL'S SIZE    "eternal things do not expand"

   Cārvāka        -> attacks the EVIDENCE       "none of this is perceived"

   The first five accept souls or substances and still find Jainism costly.
```

#### 1. Śaṃkara's critique of many-sidedness

✅ **Statement.** Śaṃkara, in the *Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya*, criticises Jaina many-sidedness by arguing that contradictory attributes cannot belong to the same thing in the same way.

✅ **The critic's argument:**

1. If the same object is said to be both existent and non-existent, determinate knowledge is threatened.
2. If everything is "somehow yes and somehow no", practical action loses clear guidance.
3. If many-sidedness asserts itself absolutely, it contradicts itself.
4. If it asserts itself only conditionally, it seems to lose universal force.

⚠️ **Presupposition.** A viable metaphysics must preserve non-contradiction and assertoric clarity.

✅ **The Jaina reply:**

1. Opposed predicates are indexed to distinct standpoints: **substance (dravya)**, **place (kṣetra)**, **time (kāla)** and **mode (bhāva)**.
2. There is no contradiction unless affirmation and negation are made in the same respect, place, time and mode.
3. *Syādvāda* is itself asserted *syāt*; this reflexive qualification is a virtue, because the doctrine obeys its own rule.
4. ⚠️ A residual objection remains: if conditional predication is always qualified, critics may say it loses final assertoric force.

#### 2. Rāmānuja's critique: opposed real attributes

✅ **Statement.** Rāmānuja presses the charge that a real substance with really opposed attributes is unintelligible.

✅ **The critic's argument:** a thing cannot be genuinely both F and not-F in the same meaningful sense; if the attributes are real and opposed, the substance becomes incoherent; if they are not real, Jaina realism weakens.

⚠️ **Presupposition.** Real predication must avoid contradiction at the level of the **object**, not merely at the level of language.

✅ **The Jaina reply.** Standpoint-indexing again: the same substance persists while modes arise and cease, so the opposition is between **aspects**, not within one unqualified predication.

⚠️ **Assessment.** The reply works best if one accepts the substance–mode distinction; it is less persuasive to schools that reject such aspect-realism, and Rāmānuja is precisely such a school.

#### 3. The Buddhist critique of a permanent-yet-changing soul

✅ **Statement.** A Buddhist critique associated with Dharmakīrti's causal-efficacy style of argument challenges the Jaina soul as permanent yet changing.

✅ **The critic's argument:**

1. If the soul expands and contracts with the body, it undergoes real change.
2. What undergoes real change cannot be absolutely permanent.
3. A permanent entity that changes faces the **causal efficacy (arthakriyā)** dilemma: if it acts, it changes; if it does not change, it cannot act.

⚠️ **Presupposition.** Causal efficacy and permanence are difficult to combine in one substance.

✅ **The Jaina reply.** Origination, cessation and permanence are simultaneously real features of every real; the substance is permanent **as substance** and changing **as mode**.

⚠️ **Assessment.** The Buddhist objection remains strong if one denies the real distinction between substance and modes — which is exactly what a momentarist does deny.

#### 4. The Nyāya critique of the soul's extension and of material action-matter

✅ **Statement.** Nyāya criticises the Jaina view that the soul is co-extensive with the body and expands or contracts like a lamp filling a room.

✅ **The critic's argument:** an eternal self should not literally expand and contract; if the self has parts or space-points, its eternality seems threatened; and material action-matter is less parsimonious than an unseen moral potency such as *adṛṣṭa*.

✅ **The Jaina reply.** Jainism invokes **space-points (pradeśa)** and the lamp simile: consciousness can pervade the body without being identical with gross matter, and karmic matter explains concrete embodiment better than a merely invisible potency.

⚠️ **Assessment.** The reply is internally coherent within Jaina pluralism, but the interaction of a conscious, space-point-bearing soul with matter remains a debated point, and it is the same difficulty that Session 8 flagged.

#### 5. The Sāṃkhya-Vedānta critique of intrinsic omniscience and beginningless bondage

✅ **Statement.** Sāṃkhya and Vedānta-style objections ask why karmic matter obscures an intrinsically omniscient soul, and what began the obscuration.

✅ **The critic's argument:** if the soul is intrinsically pure and omniscient, obscuration appears accidental and inexplicable; if karma began at some time, a first cause of bondage is required; if karma never began, liberation may seem impossible.

✅ **The Jaina reply.** The soul-karma relation is **beginningless (anādi)** but not endless; like seed-and-tree or ore-and-gold, there is no first temporal term, yet disciplined stoppage and shedding can terminate the relation.

⚠️ **Assessment.** The reply blocks the first-cause demand but leaves a metaphysical puzzle about why beginningless bondage is possible for an intrinsically perfect soul.

#### 6. The Cārvāka or empiricist critique

✅ **Statement.** A Cārvāka or modern empiricist objection rejects invisible souls, karmic particles, omniscience and the summit of the world as unverifiable.

✅ **The critic's argument.** Ordinary perception does not disclose karmic matter, disembodied perfected souls or omniscient cognition.

⚠️ **Presupposition.** Only publicly verifiable empirical entities should be admitted.

⚠️ **The Jaina reply.** Jainism combines testimony, inference, moral phenomenology and the internal logic of purification to defend these doctrines, but it cannot satisfy a strict materialist who admits only gross perception.

⚠️ **Assessment.** Use this objection as an **external** critique. Do not let it displace the named classical objections, which are what Indian-philosophy examiners are actually testing.

#### 7. The three focused comparisons that questions actually demand

**Jainism and Buddhism.** ✅ Both are renunciant (*śramaṇa*) traditions, both challenge Vedic ritual supremacy, and both reject a creator. ✅ Jainism affirms many permanent souls and permanent-cum-changing substances, whereas Buddhism rejects a substance-self. ✅ Jaina non-violence is far more radical, because even minute living beings are souls. ✅ Jaina action-matter is material, while Buddhist action-consequence is primarily volitional or mental-causal continuity. ⚠️ Historically close, metaphysically opposed.

**Jainism and Advaita Vedānta.** ✅ Ultimate reality is plural rather than non-dual; the world is real rather than *mithyā*; souls are many rather than ultimately one; bondage is karmic material obscuration rather than *avidyā* and superimposition; and liberation produces many distinct perfected souls rather than the realisation of non-duality.

**Jainism and Cārvāka.** ✅ Pluralist realism with soul and action-matter against materialist minimalism; action-matter real and material against action-consequence rejected; liberation central against liberation denied; conditional predication against suspicion of inference and testimony; austerity and non-violence against this-worldly pleasure and prudence.

#### HOW TO WRITE THE PARAGRAPH — claim → named evidence → analysis → qualification

> **Claim.** The classical objections to Jainism are not variants of a single complaint, and treating them as one is the commonest structural failure in answers to critical directives.
> **Named evidence.** Śaṃkara attacks predication in the *Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya*, Rāmānuja attacks opposed real attributes in the object, Dharmakīrti-style argument attacks a permanent-yet-changing soul through the *arthakriyā* dilemma, Nyāya attacks the soul's expansion and contraction, and Sāṃkhya-Vedānta attacks the intelligibility of beginningless obscuration in an intrinsically omniscient soul.
> **Analysis.** Since each objection targets a different commitment, each Jaina reply mobilises a different resource — the four indices, the substance–mode distinction, the three-term definition of the real, the doctrine of space-points, and beginninglessness without endlessness — so a single generic defence necessarily under-answers four of the five.
> **Qualification / link.** The replies are internally coherent but not school-neutral, since each presupposes exactly what the critic denies; the defensible verdict is therefore that Jainism is consistent within its own realism while remaining costly to anyone who rejects aspect-realism or material action-matter.

#### CORE DIAGNOSTICS — 24 ITEMS

#### MCQ 1. The systematic anchor of Jaina philosophy

With reference to the *Tattvārthasūtra*, consider the following statements.

1. It is ascribed to Umāsvāti in Śvetāmbara usage and to Umāsvāmī in Digambara usage.
2. Its authority is confined to one of the two major traditions, which is why sectarian canons differ.
3. It states the path to liberation through right vision, right knowledge and right conduct together.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. 1 and 2 only

B. 1 and 3 only

C. 2 and 3 only

D. 1, 2 and 3

**Answer: B.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: it retains statement 2, which confines the text to a single tradition, whereas the *Tattvārthasūtra* is precisely the rare work treated as authoritative across both.
- **B:** Correct: the double ascription and the joint three-jewel path formula are both standard, and together they explain why the text functions as a shared systematic grammar.
- **C:** Incorrect: it drops the double ascription that names the text's cross-sectarian standing while keeping the false restriction of its authority.
- **D:** Incorrect: statements 1 and 3 are sound, but including 2 contradicts them by denying the very cross-sectarian acceptance that makes the text quotable for both traditions.

**Examiner trap 1:** Shared textual authority is not doctrinal uniformity; the same *sūtra* text can anchor both traditions while canon, monastic practice and several details continue to diverge.

#### MCQ 2. What the title-words of the tradition actually mean

In Jaina usage, the terms *jina* and *tīrthaṅkara* respectively designate

A. one who has conquered the passions and karmic bondage, and a liberated teacher who re-establishes the crossing over rebirth

B. the creator of the ordered cosmos, and the divine agent who allots merit and demerit to embodied beings at each rebirth

C. the first teacher of the present cycle alone, and any monk who has completed the five great vows under supervision

D. a soul that has suppressed delusion temporarily, and a householder who has taken the lesser vows before ordination

**Answer: A.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Correct: *jina* is the conqueror of passions from whom the tradition takes its name, and a *tīrthaṅkara* is the ford-maker who rebuilds the crossing over the cycle of rebirth.
- **B:** Incorrect: it smuggles creation and divine allotment into a system that explains inequality by action-matter and denies a creator altogether.
- **C:** Incorrect: it confuses a title of spiritual achievement with a chronological rank and with ordinary monastic completion of the vows.
- **D:** Incorrect: temporary suppression of delusion belongs to the eleventh stage, and lesser vows belong to householders; neither term is defined that way.

**Examiner trap 2:** Reverence for perfected teachers is not theism; Jainism honours achieved perfection while denying that any being made or governs the world.

#### MCQ 3. The definition of the real

In the Jaina formula *utpāda-vyaya-dhrauvya-yuktaṃ sat*, the real is characterised by

A. persistence alone, since change belongs only to appearance and never to the substance itself

B. origination alone, since a thing is nothing more than the series of its successive productions

C. origination, decay and persistence taken together, in one and the same real

D. decay alone, since every determinate thing is defined by the moment at which it ceases

**Answer: C.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: reducing change to appearance is the static-monist error that Jainism explicitly rejects, since it cannot explain why the bracelet actually became a ring.
- **B:** Incorrect: a pure series of productions is closer to momentariness and leaves recognition of the same gold across the change unexplained.
- **C:** Correct: the real is defined by origination, decay and persistence together, which is what allows permanence to be predicated of substance and change of mode.
- **D:** Incorrect: making cessation definitional inverts the doctrine, because persistence is precisely the term that blocks a purely momentary reading.

**Examiner trap 3:** The three terms are joint, not alternative; a script that offers "permanence with some change" has already lost the definition, because change is constitutive rather than conceded.

#### MCQ 4. Substance, quality and mode

Which statement correctly states the relation among substance (*dravya*), quality (*guṇa*) and mode (*paryāya*)?

A. Quality is the abiding characteristic of a persisting substance, while mode is the changing state in which it appears

B. Quality and mode are two names for the same changing state, distinguished only by the standpoint of the observer

C. Substance is an inference from modes alone, since qualities are conventional descriptions with no bearer

D. Mode is the abiding characteristic of a substance, while quality is the momentary state it currently exhibits

**Answer: A.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Correct: qualities belong to the substance as abiding characteristics, whereas modes are its changing states, which is why consciousness is a quality of the soul while delusion is a mode.
- **B:** Incorrect: collapsing the two removes the distinction that lets Jainism assert permanence and change of one thing in different respects.
- **C:** Incorrect: treating the substance as a mere inference concedes the Buddhist nominalist case that Jainism answers by insisting that changes require something whose changes they are.
- **D:** Incorrect: it exactly reverses the two terms, which is the most frequent examinable confusion in this part of the syllabus.

**Examiner trap 4:** Consciousness is a *guṇa* of the soul; pleasure, pain, delusion and liberated omniscience are *paryāyas* — the same soul, different states.

#### MCQ 5. Substances and extended substances

Which one of the following is a real substance (*dravya*) in mainstream Jaina exposition but **not** an extended substance (*astikāya*)?

A. Space (*ākāśa*)

B. Time (*kāla*)

C. Matter (*pudgala*)

D. Medium of rest (*adharma*)

**Answer: B.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: space is an extended substance, articulated as world-space and the space beyond the world, and it accommodates the other substances.
- **B:** Correct: time is admitted as a substance but is not an extended substance, which is why the tradition speaks of five extended substances plus time.
- **C:** Incorrect: matter is extended and aggregative, and it is additionally the only substance that possesses form.
- **D:** Incorrect: the medium of rest is an extended substance co-extensive with world-space, on a par with the medium of motion.

**Examiner trap 5:** Six substances but five extended substances; writing "six *astikāyas*" converts a precise doctrine into an avoidable factual error.

#### MCQ 6. Two substances with misleading names

In Jaina ontology, *dharma-dravya* and *adharma-dravya* respectively signify

A. accumulated merit and accumulated demerit, which jointly determine the quality of the next embodiment

B. right conduct and wrong conduct, understood as the two moral orientations available to an embodied soul

C. the medium that makes motion possible and the medium that makes rest possible for souls and matter

D. the ordering principle of the cosmos and the disordering principle that produces decay within it

**Answer: C.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: merit and demerit are *puṇya* and *pāpa*, which appear in the ninefold list of categories, not in the sixfold list of substances.
- **B:** Incorrect: conduct is treated under right conduct and the vows; the substances in question are cosmological conditions, not moral orientations.
- **C:** Correct: they are enabling conditions of movement and rest, comparable to water for a swimming fish, and they are co-extensive with world-space.
- **D:** Incorrect: no Jaina text assigns them cosmic order and disorder; they enable motion and rest without themselves pushing or halting anything.

**Examiner trap 6:** The same two Sanskrit words carry a moral sense elsewhere in Indian philosophy; in Jaina ontology they are strictly media, and the shift of sense is deliberately examined.

#### MCQ 7. The Jaina conception of the soul

Which statement best expresses the Jaina conception of the soul (*jīva*)?

A. It is one universal consciousness that appears as many only through the beginningless operation of ignorance

B. It is one of innumerable distinct substances, intrinsically endowed with infinite knowledge, perception, bliss and power

C. It is a stream of momentary cognitions with no bearer, so that personal identity is a useful conventional fiction

D. It is an eternal substance that becomes conscious only when the internal organ is conjoined with it in embodiment

**Answer: B.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: this is the Advaita position; Jainism holds that souls are numerically many and remain distinct even after liberation.
- **B:** Correct: souls are innumerable, beginningless and intrinsically endowed with the infinite tetrad, which is obscured rather than absent in bondage.
- **C:** Incorrect: this is the Buddhist no-self account, which Jainism rejects because bondage and liberation require an enduring knower.
- **D:** Incorrect: this is the Nyāya account of a self that knows through conjunction with the internal organ; in Jainism consciousness is intrinsic.

**Examiner trap 7:** Intrinsic omniscience is not empirical omniscience; the soul's infinite knowledge is present as capacity and obscured in fact, which is exactly what action-matter is introduced to explain.

#### MCQ 8. What is distinctive about matter

Which feature uniquely marks matter (*pudgala*) among the six substances?

A. It alone is co-extensive with world-space and therefore accommodates every other substance within it

B. It alone lacks space-points, which is why it can be divided indefinitely without any residue remaining

C. It alone undergoes origination and decay, the remaining substances being exempt from modal change

D. It alone possesses form, that is colour, taste, smell and touch, and is therefore sense-perceptible

**Answer: D.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: accommodation is the function of space; matter occupies room rather than providing it.
- **B:** Incorrect: matter is extended and aggregative, built from indivisible atoms combining into aggregates, so indefinite division without residue is not the Jaina view.
- **C:** Incorrect: every substance has modes; origination and decay belong to the definition of the real as such, not to matter alone.
- **D:** Correct: matter is the sole substance with form, which is why sound is classed as a material modification and why karmic matter can adhere at all.

**Examiner trap 8:** In Jainism sound is a modification of matter, not a quality of ether; importing the Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika treatment of sound here is a silent cross-school error.

#### MCQ 9. Ordinary perception in the strict Jaina classification

In the strict Jaina epistemological classification, ordinary sensory cognition is

A. direct knowledge in the fullest sense, since sense-contact provides the clearest access available to an embodied knower

B. invalid cognition, because the senses are themselves products of karmic matter and therefore systematically deceive

C. only conventional direct knowledge, and strictly belongs with mediated cognition because it depends on sense and mind

D. a species of scriptural knowledge, since every perceptual judgement is already structured by inherited verbal categories

**Answer: C.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: this is the Nyāya expectation; Jainism measures directness by absence of mediation, not by sensory clarity.
- **B:** Incorrect: ordinary perception is valid for practical dealings, and its wrongness arises only under wrong orientation, not from the senses as such.
- **C:** Correct: ordinary perception is *sāṃvyavahārika pratyakṣa*, direct for practical purposes but mediated by organ, mind and karmic limitation.
- **D:** Incorrect: verbal knowledge is derived from sense-and-mind cognition rather than constituting it, so the dependence runs the other way.

**Examiner trap 9:** The Jaina and Nyāya senses of *pratyakṣa* differ; using the word without the qualifier "conventional" imports a rival classification into a Jaina answer.

#### MCQ 10. Classifying the five kinds of knowledge

Which pair is correctly classified as indirect (*parokṣa*) in the basic fivefold scheme of knowledge?

A. Clairvoyant knowledge and knowledge of others' mental modes

B. Knowledge of others' mental modes and omniscience

C. Sense-and-mind cognition and clairvoyant knowledge

D. Sense-and-mind cognition and verbal or scriptural knowledge

**Answer: D.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: both members are strictly direct, since the soul knows without the mediation of sense or mind, even though both remain limited.
- **B:** Incorrect: both are direct, and omniscience is the complete case of directness rather than an instance of mediation.
- **C:** Incorrect: it pairs one genuinely mediated kind with one strictly direct kind, so the classification is half right and therefore wrong.
- **D:** Correct: sense-and-mind cognition and verbal or scriptural knowledge are mediated, the second being derived from the first through signs and teaching.

**Examiner trap 10:** Verbal or scriptural knowledge is not blind faith; it is a mediated cognition dependent on prior cognition and communication, and Jainism grades it accordingly.

#### MCQ 11. The order of the five kinds of knowledge

Which sequence correctly states the five kinds of Jaina knowledge in ascending order?

A. Śruta, mati, avadhi, kevala, manaḥparyāya

B. Mati, avadhi, śruta, manaḥparyāya, kevala

C. Mati, śruta, avadhi, manaḥparyāya, kevala

D. Avadhi, manaḥparyāya, mati, śruta, kevala

**Answer: C.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: it inverts the first pair and places omniscience before knowledge of others' mental modes, breaking the graded removal of obscuration.
- **B:** Incorrect: it inserts clairvoyance between the two mediated kinds, separating verbal knowledge from the sense-and-mind cognition it derives from.
- **C:** Correct: the canonical ascending order runs sense-and-mind cognition, verbal knowledge, clairvoyance, knowledge of others' mental modes, and omniscience.
- **D:** Incorrect: it places the two direct but limited kinds before the mediated kinds, reversing the logic of progressive unveiling.

**Examiner trap 11:** The order is not a ranking of usefulness but a curve of karmic unveiling; each step marks how much knowledge-obscuring action-matter has been removed.

#### MCQ 12. Jaina causation

The Jaina account of causation is best described as

A. both-existence-and-non-existence of the effect, since the effect pre-exists as substance and is new as mode

B. pre-existence of the effect without qualification, since nothing genuinely new can ever be produced

C. fresh production of the effect without qualification, since the effect is entirely absent before its production

D. dependent origination without any substrate, since causal regularity is all that causal talk records

**Answer: A.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Correct: the effect is continuous with the cause as enduring substance and novel as fresh mode, which is many-sidedness applied to causal identity.
- **B:** Incorrect: unqualified pre-existence is the Sāṃkhya position, and it makes production add nothing at all.
- **C:** Incorrect: unqualified fresh production is the Nyāya position, and it leaves unexplained why clay yields a jar rather than anything whatever.
- **D:** Incorrect: dependence without a substrate is the Buddhist framing, which Jainism rejects because it dissolves the persisting bearer of change.

**Examiner trap 12:** Calling Jaina causation *satkāryavāda* is a common slip; the whole point of the Jaina label is the added respect-index that Sāṃkhya does not use.

#### MCQ 13. What many-sidedness asserts

The doctrine of many-sidedness (*anekāntavāda*) primarily asserts that

A. all judgements are equally true, since standpoints cannot be ranked and disagreement is therefore merely apparent

B. reality is unknowable in itself, so that every description reports only the classificatory habits of the describer

C. contradictory properties genuinely belong to one thing at one time and in one and the same respect

D. the real object possesses innumerable aspects, so no single unqualified proposition exhausts it

**Answer: D.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: equal truth for all judgements is relativism, whereas Jainism grades judgements by whether they respect their own conditions.
- **B:** Incorrect: unknowability contradicts Jaina realism, which holds that aspects are objectively in the thing and are genuinely reached under standpoints.
- **C:** Incorrect: identity of respect is exactly what the *syāt* operator excludes; without different indices there would be a plain contradiction.
- **D:** Correct: the thesis is that the real is *ananta-dharmātmakaṃ vastu*, so finite judgement necessarily captures an aspect rather than the whole.

**Examiner trap 13:** Many-sidedness is ontology before it is logic; a script that introduces it as tolerance or open-mindedness has lost the derivation that the doctrine of judgement depends on.

#### MCQ 14. Three doctrines, three levels

Which formulation correctly distinguishes the three doctrines?

A. Many-sidedness is metaphysical, standpoint theory is epistemic, and conditional predication is the logical-linguistic form of assertion

B. Many-sidedness is logical, standpoint theory is ethical, and conditional predication is the metaphysical basis of the other two

C. Many-sidedness and conditional predication are identical, while standpoint theory is a later scholastic elaboration of both

D. Standpoint theory is metaphysical, conditional predication is epistemic, and many-sidedness is a rule of monastic debate

**Answer: A.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Correct: the three occupy distinct levels, and the sevenfold predication is the formal schema in which the third operates.
- **B:** Incorrect: it misassigns every level and turns standpoint theory into ethics, which no Jaina exposition supports.
- **C:** Incorrect: identifying the metaphysical thesis with the mode of assertion destroys the derivation from object to knower to sentence.
- **D:** Incorrect: reducing many-sidedness to a debating convention removes the ontological claim on which the whole system rests.

**Examiner trap 14:** The examiner is testing levels, not vocabulary; naming all three terms without assigning each its level scores as recall rather than understanding.

#### MCQ 15. The first of the seven standpoints

Which one of the following is the first member of the classical list of seven standpoints (*naya*)?

A. *Saṃgraha naya*, the generic standpoint attending to shared class-character

B. *Ṛjusūtra naya*, the standpoint restricted to the thing in its present state

C. *Naigama naya*, the mixed practical or purposive standpoint

D. *Evambhūta naya*, the standpoint on which a term applies only during actual function

**Answer: C.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: the generic standpoint is second, and it narrows what the first standpoint leaves deliberately mixed.
- **B:** Incorrect: the present-state standpoint is fourth and forms the hinge between substance-directed and mode-directed views.
- **C:** Correct: the mixed practical or purposive standpoint opens the list, blending general and particular, often through intention.
- **D:** Incorrect: the function-in-act standpoint is seventh and the strictest, so placing it first inverts the entire funnel.

**Examiner trap 15:** The list moves from loosest to strictest; reciting it out of order destroys the analytical point that partiality tightens as language becomes more literal.

#### MCQ 16. Matching a standpoint with its emphasis

Which pairing of a standpoint with its characteristic emphasis is correct?

A. *Vyavahāra naya* — the etymological differentiation of synonymous words

B. *Śabda naya* — the class-character shared by every member of a kind

C. *Saṃgraha naya* — the thing considered strictly as it is at the present moment

D. *Evambhūta naya* — the term applies only while the named function is being performed

**Answer: D.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: etymological differentiation belongs to *samabhirūḍha*, while the practical standpoint concerns ordinary dealings with particulars.
- **B:** Incorrect: class-character belongs to the generic standpoint, while the verbal standpoint attends to the expression actually used.
- **C:** Incorrect: the present-moment restriction belongs to *ṛjusūtra*; the generic standpoint deliberately abstracts from present particularity.
- **D:** Correct: on the function-in-act standpoint a person counts as a singer only while singing is actually occurring.

**Examiner trap 16:** The last three standpoints are all verbal, so they are easily swapped; distinguish attention to the word, to its derivation, and to the performance it names.

#### MCQ 17. What the particle *syāt* contributes

In the doctrine of conditional predication, the particle *syāt* is best rendered as

A. "probably", marking that the assertion is likely but not certain on the evidence available to the speaker

B. "in a certain respect", marking a definite assertion restricted by substance, place, time and mode

C. "apparently", marking that the assertion holds for appearances although the thing in itself is unknown

D. "let it be granted", marking a provisional concession made only for the purposes of a formal debate

**Answer: B.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: probability is precisely the reading Jaina authors reject, since the assertion is definite and only its respect is restricted.
- **B:** Correct: the operator states the respect in which the predication holds, which is why it blocks absolutisation without weakening assertion.
- **C:** Incorrect: an appearance-reality split is alien to Jaina realism, in which aspects are objectively in the thing.
- **D:** Incorrect: a debating concession is temporary and strategic, whereas the operator is a permanent feature of correct assertion.

**Examiner trap 17:** "The jar conditionally exists" is not a guess that the jar probably exists; treating *syāt* as a hedge converts a realist doctrine into scepticism.

#### MCQ 18. The fourth of the seven predications

In the sevenfold predication (*saptabhaṅgī*), the fourth member is

A. *syād asti ca nāsti ca*, in some respect it is and it is not

B. *syān nāsti*, in some respect it is not

C. *syād asti ca avaktavyam*, in some respect it is and is inexpressible

D. *syād avaktavyam*, in some respect it is inexpressible

**Answer: D.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: the successive conjunction of affirmation and negation is the third member, not the fourth.
- **B:** Incorrect: simple negation is the second member and is one of the three primitives from which the rest are built.
- **C:** Incorrect: the combination of affirmation with inexpressibility is the fifth member, which presupposes the fourth already stated.
- **D:** Correct: inexpressibility is the third primitive and stands as the fourth member, arising when affirmation and negation are demanded simultaneously and unqualifiedly.

**Examiner trap 18:** Three primitives taken singly, in pairs and jointly give three plus three plus one; misplacing inexpressibility breaks the combinatorial derivation that explains why there are exactly seven.

#### MCQ 19. The meaning of inexpressibility

In the sevenfold predication, *avaktavya* chiefly signifies that

A. a single unqualified expression cannot convey affirmation and negation simultaneously

B. the object in question lies wholly beyond language and can be approached only in silence

C. the speaker suspends judgement because the available evidence is insufficient for assertion

D. the proposition takes a third truth-value lying between plain truth and plain falsity

**Answer: A.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Correct: inexpressibility records a limit of simultaneous unqualified expression, not a limit of reality or of the speaker's evidence.
- **B:** Incorrect: mystical ineffability is not the Jaina claim, since the same object is expressible under each separate index.
- **C:** Incorrect: suspension of judgement is scepticism, whereas each of the seven forms is an assertion under a stated condition.
- **D:** Incorrect: a third truth-value would make the operator a semantic value rather than a standpoint index, which Jaina authors do not claim.

**Examiner trap 19:** Inexpressibility is a fact about one utterance, not about the thing; the same jar is perfectly expressible once the respect is separated out.

#### MCQ 20. Why no contradiction arises

The Jaina claim that "in some respect the jar is and is not" escapes formal contradiction because

A. affirmation is literal while negation is figurative, so the two predicates never occupy the same logical plane

B. the law of non-contradiction is denied outright, and Jaina logic replaces it with a rule of graded assertion

C. one of the two predications is always merely conventional and is finally cancelled by the other

D. affirmation and negation are indexed to different substance, place, time or mode, so the respects differ

**Answer: D.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: no Jaina text makes negation figurative; both predications are literal and both are true under their own index.
- **B:** Incorrect: the doctrine preserves non-contradiction rather than abandoning it, which is precisely how it answers Śaṃkara.
- **C:** Incorrect: cancellation of one level by another is the Advaita structure of sublation, which Jaina aspect-realism does not employ.
- **D:** Correct: contradiction requires the same respect, time and sense, and the four indices guarantee that this identity never obtains.

**Examiner trap 20:** Without naming substance, place, time and mode the reply to the contradiction charge is an assertion rather than an argument, and examiners mark exactly that difference.

#### MCQ 21. The self-refutation objection

The standard self-refutation objection against many-sidedness argues that

A. the doctrine cannot explain how a single soul is reborn while remaining numerically the same individual

B. if every assertion must be qualified then the doctrine itself must be qualified, and it then loses universal force

C. the doctrine presupposes a creator who guarantees that the many aspects of a thing form a coherent whole

D. the seven predications are too few, since the number of aspects in a real thing is admittedly innumerable

**Answer: B.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: personal identity across rebirth is answered by the enduring substance, and it is not what the self-refutation charge targets.
- **B:** Correct: the dilemma is that qualification appears to weaken the thesis while non-qualification appears to violate its own rule.
- **C:** Incorrect: no critic derives a creator from many-sidedness, and Jainism denies a creator on independent grounds.
- **D:** Incorrect: the number seven is derived combinatorially from three primitives and does not claim to enumerate aspects.

**Examiner trap 21:** The Jaina reply accepts the first horn rather than escaping the dilemma: the thesis is itself asserted conditionally, and that obedience is offered as evidence of consistency.

#### MCQ 22. The 2022 absolutism charge

Which is the strongest Jaina reply to the claim that non-absolutism cannot be sustained without postulating absolutism?

A. Non-absolutism denies one-sided exclusivism by finite knowers, while omniscience remains the internal case of complete knowledge

B. Non-absolutism is a purely practical maxim of debate and therefore makes no claim about reality that could be absolute

C. Non-absolutism is asserted absolutely, and the resulting exception is accepted as the necessary cost of any general theory

D. Non-absolutism concerns only religious language, so the objection misapplies a logical test to a devotional context

**Answer: A.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Correct: the charge equivocates between denying exclusivism and denying comprehensiveness, and separating them dissolves the alleged contradiction.
- **B:** Incorrect: reducing the doctrine to a debating maxim abandons its ontological content and concedes the triviality charge instead.
- **C:** Incorrect: claiming an open exception is exactly what the objection alleges and would confirm rather than answer it.
- **D:** Incorrect: many-sidedness is asserted of reality generally, so restricting it to religious language misdescribes the doctrine.

**Examiner trap 22:** The reply does not eliminate the unconditioned; it relocates it to omniscience, and a critical answer should record that residue rather than declare a clean victory.

#### MCQ 23. What is distinctive about Jaina action-matter

Which statement best captures the Jaina conception of action and moral consequence (*karma*)?

A. It is an unseen quality residing in the self that produces its result at the appointed time without material mediation

B. It is subtle matter that flows toward the soul and adheres to it, with passion determining the depth and duration of that adhesion

C. It is a mental impression left by volition, so that continuity of character is secured without any enduring bearer

D. It is an unseen potency generated by ritual action alone, which links the act performed to the result eventually enjoyed

**Answer: B.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: an unseen quality of the self is the Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika *adṛṣṭa*, which Jainism replaces with genuinely material particles.
- **B:** Correct: action-matter is a form of matter, and the pairing of activity with passion fixes what is bound and for how long.
- **C:** Incorrect: a mental impression without a bearer is the Buddhist *vāsanā*, which cannot supply Jaina material bondage.
- **D:** Incorrect: a ritual potency is the Mīmāṃsā *apūrva*, and it is neither material nor tied to the passions in the Jaina manner.

**Examiner trap 23:** Writing "karma" without "material" loses the single thesis that makes the Jaina account distinctive among Indian theories of moral consequence.

#### MCQ 24. Two forms of bondage

Which pair correctly distinguishes the two forms of bondage recognised by the Jainas?

A. Psychic bondage is the soul's own passionate modification, while material bondage is the actual adhesion of karmic particles

B. Psychic bondage is bondage to mental objects, while material bondage is bondage to physical objects such as wealth and body

C. Psychic bondage occurs in ascetics who retain subtle pride, while material bondage occurs only in householders

D. Psychic bondage is the intention behind an act, while material bondage is the external act as observed by others

**Answer: A.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Correct: *bhāvabandha* is a modification of the soul and *dravyabandha* is the material adhesion it attracts, and the two are distinct yet interlinked.
- **B:** Incorrect: the distinction is not between kinds of object but between the soul's state and the matter that adheres to it.
- **C:** Incorrect: both forms operate for any bound soul; the ascetic-householder difference concerns the scope of vows, not the kinds of bondage.
- **D:** Incorrect: reducing the pair to intention and outward act loses the ontological claim that karmic matter really adheres.

**Examiner trap 24:** Collapsing the two forms costs either the moral psychology or the ontology; the 2024 paper asks for the distinction precisely because it carries both.

#### REMEDIAL DRILLS — 8 ERROR-TARGETING ITEMS

#### MCQ 25. The five causes of bondage

Which set correctly gives the five causes of bondage recognised in Jaina soteriology?

A. Ignorance, craving, clinging, becoming and birth

B. Influx, bondage, stoppage, shedding and liberation

C. Wrong vision, non-restraint, carelessness, passions and activity

D. Anger, pride, deceit, greed and delusion

**Answer: C.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: this is a Buddhist chain of dependent origination and has no place in a Jaina list of causes of bondage.
- **B:** Incorrect: these are stages of the soteriological sequence, four of which are remedies or outcomes rather than causes.
- **C:** Correct: wrong vision, non-restraint, carelessness, passion and activity are the standard five, with passion supplying adhesion and activity the channel.
- **D:** Incorrect: anger, pride, deceit and greed are the passions, so this option expands one cause into the whole list.

**Examiner trap 25:** The passions are one member of the list, not the list itself; a script that gives four passions plus delusion has answered a different question.

#### MCQ 26. What determines what in bondage

Which pairing of a bondage parameter with its principal determinant is correct?

A. Duration is determined by activity, while type is determined by passion

B. Intensity is determined by activity, while quantity is determined by passion

C. Type is determined by wrong vision, while duration is determined by carelessness

D. Type and quantity are determined by activity, while duration and intensity are determined by passion

**Answer: D.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: it reverses the division of labour, and duration is the parameter most clearly governed by the passions.
- **B:** Incorrect: it also reverses the pairing, since intensity of fruition tracks passion and quantity of matter tracks activity.
- **C:** Incorrect: wrong vision and carelessness are causes of bondage, not determinants of these four technical parameters.
- **D:** Correct: activity fixes which karma and how much, while passion fixes how long and how strongly, which is why passion-free activity binds only momentarily.

**Examiner trap 26:** This pairing is the technical basis of the contrast between momentary and durable bondage; reversing it silently destroys the Jaina ethics of intention.

#### MCQ 27. Destructive and non-destructive karmas

Which set contains only destructive (*ghātiyā*) karmas?

A. Vedanīya, nāma, gotra and āyus

B. Jñānāvaraṇīya, darśanāvaraṇīya, vedanīya and gotra

C. Jñānāvaraṇīya, darśanāvaraṇīya, mohanīya and antarāya

D. Mohanīya, antarāya, nāma and āyus

**Answer: C.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: all four members are non-destructive, since they determine felt experience, body, status and lifespan without obscuring essential qualities.
- **B:** Incorrect: it mixes two knowledge- and perception-obscuring karmas with two that merely structure embodiment.
- **C:** Correct: these four obstruct knowledge, perception, right orientation and power, which is why their destruction yields omniscience at the thirteenth stage.
- **D:** Incorrect: it pairs two genuinely destructive karmas with two that determine body and lifespan.

**Examiner trap 27:** Deluding karma is the most dangerous of the destructive group because it corrupts both right vision and right conduct; omitting it from the destructive set is a substantive error.

#### MCQ 28. Seven principles and nine categories

The nine categories (*padārtha*) of Jaina exposition consist of

A. the seven principles together with substance and mode, which supply the ontological framework

B. the seven principles together with merit and demerit, which make moral valence explicit

C. the six substances together with influx, bondage and liberation, which complete the cosmology

D. the seven principles together with right knowledge and right conduct, which complete the path

**Answer: B.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: substance and mode belong to the general theory of the real and are not added to the sevenfold list.
- **B:** Correct: merit and demerit are added to the seven principles, and they can also be subsumed under influx and bondage.
- **C:** Incorrect: the six substances are a different enumeration and are not combined with three members of the sevenfold list.
- **D:** Incorrect: right knowledge and right conduct belong to the three jewels and are not counted among the categories.

**Examiner trap 28:** Merit is still bondage; treating it as a stage on the way to liberation confuses a better rebirth with release from rebirth altogether.

#### MCQ 29. Stoppage and shedding

Which statement correctly distinguishes stoppage (*saṃvara*) and shedding (*nirjarā*)?

A. Stoppage removes karma already bound, while shedding prevents new karma from being bound at all

B. Stoppage belongs to householders and shedding to ascetics, the two being stages of a single vow

C. Stoppage and shedding are alternative names for the same disciplinary process, differing only by tradition

D. Stoppage blocks fresh influx of karmic matter, while shedding wears away karma already accumulated

**Answer: D.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: it exactly reverses the two functions, which is the most frequent error in answers on the soteriological sequence.
- **B:** Incorrect: both operate at every level of practice, and neither is restricted by monastic status.
- **C:** Incorrect: the sequence lists them as distinct stages precisely because bondage has two independent problems, arrival and accumulation.
- **D:** Correct: stoppage is preventive and shedding is eliminative, which is why vows and austerity are both required for liberation.

**Examiner trap 29:** Austerity accelerates shedding but does not replace stoppage; sweeping a room whose door stands open is the image the tradition itself invites.

#### MCQ 30. The eleventh stage of spiritual development

With reference to the fourteen stages, which statement is correct?

A. The eleventh stage destroys delusion permanently and therefore leads directly to the twelfth and thirteenth stages

B. The eleventh stage only suppresses delusion, so a soul reaching it by that ladder necessarily falls back

C. The eleventh stage marks the attainment of right vision for the first time and is the decisive breakthrough

D. The eleventh stage is the momentary activity-free condition immediately preceding the perfected state

**Answer: B.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: destruction of delusion is the twelfth stage, reached by the ladder of annihilation rather than by suppression.
- **B:** Correct: suppressed delusion is covered rather than destroyed, so the ladder of suppression cannot hold the eleventh stage.
- **C:** Incorrect: the first stable attainment of right vision is the fourth stage, which is the decisive breakthrough of the ascent.
- **D:** Incorrect: the momentary activity-free condition is the fourteenth stage, immediately before the perfected state.

**Examiner trap 30:** Suppression and destruction look identical from outside and have opposite futures; the two ladders exist to explain spiritual relapse without treating it as failure of insight.

#### MCQ 31. Omniscience while embodied

The condition described as *sayoga-kevalī* is best characterised as

A. a soul that has suppressed all passions and awaits the fruition of its deluding karma before liberation

B. a householder who has taken the lesser vows and attained limited clairvoyant knowledge of material things

C. an omniscient being still embodied and active, whose destructive karmas are destroyed while non-destructive karmas remain

D. a perfected soul at the summit of the world, disembodied and free from every trace of karmic matter

**Answer: C.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Incorrect: mere suppression belongs to the eleventh stage and is incompatible with the omniscience that defines this condition.
- **B:** Incorrect: lesser vows and clairvoyance belong far lower on the ladder and involve no destruction of the obscuring karmas.
- **C:** Correct: destruction of the destructive karmas yields omniscience, while the remaining non-destructive karmas sustain embodiment and activity.
- **D:** Incorrect: the disembodied perfected soul is the *siddha*, which follows only after the fourteenth stage.

**Examiner trap 31:** This is the Jaina form of liberation-in-life; denying that omniscience can coexist with embodiment misreads the destructive and non-destructive division of karmas.

#### MCQ 32. Voluntary passionless death

Which statement about *sallekhanā* is doctrinally the safest to write in an examination?

A. It is a gradual, supervised and passion-free relinquishment of food undertaken when death is already unavoidable or decline incurable

B. It is a meritorious act of self-destruction that may be undertaken by any practitioner who wishes to shorten a difficult life

C. It has been conclusively settled in Indian law as a protected religious practice, so its legal status raises no further question

D. It is identical with suicide in every philosophically relevant respect, differing only in the vocabulary used to describe it

**Answer: A.**

**Option explanations:**
- **A:** Correct: the vow requires equanimity, supervision, gradualness and the absence of any desire for death or for continued life.
- **B:** Incorrect: a wish to shorten life is itself a passion and converts the observance into a transgression of the vow's own discipline.
- **C:** Incorrect: legal holdings must not be asserted without verification, and an examination answer should report the controversy rather than settle it.
- **D:** Incorrect: suicide is sudden, passion-driven and often concealed, whereas the vow is gradual, passionless, non-violent and public.

**Examiner trap 32:** The doctrinal test is the inner state, not the outward abstention; if desire for death, attachment or hope of reward enters, the act becomes a transgression rather than a fulfilment of the vow.

#### VERIFIED PREVIOUS-YEAR QUESTIONS — TEN COMPLETE SOLUTIONS

#### PYQ 1 · 2018 · Q6(c) · 15 marks · Jainism

> **Question, as printed:** How do the Jaina philosophers explain 'bondage'? What, according to them, is the distinction between 'liberated soul' and 'bound soul'? What do the Jainas think about the condition of the 'liberated soul'? Discuss.

**Demand decoded.** Three explicit demands under a "Discuss" directive: the mechanism of bondage, the contrast between two classes of soul, and the positive condition of the liberated soul. Answer all three in order; the third is the one most scripts omit.

**Model answer (15 marks, 250–300 words).**

Jaina bondage is a real material event, not a moral metaphor. The soul is intrinsically endowed with infinite knowledge, perception, bliss and power, yet in embodied life these stand obscured. That obscuration requires a binding factor, and Jainism identifies it as action-matter (*karma-pudgala*), a subtle form of matter drawn to the soul.

The mechanism runs through five causes: wrong vision, non-restraint, carelessness, passions and activity. Activity of body, speech and mind opens the channel of influx (*āsrava*) and determines the type and quantity of karma bound; passion supplies the adhesion and determines its duration and intensity. Bondage therefore has two faces: psychic bondage (*bhāvabandha*), the soul's own passionate modification, and material bondage (*dravyabandha*), the actual adhesion of karmic particles. The relation is beginningless (*anādi*) but not endless.

The bound soul (*baddha-jīva*) is thus an embodied, karmically conditioned knower whose native capacities are veiled and whose body, status, lifespan and felt experience are determined by the non-destructive karmas. The liberated soul (*mukta-jīva*) is one from which stoppage (*saṃvara*) has excluded fresh influx and shedding (*nirjarā*) has removed the accumulated deposit, so that nothing foreign remains.

On its condition, the Jainas are precise. The liberated soul is a perfected soul (*siddha*): disembodied, activity-free, beyond rebirth, and manifesting the infinite tetrad in full. Crucially it retains numerical individuality; there is no merger into a universal consciousness as in Advaita, and no dissolution as in accounts of no-self. Being unweighted by karmic matter it rises to the summit of the world (*siddha-śilā*).

The account is internally coherent, though the interaction of formless soul and subtle matter remains its contested joint.

#### PYQ 2 · 2019 · Q6(a) · 20 marks · Jainism

> **Question, as printed:** How is reality defined by the Jainas? How is this theory of reality reflected in their view on judgements? Discuss.

**Demand decoded.** The hinge word is "reflected". The examiner wants a derivation, not two separate essays. Define reality first; then show that the theory of judgement follows from that definition rather than accompanying it.

**Model answer (20 marks, 340–400 words).**

The Jainas define the real (*sat*) as that which possesses origination (*utpāda*), decay (*vyaya*) and persistence (*dhrauvya*) together. The definition is chosen to save two facts that rival definitions sacrifice. Pure permanence cannot explain that a bracelet became a ring; pure flux cannot explain why this is recognised as the same gold. Indexing permanence to substance (*dravya*) and change to mode (*paryāya*), with quality (*guṇa*) as the abiding characteristic, secures both.

From this follows many-sidedness (*anekāntavāda*): the real is *ananta-dharmātmakaṃ vastu*, a thing constituted by innumerable aspects. Its ontology is pluralist — six substances, of which five are extended, and innumerable souls — and its causation is correspondingly qualified, the effect being continuous with the cause as substance and novel as mode.

The reflection in judgement is strict rather than analogical. Ordinary cognition is *kṣāyopaśamika*, arising from the partial destruction-cum-subsidence of knowledge-obscuring karma; it is therefore necessarily partial. A judgement by a non-omniscient knower captures an aspect, not the whole. That yields standpoint theory (*nayavāda*), and the tradition classifies seven standpoints, running from the mixed practical view through the generic, the particular and the present-state view to the three increasingly literal verbal standpoints. A standpoint that denies the remaining aspects ceases to be a standpoint and becomes its perversion (*nayābhāsa*), which is the technical form of one-sidedness (*ekāntavāda*).

Because judgement is standpoint-bound, assertion must carry the operator *syāt*: conditional predication (*syādvāda*). Combining affirmation, negation and inexpressibility under that operator, taken singly, pairwise and jointly, yields exactly seven predications (*saptabhaṅgī*) — three plus three plus one. Opposed predications are indexed to substance, place, time and mode, so existence is asserted in own-form and non-existence in other-form; contradiction would require identity of respect, which the operator excludes.

Thus the theory of judgement is not an ornament on the theory of reality but its epistemic consequence: because the object exceeds any single finite judgement, and because the knower is karmically partial, unconditioned assertion is available only to omniscience (*kevala-jñāna*).

The derivation is powerful, though Śaṃkara's residual objection — that a doctrine which qualifies even itself weakens its assertoric force — is not fully removed.

#### PYQ 3 · 2020 · Q5(a) · 10 marks · Jainism

> **Question, as printed:** Examine the concept of Karma according to Jainism. How does it bear upon their conception of Liberation?

**Demand decoded.** "Examine" asks for accurate componential analysis, and the second sentence demands the causal link. A definition without the bearing scores half.

**Model answer (10 marks, 150–200 words).**

For Jainism, action and moral consequence is not an unseen quality of the self, as in Nyāya *adṛṣṭa*, nor a ritual potency as in Mīmāṃsā *apūrva*, nor a mental impression as in Buddhist *vāsanā*. It is subtle matter (*karma-pudgala*) that flows toward the soul and adheres to it. Psychical action-matter (*bhāva-karma*), the soul's passionate modification, attracts material action-matter (*dravya-karma*).

Adhesion is analysed under four parameters: activity fixes the type and quantity bound, while passion fixes the duration and intensity. Eight karma-types divide into four destructive, which obscure knowledge, perception, right orientation and power, and four non-destructive, which determine felt experience, body, status and lifespan.

The bearing on liberation (*mokṣa*) is direct. Since bondage is a real deposit, liberation is subtraction and not acquisition: stoppage (*saṃvara*) closes the channel and shedding (*nirjarā*) removes the accumulation. Destroying the destructive karmas yields omniscience while still embodied; exhausting the non-destructive karmas yields the perfected soul.

Liberation therefore reveals what the soul always was, rather than conferring anything new.

#### PYQ 4 · 2021 · Q7(c) · 15 marks · Jainism

> **Question, as printed:** Explain the Jaina view of seven-fold (sapta-bhaṅgī) 'Naya'.

**Demand decoded.** The printed wording fuses two distinct doctrines — the sevenfold predication and standpoint theory. A safe answer explains **both** and expressly distinguishes them, rather than assuming the fusion is doctrinally correct.

**Model answer (15 marks, 250–300 words).**

The printed phrase joins two doctrines that Jaina authors keep apart, so both must be explained. Standpoint theory (*nayavāda*) concerns perspectives; the sevenfold predication (*saptabhaṅgī*) concerns propositional forms.

Standpoint theory holds that every ordinary judgement expresses a *naya*, a partial but legitimate view of a many-sided real. The classical seven are: the mixed practical or purposive standpoint (*naigama*); the generic standpoint (*saṃgraha*); the practical particular standpoint (*vyavahāra*); the present-state standpoint (*ṛjusūtra*); the verbal standpoint (*śabda*); the etymological standpoint (*samabhirūḍha*); and the function-in-act standpoint (*evambhūta*), on which a person counts as a singer only while singing. The list narrows from loosest to strictest; the first three are substance-directed, the last three mode-directed and verbal, with the present-state view as the hinge.

The sevenfold predication is generated differently. Three primitives — affirmation, negation and inexpressibility — taken singly, in pairs and jointly yield exactly seven forms: *syād asti*; *syān nāsti*; *syād asti ca nāsti ca*; *syād avaktavyam*; *syād asti ca avaktavyam*; *syān nāsti ca avaktavyam*; and *syād asti ca nāsti ca avaktavyam*. Each is governed by the operator *syāt*, which states the respect and does not mean "perhaps".

The two are related but not identical: standpoints supply the respects; predications express them in disciplined sentential form. Contradiction is avoided because opposed predications are indexed to different substance, place, time or mode. Inexpressibility marks a limit of simultaneous unqualified expression, not a third truth-value.

The doctrine is therefore a theory of conditioned assertion rather than of doubt, though critics from Śaṃkara onward hold that permanent qualification dilutes assertoric force.

#### PYQ 5 · 2022 · Q5(d) · 10 marks · Jainism

> **Question, as printed:** How does Jaina view of Karma bear upon their soteriology? Critically discuss.

**Demand decoded.** Same subject-matter as 2020 Q5(a), but the directive adds "critically": the answer must end with an evaluation, not a description.

**Model answer (10 marks, 150–200 words).**

Because Jaina action-matter is literally material, soteriology becomes a physics of removal rather than a programme of moral improvement. Activity opens the influx channel and fixes what and how much is bound; passion fixes how long and how strongly it fruits. Hence passion-free activity produces only momentary bondage (*īryāpathika*), whereas passion-coloured activity produces durable bondage (*sāmparāyika*).

Two remedies follow, because bondage has two problems. Stoppage (*saṃvara*), operated through the vows, restraint, carefulness, the virtues, contemplations and endurance of afflictions, blocks fresh influx. Shedding (*nirjarā*), operated chiefly through austerity, exhausts what is already bound. Merit does not substitute for either, since even good karma keeps the soul in the cycle of rebirth.

Critically, the doctrine gains real explanatory precision: it grounds an ethics of intention in a causal mechanism, and it explains graded spiritual progress. Its cost is equally real. Nyāya presses that material karma cannot intelligibly bind a formless consciousness, and a Cārvāka denies the whole apparatus as unverifiable.

The account is therefore coherent within Jaina ontology, not demonstrable outside it.

#### PYQ 6 · 2022 · Q6(b) · 15 marks · Jainism

> **Question, as printed:** 'The doctrine of Relativism of Jain Philosophy cannot be logically sustained without postulating 'Absolutism'.' Critically examine this view and give reasons in the favour of your answer.

**Demand decoded.** A stated view must be engaged, not paraphrased. Locate the equivocation, give the Jaina reply, then take a reasoned position and state what survives.

**Model answer (15 marks, 250–300 words).**

The quoted view rests on an equivocation between two senses of "absolute". Jaina non-absolutism (*anekāntavāda*) denies **one-sided exclusivism** by finite knowers; it does not deny that comprehensive knowledge is possible. Once the two are separated, the alleged self-contradiction dissolves.

The charge runs: the doctrine claims universal scope; universal scope appears absolute; therefore the doctrine covertly relies on what it attacks. The Jaina reply is threefold. First, the doctrine is itself asserted *syāt*, so it obeys its own rule; reflexive qualification exhibits the discipline it teaches rather than exempting itself from it. Second, the thesis is not that all judgements are equally true but that truth is aspect-conditioned, so judgements remain gradable by whether they respect their own conditions. Third, within the system omniscience (*kevala-jñāna*) is the internal case of complete knowledge, available when knowledge-obscuring karma is destroyed; ordinary discourse remains standpoint-bound because ordinary cognition is *kṣāyopaśamika*.

It should also be said that "relativism" mislabels the position. Jainism is ontologically realist: the aspects are objectively in the thing, and only finite access to them is partial. Relativism relativises truth; non-absolutism relativises exclusive claims.

Critically, the reply succeeds against the charge as printed but does not leave the field untouched. The unconditioned status has been relocated rather than abolished: omniscience now carries the completeness that finite discourse is denied. A critic may say this concedes the substance of the objection at a higher level.

My position is therefore qualified agreement with the Jaina: the doctrine is logically sustainable and not self-refuting, but it is not standpoint-free, and honesty requires saying that its consistency is purchased by admitting one privileged cognitive state.

#### PYQ 7 · 2023 · Q5(a) · 10 marks · Jainism

> **Question, as printed:** "All human knowledge is empirical and therefore relative." Critically examine Jaina theory of sevenfold judgement (saptabhaṅgīnaya) in the light of above statement.

**Demand decoded.** The quoted proposition is an empiricist thesis. The task is to test whether the sevenfold judgement is an instance of it, and to show precisely where Jainism agrees and where it refuses.

**Model answer (10 marks, 150–200 words).**

Jainism agrees with half of the statement and rejects the other half. It agrees that human knowledge is limited: ordinary cognition is *kṣāyopaśamika*, partial because knowledge-obscuring karma is only partly removed, and ordinary perception is only conventionally direct.

It refuses the inference to relativism. The sevenfold judgement — the seven forms generated by affirmation, negation and inexpressibility under the operator *syāt* — records the **respects** in which a predication holds, using the indices of substance, place, time and mode. Each form is a definite assertion, not a probability estimate; *syāt* does not mean "perhaps".

Critically, the difference from empiricist relativism is ontological. For the empiricist, relativity attaches to knowledge because there is no access beyond experience. For Jainism, relativity attaches to finite judgement while the aspects remain objectively in the object, and omniscience remains internally possible.

Hence the statement describes a Jaina premise, not a Jaina conclusion: partiality of knowing does not entail relativity of truth.

#### PYQ 8 · 2024 · Q6(c) · 15 marks · Jainism

> **Question, as printed:** What is the distinction between Bhāvabandha and Dravyabandha, according to the Jainas? Discuss.

**Demand decoded.** A single precise distinction, discussed. The answer must state both terms exactly, show their causal order, and explain why Jainism refuses to collapse them.

**Model answer (15 marks, 250–300 words).**

Jaina bondage has two forms which are distinct in nature yet inseparable in operation.

**Psychic bondage (bhāvabandha)** is a modification of the soul itself. Through passion, attachment, aversion, delusion and wrong disposition the soul undergoes an affective-volitional deformation. This is a state of the conscious substance, describable in the vocabulary of moral psychology.

**Material bondage (dravyabandha)** is the actual adhesion of karmic particles. Because matter alone possesses form, and because the embodied soul has space-points and is co-extensive with its body, subtle matter can be attracted and fixed. This is an event in the ontology of substances, not a description of an attitude.

The causal order is fixed: psychical action-matter (*bhāva-karma*) attracts material action-matter (*dravya-karma*). Inner deformation precedes and shapes outer adhesion. This is why the four parameters divide as they do — activity determines the type and quantity of matter bound, whereas passion, which is a psychic condition, determines duration and intensity. A monk's careful walking binds momentarily; an angry act binds deeply.

Jainism refuses to collapse the pair because each carries something the other cannot. Retain only psychic bondage and karma becomes a metaphor, leaving the obscuration of an intrinsically omniscient soul unexplained. Retain only material bondage and the system becomes crude physicalism in which intention is irrelevant to moral consequence.

The distinction also organises the path: stoppage works primarily on the psychic side by removing the causes of influx, while shedding works on the material deposit already formed.

Critically, the pairing preserves both agency and ontology, though the interaction of a formless soul with subtle matter — the very point Nyāya attacks — remains the doctrine's unresolved joint.

#### PYQ 9 · 2025 · Q7(c) · 15 marks · Jainism

> **Question, as printed:** Is Jaina philosophy pluralistic and realistic? Critically discuss.

**Demand decoded.** Two labels, both to be tested. "Critically" requires that each be affirmed with the correct qualifier rather than simply endorsed.

**Model answer (15 marks, 250–300 words).**

Jaina philosophy is strongly pluralistic and realistic, but its realism must be described as qualified.

It is pluralistic in three independent ways. Ontologically it admits six substances — soul, matter, medium of motion, medium of rest, space and time — of which five are extended. Psychologically it admits innumerable souls, each beginningless and numerically distinct. Soteriologically it holds that liberation produces many perfected souls at the summit of the world, with no merger into any absolute. Monistic reduction is refused at every level.

It is realistic in that substances, qualities and modes are objectively real; the external world is not appearance; and knowledge aims at real objects. Aspects are in the thing, not projected onto it, which is why many-sidedness (*anekāntavāda*) is an ontological thesis before it is a logical one.

The qualifier is epistemic. Because finite cognition is *kṣāyopaśamika* and therefore partial, judgement is standpoint-indexed and assertion carries the operator *syāt*. This yields the decisive formula: qualification of **knowledge** does not entail unreality of the **object**. Ontological realism and epistemic non-absolutism are separable, and Jainism holds both.

Critically, two costs deserve statement. First, Rāmānuja objects that a real substance bearing opposed real attributes is unintelligible, and a Dharmakīrti-style Buddhist presses that a permanent-yet-changing soul fails the test of causal efficacy; the Jaina reply, that the opposition is between aspects of substance and mode, persuades only those who already grant aspect-realism. Second, the pluralism is metaphysically expensive, requiring material action-matter and intrinsically omniscient souls that a Cārvāka will reject as unverifiable.

The defensible verdict is therefore that Jainism is genuinely pluralistic and genuinely realistic, but its realism is qualified realism rather than naive realism.

#### PYQ 10 · 2020 · Q7(c) · 15 marks · Cārvāka (supporting routed question)

> **Question, as printed:** Explain the differences of conception of Reality between Cārvāka and Jainism.
> **Routing note:** primary ownership rests with Cārvāka; it is solved here because the Jaina half of the contrast is owned by this topic and is the half most often written badly.

**Demand decoded.** "Explain the differences" is comparative and expository. Use fixed axes so that the contrast is systematic rather than anecdotal.

**Model answer (15 marks, 250–300 words).**

Cārvāka and Jainism differ on the definition of the real, on its inventory and on the reach of knowledge.

**Definition.** Cārvāka recognises as real only what is materially constituted and, in principle, perceptible. Jainism defines the real as that which possesses origination, decay and persistence together, so permanence and change are equally real in one thing, indexed to substance and mode.

**Inventory.** Cārvāka admits four elements — earth, water, fire and air — and characteristically rejects ether. Jainism admits six substances, five of them extended, including a conscious substance and two cosmological media that Cārvāka has no use for. Where Cārvāka has one kind of stuff, Jainism has an irreducibly plural ontology.

**Consciousness.** For Cārvāka, awareness emerges from the organised body, so the living body qualified by consciousness is the self. For Jainism, consciousness is the defining quality of an independent soul, and there are innumerable such souls, intrinsically endowed with infinite knowledge that karma obscures.

**Moral consequence and liberation.** Cārvāka rejects action-consequence, rebirth and liberation. Jainism makes action-matter literally material, so bondage is a real deposit and liberation is its removal.

**Knowledge.** Cārvāka restricts valid knowledge to perception and refuses inference independent standing. Jainism accepts inference and testimony as indirect, treats ordinary perception as only conventionally direct, and grades knowledge up to omniscience.

The deepest difference is methodological. Cārvāka's ontology contracts to fit one means of knowledge; Jainism's theory of knowledge expands to fit a many-sided reality. One shrinks the world to secure certainty; the other qualifies assertion to preserve the world's complexity.

#### ORIGINAL SOLVED MAINS PRACTICE — SIX MODELS

> These six questions are original practice items written in UPSC style. They are not previous-year questions and are not represented as such.

#### Original 1 · 10 marks

> **Question:** Distinguish between many-sidedness (*anekāntavāda*), standpoint theory (*nayavāda*) and conditional predication (*syādvāda*). Why is the order of derivation philosophically significant?

**Model answer (10 marks, 150–200 words).**

The three doctrines occupy different levels and are derived in a fixed order.

Many-sidedness is **metaphysical**: the real object is *ananta-dharmātmakaṃ vastu*, constituted by innumerable aspects, so no single unqualified proposition exhausts it. Standpoint theory is **epistemic**: because finite cognition is partial, every ordinary judgement expresses a *naya*, one legitimate perspective among many; a standpoint that denies the rest becomes its perversion. Conditional predication is **logical-linguistic**: assertion must carry *syāt*, stating the respect in which it holds, and the sevenfold predication is its formal schema.

The order matters for two reasons. First, it makes the logic non-arbitrary. If many-sidedness came last, the *syāt* operator would look like a debating trick; derived first, it makes qualification obligatory. Second, it protects realism. The aspects are in the object; only access is partial. Reversing the order would make Jainism a theory about language or attitude rather than about the world.

Hence a script that names the three terms but not their levels has recited without explaining.

#### Original 2 · 10 marks

> **Question:** "In Jainism the moral quality of an act is fixed by passion rather than by activity." Examine this claim with reference to the parameters of bondage.

**Model answer (10 marks, 150–200 words).**

The claim is accurate if stated with care. Karmic bondage is analysed under four parameters. Activity of body, speech and mind determines the **type** (*prakṛti*) of karma bound and the **quantity** (*pradeśa*) of matter bound. Passion determines the **duration** (*sthiti*) and the **intensity** (*anubhāga*) of its fruition.

Since duration and intensity are the parameters that actually decide how heavily an act weighs on the future of the soul, the moral seriousness of an act tracks passion rather than mere movement. The doctrinal expression of this is the contrast between momentary bondage from passion-free activity and durable bondage from passion-coloured activity: a monk's careful walking binds only momentarily, while an angry act binds deeply.

The claim must nevertheless be qualified. Activity is not morally irrelevant, since it fixes what is bound at all, and total cessation of activity belongs only to the fourteenth stage.

The precise formulation is therefore that activity determines the karmic content while passion determines its karmic weight.

#### Original 3 · 15 marks

> **Question:** Examine the Jaina claim that ordinary sense-perception is only conventionally direct. What does this reveal about the relation between Jaina epistemology and Jaina soteriology?

**Model answer (15 marks, 250–300 words).**

Jainism divides valid knowledge into direct (*pratyakṣa*) and indirect (*parokṣa*), but measures directness by absence of mediation rather than by sensory vividness. Ordinary sense-perception depends on sense-organs, the mind, signs and karmic limitation. It is therefore only **conventional direct knowledge** (*sāṃvyavahārika pratyakṣa*) and strictly belongs with mediated cognition. What is direct in the full sense is knowledge the soul has by itself: limited clairvoyance (*avadhi*), knowledge of others' mental modes (*manaḥparyāya*) and omniscience (*kevala*).

The claim is defensible on its own terms. It preserves ordinary life, since perception remains valid for practical dealings; it distinguishes practical adequacy from metaphysical finality; and it avoids the Nyāya assumption that sense-contact is the paradigm of directness.

What it reveals is that Jaina epistemology is not a free-standing theory. The five kinds of knowledge are stations on a single curve of karmic unveiling: *mati* and *śruta* are mediated, the remaining three progressively direct, and omniscience is the state in which knowledge-obscuring karma has been destroyed. Knowledge is therefore graded by **purity**, not by method.

Two consequences follow. First, conditional predication becomes obligatory rather than optional, because a *kṣāyopaśamika* knower cannot make an unconditioned claim. Second, wrong cognition is orientation-relative: *kumati*, *kuśruta* and *vibhaṅga* are cognitions held under wrong vision, so error is a spiritual as well as a logical category.

Critically, this integration is the system's strength and its exposure. It gives Jainism a unified account of knowing and liberation, but it makes the epistemology depend on an ontology of soul and karmic matter that an empiricist critic will refuse at the outset.

#### Original 4 · 15 marks

> **Question:** "Jaina non-violence is a metaphysical consequence, not a sentiment." Critically discuss with reference to the doctrine of the soul and the mechanics of bondage.

**Model answer (15 marks, 250–300 words).**

The claim is well founded. Jaina non-violence (*ahiṃsā*) is derived, not asserted.

The derivation begins in ontology. Souls are innumerable and graded from one-sense immobile beings — earth-, water-, fire-, air- and plant-bodied — to five-sense mobile beings. Every one of them is a conscious substance intrinsically endowed with the infinite tetrad and currently obscured. Injury therefore falls on a knower, not on a thing.

It continues in the mechanics of bondage. Injury is characteristically produced by passion, and passion fixes the duration and intensity of karmic adhesion. Violence is thus not merely wrong by a rule; it is causally the most efficient way to deepen bondage. Non-violence, conversely, is an operation of stoppage: it closes the channel through which the heaviest karma enters.

The doctrine is graded rather than utopian. Ascetics take the great vows; householders take the lesser vows; and carefulness in movement, speech, alms-seeking, handling objects and bodily functions extends the principle into ordinary conduct. Extreme care toward one-sense beings is an implication of the ontology, not an excess of feeling.

Critically, two points deserve statement. First, the derivation is only as strong as its premises: a critic who denies that plants or elemental bodies house souls will accept the ethics while rejecting its scope. Second, the account is intention-sensitive rather than consequence-sensitive, which is a philosophical choice with costs of its own.

Nevertheless, the central contention holds: Jaina non-violence follows from plural souls and karmic mechanics, and treating it as sentiment removes precisely the argument that makes it distinctive among Indian ethical positions.

#### Original 5 · 20 marks

> **Question:** Analyse the Jaina fourteen stages of spiritual development. How do the two ladders explain spiritual relapse, and what does this show about the relation between insight and conduct in Jainism?

**Model answer (20 marks, 340–400 words).**

The fourteen stages (*guṇasthāna*) map the soul's movement from false vision to final liberation, and they are graded by karmic condition rather than by external observance.

Stages one to three describe wrong, falling and mixed orientation. Stage four, right vision without restraint, is the decisive breakthrough: right vision (*samyag-darśana*) becomes stable enough to orient the path even though conduct has not yet followed. Stage five introduces partial restraint through lay vows. Stages six and seven mark full restraint with and then without negligence. Stages eight to ten describe progressive purification of the passions, from unprecedented purification through advanced purification to the condition in which only subtle greed remains. Stages eleven and twelve mark the crucial difference between suppression and destruction of delusion. Stage thirteen is the embodied omniscient being, and stage fourteen the momentary activity-free condition immediately preceding the perfected state.

The two ladders explain relapse without treating it as mere failure. On the ladder of suppression (*upaśama-śreṇi*), delusion is covered rather than removed; such a soul may reach the eleventh stage, but because suppressed delusion retains its potency the fall is necessary, not accidental. On the ladder of annihilation (*kṣapaka-śreṇi*), delusion is destroyed, so the twelfth stage is stable and the ascent continues to omniscience.

Three philosophical consequences follow. First, appearance and condition come apart: two souls may look identical at the tenth stage and have opposite futures, which is why Jainism refuses to identify spiritual attainment with observable conduct alone. Second, insight and conduct are interdependent rather than alternative. Right vision at stage four is genuine yet insufficient; conduct without right vision has no orientation; and the ladder records the joint operation of the three jewels. Third, omniscience is decoupled from disembodiment: destroying the destructive karmas yields omniscience at stage thirteen, while the non-destructive karmas sustain body, status, lifespan and felt experience until the end.

Critically, an Advaita-style objection holds that a fourteen-stage ladder mechanises liberation, substituting a karmic audit for insight. The Jaina reply is that insight which does not destroy karma has not yet done its work, and that the ladder simply records the difference between a covered and a destroyed defilement.

The scheme is therefore best evaluated as a psychology of purification with an unusually honest account of relapse, and its taxonomic weight is the price of that honesty.

#### Original 6 · 20 marks

> **Question:** "Jainism preserves the reality of the world at the cost of the simplicity of logic; Advaita preserves the simplicity of logic at the cost of the reality of the world." Critically examine this comparison.

**Model answer (20 marks, 340–400 words).**

The comparison is illuminating but requires correction on both sides.

On the Jaina side it is broadly accurate. Jainism refuses to treat either permanence or change as appearance. The real is defined as originating, decaying and persisting together, so both are secured — but the price is that predication must be indexed. Assertion carries the operator *syāt*; opposed predications are separated by substance, place, time and mode; and the exhaustive schema of qualified assertion contains seven forms rather than a simple affirmation and denial. Ontological economy is sacrificed too: six substances, innumerable souls and material action-matter are admitted because each performs explanatory work. The world is saved, and logic becomes intricate.

On the Advaita side the statement needs care. Advaita does not abandon the world; it grades it. The world is empirically real and only ultimately *mithyā*, and sublation, not denial, is the operative notion. What Advaita gains is a single ultimate reality with a straightforward account of contradiction: opposed characterisations belong to different levels, and the lower is finally cancelled. So the accurate formulation is that Advaita preserves logical simplicity by admitting **levels of reality**, whereas Jainism preserves ontological plurality by admitting **respects of predication**.

That reformulation locates the real difference. Both schools face the problem of apparently opposed predicates. Advaita separates them **vertically**, by rank, with the lower cancelled at realisation. Jainism separates them **horizontally**, by aspect, with nothing cancelled at all. Vertical separation buys a simple logic and requires a doctrine of appearance; horizontal separation buys an unqualified realism and requires a doctrine of conditional assertion.

Critically, each pays where the other saves. Śaṃkara charges that Jaina qualification erodes assertoric force and that a doctrine which qualifies even itself cannot claim universal scope. The Jaina replies that reflexive qualification is obedience rather than exemption, and counter-charges that treating the world as ultimately *mithyā* purchases simplicity by demoting the very reality it set out to explain. Neither charge is answerable from a neutral standpoint, because each presupposes what the other denies.

The defensible verdict is that the quoted comparison identifies a genuine trade-off but mislabels the Advaita side. Jainism trades logical simplicity for unqualified realism; Advaita trades ontological plurality, not the world itself, for a single unsublated real.

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

> **Placement note.** Everything below is enrichment. A complete, full-marks answer to any verified 2018–2025 Jainism question can be written from the Basic block and the practice sections alone. Use at most one or two lines from this block, and only in the evaluative paragraph of a 15- or 20-marker after the core case is complete.

### 1. Is the sevenfold predication a logic, or a discipline of assertion?

⚠️ **The interpretive issue.** Is the sevenfold schema best read as a proto-many-valued logic, as a modal system, or as context-indexed qualified assertion?

⚠️ **Two readings.**
- *Modern formalising reading:* the seven forms display a sophistication that invites comparison with many-valued or paraconsistent evaluation, and inexpressibility is treated as a distinct semantic value.
- *Classical reading:* the forms are standpoint-conditioned speech disciplined by an ontological many-sidedness; *syāt* is an index of respect, not a truth-value, and inexpressibility marks the failure of a single unqualified utterance rather than a gap in truth.

⚠️ **The strongest extra objection and its counter-reply.** *Objection:* seven predications collapse into contradiction once formalised. *Reply:* the predications differ by standpoint, time, respect or mode, so they are not brute simultaneous contradictories; formalisation must carry the index, and a formalism that drops it has changed the subject.

⚠️ **Answer use.** One line only, in evaluation: "read as a discipline of indexed assertion rather than as a many-valued logic, the schema is consistent, though its formalisation remains debated."

### 2. The spatiality of the soul

⚠️ **The interpretive issue.** How can an immaterial conscious substance fit bodies of different size while remaining numerically the same soul?

⚠️ **Two readings.**
- *Literal ontological reading:* the soul really contracts and expands with embodiment, possessing space-points that redistribute.
- *Restrained reading:* the doctrine marks embodiment-capacity and co-extension without materialising the soul, so "size" is a mode of embodied individuality rather than gross magnitude.

⚠️ **Objection and counter-reply.** *Objection:* a non-material reality should not have size-properties at all. *Reply:* extension here is a mode of embodied individuality, not gross physical magnitude in the materialist sense; the lamp simile is meant to convey pervasion, not volume.

⚠️ **Answer use.** Deploy only if a question asks directly about the soul's spatiality or presses the Nyāya objection; otherwise the Basic block's treatment is sufficient.

### 3. Many-sidedness beside Buddhist anti-essentialism

⚠️ **The interpretive issue.** Does Jaina non-absolutism resemble Madhyamaka emptiness, or is it a distinct realist pluralism?

⚠️ **Two readings.**
- *Jaina reading:* real substances persist through real modes, so non-absolutism operates inside a realist ontology.
- *Madhyamaka foil:* all *dharmas* are empty of own-being, so no substance survives the analysis at all.

⚠️ **Objection and counter-reply.** *Objection:* if every statement is conditional, Jainism secretly requires an absolute standpoint. *Reply:* the Jaina can concede an ontological real while denying that finite judgements exhaust it unconditionally — which is precisely the distinction developed in the Basic block for the 2022 question.

⚠️ **Answer use.** This comparison sharpens an evaluation but must never replace the printed Jaina doctrine; it is also a useful guard against writing "Jainism is like Buddhism" in comparative questions.

### A. System identity

- **Definition:** pluralistic realism with non-absolutist finite predication and a material theory of karmic bondage.
- **Syllabus (verbatim):** *Jainism : Theory of Reality; Saptabhanginaya; Bondage and Liberation.*
- **Textual anchor:** *Tattvārthasūtra* of **Umāsvāti / Umāsvāmī**; authoritative for both major traditions; ❓ shared authority ≠ doctrinal uniformity.
- **Names:** Kundakunda (real / conventional standpoints); Siddhasena Divākara, Samantabhadra (early logic); Akalaṅka, Māṇikyanandi, Hemacandra, Yaśovijaya (mature means-of-knowledge tradition).
- **Titles:** *jina* = conqueror of passions; *tīrthaṅkara* = ford-maker, liberated teacher, **not** creator; twenty-four in the present cycle; Ṛṣabha first, Pārśva twenty-third, Mahāvīra twenty-fourth and a **renewer**, not a founder.
- **Classification:** *nāstika* on the **Veda criterion only**; rebirth, action-consequence and liberation are all affirmed.
- **Non-theism:** *anīśvaravāda* about creation; reverence is for achieved perfection.

### B. The real

- **Formula:** *sat = utpāda + vyaya + dhrauvya* — origination, decay and persistence **together**.
- **Why:** pure permanence cannot explain transformation; pure flux cannot explain identity.
- **Illustration:** gold — bracelet-mode ends, ring-mode begins, gold persists.
- **Many-sidedness (anekāntavāda):** the real is *ananta-dharmātmakaṃ vastu*; ontology **before** logic.
- ⚠️ Not tolerance, not equal truth of all opinions, not indecision.

### C. Substance, quality, mode; the six substances

- **dravya** persists · **guṇa** abides with it · **paryāya** originates and decays.
- **Six substances:** soul (*jīva*), matter (*pudgala*), medium of motion (*dharma*), medium of rest (*adharma*), space (*ākāśa*), time (*kāla*).
- **Five extended substances (astikāya)**; **time is not** an *astikāya*. ❓ Digambara accounts characteristically affirm time-atoms; some Śvetāmbara discussions treat the substantial independence of time as disputed.
- *dharma* / *adharma* here = media of motion and rest, **not** merit and demerit.
- **Causation:** *sadasatkāryavāda* — continuous as substance, novel as mode; between Sāṃkhya *satkāryavāda* and Nyāya *asatkāryavāda*.

### D. The soul

- Innumerable, beginningless, numerically distinct; consciousness (*upayoga*) intrinsic.
- **Infinite tetrad (ananta-catuṣṭaya):** infinite knowledge, perception, bliss, power — obscured, not absent.
- **Taxonomy:** liberated (*mukta*) / bound (*baddha*); immobile one-sense (*sthāvara*: earth-, water-, fire-, air-, plant-bodied) / mobile (*trasa*: two- to five-sense).
- **Spatiality:** has space-points (*pradeśa*), co-extensive with the body, lamp simile; ⚠️ invites the Nyāya objection about a changing eternal.
- **Matter:** only substance with form (*rūpa*); atom (*paramāṇu*), aggregate (*skandha*); **sound is a material modification**, not a quality of ether.

### E. Knowledge

- **Direct (pratyakṣa) / indirect (parokṣa)** — directness = absence of mediation.
- Ordinary perception = **sāṃvyavahārika pratyakṣa**, conventionally direct only.
- **Five kinds, ascending:** *mati* → *śruta* (indirect) → *avadhi* → *manaḥparyāya* → *kevala* (direct).
- **Wrong cognition:** *kumati*, *kuśruta*, *vibhaṅga* — held under wrong orientation (*mithyā-darśana*).
- **Later logic admits:** recollection (*smṛti*), recognition (*pratyabhijñā*), pervasion-grasping reasoning (*tarka / ūha*), inference (*anumāna*), testimony (*śabda*). Contrast: Nyāya denies memory the status of an independent means of knowledge.
- **Ordinary cognition is kṣāyopaśamika** — the exact reason judgement is partial.

### F. Standpoints

- **Seven, in order:** *naigama* · *saṃgraha* · *vyavahāra* · *ṛjusūtra* · *śabda* · *samabhirūḍha* · *evambhūta*.
- Funnel: loosest and practical → strictest and function-in-act; first three substance-directed, *ṛjusūtra* the hinge, last three verbal and mode-directed.
- **Perversion:** standpoint + denial of the rest = *nayābhāsa* / *durnaya* = one-sidedness (*ekāntavāda*).
- **Kundakunda:** real standpoint (*niścaya-naya*) / conventional standpoint (*vyavahāra-naya*); ⚠️ not Advaita's two levels, since nothing is sublated.
- **Rival diagnosis:** momentariness absolutises *ṛjusūtra*; permanence-monism absolutises the substance-directed view.

### G. Conditional predication and the sevenfold predication

- ***syāt*** = "in a certain respect"; a **definite** assertion with a stated index; **never** "perhaps".
- **Seven forms:** *syād asti* · *syān nāsti* · *syād asti ca nāsti ca* · **syād avaktavyam** · *syād asti ca avaktavyam* · *syān nāsti ca avaktavyam* · *syād asti ca nāsti ca avaktavyam*.
- **Derivation:** three primitives singly (3) + in pairs (3) + jointly (1) = **7**.
- **Four indices:** substance (*dravya*) · place (*kṣetra*) · time (*kāla*) · mode (*bhāva*); own-form (*svarūpa*) affirms, other-form (*pararūpa*) denies.
- **Inexpressibility:** limit of a **single unqualified simultaneous** utterance; not a third truth-value, not silence.
- **Blind men and the elephant:** pedagogical, not probative; one sentence only.

### H. Evaluating non-absolutism

- **Self-refutation charge:** qualified → weak; unqualified → inconsistent. **Reply:** accept the first horn; reflexive qualification is obedience, and aspect-conditioned truth ≠ equal truth.
- **Absolutism charge (2022):** universal scope looks absolute. **Reply:** the doctrine denies one-sided exclusivism by finite knowers, not comprehensiveness; *kevala-jñāna* is the internal complete case. ⚠️ Residue: the unconditioned is relocated, not abolished.
- **Pluralism and realism (2025):** pluralistic on substances, souls and liberated individuals; realist on substances, qualities, modes and the external world; ⚠️ **qualified realism**, because judgement is standpoint-indexed. Key line: qualification of knowledge ≠ unreality of the object.

### I. Bondage

- **Action-matter is material:** *karma-pudgala*. Contrast: Nyāya *adṛṣṭa*, Mīmāṃsā *apūrva*, Sāṃkhya *saṃskāra*, Buddhist *vāsanā* — none of them material particles.
- **Five causes:** wrong vision (*mithyātva*) · non-restraint (*avirati*) · carelessness (*pramāda*) · passions (*kaṣāya*) · activity (*yoga*).
- **Two forms:** psychic bondage (*bhāvabandha*) = the soul's modification; material bondage (*dravyabandha*) = the adhesion of particles. *Bhāva-karma* attracts *dravya-karma*.
- **Four parameters:** type (*prakṛti*) and quantity (*pradeśa*) ← **activity**; duration (*sthiti*) and intensity (*anubhāga / rasa*) ← **passion**.
- **Consequence:** momentary bondage (*īryāpathika*) from passion-free activity; durable bondage (*sāmparāyika*) from passion-coloured activity.
- **Eight karma-types:** destructive (*ghātiyā*) — *jñānāvaraṇīya*, *darśanāvaraṇīya*, *mohanīya* (worst; *darśana-* and *cāritra-mohanīya*), *antarāya*; non-destructive (*aghātiyā*) — *vedanīya*, *nāma*, *gotra*, *āyus*.
- **Relation is anādi** — beginningless yet terminable; seed-and-tree, ore-and-gold.

### J. Liberation sequence

- **Sequence:** influx (*āsrava*) → bondage (*bandha*) → stoppage (*saṃvara*) → shedding (*nirjarā*) → liberation (*mokṣa*).
- **Seven principles (tattva):** soul, non-soul, influx, bondage, stoppage, shedding, liberation. **Nine categories (padārtha):** the seven **plus merit (puṇya) and demerit (pāpa)**. Merit is still bondage.
- **Stoppage is preventive; shedding is eliminative.** Both are required.
- **Liberation by subtraction:** the infinite tetrad is revealed, not produced; sooted lamp cleaned, not lit.

### K. Ethics and the fourteen stages

- **Three jewels (ratnatraya):** right vision · right knowledge · right conduct — **jointly**, not serially.
- **Five vows:** non-violence (*ahiṃsā*), truthfulness (*satya*), non-stealing (*asteya*), chastity (*brahmacarya*), non-possession (*aparigraha*); **great vows (mahāvrata)** for ascetics, **lesser vows (aṇuvrata)** for householders.
- **Wider discipline:** restraint (*gupti*), carefulness (*samiti*), the ten virtues, contemplations (*anuprekṣā*), endurance of afflictions (*pariṣaha-jaya*), right conduct (*cāritra*), austerity (*tapas*).
- **Fourteen stages:** 1 *mithyā-dṛṣṭi* · 2 *sāsvādana* · 3 *miśra* · 4 *avirata samyag-dṛṣṭi* (**breakthrough**) · 5 *deśavirata* · 6 *pramatta-saṃyata* · 7 *apramatta-saṃyata* · 8 *apūrva-karaṇa* · 9 *anivṛtti-bādara* · 10 *sūkṣma-samparāya* · 11 *upaśānta-moha* (**suppressed → necessarily falls**) · 12 *kṣīṇa-moha* (**destroyed**) · 13 *sayoga-kevalī* · 14 *ayoga-kevalī*.
- **Two ladders:** suppression (*upaśama-śreṇi*) reaches 11 and falls; annihilation (*kṣapaka-śreṇi*) reaches 12 and continues.
- **Embodied omniscience:** *sayoga-kevalī* = the Jaina liberation-in-life; destructive karmas destroyed, non-destructive karmas remain.
- **Liberated soul (siddha):** disembodied, activity-free, beyond rebirth, **numerically distinct**, at the summit of the world (*siddha-śilā*); no merger, no return.
- **Voluntary passionless death (sallekhanā / santhārā):** gradual, supervised, passion-free, undertaken when death is unavoidable; distinguished from suicide (*ātma-ghāta*) by intention, procedure, publicity and non-violence; desire for death or life makes it a transgression (*aticāra*). ❓ Do not assert legal holdings.

### L. Criticism, comparison and answer discipline

| Named critic | Objection | Jaina reply | ⚠️ Residual force |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Śaṃkara** | Many-sidedness allows contradiction and cannot assert itself consistently | Predicates indexed to substance, place, time, mode; *syādvāda* asserted *syāt* | Reflexive qualification may seem to weaken assertoric force |
| **Rāmānuja** | A real substance with opposed real attributes is unintelligible | Opposition is between aspects of substance and mode | Depends on granting aspect-realism |
| **Dharmakīrti / Buddhist** | Permanent-yet-changing soul fails the causal-efficacy (*arthakriyā*) test | *Utpāda-vyaya-dhrauvya*: permanent as substance, changing as mode | Strong if the substance–mode distinction is denied |
| **Nyāya** | Soul's expansion and material action-matter are unparsimonious | Space-points and the lamp simile; matter explains embodiment | Consciousness–matter interaction remains unresolved |
| **Sāṃkhya / Vedānta** | Intrinsic omniscience plus beginningless obscuration is puzzling | *Anādi* but terminable through stoppage and shedding | No account of why beginningless bondage is possible |
| **Cārvāka / empiricist** | Souls, karmic matter, omniscience are unverified | Inference, testimony and systematic coherence | Unpersuasive to strict perception-only materialism |

- **Comparative one-liners.** *Buddhism:* no self, momentariness, non-material action-consequence — historically close, metaphysically opposed. *Advaita:* one self, world *mithyā*, merger in liberation. *Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika:* many eternal selves, fresh production of the effect, later theism. *Sāṃkhya:* many changeless *puruṣas*, evolving *prakṛti*, pre-existent effect. *Cārvāka:* body-self, no action-consequence, no liberation.
