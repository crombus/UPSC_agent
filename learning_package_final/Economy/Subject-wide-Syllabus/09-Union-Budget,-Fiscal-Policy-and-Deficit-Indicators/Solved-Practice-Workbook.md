#### 2018 GS-III Q2 — Long-term Capital Gains Tax and Dividend Distribution Tax
**Verified routed demand (Comment, 10 marks, 150 words):** Comment on the implications of the 2018 Budget changes to the Long-term Capital Gains Tax and Dividend Distribution Tax.

**Independent historical model answer (2018 policy context; not an official UPSC key):** Budget 2018 reintroduced tax on listed-equity and equity-oriented fund long-term capital gains above the announced annual exemption threshold, while grandfathering gains accrued up to 31 January 2018. It also introduced a distribution tax on dividends paid by equity-oriented mutual funds. These measures narrowed tax asymmetry between equity and other assets and between a fund's growth and dividend options. Grandfathering reduced retrospective disruption to existing holders.

However, effective incidence depends on investor horizon, fund choice and compliance. Tax at distribution level can affect investors differently from tax on their own realised gains; investors might alter payout preferences or shift towards alternatives. Additional revenues cannot alone establish higher fairness if the burden on small savers or administrative complexity increases. Evaluate the changes against neutrality, predictability and revenue yield, not merely market reaction.

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes the two distinct 2018 measures, identifies their incentives, explains the grandfathering qualification and does not misstate later abolition of distribution tax as applicable in 2018.

#### 2019 GS-III Q12 — Public expenditure management
**Verified routed demand:** Public expenditure management challenge in post-liberalisation budget making.

**Model solution:** Public expenditure management converts legislative authority into economical, efficient and effective public services. Post-liberalisation India faces a larger and more complex spending state: centrally sponsored schemes cross government levels; public enterprises and special-purpose vehicles blur the fiscal perimeter; subsidies, guarantees and public-private partnerships create multi-year commitments; and technology generates both traceability and exclusion risks.

The principal challenges are unrealistic estimates, fragmented schemes, delayed releases, idle balances, weak procurement and contract management, incomplete projects, underfunded maintenance, poor outcome indicators and slow follow-up on audit findings. Off-budget borrowing and arrears can improve the visible annual deficit while shifting obligations forward.

Reform should integrate medium-term expenditure planning with annual grants, protect high-value capital and human-development spending, use Treasury Single Account and just-in-time release systems, strengthen project appraisal and asset registers, disclose guarantees and EBR, and connect Output Outcome Monitoring Framework indicators with independent evaluation. CGA reconciliation, CAG audit and parliamentary committees must close the accountability loop.

The objective is not merely lower spending, but transparent conversion of each authorised rupee into durable public value.

#### 2021 GS-III Q2 — Capital Budget and Revenue Budget
**Verified routed demand:** Distinguish Capital Budget and Revenue Budget and explain their components.

**Model solution:** The Revenue Budget contains revenue receipts and revenue expenditure. Revenue receipts neither create liabilities nor reduce financial assets and include tax and non-tax revenue. Revenue expenditure generally finances current administration, interest, subsidies, transfers and services without creating an asset in the Union's own books.

The Capital Budget contains capital receipts and capital expenditure. Capital receipts either create liabilities, as with borrowing, or reduce financial assets, as with loan recovery and disinvestment. Capital expenditure creates or acquires assets, extends loans or reduces liabilities.

The distinction supports deficit analysis. Revenue deficit measures current-account dissaving, while fiscal deficit captures the total borrowing gap after non-debt receipts. Yet accounting classification is not a productivity ranking. Health and education revenue expenditure can build human capability, and a capital project may be delayed or low-value. Effective capital expenditure therefore adds grants for capital-asset creation to Union capital expenditure, recognising assets created in recipient books.

Budget quality must combine classification with completion, maintenance, distribution and outcome evidence.

#### 2024 GS-III Q1 — Social-services expenditure and inclusive growth
**Question:** Examine the pattern and trend of public expenditure on social services in the post-reforms period in India. To what extent has this been in consonance with achieving the objective of inclusive growth? (150 words)

**Model solution:** Post-reform social expenditure expanded in absolute terms and through wider education, health, nutrition, housing, employment and social-protection programmes. The composition also shifted toward shared Union-State implementation, direct transfers and insurance or entitlement-based delivery. This supported school access, food security, rural employment, financial resilience and health coverage.

However, inclusive growth requires capability and distributional outcomes, not allocation alone. Spending remains uneven across States; primary health, learning quality, urban services and social-care capacity face gaps; and delayed releases, vacancies, exclusion errors and weak local institutions reduce effectiveness. Household out-of-pocket costs and unequal digital access can preserve vulnerability even when scheme coverage expands.

Policy should protect social spending during consolidation, improve outcome-based budgeting, strengthen local delivery, publish disaggregated indicators and coordinate revenue and capital components such as hospitals with staff and maintenance. The trend is broadly supportive of inclusion, but adequacy, quality and inter-State convergence remain incomplete.

#### 2025 GS-III Q11 — Fiscal Health Index
**Question:** Explain how the Fiscal Health Index can be used as a tool for assessing the fiscal performance of states in India. In what way would it encourage the states to adopt prudent and sustainable fiscal policies? (250 words)

**Model solution:** The Fiscal Health Index converts State public finance into a multidimensional benchmark rather than relying on one deficit ratio. Its dimensions can compare revenue mobilisation, expenditure quality, fiscal prudence, debt sustainability and the structure of liabilities across comparable States and years.

For diagnosis, a State can identify whether weakness arises from low own-revenue effort, high committed expenditure, inadequate capital formation, persistent deficits or debt-service pressure. A dashboard also reveals trade-offs: a State may show a contained deficit but weak development spending, or high capital expenditure with rising guarantees and repayment risk.

For incentives, transparent comparison can support competitive federalism, peer learning and medium-term reform plans. Finance departments can set measurable targets for tax administration, subsidy redesign, project completion, debt maturity and guarantee disclosure. Legislatures, citizens and lenders gain a common evidence base for scrutiny.

Limitations must be retained. Rankings are sensitive to data quality, weights, economic structure, disaster exposure and inherited debt. The Index should not mechanically determine grants or reward cuts in essential services. It should be used with State-specific context, audited accounts and outcome indicators.

Used as a diagnostic rather than a league table, the Index can encourage credible, transparent and growth-compatible fiscal correction.

#### M1 — 10 marks — 150 words

**Question:** Distinguish the Consolidated Fund, Public Account and Contingency Fund of India.

**Demand plan:** define the legal/accounting boundary, attach dated Indian evidence, trace the economic mechanism and end with a qualified reform verdict.

**Model answer (138 native-body words):**

Article 266 places Union revenues, loans raised and loan recoveries in the Consolidated Fund of India. Government expenditure is ordinarily incurred from this Fund, and withdrawal requires parliamentary authorisation through appropriation. Charged expenditure is not voted, but it is still discussed, disclosed and appropriated.

Article 266(2) creates the Public Account for money held by government in trust, including provident funds, small savings and specified deposits. Such money is repayable; routine repayment does not require parliamentary appropriation, though transfers into the account from the Consolidated Fund need authority.

Article 267 permits the Contingency Fund as an imprest at the President's disposal for urgent unforeseen expenditure pending legislative approval. After ex-post authorisation, an equivalent amount is drawn from the Consolidated Fund to recoup it.

Thus the three differ by ownership, purpose and withdrawal rule. None permits uncontrolled executive expenditure.

#### M2 — 10 marks — 150 words

**Question:** Explain why fiscal, revenue and primary deficits must be read together.

**Demand plan:** define the legal/accounting boundary, attach dated Indian evidence, trace the economic mechanism and end with a qualified reform verdict.

**Model answer (146 native-body words):**

Fiscal deficit equals total expenditure minus non-debt receipts and broadly measures the year's borrowing requirement. Revenue deficit equals revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts and reveals current-account dissaving. Effective revenue deficit deducts grants for capital-asset creation, recognising that an item booked as Union revenue expenditure may create an asset elsewhere. Primary deficit subtracts interest payments from fiscal deficit and isolates the current non-interest gap.

Together they answer different questions. A falling fiscal deficit may still conceal weak revenue balance; a high fiscal deficit may finance productive capital expenditure; and a low primary deficit may reflect a large inherited interest burden rather than low debt.

Analysis must add financing mix, debt maturity, off-budget liabilities and expenditure outcomes. It must also preserve fiscal year and vintage. Union Budget 2026-27 figures are Budget Estimates, not realised outcomes. Therefore no single deficit is a complete measure of fiscal quality or sustainability.

#### M3 — 15 marks — 250 words

**Question:** Explain how Parliament authorises and scrutinises Union expenditure.

**Demand plan:** define the legal/accounting boundary, attach dated Indian evidence, trace the economic mechanism and end with a qualified reform verdict.

**Model answer (219 native-body words):**

The Constitution separates fiscal disclosure, voting and legal withdrawal. Under Article 112, the President causes the Annual Financial Statement to be laid before both Houses. It distinguishes charged expenditure from other expenditure and separates revenue-account spending.

Under Article 113, charged expenditure may be discussed but is not submitted to vote. Other expenditure is presented ministry-wise as Demands for Grants to the Lok Sabha, on the President's recommendation. Members can use policy, economy and token cut motions to question policy, amount or a specific grievance. The guillotine, however, may dispose of undiscussed demands when allotted time expires.

After voting, Article 114 requires an Appropriation Bill covering voted grants and charged sums. No money can ordinarily be withdrawn from the Consolidated Fund without appropriation. The Finance Bill separately gives effect to taxation proposals. Article 115 provides supplementary, additional and excess grants when provision is inadequate, a new service arises or expenditure exceeds authority. Article 116 permits Vote on Account, vote of credit and exceptional grants.

Control continues after enactment. Ministries execute expenditure; the Controller General of Accounts compiles accounts; the Comptroller and Auditor General audits; and parliamentary committees examine compliance and performance.

Thus parliamentary control is not confined to Budget Day. Its effectiveness depends on realistic estimates, adequate debate, committee scrutiny, transparent supplementary demands, timely accounts and follow-up on audit findings.

#### M4 — 15 marks — 250 words

**Question:** Critically examine India's FRBM framework as a balance between fiscal discipline and flexibility.

**Demand plan:** define the legal/accounting boundary, attach dated Indian evidence, trace the economic mechanism and end with a qualified reform verdict.

**Model answer (225 native-body words):**

The Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management Act, 2003 seeks medium-term discipline, transparency and inter-generational equity. Section 3 requires fiscal-policy statements covering rolling targets, macro assumptions, strategy and expenditure commitments. Section 4 contains statutory reference targets, while sections 6 and 7 require transparency and explanation of deviations.

Rules improve credibility by constraining deficit bias, exposing assumptions and anchoring debt expectations. They can lower uncertainty and preserve space for future shocks. Yet rigid annual ceilings may become pro-cyclical: abrupt consolidation during recession can weaken demand, employment and even revenue. Numerical compliance can also be manufactured through optimistic estimates, deferred payments or off-budget borrowing.

The amended escape clause is therefore important. It permits deviation on enumerated grounds including national security, calamity, severe agricultural collapse, structural reform with unanticipated fiscal effects and a specified fall in real output growth. Annual deviation is capped at 0.5 percentage point of GDP, and reasons plus a return path must be laid before Parliament.

The current position must be dated. Union Budget 2026-27 uses a declining Central-debt path, estimating debt at 55.6 per cent of GDP and fiscal deficit at 4.3 per cent in BE 2026-27. It also withholds the next two rolling-year targets because of global uncertainty.

India therefore needs rule-bound flexibility: comprehensive coverage, realistic assumptions, protected productive expenditure, transparent escape use, independent scrutiny and a credible correction path.

#### M5 — 20 marks — 250 words

**Question:** Why can the headline fiscal deficit understate public-sector fiscal risk? Suggest reforms.

**Demand plan:** define the legal/accounting boundary, attach dated Indian evidence, trace the economic mechanism and end with a qualified reform verdict.

**Model answer (218 native-body words):**

Fiscal deficit measures Union expenditure minus non-debt receipts for a specified year. It does not automatically consolidate States, public enterprises, special-purpose vehicles, guarantees, arrears or every quasi-fiscal operation.

Off-budget borrowing arises when an entity raises resources outside the direct Budget while debt service is borne by future government expenditure. Government of India fully serviced bonds and historical National Small Savings Fund financing of Food Corporation of India subsidy illustrate the substance-over-form problem. Extra-budgetary resources can postpone recognition, weaken year-to-year comparability and reduce parliamentary visibility.

Guarantees are contingent liabilities. They need not create immediate expenditure, but default can crystallise them. Analysis must distinguish annual net additions, total outstanding exposure, probability of invocation and recovery. Public-enterprise borrowing without a government servicing obligation should not be mechanically treated as sovereign debt, but ownership, guarantee and policy direction can still create risk.

Reform requires a consolidated fiscal-risk statement identifying entity, purpose, lender, maturity, currency, guarantee and servicing source. Budget documents should reconcile headline deficit with EBR, arrears and public-private partnership commitments. Guarantee fees and ceilings should reflect risk; projects should undergo appraisal and stress testing. CGA accounts, CAG audit and parliamentary committees need timely data and follow-up. Accrual-style balance-sheet information can complement, not replace, cash accounts.

Transparency should prevent concealment without discouraging commercially viable public enterprises from borrowing on their own strength.

#### M6 — 20 marks — 250 words

**Question:** How can fiscal consolidation support growth while preserving countercyclical capacity?

**Demand plan:** define the legal/accounting boundary, attach dated Indian evidence, trace the economic mechanism and end with a qualified reform verdict.

**Model answer (222 native-body words):**

Fiscal consolidation should improve the recurring balance and debt path without applying uniform expenditure cuts. Its quality depends on revenue durability, spending composition, macroeconomic timing and transparency.

On the revenue side, a broader base, better compliance and predictable administration create more durable space than one-off dividends or asset sales. On expenditure, poorly targeted leakage and idle balances should be reduced while protecting maintenance, health, education and high-return infrastructure. Effective capital expenditure is useful because it includes Union grants that create assets outside the Centre's own books, but project completion and outcomes must still be verified.

Public capex can crowd in private investment by removing logistics, power and urban bottlenecks. Conversely, persistent borrowing in tight financial conditions may absorb savings, raise yields and crowd out private credit. Debt sustainability therefore depends on the primary balance, the interest-growth differential, maturity, currency and contingent liabilities.

Countercyclical capacity requires buffers in normal times. Automatic stabilisers should operate during downturns, while discretionary support should be temporary, targeted and capable of rapid execution. A credible FRBM architecture needs transparent escape triggers and a specified return path rather than either unlimited discretion or mechanical annual austerity.

The Union Budget 2026-27 combines a 4.3 per cent fiscal-deficit BE with continued capital spending and a declining-debt anchor. Success must be judged later against Actuals, expenditure outcomes and complete liability disclosure.
