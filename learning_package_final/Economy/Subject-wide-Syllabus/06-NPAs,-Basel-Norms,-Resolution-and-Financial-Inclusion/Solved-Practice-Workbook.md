### ORIGINAL MCQ ANSWER KEY, EXPLANATIONS AND TRAPS

### MCQ 1 — A

**Correct answer: A.***

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A - Correct:** The dated RBI rule distinguishes the due-date concept of overdue from NPA classification after more than 90 days.
- **B - Incorrect:** A missed due date establishes overdue status, but not the more-than-90-day NPA threshold.
- **C - Incorrect:** ICR is a debt-servicing diagnostic; a weak ratio alone is not an IRACP ageing trigger.
- **D - Incorrect:** Impaired collateral can increase expected loss without itself satisfying the term-loan overdue test.

**Examiner trap 1:** Do not convert `overdue on due date` into `NPA on due date`.

### MCQ 2 — B

**Correct answer: B.***

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A - Incorrect:** Ordinary use of a revolving line creates debit entries; it does not make the account out of order.
- **B - Correct:** The CC/OD test looks at excess over limit/drawing power and genuine credit turnover over 90 days.
- **C - Incorrect:** Drawing less than the sanctioned limit does not prove the account has breached drawing power.
- **D - Incorrect:** Collateral quality cannot substitute for the 90-day turnover and interest-credit tests.

**Examiner trap 2:** CC/OD uses out-of-order tests, not merely the term-loan instalment test.

### MCQ 3 — C

**Correct answer: C.***

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A - Incorrect:** Statement 2 is also correct: a long-duration crop uses the one-season exception.
- **B - Incorrect:** Statement 3 is false: price declines do not by themselves establish the prescribed overdue period.
- **C - Correct:** The current RBI framework uses two crop seasons for short-duration crops and one for long-duration crops; a price fall alone is not the recognition test.
- **D - Incorrect:** Statement 3 incorrectly converts a market-price shock into automatic loan classification.

**Examiner trap 3:** Crop-season rules are product-specific exceptions to the common 90-day shorthand.

### MCQ 4 — D

**Correct answer: D.***

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A - Incorrect:** Classification reaches the borrower's other facilities at that bank, not only the missed instalment.
- **B - Incorrect:** Security may change recovery/provisioning but does not nullify recognition for prolonged default.
- **C - Incorrect:** A branch cannot evade bank-wide borrower-wise classification by treating its facility in isolation.
- **D - Correct:** The rule prevents a lender from isolating one irregular facility while treating the same borrower's connected facilities as fully performing.

**Examiner trap 4:** Borrower-wise within one bank does not mean system-wide automatic classification by every lender.

### MCQ 5 — A

**Correct answer: A.***

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A - Correct:** The 2025 IRACP Directions define the first NPA ageing category as substandard for up to twelve months.
- **B - Incorrect:** Once classified NPA, the asset cannot remain standard simply because twelve months have not elapsed.
- **C - Incorrect:** More-than-three-years doubtful is a later ageing segment, not the first NPA category.
- **D - Incorrect:** Write-off is an accounting decision, not the automatic consequence of twelve months as NPA.

**Examiner trap 5:** NPA is the umbrella; substandard is one class within it.

### MCQ 6 — B

**Correct answer: B.***

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A - Incorrect:** One overdue day is insufficient even for ordinary term-loan NPA recognition.
- **B - Correct:** RBI's current definition moves an exposure from substandard to doubtful after twelve months in that category.
- **C - Incorrect:** Full security does not stop a substandard loan ageing into doubtful classification.
- **D - Incorrect:** Waiver releases an obligation; doubtful describes continued credit impairment, not legal forgiveness.

**Examiner trap 6:** Collateral affects provisioning and recovery, not the basic ageing definition.

### MCQ 7 — C

**Correct answer: C.***

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A - Incorrect:** Substandard and loss are correctly paired, so counting just one omits valid general rates.
- **B - Incorrect:** The unsecured substandard rate is also correctly matched; all three pairs count.
- **C - Correct:** All three pairs state the applicable general provisioning rates; identified loss retained in the books must be fully provided.
- **D - Incorrect:** Even a loss asset retained on the books must be fully provided, so none is untenable.

**Examiner trap 7:** Special exposure-specific rules can qualify the general rates, but litigation does not suspend loss provisioning.

### MCQ 8 — D

**Correct answer: D.***

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A - Incorrect:** A technical write-off can leave the lender's enforceable claim intact, unlike a waiver.
- **B - Incorrect:** A waiver concerns release of a claim, not merely recording an expense or provision.
- **C - Incorrect:** Technical write-off changes accounting presentation even if recovery action continues.
- **D - Correct:** The current RBI Resolution Directions define technical write-off without waiver of claims or prejudice to recovery.

**Examiner trap 8:** A lower reported loan book after write-off is not evidence of cash recovery.

### MCQ 9 — A

**Correct answer: A.***

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A - Correct:** PCR uses the surviving provision stock divided by the surviving gross NPA stock: 100/160.
- **B - Incorrect:** 120/200 is the **initial** PCR, not the ratio after recovery and reversal.
- **C - Incorrect:** 120/160 mixes the old numerator with the post-recovery denominator.
- **D - Incorrect:** 100/200 mixes the updated numerator with the old gross-NPA denominator.

**Examiner trap 9:** Update both numerator and denominator after a specified recovery; do not interpret PCR as CRAR.

### MCQ 10 — B

**Correct answer: B.***

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A - Incorrect:** Deposit insurance is based on eligible deposit balances and statutory limits, not corporate earnings coverage.
- **B - Correct:** ICR is a borrower cash-flow warning indicator, whereas NPA classification follows RBI prudential triggers.
- **C - Incorrect:** Weak interest cover can arise without fraud; intent cannot be inferred from a ratio below one.
- **D - Incorrect:** Capital against RWA defines a solvency ratio; ICR compares operating earnings and interest.

**Examiner trap 10:** Financial weakness and regulatory classification are related but not identical.

### MCQ 11 — C

**Correct answer: C.***

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A - Incorrect:** Recognition and viable restructuring address impairment openly instead of hiding it.
- **B - Incorrect:** Actual collateral-sale proceeds recover cash and do not disguise a failing exposure.
- **C - Correct:** Evergreening creates the appearance of repayment without restoring sustainable borrower cash flow.
- **D - Incorrect:** Fresh common equity improves bank loss-absorption, not borrower debt-service concealment.

**Examiner trap 11:** Additional finance is not automatically evergreening if independently viable and transparently recognised.

### MCQ 12 — D

**Correct answer: D.***

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A - Incorrect:** 9% is the original ratio; both eligible capital and RWA change in this scenario.
- **B - Incorrect:** A capital requirement does not compute the bank's observed ratio; 11/110 is not 11.5%.
- **C - Incorrect:** Total assets and deposits are unnecessary because eligible capital and RWA are given.
- **D - Correct:** The new numerator is 11 and denominator 110; 11/110 equals 10%.

**Examiner trap 12:** Recalculate the weighted denominator as well as the numerator; an observed CRAR does not establish that every component minimum or buffer is met.

### MCQ 13 — A

**Correct answer: A.***

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A - Correct:** Pillar 2 allows supervisors to assess risks and capital needs beyond mechanical Pillar 1 minima.
- **B - Incorrect:** Minimum capital calculation belongs to Pillar 1, not Pillar 2 supervisory assessment.
- **C - Incorrect:** Public disclosure and market discipline are Pillar 3, not the supervisory review pillar.
- **D - Incorrect:** Deposit insurance handles depositor protection; it is not a Basel capital pillar.

**Examiner trap 13:** Pillar 3, not Pillar 2, is market discipline through disclosure.

### MCQ 14 — B

**Correct answer: B.***

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A - Incorrect:** The three-pillar architecture followed the earlier credit-risk capital accord, not vice versa.
- **B - Correct:** Basel I centred on a credit-risk capital floor, Basel II introduced the three pillars, and Basel III strengthened capital while adding leverage and liquidity safeguards.
- **C - Incorrect:** Basel III strengthening came after the first capital floor, not before it.
- **D - Incorrect:** Basel III cannot precede the Basel II three-pillar architecture.

**Examiner trap 14:** Basel chronology does not mean that later standards replace supervision or disclosure.

### MCQ 15 — C

**Correct answer: C.***

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A - Incorrect:** The global Basel total-capital floor of 8% is not India's cited covered-bank standalone minimum.
- **B - Incorrect:** 11.5% includes a fully phased conservation buffer; it is not the minimum before CCB.
- **C - Correct:** India's 9 per cent total minimum is above the Basel global 8 per cent floor; 11.5 per cent includes the 2.5 per cent CCB.
- **D - Incorrect:** 5.5% is a different capital-component figure and not the total CRAR minimum.

**Examiner trap 15:** State the RBI perimeter because SFBs, Payments Banks and RRBs use separate rules.

### MCQ 16 — D

**Correct answer: D.***

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A - Incorrect:** CRR locks a proportion of bank liabilities as reserves with RBI; it is not a capital buffer.
- **B - Incorrect:** LCR tests stressed short-term cash coverage rather than constraining dividends when capital erodes.
- **C - Incorrect:** PCR is a bad-loan provisioning measure, not CET1 set aside above minimum capital.
- **D - Correct:** The CCB is common-equity capital above minima and constrains distributions when a bank enters the buffer range.

**Examiner trap 16:** CCB is not a cash reserve or an expected-loss provision.

### MCQ 17 — A

**Correct answer: A.***

**Option-specific explanations:**

- **A - Correct:** RBI retains a CCyB framework but the cited circular reported no activation; it does not permanently replace the Capital Conservation Buffer.
- **B - Incorrect:** It omits the cited circular's explicit non-activation observation in statement 2.
- **C - Incorrect:** The framework exists, and the CCyB does not permanently replace the conservation buffer.
- **D - Incorrect:** Statement 3 is false: cyclical add-ons and the standing conservation buffer are distinct.

**Examiner trap 17:** A maximum framework value is not the same as the currently activated rate.

### MCQ 18 — B

- **D - Correct:** Inclusion becomes meaningful when reachable services are used safely, suitably and repeatedly.

**Examiner trap 32:** Access counts alone can coexist with dormancy, exclusion or over-indebtedness.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 - 10 MARKS

**Question:** Distinguish overdue, out-of-order and NPA classification. Answer in 150 words.

#### Demand decoding

Separate the due-date concept from product-specific recognition tests, then close with the borrower-wise and security-value qualifications.

#### Answer plan and validation

- **Plan:** overdue -> term-loan/CC-OD/agriculture tests -> borrower-wise rule -> qualification.
- **Validated native-body count:** 143 alphanumeric words.

#### Model answer

<!-- MODEL-ANSWER-START:M1 -->
RBI's Commercial Banks IRACP Directions, 2025, updated 1 July 2026, define overdue as any amount unpaid on its contractual due date. That status is an early clock, not itself the universal NPA test.

For a term loan, principal or interest remaining overdue for more than 90 days ordinarily makes the asset non-performing. A cash-credit or overdraft account instead becomes out of order when it remains continuously above the sanctioned limit or drawing power for 90 days, has no credits for 90 days, or its credits cannot cover interest debited during the previous 90 days. Bills purchased or discounted use the more-than-90-day overdue test; agricultural advances use crop-season tests.

Thus, product design determines recognition. The classification is generally borrower-wise within a bank and cannot be postponed merely because collateral is valuable. Exact facility, due date and regulatory perimeter must precede any conclusion about stress.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 - 10 MARKS

**Question:** Why are provisioning, write-off and waiver not interchangeable responses to an NPA? Answer in 150 words.

#### Demand decoding

Compare the accounting entry, continuing recovery right and legal discharge consequence; explain why reported NPA reduction differs from recovery.

#### Answer plan and validation

- **Plan:** provision -> technical write-off -> waiver -> reporting consequences -> governance conclusion.
- **Validated native-body count:** 141 alphanumeric words.

#### Model answer

<!-- MODEL-ANSWER-START:M2 -->
Provisioning recognises expected loss by charging bank income while the loan remains on the balance sheet. RBI's IRACP Directions updated 1 July 2026 link the provision to asset class, ageing, security and realisable value. It therefore protects capital credibility before final recovery is known.

A technical write-off removes all or part of the NPA from bank-level accounting without waiving the claim at borrower level. Recovery action may continue, so a lower reported GNPA stock after write-off is not equivalent to cash collection. A waiver, by contrast, relinquishes the borrower's liability to the stated extent and may involve a government or lender policy decision.

The distinction matters for fiscal cost, borrower discipline and bank performance. Sound analysis should report gross NPAs, provisions, net NPAs, write-offs, recoveries and loan growth together. None of the three devices substitutes for appraisal, governance or viable resolution.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 - 15 MARKS

**Question:** Explain how Basel III capital, leverage and liquidity standards jointly strengthen Indian banks. Answer in 250 words.

#### Demand decoding

Organise the answer by loss absorption, non-risk-weighted leverage and two liquidity horizons; state RBI values with dates and perimeter.

#### Answer plan and validation

- **Plan:** RWA capital -> capital quality/buffer -> leverage backstop -> LCR/NSFR -> residual risks.
- **Validated native-body count:** 214 alphanumeric words.

#### Model answer

<!-- MODEL-ANSWER-START:M3 -->
Basel regulation addresses different failure channels rather than relying on one ratio. Risk-weighted capital absorbs unexpected credit, market and operational losses. Under RBI's Master Circular dated 1 April 2025 for covered scheduled commercial banks, standalone minima are CET1 5.5 per cent, Tier 1 capital 7 per cent and total CRAR 9 per cent of RWA. The 2.5 per cent Capital Conservation Buffer, met with CET1, raises effective CET1 to 8 per cent and total capital to 11.5 per cent.

Risk weights can understate exposures or invite optimisation. RBI's leverage-ratio rule, effective from the quarter beginning 1 October 2019, therefore provides a non-risk-weighted backstop: 4 per cent for domestic systemically important banks and 3.5 per cent for other covered banks.

Liquidity rules address timing rather than solvency. LCR of at least 100 per cent, fully effective from 1 January 2019, requires high-quality liquid assets against 30-day stressed net outflows. NSFR of at least 100 per cent from 1 October 2021 requires stable funding against one-year funding needs.

Together these rules improve loss absorption, constrain leverage and reduce run or rollover risk. Yet ratios remain model- and perimeter-dependent. Concentration, governance, cyber risk, hidden NPAs and stress correlations require supervision and scenario testing. Strong buffers should support disciplined intermediation, not replace sound underwriting.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 - 15 MARKS

**Question:** Compare RBI's prudential resolution framework, SARFAESI, DRTs and IBC as responses to stressed assets. Answer in 250 words.

#### Demand decoding

Compare trigger, decision-maker, remedy and best-use case across consensual resolution, secured enforcement, tribunal recovery and collective insolvency.

#### Answer plan and validation

- **Plan:** RBI framework -> SARFAESI -> DRT -> IBC -> comparative choice and implementation limit.
- **Validated native-body count:** 209 alphanumeric words.

#### Model answer

<!-- MODEL-ANSWER-START:M4 -->
India's stressed-asset architecture separates consensual repair, claim enforcement and collective insolvency. RBI's Commercial Banks Resolution of Stressed Assets Directions, 2025, updated 1 July 2026, require a 30-day review after default or a credit event. A multi-lender plan uses an ICA; 75 per cent by value and 60 per cent by number bind signatories. Classification continues during negotiation, and delayed implementation attracts additional provisioning.

SARFAESI, 2002 enables an eligible secured creditor, after section 13(2)'s 60-day notice, to use section 13(4) enforcement measures without first obtaining an ordinary civil decree. It is effective for clear, valuable security but does not coordinate all claims against a complex going concern.

The Recovery of Debts and Bankruptcy Act, 1993 establishes DRTs for adjudication and recovery of bank and financial-institution debt. DRT is a specialised recovery forum, not a corporate rescue committee.

IBC, 2016 begins a collective CIRP on NCLT admission, imposes a bounded moratorium and places commercial choice with the CoC. A compliant plan needs at least 66 per cent voting share and NCLT approval; otherwise statutory liquidation routes may follow.

No mechanism is universally superior. The correct choice depends on viability, security, creditor dispersion, information, time and going-concern value. Capacity and due process determine whether formal speed becomes actual resolution.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 - 20 MARKS

**Question:** Critically evaluate the NARCL-IDRCL model as part of India's bad-loan resolution architecture. Answer in 250 words.

#### Demand decoding

Explain the two-entity acquisition-resolution design, financing mix and guarantee; then test valuation, incentives, governance and realised recovery.

#### Answer plan and validation

- **Plan:** institutional design -> claimed gains -> retained risks -> performance tests -> complementary role.
- **Validated native-body count:** 204 alphanumeric words.

#### Model answer

<!-- MODEL-ANSWER-START:M5 -->
NARCL and IDRCL separate acquisition from resolution management. Under the architecture announced in 2021, NARCL, an RBI-registered asset reconstruction company, acquires identified legacy stressed assets. Consideration follows a 15:85 cash-to-Security-Receipt structure. IDRCL supplies resolution expertise under the agreed arrangement, while a Government guarantee of up to Rs 30,600 crore supports eligible SR shortfalls within the specified five-year design. DFS's Annual Report 2024-25 records the mechanism as operational.

The model offers four gains. Aggregation can solve coordination problems across lenders. Specialist teams can focus on restructuring, enforcement or sale. Banks receive some cash and can redirect management attention to new credit. Common ownership among public-sector sellers can accelerate decisions.

However, a transfer is not recovery. An inflated acquisition price postpones loss recognition; a low price can generate immediate bank losses. The 85 per cent SR component leaves selling banks exposed to realisation. A guarantee may create moral hazard if due diligence weakens. IDRCL-NARCL role clarity, professional independence, information quality and a deep buyer market are therefore decisive.

NARCL should be judged by transparent valuation, time to resolution, net present recovery, SR redemption and governance—not gross assets transferred. It complements, rather than replaces, IBC, SARFAESI, DRTs, ARCs, bank provisioning and better credit appraisal.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 - 20 MARKS

**Question:** Financial inclusion and financial stability are complements only under responsible credit design. Discuss. Answer in 250 words.

#### Demand decoding

Balance access, usage and quality gains against underwriting, over-indebtedness, conduct and digital risks; finish with responsible-credit safeguards.

#### Answer plan and validation

- **Plan:** access channels -> stability gains -> risk channels -> consumer safeguards -> qualified verdict.
- **Validated native-body count:** 214 alphanumeric words.

#### Model answer

<!-- MODEL-ANSWER-START:M6 -->
Financial inclusion lowers transaction costs, mobilises savings, improves payment resilience and connects households and small firms to formal credit. PMJDY, launched on 28 August 2014, uses basic accounts, RuPay, transfers and Business Correspondents. RBI's BSBD amendment dated 4 December 2025, effective 1 April 2026, strengthens zero-minimum-balance service. Priority-sector rules, MUDRA, SHG-Bank Linkage and JLGs address sector, collateral and information gaps.

These measures can strengthen stability. Broader deposits diversify funding; digital records can improve underwriting; regular saving and insurance reduce household vulnerability. RBI's FI-Index—Access, Usage and Quality—correctly treats opening an account as only the first stage.

Yet poorly governed inclusion can manufacture stress. Target-chasing may weaken appraisal; guarantees can reduce screening; multiple micro-loans can create over-indebtedness; group pressure can become coercive; digital journeys can enable fraud, opaque pricing, consent abuse and exclusion through failed authentication. Post-crisis risk aversion can create the opposite error by denying viable small borrowers.

Responsible inclusion therefore requires cash-flow appraisal, transparent annualised cost, suitable products, credit-information safeguards, bank responsibility for agents, assisted alternatives, fraud response and accessible grievance redress. Early-warning systems, honest NPA recognition, proportional capital and fair resolution complete the chain.

The aim is not riskless lending. It is a system that permits productive risk, prices it transparently, absorbs losses and preserves access without socialising every private failure.
