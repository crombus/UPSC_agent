## BASIC LEARNING SESSION

### SESSION 1 - NPA recognition: overdue, out-of-order and product-specific tests

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
CREDIT FACILITY
  |-- term loan: principal/interest overdue > 90 days
  |-- CC/OD: out of order for 90 days
  |     |-- continuously above limit/drawing power; OR
  |     |-- no credits; OR credits cannot cover 90-day interest
  |-- bills purchased/discounted: overdue > 90 days
  `-- agriculture: two crop seasons (short crop) / one (long crop)
          |
          v
      NPA RECOGNITION -> income stops accruing on unrealised basis
```

*Visual purpose: establish the examinable structure before detailed explanation.*

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

Under RBI's Commercial Banks IRACP Directions, 2025, updated 1 July 2026, an NPA is an asset that has ceased to generate income. `Overdue` means unpaid on the due date; `out of order` is the separate CC/OD test.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> NPA recognition is a rule-based accounting event, not a moral judgment on the borrower: the trigger changes with the credit product and must be applied before policy inference.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **IRACP**
- **overdue**
- **out of order**
- **90 days**
- **crop season**
- **borrower-wise classification**
- **income reversal**

#### CORE EXPLANATION

A term-loan instalment becomes overdue on its contractual due date and the loan becomes NPA when principal or interest remains overdue for more than 90 days. A cash-credit or overdraft account uses the out-of-order test, including continuous excess over limit or drawing power, absence of credits for 90 days, or credits insufficient to cover interest debited during the previous 90 days. Bills use the more-than-90-day overdue test. Agricultural advances use two crop seasons for short-duration crops and one crop season for long-duration crops. Classification is generally borrower-wise within the bank, not merely facility-wise. Security value does not ordinarily postpone recognition.

#### CLAIM -> NAMED EVIDENCE -> ANALYSIS -> QUALIFICATION

- **Claim:** Recognition must follow the contractual facility rather than a vague perception of distress.
- **Named evidence:** RBI Commercial Banks IRACP Directions, 2025, updated 1 July 2026, separately define overdue and the three-part CC/OD out-of-order test.
- **Analysis:** Product-specific triggers prevent a working-capital account with no genuine turnover from appearing regular merely because it stays under a numerical limit.
- **Qualification:** Agricultural, derivative, project and other specified exposures have tailored rules; the 90-day term-loan shorthand is not universal.

#### EVIDENCE, PRELIMS TRAP AND MAINS USE

| Exam tool | Topic-specific use |
|---|---|
| Evidence | RBI Commercial Banks IRACP Directions, 2025, updated 1 July 2026, separately define overdue and the three-part CC/OD out-of-order test. |
| Prelims trap | Overdue starts on the due date, but a term loan becomes NPA only after the prescribed overdue period; an OD/CC account is tested as out of order. |
| Mains use | Open with the recognition rule, then trace income reversal, provisioning, capital pressure and resolution. |

#### MINI RECAP

- NPA is an objective prudential classification.
- Term loans and CC/OD accounts use different tests.
- Recognition generally follows the borrower across facilities in the same bank.

### SESSION 2 - Asset classification: standard, substandard, doubtful and loss

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
PERFORMING ASSET
      |
      v
STANDARD
      |
      | NPA trigger
      v
SUBSTANDARD: NPA <= 12 months
      |
      | remains substandard 12 months
      v
DOUBTFUL: recovery in full becomes highly questionable
      |
      v
LOSS: identified uncollectible; write off or provide 100%
```

*Visual purpose: establish the examinable structure before detailed explanation.*

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

Asset classification grades recognised credit impairment. Under the RBI directions updated 1 July 2026, substandard means NPA for up to twelve months; doubtful follows twelve months in substandard; loss means identified uncollectibility even if not wholly written off.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Asset classification is a ladder of deteriorating recoverability: passage of time matters, but identified loss and realistic security value matter independently.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **standard asset**
- **substandard**
- **doubtful**
- **loss asset**
- **security value**
- **ageing**
- **upgrade**

#### CORE EXPLANATION

A standard asset does not disclose the normal problems associated with NPAs. Once the NPA trigger is met, a loan enters substandard status. Continued weakness moves it to doubtful, where provision on the secured portion rises with time while the uncovered portion is fully provided. A loss asset should be written off; if retained, it requires full provision. Upgradation is not achieved by a token payment: the 2025 Directions require payment of entire arrears of principal and interest across the borrower's facilities with that bank, subject to special restructuring rules.

#### CLAIM -> NAMED EVIDENCE -> ANALYSIS -> QUALIFICATION

- **Claim:** Classification should reveal recoverability rather than preserve an optimistic book value.
- **Named evidence:** The RBI IRACP Directions updated 1 July 2026 define substandard, doubtful and loss assets and require objective, recovery-based classification.
- **Analysis:** A time-and-realisability ladder aligns reported profit and capital with the worsening probability and severity of loss.
- **Qualification:** Ageing alone does not make every asset a loss asset; an identified uncollectible exposure may be classified as loss even before a long ageing history.

#### EVIDENCE, PRELIMS TRAP AND MAINS USE

| Exam tool | Topic-specific use |
|---|---|
| Evidence | The RBI IRACP Directions updated 1 July 2026 define substandard, doubtful and loss assets and require objective, recovery-based classification. |
| Prelims trap | Substandard and doubtful are both NPAs; `loss asset` does not mean that every rupee has already been written off. |
| Mains use | Use the ladder to show why delayed recognition overstates income and postpones recapitalisation. |

#### MINI RECAP

- Standard is performing; the other three are NPA classes.
- Substandard lasts up to twelve months as NPA.
- Loss identification and accounting write-off are related but distinct.

### SESSION 3 - GNPA, NNPA, provisioning, write-off and waiver

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
RECOGNISED BAD LOANS = GROSS NPA
             |
             | less provisions and permitted adjustments
             v
UNPROVIDED STRESS = NET NPA

PROVISION -> charge against profit; asset remains
WRITE-OFF -> accounting removal; recovery may continue
WAIVER    -> legal/policy relinquishment of borrower liability
```

*Visual purpose: establish the examinable structure before detailed explanation.*

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

GNPA measures recognised non-performing exposure before specified deductions; NNPA measures the residual after provisions and permitted adjustments. Provisioning, technical write-off and waiver have different accounting and legal effects.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> A lower net NPA can reflect stronger loss absorption without showing that the underlying borrower paid; therefore gross stock, provisions, write-offs and recoveries must be read together.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **GNPA**
- **NNPA**
- **PCR**
- **provision**
- **technical write-off**
- **waiver**
- **cash recovery**

#### CORE EXPLANATION

The provisioning coverage ratio is provisions relative to gross NPAs. Under the RBI IRACP Directions updated 1 July 2026, loss assets require 100 per cent provision if not written off; doubtful assets require 100 per cent on the unsecured portion and 25, 40 or 100 per cent on the secured portion as doubtful ageing moves through up to one year, one-to-three years and over three years. A general 15 per cent provision applies to substandard assets; qualifying unsecured substandard exposure attracts 25 per cent. A technical write-off removes the exposure at bank-accounting level without waiving the claim at borrower level.

#### CLAIM -> NAMED EVIDENCE -> ANALYSIS -> QUALIFICATION

- **Claim:** Reported asset quality improves durably only when recognition and loss absorption are separated from cosmetic balance-sheet reduction.
- **Named evidence:** RBI's 2025 IRACP Directions, updated 1 July 2026, define PCR and technical write-off and prescribe category-specific provisioning.
- **Analysis:** Provisioning reduces current profit and builds a loss buffer; write-off can reduce headline GNPA while recovery rights continue, so neither proves cash recovery.
- **Qualification:** Provisioning rates vary for specified exposures; use the general commercial-bank table only within its stated perimeter.

#### EVIDENCE, PRELIMS TRAP AND MAINS USE

| Exam tool | Topic-specific use |
|---|---|
| Evidence | RBI's 2025 IRACP Directions, updated 1 July 2026, define PCR and technical write-off and prescribe category-specific provisioning. |
| Prelims trap | Provision is not a cash reserve kept outside the bank, technical write-off is not waiver, and NNPA is not GNPA minus every provision in the accounts. |
| Mains use | Diagnose a falling NPA ratio through recoveries, upgrades, write-offs, sales and denominator growth before crediting one reform. |

#### MINI RECAP

- GNPA is before specified deductions; NNPA is the uncovered residual.
- Provision affects profit and capital.
- Write-off need not extinguish borrower liability.

### SESSION 4 - Why NPAs arise and how they damage credit transmission

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
WEAK APPRAISAL / PROJECT DELAY / SHOCK / GOVERNANCE FAILURE
                         |
                         v
                 BORROWER CASH-FLOW GAP
                         |
                         v
              DEFAULT -> NPA -> PROVISION
                         |
        +----------------+----------------+
        v                                 v
 lower bank profit/capital          weak borrower investment
        |                                 |
        +----------> weak fresh credit <--+
                    TWIN BALANCE SHEET
```

*Visual purpose: establish the examinable structure before detailed explanation.*

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

The NPA cycle is the interaction between borrower repayment failure, lender loss recognition and reduced capacity or willingness to extend new credit.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Bad loans are both a legacy stock and a flow problem: resolution cleans yesterday's balance sheet, but appraisal and governance determine whether tomorrow's stress is recreated.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **credit appraisal**
- **project delay**
- **leverage**
- **ICR**
- **Twin Balance Sheet**
- **capital erosion**
- **credit channel**

#### CORE EXPLANATION

NPAs can arise from macroeconomic slowdown, commodity or exchange shocks, land and clearance delays, weak demand, over-optimistic projections, connected lending, diversion, fraud or poor monitoring. Interest Coverage Ratio compares earnings available for interest with interest expense; a value below one signals weak servicing capacity but is not the legal NPA test. The Economic Survey 2016-17's Twin Balance Sheet diagnosis linked over-leveraged corporates with stressed banks. Provisions depress profit, losses consume capital, management becomes risk-averse and viable firms can face reduced credit.

#### CLAIM -> NAMED EVIDENCE -> ANALYSIS -> QUALIFICATION

- **Claim:** A banking NPA cycle can amplify a real-sector investment slowdown.
- **Named evidence:** The Economic Survey 2016-17 identified mutually reinforcing corporate leverage and bank stress as the Twin Balance Sheet problem.
- **Analysis:** Weak cash flows raise defaults; recognition and provisions reduce bank capital; constrained credit then slows investment and borrower recovery.
- **Qualification:** The diagnosis belongs to a historical episode and cannot substitute for current bank-level data or prove that every NPA resulted from corporate misconduct.

#### EVIDENCE, PRELIMS TRAP AND MAINS USE

| Exam tool | Topic-specific use |
|---|---|
| Evidence | The Economic Survey 2016-17 identified mutually reinforcing corporate leverage and bank stress as the Twin Balance Sheet problem. |
| Prelims trap | ICR is a financial-stress indicator, not RBI's legal classification trigger; a fall in GNPA ratio may partly reflect a larger denominator. |
| Mains use | Build a causal chain from project economics to bank capital and aggregate credit, then add governance and macro-shock qualifications. |

#### MINI RECAP

- NPAs have borrower, bank, governance and macro causes.
- ICR signals servicing capacity.
- Twin Balance Sheet is a diagnostic, not a statute.

### SESSION 5 - Restructuring, forbearance and evergreening

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
BORROWER IN FINANCIAL DIFFICULTY
          |
          +--> viable + transparent concession
          |       -> RESTRUCTURING
          |       -> recognition/provisioning continues
          |
          +--> temporary system-wide shock
          |       -> bounded FORBEARANCE
          |
          `--> fresh accommodation only to hide old default
                  -> EVERGREENING -> delayed loss + zombie credit
```

*Visual purpose: establish the examinable structure before detailed explanation.*

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

Restructuring grants a concession because of borrower financial difficulty; forbearance is regulatory relief from normal treatment; evergreening conceals stress through fresh or engineered finance without restoring viability.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> The test is economic viability and transparent recognition, not the label `restructured`: genuine repair changes sustainable cash flows, while evergreening merely changes the appearance of repayment.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **restructuring**
- **financial difficulty**
- **concession**
- **forbearance**
- **evergreening**
- **viability**
- **satisfactory performance**

#### CORE EXPLANATION

The RBI Commercial Banks Resolution of Stressed Assets Directions, 2025, updated 1 July 2026, define restructuring broadly to include changes in payment period, amount, instalments, interest, rollover or additional finance linked to difficulty. A standard account is generally downgraded to substandard on restructuring, and an NPA retains its class; upgrade requires satisfactory performance under the prescribed monitoring period. Temporary forbearance may prevent a transitory shock from causing needless failure, but repeated accommodation can capitalise unpaid interest and hide a non-viable borrower.

#### CLAIM -> NAMED EVIDENCE -> ANALYSIS -> QUALIFICATION

- **Claim:** Restructuring is credible only when the prudential cost of recognising borrower weakness is not suspended indefinitely.
- **Named evidence:** RBI's current 2025 Resolution Directions preserve asset classification during plan preparation and specify downgrade and performance conditions after restructuring.
- **Analysis:** Recognition forces lenders to compare the present value of a viable plan with recovery or insolvency instead of refinancing solely to avoid an NPA.
- **Qualification:** Not every additional facility is evergreening; working capital or interim finance can preserve viable enterprise value when independently appraised and transparently monitored.

#### EVIDENCE, PRELIMS TRAP AND MAINS USE

| Exam tool | Topic-specific use |
|---|---|
| Evidence | RBI's current 2025 Resolution Directions preserve asset classification during plan preparation and specify downgrade and performance conditions after restructuring. |
| Prelims trap | Restructured does not mean fraud, but a restructure does not automatically keep a standard classification. |
| Mains use | Contrast temporary relief with permanent concealment through viability, provisioning, monitoring and governance tests. |

#### MINI RECAP

- Restructuring changes contractual terms due to difficulty.
- Forbearance changes regulatory treatment temporarily.
- Evergreening hides rather than cures stress.

### SESSION 6 - Risk-weighted assets and the capital-adequacy denominator

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
BANK EXPOSURES
  |-- credit risk -> borrower/collateral/rating weights
  |-- market risk -> trading-price and rate movements
  `-- operational risk -> process, people, systems, external events
                  |
                  v
          RISK-WEIGHTED ASSETS (RWA)
                  |
                  v
CRAR = eligible regulatory capital / RWA x 100
not capital / total assets
```

*Visual purpose: establish the examinable structure before detailed explanation.*

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

Risk-weighted assets convert different credit, market and operational exposures into a common prudential denominator; CRAR compares eligible regulatory capital with that denominator.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Basel capital is risk-sensitive rather than asset-counting: two banks of equal size can require different capital because their exposure mix and risk weights differ.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **RWA**
- **credit risk**
- **market risk**
- **operational risk**
- **CRAR**
- **capital adequacy**
- **denominator**

#### CORE EXPLANATION

A sovereign exposure, a mortgage, an unrated corporate loan and a trading position need not receive identical risk treatment. Capital rules apply standardised or approved model methods to create RWA. CRAR then measures eligible capital divided by RWA. This protects solvency by forcing shareholders and qualifying instruments to absorb losses before depositors, but risk weights can be backward-looking, model-sensitive or vulnerable to optimisation. A zero or low risk weight does not mean zero economic risk.

#### CLAIM -> NAMED EVIDENCE -> ANALYSIS -> QUALIFICATION

- **Claim:** The denominator is as important as the capital numerator.
- **Named evidence:** The Basel framework and RBI's 1 April 2025 Master Circular organise minimum capital around credit, market and operational RWA.
- **Analysis:** If measured risk falls without a genuine reduction in exposure, a bank can report a stronger ratio without adding loss-absorbing capital.
- **Qualification:** Risk-weighting improves sensitivity but needs leverage, concentration, stress-testing and supervisory backstops.

#### EVIDENCE, PRELIMS TRAP AND MAINS USE

| Exam tool | Topic-specific use |
|---|---|
| Evidence | The Basel framework and RBI's 1 April 2025 Master Circular organise minimum capital around credit, market and operational RWA. |
| Prelims trap | CRAR is not capital divided by total assets, and a high ratio can result from lower RWA as well as higher capital. |
| Mains use | Explain both numerator quality and denominator measurement before evaluating recapitalisation or regulatory relief. |

#### MINI RECAP

- RWA weights exposures by regulatory risk.
- CRAR uses RWA, not total assets.
- Risk weights need non-risk-based and supervisory checks.

### SESSION 7 - Basel I, II and III: evolution and three pillars

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
BASEL I (1988)
credit-risk capital floor
       |
       v
BASEL II
Pillar 1: minimum capital
Pillar 2: supervisory review
Pillar 3: disclosure/market discipline
       |
       v
BASEL III
better capital quality + buffers + leverage + liquidity
response to crisis lessons, not a world banking licence
```

*Visual purpose: establish the examinable structure before detailed explanation.*

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

The Basel accords are international supervisory standards developed by the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision; national authorities implement them through domestic law and regulation.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Basel evolved from a simple capital floor to a multi-layered resilience architecture, but implementation remains national and supervision cannot be outsourced to a ratio.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **BCBS**
- **Basel I**
- **Basel II**
- **Basel III**
- **Pillar 1**
- **Pillar 2**
- **Pillar 3**

#### CORE EXPLANATION

Basel I concentrated on credit-risk capital. Basel II made capital more risk-sensitive and organised regulation around minimum requirements, supervisory review and market discipline. Basel III responded to the global financial crisis by improving the quality of capital, introducing conservation and countercyclical buffers, adding a leverage backstop and establishing liquidity standards. The Basel Committee does not license Indian banks or directly enforce Indian law; RBI applies the framework to the covered domestic perimeter.

#### CLAIM -> NAMED EVIDENCE -> ANALYSIS -> QUALIFICATION

- **Claim:** Basel III supplements rather than replaces supervision.
- **Named evidence:** BCBS standards combine minimum capital, supervisory review and disclosure, while RBI's dated circular specifies their Indian application.
- **Analysis:** Capital absorbs unexpected loss, disclosure supports discipline and supervisors address risks that formulas or markets miss.
- **Qualification:** International minima are floors, not identical national requirements, and differentiated Indian bank categories may follow separate directions.

#### EVIDENCE, PRELIMS TRAP AND MAINS USE

| Exam tool | Topic-specific use |
|---|---|
| Evidence | BCBS standards combine minimum capital, supervisory review and disclosure, while RBI's dated circular specifies their Indian application. |
| Prelims trap | Pillar 2 is supervisory review, not market disclosure; Pillar 3 is disclosure, not deposit insurance. |
| Mains use | Use the evolution to explain why post-crisis reform added capital quality, buffers, leverage and liquidity to risk-weighted minima. |

#### MINI RECAP

- Basel is a standard-setting framework.
- Basel II established three pillars.
- Basel III strengthened capital and liquidity resilience.

### SESSION 8 - CET1, AT1, Tier 2, buffers and India's dated capital minima

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
LOSS-ABSORPTION QUALITY
highest
  CET1: common equity + disclosed reserves - deductions
    |
  AT1: perpetual going-concern instruments
    |
  TIER 2: gone-concern/subordinated instruments
lowest

RBI SCB perimeter, Master Circular 01-04-2025
CET1 5.5% | Tier 1 7% | Total CRAR 9% | CCB 2.5% CET1
Effective with full CCB: CET1 8% | Total 11.5%
```

*Visual purpose: establish the examinable structure before detailed explanation.*

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

CET1 is the highest-quality going-concern capital; Additional Tier 1 is subordinated perpetual going-concern capital; Tier 2 provides lower-ranking gone-concern loss absorption. Buffers sit above minimum requirements.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Capital adequacy is not merely more capital: its quality, permanence, subordination, trigger features and position in the loss hierarchy determine whether it works in stress.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **CET1**
- **AT1**
- **Tier 2**
- **going concern**
- **gone concern**
- **CCB**
- **CCyB**
- **D-SIB surcharge**

#### CORE EXPLANATION

For scheduled commercial banks covered by RBI's Basel III Master Circular dated 1 April 2025, the standalone minima are CET1 5.5 per cent, Tier 1 capital 7 per cent and total CRAR 9 per cent, each as a share of RWA and before the Capital Conservation Buffer. The fully phased CCB is 2.5 per cent in CET1, producing effective CET1 of 8 per cent and total capital of 11.5 per cent. India has a Countercyclical Capital Buffer framework up to 2.5 per cent, but RBI had not activated it in that circular. Applicable D-SIB surcharges are additional and bank-specific.

#### CLAIM -> NAMED EVIDENCE -> ANALYSIS -> QUALIFICATION

- **Claim:** The quality of capital determines how early it can absorb loss.
- **Named evidence:** RBI's Basel III Master Circular dated 1 April 2025 places common equity at the core and specifies higher Indian minima than the Basel global floors.
- **Analysis:** CET1 absorbs losses while a bank remains operating; lower-ranking instruments add capacity but their contractual triggers and hierarchy matter.
- **Qualification:** These figures apply to the circular's stated SCB perimeter, excluding SFBs, Payments Banks and RRBs, which follow category-specific requirements.

#### EVIDENCE, PRELIMS TRAP AND MAINS USE

| Exam tool | Topic-specific use |
|---|---|
| Evidence | RBI's Basel III Master Circular dated 1 April 2025 places common equity at the core and specifies higher Indian minima than the Basel global floors. |
| Prelims trap | The Basel global 8 per cent total floor is not India's 9 per cent standalone CRAR, and CCB must not be silently counted twice. |
| Mains use | Separate minimum capital, conservation buffer, countercyclical buffer and systemic surcharge before judging resilience. |

#### MINI RECAP

- CET1 is the strongest capital layer.
- India's dated standalone CRAR minimum is 9%.
- The 2.5% CCB is met with CET1.

### SESSION 9 - Leverage Ratio, LCR and NSFR: three non-identical safeguards

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
SOLVENCY AND LIQUIDITY BACKSTOPS

Leverage Ratio = Tier 1 capital / exposure measure
  -> non-risk-weighted balance-sheet backstop

LCR = stock of HQLA / 30-day net cash outflows >= 100%
  -> short stress survival

NSFR = available stable funding / required stable funding >= 100%
  -> one-year structural funding discipline
```

*Visual purpose: establish the examinable structure before detailed explanation.*

#### DEFINITION / WHAT THIS IS CALLED

The leverage ratio constrains total exposure without risk weights; the Liquidity Coverage Ratio tests a 30-day stress buffer; the Net Stable Funding Ratio tests stable funding over a one-year horizon.

#### ANSWER-GRABBING OPENING — WRITE/ADAPT IN THE EXAM

> Capital protects against loss, while liquidity protects against timing failure; a solvent bank can be illiquid and a liquid bank can still be insolvent.

#### MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS

- **leverage ratio**

#### Model answer

<!-- MODEL-ANSWER-START:M2 -->
Provisioning recognises expected loss by charging bank income while the loan remains on the balance sheet. RBI's IRACP Directions updated 1 July 2026 link the provision to asset class, ageing, security and realisable value. It therefore protects capital credibility before final recovery is known.

A technical write-off removes all or part of the NPA from bank-level accounting without waiving the claim at borrower level. Consideration follows a 15:85 cash-to-Security-Receipt structure. IDRCL supplies resolution expertise under the agreed arrangement, while a Government guarantee of up to Rs 30,600 crore supports eligible SR shortfalls within the specified five-year design. DFS's Annual Report 2024-25 records the mechanism as operational.

The model offers four gains. Aggregation can solve coordination problems across lenders. Specialist teams can focus on restructuring, enforcement or sale. Banks receive some cash and can redirect management attention to new credit. Common ownership among public-sector sellers can accelerate decisions.

However, a transfer is not recovery. An inflated acquisition price postpones loss recognition; a low price can generate immediate bank losses. The 85 per cent SR component leaves selling banks exposed to realisation. A guarantee may create moral hazard if due diligence weakens. IDRCL-NARCL role clarity, professional independence, information quality and a deep buyer market are therefore decisive.

NARCL should be judged by transparent valuation, time to resolution, net present recovery, SR redemption and governance—not gross assets transferred. It complements, rather than replaces, IBC, SARFAESI, DRTs, ARCs, bank provisioning and better credit appraisal.

<!-- MODEL-ANSWER-START:M6 -->
Financial inclusion lowers transaction costs, mobilises savings, improves payment resilience and connects households and small firms to formal credit. PMJDY, launched on 28 August 2014, uses basic accounts, RuPay, transfers and Business Correspondents. RBI's BSBD amendment dated 4 December 2025, effective 1 April 2026, strengthens zero-minimum-balance service. Priority-sector rules, MUDRA, SHG-Bank Linkage and JLGs address sector, collateral and information gaps.

These measures can strengthen stability. Broader deposits diversify funding; digital records can improve underwriting; regular saving and insurance reduce household vulnerability. RBI's FI-Index—Access, Usage and Quality—correctly treats opening an account as only the first stage.

Yet poorly governed inclusion can manufacture stress. Target-chasing may weaken appraisal; guarantees can reduce screening; multiple micro-loans can create over-indebtedness; group pressure can become coercive; digital journeys can enable fraud, opaque pricing, consent abuse and exclusion through failed authentication. Post-crisis risk aversion can create the opposite error by denying viable small borrowers.

Responsible inclusion therefore requires cash-flow appraisal, transparent annualised cost, suitable products, credit-information safeguards, bank responsibility for agents, assisted alternatives, fraud response and accessible grievance redress. Early-warning systems, honest NPA recognition, proportional capital and fair resolution complete the chain.

The aim is not riskless lending. It is a system that permits productive risk, prices it transparently, absorbs losses and preserves access without socialising every private failure.

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

### 1. Expected loss, unexpected loss and capital

Provisioning and capital cover different layers. Provisions recognise expected credit loss associated with the loan book; regulatory capital absorbs unexpected losses and protects solvency. If recognition is delayed, stated profit and capital can both be overstated. The analytical sequence is therefore classification -> provision -> retained earnings/capital -> lending capacity.

### 2. Stock-flow and denominator effects

GNPA is a stock measured relative to gross advances. Its ratio may fall because bad loans are recovered, upgraded, sold or written off, or because advances grow faster. Credit-cost and slippage flows reveal new stress; recovery and write-off flows explain exit. A single ratio cannot identify the mechanism.

### 3. Macroprudential interaction

Microprudential regulation asks whether one bank can absorb loss. Macroprudential regulation asks whether correlated lending, common collateral, fire sales and procyclical deleveraging amplify stress. CCyB, stress tests, sectoral risk weights and D-SIB buffers address different system dimensions.

### 4. Resolution value and distribution

Recovery percentage must be assessed against liquidation value, admitted claims, time, legal cost and counterfactual enterprise value. A haircut can preserve more going-concern value than piecemeal liquidation, while a delayed plan with nominally high recovery can have weak present value. Workers, operational creditors, secured lenders and public finances can bear different consequences.

### 5. Inclusion measurement

Access is availability, Usage is actual engagement, and Quality includes suitability, literacy and protection. Aggregate improvement can conceal regional, gender, disability and digital divides. Distributional analysis should therefore combine the FI-Index architecture with disaggregated operational evidence rather than one headline number.


## CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

### 1. Recognition and classification

- RBI Commercial Banks IRACP Directions, 2025, updated 1 July 2026: overdue means unpaid on due date.
- Term loan: interest or principal overdue for more than 90 days.
- CC/OD out of order: continuously above limit/drawing power for 90 days; or no credits for 90 days; or credits cannot cover interest debited during the previous 90 days.
- Bills purchased/discounted: overdue for more than 90 days.
- Agriculture: two crop seasons for short-duration crops; one crop season for long-duration crops.
- Classification is generally borrower-wise within a bank. Security does not ordinarily postpone recognition.
- Substandard: NPA for up to twelve months. Doubtful: remained substandard for twelve months. Loss: identified uncollectible although not wholly written off.

### 2. GNPA, NNPA and loss absorption

- GNPA = recognised gross non-performing exposure.
- NNPA = residual after prescribed provisions and adjustments; use the official formula, not `GNPA minus every provision`.
- PCR = provisions / GNPA.
- General NPA provisions under the directions updated 1 July 2026:

| Category | General provision |
|---|---:|
| Substandard, secured/general | 15% |
| Substandard, unsecured | 25% |
| Doubtful unsecured portion | 100% |
| Doubtful secured portion up to 1 year | 25% |
| Doubtful secured portion 1-3 years | 40% |
| Doubtful secured portion over 3 years | 100% |
| Loss asset retained in books | 100% |

- Provision = charge against income. Technical write-off = bank-level accounting removal without waiver. Waiver = relinquishment of claim to the stated extent.
- Falling GNPA ratio may reflect recovery, upgrade, sale, write-off or denominator growth.

### 3. Stress, restructuring and governance

- Causes: weak appraisal, project delay, leverage, macro shock, fraud/diversion, connected lending and poor monitoring.
- ICR = earnings available for interest / interest expense. It is a warning indicator, not NPA law.
- Twin Balance Sheet: Economic Survey 2016-17 diagnosis of stressed corporates and banks.
- Restructuring grants concessions because of financial difficulty. It does not automatically preserve standard classification.
- Evergreening hides old stress with fresh accommodation; temporary forbearance must remain bounded.
- RBI Wilful Defaulters Directions dated 30 July 2024: deliberate capacity-based non-payment, diversion, siphoning, unauthorised disposal or specified commitment failure; Rs 25 lakh outstanding threshold.
- RBI Fraud Directions dated 15 July 2024: separate fraud-risk process, EWS/RFA, show cause, at least 21 days to reply and reasoned order.

### 4. Basel and capital

| Layer | Meaning |
|---|---|
| CET1 | highest-quality going-concern common equity |
| AT1 | perpetual subordinated going-concern instruments |
| Tier 2 | gone-concern/subordinated loss absorption |
| RWA | credit + market + operational risk-weighted exposure |
| CRAR | eligible capital / RWA x 100 |

- Basel I: initial credit-risk capital floor. Basel II: Pillars 1, 2 and 3. Basel III: stronger capital quality, buffers, leverage and liquidity.
- RBI Basel III Master Circular dated 1 April 2025, covered SCBs: CET1 5.5%; Tier 1 7%; total CRAR 9%, before buffers.
- CCB 2.5% CET1 -> effective CET1 8% and total capital 11.5%.
- CCyB framework up to 2.5%; not activated in the cited 1 April 2025 circular. D-SIB surcharge is additional and bank-specific.
- Perimeter excludes SFBs, Payments Banks and RRBs from that circular; use category-specific rules.

### 5. Leverage and liquidity

- Leverage Ratio = Tier 1 / exposure measure. RBI circular 28 June 2019, effective 1 October 2019: 4% for D-SIBs, 3.5% for other covered banks.
- LCR = HQLA / 30-day stressed net cash outflows; minimum 100% from 1 January 2019.
- NSFR = available stable funding / required stable funding; minimum 100% from 1 October 2021.
- Capital addresses solvency loss; liquidity addresses timing. Each ratio has a separate denominator and horizon.

### 6. Supervision and out-of-court resolution

- SMA-0: up to 30 days; SMA-1: more than 30 to 60; SMA-2: more than 60 to 90 under current commercial-bank resolution directions.
- AQR reveals classification divergence; it does not create the underlying loss.
- PCA dated 2 November 2021/effective 1 January 2022: capital, NNPA and leverage; NNPA bands begin at 6%, 9% and 12%; preventive, not liquidation.
- Commercial Banks Resolution of Stressed Assets Directions, 2025, updated 1 July 2026:
  - Review Period: 30 days.
  - ICA: 75% by value + 60% by number of signatories.
  - Independent credit evaluation from Rs 100 crore; two evaluations from Rs 500 crore; RP4 or better.
  - Covered-account RP clock: 180 days from end of Review Period.
  - Delay: 20% additional provision at 180-day point; another 15% at 365 days from Review Period commencement.
  - Asset classification continues while a plan is considered.

### 7. Recovery and insolvency institutions

| Route | Institution/core purpose | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| SARFAESI Act, 2002 | secured creditor enforces security; s.13(2) gives 60-day notice | not complete insolvency |
| RDB Act, 1993 | DRT adjudication and recovery; DRAT appeal | not CoC/NCLT |
| ARC | acquires and resolves financial assets; may issue SRs | transfer does not erase loss |
| IBC, 2016 | collective CIRP before NCLT | admission is not resolution |
| IBBI | regulates insolvency ecosystem | does not decide individual CIRP |

- IBC section 14: bounded moratorium after admission.
- CoC: financial-creditor voting by debt share; plan needs at least 66% under section 30(4); NCLT approval under section 31.
- Liquidation follows statutory triggers and section 53 waterfall. Haircut must be judged against time and liquidation/going-concern value.
- Personal-guarantor provisions became operational 1 December 2019; section 60 aligns forum with NCLT. Corporate and guarantor moratoria are not identical.

### 8. NARCL-IDRCL and asset transfer

- Architecture announced in 2021; DFS Annual Report 2024-25 records it as operational.
- NARCL = RBI-registered ARC and acquirer; IDRCL = resolution manager.
- Consideration: 15% cash + 85% Security Receipts.
- Government guarantee envelope up to Rs 30,600 crore supports eligible SR shortfall within the specified five-year design.
- Judge by transfer valuation, resolution time, net present recovery, SR redemption and governance. Do not treat acquisition as recovery.

### 9. Financial inclusion

- PMJDY launched 28 August 2014: basic account/payments/DBT platform; eligible overdraft up to Rs 10,000 and dated RuPay insurance conditions.
- RBI BSBD amendment dated 4 December 2025, effective 1 April 2026: no minimum balance, listed free facilities and at least four free withdrawals; specified digital payments excluded from that count.
- BC = bank agent; principal bank remains responsible.
- PSL Directions effective 1 April 2025: most domestic commercial banks 40% of ANBC/CEOBE; SFB 60% from FY 2025-26 under 20 June 2025 circular.
- PMMY: Shishu up to Rs 50,000; Kishore above Rs 50,000 to Rs 5 lakh; Tarun above Rs 5 lakh to Rs 10 lakh; Tarun Plus above Rs 10 lakh to Rs 20 lakh for eligible successful Tarun borrowers from 24 October 2024.
- NABARD SHG-Bank Linkage pilot began in 1992. SHG uses group savings/credit; JLG uses mutual liability. Neither is a bank.
- RBI FI-Index release dated 22 July 2025: Access, Usage and Quality.

### 10. Final answer spine

1. Define the asset, institution and dated perimeter.
2. Trace overdue/default -> recognition -> provision -> capital/liquidity effect.
3. Choose recovery, restructuring, transfer or collective insolvency by viability and security.
4. Assign RBI, DRT, NCLT, IBBI, ARC, NARCL/IDRCL and DICGC correctly.
5. Evaluate value, time, distribution, moral hazard and governance.
6. Link inclusion to usage, suitability and consumer protection.
7. Conclude: honest recognition plus credible loss absorption and resolution sustains responsible inclusion.
