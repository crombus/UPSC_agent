# Foreign Trade, WTO, FTAs and Protectionism - Solved Practice Workbook

## BASIC MCQS / REMEDIATION

### MCQ 1

Comparative advantage is determined primarily by:

A. Relative opportunity cost
B. Absolute output alone
C. The bilateral trade balance
D. The level of the exchange-rate reserve

**Answer: A.**

**Option-wise explanation:**
- **A - Correct:** This is correct because comparative advantage compares the opportunity cost of producing one good relative to another. Option text: Relative opportunity cost
- **B - Incorrect:** Absolute output identifies productivity scale, not the relative sacrifice that determines comparative advantage. Option text: Absolute output alone
- **C - Incorrect:** A trade balance is an outcome and does not define the production-cost basis of comparative advantage. Option text: The bilateral trade balance
- **D - Incorrect:** Reserve holdings affect resilience but do not determine the Ricardian opportunity-cost comparison. Option text: The level of the exchange-rate reserve

**Examiner trap 1: Fix relative opportunity cost before comparing absolute productivity. Focus: comparative advantage is determined primarily by:.**

### MCQ 2

Which statement best captures gains from trade?

A. Every household gains immediately
B. Aggregate gains can coexist with concentrated adjustment losses
C. Only exporters can gain
D. Imports reduce national welfare by definition

**Answer: B.**

**Option-wise explanation:**
- **A - Incorrect:** Trade theory does not remove distributional conflict, mobility costs or transition lags. Option text: Every household gains immediately
- **B - Correct:** This is correct because lower prices and specialisation gains can occur alongside worker, firm and regional displacement. Option text: Aggregate gains can coexist with concentrated adjustment losses
- **C - Incorrect:** Consumers and imported-input users can gain even when they do not export directly. Option text: Only exporters can gain
- **D - Incorrect:** Imports can expand consumption possibilities and productive input access, so their value is not intrinsically a welfare loss. Option text: Imports reduce national welfare by definition

**Examiner trap 2: Separate aggregate welfare from distribution and transition. Focus: which statement best captures gains from trade.**

### MCQ 3

An improvement in commodity terms of trade means:

A. Export volume necessarily rises
B. The merchandise balance necessarily becomes positive
C. Export prices rise relative to import prices
D. The currency necessarily appreciates

**Answer: C.**

**Option-wise explanation:**
- **A - Incorrect:** Terms of trade is a price ratio and does not mechanically determine export quantity. Option text: Export volume necessarily rises
- **B - Incorrect:** A price-ratio improvement can coexist with a deficit when quantities and values move differently. Option text: The merchandise balance necessarily becomes positive
- **C - Correct:** This is correct because the index is export-price index divided by import-price index, usually multiplied by 100. Option text: Export prices rise relative to import prices
- **D - Incorrect:** Exchange-rate movement can influence prices but is not the definition of terms of trade. Option text: The currency necessarily appreciates

**Examiner trap 3: Do not substitute a quantity or balance concept for a price ratio. Focus: an improvement in commodity terms of trade means:.**

### MCQ 4

A. Higher-cost domestic output to be replaced by lower-cost partner imports
B. Exports to rise in every member
C. All external tariffs to become common

**Answer: D.**

**Option-wise explanation:**
- **A - Incorrect:** That is trade creation, because inefficient domestic production is displaced. Option text: Higher-cost domestic output to be replaced by lower-cost partner imports
- **B - Incorrect:** Export growth does not by itself identify the counterfactual sourcing effect. Option text: Exports to rise in every member
- **C - Incorrect:** A common external tariff defines a customs union, not trade diversion. Option text: All external tariffs to become common
- **D - Correct:** This is correct because preference, rather than productive efficiency, redirects sourcing toward the partner. Option text: A lower-cost non-member source to be replaced by a higher-cost partner

**Examiner trap 4: Use the counterfactual source, not observed partner growth alone. Focus: trade diversion occurs when an fta causes:.**

### VERIFIED MAINS PYQ - 2018 GS-III

**Question:** How would the recent phenomena of protectionism and currency manipulations in world trade affect macroeconomic stability of India? (15 marks, 250 words)

**Model solution:** Protectionism can reduce India's export demand, disrupt supply chains and raise imported-input costs through retaliation. Currency manipulation by major economies can redirect capital, alter competitiveness and increase exchange-rate volatility. The macroeconomic transmission runs through the trade balance, inflation, corporate margins, investment and external financing. India can also gain from trade diversion if firms are competitive and logistics and standards permit rapid market entry. A balanced response combines WTO engagement, diversified FTAs, lawful remedies, reserve and macroeconomic credibility, competitively priced inputs and worker adjustment. The qualification is decisive: not every currency movement is manipulation, and the effect of a tariff depends on coverage, retaliation, pass-through and India's position in the value chain.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 - 10 MARKS

**Question:** Distinguish MFN treatment, national treatment and an FTA preference. Answer in 150 words.

**Model answer:**

MFN treatment requires an advantage granted to one WTO member to extend to like trade from all members, subject to agreement-specific exceptions.

National treatment compares imported and domestic products, services or intellectual property after market entry. An ordinary border tariff is therefore not itself a national-treatment violation; discriminatory internal taxation or regulation is the central concern.

An FTA preference gives qualifying partner trade better access under schedules and rules of origin. WTO law permits such regional exceptions through routes including GATT Article XXIV, GATS Article V and the Enabling Clause.

The answer line is: MFN compares foreign partners, national treatment compares foreign and domestic treatment after entry, and an FTA preference is a conditional exception. Coverage, likeness and legal status must still be checked.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 - 10 MARKS

**Question:** Distinguish anti-dumping, countervailing and safeguard measures. Answer in 150 words.

**Model answer:**

Anti-dumping duty responds to export price below normal value when an investigation proves material injury and causation. Countervailing duty instead offsets a specific foreign subsidy that benefits imports and injures domestic industry. Safeguards require neither dumping nor subsidy: they address serious injury from increased, fairly traded imports.

WTO agreements and India's Customs Tariff Act keep these routes separate. DGTR investigates, government notifies duties, and CBIC collects them. Their evidentiary thresholds are: dumping margin for anti-dumping, subsidy and benefit for countervailing action, and import surge with serious injury for safeguards.

All are conditional remedies, not permanent protection. Product scope, period, causation, review and sunset matter; safeguards additionally provide adjustment space. Therefore low price alone proves none of the three, and one remedy's test cannot be borrowed to justify another.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 - 15 MARKS

**Question:** Explain why effective protection and tariff escalation matter for manufacturing strategy. Answer in 250 words.

**Model answer:**

A final-good tariff shows nominal protection, but firms compete on value added after paying for tradable inputs. Effective protection therefore measures the percentage change in domestic value added produced by the combined tariff structure. When an output receives a high tariff and its imported inputs face low duties, effective protection can exceed the nominal rate. When essential inputs are heavily taxed, the reverse can occur.

This distinction matters for manufacturing strategy. A tariff intended to support an upstream producer may raise the cost of steel, electronics, chemicals or machinery used by downstream exporters. The protected stage gains a margin while later stages lose price competitiveness, scale and participation in global value chains.

Tariff escalation creates a related international problem. If importing markets levy low duties on raw materials but higher duties on processed products, suppliers in developing economies face weaker incentives to move into value-added exports.

Policy should therefore map input-output links and calculate protection on value added, not celebrate one headline rate. Temporary support may be justified for learning or strategic resilience, but it needs affordable inputs, competition, performance milestones and sunset review. The qualification is that technical coefficients, border prices, exemptions and product classification are required before estimating an effective rate.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 - 15 MARKS

**Question:** Assess the WTO's relevance amid impaired appellate review and fragmented trade rules. Answer in 250 words.

**Model answer:**

The WTO remains the legal framework for goods, services, intellectual property, notifications, negotiations and dispute panels, but enforcement credibility is weakened. Its member-driven structure supplies common principles such as MFN, national treatment, tariff bindings and transparency. These rules give smaller economies a reference point against arbitrary bilateral pressure.

The institutional gap is serious. The WTO states that its Appellate Body cannot review appeals because of continuing vacancies; the last sitting member's term ended on 30 November 2020. Panels can still issue reports, yet an appeal into the non-functioning stage may delay binding resolution where parties lack an agreed alternative. The missed reform objective illustrates the difficulty of consensus.

Recent outcomes show both capacity and fragmentation. The Fisheries Subsidies Agreement entered into force on 15 September 2025, but further overcapacity disciplines remain under negotiation. At MC14, members failed to agree on e-commerce, and the WTO-wide moratorium lapsed on 30 March 2026; groups then pursued narrower commitments.

Reform should restore credible review, improve accessibility, discipline notification delays and permit transparent plurilateral progress without marginalising non-participants. India benefits from preserving policy space and development concerns, but also from enforceable rules for exporters. The WTO is therefore impaired, not irrelevant: multilateral legitimacy remains valuable, while unresolved consensus and enforcement problems require institutional repair.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 - 20 MARKS

India's FTA strategy is selective. After declining to join RCEP in November 2019, India brought UAE CEPA and Australia ECTA into force in 2022, EFTA TEPA in October 2025, Oman CEPA in June 2026 and UK CETA in July 2026. By the 9 September 2026 cutoff, India-EU negotiations had concluded, while the signed India-New Zealand FTA still awaited domestic procedures.

This portfolio can diversify markets, improve services access and attract investment. Preferences can also reduce imported-input costs and connect firms to regional value chains. Yet legal access is only the first stage. Exporters must meet product-specific origin rules, standards and documentation; small preference margins or high compliance costs can suppress utilisation.

Evaluation should therefore separate trade creation from diversion and headline commitments from realised additionality. Bilateral trade may rise because of growth, exchange rates or commodity prices rather than the agreement. India should combine negotiated access with customs facilitation, laboratories, logistics, trade finance, skills and consultation with affected sectors. Sensitive products can use calibrated schedules and lawful safeguards rather than permanent exclusion. The strategy succeeds when agreements create competitive capability and resilient diversification, not merely more tariff lines or political announcements.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 - 20 MARKS

**Question:** How should India respond to protectionism while remaining integrated with global value chains? Answer in 250 words.

**Model answer:**

Protectionism abroad transmits to India through weaker export demand, higher tariffs and standards costs, supply-chain relocation, retaliation, inflation and financial-market uncertainty. Currency movements can amplify these channels, but exchange-rate adjustment alone cannot create export capacity.

India needs a two-track response. Externally, it should defend multilateral rules, use consultations and trade remedies lawfully, diversify partners, and negotiate FTAs with usable origin rules and meaningful services access. The current mix of operative UAE, Australia, EFTA, Oman and UK agreements, alongside unfinished EU and New Zealand legal processes, shows selective engagement rather than withdrawal.

Domestically, competitiveness requires reliable power and logistics, efficient customs, quality infrastructure, affordable credit, skilled labour, technology and access to competitively priced inputs. RoDTEP can neutralise eligible embedded taxes, but remission cannot substitute for productivity. Targeted support for strategic or infant sectors should identify the market failure, demand additional investment and learning, preserve competition and expire after review.

Resilience should mean diversified suppliers, inventories for critical inputs, recycling and domestic capability, not indiscriminate autarky. High tariffs on intermediates can protect one producer while taxing downstream exports and consumers. Adjustment assistance for workers and regions makes openness politically sustainable. India should therefore combine predictable rules, capability building and time-bound safeguards, judging policy by value addition, exports, jobs, consumer welfare and reduced concentration risk.
