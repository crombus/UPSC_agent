## OPTIONAL ADVANCED

### Advanced 1 - Ricardo, Heckscher-Ohlin and new trade

```text
Relative cost -> factor endowments -> scale and imperfect competition
```

Ricardo foregrounds relative labour productivity; Heckscher-Ohlin links trade to relative factor abundance; new-trade theory adds scale economies, product differentiation and first-mover effects. These are complementary lenses, not one universal forecast. Technology, institutions, logistics and firm heterogeneity shape observed trade.

### Advanced 2 - Effective protection formula

```text
ERP = (domestic value added - world-price value added) / world-price value added x 100
```

A final-good tariff can coexist with low effective protection if imported inputs are taxed heavily. Use a numerical illustration only as a hypothetical and state the input coefficients.

### Advanced 3 - Optimum-tariff argument

A large country may theoretically improve its terms of trade through a tariff if foreign exporters cut prices. Retaliation, uncertainty, WTO commitments, input costs and political capture make this a narrow theoretical result rather than a general policy prescription.

### Advanced 4 - Gravity and agreement evaluation

Trade is generally larger between economically large and geographically or institutionally proximate partners. An FTA evaluation must therefore use a counterfactual: post-agreement growth may reflect size, cycle, exchange rates or pre-existing proximity.

### Advanced 5 - Rules-of-origin political economy

Strict origin rules can protect regional producers but reduce preference use and create a noodle bowl of overlapping compliance systems. Cumulation, self-certification and risk-based verification can lower cost without abandoning enforcement.

### Advanced 6 - GATT exceptions

GATT Article XX contains general exceptions with listed policy grounds and an anti-abuse chapeau; Article XXI addresses security exceptions. Neither is a free-standing permission to ignore all other legal conditions.

### Advanced 7 - Plurilateralism after MC14

The post-MC14 e-commerce path shows a central distinction: a group commitment or plurilateral agreement can advance rules among participants without becoming a universal multilateral obligation.

### Advanced 8 - Trade-policy evaluation matrix

| Test | Question |
|---|---|
| Efficiency | Does the measure correct a market failure at lowest economy-wide cost? |
| Distribution | Which consumers, workers, regions and firms gain or lose? |
| Capability | Does it create learning, scale, standards and domestic value addition? |
| Legality | Which WTO, FTA and domestic instrument governs it? |
| Resilience | Does it reduce concentration without creating costly autarky? |
| Governance | Are milestones, transparency, review and sunset credible? |

### Mains regulatory architecture

1. Define the economic or legal instrument precisely.
2. Identify the authority: WTO body, Department of Commerce, DGFT, DGTR, Department of Revenue, CBIC or agreement committee.
3. Trace price, quantity, sourcing, investment, employment and consumer channels.
4. Separate aggregate gain from sector, worker, region and distribution effects.
5. Test WTO and FTA legality, notification, evidence and remedy conditions.
6. Use dated status: concluded, signed, ratified, in force, extended or lapsed.
7. Evaluate utilisation, additionality, value addition, standards and adjustment.
8. Conclude with predictable openness plus targeted, reviewable resilience.
