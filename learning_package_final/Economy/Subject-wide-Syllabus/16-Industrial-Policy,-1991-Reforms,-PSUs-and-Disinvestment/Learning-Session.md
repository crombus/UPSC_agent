- **Analysis:** Licensing, standards, procurement, skills, finance and competition can alter firm incentives through different mechanisms.
- **Qualification / demand link:** Each intervention needs a named market failure, measurable result and review rule.

#### EVIDENCE AND INFERENCE

- **Fact/evidence:** The canonical Economy owner connects rules, infrastructure and finance to entry, productivity, jobs and structural transformation.
- **Analytical inference:** Licensing, standards, procurement, skills, finance and competition can alter firm incentives through different mechanisms.

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED

### ADVANCED 1 — Infant-industry test

```text
TEMPORARY LEARNING SUPPORT -> PRODUCTIVITY/EXPORT MILESTONE -> SUNSET
```

Protection is defensible only where learning externalities are plausible, performance is observable and support can end. Otherwise the infant never graduates.

> **Advanced answer line:** Infant-industry policy needs export, productivity or capability discipline rather than age alone.

### ADVANCED 2 — Contestable-market logic

```text
FEW FIRMS + LOW ENTRY/EXIT BARRIERS MAY DISCIPLINE PRICE; MANY FIRMS MAY STILL COLLude
```

Market structure is not the same as competitive conduct. Entry conditions, network access and switching costs matter.

> **Advanced answer line:** Privatisation must preserve contestability, not merely change the owner's identity.

### ADVANCED 3 — Principal-agent chain

```text
CITIZEN -> GOVERNMENT -> ADMIN MINISTRY -> BOARD -> MANAGEMENT
```

Multiple principals, hidden mandates and political intervention can weaken CPSE accountability. Explicit objectives and board responsibility shorten the chain.

> **Advanced answer line:** Public ownership performs best when ownership, regulation and management roles are institutionally separated.

### ADVANCED 4 — Control premium

```text
MINORITY SHARE VALUE + POWER TO APPOINT/MANAGE = CONTROL PREMIUM
```

A controlling stake may command value beyond a pro-rata market price because it transfers decision rights.

> **Advanced answer line:** Strategic-sale valuation must recognise control, liabilities, restrictions and future cash flow together.

### ADVANCED 5 — Productive-development policy

```text
CAPABILITY DIAGNOSIS -> COORDINATION -> EXPERIMENT -> MEASURE -> WITHDRAW/SCALE
```

Modern industrial policy is a discovery process under uncertainty. Transparent experimentation and termination reduce capture.

> **Advanced answer line:** Policy capacity includes the ability to stop a weak intervention, not only launch it.

### ADVANCED 6 — Place-based transition

```text
CPSE/INDUSTRY RESTRUCTURING -> LOCAL JOB/REVENUE SHOCK -> SKILLS + CONNECTIVITY + NEW FIRMS
```

Industrial exit can create regional scarring where one enterprise anchors suppliers, housing and local demand.

> **Advanced answer line:** Worker compensation alone cannot replace a regional transition strategy.

## CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

### 1. Historical spine

- 1948: mixed economy and strategic State domain.
- IDR Act 1951: statutory licensing framework.
- 1956: Schedule A State, B progressive State role, C other private activity under controls.
- MRTP 1969: concentration and pre-entry scrutiny; FERA 1973: foreign-exchange conservation.
- 1977: small/tiny/cottage and district focus; 1980s: modernisation and selective flexibility.
- 24 July 1991: systemic delicensing, PSU, MRTP, FDI and technology reform.

### 2. 1991 distinctions

- Stabilisation restores macro balance; structural reform changes incentives and capacity.
- Liberalisation reduces controls; privatisation transfers private ownership/control.
- Globalisation deepens cross-border integration; deregulation removes or redesigns a rule.
- Delicensing != abolition of environmental, safety, land, competition or sector regulation.
- Historical 51% automatic FDI rule in specified high-priority industries is not today's universal cap.

### 3. Current licensing

- Four compulsory categories: electronic aerospace/defence; industrial explosives; specified hazardous chemicals; cigars/cigarettes and manufactured tobacco substitutes.
- Atomic Energy and core Railway operations remain reserved subject to notified exceptions for mass rapid transport and specified railway infrastructure activities.
- Current list checked 9 September 2026; item scope follows official notifications.

### 4. CPSE map

- Departmental undertaking != statutory corporation != government company. Companies Act section 2(45): at least 51% paid-up share capital held by Central/State government(s), singly or jointly; includes its subsidiary.
- DPE: public-enterprise policy; DIPAM: investment/public-asset transactions.
- Board manages; administrative ministry owns/policymakes; CAG/Parliament/SEBI/sector regulator have specified roles.
- Public-service obligation should be explicit and costed.
- Soft budget constraint = expected rescue weakens discipline.
- Public Enterprises Survey 2024-25 is the current official performance compendium.

### 5. Ratna criteria — current

- Maharatna: Navratna + Indian listing/public shareholding + three-year average turnover >Rs25,000 crore, net worth >Rs15,000 crore, PAT >Rs5,000 crore + significant global presence.
- Navratna: Schedule A + Miniratna-I + Excellent/Very Good MoU ratings in three of the preceding five years + composite score at least 60/100.
- Miniratna-I: profit three consecutive years + positive net worth + PBT at least Rs30 crore in one.
- Miniratna-II: profit three consecutive years + positive net worth.
- DPE monitoring/review instructions checked through 16 June 2026.

### 6. Disinvestment instruments

- Minority sale/OFS/IPO: equity sold, government control retained.
- ETF: basket of CPSE shares. Buyback: company repurchases shares.
- Strategic disinvestment: substantial/entire stake + management control.
- Privatisation: control transferred to private buyer.
- Asset monetisation: time-bound asset operating/revenue rights may transfer while ownership remains public.
- Closure, merger, subsidiarisation and IBC resolution are distinct.

### 7. Current PSE policy and receipts

- New PSE Policy notified 4 February 2021.
- Strategic groups: atomic/space/defence; transport/telecom; power/petroleum/coal/minerals; banking/insurance/financial services.
- Strategic: bare minimum public commercial presence; others may privatise/merge/subsidiarise/close.
- Non-strategic: privatisation where feasible, otherwise closure.
- Receipt Budget: Rs80,000 crore BE 2026-27 and Rs33,837 crore RE 2025-26 under combined miscellaneous capital receipts.
- DIPAM FY2025-26 disinvestment portal receipt: Rs16,885.56 crore, checked 9 September 2026.

### 8. Evaluation and reform

- Gains: entry, scale, competition, technology, consumer choice, FDI and productivity.
- Limits: uneven jobs, inequality, regional concentration, weak domestic R&D and incomplete factor/institution reform.
- CCI: agreements, abuse of dominance and combinations.
- IBC provides corporate resolution/exit; it is not disinvestment policy.
- Modern policy: public goods + measurable capability + competition + review/sunset + transition protection.
- PLI/MSME/semiconductor detail belongs to Topic 17.
