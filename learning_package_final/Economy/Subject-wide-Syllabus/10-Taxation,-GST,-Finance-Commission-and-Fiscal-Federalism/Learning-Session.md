### CORE SESSION 1 — Taxation as a public-finance instrument

#### VISUAL FIRST

```text
TAX BASE -> RATE -> LIABILITY -> COLLECTION -> PUBLIC SPENDING
                 |                 |
                 +-> behaviour ----+-> distribution
```

*Visual reading:* Fix the legal or economic sequence before drawing the policy conclusion.

#### DEFINITION

A tax is a compulsory levy imposed under law to finance public purposes; it is not a price paid for a proportionate individual service.

> **ANSWER-GRABBING LINE:** Tax design must be judged by revenue adequacy, equity, efficiency, certainty, simplicity and administrative feasibility together.

**MUST-WRITE KEYWORDS:** tax base, tax rate, incidence, equity, efficiency, certainty, compliance.

#### HOW TO WRITE THE ANSWER PARAGRAPH

- **Claim:** Tax design must be judged by revenue adequacy, equity, efficiency, certainty, simplicity and administrative feasibility together.
- **Named evidence:** Adam Smith's canons and modern public-finance tests identify fairness, certainty and collection economy as design constraints.
- **Analysis:** In taxation as a public-finance instrument, the named rule supports the specific inference that tax design must be judged by revenue adequacy, equity, efficiency, certainty, simplicity and administrative feasibility together.
- **Qualification / demand link:** A tax can finance a desirable service while still imposing avoidable compliance or efficiency costs.

#### EVIDENCE AND INFERENCE

- **Fact/evidence:** Adam Smith's canons and modern public-finance tests identify fairness, certainty and collection economy as design constraints.
- **Analytical inference:** Tax design must be judged by revenue adequacy, equity, efficiency, certainty, simplicity and administrative feasibility together.
- **Qualification:** A tax can finance a desirable service while still imposing avoidable compliance or efficiency costs.

#### PRELIMS TRAP

A high statutory rate does not by itself prove either high collection or fairness.

#### MAINS USE

## CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

### 1. Tax design

- A good tax balances adequacy, equity, efficiency, certainty, simplicity and administrability.
- Statutory incidence is legal remittance; economic incidence is the final real-income burden.
- Progressive, proportional and regressive describe burden relative to capacity; direct/indirect describes the base and shifting route.
- Buoyancy includes discretionary changes; elasticity attempts to remove them.
- Tax expenditure is benchmarked revenue forgone, not automatically recoverable cash or evasion.

### 2. Constitutional tax spine

- Article 246: ordinary Seventh Schedule distribution; Article 246A: special GST power; Article 248: residuary power subject to 246A.
- Article 269A: Union levy/collection and Union-State apportionment of inter-State GST; imports are deemed inter-State.
- Article 270: distribution of shareable net proceeds; specific cesses and Article 271 surcharges are excluded.
- Article 271: Union surcharge, except GST under Article 246A; proceeds accrue to the Union.
- Article 279A: GST Council; Articles 280-281: Finance Commission and parliamentary laying.
- Articles 275 and 282 are distinct grant routes; Articles 243-I and 243-Y anchor State Finance Commissions.

### 3. GST mechanism

- Supply -> place -> time -> value -> rate -> person liable -> eligible ITC.
- Intra-State: CGST + SGST/UTGST. Inter-State/import: IGST with destination settlement.
- Zero-rated exports/authorised SEZ supplies retain a credit/refund route; exempt and nil-rated supplies generally restrict attributable credit; non-GST is outside the levy.
- GST subsumed named Union and State indirect taxes but not basic customs duty, stamp duty, electricity duty, property tax or State excise on alcohol for human consumption.
- Petroleum crude, petrol, diesel, natural gas and aviation turbine fuel await the Article 279A(5) recommended date.

### 4. Council, court and current rate control

- Council composition: Union Finance Minister, Union Minister of State for Revenue/Finance and each State's nominated minister.
- Voting: Centre one-third; States together two-thirds; decision at least three-fourths of weighted votes present and voting.
- Mohit Minerals (19 May 2022): recommendations persuasive, not binding; enacted law remains binding.
- Most 56th-Council changes operated from 22 September 2025; broad structure centres on 5%, 18% and special 40%, with notification-specific exceptions.
- Registration and composition thresholds are dated 1 April 2019 and remain conditional, not universal.

### 5. Compensation chronology

- 2017 Act: five-year transition, 2015-16 base, 14% protected annual revenue growth.
- Compensation entitlement ended June 2022; later cess collection serviced COVID-era borrowing.
- Notification 03/2025-Compensation Cess (Rate), dated 31 December 2025, made specified entries nil from 1 February 2026.

### 6. Direct-tax architecture

- Income-tax Act, 2025 and Rules, 2026 commenced on 1 April 2026; earlier periods retain applicable earlier law.
- Distinguish personal income, company income and capital gains by taxpayer, base and period.
- TDS/TCS are collection-and-information mechanisms with credit/reconciliation, not independent final taxes in every case.

### 7. Fiscal federalism

- Vertical devolution divides shareable proceeds between Union and States; horizontal devolution allocates the States' share among States.
- XVI-FC 2026-31: accepted vertical share 41% of divisible-pool net proceeds.
- Horizontal weights: income distance 42.5, population 17.5, demographic performance 10, area 10, forest 10, GDP contribution 10.
- Accepted local-body grants: Rs 7,91,493 crore for 2026-31, subject to conditions; no revenue-deficit, sector-specific or State-specific grants recommended.
- Finance Commission recommends transfers; GST Council recommends GST design; Union Budget provides annual fiscal and legislative implementation.

### 8. Mains regulatory architecture

```text
CONSTITUTIONAL POWER
 -> GST / DIRECT-TAX STATUTES
 -> COUNCIL OR COMMISSION RECOMMENDATION
 -> LEGISLATION / NOTIFICATION / PRESIDENTIAL ORDER
 -> ADMINISTRATION AND SETTLEMENT
 -> INCIDENCE, EQUITY, EFFICIENCY AND FEDERAL TRUST
```

**Final answer line:** India's tax federalism succeeds when a broad and creditable tax base is combined with transparent devolution, limited non-shareable levies, proportionate compliance and institutions that harmonise without erasing legislative accountability.
