### PYQ DEMAND CARD 1 — 2024 General Studies Paper-II

**Demand:** What are the aims and objects of recently passed and enforced, The Public Examination (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024? Whether University/State Education Board examinations, too, are covered under the Act? Discuss in 250 words for 15 marks.

**Status:** Routed to the Basic owner by the audited 2024-2025 Mains ledger and confirmed word for word in the locally held official 2024 General Studies Paper-II. No official answer key is held locally, so the model solution is an authored route.

**Independent model answer (~240 words):** The Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024 addresses practices such as question-paper leaks, impersonation and tampering that undermine the credibility and fairness of public recruitment and entrance examinations. Its two exam-relevant dimensions are *purpose* and *coverage*; they must not be conflated.

**Aims and objects:** Defining and penalising unfair means is intended to deter organised interference with public examinations and protect candidates' confidence that merit, rather than access to stolen material or impersonation, determines selection. The central Act came into force on 21 June 2024 through S.O. 2422(E). Enactment, however, does not itself detect leaks: investigation, secure procedures and competent examination authorities determine practical deterrence.

**Whose examinations?** The legal criterion is the conducting *public examination authority*, not merely the venue or the label 'public'. The Schedule identifies UPSC, SSC, Railway Recruitment Boards, IBPS, NTA and central government departments and attached offices; other authorities require Central Government notification under the Act's framework. University or state education board examinations are therefore **not automatically covered** solely because the Act extends across India. Examine a specific notification or applicable separate state legislation before asserting coverage. A political announcement of adoption is not the same as a Gazette notification.

The strongest design is authority-specific offences paired with enforceable security and detection. The counterpoint is that a Union law's scope remains narrower than all Indian examinations and its success cannot be inferred from its date of commencement. No unsupported conviction or leak-reduction figure is necessary for a complete answer.

### PYQ DEMAND CARD 2 — 2018 General Studies Paper-I

**Demand:** Mention core strategies for the transformation of aspirational districts in India and explain the nature of convergence, collaboration and competition for its success. Answer in 250 words for 15 marks.

**Status:** Routed by the audited 2018-2023 Mains ledger to the Advanced owner with the ledger's own note that the printed stem is partly word-scrambled in the scan; the stem was nevertheless confirmed word for word in the locally held official 2018 General Studies Paper-I. The Basic owner records that core routing supersedes the older Advanced pointer. No official answer key is held locally.

**Independent model answer (~235 words):** NITI Aayog's Aspirational Districts Programme targets *inter-district* deprivation: variation within states that state averages can conceal. It identifies 112 districts and tracks 49 indicators across five themes—health and nutrition, education, agriculture and water resources, financial inclusion and skill development, and basic infrastructure—through the Champions of Change dashboard. The core strategy is administrative attention and coordinated use of existing programmes, not simply an extra funding stream.

**Convergence** aligns Union and State schemes at district level so that separately budgeted activities reach the same locality and remove duplicative clearance points. A health-and-nutrition intervention, for example, needs service staffing and local access as well as departmental targets; a shared district plan exposes the gaps between them. **Collaboration** requires Union, State and district administrators—including the Prabhari-officer arrangement—to exchange information and resolve implementation obstacles rather than merely transmit orders. **Competition** uses *delta ranking*, or rate of improvement: districts starting from low baselines can register progress instead of being permanently penalised by absolute-level league tables.

This architecture can make supervisors identify specific bottlenecks, but a dashboard is not direct proof that every eligible household benefited. District-submitted indicators need verification, output changes may not be attributable to this initiative alone, and a district average can conceal disadvantaged blocks. The separate Aspirational Blocks Programme uses a different, smaller unit; its 513 blocks should not be added to the district count.

Transformation therefore means sustained improvement in independently checked outcomes and access within districts, not merely a better rank. Convergence, collaboration and competition are complements only if local officials have authority, staff and usable data to act.

### PYQ DEMAND CARD 3 — 2018 General Studies Paper-II

**Demand:** Policy contradictions among various competing sectors and stakeholders have resulted in inadequate protection and prevention of degradation to environment. Comment with relevant illustrations in 150 words for 10 marks.

**Status:** Routed by the audited 2018-2023 Mains ledger to the Advanced owner as a cross-cutting demand linked to both policy design and environmental regulation, and confirmed in the locally held official 2018 General Studies Paper-II. The Basic owner answers it under core routing. No official answer key is held locally.

**Independent model answer (~150 words):** Environmental damage can result from *contradictory* public choices, not an absence of government action. A price or input incentive for water-intensive crops in a water-stressed area can pull against a separate groundwater-conservation programme. Each department meets its own target, while declining aquifers appear in neither department's performance score. Likewise, rapid infrastructure clearance can conflict with adequate impact appraisal, and energy-access expansion can collide with emission objectives.

The missing mechanism is cross-sector appraisal of spillovers before approval: use a proportionate Regulatory Impact Assessment, joint review of competing objectives and district convergence where both policies meet. Include environmental outcomes in monitoring rather than treating sanctioned projects as success. RIA is not uniformly mandatory in India, so its recommendation is not a claim of universal legal duty.

However, livelihood needs and ecological limits sometimes represent a genuine distributive conflict, not a paperwork error. Transparent political choices, affected-community consultation and reviewable safeguards are then needed; coordination cannot by itself decide whose costs count.

**Independent model answer (~155 words):** PM GatiShakti's National Master Plan uses a shared GIS-based view of multi-modal infrastructure and utility corridors. Ministries planning against a common map can align a road, railway and utility crossing before construction rather than repeatedly excavating or seeking late redesign. Government thereby reduces the information asymmetry and sequencing risk facing private bidders: a more predictable project pipeline supports better-informed participation without necessarily requiring a direct subsidy.

Yet a shared map coordinates *information*, not legal authority or contractual performance. Land, utilities and permissions may still involve states and local bodies; accurate, current layers and their participation are therefore necessary. A private partner also needs competitive procurement, disclosed service standards, realistic risk allocation and monitored dispute/exit procedures if the investment is delivered as a PPP. None follows automatically from the GIS interface.

Thus GatiShakti can reduce avoidable clearance failures and uncertainty between public and private actors. It cannot alone secure land, eliminate federal negotiation, guarantee financial viability or prove a fall in project costs without independent evidence.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Explain the implementation gap in public policy and locate its causes on the policy cycle with an Indian illustration. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer (~150 words):** The implementation gap is the difference between a policy announced or enacted and the outcome citizens actually experience. The policy cycle helps locate its origins: agenda setting may prioritise a visible symptom; formulation may set eligibility or deadlines without testing capacity; the decision may leave authority unclear; implementation then passes through multiple approvals and frontline discretion; evaluation may record transactions rather than excluded people.

Consider a welfare scheme requiring Union funding, state rules, district verification and local distribution. Pressman and Wildavsky's clearance-point argument explains how even honest approvals cumulatively delay delivery. At a ration outlet, Lipsky's street-level official may ration scarce supplies or favour easier cases; that does not, by itself, prove corruption. An eligibility rule demanding unreliable authentication creates a *design* failure visible at delivery.

Map the actual bottleneck before prescribing reform: simplify approvals where lawful, staff the outlet, record reasons and allow review. Track eligible non-recipients and recurring complaints alongside payments. A sanction or portal count alone cannot demonstrate implementation.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Explain why street-level discretion should be guided rather than eliminated in Indian welfare delivery. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer (~150 words):** Michael Lipsky's street-level bureaucracy explains why a ration dealer, health worker or teacher effectively makes policy through daily decisions about eligibility, time and scarce resources. An official facing high caseloads may prioritise easy cases ('creaming'); the exclusion of a remote beneficiary is a lived policy outcome even if the formal rule promises equal access.

Eliminating every choice is neither feasible nor fair: a health worker may need to reach an immobile patient differently from a mobile one. Unbounded discretion, however, invites inconsistent refusals and informal rationing. Define the statutory or notified entitlement centrally, fund adequate staffing, specify permissible local adaptations and require recorded reasons for deviation. An assisted offline route protects citizens who cannot authenticate digitally; an accessible appeal allows review of rejected claims.

Measure inclusion as well as throughput: who applied, who was refused, why, and whether review reversed the decision. Discretion is thus guided rather than denied. A record and appeal cannot overcome missing supplies, so resource and supervision constraints must also be corrected.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Examine the aims of the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024 and the separate question of its coverage. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer (~235 words):** The 2024 Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act protects fairness in public examinations by addressing question-paper leaks, impersonation, tampering and other defined unfair means. Its enactment and commencement on 21 June 2024, through S.O. 2422(E), are real legal milestones, but the law's *aims* and its *scope* pose different questions.

The integrity objective is to deter organised abuse of recruitment and entrance examinations and make results more credible for candidates. A defined offence can guide investigators and examining authorities; it cannot itself secure paper custody, train staff or ensure that allegations are properly investigated. Measure implementation by verified enforcement and the experience of candidates, not the passage of the Act alone.

Coverage is based on the **conducting authority**, not the mere fact that an examination is held in India. The Schedule names UPSC, SSC, Railway Recruitment Boards, IBPS, NTA and Central Government departments and attached offices; the Central Government can notify further authorities within the Act's framework. University and state education board examinations are consequently **not automatically within the central Act**. For any particular exam, establish the named authority and operative notification or consider separate state legislation. An announcement of political support does not substitute for a Gazette notification.

The design advantage is a precise, enforceable central offence framework. Its boundary is equally important: exam integrity elsewhere needs the applicable legal route and administrative safeguards. No unsupported conviction, deterrence or leak-reduction figure should be inferred merely from commencement.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Examine how convergence and pre-legislative consultation can reduce the implementation gap in Indian public policy. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer (~235 words):** Convergence and consultation attack *different* causes of the implementation gap. At formulation, the Union's 2014 Pre-Legislative Consultation Policy advises publishing draft proposals with explanatory material and ordinarily allowing around thirty days for comments. Citizens, frontline staff and affected firms can expose an unworkable document requirement or perverse incentive before it becomes an eligibility rule. A proportionate Regulatory Impact Assessment can compare alternatives and capacities, but is not a universally mandatory Indian legal procedure.

Publication is insufficient if the ministry ignores submissions. A public response matrix should show which objections altered the draft and why others were rejected. The 2014 policy is executive; comments do not create a veto, and time-critical measures may require a calibrated window.

At delivery, district or panchayat convergence aligns existing Union and state programmes around a common beneficiary and outcome. Joint scheduling of water, livelihoods and local access can avoid duplicated verification and clearance points, while named officers resolve disagreements. The Aspirational Districts Programme illustrates convergence alongside collaboration and delta-ranked competition. But an unfunded district instruction or a local body without functions and staff cannot implement a common plan.

Both mechanisms need feedback: compare eligible residents with those served, disaggregate coverage, record refusals and appeals, and examine independently checked outcomes. Consultation without capacity becomes symbolism; convergence without decision rights becomes another committee. Their joint value lies in better-designed rules *and* fewer delivery-stage bottlenecks, not in a promise of frictionless government.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Assess the claim that contradictory sectoral policies rather than an absence of policy explain inadequate environmental protection in India. Answer in about 300 words.

**Model answer (~285 words):** The proposition identifies a major governance failure: policies can each be rational against their own ministry's target yet harmful in combination. Environmental degradation may therefore coexist with extensive regulation and public expenditure. But policy conflict is not the *only* explanation; weak enforcement, capacity and genuine distributional trade-offs also matter.

Consider water-intensive cropping encouraged by input and price incentives in a water-stressed area alongside a groundwater-conservation programme. The agriculture objective appears in one departmental metric; depletion is an external cost borne across sectors and generations. Similarly, an infrastructure agency rewarded for rapid clearance may treat impact-assessment depth as delay, while an energy-access policy may increase emissions unless aligned with cleaner provision. These are structural illustrations, not claims about a particular programme's measured damage.

The policy cycle reveals the correction point. At formulation, proportionate Regulatory Impact Assessment can show environmental costs, alternatives and which department would bear them; it is not uniformly mandatory across India. Pre-legislative consultation can surface farmers', affected communities' and local authorities' information, though the 2014 executive policy does not make every comment binding. Before a decision, inter-ministerial appraisal can reconcile contradictory incentives or record an explicit, reasoned trade-off. During implementation, district convergence should align groundwater and livelihood plans rather than merely add an environmental committee. Evaluation must include outcomes such as independently assessed resource condition, not just subsidy or clearance counts.

Yet some conflicts cannot be 'coordinated away'. A livelihood today and ecological stock tomorrow may genuinely pull in opposite directions, and a faster project may deliver real social benefit. Legitimate decision-making then requires transparent distribution of costs, participation, proportionate safeguards and review; it cannot be replaced by a technocratic index. The claim is strong where incentives are unintentionally contradictory; it is incomplete where an explicit democratic value choice or failure to enforce an agreed rule explains the outcome.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Assess how far coordination instruments can substitute for authority in Indian infrastructure and service delivery. Answer in about 300 words.

**Model answer (~285 words):** Coordination instruments reduce friction between actors; they do not automatically reallocate public authority. Their value should be judged by whether they expose incompatible plans, enable a competent decision-maker to resolve them and produce affordable services for users.

PM GatiShakti layers infrastructure and utilities on a common GIS-based platform for multi-modal planning. A department can see an existing corridor before selecting an alignment; a private bidder can plan against a less opaque project pipeline. This addresses information asymmetry and sequencing failure rather than directly subsidising investment. But land and utility permissions may remain with states or local bodies: a shared map cannot legislate a transfer of powers, compel an approval or replace current data.

The Aspirational Districts Programme supplies another coordination logic: convergence of existing Union and state programmes, collaboration among administrators and competition through delta ranking. These levers direct attention to 112 districts and their locally varying bottlenecks; they do not make panchayat staffing or state adoption automatic. Self-reported dashboard indicators and district averages may conceal exclusion in disadvantaged blocks. The six-stage policy map therefore still requires legal authority, procedures, responsible agencies, funds and staff, accessible delivery and a grievance-and-review loop.

Where private delivery is used, a PPP contract must allocate risks, disclose procurement criteria, specify performance-linked payment, maintain public oversight and preserve affordability and exit. Viability Gap Funding addresses commercial weakness in suitable projects; the Hybrid Annuity Model shares construction/operation and revenue-payment risks rather than passing everything to the concessionaire. A GIS platform is not a concession agreement, and neither instrument proves beneficiary outcomes. Weak competition or optimistic forecasts can leave hidden fiscal exposure.

Thus coordination can lower clearance and information costs, but it substitutes for authority only in the limited sense of making *existing* decision rights easier to exercise. Durable results still require competent federal and local decisions, enforceable contracts, distributional safeguards and independently checked service outcomes.
