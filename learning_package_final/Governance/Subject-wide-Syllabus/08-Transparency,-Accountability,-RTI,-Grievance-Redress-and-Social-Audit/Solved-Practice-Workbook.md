### VERIFIED DESCRIPTIVE PYQ — 2020 GS-II Q2 — 10 marks; 150 words

**Question:** "Recent amendments to the Right to Information Act will have profound impact on the autonomy and independence of the Information Commission". Discuss.

**Independent model answer:**

The Right to Information (Amendment) Act, 2019 shifted Information Commissioners' tenure and service conditions from fixed statutory protections to Central rule-making. That matters because the CIC and SIC decide appeals against public authorities: if the executive whose refusals they review controls important conditions of their office, their perceived and practical independence can suffer. The central prescription for State Commissioners also raises a federal concern.

Yet a structural risk is not proof that individual Commissioners have ruled improperly. Information Commissions remain statutory bodies with powers to hear RTI appeals and specified complaints; their appointment and removal safeguards were not abolished by the amendment. Independence also depends on timely appointments, adequate staffing, reasoned decisions and compliance with disclosure orders. The remedy is to secure predictable conditions and operational resources while preserving scrutiny of actual decisions. Subsequent substitution of RTI section 8(1)(j) in November 2025 raises a *separate* issue about the scope of personal-information disclosure; it should not be retrojected into a 2020 answer about institutional autonomy.

### VERIFIED DESCRIPTIVE PYQ — 2024 GS-II Q16 — 15 marks; 250 words

**Question:** The Citizens' charter has been a landmark initiative in ensuring citizen-centric administration. But it is yet to reach its full potential. Identify the factors hindering the realisation of its promise and suggest measures to overcome them.

**Independent model answer:**

India's 1997 charter initiative made service promises public. But transparency cannot by itself establish accountability: someone must explain a breach and be able to correct it.

Charters often express easily counted activities instead of the service a citizen needs, omit a named officer and go unrevised without users' participation. Publish measurable, outcome-oriented standards designed with citizens and periodically reviewed. Vernacular counter notices and assisted filing address low awareness and digital exclusion. Administrative charters generally lack a statutory consequence; state service-guarantee laws, pioneered by Madhya Pradesh in 2010, can add a deadline, duty-holder and review for *notified* services. They cannot be assumed to apply uniformly across states.

DARPG's CPGRAMS helps route and track participating Union entities' grievances, but closure or transfer does not necessarily deliver the promised service. Link time-stamped applications to reasoned responses, feedback and an appropriate state RTS or departmental appeal; audit repeat complaints and reversals rather than only disposal. RTI can reveal the file or reason for delay, but CIC/SIC cannot grant the underlying service. Community social audit can expose scheme-wide discrepancy but is not a substitute for an individual's service appeal. Finally, invest in staff, records and genuinely integrated back-end workflows alongside one-stop access. Penalties without capacity may encourage officials to refuse applications to avoid the clock. Sevottam's charter, grievance and capability pillars together turn visibility into a feasible, reviewable service guarantee.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Distinguish social audit from information-request based transparency as accountability mechanisms in Indian governance. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer:**

RTI gives a citizen a statutory route to **existing records** held by a public authority; section 4 also requires proactive disclosure. A PIO's denial can be challenged through appeal to CIC/SIC. This creates visibility, but a commission cannot award a delayed wage simply because a muster roll is disclosed.

A **social audit** tests whether recorded expenditure and work match workers' lived experience. Under MGNREGA section 17 the Gram Sabha conducts audits; independent State Social Audit Units facilitate the process under the 2011 Rules. Workers can compare a muster roll with actual attendance and demand an explanation at a public hearing. The Meghalaya 2017 Act illustrates broader statutory community-audit design beyond a single scheme. RTI can supply source documents; the social audit uses those and local testimony to create public answerability. Neither automatically convicts a wrongdoer or compensates a worker. A recorded action-taken process and the competent wage, recovery or vigilance route are still needed.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Discuss why recent amendments affecting the transparency regime raise a question about the autonomy of the Information Commission. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer:**

Information Commissions adjudicate challenges to secrecy by public authorities. The Right to Information (Amendment) Act, 2019 moved core tenure and service conditions from the Act to Central rules for Central and State Commissioners. Executive control over conditions of an institution reviewing executive refusals can weaken its structural independence and public credibility; state commissions add a federal dimension.

The concern is qualified: changed conditions do not prove biased decisions, and statutory appellate jurisdiction and other protections survive. Secure predictable tenure, timely transparent appointments, adequate staff and compliance with reasoned orders. Distinguish institutional autonomy from the *scope* of disclosure: DPDP Act section 44(3)'s substitution of RTI section 8(1)(j) became operative in November 2025 and changes the personal-information exemption, while section 8(2)'s general public-interest override remains. Neither issue makes CIC/SIC a tribunal for the underlying pension or licence. Autonomy must be judged by institutional safeguards and actual access, not rhetoric alone.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Examine the significance of statutory social-audit legislation in strengthening governance accountability. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer:**

Social audit compares administrative records with beneficiaries' experience in a public forum. Legislation matters because it can make that comparison a recurring duty rather than an optional exercise dependent on an implementing department's consent.

MGNREGA section 17 locates social audit of scheme works in the Gram Sabha. The Audit of Schemes Rules, 2011 add facilitation by an independent State Social Audit Unit and an audit in every Gram Panchayat at least once every six months. Workers can compare muster rolls and wage entries with actual work; at a jan sunwai responsible officials must face questions on the record. The Meghalaya Community Participation and Public Services Social Audit Act, 2017 extends a statutory community-participation design across public programmes/services more generally, providing Social Audit Units, independent auditors and follow-up on findings. These are complementary models, not a claim that only Meghalaya uses a unit: MGNREGA already has one.

However, a mandated hearing is not an automatic recovery or conviction. If the unit depends on the agency under audit for access or funding, local elites dominate testimony, or officials only 'note' irregularities, findings may never result in redress. Require accessible records, inclusive hearings, an independently facilitated report, a named action-taken authority, due-process recovery or disciplinary referral where warranted, and publication of closure with reasons. Track completed corrections and recurrence, not hearing counts alone. Extending this machinery to all schemes also costs staff and resources; prioritise risk and secure capacity. Statutory audit strengthens transparency and answerability, but becomes genuine accountability only when independent verification leads to competent, reviewable follow-up.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Examine the claim that a social audit produces answerability but not necessarily enforceability. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer:**

A social audit can require an official to answer publicly without itself having the power to recover money or determine guilt. The distinction is between making a discrepancy visible and attaching a lawful consequence to it.

Under MGNREGA section 17, Gram Sabha members verify works and accounts in their area. The 2011 Audit of Schemes Rules require independent State Social Audit Unit facilitation and recurring Gram Panchayat audits. If a muster roll records wages for a person who did not work, a jan sunwai lets that worker and other residents challenge the entry face to face. RTI or section 4 disclosure may supply the records, while local testimony supplies verification. Public questioning and a written response establish answerability, not automatic reimbursement. Meghalaya's Community Participation and Public Services Social Audit Act, 2017 shows how a broader state statute can institutionalise independent auditors, community participation and follow-up obligations.

Enforceability still needs an identified agency to investigate a contested finding, correct registers, recover funds where authorised, refer suspected corruption to the competent vigilance/investigative authority and report action taken. Due process protects both workers and accused officials against automatic penalties on an untested allegation. Local power can deter testimony and dependence on the implementing department can constrain auditors; accessible hearings and genuine SAU independence reduce these risks. CAG and legislative scrutiny can support systemic follow-up but do not themselves pay individual wages. Thus statutory backing strengthens the route from hearing to action, yet its effect depends on records, independent facilitation and a verifiable corrective decision.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Assess how a citizen should be routed to the correct accountability forum and why routing decides the remedy. Answer in about 300 words.

**Model answer:**

An effective complaint starts with the *relief sought*, not the most visible portal. Disclosure, audit, investigation, adjudication and administrative correction involve different powers; sending a case to a forum without jurisdiction may produce a 'disposed' ticket without remedy.

For **missing records**, seek proactive RTI section 4 publication or apply to the PIO, then use statutory first and second appeals to CIC/SIC if access fails. The Commission can secure information and RTI compliance, not award the pension disclosed in the file. For **delayed service**, approach the responsible department and, if the service is notified, the designated officer and appeal under the competent state's RTS Act. CPGRAMS can route and track complaints relating to participating Union entities; it cannot replace a state statutory appeal or adjudicate every service. For **village-level falsified expenditure**, MGNREGA section 17's Gram Sabha audit, facilitated by an independent SAU under the 2011 Rules, can compare muster rolls with lived work; the report must then reach a named action-taken authority. CAG audit and legislative scrutiny address expenditure and systemic failures, not an individual wage claim. For **corruption**, refer a supported allegation to the competent vigilance/investigative channel or Lokpal within its statutory coverage; neither CIC nor CPGRAMS is a criminal court. Sector-regulatory orders require the appeal specified by the applicable sector law.

Routing also demands protection. Preserve records and confidential sources, offer assisted access and reasons, and avoid equating an audit allegation with guilt. The Whistle Blowers Protection Act, 2014 was enacted but remained uncommenced in the owned source's 2026 check; do not promise protection through an inoperative statute. Trace each referral through acknowledgment, competent decision, appeal and actual correction. One grievance can legitimately use several *complementary* routes—RTI for evidence, social audit for collective verification and service appeal for payment—but none can wield the other's power.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Assess the proposition that India's transparency regime has been its most successful accountability reform and its most over-claimed one. Answer in about 300 words.

**Model answer:**

The claim is persuasive if 'success' means an enduring right to see records, but exaggerated if it means automatic correction of injustice. Accountability requires transparency, an identifiable actor obliged to answer and a competent forum able to order a consequence.

The RTI Act, 2005 gives citizens access to existing information; section 4 requires specified proactive disclosure of institutional, financial and decision information. Statutory PIO obligations and first/second appeals to CIC/SIC make secrecy contestable. In MGNREGA, section 17 and the 2011 Rules connect available records to a Gram Sabha social audit facilitated by an independent State Social Audit Unit: workers can confront false muster rolls with testimony at a public hearing. The Meghalaya Community Participation and Public Services Social Audit Act, 2017 demonstrates broader statutory scope with institutional follow-up.

Yet many disclosures are stale, inaccessible or incomprehensible. A jan sunwai can expose a false wage entry without recovery; a CPGRAMS grievance may be marked disposed without benefit payment. CIC/SIC can address denial of the wage record, not direct the underlying wage entitlement; CAG's financial/performance scrutiny needs legislative and executive follow-up, and vigilance allegations require investigation and due process. Auditors reliant on implementers for records or dominated by local elites may miss the very abuses they are meant to expose. Protect inclusive hearings, identify the action-taken officer and publish reasoned closure with appeal and recurrence checks.

Legal status matters too. The 2019 RTI amendment shifting Commission service conditions to Central rules raises institutional-autonomy concerns. DPDP section 44(3)'s substitution of RTI section 8(1)(j) took effect in November 2025; the older personal-information wording is no longer current, while section 8(2)'s general public-interest override remains. The Whistle Blowers Protection Act, 2014 was enacted but had not commenced at the owned source's 2026 check. Thus transparency is an exceptional gateway to social and horizontal scrutiny, but only appropriate jurisdiction, capacity, safety and reviewable corrective action make it enforceable accountability.
