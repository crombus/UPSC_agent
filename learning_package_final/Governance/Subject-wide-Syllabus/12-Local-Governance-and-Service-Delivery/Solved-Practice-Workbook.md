### Original Mains 5 — 20 marks | about 300 words
**Question:** Assess the pros and cons of merging rural local bodies with urban local bodies and propose a defensible decision rule.

**Model answer (authored):** An urbanising fringe may remain inside a Gram Panchayat while its households use the adjoining municipality’s labour market, roads and drainage. Merging the rural body into an urban local body may therefore solve a real mismatch, but changing a jurisdiction is not in itself a service reform.

**Potential gains:** One authority can align zoning, storm-water and wastewater networks and pool engineering capacity. A coherent service area may reduce duplicate approvals, border dumping and the cost of separate plans. Municipal revenue tools could support an urban-level maintenance requirement if actually collected and allocated; a merged area may become easier to include in a city-wide mobility or resilience plan.

**Costs and distribution:** Panchayat voters may lose proportionate voice as a small ward in a larger city. Existing local tax obligations, charges and benefit eligibility can change, sometimes before services improve. A municipality lacking engineers, water assets or fiscal headroom may inherit liabilities rather than solve them. In Scheduled Areas, distinct PESA safeguards must not be silently swept aside by an administrative redesign. Boundary changes also require lawful state procedures and transparent transition of assets, debt, employees and pending works.

**Decision rule:** First demonstrate functional urban character and a shared infrastructure network; then compare both bodies’ service baselines, costs, revenue and staffing. Consult affected villages and city wards, test distributional and representation effects, set a protected transition plan and publish measurable water, waste and road targets. Where only a cross-border pipe or drain requires coordination, use DPC-integrated plans and joint service agreements without full merger. Reassess outcomes after implementation.

Thus merger is justified when integrated delivery benefits demonstrably exceed participation and fiscal disruption, with credible staffing and accountability safeguards. Otherwise service-level convergence better preserves local voice while tackling the boundary problem.

**Model answer (authored):** Urban governments are assigned important service subjects, but assignment alone does not tell a resident who can fix a water main, repair a road or plan a settlement. The critical governance problem is fragmentation of operational authority, money and answerability.
