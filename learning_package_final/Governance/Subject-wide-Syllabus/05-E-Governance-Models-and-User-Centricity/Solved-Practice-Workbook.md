### PYQ DEMAND CARD 1 — 2018 General Studies Paper-II

**Demand:** E-governance is not only about utilization of the power of new technology, but also much about critical importance of the 'use value' of information. Explain. Answer in 150 words for 10 marks.

**Status:** Routed by the audited 2018-2023 Mains ledger to the Advanced owner and answered from the Basic owner under core routing, and confirmed word for word in the locally held official 2018 General Studies Paper-II. No official answer key is held locally, so the model solution is an authored route.

**Model answer (authored):** Availability means information exists; accessibility means a citizen can obtain and understand it; **use value** means it is timely and actionable at the moment of decision. E-governance raises the first almost automatically, but not the third. A scanned English guideline may be online yet useless to a vernacular-speaking applicant. A notice before selection permits objection; one uploaded after the deadline does not. District disposal rates help compare offices but cannot explain an individual rejection. Case-specific reasons and an appeal route turn information into action. Broadcasting makes rules public; Critical Flow alerts affected users; Comparative Analysis exposes differences; Mobilisation supports collective claims; Interactive Service attaches applications and remedies to information. A certificate applicant needs a comprehensible rejection reason and a responsible officer, not just an online scheme notice. Without that duty-holder, publication improves awareness but not the power to correct a wrong decision. Timing, intelligibility, granularity and the attached action route are independent tests; the same uploaded notice can satisfy one while failing the others.

### PYQ DEMAND CARD 2 — 2019 General Studies Paper-II

**Demand:** Implementation of Information and Communication Technology (ICT) based Projects/Programmes usually suffers in terms of certain vital factors. Identify these factors, and suggest measures for their effective implementation. Answer in 150 words for 10 marks.

**Status:** Routed by the audited 2018-2023 Mains ledger to the Advanced owner with the word limit taken from the instruction block, and confirmed word for word in the locally held official 2019 General Studies Paper-II. The Basic owner answers it under core routing. No official answer key is held locally.

**Model answer (authored):** ICT projects require paired diagnosis and repair. **Process:** simplify approvals before digitisation or a land-record portal merely automates the queue. **Ownership:** assign a continuing project owner and transfer handover. **Interoperability:** specify open standards to reduce repeat document submission. **Data:** clean the authoritative source register before relying on it for decisions. **Capacity:** train officials and offer assisted access to citizens. **Incentives:** address staff resistance through role redesign and avoid contradictory paper workflows while preserving an accessible fallback. **Security and continuity:** maintain access controls, audit trails and recovery of pending applications during outages. These are different failures; an attractive interface does not repair an inaccurate record. DARPG's NeSDA parameters of end-service and integrated service delivery can test whether a valid request was actually finished and joined up. A launch event or an API count measures inputs, not effective implementation. Check a sample of rejected applications against source records and ask whether the assisted route completes service, not merely whether training sessions took place.

### PYQ DEMAND CARD 3 — 2020 General Studies Paper-II

**Demand:** The emergence of Fourth Industrial Revolution (Digital Revolution) has initiated e-Governance as an integral part of government. Discuss. Answer in 150 words for 10 marks.

**Status:** Routed by the audited 2018-2023 Mains ledger to the Advanced owner and answered from the Basic owner under core routing, and confirmed word for word in the locally held official 2020 General Studies Paper-II. No official answer key is held locally.

**Model answer (authored):** The Fourth Industrial Revolution describes convergence of data, connectivity and automated analysis. It can make e-governance an **operating layer** rather than a spare website: an illustrative State certificate application can pass through record checks, assignment, decision and review in one workflow. Joined records may expose delays before complaints arrive; shared infrastructure can support several services, though a rail is not the service or its duty-holder. However, erroneous source data can scale wrongful denials, and concentrated platforms magnify outages. Literacy and connectivity gaps make an exclusively digital channel exclusionary. Automated adverse decisions need comprehensible reasons, accessible human review and a recoverable assisted route; shared technology does not erase State responsibility for a State service. Thus digital systems may be integral to routine administration, but fair use depends on data quality, due process and continuity, not on a supposed official 4IR target or mandate. This is an analytical account of administrative change, not an assertion that predictive allocation is accurate or that any single official Indian definition is binding.

### PYQ DEMAND CARD 4 — 2023 General Studies Paper-II

**Demand:** e-governance, as a critical tool of governance, has ushered in effectiveness, transparency and accountability in governments. What inadequacies hamper the enhancement of these features? Answer in 150 words for 10 marks.

**Status:** Routed by the audited 2018-2023 Mains ledger to the Advanced owner with the word limit taken from the instruction block, and confirmed word for word in the locally held official 2023 General Studies Paper-II. The Basic owner answers it under core routing. No official answer key is held locally.

**Model answer (authored):** E-governance may improve visibility without ensuring completed services or answerable officers. **Effectiveness** suffers when defective registers, unreformed approvals and disconnected departments make an online application end at an offline counter. Clean source data, simplify workflows and measure completion. **Transparency** fails when a 'disposed' label omits eligibility rules, case reasons and timelines; supply intelligible rules and reasoned outcomes. **Accountability** fails when no officer owns a delay or an appeal merely closes a ticket; identify the duty-holder and verify actual correction. DARPG's NeSDA differentiates end-service delivery, integration and status/request tracking, which helps diagnose these distinct gaps. For a rural applicant, intermediary fees or failed authentication can frustrate even a well-designed page; maintain an affordable assisted route. A status display does not itself create a statutory penalty or enforceable remedy for every service. Judge improvement by accessible completion and correction, not page views or disposal totals. Separate a portal's operator from the officer empowered to reverse a denial; the grievance route must reach the latter.

### PYQ DEMAND CARD 5 — 2024 General Studies Paper-II

**Demand:** e-governance is not just about the routine application of digital technology in service delivery process. It is as much about multifarious interactions for ensuring transparency and accountability. In this context evaluate the role of the 'Interactive Service Model' of e-governance. Answer in 250 words for 15 marks.

**Status:** Routed to this Basic owner by the audited 2024-2025 Mains ledger and confirmed word for word in the locally held official 2024 General Studies Paper-II. No official answer key is held locally, so the model solution is an authored route.

**Model answer (authored):** The Interactive Service Model links application, payment, tracking, decision and appeal; it is not simply online publication. In a *hypothetical* State licence renewal, a transaction log can reveal delays and decision stages: **transparency**. A reasoned refusal, identifiable officer and usable review path can compel explanation and correction: **accountability**. Logging without these features documents failure rather than curing it. Broadcasting can publish rules; Critical Flow can alert affected users; Comparative Analysis can expose district delays; Mobilisation can aggregate grievances. These information functions can coexist with transactions and are not a chronological maturity ladder. DARPG's NeSDA assesses accessibility, ease of use, privacy/security, end-service and integrated service delivery and status tracking; a feature-rich screen requiring offline final approval may not deliver the service. The citizen's actual journey is decisive: vernacular error messages, low-bandwidth operation, disability access, reasonably priced assisted access and an appeal reachable even after failed authentication. A CSC can widen access but also create fee and discretion risks. Digital India's MeitY policy umbrella does not certify every departmental portal. The Interactive Model therefore creates a structural opportunity for accountability, conditional on actual completion, responsibility and reachable remedy. Measure corrected denials as well as visible case histories. A dashboard can also be gamed when a department optimises recorded disposals while applicants still repeat visits; sampled service journeys are a stronger check. A State and a Union service can share technical rails while retaining distinct authority over eligibility, deadlines and review. Evaluate the time, fee and lost wages borne by users, not only the official charge.

### PYQ DEMAND CARD 6 — 2025 General Studies Paper-II

**Demand:** e-governance projects have a built-in bias towards technology and back-end integration than user-centric designs. Examine. Answer in 150 words for 10 marks.

**Status:** Routed to this Basic owner by the audited 2024-2025 Mains ledger and confirmed word for word in the locally held official 2025 General Studies Paper-II. No official answer key is held locally, so the model solution is an authored route.

**Model answer (authored):** The claimed bias is a tendency, not proof that all government digital projects fail. Tenders can count databases and APIs precisely; citizen task completion is harder to specify. IT teams know legacy systems better than a rural user's journey, while go-live metrics reward features rather than outcomes. An illustrative land-mutation site requiring repeat records and an offline final visit displays integration work without user-centric delivery. Yet back-end interoperability can remove duplicate submission when designed around the user. Begin with task mapping and user testing, simplify approvals, write accessible completion into procurement and measure drop-off by connectivity and access channel. DARPG's NeSDA tests ease of use and end-service alongside integration, though external scores should be checked against real user experience. Preserve assisted access and human appeal after authentication failure. Shifting the success test from launch to reasoned, inclusive completion corrects the bias without rejecting useful technology. An improved website alone cannot fix incorrect underlying registers or frontline resistance; each bottleneck needs a separate owner.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Distinguish digitisation from e-governance and explain why the distinction decides whether a government portal improves an outcome. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer (authored):** Digitisation converts paper information into digital records; e-governance improves the public process around a **completed task**. If a revenue office scans mutation files but requires three physical visits and supplies no reason for delay, records are more available but the outcome is unchanged. A redesigned service repairs source records, eliminates repeat document submission, lets applicants track decisions and provides an identifiable officer and appeal. This requires administrative change, not just interface polish. DARPG's NeSDA distinguishes ease of use from end-service and integrated delivery; all matter here. An assisted or offline path should remain where connectivity fails without adding exploitative intermediary costs. Database migration is an input; the outcome is whether diverse users can complete and contest decisions accurately and affordably. For a rural applicant, an error message must be legible and an appeal must not require the same failed digital step; merely displaying a tracking number is insufficient.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Examine the claim that e-governance projects carry a built-in bias towards technology and back-end integration rather than user-centric designs. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer (authored):** Technology-first projects begin with departmental data structures; user-centred ones begin with the citizen's whole task. A tender may specify servers and database migrations yet omit whether a business can obtain a licence without repeat documents or an offline final visit. Vendors optimise measurable features, and launch metrics conceal abandonment. This illustrates the claimed bias, but integration itself can help if it removes duplicated steps. Map the applicant journey; simplify approval chains before automation; procure completion and disability/vernacular access requirements; test failed attempts with actual users. DARPG's NeSDA separately benchmarks ease of use, end-service and integration; compare those indicators with task completion and drop-off. Maintain an appeal that does not depend on a failed authentication step. The bias is an incentive problem, not an argument that back-end technology is unnecessary. An accurate source register is also indispensable: streamlined steps built on wrong records merely accelerate the wrong outcome.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Evaluate the role of the Interactive Service Model of e-governance in ensuring transparency and accountability. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer (authored):** Interactive Service is two-way government service, not a digital notice board. A *hypothetical* certificate journey covers applying, paying, tracking, decision and appeal. Its recorded stages make delays visible, reducing hidden discretion: **transparency**. Accountability additionally needs a responsible officer, reasons and meaningful review; a coloured status dashboard cannot itself correct wrongful denial. Broadcasting eligibility rules and targeted Critical Flow notices may complement this service; district comparison may pressure offices, but cannot replace individual remedy. Maturity is separate: downloading a form is not a completed transaction, and database integration does not guarantee fairness. DARPG's NeSDA tests accessibility, content, ease of use, privacy/security, end-service, integrated delivery and status/request tracking; assessment should be checked against actual abandonment. A rural applicant may rely on a CSC; assisted access helps but fees and operator discretion need oversight. Low-bandwidth design, vernacular recovery, disability testing and a human review route after failed authentication make the promise usable. Digital India's enabling policy does not certify each service. The model is a powerful *conditional* route to accountability, proved by accessible completion and corrected errors rather than by the count of online features. A carefully joined back end remains valuable when it saves users repeat submissions; interactive design should retain that benefit without confusing technical integration with final delivery. Track reasons and successful appeals, not only receipts or status views. A citizen with disability or intermittent connectivity should be included in the completion test, not hidden in an aggregate portal score.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Examine the inadequacies that hamper effectiveness, transparency and accountability in e-governance and the correctives each requires. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer (authored):** Three distinct deficiencies require separate correctives. **Effectiveness:** defective land records and unchanged approval chains force a purported online mutation to end offline; clean data, simplify process and verify final delivery. **Transparency:** 'processed' without the rule, case-specific reason or timeline hides the basis of a certificate refusal; publish intelligible rules and reasoned decisions. **Accountability:** no identified decision-maker and tickets closed without correction leave applicants helpless; assign ownership, escalation and independent checking of resolution. Status visibility alone is not a right to remedy. DARPG's NeSDA tests end-service, integration, ease of use and tracking; use its benchmarks to trigger user tests, not mere box-ticking. A CSC can assist an unconnected citizen but may add fees; assess total user cost. Union and State services can share technology yet have different decision-makers. Deadlines and penalties under right-to-service regimes depend on notified services; no uniform sanction follows from a dashboard. The test is completed, contestable service for the hardest-to-reach user. Compare rejected cases reopened after appeal with tickets mechanically closed; neither faster disposal nor greater information availability by itself demonstrates accountability. Authentication failure is a service-design question even if the underlying identity rail is managed elsewhere. A department should distinguish technical uptime from whether a citizen gets a reviewable decision. For a local certificate, the responsible State office must provide reasons and a correction channel, irrespective of which Union digital infrastructure the application uses. Test this through sampled denials, not an unverified State ranking.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Assess the proposition that e-governance is not only about the power of new technology but also about the use value of information. Answer in about 300 words.

**Model answer (authored):** Information creates public value only when citizens can **use** it at the right moment. A scanned rule may be available, a readable vernacular rule accessible, but a timely personal decision with an objection route is actionable. Broadcasting distributes eligibility rules; Critical Flow warns a specifically affected group of exclusion; Comparative Analysis enables district benchmarking; Mobilisation supports collective challenge; Interactive Service attaches application and review. They can coexist without becoming an official maturity sequence. On a hypothetical State certificate portal, aggregate disposal figures might help managers but not tell a rejected applicant why or whom to approach. A pre-deadline rejection reason permits correction; a late notice does not. Inaccurate source records need repair, and indiscriminate case-level disclosure may harm privacy. DARPG's NeSDA examines accessibility, content, ease, tracking and completion; assess whether people actually act on the information rather than counting uploads. A CSC can translate rules into practical help but can impose fees or distort instructions; measure real user costs and successful appeals. Digital India's umbrella ambitions do not prove individual portal outcomes. Public dashboards create indirect pressure, while an identifiable authority and reviewable decision are needed for individual redress. Technology is valuable when information is timely, granular, safe and linked to action; otherwise it spreads awareness without remedy. Format, timing, case-level granularity and attached action route must be tested separately: machine-readable publication can still arrive after the decision. Compare vernacular assistance and direct use for rural and urban applicants, rather than extrapolating from the most connected users. When publishing performance tables, disclose useful aggregates without exposing identifiable case data. The verdict depends on whether information changes a real decision, not on how much was uploaded.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Assess the claim that the emergence of the Fourth Industrial Revolution has made e-governance an integral part of government. Answer in about 300 words.

**Model answer (authored):** Data, connectivity and automated analysis associated with the Fourth Industrial Revolution can shift e-governance from an optional website to an administrative **operating layer**. In an illustrative State licensing service, digital records can link application, assignment, payment, decision and appeal rather than run alongside paper files. Joined records may flag delay before a complaint; common digital rails may support many public services. Yet the rail is not the licensing authority or a reasoned decision. Digital India's MeitY umbrella identifies infrastructure, governance on demand and citizen empowerment; DARPG's NeSDA tests whether access, integration and completion deliver on such ambitions. An inaccurate register can scale wrongful refusal, while concentrated platforms magnify outage risk. Automated triage must not silently turn a prediction into an unchallengeable denial: preserve reasons, human review, auditable correction and continuity for pending cases. A rural citizen on intermittent connectivity needs assisted or offline access without conflicting parallel records. National shared infrastructure also does not erase State accountability for a State service. Compare completed journeys and corrected errors with API counts and announcements. No invented official 4IR policy mandate or target is needed: digital administration can be integral, but its scale makes reliable data, federal clarity, resilience and reachable human remedy more—not less—important. Capacity for frontline officers matters as much as connectivity for claimants; role-based training, clear project ownership and clean authoritative records protect the operating layer. Cross-department interoperability is useful only if it removes repeat work and safely recovers pending cases when systems fail. Audit reasons for exclusion and review corrected claims by access route, while resisting any unsupported claim that automated prediction is itself a lawful final decision. This is a design criterion, not evidence that a particular named programme has met it.
