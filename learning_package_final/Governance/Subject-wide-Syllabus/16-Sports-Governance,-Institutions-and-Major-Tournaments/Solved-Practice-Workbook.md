### Verified chronology and institutional evidence retained

- The National Sports Governance Bill was introduced in Lok Sabha on **23 July 2025**, passed there on **11 August 2025** and in Rajya Sabha on **12 August 2025**, and received assent on **18 August 2025** as **Act No. 25 of 2025**. Neither tranche alone proves commencement of the Board's main recognition power or operational Tribunal appeals.
- The Act's national-sports-bodies design covers Olympic/Paralympic bodies, national sports federations and regional federations, alongside athlete/gender representation, ethics and public-funding transparency. SAI remains a society; NADA is statutory; the **IOA is the private National Olympic Committee**; **BCCI is a cricket governing body**, not a statutory national federation; CAS is international arbitration, not an Indian court. ### Original Mains 1 — 10 marks (about 150 words)

> Distinguish the roles of the Olympic body, an international federation, a national federation and a tournament organiser in the governance of sport.

**Model answer:** Sporting authority operates in layers, not through a single owner. The International Olympic Committee governs the Olympic Movement and Games; an international federation frames its sport-specific rules. A national federation represents and administers that sport domestically under applicable recognition and governance conditions. The Indian Olympic Association is India's National Olympic Committee, not a single federation for every sport. An organiser manages a particular competition and its entry conditions within the relevant rules.

In tennis, the Australian Open, Roland-Garros, Wimbledon and US Open cooperate through common Grand Slam rules while retaining separate event organisers. Competition entry and eligibility therefore cannot be inferred solely from an Olympic rule or a player's ranking. WADA's integrity standards and NADA's Indian anti-doping enforcement operate in a parallel layer; CAS is an arbitral dispute forum, not an organiser. Distinguishing each body's legal basis, authority and review route prevents a national policy ministry from improperly deciding technical selection while preserving public accountability for finance, welfare and fair process.

### Original Mains 2 — 10 marks (about 150 words)

> Explain why autonomy of sports federations cannot be treated as immunity from public accountability.

**Model answer:** Federation autonomy protects sporting expertise and technical choices from political interference, including arbitrary team selection by government. It cannot justify opaque financial accounts or athlete exclusion without reasons. A recognised federation may control the only viable route to national selection; an athlete cannot readily choose another federation and may lose a short career waiting for review. Monopoly power makes published criteria, conflict disclosure, safeguarding and a rapid independent appeal necessary.

India's National Sports Governance Act, 2025 creates a statutory framework for governance and athlete representation, with phased commencement recorded in January and May 2026; as of the owner's 13 August 2026 audit, recognition remained under the 2011 executive Code during transition and the new tribunal's operative jurisdiction was not established. Public-funding transparency and protection from harassment are legitimate oversight, whereas official direction of tactical rules or individual selection risks breaching sport autonomy. Accountability should govern process, finance and rights while allowing the sport's competent bodies to decide sporting merit.

### Original Mains 3 — 15 marks (about 250 words)

> Examine the shift in Indian sports governance from an executive code to a statutory framework.

**Model answer:** India's National Sports Development Code of India, 2011 supplied an executive recognition framework for sports federations, including conditions mediated through government recognition and funding. The National Sports Governance Act, 2025 (Act No. 25 of 2025, assented to on 18 August 2025) changes the **basis** of authority: it creates a legislative framework for national sports bodies, a National Sports Board and a National Sports Tribunal, with aims including governance standards, athlete representation, ethics, transparency and safeguarding. Bodies receiving public funding fall within the Act's RTI framework.

But enactment and operation are different. The Basic owner's statutory audit dated 13 August 2026 records phased commencement of selected provisions from 1 January and 12 May 2026; Gazette S.O. 2406(E) confirms the latter tranche. On that audit the main recognition-power provision had not been located among commenced provisions, so the 2011 Code and the Act's continuity arrangement remained relevant to already-recognised bodies. The Tribunal was provided for, but verified commencement did not establish functioning jurisdiction and appeals. It would be inaccurate to credit either new institution with decided cases or claim that every older mechanism vanished.

A statute can discipline monopoly federations, clarify rights, and facilitate independent, time-sensitive grievance review. Yet appointments and financing can undermine Board or Tribunal independence, while excessive ministry control over team selection may clash with Olympic/federation autonomy. Rules must be implemented by staff capable of auditing State associations as well as national bodies. Publish recognition criteria, accounts and reasoned selection decisions; protect whistleblowers and monitor remedies. India has changed the formal foundation of regulation, but fair practice must be assessed by verified commencement, independent operation and real athlete access rather than the Act's title alone.

### Original Mains 4 — 15 marks (about 250 words)

> Examine the governance conditions that make anti-doping enforcement fair as well as effective.

**Model answer:** Anti-doping protects fair competition and athletes' health through layered authority. WADA frames the global Code and standards; India's statutory NADA, under the National Anti-Doping Act, 2022, implements national testing and results management. CAS may hear appeals where the applicable rules allow; it neither runs events nor substitutes for NADA's first-line responsibilities.

Effective enforcement needs clear prohibited lists and athlete education, secure sample collection and chain of custody, independent testing, proportionate sanctions and consistent treatment irrespective of a federation's influence. Strict liability places responsibility for a prohibited substance in the athlete's body even without proof of intention; without it, intent could be almost impossible to prove. But the rule is harsh where contamination, an unsafe supplement or negligent support staff caused exposure. Notice of the allegation, access to evidence, opportunity to challenge collection or lab procedure, reasoned decisions and prompt independent review are therefore integral to effectiveness, not dispensable technicalities.

Federations should not both profit from athlete results and secretly determine every disciplinary outcome. Protect complainants from retaliation, publish anonymised case-process data and resource education outside metropolitan elite centres. A short sporting career makes delays themselves a sanction: a hearing after the competition cannot restore lost participation. India's 2025 anti-doping amendment is reported enacted, but detailed provisions were not verified from primary text in the owner and should not be given invented section numbers or commencement dates. Fair anti-doping enforcement combines scientifically sound detection with bounded jurisdiction, meaningful defence and a timely appeal, while preserving the global harmonisation needed for competition.

### Original Mains 5 — 20 marks (about 300 words)

> Assess how an objective demand about tournament eligibility should be analysed proposition by proposition.

**Model answer:** A multi-statement sports question is a rule-interpretation exercise, not a test of whether a general description sounds plausible. Start by identifying the named tournament, season and governing document. The four Grand Slams—the Australian Open, Roland-Garros, Wimbledon and US Open—cooperate through a shared governance structure and Rule Book, yet each has its own organiser. Institutional coordination is therefore compatible with distinct ownership. A statement claiming one owner merely from common rules would fail, although the common-rule proposition itself may hold.

Next separate **eligibility** from **acceptance**. An age threshold is a condition for competing, not proof of a main-draw place. Ranking-based direct acceptance depends on the applicable cut-off, available places and rules; qualifying is a separate stage with its own draw. Wild cards and protected/special entries are alternative routes, not evidence that all internationally ranked athletes are accepted. For each statement identify its quantifier—'any', 'all' and 'only' require stricter evidence than a limited rule—and identify the rule-book clause governing that entry route. Do not import a general ITF tour rule into a Grand Slam-specific entry test without checking its application.

Finally distinguish the **unit of a limit**. A rule may allocate a bounded number of wild-card slots to an event draw without imposing a lifetime cap on an individual player. Discretionary invitation does not nullify baseline eligibility, conduct or anti-doping requirements. Once each proposition has been independently tested, match the combination to options only if the original authenticated paper and reliable final key are available.

### Original Mains 6 — 20 marks (about 300 words)

> Assess the governance of a self-regulating sector in which the regulated party cannot exit, using sport as the case.

**Model answer:** In many sports an athlete cannot switch to a rival recognised national federation to gain entry to international competition. The federation sets eligibility and selection conditions, controls access to competitions and may employ the coaches whose conduct is challenged. This monopoly and the athlete's short career weaken the ordinary contractual argument that an unhappy member can simply leave. Self-regulation must therefore be tested against public-law values even where the association is private.

First identify authority: federation rules, Union recognition and international-charter obligations are not interchangeable. India's 2011 Sports Development Code continued as an executive recognition instrument during the partially commenced National Sports Governance Act, 2025 transition recorded in August 2026. The Act provides for a Board and Tribunal, but legal creation alone did not establish that their recognition and appeal jurisdictions were operational on that audit. Second, scrutinise internal governance: transparent elections, tenure and conflict controls, audited accounts, athlete and gender voice, and reasoned recognition conditions. A body with public funding and de facto monopoly must answer for how it spends and selects.

Third, secure individual rights. Publish selection criteria before trials, use independent panels where conflicts arise, give reasons and hear appeals fast enough to matter before the event. Create confidential safeguarding channels independent of coaches, protect complainants from retaliation and give injury support. Under anti-doping rules WADA supplies common standards and statutory NADA handles Indian implementation; strict liability makes notice, evidence and timely appeal particularly important.

The counterargument is real: ministry interference in sporting technical judgments can violate federation autonomy and invite international conflict. Proportionate regulation should set procedural, financial and safety floors while leaving sport-specific merits to competent bodies. Independent appointment and resourcing of future review mechanisms, State-association oversight and accessible remedies determine whether a statutory reform corrects monopoly abuse or merely replaces private capture with political control. Autonomy is a safeguard for sport, not a veto on athlete rights.
