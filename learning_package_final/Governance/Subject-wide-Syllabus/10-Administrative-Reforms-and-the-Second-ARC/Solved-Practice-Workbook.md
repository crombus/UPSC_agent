### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Distinguish the focus areas of the First and Second Administrative Reforms Commissions. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer (~151 words):** The First ARC (1966–1970), initially chaired by Morarji Desai and later by K. Hanumanthaiah, studied how to organise a post-Independence planning-era administration. Personnel administration, machinery of government and Centre–State relations therefore formed important structural questions. The Second ARC, constituted in 2005 under M. Veerappa Moily, produced fifteen reports during 2006–2009 for a more rights-conscious, technology-enabled setting. Its reports connect Right to Information (report 1), local governance (6), personnel reform (10), e-governance (11) and citizen-centric delivery (12) to accountability and effectiveness. The contrast is one of dominant problem and historical setting, not an assertion that the earlier commission ignored citizens or that the later one ignored organisational structure: report 13 itself examines Union government organisation. Both commissions made recommendations, not self-executing law. To assess any present claim, find the later government decision, implementing instrument and evidence of functioning services. This historically qualified comparison is more useful than memorising dates alone.

**Why this earns marks:** Compares context and agenda, identifies chairs and reports, and qualifies overlap and legal status.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Explain why the implementation status of an administrative-reform recommendation must be stated before the recommendation is used in an answer. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer (~153 words):** An ARC recommendation is expert advice, not automatically an operative public duty. Consider the Second ARC's *Citizen-Centric Administration* report (12): a suggested service standard cannot be cited as a citizen's enforceable remedy without an applicable later instrument. Status should therefore move through five tests: proposed in the report; accepted in principle by government; enacted or notified through an Act, rule or order; operational through staff, finance and accessible procedure; and evaluated against independent service outcomes. These stages are not interchangeable. DARPG tracks follow-up, but a line ministry or a state must exercise its own competent authority for implementation; a Union decision does not by itself staff a district counter. A charter can exist while justified grievances remain unresolved. Conversely, a later portal with similar aims does not prove direct implementation of a particular report. Identifying the exact recommendation, jurisdiction and stage prevents an examiner from mistaking a dated proposal for today's law or a publicity claim for demonstrated impact.

**Why this earns marks:** Provides the full status ladder, a named report, federal competence and a concrete outcome distinction.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Examine why the recommendations of the Second Administrative Reforms Commission have been implemented selectively rather than uniformly. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer (~247 words):** The Second ARC's fifteen reports (2006–2009) span very different authorities and incentives. Uniform implementation would require every relevant Union ministry and state government to accept, authorise, finance and operate different reforms—an unrealistic reading of an advisory commission.

A recommendation in *Promoting e-Governance* (report 11) may align with a ministry's digital capacity, while changes in *Refurbishing of Personnel Administration* (report 10) can alter postings or appraisal and provoke concentrated cadre and political resistance. *Local Governance* (6) and *State and District Administration* (15) depend heavily on state-level decisions about staff, funds and functions; Union assent alone cannot transfer local powers. *Ethics in Governance* (4) may need distinct rules and oversight, whereas technology adoption can proceed administratively. Costs, legal competence and frontline ability to execute all vary.

DARPG coordinates follow-up but cannot legislate for ministries or compel states. Even where a response accepts a proposal, an Act, rule or order is a separate stage; operational staff, budgets and grievance channels are further stages. A published citizen charter from report 12's reform family without an accessible remedy exemplifies formal rather than effective adoption. Citizens, particularly excluded claimants, benefit diffusely, while officeholders potentially losing discretion may organise resistance more readily.

Selectivity need not always indicate failure: some 2009 diagnoses must be adjusted for later law and technology, and a state may choose a different lawful mechanism. Nonetheless, opacity makes selective non-adoption hard to justify. Publish recommendation-level reasons, responsible authority, instrument and independently checked outcome, disaggregated by state. The measure of progress is not an aggregate acceptance percentage but functioning, equitable services with accountable decisions.

**Why this earns marks:** Explains incentives, federal competence, legal steps, capacity and contested interpretations through named reports instead of asserting a single status for all fifteen.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Examine the continuing relevance of the Second Administrative Reforms Commission for current Indian governance reform. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer (~244 words):** The Second ARC remains useful as a diagnostic map, not as a code of currently binding instructions. Its fifteen reports, written during 2006–2009, connect information, institutions and frontline outcomes in ways that still help frame GS-II problems. *Right to Information* (report 1) focuses disclosure and answerability; *Promoting e-Governance* (11) asks whether process redesign accompanies digitisation; *Citizen-Centric Administration* (12) directs attention to service standards and remedies. Together these lenses distinguish a new website from genuinely accessible government.

For personnel and decentralisation, *Refurbishing of Personnel Administration* (10) and *Local Governance* (6) identify different duty-holders: Union personnel systems versus states transferring functions, finance and staff. A district grievance portal will disappoint if local officers cannot correct underlying entitlements. Report 14 on financial management adds the fiscal capacity dimension; report 13 tests whether Union organisational divisions frustrate coordination. Security and disaster reports are valuable cross-links but mainly belong to GS-III rather than forming this owner's detailed core.

The limitations are equally important. Later legislation and platforms may answer some old diagnoses through new means; similarity with an ARC idea does not prove adoption or causation. DARPG's follow-up is coordinative, not a power to direct every state. Test each cited proposal against a government decision, notified instrument, functioning institution and independent outcome evidence. Where a charter has been published but complaints remain unresolved, evaluation should follow remedies rather than document counts.

Thus the ARC's lasting value lies in asking the right questions about authority, accountability and capacity. Its report titles help route research; current legal and operational evidence must supply the answer.

**Why this earns marks:** Demonstrates report-specific present relevance, explicit limits and a verifiable method for claims about adoption.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Assess the fifteen reports of the Second Administrative Reforms Commission as a diagnostic architecture rather than as a list of recommendations. Answer in about 300 words.

**Model answer (~292 words):** The Second ARC, constituted in 2005 under M. Veerappa Moily, produced fifteen reports in 2006–2009. Their strength lies less in a universal implementation checklist than in an architecture for identifying what prevents public authority from delivering fair outcomes.

An information-and-accountability cluster pairs *Right to Information* (1) with *Ethics in Governance* (4): citizens need reasons and credible oversight, not disclosure alone. A delivery cluster links *Unlocking Human Capital: Entitlements and Governance* (2), *Promoting e-Governance* (11) and *Citizen-Centric Administration* (12): design entitlements, simplify transactions and ensure grievances lead to correction rather than mere closure. *Social Capital* (9) brings civil-society participation to that chain.

An authority-and-capacity cluster joins *Local Governance* (6), *Refurbishing of Personnel Administration* (10), *Organisational Structure of Government of India* (13), *Strengthening Financial Management Systems* (14) and *State and District Administration* (15). If a district office lacks delegated powers, trained personnel and funds, a digital interface cannot itself deliver a service. Reports 3 (*Crisis Management*), 5 (*Public Order*), 7 (*Capacity Building for Conflict Resolution*) and 8 (*Combating Terrorism*) extend the architecture to shocks and conflict, but their detailed ownership is chiefly outside this GS-II routing topic.

This is a map of linked constraints, not evidence that all fifteen reports were enacted. DARPG can coordinate follow-up; relevant ministries and states must separately authorise and resource proposals. An adopted service charter may coexist with inaccessible appeals, and a later scheme's thematic similarity does not establish ARC causation. For any report, trace proposal → competent decision → instrument → field operation → independently checked outcome; allow later laws and technology to modify the original diagnosis.

The architecture is useful precisely because it reveals why single-device reforms fail. Its proper use is report-specific, jurisdiction-aware and evidence-checked rather than an indiscriminate quotation bank.

**Why this earns marks:** Covers all fifteen reports in linked functional groups, includes federal and status limits, and explains the causal benefit of diagnostic routing.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Assess why expert-commission-driven administrative reform in India delivers rigorous diagnosis and uneven adoption. Answer in about 300 words.

**Model answer (~288 words):** Expert commissions can compare departments, test institutional bottlenecks and propose linked reforms beyond a ministry's immediate horizon. The Second ARC's fifteen reports illustrate this range: report 13 examines Union organisational structure, report 10 personnel administration, report 6 local governance and report 12 citizen-facing service delivery. Together they reveal how central coordination, postings, state authority and grievance handling interact. Yet identifying a defect and exercising the power to repair it are different tasks.

The Second ARC made recommendations; DARPG coordinates government follow-up but cannot by itself enact law or compel a state. A Union ministry may welcome digitisation under report 11 while resisting loss of discretionary authority under report 4's accountability agenda. State governments must act where local functions, budgets and district staffing are at issue. Staff associations can resist changes to cadre incentives; citizens who would benefit from fairer services are dispersed. Even accepted proposals may stall between a decision, an implementing order, a staffed service and evaluated results. Where grievance disposal is reported without substantive redress, formal progress can conceal failure.

Not every instance of non-adoption is irrational. The reports date from 2006–2009; subsequent laws, capacity constraints or alternative solutions may alter the preferred instrument. Conversely, a modern platform resembling an ARC proposal is not proof of direct implementation. The correct evaluative unit is a specific recommendation, not a single acceptance percentage across fifteen diverse reports.

To improve uptake, publish decision and non-adoption reasons, allocate accountable ministries and states, record necessary rules and funds, invite frontline and citizen feedback, and commission independent outcome checks. For a service-charter recommendation, measure whether complainants secure timely corrective orders, including in poorly staffed districts. Rigorous diagnosis is the commission's comparative advantage; democratic executives and competent legislatures must still choose, fund and account for implementation. Adoption becomes meaningful only when the original problem actually diminishes.

**Why this earns marks:** Separates knowledge production from political authority, uses named report mechanisms and stakeholders, and offers testable qualified reforms.
