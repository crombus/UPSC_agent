## BASIC LEARNING SESSION

### How to use this session

This topic is not a catalogue of countries. It asks how India converts proximity into security,
connectivity and development while managing unequal size, partner-state agency, domestic politics
and outside-power competition.

```text
GEOGRAPHIC PROXIMITY
        ↓ creates
INTERDEPENDENCE + FRICTION
        ↓ managed through
CONSULTATION + NON-RECIPROCITY + CONNECTIVITY + DEVELOPMENT + PEOPLE-TO-PEOPLE TIES
        ↓ tested by
LOCAL CONSENT + POLITICAL TRANSITION + PROJECT DELIVERY + SECURITY SHOCKS
        ↓ desired result
A STABLE, PROSPEROUS AND AUTONOMOUS NEIGHBOURHOOD
        (a policy goal, never an automatic outcome)
```

### Coverage map

| Core block | What must be learnt |
|---|---|
| Scope | Eight land/maritime neighbours; Afghanistan as a strategic near-neighbour; topic firewalls |
| Policy vocabulary | Neighbourhood First, Gujral Doctrine, asymmetric interdependence, regime- versus society-oriented engagement |
| Operating mechanism | Border–connectivity–security–development nexus; bilateral delivery plus regional reassurance |
| Instruments | Grants, lines of credit, power trade, transit, rail/road/waterway links, water arrangements, HADR and exchanges |
| Country evidence | Bangladesh, Bhutan, Nepal, Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Afghanistan and Pakistan; Maldives/China only at the boundary |
| Evaluation | Announcement–delivery gap, domestic politics, federal actors, external powers, partner agency and implementation credibility |
| Exam use | Verified PYQ routes, status discipline and scalable 10/15/20-mark answer architecture |

### Learning outcomes

By the end, you should be able to:

1. identify the geographical neighbourhood without confusing it with this topic's ownership;
2. state all five Gujral Doctrine principles and explain why non-reciprocity addresses asymmetry;
3. distinguish Neighbourhood First, a regional institution, a bilateral instrument and an
   implemented project;
4. trace the border–connectivity–security–development mechanism rather than list schemes;
5. compare Bangladesh, Bhutan, Nepal, Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Afghanistan and Pakistan without using
   one uniform template;
6. distinguish government engagement, societal engagement and impermissible inference about
   political recognition;
7. test every claim for legal character, implementation stage, partner consent and outcome; and
8. write a direct Sri Lanka answer or use the framework in an application question without
   claiming false PYQ ownership.

### Evidence-control protocol

| Claim being made | Required evidence | Safe formulation |
|---|---|---|
| Current bilateral position | dated MEA brief or official statement | “The July 2026 brief records…” |
| Treaty status | treaty text/depositary or dated official position | “India stated that it was held in abeyance…” |
| Project delivery | commissioning/agency record | “The port segment was operationalised…” |
| Financial support | dated official amount and time window | “More than USD 3 billion by 3 May 2022…” |
| Political interpretation | identified scholar or clearly marked inference | “Sikri's period assessment…” / “⚠️ This suggests…” |
| PYQ wording/key | routing ledger plus official paper/key export | preserve OCR defect; do not invent missing text |

> **Evidence rule:** intention answers *what was proposed*; implementation answers *what was
> delivered*; outcome answers *what changed*. An answer must not substitute one for another.

### SESSION 1 — FOUNDATION — What this topic owns

#### Visual

```text
TOPIC 02 OWNS
├── neighbourhood-policy logic
├── Gujral Doctrine and non-reciprocity
├── diplomatic use of connectivity/development assistance
├── bilateral political constraints and partner agency
├── first-responder diplomacy
└── implementation credibility

ROUTE OUT
├── China as a major power / LAC strategy ──▶ IR Topic 03
├── Maldives' detailed maritime significance ──▶ IR Topic 04
├── SAARC/BIMSTEC institutional comparison ──▶ IR Topic 10
├── routes, rivers and border geography ──▶ Geography
├── tariffs, trade data and project economics ──▶ Economy
├── border operations / insurgency ──▶ Internal Security
└── treaty procedure and federal law ──▶ Polity
```

#### Explanation

✅ India has land or maritime borders with Pakistan, China, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Myanmar,
Sri Lanka and Maldives. The canonical source, following Shashi Tharoor, treats Afghanistan as a
**near-neighbour** because of its security relevance and the territorial change in north-western
Kashmir after 1948.

✅ Tharoor also records a history of problems, of varying difficulty, with six of the eight
bordering states. The point is structural friction, not a claim that every relationship is hostile.

⚠️ This geographic list is not the same as the topic's detailed ownership. China belongs mainly
to the major-power topic; Maldives belongs mainly to the maritime topic. Here they establish the
neighbourhood perimeter and the need for differentiated policy.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Geography creates unavoidable interdependence, but it does not erase
> the sovereignty, agency or domestic politics of the neighbouring state.

#### Exam link

- **Prelims:** identify the correct neighbour/near-neighbour distinction.
- **Mains:** begin with interdependence and asymmetry, not with “India wants friendly relations.”

#### Traps

- ❌ “All nine are direct neighbours.” Afghanistan is treated here as a strategic near-neighbour.
- ❌ “Every border issue belongs to IR.” Operational border management belongs to Internal Security.
- ❌ “Neighbourhood is a uniform bloc.” Each partner has distinct incentives and political constraints.

#### Recap

**Map first; then apply the firewall.**

### SESSION 2 — FOUNDATION — Neighbourhood First

#### Visual

```text
NEIGHBOURHOOD FIRST
├── PRIORITY ── nearby states receive sustained political attention
├── PUBLIC GOODS ── connectivity, power, trade facilitation, capacity and HADR
├── CONSULTATION ── partner interests and sensitivities matter
├── PEOPLE ── culture, education, health and mobility deepen durability
└── DELIVERY ── credibility depends on completed and usable outcomes
```

#### Explanation

⚠️ **Neighbourhood First** is India's stated priority of engaging South Asian neighbours through
connectivity, development partnership and people-to-people ties. It is a policy orientation, not
a treaty, organisation or guarantee of good relations.

Its strategic logic is simple:

1. instability next door transmits security and economic costs;
2. connectivity can convert location into market and mobility gains;
3. timely assistance can establish first-responder credibility;
4. consultation can reduce fears produced by unequal size; and
5. durable relations require benefits that survive a change of government.

✅ Tharoor cites Prime Minister Manmohan Singh's October 2011 observation during a Bangladesh
visit that India cannot realise its own destiny without a stable and cooperative neighbourhood.

#### Example

✅ The Akhaura–Agartala cross-border rail link was inaugurated on **1 November 2023**. It
illustrates how a bilateral link can improve subregional access for India's Northeast.

⚠️ Its diplomatic value depends on customs, trans-shipment rules, last-mile infrastructure and
actual use. Inauguration is therefore evidence of an asset, not proof of every claimed economic
outcome.

#### Exam link

When asked to assess Neighbourhood First, organise the body under **security, connectivity,
development, people and delivery**, then attach one country-specific qualification to each broad
claim.

#### Traps

- ❌ Neighbourhood First applies identically to every neighbour.
- ❌ A leader's visit proves policy delivery.
- ❌ Connectivity is only an economic matter; it also changes security, access and political trust.

#### Recap

**Priority + public goods + consultation + delivery.**

### SESSION 3 — CORE — Gujral Doctrine: five principles

#### Visual

```text
GUJRAL DOCTRINE
│
├── SMALLER NEIGHBOURS ── India does not demand reciprocity
├── NON-INTERFERENCE ──── no interference in internal affairs
├── NO HOSTILE USE ────── territory not used against another South Asian state
├── SOVEREIGN RESPECT ─── territorial integrity and sovereignty respected
└── PEACEFUL BILATERALISM disputes settled through peaceful bilateral negotiation
```

#### Explanation

✅ The doctrine is associated with former Prime Minister **I. K. Gujral**. Its distinctive
principle is **non-reciprocal accommodation of smaller neighbours**: India, as the larger state,
may extend cooperation without insisting on an identical concession in return.

⚠️ Non-reciprocity is not charity. It is a strategy for reducing dependence anxiety and creating
a more stable regional environment. Nor does it mean that every issue can be solved by unilateral
concession: finite shared resources, terrorism and domestic legal constraints can narrow room for
accommodation.

#### Conceptual distinction

| Term | Correct meaning | Not the same as |
|---|---|---|
| Non-reciprocity | India need not demand equal return from a smaller neighbour | absence of Indian interests |
| Non-interference | respect for partner political autonomy | indifference to developments affecting India |
| Bilateral peaceful settlement | direct negotiated management of disputes | coercive unilateral settlement |
| Sovereign respect | equal legal personality despite unequal capacity | denial of material asymmetry |

#### Example

Mangdechhu shows how a larger India can contribute finance, market access and project partnership
without requiring an identical Bhutanese concession. The example supports non-reciprocity only
when Bhutan's revenue interest, ecological concerns and diversification choices are also stated.

#### Exam link

Use the doctrine to answer the **asymmetry problem**: why should the larger state sometimes accept
unequal immediate returns to secure wider stability?

#### Trap

❌ Do not present the doctrine and India's historical regional interventions as one perfectly
consistent posture. They create a real historical tension, examined in the advanced block.

#### Recap

**Non-reciprocity manages unequal capability; the other four principles manage unequal trust.**

### SESSION 4 — CORE — Asymmetric interdependence

#### Visual

```text
INDIA'S GREATER SIZE
        │
        ├── offers market, finance, transit, emergency support
        └── can create fear of dominance or dependence
                         ↓
SMALLER NEIGHBOUR'S RESPONSE
        ├── cooperate for benefits
        ├── seek regional/multilateral reassurance
        └── diversify external partnerships
                         ↓
INDIA'S POLICY TASK
        └── convert power into predictable public goods, not presumed entitlement
```

#### Explanation

✅ Rajiv Sikri observes that Bhutan, Nepal, Maldives and Sri Lanka have historically shown greater
interest in economic integration with India than Pakistan and Bangladesh, and that smaller
neighbours can prefer a regional framework because numbers provide reassurance.

⚠️ The useful distinction is not “friendly small state versus hostile large state.” It is:

| Pattern | Likely policy requirement |
|---|---|
| Smaller neighbour | reassurance, consultation, non-reciprocity and visible local benefit |
| Larger/more complex neighbour | intensive bilateral bargaining shaped by history and security |
| Every neighbour | respect for agency, domestic politics and implementation constraints |

⚠️ A smaller neighbour's engagement with an outside power may be its own hedging strategy. India
should test the actual security or economic effect rather than treat every external partnership
as disloyalty.

#### Example

Bhutan's hydropower partnership shows how India's larger market and capital can produce a shared
asset. Maldives' diversification of external relationships shows why the smaller state may still
seek additional options. The two behaviours are compatible: cooperation with India need not imply
exclusive dependence on India.

#### Exam link

Use the **opportunity–anxiety duality** as the thesis in any “critically examine” question. Then
demonstrate one case where Indian capacity delivered a benefit and one where perception, domestic
politics or external competition constrained it.

#### Traps

- ❌ Material asymmetry does not mean legal inequality.
- ❌ A neighbour receiving Indian assistance does not lose strategic agency.
- ❌ External-power engagement does not prove an anti-India alignment without evidence of effect.

#### Recap

**Asymmetry produces both opportunity and anxiety.**

### SESSION 5 — CORE — The border–connectivity–security–development nexus

#### Visual

```text
BORDER
  ├── enables trade, movement and cultural contact
  └── transmits conflict, crime, migration pressure and political shocks
          ↓
CONNECTIVITY
  ├── rail / road / port / waterway / grid
  └── requires procedures, maintenance and local acceptance
          ↓
DEVELOPMENT
  ├── markets, electricity, health, capacity and revenue
  └── creates expectations about speed, cost and distribution
          ↓
SECURITY
  ├── stability can improve when benefits are shared
  └── vulnerability rises if routes are disrupted or dependence is politicised
```

#### Explanation

Neighbourhood instruments cannot be put into a single universal ladder. Their legal and delivery
characters differ.

| Instrument | What it can establish | What it cannot establish by itself |
|---|---|---|
| Political statement / vision | intention and agenda | legal obligation or delivery |
| MoU / framework | agreed field or process; effect depends on text | automatic operation |
| Treaty/agreement | legal commitments according to its terms and consent rules | implementation or outcome |
| Grant / line of credit | financing commitment under stated conditions | completed asset |
| Ground-breaking | construction start | commissioning |
| Operational segment | that segment works | completion of the whole corridor |
| Trade/power flow data | measurable output | causation or equitable distribution |

✅ For current relationship claims, the canonical owner treats dated MEA bilateral briefs as the
authoritative baseline: Sri Lanka and Bhutan were current to **July 2026**, and Maldives to
**June 2026**. Official briefs establish the recorded governmental position; they may not capture
every unresolved friction or local perception.

#### Two-track evidence test

```text
LEGAL / POLITICAL CHARACTER             DELIVERY / RESULTS
declaration or statement                announcement
        ↓                               ↓
MoU / framework / treaty text           approval and financing
        ↓                               ↓
consent and entry into force,            construction
where applicable                         ↓
                                        operation → output → outcome
```

⚠️ The tracks interact but are not interchangeable. A non-binding MoU may be implemented
effectively; a binding agreement may be poorly implemented.

#### Example

The April 2025 Sri Lanka package contained signed MoUs, inaugurated assets and a project
ground-breaking. Because these had different delivery stages, the visit cannot be described with
one blanket word such as “implemented.”

#### Exam link

For every named instrument, add a status verb: **announced, signed, financed, under construction,
operationalised, commissioned, handed over, producing output**. This single habit improves both
Prelims elimination and Mains evidence quality.

#### Traps

- ❌ “MoU” does not universally mean non-binding; inspect text and context.
- ❌ “Treaty” does not prove implementation.
- ❌ “Operational” may refer to one segment, not an entire corridor.
- ❌ A rise in output after an agreement does not alone establish causation.

#### Recap

**Ask two questions separately: what is the instrument, and what has actually happened?**

### SESSION 6 — CORE — Bangladesh: connectivity, water and federal constraints

#### Visual

```text
BANGLADESH
├── CONNECTIVITY ── Akhaura–Agartala rail (inaugurated 1 Nov 2023)
├── SUBREGIONALISM ─ BBIN Motor Vehicles Agreement signed 2015
├── WATER ───────── Ganga Waters Treaty 1996; expires Dec 2026
├── FEDERAL INPUT ─ Teesta shows Indian State-level interests matter
└── POLITICAL TEST ─ engagement must outlast changes of government
```

#### Explanation

✅ Bangladesh, Bhutan, India and Nepal signed the **BBIN Motor Vehicles Agreement in 2015** to
facilitate cross-border passenger and cargo movement. ✅ Bhutan's non-ratification has constrained
four-country implementation. This is not evidence that every BBIN initiative is inactive.

⚠️ Teesta is the clearest reminder that India's neighbourhood diplomacy is not made by the Union
executive alone in practical terms: state-level interests can constrain what is bilaterally
negotiable. Polity owns the constitutional doctrine; IR owns the diplomatic consequence.

📰 The **1996 Ganga Waters Treaty** is due to expire in **December 2026**. The canonical owner
records India's statement of **13 February 2026** that renewal talks had not begun. This dated
status should not be silently converted into either renewal or termination.

📰 The same owner records India's engagement with Bangladesh's interim government from
**16 August 2024**, its call for early inclusive elections on **20 August 2025**, and parliamentary
elections on **12 February 2026**. These dates evidence continued engagement, not endorsement of
one political formation.

#### Answer use

Bangladesh demonstrates all four dimensions at once: transit, trade, water and domestic political
constraint. Use it to show why a single unresolved dispute need not erase wider cooperation.

#### Trap and recap

- ❌ BBIN MVA non-ratification by Bhutan does not mean all BBIN cooperation ceased.
- ❌ The Ganga Treaty reaching its expiry date is not the same as a completed renewal or an
  asserted termination.
- ❌ Teesta should not be used to claim that Indian States conduct foreign policy independently;
  the point is their practical influence on Union diplomacy.

**Bangladesh recall:** connectivity gains + water bargaining + federal input + political continuity.

### SESSION 7 — CORE — Bhutan: hydropower and calibrated partnership

#### Visual

```text
BHUTAN HYDROPOWER COMPACT
Bhutanese resource + joint project
            ↓
electricity generation and export
            ↓
Bhutanese revenue + Indian clean-power access
            ↓
LIMITS: delay · cost escalation · ecology · diversification concerns
```

#### Explanation

✅ The **720 MW Mangdechhu Hydroelectric Project** was commissioned in **2019** and handed over
to Bhutan in **2022**. It is a strong delivered-project example because the evidence goes beyond
announcement.

📰 India committed **INR/Nu 100 billion (₹10,000 crore)** for Bhutan's **13th Five Year Plan,
2024–29**. 📰 A rail-link MoU signed on **29 September 2025** covers the proposed
Kokrajhar–Gelephu and Banarhat–Samtse links. These are a commitment and an MoU, not evidence that
the railways have been commissioned.

📰 India also expressed support for **Gelephu Mindfulness City on 12 November 2025**. Political
support for a development vision is not evidence that the city is operational.

⚠️ A balanced answer presents hydropower as mutual benefit while acknowledging project delay,
ecological effects and Bhutan's desire to diversify.

#### Exam link, traps and recap

- **Exam use:** Mangdechhu is the cleanest “delivered project” counterweight to a general argument
  about implementation failure.
- ❌ Do not call the 2025 rail MoU an operating rail network.
- ❌ Do not reduce hydropower to Indian energy access; Bhutanese revenue and agency are central.
- ❌ Political support for Gelephu Mindfulness City is not a construction-completion certificate.

**Bhutan recall:** trust is strongest when delivery is visible, benefits are mutual and dependence
concerns are addressed.

### SESSION 8 — CORE — Nepal: open interdependence and power trade

#### Visual

```text
NEPAL RELATIONSHIP
├── deep social and people-to-people links
├── transit and geographic dependence
├── power-trade opportunity
├── political sensitivity and sovereignty concerns
└── need for continuity across governments
```

#### Explanation

✅ The canonical owner records the **10,000 MW over ten years** power-import understanding dated
**2 June 2023**. It is an agreed target, not proof that 10,000 MW has already been imported.

📰 India welcomed Nepal's interim government on **12 September 2025**; elections were held on
**5 March 2026**, as recorded by the canonical source's official recheck. The analytical lesson is
not to endorse any faction. It is that engagement must remain institution- and society-oriented
through political transition.

⚠️ Nepal is a good case for explaining why closeness can intensify sensitivity: dense ties create
benefit, but also public scrutiny of perceived interference or unequal dependence.

#### Exam link, traps and recap

- **Exam use:** pair the power target with political-transition continuity to show that economics
  and legitimacy cannot be separated.
- ❌ “10,000 MW over ten years” is not present installed capacity or immediate import.
- ❌ Dense cultural links do not remove sovereignty concerns.
- ❌ Monitoring political developments does not license choosing Nepal's government.

**Nepal recall:** open interdependence magnifies both opportunity and sensitivity.

### SESSION 9 — CORE — Sri Lanka: first-responder credibility and its limits

#### Visual

```text
2022 CRISIS RESPONSE
├── USD 1 billion facility ── food, medicines, essentials
├── USD 500 million petroleum credit line
├── about USD 1 billion ── Asian Clearing Union payment deferment
├── USD 400 million ── RBI currency swap
└── medical/humanitarian consignments

BY 3 MAY 2022: > USD 3 billion recorded
FULL-YEAR OFFICIAL DESCRIPTION: about USD 4 billion
DO NOT MERGE THE TWO TIME PERIODS
```

#### Explanation

✅ By **3 May 2022**, India's High Commission recorded more than **USD 3 billion** in assistance.
The petroleum line had delivered close to **400,000 tonnes of fuel across all 25 districts**.
⚠️ Full-year descriptions commonly put support at around USD 4 billion; the date must travel with
the number.

⚠️ The response demonstrated speed, proximity and first-responder capacity. It helped preserve
basic supplies and macroeconomic space, but it did not solve Sri Lanka's structural debt, revenue,
governance and external-financing problems. Durable recovery required domestic adjustment and an
IMF-supported programme.

📰 On **5 April 2025**, seven MoUs were exchanged: HVDC power interconnection, digital
transformation, an India–Sri Lanka–UAE Trincomalee energy hub, defence cooperation, Eastern
Province grant assistance, health and medicine, and pharmacopoeial cooperation. On the same
visit, **5,000 rooftop solar units and a warehouse were inaugurated**, while the **120 MW Sampur
solar project** had a ground-breaking. These are three distinct evidentiary categories:
signed instruments, inaugurated assets and construction commencement.

#### PYQ connection

The directly owned 2022 GS-II question asks how an “age-old friend” acted during the crisis.
A complete answer separates:

1. immediate relief and liquidity;
2. diplomatic significance;
3. structural limits; and
4. longer-term partnership and Sri Lankan agency.

#### Traps and recap

- ❌ More than USD 3 billion by May and around USD 4 billion over the year are not the same datum.
- ❌ Emergency credit did not itself cure debt, revenue or governance weaknesses.
- ❌ “Age-old friend” is not a substitute for itemised assistance and analytical qualification.
- ❌ A ground-breaking is not commissioning.

**Sri Lanka recall:** timely liquidity and essentials demonstrated friendship; durable recovery
remained a Sri Lankan reform and financing task.

### SESSION 10 — CORE — Myanmar: connectivity under conflict constraints

#### Visual

```text
INDIA'S NORTHEAST
      ↓
Myanmar gateway
├── Kaladan multimodal route
│   ├── waterway + Sittwe Port operationalised: May 2023
│   └── road component still lagging: completion urged 1 Jun 2026
└── India–Myanmar–Thailand Trilateral Highway
    └── intended link to Southeast Asia; conflict/procedures impede seamless use
```

#### Explanation

⚠️ Myanmar shows why a corridor should be evaluated segment by segment. “Project exists,”
“port is operating,” and “whole corridor is seamless” are different statements.

📰 India's recorded position of **31 August 2025** supported a Myanmar-led, Myanmar-owned peace
process and inclusive elections. This is a position statement, not evidence that the political
process has produced the stated outcome.

#### Firewall

Border insurgency, fencing and operational security belong to Internal Security. Here, use
Myanmar to explain diplomatic access, connectivity and the implementation risk created by conflict.

#### Exam link, traps and recap

- **Exam use:** Kaladan is the best illustration of why binary “complete/incomplete” claims can be
  misleading.
- ❌ Sittwe Port operation does not prove completion of the road component.
- ❌ A statement supporting an inclusive process does not prove that the process occurred.
- ❌ Do not absorb border counter-insurgency detail into this IR owner.

**Myanmar recall:** strategic gateway + conflict constraint + segment-wise delivery.

### SESSION 11 — CORE — Afghanistan: strategic near-neighbour and recognition discipline

#### Visual

```text
AFGHANISTAN
├── not counted here as a present direct border in the pre-1947 technical sense
├── treated as a strategic near-neighbour
├── engagement may protect humanitarian/security interests
└── diplomatic presence ≠ legal recognition of a government
```

#### Explanation

📰 India engaged the Taliban administration through meetings on **8 January 2025** and
**10 October 2025**. India restored the Kabul Technical Mission to **Embassy status with immediate
effect on 21 October 2025**.

⚠️ The upgrade concerns diplomatic presence. The official record cited by the canonical owner
does not contain a declaration recognising the Taliban administration. Never infer recognition
merely from engagement or mission status.

#### Exam link, traps and recap

- **Exam use:** Afghanistan supplies a precise example for separating practical engagement from
  formal recognition.
- ❌ Meeting an authority does not equal recognising its government.
- ❌ Embassy status does not by itself answer the recognition question.
- ❌ “Near-neighbour” is a strategic category here, not permission to alter the direct-border list.

**Afghanistan recall:** engage for interests and people; state recognition only when the official
record does so.

### SESSION 12 — CORE — Pakistan: security conditionality and treaty-status precision

#### Visual

```text
PAKISTAN RELATIONSHIP
├── shared history and unresolved disputes
├── terrorism and military-risk constraints
├── limited space for ordinary connectivity logic
└── legal/status precision required
      ├── "held in abeyance" ≠ treaty termination
      └── military-activity understanding ≠ automatically a formal treaty
```

#### Explanation

📰 On **23 April 2025**, the Cabinet Committee on Security decided that the **Indus Waters Treaty
of 1960 would be “held in abeyance”** until Pakistan credibly and irrevocably abjures support for
cross-border terrorism. This is India's stated executive position; it must not be rewritten as
treaty termination under the treaty text.

📰 India also maintained that the Court of Arbitration was illegally constituted and its awards
“null and void,” a position restated on **16 May 2026**. This is India's stated legal position;
it should not be presented as a universally accepted adjudication of the dispute.

📰 The official record described **Operation Sindoor on 7 May 2025** as measured,
non-escalatory, proportionate and responsible. On **10 May 2025**, India recorded an
“understanding … to stop all military activities.” Use the exact status; do not automatically
upgrade “understanding” into a formal ceasefire agreement.

⚠️ Pakistan is the hard limit of a purely development-led account of the neighbourhood:
security conditions can dominate and can narrow the space for non-reciprocity.

#### Exam link, traps and recap

- **Exam use:** Pakistan provides the counter-case that prevents a neighbourhood answer from
  treating connectivity and generosity as sufficient in every relationship.
- ❌ “Held in abeyance” is not silently rewritten as termination.
- ❌ India's Court of Arbitration position is not described as universally accepted.
- ❌ An “understanding” is not automatically upgraded into a treaty.

**Pakistan recall:** security conditionality + exact legal language + calibrated escalation control.

### SESSION 13 — CORE — Maldives and China: boundary cases

#### Visual

| State | Why it enters Topic 02 | Where detailed treatment belongs |
|---|---|---|
| Maldives | smaller-neighbour asymmetry, development instruments, political continuity | IR Topic 04 for sea lanes, energy flows and maritime security |
| China | part of the geographical neighbour count | IR Topic 03 for major-power relations, boundary competition and supply chains |

#### Maldives

📰 The **India–Maldives Joint Vision of 7 October 2024** recorded a currency-swap arrangement of
USD 400 million and INR 30 billion. 📰 **FTA Terms of Reference** were signed and negotiations
launched on **25 July 2025**, alongside an **INR 4,850 crore Line of Credit** recorded in the
canonical status table. Terms of reference are not a concluded free-trade agreement, and a line
of credit is not proof that every financed asset is complete.

⚠️ Topic 02 uses Maldives to demonstrate small-state asymmetry, political transition and
development engagement. Topic 04 owns its detailed sea-lane, energy-flow and maritime-security
significance, including the 2024 GS-II application PYQ.

#### China

China belongs in the geographic list of bordering states, but its major-power relationship,
boundary competition and supply-chain strategy are owned by Topic 03. Do not duplicate that
analysis here.

#### Exam link, traps and recap

- **Exam use:** Maldives demonstrates how the same state can appear in two answers for different
  reasons: asymmetry and development here; sea lanes and maritime security in Topic 04.
- ❌ A currency-swap arrangement is not a grant.
- ❌ A line of credit is not a completed project.
- ❌ China being a neighbour does not make Topic 02 the owner of the India–China major-power
  relationship.

**Boundary-case recall:** mention only what the demand needs, then route specialised analysis to
the proper owner.

### SESSION 14 — CORE — Regime-oriented versus society-oriented engagement

#### Visual

```text
REGIME-ORIENTED
leader/government relationship
        ↓
fast coordination
        ↓
HIGH discontinuity risk after political change

SOCIETY-/INSTITUTION-ORIENTED
people · universities · health · culture · local institutions · business
        ↓
slower, broader constituency
        ↓
GREATER durability, but no guarantee of goodwill
```

#### Explanation

✅ Sikri argues that India should move away from excessive regime orientation towards
people-to-people relations. The reason is not sentimental: a relationship tied too closely to one
incumbent can lose legitimacy or continuity when political power changes.

⚠️ Society orientation is a risk-reduction strategy, not a substitute for state-to-state
diplomacy. Governments sign agreements and control security policy; social links make cooperation
more resilient and locally intelligible.

📰 Bangladesh, Nepal and Sri Lanka experienced changes of government or governing arrangement in
the 2024–26 cycle recorded by the canonical owners. India's continued engagement supplies a dated
test of continuity; it does not prove that every bilateral difficulty was resolved.

#### Example

India's engagement with Bangladesh's interim arrangement, Nepal's interim government and Sri
Lanka's post-2024 government illustrates continuity at the state level. Health, education,
cultural and community-facing projects provide the wider social base through which continuity can
be made less dependent on one leadership.

#### Exam link

Use a two-column evaluation: **advantages of government-level speed** versus **durability of
society-level legitimacy**. The conclusion should combine both rather than selecting one
exclusively.

#### Traps

- ❌ People-to-people engagement is not covert regime change.
- ❌ Society orientation does not make formal diplomacy unnecessary.
- ❌ Continued engagement does not prove that every inherited commitment was implemented.

#### Recap

**Government channels decide; societal constituencies help agreements endure.**

### SESSION 16 — CORE SYNTHESIS — Constraints and calibrated responses

| Constraint | Why it matters | Calibrated response |
|---|---|---|
| Structural asymmetry | India's capability can be read as dominance | consultation, transparency and non-reciprocal public goods |
| Partner domestic politics | commitments can be contested after transition | broaden institutional and people-to-people constituencies |
| Indian federal/border actors | state interests affect water and movement bargains | early federal consultation |
| Outside-power presence | neighbours diversify options; India faces competition | compete on reliability, local demand and sustainable delivery |
| Project delay/cost | weakens credibility | realistic sequencing, transparent milestones and maintenance |
| Security shocks | can overwhelm development logic | retain crisis channels and status-precise conditionality |
| Finite shared resources | generosity has distributional limits | data-sharing, joint institutions and negotiated burden-sharing |

⚠️ **Outside-power caution:** Sikri's criticism of US influence over South Asia described the
period of his book and the UPA government. It is evidence of a recurring analytical concern, not
a current factual description. Any contemporary claim needs its own date and source.

#### Worked synthesis

Suppose a neighbouring state signs an infrastructure framework with an external power. A weak
answer calls this a loss of Indian influence. A complete answer asks:

1. Is the instrument binding, financed or operating?
2. Did the partner request it and what local need does it address?
3. Does it create a specific military, debt, access or standards risk?
4. Which Indian project or regional mechanism offers a credible alternative?
5. Can India respond without denying the partner's sovereign choice?

#### Recap

**Compete on relevance and delivery; do not assume entitlement.**

### SESSION 17 — CORE SYNTHESIS — Answer architecture

#### Eight-step spine

```text
1. DEFINE the relationship/problem precisely
2. STATE India's interest: security / growth / access / people / autonomy
3. IDENTIFY the partner's interest and domestic constraint
4. NAME the instrument and its exact status/date
5. EXPLAIN the mechanism: how should it create benefit or reassurance?
6. TEST delivery and outcome; separate segment from whole
7. QUALIFY with asymmetry, local consent, federal politics or external competition
8. CONCLUDE with a differentiated, consultative and implementation-led way forward
```

#### Scale by marks

| Demand | Minimum architecture |
|---|---|
| 10 marks / 150 words | thesis; 3 dimensions; 1 named example; 1 limitation; qualified close |
| 15 marks / 250 words | mechanism; 4–5 dimensions; 2–3 neighbours; counterpoint; reforms |
| 20 marks / 250–300 words | differentiated comparison; legal/delivery status; internal and external constraints; prioritised roadmap |

#### Directive discipline

- **Discuss:** cover both contribution and limits.
- **Examine:** reveal the mechanism and test the proposition.
- **Critically examine:** weigh achievement against counter-evidence.
- **Evaluate:** judge outcomes, not intentions or inputs alone.

#### Model opening

> India's neighbourhood policy is an exercise in managing asymmetric interdependence: it seeks
> to convert India's greater capacity into reliable public goods while preserving the agency and
> political confidence of neighbouring states.

#### Model conclusion

> Neighbourhood First will be durable when consultation, local benefit and completed projects make
> cooperation valuable across governments; proximity gives India responsibility and opportunity,
> but not automatic primacy.

### A2. Regional reassurance does not replace bilateral delivery

✅ Sikri's observation is that smaller neighbours can value a regional framework because numbers
provide security. ⚠️ Yet a power line, hospital, rail link or water arrangement usually requires
detailed bilateral execution.

Therefore:

```text
REGIONAL FRAMEWORK ── political reassurance, norms and wider participation
           +
BILATERAL MECHANISM ─ project design, finance, land, customs, operation
           =
POTENTIALLY DURABLE COOPERATION
```

Treat the two levels as complements, not substitutes.

### A3. Non-interference versus historical influence

✅ Sikri cites India as an important or decisive factor in crises involving Bangladesh (1971),
Sri Lanka (1987) and Nepal (1950–51 and later crises). ✅ The Gujral Doctrine, by contrast,
contains a non-interference principle.

| Analytical layer | What may safely be said | What must not be inferred |
|---|---|---|
| Historical record | India was involved in specified regional crises | intervention is a permanent doctrine |
| Contemporary principle | non-interference is a stated Gujral principle | India can ignore developments affecting its security |
| Practical requirement | India closely follows neighbouring political trends | observation confers authority to choose a neighbour's regime |
| Answer judgement | policy contains a continuing tension | every historical episode had the same legal or political character |

⚠️ A mature answer does not force these into one timeless doctrine. It distinguishes:

- historical episodes and their specific contexts;
- the later stated doctrine of non-interference;
- the unavoidable need to monitor neighbouring political developments that affect India; and
- the risk that close observation becomes perceived intervention.

### A4. The extra-regional-power variable

✅ Sikri's sharp criticism of US influence over India's South Asia policy is a **dated,
author-specific assessment** from the book's political period.

⚠️ Its durable analytical value is narrower: external powers can alter a neighbour's choices and
India's bargaining environment. It must not be copied as a current factual description without a
new dated source.

The policy response should not assume exclusive influence. India can instead compete through:

1. speed and proximity;
2. transparent and locally demanded projects;
3. predictable maintenance and financing;
4. non-exclusive partnerships; and
5. respect for the neighbour's sovereign choices.

```text
OUTSIDE PROJECT / PARTNERSHIP
          ↓
VERIFY status, finance, control and access
          ↓
ASK partner's purpose and local benefit
          ↓
IDENTIFY concrete Indian risk, if any
          ↓
RESPOND with a credible alternative / safeguard
          ↓
AVOID unsupported “sphere of influence” entitlement
```

### A5. Smaller-state hedging

⚠️ Just as India diversifies major-power relations to preserve autonomy, a smaller neighbour may
diversify partnerships to reduce dependence on India. The analogy is not perfect because material
capacity differs, but it corrects an India-only view of agency.

**Analytical payoff:** ask whether an outside partnership creates a concrete security risk,
improves the neighbour's bargaining power, finances a public good, or does several of these at
once.

| If the neighbour… | Do not immediately write… | Test instead… |
|---|---|---|
| accepts external finance | “anti-India alignment” | debt terms, asset control, local utility |
| conducts defence contact | “alliance” | obligations, access, duration and capability |
| renegotiates an Indian project | “hostility” | domestic mandate, price, environment, distribution |
| retains several partners | “inconsistency” | autonomy-seeking and risk diversification |

### A6. Water as the hardest non-reciprocity case

⚠️ Non-reciprocal generosity is easiest where benefits can expand; it is harder where a resource
is finite, seasonal and shared. Water therefore combines:

- hydrology and climate variability;
- domestic federal interests;
- treaty or arrangement status;
- agriculture and livelihoods;
- security conditionality; and
- long-term trust.

📰 The IWT abeyance decision of **23 April 2025** and the pending **December 2026** expiry of the
Ganga Waters Treaty are different legal and political situations. They should not be collapsed
into a common claim that “India has ended water treaties.”

```text
SHARED RIVER
├── physical flow / seasonality
├── treaty or arrangement
├── national distribution
├── Indian State-level interests
├── livelihoods and agriculture
├── security relationship
└── data-sharing / dispute-management capacity
```

⚠️ This is why “be generous” is not a sufficient policy prescription. Sustainable accommodation
requires usable data, domestic consultation and a settlement the parties can implement.

### A7. Implementation gaps are often partial, not total

The most accurate evaluation is frequently:

```text
NOT: announcement versus nothing
BUT: some financing + some assets + one operational segment + one delayed segment
```

✅ Kaladan supplies the best illustration: the waterway and Sittwe Port were operationalised,
while the road component lagged. ✅ The Sri Lanka April 2025 visit combined inaugurated assets,
new MoUs and a solar-project ground-breaking. Each item must retain its own status.

| Evaluation error | Better formulation |
|---|---|
| “Kaladan is complete” | waterway/port operational; road segment pending at the cited date |
| “Kaladan has failed” | some major components operate; full corridor benefit remains constrained |
| “Sri Lanka projects were delivered in April 2025” | separate inaugurated assets, signed MoUs and ground-breaking |
| “Bhutan rail connectivity exists” | a 2025 MoU covers two proposed links |

### A8. Costs and trade-offs

- **Non-reciprocity:** fiscal and political costs without guaranteed matching concessions.
- **Political engagement:** necessary awareness can conflict with perceived non-interference.
- **Regional/bilateral balance:** reassurance and delivery demand different forums.
- **External competition:** presumed primacy becomes less credible.
- **Project risk:** delay and cost escalation weaken trust.
- **People-to-people approach:** improves resilience but cannot neutralise elite conflict or
  security shocks.

| Policy choice | Benefit | Cost or residual risk |
|---|---|---|
| Non-reciprocal finance | reassurance and quicker public goods | fiscal exposure; contested domestic return |
| Bilateral project delivery | specificity and accountability | dependence on partner politics and local clearance |
| Regional framework | reassurance and wider legitimacy | slower consensus; institution may lack delivery capacity |
| Close political engagement | awareness and crisis access | perception of interference or regime preference |
| Security conditionality | protects core interests | narrows cooperative space and raises escalation risk |
| Society orientation | continuity across governments | slower payoff; cannot replace official agreement |

### A9. Advanced Mains synthesis

> **Advanced thesis:** The decisive test of India's neighbourhood policy is whether the
> border–connectivity–security–development nexus survives political transition and converts
> asymmetry into accepted, usable cooperation rather than dependence anxiety.

Use five moves:

1. identify the asymmetry;
2. show the partner's agency;
3. pair regional reassurance with bilateral delivery;
4. distinguish legal status, delivery and outcome; and
5. end with society-oriented, consultative and maintainable public goods.

### Scope and firewall

- Eight land/maritime neighbours: Pakistan, China, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Sri Lanka,
  Maldives.

  the source's technical formulation.

### Core vocabulary

- **Neighbourhood First:** priority engagement through connectivity, development and people.
- **Gujral Doctrine:** non-reciprocity for smaller neighbours; non-interference; no hostile use
  of territory; sovereign/territorial respect; peaceful bilateral settlement.
- **Asymmetric interdependence:** greater Indian capacity creates both opportunity and anxiety.
- **Regime orientation:** ties concentrated on an incumbent.
- **Society orientation:** wider people/institution links designed to survive transitions.

### Operating logic

```text
proximity → interdependence → asymmetry → reassurance + public goods →
domestic-political filter → delivery test → trust or credibility loss
```

- Smaller neighbours may prefer regional reassurance; substantive projects still need bilateral
  execution.
- A neighbour's external partnerships may be hedging; assess concrete effects.
- India's federal and border actors affect diplomatic feasibility.

### Status discipline

- Policy, doctrine, joint vision, MoU, treaty, financing and operation are not synonyms.
- Legal/political character and delivery/results are separate tracks.
- Ground-breaking ≠ commissioning.
- Operational segment ≠ completed corridor.
- Target ≠ achieved output.
- Embassy status ≠ recognition.
- “Held in abeyance” ≠ treaty termination.
- Terms of reference ≠ concluded FTA.

### Eight-step answer spine

1. define the exact problem;
2. state India's interest;
3. state the neighbour's interest/constraint;

### Qualified conclusion

India cannot choose whether developments in its neighbourhood matter; geography has already made
that choice. It can choose how to respond. A durable policy converts greater capability into
reliable, locally valued public goods, preserves partner agency, engages societies as well as
governments, and states honestly what has been promised, built, operated and achieved.
