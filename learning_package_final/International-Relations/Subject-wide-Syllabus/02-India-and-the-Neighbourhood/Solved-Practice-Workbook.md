## BASIC MCQS / REMEDIATION

### Q1. Consider the following statements about the geographical scope of this topic.

1. India is treated as sharing a land or maritime border with eight countries.
2. Afghanistan is treated as a strategic near-neighbour in the canonical source.
3. Every one of the nine countries is treated as a present direct land neighbour.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(A) 1 and 2 only
(B) 2 and 3 only
(C) 1 and 3 only
(D) 1, 2 and 3

**Answer: A.**

**Why:** ✅ The eight are Pakistan, China, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Sri Lanka and
Maldives. Afghanistan is treated as a near-neighbour; statement 3 erases that distinction.

---

### Q2. Which description best captures Neighbourhood First?

(A) A collective-defence treaty for South Asia
(B) A connectivity-led neighbourhood policy priority
(C) A reciprocity rule requiring comparable concessions from each neighbour
(D) A regional organisation replacing bilateral diplomacy

**Answer: B.**

**Why:** ⚠️ Neighbourhood First is a policy orientation. It is neither a treaty nor an
organisation, and it does not impose uniform reciprocity.

---

### Q3. Which principle is the distinctive asymmetry-management element of the Gujral Doctrine?

(A) Collective defence against external intervention
(B) Reciprocity calibrated uniformly across South Asian states
(C) Non-reciprocal accommodation of smaller neighbours
(D) Compulsory regional adjudication of bilateral disputes
**Answer: C.**

**Why:** ✅ The doctrine's distinctive principle is that India need not demand reciprocity from
smaller neighbours. Peaceful bilateral settlement, not a compulsory regional tribunal, is part of
the formulation.

---

### Q4. Which of the following is **not** one of the five principles associated with the Gujral Doctrine?

(A) Non-interference in another state's internal affairs
(B) Respect for territorial integrity and sovereignty
(C) No use of one's territory against another South Asian state
(D) Mandatory economic integration through a customs union

**Answer: D.**

**Why:** ✅ A compulsory customs union is not a principle of the doctrine.

---

### Q5. “Asymmetric interdependence” most accurately means that:

(A) Unequal capacity creates cooperation and dependency anxiety
(B) Smaller neighbours' bargaining leverage becomes negligible in practice
(C) Material-capability parity defines India's neighbourhood relationships
(D) geography removes domestic political constraints

**Answer: A.**

**Why:** ⚠️ Asymmetry does not erase sovereignty or agency. It makes reassurance, consultation
and the local distribution of benefits central to policy design.

### Q6. Why, according to the canonical Sikri-based distinction, may smaller neighbours prefer a regional framework?

(A) Regional forums displace bilateral bargaining for specific projects
(B) Regional participation can reassure smaller partners
(C) Regional participation assures project finance after political endorsement
(D) Regional participation excludes parallel ties with outside powers
**Answer: B.**

**Why:** ✅ Sikri's point is that “numbers” can provide a sense of security. Specific projects
still commonly require bilateral negotiation and delivery.

### Q7. A society-oriented neighbourhood policy differs from an excessively regime-oriented policy because it:

(A) treats official channels as secondary to civil-society engagement
(B) transfers treaty-making power to civil society
(C) builds durable institutional and social constituencies
(D) makes favourable public opinion durable across political turnover
**Answer: C.**

**Why:** ✅ Sikri recommends a shift towards people-to-people relations. ⚠️ This reduces
discontinuity risk but cannot guarantee goodwill.

### Q8. Which sequence is the most defensible delivery test for a connectivity project?

(A) Outcome → announcement → financing → construction
(B) Treaty → alliance → automatic operation → outcome
(C) MoU → treaty → commissioning as standard sequence
(D) Announcement → financing/approval → construction → operation → output/outcome

**Answer: D.**

**Why:** ⚠️ This is a delivery sequence, not a universal legal ladder. The legal character of a
MoU, framework or agreement must be assessed separately.

#### PYQ-1 · 2020 · Preliminary Examination · General Studies Paper I · Question 64

> Consider the following statements:
>
> 1. The value of Indo-Sri Lanka trade has consistently increased in the last decade.
> 2. “Textile and textile articles” constitute an important item of trade between India and Bangladesh.
> 3. In the last five years, Nepal has been the largest trading partner of India in South Asia.
>
> Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
>
> (a) 1 and 2 only
> (b) 2 only
> (c) 3 only
> (d) 1, 2 and 3

**OCR fidelity note.** Local OCR of the official scan reads the four rows as `1and2only`,
`2only`, `3only` and `1,2and3`. The rendering above only normalises spaces, commas and
parentheses; no option content is inferred.

**Reasoned answer: (b) 2 only — INFERRED ANSWER, NOT OFFICIALLY VERIFIED LOCALLY.**

**Solution.**

- **Statement 1 is incorrect:** the absolute “consistently increased” is too strong; bilateral
  trade varied during the referenced decade.
- **Statement 2 is correct:** textiles and textile articles are an important component of
  India–Bangladesh trade.
- **Statement 3 is incorrect:** Nepal was not India's largest South Asian trading partner over
  the stated period.

**Transferable lesson:** in trade statements, test absolute words such as *consistently* and
*largest* against the exact time window.

#### PYQ-2 · 2022 · Mains · General Studies Paper II · Question 9 · 10 marks · 150 words

> “India is an age-old friend of Sri Lanka.” Discuss India's role in the recent crisis in
> Sri Lanka in the light of the preceding statement.
> *(Answer in 150 words)*

**Text verification:** neutrally rendered from the local official-paper OCR
`knowledge-export\Mains PYQ\QP-CSM-22-GENERAL-STUDIES-PAPER-II-190922.pdf.md`; punctuation and
line breaks are normalised without changing words.

**Demand decode:** “Discuss” requires both contribution and limitation. “Age-old friend” invites
historical proximity, but the answer's core is the measurable 2022 response and its significance.

**Original model answer (148 words; not an official solution).**

India's response translated proximity into first-responder support. By 3 May 2022, India's High
Commission recorded assistance exceeding USD 3 billion: a USD
1 billion facility for food, medicines and essentials, a USD 500 million petroleum credit line,
about USD 1 billion in Asian Clearing Union payment deferment and a USD 400 million RBI currency
swap. The petroleum facility supplied nearly 400,000 tonnes of fuel across all 25 districts,
while rice and medical consignments protected essential consumption.

This assistance provided liquidity, preserved basic supplies and demonstrated Neighbourhood First
credibility without waiting for a wider multilateral response. Full-year support was described at
around USD 4 billion.

However, emergency financing could not correct Sri Lanka's debt, revenue, governance and
external-financing weaknesses. Sustainable recovery required Sri Lankan ownership, domestic
adjustment and an IMF-supported programme. Friendship was therefore most valuable as timely
support that preserved agency, not as a substitute for reform.

**Better-answer edge:** carry the date with each amount and distinguish relief from structural
recovery.

#### PYQ-3 · 2023 · Preliminary Examination · General Studies Paper I · Question 10

> With reference to India's projects on connectivity, consider the following statements:

> 1. East-West Corridor under Golden Quadrilateral Project connects Dibrugarh and Surat.

> (a) Only one
> (b) Only two
> (c) All three
> (d) None

substantive word.

**Reasoned answer: (d) None — INFERRED ANSWER, NOT OFFICIALLY VERIFIED LOCALLY.**

**Solution.**

1. Incorrect: the East-West Corridor is associated with **Porbandar–Silchar**, not

3. Incorrect: the BCIM corridor was associated with **Kolkata–Kunming**, not Varanasi–Kunming.

**Transferable lesson:** project names are insufficient; revise endpoints, route, partner and
delivery status.

#### PYQ-6 (application) · 2022 · Mains · General Studies Paper II · Question 10 · 10 marks · 150 words

**Owner: IR Topic 10 — Regional, Global and Minilateral Groupings.**

> Do you think that BIMSTEC is a parallel organisation like the SAARC? What are the similarities
> and dissimilarities between the two? How are Indian foreign policy objectives realized by
> forming this new organisation?
> *(Answer in 150 words)*

**Text verification:** neutrally rendered from the local official-paper OCR. The paper uses
“realized”; that spelling is retained.

**Why it is only application here:** Topic 02 supplies the neighbourhood, asymmetry and
connectivity logic. Topic 10 owns membership, mandate and institutional comparison.

**Original model answer (147 words; not an official solution).**

BIMSTEC is not simply a parallel SAARC. Both promote regional cooperation, use summit and
sectoral mechanisms, and include South Asian states. Yet their geographies and strategic
contexts differ: SAARC is South Asian, includes Pakistan and is constrained by India–Pakistan
political deadlock; BIMSTEC connects South and Southeast Asia around the Bay of Bengal, excludes
Pakistan and includes Myanmar and Thailand.

For India, BIMSTEC advances Neighbourhood First and Act East simultaneously. It provides a forum
for transport and energy connectivity, disaster management, security cooperation and closer
integration of India's Northeast with the Bay of Bengal economy. It also permits functional
subregional cooperation when SAARC-wide consensus is unavailable.

However, BIMSTEC should not be treated as a substitute that makes SAARC irrelevant. Its own
implementation capacity and project delivery require strengthening. India's objective is best
served by pragmatic, issue-based regionalism: use each institution where its membership and
mandate can produce results.

Maldives' importance arises from location, not size. Its island chain lies near major Indian Ocean

#### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Explain how the Gujral Doctrine seeks to manage structural asymmetry between India
and its smaller neighbours. *(Answer in 150 words.)*

**Model answer (148 words).**

The Gujral Doctrine converts India's unequal material size from a demand for reciprocity into a
responsibility for reassurance. Its distinctive principle is that India need not seek matching
concessions from smaller neighbours. The other principles—non-interference, prevention of hostile
territorial use, respect for sovereignty and territorial integrity, and peaceful bilateral
settlement—address fears of domination.

Non-reciprocity can support public goods such as connectivity, capacity building and emergency
assistance. The 720 MW Mangdechhu project, commissioned in 2019 and handed over to Bhutan in
2022, illustrates mutual benefit rather than one-sided charity.

However, asymmetric generosity has fiscal and political costs, and it is hardest where finite
resources or security concerns are involved. The Indus Waters Treaty being held “in abeyance”
from 23 April 2025 shows that accommodation can meet security conditionality. The doctrine is
therefore a stabilising guide, not an unconditional rule: consultation, local benefit and precise
implementation remain essential.

#### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Why is the announcement–delivery distinction central to evaluating India's
neighbourhood connectivity policy? Illustrate. *(Answer in 150 words.)*

**Model answer (142 words).**

Connectivity produces diplomatic value only when an announced route becomes a usable service.
Evaluation must therefore separate political announcement, financing, construction, segment
operation, whole-corridor operation and measurable use.

The Kaladan project demonstrates this discipline. Its waterway and Sittwe Port were
operationalised in May 2023, while the road component still lagged when both governments called
for completion on 1 June 2026. Calling the entire corridor either “completed” or “inactive” would
misstate partial delivery. Similarly, the Bhutan rail MoU of 29 September 2025 covers proposed
Kokrajhar–Gelephu and Banarhat–Samtse links; it does not establish commissioned railways.

Delivery also depends on customs, border procedures, maintenance, conflict conditions and local
consent. The Akhaura–Agartala link's 1 November 2023 inauguration establishes an asset, not every
economic outcome attributed to it. Credibility comes from transparent milestones, completed
last-mile links and evidence of sustained use, not the number of agreements announced.

#### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** “A neighbourhood policy centred on regimes is vulnerable to every political
transition.” Examine the case for a society-oriented Indian approach. *(Answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer (211 words).**

A regime-oriented policy relies heavily on the goodwill of the incumbent leadership. It can
accelerate decisions, but agreements and perceptions may be contested when power changes.
Rajiv Sikri therefore argues that India should move away from excessive regime orientation
towards people-to-people relations.

A society-oriented approach broadens the constituency for cooperation. Education, health,
cultural exchange, business networks, local infrastructure and professional capacity building
make the relationship visible beyond capitals. During the 2024–26 cycle, changes of government
or governing arrangement in Bangladesh, Nepal and Sri Lanka tested whether engagement could
continue with successor authorities. Continued official contact illustrates continuity, though
it does not prove that all disputes were resolved.

The approach also helps manage asymmetry. Benefits designed with local institutions can reduce
the perception that projects serve only Indian strategic interests. Sri Lanka's 2022 crisis
support—fuel, essentials, payment deferment and a currency swap—gained meaning because it
addressed immediate social needs.

Yet society links cannot replace governments, which control treaties, security and project
approvals. Nor do cultural ties neutralise nationalism, misinformation or outside-power
competition. India should combine stable state-to-state channels with transparent projects,
local consultation, scholarships, health cooperation and subnational links. The objective is not
to influence electoral outcomes, but to make mutually beneficial cooperation legitimate and
durable regardless of who governs.

#### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** India's neighbourhood policy must be differentiated rather than uniform. Discuss
with examples from Bangladesh, Bhutan, Myanmar and Pakistan. *(Answer in 250 words.)*

**Model answer (206 words).**

Geographic proximity creates common interdependence, but history, size, regime conditions and
security threats make a uniform neighbourhood template ineffective.

With Bangladesh, connectivity and water operate together. The Akhaura–Agartala rail link,
inaugurated on 1 November 2023, can improve Northeast access, while Teesta shows how Indian
federal interests constrain bilateral diplomacy. The 1996 Ganga Waters Treaty's December 2026
expiry requires exact status and negotiated renewal rather than assumption.

Bhutan exemplifies high-trust development partnership. The 720 MW Mangdechhu project was
commissioned in 2019 and handed over in 2022, linking Bhutanese revenue with Indian clean-power
access. Yet delay, costs, ecology and Bhutanese diversification concerns require consultation.

Myanmar is simultaneously an Act East gateway and a conflict-affected partner. Kaladan's
waterway and Sittwe Port were operationalised in May 2023, but the road segment lagged; policy
must combine engagement with realistic security and delivery assessment.

Pakistan places terrorism and military risk ahead of ordinary connectivity logic. India's
23 April 2025 decision to hold the Indus Waters Treaty “in abeyance” illustrates explicit
security conditionality, not a generic development partnership.

Thus differentiation should operate within common principles: sovereign respect, partner agency,
precise status claims and reliable delivery. Neighbourhood First is coherent when its objectives
are common but its instruments are calibrated to each bilateral context.

#### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Critically evaluate Neighbourhood First as a strategy for converting India's
structural asymmetry into regional stability. *(Answer in 300 words.)*

**Model answer (268 words).**

Neighbourhood First treats regional stability as a condition of India's own security and
development. Its central challenge is asymmetric interdependence: India's market, finance and
emergency capacity offer public goods, but the same scale can create fears of dominance.

The policy has three strengths. First, connectivity can convert geography into shared benefit.
The Akhaura–Agartala rail link, inaugurated on 1 November 2023, and the BBIN Motor Vehicles
Agreement illustrate subregional access, although procedures and Bhutan's non-ratification
constrain full use. Second, development partnerships can create mutual stakes. Mangdechhu,
commissioned in 2019 and handed over to Bhutan in 2022, links power access with Bhutanese revenue.
Third, first-responder assistance builds credibility. By 3 May 2022, India had recorded more than
USD 3 billion in support to Sri Lanka, protecting fuel and essential supplies.

The record also reveals limitations. Project announcement can outpace completion: Kaladan's
waterway and Sittwe Port operate, but its road component lagged. Domestic politics can interrupt
regime-centred ties; Teesta shows that Indian federal actors also matter. Neighbours may diversify
external partnerships to preserve their own autonomy, while security shocks can overwhelm
development logic, as the Pakistan relationship demonstrates.

The Gujral Doctrine supplies a useful response through non-reciprocity and non-interference, but
neither principle is unconditional. Finite water, fiscal costs and terrorism create genuine
trade-offs.

India should prioritise locally demanded projects, transparent finance, realistic milestones,
maintenance, federal consultation and wider social constituencies. It should pair regional
reassurance with bilateral delivery and assess outside partnerships by concrete effects rather
than presumed disloyalty. Neighbourhood First succeeds not when India asserts primacy, but when
its greater capability produces reliable benefits that neighbouring societies choose to sustain.

#### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Analyse the border–connectivity–security–development nexus in India's relations
with its neighbours. How should implementation and legal-status discipline shape policy?
*(Answer in 300 words.)*

**Model answer (259 words).**

Borders simultaneously enable exchange and transmit insecurity. India's neighbourhood policy
therefore links connectivity, development and security rather than treating them as separate
sectors.

Connectivity can create markets, power exchange and access. The Akhaura–Agartala rail link can
support Northeast connectivity; the India–Nepal understanding of 2 June 2023 envisages power
imports of 10,000 MW over ten years; Mangdechhu supplies a delivered hydropower example.
Development benefits can build constituencies for stability, while emergency assistance—as in
Sri Lanka in 2022—can prevent economic distress from becoming a wider humanitarian and security
crisis.

The same links create vulnerabilities. Conflict in Myanmar delays the road component of Kaladan.
Water negotiations involve livelihoods and Indian federal actors. Greater dependence can
generate sovereignty concerns, and politically contested projects may not survive a change of
government. With Pakistan, terrorism-related security conditionality can dominate cooperative
logic.

Policy therefore requires two-track status discipline. The legal-political track asks whether an
instrument is a statement, MoU, treaty or other agreement and what its terms establish. The
delivery track asks whether it is announced, financed, built, operating and producing outcomes.
There is no universal MoU–framework–treaty ladder. Thus the Maldives FTA Terms of Reference of
25 July 2025 are negotiations, not an FTA; Bhutan's 29 September 2025 rail MoU is not an operating
railway; Kaladan's functioning port does not complete its road.

India should sequence projects, publish milestones, secure local consent, consult border states,
fund maintenance and preserve crisis channels. A successful nexus is one in which connectivity
reduces rather than relocates insecurity, and verified public benefit converts proximity into
long-term confidence.
