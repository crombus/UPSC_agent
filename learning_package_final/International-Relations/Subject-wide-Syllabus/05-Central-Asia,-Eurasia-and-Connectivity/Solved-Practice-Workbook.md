### Routed PYQ 1 — 2018 GS-II Q10 (10 marks)
**Source-bounded prompt:** The audited 2018–23 routing ledger records the demand as outside powers in Central Asia and India joining the Ashgabat Agreement; *discuss the implications*. Its scanned word-limit tail was corrupted: the ledger assigns 150 words, not a reconstructed quotation. The Basic owner supersedes an older Advanced route.

**Model answer (independent; not an official key):** Central Asia's position between major powers gives Russia and China geographic advantages, while India's trade and security interests confront the absence of direct land access. Joining the Ashgabat Agreement, signed in 2011 and effective for India from 3 February 2018, therefore has strategic as well as commercial implications. Its multimodal transit framework connects Central Asia with the Persian Gulf and Gulf of Oman. It offers a rules-based complement to Chabahar and the INSTC and reduces reliance on politically difficult passage through Pakistan. It also gives India a concrete way to engage Central Asian partners without claiming a privileged sphere of influence. Yet a transit framework neither finances missing rail links nor compels customs agencies to clear cargo; Iran-related sanctions and insurance further complicate the route. Sikri's alternative pipeline via Xinjiang and the Karakoram likewise substitutes a different dependency. Accession expands India's options, but competitive outcomes depend on the CARs' own decisions and demonstrable corridor delivery, not India's signature alone.

## Original Mains practice — model answers

### Original Mains 1 — 10 marks; answer in 150 words
**Discuss how the Ashgabat Agreement complements, but cannot replace, physical connectivity to Central Asia.**

**Model:** India seeks access to landlocked Central Asian markets despite lacking a direct land border. The Ashgabat Agreement, signed in 2011 and joined by India in 2018, establishes a multimodal transit framework linking Central Asia with the Persian Gulf and Gulf of Oman. Rules facilitate movement; Chabahar supplies a port while INSTC envisages a wider route. These roles are complementary, not interchangeable. A transit framework cannot build rail segments, maintain roads or provide shipping services. Customs clearance, financing, insurance and decisions by Iranian and Central Asian authorities shape costs and reliability. The US withdrawal of its Chabahar sanctions waiver effective September 2025 illustrates commercial exposure; it does not itself impose an Indian legal prohibition. India's accession widens the menu beyond Pakistan-dependent land access, but claims of faster or cheaper trade must be demonstrated with dated operational evidence. Policy success lies in closing physical and procedural gaps, not merely accumulating agreements.

### Original Mains 2 — 10 marks; answer in 150 words
**Examine why India's SCO participation does not automatically translate into influence over Central Asian routes.**

**Model:** The SCO offers India a continuing platform for discussing security and regional cooperation with Central Asian republics. Its breadth is also a constraint: Russia, China and Pakistan sit alongside India, and member governments retain control of their own borders, customs and infrastructure. India's full membership since 2017 therefore allows participation, not a veto over rival corridors or a transit right through Pakistan. The dedicated India-Central Asia Dialogue and first summit of 2022 allow more focused engagement with the five CARs, while bilateral ties, such as those documented in the dated India-Uzbekistan brief, accommodate individual preferences. Physical access still depends on agreements, functioning links and financing; the Iran-linked Chabahar and INSTC options also face sanctions exposure. SCO statements endorsing connectivity demonstrate an interest and negotiating channel, not guaranteed cargo or privileged status. India's best strategy is to combine inclusive multilateral diplomacy with CAR-specific agreements and verifiable investment in route reliability.

### Original Mains 3 — 15 marks; answer in 220–250 words
**Critically examine whether maritime-linked connectivity removes the structural constraints on India's Central Asian engagement.**

**Model:** India's lack of direct land access to the five Central Asian republics is a structural problem, not merely an engineering backlog. Transit through Pakistan is politically constrained; Afghanistan could be a land bridge, but its security and access conditions are uncertain. Sikri's hypothetical pipeline avoiding both would traverse Xinjiang and the Karakoram/Himalaya, exchanging one political constraint for exceptional terrain and Chinese cooperation.
