# Central Asia, Eurasia and Connectivity — Complete Learning Session

> **Core thesis:** India has substantial political, security, economic and resource interests in Central Asia, but no direct land access. The quality of policy therefore depends on whether diplomatic instruments and multimodal links can convert intent into reliable movement across contested transit space.
>
> **Evidence rule:** ✅ means directly supported by the canonical owners, audited PYQ ledgers or locally held official exports. ⚠️ means analysis. A proposal, MoU, agreement, announced finance, signed contract, commissioned segment and regularly operating end-to-end corridor are not interchangeable.

## BASIC LEARNING SESSION

### SESSION 1 — FOUNDATION — Locate the region before discussing policy

#### VISUAL

```text
                 RUSSIA
                   ↑
CASPIAN ← KAZAKHSTAN — KYRGYZSTAN
   ↑          |          |
TURKMENISTAN — UZBEKISTAN — TAJIKISTAN
                   ↓
             AFGHANISTAN
                   ↓
             PAKISTAN / IRAN
                   ↓
                  INDIA

Five CARs = Kazakhstan + Kyrgyzstan + Tajikistan + Turkmenistan + Uzbekistan
Not CARs = Afghanistan; Mongolia
```

#### SIMPLE EXPLANATION

✅ The five republics are Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan. India's problem is not identifying interests; it is reaching a landlocked region without a direct border. Pakistan and Afghanistan lie between India and Central Asia. The map therefore controls the policy.

#### EXAMPLE

⚠️ A statement that Central Asian resources are valuable is incomplete. The examiner expects the second sentence: resource availability does not establish deliverability to India.

#### EXAM LINK

Open a connectivity answer with: **“India's Central Asia policy is shaped by a mismatch between strategic interest and direct territorial access.”**

#### TRAPS

- Central Asia is not a single country or a homogeneous bloc.
- Afghanistan is a gateway to the region but is not one of the five CARs.
- A map line is not proof of a usable route.

#### RECAP

Five republics; landlocked region; no direct India-CAR land border; access is the analytical spine.

### SESSION 5 — CORE — The two-route geography problem

#### VISUAL

```text
INDIA → CENTRAL ASIA
├── Route A: Pakistan / Afghanistan
│   ├── Pakistan transit unavailable in the relationship context
│   └── Afghanistan adds security and political uncertainty
└── Route B: Xinjiang → Karakoram → Himalaya
    ├── geographically extreme
    └── requires Chinese cooperation
                 ↓
Neither route is merely an engineering choice.
```

#### SIMPLE EXPLANATION

✅ Rajiv Sikri's source-based formulation is that a Eurasia-to-India energy pipeline avoiding Afghanistan and Pakistan would have to pass via Xinjiang and then cross the Karakoram and Himalayan ranges. This shows why the access problem is structural: one route is politically/security constrained; the other is geographically extreme and China-dependent.

#### EXAMPLE

⚠️ “Build another road” is not a solution if the alternative still depends on difficult terrain, another major power and unresolved political relations.

#### EXAM LINK

Put the two routes side by side. This converts a generic “connectivity challenge” into a causal explanation.

#### TRAPS

- Do not describe the shortest route as available merely because it exists on a map.
- Do not reduce a political constraint to engineering cost.
- Do not assume a China-routed alternative is under India's unilateral control.

#### RECAP

Route A: political/security blockage. Route B: terrain + China dependency. Hence maritime-linked alternatives.

### SESSION 6 — CORE — The corridor-status ladder

#### VISUAL

```text
CONCEPTION / MAP
      ↓
POLITICAL STATEMENT OR MoU
      ↓
AGREEMENT / RULES FRAMEWORK
      ↓
FINANCE ANNOUNCED OR ARRANGED
      ↓
CONTRACT / CONSTRUCTION
      ↓
SEGMENT COMMISSIONED
      ↓
SEGMENT OPERATING
      ↓
END-TO-END REGULAR SERVICE
      ↓
COMMERCIAL VIABILITY + RELIABILITY
```

#### SIMPLE EXPLANATION

The Master Framework requires exact status language:

| Stage | What it proves | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| Conception / announced route | Strategic intention and proposed alignment | Legal commitment, finance or operation |
| MoU / political framework | Intent to cooperate | Self-executing obligation or completed infrastructure |
| Agreement | Rules or commitments as stated | Finance, construction or traffic |
| Announced credit/equipment | Proposed financial support | Disbursement, procurement or delivery |
| Commissioned segment | A specific asset is ready for use | Whole-corridor completion |
| Operating segment | Movement occurs on that segment | Regular end-to-end, competitive service |
| Commercially viable corridor | Reliability, volume and cost support continued use | Permanent immunity from geopolitical risk |

#### EXAMPLE

✅ The source set verifies that INSTC has operating segments, but does not verify a completed full-capacity end-to-end corridor. It does not provide a safe basis for naming every commissioned segment, throughput or service frequency; those claims are therefore omitted.

#### EXAM LINK

Use verbs as evidence: **proposed, signed, joined, announced, contracted, commissioned, operating, commercially viable**.

#### TRAPS

- MoU ≠ treaty.
- Finance announced ≠ finance disbursed.
- Port operation ≠ corridor completion.
- Trial/segment movement ≠ regular end-to-end route.

#### RECAP

Status is a ladder. Never jump from intention to operation.

### SESSION 7 — CORE — Chabahar: port contract, financing and sanctions exposure

#### VISUAL

```text
INDIA
  ↓ sea
CHABAHAR, IRAN
  ↓ Iranian land links
CENTRAL ASIA / EURASIA

13 May 2024 contract
├── parties: India Ports Global Limited + Iran's Ports and Maritime Organisation
├── duration: 10 years
├── Lok Sabha Q2080: IPGCFZ operated terminal since 2018
├── Lok Sabha Q2080: USD 120 million fully transferred by August 2025
└── DD News, 13 May 2024: separate USD 250 million credit window

Terminal operation + transferred commitment ≠ completed end-to-end corridor
```

#### SIMPLE EXPLANATION

✅ Lok Sabha Unstarred Question No. 2080, answered on 31 July 2026, confirms only the relevant current-status points used here: IPGCFZ has operated the Shahid Beheshti Terminal since 2018, and India's USD 120 million commitment was fully transferred by August 2025.

✅ The Government of India DD News report dated 13 May 2024 separately records the 10-year contract and a USD 250 million credit window for mutually identified Chabahar-related infrastructure. The transferred USD 120 million proves delivery of that component; the separate credit window does not by itself prove disbursement or corridor completion.

✅ The United States announced revocation of the earlier Chabahar waiver effective 29 September 2025 and subsequently provided a conditional waiver until 26 April 2026. Lok Sabha Q2080 confirms that the conditional waiver ended on 26 April 2026. Waiver expiry must not be converted into a claim that terminal operations ended.

#### SANCTIONS VERSUS LEGAL OBLIGATION

- **Exposure:** banks, insurers, shippers or firms may face risk under another state's domestic sanctions system.
- **Legal obligation on India:** would require a binding rule applicable to India under international or domestic law.
- **Correct conclusion:** unilateral sanctions can constrain finance and participation without automatically becoming an international legal duty binding India.

#### EXAMPLE

A private financier may avoid a project because of secondary-exposure risk even when India is not legally obliged by the sanctioning state's domestic measure.

#### EXAM LINK

Write: **“The Iran route substitutes sanctions and finance dependence for Pakistan-transit dependence; it does not eliminate external dependence.”**

#### TRAPS

- Contract ≠ complete corridor.
- The USD 120 million commitment was transferred; do not relabel it as merely announced or undisbursed.
- The separate USD 250 million credit window ≠ proof of disbursement.
- Sanctions exposure ≠ international legal obligation on India.
- Conditional waiver expiry on 26 April 2026 ≠ proof that port operations ceased.

#### RECAP

Chabahar is an operating terminal and contracted port instrument with the USD 120 million commitment transferred, but wider corridor value still depends on connected links, the separate credit facility and sanctions-sensitive commercial participation.

### SESSION 8 — CORE — INSTC: route conception and operating reality

#### VISUAL

```text
INDIA → IRAN → CENTRAL ASIA / EURASIA → EUROPE
             multimodal movement
        sea + road + rail connections

Verified source-bounded status:
✓ connectivity framework
✓ operating segments exist
✗ not verified as one completed full-capacity end-to-end corridor
✗ no verified throughput/frequency claim imported here
```

#### SIMPLE EXPLANATION

✅ The 2025 Prelims paper describes India as a founding member of the International North-South Transport Corridor and tests the route as connecting India to Central Asia to Europe via Iran. ✅ The official Set-A key marks option A. The canonical owner treats INSTC as a developing framework with operating segments.

⚠️ Operating routes should be described only at the level verified: movement can occur through parts of the network, but this is not evidence that every missing link, customs process, financial channel and return-load problem has been solved.

#### EXAMPLE

A shipment completing one route demonstrates use. It does not establish stable frequency, uniform customs performance or commercial competitiveness for the entire corridor.

#### EXAM LINK

Pair each benefit with a condition: diversification **if** links connect; lower dependence **if** finance, customs and security permit regular service.

#### TRAPS

- INSTC is multimodal, not a single railway.
- Route conception is not identical to every actually used itinerary.
- “Operating segments” is narrower than “fully operational corridor.”

#### RECAP

Route: India–Iran–Central Asia–Europe. Status: developing network with operating segments, not verified full-capacity completion.

### SESSION 9 — CORE — Ashgabat Agreement: rules and transit, not project finance

#### VISUAL

```text
ASHGABAT AGREEMENT
25 April 2011: signed at Ashgabat
3 February 2018: India joined with effect
Purpose: multimodal transport and transit corridor
         Central Asia ↔ Persian Gulf / Gulf of Oman

Adds: facilitation and transit framework
Does not add by itself: construction finance or seamless operations
```

#### SIMPLE EXPLANATION

✅ The agreement was signed on 25 April 2011 to establish an international multimodal transport and transit corridor between Central Asia and the Persian Gulf/Gulf of Oman. India joined with effect from 3 February 2018.

Its value lies in rules and transit facilitation. Its effect depends on connected port, road and rail segments, customs coordination, finance and the Iran risk environment.

#### EXAMPLE

Membership can improve the legal-administrative framework while cargo movement remains constrained by an incomplete link or unavailable financing.

#### EXAM LINK

For the 2018 PYQ, discuss both implications: **strategic diversification** and **implementation dependence**.

#### TRAPS

- Agreement ≠ infrastructure-finance mechanism.
- Membership ≠ commissioned segment.
- Joining date must not be confused with the 2011 signature date.

#### RECAP

2011 signed; India joined effective 2018; facilitation framework; value contingent on physical and commercial links.

Economic integration is pursued but not completed in the verified source set.

### SESSION 11 — CORE — SCO, great-power competition and India's realistic space

#### VISUAL

```text
CENTRAL ASIA'S EXTERNAL FIELD
├── Russia: historical and geographic proximity
├── China: proximity, scale and connectivity interests
├── Western and other actors: strategic/economic presence
├── Central Asian republics: sovereign multi-vector choices
└── India: diplomacy + security + economic + connectivity instruments

Result: competitive field, not Indian determinism
```

#### SIMPLE EXPLANATION

✅ Sikri's “New Great Game” framing describes a region abutting major powers and therefore attracting foreign presences. ⚠️ It should not erase Central Asian agency or imply that India can determine outcomes.

✅ As of 3 August 2026, the canonical owner records 10 SCO full members, 2 observers (Afghanistan and Mongolia) and 15 dialogue partners. Pakistan and Iran are full members, not observers; Belarus joined as a full member in 2024. A 1 September 2025 decision envisaged consolidating observer/dialogue-partner categories into an SCO Partner status, but enabling amendments had not entered into force in the source record.

✅ The 25th SCO Summit met at Tianjin on 31 August–1 September 2025 and adopted the Tianjin Declaration. India's official statement at that summit explicitly named Chabahar and INSTC. At the 26th SCO Summit on 1 September 2026, the official statements addressed connectivity and respect for sovereignty generally; they did not name Chabahar or INSTC. Neither general advocacy nor explicit naming proves corridor completion.

#### EXAMPLE

India's SCO membership offers access to continental discussions. The presence of China, Russia and Pakistan means outputs are negotiated and cannot be treated as India-centred.

#### EXAM LINK

A balanced verdict: India should use the SCO, but not confuse forum access with market or transit access.

#### TRAPS

- Pakistan and Iran are not current observers in the source cutoff.
- Book-period membership descriptions require date correction.
- Summit advocacy ≠ project implementation.

#### RECAP

SCO supplies a platform; great-power rivalry and mixed membership limit privileged influence.

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

### A1. Work-around, not solution

⚠️ The Iran route bypasses Pakistan but creates dependence on Iranian infrastructure, customs, finance and sanctions-sensitive commercial participation. Strategic diversification reduces concentration risk; it does not create autonomy from geography.

### A2. Afghanistan as bridge and barrier

⚠️ Afghanistan is simultaneously the geographically direct bridge and a security/political constraint. This tension explains why India's shortest conceptual route is not its most usable route.

### A3. Functional cooperation with China

✅ Sikri argues that India should seek an understanding with China on energy because both are major consumers seeking supplies from similar sources and unchecked competition benefits producers. This is issue-specific cooperation amid broader strategic mistrust—not a claim of strategic convergence.

### A4. New Great Game without erasing local agency

⚠️ Russia and China possess greater geographic proximity, while other powers also compete. A superior answer treats CARs as sovereign actors using multiple relationships, not prizes divided by outsiders.

### A5. The announcement-delivery matrix

| 2012 policy | Declared policy | Broad-based intent | Achieved integration |
| Summit/Dialogue | Consultation | Political prioritisation | Binding outcome |

### A6. Current-status discipline

- **Source cutoff:** canonical current claims were rechecked up to 6 September 2026.
- **Safe to state:** India's 25th SCO Summit statement in Tianjin explicitly named Chabahar and INSTC; the 26th Summit statement addressed connectivity and sovereignty generally without naming them.

## CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

### The map that determines policy

- Five CARs: Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan.
- India has no direct land access; Pakistan/Afghanistan create the first-route constraint.
- Alternative via Xinjiang–Karakoram–Himalaya is geographically extreme and China-dependent.
- Therefore connectivity is structural geopolitics, not merely transport engineering.

### Policy and institutional spine

- Connect Central Asia Policy: 2012; political + security + economic + cultural.
- First India-Central Asia Summit: virtual, 27 January 2022.
- Fourth Dialogue: New Delhi, 6 June 2025; envisaged preparation for a second summit.
- Third Security Council Secretaries/NSA meeting: Bishkek, 16 October 2025.
- India-Uzbekistan bilateral brief: dated current as on April 2026; use it for country-specific current claims.
- SCO: platform with mixed membership; not a transit right or privileged-access mechanism.
- 25th Tianjin summit: 31 August–1 September 2025; Tianjin Declaration adopted; India's official statement explicitly named Chabahar and INSTC.
- 26th SCO Summit: 1 September 2026; official statements referred to connectivity and sovereignty generally, without naming Chabahar or INSTC.

### Corridor evidence ladder

**Conception → MoU/political intent → agreement/rules → finance announced → contract/construction → commissioned segment → operating segment → regular end-to-end service → commercial viability.**

Never skip a rung. The source set does not verify every commissioned INSTC segment, throughput or frequency.

### Chabahar

- DD News (13 May 2024): 10-year contract signed by India Ports Global Limited and Iran's Ports and Maritime Organisation.
- Lok Sabha Q2080 (31 July 2026): IPGCFZ has operated Shahid Beheshti Terminal since 2018.
- Lok Sabha Q2080: India's USD 120 million commitment was fully transferred by August 2025.
- DD News: USD 250 million is a separate credit window for mutually identified Chabahar-related infrastructure.
- Terminal operation and transferred finance ≠ end-to-end corridor completion.
- US waiver revoked effective 29 September 2025.
- Conditional waiver ended on 26 April 2026; the Government of India remained engaged with stakeholders on the implications.
- Waiver expiry ≠ proof that terminal operations ended.
- Sanctions exposure can deter entities; it is not automatically an international legal obligation on India.

### INSTC

- Multimodal route tested in 2025 Prelims: India → Central Asia → Europe via Iran.
- India is identified in the official question as a founding member.
- Operating segments exist; completed full-capacity end-to-end operation is not verified.
- Avoid unsupported claims on time savings, cost savings, throughput or regularity.

### Ashgabat Agreement

- Signed: 25 April 2011.
- India joined with effect: 3 February 2018.
- Links Central Asia with Persian Gulf/Gulf of Oman through a multimodal transport/transit framework.
- Facilitation/rules ≠ infrastructure finance or automatic seamless movement.

### Eurasian economic negotiation

- Proposed Comprehensive Economic Cooperation Agreement became the India-EAEU FTA negotiation route.
- Formal start: 3 June 2017, St Petersburg.
- Second round: Moscow, 22–25 June 2026.
- Verified status: under negotiation; not signed or in force.

### Advanced refinements

- Iran route substitutes one external dependency for another.
- Central Asian states retain agency amid Russia-China-Western competition.
- India-China energy coordination can coexist with strategic rivalry.
- Great-power competition sets limits; it does not make Indian engagement futile.

### Rapid trap sheet

1. CARs do not include Afghanistan or Mongolia.
2. Broad-based policy is not energy-only.
3. SCO membership is not a corridor.
4. Dialogue is not a treaty.
5. Agreement is not financing.
6. USD 120 million was transferred; the separate USD 250 million credit window must not be treated as disbursed without evidence.
7. Port contract is not end-to-end corridor completion.
8. Operating segment is not full-capacity network operation.
9. Sanctions exposure is not automatically a legal obligation on India.
10. Negotiation is not signature or entry into force.

### PYQ route and final verdict

- Direct: 2018 GS-II Q10; 2024 GS-II Q10; 2025 Prelims GS-I Q62.
- Shared: none recorded in audited ledgers.
- Application: 2021 and 2023 SCO questions, owned by Topic 10.

> **Qualified conclusion:** India's Central Asia policy has built a real diplomatic and corridor architecture, but strategic success depends on converting frameworks, contracts and operating segments into reliable end-to-end movement while managing geography, partner agency, finance, customs, security and sanctions exposure. The safest answer is neither “no connectivity” nor “corridor completed,” but **partial, developing and externally constrained connectivity**.
