## BASIC MCQS / REMEDIATION

### Q1. Which formulation best captures India's West Asia policy?

A. A non-exclusive portfolio centred on energy, with diaspora, technology, connectivity and
Palestine-related interests managed across the Gulf, Israel and Iran
B. A defence partnership with Israel that displaced Gulf, Iran and Palestine-related policy tracks
C. A GCC-centred policy in which the Iran track is subordinated to Gulf consensus
D. A connectivity-first policy in which energy security, diaspora protection and regional diplomacy are secondary instruments
**Answer: A**

The policy is a portfolio because different partners serve different interests. B, C and D erase
parallel tracks. The exam trap is to convert a region of competing relationships into one bloc.

### Q2. The energy trilemma consists of:

A. production, refining and distribution
B. security, affordability and sustainability
C. imports, exports and reserves
D. oil, gas and electricity

**Answer: B**

Security concerns reliable supply, affordability concerns cost exposure, and sustainability
concerns the long-run transition. The other options are sectoral categories, not the evaluative
trilemma.

### Q3. Consider the following statements.

1. Supplier diversification can reduce concentration risk.
2. Supplier diversification necessarily lowers aggregate crude-oil import dependence.
3. Route concentration may survive even when the supplier basket widens.

Which are correct?

A. 1 and 2 only
B. 2 and 3 only
C. 1 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3

**Answer: C**

Statements 1 and 3 are correct. A wider source basket spreads supplier risk, but total dependence
can remain high and several suppliers may still use the same chokepoint. 📰 The complete Economic
Survey 2025-26 shift for April-November FY26 records higher shares for the US and UAE and also for
Egypt, Nigeria and Libya, while the shares of Russia, Saudi Arabia, Iraq and Venezuela declined.

### Q4. Which option correctly identifies two historical constraints on the Iran energy track?

A. Refinery flexibility made crude grades readily substitutable, while the pipeline avoided Pakistan transit.
B. Refinery design fixed global oil prices, while the proposed pipeline was already operating.
C. Both issues are domestic taxation questions with limited foreign-policy consequences.
D. Refinery configuration raised switching costs, while the proposed Iran-Pakistan-India pipeline exposed energy security to transit through Pakistan.

**Answer: D**

✅ Tharoor notes that many Indian refineries were configured for Iranian crude and that the
proposed Iran-Pakistan-India pipeline remained “a serious attraction” despite US pressure, while
India was reluctant to route energy security through Pakistan. These are physical and
geopolitical constraints, not evidence of an operating pipeline. Geography owns route detail and
Economy owns project viability.

### Q5. Which is the correct description of the India-GCC Joint Action Plan?

A. A 2024–2028 Riyadh framework adopted on 9 September 2024
B. A 2024 defence alliance establishing reciprocal military commitments among GCC states and India
C. A 2025 technology agreement between India, Israel and the UAE
D. A 2026 petroleum-purchase contract between India and Saudi Aramco
**Answer: A**

It is the dated regional framework for strategic dialogue. It is neither an alliance nor a trade
union nor a transport corridor.

### Q6. Which statement correctly preserves the India-Oman CEPA status sequence?

A. It entered into force before it was signed.
B. Signed December 2025; effective June 2026 after domestic procedures.
C. It remains a political declaration pending negotiation of treaty text.
D. Signature placed the announced trade outcomes into immediate operation.
**Answer: B**

📰 PIB records both distinct milestones: signature in Muscat on 18 December 2025 and entry into
force on 1 June 2026 after both sides completed internal processes. B preserves the legal-status
sequence; A reverses it, C understates it and D confuses legal force with measured delivery.

### Q7. “Partner asymmetry” requires an answer to ask:

A. the aggregate value of Indian imports from the partner
B. the partner's material weight relative to India
C. both sides' interests and their specific points of divergence
D. the partner's formal endorsement of India's preferred regional order
**Answer: C**

Asymmetry analysis is two-sided. A partner may value India's market, technology or investment
while retaining independent ties and priorities.

B. Book-period figures can be combined into one current total.
C. Diaspora relevance is principally a function of remittance value.

The canonical owner separates Sikri's and Tharoor's period-specific estimates from MEA's January
2026 country table. It also separates population, citizenship and OCI categories.

### Q9. Operation Sindhu is best used to show:

A. that West Asia policy includes a continuing crisis-time consular-protection duty
B. evacuation operations necessarily amount to military intervention
C. regional conflict has ceased to create risks for Indian nationals
D. that diaspora data and remittance data are identical

**Answer: A**

📰 The operation evacuated 4,415 Indians from Iran and Israel by 27 June 2025. It demonstrates
exposure and consular coordination, not the end of risk.

### Q10. Which pair is correctly matched?

A. 1988 — full India-Israel diplomatic relations
B. 1992 — full India-Israel diplomatic relations
C. 2017 — recognition of Palestine
D. 2020 — launch of the India-Israel Industrial R&D Fund

**Answer: B**

India and Israel established full diplomatic relations in 1992. India recognised Palestine in

### Q11. The Indo-Israel Agricultural Project's Centres of Excellence principally demonstrate:

A. automatic nationwide productivity gains
B. a defence-technology transfer mechanism
C. agricultural demonstration technologies
D. a binding minilateral obligation

**Answer: C**

The centres translate bilateral ties into applied agricultural learning. Wider impact still
depends on extension, affordability and scaling.

### Q12. Which statement correctly describes the 26 February 2026 India-Israel joint statement?

A. It converted the relationship into a treaty alliance covering AI, cybersecurity, semiconductors, quantum computing, biotechnology, defence platforms and space.
B. It restored full diplomatic relations after a formal break and reopened resident embassies in 2026.
C. It certified immediate operation of the listed AI, semiconductor, cyber, quantum and space projects.
D. It elevated ties to a “Special Strategic Partnership for Peace, Innovation and Prosperity” and set priorities without proving delivery.
**Answer: D**

The joint statement identifies AI, cybersecurity, semiconductors, quantum computing,
biotechnology, agriculture/water, defence platforms and space; welcomes an NSA-led Critical and
Emerging Technologies initiative; and notes the Defence Cooperation MoU signed on 4 November
2025. It is a political status and roadmap, not a treaty alliance or delivery certificate. The
I4F, launched in 2017, remains a separate implementation mechanism.

### Q13. Tharoor's “not at the expense of” formulation supports which interpretation?

A. Deep Israel ties without sacrificing Arab and Muslim-world relations.
B. India maintains comparable distance from the principal regional actors.
C. India owes treaty-based support to each regional partner simultaneously.
D. India avoids issue-specific positions.

**Answer: A**

The phrase anchors active, non-exclusive balancing. It does not mean equidistance, treaty
alignment or avoidance of choices.

Recognition in 1988 is a stable anchor. It coexists with full diplomatic relations with Israel

### Q17. Which chronology is correct?

A. JCPOA concluded 14 July 2015 → US withdrawal announced 8 May 2018 → Significant Reduction Exceptions ended May 2019
B. US withdrawal announced 14 July 2015 → JCPOA concluded 8 May 2018 → exceptions ended May 2024
C. JCPOA concluded May 2019 → Chabahar contract 13 May 2024 → US withdrawal announced 8 May 2018
D. Resolution 2231 adopted in 2005 → JCPOA concluded 2015 → exceptions ended May 2018
**Answer: A**

The chronology explains how a diplomatic opening was followed by renewed exposure for Indian
energy, banking, insurance and connectivity channels.

### Q18. What followed the end of US Significant Reduction Exceptions in May 2019?

A. India eliminated aggregate crude dependence through domestic substitution.
B. India stopped Iranian crude imports and changed suppliers.
C. The JCPOA entered full operation after the exception period ended.
D. Chabahar obtained a permanent sanctions exemption covering all transactions.
**Answer: B**

Supplier substitution reduced immediate sanctions exposure, but narrowed the Iran relationship
and did not remove aggregate dependence.

### Q19. Which statement correctly distinguishes sanctions exposure from obligation?

A. Domestic sanctions law creates an international obligation binding India.
B. Unilateral sanctions have no material effect on third-country firms.
C. Foreign sanctions create exposure, not an automatic international-law duty for India.
D. Commercial exposure and legal obligation are synonyms.

**Answer: C**

Practical compliance may be driven by market access and financial risk. The source of legal duty
still has to be identified; exposure is not itself proof of obligation.

### Q20. Which statement about Chabahar is accurate?

A. It became an I2U2 connectivity project through a four-government instrument signed on 13 May 2024.
B. The 13 May 2024 contract certified a completed India–Europe corridor with regular end-to-end service.
C. Its US non-exposure period became an irrevocable treaty entitlement lasting through the ten-year contract.
D. Ten-year contract signed 13 May 2024; conditional US non-exposure expired 26 April 2026, leaving current exposure unresolved.
**Answer: D**

The contract is an instrument, not a guaranteed outcome. The United States announced revocation
effective 29 September 2025, then provided a conditional waiver/non-exposure period through
26 April 2026. That period has expired. This sequence illustrates why a foreign executive
exception is reversible and why post-expiry exposure must be assessed without calling it an
automatic UN or international-law obligation.

### Q21. I2U2 consists of:

A. India, Israel, the UAE and the United States
B. India, Iran, the UAE and the United States
C. India, Israel, Saudi Arabia and the European Union
D. India, Iraq, the United Kingdom and the United States

**Answer: A**

I2U2 is an issue-based minilateral focused on food, water, energy and technology. It is not a
collective-defence alliance.

### Q22. Which is the most evidence-disciplined claim about I2U2?

A. Official four-party leaders' summits met annually from 2024 to 2026 and approved the two proposed projects.
B. The 2023 official Business Forum and 23 April 2025 N7 dialogue show activity, not a four-government decision or delivered project.
C. It operates as a treaty organisation whose supranational body can enforce project commitments against members.
D. Both announced projects entered sustained operation before the April 2025 dialogue reviewed their outcomes.
**Answer: B**

The inaugural official Business Forum met in Abu Dhabi on 22 February 2023 and assessed the
announced food-security and clean-energy projects. The N7-hosted New Delhi dialogue brought
together senior officials and experts from all four countries, assessed projects and explored
new sectors. B preserves both facts and status: a convening is not
automatically an intergovernmental joint outcome or project delivery.

### Q23. Which set lists the eight IMEC MoU parties recorded for 9 September 2023?

Israel and Jordan may appear in route discussions, but they are not among the eight signatories

### Q24. Which sequence best represents a defensible corridor delivery ladder?

D. concept → MoU/framework → finance/contracts → construction → operating service → measured outcome

It is the principal common-route chokepoint for Gulf energy shipments. Supplier diversity does

### Q26. Why is regional-conflict risk in West Asia described as compounding?

The interests covary. A single shock can reach several channels at once, so a long list of

### PART A — DIRECT MAINS PYQS

#### PYQ 1 · 2018 · GS-II · Q9 · DIRECT · 10 marks · 150 words

> "India's relations with Israel have, of late, acquired a depth and diversity, which cannot be
> rolled back." Discuss.

**Verification note.** The official scan was visually checked: it contains the word
**“acquired”** and prints **“Answer in 150 words”** with **10 marks**. The local OCR export omits
“acquired” and corrupts the word-limit tail; that OCR defect is recorded separately rather than
allowed to override the scan.

**Demand decoding.** “Discuss” requires both the evidence for depth/diversity and the political
limits on irreversibility.

**Original model answer (148 words).**

India-Israel ties combine durable political-security cooperation with domains beyond arms.

Full diplomatic relations began in 1992; the first prime-ministerial visit in July 2017 made the
partnership openly political. On 26 February 2026, a joint statement elevated ties to a
“Special Strategic Partnership for Peace, Innovation and Prosperity”. Defence procurement,
counter-terrorism and Barak-8 co-development provide a strategic core; the statement also
welcomed the Defence Cooperation MoU of 4 November 2025. Agricultural Centres of Excellence and
the I4F add water, farming and civilian innovation, while the 2026 statement identifies AI,
cybersecurity, semiconductors, quantum, biotechnology and space as cooperation priorities.

Yet “cannot be rolled back” should be qualified. A declared partnership and roadmap do not prove
every project delivered; technology-transfer depth, scaling and regional conflict remain
constraints. India also recognises Palestine and supports a negotiated two-state solution.
Thus, the partnership is resilient, but depends on Arab/Gulf ties and humanitarian credibility.

**Why this earns marks.** It proves both “depth” and “diversity” with named evidence, then
qualifies the absolute phrase in the stem.

#### PYQ 2 · 2018 · GS-II · Q20 · DIRECT · 15 marks · 250 words

> In what ways would the ongoing US-Iran Nuclear Pact Controversy affect the national interest of
> India? How should India respond to this situation?

**Demand decoding.** Two limbs: channels of impact, then a calibrated Indian response.

**Original model answer (250 words).**

The US-Iran nuclear controversy affects India through energy, finance, connectivity and strategic
autonomy rather than through one channel alone. The JCPOA was concluded on 14 July 2015 and
endorsed by UN Security Council Resolution 2231; the United States announced withdrawal on
8 May 2018 and restored sanctions in stages.

**Effects on national interest.** First, Iran had been an important energy partner. The end of US
Significant Reduction Exceptions in May 2019 led India to cease Iranian crude purchases and
substitute suppliers. This reduced immediate sanctions exposure but did not reduce aggregate
import dependence. Second, Tharoor's observation that several Indian refineries were configured
for Iranian crude shows that substitution carries physical and commercial costs. Third, banking,
insurance and shipping restrictions can impede trade even where India does not accept a foreign
sanction as an international-law obligation. Fourth, pressure on Chabahar constrains India's
Pakistan-bypassing connectivity toward Afghanistan and Central Asia. Finally, the dispute tests
India's ability to preserve Iran ties while deepening relations with the United States, Israel
and the Gulf.

India should diversify suppliers and payments, maintain dialogue and protect viable connectivity
through negotiated arrangements. The Chabahar exception
announced for revocation from 29 September 2025 was followed by a conditional non-exposure period
through 26 April 2026; that period has expired. India must therefore assess current US banking,
insurance and secondary-sanctions exposure separately from any UN or international-law
obligation, while accelerating domestic energy resilience.

Thus, strategic autonomy requires risk reduction without abandoning Iran or allowing one
partnership to dictate the entire portfolio.

**Why this earns marks.** It answers both directives, traces four mechanisms, uses dates and
distinguishes legal obligation from commercial exposure.

#### PYQ 3 · 2025 · GS-II · Q19 · DIRECT · 15 marks · 250 words

> "Energy security constitutes the dominant kingpin of India's foreign policy, and is linked with
> India's overarching influence in Middle Eastern countries." How would you integrate energy
> security with India's foreign policy trajectories in the coming years?
>
> (Answer in 250 words)

**Verification note.** Exact English text is reproduced from the locally held official export.
The audited ledger records this as Q19, 15 marks, 250 words.

**Demand decoding.** Accept energy's centrality but integrate it with the wider foreign-policy
portfolio; “coming years” requires direction, not prediction.

**Original model answer (243 words).**

Energy security remains the organising interest of India's West Asia policy, but influence
cannot rest on hydrocarbon purchase alone. PPAC records crude-oil import dependence of 88.2% in
FY2024-25 and about 88.7% provisionally in FY2025-26. Policy must therefore combine security,
affordability and sustainability.

First, India should diversify suppliers and overseas equity while recognising that a wider
supplier basket manages concentration risk rather than aggregate dependence. Refinery
configuration, freight and the Strait of Hormuz create residual switching and route risks.

Second, energy diplomacy should use the India-GCC Joint Action Plan 2024-2028, UAE and Oman
CEPAs, and Saudi/Qatar bilateral mechanisms. Partner interests in investment, technology and
economic diversification must be built into projects.

Third, the portfolio must remain non-exclusive. Israel contributes defence, agricultural and
innovation capabilities; Iran remains relevant to energy and Chabahar connectivity. The
conditional Chabahar non-exposure period expired on 26 April 2026. India must simultaneously
sustain its recognition of Palestine and support for a negotiated two-state solution.

Fourth, diaspora protection must be treated as energy policy's human-security counterpart.
Operation Sindhu's evacuation of 4,415 Indians by 27 June 2025 shows how regional escalation can
affect citizens, shipping and diplomacy together.

Finally, I2U2 and IMEC can connect energy with food, technology and logistics, but an MoU or
project announcement must not be treated as delivered infrastructure.

India should therefore pursue diversified supply, resilient routes, credible humanitarian
diplomacy and a faster domestic transition. Influence will follow reliable partnership and
delivery, not dependence alone.

**Why this earns marks.** It integrates the trilemma, institutions, partners, diaspora,
connectivity and transition while preserving status and uncertainty.

### PART B — SHARED / APPLICATION MAINS PYQ

#### PYQ 4 · 2022 · GS-II · Q19 · SHARED/APPLICATION · 15 marks · 250 words

**Primary owner:** Topic 10 — Regional, Global and Minilateral Groupings.
**Topic 06 contribution:** West Asia, food/energy/technology complementarities and delivery risk.

> How will I2U2 (India, Israel, UAE and USA) grouping transform India's position in global
> politics?
>
> (Answer in 250 words)

**Verification note.** Neutrally line-joined from the locally held official-paper export; no
missing word is supplied.

**Original model answer (250 words).**

I2U2 can strengthen India's global position by demonstrating that strategic autonomy is
compatible with minilateral cooperation. It combines India's market and implementation
capacity, Israel's technology, UAE capital and logistics, and US convening and innovation
networks. The official inaugural Business Forum met in Abu Dhabi on 22 February 2023 and
assessed the announced projects.

First, projects in food security, water, energy and technology can move India's West Asia
engagement beyond a buyer-seller energy relationship. This gives India a role in producing
regional public goods. Second, cooperation with Israel and an Arab Gulf state in the same forum
shows India's capacity for non-exclusive partnerships and benefits from the regional space
created by Arab-Israel normalisation. Third, issue-based cooperation can attract investment and
technology without the collective-defence obligations of an alliance. Fourth, I2U2 complements
bilateral Gulf ties and prospective connectivity through IMEC, thereby linking West Asian
engagement with wider supply-chain and logistics ambitions.

Its transformative effect, however, is conditional. A minilateral has no supranational
enforcement; project finance, commercial viability and sustained political attention determine
delivery. Regional conflict can strain Israel-Arab cooperation, while visible association with
the United States can create autonomy concerns. An N7-hosted high-level dialogue in New Delhi on
23 April 2025 brought senior officials and experts from all four countries together to assess
projects, but it was not a four-government leaders'/ministerial meeting or joint governmental
outcome.

I2U2 can therefore enhance India's image as a pragmatic bridge and project partner, but only
delivered projects—not membership alone—will transform its global position.

**Why this earns marks.** It explains the mechanism of transformation, names complementarities
and qualifies the claim through institutional and regional limits.

#### 2018 Prelims GS-I · Q24 · DIRECT · neutral rendering

> The term "two-state solution" is sometimes mentioned in the news in the context of the affairs
> of: (a) China (b) Israel (c) Iraq (d) Yemen.

**TEXT-BASED / WORKBOOK INFERENCE: (b) Israel. OFFICIAL KEY UNAVAILABLE.**

**Option-by-option solution**

- **(a) China — incorrect.** The question's standard international-affairs usage is not the
  cross-Strait issue.

#### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

> Distinguish supplier diversification from reduction of energy-import dependence in the context
> of India's West Asia policy. (Answer in not more than 150 words)

**Demand decoding.** Define both concepts, explain the policy significance and give one
qualification.

**Model answer (137 words).**

Supplier diversification changes **where** India imports energy from; dependence-reduction
changes **how much** of consumption must be imported. The distinction is crucial for West Asia
policy.

PPAC records crude-oil import dependence of 88.2% in FY2024-25 and about 88.7% provisionally in
FY2025-26. The Economic Survey 2025-26 nevertheless records a wider supplier basket, including
higher US and UAE shares in April-November FY26. Egypt, Nigeria and Libya also gained share,
while Russia, Saudi Arabia, Iraq and Venezuela declined. Thus, concentration risk can fall while
aggregate dependence remains high.

Diversification and overseas equity stakes improve bargaining space and reduce a single
supplier's disruption risk. However, refinery configuration, freight, insurance and common
passage through the Strait of Hormuz preserve switching and route exposure.

Therefore, diversification is necessary risk management; genuine dependence-reduction requires
domestic production, efficiency, substitutes and a sustained energy transition.

**Why this earns marks.** It defines, contrasts, applies data and gives the long-run policy
implication.

#### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

> Why is diaspora welfare an integral, rather than incidental, dimension of India's West Asia
> policy? (Answer in not more than 150 words)

**Demand decoding.** Explain the causal importance of diaspora, not merely its size.

**Model answer (134 words).**

Diaspora welfare is integral because a large Indian presence converts regional stability into a
direct state responsibility. MEA's January 2026 table records 4,344,008 overseas Indians in the
UAE and 2,750,551 in Saudi Arabia, while older book estimates are period-specific and should not
be treated as current citizenship totals.

The community deepens people-to-people and economic ties, but also creates exposure. A conflict
can simultaneously threaten livelihoods, consular access, transport and family security.
Operation Sindhu, which evacuated 4,415 Indians from Iran and Israel by 27 June 2025, shows the
mechanism: host-government coordination and diplomatic access become immediate foreign-policy
tasks.

Diaspora policy must therefore include documentation, warning systems, labour and welfare
engagement, contingency planning and evacuation diplomacy. Remittances matter, but reducing the
community to remittance flows ignores the sovereign duty of protection.

**Why this earns marks.** It links scale to obligation, uses a dated operation and separates
population from remittance analysis.

#### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

> Analyse how India can deepen relations with Israel while sustaining its Palestine position and
> Arab/Gulf partnerships. (Answer in not more than 250 words)

**Demand decoding.** Explain the mechanism of simultaneous engagement and its credibility limits.

**Model answer (246 words).**

India can sustain the three relationships by treating them as distinct but connected interest
tracks rather than mutually exclusive camps.

With Israel, full diplomatic relations since 1992 have expanded into defence, Barak-8
co-development, agricultural Centres of Excellence and the India-Israel Industrial R&D Fund
launched in 2017. The 26 February 2026 joint statement elevated ties to a “Special Strategic
Partnership for Peace, Innovation & Prosperity” and identified critical technologies and a
defence roadmap. These instruments meet Indian needs in security, water, farming and innovation,
but the declaration does not itself prove delivery.

With Palestine, India recognised the State of Palestine in 1988 and supports a negotiated,
sovereign and viable two-state solution alongside Israel. Humanitarian assistance, civilian
protection and dialogue preserve the normative basis of this position. Engagement with Israel
must therefore not be presented as endorsement of every Israeli policy.

With the Gulf, energy, investment and millions of Indian residents create enduring stakes. The
India-GCC Joint Action Plan 2024-2028 and bilateral mechanisms with the UAE, Saudi Arabia and
Qatar institutionalise these ties. I2U2 also shows that Israel and an Arab Gulf state can
cooperate with India in a focused minilateral.

The approach requires consistent language, partner consultation and avoidance of zero-sum
signalling. Tharoor's phrase—Israel ties “not at the expense of” Arab and Muslim friendships—is
the correct principle. Yet regional war can strain political acceptance and corridor delivery.

India should therefore preserve issue-specific cooperation, credible humanitarian diplomacy and
independent judgement. Balance is active management, not equidistance.

**Why this earns marks.** It covers all three relationship tracks, names instruments and explains
how credibility is maintained.

#### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

> Examine Chabahar as a test of India's strategic autonomy under sanctions pressure.
> (Answer in not more than 250 words)

**Demand decoding.** Evaluate both the strategic utility and the limits imposed by third-country
financial power.

**Model answer (219 words).**

Chabahar tests strategic autonomy because it joins a clear Indian connectivity interest to an
external sanctions environment India does not fully control.

The port offers access toward Afghanistan and Central Asia without routing through Pakistan.
India signed a 10-year contract on 13 May 2024, showing willingness to preserve a long-term Iran
track while also deepening relations with the United States, Israel and Gulf states.

The constraint is not only diplomatic. Banking, insurance, shipping and technology providers may
avoid a transaction because of secondary-sanctions exposure or over-compliance. The United States
announced revocation effective 29 September 2025, but later supplied a conditional
non-exposure period through 26 April 2026. Its expiry demonstrates that an executive exception
can protect an Indian interest temporarily but remains reversible.

Post-expiry commercial and secondary-sanctions exposure must not be confused with an automatic
UN or international-law obligation on India. Legal duty, commercial risk and political pressure
require separate analysis. Nor does a port contract prove an operating end-to-end corridor:
onward infrastructure, finance, customs, security and freight demand determine delivery.

India should maintain dialogue with Iran and sanctioning powers, diversify finance and logistics
where lawful, sequence investment against verified exemptions and develop alternative routes.

Thus, Chabahar demonstrates that autonomy is not freedom from constraints; it is the capacity to
pursue an interest while pricing legal, financial and delivery risks.

**Why this earns marks.** It uses the contract/waiver sequence to distinguish autonomy, exposure,
obligation and implementation.

#### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 MARKS

> “India's West Asia policy faces correlated rather than isolated risks.” Critically examine with
> reference to energy, diaspora and connectivity. (Answer in not more than 300 words)

**Demand decoding.** Establish the common-shock mechanism, test it across three sectors and
qualify the proposition.

**Model answer (268 words).**

India's West Asia risks are correlated because the same regional shock can affect energy supply,
citizens abroad and connectivity projects simultaneously.

**Energy:** PPAC records crude import dependence of 88.2% in FY2024-25 and about 88.7%
provisionally in FY2025-26. Supplier diversification reduces concentration, but Gulf cargoes can
retain common exposure to the Strait of Hormuz. Conflict also raises freight, insurance and
payment risks. Thus, source diversity may coexist with route dependence.

**Diaspora:** MEA's January 2026 table records over four million overseas Indians in the UAE and
over two million in Saudi Arabia. Regional escalation therefore creates an immediate protection
obligation. Operation Sindhu's evacuation of 4,415 Indians from Iran and Israel by 27 June 2025
shows that the human-security effect can arise alongside energy and diplomatic disruption.

**Connectivity:** IMEC rests on an eight-party MoU of 9 September 2023; Chabahar on a 10-year
contract of 13 May 2024. Neither instrument guarantees end-to-end operation. Conflict can alter
route security and political acceptance, while sanctions can obstruct Chabahar's banking,
insurance and logistics. The announced revocation effective 29 September 2025, subsequent
conditional non-exposure period through 26 April 2026 and its expiry illustrate the changing
commercial exposure.

The proposition needs qualification. Not every conflict produces every effect, and bilateral
institutions, alternative suppliers and evacuation capacity provide buffers. I2U2 and the
India-GCC Joint Action Plan can improve coordination, but forums do not themselves deliver
resilience.

India should reduce volume, source and route dependence; maintain strategic reserves and
contingency diplomacy; protect expatriates; diversify corridors; and judge initiatives through
finance, construction and operation.

Hence, resilience requires managing common points of failure, not merely accumulating separate
partnerships.

**Why this earns marks.** It proves covariance through three mechanisms, adds counter-evidence
and ends with a targeted resilience framework.

#### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

> Evaluate the strategic promise and implementation limits of I2U2, IMEC and Chabahar in India's
> West Asia connectivity strategy. (Answer in not more than 300 words)

**Demand decoding.** Compare legal/institutional form, strategic use and delivery conditions;
do not treat all three as corridors.

**Model answer (298 words).**

I2U2, IMEC and Chabahar diversify India's instruments but are not interchangeable corridors.

**I2U2** is an issue-based minilateral of India, Israel, the UAE and the United States. Its promise
lies in combining Indian scale, Israeli technology, UAE capital/logistics and US convening for
food, water, energy and technology projects. Its limits are political attention, finance and the
absence of supranational enforcement. The official inaugural Business Forum met in Abu Dhabi on
22 February 2023. An N7-hosted high-level dialogue in New Delhi on 23 April 2025 brought senior
officials and experts from all four countries together and assessed projects; it was not a
four-government leaders'/ministerial meeting or joint governmental outcome.

**IMEC** is a transcontinental corridor initiative based on an eight-party MoU signed on
9 September 2023. It can link India, the Gulf and Europe, support logistics diversification and
embed India in regional economic networks. However, a MoU and a reference to “commencement of
work” do not prove financing, construction, an operating segment or commercial viability.
Regional conflict, standards, customs coordination and partner incentives remain decisive.

**Chabahar** is a bilateral port/connectivity instrument serving access toward Afghanistan and
Central Asia. The 10-year contract signed on 13 May 2024 gives it a firmer operational instrument
than a corridor concept, but onward links, cargo demand and finance still matter. After revocation
was announced effective 29 September 2025, a conditional non-exposure period ran through
26 April 2026 and expired. Current US commercial and secondary-sanctions exposure is not
automatically a UN or international-law obligation on India.

They are complementary: I2U2 convenes projects, IMEC frames an India-Gulf-Europe route, and
Chabahar preserves an Iran-Central Asia option. India should sequence investment, publish
milestone evidence, diversify finance and consult partners.

Their strategic value will be measured by reliable services and economic use, not by the number
of announcements.

**Why this earns marks.** It compares form, function and limits, uses exact status anchors and
delivers a graded verdict rather than promotional description.
