### SESSION 1 — Scope, principles and African agency

#### VISUAL

```text
AFRICAN PRIORITIES / AGENDA 2063
              |
              v
INDIA-AFRICA POLITICAL DIALOGUE
              |
              v
CHOICE OF INSTRUMENT
grant | concessional LoC | training | institution | digital platform | trade
              |
              v
PARTNER-COUNTRY / AU / REC OWNERSHIP
              |
              v
DELIVERY + MAINTENANCE + LOCAL USE
              |
              v
DEVELOPMENT OUTCOME — or an identifiable implementation gap
```

*The relationship is credible when the partner's priority survives every stage of the project
cycle.*

#### Core idea

✅ The official MEA Africa brief describes India-Africa relations as a multi-faceted partnership
covering political engagement, economic cooperation, development partnership, capacity building
and people-to-people ties. It states that engagement is based on **mutual respect, equality and a
demand-driven partnership aligned with African priorities and Agenda 2063**.

✅ The African Union calls **Agenda 2063** Africa's own blueprint for inclusive and sustainable
development and links it to the Pan-African vision of an integrated, prosperous and peaceful
Africa driven by its own citizens.

⚠️ Therefore, “demand-driven” is not a decorative adjective. It requires:

1. the African state, AU or regional economic community to identify the priority;
2. the instrument to fit that priority;
3. local institutions to participate in design and implementation;
4. skills, maintenance and data-control capacity to remain after launch; and
5. outcomes to be assessed from the partner's perspective, not only India's diplomatic gain.

#### Topic boundary

| This topic owns | Primary owner elsewhere |
|---|---|

#### Exam use

> ⚠️ India's comparative proposition in Africa is not “aid without interests”; it is a
> South-South, demand-driven partnership in which human-capital and institutional capacity are
> prominent instruments. Its credibility still depends on finance terms, delivery, maintenance
> and African ownership.

#### Traps

- ❌ Africa is a single negotiating actor.
  ✅ The AU, regional economic communities and 54 states have different mandates and priorities.
- ❌ Demand-driven design proves successful implementation.
  ✅ Design, financing, delivery, adoption and outcome are separate tests.
- ❌ Partnership means India has no strategic or commercial interests.
  ✅ Mutual benefit is compatible with partnership; opacity or one-sided control is not.

### SESSION 2 — India-Africa Forum Summit: architecture, chronology and current status

#### TIMELINE

```text
IAFS-I                 IAFS-II                    IAFS-III
New Delhi              Addis Ababa                New Delhi
8-9 Apr 2008  ------>  24-25 May 2011  --------> 26-29 Oct 2015
     |                                               |
structured summit framework                         last summit held
                                                     |
                                  23 Apr 2026: IAFS-IV theme/logo/site launched
                                                     |
                                  planned for New Delhi, 28-31 May 2026
                                                     |
                                  21 May 2026: India and AU agree to convene later
                                                     |
                                  26 Sep 2026 cut-off: no held summit/new date verified
```

#### What IAFS is

✅ IAFS is the umbrella summit-level political framework for structured India-Africa engagement.
The first three summits were held in 2008, 2011 and 2015.

📰 The AU's **15 May 2026** media advisory described IAFS-IV as the premier high-level
India-AU platform, scheduled for **28-31 May 2026** in New Delhi under the theme **“IA SPIRIT:
India-Africa Strategic Partnership for Innovation, Resilience, and Inclusive Transformation.”**
Its listed outcomes were expected outcomes, not adopted decisions.

📰 The joint India-AU release dated **21 May 2026** stated that, after consultations concerning
the evolving health situation in parts of Africa and the need for full participation, the two
sides agreed that IAFS-IV should be convened at a later date. New dates were to be decided through
mutual consultation.

#### Status discipline

| Claim | Safe status |
|---|---|
| Theme/logo/website launched on 23 April 2026 | preparatory announcement |
| AU advisory listed declaration/framework as expected outcomes | agenda/expectation |
| Summit scheduled for 28-31 May 2026 | planned |
| Joint release of 21 May | deferred to a later date |
| “IAFS-IV adopted a declaration” | unsupported at the cut-off |
| “IAFS-IV was held in 2026” | false on the verified record |

⚠️ The interval after IAFS-III reveals a **political-architecture continuity gap**. It does not
prove that every India-Africa programme stopped: bilateral, training, credit and digital
instruments continued through other channels.

### SESSION 3 — Development-finance grammar: grant, LoC and technical assistance

#### COMPARISON TABLE

| Instrument | Financial character | Repayment | Main implementing route | What it proves | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| **Grant-in-aid** | non-repayable public assistance | No | Government of India/MEA with host government and project agency | an approved non-debt contribution | completion or measured impact |
| **Concessional Line of Credit (LoC)** | loan on concessional terms for identified projects | Yes | Government of India under IDEAS through Exim Bank; partner-country project machinery | financing commitment/availability | full disbursement, completion or debt-free aid |
| **Technical assistance** | training, experts, centres, systems and knowledge transfer | normally not a sovereign loan | MEA's Development Partnership Administration, ITEC, Indian institutions/experts and partner bodies | capacity input | automatic local adoption or lasting institutional capability |
| **Commercial trade/investment** | market transaction or private/public investment | commercial | firms, banks and regulators | economic engagement | development assistance |

#### Canonical foundation

✅ Shashi Tharoor records that at the **first IAFS** India announced a **US$500 million grant**
specifically for human-resource-development and capacity-building projects. This is a
**book-period announcement**, not a current disbursement total.

📰 MEA's Africa brief, whose PDF metadata is dated **18 April 2026**, records:

- more than **190 LoCs** worth over **US$10 billion** to **41 African countries**;
- **220 projects** worth **US$4.5 billion** successfully completed;
- grant assistance exceeding **US$700 million since 2015**.

⚠️ These three figures are not interchangeable. “LoCs extended” is a commitment stock;
“projects completed” is a delivery count/value; “grant assistance” is non-repayable finance.

📰 The same brief records India-Africa trade of **US$81.99 billion in FY2024-25**
(Indian exports US$42.6 billion; imports US$39.2 billion), describes India as Africa's
fourth-largest trading partner and among its top five investors, and gives cumulative Indian
investment of approximately **US$80 billion during 1996-2025**. These figures establish economic
breadth; they are not development-assistance totals.

#### The six-rung evidence ladder

```text
1 ANNOUNCED
      ↓
2 FINANCING APPROVED / LoC EXTENDED / GRANT SANCTIONED
      ↓
3 CONTRACTED + IMPLEMENTING AGENCY IDENTIFIED
      ↓
4 DISBURSED / CONSTRUCTED / PLATFORM DEPLOYED
      ↓
5 OPERATED + MAINTAINED + USED
      ↓
6 OUTCOME MEASURED
```

> 🔑 **Mnemonic: A-F-C-D-O-O** — Announcement, Finance, Contract, Delivery, Operation, Outcome.

#### Source-snapshot caution

MEA's general LoC webpage separately states that **196 LoCs worth US$12 billion to 42 African
countries** had been extended **as on August 2024**. The April 2026 Africa brief uses the
more-than-190/over-US$10-billion/41-country formulation. Different official snapshots or counting
scopes must not be merged into a fictitious single statistic. In an answer, cite one source and
its date.

### SESSION 4 — Human-capital partnership and joint institutions

#### CAPACITY STACK

```text
TRAINING POSITIONS
        +
SCHOLARSHIPS / STUDENT MOBILITY
        +
JOINT INSTITUTIONS ON AFRICAN SOIL
        +
INDIAN CAMPUSES / IT CENTRES / VOCATIONAL CENTRES
        |
        v
skills + networks + institutional capability
        |
        v
only if participation, completion, staffing and local use persist
```

#### Book-period canonical evidence

- ✅ Tharoor cites **1,600 training positions** under India's technical-cooperation programme
  specifically for Africa.
- ✅ He estimates **10,000-15,000 African students** studying in India at any given time. Treat
  this as a book-period range, not a current annual enrolment statistic.
- ✅ He describes India as being in the process of establishing **19 institutions** jointly with
  the African Union Commission and member states:
  - India-Africa Institute of Information Technology;
  - India-Africa Institute of Foreign Trade;
  - India-Africa Institute of Educational Planning and Administration;
  - India-Africa Diamond Institute;
  - ten vocational training centres; and
  - five human-settlement institutes.

⚠️ “In the process of establishing” cannot be rewritten as “all 19 are currently completed and
functioning”.

#### Current official evidence

📰 The April 2026 MEA brief records:

- **8 vocational training centres** and **7 IT centres** set up in Africa;
- more than **70,000 scholarships and skill-development slots since 2015** across ITEC,
  defence ITEC, C.V. Raman, ICCR and e-VBAB schemes;
- more than **40,000 officials trained under ITEC in the last ten years**, despite two years of
  COVID disruption;
- **15,116 e-VBAB scholarships offered**;
- IIT Madras's Zanzibar campus in Tanzania and the National Forensic Sciences University campus
  in Uganda as institutional examples.

✅ The Economic Survey 2025-26 separately states that ITEC had trained **more than two lakh
persons from over 160 countries** in civilian and defence sectors. That is a **global ITEC
snapshot**, not an Africa-only count.

📰 MEA's undated live development-partnership page, accessed **26 September 2026**, estimates that
ITEC has trained close to
**400,000 government officials** since 1964 and currently offers about **12,000 civilian/
paramilitary** and **3,900 defence** slots annually. This is a later programme-wide webpage
snapshot; it does not retroactively turn the Survey's figure into an Africa figure.

#### Input-output-outcome discipline

| Stage | Example | Safe inference |
|---|---|---|
| Input | slot or scholarship offered | opportunity created |
| Output | participant completes course | training delivered |
| Institutional output | centre established and staffed | local platform exists |
| Outcome | service quality, employability or administrative performance improves | development effect, requiring evidence |

### SESSION 5 — Digital succession: Pan-African e-Network to e-VBAB

#### VISUAL

```text
PAN-AFRICAN e-NETWORK
tele-education + telemedicine + connectivity
implemented by TCIL; historical project
                |
                | concluded in 2017
                v
MEA-TCIL e-VBAB IMPLEMENTATION AGREEMENT
10 September 2018
                |
                v
e-VIDYABHARATI + e-AAROGYABHARATI
launched in 2019
online education + telemedicine
                |
                v
15,116 scholarships OFFERED (MEA April 2026 brief)
```

✅ The Pan-African e-Network is the historical precursor. MEA records it as concluded in 2017.
It must not be described as the current flagship.

✅ The e-VidyaBharati and e-ArogyaBharati Network Project (**e-VBAB**) is the successor. MEA and
Telecommunications Consultants India Limited (**TCIL**) signed the implementation agreement on
**10 September 2018**; the project was launched in **2019**.

✅ An official High Commission of India page recorded **22 participating African countries** in
September 2023. The April 2026 MEA Africa brief directly records **15,116 scholarships offered**.
The two indicators describe participation and offers; neither proves course completion, health
outcomes or employment.

#### Project-ownership card

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Political owner/funder | Government of India through MEA |
| Implementing partner | TCIL for the network implementation |
| Partner side | participating African governments/institutions |
| Service layer | Indian universities and hospitals linked to African learners/professionals |
| Outcome test | enrolment, completion, clinical use, local technical capability, uptime and maintenance |

#### Traps

- ❌ PAeNP and e-VBAB are simultaneous names for one current platform.
- ❌ Scholarships offered = degrees completed.
- ❌ A network launch proves continent-wide access.
- ✅ Write the succession, actor and measurable stage.

### SESSION 6 — The contemporary DPI layer and digital sovereignty

#### FOUR CURRENT ROUTES

```text
REGIONAL RULE/COOPERATION FRAME
India-SADC MoU, 2 July 2024
ICT + connectivity + access + DPI
             |
             +-----------------------------+
             |                             |
     BILATERAL INDIA STACK MoUs       IMPLEMENTATION AGREEMENT
     7 African countries              Kenya-DigiLocker
             |                             |
             +--------------+--------------+
                            |
                    OPEN-SOURCE PLATFORM
                    MOSIP partnerships in
                    19 African countries
```

#### India-SADC MoU

✅ SADC and India signed a new **Memorandum of Understanding on Economic Cooperation** on
**2 July 2024** at the SADC Secretariat. It was signed by SADC Executive Secretary
**Elias Mpedi Magosi** and India's High Commissioner to Botswana and Special Representative to
SADC **Bharath Kumar Kuthati**.

✅ It takes account of SADC's **Regional Indicative Strategic Development Plan 2020-2030** and
**Digital Transformation Strategy**. Its ten cooperation fields are:

1. industrialisation;
2. human and social development;
3. new and emerging technologies—ICT development, connectivity, access and DPI;
4. trade and investment;
5. disaster-risk management;
6. women-led development;
7. private-sector development;
8. space cooperation;
9. green growth and green infrastructure; and
10. research and innovation.

✅ It follows the earlier India-SADC economic-cooperation MoU of **14 October 1997**.

⚠️ It is a cooperation framework, not evidence that a common SADC-wide DPI system has entered
operation.

#### April 2026 official digital snapshot

📰 MEA records:

- India Stack MoUs with **seven** African countries—Kenya, Sierra Leone, Tanzania, The Gambia,
  Ethiopia, Lesotho and Seychelles;
- Kenya as the first foreign country to sign an **Implementation Framework Agreement** with
  India to adopt **DigiLocker**;
- the **Modular Open Source Identity Platform (MOSIP)**, developed by IIIT Bangalore, partnering
  with **19 African countries**.

#### Digital-sovereignty test

Digital sovereignty asks whether partnership strengthens an African state's control over:

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Legal authority | Who defines identity, privacy, grievance and exclusion rules? |
| Data | Where is data held, who accesses it, and under which law? |
| Technology | Can local institutions maintain, audit and adapt the system? |
| Procurement | Is the partner locked into one vendor or architecture? |
| Interoperability | Can systems communicate without surrendering policy control? |
| Inclusion | Can people without devices, connectivity or documents obtain services? |

✅ The **African Digital Compact**, adopted at the AU Executive Council session in Accra on
**18-19 July 2024**, is Africa's common position on digital transformation. It emphasises
inclusion, digital rights and a secure and equitable digital environment and is anchored in
Agenda 2063 and the AU Digital Transformation Strategy.

⚠️ Alignment with an African framework supports the demand-driven claim; it does not by itself
prove that sovereignty, privacy, inclusion or cyber-security problems are solved.

### SESSION 7 — Health, agriculture and education as development partnership

#### SECTOR MATRIX

| Sector | Instrument/evidence | Development mechanism | Qualification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Health | PAeNP/e-ArogyaBharati telemedicine; Indian medicines, vaccines and training | lower-cost access and continuing institutional links | regulation, procurement, connectivity and local capacity determine use |
| Education | e-VidyaBharati, ICCR/ITEC scholarships, African students in India, IIT Zanzibar | skills, professional networks and local institution building | offer/enrolment/completion/outcome must remain separate |
| Agriculture | mechanisation, irrigation, seed/fertiliser and processing cooperation | food-security and productivity capacity | country ownership, ecology, affordability and maintenance matter |
| Pharmaceuticals | generics, vaccines, training and manufacturing cooperation | access plus industrial capability | imports alone do not create local manufacturing or resilient supply |

📰 The April 2026 MEA brief states that India supplies nearly **50% of Africa's generic
medicines**, records COVID-period medical assistance and vaccine supply, and identifies
pharmaceutical-manufacturing cooperation in several countries. Use these as dated official
aggregate claims, not as proof of identical country-level outcomes.

📰 The same brief describes agricultural projects and technology parks in named African
countries. In a Mains answer, one named example is stronger than an undifferentiated continental
claim.

#### Causal chain

```text
affordable product / training / platform
                  ↓
access improves
                  ↓
local personnel + regulation + maintenance
                  ↓
institutional capability
                  ↓
health / education / food-security outcome
```

The middle stages cannot be skipped.

### SESSION 8 — Defence, MAHASAGAR and sustainability cooperation

#### SECURITY ARCHITECTURE

```text
POLITICAL DIALOGUE
Lucknow Declaration, Defence Ministers' Conclave (Feb 2020)
Gandhinagar Declaration, India-Africa Defence Dialogue (18 Oct 2022)
                         |
                         v
TRAINING / EXERCISE
AFINDEX-23, Pune, 16-29 Mar 2023
UN peacekeeping + humanitarian mine action
                         |
                         v
MARITIME COOPERATION
anti-piracy | hydrography | port capacity | blue economy
                         |
                         v
NO ALLIANCE CREATED; CAPABILITY AND DELIVERY REQUIRE EVIDENCE
```

✅ PIB records the **Gandhinagar Declaration** at the India-Africa Defence Dialogue held on
**18 October 2022**, building on the 2020 Lucknow Declaration.

✅ AFINDEX was held in India in **2019** and again from **16-29 March 2023** at Aundh, Pune.
PIB records the second edition's training in humanitarian mine action and UN-mandated
peacekeeping operations. It was a field-training exercise, not a defence alliance.

📰 The April 2026 MEA brief adds **AIKEYME 2025**, a six-day multilateral naval exercise held
from **13-18 April 2025** in Dar-es-Salaam, and lists anti-piracy, hydrographic surveys, port
development and blue-economy support as maritime cooperation routes.

#### MAHASAGAR: the Africa/development-partnership bridge

✅ At **Port Louis on 12 March 2025**, India launched MAHASAGAR as the declared vision
**Mutual and Holistic Advancement for Security and Growth Across Regions**. It is
Global-South-facing and expressly encompasses:

```text
trade for development
        +
capacity building for sustainable growth
        +
mutual security for a shared future
        +
technology sharing | concessional loans | grants
```

✅ Its relevance here is specific: Mauritius is an African and Indian Ocean partner, while the
listed instruments—capacity building, technology sharing, concessional loans and grants—connect
India's wider Global South vision directly to the established India-Africa development-partnership
toolkit.

⚠️ **Exact status:** MAHASAGAR is a **declared vision—not a treaty, dedicated fund or delivery mechanism—and
does not replace SAGAR**. Nor does the declaration prove that any particular African project has
been implemented.
Detailed maritime doctrine and architecture belong to Topic 04. Topic 07 uses MAHASAGAR only to
explain how Africa-facing development and security instruments are placed inside a wider
Global-South proposition.

#### Sustainability platforms

- ✅ The **International Solar Alliance** Framework Agreement entered into force on
  **6 December 2017**; its headquarters are in Gurugram.
- ✅ The **Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure** was launched at the UN Climate Action
  Summit on **23 September 2019** and is headquartered in New Delhi.
- 📰 MEA's April 2026 brief records 39 African countries in ISA and nine in CDRI.

⚠️ Membership creates an institutional route; it is not proof that a specific solar or resilient
infrastructure project has been financed, completed or produced a measured benefit.

### SESSION 9 — Representation: from advocacy to one delivered institutional outcome

#### VISUAL

```text
AFRICAN REPRESENTATION DEFICIT
             |
             +--> G20: AU admitted as a permanent member, 9 Sep 2023
             |
             +--> UNSC reform: India supports Africa's claim
                    through the Ezulwini Consensus / Sirte Declaration
             |
             +--> BRICS: Egypt and Ethiopia included from the 2023 expansion process
```

✅ The **African Union became a permanent member of the G20 on 9 September 2023** at the New
Delhi Summit. This is a delivered institutional-status outcome, not merely an Indian statement of
support.

✅ MEA's April 2026 brief also records India's support for African representation in a reformed
UN Security Council according to the **Ezulwini Consensus and Sirte Declaration**.

⚠️ Support for UNSC reform is an advocacy position, not a completed reform. AU membership of the
G20 also does not itself solve African debt, finance, trade or implementation constraints.

#### Why representation belongs in a development answer

Development partnership has two dimensions:

1. **material capacity**—finance, skills, infrastructure, health and digital systems; and
2. **rule-making voice**—participation in institutions that shape finance, trade, technology and
   security agendas.

An answer is incomplete if it celebrates representation but ignores delivery, or lists projects
without recognising Africa's claim to institutional voice.

### SESSION 10 — Political risk, regional diversity and the China/FOCAC comparator

#### RISK MAP

```text
AFRICA IS NOT ONE RISK ZONE

Sahel / selected states -> coups, insecurity, sanctions or regime-transition risk
Horn / Red Sea          -> conflict and maritime-route exposure
Island/coastal partners -> blue economy, climate and connectivity priorities
Regional communities    -> SADC, ECOWAS, EAC, COMESA: different mandates
AU continental level    -> representation, norms and Agenda 2063
```

✅ Two direct objective PYQs route political instability here:

- **2023 Prelims Q98:** the common feature of Chad, Guinea, Mali and Sudan;
- **2024 Prelims Q91:** instability and coups in the Sahel.

⚠️ Their lesson is not “Africa is unstable”. It is that project design needs country and
subregional risk assessment. A coup can affect contracts, payments, security, legitimacy and
maintenance, but effects vary by country and project.

#### Comparator without caricature

China's Forum on China-Africa Cooperation and large infrastructure finance give African states
another partnership route. A high-quality comparison asks:

| Test | India | Any alternative partner |
|---|---|---|
| Partner priority | Was the project requested and locally integrated? | same test |
| Finance | grant, concessional loan or commercial finance? | same test |
| Procurement | who supplies and contracts? | same test |
| Delivery | on time, functional and maintained? | same test |
| Local capability | skills, jobs and technology retained? | same test |
| Sovereignty | debt, data, policy and strategic autonomy protected? | same test |

⚠️ India's strengths—training, affordable technology, medicines and demand-driven rhetoric—do
not excuse delivery delay. China's scale does not justify describing every Chinese project as
extractive. African states exercise agency and frequently diversify partners.

### SESSION 11 — Integrated answer architecture

#### MASTER ANSWER SPINE

```text
QUESTION / DIRECTIVE
        |
1 DEFINE THE PARTNERSHIP CLAIM
        |
2 IDENTIFY AFRICAN ACTOR + PRIORITY
state | AU | SADC/other REC | institution
        |
3 NAME INDIA'S INSTRUMENT
grant | LoC | ITEC | joint institution | platform | MoU | exercise
        |
4 STATE ACTOR AND STATUS
MEA/DPA | Exim Bank | TCIL | partner government/AU/REC
announced | financed | delivered | operating | measured
        |
5 EXPLAIN THE MECHANISM
how the instrument creates capacity, access, resilience or voice
        |
6 TEST THE CLAIM
maintenance | debt | procurement | data | inclusion | politics | scale
        |
7 GIVE A CALIBRATED WAY FORWARD
local ownership + transparent milestones + lifecycle support + regional tailoring
        |
8 QUALIFIED VERDICT
partnership is credible where African priorities become sustained outcomes
```

#### Directive map

| Directive | Required move |
|---|---|
| Elaborate | unpack every phrase in the proposition and evidence each |
| Examine | test the claim against positive and limiting evidence |
| Discuss | give breadth, interaction and counterpoint |
| Critically examine/evaluate | judge implementation and alternatives, not intention alone |

#### Mark-wise scaling

- **10 marks / 150 words:** thesis + three instruments + one status qualification + verdict.
- **15 marks / 250 words:** finance, human capital, digital, political voice and one security/
  sectoral dimension + implementation test.
- **20 marks / up to 300 words:** full results chain, African heterogeneity, comparator, partner
  agency, alternative explanation and prioritised reforms.

#### Final checklist

- Did I identify the African state, AU or regional community rather than say only “Africa”?
- Did I distinguish grant, LoC, technical assistance and commercial investment?
- Did I separate announced, financed, contracted, delivered, operating and measured?
- Did I name the implementing and partner agency where known?
- Did I distinguish PAeNP from e-VBAB?
- Did I avoid converting a scholarship offer into completion or outcome?
- Did I treat the SADC MoU as a framework rather than a deployed regional DPI?
- Did I integrate African digital priorities, data governance and maintenance?
- Did I avoid generalising the Sahel to the continent?

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

> Use this block to upgrade a sound core answer. Do not let it displace the Basic sequence.

### 1. Human-resource development versus infrastructure finance

✅ Tharoor's emphasis on human capital distinguishes India's self-description from a model judged
mainly by physical infrastructure volume. Yet the distinction is one of emphasis, not exclusivity:
India also finances power, water, transport, agriculture and digital-connectivity projects through
LoCs.

⚠️ The sharper comparison is therefore:

```text
PHYSICAL ASSET ONLY
        versus
ASSET + SKILLS + LOCAL INSTITUTION + MAINTENANCE CAPABILITY
```

A railway, IT centre or digital platform becomes a long-term partnership only when local systems
can operate, finance, repair and adapt it.

### 2. Co-development versus one-way transfer

The canonical evidence for co-development is structural:

- joint institution building with the African Union Commission and member states;
- partner-country nomination of ITEC participants;
- the SADC MoU's alignment with SADC's own RISDP and Digital Transformation Strategy;
- Agenda 2063 as an African-owned reference point.

⚠️ Co-development is not proven by the word “joint”. Test agenda-setting power, procurement,
staffing, intellectual property, data governance and evaluation authority.

### 3. Non-intrusive support

✅ Tharoor characterises India's support for democratic institutions as **“non-intrusive”**.
This supplies a textual basis for mutual-respect claims.

⚠️ It should not be inflated into:

- absence of political interest;
- uniform success across every partner;
- immunity from local criticism; or
- proof that every project lacks conditions.

The defensible claim is that India presents cooperation as non-prescriptive and partner-priority
led; country-level practice still requires dated evidence.

### 4. Digital sovereignty is institutional, not merely technological

```text
OPEN / AFFORDABLE TECHNOLOGY
            +
LOCAL LEGAL CONTROL
            +
DATA GOVERNANCE
            +
SKILLS + CYBERSECURITY
            +
INTEROPERABILITY + EXIT OPTIONS
            =
SOVEREIGN DIGITAL CAPABILITY
```

⚠️ Open-source software may reduce vendor dependence but cannot by itself guarantee privacy,
inclusion or institutional control. Conversely, local hosting does not guarantee accountability.
The correct unit of analysis is the whole governance system.

### 5. The implementation-and-maintenance test

Apply five questions to every institution or platform:

1. **Functionality:** is it operational?
2. **Use:** are intended users actually using it?
3. **Capability:** can local personnel run and adapt it?
4. **Finance:** is lifecycle funding available?
5. **Outcome:** has a development indicator improved?

⚠️ “Long-term institutional partnership” is an outcome claim. The evidence must extend beyond
launch, training-slot or scholarship-offer counts.

### 6. Summit continuity versus programme continuity

The post-2015 summit gap weakens regular apex-level political steering. Simultaneously, LoCs,
ITEC, digital agreements, bilateral mechanisms, defence dialogues and sectoral projects continued.

⚠️ The analytical conclusion is neither “IAFS is irrelevant” nor “the gap does not matter”.
Programmes can continue without a summit, but a delayed summit can reduce review, prioritisation,
political visibility and collective accountability.

### 7. Finance quality and project ownership

An advanced project audit should identify:

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Who selected the project? | demand-driven credibility |
| Grant, LoC or commercial finance? | repayment and fiscal implications |
| Who is the borrower and lender? | legal and debt ownership |
| Who procures/implements? | local value and accountability |
| What was disbursed? | finance-to-delivery gap |
| Who operates and maintains? | sustainability |
| What outcome was measured? | development effectiveness |

LoCs extended through Exim Bank under IDEAS are concessional loans, not grants. A project may be
partner-selected yet still face procurement, debt, exchange-rate, maintenance or local-capacity
constraints.

### 8. Input, output and outcome metrics

| Weak claim | Better formulation |
|---|---|
| “15,116 students benefited” | “15,116 e-VBAB scholarships were offered; completion/outcomes require separate data.” |
| “US$10 billion was spent” | “LoCs worth over US$10 billion were extended; disbursement is separate.” |
| “The SADC DPI was implemented” | “The 2024 MoU created a cooperation framework including DPI.” |
| “IAFS-IV delivered a new framework” | “A framework was an expected outcome before the summit was deferred.” |
| “AU entry solved representation” | “AU permanent G20 membership delivered one representation gain; other deficits remain.” |

### 9. African agency and regional differentiation

African agency operates at several levels:

- national governments choose projects and nominees;
- regional communities such as SADC formulate regional strategies;
- the AU articulates continental frameworks and representation claims;
- universities, hospitals, firms and civil society determine practical adoption;
- citizens experience inclusion, exclusion and service outcomes.

⚠️ A single “Africa policy” must therefore be combined with country and subregional strategies.

### 10. Advanced thesis

> ⚠️ India's strongest Africa proposition is a demand-driven combination of concessional finance,
> human-capital investment, joint institutions and adaptable digital tools, reinforced by support
> for African voice in global governance. Its comparative value, however, is established only
> where the partner owns the priority, the correct financing instrument is disclosed, the
> implementing chain is accountable, and operation, maintenance and outcomes are verified.

### 11. MAHASAGAR as a cross-regional development proposition

MAHASAGAR adds a wider political frame to familiar India-Africa instruments rather than creating
a new financing category. Its stated combination of **trade for development**, **capacity building
for sustainable growth**, **mutual security for a shared future**, **technology sharing**,
**concessional loans** and **grants** allows an answer to connect African partnership with India's
Global South diplomacy.

⚠️ The analytical gain is not to repeat Topic 04's maritime detail. It is to test whether an
Africa-facing initiative under this vision identifies the African priority, uses the correct
grant/loan/technical instrument, preserves partner ownership and reaches operation and outcome.
The phrase remains a declared vision until such instruments and results are separately evidenced.

### Finance and assistance distinctions

- **Grant:** non-repayable; IAFS-I US$500 million HRD/capacity-building announcement is
  book-period evidence.
- **LoC:** concessional, repayable project finance; GoI under IDEAS through Exim Bank.
- **Technical assistance:** training, experts, CEITs, systems and institutional capacity through
  MEA/DPA/ITEC and partner bodies.
- **Commercial trade/investment:** not development assistance merely because it occurs in Africa.
- **Evidence ladder:** announced → financed → contracted → delivered → operating → outcome.

### Answer skeleton

1. Define the claim and African actor.
2. Name the instrument and its finance/legal character.
3. Name Indian and partner implementation responsibility.
4. Cite a dated status.
5. Explain the development mechanism.
6. Separate input, output and outcome.

### Last-minute traps

- Grant ≠ LoC ≠ technical assistance ≠ investment.
- LoC extended ≠ amount disbursed.
- Project completed ≠ development outcome measured.
- MoU ≠ entry into force or implementation.
- Scholarship offered ≠ course completed.
- PAeNP ≠ current e-VBAB.
- Jointly planned institution ≠ currently functioning institution.
- IAFS-IV announced/scheduled ≠ held.
- MAHASAGAR vision ≠ treaty/fund/project delivery; MAHASAGAR does not replace SAGAR.
- AU/G20 membership ≠ settlement of all representation deficits.
- Africa ≠ one political, economic or security unit.

### Qualified conclusion

India's Africa partnership is strongest where solidarity becomes a partner-selected project,
transparent finance, shared implementation, local capability and maintained service. The exam
standard is therefore not the size of the announcement but the quality of African ownership and
the evidence of durable outcomes.
