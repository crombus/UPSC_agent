## BASIC MCQS / REMEDIATION

### Q1. Which formulation best captures India's stated development-partnership model in Africa?

A. African priorities guide mixed instruments whose delivery and local ownership must be tested.
B. One standard Indian template applies equally to every African state.
C. Commercial trade and non-repayable assistance are treated as the same instrument.
D. Summit declarations automatically bind all African governments without later consent or
domestic implementation.

**Answer: A**

MEA describes a demand-driven partnership aligned with African priorities. The other choices erase African agency or collapse distinct legal and financial instruments.

### Q2. Arrange these IAFS developments chronologically: 1. IAFS-III 2. IAFS-I 3. IAFS-IV theme/logo/site launch 4. IAFS-II

A. 1-2-4-3
B. 2-4-1-3
C. 2-1-4-3
D. 4-2-1-3

**Answer: B**

IAFS-I was held in 2008, IAFS-II in 2011 and IAFS-III in 2015; the IAFS-IV preparatory launch occurred in April 2026.

### Q3. Match the instrument with its financial character: 1. Grant 2. LoC 3. Technical assistance; a. Skills/institutional support b. Concessional repayable finance c. Non-repayable assistance

A. 1-a, 2-c, 3-b
B. 1-b, 2-a, 3-c
C. 1-c, 2-b, 3-a
D. 1-c, 2-a, 3-b

**Answer: C**

A grant is non-repayable, a Line of Credit is concessional but repayable, and technical assistance transfers skills, experts or institutional capability.

### Q4. Which of the following is NOT supported by the verified IAFS-IV record at the cut-off?

**Answer: D**

The joint release deferred the summit; no later held summit was verified by the cut-off. Preparatory material and expected outcomes cannot be reported as adopted decisions.

#### PYQ 1 · 2021 · General Studies Paper II · Q9 · DIRECT · 10 marks · 150 words

> “If the last few decades were of Asia's growth story, the next few are expected to be of
> Africa's.” In the light of this statement, examine India's influence in Africa in recent years.
>
> (Answer in 150 words)

**Verification note.** Exact English wording is reproduced from the locally held official
question-paper PDF `QP-CSM-21-GENSTUDIESPAPER-II-110122.pdf`. The paper provides no official
solution.

**Demand decoding.** “Examine” requires the basis of influence, evidence across sectors, and a
judgment qualified by delivery, competition and African agency.

**Original model answer (within 150 words).**

Africa's demographic, market and resource potential makes influence there important, but India's
claim rests more on partnership quality than scale.

India combines concessional finance with capacity building. The April 2026 MEA brief records
190-plus Lines of Credit worth over US$10 billion to 41 countries, alongside 220 completed
projects worth US$4.5 billion. ITEC, scholarships, vocational and IT centres build human capital,
while e-VBAB links education and telemedicine. Affordable medicines, agriculture cooperation,
AFINDEX and maritime engagement broaden the relationship. India's support helped secure
permanent G20 membership for the African Union in 2023, adding a representation dimension.

Influence is nevertheless uneven. LoC extension is not disbursement; scholarships offered are not
completed training; summit-level dialogue paused after IAFS-III; political instability,
maintenance gaps and competition from other partners matter.

Thus, India's influence is significant where African priorities become timely, locally owned and
sustained outcomes, not merely announcements.

**Why this earns marks.** It covers material, institutional, digital, security and representation
channels, then tests the proposition instead of merely praising the relationship.

#### PYQ 2 · 2025 · General Studies Paper II · Q9 · DIRECT · 10 marks · 150 words

> India-Africa digital partnership is achieving mutual respect, co-development and long-term
> institutional partnerships. Elaborate.
>
> (Answer in 150 words)

**Verification note.** Exact English wording is reproduced from the locally held official
question-paper PDF `UPSC Mains 2025 GS Paper 2.pdf`. No official UPSC model answer is claimed.

**Demand decoding.** “Elaborate” requires each phrase—mutual respect, co-development and
long-term institutions—to be independently explained and evidenced.

**Original model answer (within 150 words).**

India-Africa digital cooperation increasingly joins technology transfer with partner-defined
institution building.

**Mutual respect** appears in the demand-driven model: the 2024 India-SADC MoU aligns digital
cooperation with SADC's own RISDP and Digital Transformation Strategy. **Co-development** is
visible in joint institutions and platforms linking Indian universities, hospitals and technical
agencies with African counterparts. The Pan-African e-Network, concluded in 2017, was succeeded
by e-VBAB after the 2018 MEA-TCIL agreement; MEA's April 2026 brief records 15,116 scholarships
offered. The same brief records India Stack MoUs with seven African countries, Kenya's DigiLocker
implementation agreement and MOSIP partnerships in nineteen countries.

Yet long-term partnership cannot be inferred from signing or offer counts. Local legal control,
data governance, inclusion, cybersecurity, staffing, maintenance and measured use remain the
tests.

Therefore, the partnership is genuinely co-developmental when African institutions can govern,
adapt and sustain the digital capability.

**Why this earns marks.** It unpacks all three terms, uses a dated succession and current
instruments, and qualifies “achieving” through sovereignty and maintenance.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question.** Distinguish grants, concessional Lines of Credit and technical assistance in India's
development partnership with Africa. Why does this distinction matter?
**Answer in 150 words.**

**Demand.** Define all three, connect each to an implementation route and explain the analytical
value of the distinction.

**Original model answer (within 150 words).**

India uses complementary but legally and financially distinct instruments in Africa.

A **grant** is non-repayable public assistance; the US$500 million HRD and capacity-building
commitment announced at IAFS-I is the canonical example, though announcement is not disbursement.
A **Line of Credit** is concessional, repayable project finance extended by the Government of
India under IDEAS through Exim Bank; MEA's April 2026 brief records over 190 LoCs worth more than
US$10 billion to 41 African countries. **Technical assistance** transfers skills and institutional
capacity through ITEC training, experts, centres of excellence and digital systems.

The distinction matters because each creates different obligations and evidence. A LoC affects
the borrower's debt and procurement; a grant does not require repayment; training must be judged
by completion and institutional use. Combining them exaggerates aid and hides responsibility.

Therefore, credible assessment must name the instrument, implementing agency, partner owner,
delivery stage and outcome.

**Why this earns marks.** It defines, exemplifies, analyses implications and ends with an
operational test.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

**Question.** Trace the evolution of India's digital partnership with Africa from the
Pan-African e-Network to contemporary DPI cooperation.
**Answer in 150 words.**

**Demand.** Establish chronology, name actors and avoid turning agreements into outcomes.

**Original model answer (within 150 words).**

India's digital partnership has moved from networked service delivery to broader public-digital
cooperation.

The Pan-African e-Network linked African partners with Indian education and health institutions;
MEA records it as concluded in 2017. The MEA-TCIL implementation agreement of 10 September 2018
created its successor, e-VBAB, launched in 2019 for online education and telemedicine. MEA's
April 2026 brief records 15,116 e-VBAB scholarships offered.

The partnership now includes DPI. The India-SADC MoU of 2 July 2024 covers ICT, connectivity,
access and DPI while aligning with SADC's own strategy. MEA also records India Stack MoUs with
seven African countries, Kenya's DigiLocker Implementation Framework Agreement and MOSIP
partnerships in nineteen countries.

This evolution supports co-development, but status matters: a MoU, partnership or scholarship
offer is not proof of adoption or outcome. Data governance, inclusion, local skills,
cybersecurity and maintenance determine durable value.

**Why this earns marks.** It gives a clean dated succession and a qualified institutional
conclusion.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

**Question.** “Demand-driven” and “co-development” are meaningful only when African agency is
visible throughout the project cycle. Examine India's Africa partnership in this light.
**Answer in 250 words.**

**Demand.** Test the proposition across design, finance, implementation and outcome.

**Original model answer (within 250 words).**

India presents its Africa partnership as mutual, non-prescriptive and aligned with African
priorities. The claim is credible only if agency extends beyond selecting a project title.

At the **agenda stage**, MEA links engagement to Agenda 2063, while the 2024 India-SADC MoU
expressly takes account of SADC's RISDP 2020-2030 and Digital Transformation Strategy. This
locates priorities in African frameworks. At the **design stage**, the canonical nineteen
institutions were to be established jointly with the African Union Commission and member states,
supporting co-development rather than a wholly imported facility.

At the **finance stage**, agency requires transparent choice among grants, concessional LoCs and
commercial investment. LoCs under IDEAS are repayable through Exim Bank; calling them grants
would conceal debt and procurement implications. At the **implementation stage**, partner
ministries, universities, hospitals and regional bodies must share governance with Indian
agencies such as MEA, TCIL and training institutions.

Digital cooperation sharpens the test. Kenya's DigiLocker agreement, MOSIP partnerships and the
SADC DPI framework can support adaptable capacity, but legal authority, data control,
cybersecurity, inclusion and exit options must remain locally governed. At the **outcome stage**,
scholarships offered, centres opened and LoCs extended are insufficient without evidence of use,
maintenance and public benefit.

Thus, India's demand-driven advantage is real as a design principle, but co-development becomes
substantive only when African actors control priorities, implementation choices and evaluation.

**Why this earns marks.** It follows the whole project cycle and subjects the positive claim to a
clear agency test.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

**Question.** Assess the significance of the India-Africa Forum Summit process despite the long
gap after IAFS-III.
**Answer in 250 words.**

**Demand.** Explain the summit's function, assess the gap, and distinguish political architecture
from continuing programmes.

**Original model answer (within 250 words).**

The India-Africa Forum Summit gives dispersed bilateral and sectoral cooperation an apex
political framework. IAFS-I was held in New Delhi in 2008, IAFS-II in Addis Ababa in 2011 and
IAFS-III in New Delhi in 2015.

Its significance is threefold. First, it aggregates priorities across India, African states and
the African Union, allowing development, capacity building, trade, health, education and security
to be reviewed together. Second, summit-level commitments provide political authority for
instruments such as grants, training positions and joint institutions. Third, the process
symbolises South-South partnership and can align cooperation with Agenda 2063.

The gap after 2015 is nevertheless consequential. It weakens regular collective review,
visibility and accountability and can allow old announcements to circulate without updated
delivery evidence. IAFS-IV's theme, logo and website were launched on 23 April 2026, and an AU
advisory scheduled the summit for 28-31 May. However, the India-AU joint release of 21 May agreed
to convene it later because of the evolving health situation and participation concerns. At the
26 September cut-off, no held summit or new date was officially verified.

The gap does not mean cooperation stopped: LoCs, grants, ITEC, e-VBAB, SADC engagement and defence
dialogues continued through other mechanisms.

IAFS therefore remains strategically important, but its value depends on regular review,
publicly tracked commitments and continuity between summit declarations and programme delivery.

**Why this earns marks.** It neither dismisses the summit nor hides the continuity gap and uses
the exact 2026 status.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 MARKS

**Question.** Evaluate India's influence in Africa across development, digital, health, security
and global-governance domains. What constrains the conversion of goodwill into durable influence?
**Answer in 300 words.**

**Demand.** Build a multi-domain evaluation, explain causal mechanisms and reach a graded verdict.

**Original model answer (within 300 words).**

India's influence in Africa rests on historical solidarity, development instruments and support
for African voice, but it is uneven and delivery-dependent.

**Development finance:** MEA's April 2026 brief records over 190 Lines of Credit worth more than
US$10 billion to 41 countries, 220 completed projects worth US$4.5 billion and grants exceeding
US$700 million since 2015. These can create infrastructure and goodwill, but LoC extension is not
disbursement and completion is not automatically a measured social outcome.

**Human capital and digital cooperation:** ITEC, scholarships, vocational and IT centres, student
mobility and e-VBAB create professional networks. The digital track now includes the 2024 SADC
MoU, India Stack MoUs with seven countries, Kenya's DigiLocker agreement and MOSIP partnerships
in nineteen countries. Affordable, adaptable systems strengthen influence only when African
institutions control law, data, cybersecurity and maintenance.

**Health and agriculture:** medicines, vaccines, telemedicine, training, mechanisation and
processing connect diplomacy to development. Regulation, local manufacturing and supply
continuity qualify aggregate claims.

**Security and maritime cooperation:** the Lucknow and Gandhinagar declarations, AFINDEX,
peacekeeping training, anti-piracy and blue-economy cooperation widen the relationship without
creating an alliance. MAHASAGAR, launched as a declared vision at Port Louis on 12 March 2025,
places trade for
development, capacity building, mutual security, technology sharing, concessional loans and grants
inside a wider Global South vision; it is not a treaty, dedicated fund or delivery mechanism and does not replace SAGAR.

**Global governance:** India's support for AU permanent G20 membership produced a concrete result
in 2023; UNSC reform remains unfinished advocacy.

Constraints include the post-2015 IAFS gap, project delay, maintenance and skills deficits,
repayment/procurement concerns, political instability, commercial gaps and partner competition;
African actors diversify.

India's influence is therefore substantial but not automatic: durable influence follows
partner-selected projects, transparent finance, reliable delivery and sustained local
capability.

**Why this earns marks.** It evaluates five domains, distinguishes status levels and supplies a
clear causal verdict.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

**Question.** Compare India's development-partnership proposition in Africa with alternative
external-partner models. Suggest a framework that protects African ownership while improving
delivery.
**Answer in 300 words.**

**Demand.** Compare without caricature, then propose an implementable lifecycle framework.

**Original model answer (within 300 words).**

India differentiates its Africa partnership through demand-driven projects, capacity building,
affordable technology and non-intrusive political language. Tharoor's evidence on training,
student mobility and jointly planned institutions supports a human-capital emphasis, while ITEC,
e-VBAB and current DPI cooperation add institutional depth. Alternative partners, including
China through FOCAC, may offer larger infrastructure finance, faster construction or different
commercial packages.

The comparison should not become propaganda. India's LoCs remain repayable; Indian procurement
requirements, delays and maintenance gaps can reduce local value. Likewise, Chinese or Western
engagement cannot uniformly be labelled extractive or conditional: terms, sectors and outcomes
vary, and African states deliberately diversify partners.

A partner-ownership framework should contain six stages:

1. **Priority:** the state, AU or regional community publishes the development need and its link
   to Agenda 2063 or a national plan.
2. **Instrument:** disclose whether support is a grant, concessional LoC, technical assistance or
   commercial investment, including repayment and procurement terms.
3. **Joint design:** specify local content, skills transfer, data governance, environmental and
   inclusion safeguards.
4. **Accountable delivery:** identify the Indian implementing agency, African owner, contractor,
   milestones, disbursement and grievance route.
5. **Lifecycle capability:** fund training, cybersecurity, repair, software adaptation and
   recurrent operation rather than only construction or launch.
6. **Outcome review:** jointly publish use, reliability and development indicators, permitting
   redesign or exit.

India should also restore regular summit-level review while using bilateral and regional
mechanisms between summits.

Its comparative advantage will not come from claiming moral superiority or matching another
partner's announcement scale. It will come from transparent terms, African control and dependable
full-lifecycle delivery.

**Why this earns marks.** It recognises comparative strengths and weaknesses, preserves African
choice and supplies a concrete reform architecture.

### Mains self-audit

Before finalising any answer, check:

- **Directive:** did you elaborate, examine, discuss or evaluate as asked?
- **Actor:** did you identify state, AU, SADC/another REC or local institution?
- **Instrument:** did you distinguish grant, LoC, technical assistance, MoU and trade?
- **Agency:** did you name both Indian and African implementation responsibility?
- **Status:** did you separate announcement, finance, delivery, operation and outcome?
- **MAHASAGAR:** did you identify its full form, Port Louis launch and development instruments
  without calling it a treaty, fund, delivered project or replacement for SAGAR?
- **Evidence:** did you use two to five dated anchors rather than a catalogue?
- **Qualification:** did you test maintenance, debt, procurement, data, inclusion and political
  risk?
- **Conclusion:** did you make a graded judgment rather than predict success?
