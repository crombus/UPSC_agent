### PYQ 2021 GS-II Q9 — Examine India's influence in Africa (150 words)

**Independent exam-length model answer (~150 words):** Africa's expected growth creates openings for India, but influence must be measured by sustained African uptake, not Indian announcements. The India–Africa Forum Summits provide political dialogue; the first summit's announced USD 500 million HRD grant, technical-cooperation training positions and jointly envisaged institutes target local skills. The Pan African e-Network's successor e-VBAB supports tele-education and telemedicine. MEA's April 2026 brief reports India–Africa trade of USD 81.99 billion in FY2024–25 and more than 190 Lines of Credit extended to 41 African countries; these indicate commercial exchange and finance availability, not completed projects. Maritime engagement and the African Union's permanent G20 seat at New Delhi broaden India's diplomatic reach. Yet African governments choose among multiple external partners, while debt, security risks, project delays and maintenance constrain any offer's influence. India's comparative advantage in accessible skills and technology matters where African-defined priorities become operating institutions, rather than wherever credit is announced or a summit held.

### PYQ 2025 GS-II Q9 — Elaborate the digital partnership's three claims (150 words)

**Independent exam-length model answer (~150 words):** India–Africa digital cooperation carries three separate claims. **Mutual respect** entails African choice of priorities; the India–SADC MoU of July 2024 aligns its ICT, connectivity and DPI pillar with SADC's own development and digital strategies. **Co-development** means shared institutional agency: Tharoor describes nineteen institutes being established jointly with the AU Commission and member states, not simply designed in India and exported. **Long-term partnership** requires usable services. The Pan African e-Network concluded in 2017; e-VBAB, launched in 2019, succeeded it with tele-education and telemedicine. MEA's April 2026 brief lists 22 participating African countries and 15,116 scholarships *offered*, not courses completed. A SADC memorandum identifies potential cooperation, not finished continent-wide DPI. Sustained local governance, data control, trained staff, financing and independently measured service use must verify the long-term claim. Thus design can express mutual respect, while observed benefits, rather than signatures or offer totals, establish whether co-development endures.

### Original Mains 1 — 10 marks, ~150 words

**Question:** Distinguish the founding HRD grant, technical-cooperation training and Lines of Credit in India's partnership with Africa. Assess their different delivery risks.

**Model answer (~150 words):** India's development instruments have different obligations and metrics. Tharoor records that at the first India–Africa Forum Summit India announced a USD 500 million grant for human-resource development and capacity-building projects. Its stated purpose is clear, but the announcement alone does not show expenditure or completed projects. He also records 1,600 Africa-specific technical-cooperation positions: places *offered* are not a count of graduates or local jobs. The Economic Survey 2025–26 reports ITEC training of more than two lakh people globally across over 160 countries; attributing that global total to Africa would mislead. MEA's April 2026 brief lists more than 190 Lines of Credit worth over USD 10 billion extended to 41 African countries. Unlike grants, these credit facilities raise repayment and implementation issues; extension does not establish disbursement. The mix can finance assets and build skills, but partner-selected needs, sustainable borrowing, local staffing and measured use determine whether these distinct commitments produce development.

### Original Mains 2 — 10 marks, ~150 words

**Question:** Explain why the Pan African e-Network cannot itself establish current universal African adoption of Indian DPI.

**Model answer (~150 words):** The Pan African e-Network sought to narrow the digital divide through tele-education and institutional connectivity, but MEA records that it concluded in 2017. Its successor, e-VidyaBharati/e-ArogyaBharati (e-VBAB), followed an MEA–TCIL implementation agreement in September 2018 and launched in 2019, with education and remote-health services. MEA's April 2026 brief cites 22 participating African countries and 15,116 scholarships *offered*, not a universal DPI rollout or completed-graduate total. The July 2024 India–SADC MoU includes ICT connectivity, access and DPI among wider economic-cooperation areas, aligned with SADC's own plans; an MoU is not proof of nationally deployed systems. The correct assessment checks which states requested which services, whether local institutions control data and procurement, and whether trained operators maintain accessible, regularly used facilities across the different participating states. The legal character of each agreement and the accessibility of each service must be distinguished. Calling a concluded precursor a currently deployed continent-wide DPI platform confuses chronology, project scope and measured outcome.

### Original Mains 3 — 15 marks, ~220–250 words

**Question:** Assess whether demand-driven co-development adequately describes India's institutional partnership with Africa, distinguishing design from demonstrated performance.

**Model answer (~230 words):** 'Demand-driven' describes who sets priorities; 'co-development' concerns who builds and governs the response. Both are defensible design claims but cannot be certified by a communiqué. At the first India–Africa Forum Summit India announced a USD 500 million human-resource-development grant. Tharoor also records 1,600 Africa-specific training positions and nineteen institutions *in the process of being established jointly* with the AU Commission and member states, including vocational and human-settlement institutes. Joint design can embed African priorities, yet the reported totals do not establish that all centres are staffed and operating.

Tharoor characterises India's support for democratic institutions as non-intrusive. This supports mutual respect rather than a uniform imposed model, but cannot establish equally successful implementation everywhere. The India–SADC MoU of July 2024 situates ICT and DPI cooperation alongside SADC's RISDP 2020–2030 and Digital Transformation Strategy: a concrete reference to an African partner's plan, not proof that systems have been deployed. MEA's April 2026 tally of 22 e-VBAB participating countries shows reach; the scholarship-offer total does not show learning outcomes.

The stronger performance test examines African decision rights, resources actually spent, curriculum relevance, local operators, accessible services and recurrent budgets. Sahel risks and differing regional priorities make a continent-wide conclusion especially unsound. Co-development is most credible where partners choose, run and evaluate durable facilities themselves; where only agreements and opportunities are documented, the model remains a promise requiring verification.

### Original Mains 4 — 15 marks, ~220–250 words

**Question:** Evaluate India's digital and health cooperation with Africa through access, continuity and data sovereignty.

**Model answer (~235 words):** Connectivity has developmental value when learners and patients can use a service under rules accepted by African partners. The Pan African e-Network linked Indian and African institutions, but MEA records that the original project concluded in 2017. Its e-VBAB successor was agreed for implementation with TCIL in September 2018 and launched in 2019. Its e-VidyaBharati and e-ArogyaBharati limbs support education and medicine. MEA's April 2026 brief reports 22 participating countries and 15,116 scholarships offered: neither figure measures completion, clinical quality or inclusion of remote communities.

The 2024 India–SADC economic-cooperation MoU lists ICT development, connectivity, access and DPI in its new-technologies pillar, aligned with SADC's own RISDP and digital strategy. It is a plausible route to partnership, not an audit of deployed health systems. Clinics require staff, electricity, accessible interfaces, language support and referral pathways. Digital sovereignty also requires meaningful partner control of health and education data, technical standards and procurement. The MoU's alignment with regional strategy cannot settle these operating questions.

A serious appraisal compares actual enrolment and course completion with scholarship offers; records repeat teleconsultations and clinical follow-up; and audits downtime, privacy safeguards and local technical staffing. Affordable Indian medicines may complement remote care if procurement and regulation function. India can contribute tools and training, but African institutions must retain authority over deployment and evaluation. The test of partnership is equitable, sustained use, not the number of logos or offered places.

### Original Mains 5 — 20 marks, ~270–310 words

**Question:** Analyse India's influence in Africa across capacity building, commerce, security and multilateral representation, comparing promise with verifiable outcomes.

**Model answer (~290 words):** India has several routes to African influence, but their effects vary across partners and cannot be read directly from aggregate announcements. The IAFS framework held summits in New Delhi in 2008, Addis Ababa in 2011 and New Delhi in 2015. Tharoor describes the first summit's USD 500 million HRD grant announcement, 1,600 Africa-specific training positions and nineteen institutes under joint establishment with the AU Commission and member states. Trade measures exchange; an extended facility does not prove project disbursement, fiscal sustainability or completion. The Lucknow 2020 and Gandhinagar 2022 defence dialogues and AFINDEX field exercises signal security training rather than alliance obligations. Sahel instability and other available partners, including China/FOCAC, demand country-specific analysis without denying African agency.

India's comparative advantage may lie in responsive skills, affordable technology and jointly governed institutes. Influence becomes durable only when a partner requests, maintains and measures a service. Ceremony, trade totals and loan face values remain weaker proxies for locally experienced benefit.

### Original Mains 6 — 20 marks, ~270–310 words

**Question:** Examine opportunities and limits of aligning India–Africa finance, digital and climate cooperation with African-owned development priorities.

**Model answer (~285 words):** Africa's Agenda 2063 is the AU's own vision of an integrated, prosperous and peaceful continent, originating in the 2013 jubilee declaration and organised through aspirations, flagship programmes and ten-year implementation plans. It is an alignment framework, not a list of India-financed projects. India should begin with national and regional requests rather than assume a uniform continental demand. The India–SADC MoU signed in July 2024 illustrates a design principle: its ICT, access, connectivity and DPI pillar references SADC's RISDP 2020–2030 and Digital Transformation Strategy. Yet a regional memorandum does not bind every AU state or show any national DPI adoption.

Financing can support partner-selected assets if debt and delivery are transparent. MEA's April 2026 brief lists over 190 Lines of Credit worth over USD 10 billion extended to 41 countries: facilities, not fully disbursed project benefits. The first IAFS USD 500 million HRD grant announcement and nineteen institutions in joint establishment with the AU Commission and member states offer a complementary capacity route. Local decision rights, actual spending, skills and maintenance determine whether the announced framework yields agency. The e-VBAB education/health successor to the concluded Pan African e-Network illustrates both reach and limits: participating countries and scholarships offered are not course completions or clinical outcomes.

Climate cooperation provides another bridge. The India-headquartered ISA offers a treaty-based solar platform and CDRI a resilient-infrastructure knowledge network. Neither membership alone establishes a financed African installation. Sahel security risks, diverse regulatory capacity and African choice among external partners complicate a single template. A sound partnership combines affordable financing with local expertise, data safeguards and indicators selected by partners. India should claim success when African institutions demonstrate sustained benefits against their own plans, not upon a credit announcement or signed agreement.
