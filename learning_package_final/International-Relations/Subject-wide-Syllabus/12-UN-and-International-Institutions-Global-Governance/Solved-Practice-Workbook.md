### Original Mains 6 — 20 marks

**Directive / limit:** 20 marks · 300 words.

**Question:** International law often advances through treaties and courts even when political organs are deadlocked. Analyse, while distinguishing adoption, jurisdiction and implementation. Answer in not more than 300 words.

**Model answer (234 words):**

Political deadlock does not end international law-making; it redirects it. Security Council paralysis can coexist with treaty negotiation, judicial clarification and administrative adaptation. But each route has a different legal threshold.

A treaty text first must be adopted, then opened for signature, accepted through ratification or accession, brought into force after its threshold, and implemented. The BBNJ Agreement illustrates the chain: it entered into force on 17 January 2026, while India's earlier signature did not make India a party without deposited ratification. The WHO Pandemic Agreement was adopted in 2025, but on 18 September 2026 its PABS annex remained under negotiation; WHO said completion would pave the way for signature and ratification. Adoption therefore was not operation.

Courts also require jurisdictional gateways. ICJ contentious judgments bind consenting State parties to the case; advisory opinions are generally non-binding but can clarify law and shape diplomacy. The ICC prosecutes individuals under the Rome Statute and can receive a Security Council referral even where ordinary territorial or nationality links are absent. India is not a Rome Statute party.

These routes can generate norms and focal points despite geopolitical rivalry, but implementation still depends on domestic law, finance, cooperation and institutional legitimacy. They also cannot substitute fully for a Council capable of maintaining peace. The proper conclusion is plural: legal progress can bypass one blocked organ, but authority, consent, entry into force and compliance must be proved separately.
