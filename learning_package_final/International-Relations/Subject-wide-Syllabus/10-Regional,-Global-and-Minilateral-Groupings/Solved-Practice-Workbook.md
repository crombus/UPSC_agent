#### 2020 GS-II Q19

**Demand:** The 'Quadrilateral Security Dialogue (QUAD)' is transforming itself into a trade bloc from a military alliance, in present times. Discuss. (15 marks; 250 words)

**Provenance:** Audited 2018–23 ledger and Basic owner; locally held 2020 official paper unavailable. Core route supersedes older Advanced ownership. No official Mains key claimed.

**Independent model answer:** The proposition contains two false starting points: Quad is neither a collective-defence military alliance nor an established trade bloc. India, Australia, Japan and the United States cooperate as a non-treaty minilateral. Some partners have bilateral security treaties, but those obligations do not extend to India through Quad. Nor do Quad meetings create a common external tariff or a multilateral free-trade agreement.

Its agenda has nonetheless widened. Maritime domain awareness, resilient supply chains, technology and critical-minerals cooperation can protect economic and strategic interests simultaneously. The September 2024 Wilmington leaders' summit demonstrates political coordination; subsequent ministerial announcements show directions for cooperation, not proof of completed projects. For India, such practical coordination supports Indo-Pacific access and diversification without surrendering strategic autonomy. The coalition can complement ASEAN-centred dialogue and India's relations with continental powers, rather than displace either.

Its limits are institutional and political. Partners' different threat perceptions and treaty relationships constrain common action. Each announced framework needs country-level budgets, legal approvals and implementation evidence before it can be called a delivered outcome. Quad is best understood as an evolving functional partnership linking security-related and economic resilience concerns, not as a transformation from one formal bloc into another. The misleading premise should be corrected before assessing real activities.

#### 2021 GS-II Q19

**Demand:** Critically examine the aims and objectives of the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation. What importance does it hold for India? (15 marks; 250 words)

**Provenance:** Audited 2018–23 ledger and locally held official paper. Core route supersedes older Advanced ownership. No official Mains key claimed.

**Independent model answer:** The SCO provides a Eurasian platform for security dialogue, counterterrorism cooperation and economic/connectivity discussion. India became a full member in 2017. Its importance lies in access to continental interlocutors, including Central Asian states, Russia and China, through a forum in which India otherwise has limited geographic connectivity.

India can raise terrorism and instability, seek energy and transport cooperation and maintain communication even when bilateral relations are strained. The summit and declaration process gives an additional diplomatic venue, not a substitute for Indian bilateral policy. Participation alongside Pakistan can keep regional security on the table; it does not mean that India and Pakistan agree about threats or cross-border terrorism.

Critical scrutiny is essential. India's geographic access to Central Asia is constrained by transit politics; proposed connectivity must be tested against sovereignty and practical routes. China and Russia's larger roles, Pakistan's presence and diverging sanctions or security positions impede consensus. A joint declaration cannot by itself deliver a railway, energy contract or intelligence result. Engagement should thus be judged by verifiable contacts and feasible projects, not by membership alone.

For India, the SCO is useful precisely as one component of diversified diplomacy: it keeps a continental channel open while India pursues maritime cooperation through Quad and other platforms. The balance is access without automatic alignment, and an agenda of cooperation without pretending rivalries have disappeared.

#### 2022 GS-II Q10

**Demand:** Do you think that BIMSTEC is a parallel organisation like SAARC? What are the similarities and dissimilarities between the two? (10 marks; 150 words)

**Provenance:** Audited 2018–23 ledger and locally held official paper. Core route supersedes older Advanced ownership. No official Mains key claimed.

**Independent model answer:** Both SAARC and BIMSTEC organise geographically proximate states for regional cooperation; neither replaces India's bilateral diplomacy. Yet they are not duplicate organisations. SAARC covers South Asia including Pakistan, while BIMSTEC links Bay of Bengal countries across South and Southeast Asia and does not include Pakistan. SAARC's last summit was held in 2014, whereas BIMSTEC's Charter entered into force in May 2024 and its sixth summit adopted Bangkok Vision 2030 in April 2025. These markers suggest different recent institutional momentum, not that SAARC was dissolved. BIMSTEC can advance connectivity when India–Pakistan tensions impede SAARC; its own maritime transport agreement entered into force in 2026 initially for only four members, showing implementation limits. Thus BIMSTEC is parallel in serving some overlapping regional goals, but distinct in membership, geographic reach and instruments. India should use both where their memberships can deliver genuine, verified cooperation.

#### 2022 GS-II Q19

**Demand:** How will I2U2 (India, Israel, UAE and USA) grouping transform India's position in global politics? (15 marks; 250 words)

**Provenance:** Audited 2018–23 ledger and locally held official paper. Core route supersedes older Advanced ownership. No official Mains key claimed.

**Independent model answer:** I2U2 joins India, Israel, the United Arab Emirates and the United States around practical cooperation rather than a geographically contiguous region or mutual-defence treaty. Its possible contribution to India's position lies in connecting Indian skills and markets to West Asian capital, technology and US partnerships on food, water, energy and other agreed fields.

For India, an agricultural technology or clean-energy partnership could combine investment with Indian demand and capacity. If projects attract finance, obtain regulatory approval and deliver outputs, they may diversify supply chains and strengthen links between South Asia and West Asia. Such cooperation complements rather than supersedes India's separate relationships with Iran, the Gulf countries and other powers. Participation can demonstrate issue-based multi-alignment: India can work with US partners on defined projects without joining a military bloc.

Transformation, however, must not be assumed from the grouping's name or an announcement. The four states have different security priorities and domestic regulatory constraints. A proposal for a food corridor is not a functioning corridor; a memorandum is not a measured change in employment or food availability. Success requires viable financing, land and water safeguards, technology access, transparent implementation and benefits for host communities.

I2U2 therefore offers India a potentially important bridge between regions and sectors, not a predetermined new geopolitical rank. Assess it by completed cooperative ventures, maintained room for independent policy and the ability to manage regional sensitivities, rather than claiming that a four-state summit alone has transformed India's global influence.

#### 2023 GS-II Q9

**Demand:** A virus of conflict is affecting the functioning of the SCO. Point out the role of India in mitigating the problems. (10 marks; 150 words)

**Provenance:** Audited 2018–23 ledger and locally held official paper; 2023 limit from instruction block. Core route supersedes older Advanced ownership. No official Mains key claimed.

**Independent model answer:** The SCO brings India, China, Pakistan, Russia and Central Asian partners into a common Eurasian forum, but rival security perceptions and bilateral tensions limit consensus. India cannot resolve these disputes by merely sharing membership. It can nevertheless insist on clearly framed counterterrorism cooperation, raise concerns about cross-border violence and advocate connectivity consistent with sovereignty. Continued engagement with Central Asian states and Russia preserves channels even when India–China or India–Pakistan relations are strained. India should test proposed projects for feasible routes and financing, and distinguish a summit declaration from tangible implementation. It may support practical, narrowly scoped cooperation on public health or energy where interests converge, while resisting language that obscures unresolved disagreements. The realistic role is to keep dialogue open, insist on transparent rules and pursue deliverable interests without pretending that the SCO itself is a mechanism for compulsory settlement of bilateral conflicts.

#### 2023 GS-II Q19

**Demand:** The expansion and strengthening of NATO and a stronger US–Europe strategic partnership works well for India. What is your opinion? Give reasons. (15 marks; 250 words)

**Provenance:** Audited 2018–23 ledger and locally held official paper; 2023 limit from instruction block. Core route supersedes older Advanced ownership. No official Mains key claimed.

**Independent model answer:** A stronger NATO cannot be classed as uniformly beneficial or harmful to India. NATO is a treaty alliance with collective defence under Article 5; India is neither a member nor covered by its guarantee. Finland's accession in 2023 and Sweden's in 2024 consolidated Euro-Atlantic coordination, but their security commitments run among allies, not toward New Delhi.

For India, greater US–European coordination may improve opportunities for bilateral technology, maritime and industrial partnerships. It can also make Western positions on export controls and sanctions more coordinated, affecting Indian firms and defence relationships. NATO consolidation amid Russia–West confrontation may complicate India's access to Russian defence spares, payment channels and energy while increasing diplomatic pressure to choose sides. Attention and industrial capacity directed toward Europe's security can reduce resources available for other theatres. None of these effects is automatic: their scale depends on specific policies, Indian diversification and partner decisions.

India should deepen engagement with individual European states and the EU, diversify critical defence supplies and protect lawful energy and technology interests. It can support dialogue on European security without adopting an Atlantic alliance template for the Indo-Pacific. Quad, in which India participates, lacks NATO's treaty-defence obligation and should not be mislabeled a parallel alliance.

The judgment is therefore conditional. A capable Europe can be a useful economic and strategic partner, but a more polarised confrontation carries material risks. India's preferred course is issue-based cooperation with Europe alongside independent relations with other powers, measured by concrete trade, technology and security outcomes rather than an abstract endorsement of NATO expansion.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Distinguish regionalism from minilateralism as organising principles for India's diplomatic engagement, with reference to dated institutional evidence. Answer in about 150 words.

**Independent model solution:** Regionalism generally organises geographically linked states for broad cooperation; minilateralism selects a small set of partners for focused tasks even when they are not neighbours. BIMSTEC is a Bay of Bengal regional body with a Charter that entered into force in May 2024. Its 2025 Bangkok summit adopted Bangkok Vision 2030, but even its maritime transport agreement entered into force initially for only four members in 2026: institutional deepening and universal delivery are different claims. Quad connects India, Australia, Japan and the United States on functional Indo-Pacific questions without a collective-defence treaty or common tariff. Its statements can coordinate policy; each project still needs implementation. India uses both forms for different interests: BIMSTEC for neighbourhood connectivity and Quad for selective maritime and technological coordination. Neither format alone guarantees results, and preserving ASEAN's claimed centrality is compatible with issue-based cooperation. Legal commitments and actual delivery must always be assessed separately.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Comment on the proposition that the entry into force of the Bay of Bengal grouping's Charter marks a genuine deepening of South Asian regionalism. Answer in about 150 words.

**Independent model solution:** The BIMSTEC Charter, adopted in 2022 and in force from 20 May 2024, formalises a Bay of Bengal organisation whose origins lie in the earlier BIMST-EC arrangement. This is a real legal and institutional change rather than another summit slogan. The sixth summit at Bangkok in April 2025 adopted Bangkok Vision 2030 and Rules of Procedure, showing sustained agenda-setting. Unlike SAARC, whose last summit was in 2014, BIMSTEC links South and Southeast Asian states without Pakistan; it creates opportunities for India to pursue regional connectivity despite India–Pakistan frictions. Yet a Charter is not a completed free-trade area. The maritime transport agreement, signed in April 2025, entered into force in May 2026 initially for Bhutan, India, Myanmar and Thailand, not all seven members. Effective deepening requires additional participation, financed routes and actual use. The Charter strengthens the framework; delivery remains a separate test. For India, the political and implementation tests are both indispensable.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Examine why India's participation in overlapping regional, global and minilateral groupings should be read as multi-alignment rather than indecision. Answer in about 250 words.

**Independent model solution:** India's memberships look inconsistent only if all institutions are treated as interchangeable blocs. At the regional level, SAARC retains a South Asian mandate despite stagnation, while BIMSTEC's Charter in force since 2024 creates a Bay of Bengal pathway. IORA addresses Indian Ocean concerns and IBSA connects Southern democracies across continents. SCO opens a Eurasian security channel even with China and Pakistan inside it. G20 and BRICS provide economic-governance discussions; Quad and I2U2 organise narrower, issue-based cooperation. These settings address non-identical partners and problems.

Diversification protects Indian options. BIMSTEC's Bangkok Vision 2030 does not resolve SAARC's India–Pakistan obstacle; it offers another route for regional engagement. Quad maritime and technology initiatives do not give India NATO-style collective defence, nor does SCO membership make India accept another member's connectivity preferences. BRICS partner status cannot be confused with full membership; the 2025 Rio declaration demonstrates a policy statement, not delivered development finance. India can choose the appropriate forum without treating one relationship as exclusive.

The strategy is not costless. Competing summits consume diplomatic capacity; BRICS and SCO face heterogeneous interests; even BIMSTEC's 2026 maritime agreement initially operated for four of seven states. To show that overlap is purposeful rather than indecision, India must match a specific interest to each platform, record who is legally bound and measure projects after announcement. Strategic autonomy is preserved by choices and credible implementation, not by the number of memberships on a list.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Examine the claim that participation tiers, rather than headline membership, are where the real distinction between these groupings lies. Answer in about 250 words.

**Independent model solution:** A grouping's headline membership often obscures the decision rights that matter. BRICS's partner-country category, created through the 2024 Kazan process, broadens participation but does not make a partner a full BRICS member. New Development Bank membership is another independent legal fact; it cannot be inferred from either a BRICS partnership or even the political grouping's entire roster.

ASEAN admits Southeast Asian members and engages India as a dialogue partner; the latter can participate in cooperation without becoming an ASEAN member. Its trade agreement with India likewise does not enrol India in RCEP. SCO distinguishes full members, observers and dialogue partners; India's full membership since 2017 gives it direct participation but does not compel Pakistan or China to accept Indian preferences. IORA distinguishes members from dialogue partners. The G20 permits guest countries, which must not be counted as permanent members; the African Union's permanent admission in 2023 is a different status change.

Tiers affect consultation, rule-making and expectations. They also vary across institutions: an invitation to a summit is not a treaty accession, and holding a development-bank share is not identical to voting in a political forum. India should verify each organisation's charter and dated roster, then ask whether an initiative was merely proposed, adopted, funded or delivered.

Membership matters where formal decision-making is restricted to full members. The conclusion is not that headlines are useless, but that rights, accession procedures and implementation determine what India can achieve through each relationship.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Assess the proposition that institutional form, and not diplomatic energy, decides what a grouping can deliver for India. Answer in about 300 words.

**Independent model solution:** Legal form helps determine what a grouping can credibly promise. NATO's Article 5 creates collective-defence obligations among allies; India has no membership or guarantee. BIMSTEC's Charter, in force since 2024, formalises institutional rules, whereas Quad is a non-treaty coalition that depends on the political choices and resources of four governments. Calling both security alliances would erase a crucial difference. The G20's informal economic forum can coordinate agendas and declarations, but cannot itself legislate national budgets.

Form also operates within each body. BIMSTEC's maritime transport agreement was signed in 2025, but its entry into force in 2026 initially covered four of seven members. A binding agreement among four parties cannot be called an operational transport network for the whole region. The 2025 Bangkok Vision 2030 is an adopted strategic vision, while India's 2026 BRICS chairship launch records a process, not a completed summit result. A Quad mineral framework is not automatically a producing mine or a diversified supply chain.

Yet diplomatic energy still matters. Negotiation can bring new parties into an agreement, secure finance and remove bureaucratic barriers. IBSA illustrates how a trilateral may identify joint projects, though a proposal is not a functioning institution. Conversely, SAARC's continued legal existence has not produced a recent summit: institutional form alone does not overcome political conflict. Even membership in SCO offers India a continental seat but no power to erase rivalry.

India should match the forum to the problem: chartered regional institutions for durable rules, focused minilaterals for flexible projects and global economic forums for agenda-setting. Then distinguish political agreement, legal effect, financing and measured delivery. Form sets the range of possible obligations; sustained consent and execution decide whether any promised benefit reaches people.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Assess how India should present the record of its grouping diplomacy without either overstating success or conceding incoherence. Answer in about 300 words.

**Independent model solution:** An honest record starts with classification. SAARC is a South Asian regional institution, not dissolved because summits stalled after 2014. BIMSTEC gained legal structure when its Charter entered into force in 2024; the 2025 Bangkok summit adopted a vision, but the maritime transport agreement came into force initially for only four of seven members in 2026. One institutional success does not erase the other's opportunity cost or prove Bay of Bengal-wide delivery.

At the continental level, SCO membership gives India a Eurasian forum despite China and Pakistan's presence; a seat is not a guarantee of consensus. IBSA and RIC are both trilaterals, but a dated 2025 IBSA leaders' meeting cannot be cited as proof of a corresponding 2025–26 RIC meeting. India's 2026 BRICS chairship launch was an announcement of a theme and website; it is not itself a declaration from a completed summit. BRICS partners are not full members, and New Development Bank accession requires separate evidence.

Avoiding triumphalism also means acknowledging gaps. Sikri's older statement that India was outside both APEC and ASEM is period-bound: India joined ASEM in 2007, while APEC remained a non-membership case in the dated owner. NATO's consolidation may affect India indirectly through Russia–West friction, but India is not a NATO ally; Quad cooperation creates no Article 5 obligation. India was announced as intended host of a Quad leaders' summit, yet intention must not be narrated as a verified occurrence.

This bounded account is not evidence of incoherence. Different forums address neighbours, oceans, continental access, economic governance and narrow practical projects. India should publish dated membership/status and implementation tests, recognise diplomatic bandwidth costs, and retain freedom to cooperate across rival constellations. Strategic autonomy is credible when outcomes are demonstrated, limits admitted and future commitments labelled as such.
