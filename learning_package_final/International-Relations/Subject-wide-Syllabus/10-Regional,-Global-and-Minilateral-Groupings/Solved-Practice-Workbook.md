## PART I — 40 ORIGINAL COVERAGE-LED MCQS

### Q1. Which description most accurately distinguishes a grouping from a treaty alliance?

A. A grouping may coordinate policy without a collective-defence obligation, whereas an alliance rests on defined security commitments.
B. A grouping always lacks permanent institutions and written rules, whereas an alliance always has a supranational legislature and court.
C. A grouping is necessarily regional, whereas an alliance must include states from different continents.
D. A grouping admits only members, whereas an alliance may admit observers and dialogue partners.

**Answer: A.**

- **A:** Correct; legal commitments, not diplomatic intensity, distinguish an alliance from a loose grouping.
- **B:** Incorrect; some groupings have secretariats, while alliances need not possess a supranational legislature.
- **C:** Incorrect; BRICS and G20 are groupings without a single contiguous regional base.
- **D:** Incorrect; participation tiers occur in organisations and groupings and do not define an alliance.

### Q2. Arrange the following stages in the most defensible legal sequence:

1. Entry into force
2. Signature
3. Ratification or accession
4. Negotiation

A. 2–4–3–1
B. 4–2–3–1
C. 4–3–2–1
D. 2–3–1–4

**Answer: B.**

- **A:** Incorrect; negotiation ordinarily precedes signature.
- **B:** Correct; negotiation leads to signature, consent to be bound, and then entry into force under the treaty's conditions.
- **C:** Incorrect; ratification cannot normally precede authentication/signature of the negotiated text.
- **D:** Incorrect; negotiation cannot be the final stage after entry into force.

### Q3. Which one of the following is a participation status rather than proof of full membership?

A. Depositary state
B. Founding signatory
C. Dialogue partner
D. Treaty party

**Answer: C.**

- **A:** Incorrect; a depositary performs treaty-administration functions and may also be a party.
- **B:** Incorrect; the label concerns origin and signature, though later consent requirements still matter.
- **C:** Correct; a dialogue partner participates under limited arrangements without full member rights.
- **D:** Incorrect; a treaty party has consented to be bound by that treaty.

### Q4. A summit declaration announces a corridor and creates a working group. What is the safest conclusion?

A. The corridor has entered into force as a treaty binding every participating state under international law.
B. Every member has completed domestic ratification.
C. Physical operations have begun throughout the route.
D. Political intent and an implementation mechanism exist, but delivery still needs separate evidence.

**Answer: D.**

- **A:** Incorrect; declarations and treaties have different legal character.
- **B:** Incorrect; a working group does not prove domestic consent procedures.
- **C:** Incorrect; an announced corridor is not an operating corridor.
- **D:** Correct; it preserves the declaration-versus-delivery distinction.

**Ownership:** Direct status/regime question — Topic 10.

## PART IV — SIX ORIGINAL MAINS QUESTIONS WITH MODEL ANSWERS

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 marks — 150 words

**Question:** Distinguish a treaty organisation, an informal forum and a minilateral coalition.
Why does the distinction matter for evaluating outcomes?

<!-- ORIGINAL-MAINS-1-ANSWER-START -->
A treaty organisation rests on a constitutive legal instrument defining membership, organs and
obligations; BIMSTEC after its Charter entered into force in 2024 is an example. An informal forum
coordinates policy without comparable legal personality or binding rules; the G20 illustrates
this form. A minilateral coalition has a small, self-selected membership focused on limited tasks;
the Quad is non-treaty and combines strategic consultation with functional initiatives.

The distinction determines what evidence an answer may claim. A charter can establish legal
continuity but not prove delivery. A G20 declaration records political consensus but depends on
national and institutional implementation. A Quad initiative can begin without ratification, yet
may lack a permanent enforcement structure.

Therefore, effectiveness cannot be inferred from labels. The examiner should see legal basis,
decision rule, implementation vehicle and measured outcome before judging whether cooperation is
symbolic or operational.
<!-- ORIGINAL-MAINS-1-ANSWER-END -->

**Why this earns marks:** It defines all three forms, uses named examples and connects
classification directly to evaluation.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 marks — 150 words

**Question:** Why must “member”, “partner”, “observer” and “invitee” be kept separate in
international-relations answers?

<!-- ORIGINAL-MAINS-2-ANSWER-START -->
Participation labels distribute different rights, duties and political signals. A member acts
under the organisation's constitutive rules and normally participates in decision-making. A
partner or dialogue partner receives structured access without full member rights; BRICS partner
countries and IORA dialogue partners illustrate this distinction. An observer attends under
limited rules, as Afghanistan and Mongolia do in the SCO. An invitee participates in a particular
meeting and gains no continuing status merely by attendance.

Collapsing these categories creates factual and analytical error. It can exaggerate the size of a
bloc, falsely attribute legal obligations, or treat summit access as voting power. Chairship is
also temporary agenda stewardship, not a superior category of membership.

Precise status language therefore protects both Prelims elimination and Mains causation: it shows
what a state can decide, what it must implement and what its participation actually proves.
<!-- ORIGINAL-MAINS-2-ANSWER-END -->

**Why this earns marks:** It defines each tier, supplies current examples and explains the
consequence of status inflation.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 marks — 250 words

**Question:** “BIMSTEC's institutional deepening demonstrates both the promise and limits of
regionalism.” Discuss.

<!-- ORIGINAL-MAINS-3-ANSWER-START -->
BIMSTEC demonstrates the promise of regionalism because it links seven Bay of Bengal states and
bridges South Asia with Southeast Asia. For India, this geography joins Neighbourhood First, Act
East, maritime cooperation and development of the eastern region.

Institutionally, the progression is significant. The Charter adopted at Colombo in March 2022
entered into force on 20 May 2024, giving the organisation a firmer legal foundation. The sixth
summit at Bangkok in April 2025 adopted Bangkok Vision 2030 and Rules of Procedure. The Maritime
Transport Cooperation Agreement, signed in April 2025, entered into force in May 2026. Specialised
centres in Thimphu, Bengaluru, NOIDA and Colombo can translate broad cooperation into sectoral
capacity.

Yet these gains expose the limits. The maritime agreement initially operated only among Bhutan,
India, Myanmar and Thailand, demonstrating variable-speed ratification. The 2004 FTA framework
has not produced a verified completed FTA. Members differ in capacity, domestic politics and
connectivity priorities, while regional instability can delay projects.

BIMSTEC should therefore be judged neither as a symbolic talk shop nor as a completed integration
project. Its promise lies in a chartered, functional Bay framework; its limitation is the gap
between common declarations, uneven legal participation and physical delivery. India should
support financing, secretariat capacity, measurable milestones and accession by remaining members
without claiming that BIMSTEC has replaced SAARC.
<!-- ORIGINAL-MAINS-3-ANSWER-END -->

**Why this earns marks:** It combines chronology, legal precision, implementation evidence and a
balanced prescription.

with Russia, China and Pakistan, and a voice on Eurasian connectivity and stability. Quad supports
maritime-domain awareness, resilient supply chains, critical technologies and public goods with
Australia, Japan and the United States. Using both therefore diversifies India's partnerships and
prevents dependence on a single power centre.

Nevertheless, overlap is not cost-free. China is both an SCO partner and India's strategic
competitor; Pakistan constrains counter-terrorism convergence; India's sovereignty objections
limit support for some connectivity projects. Within Quad, three partners have US treaty links
while India does not, creating different risk appetites. External powers may also demand greater
alignment during crises.

Strategic autonomy is thus not equidistance or indecision. It is the capacity to choose cooperation
issue by issue while retaining independent judgement. India should define platform-specific
objectives, resist alliance terminology, diversify defence and technology dependence, and measure
both forums by delivery. Simultaneous membership is coherent when each platform adds a distinct
capability; it becomes contradiction only if commitments become mutually exclusive.
<!-- ORIGINAL-MAINS-4-ANSWER-END -->

**Why this earns marks:** It compares legal form and utility, acknowledges partner asymmetries and
defines the conditions under which overlap remains coherent.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 marks — 300 words

**Question:** Evaluate India's five-layer architecture of regional, trans-regional, continental,
global and minilateral groupings as an instrument of multi-alignment.

<!-- ORIGINAL-MAINS-5-ANSWER-START -->
India's grouping diplomacy is a diversified portfolio designed to convert different national
interests into cooperation without exclusive bloc alignment.

At the **regional** level, SAARC retains South Asian legitimacy but remains constrained; BIMSTEC
adds a Bay of Bengal bridge to Southeast Asia, strengthened by its 2024 Charter and 2025 Bangkok
Vision. Mekong-Ganga Cooperation supports mainland Act East engagement. At the
**trans-regional** level, IORA widens maritime cooperation across the Indian Ocean, while IBSA
connects three Southern democracies. At the **continental** level, SCO provides Eurasian access,
counter-terrorism dialogue and contact with Central Asia despite China-Pakistan constraints.

At the **global-governance** level, G20 enables macroeconomic agenda-setting across advanced and
developing economies; BRICS advances reform and development-finance claims, while the NDB supplies
a separate institutional vehicle. At the **minilateral** level, Quad and I2U2 concentrate willing
partners on maritime, technology, energy, food and connectivity tasks. RIC preserves a channel
among major Eurasian powers, although no later official meeting than November 2021 was verified.

This architecture strengthens autonomy through functional fit, redundancy and bargaining options.
Stagnation in SAARC or exclusion from APEC need not disable other routes. Simultaneous SCO and
Quad participation also signals that India does not accept a single-bloc identity.

However, more platforms do not automatically mean more influence. Consensus can dilute outcomes;
expanded membership can weaken coherence; diplomatic bandwidth is finite; and summit declarations
may outpace finance, ratification and project delivery. The BIMSTEC maritime agreement initially
binding only four members exemplifies variable geometry.

India should therefore use a disciplined portfolio: define the interest, select the appropriate
platform, state its legal and participation status, fund implementation, publish measurable
milestones and close ineffective duplication. Multi-alignment succeeds when overlapping
institutions create complementary capabilities rather than diplomatic congestion.
<!-- ORIGINAL-MAINS-5-ANSWER-END -->

**Why this earns marks:** It covers all five layers, links named evidence to functions, evaluates
costs and offers a conditional judgement.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 marks — 300 words

**Question:** “The decisive test of a grouping is not the ambition of its declaration but the
credibility of its delivery chain.” Analyse with reference to India's major groupings.

<!-- ORIGINAL-MAINS-6-ANSWER-START -->
A declaration is a political output; delivery requires legal authority, finance, implementing
agencies, national action and measurable results. Groupings must therefore be assessed through a
chain: announcement, adoption or signature, ratification, entry into force, operation and outcome.

BIMSTEC illustrates both progress and caution. Its Charter was adopted in 2022 and entered into
force in 2024, while Bangkok Vision 2030 was adopted at the 2025 summit. The Maritime Transport
Cooperation Agreement was signed in 2025 and entered into force in May 2026, but initially only
among four members. Legal progress is real; region-wide delivery remains incomplete.

BRICS declarations can coordinate positions on governance reform and development, and the New
Delhi Declaration of September 2026 is the latest summit anchor. Yet expanded representation does
not itself prove common geopolitical purpose. The NDB is more concrete because lending depends on
a separate treaty institution, membership and project cycle.

The G20 can set macroeconomic and development agendas, but as an informal forum it relies on
states and international institutions to execute commitments. Quad and I2U2 can move quickly
without ratification, but announced maritime, energy, technology or infrastructure initiatives
still require budgets and agencies. SCO's 2026 Bishkek summit approved 28 documents, yet internal
rivalries may constrain implementation.

India should demand status-specific monitoring: treaty deposits and commencement dates for legal
instruments; finance, contracts and commissioned assets for projects; and published milestones for
voluntary frameworks. It should also distinguish member, partner, observer and invitee when
assigning responsibility.

Thus summit ambition matters for agenda-setting, but credibility arises only when the appropriate
legal and administrative chain produces observable public goods.
<!-- ORIGINAL-MAINS-6-ANSWER-END -->

**Why this earns marks:** It applies one analytical test across diverse institutions, supplies
dated evidence and separates legal operation from physical results.
