# Globalisation, Trade Agreements and External-Policy Effects — Solved Practice Workbook

## ORIGINAL MCQS — EXACTLY 40

### Q1. Which formulation best describes the present relationship between globalisation and sovereign nationalism?

A. Cross-border integration continues, while states selectively reassert control over strategic flows.
B. Sovereign nationalism has ended international trade and technology exchange.
C. Globalisation survives only where states have abolished domestic industrial policy.
D. The two concepts cannot operate simultaneously within the same economy.

**Answer: A**

- **A — Correct:** Integration and national control coexist across different sectors.
- **B — Incorrect:** “Waning” or reconfiguration is not the end of exchange.
- **C — Incorrect:** States may integrate externally while supporting domestic industry.
- **D — Incorrect:** An FTA and technology controls can coexist in one policy portfolio.

### Q2. Which of the following is most accurately classified as a non-tariff measure?

A. An additional customs duty on imported steel
B. A mandatory conformity-assessment certificate for imported machinery
C. A negotiated reduction in the customs rate on wine
D. A tariff-rate quota expressed through a lower in-quota duty

**Answer: B**

- **A — Incorrect:** This is a tariff.
- **B — Correct:** Certification changes market access without being a customs duty.
- **C — Incorrect:** This is a tariff concession.
- **D — Incorrect:** It combines a quantitative threshold with tariff treatment; it is not merely a conformity rule.

### Q3. What most clearly distinguishes an export control from an ordinary protective tariff?

A. It always applies equally to every product and destination.
B. It can be imposed only after a WTO appellate ruling.
C. It targets specified goods, technologies, end-users or destinations for security reasons.
D. It necessarily eliminates all trade with the targeted economy.

**Answer: C**

- **A — Incorrect:** Export controls are commonly selective.
- **B — Incorrect:** They arise under domestic export-control authority, subject to applicable law.
- **C — Correct:** Security-sensitive scope is the central distinction.
- **D — Incorrect:** Controls may require licences rather than impose a total embargo.

### Q4. Which feature distinguishes a customs union from a free trade agreement?

A. Every customs union covers services, while an FTA cannot.
B. An FTA prohibits rules of origin, while a customs union requires them.
C. A customs union has no internal tariff preferences among members.
D. Customs-union members apply a common external tariff to non-members.

**Answer: D**

- **A — Incorrect:** Services coverage depends on the agreement.
- **B — Incorrect:** FTAs commonly require rules of origin because external tariffs differ.
- **C — Incorrect:** Internal liberalisation is a defining element.
- **D — Correct:** The common external tariff is the distinguishing feature.

### Q5. Which sequence correctly orders the principal stages of a trade agreement?

A. Negotiation → conclusion → signature → approval → entry into force → implementation → utilisation → impact
B. Signature → negotiation → domestic approval → conclusion → implementation → measured impact
C. Conclusion → entry into force → signature → utilisation → domestic approval → measured impact
D. Negotiation → implementation → signature → domestic approval → conclusion → measured impact

**Answer: A**

- **A — Correct:** It preserves the legal and economic stages in order.
- **B — Incorrect:** Negotiation precedes signature and conclusion.
- **C — Incorrect:** Entry into force cannot precede signature and required approvals.
- **D — Incorrect:** Implementation cannot precede an operative agreement.

### Q6. India-EU FTA negotiations were concluded on 27 January 2026. What follows from that fact alone?

A. Preferential tariffs became legally available on that date.
B. Negotiators reached conclusion, but signature and entry into force still required separate proof.
C. The agreement was ratified by every competent institution in India and the EU.
D. Measured gains in exports and employment can be attributed to the agreement.

**Answer: B**

- **A — Incorrect:** Conclusion does not activate preferences.
- **B — Correct:** Each later legal stage needs independent evidence.
- **C — Incorrect:** No such ratification follows from conclusion.
- **D — Incorrect:** Impact requires implementation and causal assessment.

### Q7. Bilateral trade rises after an FTA enters into force. Which inference is methodologically strongest?

A. The FTA alone caused the entire observed increase in trade.
B. The increase proves equal gains across sectors, firms and regions.
C. The agreement may contribute, but other causes and utilisation require testing.
D. Rules of origin become irrelevant once aggregate bilateral trade rises.

**Answer: C**

- **A — Incorrect:** This confuses sequence with causation.
- **B — Incorrect:** Aggregate gains can conceal distributional losses.
- **C — Correct:** It applies a counterfactual and mechanism test.
- **D — Incorrect:** Utilisation depends heavily on origin compliance.

### Q8. Which statement correctly separates a trade agreement from a strategic framework?

A. Every strategic framework automatically lowers the parties' customs duties.
B. A joint strategic statement is legally identical to a ratified trade treaty.
C. A trade agreement cannot contain cooperation on strategic supply-chain resilience.
D. A strategic framework can coordinate policy without granting tariff preferences.

**Answer: D**

- **A — Incorrect:** Political coordination does not automatically amend tariff schedules.
- **B — Incorrect:** Their legal character differs.
- **C — Incorrect:** Modern agreements can include resilience and economic-security provisions.
- **D — Correct:** Political direction and legal market access must be distinguished.

### Q9. With reference to the WTO, which statement is correct?

- **B — Incorrect:** Members enforce rules under agreed procedures.

War’, especially keeping in mind the interest of India?”

**Model answer (223 words):**

The WTO remains the common rulebook for international commerce, but trade wars expose weaknesses
in rule-updating, enforcement and distributive legitimacy.

First, dispute settlement needs restoration. The Appellate Body is unable to hear appeals because
of vacancies; without binding finality, members can appeal panel reports into institutional
limbo. Second, notification and subsidy disciplines require greater transparency, while
obligations must remain feasible for developing states. Third, consensus-based negotiation needs
more inclusive issue-wise pathways without allowing plurilateral initiatives to erode the rights
of non-participants.

India's interests add a distributive agenda. WTO agriculture rules should reach a permanent
solution on public stockholding for food security, preserve meaningful special and differential
treatment, and address unequal subsidy capacity. Services negotiations should recognise movement
of professionals, while goods rules should discipline arbitrary tariffs and non-tariff barriers.
Digital-trade discussions must preserve taxation, data and development policy space. Trade and
climate measures should be transparent, non-discriminatory and sensitive to capability and
historical responsibility.

India should combine coalition-building among developing members with constructive proposals on
transparency, facilitation and dispute reform. It should also continue using FTAs for additional
market access, but not at the cost of abandoning multilateral most-favoured-nation rules.

Thus, WTO survival requires both effectiveness and legitimacy: enforceable rules, credible
appeal, inclusive negotiation and development-sensitive disciplines. Reform that accelerates
decisions but ignores food security and unequal capacity would not command durable consent.

Globalisation is waning in its universal, efficiency-first form, not disappearing. Cross-border

### Application PYQ M4 — 2018 GS-III Q12 — 15 marks, 250 words

**Primary owner:** Economy Topic 20.
**Official wording:** “How would the recent phenomena of protectionism and currency manipulations
in world trade affect macroeconomic stability of India?”

**Model answer (194 words):**

Protectionism and competitive currency action transmit external policy into India's growth,
inflation, exchange rate and financial stability.

Higher foreign tariffs reduce price competitiveness and export demand, especially for
trade-exposed manufacturing. Retaliation fragments supply chains and delays investment because
firms cannot predict market access. Import restrictions can raise input costs where Indian

logistics, standards and productivity rather than rely only on retaliatory protection. Trade
remedies must be evidence-based and temporary, while monetary and fiscal coordination should
manage inflation and volatility.

Therefore, the danger lies not only in a higher tariff level but in uncertainty, retaliation and
currency overshooting. Resilience requires open diversification and macroeconomic buffers, not
indiscriminate protectionism.

### Application PYQ M5 — 2023 GS-III Q14 — 15 marks, 250 words

agricultural subsidies.”

**Model answer (199 words):**

Farm support combines explicit budgetary transfers with price, credit and input interventions.

solution and meaningful special and differential treatment. Domestically, support should become
more transparent, resource-efficient and targeted, while protecting small farmers and consumers.

## SIX ORIGINAL MAINS QUESTIONS WITH MODEL ANSWERS

### Original Mains 1 — 10 marks — answer in not more than 150 words

**Question:** Distinguish tariffs from non-tariff measures and explain why the distinction matters
for India's trade diplomacy.

**Model answer (141 words):**

A tariff is a customs duty that directly changes the landed price of an import. A non-tariff
measure changes the conditions of market access through quotas, licensing, sanitary and
phytosanitary rules, technical standards, conformity assessment, local-content conditions or
administrative procedures.

The distinction matters because the diplomatic remedy depends on the instrument. Tariffs are
negotiated through schedules and preferences; standards require equivalence, testing capacity and
regulatory dialogue; trade remedies require evidence of dumping, subsidy or injury. A legitimate
health or safety NTM must also be separated from discriminatory application.

For India, reducing a partner's tariff may yield little if costly certification or restrictive
origin rules prevent firms from using the preference. Therefore negotiations must address both
border duties and behind-the-border compliance, while domestic policy equips MSMEs to satisfy
standards. Market access is effective only when it is legally granted and practically usable.

### Original Mains 2 — 10 marks — answer in not more than 150 words

**Question:** Why must “entry into force” and “impact” be separated while evaluating a trade
agreement?

**Model answer (129 words):**

Entry into force is a legal event: treaty obligations become operative on the date specified by
the agreement after required approvals. Impact is an empirical claim about additional trade,
investment, jobs, resilience or distribution.

The intervening chain matters. Customs systems and regulations must implement commitments; firms
must understand tariff schedules, satisfy rules of origin and claim preferences; domestic
producers must adjust. Trade may then change because of the agreement, but also because of demand,
prices, exchange rates, logistics or unrelated reforms.

India-EFTA TEPA illustrates the distinction. Its investment and employment figures are treaty
objectives, not proof of delivery. Evaluation should examine realised investment, preference
utilisation, additionality and sectoral effects against a counterfactual.

Thus, legal operation is necessary evidence of obligation, but insufficient evidence of causal or
equitable economic success.

### Original Mains 3 — 15 marks — answer in not more than 250 words

**Question:** “Preferential trade agreements sustain globalisation while fragmenting it.”
Discuss with reference to India.

**Model answer (195 words):**

Preferential agreements sustain globalisation by lowering barriers, creating legal certainty and
deepening goods, services, investment and regulatory links among selected partners. India's EFTA
TEPA, UK CETA and Oman CEPA show continued integration even when multilateral liberalisation is
slow.

Yet the same process fragments the system. Each agreement may contain different tariff schedules,
rules of origin, standards, certification, safeguards, digital rules and dispute procedures.
Firms—especially MSMEs—must navigate multiple rulebooks. Non-parties may suffer preference
erosion, while large partners can exercise greater bargaining power. Overlapping agreements can
also shift production to satisfy origin rules rather than underlying efficiency.

India's strategy demonstrates selective integration. It seeks market access through bilateral
agreements but did not join RCEP on the terms offered, retaining policy space for sensitive
sectors. This is not a rejection of trade; it is bargaining over the distribution and security of
integration.

India should preserve the WTO's most-favoured-nation baseline, negotiate transparent and
compatible rules, simplify origin procedures, publish utilisation data and provide adjustment
support. Strategic frameworks should supplement, not be mislabelled as, trade agreements.

Therefore, preferentialism is both a bridge and a boundary: it keeps exchange moving, but makes
globalisation more selective, legally complex and uneven.

### Original Mains 4 — 15 marks — answer in not more than 250 words

**Question:** Examine how foreign tariff, technology and climate policies transmit into India's
external and domestic choices.

**Model answer (203 words):**

Foreign policy measures reach India through three connected channels.

The **trade channel** operates through tariffs, quotas, remedies and standards. These alter export
prices, input costs and bargaining leverage. Rapid policy changes can delay contracts and
investment even when a tariff is later removed.

The **technology channel** uses export licences, investment screening, trusted-vendor rules and
data standards. It can restrict access to advanced inputs while creating incentives for
diversification and domestic capability. Supply-chain partnerships may benefit India, but only if
announced investment becomes operating capacity.

The **climate channel** is illustrated by EU CBAM. Importer authorisation, embedded-emissions
reporting and certificate obligations transmit EU regulation to foreign producers through market
access. The measure can encourage cleaner production, yet India may contest equity,
proportionality and insufficient finance or technology transfer.

These channels overlap: a climate rule can protect industry; a trade framework can include export
controls and investment review. Domestic effects are also unequal across exporters,
import-competing firms, workers, consumers, MSMEs and regions.

India therefore needs coordinated trade, industrial, technology and climate diplomacy:
diversified markets and suppliers, standards and carbon-accounting capacity, WTO engagement,
selective FTAs and time-bound adjustment support. The objective is not insulation, but the ability
to absorb external shocks while retaining access and strategic choice.

### Original Mains 5 — 20 marks — answer in not more than 300 words

**Question:** Critically assess India's emerging trade-agreement strategy in an era of
security-conditioned globalisation.

**Model answer (265 words):**

Security-conditioned globalisation describes continued cross-border exchange whose terms are
increasingly shaped by resilience, technology control, climate policy and strategic trust.
India's trade-agreement strategy seeks opportunity without surrendering policy space.

Its first feature is selective bilateralism. UAE CEPA, Australia ECTA, EFTA TEPA, UK CETA and Oman
CEPA are in force, while the EU FTA is only concluded and the New Zealand FTA is signed with a
future operative date. This varied portfolio expands market options and regulatory cooperation.

Second, India preserves negotiating boundaries. Its 2019 RCEP non-joining reflected concerns over
adjustment, origin rules and sensitive sectors. This was not autarky, because other negotiations
continued.

Third, agreements increasingly cover services, mobility, investment, digital rules, standards and
supply-chain resilience, not tariffs alone. This can improve access to capital and technology.

However, four risks require qualification. Different rules create compliance fragmentation;
formal preferences may remain unused; powerful partners may link market access to security or
regulatory demands; and aggregate trade growth may conceal losses among MSMEs, workers or regions.
Objectives such as EFTA-linked investment cannot be counted as realised outcomes.

India should negotiate through a published interest matrix: offensive export interests,
defensive sensitivities, technology and mobility gains, RoO design, safeguard triggers and
distributional adjustment. It should strengthen customs, testing, carbon measurement and firm
awareness, while reporting utilisation and additionality. WTO reform remains essential because
bilateral agreements cannot provide universal baseline rules.

India's strategy is credible when it combines diversified access with resilience and domestic
capability. Its success must be judged not by the number of agreements signed, but by usable
preferences, additional investment, technological upgrading and fairly managed adjustment.

### Original Mains 6 — 20 marks — answer in not more than 300 words

**Question:** Propose a coherent Indian response to the simultaneous weakening of multilateral
trade governance and rise of unilateral economic measures.

**Model answer (252 words):**

The present challenge has two parts: the WTO's negotiating and appellate weaknesses reduce common
predictability, while tariffs, sanctions, export controls, subsidies and carbon measures allow
powerful states to transmit domestic priorities abroad.

India needs a layered response.

**Multilateral layer:** support restoration of binding appellate review, stronger notifications
and workable committee processes. Build developing-country coalitions for special and
differential treatment, a permanent public-stockholding solution and fair disciplines on
subsidies and climate-linked trade measures.

**Preferential layer:** negotiate selective FTAs where market, services, technology and mobility
gains exceed adjustment costs. Preserve exact stage discipline and do not announce concluded
talks as an operative agreement. Simplify rules of origin and publish utilisation data.

**Resilience layer:** map concentration in critical inputs, diversify suppliers and routes, build
selective domestic capacity, maintain strategic stocks where justified and cooperate with trusted
partners. De-risking should avoid expensive blanket decoupling or a new single-bloc dependence.

**Capability layer:** improve logistics, customs, standards laboratories, digital compliance,
carbon accounting, innovation and skills. These convert negotiated rights into practical access.

**Distribution layer:** use transparent safeguards and time-bound support for workers, MSMEs and
regions exposed to import competition. Consumers and downstream producers must also be considered.

**Diplomatic layer:** distinguish foreign domestic measures from obligations binding on India,
contest discriminatory action through available legal and political channels, and coordinate
trade, technology, climate and security negotiations.

This approach recognises that neither multilateralism nor bilateralism is sufficient alone.
India's objective should be open resilience: preserve beneficial interdependence, reduce coercive
vulnerability and shape rules through diversified partnerships without abandoning strategic
autonomy.
