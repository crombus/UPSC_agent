### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 marks | Answer in 150 words

**Explain** how central dogma, recombinant DNA and PCR occupy different stages of biotechnology.

**Model:** DNA stores sequences, transcription makes RNA and translation yields protein; that information rail identifies what a tool measures or changes. Recombinant DNA joins a chosen insert into a vector: restriction enzymes cut at recognised sites, ligase joins DNA and host transformation and markers help select cells expressing the product, as in recombinant insulin. PCR instead amplifies a chosen DNA segment by denaturation, primer annealing and thermostable-polymerase extension; it neither expresses a protein nor by itself integrates a gene. For an RNA-virus assay, reverse transcriptase first makes complementary DNA; real-time measurement monitors subsequent amplification and is an independent axis. Thus a positive molecular signal requires sampling, controls and interpretation rather than establishing pathogen viability or disease severity. This distinction allows UPSC answers to separate production from detection and to test the claimed clinical inference.

A high-quality example locates the method on the DNA-to-protein information rail rather than calling every nucleic-acid manipulation a treatment.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 marks | Answer in 150 words

**Model:** DBT, under the Ministry of Science and Technology, supports biotechnology missions and research; its established Section 8 public sector enterprise BIRAC funds, incubates and connects innovators to industry. A recombinant protein or engineered microbe begins as proof of concept but needs stable cell lines, pilot batches, controlled oxygen and pH, purification and quality systems before reliable manufacture. BioNEST helps incubation and the National Biopharma Mission illustrates translation support. Such assistance does not grant market authorisation: CDSCO regulates medicines, while environmental release of GMOs involves GEAC under a different framework. Even after technical validation, financing, procurement, prices and access determine social use. Therefore the appropriate performance measure is a safely validated, affordable product reaching users, not the number of calls or laboratory papers. Institutional role clarity avoids treating translational funding as proof of regulatory safety.

Track each funded idea against reproducible output, validated quality, independent approval and actual patient access; each step has a different institutional owner.

Its six thematic sectors are bio-based chemicals and enzymes; functional food and smart proteins; precision biotherapeutics; climate-resilient agriculture; carbon capture and its utilisation; and futuristic marine and space research. They describe areas of policy attention, not six already successful industries. Consider microbial enzyme production: a genetically characterised strain may yield pectinase in a flask, yet oxygen transfer, feedstock cost, contamination, recovery and purification can change results in a larger vessel. Likewise climate-resilient traits require local agronomic and biosafety evaluation; precision therapies require clinical validation and regulated supply. DBT designs mission support; BIRAC provides an industry–academia translation interface; neither supplants CDSCO or GEAC in its respective regulatory domain. The July 2025 report of over 2,000 first-round proposals measures interest in calls, not approved medicines, operating hubs or employment delivered. A credible implementation dashboard should distinguish proposals, funded pilots, validated output, regulatory milestones, market adoption and lifecycle environmental performance. India can turn the policy into green production if shared biofoundries, scale-up finance, skills, standards and procurement work together, with transparent safety and access checks. BioE3 is a direction and opportunity, not a completed production result.

A biofoundry can speed design-build-test-learn iterations, yet measured industrial yield and lifecycle impact require process-specific pilots. Carbon-use research should establish whether capture, conversion and energy inputs deliver a net environmental benefit rather than assuming circularity from the policy name.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 marks | Answer in 250 words

**Discuss** GenomeIndia's research value while evaluating limitations of reference genomics and genetic medicine.

**Model:** DBT announced completion of 10,000 genome sequences from the Indian population under GenomeIndia on 28 February 2024. A population reference helps scientists compare variants, investigate rare diseases and explore pharmacogenomic associations in a genetically varied population. This is an infrastructure and research milestone, not a complete clinical diagnosis of all participants or a guarantee that every community is adequately represented. Sample design, annotation, reference bias and independent clinical validation govern what a finding means. Sensitive genomic sequences also require consent, security and safeguards against exclusion or misuse. Genetic medicine is a different downstream activity: an engineered viral vector or lipid nanoparticle can deliver material to selected cells; some treatments compensate for a defective gene while others alter a relevant sequence. Somatic treatment is not automatically inherited by offspring, and delivery of a payload does not rewrite a person's whole genome. Evidence of benefit needs a precisely defined target, safe delivery, off-target assessment where applicable, trials, manufacturing consistency, regulation and accessible pricing. Indian programmes such as GenomeIndia can improve the knowledge base, but translation into equitable care cannot be inferred from the headline sequence count alone. An answer should separate dataset, disease association, diagnosis and approved therapy as distinct evidence rungs.

A pharmacogenomic association describes a statistical relationship, whereas treatment decisions also depend on functional interpretation, clinical history and medicines available to patients. Clear governance of data access and representation is thus an essential complement to scientific sequencing capacity. Informed consent should clarify possible familial implications of genomic findings and guard against discrimination in downstream use.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 marks | Answer in 250 words

**Evaluate** whether microbes and enzymes can materially improve farmer returns from perishables and biomass without claiming universal deployment.

**Model:** Farmer benefit depends on where a biological process enters the value chain. In food processing, fermentation-derived pectinase and cellulase can improve mango or guava pulp extraction and juice clarification; a processor may purchase surplus or lower-grade fruit rather than reject it. Controlled lactic fermentation or yeast conversion can transform perishable vegetables or fruit into longer-marketable pickles, beverages or vinegar. In biomass management, anaerobic consortia convert separated organic waste to biogas; microbial enzymes can assist conversion of crop residue to fermentable sugars. These are processing and energy routes, not proof that genetic modification increased yields. DBT's BioE3 agenda and BIRAC incubation may aid process design and pilot-scale links to firms, but plants need hygiene, appropriate pH, oxygen or anaerobic control, testing, packaging and outlets. For fuels, collection costs, feedstock diversion from soil, land/water competition and lifecycle emissions may erase a claimed gain. For processed produce, purchase terms, local demand and farmer organisations determine whether extra processor value reaches farmers. Policy should test realised farmgate returns, post-harvest losses, safe product quality and environmental externalities against a baseline. Thus biotechnology offers concrete mechanisms and new market opportunities, but not a guaranteed nationwide income or energy outcome merely because a strain works in a flask.

Measuring the fraction of surplus harvest that processors purchase is more informative than counting enzyme proposals; compare safe stored-product yield with an untreated baseline. Public support for shared processing infrastructure can help small producers participate, but only contract transparency and competitive buyers transmit value to farms.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 marks | Answer in 250 words

**Critically examine** biotechnology's platform-to-market path across agriculture, medicine, industry and environment.

**Model:** Biotechnology uses living systems and engineering to turn biological mechanisms into services or products. In agriculture, totipotency-based tissue culture can multiply planting material and marker diagnostics can identify crop disease; neither by itself makes a transgenic crop. In medicine, recombinant insulin illustrates vector-mediated gene expression, while molecular assays detect nucleic-acid or protein signatures and stem-cell/gene therapies target different clinical mechanisms. In industry, fermentation and cell culture yield enzymes or biologics, requiring controlled feedstock, pH, oxygen, sterility and downstream purification. Environmental uses include microbial waste treatment and biofilters, whose functioning depends on flow and maintenance. DBT supports mission design; BIRAC's BioNEST and translational architecture help incubate ventures; Bio-RIDE consolidates research/innovation/entrepreneurship support; BioE3 promotes high-performance biomanufacturing across named sectors. But each step from sequence, proof of concept and pilot to regulated manufacture and affordable adoption is a separate claim. CDSCO's medicine remit and GEAC's environmental GMO-release role must not be collapsed into BIRAC funding. Safety, equity, privacy for genome data, lifecycle impact and realistic scale remain live questions. India's priority is not a larger tally of proposals alone but validated and accessible products with transparent process controls and appropriate review. Biotechnology contributes across sectors precisely because it is more than GM crops; its social value must be measured after, not before, trustworthy deployment.

BioE3 announcements and Bio-RIDE support should be evaluated separately from treatment licensing or crop release, as institutional labels alone do not establish efficacy. For energy claims, report lifecycle net benefits against alternative uses for biomass, including maintaining soil organic matter.
