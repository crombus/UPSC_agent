#### 2018 GS-III — Bose-Einstein statistics (cross-owner)

**Demand:** Explain the contribution of Bose-Einstein statistics to physics. **Written supporting solution:** S. N. Bose's counting method treated indistinguishable particles differently from classical distinguishable objects; Einstein extended the approach. Bosons can share a quantum state, making Bose-Einstein distributions crucial to explaining phenomena such as bosonic quantum systems. A conceptual prediction and its later experimental uses have separate dates and collaborators. Topic 21 owns the statistical physics and the full question; Topic 26 contributes only the attribution rule: do not invent a Nobel award for Bose or attribute every later Bose-Einstein-condensate experiment to his original paper. The named scientist's contribution matters more than an unverified prize association.

#### 2021 GS-III — blue LED and everyday life (cross-owner)

**Demand:** Explain how the blue LED invention recognised by the 2014 Physics prize affects everyday life. **Written supporting solution:** The prize recognised Isamu Akasaki, Hiroshi Amano and Shuji Nakamura for inventing efficient blue light-emitting diodes. An LED produces light through semiconductor electron-hole recombination; efficient blue emission enabled bright white LED lighting, including blue LED plus phosphor conversion. Lower energy consumption and longer lifetime are potential advantages relative to older lighting, conditional on the installed product and its lifecycle. Topic 21 owns the semiconductor mechanism and substantive everyday-life Mains answer. Topic 26 tests the citation boundary: invention and subsequent mass production, installation, access, electricity savings and e-waste outcomes are distinct claims. Do not equate the 2014 award date with first sale, or presume that every Indian streetlight was replaced.

### Original GS-III Mains practice — independent model answers

#### Original Mains 1 — 10 marks

**Question:** Explain the minimum evidence needed to identify a Nobel laureate and distinguish biographical clues. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer:** A laureate identification is a corroboration task, not a memory test of famous names. Begin with the official prize category and award year; then match the official spelling, motivation, scientific mechanism and biography. John Clarke, who shared the 2025 Physics prize, is recorded by NobelPrize.org as born in Cambridge, United Kingdom, in 1942 and affiliated at award time with the University of California, Berkeley. UK birthplace does not become US birthplace or determine his citizenship merely because the employer is American. His co-laureates Michel H. Devoret and John M. Martinis must not inherit his biographical clues; his one-third prize share is not sole authorship. The official citation specifically concerns macroscopic quantum mechanical tunnelling and energy quantisation in an electric circuit, not a completed quantum computer. Record a direct India connection only if documented, not because a person has a suggestive name or studies in India. Finally separate announcement date from experiment date, institution from scientist and prize share from sole authorship. This method prevents biographical elimination from corrupting scientific attribution.

#### Original Mains 2 — 10 marks

**Question:** Distinguish theory, observation, experiment, discovery, invention and development in prize attribution. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer:** An observation records an effect; a theory proposes an explanatory and predictive account; an experiment tests a claim under controlled conditions. A discovery establishes a phenomenon or relationship, while an invention creates a device or process and development improves a usable method or architecture. These stages can overlap but should not be silently substituted. C. V. Raman's 1930 Physics award concerned scattering of light and the effect named for him; a later spectrometer using inelastic scattering is an application, not the exact awarded act. Similarly the 2025 Chemistry citation says development of metal-organic frameworks, not deployment of a national water system. A theory may guide an experiment and many researchers may refine later instruments; the prize names particular recipients and contributions, not every contributor to the whole discipline. Record the research date, publication, patent, award and product rollout separately. Independent replication, performance tests, regulation and measured public outcomes may intervene. Thus official citation wording fixes the recognition while mechanism explains why it matters.

#### Original Mains 3 — 15 marks

**Question:** Analyse the 2025 Physics prize through its official citation, superconducting-circuit mechanism and technology boundary. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer:** The 2025 Nobel Prize in Physics recognised John Clarke, Michel H. Devoret and John M. Martinis for the discovery of macroscopic quantum mechanical tunnelling and energy quantisation in an electric circuit. Announced on 7 October 2025, this is an award date, not a claim that the first experiment occurred that day. In a superconducting circuit including a Josephson junction, a collective electrical state behaved quantum mechanically: it could escape the zero-voltage state by tunnelling and absorb or emit particular energy amounts, showing discrete rather than continuously arbitrary energy levels. The point is that quantum phenomena could be observed in an engineered electrical circuit large enough to be termed macroscopic, not that ordinary computers ceased to be useful.

A bridge to quantum computing is scientifically reasonable because controlled superconducting circuits are relevant to quantum hardware. But demonstrating a circuit's tunnelling and quantisation does not itself prove a scalable, error-corrected or fault-tolerant general-purpose machine. Replication, control, coherence, engineering and useful workloads are further tests. John Clarke's Nobel facts page lists Cambridge, UK, as birthplace and UC Berkeley as affiliation at award: birthplace is not affiliation or nationality. Sharing a prize also does not assign him sole credit for all superconducting circuits.

For India's research strategy, support experimental physics and instrumentation alongside scientific training and independently tested hardware claims. The examiner should link citation → mechanism → possible application → limiting evidence rather than announcing commercial quantum superiority from one honoured experiment.

#### Original Mains 4 — 15 marks

**Question:** Discuss the 2025 Chemistry and Medicine prizes by separating awarded discoveries from possible applications and commercialisation. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer:** The 2025 Chemistry prize, announced 8 October, recognised Susumu Kitagawa, Richard Robson and Omar M. Yaghi for the development of metal-organic frameworks (MOFs). Metal nodes joined by organic linkers form porous architectures whose pore environment can be adjusted. Water harvesting, carbon-dioxide capture, toxic-gas storage and catalysis are plausible research uses. The citation, however, is for a material architecture: scalable capture yields, safe operation, regeneration energy and lifecycle benefit must be demonstrated separately. Neither a Nobel prize nor porous structure alone establishes municipal water security.

The 2025 Physiology or Medicine prize, announced 6 October, recognised Mary E. Brunkow, Fred Ramsdell and Shimon Sakaguchi for discoveries concerning peripheral immune tolerance. Regulatory T cells restrain inappropriate attacks outside central immune-selection sites. Sakaguchi identified such cells; Brunkow and Ramsdell linked autoimmune vulnerability to Foxp3, and subsequent work connected Foxp3 to regulatory-T-cell development. This mechanism offers research avenues in autoimmunity, cancer and transplantation, but a pathway is not itself a licensed therapy. Trials must test safety and effectiveness in relevant patients; regulatory decisions and population outcomes follow only with separate evidence.

Both prizes illustrate a progression from basic mechanism to engineered intervention and finally public value. Indian water and health programmes could investigate these technologies, but should compare alternatives, verify outcomes under local conditions and avoid naming a prize as evidence of deployment. Cite category, year, motivation and named scientists precisely; classify applications as prospective until verified.

#### Original Mains 5 — 20 marks

**Question:** Examine how recent Nobel anchors in quantum physics, MOFs, immune tolerance and microRNA reveal the path from basic science to application. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer:** Nobel citations recognise specified contributions, not whole technologies. The 2025 Physics prize to Clarke, Devoret and Martinis concerns macroscopic tunnelling and quantised energy in an electric circuit. Superconducting Josephson-junction experiments help explain possible quantum hardware, but not a deployed fault-tolerant computer. The 2025 Chemistry prize to Kitagawa, Robson and Yaghi concerns development of metal-organic frameworks: porous metal-node/organic-linker networks can be tuned for adsorption and reactions, but water capture or carbon removal at policy scale requires field and lifecycle evidence.

The 2025 Medicine prize to Brunkow, Ramsdell and Sakaguchi recognises peripheral immune tolerance. Regulatory T cells and Foxp3 illuminate control of self-reactivity, rather than certify a specific autoimmune therapy. The 2024 Medicine prize to Ambros and Ruvkun recognised microRNA and post-transcriptional gene regulation: small RNAs can suppress protein production by interaction with messenger RNA, but therapeutic efficacy and safety must be tested independently. The prizes belong to different categories and years; mixing the microRNA award with the 2025 immune-tolerance citation defeats sound attribution.

In each case a useful answer progresses from official motivation and mechanism to independent replication, engineering, clinical or environmental validation, regulation, scale and measured benefit. India's quantum, materials and biomedical researchers need reproducible tools and appropriate testing infrastructure; a product press release, patent, single experiment or award cannot substitute for that chain. Prize-year chronology, exact recipient identity and prospective versus realised application are safeguards against exaggerating science-policy impact.

#### Original Mains 6 — 20 marks

**Question:** Evaluate scientific attribution and Indian relevance through Raman, Khorana, Ramakrishnan and routed PYQs. Answer in about 250 words.

**Model answer:** Scientific relevance is demonstrated through a mechanism and its evidence, not inferred from a scientist's birthplace or an institution's publicity. C. V. Raman, affiliated at award time with Calcutta University, won the 1930 Physics prize for scattering of light and discovery of the Raman effect; photons that change energy on scattering provide a basis for later material spectroscopy. The later instrument is not identical to the original citation. Har Gobind Khorana shared the 1968 Medicine prize for interpreting the genetic code and its function in protein synthesis; constructed RNA chains helped map codons to amino acids. His recorded birthplace in Raipur, India, differs from University of Wisconsin, his award-time affiliation. Venkatraman Ramakrishnan shared the 2009 Chemistry prize for ribosomal structure and function, studied using X-ray crystallography; later antibiotic relevance is an application, not the citation. He was born in Chidambaram, Tamil Nadu, and was affiliated with MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology at award.

These records invite an India-centric discussion of research education, instrumentation, collaborative work and translational capacity, not unsupported claims that birth alone attributes a prize to an Indian institution. The routed 2026 Prelims Q81 tests a 2025 recipient through biography and work: its provisional local key must not be guessed. The 2018 Bose-Einstein-statistics and 2021 blue-LED Mains demands belong substantively to Physics Topic 21. Topic 26 contributes chronology and attribution only: the 2014 blue-LED prize cannot itself prove every lighting rollout. Credit should follow official contribution, while public benefits need independent evidence of development, access and measurable outcomes.
