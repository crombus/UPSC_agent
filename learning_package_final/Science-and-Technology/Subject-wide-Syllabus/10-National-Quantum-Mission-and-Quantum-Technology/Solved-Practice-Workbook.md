### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Explain superposition, measurement and decoherence without equating a qubit with two classical bits. Answer in 150 words.

**Model answer:** A classical bit takes one readable value at a time; a qubit has a state described by amplitudes that can combine alternatives before measurement. This does not permit the observer to read both classical answers freely: measurement produces an outcome and changes the state. Useful quantum algorithms arrange transformations so desired outcomes become more likely through interference, rather than winning automatically because the system is 'parallel'. Entanglement can correlate several qubits, but it does not let them send controllable messages faster than light. Environmental coupling destroys useful coherence; depending on the physical platform, engineers reduce noise through isolation, control, shielding or low temperatures. For India's NQM, the distinction matters because a target number of physical qubits measures hardware scale, not the duration or fidelity of useful computation. The evaluative question is whether a defined task, error profile and classical comparator support an advantage, not whether a machine has a striking qubit headline.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Distinguish QKD from post-quantum cryptography and explain why each needs a wider security system. Answer in 150 words.

**Model answer:** Quantum key distribution uses quantum states to establish shared key material; disturbance can reveal eavesdropping under the protocol's assumptions. The key can then be used in a broader encrypted communications system; QKD does not itself make every message secure. Parties must authenticate each other and secure detectors, relays and endpoints. Post-quantum cryptography instead uses classical algorithms designed to resist known quantum attacks, normally through software and standards migration; it does not require a special quantum link. A public department with archived sensitive records should inventory long-lived cryptography and plan upgrades, even if a future attack-capable quantum computer has not been demonstrated. Dedicated high-value links might separately justify testing QKD against infrastructure costs and threat models. Thus the choice is not between magical physics and obsolete mathematics. Assess geography, authentication, interoperability, operational risk and the lifetime of information; neither tool replaces competent key management or cyber hygiene. The key question is which threat the organisation faces and whether a proposed solution is actually deployed in the intended setting.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Explain why physical-qubit targets do not establish fault-tolerant quantum capability. Answer in 250 words.

**Model answer:** Quantum algorithms depend on coherent states and sufficiently accurate operations. Environmental interaction causes decoherence, while gates and measurement introduce errors. A physical qubit is a hardware unit; a logical qubit encodes protected information across multiple physical units, with error-syndrome measurement and correction. This protection has resource overhead and requires physical errors to be adequately controlled. Therefore a large count of noisy physical qubits does not tell us how many reliable logical qubits exist, how deep a circuit can run, or whether an application beats a classical alternative. DST's NQM, approved on 19 April 2023, sets an ambition of 50–1,000 **physical** qubits over eight years. That is a hardware-scaling target, not a report of 1,000 fault-tolerant logical qubits or accomplished general-purpose quantum advantage. Compare candidate platforms on coherence, gate fidelity, connectivity, manufacture and control costs rather than on count alone. A credible success claim should state device conditions, reproducible errors, error-correction evidence, task definition and fair classical benchmarking. Materials and control research under the mission can improve these conditions even before a useful processor exists. In sum, hardware progress is meaningful, but mission targets, research demonstrations, error-corrected operation and economic use are different maturity stages. For instance, increasing physical-qubit count while gate fidelity deteriorates may reduce usable circuit depth. A published experiment should distinguish repeated measured operation from projected performance; even a logical-qubit demonstration would not alone establish commercial utility for every workload.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Compare quantum computing, communication, sensing and materials in the NQM and assess their readiness separately. Answer in 250 words.

**Model answer:** The National Quantum Mission is a DST-led portfolio, not a single quantum-computer purchase. Cabinet approval on 19 April 2023 carried a stated ₹6,003.65-crore outlay for 2023–24 to 2030–31. Its four verticals have different objects and evidentiary tests. Computing uses controlled qubits and interference for selected algorithms; physical-qubit scale alone does not establish useful error-corrected computation. Communication includes quantum key distribution, which can reveal disturbance in key exchange, but demands authentication and secure endpoints. Sensing and metrology use quantum sensitivity for precise field measurements and clocks: calibrated uncertainty in the intended environment matters more than a processor qubit count. Materials and devices provide enabling components for all three. DST's announced thematic hubs organise research across verticals; their creation is not evidence that every proposed link, sensor and machine is a commercial public service. An Indian evaluation should record researchers and technical groups as ecosystem indicators while separately testing repeatable physical performance, integration, affordability and deployment. QKD and post-quantum classical cryptography should not be conflated when designing security; the latter addresses algorithm migration without a specialised quantum channel. India's sensible strategy is to build talent and engineering depth across competing modalities while demanding dated and independent evidence for each application claim. The four thematic hubs are institutional arrangements with different tests: a sensing hub should report calibrated uncertainty, a communication hub link-level key quality, and a computing hub reproducible error data. A materials hub is judged by enabling components, not by a claimed nationwide network.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Evaluate India's NQM as a capability mission rather than a race to announce the biggest qubit count. Answer in 250 words.

**Model answer:** A quantum capability chain begins with basic science and suitable materials, continues through manufacturable components and low-error control, and only then reaches reliable applications. DST's NQM was approved on 19 April 2023 for 2023–24 to 2030–31, with four verticals: computing, communications, sensing/metrology, and materials/devices. The stated 50–1,000 physical-qubit target in eight years is one research ambition, not a useful logical-qubit inventory. Decoherence and gate errors make a raw hardware count insufficient; logical error correction consumes multiple physical units. On the communication side, a satellite-QKD distance objective cannot prove a nationwide protected network, since authentication and operational links must still be established. Sensors may deliver separately testable precision improvements before universal computing becomes practical. DST's October 2024 thematic-hub announcement and later reporting of technical groups demonstrate institution-building, but hubs are not commercial machines. Success indicators should include reproducible error metrics, appropriate classical benchmarks, field calibration, trained teams, startup access and independent audits. Public procurement should label a target, prototype, demonstration and deployed service separately. An India-centric mission can strengthen technological options and cyber readiness without assuming every quantum device solves the same problem. The strongest verdict recognises incremental gains while refusing to promote an announced count into unverified fault tolerance. Beyond qubits, evaluate the pipeline for materials, fabrication, electronics, control software and skilled operators. A startup prototype can be valuable without meeting a deployment threshold. By labelling evidence honestly, India can compare progress across disciplines without forcing unrelated outputs into one league table.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Assess an Indian quantum-security strategy combining QKD, PQC and cryptographic readiness. Answer in 250 words.

**Model answer:** Quantum threats and quantum opportunities demand distinct responses. A future sufficiently capable fault-tolerant computer could threaten some currently deployed public-key methods, especially where an adversary stores encrypted data for later attack. Agencies should inventory sensitive long-life records, algorithm dependencies and certificates, then plan standards-based migration to post-quantum **classical** cryptography. Such migration runs on conventional systems and needs compatibility testing, key management and secure deployment. Quantum key distribution, a different technology within NQM's communications vertical, can distribute key material through quantum-state exchange so interception disturbs the state under protocol assumptions. But a QKD link does not encrypt every message automatically: authenticated endpoints, trustworthy devices and secure relays remain necessary. A high-value Indian government link might justify a controlled QKD pilot with documented distance and failure rates; that result cannot be called national coverage. Parallel investments in sensing and computing can build expertise but should not be booked as cyber protection. DST's mission approval and satellite-QKD distance target are goals, not operational proof. Set priorities by threat, geography, information lifetime and cost. The resulting layered strategy uses PQC migration for scalable algorithmic exposure and carefully evaluated QKD for suitable links, with endpoint security and incident response for both. Neither requires claiming that an attack-capable quantum computer already exists. Post-quantum migration should also inventory legacy devices that cannot readily accept software updates. For QKD, an optical-link demonstration needs documented endpoint authentication and independent testing against implementation flaws. Procurement should demand measurable security outcomes rather than the label 'quantum-secure'.
