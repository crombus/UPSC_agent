## PYQS AND ANSWER PRACTICE

### PYQ provenance and ownership boundary

**No audited 2018–2026 objective or descriptive PYQ is directly routed to Topic 24** in the paired owner/workbook. Do not label an original institutional question a PYQ or invent an official key. The prior workbook identified cross-owned demands in biotechnology R&D (2018/2021 GS-III; substantive Topic 13), quantum/physics discovery impact (2018/2021 GS-III; substantive Topic 21), and semiconductor-mission industry (2025 GS-III; substantive Topic 11). Exact original stems and answers are not reconstructed here. The following are **supporting institutional analyses, not purported independent solutions to those full papers**:

- **Biotechnology R&D, 2018/2021 GS-III, cross-owner:** DBT is the biotechnology Department; university and CSIR-CCMB/IGIB research may supply upstream evidence, while ANRF-style grants support a cross-sector pipeline. Research findings require validation, regulation and health-system delivery before a technology claim; Topic 13 owns biological/technology substance.
- **Physics discoveries and blue-LED impact, 2018/2021 GS-III, cross-owner:** CSIR-NPL measurement and CSIR-CEERI electronics research are examples of enabling institutions; recognition of Bose–Einstein statistics or an LED mechanism does not establish a grant's causal effect on a specific commercial device. Topic 21 owns the physics and independent descriptive solutions.
- **Semiconductor industrial mission, 2025 GS-III, cross-owner:** distinction among basic research, metrology, design, fabrication, packaging and testing is essential. DST/ANRF research support can complement, but cannot replace, actual chip-sector mission delivery and verified manufacturing output; Topic 11 owns the semiconductor policy and exact PYQ.

### Six original Mains questions and model answers

### Original Mains 1 — 10 marks / 150 words

**Question:** Distinguish the legal and functional identities of DST, DSIR, CSIR and ANRF. Answer in 150 words.

**Model answer:** The Department of Science and Technology is a government Department for science promotion, policy and coordination; it is not a single laboratory or ministry. The Department of Scientific and Industrial Research is a separate Department in the same ministry and administratively houses CSIR. CSIR is an autonomous society with a performing laboratory network: its official profile reported 37 national laboratories when reviewed in August 2026. CSIR-NPL serves metrology while CSIR-NCL focuses on chemistry. ANRF, established by the ANRF Act, 2023 and in force from 5 February 2024, is a statutory apex research-funding and strategic-direction body, not a CSIR unit or DST sub-office. A grant may fund a university or laboratory, but the funder does not thereby perform the experiment. This division permits policy, funding and laboratory capacity to complement each other. Effective coordination still requires peer review, capable host institutions and evidence that results move beyond calls and papers.

### Original Mains 2 — 10 marks / 150 words

**Question:** Explain how ANRF changed SERB's legal position while qualifying its funding promise. Answer in 150 words.

**Model answer:** Section 27 of the ANRF Act, 2023 repealed the SERB Act, 2008 and dissolved the separate SERB statutory body, transferring its functions and related matters to the successor framework subject to legal savings. ANRF has broader strategic direction and ecosystem-building ambitions than a narrow competitive-grants council. Under section 5 the Prime Minister is ex officio President of its Governing Board; under section 7 the Principal Scientific Adviser chairs the Executive Council. The announced ₹50,000-crore design over 2023–28 anticipated roughly ₹36,000 crore from non-government sources such as industry and philanthropy; this is not proof of full Union budget appropriation, private receipts or spent grants. As of the 2026 portal review ANRF issued competitive calls, demonstrating operation at the call stage, not economy-wide research impact. Whether its reforms broaden university participation and actually crowd in finance needs audited receipts, timely awards and measured outputs.

### Original Mains 3 — 15 marks / 250 words

**Question:** Analyse the steps that turn basic research funding into socially usable Indian technology. Answer in 250 words.

**Model answer:** A grant enables work, not an automatic product. DST can help set horizontal S&T priorities; ANRF can fund investigators or mission-linked teams; a university or CSIR laboratory performs and tests the research. For example, CSIR-CEERI's electronics-device work and CSIR-NPL's metrology can support device design and trustworthy measurements, while industry may supply scale-up and manufacturing capacity. The chain requires a defined question, independent replication, prototype testing, measurement traceability, standards or approvals as relevant, and procurement or market adoption. An ANRF call is evidence of an invitation to propose; an award is evidence of funding; neither alone proves a deployed sensor, commercial chip yield or public benefit. The statutory transition from SERB to ANRF widened the intended role beyond grant disbursal, but central strategy must not suppress disciplinary peer review or researcher autonomy. More universities need proposal support, shared equipment, mentorship and grant administration so elite campuses do not capture all opportunities merely through existing capacity. Industry cofunding can help translation where firms have absorptive ability; it cannot replace long-horizon public support for uncertain science. Measurable outcomes require evidence that a tested technology performs under Indian operating conditions and reaches relevant users. A balanced system therefore supports both investigator-led discovery and carefully chosen mission programmes, evaluating knowledge creation separately from product deployment and social use. A regional university with no grant-management team may win fewer competitive awards even with promising investigators. Mentored application support and access to national facilities can reveal such hidden capacity rather than simply relabel applicants.

### Original Mains 4 — 15 marks / 250 words

**Question:** Discuss how horizontal S&T institutions complement but do not replace sectoral mission bodies. Answer in 250 words.

**Model answer:** Horizontal institutions build capability across scientific fields. DST promotes policy and coordinates science programmes; ANRF finances and shapes research across eligible institutions; CSIR's autonomous network under DSIR performs discipline-specific work, such as NPL metrology, NCL chemistry and IGIB genomics. These can serve a sectoral objective without taking over that sector's legal mandate. ISRO is an organisation under the Department of Space; DAE and DBT are Departments; DRDO is under the Ministry of Defence and MeitY is a Ministry. ICMR under MoHFW conducts biomedical research, while ICAR under DARE anchors agricultural research. Conflating them as DST field units conceals who executes a mission or approves a product. For example, a semiconductor programme needs skilled graduates and reliable measurements, but ANRF grants cannot by themselves commission a fabrication line or certify production yield. A DBT-linked biotechnology pathway may draw on university and CSIR molecular research, yet clinical approval sits with a regulator and field delivery with the health system. Conversely, vertical missions without general research capacity risk brittle dependencies on imported techniques and narrow skills. India should use shared infrastructure, transparent funding and institutional collaboration, while recording which agency funds, which performs, which regulates and which delivers. Complementarity is real only when a chain from research through testing to outcome is demonstrated; the name of a mission does not prove field impact. Shared research can travel between sectors: improved spectroscopy can help health and industrial quality control, but each receiving regulator and implementing institution must test its own performance and safety requirements.

### Original Mains 5 — 20 marks / 250 words

**Question:** Critically assess ANRF's ambition to mobilise non-government finance while expanding equitable scientific capacity. Answer in 250 words.

**Model answer:** ANRF's statutory mandate to seed and promote research has greater scope than the former SERB's grant identity; section 27 of the ANRF Act repealed SERB's governing law. ### Original Mains 6 — 20 marks / 250 words

**Question:** Evaluate institutional claims about national STI policy, research calls and translation using a status-and-evidence ladder. Answer in 250 words.

**Model answer:** Formal status precedes implementation claims. The DST policy stack reviewed in 2026 identified STI Policy 2013 as the last clearly adopted national policy, while STIP 2020 had been drafted and consulted upon without a verified formal adoption notification. Vigyan Dhara is a consolidated DST umbrella scheme, not evidence that the draft policy automatically became law; Scientific Social Responsibility concerns knowledge-sharing rather than a research regulator. On the statutory side, ANRF was established by its 2023 Act and commenced on 5 February 2024. Its 2024 Governing Board meeting and first PMECRG/MAHA-EV initiatives, followed by portal-listed 2026 calls, provide progressively stronger evidence of governance and programme operation. Yet a call, approved proposal and grant payment are different rungs; a prototype, patent filing, independent validation and deployed social benefit require further proof. CSIR's laboratories may produce research, but a laboratory's research output does not itself signify market adoption or regulatory approval. PRIP provides a cautionary example: pharmaceutical-MedTech research support is administered by the Department of Pharmaceuticals, not by DST or ANRF simply because it advances science. India's review should name legal parent, programme owner, date, funds actually received, participants and evidence of outcomes. This hierarchy allows policymakers to value active programmes while resisting inflated claims that projected finance, policy drafts or early projects have already delivered mature technological sovereignty. Equity matters at each rung: a technically sound prototype may fail to serve smaller towns if maintenance, affordability or local skill is absent. Impact measurement should distinguish intended users from beneficiaries actually reached.
