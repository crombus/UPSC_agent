### Routed 2018 Prelims GS-I — Indian GM mustard genes and properties

**Independent concept solution:** DMH-11 uses a transgenic barnase–barstar system in hybrid seed production: barnase generates male sterility in one parent and barstar restores fertility; the bar gene confers herbicide tolerance as a marker/trait and is not an insecticidal cry gene. A conventional cross between parental lines can also make a hybrid, but the inserted genetic construct makes this particular hybrid transgenic. The GEAC environmental-release decision for seed production and testing under conditions is distinct from unqualified commercial cultivation. Since the locally routed original objective key is unavailable, this solves the biological demand without supplying a purported official letter.

### Routed 2019 Prelims GS-I — biotechnology and genetic modification

**Independent concept solution:** Conventional crossing mixes parental alleles; transgenesis deliberately inserts a construct; targeted editing uses a guide RNA and nuclease to make a break, after which repair determines whether SDN-1 indels, template-based SDN-2 defined substitutions or SDN-3 substantial insertions arise. The no-exogenous-DNA SDN-1/2 regulatory distinction applies subject to India's specified 2022 conditions; all editing is not exempt. A regulatory conclusion requires knowing the *final product*, retained inserted sequence and applicable review, not just hearing “CRISPR.” No historic objective answer option inferred.

### Routed 2019 GS-III — genetic engineering and agriculture

**Independent model:** Genetic engineering can introduce traits that address defined constraints, but benefit depends on crop, mechanism and locality. Bt cotton's bacterial cry proteins act against susceptible insect pests, not weeds; local resistance management matters. DMH-11's barnase–barstar transgenic fertility-restoration system concerns hybrid seed production and has a separate conditional environmental-release and judicial history. CRISPR can produce SDN-1 indels or SDN-2 template-directed edits without a retained foreign sequence; it does not automatically create a transgenic organism. India's RCGM under DBT reviews research-stage work whereas GEAC under MoEFCC appraises environmental release; FSSAI has a distinct food-safety remit. Field efficacy, non-target impacts, seed pricing, farmer choice and access to alternatives must inform any rollout. Neither a laboratory trait nor a policy press release demonstrates realised farmer income.

### Independently solved routed 2018 GS-I Q64 — identifying CRISPR-Cas9

CRISPR-Cas9 names a programmable guide-RNA/nuclease system for editing a selected DNA site; Belle II is a particle-physics experiment and blockchain a distributed-record technology. Similar news prominence does not make these three equivalent biological interventions. The edit outcome follows repair, and foreign DNA insertion is not mandatory. Official objective option unavailable.

### Independently solved routed 2019 GS-I Q93 — artificial chromosomes

An artificial chromosome is an engineered DNA carrier containing chromosome-like functional elements for maintenance and study of substantial genetic material. It is distinct from a microsatellite marker or a conventional short plasmid insert; using a carrier does not demonstrate successful therapy. The routed question is described neutrally; original options and official key are not supplied.

### Independently solved routed 2019 GS-I Q96 — RNA interference

RNA interference uses small RNA-guided complexes to suppress or degrade a complementary messenger RNA, lowering gene expression. Research applications include crop traits and medically relevant targets. Unlike Cas9 genome editing, it need not cut the DNA sequence. Dose, target specificity and delivery govern usefulness; no inferred option letter.

### Independently solved routed 2019 GS-I Q99 — Cas9 protein

Cas9 is a nuclease directed by a guide RNA to cut selected DNA. The target can then be repaired by NHEJ or template-directed pathways, producing distinct outcomes. Cas9 is not itself a whole gene, a vaccine or the replacement template. An official option letter cannot be reconstructed from this neutral demand.

### Independently solved routed 2020 GS-I Q37 — pronuclear transfer

Pronuclear transfer moves intending parents' nuclear pronuclei into a donor zygote containing healthier mitochondria; it addresses risk from selected mitochondrial disorders, not all nuclear DNA diseases. Because the resulting mitochondrial contribution can be inherited, ethical and regulatory questions differ from ordinary somatic gene therapy. The original official objective key is unavailable locally.

### Independently solved routed 2020 GS-I Q44 — embryo modification and stem cells

Editing an embryo can potentially affect future germline inheritance; transplanting or editing tissue-restricted somatic stem cells primarily affects treated cells. These interventions differ in developmental stage, inheritance and ethics. A claim about existing human clinical approval needs its own verified evidence; no approval is inferred from question routing and no option letter is asserted.

### Independently solved routed 2021 GS-I Q66 — mitochondrial disease and replacement

Mitochondria contain their own DNA and are typically inherited maternally. Replacing a disease-associated mitochondrial cytoplasm while retaining parental nuclear material is conceptually different from rewriting the entire genome. Pronuclear transfer entails ethical and legal considerations, and a concept explanation is not an approval statement. Official historical key unavailable.

### Independently solved routed 2021 GS-I Q67 — Bollgard technology

Bollgard cotton uses a Bt-derived cry gene for insect resistance; Bollgard-II stacks two cry genes to broaden or prolong pest control. Stacking can slow resistance but does not eliminate refuge and monitoring needs. Bt insecticidal action must not be confused with herbicide tolerance. The original question's official answer option is not reconstructed.

### Independently solved routed 2023 GS-I Q70 — microsatellite DNA

Microsatellites are short tandem DNA repeats whose variable lengths can act as markers for genetic diversity, relatedness and population studies. A marker pattern is not by itself proof that the repeat causes a studied trait. It is neither an artificial chromosome nor a Cas9 enzyme. No unavailable official objective option inferred.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 marks | Answer in 150 words

**Explain** the CRISPR-Cas9 mechanism and its three repair-outcome categories.

**Model:** A designed guide RNA directs Cas9 to a compatible genomic target, where the nuclease makes a double-strand break. Cellular repair, not the word CRISPR alone, determines the product. With no supplied repair template, non-homologous end joining may produce small insertion/deletion mutations, typically classified as SDN-1. A short homologous template may guide a precise small alteration, the SDN-2 route. A substantial insertion at a chosen location is SDN-3; the inserted sequence can be cisgenic or transgenic. This distinction matters in India: the 2022 treatment exempts specified SDN-1/SDN-2 genome-edited plants without exogenous DNA from Rules 7–11 of the 1989 Rules, not all edited organisms from every safety check. Check the final sequence, off-target possibilities and trait performance rather than claiming a targeted cut guarantees no unintended consequences. Conventional hybrid crossing and introduction of a foreign transgene are separate interventions.

The assessment depends on the resulting edit and retained material: the nuclease technique by itself does not identify a crop as transgenic or exempt.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 marks | Answer in 150 words

**Examine** why Bt cotton and DMH-11 cannot be treated as equivalent approvals or equivalent traits.

**Model:** Bt cotton expresses bacterial cry proteins that affect susceptible insect pests; it is not herbicide tolerance simply because it is a GM crop. The reviewed PIB statement of August 2024 described it as the only GM crop approved for commercial cultivation in India. DMH-11 mustard uses transgenic barnase to create male sterility and barstar to restore fertility in hybrid breeding; its bar marker has a different herbicide-tolerance role. GEAC's 2022 recommendation and the government's 2023 statement concerned conditional environmental release for seed production and testing before commercial release, not a Bt-like cultivation status. The July 2024 Supreme Court outcome was split and cannot be cited as unanimous final authorisation. Each claim must specify crop, biological function, release stage and document date; both environmental risk and farmer performance require crop-specific evidence. A hybrid is not automatically transgenic, although DMH-11 is both.

The legal chronology should be stated with source dates; later scrutiny or a court split must not be rewritten as fresh authorisation.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 marks | Answer in 250 words

**Discuss** India's staged GMO biosafety architecture and the differentiated treatment of certain genome-edited plants.

**Model:** Biosafety governance follows the intervention and proposed exposure. Under the 1989 Rules, RDAC supplies advice, IBSC provides institutional oversight, RCGM under DBT handles research-stage/contained work, and GEAC under MoEFCC appraises large-scale use and environmental release. Other committees perform sectoral and state/district monitoring; FSSAI's food-safety remit should not be confused with GEAC's environmental task. A lab protocol, field-trial permission, conditional environmental release, food approval and commercial cultivation are separate rungs. India differentiated genome editing through the MoEFCC order of 30 March 2022 and DBT safety guidelines of 17 May 2022: specified SDN-1/2 plants without exogenous DNA received exemption from Rules 7–11, not an all-purpose safety waiver. SDN-1 involves small NHEJ indels, SDN-2 a short repair-template-directed change; a substantial SDN-3 insertion is different even if inserted DNA comes from a related species. Evaluate off-target alterations, gene flow, non-target organisms, herbicide-use incentives and pest resistance where relevant. Bt cotton demonstrates commercial adoption of a cry-protein insect-resistance trait; DMH-11's conditional release and divided judicial review show why approval-stage wording matters. Transparent trait-specific data, public consultation, independent assessment and post-release monitoring can strengthen trust without conflating hazard with benefit. The governing test is what was changed, where it will be used, and which specific legal clearance has actually been documented.

An IBSC’s contained-lab oversight is not a national commercial decision; likewise environmental release is not a substitute for food-safety or seed-quality evaluation. The Cartagena Protocol informs transboundary LMO biosafety, while Nagoya addresses access and benefit-sharing, not the same authorisation step.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 marks | Answer in 250 words

**Analyse** opportunities and limitations of genome-edited Indian rice without confusing a variety announcement with proved widespread benefit.

**Model:** Guide-RNA-directed Cas9 editing can alter a crop's existing genome at a selected site. NHEJ produces SDN-1 indels; a short template enables defined SDN-2 changes. If no exogenous DNA remains and the stipulated classification is met, India's 2022 differentiated framework can apply. ICAR's April 2025 announcement of genome-edited rice varieties, including DRR Rice 100 (Kamla), demonstrates a development milestone, not a measured national-income gain or a transgenic commercial-cultivation approval. A shorter growth period or more grains per panicle is a trait claim to test under locally replicated conditions; yield, water use, grain quality, pest response and farmer prices depend on agroecology and management. Selection of an edit also does not itself settle unintended sequence changes, ecological impacts or food quality. RCGM research oversight and GEAC environmental release roles must be kept separate from seed certification, extension and food-safety issues. Compare edited lines with locally adapted conventional varieties using transparent field controls and suitable independent review. Farmer choice, affordable quality seed and regional fit determine realised benefit. An answer should recognise the promise of precise breeding while declining both blanket deregulation and the claim that all editing inserts foreign DNA. The evidence ladder runs from genotype and trials through applicable permissions to observed adoption and outcomes.

Compare the edited variety with Samba Mahsuri and other locally relevant checks across water stress and pest pressure rather than attributing all yield change to one mutation. Dissemination also depends on validated seed multiplication and informed extension advice. This separates demonstration of a genotype from successful production across diverse Indian agro-climatic zones and farmer conditions.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 marks | Answer in 250 words

**Critically evaluate** DMH-11's technology, regulatory status and judicial controversy in an Indian oilseed-policy response.

**Model:** DMH-11 is a transgenic mustard hybrid: barnase induces male sterility in one parent, barstar restores fertility in the hybrid, and the bar gene has a separate herbicide-tolerance function. The technique assists hybrid-seed production; whether a variety improves oilseed supply depends on comparative agronomic results, farmer uptake and ecological impacts. GEAC's 147th meeting of October 2022 considered conditional environmental release. PIB's February 2023 account described release for seed production/testing subject to conditions before commercial release. Neither statement should be silently upgraded to unrestricted commercial cultivation. The Supreme Court delivered a split verdict in July 2024; the disagreement prevents citing a single uncontested judicial endorsement. Relevant biosafety questions include pollen-mediated gene flow, non-target ecology, herbicide-use implications, long-term monitoring and the adequacy of independent evidence; claims must be proportional to trait and exposure. Set these against the potential to improve breeding options and domestic edible-oil productivity without assuming every mustard hybrid is transgenic. Institutional transparency and consultation improve public trust; GEAC's environmental role differs from RCGM's research role and FSSAI's food-safety remit. A reasoned path demands crop-specific trial evidence, precise status reporting, enforceable conditions and farmer choice. Oilseed-security objectives neither erase biosafety duties nor prove from a laboratory mechanism alone that the policy will succeed.

A strong recommendation therefore records the exact status of the conditional GEAC step, whether the field evidence is independently reproducible, and how monitoring conditions would be enforced. An oil-import reduction goal cannot specify the final net benefit without adoption and yield data. Record crop-specific endpoints and control plots transparently, so a decision can be revisited if new field evidence changes the estimated benefit or environmental risk.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 marks | Answer in 250 words

**Assess** whether precision editing makes risk assessment of crops unnecessary.

**Model:** Precision names the ability to select a target site, not a universal guarantee that the final organism or its deployment is risk-free. Cas9 and a guide RNA can produce targeted breaks, but NHEJ and template-guided repair generate distinct SDN-1 and SDN-2 outcomes; SDN-3 may insert substantial DNA, including cisgenic material. Sequence analysis can examine intended and unintended changes, yet environmental effects depend on trait, species, receiving ecosystem and farming practice. A pest-resistance crop can create selection pressure and require resistance management; a herbicide-tolerant crop can alter spraying incentives. Conversely, a specific edit without exogenous DNA may warrant a proportionate route rather than being treated identically to an unrelated transgenic insertion. India's 2022 exemption for qualifying SDN-1/2 plants from particular 1989 Rules provisions illustrates differentiation, not a general finding of no risk. RCGM, GEAC and relevant food authorities have distinct mandates at different evidence stages. Compare locally replicated field outcomes, gene-flow pathways, non-target effects, agronomic gains and farmer costs. Revisit decisions through post-release monitoring where needed. The policy question is proportionate, transparent, evidence-based oversight: neither “all edits are GM” nor “a precise edit needs no review” describes the scientific or Indian regulatory distinction.

Use tiered assessment proportionate to the edit, exposure and trait, with independent testing able to detect discrepancies before large-scale dissemination. Farmer-facing communication should distinguish molecular precision from the much wider question of agronomic and ecological predictability. The decision should specify monitoring responsibilities, remedial steps and public disclosure if unexpected gene flow or agronomic harm appears.
