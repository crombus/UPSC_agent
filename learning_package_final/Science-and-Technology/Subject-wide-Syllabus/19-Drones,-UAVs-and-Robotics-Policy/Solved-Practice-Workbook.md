## PYQS AND ANSWER PRACTICE

### Audited routed PYQs and independent solutions

### PYQ — 2020 Prelims — civilian applications

**Provenance/demand:** Verified routed demand concerns drone use in agriculture, volcanic observation and wildlife research; original official objective option mapping is not supplied by the paired ledger.

**Independent model:** A drone can carry agricultural imaging or spraying payloads, survey volcanic regions from a safer distance and observe wildlife when mission, weather and law permit. The aircraft is only part of a UAS, which includes command link and operator/control station. Technical ability to photograph a region does not guarantee payload range, battery endurance or an authorised airspace corridor. Wildlife work raises disturbance and privacy concerns; agricultural spraying needs compatible payload and trained operators. An objective stem testing whether these applications are possible must not be answered by inventing actual nationwide deployments or an unmapped official option letter.

### PYQ — 2025 Prelims — UAV capabilities

**Provenance/demand:** Audited objective demand distinguishes UAV types and capabilities; no locally mapped official answer letter is asserted.

**Independent model:** A multirotor can hover for local inspection or farm spraying but consumes power to sustain hover. A fixed-wing aircraft can survey a larger route more efficiently but normally requires forward airspeed and cannot simply hover. A hybrid vertical-take-off/landing design trades added mechanical and control complexity for vertical launch and efficient cruise. Neither platform automatically carries an unlimited payload or receives BVLOS authorisation. Rotor count, sensor selection, weather, energy budget and command-link resilience affect realised capability. Compare the particular claims in the original objective statements rather than transferring one aircraft type’s strengths to every UAV.

### PYQ — 2023 GS-III border threats / 2026 Prelims swarm distinctions

**Provenance/demand:** Paired routes include a descriptive border-UAV threat and a later objective demand on drone-swarm coordination; separate dates and formats, and do not claim an unmapped objective key.

**Independent model:** Border drones create low-altitude detection, illicit-delivery and surveillance challenges. A loitering munition is a one-way attack weapon, not merely a civilian drone with a camera. An effective Indian counter-UAS sequence combines lawful detection by RF, radar or optical sensors, identification, command and civilian-airspace coordination, then proportionate mitigation such as jamming, capture or other authorised measures; jamming can itself disrupt friendly systems. A swarm uses communication and coordination among several platforms, but a demonstration of cooperative flight does not prove autonomous target selection, combat readiness or a procurement order. Link spoofing, communications loss and sensor errors can break coordinated behaviour. Separate civil DGCA certification and AAI airspace operations from defence acquisition and security-agency counter-drone responsibilities. Measure operational effectiveness and collateral risk, not just announced technology.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Distinguish UAV, UAS, RPAS and drone, and explain why unmanned does not mean autonomous. Answer in about 150 words.

**Independent model answer:** A UAV is the aircraft itself; an unmanned aircraft system adds the command-and-control link, remote station and associated equipment. “Drone” is a colloquial umbrella term, whereas RPAS specifically denotes a remotely piloted system with a remote pilot in command. An aircraft can lack an onboard crew but still be steered continuously by a trained human from the ground. Another system may execute a fixed programmed route, and a more autonomous system can sense a changing environment and choose actions. These are distinct degrees of decision-making. For an Indian crop-spraying service, remote-pilot competence, aircraft identification, payload approval and airspace checking remain relevant even if the device has automatic stabilisation. Digital Sky supports registration and permission workflows; it is not a substitute for AAI air-traffic services. Therefore “unmanned” establishes pilot location, not the absence of human command, accountability or civil-aviation compliance.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Explain the sensing-control-actuation loop and its application to stable drone flight. Answer in about 150 words.

**Independent model answer:** A drone’s closed control loop connects sensing, decision, physical actuation and feedback. Inertial and position sensors estimate attitude and location; a flight controller compares the measured state with the desired path. It then adjusts rotor motor speeds through actuators, causing a corrective force, and reads new measurements to repeat the cycle. For an Indian survey mission, wind gusts may displace a multirotor; repeated corrections help stabilise its camera payload. Accuracy depends on sensor calibration, reliable software, propulsion headroom and power supply. A compromised navigation input, delayed radio link or weak battery can defeat the loop despite a sophisticated airframe. Remote-pilot commands and onboard stabilisation can operate together; automatic stabilisation is not proof that the aircraft autonomously chooses missions. Safe use additionally requires attention to zone permissions and weather. A successful laboratory hover does not demonstrate BVLOS reliability or authorised routine delivery service.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Compare multirotor, fixed-wing and hybrid VTOL UAVs through mission and payload trade-offs. Answer in about 250 words.

**Independent model answer:** Airframe design determines the services a drone can perform, not its compliance status. Multirotors generate lift by rotor thrust and can take off vertically, hover and manoeuvre precisely. This suits Indian plot-level agricultural spraying, crop inspection and constrained urban infrastructure checks. Hover consumes energy continuously, however, so range and payload endurance are limited by batteries and weather.

Fixed-wing aircraft use wing lift during forward flight and can cover a larger survey corridor with comparatively efficient cruise; an inhabited-land mapping mission may exploit that endurance if launch and recovery conditions permit. Ordinary fixed-wing flight does not imply stationary hover, and a long-range mission may also face communication and BVLOS regulatory constraints. Hybrid VTOL platforms combine vertical launch with winged forward cruise, enabling remote-area surveying without a runway but adding control transitions, mass and maintenance demands.

Payload is the mission apparatus carried beyond the platform: a high-resolution camera, spray tank or sensor changes weight, power budget and data governance. Stronger motors alone cannot establish all-weather endurance or unrestricted operations. Compare route length, safe launch space, hover needs, payload, command links and local weather before selecting the design. Then apply the Indian Drone Rules, zone map, DGCA certification and remote-pilot provisions as relevant; capability does not waive AAI airspace responsibilities. An appropriate mission uses a tested vehicle–payload–operator system, not a claim that every flying camera performs every public-service task.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Examine VLOS, BVLOS, drone-swarm coordination and AI-enabled autonomy as distinct operating challenges. Answer in about 250 words.

**Independent model answer:** VLOS describes an operating condition: a remote pilot can directly maintain visual contact with the aircraft. BVLOS crosses that sight boundary and demands more robust communications, navigation, detect-and-avoid procedures and contingency handling. Indian BVLOS activity in the sources has proceeded under experimental or sandbox authorisations; an approved trial is not a general permission to operate nationwide. Neither term by itself tells us whether a drone is autonomous or remotely controlled.

A swarm is a multi-aircraft coordination arrangement. Members need timing, relative position, communications and conflict avoidance; radio jamming or spoofed location can disrupt the collective even if each platform flies stably alone. AI-enabled autonomy describes the decision layer that selects actions from sensed information. Fixed automation merely executes preset instructions; the greater decision latitude of autonomy raises questions about explainability, mission constraints, liability and meaningful human oversight. A human remotely commanding multiple platforms is not automatically an autonomous swarm.

For an Indian disaster-survey pilot, first match flight mode to authorised airspace, then verify sensor reliability, link redundancy, weather tolerance and data safeguards. DGCA regulates civil certification and training; AAI handles air-traffic services. An experimental coordination demonstration should be reported as such, never as proof of routine BVLOS approval or military acquisition. Building capability and obtaining permission are independent conditions for safe scale.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Discuss counter-UAS architecture and the safety, privacy, cybersecurity and liability challenges of dual-use drones. Answer in about 250 words.

**Independent model answer:** Unmanned aircraft offer disaster imagery and farm services but can also enable illicit payload delivery, surveillance and one-way attack. Counter-UAS is a security architecture, not simply DGCA pilot licensing. Radar, radio-frequency, acoustic and optical inputs detect a possible threat; identification must distinguish an authorised drone from a bird or friendly aircraft. A lawful response can include tracking, jamming, spoofing, capture or other proportionate mitigation, coordinated with air-traffic and security authorities. A jammer can interrupt friendly communications, so detection is not itself neutralisation and response must consider collateral effects.

Safety concerns include loss of command link, geofence failure, bad weather and rotor or battery malfunction. Cybersecurity matters because falsified position or hijacked control can defeat return-to-home behaviour. Persistent imaging raises privacy and retention concerns even where the flight itself is technically feasible. As software gains decision latitude, responsibility among manufacturer, deployer and operator needs clear allocation and meaningful human oversight. A loitering munition is a weapon within defence acquisition and operational rules, not a civil agricultural platform governed solely by an online permit.

India should combine DGCA civil certification, AAI airspace services, BCAS aviation-security functions and security-agency countermeasures with trained operators, incident logs and auditable authorisation. Demonstrated interception, announced prototypes, procurement contracts and deployed operational coverage are distinct evidence rungs. Protection should be evaluated against both successful threat reduction and civil-rights safeguards.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Critically evaluate India’s civil-drone ecosystem through the Drone Rules, Digital Sky, certification, institutions, civilian applications and the approval-to-deployment boundary. Answer in about 250 words.

**Independent model answer:** India’s civil-drone policy combines differentiated risk regulation with a developing industrial and service ecosystem. The Drone Rules, 2021 organise aircraft by all-up mass and use a colour-coded airspace map. Nano, micro, small, medium and large classes have different burdens; green flights have altitude conditions, yellow areas require the concerned traffic authority and red areas require Central Government permission. The 2023 amendment shows that a current legal answer must be checked against the amended Gazette, not reconstructed from a portal screenshot.

MoCA anchors rules and policy, DGCA handles certification and remote-pilot training, AAI manages airspace and air-traffic services, and BCAS covers aviation security. Digital Sky facilitates aircraft identification, registration and permission workflows, not direct traffic control. Manufacturing PLI and the distinction between restricted complete-drone imports and freer component imports address supply capability, but do not prove indigenous sensors or certified mission performance. SVAMITVA links drone-based inhabited-area survey to property cards; Namo Drone Didi linked selected women SHGs to agricultural rental services during its sourced FY 2023–24 to FY 2025–26 period, not an unverified permanent entitlement.

Wider benefits in farming, inspections and disaster assessment depend on payload, endurance, operator skill, safe airspace and privacy safeguards. A notified scheme, registered drone, approved experimental BVLOS flight and routinely deployed service are four distinct stages. Evaluate actual trained operators, verified flight permissions, useful mapped output and incident rates before declaring a national drone capability; defence procurement should be separately sourced.
