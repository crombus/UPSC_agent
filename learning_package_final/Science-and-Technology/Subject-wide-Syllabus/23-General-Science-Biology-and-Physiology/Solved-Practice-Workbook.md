### Routed descriptive PYQ — 2022 GS-III Q6, natural cellulose decomposition (10 marks / 150 words)

**Model answer:** Cellulose is a structural polysaccharide in plant cell walls. Fallen leaves and other litter do not break down solely through sunlight or physical fragmentation: fungi and bacteria secrete cellulase enzymes that hydrolyse cellulose into smaller sugars. Microbes use these sugars for growth and respiration, transferring carbon to their biomass, soil food webs and, under aerobic conditions, ultimately carbon dioxide; anaerobic conditions may produce different products including methane. Detritivores can shred litter and expand its surface area for microbial attack. Moisture, oxygen, temperature and the decomposer community shape the rate, while lignin-rich tissue may protect cellulose from enzymes. This natural recycling helps soil nutrient turnover and offers a conceptual basis for composting and biomass conversion. Yet a decomposition mechanism alone does not establish a commercially efficient biofuel process: pretreatment, controlled microbes, collection and energy balance impose additional constraints. Managing Indian crop residues therefore requires biology and context-specific environmental safeguards.

### Six original Mains questions and model answers

### Original Mains 1 — 10 marks / 150 words

**Question:** Distinguish cell transport, respiration and chloroplast function through a crop-stress example. Answer in 150 words.

**Model answer:** A cell's membrane selects exchanges; water moves by osmosis along a water-potential gradient, while active transport uses energy to move selected solutes against an electrochemical gradient. In a drought-stressed crop, closing stomata reduces water loss but can also restrict carbon dioxide uptake. Chloroplasts in photosynthetic tissues use light to store energy in organic molecules; mitochondria release usable ATP through respiration in both plant and animal cells. Plants therefore respire day and night, rather than replacing respiration with photosynthesis in sunlight. Xylem transports water and minerals upward, aided by transpiration pull; phloem distributes sugars from source tissues. A crop's productivity cannot be inferred solely from chlorophyll content: moisture, nutrient supply, temperature and photosynthetic pathway matter. Research on stress tolerance must be tested in actual Indian growing conditions. Mechanism connects cell transport to water use and yield, but a measured laboratory trait does not guarantee field performance.

### Original Mains 2 — 10 marks / 150 words

**Question:** Explain how gene regulation and cell division jointly enable development without genetic determinism. Answer in 150 words.

**Model answer:** DNA stores hereditary information; transcription forms RNA, and translation at ribosomes assembles proteins. Regulatory microRNAs can modify expression after transcription, showing why RNA is more than a messenger. Most human somatic cells share the same genome but differ in proteins produced and hence specialised functions. Mitosis generates similar daughter cells for growth and repair; meiosis reduces chromosome number and, with recombination, produces varied gametes. Mutations add variation, yet a change in a DNA sequence need not alter the encoded amino acid or cause disease. India's research institutes such as CSIR-CCMB and CSIR-IGIB use molecular analysis in genetics and health, but identifying an association does not establish a single-gene cause of a complex outcome. Nutritional and environmental exposures also influence phenotype. The 2024 Nobel on microRNA highlighted post-transcriptional regulation; it did not imply that every discovered regulatory RNA is a licensed therapy.

### Original Mains 3 — 15 marks / 250 words

**Question:** Analyse digestion, circulation, respiration and excretion as connected human homeostatic processes. Answer in 250 words.

**Model answer:** Food supplies nutrients but cannot sustain cells without processing and delivery. Salivary enzymes begin carbohydrate breakdown; stomach pepsin works in acidic conditions, while bile emulsifies lipids and pancreatic enzymes support breakdown in the intestine. The small intestine is the principal site of nutrient absorption, with different molecules entering circulation through appropriate pathways. In the lungs, alveoli exchange gases; haemoglobin in red blood cells carries oxygen through the circulation to tissues. Cellular respiration in mitochondria uses fuel and oxygen to release ATP: breathing air into lungs is not itself intracellular respiration. The four-chambered heart separates pulmonary and systemic circuits; the pulmonary artery carries relatively deoxygenated blood away from the heart, demonstrating that artery describes direction rather than oxygen content. Carbon monoxide can impair oxygen delivery through strong haemoglobin binding even when air contains oxygen. Kidneys complete the balance by filtering plasma and selectively reabsorbing and secreting substances through nephrons; dialysis can replace part of waste/fluid clearance but not every kidney function. Endocrine feedback, including insulin and glucagon, coordinates nutrient handling across these organs. In India, anaemia screening must consider iron intake, infection and access to care rather than assume every breathlessness complaint is a lung disease. A failure of any link—digestion, transport, gas exchange or excretion—affects whole-body function. Integrated assessment must use clinical measurements and address causes, not treat organ labels as isolated explanations. A symptom such as fatigue could reflect impaired nutrient absorption, low haemoglobin, hypoxia or metabolic disturbance; laboratory and clinical evidence must discriminate among these causes before attributing it to one organ.

### Original Mains 4 — 15 marks / 250 words

**Question:** Examine how nervous, endocrine, innate and adaptive responses differ while jointly protecting homeostasis. Answer in 250 words.

**Model answer:** A rapid withdrawal from a hot surface follows a reflex arc: a sensory neuron, spinal processing and a motor neuron can produce movement before full conscious perception. Endocrine signals act differently. For example, pancreatic insulin and glucagon circulate in blood and help keep glucose within a functional range through feedback, while nervous inputs coordinate fast behavioural responses. Both systems interact rather than divide physiology into exclusive silos. Immune defence adds another timing axis. Skin, mucus, phagocytes and complement provide prompt innate protection without the same antigen-specific memory as adaptive immunity. Antigen-presenting cells can stimulate B and T lymphocytes; B-lineage plasma cells make antibodies and T cells coordinate or kill infected cells. Memory cells enable a faster specific response upon later exposure. An antigen can be recognised without necessarily causing harmful inflammation: tolerance normally restrains responses to self, with failure contributing to autoimmune disease. In India, vaccination programmes harness immune memory, but vaccine-platform design, product approval and cold-chain implementation are separate applied issues. ICMR supports biomedical research; NCDC/IDSP with states monitor outbreaks, and CDSCO regulates product approval. No antibody titre alone represents the entire immune state; cellular responses, exposure and host health affect outcome. Effective public health combines biological understanding, field surveillance and regulation while preserving the distinct mechanism and timing of each arm of homeostasis. A self-antigen can bind an immune receptor without automatically provoking disease; failures of tolerance require a different response from infection. Population coverage figures likewise cannot replace individual assessment of protection.

### Original Mains 5 — 20 marks / 250 words

**Question:** Analyse infections, antimicrobial resistance and biofilms through a One Health framework for India. Answer in 250 words.

**Model answer:** Pathogen identity determines an intervention. Dengue is viral and Aedes-transmitted, whereas malaria is caused by Plasmodium and spread by female Anopheles; antibiotics directed against bacterial targets do not cure either parasite or virus automatically. Bacterial infections may require appropriate antibiotics, but intensive or inappropriate exposure selects resistant strains in people, livestock and environmental reservoirs. It is microbial populations—not the patient's body—that become resistant. Biofilms on implants or food-processing surfaces add a second challenge: attached microbial communities and their matrices may impede antimicrobial penetration and clearance. Sanitation, device care and diagnostics complement treatment. Not all microbes are pathogens: gut microorganisms can aid nutrition and colonisation resistance, and broadly disrupting them can have costs. India's National Action Plan on AMR and prescription controls address use, while One Health calls for joined human, animal and environmental surveillance. ICMR provides biomedical research, but operational disease monitoring runs through NCDC/IDSP and states; a promising culture finding is not a national reduction in resistant infection. WHO's validation of India's trachoma elimination as a public-health problem on 8 October 2024 demonstrates a threshold achievement, not eradication of every possible infection or an end to hygiene needs. Prevention must respond to each transmission route and monitor emerging resistance. Progress should be measured through verified burden, appropriate prescribing and safe field practice rather than extrapolating a laboratory sensitivity result to every patient. Managing resistance also means considering residue-bearing wastewater and veterinary use; narrow hospital prescribing rules alone will not control environmental selection. Microbial species and patient vulnerability must inform diagnostic and isolation decisions.

### Original Mains 6 — 20 marks / 250 words

**Question:** Discuss crop water adaptation, classification and cellulose decomposition as biological foundations for Indian food and residue policy. Answer in 250 words.

**Model answer:** Plant productivity depends on water use, carbon fixation and nutrient transport. A waxy cuticle or reduced leaves can limit evaporation in dry conditions; stomatal closure also saves water, but restricts carbon-dioxide entry and may lower photosynthetic rate. C4 and CAM pathways can improve performance under particular heat or drought regimes, but neither guarantees higher yield in every climate. Xylem moves water and minerals while phloem redistributes sugars; plants respire continuously even when photosynthesis ceases at night. Botanical classification prevents food-use shortcuts: groundnut, soybean and horse gram are legumes in Fabaceae, despite differing uses and habits. Cassava is a shrub, ginger a rhizomatous herb and Malabar spinach a climbing vine; papaya is a large herbaceous plant rather than a conventional woody tree. After harvest, fungal and bacterial cellulases digest cellulose in residues, helping carbon and nutrient recycling. Lignin content, moisture and oxygen constrain decay; open-field burning, composting and industrial biomass conversion have different environmental and economic outcomes. Indian crop policy therefore needs site-specific agronomy and residue-handling capacity, not a generic claim that every drought-adapted species or decomposer works equally well. Field trials, soil measurements and safe handling connect these mechanisms to food-security benefits. Plant physiology explains the possible responses; classification and evidence prevent misleading applications. Where residue collection is expensive, a technically feasible microbial decomposition process may still be uneconomic; regional equipment, farmer incentives, air quality and soil-carbon effects belong in the same policy comparison. Nor should field yield from one season be taken as proof of climate resilience across regions.
