## PYQS AND ANSWER PRACTICE

### Verified routed PYQs — independent models, no invented objective keys

### PYQ — 2022/2025 Prelims — monazite, thorium and REE properties

**Provenance/demand:** Paired audited objective demands cover monazite, thorium, rare-earth identity and uses. Original official option-to-key mapping unavailable locally.

**Independent model:** Monazite-bearing beach sands contain rare-earth elements and thorium; Indian processing has an institutional link to IREL under the Department of Atomic Energy. Rare earths comprise fifteen lanthanides, scandium and yttrium, not every mineral assessed as critical. India’s light-REE-rich monazite does not by itself provide dysprosium and terbium inputs or finished high-temperature magnets. Mineral occurrence must not be equated with an economically recoverable reserve, refined oxide, metal alloy or assembled motor. An objective question about monazite or rare-earth properties should therefore test the specific mineral, institutional and process statements at the question date, without assigning an official answer letter from an unmapped ledger.

### PYQ — 2023/2025 Prelims — carbon fibre and battery minerals

**Provenance/demand:** Audited objective routes concern carbon-fibre application/recycling and distinctions among lithium, cobalt, nickel and graphite in battery cells; no official choice letter is inferred.

**Independent model:** Carbon-fibre composites are lightweight, strong engineered materials useful in aircraft and automobiles. Fibres are usually embedded in a polymer matrix; reclaiming material of comparable performance can be difficult, so application does not prove easy recyclability. In batteries, lithium participates in common lithium-ion chemistries; graphite commonly serves as anode; nickel and cobalt occur in NMC/NCA cathodes but are not required by LFP cathodes. Sodium-ion avoids lithium. These statements describe different electrode and chemistry roles, not a blanket rule that every EV battery needs each listed mineral. Check the original statement wording before forming an objective combination; this is an independent concept model, not an official keyed answer.

### PYQ — 2025/2026 Prelims — MSP, MMDR and critical-mineral mission

**Provenance/demand:** Audited objective demand covers plurilateral coordination, MMDR statutory distinctions and the national mission; no unmapped official option letter is asserted.

**Independent model:** The Mineral Security Partnership mobilises investment and coordination across critical-mineral chains, not an automatic mining title or an Indian statutory regulator. The 2023 Ministry of Mines expert report identified 30 minerals for India; this policy list differs from the MMDR Part D auction list and Seventh Schedule exploration-licence list. MMDR section 11D gives central auction of Part D concessions while the State grants them; section 10BA creates a competitively allocated exploration licence, not a mining lease. The NCMM was approved on 29 January 2025 with a total planned ₹34,300 crore for FY 2024–25 to FY 2030–31, including ₹16,300 crore government expenditure and ₹18,000 crore expected PSU/stakeholder investment. Approval is not actual spending or installed refining capacity. The statutory Part D count changed from 24 to 25 on 27 January 2026 with coking coal; date matters for later questions.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Distinguish advanced, composite and smart materials through the structure–property–processing relationship. Answer in about 150 words.

**Independent model answer:** Material performance is produced by composition, internal structure and processing, not by a prestigious label. An advanced material is engineered for demanding properties or functions; a composite combines distinct constituents, such as carbon fibres reinforcing a polymer matrix. The fibre can raise specific strength and stiffness for aircraft or automobile structures, while the matrix distributes load and fixes shape. A smart material changes behaviour in response to a stimulus; an application must identify which response and operating conditions are useful. Processing routes determine defects, fibre orientation, costs and recoverability. For an Indian vehicle component, low mass might save operational energy but expensive fabrication and difficult separation of polymer-bonded fibres complicate lifecycle benefits. Likewise a strong laboratory specimen is not automatically a safe, affordable and recyclable production component. The defensible comparison is structure → measured property → scalable process → field performance → disposal, not “newer means better” or “all composites are rare-earth materials.”

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Define rare earth elements and distinguish light and heavy REEs from the wider critical-minerals category. Answer in about 150 words.

**Independent model answer:** Rare-earth elements are a defined group of seventeen: the fifteen lanthanides plus scandium and yttrium. Light REEs such as lanthanum, cerium, praseodymium and neodymium differ in supply and functions from heavier elements including dysprosium and terbium, which help high-temperature permanent magnets. A critical mineral, in contrast, is a policy assessment of economic or strategic importance together with supply vulnerability; it is neither a fixed chemical group nor synonymous with REEs. Lithium can be critical but is not a rare earth. India’s monazite-bearing beach sands are comparatively light-REE-rich and contain thorium; their geological presence does not settle heavy-REE access, separation, alloy making or magnet manufacture. Nor should an identified resource be described as an economically recoverable reserve without evidence. The Ministry of Mines’ 2023 assessment of thirty critical minerals is a dated expert list, not the count of the elements in the REE group or the statutory Part D list.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Explain permanent magnets and battery-mineral uses while preserving chemistry, electrode and component-manufacturing boundaries. Answer in about 250 words.

**Independent model answer:** Materials security starts by specifying the component and its operating conditions. Rare-earth permanent magnets use neodymium/praseodymium-related inputs, while dysprosium or terbium can help maintain magnetic performance at elevated temperatures. India’s light-REE-rich monazite therefore cannot by itself secure all high-temperature motor magnets. Mining ore is followed by chemical separation, oxides, metals, alloys and magnet fabrication; a domestic deposit with imported final magnets still indicates downstream dependence. Not every traction motor necessarily uses the same magnet design.

Battery mineral bills depend on cell chemistry and electrode. NMC/NCA cathodes require nickel and cobalt, but LFP cathodes use lithium iron phosphate and avoid those two metals. Lithium remains in LFP; sodium-ion substitutes sodium for lithium and may face lower energy density. Graphite is a common lithium-ion anode input, not a rare-earth cathode material. Assigning cobalt, nickel and dysprosium to every EV battery confuses both chemical families and motor components.

For Indian bus or electronics manufacturing, resilience requires qualified separated and refined inputs, component production, standards, reliable offtake and recycling. Strategic planning should map supply by material grade and use case, not simply list ore tonnages or one universal set of EV minerals. New cell chemistries can substitute some inputs, but tested cost, safety and performance determine practical adoption. A mine licence is not a magnet factory, and a laboratory cell is not an operational vehicle pack.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Examine the mining–beneficiation–refining–processing chain and distinguish resources, reserves, production and refining capability. Answer in about 250 words.

**Independent model answer:** A mineral value chain proceeds from geological survey and exploration through an economically viable mine, beneficiation of ore, separation/refining, metals and alloys, then component manufacture. GSI identifies geological potential, IBM addresses mining plans and scientific conservation, and concession and auction responsibilities must be specified under the MMDR law. Beneficiation increases ore grade but does not produce battery-grade salt or magnet-ready alloy. Similar rare-earth chemical properties make separation especially demanding.

Resources are identified occurrences. Reserves are an economically and legally extractable subset at prevailing technology and price; both differ from mined output. Even a producing mine may send concentrate abroad for refining. Conversely imported ore processed domestically may support industrial capacity despite limited domestic extraction. Therefore a figure for resources, a lease auction, a commissioned mine, a refinery and a final magnet need separate evidence and dates.

India’s 2023 MMDR amendment gave the Centre Part D auctions under section 11D while States grant concessions; section 10BA established an exploration licence for Seventh Schedule minerals, not an automatic right to mine. The January 2025 National Critical Mineral Mission covers mining and beneficiation as well as processing and recycling, but approved outlay does not prove a domestic supply stream. Build geological knowledge and downstream refining with environmental safeguards, local consent and credible mine closure. An adequate self-reliance test follows usable grade material through to real Indian components rather than stopping at an announced discovery.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Discuss a resilient critical-mineral strategy through diversification, overseas access, recycling, substitution and responsible mining. Answer in about 250 words.

**Independent model answer:** Critical minerals are economically important materials vulnerable to disrupted supply, not a permanent chemical class. An Indian strategy must address geological, midstream, manufacturing and geopolitical risks together. GSI exploration can improve domestic resource knowledge, followed by evidence of economically extractable reserves and responsible mine development. Concentrated separation, refining and magnet manufacture can still force imports after local ore extraction; processing capacity and qualified component factories therefore deserve equal priority.

KABIL, a NALCO–HCL–MECL venture under the Ministry of Mines, pursues overseas mineral access. Its October 2024 exploration commencement in Argentina is a dated early step, not proof of lithium deliveries. The Mineral Security Partnership coordinates investment and diversification; neither an MoU nor membership automatically yields binding offtake. Stockpiling can cushion shocks but must be replenished. Chemistry-aware substitution changes requirements: LFP avoids nickel and cobalt, sodium-ion avoids lithium, while different motor designs can alter rare-earth demand. However performance, scale, costs and safety must be demonstrated.

Urban mining of end-of-life batteries and electronics supplies secondary feedstock, complemented by Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022, yet recovery volume and purity constrain near-term replacement of primary materials. Mining and processing must manage tailings, water, land, community consent, rehabilitation and closure rather than externalise costs to producing regions. Track production, domestic refined output, component-grade yields, recycling recovery and supplier concentration separately. Resilience means multiple qualified routes with safeguards, not a single mine, trade agreement or optimistic resource statistic.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Critically evaluate India’s critical-mineral architecture through missions, institutions, law, deep-ocean exploration, routed PYQs and the volatile-status firewall. Answer in about 250 words.

**Independent model answer:** India has moved toward a full-chain critical-mineral approach, but institutional labels and legal lists must remain distinct. GSI surveys and explores; IBM oversees mining plans and conservation; IREL under DAE processes rare-earth-bearing monazite with its thorium overlap; KABIL seeks overseas assets. NIOT/MoES deep-ocean polymetallic-nodule exploration under the International Seabed Authority is not a State land concession or a producing mine.

The 2023 MMDR amendment inserted a Part D strategic list for Centre-led auctions under section 11D, while States grant concessions and receive statutory proceeds. Section 10BA introduced a competitively auctioned exploration licence for Seventh Schedule minerals, valid initially five years with possible two-year extension; reconnaissance and prospecting do not authorise mining. The 2023 expert assessment identified thirty critical minerals; Part D initially had twenty-four and, after the 27 January 2026 coking-coal entry, twenty-five; the Seventh Schedule list of twenty-nine is a third category. The National Critical Mineral Mission approved on 29 January 2025 covers FY 2024–25 to FY 2030–31 and a ₹34,300-crore planned total combining government expenditure and expected investment, not a verified disbursal figure.

Past routed objective questions on monazite, carbon-fibre recovery, battery inputs and partnership institutions test chemical group, material function, chain stage and legal date. India should track refined metals, magnet/cell manufacture and recovery rather than count exploration blocks as finished supply. Strategic expansion needs water, tailings, community and biodiversity safeguards. Avoid converting a survey, auction, MoU, proposed outlay or offshore research into proven reserves or delivered equipment.
