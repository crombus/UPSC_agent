### Independently solved routed 2018 Prelims GS-I Q84 — 3D printing

3D printing constructs a three-dimensional object additively from digital layers. It can support prototyping and selected biomedical, industrial or construction applications subject to material, process and quality constraints. Its feature scale is not necessarily 1–100 nm: additive manufacture and nanotechnology can intersect where a nanoscale filler or feature is verified, but neither term implies the other. ### Independently solved routed 2020 Prelims GS-I Q41 — carbon nanotubes

A carbon nanotube is a cylindrical nanostructure, different from flat graphene and closed-cage C60. Strength and electrical/thermal transport can support composite materials and sensitive sensor electrodes; functionalised tubes are also investigated as carriers in medicine. A conceivable application is not evidence that every tube preparation is clinically safe or marketed. Length, wall structure, surface treatment and dose change performance and exposure. No unavailable objective key is supplied.

### Independently solved routed 2022 Prelims GS-I Q98 — natural particles, cosmetics and safety

Nanoscale particles can arise naturally as well as be deliberately engineered. A nanoemulsion disperses very small droplets; other engineered particles may alter sunscreen or cosmetic formulation behaviour. Safety cannot be inferred from “natural,” “nano,” small mass or consumer availability: particle chemistry, aggregation, exposure route, dose, persistence and relevant testing govern a product-specific conclusion. ### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 marks | Answer in 150 words

**Explain** why a quantum dot and a nanoporous adsorbent exhibit distinct nanoscale advantages.

**Model:** At about 1–100 nm, a material can display a greater fraction of atoms at its surface and changed electron behaviour. A nanoporous adsorbent exposes accessible binding sites, potentially improving contaminant capture in water; success also depends on pore chemistry, flow, competing solutes and safe disposal. A semiconductor quantum dot instead uses size-dependent energy levels: reducing dot dimensions can widen its effective band gap and shift emitted light toward shorter wavelengths. Tunable emission supports imaging and display research, although brightness and toxicity depend on composition and coating. Surface effects and quantum confinement are not interchangeable explanations for every nanoscale observation. Both claims need measured size distribution and suitable comparison to the bulk material. A laboratory adsorption graph or emission spectrum alone establishes neither safe drinking-water supply nor clinical approval; practical utility requires characterisation, stability, exposure assessment and validated application performance.

The dry powder and dispersed liquid may have different measured sizes; document the medium and aggregation state before extrapolating from optical or adsorption measurements.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 marks | Answer in 150 words

**Distinguish** nanoparticle, nanotube, nanosheet and nanocomposite for a proposed biosensor.

**Model:** A nanoparticle has all three external dimensions in the nanoscale; a nanotube or nanofibre has two nanoscale transverse dimensions and may be much longer; a nanosheet has one nanoscale thickness with extended lateral area. A nanocomposite combines phases with at least one nanoscale constituent, so it describes a material architecture rather than a single required shape. In a biosensor, a carbon nanotube's conductive geometry may help transduce binding, graphene supplies a thin conductive surface, and a tailored nanoparticle can offer recognition or optical signal. Their useful properties must be tested against the actual analyte, not assumed from a shape name. Characterise primary size and aggregation in the relevant liquid, surface functionalisation, selectivity and reproducibility. Clinical use additionally requires biocompatibility, validated diagnostic accuracy and appropriate regulation. Equating all nano-objects to spherical particles obscures the operating mechanism and risk profile.

A claim about device sensitivity needs signal-to-noise, cross-reactivity, reproducibility and stability data against a relevant conventional sensor. Material category alone does not supply a diagnostic threshold.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 marks | Answer in 250 words

**Analyse** characterisation and toxicology requirements before deploying a nanoparticle in water treatment.

**Model:** A high-surface-area nanoparticle may bind pollutants efficiently, but laboratory adsorption is only one point on an evidence ladder. First characterise primary size and size distribution, shape, composition, surface chemistry and coating. Measure agglomeration and hydrodynamic size in real water because salt, organic matter and pH can change dispersion. Test selectivity in the presence of competing solutes, flow and contact time, regeneration, performance over cycles and whether particles or dissolved constituents leach into treated water. Exposure can arise during manufacture, consumer use and spent-media disposal; toxicity depends on chemistry, dose, route and duration rather than the term nanoscale alone. A carbon nanotube, graphene sheet, metal oxide and polymeric dendrimer do not share one toxicology result. INST and CSIR research capacity may support testing, but a DST proposal call cannot stand in for potable-water performance or a safety certificate. Compare against existing filtration in cost per volume, accessible maintenance and verified contaminant reduction. Lifecycle assessment should account for production inputs and environmental fate. Adoption is warranted where replicated field measurements and management of residual hazards outperform alternatives. The meaningful chain is measured structure → mechanistic adsorption → operating durability → low exposure and safe disposal, not a label promising clean water.

A safety dossier should identify relevant exposure routes for workers and downstream users rather than infer harmlessness from a lower mass dose. Reproducible field performance requires controls for changing contaminant concentration and routine membrane or filter maintenance. Such validation should include transparent limits of detection and independent measurements across both seasonal and source-water variability.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 marks | Answer in 250 words

**Critically examine** whether all nanoscale innovations produce superior and safer outcomes than bulk technologies.

**Model:** Nanotechnology is not a blanket quality category. Increasing accessible surface area can improve catalysis and adsorption, while quantum confinement can tune the optical response of semiconductor dots. Evaluate toxicity in organisms and soils plus environmental fate over the whole lifecycle. DST's research programme and the Nano Electronics Roadshow can support capability or dialogue without proving outputs or approval. Standard-setting, transparent trials and post-deployment monitoring help distinguish justified uses from marketing hype. The qualified verdict is application-specific: adopt where a demonstrated size-dependent property improves a real outcome at acceptable cost and exposure; reject assertions that smaller automatically means superior or safer.

Natural aerosol nanoparticles and manufactured cosmetic formulations may overlap in size yet differ greatly in coating, concentration and exposure route. A safe decision therefore demands product-specific toxicology and benefits against an established bulk alternative, rather than a single sweeping nano category. Separating genuine improved outcomes from aesthetic novelty supports proportionate standards and protects public trust in the technology.
