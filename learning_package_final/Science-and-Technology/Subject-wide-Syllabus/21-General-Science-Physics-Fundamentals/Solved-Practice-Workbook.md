### Routed Mains PYQ — 2018 GS-III Q5, Bose–Einstein statistics (10 marks / 150 words)

**Model answer:** Bose–Einstein statistics describes indistinguishable bosons—particles of integer spin—which may occupy the same quantum state. S. N. Bose's photon counting, developed further by Einstein for material particles, changed the classical assumption that particles are distinguishable. Identical fermions instead obey Fermi–Dirac statistics and Pauli exclusion. Under suitable cooling and trapping, very many bosons can accumulate in a low-energy state; Bose–Einstein condensates make collective quantum behaviour experimentally accessible. Photon statistics also contribute to understanding light, while a practical laser additionally needs stimulated emission, optical gain and suitable engineering. The revolution was conceptual as well as experimental: statistics now links microscopic particle identity to observable collective effects. It would be inaccurate to claim all particles obey Bose statistics or that the abstract counting rule alone constitutes a deployable technology. Photons are bosons, whereas electrons are fermions; this explains why counting both as distinguishable classical particles misses physical behaviour. Experiments must still control temperature and interactions to reveal a condensate.

### Routed Mains PYQ — 2021 GS-III Q16, blue LEDs (15 marks / 250 words)

**Model answer:** An efficient blue light-emitting diode completed a crucial missing colour in solid-state lighting. In a forward-biased semiconductor junction, injected electrons and holes recombine, releasing photons whose energy reflects the band gap. The 2014 Nobel Prize recognised efficient blue LEDs, not all LEDs. Blue light can excite a phosphor that emits additional wavelengths to make white lighting; red-green-blue devices also underpin colour displays. Unlike a filament heated until it glows, a well-designed LED assembly can deliver useful illumination with lower electrical demand and longer service life. That matters for Indian household, street and decentralised lighting where saved electricity can reduce pressure on supply. However, the device is only one layer: driver losses, thermal management, light quality, maintenance and disposal determine field results. Nor is every bright LED a laser: ordinary LED emission does not thereby become a coherent stimulated-emission beam. Electricity-source mix also affects emissions from lighting. The invention therefore changed the practical options for illumination and display, but numerical savings and access gains must be measured for the specified installation, not assumed from the Nobel award alone. White LEDs can be packaged for many lighting contexts: a municipal street fitting, a small household bulb and a solar-charged lamp face different operating conditions. Their energy saving should be evaluated against a comparable useful light level and service lifetime, not merely a lower rated wattage. Diffusers and appropriate colour quality can improve visibility; glare and poor heat sinking can undercut use. Solid-state emitters also serve signalling and backlit screens, broadening daily-life impact beyond electricity bills. Manufacturing cost and repairability affect who actually benefits, so adoption figures alone do not measure equitable service.

### Six original Mains questions and model solutions

### Original Mains 1 — 10 marks / 150 words

**Question:** Distinguish dimensional consistency from causal explanation in vehicle safety. Answer in 150 words.

**Model answer:** Dimensional analysis tests whether terms of an equation can be compared: force is mass × length × time⁻²; impulse is mass × length × time⁻¹; work is mass × length² × time⁻². Thus equating impulse and energy is invalid, but correct dimensions do not establish the numerical coefficient or a mechanism. In a collision, change in momentum equals impulse; a crumple zone extends stopping time and can reduce mean force for the same momentum change. The work-energy theorem instead connects net work to change in kinetic energy; deformation transforms much of that energy. Neither relation alone guarantees occupant safety: peak accelerations, belt fit and cabin intrusion also matter. An accelerometer can detect deceleration, but calibration and interpretation determine whether it identifies a crash reliably. CSIR-NPL's metrological role illustrates the importance of traceable measurements, not automatic field safety. A defensible safety claim needs dimensions, measured uncertainty, causal transfer and specific operating conditions.

### Original Mains 2 — 10 marks / 150 words

**Question:** Explain orbital weightlessness and distinguish geostationary observation from polar Earth observation. Answer in 150 words.

**Model answer:** Gravity acts on an orbiting craft and supplies centripetal acceleration. Craft and occupants fall together around Earth, removing much of the supporting force normally felt from a floor; apparent weightlessness is continuous free fall, not gravity's disappearance. Geostationary orbit requires an equatorial orbit with a period of one sidereal day and permits a largely fixed view of a region. Near-polar or sun-synchronous observation instead samples changing ground tracks, making it appropriate for different imaging goals; its plane cannot be converted by renaming it. Escape speed describes departure without further propulsion in an idealised energy model, not the speed needed for a bound orbit. ISRO mission design also has to account for launch energy, atmospheric drag where relevant, perturbations, pointing and ground processing. Therefore the same gravitational physics enables distinct platforms, but a selected orbit must match the actual task and its constraints.

### Original Mains 3 — 15 marks / 250 words

**Question:** Analyse how pressure, phase change and heat-transfer modes shape safe and efficient Indian infrastructure. Answer in 250 words.

**Model answer:** Temperature measures thermal state; heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference. In a sealed pressure cooker, greater pressure raises the boiling temperature of water and can accelerate cooking; low atmospheric pressure on a mountain lowers the boiling temperature in an open pot. Pressure does not create heat: fuel or electricity must still supply energy, and a pressure-release mechanism is essential for safety. Steam condensing on skin releases latent heat, which explains its extra thermal harm even if steam and boiling water begin at the same temperature. Industrial boilers and power plants must manage three distinct heat paths. Conduction passes energy through walls, convection moves energy with fluids, and radiation transfers energy even through vacuum. Insulation, heat exchangers and reflective coatings therefore target different losses; no single material eliminates every path. The first law constrains energy accounting, while the second rules out a cyclic heat engine converting all incoming heat into work without other effect. Waste-heat recovery can improve useful service, but feasibility depends on temperature grades, maintenance and cost. India's electricity and process-heat choices should compare delivered useful energy, measured losses and safety provisions, rather than nominal capacity alone. The correct intervention depends on the actual mechanism: modify pressure for boiling conditions, improve insulation for heat leakage and improve process design for efficiency; field monitoring is still needed to establish outcomes. For example, an industrial steam network can lower fuel use only if the recovered heat reaches a suitable load; without that temperature match, nominal recovery is not useful service.

### Original Mains 4 — 15 marks / 250 words

**Question:** Discuss wave-based detection and communication through the operating limits of radar, sonar and optical fibre. Answer in 250 words.

**Model answer:** Sound is a mechanical wave requiring a medium; radio and visible light are electromagnetic waves that can propagate in vacuum. This distinction determines where a detection system can operate. Sonar uses sound in water to probe submerged targets, but water temperature layers and ambient noise can distort returns. Radar uses radio-wave echoes to detect or range suitable targets, with clutter, geometry and target reflectivity limiting interpretation. A radar echo does not directly retrieve stored data from a submerged aircraft recorder. Visible-light communication modulates light to transmit information; opaque obstructions and alignment constrain it differently from radio links. Optical fibre uses a higher-index core and lower-index cladding to guide light; the simple ray picture invokes total internal reflection above the critical angle. Source frequency remains fixed when light crosses a boundary even though speed and wavelength change. Fibre links still experience attenuation, bends and installation faults. India's telecom networks, weather observation and aviation investigation therefore need different sensors and infrastructure, not a universal claim that all wave technologies detect equally. A calibrated accelerometer may register a crash, while a crash-survivable recorder preserves data: localisation and recovery are further tasks. Even safety classification is wavelength-specific: ionising X-rays cannot be treated as ordinary visible-light links. Wave speed, medium, source, detector, field environment and the inference chain must be stated before asserting a technology's suitability. This also prevents confusing an optical receiver with an emitting device: a fibre merely carries signals, while transmitter modulation, receiver sensitivity and network maintenance determine usable communication.

### Original Mains 5 — 20 marks / 250 words

**Question:** Examine the chain from electromagnetic induction to semiconductor electronics, and why a component demonstration does not prove an industrial ecosystem. Answer in 250 words.

**Model answer:** A generator uses changing magnetic flux to induce emf and convert mechanical input into electrical output. A transformer uses time-varying flux to change AC voltage; a plain transformer cannot provide steady transformed output from steady DC. For fixed transmitted power, higher voltage permits lower current and reduces resistive I²R line heating, although insulation and conversion have costs. In semiconductor devices, pentavalent doping produces n-type silicon with electrons as majority carriers; trivalent doping produces p-type material with holes. A junction rectifies; an LED emits light from recombination, whereas a photovoltaic cell separates photo-generated carriers. A bipolar transistor uses base current to control conduction; a MOSFET uses an insulated gate electric field, helping minimise ideal static gate-control current while retaining switching and leakage losses. These principles serve India's grid, computing and power electronics. CSIR-CEERI's device research and CSIR-NPL's metrology help translate principle into reliably measured hardware; India Semiconductor Mission addresses the wider manufacturing ecosystem. Yet a working transistor in a lab does not demonstrate high-yield wafer fabrication, packaging, testing, materials security or qualified personnel. Likewise, a programme announcement is not evidence of commissioned output. A credible capability claim must follow mechanism through measured performance, yield, production qualification and deployment. The physics enables the chain; industrial institutions and evidence establish whether it operates at scale. In policy terms, packaging and testing need defect monitoring as seriously as the transistor gate needs physical design; neither a circuit layout nor an announced facility measures reliable field output.

### Original Mains 6 — 20 marks / 250 words

**Question:** Evaluate the different mechanisms and risk boundaries of radiation, lasers and quantum technologies in Indian public applications. Answer in 250 words.

**Model answer:** Radiation is not a single hazard. Alpha particles are helium nuclei, weakly penetrating externally but dangerous when internalised; beta particles are fast electrons or positrons; gamma rays are highly penetrating photons. Half-life measures the statistical decay of a population and ordinary heating cannot immediately deactivate a radionuclide. Controlled isotopes can support diagnosis, therapy or industrial measurement, but dosimetry and exposure pathways must be assessed separately. Fission splits heavy nuclei while fusion joins light nuclei; an experimental fusion result is not commercial power. A laser instead uses stimulated emission to produce comparatively coherent and directional light. Its use in surgery or precision processing has wavelength-, power- and exposure-specific risks; it is not automatically ionising gamma radiation. The 2023 Nobel recognition of attosecond light pulses illustrates how optical timing reveals electron dynamics, but experimental insight is not itself a deployed diagnostic. Bose–Einstein statistics describes identical bosons' ability to share quantum states; fermions obey exclusion. This conceptual distinction supports frontier research without constituting a manufactured product. India's DAE/BARC ecosystem relates to radiation applications, while DST's National Quantum Mission concerns quantum capability; neither title alone establishes a safe, scaled outcome. Metrology, safety controls, evidence of effectiveness and the maturity of a device must accompany claims of public benefit. Correct classification of the physical process helps avoid both indiscriminate fear and unjustified optimism. Separating a controlled radiotherapy dose from uncontrolled contamination requires knowing source, half-life, shielding geometry and patient exposure; public trust depends on these verifiable safeguards rather than reassurance alone.
