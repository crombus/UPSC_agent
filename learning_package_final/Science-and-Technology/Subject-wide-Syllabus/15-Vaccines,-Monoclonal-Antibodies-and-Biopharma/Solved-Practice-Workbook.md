### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 marks | Answer in 150 words

**Explain** active versus passive immunity using one vaccine and one monoclonal-antibody example.

**Model:** An inactivated vaccine introduces non-replicating antigen that primes a person's B and T cells; their antibodies and memory cells develop after an interval, so protection can be durable although boosters may be needed. By contrast, a monoclonal antibody manufactured in cell culture directly binds a chosen epitope after administration. Its action can start quickly, useful when immediate targeting is needed, but supplied protein is cleared and does not itself establish immune memory. The same distinction applies whether the target is an infectious agent or a cancer-associated receptor; indication and benefit require product-specific evidence. A vaccine is therefore generally active immunisation, while a mAb is passive/direct biologic intervention. Neither should be confused with an antiviral, which pharmacologically blocks viral replication. Safety and access depend on appropriate trial evidence, regulatory assessment and reliable supply rather than immunological labels alone.

A patient’s clinical indication and risk profile matter: short-lived passive protection can be useful despite lacking memory, while active immunisation may be inappropriate during a rapidly evolving acute need.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 marks | Answer in 150 words

**Differentiate** mRNA, DNA and viral-vector vaccine routes and their delivery limitations.

**Model:** mRNA formulated in lipid nanoparticles reaches the cytoplasm, where host ribosomes translate it into antigen; nuclear entry is not required for antigen translation. DNA vaccines instead need cellular nuclear entry to transcribe antigen-coding DNA into mRNA before protein is made. India's ZyCoV-D illustrates needle-free jet delivery for a DNA platform. A viral-vector vaccine uses an engineered, usually replication-deficient virus to carry antigen code into cells; pre-existing immunity to its vector can matter. All three seek active immune priming, not direct passive antibody supply, but their delivery formulations, production quality, safety evidence and cold-chain specifications differ by product. An India-specific procurement decision should verify the approved product's label and validated transport limits rather than assuming all nucleic-acid vaccines need the same temperature. None of the mechanisms alone proves a particular trial efficacy or current authorisation.

An assay confirming antigen expression does not establish protective clinical benefit; comparative trials and continuing AEFI surveillance are necessary to evaluate product-specific outcomes.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 marks | Answer in 250 words

**Examine** vaccine-platform choice through immunology, contraindications and distribution equity.

**Model:** Platform choice changes the biological input and the operational burden. Live-attenuated vaccines use a weakened replicating agent and can generate strong memory but present particular risks in immunocompromised recipients and generally in pregnancy. Inactivated vaccines supply a non-replicating whole organism; protein subunits require adequate immune stimulation, often using an adjuvant. Toxoids prime protection against an inactivated toxin rather than the bacterium, while genome-free virus-like particles present structural epitopes without infectious genetic material, as with HPV vaccines including Cervavac. Viral vectors deliver antigen instructions; mRNA and DNA vaccines use distinct intracellular routes, with DNA requiring nuclear transcription. Each can produce active immunity, but time to response, dosing, contraindications, manufacturing inputs and storage differ. Cold-chain failures can destroy potency despite high laboratory efficacy. For Indian district programmes, compare validated product-specific storage ranges, last-mile capacity, wastage, trained staff, antigen match and safety surveillance; never claim all mRNA vaccines have one immutable temperature or that every inactivated product is equally effective. Clinical evidence is phase-specific: early studies address safety and dosing, larger trials efficacy, and AEFI surveillance follows rollout. CDSCO/DCGI assess regulated products while ICMR generates research evidence. Public-health value is best measured by protection achieved safely and equitably, not platform novelty alone.

For example, an HPV virus-like particle contains no viral genome, whereas toxoid protection addresses the toxin of tetanus. Such distinctions matter for explaining why one product cannot simply replace another in a procurement list. Exposure risk, age and delivery infrastructure must also inform choice.
