### 2018 GS-I Mains — why India needs IRNSS and how it helps navigation

**Model answer:** IRNSS, operationally NavIC, gives India an independently managed regional position, navigation and timing capability for India and surrounding areas. ### 2025 Prelims GS-I — nature and function of GAGAN
**Provenance:** Verified routed objective concept; no official Set-A answer letter is reproduced or inferred.
**Model solution:** GAGAN is the GPS Aided GEO Augmented Navigation system, an SBAS jointly developed by ISRO and AAI for aviation. ## Six original Mains questions with independent solved models

### Original Mains 1 — 10 marks
**Question:** Distinguish NavIC from GAGAN in architecture, purpose and strategic value. Answer in about 150 words.
**Model answer:** NavIC is an independently controlled Indian regional satellite constellation providing position, navigation and timing signals across India and surrounding areas. A compatible receiver times signals from several spacecraft, uses their known positions and computes location; accurate clocks and ground control are essential. Its open Standard Positioning Service differs from encrypted Restricted Service for authorised users. Nominal geostationary and inclined-geosynchronous geometry should not be mistaken for a live functional count. GAGAN is different: ISRO and AAI developed this aviation satellite-based augmentation system to improve GPS accuracy and, critically, signal integrity. Reference stations observe errors, a master system computes corrections and alerts, and GEO payloads broadcast them to compatible aircraft. DGCA certification and operational uptake concern aviation performance, not sovereign ownership of GPS. NavIC helps reduce dependence on foreign navigation signals across public applications; GAGAN helps aircraft use GPS more safely. Both require robust ground infrastructure and receivers, but an augmented foreign signal cannot replace an independent PNT constellation.

### Original Mains 2 — 10 marks
**Question:** Explain why a satellite launch is not the same as a satellite service. Answer in about 150 words.
**Model answer:** Launch confirms that a rocket delivered a spacecraft to a trajectory; it does not establish that the spacecraft reached its final operating orbit, that its payload works, or that users received a useful product. A navigation craft needs successful orbit raising, functioning atomic clocks, ground-control updates and compatible receivers. The source’s dated NVS-02 account separates successful injection from a later orbit-raising anomaly; it is not evidence of current constellation status. Similarly, Earth-observation imagers require downlink, calibration, processing and interpretation before departments can use flood or crop maps. Communication payloads need functioning transponders and terrestrial terminals; weather soundings need forecast analysis and warning dissemination. GAGAN needs measurement, integrity computation, uplink, broadcast, compatible aircraft and aviation certification. Thus evaluate each link: spacecraft commissioning, space-to-ground data, ground processing and user uptake. Count launched satellites, spacecraft in intended orbit, operational payloads and available public services separately, stating the source date and service definition.

### Original Mains 3 — 15 marks
**Question:** Analyse the space-ground-user chain behind Indian satellite applications. Answer in about 250 words.
**Model answer:** Space hardware is an enabling layer, not the final public service. An Earth-observation satellite senses reflected or emitted energy; ground stations receive scenes, laboratories calibrate and process them, and users at NRSC/Bhuvan and departments translate products into land, water, agriculture and disaster decisions. Optical data can be interrupted by clouds; revisit and processing delays affect usefulness. Communication satellites such as INSAT/GSAT relay signals using transponders, but terrestrial gateways and household or field terminals complete broadcasting and warning chains. INSAT-3D-family imaging and sounding support forecasting only when meteorological institutions interpret and disseminate timely alerts. NavIC spacecraft broadcast accurately timed ranging signals; ground control maintains orbits and clocks while chipsets, standards and receivers turn signals into tracking, fisheries or timing applications. GAGAN adds an especially demanding safety chain: reference stations measure GPS errors, central processing calculates corrections and integrity, uplinks feed GEO payloads and certified aircraft receivers use the warnings. An aviation alert is only as reliable as its weakest segment. Distinguish orbit from payload: GEO may allow persistent regional view, while polar low orbit gives repeat coverage, but neither determines the service alone. Indian policy should therefore measure usable data, receiver access, institutional integration and resilience, not cumulative spacecraft launches. Ground verification and user training improve conversion of technical capacity into public outcomes; maintenance and privacy safeguards matter as deployment expands.

### Original Mains 6 — 20 marks
**Question:** Design a resilient PNT and aviation-augmentation strategy for India. Answer in about 250 words.
**Model answer:** A resilient strategy should begin by separating two missions.
