## PYQS AND ANSWER PRACTICE

### PYQ DEMAND CARD — 2018 GS-III Q19 (15 marks, 250 words)
**Demand:** Discuss strengths and weaknesses of the personal-data-protection committee's report in cyberspace. **Direct model:** In its 2018 context, the Srikrishna Committee put personal autonomy, accountable processing and regulator-led oversight at the centre of India's data debate. This helped move beyond a security-only view: an encrypted but unlawfully repurposed database still violates informational autonomy. Critically examine broad government access, implementation burdens, uneven user bargaining power and the difficulty of independent oversight. Strong breach security, usable consent and accessible remedies need resources and transparent exceptions. Do **not** describe the subsequent 2023 Act or 2025 Rules as text of the 2018 report; those are later developments. The balanced verdict is rights-based processing coupled with proportional, auditable exemptions and cyber resilience.

### PYQ DEMAND CARD — 2024 GS-III Q10 (10 marks, 150 words)
**Demand:** Describe context and salient features of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. **Direct model:** Puttaswamy's 2017 recognition of privacy as a fundamental right, the 2018 committee process and later bill/draft debates set the context for the 2023 statute. The Act regulates digital personal-data processing, with Data Principals and purpose-setting Fiduciaries; consent or specified 'certain legitimate uses' provide legal grounds. It envisages notice, access/correction/erasure and grievance routes, security duties, special child protections and additional obligations for notified Significant Data Fiduciaries. A Board adjudicates financial penalties, with TDSAT hearing appeals; section 17 provides bounded but debated exemptions. Unlike CERT-In incident directions, these are data-governance duties. **Status caveat:** By the owner's 2 August 2026 audit the 13 November 2025 notification had commenced establishment provisions but scheduled most substantive duties for an eighteen-month tranche; recheck later notifications before claiming operational enforcement.

### PYQ DEMAND CARDS — 2019 Prelims GS-I Q88 and Q94 (objective; no final key held)
**Demand:** GDPR adoption/implementation; digital-signature characteristics. **Concept route:** GDPR is European law, not India's DPDP text. A digital signature authenticates key-linked authorship and integrity; it does not by itself encrypt contents, verify device security or establish statutory consent. Apply to the original wording; 2018–2023 official objective keys were unavailable locally. No letter is supplied.

### PYQ DEMAND CARDS — 2020 Prelims GS-I Q46; 2022 Prelims GS-I Q32 (objective; no final key held)
**Demand:** PKI in Indian digital security; Web3, blockchain and user control. **Concept route:** Certificates bind public keys to identities under a trust chain; key and endpoint compromise remain possible. Distributed records may enable user-control designs but neither automatically guarantee privacy nor remove processing duties; immutable personal records create an erasure/correction tension. These are concept solutions, not official answer letters.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Distinguish a Data Principal, Fiduciary, Processor and Consent Manager in an Indian service. Answer in 150 words.

**Model answer:** Consider a patient using a district telemedicine platform. The patient is the Data Principal: the digital health record concerns that individual. The platform determines why records are collected and how consultations are processed, so it is the Data Fiduciary even if it outsources cloud hosting. A contractor handling records on the platform's behalf is a Data Processor; outsourcing does not remove the Fiduciary's responsibility for lawful processing and security. A Board-registered Consent Manager, where used, offers an interface for the individual to give, review or withdraw consent; it is not automatically every cloud vendor. For consent-based operations, withdrawal changes the lawful basis for continued processing, subject to any independently applicable statutory ground. The Act's provisions have phased commencement: under the November 2025 notification the Consent Manager framework follows a one-year tranche, while most core processing duties follow eighteen months. State legal design and present force separately; a cyber incident at the same platform also engages distinct security-response duties.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Explain why cybersecurity compliance does not alone establish compliance with data-protection law. Answer in 150 words.

**Model answer:** Cybersecurity protects networks, devices and information against compromise; data protection governs whether and why personal data are collected, shared, retained and subject to individual rights. A hospital may encrypt records, use multifactor authentication and retain logs, yet still repurpose patient data for unrelated advertising without an appropriate legal ground. Conversely, a clear consent notice will not stop ransomware. The DPDP Act designs notice, consent or certain legitimate uses, security safeguards, rights and a Board-based remedy, subject to phased commencement. CERT-In's IT Act section 70B role and its 2022 directions cover specified cyber incidents, including six-hour reporting and operational logs; NCIIPC focuses on notified Critical Information Infrastructure under section 70A. A hospital breach may require both legal analysis and technical containment. Apply each institution's own trigger and date instead of treating CERT-In as a privacy tribunal or assuming encryption creates valid consent. The diagnostic question is whether the organisation had a legal basis for a given processing purpose as well as an effective control against theft.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Discuss how staged commencement affects interpretation of rights and duties in the DPDP Act. Answer in 250 words.

**Model answer:** A statute can exist before all its provisions become enforceable. The DPDP Act was enacted in 2023, but G.S.R. 843(E), published on 13 November 2025, set different start times; the DPDP Rules, 2025 were notified separately as G.S.R. 846(E) and also phased. Definitions, establishment of the Data Protection Board and section 44(3)'s change to RTI section 8(1)(j) formed part of the immediate tranche. The framework for Consent Manager registration follows one year after publication. Core provisions on notice, consent, specified legitimate uses, Data Principal rights, Fiduciary and notified Significant Fiduciary duties, section 16 transfers and penalties were assigned the eighteen-month tranche. At the source's 2 August 2026 verification, those later core provisions were enacted and scheduled, not yet fully in force. The Board's legal establishment was separate from operational capacity: recruitment for its Chairperson and four Members was advertised in May 2026. Thus a model answer can explain statutory rights while qualifying whether a person could then obtain an operational Board remedy. CERT-In's 2022 incident directions meanwhile follow a separate IT Act pathway. A computed May 2027 date from the eighteen-month schedule is not proof that no later notification changed status. For exam use, check MeitY/India Code before stating that any tranche is currently effective. Legal precision depends on distinguishing enacted, rules-notified, commenced and actually administered. An immediate change to the RTI exemption and a deferred personal-data access right can coexist; this asymmetry is central to transparency analysis. Administrative capacity, including real case intake and reasoned orders, should be tested separately from statutory power to create the Board.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Analyse consent, certain legitimate uses and exemptions under DPDP without importing draft-law concepts. Answer in 250 words.

**Model answer:** DPDP's processing design is not a generic permission to use any data that an organisation holds. For a digital-personal-data operation, identify the Data Fiduciary, the specified purpose and an applicable lawful ground. Consent should be linked to clear notice and an actual choice; if consent is withdrawn, consent-based processing must stop unless another legitimate ground exists. Section 7 instead enumerates 'certain legitimate uses', such as conditions related to voluntarily provided data; the enacted Act does not use the superseded 2022 draft label 'deemed consent' as an open-ended shortcut. Rights of access, correction/erasure and grievance are balanced against duties of Data Principals under section 15, including not impersonating others. Section 17 includes processing-specific exemptions and a power in subsection (2)(a) to exempt a State instrumentality by Central Government notification on stated grounds; it is not automatic immunity for every government database. Section 16 envisages cross-border transfers subject to notified restrictions and stricter applicable sectoral law, not blanket localisation. These design features warrant debate over state access and effective oversight. Distinguish the debate from operative obligations: the 13 November 2025 commencement schedule put most substantive provisions in the eighteen-month tranche, and current force must be rechecked. The answer's test is whether each use has a lawful basis, accountable actor, appropriate limit and usable remedy. Suppose a welfare department shares an applicant list with a contractor: one should separately test the original collection ground, scope of contracted processing and existence of a relevant State notification. A blanket 'government data' label does not answer any of these questions.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Evaluate DPDP's privacy–transparency balance, exemptions and regulatory capacity. Answer in 250 words.

**Model answer:** Data protection aims to protect informational autonomy while allowing necessary public administration. In Puttaswamy (2017), privacy was recognised as a fundamental right; the DPDP Act, 2023 created a digital-personal-data framework of lawful processing, rights, security safeguards and a Data Protection Board, with penalties set out in a financial schedule. Yet the balance depends on institutional and legal detail. Section 17 has specific exceptions and permits notified exemptions for State instrumentalities on listed grounds; treating this as either nonexistent or an automatic blanket exemption is mistaken. Section 44(3) substituted RTI Act section 8(1)(j)'s personal-information exemption in the immediate November 2025 tranche, raising a distinct transparency question even before core consent and rights duties commenced. Debate whether privacy-based withholding could conceal public-interest accountability, while recognising legitimate personal privacy in disclosures. The Board's legal creation in 2025 differed from its staffing status: MeitY advertised recruitment of the Chairperson and Members in May 2026. Formal rights without accessible investigation and appeal may underperform. Design oversight with reasoned, reviewable exemption decisions, transparent orders, proportionate disclosure and robust technical safeguards. A Board financial penalty is not automatic compensation to a harmed individual; TDSAT is the appeal forum. Assess this architecture against actual commencement and capacity using updated official notifications rather than asserting all provisions were operational at enactment. In a welfare database, information about beneficiaries can be genuinely private while the criteria for distributing public benefits remain matters of public accountability. Good practice redacts sensitive personal details rather than treating every official decision as either fully public or wholly secret.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Discuss an integrated response to a major personal-data breach in Indian critical infrastructure, separating institutions and legal triggers. Answer in 250 words.

**Model answer:** A breach of a digitally stored power-grid workforce database raises both privacy and operational-security concerns, but agencies have different mandates. The organisation should isolate affected systems, preserve forensic evidence and logs, restore trusted operations, review access controls and test backups. CERT-In, the section 70B IT Act incident-response body, receives reports of specified incidents under its 28 April 2022 directions, which cite a six-hour reporting interval and 180-day in-India ICT-log retention; assess the incident against their exact triggering conditions. NCIIPC's section 70A mandate focuses on Critical Information Infrastructure: verify whether the affected asset is a notified protected system rather than assuming every energy-sector server automatically qualifies. If personal data are involved, identify the Data Fiduciary, affected Principals, scope of disclosure, security safeguards and any applicable DPDP breach duties; the 2025 notification scheduled most substantive DPDP obligations in a later tranche, so check their operative status at incident date. The Data Protection Board's statutory adjudication is not a substitute for containment or CERT-In response. If fraud follows, the MHA's I4C reporting pathway is a further distinct route. Prevention requires limited privileges, encryption, audited vendors, incident drills and lawful minimisation. Effective accountability means communicating verified facts and providing remedies without collapsing privacy, cybercrime, CII protection and technical incident response into one institution. The breach response should not be reduced to forwarding an alert: confirm affected records, revoke compromised credentials, document the timeline and provide intelligible updates. Independent post-incident review can identify whether poor vendor controls or excessive retention widened the harm.
