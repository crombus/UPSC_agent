## BASIC LEARNING SESSION

### 1. Cyclone risk is a compound coastal and inland hazard

```text
WARM OCEAN + MOIST UNSTABLE AIR + CORIOLIS
                 + DISTURBANCE + LOW VERTICAL SHEAR
                              ↓
                      TROPICAL CYCLONE
                              ↓
       wind + storm surge + waves + intense rain
                              ↓
 river/urban flood + erosion + landslide + lifeline failure
```

A tropical cyclone is an organised low-pressure system with rotating winds and deep convection. Disaster risk arises from several linked hazards, not wind speed alone.

The canonical six cyclogenesis conditions are:

1. sufficiently warm sea-surface temperature;
2. atmospheric instability;
3. high lower-to-middle-tropospheric humidity;
4. sufficient Coriolis force;
5. a pre-existing low-level disturbance; and
6. low vertical wind shear.

The eye is comparatively calm; the eyewall around it contains the strongest winds and intense convection. The canonical source places peak winds roughly 20–30 km from the centre.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** A cyclone is a compound risk system in which wind, surge, waves and rainfall create different impact pathways requiring different protective actions.

**Must-write keywords:** cyclogenesis · low pressure · Coriolis · low shear · eye · eyewall · compound hazard.

**How to write the paragraph**

- **Claim:** Wind classification does not describe the whole disaster.
- **Evidence:** Coastal inundation may be surge-led while inland loss may be rainfall- and river-flood-led.
- **Analysis:** Preparedness must forecast and communicate each hazard component.
- **Qualification:** Impact varies with track, intensity, size, speed, tide, bathymetry, landfall geometry and vulnerability.

---

### 2. Keep cyclone hazard components separate

| Component | Mechanism | Typical management implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Extreme wind** | pressure-gradient-driven rotating flow, strongest around the eyewall | wind-resistant structures, roof/connection safety, shutdown |
| **Storm surge** | abnormal sea-level rise driven mainly by cyclone wind stress and low pressure | surge forecast, coastal evacuation, embankment/shelter planning |
| **Storm tide** | storm surge combined with astronomical tide | time-of-landfall and tide phase matter |
| **Heavy rain** | deep moist convection and rainbands | flash, riverine and urban-flood readiness |
| **High waves** | wind-generated sea state superimposed on raised water | ports, vessels, shoreline structures and erosion |
| **Cascades** | power, telecom, transport, health, water and supply-chain failures | continuity and restoration plans |

Storm surge is not a tsunami. A tsunami begins with sudden water displacement; storm surge is meteorological and evolves with cyclone wind, pressure and coast.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Cyclone preparedness fails when one wind category is allowed to conceal surge, rainfall, waves and cascading service failure.

**Must-write keywords:** wind · storm surge · storm tide · rainfall · waves · cascading risk · lifelines.

### 3. Why storm surge becomes destructive

```text
CYCLONE WIND pushes water coastward
             +
LOW PRESSURE raises sea level
             +
SHALLOW SHELF / CONCAVE COAST / RIVER MOUTH
             +
ASTRONOMICAL TIDE + WAVES
             ↓
TOTAL COASTAL WATER LEVEL + INUNDATION
```

Surge severity depends on the storm's wind field and pressure, forward motion and angle of approach, near-shore bathymetry, coastal shape and tide at landfall. A shallow shelf or funnel-shaped coast can amplify water accumulation.

**Storm surge** is the meteorological anomaly above predicted tide. **Storm tide** is the observed/forecast water level produced when surge is added to the astronomical tide. Wave action can then add overtopping and erosion.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Storm-surge risk is the joint product of cyclone forcing and coastal geometry, which is why the same wind category can produce unequal coastal flooding.

**Must-write keywords:** wind stress · pressure deficit · bathymetry · shelf slope · coastal geometry · astronomical tide · storm tide.

**UPSC trap:** Do not quote one surge height for an entire coast or equate the surge with the tide.

### 4. IMD classification and naming threshold

IMD/RSMC New Delhi classifies North Indian Ocean disturbances by maximum sustained wind:

| System | Wind speed |
|---|---|
| Depression | 17–27 kt |
| Deep Depression | 28–33 kt |
| Cyclonic Storm | 34–47 kt |
| Severe Cyclonic Storm | 48–63 kt |
| Very Severe Cyclonic Storm | 64–89 kt |
| Extremely Severe Cyclonic Storm | 90–119 kt |
| Super Cyclonic Storm | at least 120 kt |

A system receives a cyclone name from the **Cyclonic Storm** stage, beginning at 34 kt. Depression and Deep Depression remain important because they can still produce damaging rain and flooding.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Wind-speed classification communicates intensity; it does not by itself forecast surge, rain, landfall exposure or loss.

**Must-write keywords:** Depression · Deep Depression · Cyclonic Storm · 34 kt · naming threshold · sustained wind.

### 5. Forecast, warning, delivery and impact are different stages

```text
OBSERVE
satellite + radar + surface/ocean data
        ↓
ANALYSE / FORECAST
track + intensity + landfall + rain + wind + surge
        ↓
WARN
bulletin stage + colour/action message
        ↓
DELIVER
national → State → district → local → household/boat
        ↓
ACT
evacuate + shelter + close port + secure lifelines
        ↓
VERIFY
receipt + comprehension + movement + protection + outcome
```

An accurate track forecast is not the same as a warning received. Receipt is not the same as comprehension. Comprehension is not the same as an accessible evacuation or safe shelter.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** The central governance test is not whether IMD issued a bulletin, but whether differentiated hazard information produced timely, inclusive protective action.

**Must-write keywords:** observation · forecast · warning · dissemination · comprehension · protective action · outcome.

### 6. IMD's two warning languages

#### A. Lead-time bulletin sequence

| Stage | Minimum lead indicated in the canonical official SOP | Function |
|---|---:|---|
| Pre-Cyclone Watch | 72 hours | early heads-up on possible development and affected region |
| Cyclone Alert | 48 hours | firmer threat and preparedness initiation |
| Cyclone Warning | 24 hours | detailed landfall, hazard and action information |
| Post-Landfall Outlook | 12 hours | inland continuation and post-landfall impacts |

#### B. Colour/action code

| Colour | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Green | no warning |
| Yellow | be updated |
| Orange | be prepared |
| Red | take action |

The systems answer different questions. The bulletin sequence expresses **lead time and evolution**; the colours express **action urgency**. A red warning is a forecast-based call to act, not proof that the maximum forecast impact will occur.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** IMD's stage tells the user when the threat is approaching; its colour tells the user how urgently to prepare or act.

**Must-write keywords:** 72–48–24–12 · Pre-Cyclone Watch · Alert · Warning · Post-Landfall Outlook · Green/Yellow/Orange/Red.

### 7. Institutional chain

| Actor | Core role |
|---|---|
| **IMD / RSMC New Delhi** | cyclone monitoring, classification, track/intensity/rain/wind/surge forecast and bulletins |
| **ACWCs** | Kolkata, Chennai and Mumbai; area warning coordination |
| **CWCs** | Bhubaneswar, Visakhapatnam and Ahmedabad; cyclone warning functions |
| **NDMA / MHA** | national guidelines, policy coordination and NCRMP implementation history |
| **State / SDMA / SEC** | State preparedness, evacuation policy and interdepartmental coordination |
| **District / DDMA / local government** | route, shelter, transport, communication, local evacuation and relief |
| **NDRF / SDRF / Coast Guard / Navy** | pre-positioned specialist response and maritime support as tasked |
| **community institutions** | warning relay, assisted evacuation, shelter management and feedback |

NDMA's 2008 cyclone guidelines use a multi-sectoral premise. No single institution owns the complete chain.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** IMD owns the forecast; civil authorities own protective decisions; responders support execution; communities determine whether the last mile works.

**Must-write keywords:** IMD · RSMC · ACWC · CWC · NDMA · SDMA/DDMA · NDRF · local authority.

### 8. Warning delivery and inclusive evacuation

```text
HAZARD-SPECIFIC MESSAGE
  ├─ wind → secure roofs, trees, utilities
  ├─ surge → leave mapped coastal inundation zone
  ├─ rain → avoid low crossings and flood routes
  └─ waves → close ports/beaches and recall vessels
               ↓
MULTI-CHANNEL DELIVERY
               ↓
ASSISTED MOVEMENT + TRANSPORT + SHELTER
               ↓
ACCOUNTING + NO PREMATURE RETURN
```

Warnings should be multilingual, location-specific and accessible through audio, visual and text channels. Evacuation plans must account for fishers at sea, tourists, children, older persons, persons with disabilities, patients, migrants and livestock.

Routes must remain usable under rain, fallen trees, waterlogging and power failure. Household action depends on transport, trust, shelter accessibility and permission to move livelihoods or animals—not warning receipt alone.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Last-mile preparedness converts a probabilistic forecast into a feasible movement decision for the person most likely to be left behind.

**Must-write keywords:** impact-based forecast · multilingual warning · assisted evacuation · route redundancy · transport · accountability · livestock.

### 9. Shelters, cattle mounds and evacuation roads

A multi-purpose cyclone shelter is not merely a building. It requires:

- safe location outside the relevant surge/flood exposure;
- wind- and flood-resilient structure;
- all-weather access and route signage;
- water, sanitation, lighting, power backup and communications;
- accessible entry, internal space and toilets;
- space or linked arrangements for livestock;
- trained management, keys, maintenance and drills;
- post-event use compatible with its emergency function.

The canonical owner pairs shelters with **cattle mounds** and **all-weather evacuation roads** because evacuation can fail when households refuse to abandon animals or when roads flood before landfall.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Shelter capacity is a system of safe structure, reachable route, inclusive services and trusted operation—not a building count.

**Must-write keywords:** multi-purpose shelter · cattle mound · all-weather road · accessibility · maintenance · occupancy · route.

### 10. NCRMP: project achievement and post-project duty

The National Cyclone Risk Mitigation Project combined:

- improved early-warning dissemination;
- cyclone shelters and evacuation access;
- saline embankments and coastal protection;
- local response capacity;
- disaster-risk-management capacity in participating States.

The canonical source records a thirteen-State/UT, Category I–Category II framework. Category I included Andhra Pradesh, Gujarat, Odisha, Tamil Nadu and West Bengal; Category II covered the remaining listed coastal States/UTs. This is a **differentiated vulnerability classification**, not a declaration that Category II is safe.

📰 MHA/PIB reported on 1 April 2026 that **Phase I closed in December 2018** and **Phase II in March 2023**. NCRMP should therefore be described as a completed project. Its assets now create a State/local operation-and-maintenance obligation; no verified NCRMP-III should be invented.

📰 A PIB reply dated 4 February 2025 reported multi-purpose cyclone shelters created under the project as: Odisha 316, Andhra Pradesh 219, West Bengal 146, Gujarat 76, Kerala 17, Goa 11 and Karnataka 10. These are dated construction outputs, not proof that each asset is currently accessible, maintained or fully functional.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** NCRMP shifted cyclone governance from relief toward mitigation, but completed construction transfers the live test to maintenance, staffing, accessibility and actual shelter use.

**Must-write keywords:** NCRMP · Category I/II · completed phases · shelter · embankment · evacuation road · O&M hand-off.

### 11. Resilient infrastructure and continuity

Cyclone-resilient infrastructure must address different failure modes:

| System | Risk | Preparedness test |
|---|---|---|
| housing/shelter | roof loss, wind pressure, flooding | connections, openings, elevation, safe occupancy |
| power | poles, substations, saltwater and tree fall | sectional isolation, backup and rapid restoration |
| telecom | tower failure and power loss | redundancy, portable systems and priority restoration |
| roads/bridges | debris, washout and waterlogging | alternate access and drainage |
| water/sanitation | saline ingress and contamination | protected sources, backup treatment and testing |
| health | structural, utility and staff disruption | safe building plus continuity plan |
| ports | wind, waves, surge, cargo and channel obstruction | shutdown, vessel safety, spill control and reopening |

Design standards, maintenance and operating protocols are distinct. A structure that remains standing but loses power, water, access or staff may still fail as a lifeline.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Cyclone resilience is functional continuity under compound wind-water stress, not structural survival alone.

**Must-write keywords:** lifeline · redundancy · backup power · drainage · saline ingress · port continuity · restoration.

### 12. Embankments and ecosystem buffers

```text
COASTAL PROTECTION PORTFOLIO
│
├─ engineered → saline embankment / shelter / resilient road / wall
├─ ecosystem → mangrove / dune / wetland / coastal vegetation
└─ non-structural → zoning / warning / evacuation / maintenance
```

Saline embankments can reduce surge ingress but may breach, overtop, obstruct drainage or deteriorate without maintenance. Mangroves, dunes, wetlands and coastal vegetation can attenuate water and waves and support livelihoods, but performance depends on health, width, continuity, bathymetry and event severity.

Ecosystem restoration is not a substitute for evacuation or resilient lifelines. Engineering should not sever sediment movement, fisheries access or wetland drainage without evaluating transferred risk.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** The safest coast combines risk-sensitive siting, maintained engineering and functioning ecosystems because each layer has different failure modes.

**Must-write keywords:** saline embankment · bio-shield · mangrove · dune · wetland · overtopping · transferred risk.

### 13. Livelihoods, social protection and shelter governance

Cyclone losses are distributed unequally:

- fishers and harbour workers face at-sea and shore-based risk;
- informal settlements may occupy exposed land with weak construction;
- women may face privacy, sanitation and safety barriers in shelters;
- persons with disabilities, older persons and patients need assisted transport and care;
- children require identification and protection procedures;
- livestock and tools shape willingness to evacuate;
- daily-wage workers face income loss even when physical evacuation succeeds.

Shelters need inclusive design, separate and safe sanitation, health support, grievance channels and transparent occupancy management. Recovery should restore boats, nets, farms, shops and market links, not only houses.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Evacuation is socially credible only when shelters protect dignity and recovery protects the livelihood that people were asked to leave.

**Must-write keywords:** fisherfolk · informal housing · disability · gender · sanitation · livestock · livelihood continuity.

---

### 14. Response, post-landfall hazards and recovery

Landfall is not the end of danger. The Post-Landfall Outlook addresses continuing wind and rain inland; river and urban flooding, landslides, electrocution, contaminated water, blocked roads and disease risk may follow.

Response priorities:

1. maintain warning and public information;
2. account for evacuated and missing persons;
3. search, rescue and emergency health;
4. isolate unsafe power, fuel and hazardous cargo;
5. restore access, water, sanitation, telecom and health services;
6. assess housing, crops, fisheries and ecosystem damage;
7. support safe, voluntary return;
8. Build Back Better using observed failure evidence.

Cyclone Remal (May 2024) is a dated official preparedness case: IMD forecasting was linked to pre-positioning and maritime readiness by national and State actors. Use it to demonstrate the warning-to-action chain, not to infer unverified coast-wide avoided losses.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Post-landfall management must follow the cyclone inland and through service restoration rather than ending at coastal landfall.

**Must-write keywords:** post-landfall outlook · cascading flood · search and rescue · utility safety · safe return · Build Back Better.

### 15. Integrated cyclone-resilience framework

```text
RISK KNOWLEDGE
track + intensity + surge + rain + exposure
        ↓
RISK GOVERNANCE
IMD forecast + civil authority + land use + standards
        ↓
INVESTMENT
shelter + road + embankment + lifeline + ecosystem
        ↓
EARLY ACTION
clear warning + assisted evacuation + port shutdown
        ↓
RESPONSE / RECOVERY
service continuity + livelihood restoration + BBB
        ↓
LEARNING
verify forecast, route, shelter, asset and outcome performance
```

A complete answer must distinguish:

- intensity classification from impact forecast;
- bulletin stage from colour code;
- warning issue from warning receipt;
- shelter construction from usable shelter;
- NCRMP project completion from asset maintenance;
- reduced mortality from wider economic and livelihood resilience.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** India's cyclone capability is strongest when forecast skill, local evacuation, maintained shelters, resilient lifelines and ecosystem protection operate as one auditable chain.

**Must-write keywords:** multi-hazard · impact-based warning · warning-to-action · NCRMP · continuity · ecosystem-based DRR · outcome.

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

### A. Warning-to-action conversion is the binding constraint

The Advanced owner records failure to respond adequately to warnings, planning and coordination gaps, poor risk understanding and weak grassroots participation. These are separate from forecast accuracy. Audit the chain as: forecast quality -> message content -> channel reliability -> trusted receipt -> feasible action -> safe shelter -> outcome.

### B. Differentiated investment and residual risk

NCRMP's Category I/II split recognises unequal vulnerability and supports prioritisation. It can also create a false binary: lower-frequency Category II coasts retain severe residual risk. Allocation should combine hazard probability, exposed people and assets, evacuation time, lifeline criticality and vulnerability rather than category label alone.

### C. Technology and network limits

The canonical owner treats Aircraft Probing of Cyclone as a recommended capability, not proof of nationwide deployment. It also records source-period concerns about an under-equipped apex operations centre and fragmented agency networks. These should be used as institutional design lessons unless dated evidence confirms current status.

The same source recommends a **National Disaster Communication Infrastructure** linked with NDMA/MHA and coastal SDMAs, and a **Cyclone Disaster Management Information System** spanning the disaster cycle. Treat both as source-period recommendations unless a dated official source establishes present deployment.

### D. Project-to-maintenance transition

NCRMP's completed phases created shelters, roads and embankments. Once construction finance ends, routine State and local budgets must fund inspection, repairs, staffing, power, water, sanitation, access and drills. Asset count is therefore an output; verified readiness is the outcome.

### E. Forecast uncertainty and public trust

Tracks, intensity, rainfall and surge forecasts update as observations change. A forecast-based red warning may not culminate in the maximum impact, but precautionary action can still be justified. Communication should explain probability and revision without labelling every over-forecast a false alarm.

### F. Advanced synthesis

Cyclone resilience joins differentiated hazard information to differentiated protective action. The most defensible evaluation asks not merely whether mortality fell, but whether displacement, livelihood loss, infrastructure downtime, shelter exclusion and ecological degradation also fell without transferring risk elsewhere.

## CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

### Cyclogenesis and hazard components

- Six conditions: warm SST, instability, humidity, Coriolis, disturbance, low vertical shear.
- Eye relatively calm; eyewall holds strongest winds and convection.
- Compound hazards: wind, surge, storm tide, waves, rain, river/urban flood and cascading lifeline failure.
- Storm surge = wind/pressure anomaly above predicted tide; storm tide = surge + astronomical tide.

### IMD classification and warning

- Depression 17–27 kt; Deep Depression 28–33; Cyclonic Storm 34–47.
- Severe 48–63; Very Severe 64–89; Extremely Severe 90–119; Super at least 120 kt.
- Naming begins at Cyclonic Storm.
- Lead-time stages: 72/48/24/12 h.
- Colours: Green/no warning; Yellow/update; Orange/prepare; Red/act.
- Stage ≠ colour; warning ≠ guaranteed impact.

### Institutions

- IMD/RSMC: observation, forecast and bulletins.
- ACWCs: Kolkata, Chennai, Mumbai.
- CWCs: Bhubaneswar, Visakhapatnam, Ahmedabad.
- NDMA/MHA: guidelines and NCRMP history.
- State/district/local authorities: evacuation, shelter operation and local action.
- NDRF/SDRF/ICG/Navy: specialist response as tasked.

### Preparedness portfolio

- impact-specific, multilingual warning;
- assisted evacuation, livestock and transport plan;
- maintained multi-purpose shelters, cattle mounds and all-weather roads;
- resilient housing, power, telecom, water, health, transport and ports;
- saline embankments plus mangroves, dunes, wetlands and vegetation;
- safe return, livelihood recovery and Build Back Better.

### NCRMP and current status

- Category I/II = differentiated vulnerability, not risk/risk-free.
- Phase I closed December 2018; Phase II March 2023.
- Dated 4 February 2025 shelter outputs: Odisha 316; Andhra Pradesh 219; West Bengal 146; Gujarat 76; Kerala 17; Goa 11; Karnataka 10.
- Do not invent NCRMP-III.
- Post-project test: O&M, accessibility, staffing, drills and performance.
- Current coastline planning baseline: 11,098.81 km after 2025 remeasurement.

### Source-period exposure and cascading evidence

- Canonical document-period frame: thirteen cyclone-prone States/UTs across 84 coastal districts.
- Source-period asymmetry: over 58% of Bay of Bengal cyclonic storms approached/crossed the east coast in October–November, against 25% of Arabian Sea storms reaching the west coast; verify against current IMD climatology before using as a present statistic.
- October 1994 Odisha cyclone: historical illustration of cyclone-generated flooding; use as a cascade example without adding unsourced loss numbers.

### Examiner traps

- cyclone ≠ wind alone;
- surge ≠ tide; storm tide combines them;
- eye ≠ strongest-wind zone;
- named storm begins at Cyclonic Storm, not Depression;
- warning stage ≠ colour code;
- Red ≠ certainty;
- bulletin issued ≠ warning received or acted upon;
- shelter built ≠ maintained, accessible shelter;
- Category II ≠ no risk;
- completed project ≠ maintained assets;
- mangrove or embankment ≠ complete protection.

### Final qualified conclusion

Cyclone resilience is not a forecast or shelter count. It is the maintained conversion of multi-hazard forecasts into inclusive evacuation, protected lifelines, ecosystem-aware coastal development and recoveries that reduce the next event's risk.
