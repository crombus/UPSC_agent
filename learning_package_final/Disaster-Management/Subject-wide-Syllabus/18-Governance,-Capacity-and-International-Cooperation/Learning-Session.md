## BASIC LEARNING SESSION

### 1. Governance, coordination, capacity and accountability are not synonyms

| Term | Core question |
|---|---|
| policy | what direction and objectives? |
| governance | who has authority, rules and resources? |
| coordination | how do actors align decisions and information? |
| capacity | can people and systems perform? |
| accountability | who explains results and corrects failure? |

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Coordination without assigned authority diffuses blame; authority without capability produces plans without performance.

### 2. Multi-level architecture and subsidiarity

```text
Union standards/support/escalation
        ↕
State planning/resources/line departments
        ↕
district incident coordination
        ↕
ULB/PRI/service providers/community
        ↕
household and local risk action
```

Subsidiarity places action close to risk while higher levels supply standards, surge capacity, finance and inter-jurisdiction coordination. It does not mean abandoning low-capacity local bodies. ULBs, PRIs, utilities, health systems and districts retain distinct mandates.

### 3. Whole-of-government and whole-of-society

Whole-of-government links departments, levels and public utilities. Whole-of-society includes communities, civil society, volunteers, academia, media, private operators and critical-service providers. Participation does not privatise or dilute the State's duty; private and volunteer roles need standards, safety, data protection and accountability.

### 4. Plans become capability through an operating chain

```text
risk assessment → plan/SOP → assigned people and authority
→ equipment/finance/data → interoperable communication
→ exercise → after-action correction → maintenance/retest
```

A plan proves documented intent. Capability additionally requires trained staff, usable procedures, equipment, finance, authority, exercises and institutional memory. Training attendance is an output; deployable skill requires selection, refresher cycles, supervision, equipment and retention.

### 5. Exercises test only what is designed and corrected

Tabletop, functional and field exercises test different layers. Scenarios should include compound hazards, communication failure, absent staff, surge, vulnerable groups and handover. An exercise validates only the functions actually tested. After-action findings need an owner, deadline, budget and retest; otherwise the drill becomes ceremony.

### 6. Coordination and interoperability

Coordination requires shared terminology, contact routes, data standards, mutual-aid procedures, interoperable communications and lead/support roles before impact. A common operating picture should preserve source confidence and uncertainty rather than merge all reports into false certainty.

Social media can support many-to-many communication and local coordination, as the Hudhud-era WhatsApp example illustrates, but cannot replace authoritative warning infrastructure. Rumours, hacking, outages and exclusion require verified briefings, multi-channel fallback and correction protocols.

### 7. Data, learning and accountability

Risk, loss, expenditure, exercise and after-action data should support review and institutional memory. The 2025 Amendment Act adds a disaster-database definition and related NDMA/SDMA duties. A database or report does not prove data quality, publication, use or correction.

```text
assigned duty → resources/standard → reporting → independent/legislative review
→ grievance → corrective action → retest
```

The National Platform for Disaster Risk Reduction (NPDRR), chaired by the Union Home Minister, reviews, appraises and advises on implementation. It is not a binding enforcement authority over States.

### 8. Yokohama–Hyogo–Sendai evolution

| Framework | Period/form | Exam distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Yokohama Strategy | 1994; ten principles | prevention, preparedness, participation and national responsibility with international support |
| Hyogo Framework for Action | 2005–15; five priorities | first detailed multi-sector action framework |
| Sendai Framework | 2015–30; four priorities, seven targets | measurable loss/capacity targets; broader risk governance |

All are voluntary/non-binding, not treaties.

### 9. Sendai's four priorities and seven targets

**Priorities:** understand risk; strengthen risk governance; invest in resilience; enhance preparedness and Build Back Better.

**Targets A–D:** reduce mortality, affected people, direct economic loss relative to GDP, and critical-infrastructure/basic-service disruption. **Targets E–G:** increase national/local DRR strategies, international cooperation to developing countries, and access to multi-hazard warning/risk information. Target E alone used **2020**; the others use **2030**.

The 2023 Midterm Review's Political Declaration became UNGA resolution **77/289**. It is an accountability and acceleration marker, not proof that targets were achieved.

### 10. UNDRR, GPDRR and international review

UNDRR coordinates the UN DRR agenda. The Global Platform for Disaster Risk Reduction supports review and knowledge exchange and feeds the wider sustainable-development discussion. Neither enforces national law. Their influence is normative, technical, reputational and convening.

### 11. Early Warnings for All

EW4All seeks universal protection from hazardous weather, water and climate events by the end of **2027**. Its pillars are: risk knowledge—UNDRR; detection/observation/forecasting—WMO; dissemination/communication—ITU; preparedness/response—IFRC. WMO and UNDRR co-lead the initiative.

Coverage is not only a sensor or message count. End-to-end effectiveness requires authoritative warning, multi-channel reach, comprehension, accessible action and feedback.

### 12. CDRI's exact cooperation role

India launched CDRI on 23 September 2019. It supports infrastructure risk knowledge, standards, finance and recovery cooperation. It is headquartered in New Delhi and has international-organisation status. Its membership is dynamic, so an unstable snapshot total is omitted.

CDRI membership or a report demonstrates a platform/output, not resilient assets or reduced loss. Domestic utilities and regulators remain responsible for adoption and delivery.

### 13. Regional and bilateral cooperation

SAARC, BIMSTEC, SCO, BRICS, G20 and bilateral routes can support exercises, common procedures, warning, research, training and mutual assistance. The source records a 2015–23 Indian hosting chronology, but those dated events should demonstrate historical convening—not assumed current intensity or local outcome.

Regional cooperation is strongest where hazards cross borders: cyclones, tsunamis, river basins, earthquakes, epidemics and supply chains. Agreements need contact points, request procedures, customs/immigration facilitation, interoperable standards and exercises.

### 14. International response mechanisms and legal status

- **OCHA:** humanitarian coordination support.
- **UNDAC:** rapid assessment, information management and coordination in sudden-onset emergencies, deployed at the affected government's request/consent.
- **INSARAG:** international urban search-and-rescue coordination/classification guidance; supports requested assistance and respects affected-State sovereignty.
- **GPDRR/UNDRR:** review/knowledge, not operational command.
- **CDRI:** infrastructure cooperation, not relief command.

An offer of help does not create automatic entry. The affected State retains sovereignty and national leadership; domestic law, consent, entry/customs rules and applicable agreements govern assistance.

### 15. HADR: sovereignty and humanitarian effectiveness

Humanitarian Assistance and Disaster Relief (HADR) may involve civilian agencies, armed forces, regional partners and international organisations. Disaster-management analysis should focus on request, mission scope, coordination, logistics, medical/search-rescue support, handover and accountability; tactical/security detail belongs to Internal Security.

Sovereignty and timely access are not absolute opposites: pre-agreed procedures, designated contact points and transparent offers reduce delay while preserving consent. Assistance should complement national systems and leave usable capacity, not parallel structures.

### 16. Localisation

Localisation gives national and local responders meaningful leadership, resources, information and voice, while international actors provide requested surge, expertise or finance. Proximity alone does not prove inclusion; local elites can exclude women, minorities, migrants or disabled persons.

```text
global framework/support → national translation → State/district capability
→ local authority/community power + resources → measurable risk outcome
```

The central gap is between international visibility and local capability. Hosting a summit, signing an agreement or joining a coalition cannot substitute for functioning district plans, drills, warnings and services.

### 17. India-specific cooperation evidence and media governance

The canonical source records SAADMex 2015, the 2016 BRICS disaster-management meeting and AMCDRR, SAARC DM Centre/GIDM activity and BIMSTEC DMEx in 2017, IWDRI and an India–Japan workshop in 2018, SCOJtEX 2019, and G20 DRR/World Congress activity in 2023. Use this as a dated 2015–23 convening chronology, not proof of current frequency or local outcomes.

NPDRR's canonical composition includes Union/State political representatives, metro mayors, MPs and ULB representatives; this breadth supports review and advice but not directive enforcement. CDRI's current cooperation outputs include IRIS, the Global Infrastructure Resilience Report series and DRI Connect.

Media can expose preparedness gaps and carry factual public information, but exaggeration and sensationalism can cause panic. Formal briefings, verified updates, uncertainty statements and rumour correction should preserve both scrutiny and operational clarity.

### 18. Cooperation output versus outcome

```text
agreement signed → contact points/rules → exercise/training/data exchange
→ deployable interoperable capability → action during event
→ improved warning/response/recovery → reduced loss
```

Evidence must stop at the verified rung. Meeting attendance, membership and exercise completion are useful outputs; outcomes require capability and risk evidence.

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

### A. Network governance
Disasters cross administrative and sector boundaries, so hierarchy alone is insufficient. Mutual aid and networks need a clear lead, decision rights and escalation to avoid consensus delay.

### B. Adaptive governance
Plans should update through scenario monitoring, after-action learning and reversible decisions. Flexibility must retain legal authority and audit trails.

### C. Localization trade-offs
Direct local finance and leadership improve relevance, but fiduciary, safeguarding and exclusion risks need proportional support rather than recentralisation.

### D. Humanitarian diplomacy
Diplomacy can negotiate access, customs, visas, data and protection before crises. Consent remains necessary; political neutrality does not eliminate accountability.

### E. Metrics and incentives
Counting plans, trainees, drills or members can reward visible activity. Capability metrics should test warning-to-action, interoperability, deployment, correction and local inclusion.

### F. Media governance
Scheduled authoritative briefings, rumour correction, journalist safety and transparent uncertainty can preserve scrutiny without sensationalism or censorship.

## CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

### Domestic governance
- policy, governance, coordination, capacity and accountability answer different questions.
- Subsidiarity: local action plus higher-level standards, finance and surge.
- Plan → people/authority/equipment/finance/data → exercise → correction → maintenance.
- NPDRR reviews/advises; it does not direct States.

### Global framework
- Yokohama 1994: 10 principles; Hyogo 2005–15: 5 priorities; Sendai 2015–30: 4 priorities + 7 targets.
- Sendai is voluntary; E alone had 2020 deadline.
- Midterm Review declaration = UNGA 77/289.
- EW4All: 2027; UNDRR/WMO/ITU/IFRC pillars.

### Mechanism/status map
- UNDRR/GPDRR = coordination/review; UNDAC = requested assessment/coordination; INSARAG = requested USAR coordination standards; CDRI = infrastructure cooperation.
- CDRI membership is dynamic; use a dated official source if a count is specifically required.
- International aid requires affected-State consent and domestic coordination.

### Localisation/accountability traps
- participation ≠ transfer of State duty; proximity ≠ inclusion.
- agreement/summit/membership ≠ implementation/capability/outcome.
- database/report/drill ≠ corrective action.
- international leadership and domestic localisation are separate evidence categories.

### Qualified conclusion
International cooperation is a capacity multiplier, not a substitute for sovereign responsibility. It earns value only when standards, warning, finance and surge support become interoperable, inclusive local capability and measurable risk reduction.
