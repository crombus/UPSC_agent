## BASIC LEARNING SESSION

### 1. Outbreak, epidemic, pandemic and endemic are not synonyms

| Term | Exam-ready meaning | Critical caution |
|---|---|---|
| **Outbreak** | cases above the expected level in a defined place, group or period; often used for a localised epidemic | depends on baseline and surveillance |
| **Epidemic** | disease occurrence clearly above normal expectancy in a community or region | does not require international spread |
| **Pandemic** | an epidemic spreading across countries/continents with wide population reach | geographic spread does not itself measure severity |
| **Endemic** | usual or persistent presence in a population/area | does not mean harmless or incapable of outbreaks |
| **Public-health emergency** | event threatening population health and requiring coordinated exceptional action | domestic legal status is separate from WHO status |

A **PHEIC** under the International Health Regulations is an extraordinary event determined to pose public-health risk to other States through international spread and potentially require coordinated international response. The amended IHR's **pandemic emergency** is a higher alert for a qualifying communicable-disease PHEIC; it is not a synonym for every pandemic.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Epidemiological labels describe occurrence and spread; legal emergency labels determine authority and coordination, and neither alone measures severity.

**Must-write keywords:** baseline · outbreak · epidemic · pandemic · endemic · PHEIC · pandemic emergency · severity.

**Paragraph model:** *Claim:* “pandemic” does not mean “most lethal.” *Evidence:* the label principally reflects wide international spread. *Analysis:* severity and system strain require separate indicators. *Qualification:* WHO/IHR and domestic emergency determinations follow distinct legal tests.

### 2. Biological-hazard risk requires route, severity and capacity

```text
AGENT / TOXIN + RESERVOIR
          ↓
route: water/food · vector · respiratory/contact · blood/sexual · zoonotic
          ↓
exposure pattern + susceptibility + immunity
          ↓
transmission/severity + health-system capacity
          ↓
outbreak consequence
```

The local source groups water/food-borne, vector-borne, person-to-person and airborne/fomite routes, with examples such as cholera, dengue, malaria, influenza and measles. A biological disaster may be natural, accidental or deliberate; deliberate biological operations remain an Internal Security boundary.

**Hazard** is the biological agent/toxin and transmission potential. **Exposure** is contact through the relevant route. **Vulnerability** includes unsafe water, crowding, age/comorbidity, low immunity, poverty and weak access. **Capacity** includes surveillance, laboratories, primary care, workforce, supplies, communication and trust.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Public-health risk is pathogen × exposure × vulnerability moderated by immunity, behaviour and health-system capacity.

**Must-write keywords:** reservoir · route of transmission · susceptibility · immunity · attack pattern · health-system capacity · uncertainty.

**Paragraph model:** *Claim:* the same pathogen can produce unequal disaster. *Evidence:* unsafe water concentrates enteric exposure while crowded housing increases respiratory exposure. *Analysis:* social conditions shape transmission and care access. *Qualification:* association does not establish individual causation without investigation.

### 3. Surveillance is an action cycle, not case counting

```text
SIGNAL / S-P-L REPORT / MEDIA OR LAB ALERT
        ↓
verify + analyse trend, place, person and time
        ↓
Rapid Response Team investigation
        ↓
case definition + sampling + source/contact/environment inquiry
        ↓
proportionate control + clinical response + communication
        ↓
feedback, evaluation and surveillance adjustment
```

NCDC's IDSP describes decentralised, laboratory-based, IT-enabled surveillance through Central, State and District Surveillance Units. Its **S, P and L forms** record suspected cases (health workers), presumptive cases (clinicians) and laboratory-confirmed cases (laboratory staff). Rising trends are investigated by Rapid Response Teams.

Syndromic data may be early but nonspecific; laboratory confirmation is specific but can be delayed by sampling, transport and capacity. Media/event signals can identify unusual events but require verification.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Surveillance succeeds when a signal triggers verified local action; a dashboard that accumulates late data is not early warning.

**Must-write keywords:** IDSP/IHIP · S/P/L forms · signal · case definition · RRT · laboratory turnaround · feedback.

**Paragraph model:** *Claim:* laboratory confirmation alone may arrive too late for first action. *Evidence:* IDSP combines suspected, presumptive and confirmed reporting. *Analysis:* graded evidence allows early investigation while preserving diagnostic certainty. *Qualification:* provisional signals must be updated transparently.

### 4. One Health joins sectors without erasing mandates

```text
HUMAN HEALTH ─┐
ANIMAL HEALTH ├─→ shared surveillance/risk assessment → joint outbreak response
ENVIRONMENT ──┘
food · water · wildlife · vectors · antimicrobial resistance interfaces
```

One Health recognises that human, domestic-animal, wildlife and environmental health are interdependent. NCDC's current framework includes zoonoses programmes and intersectoral coordination; IDSP also names zoonotic coordination. The local source's 2006 avian-influenza lesson and 1994 Surat plague lesson show why health, animal husbandry, sanitation, home, communication and media cannot work in silos.

MoHFW/NCDC, State health departments, animal-health authorities, laboratories, local bodies, water/sanitation agencies and environment/wildlife authorities retain their own expertise and accountability.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** One Health is a coordination architecture across interfaces, not the transfer of all animal and environmental functions to the health ministry.

**Must-write keywords:** zoonosis · spillover · human–animal–environment interface · joint risk assessment · intersectoral coordination.

**Paragraph model:** *Claim:* a zoonotic signal should not wait inside one department. *Evidence:* NCDC/IDSP explicitly provide intersectoral coordination for zoonotic disease. *Analysis:* animal and human signals can reveal risk earlier together. *Qualification:* data-sharing needs lawful purpose, standards and attribution discipline.

### 5. Prevention and mitigation are route-specific

| Transmission/risk route | Prevention/mitigation |
|---|---|
| water/food | safe water, sanitation, food safety, surveillance and rapid source control |
| vector | habitat/source reduction, vector surveillance and context-appropriate control |
| respiratory/contact | ventilation, hygiene, risk-based masking/PPE, isolation and crowd/workplace measures |
| vaccine-preventable | routine immunisation, coverage-gap detection, outbreak response vaccination where indicated |
| zoonotic | animal surveillance, biosecurity, occupational protection and One Health coordination |
| post-disaster | safe water, waste/carcass management, shelter health and continuity of primary care |

Prevention reduces probability or exposure; **preparedness** builds capability before the event. Vaccination, isolation, quarantine and movement measures must be legally authorised, evidence-based and appropriate to the disease and stage.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Disease control must interrupt the actual transmission route; generic sanitation or lockdown lists are not a substitute for epidemiological diagnosis.

**Must-write keywords:** WASH · vector control · vaccination · ventilation · isolation · quarantine · biosecurity · proportionality.

**Paragraph model:** *Claim:* flood relief can become epidemic prevention. *Evidence:* the local source links contaminated water and inadequate carcass/waste disposal to post-disaster disease. *Analysis:* WASH and surveillance interrupt secondary disaster. *Qualification:* outbreaks should not be presumed without evidence.

### 6. Preparedness is capacities held before case surge

Preparedness includes:

- current plans, contact lists, legal decision paths and emergency operations;
- trained epidemiologists, microbiologists, clinicians, nurses, laboratory and field staff;
- sample transport, biosafety, quality assurance and surge laboratory networks;
- scalable beds, oxygen, medicines, PPE, vaccination and referral logistics;
- infection-prevention controls and health-worker protection;
- continuity plans for maternal, child, chronic, emergency and mental-health care;
- communication, community engagement and misinformation response;
- procurement, stock rotation and mutual aid;
- exercises followed by corrective-action closure.

The local source identifies district/sub-district workforce and laboratory/primary-health infrastructure gaps as structural constraints that pre-dated COVID-19.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Preparedness is not a warehouse; it is trained, connected and exercised capacity that can scale without collapsing routine care.

**Must-write keywords:** surge capacity · stock rotation · referral network · IPC · workforce reserve · continuity plan · exercise.

**Paragraph model:** *Claim:* stockpiles can fail despite adequate purchase. *Evidence:* supplies need forecasting, storage, rotation and distribution. *Analysis:* last-mile logistics determine availability. *Qualification:* quantity without demand, expiry and deployment data is not readiness.

### 7. Outbreak response follows investigation, control and care

```text
detect signal → verify outbreak → define cases
→ describe person/place/time → test hypotheses
→ laboratory/environment/animal investigation
→ control source and transmission
→ clinical surge + protect health workers
→ communicate uncertainty → monitor effect → adapt
```

An incident-management structure should integrate surveillance, laboratories, clinical care, logistics, WASH/vector/animal/environment functions, public information and administration. **Isolation** separates ill/infected persons as clinically and legally appropriate; **quarantine** restricts or monitors exposed persons who are not yet ill, subject to law and proportionality.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Outbreak response is iterative: act on the best available evidence, measure effect and revise as uncertainty narrows.

**Must-write keywords:** case definition · line list · contact · isolation · quarantine · RRT · incident management · adaptive response.

**Paragraph model:** *Claim:* early measures need not await perfect certainty. *Evidence:* IDSP RRTs investigate rising trends. *Analysis:* delay can permit transmission, while review limits overreach. *Qualification:* provisional decisions require transparent criteria and sunset/reassessment.

### 8. Public-health authority is federal and function-specific

| Level/actor | Principal role | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **State governments** | public health, hospitals and service delivery are principally State responsibilities | national coordination remains possible |
| **MoHFW** | national policy, advisories, programme and intergovernmental coordination | does not run every district response |
| **NCDC / IDSP** | surveillance architecture, technical guidance, outbreak support and RRT ecosystem | data/technical role is not all executive authority |
| **ICMR and laboratories** | research, diagnostics and scientific guidance within remit | not district incident command |
| **district/local bodies** | investigation, WASH/vector control, care, local restrictions and community action under law | capacity varies and needs State support |
| **MHA/NDMA** | general disaster coordination when the DM Act is lawfully invoked; MHA is the source-identified nodal ministry for biological-warfare consequences | hostile-operation detail remains an Internal Security boundary |

Constitutionally, **State List Entry 6** covers public health and sanitation; **Concurrent List Entry 29** covers prevention of inter-State spread of infectious/contagious disease affecting humans, animals or plants. The canonical source associates the DM Act's general legal basis with **Concurrent List Entry 23** on social security and social insurance. Federalism requires national standards and pooled resources with locally calibrated implementation.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Health is State-led but epidemics cross borders; effective federalism combines national coordination with accountable local public-health execution.

**Must-write keywords:** State List Entry 6 · Concurrent List Entry 29 · MoHFW · NCDC · IDSP · district · cooperative federalism.

**Paragraph model:** *Claim:* State responsibility does not mean Union absence. *Evidence:* inter-State disease control lies in the Concurrent List and COVID used national DM coordination. *Analysis:* standards and logistics can be national while field action remains local. *Qualification:* powers must be traced to the actual law/order used.

### 9. Indian legal framework: permanent statute versus temporary measure

```text
Epidemic Diseases Act 1897 (current text includes 2020 amendment)
  ├─ State special measures for dangerous epidemic disease
  └─ Union power concerning ships/ports under the Act

Disaster Management Act 2005
  └─ used for nationwide COVID coordination/orders
     ↓
MHA decision March 2022:
no further COVID-containment orders under DM Act after expiry

LESSON → temporary direction/lockdown ≠ permanent public-health law
```

The Epidemic Diseases Act remains on India Code and was amended in 2020, including protections relating to violence against healthcare service personnel. The local source's recommendation to replace the 1897 Act is not evidence that repeal occurred.

COVID-19 demonstrated the reach of the DM Act for whole-of-government coordination. It also demonstrated the risk of using a generic disaster law for a health emergency requiring graded epidemiological measures, legislative safeguards, review and health-sector leadership. MHA's March 2022 communication ended further DM Act COVID-containment directions after the then order expired.

Likewise, the local source's statements that India had “no policy” on biological disasters and an old contingency plan are document-period criticism, not current-status findings. Current answers should use NCDC/IDSP, NDMA and MoHFW material rather than repeat those lines as present fact.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** COVID emergency orders were time-bound exercises of existing power, not permanent amendments authorising indefinite lockdown by default.

**Must-write keywords:** Epidemic Diseases Act 1897 · 2020 amendment · DM Act 2005 · temporary order · sunset · review · proportionality.

**Paragraph model:** *Claim:* a past lockdown cannot be cited as standing policy. *Evidence:* MHA discontinued COVID-specific DM Act containment orders in March 2022. *Analysis:* future restrictions need a current legal basis and risk assessment. *Qualification:* States may still use applicable health law and lawful local powers.

### 10. Risk communication is a two-way public-health intervention

Credible communication should:

1. state what is known, uncertain and changing;
2. give specific actions by audience and place;
3. use trusted local messengers, languages and accessible formats;
4. explain why measures change;
5. correct misinformation without stigmatising people or places;
6. publish regular updates and channels for questions;
7. protect privacy and avoid identifiable case disclosure;
8. measure reach, comprehension, trust and action—not posts issued.

Fear-based communication may hide symptoms or contacts. Overconfidence damages trust when advice changes. Uncertainty should be explained, not concealed.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Risk communication is successful when people can understand and act, not when an authority has merely transmitted a message.

**Must-write keywords:** transparency · uncertainty · trusted messenger · two-way communication · stigma · privacy · infodemic.

**Paragraph model:** *Claim:* changing advice need not imply incompetence. *Evidence:* surveillance and evidence evolve during outbreaks. *Analysis:* explaining the reason and uncertainty preserves trust. *Qualification:* unexplained reversals and inconsistent enforcement still require accountability.

### 11. Continuity and equity are core response tests

| Risk group/system | Typical barrier | Required protection |
|---|---|---|
| migrant/informal workers | income loss, crowded housing, mobility and documentation gaps | food/cash, safe transport, shelter and portable access |
| women/children | care burden, violence risk, maternal/child service disruption | safe services, continuity and gender-responsive support |
| older/disabled/chronically ill people | access, communication and treatment interruption | accessible alerts, medicines, carers and referral continuity |
| health/frontline workers | exposure, fatigue, stigma and moral distress | PPE/IPC, staffing, rest, insurance/support and transparent duty allocation |
| non-outbreak patients | postponed diagnosis/treatment | protected essential-care pathways |
| digitally excluded groups | app, language, device or connectivity barrier | assisted/offline channels and human outreach |

Uniform restriction can create unequal harm. Measures should be necessary, proportionate, time-limited, reviewed and accompanied by support. Public and private health facilities share duties to patients and staff, though financing and mandate differ.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** An epidemic measure that suppresses transmission but collapses food, income or essential care transfers rather than manages risk.

**Must-write keywords:** continuity of essential services · proportionality · least restrictive · social protection · digital divide · health-worker safety.

**Paragraph model:** *Claim:* movement restrictions have health effects beyond infection. *Evidence:* COVID exposed migrant transport and non-COVID care disruptions. *Analysis:* social protection and continuity are part of disease control because they enable compliance and prevent secondary harm. *Qualification:* calibrate to local risk.

### 12. Technology supports public-health functions; it does not replace them

| Function | Technology examples | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| detection/diagnosis | RT-PCR and laboratory networks | sampling, quality, turnaround and access |
| surveillance | IDSP/IHIP data, dashboards and analytics | under-reporting, interoperability and interpretation |
| exposure notification | digital contact/risk tools such as Aarogya Setu in COVID | smartphone access, accuracy, privacy and adoption |
| clinical continuity | telemedicine and remote consultation | examination, connectivity and exclusion limits |
| logistics | inventory, oxygen/bed/supply dashboards | data quality and physical delivery |
| vaccination | CoWIN in the vaccine phase | digital access and actual service availability |
| research | sequencing, modelling and data platforms | model uncertainty and representativeness |

The 2020 direct PYQ asks how technology was **sought** to aid management, not whether it solved the pandemic. Each tool should be linked to a function and limitation.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** A health technology creates value only when reliable data, trained people, supplies and equitable access complete its service pathway.

**Must-write keywords:** RT-PCR · IDSP/IHIP · Aarogya Setu · CoWIN · telemedicine · interoperability · privacy · digital divide.

**Paragraph model:** *Claim:* an app is not contact tracing by itself. *Evidence:* digital proximity signals require testing, human follow-up and isolation support. *Analysis:* technology can accelerate a workflow but cannot supply trust or care. *Qualification:* privacy and exclusion safeguards are essential.

### 13. Recovery means restored health functions and learned institutions

Recovery includes surveillance for recurrence and sequelae; backlog clearance; restored routine immunisation, maternal, chronic and mental-health care; workforce recovery; education and livelihood restoration; evaluation of excess morbidity/mortality; procurement and stockpile reform; and memorialised lessons.

```text
acute control
   ↓
safe reopening / continuity restoration
   ↓
backlogs + long-term health + livelihood support
   ↓
after-action review
   ↓
law/plan/workforce/lab/supply reform
```

Cases falling is not proof that every measure caused the decline. Recovery attribution needs counterfactual caution and disaggregated evidence.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Public-health recovery is the restoration of essential capabilities and trust, not merely the withdrawal of restrictions.

**Must-write keywords:** sequelae · backlog · excess mortality · workforce recovery · after-action review · safe reopening · institutional learning.

**Paragraph model:** *Claim:* ending an emergency order does not end the emergency's effects. *Evidence:* delayed care, learning loss and livelihood damage can persist. *Analysis:* recovery needs cross-sector indicators. *Qualification:* distinguish outbreak trend from policy-attributed outcome.

### 14. IHR, PHEIC, pandemic emergency and Pandemic Agreement

| Instrument/status | Verified position on 27 Sep 2026 | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **IHR (2005), amended 2024** | amendments entered into force 19 Sep 2025 for States Parties not rejecting them; introduced National IHR Authorities and “pandemic emergency” | WHO cannot compel domestic lockdowns or policy |
| **PHEIC** | extraordinary event posing international-spread risk and potentially requiring coordinated international response; WHO Director-General determines under IHR | not identical to domestic emergency or pandemic |
| **pandemic emergency** | higher alert within a qualifying communicable-disease PHEIC | not a replacement for PHEIC |
| **WHO Pandemic Agreement** | adopted 20 May 2025 | adopted ≠ open for signature ≠ ratified ≠ in force |
| **PABS annex** | negotiations still ongoing in WHO report dated 18 Sep 2026 | Agreement opens for signature only after annex adoption; 60 ratifications needed for entry into force |

IHR aims to control international spread while avoiding unnecessary interference with traffic and trade. International coordination does not remove national sovereignty or domestic legal safeguards.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Global health law coordinates notification and cooperation; it does not hand WHO a power to legislate or impose lockdowns within States.

**Must-write keywords:** IHR · PHEIC · pandemic emergency · National IHR Authority · sovereignty · Pandemic Agreement · PABS · ratification.

**Paragraph model:** *Claim:* the Pandemic Agreement is not currently binding. *Evidence:* WHO says PABS negotiations were continuing on 18 September 2026 and signature follows annex adoption. *Analysis:* adoption is only one treaty stage. *Qualification:* recheck status at the exam date.

NCDC's current IDSP page confirms Central, State and District Surveillance Units, S/P/L reporting, RRT investigation, laboratory strengthening, media verification, metropolitan units and intersectoral zoonotic coordination. These establish architecture and reported activity, not uniform timeliness, completeness or outbreak control.

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

### A. Threshold and denominator politics

Reported cases depend on testing, care-seeking, definitions and reporting completeness. Positivity, admissions, severity, excess mortality and syndromic signals answer different questions. Decision thresholds should be transparent and revised as data quality changes.

### B. Legal calibration

Emergency powers can accelerate coordination but risk overbreadth. Measures should have a lawful purpose, scientific basis, necessity, proportionality, non-discrimination, review and sunset. Temporary COVID orders are precedents to evaluate, not permanent default law.

### C. Surveillance–privacy trade-off

Named or linkable data can accelerate contact and service delivery but create exclusion, function creep and stigma. Collect the minimum necessary, control access, state retention limits and preserve assisted/offline alternatives.

### D. One Health governance problem

Joint committees alone do not ensure shared incentives or interoperable data. Define triggers, ownership, laboratory referral, attribution standards and financing across human, animal, food, water and wildlife systems.

### E. Continuity under surge

Beds, oxygen and staff diverted to one disease can increase mortality elsewhere. Protected pathways, reserve rosters, referral coordination and essential-medicine continuity make containment sustainable.

### F. International equity

The Pandemic Agreement's unresolved PABS annex concentrates the real bargain: rapid pathogen-material/sequence sharing versus timely, fair access to diagnostics, therapeutics and vaccines. India's producer and public-health interests span both sides.

### G. HEPR systems lens

WHO's Health Emergency Prevention, Preparedness, Response and Resilience framework, reported to WHA78 in May 2025, treats surveillance, collaborative protection, scalable care, access to countermeasures and emergency coordination as connected systems. It is a benchmark, not evidence that any country has implemented each capacity.

### H. Accountability dashboard

Track signal-to-verification time, test turnaround, RRT deployment, effective reproduction/severity trends where appropriate, health-worker infection, bed/oxygen continuity, non-outbreak service disruption, message comprehension, social-protection reach, inequity, recovery backlog and after-action reform. Do not use app downloads or orders issued as outcome proxies.

## CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

### Definitions and statuses

- outbreak = above expected in defined place/group/time.
- epidemic = above expected in community/region.
- pandemic = wide international spread; not severity label.
- endemic = usual/persistent presence; not harmlessness.
- domestic emergency ≠ PHEIC ≠ pandemic emergency.
- PHEIC determined under IHR by WHO Director-General.

### Risk and routes

- hazard/agent + route + exposure + vulnerability − capacity.
- water/food, vector, respiratory/contact, blood/sexual and zoonotic routes.
- post-disaster WASH, waste/carcass and shelter failures can produce secondary outbreaks.
- deliberate biological operations are an Internal Security boundary.

### Surveillance

- IDSP: Central/State/District units; laboratory-based IT-enabled system.
- S = suspected/health worker; P = presumptive/clinician; L = laboratory confirmed.
- signal → verify → RRT → case definition/investigation → control → feedback.
- syndromic speed and laboratory specificity must be combined.

### One Health

- human + animal + environment interfaces.
- NCDC/IDSP zoonotic coordination; Surat 1994 and avian influenza 2006 are source lessons.
- joint action must retain sector mandates and data standards.

### Prevention, response, recovery

- prevention: WASH, vector control, vaccination, ventilation/IPC, biosecurity.
- preparedness: workforce, labs, supplies, surge, continuity, drills.
- response: investigate, isolate cases, quarantine exposed where lawful, care and communicate.
- recovery: sequelae, backlogs, routine services, livelihoods, workforce and learning.

### Authority and law

- health/public health: State List Entry 6.
- inter-State infectious-disease prevention: Concurrent List Entry 29.
- canonical DM Act basis: Concurrent List Entry 23; use with the Act/order actually invoked.
- MoHFW/NCDC coordinate technically; States/districts execute services and local action.
- MHA is the source-identified nodal ministry for biological-warfare consequences; operational security detail stays outside this topic.
- Epidemic Diseases Act 1897 remains; 2020 amendment is statutory.
- DM Act 2005 was used for COVID coordination.
- COVID-specific DM Act containment directions ended after March 2022; lockdown is not permanent law.

### Technology

- RT-PCR/labs: diagnosis; IDSP/IHIP: surveillance; Aarogya Setu: contact/risk tool; CoWIN: vaccine-phase delivery; telemedicine/logistics systems: continuity.
- app/dashboard ≠ data quality, access, action or outcome.
- privacy, interoperability, language and digital access are integral.

### International framework

- amended IHR entered into force 19 Sep 2025 for applicable States Parties.
- new pandemic-emergency alert; PHEIC remains distinct.
- WHO cannot compel national lockdowns.
- Pandemic Agreement adopted 20 May 2025.
- as of 18 Sep 2026 PABS annex negotiations continued; not yet open for signature/in force.
- entry into force requires 60 ratifications after signature stage opens.

### PYQ routes

- direct: 2020 GS-III Q15 technology in COVID management.
- shared: 2020 GS-II Q9 WHO; 2026 GS-III Q18 community participation.
- application: 2020 GS-III Q18 proactive DM; 2023 Prelims Q75 health-system orientation/federal role; 2024 GS-III Q17 resilience.

### Qualified conclusion

Public-health resilience joins route-specific prevention, sensitive surveillance, One Health and federal coordination, proportionate law, trusted communication, equitable continuity and recovery—without mistaking a temporary order, digital platform or treaty adoption for permanent authority or proven outcome.
