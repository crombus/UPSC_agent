## BASIC MCQS / REMEDIATION

### Q1. Why are drought and cumulative heat described as slow-onset risks?

A. Their effects accumulate across water, health, livelihoods and ecosystems before full visibility.
B. Their physical triggers occur at one instant and end before institutions respond.
C. Their impacts remain confined to weather stations and do not reach households.
D. Their declaration precedes any measurable environmental or social stress.

**Answer: A**

- **A — Correct.** Cumulative impacts and delayed recognition define the governance problem.
- **B — Incorrect.** This describes a sudden-onset pattern.
- **C — Incorrect.** Loss propagates through multiple systems.
- **D — Incorrect.** Formal recognition may follow earlier stress.

**Examiner trap:** Slow onset does not mean low consequence.

### Q2. Which condition best represents meteorological drought?

A. Reservoir storage falls after several deficient seasons.
B. Rainfall remains prolongedly inadequate or badly distributed.
C. Soil moisture cannot meet a crop's growth-stage requirement.
D. Ecosystem productivity falls because water stress persists.

**Answer: B**

- **A — Incorrect.** This is hydrological drought.
- **B — Correct.** Rainfall deficiency or maldistribution defines the meteorological type.
- **C — Incorrect.** This is agricultural drought.
- **D — Incorrect.** This is ecological drought.

**Examiner trap:** Name the drought type before selecting the indicator.

### Q3. Which observation most directly indicates agricultural drought?

A. Annual river flow falls below a reservoir operating target.
B. A forest loses productivity after prolonged ecosystem stress.
C. Soil moisture is inadequate for sowing or crop growth.
D. Weekly rainfall at one gauge falls below normal.

**Answer: C**

- **A — Incorrect.** This is hydrological stress.
- **B — Incorrect.** This indicates ecological drought.
- **C — Correct.** Crop-root-zone moisture is the defining indicator.
- **D — Incorrect.** This is a meteorological observation.

**Examiner trap:** Agricultural drought can occur even when annual rainfall alone looks less extreme.

### Q4. Which pair is correctly matched?

A. Hydrological drought—shortage of crop-root-zone moisture.
B. Ecological drought—departure of maximum temperature from normal.
C. Meteorological drought—reduced reservoir and aquifer storage.
D. Hydrological drought—water bodies and aquifers below replenishable availability.

**Answer: D**

- **A — Incorrect.** Crop-root-zone moisture defines agricultural drought.
- **B — Incorrect.** Temperature departure concerns heat-wave criteria.
- **C — Incorrect.** Rainfall deficiency defines meteorological drought.
- **D — Correct.** It identifies storage and groundwater stress.

**Examiner trap:** Hydrological drought often lags rainfall deficiency.

### Q5. Which sequence best represents a common drought cascade?

A. Rainfall deficit → soil-moisture stress → crop loss → storage/ecosystem stress.
B. Reservoir recovery → rainfall deficit → crop surplus → ecological recovery.
C. Heat alert → cyclone surge → aquifer recharge → crop failure.
D. Flood warning → groundwater decline → snowfall → drought declaration.

**Answer: A**

- **A — Correct.** It follows the meteorological-to-system cascade.
- **B — Incorrect.** The direction of change is inconsistent.
- **C — Incorrect.** It joins unrelated mechanisms.
- **D — Incorrect.** It does not form a defensible drought sequence.

**Examiner trap:** The cascade is common but timing varies by storage, irrigation and demand.

**Examiner trap:** Declaration does not rest on rainfall alone.

### Q9. Which strategy best reflects drought water budgeting?

- **B — Incorrect.** Tankers alone can mask worsening source stress.
- **C — Incorrect.** Unchecked extraction deepens hydrological drought.
- **D — Incorrect.** A water body depends on its catchment system.

**Examiner trap:** Supply augmentation without demand reform can intensify scarcity.

### Q10. Why is groundwater central to slow-onset drought?

- **B — Correct.** Extraction and slow recharge create multi-season vulnerability.
- **C — Incorrect.** Groundwater supports both uses widely.
- **D — Incorrect.** Heat-wave criteria use temperature observations.

**Examiner trap:** A normal monsoon does not instantly repair an overdrawn aquifer.

- **B — Incorrect.** Agronomic suitability and livelihood returns matter.
- **C — Correct.** This addresses both water and farmer viability.
- **D — Incorrect.** Emergency supply does not correct the incentive.

**Examiner trap:** Crop change is an economic transition, not a technical instruction alone.

### Q12. Which action best represents anticipatory social protection during drought?

- **B — Incorrect.** Vulnerability extends beyond titled irrigated farmers.
- **C — Incorrect.** Those are late distress indicators.
- **D — Correct.** It protects assets and human development before collapse.

**Examiner trap:** Slow onset creates time for early action; delayed relief wastes that advantage.

### PYQ 1 — 2025 PRELIMS GS-I Q64 — DIRECT

**Question — official Set-A wording with line breaks normalised:**

The World Bank warned that India could become one of the first places where wet-bulb temperatures routinely exceed 35°C. Which of the following statements best reflect(s) the implication of the above-said report?

1. Peninsular India will most likely suffer from flooding, tropical cyclones and droughts.
2. The survival of animals including humans will be affected as shedding of their body heat through perspiration becomes difficult.

Select the correct answer using the code given below.

A. 1 only
B. 2 only
C. Both 1 and 2
D. Neither 1 nor 2

**Official Set-A answer: B**

- **A — Incorrect.** Statement 1 lists hazards not logically implied by crossing the wet-bulb threshold.
- **B — Correct.** High wet bulb limits evaporative cooling through perspiration and threatens animals and humans.
- **C — Incorrect.** Statement 1 does not explain the physiological implication.
- **D — Incorrect.** Statement 2 is correct.

**Evidence caution:** Around 35°C wet bulb is a theoretical upper heat-dissipation benchmark, not IMD's heat-wave declaration criterion and not a safe/unsafe cliff for every person.

### PYQ 2 — 2020 GS-I Q14 — SHARED

**Question — exact English wording reconstructed from official local OCR:** “The interlinking of rivers can provide viable solutions to the multi-dimensional inter-related problems of droughts, floods and interrupted navigation. Critically examine.” **(15 marks, 250 words)**

**Model answer (within 250 words):**

River interlinking seeks to transfer water from perceived surplus basins to deficit basins, create storage and support navigation. It may provide seasonal drought relief, irrigation and drinking water; reservoirs and transfers may moderate selected floods; and regulated flows may improve some navigation reaches.

Its viability is conditional. “Surplus” and “deficit” vary by season, ecological need and climate. Large storage cannot control every extreme flood and may shift peaks downstream. Transfers alter sediment, floodplain fertility, wetlands, fisheries and river connectivity. Reservoirs cause displacement and evaporation; canals require energy and land. Inter-State and international basins create allocation disputes, while high capital and maintenance costs may crowd out local watershed, aquifer and demand-management solutions.

For drought, the first priorities should include crop-water alignment, groundwater accounting, tank and wetland restoration, micro-irrigation where suitable, reuse and anticipatory livelihood support. For floods, floodplain zoning, basin forecasts, wetland storage, transparent reservoir rule curves and downstream warnings remain necessary even with links. Navigation needs minimum ecological flows and terminal/logistics systems, not water transfer alone.

Therefore, interlinking can be one basin-specific option after transparent hydrological, social, economic and ecological appraisal. It is not a universal substitute for decentralised water management or climate-resilient basin governance.

**Why this earns marks:** It assesses benefits and trade-offs across all three demands and offers a qualified, multi-scale verdict.

### PYQ 3 — 2024 GS-III Q17 — APPLICATION

**Question — local official-paper OCR reproduced verbatim except joined line breaks:** “What is disaster resilience? How is it determined? Describe various elements of a resilience framework. Also mention the global targests of Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction (2015-2030)” **(15 marks, 250 words)**

**OCR note:** `targests` is retained from the official local extraction and means “targets.”

**Model answer (within 250 words):**

Disaster resilience is the ability of a community or system to resist, absorb, adapt to, respond to and recover from hazards while maintaining or restoring essential functions. It is determined through hazard, exposure, vulnerability and usable capacity, verified by outcomes.

For slow-onset drought and heat, a resilience framework includes multi-indicator monitoring; early declaration or HAP activation; water budgeting; crop and livelihood contingency; groundwater and ecosystem protection; occupational rest-water-shade protocols; accessible cooling; trained health facilities; social protection; and long-term cool housing and urban design. Wet-bulb physiology, IMD heat criteria and the Drought Manual serve different purposes and should not be conflated. National relief eligibility is useful, but it does not prove ward-level delivery or reduced illness.

Sendai’s targets are to reduce **A** mortality, **B** affected people, **C** direct economic loss relative to global GDP and **D** critical-infrastructure damage/basic-service disruption; and increase **E** national/local DRR strategies, **F** international cooperation for developing countries and **G** access to multi-hazard warning and risk information.

Sendai is voluntary and non-binding. Slow-onset resilience is demonstrated when early support prevents distress migration, crop/livestock loss and avoidable heat illness while long-term adaptation lowers exposure.

**Why this earns marks:** It answers every clause, names all seven targets and applies resilience to the distinctive timing of drought and heat.

## ORIGINAL MAINS PRACTICE

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Distinguish meteorological, agricultural, hydrological and ecological drought and explain why the distinction matters. **Answer in 150 words.**

**Model answer (within 150 words):**

**Meteorological drought** is prolonged inadequate or maldistributed rainfall. **Agricultural drought** occurs when soil moisture cannot meet crop needs, affecting sowing, growth or yield. **Hydrological drought** appears when river flow, reservoirs, lakes or groundwater fall below replenishable demand. **Ecological drought** reduces ecosystem productivity and function through persistent water stress.

The types often form a cascade, but storage, irrigation, soils, crops and demand alter timing. Consequently, one rainfall percentage cannot guide the whole response. Meteorological monitoring supports early warning; crop and soil-moisture data guide advisories and loss assessment; reservoir and aquifer data guide water allocation; vegetation and habitat indicators guide ecological protection.

India's Drought Manual therefore uses rainfall, vegetation/crop condition, sowing progress, soil moisture and hydrological indicators. Correct classification prevents delayed or misdirected relief and connects declaration to water, agriculture, livelihood and ecosystem measures.

**Why this earns marks:** It defines all four types, explains their relationship and derives the governance consequence.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 2 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Distinguish IMD heat-wave declaration criteria from wet-bulb temperature as a heat-health metric. **Answer in 150 words.**

**Model answer (within 150 words):**

IMD heat-wave declaration uses **dry-bulb maximum temperature**, departure from the station's normal and persistence. Entry requires at least 40°C in plains or 30°C in hills; coastal stations need at least 37°C and a departure of 4.5°C. Departure of 4.5–6.4°C is Heat Wave and above 6.4°C Severe; actual maxima of at least 45°C and 47°C are independent triggers. Criteria must occur at two stations in a subdivision for two days.

**Wet-bulb temperature** combines heat and humidity by showing the lowest temperature achievable through evaporation. It therefore indicates whether sweating can cool the body. Around 35°C is a theoretical upper benchmark under protective assumptions; dangerous heat stress occurs below it.

Thus IMD criteria classify a meteorological event, whereas wet bulb helps assess physiological danger. Neither should replace vulnerability, work-intensity, housing, water or health evidence.

**Why this earns marks:** It gives exact official criteria and preserves the declaration-versus-physiology boundary.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 3 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Evaluate Heat Action Plans as instruments for reducing heat-wave mortality and livelihood loss. **Answer in 250 words.**

**Model answer (within 250 words):**

Heat Action Plans convert forecasts into coordinated local prevention. Ahmedabad's 2013 plan is the canonical first example; by June 2025, official information recorded more than 250 cities and districts across 23 heat-prone States with HAPs.

A complete HAP integrates four capacities: forecasting/early warning, health-professional training, community outreach and inter-agency/civil-society coordination. Immediate measures include targeted alerts, work and school timing, water, shade/cooling spaces, transport assistance, clinical triage, supplies and surveillance. Long-term measures include cool roofs, ventilation, trees, water bodies, heat-sensitive housing and reliable power/health infrastructure. ASHA workers, anganwadis, SEWA and local committees can reach vulnerable groups.

Strengths are anticipatory action, defined roles and the ability to tailor thresholds and messages. Weaknesses include coarse ward data, poorly protected outdoor workers, inaccessible cooling, weak night-heat planning, uneven health reporting, voluntary employer compliance and uncertain recurring finance. Plan coverage is an adoption metric, not proof of activation or reduced mortality.

Evaluation should track forecast-to-message time, reach among target groups, work-hour compliance, cooling/water access, hospital readiness, heat illness and deaths, and post-season reform. HAPs are necessary but effective only when funded, localised and linked to enforceable occupational and urban adaptation.

**Why this earns marks:** It balances design, measures, named delivery channels, limitations and measurable outcomes.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 4 — 15 MARKS

**Question:** Discuss an integrated drought strategy combining water, agriculture and social protection. **Answer in 250 words.**

**Model answer (within 250 words):**

Drought management should follow the meteorological-agricultural-hydrological-ecological cascade rather than wait for crop loss.

**Monitoring and trigger:** combine rainfall, vegetation, sowing, soil moisture, reservoir and groundwater indicators through State Drought Monitoring Cells and the Agriculture Ministry Manual. Pre-agreed triggers should activate early action.

**Water:** prioritise safe drinking and livestock water; prepare reservoir and aquifer budgets; repair leakage; restore tanks, ponds, wetlands and catchments; regulate extraction; reuse water and apply demand management.

**Agriculture:** issue crop and sowing advisories, use suitable varieties and protective irrigation, conserve soil moisture, secure seed and fodder, support livestock health and improve insurance/loss assessment. Crop diversification must include price, procurement, credit, processing and extension; the Bundelkhand Mentha example shows why income cannot be ignored.

**Social protection:** scale employment, food/cash, nutrition and migration support before distress sale, school withdrawal or malnutrition. Target tenants, labourers, pastoralists, women and land-poor households, not just titled farmers.

**Long term:** align land and crop incentives with water availability, strengthen local water institutions, protect ecosystems and publish indicator-to-action performance.

The strategy should measure avoided livelihood erosion, groundwater stabilisation and ecosystem recovery, not merely relief distributed after declaration.

**Why this earns marks:** It links official indicators to sector action, recognises crop-income trade-offs and uses anticipatory protection.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 5 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Critically examine India's governance of drought and heat waves as slow-onset disasters. **Answer in 300 words.**

**Model answer (within 300 words):**

India has increasingly formal tools for drought and heat, but slow onset creates delayed visibility and uneven delivery.

**Drought architecture:** the Agriculture Ministry Manual uses rainfall, vegetation, sowing, soil-moisture and hydrological indicators and grades Normal, Moderate and Severe conditions. NDMA guidance supports State monitoring cells, NRSC mapping and watershed approaches. This is stronger than a single rainfall or crop-loss threshold.

**Heat architecture:** IMD has precise regional, departure, absolute and persistence criteria. NDMA-guided HAPs connect forecasts to health, water, work and community action; NCDC's 2021 plan strengthens clinical management and surveillance. MHA's 2026–31 response-fund page now includes heatwave and lightning among fourteen SDRF disasters.

**Gaps:** declaration can follow earlier asset and income loss. District averages conceal hot wards and exposed workplaces. HAP adoption is uneven in activation, cooling access, worker protection, night-heat response and health data. Drought response can over-emphasise tankers and compensation while groundwater, crop incentives and ecosystems deteriorate. Relief finance is not long-term adaptation finance.

Reform should use anticipatory triggers for water, fodder, employment, cash and health action; aquifer and crop-water budgeting; viable diversification; occupational rest-water-shade rules; accessible cooling; cool housing and urban design; and outcome dashboards. ASHA, anganwadi, SEWA and local water institutions can strengthen last-mile delivery.

Governance should be judged by avoided distress migration, preventable heat illness, livelihood loss and ecological decline—not by declaration, plan count or relief eligibility alone.

**Why this earns marks:** It evaluates institutions, current legal-finance status, implementation gaps, equity and outcome-based reform.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 6 — 20 MARKS

**Question:** Design a district-level framework for compound drought and heat-wave risk. **Answer in 300 words.**

**Model answer (within 300 words):**

A district should treat drought and heat as a connected water-food-health-livelihood emergency.

1. **Risk map:** combine rainfall, vegetation, sowing, soil moisture, reservoirs, aquifers, temperature, humidity, hot nights, housing, occupation, health and vulnerable populations.
2. **Triggers:** align the Drought Manual's composite indicators with staged drought action; align IMD heat forecasts with locally defined HAP activation. Keep wet-bulb physiology separate from meteorological declaration.
3. **Water:** publish a source-demand budget; prioritise drinking and livestock needs; protect quality; regulate extraction; repair systems and restore recharge, tanks and wetlands.
4. **Agriculture/livestock:** deploy contingency crops, soil-moisture practices, protective irrigation, fodder, veterinary care, insurance and market-supported diversification.
5. **Heat protection:** issue targeted alerts; modify work/school/event timing; provide rest, water, shade and accessible cooling; prepare hospitals, ambulances, supplies and surveillance.
6. **Social protection:** trigger employment, cash/food, nutrition and migration support before distress; include informal workers, tenants, women, older people, children and persons with illness/disability.
7. **Long-term adaptation:** cool roofs, ventilation, trees, water bodies, resilient power/health systems, aquifer governance and crop-water incentive reform.
8. **Accountability:** record trigger date, support release, target reach, water/cooling access, crop and livestock loss, heat illness, migration and ecosystem recovery.

SDRF eligibility supports immediate relief but does not replace mitigation. Success is early, equitable protection and lower cumulative loss across seasons.

**Why this earns marks:** It integrates both hazards, multiple sectors, exact trigger boundaries, inclusion, finance and measurable outcomes.
