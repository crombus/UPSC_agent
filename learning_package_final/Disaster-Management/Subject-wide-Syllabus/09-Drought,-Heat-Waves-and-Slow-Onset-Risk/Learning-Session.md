## BASIC LEARNING SESSION

### 1. Slow-onset risk is cumulative, not sudden

```text
RAINFALL DEFICIT / PERSISTENT HEAT
              ↓
SOIL MOISTURE / WATER / BODY HEAT STRESS
              ↓
CROP + LIVESTOCK + HEALTH + ECOSYSTEM IMPACT
              ↓
INCOME LOSS + DEBT + MIGRATION + SERVICE DEMAND
              ↓
FORMAL DECLARATION / RELIEF MAY ARRIVE LATE
```

Drought is a serious shortfall in water availability, often driven by rainfall deficiency but also by demand, storage, land and management. It evolves over months or years across a wide area. Heat waves develop faster, yet their cumulative health and livelihood burden can remain institutionally under-visible.

Slow onset creates an **institutional visibility lag**: thresholds and declarations may recognise the event after households have already sold assets, reduced food intake, migrated or accumulated heat exposure.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Slow-onset disaster management must act on deteriorating indicators before cumulative loss becomes an officially visible emergency.

**Must-write keywords:** slow onset · creeping emergency · cumulative risk · visibility lag · anticipatory action · livelihood erosion.

### 2. Four drought types form a cascade

| Type | Core indicator | Primary impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Meteorological drought** | prolonged inadequate or maldistributed rainfall | climatic water deficit begins |
| **Agricultural drought** | insufficient soil moisture for crop needs | crop stress, sowing failure and yield loss |
| **Hydrological drought** | reduced river, reservoir, lake or groundwater availability | water-supply and irrigation stress |
| **Ecological drought** | water shortage reduces ecosystem productivity/function | habitat, vegetation and ecosystem-service loss |

```text
meteorological
      ↓
agricultural
      ↓
hydrological
      ↓
ecological

sequence is common, not mechanically identical in every place
```

The canonical source uses rainfall below 90% of average for meteorological drought and identifies low soil moisture for agricultural drought. The current declaration process, however, does not rely on one rainfall or crop-loss threshold.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Drought is a cascading systems failure, so rainfall, crops, storage and ecosystems require separate indicators and responses.

**Must-write keywords:** meteorological · agricultural · hydrological · ecological · soil moisture · storage · ecosystem productivity.

---

### 3. Drought declaration is multi-indicator governance

The Ministry of Agriculture's **Manual for Drought Management (2016, updated to December 2020)** uses five indicator families:

1. rainfall;
2. vegetation/crop-condition indices;
3. crop-sowing progress;
4. soil moisture; and
5. hydrological indicators, including surface and groundwater.

Drought is graded Normal, Moderate or Severe through a composite process. A single guessed crop-loss percentage should not replace the Manual.

The canonical source also records older IMD drought situations:

- drought week;
- agricultural drought;
- seasonal drought;
- drought year; and
- severe drought year.

These taxonomies serve monitoring; declaration and relief must use the current official process.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Composite drought assessment reduces the error and gaming risk of declaring a complex livelihood crisis through one binary rainfall number.

**Must-write keywords:** Drought Manual · rainfall · vegetation index · sowing progress · soil moisture · hydrology · Normal/Moderate/Severe.

### 4. India's drought-risk drivers

Drought risk combines climatic variability with development choices:

- concentrated and uneven monsoon rainfall;
- rainfall variability, El Niño and changing climate patterns;
- depleted groundwater and declining per-capita water availability;
- deforestation, wetland loss and degraded catchments;
- abandonment of traditional harvesting systems;
- water-intensive crops in dry regions;
- inefficient irrigation, leakage and unpriced extraction;
- poverty, debt and weak livelihood diversification.

The canonical owner names **Mentha cultivation in Bundelkhand** as a water-intensive cash-crop example. It demonstrates a real policy trade-off: changing crops can protect water but reduce farmer income unless remunerative alternatives and market support exist.

Document-period exposure figures—about 68% of India drought-prone in varying degree, around 1,150 mm average annual rainfall, over 80% falling within fewer than 100 days, and 21% of area below 700 mm—must not be presented as current without fresh official verification.

The same source groups extreme drought proneness in western Rajasthan/Marusthali and Kachchh; severe proneness across parts of eastern Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, eastern Maharashtra and the interior peninsula; and moderate proneness across adjoining north-western, central and peninsular belts. Treat this as a broad source zonation, not a current district declaration map.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Drought becomes disaster where monsoon variability meets groundwater depletion, water-intensive incentives and weak livelihood buffers.

**Must-write keywords:** monsoon concentration · groundwater · watershed · crop choice · Mentha · perverse incentive · livelihood.

### 5. Drought monitoring and institutional chain

```text
IMD RAINFALL + NRSC VEGETATION
        + CROP SOWING + SOIL MOISTURE
        + RESERVOIR / GROUNDWATER
                    ↓
STATE DROUGHT MONITORING CELL / SDMA
                    ↓
ASSESSMENT + DECLARATION + RESPONSE PLAN
                    ↓
WATER + AGRICULTURE + LIVELIHOOD + RELIEF ACTION
                    ↓
REVIEW OUTCOMES / RESTORE RESILIENCE
```

NDMA's 2010 drought guidance supports State Drought Monitoring Cells, vulnerability mapping with NRSC, control rooms and watershed approaches. The Agriculture Ministry Manual controls declaration. District agriculture, water, rural-development, health and social-protection systems execute measures.

Distinguish:

- **monitoring:** evidence of stress;
- **declaration:** formal classification under the Manual;
- **relief:** immediate eligible assistance;
- **mitigation/adaptation:** measures that reduce next season's risk.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Drought governance is credible when the same indicators that trigger declaration also guide targeted, time-bound water, crop and livelihood action.

**Must-write keywords:** Drought Monitoring Cell · SDMA · NRSC · vulnerability map · declaration · relief · mitigation.

### 6. Water-security strategy

| Time horizon | Measures |
|---|---|
| immediate | drinking-water prioritisation, tanker/source quality control, livestock water, emergency repairs |
| seasonal | reservoir and groundwater budgeting, irrigation scheduling, crop advisories, recharge and harvesting maintenance |
| long term | watershed treatment, aquifer management, restored tanks/wetlands, reuse, leakage control and demand reform |

Supply expansion alone can induce more demand. A durable plan combines:

- source protection and water-quality monitoring;
- groundwater accounting and extraction control;
- micro-irrigation where agronomically and economically suitable;
- tank, pond and traditional harvesting restoration;
- wastewater reuse for suitable non-potable demand;
- crop-water budgeting and basin/aquifer coordination;
- equitable household and livestock allocation.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Drought resilience is water budgeting across source, demand and equity—not emergency tankers after storage has already collapsed.

**Must-write keywords:** water budget · aquifer · watershed · recharge · demand management · reuse · drinking-water priority.

### 7. Agriculture, livelihoods and social protection

Agricultural response should be stage-specific:

- adjust sowing date and crop/variety to rainfall and soil moisture;
- use contingency plans, advisories and protective irrigation;
- conserve soil moisture and reduce evaporation;
- secure fodder, livestock health and drinking water;
- protect seed, credit and market access;
- use insurance and transparent loss assessment where applicable;
- provide employment, cash/food support and migration assistance.

Crop diversification is not costless. A lower-water crop must also be viable in yield, price, procurement, processing and farmer knowledge. Social protection should trigger before distress sale, school withdrawal, malnutrition or unsafe migration.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Drought policy succeeds when water-saving agronomy and income security change together; prescribing a crop without a market merely transfers risk to the farmer.

**Must-write keywords:** contingency crop · protective irrigation · fodder · insurance · employment · anticipatory social protection · migration.

### 8. IMD heat-wave criteria

Heat-wave declaration is region- and station-specific.

| Setting | Entry condition |
|---|---|
| plains | maximum temperature at least 40°C |
| hilly regions | maximum temperature at least 30°C |
| coastal station | departure at least 4.5°C and actual maximum at least 37°C |

Where entry conditions apply:

- **Heat Wave:** departure from normal 4.5°C to 6.4°C;
- **Severe Heat Wave:** departure above 6.4°C.

Independent actual-temperature triggers:

- at least 45°C = Heat Wave;
- at least 47°C = Severe Heat Wave.

The criteria must be met at at least two stations in a meteorological subdivision for two consecutive days; declaration occurs on the second day.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** IMD heat-wave status combines regional baseline, actual temperature, departure and persistence; 45°C is an independent trigger, not the sole national definition.

**Must-write keywords:** 40°C plains · 30°C hills · coastal 37°C · departure 4.5–6.4°C · above 6.4°C · two stations/two days.

### 9. Wet-bulb temperature is a physiological metric, not IMD declaration

Wet-bulb temperature combines heat and humidity by representing the lowest temperature achievable through evaporation. As humidity rises, sweat evaporates less effectively and the body struggles to shed heat.

Around **35°C wet-bulb** is a theoretical upper benchmark for sustained human heat dissipation under highly protective assumptions such as shade, rest and adequate water. Dangerous illness and work impairment occur below it, depending on exertion, health, age, acclimatisation, clothing, wind and cooling access.

It is not:

- a routinely observed national threshold;
- the IMD heat-wave declaration criterion;
- a safe/unsafe cliff for every person.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Dry-bulb temperature describes ambient heat; wet-bulb temperature reveals how humidity limits evaporative cooling and therefore human survivability.

**Must-write keywords:** wet bulb · humidity · evaporation · perspiration · physiological heat stress · 35°C benchmark · not IMD threshold.

### 10. Heat risk is hazard × exposure × vulnerability

High temperature becomes disaster through:

- outdoor and informal work;
- hot indoor workplaces and poorly ventilated housing;
- urban heat islands and low tree cover;
- water insecurity and power/cooling inequality;
- age, pregnancy, illness and medication;
- homelessness, migration and social isolation;
- hot nights that prevent recovery;
- health facilities unprepared for heat illness.

Occupational schedules must reflect local heat and humidity, work intensity, clothing and rest/water/shade. A generic advisory to “avoid the sun” is inadequate for people whose income depends on exposure.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Heat-wave mortality is socially produced when exposure is compulsory and cooling, water, housing and healthcare are unequal.

**Must-write keywords:** outdoor worker · urban heat island · hot night · hydration · rest-shade-water · housing · health system.

### 11. Heat Action Plans: forecast to health protection

```text
IMD FORECAST / LOCAL RISK THRESHOLD
              ↓
NODAL AGENCY ACTIVATES HAP
              ↓
TARGETED MESSAGE + WORK/SCHOOL/HOSPITAL ACTION
              ↓
WATER + SHADE + COOL SPACE + CLINICAL READINESS
              ↓
SURVEILLANCE + REVIEW + LONG-TERM COOLING
```

India's first Heat Action Plan was implemented in Ahmedabad for summer 2013. NDMA's October 2019 guidance supports locally tailored plans.

Four linked HAP capacities:

1. forecasting and early warning;
2. healthcare-professional capacity;
3. community outreach; and
4. inter-agency/civil-society coordination.

Named community channels in the canonical owner include Mahila Arogya Samitis, SEWA, ASHA workers, anganwadis and municipal councils.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** A Heat Action Plan is effective only when a forecast activates pre-assigned health, labour, water, school and community actions at neighbourhood level.

**Must-write keywords:** HAP · early warning · health surveillance · ASHA · anganwadi · SEWA · inter-agency.

### 12. Short-term heat action and long-term cooling

| Immediate/seasonal preparedness | Long-term risk reduction |
|---|---|
| alerts and targeted outreach | cool roofs and passive ventilation |
| work/rest/hour modification | trees and shaded public space |
| drinking water and ORS access | restored water bodies and blue-green networks |
| cooling spaces and transport support | heat-sensitive building and urban design |
| hospital triage, supplies and surveillance | reliable electricity and resilient health facilities |
| school, event and worker protocols | housing and occupational standards |

MoHFW/NCDC's **National Action Plan on Heat-Related Illnesses (July 2021)** covers clinical management, hospital preparedness, surveillance and investigation of suspected heat-related deaths.

Short-term relief cannot substitute for long-term adaptation; long-term urban greening cannot replace an emergency clinical response during a severe episode.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Heat resilience needs two clocks: hours-to-days for warning and care, and years for housing, roofs, shade, water and labour protection.

**Must-write keywords:** cool roof · ventilation · shade · cooling space · hospital preparedness · surveillance · two time horizons.

### 13. Cold wave and lightning: boundary distinctions

Cold wave/frost and lightning sit beside drought and heat in the wider hydro-meteorological portfolio.

**Cold wave/frost**

- localised plans are State- and district-specific;
- the canonical source describes a minimum-temperature drop of more than 4°C persisting roughly three to five days, with January as the peak month; use current IMD guidance for an operational declaration;
- measures include crop-contingency planning, light irrigation, pruning and frost protection;
- schools/public buildings may serve as shelters;
- water lines and exposed infrastructure may require insulation.

**Thunderstorm/lightning**

- rapid-onset hazard, unlike drought;
- NDMA's March 2019 guidelines govern prevention and management;
- **Damini**, developed by IITM Pune under MoES, provides public lightning alerts;
- minutes-scale warning means behavioural action—proper shelter, avoiding isolated trees/open fields—is decisive.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Slow drought, forecastable heat and minutes-scale lightning require different warning horizons even when they share vulnerable populations.

**Must-write keywords:** cold wave · frost · local plan · lightning · Damini · nowcasting · behavioural action.

### 14. Relief eligibility, plans and outcomes

📰 MHA's current response-fund page lists fourteen disasters covered under SDRF, including **drought, frost/cold waves, heatwave and lightning**. This is a 2026–31 policy status.

The distinction is crucial:

- **notified-disaster status** permits immediate-relief expenditure under applicable norms;
- **HAP adoption** establishes an administrative plan;
- **implementation** requires staff, water, cooling, worker protection and health readiness;
- **outcome** requires evidence of reduced preventable illness, death and livelihood loss.

Relief finance is not mitigation finance. HAP financing may pool government and private resources, but no current universal financing model should be invented without a dated source.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** National relief eligibility improves the response framework; it does not by itself deliver ward-level cooling, safer work or measurable heat-health outcomes.

**Must-write keywords:** SDRF · notified disaster · immediate relief · HAP adoption · implementation · outcome · mitigation finance.

### 15. Integrated slow-onset governance

```text
MULTI-INDICATOR MONITORING
rain + crops + soil + storage + temperature + health
                       ↓
EARLY TRIGGER
declaration / HAP activation / anticipatory support
                       ↓
WATER + AGRICULTURE + WORK + HEALTH + SOCIAL PROTECTION
                       ↓
LONG-TERM ADAPTATION
watershed + crop incentives + cool settlement + resilient services
                       ↓
OUTCOME REVIEW
exposure, illness, livelihood, ecosystem and migration
```

A complete answer distinguishes:

- rainfall drought from crop, storage and ecosystem drought;
- dry-bulb heat-wave criteria from wet-bulb physiology;
- plan adoption from legal obligation and implementation;
- emergency water/health relief from long-term adaptation;
- physical scarcity from governance performance;
- formal declaration from cumulative household loss.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Slow-onset resilience is anticipatory governance that links environmental indicators to early social protection and structural adaptation before losses become irreversible.

**Must-write keywords:** multi-indicator · anticipatory finance · water-food-health nexus · HAP · social protection · adaptation · accountability.

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

### A. Institutional visibility lag

Drought damage accumulates before composite declaration; heat illness can accumulate across homes and workplaces before administrative activation. Triggers should therefore support anticipatory action rather than serve only as a gate for post-loss relief.

### B. Composite indicators versus threshold gaming

Rainfall alone can misclassify areas where irrigation or storage buffers crops, while crop reports alone may be delayed or influenced by incentives. The Drought Manual's rainfall, vegetation, sowing, soil-moisture and hydrological matrix is more robust, though data quality and ground verification remain essential.

### C. CWMI and Falkenmark measure different things

NITI Aayog's Composite Water Management Index is a governance-performance framework with nine sectors and 28 indicators. The Falkenmark Index is a physical per-capita water-scarcity threshold around 1,000 m³ annually. Good management does not erase physical scarcity; high endowment does not prove good governance.

### D. Data granularity and urban heat

District averages can conceal hot wards, informal settlements, unventilated workplaces and power/water deficits. HAP targeting needs sub-district temperature, humidity, land-surface, housing, occupation and health data while protecting privacy and avoiding exclusion.

### E. Anticipatory action and finance

Slow onset provides a wider forecast window than many rapid hazards. Pre-agreed triggers can release water, fodder, health, employment or cash support before distress sale and migration. Relief, seasonal preparedness and long-term adaptation require different funding windows.

### F. Crop-water-income trade-off

Discouraging water-intensive Mentha or other profitable crops without a viable income alternative can worsen household vulnerability. Reform must align agronomy with prices, procurement, value chains, credit and extension.

### G. HAP-exists fallacy

A plan may coexist with weak ward data, unused communication channels, inadequate cooling, unenforced work-hour changes or untrained facilities. Evaluate activation time, message reach, worker protection, water/cooling access, clinical readiness and outcomes.

### H. Accountability

Track drought and heat separately across warning, action and outcome: indicators observed; trigger reached; support released; target group reached; water/cooling delivered; crop/health loss; migration; ecosystem condition; and post-season reform.

## CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

### Slow-onset logic

- cumulative stress → livelihood/health/ecosystem loss → delayed visibility.
- act before formal loss becomes irreversible.
- anticipatory action differs from post-event relief.

### Drought taxonomy and declaration

- meteorological → rainfall;
- agricultural → soil moisture/crop;
- hydrological → river/reservoir/groundwater;
- ecological → ecosystem productivity.
- Manual indicators: rainfall, vegetation, sowing, soil moisture, hydrology.
- Normal/Moderate/Severe; no universal crop-loss cut-off.

### Drought management

- State Drought Monitoring Cell/SDMA + NRSC mapping.
- water budget, watershed, aquifer, tanks/wetlands, reuse and demand management.
- crop contingency, soil moisture, protective irrigation, fodder and livestock.
- insurance, employment, cash/food and migration support.
- Mentha/Bundelkhand = crop-profit versus water-security tension.

### Heat criteria

- plains at least 40°C; hills at least 30°C.
- coastal: at least 37°C actual and departure at least 4.5°C.
- departure 4.5–6.4°C = Heat Wave; above 6.4°C = Severe.
- actual at least 45°C = Heat Wave; at least 47°C = Severe.
- at least two stations, two consecutive days; declare on day two.

### Wet-bulb and vulnerability

- wet bulb = evaporative-cooling limit under temperature + humidity.
- around 35°C = theoretical upper benchmark, not IMD declaration.
- dangerous stress occurs below it depending on work, health, wind and cooling.
- priority groups: outdoor workers, older people, children, pregnant people, ill persons, homeless and poor housing.

### HAP architecture

- forecast/early warning;
- health-professional capacity;
- community outreach;
- inter-agency/civil-society coordination.
- immediate: alerts, hours, water, shade/cooling, hospital readiness.
- long term: cool roofs, ventilation, trees, water bodies, housing and labour protection.
- Ahmedabad first HAP, summer 2013.
- 250+ cities/districts in 23 States as of 7 June 2025: adoption, not outcome.

### Other hazards and finance

- cold wave/frost: location-specific State/district plans.
- lightning: NDMA 2019 guidance + Damini; minutes-scale behavioural action.
- MHA 2026–31 list includes heatwave/lightning among fourteen SDRF disasters.
- relief eligibility ≠ HAP implementation or mitigation.

### Document-period cautions

- 68% drought-prone, 820 million near/below Falkenmark and CWMI rankings require current verification.
- canonical rainfall and drought-zone figures are context, not live status.
- the source's agricultural-drought-prone classification excludes areas with over 30% gross cropped area irrigated; do not confuse this planning classification with current drought declaration.
- no annual heat/lightning death figure without a dated official source.

### Final qualified conclusion

Drought and heat resilience require correct thresholds, but not threshold-only governance. Multi-indicator monitoring must trigger early water, livelihood, worker and health protection, while long-term crop, housing, cooling and ecosystem reforms reduce recurring exposure.
