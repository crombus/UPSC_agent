# Disaster Finance, Risk Transfer and Build Back Better — Complete Learning Session

> **Local evidence checked:** *VisionIAS Value Added Material Disaster Management*, pp. 3–5, 13–16 and 61–64.
> **Current official check (27 September 2026):** MHA response/mitigation-fund page, Sixteenth Finance Commission report/explanatory memorandum and Disaster Management (Amendment) Act, 2025.
> **Evidence rule:** ✅ fact · ⚠️ analysis · ❌ trap.

## BASIC LEARNING SESSION

### 1. Disaster finance must answer when, for what and for whom

```text
BEFORE EVENT                         AFTER EVENT
risk reduction + preparedness       immediate relief + recovery
retention/reserves/contingent        budget reallocation/borrowing/
credit/insurance/pools               response funds/appeals
```

**Ex-ante finance** is arranged before impact for mitigation, preparedness, contingent response or pre-agreed recovery. **Ex-post finance** is mobilised after impact and may be necessary but slower and fiscally disruptive. Timing alone does not make finance risk-reducing: a pre-arranged credit line improves liquidity but does not physically lower exposure.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** The quality of disaster finance depends not only on money available, but on whether the instrument matches the loss layer and changes risk or merely pays after it.

### 2. Four risk strategies are distinct

| Strategy | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| accept/retain | bear the risk | budget reserve/deductible |
| avoid | do not create exposure | no-build zone |
| reduce | lower probability/consequence | retrofit, wetland restoration |
| transfer | shift defined financial loss | insurance, catastrophe bond |

Risk transfer cannot substitute for risk reduction: insurance may finance rebuilding but cannot reopen a blocked evacuation route or prevent death. Conversely, retaining frequent small losses may be cheaper than insuring them.

### 3. The statutory four-fund grid

| Level/purpose | Response | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Union | **National Disaster Response Fund**, s.46 | **National Disaster Mitigation Fund**, s.47 |
| State | **State Disaster Response Fund**, s.48 | **State Disaster Mitigation Fund**, s.48 |

Response funds meet eligible immediate post-event needs; mitigation funds finance ex-ante risk reduction. NDRF here is a **fund**, not the National Disaster Response **Force**. The National fund supplements a State when a severe disaster exceeds available SDRF resources; it is not an automatic first call.

Section 47(1) commenced on 5 February 2021, so the dedicated national mitigation window is newer than the response architecture.

### 4. Current FC16 architecture, 2026–31

The MHA page checked on 27 September 2026 records:

| Fund | Current award figure/rule |
|---|---|
| State disaster funds | **₹2,04,401 crore** total |
| SDRF | **₹1,63,521 crore (80%)** |
| SDMF | **₹40,880 crore (20%)** |
| national NDRF+NDMF pool | **₹79,406 crore** |
| national split | 75% earmarked for NDRF; maximum 25% for NDMF |
| Centre:State share | 75:25 generally; 90:10 for specified North-Eastern/Himalayan States |

FC16 also uses 70% past expenditure and 30% Disaster Risk Index for inter-State allocation, provides 5% annual increase, permits flexibility within SDRF between relief and recovery, but not SDRF-to-SDMF transfer, and shifts preparedness/capacity-building to mitigation funds.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** India now has a four-fund architecture, but its design still gives the smaller, approval-dependent share to ex-ante mitigation.

### 5. Historical reform trajectory: recommendation to current architecture

The 2019 Standing Committee on Finance recommended wider assistance heads, a separate mitigation fund, a 10% centrally sponsored scheme restoration earmark and a 15% annual SDRF increase for 2020–25. The central mitigation recommendation was later implemented through commencement of s.47(1) and FC15's SDMF/NDMF windows; FC16 used a 5% annual increase, while no equivalent FC16 rule establishes the proposed CSS earmark.

FC15 (2021–26), now the preceding award, provided State disaster funds of **₹1,60,153 crore**—SDRF ₹1,28,122 crore and SDMF ₹32,031 crore—and national funds of **₹68,463 crore**—NDRF ₹54,770 crore and NDMF ₹13,693 crore. Within the State corpus its sub-windows were 40% response/relief, 30% recovery/reconstruction, 10% preparedness/capacity and 20% mitigation. Do not swap the 30% recovery and 20% mitigation shares.

VisionIAS records historical NCCD revenue falling from ₹5,690 crore (2015–16) to ₹2,500 crore (2018–19) as GST narrowed its scope. Treat those as document-period evidence of revenue-base risk, not current receipts.

### 6. Notified disaster and local-window boundaries

MHA's current page lists 14 SDRF disasters: avalanche, cloudburst, cyclone, drought, earthquake, fire, flood, frost/cold waves, hailstorm, heatwave, landslide, lightning, pest attack and tsunami. A State may use up to **10%** of annual SDRF funds for State-notified local natural disasters outside that list, subject to notified norms and State Executive Committee approval.

❌ Heatwave and lightning should no longer be described as relying only on the local-disaster window. Listing affects relief eligibility; it does not prove adequate prevention or outcomes.

### 7. Response, compensation, recovery and mitigation are not one pot

Immediate relief supports notified eligible needs after impact. Compensation does not equal complete livelihood restoration, infrastructure reconstruction or long-term recovery. **Mitigation** reduces future risk before the next event. **Recovery** restores and improves functions after the event. Funding labels, eligible items, institutions and time horizons differ.

```text
relief → life-safety/basic needs
rehabilitation → services, housing, livelihoods
reconstruction → durable assets/systems
BBB → reconstruct with lower future risk
```

### 8. Pre-arranged finance and risk layering

Risk layering matches instruments to frequency and severity:

```text
frequent / lower loss → household/agency retention + budget reserve
medium layer          → contingency fund/credit + insurance/pool
rare / severe layer   → sovereign support + reinsurance/cat bond/external aid
```

Pre-arranged finance sets eligibility, trigger, amount or drawdown process before a disaster. It can accelerate liquidity, but speed does not guarantee adequacy, targeting or risk reduction.

### 9. Indemnity and parametric insurance

**Indemnity insurance** pays verified covered loss, subject to exclusions, deductibles and limits; alignment may be better but assessment can delay settlement. **Parametric insurance** pays when an agreed index crosses a threshold, regardless of exact loss; it can be fast but creates **basis risk**—actual severe loss with little/no payout, or payout despite limited local loss.

Insurance design must specify insured interest, peril, trigger, attachment, limit, exclusions, claims process and consumer protection. Low-income households may face unaffordable premiums, low awareness or basis-risk exposure.

### 10. Pools and catastrophe bonds

A **risk pool** combines participants/exposures under common financing to spread volatility. Diversification fails if members face highly correlated hazards or governance and pricing are weak.

A **catastrophe bond** transfers a specified catastrophe layer to capital-market investors; trigger occurrence can reduce principal/interest. CDRI names catastrophe bonds and budget reserve funds conceptually, but do not claim widespread Indian deployment without a dated official source.

### 11. Social protection is household disaster finance

Scalable cash, food, employment, health and housing support can protect households that private insurance does not reach. Shock-responsive social protection needs registries, portable identity/payment access, contingency rules and grievance systems. It should complement universal/basic services and relief, not exclude informal workers or migrants because pre-disaster databases are incomplete.

> **Answer-grabbing line:** Sovereign risk transfer protects the public balance sheet only if fiscal liquidity is translated into timely household and service protection.

### 12. Damage, loss and needs assessment serve different decisions

- **Damage:** physical destruction at replacement/repair value.
- **Economic loss:** flow reductions such as output or income.
- **Needs:** resources and reforms required for recovery and BBB.

Using one estimate for all three distorts finance. Transparent baselines, unit costs, double-count controls, indirect-loss methods and distributional data are essential for assistance, insurance pricing and accountability.

### 13. Build Back Better is not “build back more”

BBB, reflected in Sendai Priority 4, uses recovery, rehabilitation and reconstruction to reduce future risk through safer location, codes, resilient services, institutions and livelihoods. CDRI's source formulation applies BBB to physical design **and management systems**.

```text
post-disaster assessment → safe siting/design → inclusive recovery choice
→ resilient service networks → maintenance finance → monitored risk reduction
```

Safeguards include participation, tenure and livelihood security, accessibility, environmental assessment, grievance remedy and avoiding rushed reconstruction in the same hazardous location.

### 14. Fiscal, federal and equity trade-offs

- **Federal:** SDRF-first design places initial responsibility and assessment capacity on States; capacities vary.
- **Fiscal:** more ex-ante mitigation competes with immediate relief and other spending, yet underinvestment raises future contingent liabilities.
- **Incentive:** expenditure-weighted allocation may reward spending, not risk reduction; the Disaster Risk Index partly corrects this.
- **Equity:** risk-based premiums may price out high-risk poor households; flat subsidies may weaken risk signals; deductibles can reduce moral hazard but delay recovery for those without savings.
- **Intergenerational:** debt-financed recovery shifts cost forward; unsafe rebuilding shifts risk forward.

### 15. Current legal transparency and project-status distinctions

The 2025 Amendment Act, in force from 9 April 2025, defines a disaster database in s.2(da) to include fund allocation and expenditure; NDMA and SDMAs receive related duties under ss.6(2)(v) and 18(2)(p). This establishes a legal transparency architecture, not proof that every database field is publicly operational.

Named mitigation approvals include UFRMP Phase II (**₹2,444.42 crore**) and Assam wetland restoration (**₹519.04 crore**). Approval/sanction ≠ release ≠ utilisation ≠ completed asset ≠ maintained performance ≠ avoided loss.

## OPTIONAL ADVANCED DEPTH — NOT REQUIRED FOR A CORE ANSWER

### A. The mitigation asymmetry
The live critique is not “India has no mitigation fund.” It is proportion (20%), procedure (project/HLC approval rather than formula flow) and transferability (no SDRF-to-SDMF transfer).

### B. Liquidity, solvency and adequacy
Fast finance solves liquidity; it may not cover total loss or preserve long-term debt sustainability. Layered finance must distinguish immediate cash from ultimate fiscal burden.

### C. Basis risk and trigger politics
Parametric triggers trade assessment delay for mismatch risk. Transparent indices, local granularity, appeals and complementary relief are essential.

### D. Moral hazard versus affordability
Risk-based pricing and deductibles create incentives, but can exclude poor households and local bodies. Premium support should be targeted and linked to feasible mitigation, not punitive pricing of inherited risk.

### E. BBB allocation conflict
Speed, owner-driven recovery, safer siting, heritage, tenure, livelihoods and environmental safeguards can conflict. “Better” must be defined with affected people, not only engineers or financiers.

### F. Data and accountability
Standardised loss data is needed for pricing, pools and audits. Yet database creation, public access, data quality and demonstrated use are separate evidence rungs.

## CONSOLIDATED REGISTER NOTES

### Four-fund grid and current award
- s.46 NDRF fund; s.47 NDMF; s.48 SDRF + SDMF; fund ≠ force.
- FC16 2026–31: State funds ₹2,04,401 crore = SDRF ₹1,63,521 crore + SDMF ₹40,880 crore; national pool ₹79,406 crore.
- State split 80:20 response:mitigation; national rule 75% NDRF, max 25% NDMF.
- Centre:State 75:25 generally, 90:10 specified NE/Himalayan States.

### Exact boundaries
- response/relief ≠ mitigation ≠ complete recovery;
- ex-ante timing ≠ physical risk reduction;
- insurance payout ≠ avoided death/damage;
- notified list = 14; local window up to 10% for outside-list State-notified natural disasters;
- sanction ≠ release/utilisation/completion/outcome.

### Instruments
- retention/reserves, contingent finance, indemnity and parametric insurance, pools, catastrophe bonds, social protection.
- Parametric speed ↔ basis risk; pooling requires real diversification.

### BBB and accountability
- BBB = safer siting/design + resilient services + institutions/livelihoods + maintenance.
- s.2(da), 6(2)(v), 18(2)(p) create finance-data duties; operational/public use must be verified.
- Final test: timely equitable protection, safer reconstruction, lower future risk and sustainable fiscal burden.
