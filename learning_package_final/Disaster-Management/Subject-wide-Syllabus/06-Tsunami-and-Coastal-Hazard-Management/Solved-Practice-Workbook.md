### PYQ DEMAND CARD 1 — 2025 GS-I

**Demand:** Explain tsunami formation and consequences. (Explain · 10 marks · about 150 words)

**Status:** This card focuses on the disaster-management application; the physical-process explanation is a GS-I Geography demand.

**Model answer (145 words):** A tsunami is a series of long waves generated by sudden displacement of a large water volume, most commonly vertical seabed movement in an undersea earthquake, but also landslides, volcanic activity or meteorite impacts. Generation is not impact: an undersea earthquake does not automatically produce a destructive tsunami, since trigger distance, magnitude, bathymetry and coastal geometry govern whether and how waves affect a coast. In deep water the waves travel fast with low amplitude; entering shallow water, they shoal, slow and grow in height, producing run-up, inundation and a damaging return flow. Consequences include coastal flooding, structural damage, loss of life and livelihoods, and disruption to ports, fisheries and tourism. India's Tsunami Early Warning Centre (ITEWC) at INCOIS integrates seismic analysis with sea-level observation to confirm generation and update threat assessments, but the local evacuation all-clear remains a competent-authority decision, not a warning-centre output alone.

**Examiner note:** Named evidence (seabed displacement, shoaling/run-up, ITEWC's detection-to-advisory chain) does the evidentiary work; do not invent an unsourced wave-height, speed or casualty figure.

### ORIGINAL MAINS 1 — 10 MARKS

**Question:** Distinguish tsunami generation from tides and cyclone-driven storm surge. Answer in about 150 words.

**Model answer (138 words):** A tsunami is not an astronomical tide and not a cyclone-driven storm surge. Tides follow predictable gravitational cycles of the moon and sun; a storm surge is chiefly wind-and-pressure driven, building up during a cyclone's approach. A tsunami's generation instead begins with sudden displacement of a large water volume, most commonly vertical seabed movement during an undersea earthquake, though landslides, volcanic activity and meteorite impacts are alternative triggers.
