## ANSWER KEY AND SOLVED MODELS

### MCQ KEY AND EXPLANATIONS

### Q1. Why is the phrase 'political vacuum' inadequate for 1707-1740s India?

**Answer: A.**

**Explanation by option:**

- **A.** Correct: this describes an active redistribution of organised authority, not an empty stage.
- **B.** Wrong: extensive territory and revenue control changed hands among regional claimants, so nothing stayed static.
- **C.** Wrong: Company commercial and later political presence expanded in this period; it did not withdraw.
- **D.** Wrong: this directly contradicts the imperial contraction that is the topic's central premise.

**Trap logic:** 'Vacuum' tempts an image of an empty stage; the exam-safe answer must instead name the actual claimants who filled it.

### Q2. Which was a central military weakness of the later Mughal host?

**Answer: B.**

**Explanation by option:**

- **A.** Wrong: no such prohibition existed; cavalry remained a core part of mansab-based service.
- **B.** Correct: Karnal shows how divided command nullified otherwise considerable numerical resources.
- **C.** Wrong: the later Mughal army stayed composite (cavalry, artillery, infantry, elephants), not solely European-trained.
- **D.** Wrong: Mughal forces retained artillery; the Karnal failure was of coordination, not equipment.

**Trap logic:** Do not reduce a command-and-coordination failure to a crude technology-deficit explanation.

### Q3. Which economic statement about the period is most defensible?

**Answer: C.**

**Explanation by option:**

- **A.** Wrong: 1717 marks an expansion of Company trading privilege in Bengal, not an end to Bengal's trade.
- **B.** Wrong: Delhi and regional commerce showed resilience even after the severe 1739 shock.
- **C.** Correct: resources were redistributed toward regions even as central remittance fell.
- **D.** Wrong: revenue retained regionally still circulated within provincial markets and treasuries.

**Trap logic:** Treat Delhi's fiscal loss and India's overall economic condition as separate questions.

### Q4. Nadir Shah's settlement with Muhammad Shah did which of the following?

**Answer: D.**

**Explanation by option:**

- **A.** Wrong: the Sayyids had already fallen from power nearly two decades earlier, in 1720, and played no role in 1739.
- **B.** Wrong: Muhammad Shah continued to reign as emperor after Nadir Shah's withdrawal.
- **C.** Wrong: no permanent Persian annexation of the Gangetic plain occurred; Nadir withdrew after plunder.
- **D.** Correct: Nadir extracted wealth and ceded frontier territory west of the Indus, then withdrew, leaving Muhammad Shah as emperor.

**Trap logic:** Separate plunder-and-withdrawal from territorial annexation and dynastic replacement.

### Q5. Which of the following was NOT a consequence of the 1739 invasion?

**Answer: A.**

**Explanation by option:**

- **A.** Correct (the false/overstated claim to identify): no evidence supports uniform destruction of all Indian trade and urban life from one invasion.
- **B.** Wrong as an answer here because this was a genuine consequence: the frontier's exposure was real and lasting.
- **C.** Wrong as an answer here because provincial assertion did intensify following the shock to central prestige.
- **D.** Wrong as an answer here because prestige loss was severe and well evidenced.

**Trap logic:** 'Not a consequence' questions invert ordinary recall — the correct choice is the overstated claim, not a true one.

### Q6. Which causal ordering of Mughal decline is strongest?

**Answer: B.**

**Explanation by option:**

- **A.** Wrong: fabricates a causal link; no evidence connects cultural change to jagir disappearance in this sequence.
- **B.** Correct: this traces the self-reinforcing fiscal-military feedback loop central to the topic's mechanism.
- **C.** Wrong: internally false, since Company trade expanded rather than ended in this period.
- **D.** Wrong: reverses chronology; Aurangzeb's pre-1707 Deccan wars could not be caused by an invasion in 1739.

**Trap logic:** A strong answer traces a causal loop between named mechanisms, not an arbitrary pair of unconnected events.

### Q7. Which statement best captures Mughal institutional continuity after 1707?

**Answer: C.**

**Explanation by option:**

- **A.** Wrong: successor states typically retained and repurposed Mughal ranks and offices rather than abolishing them.
- **B.** Wrong: the khutba retained real symbolic and legitimating value even as central command weakened.
- **C.** Correct: appropriation of Mughal forms lowered the legitimacy cost of building new regional states.
- **D.** Wrong: Persian remained the chancery and administrative language well beyond 1707.

**Trap logic:** Institutional survival is evidence against total collapse, but it is not proof of unchanged central power.

### Q8. Which is the best historiographical synthesis for this topic?

**Answer: D.**

**Explanation by option:**

- **A.** Wrong: contradicts the regional-variation evidence that is central to the decline-versus-transition debate.
- **B.** Wrong: denies the well-evidenced contraction of enforceable central authority.
- **C.** Wrong: personalises a structural, multi-causal institutional process.
- **D.** Correct: this two-level, regionally qualified verdict best incorporates the strongest evidence from all schools.

**Trap logic:** Balanced synthesis does not mean granting every competing school equal explanatory weight.

### Q9. Why could Mughal legitimacy survive military contraction?

**Answer: A.**

**Explanation by option:**

- **A.** Correct: existing institutional vocabulary made imperial sanction useful even to functionally autonomous powers.
- **B.** Wrong: this contradicts the provincial fiscal autonomy documented across Bengal, Awadh and Hyderabad.
- **C.** Wrong: no such prohibition existed; regional and local coinage continued to circulate.
- **D.** Wrong: regional rulers built substantial independent military-fiscal establishments.

**Trap logic:** Symbolic authority can retain real transactional value even after coercive power has weakened.

### Q10. Which answer line is strongest on Aurangzeb's responsibility for later decline?

**Answer: B.**

**Explanation by option:**

- **A.** Wrong: the empire continued to function formally for decades after 1707.
- **B.** Correct: this balances inherited strain against the independent weight of later succession, faction and jagirdari mechanisms.
- **C.** Wrong: his inherited fiscal-military strain directly shaped the conditions of the post-1707 crisis.
- **D.** Wrong: over-personalises a multi-causal structural process into one ruler's policy.

**Trap logic:** The strongest answer avoids both fully exonerating and wholly blaming a single ruler.

### Q11. Which statement about the 'dark age' characterisation is most defensible?

**Explanation by option:**

- **A.** Wrong: a single example can neither confirm nor refute an all-India generalisation.
- **B.** Wrong: cultural production continued and even flourished in Muhammad Shah's and regional courts.
- **C.** Correct: disruption was real, but uniform application across every region is unsupported.
- **D.** Wrong: 'dark age' is a rhetorical label, not a measured statistical category.

**Trap logic:** Use levels, regions and social groups separately rather than adopting or rejecting one verdict word.

### Q12. Which distinction best captures Mughal decline after 1707?

- **C.** Wrong: the dynasty continued in title through Muhammad Shah and later emperors.
- **D.** Correct: this states the level-specific decline the topic requires — authority, not dynasty or vocabulary, contracted.

**Trap logic:** 'Decline' names a level of enforceable authority, not a single moment of total disappearance.

### Q13. What did the Sayyid Brothers' kingmaking primarily demonstrate?

- **A.** Correct: the Sayyids controlled enthronement and deposition while remaining kingmakers, not emperors.

- **C.** Wrong: conflates the 1713-1720 Sayyid period with the unrelated 1739 Nadir Shah invasion.
- **D.** Wrong: jagirs remained transferable revenue assignments, not private hereditary property, throughout this period.

**Trap logic:** Kingmaking demonstrates the weakness of monarchical arbitration, not the abolition of monarchy itself.

### Q14. Why is a high jama not proof of a strong imperial treasury?

**Explanation by option:**

- **A.** Wrong: zamindars and revenue farmers remained central to collection regardless of the jama figure.

- **C.** Wrong: jama is a fiscal-assessment term, unconnected to campaign or victory records.
- **D.** Wrong: jama measured an assessed revenue obligation, not the volume of circulating coin.

**Trap logic:** A large paper assessment can conceal a genuinely weak realised treasury.

- **D.** Wrong: over-weights one factor against succession, faction and jagirdari-agrarian mechanisms.

**Trap logic:** Symbol and substance must be evaluated as separate, not identical, forms of evidence.

### Mains 20M-1 - Why did Mughal central authority decline between 1707 and the 1740s? Present a weighted multi-causal explanation.

### Model answer

Mughal central authority declined because the fiscal-military contract joining emperor, nobility, province and countryside stopped reproducing revenue and service — a deeper interactive crisis, not one cause.

**1. Inherited over-extension:** Aurangzeb left a formidable empire, but the long Deccan war tied down forces and never ended Maratha mobility — a difficult inheritance, not inevitable collapse.

**2. Succession and arbitration:** With no fixed rule, Jajau in 1707 and later contests drained soldiers and made the throne depend on noble coalitions; Farrukhsiyar's 1719 deposition showed kingmakers could control monarchy.

**3. Jagirdari-fiscal mechanism:** Satish Chandra's *be-jagiri* thesis links growing claimants to inadequate assignments. Jama exceeded hasil; ijara contractors pressed cultivators; nobles in arrears kept incomplete contingents.

**4. Agrarian and regional feedback:** Irfan Habib links extraction to peasant and zamindar resistance; Jats, Sikhs and Bundelas withheld revenue. Yet Murshid Quli Khan's Bengal stayed fiscally vigorous as remittance fell.

**5. Provincial and Maratha reordering:** Saadat Khan's Awadh and Nizam-ul-Mulk's Hyderabad converted office into autonomy while keeping legitimacy; Maratha chauth and sardeshmukhi shifted from exaction to bargaining after 1718-19.

**6. Military-command failure:** The army kept artillery and numbers, but paper mansabs and factional command cut usable strength; at Karnal (1739) divided command met Nadir's mobility.

**7. External shock:** Nadir's victory and loss west of the Indus devastated prestige, though weakness predated it.

### Mains 20M-2 - Was the eighteenth century in India a 'dark age' of decline or an age of regional transformation? Critically discuss with reference to 1707-1740s.

### Model answer

Neither label is sufficient alone. The period was a darkening of Mughal central fiscal-military capacity and, simultaneously, an uneven transformation of political and economic power toward regions.

**Evidence for decline:** Succession contests after 1707 empowered factions; Farrukhsiyar's deposition by the Sayyids in 1719 eroded monarchical prestige. Jagir competition, jama-hasil gaps and ijara shifted pressure onto cultivators; Jat, Sikh and zamindar resistance disrupted revenue. Provincial remittance weakened, mansabdars under-maintained contingents, and Nadir Shah's 1739 Karnal victory brought massacre, plunder and frontier loss. **Evidence for transformation:** Bengal under Murshid Quli Khan built a strong revenue order backed by zamindars and bankers.
