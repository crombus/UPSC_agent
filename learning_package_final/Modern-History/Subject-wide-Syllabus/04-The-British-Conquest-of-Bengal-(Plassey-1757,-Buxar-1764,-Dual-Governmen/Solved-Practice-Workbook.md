## ANSWER KEY AND OPTION-BY-OPTION EXPLANATIONS

### Q1 — Answer and reasoning

**Answer: A.**

**Explanation (option-by-option):**

- **A.** Correct: control of credit and treasury flows made the Jagat Seths indispensable to the court and influential in the Plassey conspiracy.

- **B.** Wrong: military command belonged to office-holders such as the Mir Bakshi, not the banking house.

- **C.** Wrong: the banking house financed the state but did not possess the imperial Diwani.

- **D.** Wrong: it was an Indian banking network centred on Murshidabad, not a European Calcutta agency house.

**Trap:** Do not reduce bankers to passive financiers; their control of credit could shape political coalitions without making them formal rulers.

### Q2 — Answer and reasoning

**Answer: B.**

**Explanation (option-by-option):**

- **A.** Wrong: the Nawabship continued as a powerful Mughal successor-state institution.

- **B.** Correct: succession insecurity and divided elite loyalties narrowed Siraj's room for manoeuvre before open conflict.

- **C.** Wrong: Bengal possessed a wealthy and functioning revenue system; the crisis was political, not administrative vacuum.

- **D.** Wrong: the Company received the Diwani only in 1765.

**Trap:** Do not explain Siraj's fall solely through youth or temperament; the structure of court faction was decisive.

### Q3 — Answer and reasoning

**Answer: C.**

**Explanation (option-by-option):**

- **A.** The 1717 farman was a Company-only trade privilege from the Nawab; it granted no wartime licence to seize other Europeans' factories.

- **B.** Mir Jafar's alliance with Clive was still forming in early-to-mid 1757 and he issued no such order; this option assigns agency to an actor not yet in command of events.

- **C.** Correct: this was a calculated pre-emptive move, tied to the wider Anglo-French war, to remove Siraj's potential French counterweight while the Plassey conspiracy was maturing.

- **D.** No such Chandernagore attack on Fort William occurred in 1756; the causal direction here is invented.

**Trap:** Do not assume a defensive-retaliation logic mirrors the earlier Calcutta crisis; no such French provocation exists in the record, so the real driver is pre-emption, not retaliation.

### Q4 — Answer and reasoning

**Answer: D.**

**Explanation (option-by-option):**

- **A.** No independent verification by other Company officials exists; the account's defining weakness is precisely the absence of such corroboration.

- **B.** Holwell was not anonymous and was not a neutral Bengali chronicler; he was a named, interested Company official, the opposite of this description.

- **C.** No forensic examination has settled a death toll; the episode remains explicitly disputed in scholarship, so claiming settlement overstates the evidence.

- **D.** Correct: Holwell was a Company official and self-described survivor with a direct personal and institutional stake in the story, and no independent account corroborates his specific casualty figures.

**Trap:** Do not assume 'well-known history' must mean 'forensically settled'; the correct exam position is explicit agnosticism about scale, not false certainty either way.

## PYQS AND ANSWER PRACTICE

### PYQ Application 1 - Company armies and the Buxar lesson

**Demand:** Use the 2022 GS-I question on Company armies defeating larger Indian forces to build a Bengal-specific application.

**Workbook model:** Buxar shows that numerical strength did not automatically create military effectiveness. Mir Qasim, Shuja-ud-Daula and Shah Alam II brought a broad coalition, but each component retained separate political aims, treasuries and command structures. Hector Munro's force possessed a more unified chain of command, regular finance and disciplined battlefield coordination. The lesson is therefore institutional rather than racial or technological: the Company converted revenue, credit and central command into sustained military capacity. The Bengal case should be used as one illustration within the wider all-India answer, not as a complete explanation for every later Company victory.

### PYQ Application 2 - Why famine incidence increased

**Demand:** Use the 2022 GS-I famine question without claiming that Company taxation caused drought.

**Workbook model:** The Bengal famine of 1770 illustrates how an environmental shock became a governance disaster. Poor harvests, drought and disease created scarcity, but rigid revenue demands, commercial priorities and the confused authority of the Dual Government weakened adaptation and relief. The Company possessed effective revenue power while the Nawab's establishment retained nominal public responsibility without adequate resources. Bengal therefore supports a layered explanation: climatic stress triggered the crisis, while extractive and unaccountable institutions magnified exposure, reduced corrective capacity and deepened mortality. Wider colonial famine policy must be added separately for an all-India answer.

### ORIGINAL MAINS PRACTICE — WORKBOOK MODELS

### Original Mains 1 - 10 marks | 150 words

**Question:** Explain how commercial privilege became a sovereignty conflict in Bengal before Plassey.

**Model answer:** The conflict began because a bounded commercial concession was stretched into political immunity. Farrukhsiyar's 1717 farman protected the Company's registered trade, but Company servants increasingly used dastaks for private transactions. This reduced Nawabi customs revenue and disadvantaged Indian merchants who still paid duties. The dispute widened when the Company fortified Calcutta without permission and resisted the Nawab's jurisdiction over persons and property within its settlement. Siraj-ud-Daulah therefore confronted more than a foreign merchant: he confronted a corporation claiming fiscal and territorial exceptions inside Bengal. The fall of Fort William in June 1756 expressed this struggle over sovereignty. Plassey followed only after the Company combined commercial grievance, military recovery and court conspiracy. Thus trade privilege did not mechanically cause conquest, but its unauthorised extension created the institutional conflict from which political intervention grew.

### Original Mains 2 - 10 marks | 150 words

**Question:** Distinguish the historical significance of Plassey from that of Buxar.

**Model answer:** Plassey and Buxar were decisive in different ways. Plassey (23 June 1757) was primarily a political coup enabled by conspiracy. Mir Jafar, Rai Durlabh and the Jagat Seths helped neutralise Siraj-ud-Daulah's numerical strength, allowing the Company to install a dependent Nawab and extract payments and concessions. Buxar (22 October 1764), by contrast, was a genuine field victory over Mir Qasim, Shuja-ud-Daula and Shah Alam II. It demonstrated military predominance beyond Bengal's succession politics. Buxar also created the leverage for the two Allahabad treaties of August 1765, through which the Company received the Diwani and preserved Awadh as a buffer. Plassey opened Bengal's political system to Company control; Buxar made that control militarily credible and legally convertible into revenue sovereignty.

### Original Mains 3 - 15 marks | 250 words

**Question:** Assess Mir Qasim's programme as an attempt to recover Nawabi capacity.

**Model answer:** Mir Qasim's programme was a state-recovery project rather than merely a personal quarrel with the Company. Installed in 1760, he shifted his capital to Munger to reduce dependence on Company-dominated Murshidabad, reorganised the army and tried to strengthen fiscal administration. The central conflict concerned inland trade. Company servants used duty exemptions to expand private commerce, while Indian merchants remained taxed. Mir Qasim's abolition of internal duties in 1763 sought equality by removing the Nawab's own duties for everyone. This restored competitive parity but sacrificed revenue, showing how sharply Company privilege constrained Nawabi choice. The Company opposed the measure because equality removed its servants' advantage. War followed, and Mir Qasim's defeat pushed him toward Shuja-ud-Daula and Shah Alam II before Buxar. His programme therefore revealed both the possibilities and limits of regional recovery: administrative relocation, military reform and fiscal equalisation could challenge Company privilege, but they could not overcome the corporation's combined military, financial and political resources.

### Original Mains 4 - 15 marks | 250 words

**Question:** Analyse the strategic logic of the two Allahabad treaties of August 1765.

**Model answer:** The Allahabad settlement converted Buxar's military outcome into two complementary forms of power. On 12 August 1765, Shah Alam II granted the Company the Diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa. The grant supplied legal revenue title in Mughal form, while the emperor received tribute and the districts of Kora and Allahabad. On 16 August, the separate treaty with Shuja-ud-Daula restored Awadh after indemnity and alliance obligations. The Company did not annex Awadh because a dependent buffer was strategically cheaper than direct rule and useful against powers farther west. The two instruments therefore performed different tasks: one institutionalised fiscal sovereignty in the east; the other organised strategic depth beyond Bengal. Their combined logic was selective expansion. The Company preferred recognised title, revenue access and indirect influence where these served its interests better than immediate annexation. Calling them simply the Treaty of Allahabad is acceptable shorthand only if their distinct parties, dates and functions remain clear.

### Original Mains 5 - 20 marks | 250 words

**Question:** Examine how finance and Indian institutional actors shaped the Company's conquest of Bengal.

**Model answer:** Finance was not a background condition of Bengal's conquest; it was one of its principal mechanisms. The Jagat Seth banking house connected court politics, credit and large transfers, and supported regime change when Siraj-ud-Daulah appeared threatening to established interests. Mir Jafar offered the Company a client relationship through which compensation, concessions and the 24 Parganas grant could flow. Rai Durlabh's office and battlefield inactivity helped convert conspiracy into military outcome, while Amichand acted as a broker within the negotiations. After Plassey, repeated demands weakened Mir Jafar and encouraged his replacement by Mir Qasim. Yet Indian institutional agency also resisted Company power. Mir Madan and Mohan Lal fought at Plassey, and Mir Qasim attempted fiscal and military recovery from Munger. After the Diwani, Indian deputies remained indispensable to revenue administration because the Company lacked sufficient local capacity. The collaboration interpretation is therefore useful, but it requires qualification. Indian actors pursued their own interests; they did not collectively design colonial rule. As Company control over force, credit and revenue expanded, the relationship became increasingly unequal. Conquest was institutionally co-produced, but its durable gains accrued overwhelmingly to the Company.

### Original Mains 6 - 20 marks | 250 words

**Question:** Discuss the relationship between the Dual Government and Bengal's vulnerability during the famine of 1770.

**Model answer:** The Dual Government did not create drought, but it made Bengal less capable of absorbing and governing scarcity. From 1765 the Company possessed the substantive Diwani and drew revenue through Indian deputies, while the Nawab's establishment retained nominal responsibility for policing, justice and public order without commensurate resources. This divided authority encouraged revenue extraction while obscuring who was accountable for corrective action. When poor harvests, drought and disease intensified scarcity in 1769-70, the institutional system lacked a clear and adequately funded relief authority. Revenue rigidity and commercial priorities could therefore amplify distress even though the environmental trigger remained real. The famine must not be reduced to one cause, and exact mortality figures should be presented as disputed estimates rather than census facts. Its historical significance lies in what it exposed: Company power had grown faster than its willingness to accept public responsibility. Warren Hastings's 1772 termination of the Dual Government did not erase colonial extraction, but it acknowledged that the fiction of Nawabi responsibility could no longer conceal the Company's effective authority.
