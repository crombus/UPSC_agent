## PYQS AND ANSWER PRACTICE

### Verified PYQ coverage

Two direct Prelims questions are covered: UPSC Prelims 2019 GS-I Q4 on the Charter Act of 1813 and UPSC Prelims 2023 GS-I Q50 on the Governor-General of India designation. No direct Mains question and no additional 2026 Prelims or GS-I Mains question is assigned to this topic. The stems and option order below follow public paper reproductions; because an official answer key is not available in the study library, each answer remains clearly labelled as inferred.

#### SOLVED ROUTED PRELIMS PYQs

### PYQ 1 — UPSC Prelims 2019 GS-I, Q4

**Question (public-paper wording):** Consider the following statements about `the Charter Act of 1813`:

1. It ended the trade monopoly of the East India Company in India except for trade in tea and trade with China.
2. It asserted the sovereignty of the British Crown over the Indian territories held by the Company.
3. The revenues of India were now controlled by the British Parliament.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

A. 1 and 2 only
B. 2 and 3 only
C. 1 and 3 only
D. 1, 2 and 3

**INFERRED ANSWER — NOT OFFICIALLY VERIFIED (confidence: high):** **A. 1 and 2 only.**

**Reasoning:** Statement 1 is correct because the 1813 Act opened Indian trade to other British subjects while preserving the Company's monopoly over tea and China trade. Statement 2 is correct because the Act explicitly asserted Crown sovereignty over the Company's Indian territories. Statement 3 is not supported by the Act in the form stated: Parliament deepened supervision, but Indian revenues were not simply placed under direct day-to-day parliamentary control by this statute.

### PYQ 2 — UPSC Prelims 2023 GS-I, Q50

**Question (public-paper wording):** Which one of the following Acts of British India strengthened the Governor-General of Bengal's position and designated him as the Governor-General of India?

A. The Regulating Act
B. The Pitt's India Act
C. The Charter Act of 1793
D. The Charter Act of 1833

**INFERRED ANSWER — NOT OFFICIALLY VERIFIED (confidence: high):** **D. The Charter Act of 1833.**

**Reasoning:** The Regulating Act created only the Governor-General of Fort William/Bengal. Pitt's India Act reworked the council and metropolitan supervision but did not create an all-India title. The Charter Act of 1793 renewed the existing arrangement. The Charter Act of 1833 is the statute that redesignated the office as Governor-General of India and widened its legislative-executive reach.

#### Evidence and answer-writing drills

### Drill 1 -- Name the crisis as fiscal and political, never humanitarian

**Claim:** State the 1772-73 crisis as Company bankruptcy risk, an emergency government loan, and parliamentary anxiety over unaccountable patronage and mismanagement; never lead with, or imply, a humanitarian motive tied to the 1770 famine as Parliament's primary driver.

**Exam move:** A question asking "why did Parliament intervene in Company affairs in 1773" is answered with the financial-crisis-and-accountability mechanism first; the famine belongs in the background context, not the causal headline.

### Drill 2 -- State the Regulating Act's exact institutional design, not a vague "more control" answer

**Claim:** Name the Governor-General of Fort William/Bengal, the four-member Council, the narrower Bengal-subordination-of-presidencies wording, the Court of Directors' reporting duty, and the Supreme Court/1774 Charter together as the Act's five components.

**Exam move:** A statement-based question on the Regulating Act tests these five components independently -- know which specific component each option is really describing before selecting an answer.

### Drill 3 -- The Regulating Act failed on Council arithmetic, not personal failure

**Claim:** Name the 3-1 Hastings-Council split (1774-76) as the structural cause of failure, Monson's 1776 death as the contingent (non-statutory) resolution, and Pitt's India Act's smaller council (1784) as the actual statutory fix.

**Exam move:** Reject any answer attributing the Regulating Act's failure to Warren Hastings's personal competence alone; the vote-arithmetic mechanism is the examinable cause.

### Drill 4 -- Handle Nand Kumar source-critically, never as settled fact

**Claim:** State the documented facts (the corruption accusation against Hastings, the separate 1770 forgery charge, the 1775 trial and execution, the Hastings-Impey personal closeness) and explicitly flag the "deliberate silencing" reading as a widely held but contested interpretation.

**Exam move:** A question inviting assessment of the Nand Kumar episode rewards naming the source-critical caution explicitly, not simply retelling the narrative as though the causal link to Hastings were proven.

### Drill 5 -- Distinguish the Patna and Cossijurah jurisdictional zones precisely

**Claim:** Name Patna as a personal-law/civil-court jurisdiction clash and Cossijurah as a revenue-administration jurisdiction clash; state both as instances of the same underlying Supreme-Court-versus-Company-courts overlap.

**Exam move:** A question on "why was the Amending Act of 1781 needed" is answered by naming both cases and the general overlap they exposed, not by naming only one case.

### Drill 6 -- State exactly what the 1781 Act fixed, and exactly what it left untouched

**Claim:** Name the four precise 1781 corrections (official-acts exemption, revenue-matter exclusion, Hindu/Muslim personal-law direction, provincial-appeal routing) and state explicitly that the Council-deadlock problem was untouched by this Act.

**Exam move:** Reject any answer crediting the 1781 Act with fixing the executive deadlock; that required 1784's smaller council and 1786's override power, entirely separate fixes.

### Drill 7 -- Never conflate Bengal's Dual Government with Britain's dual control

**Claim:** Name the Bengal Dual Government (1765-72, Nawab's Nizamat vs Company's Diwani, Topic 04) and Pitt's India Act's dual control (1784 onward, Board of Control vs Court of Directors, both in Britain) as two entirely distinct institutions.

**Exam move:** Any option using the word "dual" in a governance-structure question must be checked against which decade, which location and which two bodies it actually names before selecting it.

### Drill 8 -- Route civil-service reform through its exact four-step sequence

**Claim:** State the sequence precisely: an 1833 formal non-discrimination promise, an 1853 Act ending patronage and opening the competitive principle, an 1854 Macaulay Committee designing the mechanism, and a first 1855 examination operationalising it.

**Exam move:** Reject any answer that credits 1833 with opening the civil service in practice, or that credits 1853 alone with instantly producing full competitive recruitment; both are promise-versus-practice traps.

### Drill 9 -- State the 1813 Charter Act's three provisions as independently verifiable

**Claim:** Name trade liberalisation (with the tea/China exception), the Crown-sovereignty assertion, and the missionary/education gesture as three separate statutory moves, each checkable on its own.

**Exam move:** This is this topic's single most heavily tested Act; a Prelims statement-set question should be answered statement by statement against this three-part checklist, never by a single undifferentiated "1813 was a reforming Act" impression.

### Drill 10 -- Keep the 1833 Act's two centralisations separate

**Claim:** Name the executive/titular centralisation (Governor-General of Bengal to Governor-General of India) and the legislative centralisation (all-India law-making power concentrated in the Governor-General-in-Council) as two distinct movements that happen to arrive in the same Act.

**Exam move:** A question naming "the Act that created the Governor-General of India" tests the titular centralisation specifically; do not answer it by describing the Law Member or Law Commission instead, which are separate provisions of the same Act.

### Drill 11 -- The 1858 Act and the Queen's Proclamation are two different documents

**Claim:** Name the Government of India Act, 1858 as the constitutional-legal transfer instrument (abolishing Company rule, the Board of Control and the Court of Directors, creating the Secretary of State and Council of India) and the Queen's Proclamation of 1 November 1858 as the separate political-policy announcement (non-interference in religion, treaty recognition, formal equality before law, qualified amnesty).

**Exam move:** A question asking specifically what the 1858 Act itself provided should not be answered with Proclamation content (religious non-interference, amnesty), and vice versa; keep the two documents' content in two separate mental boxes.

### Drill 12 -- State 1858 as rupture-in-sovereignty alongside continuity-in-administration

**Claim:** Name the abolished apex bodies (Company, Board of Control, Court of Directors) alongside the retained or continued elements (the Governor-General's office, now also Viceroy; the 1853-55 civil-service principle; seven of the new Council of India's first fifteen members being ex-Company men) in the same answer.

**Exam move:** A "how much did 1858 actually change" question rewards naming both halves explicitly -- rupture at the apex, continuity below it -- rather than treating 1858 as either a total revolution or a mere formality.

### Drill 13 -- Keep the topic's endpoint at 1858, not beyond it

**Claim:** State explicitly that the Indian Councils Act of 1861 and all later representative-council reforms are Topic 12's material, and that the Revolt of 1857's own causes and course are Topic 11's material, referenced here only as this topic's trigger.

**Exam move:** When a question edges toward post-1858 councils or the Revolt's own detailed narrative, name the boundary explicitly rather than silently absorbing that material as though it belonged to this topic.

### Mains practice: solved model answers

Use the following answer limits: **10 marks = 150 words, 15 marks = 250 words, 20 marks = 250 words.** The counted limit applies to the **Model answer** line only; the answer-plan, marks note and improvement note sit outside the word limit.

### Mains 1 - 10 marks | 150 words

**Question:** Examine why Parliament's 1772-73 intervention in East India Company affairs is better explained by fiscal crisis and political accountability concerns than by humanitarian motive.

**Demand decoding:** Show the proximate cause of intervention, distinguish background famine from immediate trigger, and connect solvency crisis to patronage-and-accountability anxiety.

**Model answer:** Parliament intervened in 1772-73 because the East India Company had become both a territorial sovereign in Bengal and a financially unstable corporation in Britain. The immediate trigger was the Company's cash crisis and its request for a government-backed loan in 1772, which exposed mismanagement to Parliament. British political society also feared the patronage power of returning Company "nabobs" and the constitutional anomaly of a merchant body exercising revenue, war and justice powers without settled accountability. The 1770 Bengal famine formed part of the background evidence of misgovernment, but it was not Parliament's own primary legislative trigger. The Regulating Act, 1773 therefore arose chiefly from fiscal alarm and political-accountability pressure, not from a humanitarian project to protect Bengal's peasantry.

**Executable answer plan:** Open with the 1772 solvency crisis, add the nabob-patronage/accountability concern, then qualify the famine as background rather than proximate cause.

**Why this earns marks:** It names the actual trigger (loan request), links it to British constitutional concern, and resists the common humanitarian oversimplification.

**How to improve this answer:** Add one named parliamentary committee of 1772 if you need a sharper evidence anchor.

### Mains 2 - 10 marks | 150 words

**Question:** Distinguish the institutional design of the Regulating Act, 1773 from that of Pitt's India Act, 1784, with particular reference to the size of the Bengal Council.

**Demand decoding:** Make the comparison explicit: 1773 design, 1784 correction, and why council size mattered constitutionally.

**Model answer:** The Regulating Act, 1773 created a Governor-General of Fort William (Bengal) with a **four-member Council**, a structure that quickly produced deadlock because Hastings could be outvoted by a standing hostile majority. Pitt's India Act, 1784 answered by reducing the council to a **Governor-General-plus-three**, so the Governor-General now needed only one ally for a working majority. The second major design difference was metropolitan: 1773 had imposed reporting duties on the Court of Directors, but 1784 created the **Board of Control** to "superintend, direct and control" civil, military and revenue affairs from Britain. Thus 1773 was Parliament's first imperfect regulatory framework, whereas 1784 was a sharper corrective at both Bengal and London levels.

**Executable answer plan:** Compare under two heads: Bengal executive structure (4 vs 3 council members) and British supervisory structure (reporting duty vs Board of Control).

**Why this earns marks:** It distinguishes both acts institutionally instead of merely listing provisions in sequence.

**How to improve this answer:** Add the bounded follow-up point that the 1786 Act strengthened the Governor-General further without merging it into the 1784 reform.

### Mains 3 - 15 marks | 250 words

**Question:** Evaluate the Charter Act of 1813 as a bundle of three distinct statutory provisions, and explain why this makes it one of this topic's most heavily tested Prelims Acts.

**Demand decoding:** Separate the Act's trade, sovereignty, and missionary-education provisions, then explain why that structure is ideal for statement-based questions.

**Model answer:** The Charter Act of 1813 matters because it combines three independently testable provisions inside one statute. **First**, it ended the Company's monopoly of trade with India for other British subjects, but **retained the tea trade and all trade with China** as Company preserves. **Second**, it asserted the "undoubted sovereignty of the Crown of the United Kingdom" over the Company's Indian territories, reinforcing Crown supremacy without yet abolishing Company administration. **Third**, it permitted Christian missionaries to enter Company territory and earmarked **one lakh rupees annually** for literature, learned Indians and useful knowledge, though actual disbursal lagged for years. UPSC repeatedly tests this Act because each proposition can be separated into true and false statements: one may remember trade opening yet forget the tea-China exception, or recall education while missing the Crown-sovereignty assertion. The Act is therefore a compact bundle of economic, constitutional and cultural provisions whose internal distinctions reward precise elimination.

**Executable answer plan:** Use three subheads—trade, sovereignty, missionary-education—then end by explaining why each becomes an independent Prelims statement.

**Why this earns marks:** It keeps the three clauses analytically separate and links statutory detail directly to UPSC question design.

**How to improve this answer:** Quote the tea/China exception explicitly if you want one more high-yield precision point.

### Mains 4 - 15 marks | 250 words

**Question:** Discuss the two distinct centralisations -- executive/titular and legislative -- introduced together by the Charter Act of 1833.

**Demand decoding:** Explain how 1833 centralised authority in two different ways, and do not collapse title change into law-making change.

**Model answer:** The Charter Act of 1833 centralised British Indian government through **two distinct but simultaneous moves**. The first was **executive/titular centralisation**: the Governor-General of Bengal became the **Governor-General of India**, with Lord William Bentinck the first holder of the all-India title. This widened the constitutional scope of the office beyond Bengal. The second was **legislative centralisation**: legislative authority for all British India was concentrated in the Governor-General-in-Council, reducing the earlier autonomy of presidency governments in law-making. The Act also added a **Law Member**, first held by Macaulay, and provided for a **Law Commission**, deepening the codification project. These developments must be kept separate because a question on "the Act that created the Governor-General of India" tests the titular shift, whereas a question on all-India legislation tests the law-making shift. Alongside both, the Act ended the Company's commercial character, but that is a third, economic change rather than part of either centralisation track.

**Executable answer plan:** Write under three mini-heads: titular centralisation, legislative centralisation, and bounded caveat that commercial wind-up is related but analytically separate.

**Why this earns marks:** It shows why 1833 is a structural turning point without flattening different constitutional changes into one slogan.

**How to improve this answer:** Add one line on why codification remained incomplete within 1833-1858 if the examiner expects a stronger qualification.

### Mains 5 - 20 marks | 250 words

**Question:** Trace the evolution of the Governor-General's office from 1773 to 1858, and examine the institutional significance of each stage.

**Demand decoding:** Track the office chronologically and explain what each step solved: weak Bengal office, deadlock correction, all-India executive, and Crown representation.

**Model answer:** The Governor-Generalship is the clearest institutional ladder in this topic. **In 1773**, the Regulating Act created the Governor-General of Fort William/Bengal, with Warren Hastings its first holder, but the office was structurally weak because a four-member council could outvote it. **In 1784**, Pitt's India Act reduced the council to three members, easing the deadlock problem; **in 1786**, Cornwallis received a bounded override power and combined the office with Commander-in-Chief, strengthening executive authority further. **In 1833**, the Charter Act upgraded the office into the **Governor-General of India**, with William Bentinck first holding the all-India title alongside central legislative authority. **In 1858**, the Government of India Act retained the office but added the **Viceroy** title, with Canning becoming the first Governor-General-and-Viceroy under direct Crown rule. Each stage answered a specific institutional weakness, so the sequence shows the gradual construction of a stronger, more centralised imperial executive.

**Executable answer plan:** Use a four-stage ladder—1773, 1784-86, 1833, 1858—and connect every stage to the problem it corrected.

**Why this earns marks:** It ties title changes to institutional purpose instead of reciting names and dates in isolation.

**How to improve this answer:** Add the office-holders in the opening line of each stage if you want a still sharper factual frame.

### Mains 6 - 20 marks | 250 words

**Question:** "Constitutional reform in colonial India between 1773 and 1858 was a story of parliamentary sovereignty steadily displacing corporate autonomy." Critically evaluate this statement with reference to at least four named Acts.

**Demand decoding:** Test both halves of the quotation—`displacing corporate autonomy` and `steadily`—using named Acts and a final qualification.

**Model answer:** The statement is broadly correct, but only if `steadily` is read as **crisis-punctuated** rather than smooth. The **Regulating Act, 1773** imposed reporting obligations and created a Governor-General-in-Council, signalling that Parliament would no longer ignore Company government. **Pitt's India Act, 1784** went further by creating the Board of Control, so Company administration was now politically supervised from Britain. The **Charter Act of 1813** breached monopoly by opening Indian trade to other British subjects while asserting Crown sovereignty over Company territories. The **Charter Act of 1833** ended the Company's commercial activity altogether, turning it into a purely administrative agency. Finally, the **Government of India Act, 1858** abolished Company rule, the Board of Control and the Court of Directors, completing the transfer to direct Crown sovereignty. Yet the process was not linear in tempo, and displacement of corporate autonomy did not instantly erase continuity in personnel and administrative machinery below the top.

**Executable answer plan:** Use four named acts as rungs, then add one sentence qualifying the word `steadily` and another distinguishing sovereignty from administrative continuity.

**Why this earns marks:** It answers the quotation critically rather than treating it as a prompt for mere chronology.

**How to improve this answer:** Add the 1793 pause or the 1853 legislative/civil-service step if you need one more nuance about uneven pace.
