### Matching answer key and option-by-option reasoning

#### MCQ 01 - Q1

**Answer: A.** `darshana` literally means view/seeing, and historically names a standpoint or way of seeing reality.

- [Q01-A] Correct: `darshana` literally means view/seeing, and historically names a standpoint or way of seeing reality.
- [Q01-B] Wrong: the grouped "six systems" canon is a later doxographical construction, not the original sense of the word.
- [Q01-C] Wrong: this invents an administrative meaning with no textual support.
- [Q01-D] Wrong: `darshana` has no military or ordinance sense in this context.

**Examiner trap:** Candidates who have memorised "six darshanas" default to defining the word as the list itself rather than as the standpoint the word actually names.

#### MCQ 02 - Q2

**Answer: B.** Statement 1 is accurate and statement 2 wrongly claims a settled single-author, precisely dated status for a text the package treats as layered.

- [Q02-A] Wrong: this accepts statement 2, which overclaims textual certainty the sources do not support.
- [Q02-B] Correct: statement 1 is accurate and statement 2 wrongly claims a settled single-author, precisely dated status for a text the package treats as layered.
- [Q02-C] Wrong: this drops the accurate statement 1 and keeps only the overclaiming statement 2.
- [Q02-D] Wrong: this wrongly rejects statement 1, which is a secure classification recorded in the tradition.

**Examiner trap:** Candidates treat a genuinely useful classification as proof of unrelated claims (single authorship, exact date) that the same source does not establish.

#### MCQ 03 - Q3

**Answer: C.** The hymn's value lies in open questioning, not in anticipating one later school.

- [Q03-A] Wrong: Charvaka is a much later, separately attested materialist tradition; the hymn does not found or announce it.
- [Q03-B] Wrong: Vedanta develops from the Upanishads and the Brahma Sutra tradition, centuries of textual layering away from this hymn.
- [Q03-C] Correct: the hymn's value lies in open questioning, not in anticipating one later school.
- [Q03-D] Wrong: the hymn questions rather than rejecting the sacrificial-ritual world it belongs to.

**Examiner trap:** Candidates convert a genuinely open, questioning hymn into proof of a fully formed later school just because both use searching language.

#### MCQ 04 - Q4

**Answer: D.** This restores plurality and institutional grounding as the accurate description.

- [Q04-A] Wrong: this over-corrects into an equally false opposite claim; materialism (Charvaka) was one current among many, not the whole field.
- [Q04-B] Wrong: individual traditions such as Samkhya, Vedic speculation and early Buddhism/Jainism are far older than the Gupta period.
- [Q04-C] Wrong: this ignores the deep social, economic and political embedding (purusharthas, patronage, courts) taught throughout the package.
- [Q04-D] Correct: this restores plurality and institutional grounding as the accurate description.

**Examiner trap:** Candidates who correctly reject "timeless consensus" often overcorrect into an equally false universal claim (pure materialism, or no social connection) instead of the balanced plural-field verdict.

#### MCQ 05 - Q5

**Answer: A.** The Upanishads remain `shruti` while shifting prestige toward brahman, atman, karma, rebirth and release.

- [Q05-A] Correct: the Upanishads remain `shruti` while shifting prestige toward brahman, atman, karma, rebirth and release.
- [Q05-B] Wrong: this reverses the chronology; Puranic compilation is later than the early Upanishadic corpus.
- [Q05-C] Wrong: Agamas belong to the separate Jain canonical tradition, not to the Vedic Upanishads.
- [Q05-D] Wrong: this invents an architectural function unrelated to the Upanishads' actual content.

**Examiner trap:** Candidates who know the Upanishads are "philosophical" sometimes wrongly infer they broke away from the Vedic corpus altogether.

#### MCQ 06 - Q6

**Answer: B.** Urbanisation widened audiences, donors and mobility, which is enabling context, not a monocausal explanation of doctrine.

- [Q06-A] Wrong: Buddhism, Jainism, Ajivikas and materialist critique are conceptually distinct responses, not one identical protest bloc.
- [Q06-B] Correct: urbanisation widened audiences, donors and mobility, which is enabling context, not a monocausal explanation of doctrine.
- [Q06-C] Wrong: this collapses a genuinely rich argumentative field into a single economic cause.
- [Q06-D] Wrong: Mimamsa and Vedanta show continuing Brahmanical ritual-philosophical traditions well beyond this period.

**Examiner trap:** Candidates who correctly cite urbanisation as context then wrongly treat it as a complete causal explanation of specific doctrines.

#### MCQ 07 - Q7

**Answer: C.** `anatman` is the denial of a permanent, independent self-substance, argued through dependent origination and impermanence.

- [Q07-A] Wrong: Buddhism does not deny conventional persons or consequences; it denies only a fixed underlying self behind them.
- [Q07-B] Wrong: karma remains central to early Buddhist ethics and rebirth theory; it is not what `anatman` denies.
- [Q07-C] Correct: `anatman` is the denial of a permanent, independent self-substance, argued through dependent origination and impermanence.
- [Q07-D] Wrong: ethical discipline (the path) is reinforced, not denied, by the non-self teaching.

**Examiner trap:** Candidates equate "no fixed self" with "no persons at all," turning a precise metaphysical denial into an implausible denial of everyday experience.

#### MCQ 08 - Q8

**Answer: D.** This restores the layered, cumulative expansion model in place of the crude "single schism" claim.

- [Q08-A] Wrong: this denies a real and well-attested historical development.
- [Q08-B] Wrong: monasteries and scholastic centres were central to Abhidharma, Madhyamaka and Yogacara transmission.
- [Q08-C] Wrong: Mahayana's roots and early scholastic developments are demonstrably ancient, well before the Gupta period.
- [Q08-D] Correct: this restores the layered, cumulative expansion model in place of the crude "single schism" claim.

**Examiner trap:** Candidates reach for a single dramatic "break" narrative instead of the historically accurate picture of gradual, layered doctrinal growth.

#### MCQ 09 - Q9

**Answer: A.** `shunyata` denies inherent, independent self-existence while allowing conventional, dependently-arisen existence.

- [Q09-A] Correct: `shunyata` denies inherent, independent self-existence while allowing conventional, dependently-arisen existence.
- [Q09-B] Wrong: many-sidedness (`anekantavada`) is a distinct Jaina doctrine, not Madhyamaka's teaching.
- [Q09-C] Wrong: Madhyamaka does not argue for a creator deity; it is a critique of inherent existence.
- [Q09-D] Wrong: fatalism describes Ajivika `niyati`, a separate and unrelated tradition.

**Examiner trap:** Candidates mistake "emptiness" for outright nihilism ("nothing exists") instead of the precise claim that nothing possesses independent inherent essence.

#### MCQ 10 - Q10

**Answer: B.** Yogacara is a bounded historical idea about consciousness and representation, situated within Buddhist soteriology.

- [Q10-A] Wrong: Charvaka is a materialist, perception-privileging school; Yogacara's idealist-leaning analysis of mind is a different project entirely.
- [Q10-B] Correct: Yogacara is a bounded historical idea about consciousness and representation, situated within Buddhist soteriology.
- [Q10-C] Wrong: Vedic ritual hermeneutics is Mimamsa's domain, unconnected to Yogacara.
- [Q10-D] Wrong: Yogacara analyses cognition in detail; it does not deny that cognition occurs.

**Examiner trap:** Candidates caricature any "mind-centred" Buddhist school as simple solipsism instead of a structured analysis of consciousness within a liberation framework.

#### MCQ 11 - Q11

**Answer: C.** `anekantavada` is the many-sidedness thesis, `syadvada` is its conditional-assertion language, and `nayavada` is the doctrine of partial viewpoints.

- [Q11-A] Wrong: this swaps the core doctrine (many-sidedness) with its linguistic expression (conditional assertion).
- [Q11-B] Wrong: `syadvada` (conditional assertion) and `nayavada` (partial viewpoints) are not interchangeable senses.
- [Q11-C] Correct: `anekantavada` is the many-sidedness thesis, `syadvada` is its conditional-assertion language, and `nayavada` is the doctrine of partial viewpoints.
- [Q11-D] Wrong: all three pairings are in fact accurate as stated.

**Examiner trap:** Candidates learn "Jain epistemology = many-sidedness" as one undifferentiated blob and cannot distinguish the doctrine from its qualifying language or its viewpoint theory.

#### MCQ 12 - Q12

**Answer: D.** This restores Ajivikas as a distinct current known chiefly through hostile testimony, anchored by Makkhali Gosala and `niyati`.

- [Q12-A] Wrong: Ajivikas are classed as heterodox (`nastika`), not among the six orthodox systems.
- [Q12-B] Wrong: no securely preserved internal Ajivika scripture survives; the tradition is known mainly through opponents.

### Adjacent PYQ 3 - GS-I Mains 2020 Q11 (adjacent owner route, verbatim demand preserved in Art and Culture owner)

**Model answer:** Indian monuments and art were not mechanically "designed by philosophy," but philosophical traditions strongly shaped their symbolic programmes, ritual use and visual logic. ### Original Mains Question 1 - 10 marks - 150 words

How did early Upanishadic speculation transform the meaning of Vedic ritual without simply rejecting the Vedic world?

**Model answer:** Early Upanishadic thought transformed rather than simply discarded the Vedic world. The Brahmanas and Aranyakas had already begun symbolic interpretation of sacrifice; the early Upanishads pushed this further by shifting prestige toward knowledge of brahman, atman, karma, rebirth and release. Dialogues such as Yajnavalkya's and the teaching of Uddalaka to Shvetaketu show that inner knowledge, not only outer performance, became central. Ritual language was re-read rather than erased: sacrifice became interiorised, equivalence-seeking and philosophically charged. This was not a total break. The Upanishads remain within shruti, and they do not uniformly denounce Vedic ritual. The transformation therefore lies in redirection of authority from external efficacy alone toward reflective knowing and liberation while preserving continuity with the Vedic textual world.

**Why this earns marks:** It gives continuity plus change, names the transition texts and avoids the simplistic "anti-Vedic revolt" line.

### Original Mains Question 6 - 20 marks - 250 words

Trace the movement from late Vedic speculation to classical darshana systematisation, bringing out both continuity and rupture.

**Model answer:** The movement from late Vedic speculation to classical darshana systematisation involved both continuity of vocabulary and rupture in emphasis, method and institutional location. Vedic and later Vedic texts supplied categories such as rta, sacrifice, sacred speech and speculative questioning. Aranyakas and early Upanishads interiorised ritual and developed brahman, atman, karma, rebirth and moksha through dialogue and paradox. The shramana field then radicalised the debate: Buddhism reframed bondage through non-self and conditioned arising; Jainism through soul and karmic matter; Ajivikas through fate; materialists through perceptual critique. Over time, sutra traditions such as Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Mimamsa, Yoga and the Brahma Sutra compressed and systematised distinctive positions. Grammar, commentary and public disputation deepened the field further. Continuity lies in long concern with order, duty, speech, liberation and knowledge. Rupture lies in new critiques of ritual centrality, sharper school identities and more technical epistemological and metaphysical debate. The process was uneven, and later six-school doxography can falsely tidy it up. Ancient Indian philosophy thus moved from layered speculative beginnings to increasingly explicit schools without ever losing the marks of cumulative borrowing and contest.

**Why this earns marks:** It is chronological, comparative and source-aware, which is exactly what a long GS-I answer on this topic requires.
